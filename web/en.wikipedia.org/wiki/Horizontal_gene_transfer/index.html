Horizontal gene transfer

Horizontal (or lateral) gene transfer is the movement of genetic material between organisms other than by parent-to-offspring inheritance.


Bacterial conjugation, one route of horizontal gene transfer.

Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) is the movement of genetic material between organisms other than by the ("vertical") transmission of DNA from parent to offspring during reproduction. It is a major force in the evolution of bacteria and archaea, and also occurs, less frequently, in eukaryotes. HGT is the primary mechanism for the spread of antibiotic resistance in bacteria, and plays an important role in the evolution of bacteria that can degrade novel compounds such as human-made pesticides.

Mechanisms in prokaryotes

Three classic mechanisms move DNA between bacterial cells. In transformation, competent cells take up free DNA from their environment — the phenomenon Griffith demonstrated in 1928 with pneumococci. In transduction, bacteriophages accidentally package and carry host DNA between cells. In conjugation, a donor cell transfers a plasmid (or chromosomal DNA in Hfr strains) to a recipient through a sex pilus. Mobile genetic elements — plasmids, transposons, integrons and prophages — are the chief vehicles of transfer.

History and discovery

Evidence accumulated from the 1940s (Avery's transforming principle, Lederberg and Tatum's conjugation experiments, Zinder and Lederberg's transduction) and exploded with genome sequencing, which revealed that bacterial genomes are mosaics of vertically inherited and horizontally acquired DNA. Some scientists argue this makes the early "tree of life" better pictured as a web or network.

In eukaryotes

HGT also occurs in eukaryotes: endosymbiotic gene transfer moved most genes of the mitochondrial and plastid ancestors into the host nucleus; bdelloid rotifers carry many genes of bacterial and fungal origin; parasitic plants exchange genes with hosts; and viruses can pick up and deliver host genes. Claims of extensive bacterial-to-human gene transfer in the draft human genome sequence were largely retracted on reanalysis, though some transferred genes in animals (e.g. carotenoid genes in aphids from fungi) are well documented.

Significance

HGT accelerates innovation by letting organisms acquire ready-made adaptations — antibiotic resistance, virulence factors, new metabolic pathways — in a single step. It complicates phylogenetics and species concepts in microbiology, and has practical importance in medicine, agriculture and biotechnology.
