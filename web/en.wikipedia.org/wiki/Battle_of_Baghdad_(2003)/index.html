Battle of Baghdad (2003)

The Battle of Baghdad, also known as the Fall of Baghdad, was the concluding battle of the invasion phase of the Iraq War, fought in April 2003. After a three-week advance from Kuwait, United States forces — principally the Army's 3rd Infantry Division and the 1st Marine Division — seized Baghdad International Airport, conducted armored "thunder runs" through the capital, and by 9 April 2003 had taken control of the city, ending the rule of Saddam Hussein.

Background

The 2003 invasion of Iraq began on 20 March 2003. Coalition forces advanced rapidly north, bypassing many cities, and destroyed Iraq's best units in engagements at Nasiriyah, Najaf and Karbala. By early April, American forces had closed on Baghdad, whose defense rested on the Republican Guard divisions deployed around the capital.

Battle

On 3–4 April the 3rd Infantry Division captured Baghdad International Airport. On 5 April, a battalion-sized armored column — the first "thunder run" — drove through the city to test defenses, and on 7 April a second, larger thunder run seized key government districts, including presidential palaces on the Tigris, and stayed. Marines crossed the Diyala River from the east, fighting at the Baghdad University area. Resistance collapsed; on 9 April Marines assisted Iraqis in pulling down the statue of Saddam Hussein in Firdos Square before television cameras, and organized fighting in the capital ended, though looting and disorder followed.

Aftermath

The fall of Baghdad ended the conventional phase of the war; President George W. Bush declared the end of major combat operations on 1 May 2003. Saddam Hussein went into hiding and was captured in December 2003. The collapse of order in Baghdad, including widespread looting of ministries and museums, foreshadowed the insurgency that followed.
