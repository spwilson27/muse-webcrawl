Audi A4

The Audi A4 is a line of luxury compact executive cars produced since 1994 by the German car manufacturer Audi, a subsidiary of the Volkswagen Group. The A4 has been built in five generations and is available as a sedan and as a station wagon (badged the Avant). It succeeded the Audi 80 series and competes principally with the BMW 3 Series and Mercedes-Benz C-Class. Performance versions are sold as the S4 and RS 4.

Generations

  • B5 (1994–2001): launched the A4 name with a new platform, five-valve engines and the Tiptronic automatic gearbox; the first RS 4 Avant appeared in 1999 with a 380 PS biturbo V6.
  • B6 (2000–2006): stiffer body, new multitronic CVT option and the return of a cabriolet body style.
  • B7 (2004–2008): a heavy facelift of the B6 with revised styling, FSI direct-injection engines and a new RS 4 with a 420 PS V8.
  • B8 (2007–2015): moved to the modular longitudinal platform (MLB), relocating the engine behind the front axle line for better weight distribution; introduced the related A5 coupé.
  • B9 (2015–2024): lighter MLB Evo platform, virtual cockpit digital instruments and a range of turbocharged petrol and diesel engines with mild-hybrid assistance.

End of the nameplate

Under Audi's revised naming strategy of the 2020s, even numbers were reserved for electric vehicles and odd numbers for combustion models; accordingly the A4's combustion successor was launched in 2024 as the new Audi A5, ending three decades of the A4 badge. The A4 name is expected to return on a future electric model.

Motorsport

The A4 achieved major touring-car success: Frank Biela won the 1996 British Touring Car Championship and the worldwide Super Touring cup series titles in the quattro A4, and A4 DTM cars won the German touring car championship in 2004, 2007, 2008 and 2009.
