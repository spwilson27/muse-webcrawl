IPTG (isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside) is a molecular biology reagent used to induce protein expression under the control of the lac operator. It is a synthetic analogue of allolactose, the natural inducer of the lac operon in Escherichia coli.

Mechanism

IPTG binds the lac repressor (LacI), causing it to release the operator DNA and allowing transcription of genes downstream of the lac promoter. Unlike lactose or allolactose, IPTG is not hydrolysed by β-galactosidase — the sulfur in its thioglycosidic bond makes it resistant to cleavage — so its concentration stays constant during an experiment. For this reason IPTG is called a gratuitous inducer: it induces the operon without being metabolised.

Uses

IPTG is routinely used with expression plasmids (such as the pET system in strain BL21(DE3), where it induces T7 RNA polymerase) to trigger production of recombinant proteins. Together with the chromogenic substrate X-gal, it is used in blue–white screening of bacterial colonies during cloning: colonies that take up a plasmid with an intact lacZα fragment turn blue on X-gal/IPTG plates, while colonies with an insert disrupting lacZα remain white.

Handling

IPTG is typically used at concentrations of 0.1–1 mM in culture media. Because it is not consumed, induction strength depends on the concentration added, allowing tunable expression in research settings.
