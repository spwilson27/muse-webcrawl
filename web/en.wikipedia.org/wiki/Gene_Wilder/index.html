Gene Wilder

Gene Wilder (born Jerome Silberman; June 11, 1933 – August 29, 2016) was an American actor, comedian, writer, and filmmaker. Known for his neurotic yet gentle comic persona, he is best remembered for his collaborations with Mel Brooks and Richard Pryor, and for playing Willy Wonka in Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory (1971).

Early life

Wilder was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, to Jeanne (Baer) and William J. Silberman, a manufacturer of novelty items. He first took an interest in acting at age eight to cheer up his mother, who had suffered a heart attack. He studied communication and theatre arts at the University of Iowa and later trained at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School in England and with Lee Strasberg at the Actors Studio in New York.

Career

After stage work, Wilder made his film debut in Bonnie and Clyde (1967) and gained wide attention as Leo Bloom in Mel Brooks's The Producers (1967), earning an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actor. He starred as Willy Wonka in 1971, and achieved major success with Brooks's Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein (both 1974), the latter co-written with Brooks and earning them an Oscar nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay. With Richard Pryor he made a popular comic pairing in Silver Streak (1976), Stir Crazy (1980), See No Evil, Hear No Evil (1989), and Another You (1991). Wilder also directed several films, including The Woman in Red (1984). He later largely retired from acting to write, publishing a memoir, Kiss Me Like a Stranger (2005), and several novels.

Personal life and death

Wilder was married four times; his marriage to comedian Gilda Radner lasted until her death from ovarian cancer in 1989, after which he co-founded Gilda's Club, a support organization for people living with cancer. He died in 2016 of complications from Alzheimer's disease, a diagnosis his family kept private during his lifetime.
