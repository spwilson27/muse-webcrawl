CHSH inequality

CHSH inequality
Field: Quantum mechanics
Proposed: 1969
Authors: Clauser, Horne, Shimony, Holt
Classical bound: |S| ≤ 2
Quantum maximum: 2√2 (Tsirelson bound)

The CHSH inequality is a form of Bell's inequality proposed in 1969 by John Clauser, Michael Horne, Abner Shimony and Richard Holt. It gives a testable bound on correlations between measurements on two separated systems that any local hidden-variable theory must obey, but which quantum mechanics can violate.

Formulation

Two observers each choose between two measurement settings and obtain outcomes ±1. Combining the four correlation functions gives a quantity S. Local realism requires |S| ≤ 2, whereas quantum mechanics permits up to 2√2 ≈ 2.828, the Tsirelson bound.

Experiments

Experiments beginning with those of Alain Aspect in 1982 consistently violated the CHSH inequality in agreement with quantum mechanics. "Loophole-free" Bell tests in 2015 simultaneously closed the detection and locality loopholes; Clauser, Aspect and Anton Zeilinger shared the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics for this work.
