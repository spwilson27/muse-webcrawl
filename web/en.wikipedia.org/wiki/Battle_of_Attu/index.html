The Battle of Attu, codenamed Operation Landcrab, was fought between 11 and 30 May 1943 on Attu Island in Alaska's Aleutian chain. It was the only battle of the Second World War fought on incorporated American territory, and it ended the Japanese occupation of the island that had begun in June 1942 as part of the Aleutian Islands Campaign.

Background

Japanese forces had occupied Attu and neighbouring Kiska in June 1942, partly as a diversion for the Midway operation. The garrison on Attu numbered about 2,600 men under Colonel Yasuyo Yamasaki. In May 1943 a US task force under Rear Admiral Thomas Kinkaid landed some 11,000 troops of the 7th Infantry Division, many of them trained for desert warfare and poorly equipped for the Aleutian cold, fog and muskeg.

The battle

American landings at Massacre Bay and Holtz Bay on 11 May met stubborn resistance from Japanese positions in the hills. Progress was slow in atrocious weather, and the initial commander was replaced by Major General Eugene Landrum. Gradually the Japanese were compressed into a pocket around Chichagof Harbor.

On the night of 29 May, Yamasaki led one of the largest banzai charges of the Pacific War: the remaining Japanese troops broke through the American front line and fought hand-to-hand in rear areas before being annihilated. Of the garrison, only 28 men were taken prisoner; the rest died, many by suicide. American losses were about 549 killed and over 1,100 wounded, with thousands more suffering cold-weather injuries.

Aftermath

With Attu retaken, the Japanese evacuated Kiska unobserved in July 1943, ending the Aleutian campaign. The battle's ferocity, and the mass suicide charge, foreshadowed later island battles such as Saipan and Iwo Jima.
