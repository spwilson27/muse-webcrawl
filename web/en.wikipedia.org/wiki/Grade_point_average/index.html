Grade point average

Transcripts typically report term and cumulative GPAs alongside course grades.

The grade point average (GPA) is a number representing the average value of a student's accumulated final grades in a course of study. It is calculated by converting letter (or percentage) grades into grade points, multiplying each course's points by its credit weight, and dividing the total by the number of credits attempted. GPA is most widely used in the United States and Canada, and in systems modelled on them, though analogous averages exist worldwide.

The 4.0 scale

The most common scale assigns points to letter grades as follows, with plus and minus modifiers at many institutions:

Letter gradeGrade points
A4.0
B3.0
C2.0
D1.0
F0.0

Plus and minus grades commonly adjust by 0.3 (e.g. B+ = 3.3, A− = 3.7), and some schools award 4.3 for an A+. A GPA of 4.0 therefore represents straight A's. For example, a student earning an A (4.0) in a 3-credit course and a B (3.0) in a 4-credit course has a GPA of (4.0×3 + 3.0×4) / 7 = 24/7 ≈ 3.43.

Weighted and unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats all courses equally on the 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA, common in U.S. high schools, awards extra points for advanced courses — honours, Advanced Placement (AP) or International Baccalaureate (IB) classes — so that an A in an AP course may count as 5.0, allowing cumulative weighted GPAs above 4.0. Universities typically recalculate or consider both figures, and weighting practices differ enough between schools that direct comparison is difficult.

Term, cumulative and major GPA

Institutions report GPA per term (semester/quarter) and cumulatively over a student's whole enrolment; a "major GPA" restricts the calculation to courses in the student's field. Academic standing policies — Dean's List honours (often 3.5+), academic probation (often below 2.0), and graduation requirements — are usually defined in GPA terms. Latin honours (cum laude, magna cum laude, summa cum laude) are often, though not always, awarded on cumulative GPA thresholds.

International variations

Many countries use different scales: percentage marks (India, China), 10-point CGPA (India, the Netherlands roughly reversed in meaning), German grades from 1.0 (best) to 5.0, or British degree classifications rather than averages. Conversion tables published by credential evaluators and the ECTS grading system in Europe attempt to map between systems, but any conversion is approximate, since grade distributions differ between institutions and countries.

Criticism

Critics point to grade inflation, the compression of achievement into a single number, incentives to choose easier courses, and the stress associated with GPA competition. Defenders note that GPA correlates moderately with later academic success and provides a simple common metric for admissions and employers.
