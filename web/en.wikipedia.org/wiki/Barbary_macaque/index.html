Overview

The Barbary macaque (Macaca sylvanus) is a macaque species native to the Atlas Mountains of Algeria and Morocco, with an introduced — and famous — population on the Rock of Gibraltar, which makes it the only wild non-human primate in Europe. It is unusual among primates for its near-taillessness (hence the misleading name "Barbary ape").

Biology and conservation

Barbary macaques live in multi-male, multi-female troops of 10–100, are notable for extensive male care of infants (males use infants as social "buffers" in encounters), and endure snowy Atlas winters with long, dense fur. The species is listed as Endangered: wild populations (perhaps 8,000–10,000, mostly in Morocco's Middle Atlas cedar forests) fall to habitat loss and the illegal pet and photo-prop trade. Gibraltar's roughly 200–300 "rock apes" are a tourist emblem, managed by the Gibraltar Ornithological and Natural History Society; legend holds Britain will keep the Rock while the apes remain — Churchill ordered reinforcements in 1942.
