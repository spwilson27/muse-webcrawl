Dongbei

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dongbei (simplified Chinese: 东北; traditional Chinese: 東北; pinyin: Dōngběi), literally "the Northeast", is a region of China corresponding broadly to historical Manchuria. It comprises the three provinces of Liaoning, Jilin, and Heilongjiang; the four prefecture-level cities of eastern Inner Mongolia are sometimes included in broader definitions.

Geography and climate

Dongbei borders Russia, North Korea, and Mongolia, and is drained by the Songhua, Liao, and Amur rivers. It has fertile black soils (chernozem) that make it one of China's principal grain and soybean belts, and a severe continental climate with long, bitterly cold winters — Harbin, the largest northern city, is famous for its ice festival.

Economy and culture

In the planned-economy era Dongbei was China's heavy-industry heartland, built around steel at Anshan, oil at Daqing, and machinery at Shenyang. Since the 1990s its state-owned industries have struggled, earning it the nickname of China's "rust belt", and prompting state "Revitalize the Northeast" campaigns. The region has a distinctive dialect (Northeastern Mandarin), hearty wheat- and stew-based cuisine, and a reputation in Chinese popular culture for blunt humour, nurtured by errenzhuan folk theatre and television comedy.
