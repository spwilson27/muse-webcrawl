The Special Committee on the Situation with regard to the Implementation of the Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples, commonly known as the Special Committee on Decolonization or C-24, is a committee of the United Nations General Assembly established in 1961 to monitor the implementation of the Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples (General Assembly Resolution 1514 (XV) of 14 December 1960).

History

The committee was created by General Assembly Resolution 1654 (XVI) on 27 November 1961, initially with 17 members; its membership was later expanded to 24, giving rise to its informal name "Committee of 24". Its mandate is to examine the application of the 1960 Declaration, to review political, economic, social, and educational conditions in Non-Self-Governing Territories, and to make recommendations to the General Assembly on the progress and extent of decolonization.

Work

The Special Committee holds an annual session in New York, conducts visiting missions to territories with the consent of the administering powers, and organises regional seminars, alternating between the Caribbean and the Pacific, to hear the views of representatives of the territories. Each year the committee adopts resolutions and decisions on individual territories and on cross-cutting issues, which are transmitted to the General Assembly, principally through its Fourth Committee (Special Political and Decolonization).

Non-Self-Governing Territories

As of the 2020s, 17 territories remain on the United Nations list of Non-Self-Governing Territories, including American Samoa, Anguilla, Bermuda, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, the Falkland Islands (Malvinas), French Polynesia, Gibraltar, Guam, Montserrat, New Caledonia, Pitcairn, Saint Helena, Tokelau, the Turks and Caicos Islands, the United States Virgin Islands, and Western Sahara. The General Assembly has proclaimed successive International Decades for the Eradication of Colonialism, the fourth covering 2021–2030.

Criticism

Administering powers such as the United States and the United Kingdom have at times boycotted or criticised the committee, arguing that it applies an outdated anti-colonial framework and disregards the freely expressed wishes of territorial populations. Supporters counter that the committee remains the principal UN forum dedicated to completing the decolonization process.
