The Kataeb Regulatory Forces (Arabic: قوات الكتائب النظامية), also known as the Phalange militia, was the military wing of the Lebanese Kataeb Party (Phalange) during the early years of the Lebanese Civil War. Founded as a party defence organisation in 1961, it grew by 1975 into one of the largest Christian militias in Lebanon.

History

The Kataeb Party, founded by Pierre Gemayel, maintained security detachments that were formalised as the Regulatory Forces in 1961, partly in response to the 1958 Lebanon crisis. Led in wartime by William Hawi and later by Bashir Gemayel, the militia fought the alliance of Palestinian factions and Lebanese leftist and Muslim movements from April 1975, including in the battles for the Beirut hotel district and the sieges of Karantina and Tel al-Zaatar.

Lebanese Forces

In 1976 the Kataeb militia became the core of the Lebanese Forces (LF), an umbrella of Christian militias. In July 1980, Bashir Gemayel forcibly unified the Christian militias under his command in the "Unification of the Rifle" campaign, absorbing the Regulatory Forces fully into the LF. After the war, the Kataeb Party retained no independent militia.
