Patrice Lumumba

Patrice Émery Lumumba (2 July 1925 – 17 January 1961) was a Congolese politician and independence leader who served as the first Prime Minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from June until September 1960.

Independence

Lumumba founded the MNC and won the 1960 elections. At independence ceremonies on 30 June 1960 he delivered a famous speech denouncing colonialism. Within days the army mutinied and Katanga seceded under Moise Tshombe with Belgian support, beginning the Congo Crisis.

Downfall and murder

Dismissed by President Kasa-Vubu amid Cold War tensions, Lumumba was arrested by Mobutu's forces, transferred to Katanga, and executed in January 1961 with Belgian involvement; U.S. and Belgian plots against him were later documented. Belgium formally apologised in 2002 and returned his tooth, a relic, to his family in 2022.

Legacy

He is a national hero in Congo and a symbol of African independence. Laurent-Désiré Kabila claimed his mantle; see Zaire.
