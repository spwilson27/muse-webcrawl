65803 Didymos (provisional designation 1996 GT) is a sub-kilometre near-Earth asteroid and potentially hazardous object belonging to the Apollo group. It forms a binary system with a small satellite, Dimorphos. Didymos gained worldwide attention as the target system of NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART), the first mission to deliberately alter the motion of a celestial body, and of the European Space Agency's follow-up Hera mission.

Discovery and naming

Didymos was discovered on 11 April 1996 by the Spacewatch survey at Kitt Peak National Observatory. Its binary nature was revealed in 2003 through photometric and radar observations from Arecibo and other observatories. The name "Didymos" is Greek for "twin", referring to the binary system; the satellite's name, Dimorphos, means "having two forms", anticipating that its orbit would be changed by human intervention.

Physical characteristics

The primary body is roughly 780 metres in mean diameter, an oblate, top-shaped rubble-pile typical of rapidly rotating asteroids; its rotation period of about 2.26 hours is close to the limit at which loose material would fly off its equator. Dimorphos, about 160 metres across, orbited Didymos at a distance of about 1.18 km with a period of 11 hours 55 minutes before the DART impact. The system is classified as an S-type (siliceous) asteroid.

DART and Hera

On 26 September 2022, the DART spacecraft struck Dimorphos at about 6.6 km/s, shortening its orbital period by roughly 33 minutes — far more than the success threshold — and demonstrating kinetic impactor deflection as a planetary defence technique. The impact ejected a large plume and tail of debris observed by telescopes worldwide. ESA's Hera spacecraft, launched in October 2024, is travelling to the system to survey the impact crater and measure Dimorphos's mass and internal structure, completing the assessment of the deflection experiment.
