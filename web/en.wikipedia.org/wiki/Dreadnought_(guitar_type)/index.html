Dreadnought (guitar type)

The dreadnought is a type of acoustic guitar body developed by American guitar manufacturer C. F. Martin & Company. The style, which was developed in 1916, features a large body with a square shoulder design. The dreadnought body style was designed in 1916 to combine a larger body, and hence greater volume, with a tone that suited vocal accompaniment; it was named after the HMS Dreadnought, a large British battleship, because of the guitar body's larger size compared with other acoustic guitars of the era.

The dreadnought quickly became one of the most popular acoustic guitar body shapes, widely copied by other manufacturers, and is especially associated with bluegrass, folk, country, and rock music, where its strong bass response and projection allow it to hold its own alongside other instruments.

History

Martin developed the dreadnought body in 1916 for the Oliver Ditson Company, a Boston-based music publisher and retailer, which sold the instruments under its own brand. Following Ditson's closure in the late 1920s, Martin began producing dreadnoughts under its own name in 1931, initially with models such as the D-1 and D-2. These early Martin dreadnoughts had 12 frets clear of the body; in 1934 Martin shifted to the 14-fret design, lengthening the neck joint, which became the standard configuration.

Early dreadnoughts featured a slotted headstock and a wider neck, but the design soon settled into the familiar form: a body roughly 15.6 inches (40 cm) wide at the lower bout, with square shoulders, a solid headstock, and a 25.4-inch scale length.

Characteristics

The dreadnought's large body produces a loud, rich tone with pronounced bass. Traditionally it is built with a spruce top and mahogany or rosewood back and sides. Its volume and strong low end make it popular for flatpicking and strumming, although some players find its bass-heavy balance less suited to delicate fingerstyle playing. Many modern variants exist, including cutaway models and dreadnoughts with modified bracing or body depths.
