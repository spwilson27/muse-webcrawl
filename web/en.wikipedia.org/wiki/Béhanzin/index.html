Béhanzin (c. 1845 – 10 December 1906) was the eleventh (not counting Adandozan) King of Dahomey, in present-day Benin, reigning from 1889 until his deposition by France in 1894. He is remembered for his resistance to French colonisation during the Franco-Dahomean Wars.

Reign and resistance

Béhanzin, born Prince Kondo, succeeded his father Glele in 1889. He refused to cede territory and trading rights demanded by France and fought two wars (1890 and 1892–1894) against French forces under Alfred-Amédée Dodds. Dahomean forces, including the famous women soldiers known to Europeans as the Dahomey Amazons, resisted fiercely, but French firepower prevailed; Béhanzin burned his capital Abomey before fleeing, and surrendered in January 1894.

Exile and legacy

The French exiled Béhanzin first to Martinique and then to Algeria, where he died in 1906. His remains were returned to Abomey in 1928. Béhanzin is honoured in Benin as a national hero and symbol of resistance; the royal treasures looted from his palaces became the subject of restitution claims, and France returned 26 royal works to Benin in 2021.
