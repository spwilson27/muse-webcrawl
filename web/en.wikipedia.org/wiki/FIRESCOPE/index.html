FIRESCOPE

FIRESCOPE (Firefighting Resources of Southern California Organized for Potential Emergencies) is a cooperative programme established in California in 1972 in response to disastrous wildland fires in 1970 that exposed failures of coordination among fire agencies. It is best known for developing the Incident Command System (ICS), which became the national standard for emergency incident management in the United States.

History

After the 1970 fire season, in which 16 people died and hundreds of homes were destroyed during a 13-day period, Congress funded research to improve multi-agency fire response. Partner agencies including the U.S. Forest Service, the California Department of Forestry, and county and city fire departments developed common terminology, modular organisation, unified command, and standardised resource typing and communications.

Legacy

The Incident Command System developed under FIRESCOPE was adopted nationwide and, after the September 11 attacks, incorporated into the National Incident Management System (NIMS) in 2004. FIRESCOPE continues as a partnership that maintains California's incident management standards, resource typing and mutual-aid coordination.
