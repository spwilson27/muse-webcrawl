Broadcasting House is the headquarters of the BBC, located in Portland Place and Langham Place in central London. The original Art Deco building was designed by George Val Myer with F. J. Watson and opened in 1932 as the BBC's first purpose-built home for radio broadcasting. Its Portland stone facade features sculptures by Eric Gill — Prospero and Ariel over the entrance — and works by Gilbert Bayes representing the elements, which attracted controversy at the time and since.

The building

The original block contains the historic Radio Theatre (concert hall) and a suite of studios arranged around a central core, built like a stone-clad liner. The building survived Second World War bombing, during which broadcasts continued from within. In the 2000s a major redevelopment added a large modern extension east of the original building, completed in phases and fully occupied by 2013. The extension contains the BBC Newsroom — one of the largest live newsrooms in the world — and studios for BBC News, the BBC World Service and BBC Radio; the project also created the open Piazza and a memorial to journalists killed on duty on the roof line.

Occupants

Broadcasting House is home to BBC Radio 3, Radio 4 and the World Service, the BBC News division, and programmes including the Today programme, the BBC News at Six and Ten, and The One Show, which broadcasts from a studio at the front of the building. The consolidation brought together BBC journalism formerly spread across Television Centre, Bush House and other London sites.
