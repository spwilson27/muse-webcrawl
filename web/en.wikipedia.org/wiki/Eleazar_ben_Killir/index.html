Eleazar ben Killir

EraLate 6th – early 7th century (Byzantine Palestine)
OccupationPaytan (liturgical poet)
Known forPiyyutim (liturgical poems) in classical Hebrew

Eleazar ben Killir (also spelled Qillir, Kallir, or Kalir) was a Byzantine-era Jewish poet from the Land of Israel, conventionally dated to the late 6th or early 7th century CE. He was the most prolific and influential of the early paytanim, the composers of piyyutim — liturgical poems inserted into Jewish prayer services.

Works

Killir composed hundreds of piyyutim for the festivals, fast days, and special Sabbaths of the liturgical year: kerovot for the Amidah prayer, poems for the Shema, elegies (kinnot) for Tisha B'Av, and poems for weddings and circumcisions. Many of his compositions entered both Ashkenazi and Sephardi prayer books and are still recited today. His name is preserved in acrostics within his poems.

Style and language

His Hebrew is famously dense and innovative: he coined new words, used rare biblical forms, and packed his verse with allusions to rabbinic midrash, making his poems difficult even for medieval commentators, who wrote extensive explanations. Legend embroidered his biography — including tales about his rivalry with the poet Yannai and the origin of his name — but little of his actual life is known.

Legacy

Eleazar ben Killir's work set the model for classical piyyut and strongly influenced later Hebrew liturgical poetry in Palestine, Italy, and Ashkenaz.
