San Luis Potosí

San Luis Potosí is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of the same name. Located in north-central Mexico at an elevation of about 1,864 metres, the city was founded on 3 November 1592 and became one of the most important mining and commercial centres of colonial New Spain. Its historic centre, with well-preserved colonial churches and plazas, was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro in 2010.

History

The city takes its name from Saint Louis IX of France and from Potosí, the fabulously rich silver mountain in Bolivia, reflecting hopes — largely fulfilled — that the mines of Cerro de San Pedro nearby would rival it in wealth. Gold and silver discoveries in 1592 drew miners to the arid highland region, whose land had been contested in the Chichimeca War. San Luis Potosí later played a role in Mexican independence and reform politics; in 1863, during the French intervention, Benito Juárez briefly made it the capital of the republic. In 1910 Francisco I. Madero drafted the Plan de San Luis Potosí, the call to arms that began the Mexican Revolution.

Geography and climate

The city sits on the Mexican Plateau with a semi-arid climate: mild days, cool nights, and most rain falling in summer. Its metropolitan area, including the adjacent city of Soledad de Graciano Sánchez, has a population of well over one million.

Economy

Modern San Luis Potosí is a major industrial and logistics hub, with automotive plants, steel, food processing, and mining-related industries. Its central location on the Mexico City–Monterrey–United States corridor makes it a key transport crossroads.

Culture

Landmarks include the cathedral, the churches of San Francisco and El Carmen, the Alameda park, the Teatro de la Paz, and the Leonora Carrington Museum. The city's dramatic Holy Week silent procession (Procesión del Silencio) is among Mexico's most famous.
