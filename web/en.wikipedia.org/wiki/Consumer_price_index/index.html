Consumer price index

The consumer price index (CPI) is a price index measuring the average change over time in the prices paid by consumers for a representative market basket of goods and services. It is the most widely used measure of inflation.

Methodology

Statistical agencies (the Bureau of Labor Statistics in the US, the ONS in the UK) survey tens of thousands of prices monthly across categories such as food, housing, transport, health and recreation, weighted by household expenditure shares. The index is typically a Laspeyres-type fixed-basket index, rebased periodically.

Uses

  • Indexing Social Security benefits, pensions and tax brackets to inflation.
  • Adjusting wages and contracts (cost-of-living adjustments).
  • Guiding monetary policy: central banks target inflation, e.g. the Federal Reserve's 2% target measured with PCE but watched alongside CPI.

Limitations

The CPI faces substitution bias, quality-change bias (hedonic adjustment), and may not reflect any individual household's experience. Core CPI excludes volatile food and energy prices.
