Field: Text linguistics
Key theorist: Egon Werlich

Text types

Text types in literature theory are classifications of texts based on their dominant communicative purpose and internal structure. German linguist Egon Werlich distinguished five idealised types: narration, description, exposition, argumentation and instruction (directive texts). Real texts usually combine several types, with one predominating.

The five types

Narrative texts recount events in time (novels, reports of incidents). Descriptive texts portray phenomena in space (travel writing, product descriptions). Expository texts explain concepts, either by identification or analysis (essays, textbooks). Argumentative texts evaluate propositions and seek agreement or disagreement (editorials, reviews). Instructive texts aim to direct the reader's behaviour (recipes, manuals, laws).

Use

The typology is widely used in writing pedagogy and translation studies, where recognising a text's type helps writers and translators choose appropriate structure, tense and register. It relates to, but differs from, genre, which is defined by social convention and form.
