Bökh

Bökh is the traditional form of Mongolian wrestling and one of the "three games of men" (with horse racing and archery) performed at the Naadam festival. Wrestlers (bökh) wear distinctive open-chested jackets called zodog, and matches are won by forcing any part of the opponent's body other than the feet to touch the ground.

Tradition

Bökh has been practised for centuries and is documented as early as the era of the Mongol Empire. Wrestlers earn animal-themed titles such as falcon, elephant, lion and giant (avarga) according to the number of rounds won at Naadam. The open-chested jacket is traditionally explained by a legend of a female wrestler who defeated all comers.

Rules

There is no weight class and historically no time limit at Naadam; matches use ritualised movements, including an eagle dance performed before and after each bout.
