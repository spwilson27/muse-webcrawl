Jicky is a perfume by the French house of Guerlain, created in 1889 by Aimé Guerlain. It is widely regarded as one of the first modern perfumes, because it was among the earliest fragrances to combine natural essences with synthetic aroma chemicals, notably coumarin and vanillin. The fragrance is generally classified as a fougère and, although today marketed mainly to women, was originally aimed at men.

History

Aimé Guerlain named the perfume, according to family tradition, after a childhood sweetheart, or alternatively after his nephew Jacques Guerlain, whose nickname was "Jicky". The composition was revolutionary for its time: alongside lavender, bergamot, lemon and rosemary it employed synthetic coumarin — first isolated from tonka beans and synthesised in the 1860s — and vanillin, which gave the scent its characteristic warm, powdery sweetness. A trace of civet added a sensual, animalic undertone.

Composition

Jicky opens with bright citrus and aromatic lavender before settling into a soft heart of herbs and a base of vanilla, tonka and musk-like notes. Perfume historians often pair it with Fougère Royale by Houbigant (1882) as a founding work of modern perfumery. The fragrance has remained in production for well over a century, although its formula has been adjusted over the years, including in response to regulations restricting ingredients such as civet and certain musks.

Legacy

Jicky is frequently cited in lists of the greatest perfumes ever made and is preserved in the collection of the Osmothèque, the perfume archive in Versailles. It established a template — the abstract composition blending naturals and synthetics — that came to define twentieth-century perfumery.
