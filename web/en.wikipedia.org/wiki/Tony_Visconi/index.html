Anthony Edward Visconti (born 24 April 1944) is an American record producer, musician and singer. Since the late 1960s he has worked with many highly influential acts, most famously David Bowie, producing or co-producing albums across five decades of Bowie's career, from Space Oddity (1969) to the final album Blackstar (2016).

Career

Raised in Brooklyn, Visconti played in show bands before moving to London in 1967 as an in-house producer for Essex Music. He discovered and produced T. Rex, engineering Marc Bolan's glam-rock breakthrough with albums such as Electric Warrior (1971), and produced the Moody Blues' later work including Sur la Mer (1988). With Bowie he produced The Man Who Sold the World, Diamond Dogs, the Berlin-era albums Low, "Heroes" and Lodger, Scary Monsters, and the comeback records Heathen, Reality, The Next Day and Blackstar, also playing bass and recorder in the short-lived band Hype. His Eventide Harmonizer treatment of Dennis Davis's snare on Low helped define the gated drum sound of the 1980s.

Later work

Visconti has also produced Morrissey (Ringleader of the Tormentors), The Kaiser Chiefs, Angélique Kidjo, Esperanza Spalding and Dandy Warhols offshoots, and co-founded the Bowie tribute band Holy Holy. His autobiography, Bowie, Bolan and the Brooklyn Boy, appeared in 2007.
