Fish meal

Fish meal is a commercial product made from whole wild-caught fish, bycatch and fish by-products (trimmings), cooked, pressed, dried and ground into a protein-rich powder. It is used mainly as a high-protein feed ingredient in aquaculture, livestock and pet food, and historically as fertiliser.

Main producersPeru, Chile, China, Thailand, United States
Typical protein60–72%
Key sourcesAnchoveta, menhaden, sandeel, capelin, trimmings
Co-productFish oil

Production

Peru's anchoveta fishery is the world's largest source. Whole fish are cooked, pressed to separate oil and press cake, dried and milled; antioxidants are added to prevent spontaneous combustion during shipping. Fish meal grades vary by protein, ash and freshness (TVN, histamine).

Uses

Aquaculture consumes the majority of global supply, feeding farmed salmon, shrimp and marine fish; poultry, pigs and pets account for most of the rest. Its amino-acid profile, digestibility and omega-3 content make it hard to replace fully, though insect meal, algal and single-cell proteins are growing alternatives.

Environmental concerns

Reduction fisheries divert forage fish from marine food webs and from direct human consumption in regions such as West Africa, where fishmeal plants have been criticised for harming local food security. Certification schemes and use of processing trimmings aim to reduce impact.
