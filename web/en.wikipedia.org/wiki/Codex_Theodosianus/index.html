The Codex Theodosianus (Theodosian Code) was a compilation of the laws of the Roman Empire promulgated under the Christian emperors since 312 AD. It was produced in the Eastern Roman Empire and published by the emperor Theodosius II on 15 February 438, taking effect in both the Eastern and Western empires on 1 January 439.

Content

The Code contains more than 2,500 constitutions arranged in sixteen books by subject, covering subjects from taxation, coinage and the imperial post to religious policy, including extensive legislation on Christianity, heresy, paganism and Judaism. Book 16 is devoted entirely to religious matters.

Influence

The Code was a principal source for the later Corpus Juris Civilis of Justinian and influenced early medieval Germanic law codes in the West, notably the Breviary of Alaric (506 AD), through the interpretationes appended to its laws.
