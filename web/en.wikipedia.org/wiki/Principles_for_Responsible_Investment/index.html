Principles for Responsible Investment

Founded: 2006
Type: International investor network
Backed by: United Nations (UNEP FI and UN Global Compact)
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

The Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI) is a United Nations-supported international network of investors working to implement six aspirational principles for incorporating environmental, social and corporate governance (ESG) factors into investment practice.

History

The principles were launched in April 2006 at the New York Stock Exchange, following an initiative convened by UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan in 2005, in partnership with the UN Environment Programme Finance Initiative and the UN Global Compact. Membership grew from a few dozen signatories to several thousand asset owners, investment managers and service providers representing tens of trillions of US dollars in assets under management.

The six principles

Signatories commit to: (1) incorporate ESG issues into investment analysis and decision-making; (2) be active owners and incorporate ESG issues into ownership policies and practices; (3) seek appropriate disclosure on ESG issues by investee entities; (4) promote acceptance and implementation of the principles within the investment industry; (5) work together to enhance effectiveness in implementing them; and (6) report on activities and progress.

Criticism

The PRI has faced criticism that membership historically required little verification, enabling "greenwashing"; in response it introduced minimum requirements and delisted signatories that failed to meet them.
