Multidrug resistance

Multiple drug resistance (MDR), multidrug resistance or multiresistance is antimicrobial resistance shown by a species of microorganism to at least one antimicrobial drug in three or more antimicrobial categories. MDR pathogens are a major global public-health threat in hospitals and communities.

Examples

Clinically important examples include multidrug-resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB), methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE), and multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria such as carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii and Enterobacterales. Mechanisms include efflux pumps, drug-inactivating enzymes, target modification, and reduced permeability, and resistance genes spread by horizontal gene transfer on plasmids.

In cancer

The term is also used in oncology for tumour cells that develop resistance to many structurally unrelated chemotherapy drugs, often through overexpression of ABC transporter efflux pumps such as P-glycoprotein.
