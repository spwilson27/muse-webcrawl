XEW-TDT

XEW-TDT is a television station in Mexico City, Mexico, and the flagship station of the Las Estrellas network owned by Televisa. It broadcasts on virtual channel 2, the network's nationwide channel position, and is the digital successor to XEW-TV, one of the first television stations in Mexico.

History

XEW-TV signed on in 1951, founded by Emilio Azcárraga Vidaurreta, and took its call sign from the pioneering radio station XEW-AM, "the Voice of Latin America", which Azcárraga had launched in 1930. The station became a cornerstone of Telesistema Mexicano and later Televisa. Channel 2 has long been regarded as the most important channel in Mexican television and is home to the network's flagship newscasts, telenovelas and entertainment programming.

Digital television

XEW converted to digital broadcasting as XEW-TDT on RF channel 32, retaining virtual channel 2. As with other Mexican stations, its analog signal was shut off during Mexico's digital transition, which concluded in Mexico City in December 2015. The station broadcasts the Las Estrellas schedule in high definition from transmitters serving the Valley of Mexico, principally Cerro del Chiquihuite.

Programming

As the Las Estrellas flagship, XEW-TDT originates network programming distributed to affiliated stations across Mexico, including prime-time telenovelas, the morning magazine programme Hoy, and the evening national newscast.
