The Orion Nebula (also known as Messier 42, M42, or NGC 1976) is a diffuse nebula in the Milky Way situated south of Orion's Belt in the constellation of Orion, in the "sword" hanging from the belt. It is one of the brightest nebulae and is visible to the naked eye in the night sky.

Characteristics

M42 is located at a distance of about 1,344 light-years and is the closest region of massive star formation to Earth. The nebula is an enormous cloud of gas and dust roughly 24 light-years across, illuminated and sculpted by the hot young stars of the Trapezium Cluster at its heart — in particular Theta-1 Orionis C, whose ultraviolet radiation ionises the surrounding hydrogen and powers the nebula's glow.

Star formation

Telescopes including Hubble have revealed hundreds of protoplanetary disks ("proplyds") and brown dwarfs within M42, making it a key laboratory for studying how stars and planetary systems form. The bright "Orion Bar" is a wall of dense gas being eroded by the Trapezium's radiation.

History of observation

The nebulosity was first described telescopically by Nicolas-Claude Fabri de Peiresc in 1610 and drawn by Christiaan Huygens in 1659; Charles Messier catalogued it in 1769. The nearby smaller nebula M43 (De Mairan's Nebula) is separated from M42 by a dark lane of dust.
