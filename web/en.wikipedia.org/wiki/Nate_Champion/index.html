Nate Champion

Nathan D. Champion (September 29, 1857 – April 9, 1892) was an American rancher in Wyoming who became a pivotal figure in the Johnson County War of 1892. A small rancher accused by the powerful Wyoming Stock Growers Association of cattle rustling, Champion was besieged and killed by an invading party of hired gunmen, and his diary of the siege became famous.

Life

Born in Texas, Champion drifted north as a cowboy and settled in Johnson County, Wyoming, where he worked for cattle outfits and eventually ran his own small herd. When the large cattle companies organised against alleged rustlers and homesteader "grangers", Champion — popular locally and a leader among the small ranchers — was placed on a "dead list" of men to be killed or driven out.

Death

In November 1891 Champion survived an attack by gunmen at a cabin on the Powder River. On April 9, 1892, a party of about fifty "Invaders" — Texas gunmen hired by the stock growers — surrounded the KC Ranch cabin where Champion and Nick Ray were staying. Ray was shot and dragged inside dying; Champion held the cabin alone for hours, keeping a remarkable journal of the siege as it unfolded. When the invaders set the cabin afire with a wagon loaded with pitch pine, Champion ran out shooting and was killed. His charred journal was recovered and published, turning public opinion decisively against the invaders.

Legacy

Champion's stand became one of the most famous episodes of the range wars of the American West. He has been portrayed in fiction and film, including Michael Cimino's Heaven's Gate (1980), where he was played by Christopher Walken, and historian accounts often treat him as a folk hero of the small settler against the cattle barons.
