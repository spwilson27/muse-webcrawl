In the geometry of triangles, the Feuerbach point is a triangle center defined from the triangle's incircle and nine-point circle. It is the point at which those two circles are tangent to each other.

Feuerbach's theorem

The German mathematician Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach published in 1822 the theorem that the nine-point circle of a triangle is tangent internally to the triangle's incircle and tangent externally to each of its three excircles. The point of tangency between the incircle and the nine-point circle is called the Feuerbach point.

Properties

The Feuerbach point lies on the line connecting the centers of the incircle and the nine-point circle. It also has notable collinearity properties: it lies on the line through the orthocenter and other classical centers in certain configurations, and it is the center of the Feuerbach hyperbola, a rectangular hyperbola passing through the triangle's vertices, orthocenter, and incenter. In Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers, the Feuerbach point is catalogued as X(11).
