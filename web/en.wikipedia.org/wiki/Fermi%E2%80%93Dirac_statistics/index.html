Fermi–Dirac statistics

Fermi–Dirac statistics describe the statistical distribution of particles over energy states in systems consisting of many identical particles that obey the Pauli exclusion principle — namely fermions, particles with half-integer spin.

The distribution

The average number of particles in an energy state is given by a distribution function involving the Fermi energy, temperature, and Boltzmann's constant. At absolute zero, fermions fill all states up to the Fermi energy, forming a "Fermi sea".

History and applications

The statistics were introduced independently by Enrico Fermi and Paul Dirac in 1926. They underpin the theory of electrons in metals and semiconductors, white dwarf stars, and neutron stars, in contrast to Bose–Einstein statistics for bosons and Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics for classical particles.
