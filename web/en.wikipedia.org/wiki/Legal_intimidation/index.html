Legal intimidation

Legal intimidation is the use of legal action, or threats of legal action, to discourage an opponent from exercising their legal rights or from taking some action, rather than to resolve a genuine dispute. Because litigation is expensive and time-consuming, a wealthier party may coerce a weaker one into backing down even where the claim itself is weak.

Forms

  • SLAPP suits — strategic lawsuits against public participation, filed to silence critics, journalists, or activists.
  • Cease and desist letters — threats of suit used to stop speech or conduct the sender dislikes, sometimes beyond what the law would support.
  • Libel tourism and abusive subpoenas — choosing oppressive forums or using compulsory process to harass.
  • Vexatious litigation — repeated unfounded proceedings.

Legal responses

Many jurisdictions have adopted countermeasures. Most U.S. states have anti-SLAPP statutes allowing early dismissal of such suits and recovery of legal fees. Courts can sanction frivolous filings, strike out abusive claims, and make vexatious litigant orders. In 2024 the European Union adopted an anti-SLAPP directive protecting journalists and human rights defenders against manifestly unfounded cross-border civil claims.
