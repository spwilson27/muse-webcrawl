Cresta Run

A rider on the Cresta Run, St. Moritz.

The Cresta Run is a natural ice skeleton racing toboggan track in St. Moritz, Switzerland. Built in 1884 by British winter sports enthusiasts, it is the oldest and one of the last remaining natural ice runs in the world, and is considered the birthplace of skeleton racing. The run is operated by the St. Moritz Tobogganing Club (SMTC), founded in 1887.

The run

The track is roughly 1,214 metres (about three-quarters of a mile) long, dropping some 157 metres from St. Moritz to Celerina through ten corners with names such as Church Leap, Battledore and the notorious Shuttlecock Corner, where riders who fail to negotiate the bend are thrown into bales of straw. Riders lie head-first on a skeleton toboggan, steering with rakes on their boots and by shifting their weight, reaching speeds of up to 130 km/h. The course is rebuilt from snow and ice each winter and is dismantled in the spring.

Traditions

The club is famously private and amateur in spirit. Beginners ("learners") typically start partway down at Junction, graduating to the full course from "Top". Women were banned from riding the run for decades — a restriction rooted in 1920s medical opinion — and were fully admitted only in the late 2010s. Famous races include the Grand National and the Curzon Cup, and the record for the full course stands at under 51 seconds.
