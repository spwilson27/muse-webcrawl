The Krämerbrücke ("Merchants' Bridge") is a medieval bridge in Erfurt, Germany, lined on both sides with inhabited half-timbered houses, making it the longest continuously inhabited bridge in Europe.

History

A wooden bridge stood on the site by the 12th century; after several fires it was rebuilt in stone in 1325. Traders' stalls on the bridge gradually became permanent shops and houses. The bridge originally had churches at both ends; the Ägidienkirche at the eastern end survives.

Today

The bridge's 32 houses contain small shops, galleries and workshops selling crafts, antiques and Thuringian specialities. The annual Krämerbrückenfest celebrates the bridge as a symbol of Erfurt.
