The 1967 Tour de France was the 54th edition of the Tour de France, held from June 29 to July 23, 1967. It was won by Frenchman Roger Pingeon, but the race is chiefly remembered for the death of English rider Tom Simpson on the slopes of Mont Ventoux during stage 13, the first officially recorded doping-related death in the Tour's history and a turning point in cycling's confrontation with performance-enhancing drugs.

Race summary

The race reverted to national teams after five years of trade teams. Pingeon seized the yellow jersey with a long solo breakaway on stage 5 to Jambes and defended it through the mountains, winning by nearly three minutes over Spain's Julio Jiménez, who took the mountains classification. Jan Janssen won the points classification.

Death of Tom Simpson

On July 13, in extreme heat on Mont Ventoux, Simpson—world champion in 1965 and Britain's leading rider—weaved across the road and collapsed about a kilometre from the summit. He was airlifted to hospital in Avignon, where he died. Amphetamines were found in his jersey pocket and in his bloodstream, and the official cause of death combined heart failure, heat exhaustion, alcohol, and drugs. A granite memorial near the summit marks the spot and remains a pilgrimage site for cyclists.

Legacy

Simpson's death led the Tour to introduce formal doping controls the following year. The 1967 race is frequently cited as the moment cycling could no longer ignore the pharmacological escalation of the professional peloton.
