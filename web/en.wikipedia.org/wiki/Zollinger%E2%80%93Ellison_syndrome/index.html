Zollinger–Ellison syndrome

Cause: Gastrinoma (gastrin-secreting tumour)

Symptoms: Recurrent peptic ulcers, diarrhoea, abdominal pain

Treatment: Proton pump inhibitors; surgery

Zollinger–Ellison syndrome is a rare condition in which a gastrin-secreting tumour (gastrinoma), usually in the pancreas or duodenum, drives the stomach to produce excessive acid, causing severe and recurrent peptic ulcers. It was described by Robert Zollinger and Edwin Ellison in 1955.

Features

Patients develop multiple or unusually located ulcers, gastro-oesophageal reflux and diarrhoea from acid inactivation of pancreatic enzymes. About a quarter of gastrinomas occur as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN 1).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on markedly raised fasting gastrin levels with acidic gastric pH and on imaging to localise the tumour. High-dose proton pump inhibitors control acid output in most patients; surgery may cure localised sporadic tumours.
