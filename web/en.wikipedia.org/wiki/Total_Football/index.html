Total Football

Total Football (Dutch: totaalvoetbal) is a tactical system in association football in which any outfield player can take over the role of any other player in a team. A player who moves out of his position is replaced by another from his team, thus retaining the team's intended organisational structure. In this fluid system, no one player is fixed in a predetermined role; anyone can successively play as an attacker, a midfielder and a defender.

History

The foundations of Total Football were laid by Jack Reynolds, the English manager of Ajax Amsterdam in the early twentieth century, and developed further by the great Austrian "Wunderteam" and Gusztáv Sebes's Hungary side of the 1950s. The system was perfected at Ajax under Rinus Michels in the late 1960s and early 1970s, with Johan Cruyff as its on-field orchestrator. Ajax won three consecutive European Cups (1971–1973) playing this style.

Michels took the system to the Netherlands national team, which reached the 1974 World Cup final with Cruyff as captain and talisman; although they lost to hosts West Germany, the Dutch are widely remembered as the tournament's defining side. Cruyff later implemented the philosophy as manager of Ajax and FC Barcelona, where his "Dream Team" of the early 1990s won four consecutive La Liga titles and the 1992 European Cup.

Tactics

Total Football demands technically gifted, intelligent players who are comfortable in multiple positions, relentless pressing when possession is lost, the maintenance of a high defensive line with an offside trap, and the deliberate creation and exploitation of space: players move to draw opponents out of shape, and the team expands the pitch when attacking and contracts it when defending.

Legacy

The philosophy profoundly influenced modern football, from Arrigo Sacchi's AC Milan to Pep Guardiola's Barcelona and Manchester City sides, whose positional play (juego de posición) is a direct descendant of the Michels–Cruyff model.
