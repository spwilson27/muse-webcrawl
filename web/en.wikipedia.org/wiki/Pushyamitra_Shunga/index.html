Pushyamitra Shunga (IAST: Puṣyamitra Śuṅga) was the founder and first ruler of the Shunga Empire in north India. A general (senapati) of the last Mauryan emperor, Brihadratha, he assassinated his master around 185 BCE and established his own dynasty, which ruled for roughly a century.

Reign

Pushyamitra's empire stretched across north and central India. He is credited in later texts with performing the Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice), and the Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva refers to him as a performer of two Ashvamedhas. His reign saw conflict with the Indo-Greeks, possibly including the campaigns associated with Demetrius or Menander.

Religion and controversy

Buddhist texts such as the Divyavadana portray Pushyamitra as a persecutor of Buddhism, but historians debate the accuracy of these accounts; archaeological evidence suggests Buddhist monuments such as Bharhut and Sanchi continued to be maintained and expanded during Shunga rule.
