Junior Cycle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Irish secondary school students]

The Junior Cycle is the first stage of secondary education in Ireland, typically undertaken by students aged 12 to 15 over three years. It was introduced in 1989 to replace the former Intermediate Certificate and Day Group Certificate examinations, and culminates in the Junior Certificate examinations administered by the State Examinations Commission.

Curriculum

Students generally study a broad range of subjects, including English, Irish, mathematics, science, history, geography, a modern language, and optional subjects such as art, music, home economics, and business studies. Under reforms phased in from 2014, the new Junior Cycle framework added classroom-based assessments, short courses, and a greater emphasis on key skills and wellbeing, with final examinations accounting for a reduced share of results in reformed subjects.

Progression

After the Junior Cycle, students proceed to the Senior Cycle, sometimes via an optional Transition Year, leading to the Leaving Certificate.
