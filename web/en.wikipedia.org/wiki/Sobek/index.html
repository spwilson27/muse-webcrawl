Sobek (also called Sebek) was an ancient Egyptian deity with a complex and elastic history and nature. He is associated with the Nile crocodile or the West African crocodile and is represented either in its form or as a human with a crocodile head. Sobek was also associated with pharaonic power, fertility, and military prowess, but served additionally as a protective deity with apotropaic qualities, invoked particularly for protection against the dangers presented by the Nile.

Origins

Sobek first appeared in the Old Kingdom, most notably in the Pyramid Texts. Some of his epithets, such as "he who loves robbery," suggest a more sinister aspect of this deity. The god gained popularity in the Middle Kingdom, when Amenemhat III devoted considerable resources to building projects in the Faiyum, the region most closely associated with Sobek.

Cult centers

The entire Faiyum region — the "Land of the Lake" — served as the cult center of Sobek. Most of the Faiyum towns developed their own local version of the god, such as Soknebtunis at Tebtunis, Sokonnokonni at Bacchias, and Souxei at Dionysias. At Crocodilopolis, later Ptolemais Euergetis or Arsinoe, a live sacred crocodile named Petsuchos was kept in the temple and worshipped as an incarnation of Sobek.

Another principal cult center was Kom Ombo in Upper Egypt, where Sobek shared a dual temple with the falcon god Horus the Elder.

Character

Sobek was a god of ambivalent character, both feared as the dangerous crocodile of the Nile and revered as the creator of the world who emerged from the dark waters to create order. He was associated with the inundation of the Nile, and hence with fertility.
