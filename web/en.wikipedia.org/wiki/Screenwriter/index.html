Screenwriter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A screenwriter (also called a scriptwriter, scribe, or scenarist) is a person who writes screenplays—works on which mass media, such as films, television programs, and video games, are based. Screenwriters may write original works or adapt existing material, and in television a writer may also serve as producer or showrunner.

The craft

Screenplays are written in a standardised format—traditionally Courier 12-point, roughly one page per minute of screen time—combining scene headings ("slug lines"), action description, character names, dialogue, and parentheticals. Structure is commonly analysed in three acts, though many models exist, from Syd Field's paradigm to Blake Snyder's "Save the Cat" beat sheet.

Most professional screenwriters are commissioned to write, rewrite, or polish material: feature assignments proceed through treatments, first drafts, rewrites, and "production polish" steps. Because films are collaborative, many writers may contribute to a finished film; the Writers Guild of America arbitrates screen credits and compensates writers under its Minimum Basic Agreement.

Industry and representation

Screenwriters are typically represented by agents and managers, and in the United States and many other countries belong to guilds or unions that set minimum terms and residuals. Spec scripts—written "on speculation" without commission—are a common route into the profession. The profession has been shaped by notable labour actions, including the Writers Guild strikes of 1988, 2007–08, and 2023.
