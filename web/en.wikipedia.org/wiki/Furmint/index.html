Furmint

Furmint is a white Hungarian wine grape variety best known as the principal grape of Tokaji, the celebrated sweet wine of Hungary's Tokaj region. The grape produces wines with naturally high acidity and great capacity for ageing, and its thin skin makes it highly susceptible to botrytis cinerea ("noble rot"), which concentrates the sugars of the berries used for Tokaji aszú.

History

Furmint has been documented in Hungary since at least the 16th century and takes its name, according to one theory, from the French word froment ("wheat"), after the wheat-gold colour of its wine. DNA profiling has shown it to be a parent or close relative of several other Central European varieties.

Viticulture and wines

The variety ripens late and buds early, and it thrives in the volcanic soils of Tokaj. Besides botrytised sweet wines, Furmint is increasingly vinified dry, yielding steely, mineral wines compared in style to dry Riesling. It is also grown in Austria (notably in Burgenland, where it was once common around Rust), Slovenia (as Šipon), Croatia (as Mosler), and Slovakia.

Synonyms

Furmint is known under numerous synonyms, including Mosler, Šipon, Posipel, Zapfner, and Som.
