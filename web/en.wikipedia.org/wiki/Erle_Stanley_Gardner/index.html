Erle Stanley Gardner (July 17, 1889 – March 11, 1970) was an American lawyer and author, best known as the creator of Perry Mason, the fictional criminal defence attorney who, with secretary Della Street and investigator Paul Drake, featured in more than 80 novels and short stories. Gardner was at his death among the best-selling American authors of all time.

Career

Admitted to the California bar in 1911, Gardner practised law in Ventura while writing pulp fiction at night, contributing prolifically to magazines such as Black Mask. The first Perry Mason novel, The Case of the Velvet Claws, appeared in 1933. Mason became a cultural phenomenon through novels, films, a radio series and the long-running television series (1957–66) starring Raymond Burr. Under the pen name A. A. Fair, Gardner also wrote the Cool and Lam mysteries, and he produced travel books about Baja California and Mexico.

The Court of Last Resort

Drawing on his legal experience, Gardner founded the Court of Last Resort, a project publicised in Argosy magazine that investigated cases of possibly wrongfully convicted prisoners, helping free several innocent defendants. He died at his ranch, Rancho Paisano, in Temecula in 1970.
