Sodium–potassium pump

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sodium–potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase) is an enzyme found in the plasma membrane of all animal cells. It is a P-type ATPase that pumps sodium ions out of the cell and potassium ions into the cell, both against their concentration gradients, using energy from the hydrolysis of ATP.

Mechanism

In each cycle the pump binds three intracellular sodium ions, is phosphorylated by ATP, changes conformation and releases the sodium ions outside the cell; it then binds two extracellular potassium ions, dephosphorylates, and releases them inside. The resulting electrochemical gradient is essential for secondary active transport, electrical excitability of neurons and muscles, osmotic balance and cell volume regulation.

Discovery

The pump was identified in 1957 by the Danish physiologist Jens Christian Skou, who shared the 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of this 'first molecular machine'.

Pharmacology

Cardiac glycosides such as digoxin and ouabain inhibit the pump; digoxin has been used to treat heart failure by raising intracellular calcium in cardiac muscle.
