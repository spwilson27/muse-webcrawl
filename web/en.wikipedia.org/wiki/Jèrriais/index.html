Native to: Jersey, Sark (as Sercquiais)
Language family: Indo-European, Romance, Gallo-Romance, langue d'oïl, Norman
Official status: Recognized regional language in Jersey

Bilingual signage in English and Jèrriais in Jersey.

Jèrriais (Jersey Norman) is the form of the Norman language spoken in Jersey, one of the Channel Islands. It has been in decline over the past century as English has become the dominant language of the island for government, commerce, and education, but efforts have been made to preserve and revive it, and it is recognized as a regional language by the British and Irish governments within the framework of the British–Irish Council.

Jèrriais is closely related to Guernésiais, spoken in Guernsey, and to Sercquiais, the variety of Sark, which itself derives from the Jèrriais brought by colonists from Jersey in the sixteenth century.

History

Norman speech arrived in the Channel Islands with Norse settlers and the islands' incorporation into the Duchy of Normandy. For centuries Jèrriais was the everyday language of most islanders, with French used for formal and legal purposes. A literature in Jèrriais developed in the nineteenth century, led by writers such as Robert Pipon Marett (Laelius), and a standard dictionary, the Dictionnaithe Jèrriais–Angliais, was published in 2005.

Status and revival

Census figures show only a few thousand fluent speakers, most of them elderly, and the language is classified as severely endangered. Revival measures include teaching in schools, the work of L'Office du Jèrriais, bilingual signage, and official recognition: in 2019 Jèrriais was given official status alongside English and French in the States of Jersey, and Jersey's banknotes and public documents have featured the language.
