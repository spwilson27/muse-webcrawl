Lachin (Azerbaijani: Laçın, lit. "falcon"; Armenian: Բերձոր, Berdzor) is a town in Azerbaijan and the administrative centre of the Lachin District, in the Lesser Caucasus mountains. It lies on the road connecting Khankendi (Stepanakert) with Armenia, in the strategic corridor that bears its name—the Lachin corridor, long the only road link between Armenia and the Nagorno-Karabakh region.

History

The settlement was known in the early twentieth century as Abdallar; it was renamed Lachin in 1923 and became the administrative centre of the short-lived Kurdistan Uyezd ("Red Kurdistan") of Soviet Azerbaijan before being absorbed into the Lachin District in 1930. Under Soviet rule the town had a mixed population with a substantial Kurdish and Azerbaijani presence.

During the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, Armenian forces captured Lachin in May 1992, securing the corridor between Armenia and Karabakh; the Azerbaijani and Kurdish population fled. The town subsequently came under the administration of the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh, was renamed Berdzor, and was resettled by Armenians.

Return to Azerbaijani control

Under the ceasefire agreement ending the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh War, the Lachin District was returned to Azerbaijan on 1 December 2020, while the corridor itself—kept under Russian peacekeeping control—continued to pass near the town. In August 2022, after a new road bypassing the town was completed, Azerbaijani forces took full control of Lachin, and its Armenian residents departed. Following Azerbaijan's offensive of September 2023 and the dissolution of the Artsakh republic, Azerbaijan began resettling Azerbaijani families in the town as reconstruction proceeded.
