Cameron Gordon (mathematician)

Cameron Gordon
Born: 1945
Field: mathematics — low-dimensional topology
Institution: University of Texas at Austin
Known for: work on knot theory and 3-manifolds

Cameron McAllan Gordon (born 1945) is a British-American mathematician working in low-dimensional topology, long a professor at the University of Texas at Austin, where he held the Sid W. Richardson Foundation Regents Chair. He received his doctorate from the University of Cambridge in 1971 under F. H. J. Nicholson.

Work

Gordon is known for contributions to knot theory and the topology of 3-manifolds, including his work with John Luecke on the result that knots are determined by their complements (the Gordon–Luecke theorem, 1989) and on Dehn surgery, and with Luecke and others on the cabling conjecture. His research helped shape the modern theory of exceptional surgeries on knots.

Honours

Gordon was an invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians in 1994 and is a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.
