Susan G. Komen for the Cure

Susan G. Komen, formerly known as Susan G. Komen for the Cure and originally the Susan G. Komen Breast Cancer Foundation, is a major breast cancer charity based in the United States. It was founded in 1982 by Nancy G. Brinker in memory of her sister, Susan G. Komen, who died of breast cancer at age 36.

Activities

The organisation funds breast cancer research, community health, education and advocacy. Its best-known fundraising event is the Susan G. Komen Race for the Cure, a series of 5K run/walk events begun in 1983 that grew into one of the largest charity race series in the world, together with the three-day, 60-mile Komen 3-Day walks. Komen helped popularise the pink ribbon as the symbol of breast cancer awareness.

History and controversies

The foundation changed its name to Susan G. Komen for the Cure in 2007 and shortened it to Susan G. Komen in 2016. In 2012 it faced a public backlash after briefly reversing funding for Planned Parenthood, after which donations and race participation declined. It has also faced scrutiny over executive pay and the proportion of funds spent on screening and treatment versus research.
