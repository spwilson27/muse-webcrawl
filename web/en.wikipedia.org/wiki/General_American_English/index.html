General American English

General American English (GAE) is the umbrella variety of American English spoken by a majority of Americans and widely perceived as the standard, regionally neutral accent — historically associated with the Midland and Great Lakes regions rather than with distinctly marked Southern, New England or New York speech.

Features

General American is rhotic (pronouncing /r/ in all positions), has the father–bother merger, and typically the cot–caught merger for younger speakers, flapping of intervocalic /t/, and a lack of the marked features of other regional dialects. It functions as the reference accent in broadcasting, education and dictionaries of American pronunciation.
