Number sense

Number sense refers to a group of key understandings about numbers and numerical operations, together with the ability and inclination to use this understanding flexibly to make mathematical judgements and to develop useful strategies for handling numbers and operations. In cognitive science, the term also denotes the innate, approximate ability to perceive and compare quantities without counting, known as the approximate number system (ANS).

Approximate number system

Humans and many other animals can rapidly judge which of two sets is more numerous, with accuracy governed by the ratio between the quantities (Weber's law) rather than their absolute difference. This ability is present in infants and is thought to be localized partly in the intraparietal sulcus. It provides a foundation on which symbolic arithmetic is built.

In education

In mathematics education, a child with good number sense understands numbers' meanings and relationships, recognizes relative magnitudes, composes and decomposes numbers, and can estimate and judge the reasonableness of results. Poor number sense is associated with mathematical difficulties, and developmental dyscalculia may involve a core deficit in the approximate number system in some children.

Research

The concept was developed by researchers including Stanislas Dehaene, who proposed the "number sense" as an evolved adaptation, and Robert Siegler, who studied how numerical magnitude representations develop from logarithmic to linear with age and education.
