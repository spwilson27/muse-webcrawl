Winchester is an independent city in the northern Shenandoah Valley of Virginia, United States, and the seat of Frederick County (though administratively separate from it). Founded in 1752, it is the oldest city west of the Blue Ridge Mountains in Virginia and a centre of the region's apple industry.

History

The area was settled by European colonists in the 1730s along the Great Wagon Road. The young George Washington surveyed in the region and, during the French and Indian War, commanded Virginia forces from Winchester, building Fort Loudoun there. Winchester was a strategic prize in the American Civil War: lying at the northern end of the Shenandoah Valley, it reportedly changed hands dozens of times, and three major battles were fought nearby in 1862, 1863 and 1864. Stonewall Jackson's Valley Campaign made extensive use of the city, and it served as a Confederate supply hub early in the war.

Economy and culture

Winchester is known for apples and hosts the annual Shenandoah Apple Blossom Festival, held since the 1920s. Shenandoah University is located in the city. Historic sites include Washington's office museum, Stonewall Jackson's headquarters, and the grave of country singer Patsy Cline, who was born in Winchester.
