Aquila (constellation)

GenitiveAquilae
Brightest starAltair (α Aql), magnitude 0.76
SymbolismThe Eagle
Area652 sq. deg.

Aquila is a constellation on the celestial equator, representing an eagle. It was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. In Greek mythology the eagle carried Zeus's thunderbolts, and in another tradition it was the eagle that abducted Ganymede.

Stars

Its brightest star, Altair (Alpha Aquilae), is the 12th-brightest star in the sky and forms one corner of the Summer Triangle asterism, with Vega in Lyra and Deneb in Cygnus. Altair is notable for its extremely rapid rotation, which flattens it into an oblate spheroid. Other named stars include Alshain (Beta Aquilae) and Tarazed (Gamma Aquilae), which flank Altair in the eagle's head.

Deep-sky objects

Aquila lies in a rich region of the Milky Way and contains planetary nebulae such as NGC 6751 (the Glowing Eye) and the open cluster NGC 6709. Two notable novae have been observed in the constellation, including Nova Aquilae 1918, which briefly rivalled Sirius in brightness.
