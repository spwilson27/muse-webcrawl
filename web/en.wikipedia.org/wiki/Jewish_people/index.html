The Jews, or the Jewish people, are an ethnoreligious group and nation originating from the Israelites of ancient Israel and Judah. Jewish ethnicity, religion, and community are strongly interrelated, as Judaism is the ethnic religion of the Jewish people, although observance varies from strict to none. In the broader sense, the Jewish people form a community with shared historical, cultural, and religious traditions.

History

Jewish history spans more than three millennia. According to tradition and historical scholarship, the Jewish people emerged in the ancient Near East, centered on the kingdoms of Israel and Judah. Following conquests and exiles — including the Babylonian exile and the Roman destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE — Jewish communities spread throughout the Mediterranean, Europe, and the Middle East in the diaspora. Over the centuries, distinct communities developed, including Ashkenazi Jews in Central and Eastern Europe, Sephardi Jews in Iberia and the Mediterranean, and Mizrahi Jews in the Middle East and North Africa.

In the 19th and 20th centuries, emancipation, pogroms, and the rise of Zionism reshaped Jewish life. During the Holocaust, Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered approximately six million Jews. In 1948 the State of Israel was established. Today the largest Jewish populations are in Israel and the United States, with smaller communities worldwide.

Identity and religion

Jewish identity is traditionally transmitted by matrilineal descent or by conversion to Judaism. The religion centers on the covenant between God and the Jewish people, the Torah, and a body of law and tradition (halakha). Major movements include Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and Reconstructionist Judaism, alongside secular and cultural forms of Jewish identity. Hebrew is the liturgical and national language; historical vernaculars include Yiddish and Ladino (Judeo-Spanish).

Culture

Jewish culture encompasses religious practice, philosophy, literature, cuisine, music, and humor, varying widely among communities. Festivals such as Passover, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, and Hanukkah structure the calendar.
