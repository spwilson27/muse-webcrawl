Anhui clique

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Anhui clique (Chinese: 皖系; pinyin: Wǎn Xì) was one of several rival military and political factions that split from the Beiyang Army during China's Warlord Era in the early Republic of China. It was named after Anhui province, the home of its principal leader, Duan Qirui. The clique dominated the government in Beijing for much of the period from 1916 to 1920.

History

After the death of Yuan Shikai in 1916, Duan Qirui, premier of the republic, built a power base among Beiyang generals who had served under him. Many of the clique's leading generals came from Anhui, and the group controlled the Beijing government through alliances with provincial military governors. The clique enjoyed Japanese support, receiving the Nishihara loans in exchange for concessions, including rights in Shandong and Manchuria — an arrangement that provoked public outrage and helped trigger the May Fourth Movement of 1919.

The clique's best-known political vehicle was the Anfu Club, which dominated the 1918 National Assembly elections; the resulting body became known as the Anfu Parliament. In July 1920 the rival Zhili clique of Wu Peifu and Cao Kun, allied with Zhang Zuolin's Fengtian clique, defeated the Anhui forces in the Zhili–Anhui War in a matter of days. Duan resigned, the Anfu Parliament was dissolved, and the clique ceased to be a major national force, though Duan briefly returned as head of state in 1924–1926 as a compromise figurehead.

Notable members

Besides Duan Qirui, leading figures included Xu Shuzheng, the clique's energetic military organiser, as well as generals and politicians such as Jin Yunpeng, Duan Zhigui, and Lu Yongxiang. The clique's diplomacy, particularly its dealings with Japan, influenced the career of officials like Cao Rulin, a target of May Fourth protesters.
