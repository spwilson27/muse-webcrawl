Boys Nation is an annual forum concerning civic training, government, and leadership for young men, run by the American Legion since 1946. Each summer, two "senators" from each of 49 state-level Boys State programmes (Hawaii does not send delegates) travel to Washington, D.C., for a week of mock federal government, giving a total of 98 delegates. The parallel programme for young women, Girls Nation, is run by the American Legion Auxiliary.

Programme

Delegates are selected as outstanding participants at their state Boys State sessions, where high school juniors run mock city, county, and state governments. At Boys Nation, the senators form two mock political parties, draft and debate bills in a mock Senate, elect a president and vice-president of Boys Nation, and hold party conventions. The week's activities take place in and around Washington, D.C., and include visits to the U.S. Capitol, federal agencies and monuments, and Arlington National Cemetery. Delegates traditionally meet the President of the United States at the White House, a tradition interrupted only rarely.

The most famous such meeting took place on 3 July 1963, when Arkansas delegate Bill Clinton shook hands with President John F. Kennedy in the Rose Garden—a photograph and film clip that featured prominently in Clinton's later political career and in the history of the programme.

Notable alumni

Alumni of Boys State and Boys Nation include numerous members of Congress, governors, judges, and other public figures. Bill Clinton attended Arkansas Boys State and Boys Nation in 1963. Other notable alumni of the broader Boys State/Nation system include former Senate Majority Leader Tom Daschle, astronaut Neil Armstrong (Boys State), news anchor Tom Brokaw (Boys State), and former FEMA director Michael D. Brown. The programme is regarded as one of the most prestigious civic honours for American high school students.
