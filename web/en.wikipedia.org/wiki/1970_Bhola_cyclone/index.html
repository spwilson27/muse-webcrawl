1970 Bhola cyclone

FormedNovember 3, 1970
LandfallNovember 12, 1970, East Pakistan
Lowest pressure966 hPa
Estimated deaths300,000–500,000
Areas affectedEast Pakistan and West Bengal

The 1970 Bhola cyclone was a devastating tropical cyclone that struck East Pakistan, now Bangladesh, and India's West Bengal on November 12, 1970. It remains the deadliest tropical cyclone recorded and one of the deadliest natural disasters in modern history. A storm surge inundated low islands and coastal areas of the Ganges Delta at night, killing an estimated 300,000 to 500,000 people.

Impact

The cyclone reached landfall near the densely populated delta with peak winds around 115 mph (185 km/h). The surge, reported up to about 10 metres in places, swept away villages on islands such as Bhola, Hatia and Sandwip. Fishing fleets were destroyed, crops and livestock were lost, and communications failed across the affected districts.

Political consequences

The Pakistani central government's slow and inadequate relief response deepened Bengali anger before the December 1970 elections. The Awami League's victory, the subsequent political crisis and the Bangladesh Liberation War followed in 1971. The disaster helped drive improvements in cyclone shelters, warning systems and disaster preparedness in Bangladesh, though the delta remains highly exposed to storm surge.
