The Reader is a 2008 romantic drama film directed by Stephen Daldry and written by David Hare, adapted from Bernhard Schlink's 1995 German novel Der Vorleser. It stars Kate Winslet as Hanna Schmitz, and Ralph Fiennes and David Kross as the older and younger versions of Michael Berg. The film was the last work of producers Sydney Pollack and Anthony Minghella, both of whom died before its release.

Plot

In 1958 Neustadt, 15-year-old Michael Berg, taken ill on his way home, is helped by Hanna, a tram conductor in her thirties. They begin a summer affair in which, at her insistence, he reads aloud to her from the classics — Homer, Chekhov, Lady Chatterley's Lover. Hanna abruptly disappears. Eight years later, Michael, now a law student attending a war-crimes trial as part of a seminar, is shocked to find Hanna among the defendants: she had been an SS guard at Auschwitz, and she accepts a life sentence rather than admit the secret she has guarded all her life — she is illiterate. Years later, Michael begins sending her cassette tapes of himself reading books; Hanna teaches herself to read and write in prison, and shortly before her release in 1995 she takes her own life. Michael carries out her final wish by donating her savings to a Jewish literacy foundation.

Reception and awards

The film received mixed reviews from critics, some faulting its treatment of a perpetrator's story, but Winslet's performance was widely acclaimed. It was nominated for five Academy Awards, including Best Picture, and Winslet won the Academy Award for Best Actress (along with the BAFTA and Golden Globe for the role). It grossed over $108 million worldwide.
