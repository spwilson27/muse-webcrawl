Sevanavank (Armenian: Սևանավանք, "Sevan Monastery") is a monastic complex on the northwestern shore of Lake Sevan in Armenia. It was founded in 874 by Princess Mariam, daughter of King Ashot I, on what was then an island — the "Island of Sevan." Soviet-era drainage of the lake lowered the water level by some 20 metres, turning the island into the present peninsula.

History

According to an inscription in one of the churches, the monastery was founded in 874. Monks had earlier established a small community on the island, and the site had been inhabited since prehistoric times; in the eighth century a fortress and pagan temple remains were noted there. The monastery was a place of exile for errant monks of Etchmiadzin and was known for strict discipline. In the ninth century the monks of Sevan participated in resistance to Arab incursions.

Architecture

Two churches survive: Surp Arakelots (Holy Apostles) and Surp Astvatsatsin (Holy Mother of God), both cruciform in plan with conical domes, built of rough-hewn dark tuff that gives the monastery its name (from sev, "black"). The ruined gavit (narthex) of Surp Arakelots preserves carved wooden doors and capitals, and the site is rich in khachkars (carved cross-stones), including unusual ones of green stone. Today Sevanavank is one of Armenia's most visited monuments.
