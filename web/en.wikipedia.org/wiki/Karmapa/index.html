Karmapa

The Karmapa (Tibetan: རྒྱལ་དབང་ཀརྨ་པ་, "the one who carries out Buddha-activity") is the head of the Karma Kagyu school, the largest sub-school of the Kagyu tradition of Tibetan Buddhism. The line is the oldest tulku lineage in Tibet: beginning with Düsum Khyenpa (1110–1193), a disciple of Gampopa, each Karmapa is believed to be the deliberate reincarnation of his predecessor, identified in childhood by a prediction letter left by the previous holder.

History

The Karmapas enjoyed the patronage of Mongol and Ming emperors; the 5th Karmapa, Deshin Shekpa, was received at Nanjing by the Yongle Emperor in 1407. The 16th Karmapa, Rangjung Rigpe Dorje, fled Tibet after 1959 and established his seat in exile at Rumtek in Sikkim, spreading the Kagyu teachings widely in the West.

The 17th Karmapa controversy

After the 16th Karmapa's death in 1981, two rival candidates were recognised as the 17th Karmapa: Ogyen Trinley Dorje, recognised by the Dalai Lama and by Beijing, who escaped from Tibet to India in 2000, and Trinley Thaye Dorje, recognised by the influential Shamar Rinpoche. The dispute divided the Karma Kagyu school and led to prolonged litigation over Rumtek Monastery. In 2018 the two claimants met in France and issued a joint statement hoping for reconciliation.
