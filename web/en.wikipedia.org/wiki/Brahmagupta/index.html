Brahmagupta

Bornc. 598 CE, Bhinmal, present-day Rajasthan, India
Diedc. 668 CE
Known forArithmetic with zero and negative numbers; Brahmagupta's formula; interpolation formula; solutions of Pell-type equations
FieldsMathematics, astronomy

Brahmagupta (c. 598 – c. 668 CE) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer. He is the author of two early works on mathematics and astronomy: the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (BSS, "correctly established doctrine of Brahma"), a theoretical treatise written in 628, and the Khaṇḍakhādyaka ("edible bite"), a more practical text written in 665.

Mathematics

Brahmagupta gave the first clear rules for arithmetic involving zero and negative numbers (which he called "debts" and positive numbers "fortunes"), including the rule that a number divided by zero is a fraction with zero as denominator. He gave formulas for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral (Brahmagupta's formula, generalising Heron's formula), a method for generating Pythagorean triples, an interpolation formula, and solutions to quadratic equations and to the Pell-type equation x² − Ny² = 1 using the bhāvanā composition method. He also described how to solve linear Diophantine equations — the earliest known general treatment of the problem later associated with the Chinese remainder tradition and with Diophantus.

Astronomy

Working at the astronomical centre of Ujjain, Brahmagupta refined eclipse predictions and planetary models, criticised aspects of Aryabhata's work (including the rotation of the Earth), and described gravity as an attractive force drawing objects toward the Earth — an early qualitative account sometimes noted by historians of science.

Legacy

His work was translated into Arabic as Sindhind in the eighth century and influenced Islamic and, through them, later European mathematics.
