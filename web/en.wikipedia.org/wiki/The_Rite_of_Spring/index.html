The Rite of Spring (French: Le Sacre du printemps) is a ballet and orchestral concert work by the Russian composer Igor Stravinsky. Written for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes and choreographed by Vaslav Nijinsky, with designs by Nicholas Roerich, it depicts an ancient Slavic ritual in which a young girl is chosen as a sacrifice and dances herself to death to propitiate the gods of spring. Its premiere in Paris on 29 May 1913 caused one of the most famous scandals in the history of music.

The premiere and the "riot"

The music — with its pounding, irregular rhythms, bitonal chords, extreme dissonance and a famously stratospheric opening bassoon solo — together with Nijinsky's knock-kneed, stomping choreography, shocked the fashionable Paris audience. Laughter and catcalls began almost immediately; supporters and opponents came to blows, and the uproar became so loud that the dancers could not hear the orchestra. Historians debate how violent the evening truly was, but the scandal made Stravinsky famous overnight, and within a year the score was being cheered at concert performances.

Musical significance

The Rite revolutionised 20th-century music: rhythm is treated as the primary structural force, harmony is stacked into grinding polychords, and folk melodies are fragmented and repeated in mosaic-like blocks. Along with The Firebird and Petrushka, it forms the trilogy of early ballets that launched Stravinsky, and it remains one of the most performed and recorded works of the modern repertoire, influencing composers from Bartók to the present day and echoing in film scores from Fantasia (1940), where Disney animated it with dinosaurs, onward.
