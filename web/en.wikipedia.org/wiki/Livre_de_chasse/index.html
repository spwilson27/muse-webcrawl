Livre de chasse

The Livre de chasse (Book of the Hunt) is a medieval treatise on hunting written between 1387 and 1389 by Gaston III, Count of Foix, also known as Gaston Phébus (or Febus), one of the most celebrated huntsmen of his age. Written in French, it became the standard authority on medieval hunting practice and remained influential for centuries.

Contents

The work describes the animals of the chase, their habits and behaviour, the care and training of hunting dogs and hounds, and the methods of hunting game with hounds, including the ritualised hunting of the stag and other beasts. It combines practical instruction drawn from Gaston's own long experience with the natural-history lore of the period.

Manuscripts

Some 44 manuscripts of the text survive. The most famous is a sumptuously illuminated copy made in Paris around 1407 (now in the Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS français 616), whose miniatures of hunting scenes are among the best-known images of medieval aristocratic life.

Legacy

The Livre de chasse was translated into English in the early fifteenth century by Edward of Norwich, second Duke of York, as The Master of Game, an adaptation that added chapters of its own and transmitted Gaston's hunting lore into English tradition.
