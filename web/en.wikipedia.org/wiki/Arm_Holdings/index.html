Arm Holdings plc is a British semiconductor and software design company based in Cambridge, England, whose primary business is the design of central processing unit (CPU) cores that implement the Arm architecture family. It also designs other intellectual property (IP) — graphics processors, systems software, and development tools — and licenses these to chipmakers rather than manufacturing chips itself.

History

Arm was founded in 1990 as Advanced RISC Machines Ltd, a joint venture between Acorn Computers, Apple, and VLSI Technology, to develop the processor that powered Apple's Newton handheld. The Arm architecture's low power consumption made it dominant in mobile phones; by the 2010s virtually all smartphones used Arm-based processors. SoftBank Group acquired Arm in 2016 for about £24 billion. A proposed sale to Nvidia, announced in 2020, was abandoned in 2022 amid regulatory opposition. Arm returned to public markets with an IPO on the Nasdaq in September 2023, one of the largest technology listings of that year; SoftBank retained a large majority stake.

Business model and products

Licensees such as Apple, Qualcomm, Samsung, MediaTek and Nvidia pay licence fees and royalties for Arm cores (the Cortex and Neoverse families) or for the right to design their own cores implementing the Arm instruction set. Arm-based chips ship in the tens of billions annually, in phones, tablets, laptops, servers, embedded systems and vehicles.
