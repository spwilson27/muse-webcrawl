Truth and Reconciliation Commission (Peru)

Comisión de la Verdad y Reconciliación
Established:2001
Chair:Salomón Lerner Febres
Final report:28 August 2003
Period examined:1980–2000

The Peruvian Truth and Reconciliation Commission (Spanish: Comisión de la Verdad y Reconciliación, CVR) was a truth commission created in 2001 by interim president Valentín Paniagua to investigate the human rights abuses committed during Peru's internal armed conflict of 1980–2000, principally between the Maoist insurgency Shining Path (Sendero Luminoso), the Túpac Amaru Revolutionary Movement (MRTA), and the Peruvian state's security forces.

Work and findings

Chaired by the philosopher Salomón Lerner Febres, the commission collected some 17,000 testimonies, held public hearings — a first in Latin America to be broadcast — and documented massacres, forced disappearances, torture, and sexual violence. Its nine-volume final report, delivered on 28 August 2003, estimated that the conflict caused approximately 69,280 deaths and disappearances — far exceeding earlier estimates. The report attributed about 54 per cent of the deaths to Shining Path, holding it principally responsible for the violence, while also finding state agents responsible for a substantial share (about 30 per cent) and the MRTA for a small fraction. It emphasised that roughly three-quarters of the victims were indigenous Quechua-speakers from the rural highlands, and condemned the indifference of Peru's coastal elites and successive governments.

Legacy

The CVR recommended institutional reforms, reparations to victims, and prosecutions. Its work underpinned later trials, including the 2009 conviction of former president Alberto Fujimori for human rights crimes, and the creation of a comprehensive reparations programme and the Lugar de la Memoria museum in Lima. Its findings remain politically contested in Peru, particularly by sectors of the military and supporters of Fujimori.
