Line at infinity

The line at infinity is a line which is added to the real (affine) plane in order to give closure to, and remove exceptions from, the incidence properties of the resulting projective plane. The line at infinity is the locus of the points at infinity — one for each direction (slope) in the affine plane — where families of parallel lines are deemed to meet. In homogeneous coordinates, it is the line z = 0, often denoted ℓ∞.

Projective geometry

In the projective plane, every pair of distinct lines intersects in exactly one point. Lines that are parallel in the affine plane intersect at a point on the line at infinity, and each point at infinity corresponds to a pencil of mutually parallel affine lines. The added line is not distinguished in any way within pure projective geometry: any line can serve as the line at infinity depending on the choice of affine patch.

Hyperbolic geometry

The term is also used in the Poincaré disk model of hyperbolic geometry with a different meaning: there, the boundary circle is sometimes called the line at infinity, and points on it (not part of the hyperbolic plane itself) are called ideal points, at which parallel lines meet asymptotically.
