Speech privacy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Placeholder image — sound masking systems are used to improve speech privacy in open offices.

Speech privacy is the inability of an unintended listener to understand the speech of another person — or, conversely, the degree to which speech can be overheard and understood. It is an important design goal in offices, hospitals, law firms, and other settings where confidential conversations occur, and a legal concern in fields subject to privacy regulations.

Measurement

Speech privacy is quantified with metrics such as the Articulation Index and the Privacy Index, derived from how much speech sound is attenuated between speaker and listener and how much background sound masks what remains. Architectural acoustics describes three complementary strategies, sometimes called the "ABCs": Absorb sound with soft surfaces, Block it with barriers and partitions, and Cover it with sound masking.

Sound masking

Sound masking systems add a low level of electronically generated background sound, typically through loudspeakers in the ceiling plenum, raising the ambient noise floor so that distant speech becomes unintelligible sooner. Speech privacy is a central requirement in healthcare design, where consultations must not be overheard, and in open-plan offices, where it reduces distraction as well as protecting confidentiality.
