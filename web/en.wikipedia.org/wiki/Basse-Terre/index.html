Basse-Terre is a commune in the French overseas department of Guadeloupe in the Lesser Antilles. It is the prefecture — the administrative capital — of Guadeloupe, although the larger city of Pointe-à-Pitre is the economic centre. The name also applies to the western of Guadeloupe's two main islands, a mountainous volcanic island dominated by the active Soufrière volcano (1,467 m).

Geography and history

The town lies on the southwestern coast of Basse-Terre island, sheltered beneath the Soufrière massif. Founded in 1643, it became the colony's first capital and port, defended by Fort Delgrès (formerly Fort Saint-Charles). Hurricanes and volcanic activity have repeatedly damaged it, and in the 20th century much commerce shifted to Pointe-à-Pitre on Grande-Terre.

Landmarks

Sights include the cathedral of Notre-Dame de Guadeloupe (18th century), Fort Delgrès, and the nearby Guadeloupe National Park with the Carbet Falls. The island's lush rainforest and the Cousteau marine reserve at Pigeon Island attract divers and hikers.
