Fielding (cricket)

Fielding
SportCricket
Players11 fielders, including bowler and wicket-keeper
Key skillsCatching, throwing, ground fielding, anticipation
Governing rulesLaws of Cricket (MCC), Law 28 – The fielder

Fielding in the sport of cricket is the action of fielders in collecting the ball after it is struck by the striking batter, to limit the number of runs scored by the batting team or to dismiss a batter by catching the ball in flight or running them out. The fielding side consists of eleven players, including the bowler and the wicket-keeper, positioned by the captain around the field.

Fielding positions

Positions have conventional names, defined relative to the striker and whether they are on the off side or leg side: slip, gully, point, cover, mid-off, mid-on, mid-wicket, square leg, fine leg, third man, long on and long off, among others. Field placements are described as attacking or defensive depending on how close fielders stand to the bat and how many are placed in run-saving boundary positions.

Restrictions

Under Law 28, a fielder may field the ball with any part of the body but may not use clothing or equipment to extend the hand, and wilfully fielding the ball otherwise incurs penalty runs. In limited-overs cricket, playing conditions impose additional restrictions on the number of fielders allowed outside a 30-yard circle during powerplay and later overs.

Skills and evolution

Modern fielding emphasises athletic ground fielding, direct-hit throws at the stumps, relay throws and diving boundary saves; specialist fielding coaches are now standard in professional teams. Exceptional fielders such as Jonty Rhodes popularised the diving run-out and transformed expectations of fielding standards in the 1990s.
