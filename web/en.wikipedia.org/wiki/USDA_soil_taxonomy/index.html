USDA soil taxonomy

The USDA soil taxonomy is a hierarchical system of soil classification developed by the United States Department of Agriculture's soil survey staff. First published in full in 1975 in the monograph Soil Taxonomy, it classifies soils according to objectively measurable properties — including soil moisture and temperature regimes, diagnostic horizons, and features such as clay accumulation or organic matter content — rather than presumed genesis.

Hierarchy

The system has six categories of decreasing generality: order, suborder, great group, subgroup, family, and series. There are twelve soil orders, whose names are formed with the suffix "-sol" (from Latin solum, soil): Alfisols, Andisols, Aridisols, Entisols, Gelisols, Histosols, Inceptisols, Mollisols, Oxisols, Spodosols, Ultisols, and Vertisols. Soil names are constructed from Latin and Greek formative elements that convey the soil's key properties — for example, Mollisols (from mollis, soft) are the dark, fertile grassland soils.

Use

Soil taxonomy underpins the National Cooperative Soil Survey and the county soil surveys of the United States, and it is used in many other countries alongside the international World Reference Base for Soil Resources (WRB). Diagnostic horizons central to the system include the mollic and umbric epipedons (dark surface layers) and subsurface argillic (clay-rich), spodic, and oxic horizons.
