Seal of the President of the United States

The Seal of the President of the United States is used to mark correspondence from the President and as a symbol of the presidency. It consists of the presidential coat of arms — an American bald eagle, shield on its breast, holding an olive branch and thirteen arrows, with the motto E pluribus unum — encircled by the words "Seal of the President of the United States" and a ring of 50 stars.

History

The design derives from the Great Seal of the United States. Early presidents used various personal and official seals; a distinctive presidential seal was made for Rutherford B. Hayes in 1880. In 1945 President Harry S. Truman standardised the arms by executive order, turning the eagle's head toward the olive branch of peace, and in 1959–60 the ring of stars was updated to 49 and then 50 as Alaska and Hawaii joined the Union. Executive Order 10860 (1960) fixed the current design.

Use

The seal appears on the presidential flag, on podiums, in the Oval Office carpet, on the side of Air Force One and Marine One, and on official documents. Its commercial use is restricted by federal law (18 U.S.C. § 713) to prevent false impressions of government endorsement.
