Social Sciences Citation Index

The Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI) is a multidisciplinary citation index covering the journal literature of the social sciences. It was developed by Eugene Garfield's Institute for Scientific Information (ISI) and launched in 1972; it is now produced by Clarivate and made available as part of the Web of Science platform.

Coverage

The SSCI indexes several thousand journals across more than 50 social science disciplines, including anthropology, economics, history, law, linguistics, political science, psychology, public health, sociology and urban studies. Records trace citations between articles, allowing researchers to follow how ideas develop and to compute bibliometric indicators.

Use and criticism

Alongside its siblings, the Science Citation Index and the Arts & Humanities Citation Index, the SSCI underpins journal-level metrics such as the Journal Impact Factor published in Journal Citation Reports. Critics note its bias toward English-language and North American journals, and debate the use of its metrics in hiring and promotion decisions.
