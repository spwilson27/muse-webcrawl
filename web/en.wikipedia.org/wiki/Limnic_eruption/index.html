A limnic eruption, also known as a lake overturn, is a rare type of natural disaster in which dissolved carbon dioxide (CO2) suddenly erupts from deep lake water, forming a gas cloud capable of suffocating wildlife, livestock and humans. Such eruptions may also displace water and cause tsunamis within the lake.

Mechanism

For a limnic eruption to occur, a lake must be saturated with gas, usually CO2 of volcanic origin that seeps up from magma beneath the lake bed. In deep, stratified (meromictic) tropical lakes the bottom water remains undisturbed for long periods, and the high pressure at depth keeps large quantities of gas in solution. A trigger — such as a landslide, an earthquake, heavy rain, or gradual saturation reaching a critical point — can cause some of the deep water to rise. As it rises and pressure falls, CO2 comes out of solution in bubbles, which lift still more water in a runaway feedback, and the gas erupts from the lake in a fountain or jet. Because CO2 is denser than air, the released cloud hugs the ground and flows down valleys, displacing breathable air and asphyxiating people and animals in its path.

Historical events

Only two limnic eruptions have been observed and recorded. On 15 August 1984, Lake Monoun in Cameroon erupted, killing 37 people. On 21 August 1986, a much larger eruption at Lake Nyos, also in Cameroon, released an estimated one to two million tonnes of CO2; the cloud travelled as far as 25 kilometres from the lake and killed approximately 1,746 people and thousands of cattle. The Nyos disaster led to international scientific investigation and to the installation of degassing pipes that siphon gas-rich bottom water to the surface in controlled fountains, gradually reducing the stored gas. Similar pipes operate at Monoun. Lake Kivu, on the border of Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, contains far larger quantities of CO2 and methane near a much larger population and is monitored as a potential future site; methane is also extracted from Kivu for power generation.
