Progressive scan

Progressive scanning (also known as noninterlaced scanning) is a format of displaying, storing, or transmitting moving images in which all the lines of each frame are drawn in sequence. This is in contrast to interlaced video used in traditional analog television systems, in which only the odd lines, then the even lines of each frame are drawn alternately.

Advantages

Progressive scan avoids interlace artifacts such as combing on moving edges and line twitter, and generally produces a sharper, more stable image, especially with fast motion. It became standard for computer monitors and is the basis of HDTV formats such as 720p and 1080p, the "p" in these designations denoting progressive scan.

Uses

DVDs, Blu-ray discs, streaming video, and modern digital television and games consoles all commonly use progressive formats; interlaced source material is often converted by deinterlacing.
