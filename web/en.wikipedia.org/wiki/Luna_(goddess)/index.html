Luna (goddess)

Luna is the divine embodiment of the Moon in Roman religion and mythology. She is the female counterpart of Sol, the Sun. In Roman art, Luna is often depicted driving a two-horse chariot (biga) across the night sky, wearing a crescent moon on her head or in her hair.

Worship

Luna had a temple on the Aventine Hill in Rome, traditionally said to have been dedicated in the 6th century BC, and a smaller shrine on the Capitoline. Her festival was celebrated on 31 March. She was one of the deities addressed in the Carmen Saeculare and was invoked alongside Sol in solemn prayers, the pair representing the regular cycle of day and night and the passage of time.

Identification with Greek deities

Luna was identified with the Greek Selene, from whom she borrowed myths such as the love for the sleeping Endymion. She was also assimilated to Diana, so that Diana, Luna and Hecate could be regarded as a triple goddess (Diva triformis) of the sky, earth and underworld.

The Latin word luna gives the English "lunar" and survives in the names of Monday in Romance languages (French lundi, Spanish lunes).
