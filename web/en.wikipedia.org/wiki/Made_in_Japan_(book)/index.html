Made in Japan (book)

Made in Japan: Akio Morita and Sony is an autobiographical book by Akio Morita, co-founder of the Sony Corporation, written with Edwin M. Reingold and Mitsuko Shimomura. It was first published in 1986 and became an international bestseller.

Content

In the book, Morita recounts his upbringing in a family of sake brewers in Nagoya, his wartime service as a naval engineer, and the founding in 1946 of the company that became Sony, together with Masaru Ibuka. He describes the development of landmark products such as the transistor radio, the Trinitron television, and the Walkman, and sets out his philosophy of management, innovation, and global trade.

Themes

Morita argued that Japanese industry succeeded through long-term thinking, investment in people and technology, and attention to quality, and he contrasted these values with what he saw as short-term financial thinking in some Western companies. The book also addressed trade friction between Japan and the United States during the 1980s and advocated harmonious international business relations.

Reception

The book was widely read by business audiences and helped shape Western perceptions of Japanese management practices during Japan's economic ascendancy in the 1980s.
