Henry Joseph Darger Jr. (12 April 1892 – 13 April 1973) was an American writer and artist who worked as a hospital custodian in Chicago and lived a reclusive life. He is regarded as one of the most celebrated figures of outsider art, famous for a vast illustrated manuscript discovered only shortly before his death.

Life

Darger's mother died when he was four; his father, disabled and impoverished, placed him in a Catholic orphanage, and as a boy he was committed to an asylum for "feeble-minded" children in Lincoln, Illinois, from which he eventually escaped and returned to Chicago. He spent his adult life in menial hospital jobs, attending Mass daily, and living alone in a room in Lincoln Park.

The Realms of the Unreal

For decades Darger secretly wrote and illustrated an epic fantasy of more than 15,000 densely typed pages, generally known as In the Realms of the Unreal. It tells of the Vivian Girls, seven princesses of the Christian nation of Abbieannia, who lead a rebellion against child slavery in the evil kingdom of Glandelinia. He accompanied the text with hundreds of large watercolour paintings, often made by tracing and collaging images from magazines, comics, and colouring books. He also wrote a 10,000-page sequel, an autobiography, and kept a decades-long weather diary.

Discovery and legacy

His landlord, the photographer Nathan Lerner, discovered the work in 1972–73 as Darger, in failing health, moved to a nursing home. Asked about the manuscripts, Darger reportedly said it was too late to publish them. After his death the work was exhibited worldwide; the Henry Darger Study Center opened at the American Folk Art Museum in New York, and his art has influenced numerous writers, musicians, and filmmakers.
