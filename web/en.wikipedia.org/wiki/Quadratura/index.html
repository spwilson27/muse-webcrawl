Quadratura

Quadratura is a Baroque style of illusionistic ceiling painting in which architecture painted on a flat surface appears to extend the real architecture of a room into imagined space, often opening onto sky, clouds, and figures. It is a form of trompe-l'œil.

Development

The technique matured in 17th-century Italy. Andrea Pozzo's ceiling in Sant'Ignazio, Rome (1691–1694), is a canonical example, using a single perspective point marked in the nave floor. Quadratura painters were often distinct specialists from figure painters, collaborating on vast decorative schemes in churches and palaces across Europe.
