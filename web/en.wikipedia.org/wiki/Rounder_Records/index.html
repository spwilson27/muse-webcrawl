Rounder Records

Rounder Records is an American record label founded in 1970 in Somerville, Massachusetts, by Ken Irwin, Bill Nowlin and Marian Leighton-Levy. Originally devoted to folk, bluegrass and string-band music, it grew into one of the most important independent labels for American roots music, with a catalogue of thousands of titles spanning blues, Cajun and zydeco, reggae, country, and world music. It has been part of the Concord Music Group since 2010 and is now based in Nashville, Tennessee.

History

The three founders, then students and folk-music enthusiasts in Boston, named the label after both a term for a hobo and the folk group the Holy Modal Rounders. Rounder's first release, in 1970, was an album by old-time banjo player George Pegram. Through the 1970s the label built a reputation with recordings by bluegrass and old-time artists and with the acquisition and licensing of historic material; it helped document regional traditions such as Louisiana Cajun music and North Carolina string bands at a time when major labels largely ignored them.

From the 1980s Rounder broadened its scope, releasing Robert Plant and Alison Krauss's Grammy-winning collaboration Raising Sand (2007) in partnership with Krauss's longtime label home; Krauss and Union Station recorded for Rounder for decades. The label's roster and reissue programmes have encompassed artists such as Doc Watson, Norman Blake, Tony Rice, Béla Fleck, Nanci Griffith, Mary Gauthier, and the reggae catalogue of Heartbeat Records, among many others. Rounder releases have won numerous Grammy Awards, and in 2010 the founders entered an arrangement under which Concord acquired the label while Irwin, Nowlin and Leighton-Levy remained involved in its direction.

Legacy

Rounder is regarded as a model of the mission-driven independent label: commercially sustainable while preserving and promoting vernacular American music. Its vast archive of recordings, field documentation and reissues is an important resource for scholars of roots music.
