The Cauca Valley (Spanish: Valle del Cauca) is the broad inter-Andean valley of the Cauca River in southwestern Colombia, lying between the Cordillera Occidental and the Cordillera Central at roughly 1,000 metres elevation. It forms the core of the department of Valle del Cauca, whose capital, Cali, is Colombia's third-largest city.

Geography

The valley runs for about 200 km north–south and averages some 20–25 km in width. The Cauca River, Colombia's second most important river, flows northward through its flat alluvial floor before joining the Magdalena River. The level terrain, fertile soils and equable warm climate make the valley one of Colombia's most productive agricultural regions, though the river's flooding historically created marshes and required extensive drainage and flood-control works, including the Salvajina dam (1985).

Economy and history

In pre-Columbian times the valley was inhabited by peoples of the Calima and related cultures known for fine goldwork. Spanish colonisation founded Cali in 1536. From the colonial hacienda era to the present, sugarcane has dominated: the valley is Colombia's principal sugar-producing region, with large mills (ingenios) around Palmira and Cali, alongside coffee on the slopes, cattle ranching, and the Pacific port of Buenaventura, the country's main harbour, connected across the western cordillera.
