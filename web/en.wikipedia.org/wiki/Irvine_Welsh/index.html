Irvine Welsh, author of Trainspotting.

Irvine Welsh (born 27 September 1958) is a Scottish novelist, playwright, and screenwriter. His 1993 novel Trainspotting, a loosely structured series of episodes in the lives of a group of Edinburgh heroin addicts written largely in Scots dialect, became an international bestseller and was adapted into a celebrated 1996 film directed by Danny Boyle. Welsh's work is noted for its vernacular voice, dark humour, and unflinching depiction of addiction, poverty, and working-class life in post-industrial Scotland.

Early life

Welsh was born in Leith, the port district of Edinburgh, and grew up in Muirhouse. He left school at sixteen and worked in a series of jobs, moved to London in the late 1970s where he played in punk bands and worked in property, and returned to Edinburgh, later studying for an MBA at Heriot-Watt University. He began writing fiction in the late 1980s, drawing on the rave and drug cultures he had experienced.

Career

Trainspotting (1993) was followed by the short-story collection The Acid House (1994) and Marabou Stork Nightmares (1995), narrated by a protagonist in a coma. Ecstasy (1996) became the first paperback original to top British bestseller lists, and Filth (1998), narrated by a corrupt detective sergeant, was adapted for film in 2013 with James McAvoy. Later novels revisit the Trainspotting characters: Porno (2002), the prequel Skagboys (2012), The Blade Artist (2016), and Dead Men's Trousers (2018). Other works include Glue (2001), The Bedroom Secrets of the Master Chefs (2006), and Crime (2008), adapted as a television series.

Style and themes

Welsh writes in a phonetic rendering of Edinburgh Scots, mixing dialect narration with standard English, and uses multiple first-person voices and unreliable narrators. Recurring themes include addiction, masculinity, sectarianism, football casual culture, and the social dislocations of Thatcher-era deindustrialisation. Critics have compared his vernacular energy to James Kelman and Anthony Burgess.

Adaptations

Danny Boyle's Trainspotting (1996), scripted by John Hodge, was a major cultural phenomenon; its sequel T2 Trainspotting followed in 2017. The Acid House (1998), Filth (2013), and the TV series Crime (2021) also derive from his books. Welsh co-founded production ventures and has written for the stage and screen.
