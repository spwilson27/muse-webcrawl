Derby (association football)

In association football, a derby (in the United States, a derby game; in the United Kingdom, local derby) is a match between local rival clubs. Derbies have a long history in football and are generally considered the most important fixtures of a season for the supporters of the clubs involved, often generating intense atmosphere, heightened media coverage, and occasionally crowd trouble.

Origin of the term

The word "derby" in the sporting sense derives from the horse race The Derby founded by the Earl of Derby in 1780. Its application to football matches between local teams is often traced to the town of Ashbourne in Derbyshire, where the Royal Shrovetide Football Game has been played for centuries between the "Up'ards" and the "Down'ards" of the town. Another popular theory links the term to a match between Liverpool and Everton, the two clubs in the city associated with the Earl of Derby's residence, Knowsley Hall.

Characteristics

Derbies are usually contested between clubs from the same city or region, but the term is also applied to fixtures between historic rivals from different places, such as El Clásico between FC Barcelona and Real Madrid. Such matches frequently transcend sport, reflecting divisions of geography, class, religion, politics, or history. Because form is often said to "go out the window" in derbies, they are notoriously unpredictable, and victories in them carry a prestige beyond the three points at stake.
