Marquis

A marquis (feminine: marquise) is a rank of nobility in many European peerages. In the British peerage the spelling is marquess, and the rank sits below a duke and above an earl (count).

Origins

The title derives from the Old French marchis, a ruler of a march — a border province of the Carolingian Empire held by a margrave. Because marches were frontier territories exposed to invasion, their lords required great military power and were often ranked above counts of the interior.

By country

Marquises were common in France, Spain, Italy and Portugal. In Britain the title was rare and late; the first English marquess was Robert de Vere, Marquess of Dublin, in 1385. Equivalent titles include the German Markgraf (margrave) and the Japanese kōshaku.
