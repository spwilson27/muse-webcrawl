Frederick Chiluba

Frederick Jacob Titus Chiluba (30 April 1943 – 18 June 2011) was a Zambian politician who served as the second president of Zambia from 1991 to 2002. A trade union leader, he led the Movement for Multi-Party Democracy (MMD) to victory over Kenneth Kaunda in Zambia's first multiparty elections in decades.


In office: 2 November 1991 – 2 January 2002
Preceded by: Kenneth Kaunda
Succeeded by: Levy Mwanawasa
Born: Kitwe, Northern Rhodesia

Early life

Chiluba was born in Kitwe and worked as a bus conductor and in mining-related jobs before rising in the Zambia Congress of Trade Unions, which he chaired.

Presidency

Chiluba's government pursued market liberalisation, privatisation of state-owned copper mines and other enterprises, and political reforms. Economic hardship and corruption allegations marred his second term. He declared Zambia a Christian nation in 1991.

After office

After leaving office he faced corruption prosecutions in Zambia and a civil case in London; he was acquitted in Zambia in 2009. He died in Lusaka in 2011. Compare Félix Houphouët-Boigny, another long-serving African leader of the era.
