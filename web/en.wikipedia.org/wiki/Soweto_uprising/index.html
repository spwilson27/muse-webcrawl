The Soweto uprising was a series of demonstrations and protests led by Black schoolchildren in South Africa under the apartheid regime, beginning on 16 June 1976 in Soweto, a township outside Johannesburg. It became a turning point in the struggle against apartheid.

The protests

The immediate cause was the government's Afrikaans Medium Decree of 1974, which required that certain subjects in Black schools be taught in Afrikaans — a language associated with the oppressor — rather than English. On 16 June, an estimated 10,000–20,000 students marched peacefully to protest. Police opened fire on the crowd; among the first killed was 12-year-old Hector Pieterson, whose dying image, photographed by Sam Nzima being carried by Mbuyisa Makhubo with his sister Antoinette running alongside, became an iconic indictment of apartheid seen around the world.

Aftermath

The uprising spread across South Africa; official figures put the death toll at 176, though estimates run to 700, most of them young people. Thousands of students fled into exile and joined the armed wings of the ANC and PAC, revitalising the liberation struggle. International condemnation intensified, the UN Security Council condemned the regime, and the government eventually scrapped the Afrikaans decree. In post-apartheid South Africa, 16 June is observed as Youth Day, and the Hector Pieterson Museum in Orlando West, Soweto, commemorates the events.
