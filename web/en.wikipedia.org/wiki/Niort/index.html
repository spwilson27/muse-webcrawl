Niort (French pronunciation: [njɔʁ]) is a commune in the Deux-Sèvres department of western France, of which it is the prefecture. It lies on the Sèvre Niortaise river at the southern edge of the Marais Poitevin marshland, sometimes called the "Green Venice".

History

The town grew around a castle raised in the 12th century under the Plantagenet kings of England; the massive twin-towered Donjon survives as a landmark and museum. Niort prospered on river trade, tanning, and the manufacture of chamois leather and gloves. It was fiercely contested during the Wars of Religion in the 16th century and was the birthplace of Madame de Maintenon, the secret wife of Louis XIV.

Economy

Niort is a major centre of the French mutual insurance industry, hosting the headquarters of large mutual insurers such as MAIF, MAAF, and Macif, whose presence gives the town an unusually large office workforce. It serves as the gateway town for tourism in the Marais Poitevin.

Landmarks

Besides the Donjon, sights include the Gothic churches of Notre-Dame and Saint-André, the Pilori (former town hall) in Renaissance style, and the old market halls.
