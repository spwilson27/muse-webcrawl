Red Fort

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Red Fort (Lal Qila) is a historic fortified palace complex in Old Delhi, India. Commissioned in 1638 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan when he moved his capital from Agra to Delhi, it served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years. Built of red sandstone, the complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a national symbol: the Prime Minister of India addresses the nation from its ramparts each Independence Day, 15 August.

History

Construction began in 1638 and was completed about a decade later, forming the centrepiece of the new walled city of Shahjahanabad. The fort was plundered by Nadir Shah of Persia in 1739, when the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond were carried away. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah II, was exiled by the British, who demolished many of the fort's interior palaces and converted it to a military garrison.

Architecture

The fort covers about 103 hectares (254 acres) enclosed by 2.4 km of defensive walls of red sandstone rising up to 33 metres. Its architecture blends Persian, Timurid and Indian traditions. Major surviving structures include the Lahori Gate, the Diwan-i-Am (hall of public audience), the Diwan-i-Khas (hall of private audience) with its famous inscription "If Paradise be on Earth, it is this, it is this, it is this", the Moti Masjid (Pearl Mosque), and the Rang Mahal.
