Cantor space

In mathematical logic and topology, a Cantor space is a topological abstraction of the classical Cantor set: a space homeomorphic to the Cantor set itself. The canonical example is the set of all infinite sequences of binary digits, written 2ℕ or {0,1}ℕ, with the product topology.

Characterization

By a theorem of Brouwer, a topological space is a Cantor space if and only if it is non-empty, perfect (every point is a limit point), compact, totally disconnected, and metrizable. Basic open sets are determined by finite initial segments of a sequence, so two sequences are close when they agree on a long initial segment.

Applications

Cantor space is fundamental in descriptive set theory, where it serves (alongside Baire space) as a standard Polish space in which subsets of the reals are analysed, and in computability theory and logic, where points represent infinite computations or branches through trees. Every compact metrizable space is a continuous image of the Cantor space.

The Cantor set in [0,1] — points whose ternary expansion uses only 0s and 2s — is homeomorphic to this sequence space.
