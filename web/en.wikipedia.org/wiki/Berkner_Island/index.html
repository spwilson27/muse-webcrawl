Berkner Island (also called Berkner Ice Rise or Hubley Island) is an Antarctic island — more precisely an ice rise completely embedded between two ice shelves — in the southern Weddell Sea. With an area of roughly 43,900 km² it is the second-largest island of Antarctica after Alexander Island and one of the largest islands in the world. It separates the Ronne Ice Shelf to the west from the Filchner Ice Shelf to the east.

Geography

The island is about 320 kilometres long and 135 kilometres wide, and its ice surface rises to about 869 metres at the Thyssenhöhe in the south. Because the feature is entirely ice-covered and surrounded by floating ice shelves rather than open sea, its status as an "island" rests on the bedrock (or grounding) beneath the ice; many geographers classify it as an ice rise of exceptional size. Its northern tip, at about 79°20′ S, is the point where the Filchner and Ronne shelves meet the sea.

Discovery and naming

Berkner Island was discovered by members of the United States party at Ellsworth Station during the International Geophysical Year (1957–58) and was named by the Advisory Committee on Antarctic Names for Lloyd Viel Berkner, the American physicist and engineer who helped plan the IGY. It was further charted from aerial photography and satellite imagery in later decades.

Scientific research

Though it has no permanent station, Berkner Island has been an important site for Antarctic glaciology. A deep ice core was drilled near its summit in the 1990s and 2000s by British and German teams, reaching bedrock at about 948 metres and providing a climate record extending back roughly 100,000 years or more, used to study the history of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet and past changes in the Weddell Sea. The island's position makes it sensitive to changes in the surrounding ice shelves.
