Eleazar

Eleazar (Hebrew: אֶלְעָזָר, Elʿāzār, "God has helped") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the third son of Aaron and Elisheba. He succeeded his father as High Priest of the Israelites after Aaron's death on Mount Hor.

Biblical account

Eleazar and his brother Ithamar survived the death of their elder brothers Nadab and Abihu, who were consumed by fire after offering "strange fire" before the Lord. Eleazar was charged with the oversight of the tabernacle and its vessels, and he assisted Moses in the census of Israel and in distributing the land of Canaan alongside Joshua. He supervised the burning of the red heifer, whose ashes were used for purification.

Eleazar married a daughter of Putiel and was the father of Phinehas, who succeeded him as high priest. According to the Book of Joshua, Eleazar died and was buried at Gibeah, the town given to Phinehas in the hill country of Ephraim.

Other figures

Several other biblical figures share the name, including Eleazar son of Dodo, one of David's mighty warriors, and Eleazar the scribe, a martyr in 2 Maccabees who refused to eat pork under the persecution of Antiochus IV.
