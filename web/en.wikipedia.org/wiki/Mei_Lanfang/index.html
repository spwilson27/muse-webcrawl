Mei Lanfang (Chinese: 梅蘭芳; 22 October 1894 – 8 August 1961) was a Chinese singer, dancer and actor of Peking opera who specialised in dan roles — the performance of female characters by male actors. He is regarded as the most famous Peking opera artist in modern Chinese theatre history and the foremost exponent of the "Mei School" of performance.

Career

Born into a family of Peking opera performers, Mei debuted on stage at the Guanghe Theatre in 1904 at the age of ten. Over a career of more than fifty years he refined the singing, dancing, gesture and costume of the dan role, introducing innovations in choreography, stage design and make-up, and creating new plays and dance pieces such as The Drunken Beauty (Guifei Zuijiu) and Farewell My Concubine (Bawang Bieji), in which he played the tragic concubine Yu Ji.

International tours

Mei undertook landmark foreign tours: to Japan (1919, 1924, 1956), the United States (1930), where his performances were acclaimed in New York and other cities, and the Soviet Union (1935), where his artistry influenced theatre practitioners including Sergei Eisenstein and Bertolt Brecht — Brecht's theory of the alienation effect is often linked to his experience of Mei's acting.

Later life and legacy

During the Second Sino-Japanese War, Mei famously grew a moustache and refused to perform for the Japanese occupation authorities. After 1949 he remained in the People's Republic of China, serving as director of the China Beijing Opera Theatre. He is honoured as one of the "Four Great Dan" of Peking opera, alongside Shang Xiaoyun, Cheng Yanqiu and Xun Huisheng. The 2008 film Forever Enthralled dramatised his life.
