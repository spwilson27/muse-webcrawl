Fisher-Price, Inc. is an American company that produces educational toys for infants, toddlers and preschoolers, headquartered in East Aurora, New York. It was founded in 1930 during the Great Depression by Herman Fisher, Irving Price, Price's illustrator-wife Margaret Evans Price, and Helen M. Schelle.

History

The company's first toys were wooden pull-toys, beginning with the "Doctor Doodle" duck in 1931. In the 1950s Fisher-Price began using plastic, which allowed brighter colours and greater durability; the "Buzzy Bee" of 1950 was among its first plastic toys. In 1960 the company introduced the "Little People" line of small figures, which became one of its signature product families, originally made of wood and later of plastic.

Fisher-Price became a subsidiary of the Quaker Oats Company in 1969, was spun off in 1991, and was acquired by Mattel in 1993.

Products

Well-known Fisher-Price products include the Rock-a-Stack, the See 'n Say talking toy, the Chatter Telephone, Power Wheels ride-on vehicles, and the Little People playsets. The brand also encompasses the Imaginext line of themed figures and playsets for older preschoolers, and licences for characters from media franchises.

Child research

The company operates a Play Lab at its East Aurora campus, where children's play is observed to inform product design — a practice dating back to the company's early decades.
