Asphyxia

Specialty: Emergency medicine, forensic pathology

Causes: Airway obstruction, strangulation, drowning, gas displacement, chest compression

Asphyxia or asphyxiation is a condition of deficient supply of oxygen to the body which arises from abnormal breathing. It causes generalised hypoxia, primarily affecting the tissues and organs. If prolonged, asphyxia leads to loss of consciousness, brain injury and death. The brain is especially sensitive: irreversible damage begins after roughly four to six minutes without oxygen.

Types and causes

  • Mechanical asphyxia — physical interference with breathing, including smothering, choking on foreign bodies, strangulation (ligature or manual), hanging, and traumatic/compressive asphyxia from chest compression.
  • Environmental asphyxia — displacement of oxygen by inert gases (nitrogen, helium, argon) or simple asphyxiants in confined spaces.
  • Chemical asphyxia — agents such as carbon monoxide or cyanide that prevent oxygen transport or cellular use.
  • Perinatal asphyxia — oxygen deprivation around birth.
  • Drowning is commonly classified as a form of asphyxia.

Signs and treatment

Signs may include cyanosis, petechial haemorrhages, congestion and involuntary gasping. Emergency treatment is to restore the airway and breathing — removing obstructions, rescue breathing and cardiopulmonary resuscitation. In forensic medicine, asphyxia is a frequent focus of investigation in deaths by hanging, strangulation and suffocation.
