Do it yourself

Do it yourself (DIY)
DefinitionCreating, modifying, or repairing things without professional help
Popularised1950s home-improvement culture; later punk and indie subcultures
RelatedMaker movement, self-sufficiency, home improvement

Do it yourself (DIY) is the method of building, modifying, or repairing things by oneself without the direct aid of professionals or certified experts. The phrase "do-it-yourself" came into common usage in the early 20th century and was popularised in the 1950s in connection with home-improvement projects, as hardware stores, magazines, and later television programmes catered to suburban homeowners.

History and culture

Beyond household repair, DIY became an ethic within subcultures: punk rock bands self-recorded and self-released music (exemplified by labels like Dischord Records), fans produced zines, and independent publishers and filmmakers bypassed established gatekeepers. The internet and video tutorials greatly expanded access to instructional knowledge, and the 2000s–2010s "maker movement" extended DIY to electronics, 3D printing, and crafts, supported by hackerspaces and community workshops.

Motivations and criticism

People pursue DIY to save money, customise outcomes, gain skills, or for the satisfaction of making. Critics note safety risks in fields such as electrical and structural work, the potential for low-quality results, and that "DIY" marketing can shift labour costs onto consumers. Research also associates DIY with self-sufficiency and resilience, but its benefits depend on access to tools, space, and time.
