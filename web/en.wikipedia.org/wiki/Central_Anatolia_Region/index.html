Central Anatolia Region

The Central Anatolia Region occupies Turkey's central plateau.

The Central Anatolia Region (Turkish: İç Anadolu Bölgesi) is one of Turkey's seven geographical regions, covering the central Anatolian plateau. It is the second-largest region of the country by area and includes the capital, Ankara, as well as major cities such as Konya, Kayseri, and Eskişehir.

Geography

The region consists largely of a high, semi-arid plateau roughly 900–1,200 metres above sea level, bordered by the Pontic Mountains to the north and the Taurus Mountains to the south. It includes Lake Tuz, one of the world's largest hypersaline lakes, and the volcanic landscapes of Cappadocia to the east. The Kızılırmak, Turkey's longest river, arcs through the region.

Climate and economy

The climate is continental steppe: hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters with limited rainfall, much of it falling in spring. The plateau is Turkey's principal wheat-growing area, alongside sugar beet, barley, and pulses. Ankara hosts the national government and substantial industry; Konya is a major agricultural and manufacturing centre; Cappadocia draws tourism for its rock-cut churches and "fairy chimney" formations.

History

Central Anatolia was home to the Hittite Empire, whose capital Hattusa lay near modern Boğazkale, and to Phrygians, Galatians, Romans, Byzantines, Seljuks — Konya was the Seljuk capital — and Ottomans in succession.
