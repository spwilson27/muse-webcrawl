Joachim Murat

Joachim Murat (25 March 1767 – 13 October 1815) was a French military commander and statesman who served during the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars. A Marshal of the Empire and one of Napoleon's most flamboyant cavalry commanders, he married Napoleon's sister Caroline Bonaparte and was made King of Naples from 1808 to 1815.

Early career

The son of an innkeeper in southwestern France, Murat joined a cavalry regiment in 1787 and rose quickly during the Revolution. He came to Napoleon's attention in 1795, when he delivered the cannon used to disperse the royalist rising of 13 Vendémiaire, and served with distinction in Italy and Egypt before helping Napoleon seize power in the coup of 18 Brumaire.

Marshal and King of Naples

Created a Marshal in 1804, Murat led the cavalry in Napoleon's greatest campaigns, including the great charge at Eylau in 1807. In 1808 he was given the throne of Naples, where he modernised the administration and army but chafed under Napoleon's control. He commanded the cavalry of the Grande Armée in the disastrous Russian campaign of 1812.

Downfall and death

After Leipzig in 1813, Murat tried to save his throne by negotiating with Austria, but in 1815 he gambled on supporting Napoleon during the Hundred Days and lost his kingdom. Attempting to recover it with a small expedition, he was captured in Calabria and shot by firing squad at Pizzo on 13 October 1815, facing the executioners in full dress uniform.
