Trapezoidal thread form

Trapezoidal thread forms are screw thread profiles with trapezoidal outlines. They are the most common forms used for lead screws (power screws), transmitting motion and force in machinery, because the broad thread root is strong and the form is relatively easy to manufacture. The original and best-known examples are the Acme thread (imperial, with a 29° included angle) and the metric Trapezoidal thread (Tr) standardised in ISO 2901/2903 with a 30° angle.

Characteristics

The trapezoidal profile is a compromise between the square thread, which is efficient but difficult to cut and weak at the root, and the V-thread, which is strong for fastening but creates high friction and radial bursting forces. Trapezoidal threads have good strength, moderate efficiency, and can be cut with a single-point tool or die; split nuts can be used to compensate for wear and backlash. They are typically used in lead screws of lathes, vice screws, jacks, and valve stems.

Variants

  • Acme thread — general-purpose, stub, and modified forms used in North America.
  • Metric trapezoidal (Tr) — ISO standard profile with 30° flanks.
  • Buttress thread — an asymmetric relative designed to carry heavy load in one direction.
