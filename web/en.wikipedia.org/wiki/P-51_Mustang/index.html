North American P-51 Mustang

P-51 Mustang
Manufacturer: North American Aviation
First flight: 26 October 1940
Number built: more than 15,000
Role: Fighter / fighter-bomber
Engine: Rolls-Royce Merlin / Packard V-1650 (later variants)

The North American Aviation P-51 Mustang is an American long-range, single-seat fighter and fighter-bomber used during World War II and the Korean War. Designed in 1940 by a team led by Edgar Schmued to a British specification, the Mustang was transformed when the early Allison engine was replaced by the Rolls-Royce Merlin, producing one of the war's outstanding fighters — fast, manoeuvrable, and with the range to escort bombers from England to Berlin and back.

Development

North American designed the NA-73X in just over 100 days for the British Purchasing Commission. Early Mustangs with the Allison V-1710 performed well at low altitude but lacked high-altitude power. A Rolls-Royce test pilot's suggestion to fit the Merlin 61 led to the P-51B/C and the definitive P-51D with a bubble canopy and six .50-calibre machine guns. The laminar-flow wing and the low-drag Meredith-effect radiator bath contributed to its exceptional speed and range.

Service

From late 1943, Merlin Mustangs escorted USAAF daylight bombers deep into Germany, destroying the Luftwaffe's fighter force in the air and on the ground; Luftwaffe chief Hermann Göring reportedly said that seeing Mustangs over Berlin told him the war was lost. Mustangs also served in the Pacific, in Korea as the F-51, and with dozens of air forces; more than 15,000 were built. Many survive as prized warbirds and air racers, including the heavily modified Voodoo and Strega.
