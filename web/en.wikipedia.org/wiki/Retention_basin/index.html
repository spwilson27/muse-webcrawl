Retention basin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A retention basin, sometimes called a retention pond, wet pond, or wet detention basin, is a type of artificial pond with permanent pool of water, designed to manage stormwater runoff and improve water quality. Unlike a detention basin (dry pond), which temporarily stores water and drains completely between storms, a retention basin maintains a permanent pool. Retention basins are used on construction sites and in residential, commercial, and industrial developments, and are considered a standard best management practice in stormwater management.

Function

As land is developed, impervious surfaces such as roads, roofs, and parking lots increase both the volume and the rate of runoff, carrying pollutants including sediment, nutrients, oils, and heavy metals into receiving waters. A retention basin captures runoff, holds it in the permanent pool, and releases it slowly through a riser and outlet structure. The residence time allows sediment and attached pollutants to settle, while biological processes in the pond and its fringe vegetation take up nutrients. The basin also reduces downstream peak flows and the risk of flooding.

Design

A typical retention basin includes a forebay where incoming sediment settles, a main permanent pool (often 0.9–2.4 metres deep, shallow enough for safety and to support aquatic plants yet deep enough to discourage complete vegetative cover), side slopes, an embankment with a principal spillway (commonly a concrete riser and barrel), an emergency spillway for extreme events, and provisions for maintenance access and sediment removal. Littoral shelves planted with wetland vegetation improve pollutant removal and discourage geese. Design volumes are based on the contributing drainage area and local stormwater regulations.

Benefits and limitations

Retention ponds are among the most effective structural practices for removing suspended solids and phosphorus, provide wildlife habitat and aesthetic amenities, and can increase adjacent property values when landscaped. Limitations include the land area they require, the risk of thermal heating of discharged water, safety concerns (often addressed by gentle slopes and landscaping rather than fencing), possible mosquito breeding if the pool stagnates, clogging of outlets, and the need for periodic dredging of accumulated sediment.
