James IV (17 March 1473 – 9 September 1513) was King of Scotland from 1488 until his death at the Battle of Flodden in 1513, the last Scottish monarch — and the last British king — to be killed in battle. His reign is generally regarded as one of the most successful of any Stewart monarch.

Accession

James came to the throne at fifteen after his father, James III, was killed following the Battle of Sauchieburn, in which the young prince had been the figurehead of the rebel lords — a guilt he atoned for by wearing an iron chain around his waist. Despite this beginning, he proved an energetic and able ruler.

Reign

James IV subdued the Lordship of the Isles, strengthened royal justice, modernized the navy (launching the great ship Michael, the largest of her time), patronized printing (Scotland's first press, 1507) and education, and spoke Gaelic as well as several European languages — a true Renaissance prince. In 1503 he married Margaret Tudor, sister of Henry VIII, a union remembered as the "Marriage of the Thistle and the Rose," which eventually led to the Union of the Crowns in 1603.

Flodden and death

Bound by the Auld Alliance with France, James invaded England in 1513 while Henry VIII campaigned in France. At Flodden Field on 9 September his army was destroyed by the Earl of Surrey; the king, many nobles, and perhaps ten thousand Scots were killed — a national catastrophe long mourned in the lament "The Flowers of the Forest."
