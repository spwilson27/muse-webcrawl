Skidmore, Owings & Merrill


Founded: 1936, Chicago
Founders: Louis Skidmore, Nathaniel Owings, John O. Merrill
Noted works: Lever House, Burj Khalifa, One World Trade Center

Skidmore, Owings & Merrill LLP (SOM) is an American architectural, urban planning and engineering firm founded in Chicago in 1936. A pioneer of the International Style glass-box skyscraper, it became one of the largest and most influential architecture practices in the world.

History

Louis Skidmore and Nathaniel Owings opened their first office in 1936; engineer John O. Merrill joined in 1939. The firm's Lever House in New York (1952), designed by Gordon Bunshaft, defined the curtain-wall office tower. Structural engineer Fazlur Rahman Khan later introduced tubular structural systems that made supertall buildings economical.

Notable projects

SOM's works include the Sears (Willis) Tower and John Hancock Center in Chicago, the Burj Khalifa in Dubai — the world's tallest building — and One World Trade Center in New York.
