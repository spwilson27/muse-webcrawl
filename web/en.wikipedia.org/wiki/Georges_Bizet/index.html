Georges Bizet (born Alexandre César Léopold Bizet; 25 October 1838 – 3 June 1875) was a French composer of the Romantic era. Best known for his final opera Carmen, he achieved few successes during his short life, and most of his works were neglected after his death; today he is regarded as one of the outstanding French composers of the 19th century.

Life

A prodigy, Bizet entered the Paris Conservatoire aged nine and won the prestigious Prix de Rome in 1857. Struggling to establish himself in Paris, he made a living largely as an arranger and piano teacher while composing works including the opera Les pêcheurs de perles (The Pearl Fishers, 1863), the L'Arlésienne incidental music (1872), and a youthful Symphony in C, written at 17 and only rediscovered and premiered in 1935.

Carmen

Carmen, based on Prosper Mérimée's novella, premiered at the Opéra-Comique on 3 March 1875. Its realism, its working-class and criminal characters, and its tragic ending shocked its first audiences and critics, and the run was considered a failure. Bizet died of a heart attack three months later, aged 36, convinced the work had failed. Within a few years Carmen was triumphing in Vienna and across Europe; it is now among the most performed operas in the world, celebrated for its brilliant orchestration and unforgettable melodies such as the Habanera and the Toreador Song.
