CSS Alabama was a screw sloop-of-war built in 1862 for the Confederate States Navy at Birkenhead on the River Mersey in England. She served as a successful commerce raider, attacking, capturing, and burning Union merchant ships in atolls and shipping lanes around the world. She was sunk in June 1864 by USS Kearsarge at the Battle of Cherbourg outside the port of Cherbourg, France.

Construction and escape

The ship was built secretly by John Laird Sons and Company as hull No. 290, known by the cover name Enrica, in apparent violation of Britain's neutrality. Confederate agent James Dunwoody Bulloch supervised her construction. She slipped out of Liverpool in July 1862, ostensibly on a trial run, and rendezvoused at the Azores with supply ships carrying her guns and her captain, Raphael Semmes, who commissioned her as CSS Alabama on 24 August 1862.

Raiding career

Over 22 months Alabama cruised the Atlantic, the Caribbean, the South Atlantic, the Indian Ocean and as far as the South China Sea. She captured and burned dozens of Union merchantmen — 65 prizes in all are commonly credited to her — and sank the Union gunboat USS Hatteras off Galveston in January 1863, the only Union warship sunk by a Confederate raider on the high seas. She never entered a Confederate port.

Sinking and legacy

Needing repairs, Semmes put into Cherbourg in June 1864, where USS Kearsarge awaited him outside French territorial waters. On 19 June 1864 the two ships fought a celebrated duel watched by spectators, including the artist Édouard Manet, who painted the battle. Alabama, outgunned and burdened by deteriorated powder and shells, sank; Semmes and much of the crew were rescued by the British yacht Deerhound.

The diplomatic fallout — the Alabama Claims — saw Britain eventually pay the United States $15.5 million under the Treaty of Washington (1871) and the Geneva arbitration. The wreck of Alabama was located in 1984 and more than 300 artifacts have been recovered.
