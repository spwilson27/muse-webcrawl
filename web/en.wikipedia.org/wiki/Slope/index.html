Slope

In mathematics, the slope or gradient of a line is a number that describes the direction and steepness of the line. Slope is often denoted by the letter m; there is no clear answer to the question why the letter m is used for slope, but its earliest use in English appears in O'Brien (1844) who wrote the equation of a straight line as "y = mx + b", and it can also be found in Todhunter (1888) who wrote it as "y = mx + c".

Slope is calculated by finding the ratio of the "vertical change" to the "horizontal change" between two distinct points on a line. Sometimes the ratio is expressed as a quotient ("rise over run"), giving the same number for every two points on the same line. A line that is decreasing has a negative "rise".

Definition

The slope of a line in the plane containing the x and y axes is generally represented by the letter m, and is defined as the change in the y coordinate divided by the corresponding change in the x coordinate, between two distinct points on the line. In mathematical language:

m = Δy / Δx = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1)

where Δ (the Greek letter delta) conventionally symbolises a difference or change.

Examples

  • A horizontal line has slope 0, since the y coordinate does not change.
  • A vertical line has undefined slope, since the x coordinate does not change and division by zero is undefined.
  • A line rising to the right has positive slope; a line falling to the right has negative slope.
  • A 45° line rising to the right has slope 1.

Algebra and geometry

If y is a linear function of x, then the coefficient of x is the slope of the line created by plotting the function. Hence, if the equation of the line is given in the form y = mx + b, then m is the slope. This form is called the slope–intercept form, because b is the y-intercept of the line.

The slope of a line is related to the angle θ it makes with the positive x-axis by m = tan θ. In calculus, the slope of the tangent line to a curve at a point is given by the derivative at that point.

Applications

The concept of slope applies directly to grades or gradients in geography and civil engineering. Through trigonometry, the slope m of a road is related to its angle of incline. Slope is also a key concept in statistics, where the slope of a regression line measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between variables.
