The Willis Tower, originally and still commonly known as the Sears Tower, is a 110-story skyscraper in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois. Designed by architect Bruce Graham and engineer Fazlur Rahman Khan of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, it opened in 1973 as the headquarters of retailer Sears. At 1,451 feet (442 metres) it was the tallest building in the world for nearly 25 years, and it remains the third-tallest building in the Western Hemisphere.

History

In 1969 Sears, Roebuck and Company, then the largest retailer in the world, decided to consolidate its roughly 350,000 employees into a single headquarters in downtown Chicago. Construction began in 1970 and the building topped out in 1973. Sears sold the tower in the 1990s. In 2009 the London-based insurance broker Willis Group Holdings obtained naming rights, and the building was renamed Willis Tower, though many Chicagoans continue to call it the Sears Tower. Blackstone Group purchased the building in 2015 for a reported $1.3 billion.

Architecture

The tower uses Khan's innovative "bundled tube" structure: nine square tubes, each about 75 feet on a side, rise to different heights, giving the building its distinctive stepped silhouette and great resistance to wind loads. It is clad in black anodized aluminium and bronze-tinted glass, containing some 4.5 million gross square feet of floor space and 104 elevators.

Skydeck

The Skydeck, on the 103rd floor, is one of Chicago's most popular tourist attractions. In 2009 it added "The Ledge," a set of glass balconies extending about four feet beyond the building's facade, 1,353 feet above the ground, offering visitors a direct view down to the street.
