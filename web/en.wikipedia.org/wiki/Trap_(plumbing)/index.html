Trap (plumbing)

In plumbing, a trap is a device that retains a small amount of water after each use, forming a water seal that prevents sewer gases from entering buildings through drain fixtures. Traps are required by most building codes wherever a plumbing fixture is connected to a drainage system.

Types

The most common modern form is the P-trap, whose curved profile resembles the letter P when viewed from the side: the waste pipe descends from the fixture tailpiece, curves through a U-shaped bend, and runs horizontally to the wall. Older or rarer forms include the S-trap (discharging downward through the floor), the bottle trap, the drum trap, and the bell trap. The U-bend portion holds a standing pool of water—typically 50–100 mm deep—known as the trap seal.

Function

The water seal blocks gases such as hydrogen sulfide, methane, and other decomposition products of the sewer from rising into occupied rooms. It also impedes insects and vermin entering via the drain. Because the seal can be lost through evaporation in rarely used drains, through siphonage caused by poorly vented discharge, or through capillary action, traps are paired with a venting system that equalizes pressure in the drain lines. Where evaporation is a problem, trap primers or deeper seals may be installed.

History

The U-bend trap was patented by Thomas Crapper in the late nineteenth century, improving on the earlier S-bend of Alexander Cummings (1775). Its adoption, together with vented soil stacks, made indoor water closets hygienically practical and transformed urban sanitation.
