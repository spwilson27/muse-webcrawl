Selena

Selena Quintanilla-Pérez
Born: April 16, 1971, Lake Jackson, Texas, U.S.
Died: March 31, 1995 (aged 23), Corpus Christi, Texas
Occupation: Singer, songwriter, fashion designer
Years active: 1981–1995

Selena Quintanilla-Pérez (April 16, 1971 – March 31, 1995), known mononymously as Selena, was an American Tejano singer. Called the "Queen of Tejano music", her contributions to music and fashion made her one of the most celebrated Mexican-American entertainers of the late 20th century.

Selena began performing with her siblings in the band Selena y Los Dinos, managed by her father Abraham Quintanilla. She won Female Vocalist of the Year at the Tejano Music Awards for nine consecutive years. Her album Entre a Mi Mundo (1992) topped the Billboard Regional Mexican Albums chart, and Amor Prohibido (1994) became one of the best-selling Latin albums in United States history, popularising Tejano music among mainstream audiences.

Death and legacy

On March 31, 1995, Selena was shot and killed by Yolanda Saldívar, the former president of her fan club, following a dispute over embezzled funds. Her posthumous crossover album Dreaming of You (1995) debuted atop the Billboard 200, making her the first Latin artist to do so. Her life was dramatised in the 1997 film Selena, starring Jennifer Lopez, and in the Netflix series Selena: The Series (2020–2021). She remains one of the best-selling female Latin artists of all time.
