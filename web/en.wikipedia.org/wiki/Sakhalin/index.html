Sakhalin (Russian: Сахалин; Japanese: 樺太, Karafuto) is a long, narrow island in the Russian Far East, stretching some 950 km between the Sea of Okhotsk to the east and the Sea of Japan to the west, separated from mainland Asia by the Strait of Tartary and from Hokkaido, Japan, by the La Pérouse Strait. It is Russia's largest island.

History

Sakhalin was historically inhabited by the Ainu, Nivkh and Orok peoples. Russian and Japanese rivalry over the island ran through the 19th century: the Treaty of Shimoda (1855) left it in joint possession, but in 1875 Russia gained the whole island in exchange for the Kurils. Defeat in the Russo-Japanese War forced Russia to cede the southern half (Karafuto Prefecture) in 1905; the Soviet Union retook the south in 1945, expelling some 400,000 Japanese and Korean residents. Anton Chekhov's account of his 1890 journey, Sakhalin Island, exposed the island's Tsarist penal colonies to the world. In 1983 Korean Air Lines Flight 007 was shot down near Sakhalin by a Soviet interceptor.

Geography and economy

The island is mountainous and heavily forested, with a harsh continental climate. Its economy rests on oil and natural gas from offshore fields in the Sea of Okhotsk (the Sakhalin-I and Sakhalin-II projects, including Russia's first LNG plant, opened 2009 at Prigorodnoye), fishing, coal and timber. A rail ferry links the island to the mainland port of Vanino; a bridge or tunnel across the Nevelskoy Strait has been repeatedly proposed. The 1995 Neftegorsk earthquake devastated the northern settlement of the same name.
