The Andean flamingo (Phoenicoparrus andinus) is a species of flamingo native to the high Andes of South America, where it inhabits shallow saline and alkaline lakes at elevations between roughly 3,500 and 4,500 metres in Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina. It is one of the rarest flamingos in the world, with a population estimated in the tens of thousands, and is classified as Vulnerable.

Description

The Andean flamingo has pale pink plumage with a distinctive large black triangular patch formed by the folded flight feathers, crimson streaking on the neck and breast, and — uniquely among flamingos — yellow legs and feet. Its deep-keeled bill is pale yellow with a black tip, finely adapted for filtering tiny diatoms and algae from lake water.

Behaviour and breeding

Highly gregarious, Andean flamingos feed by sweeping their bills through shallow water and breed colonially beside remote salt lakes, laying a single chalky-white egg on a mud cone. Breeding is irregular and depends on water levels and food supply, which are strongly influenced by El Niño and local climate. The species shares its habitat with the Chilean flamingo and the smaller James's flamingo.

Threats and conservation

Populations declined sharply in the twentieth century owing to egg collection, hunting, and habitat degradation. Current threats include water diversion and lithium and borate mining in the salt flats (salares) of the Andean plateau, disturbance, and drought. Coordinated censuses and wetland protection across the four range countries are central to conservation efforts.
