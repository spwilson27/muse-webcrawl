Golestan Province

Golestan Province (Persian: استان گلستان, "Land of Flowers") is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the north-east of the country, south-east of the Caspian Sea. Its capital is the city of Gorgan. The province was created in 1997 by separating the eastern part of Mazandaran Province. It has a population of roughly 1.9 million and covers an area of about 20,000 km².

Geography

Golestan lies between the Alborz mountain range and the Turkmen steppe, bordering Turkmenistan to the north, North Khorasan Province to the east, Semnan Province to the south and Mazandaran Province and the Caspian Sea to the west. Its varied terrain ranges from the humid Caspian lowlands and dense Hyrcanian forests — inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage property — to dry steppe near the border. Golestan National Park, one of Iran's oldest protected areas, lies in the east of the province.

People and culture

The province is ethnically diverse: Persians, Turkmens, Mazandaranis, Azeris, Kurds, Baloch and Kazakhs all live there. Iranian Turkmens, concentrated in the north around Gonbad-e Kavus and Turkmen Sahra, are known for horse breeding (especially the Akhal-Teke and Turkmen horse), carpet weaving and distinctive dress. The city of Gonbad-e Kavus contains the Gonbad-e Qabus tower, a 1,000-year-old brick monument and UNESCO World Heritage Site, among the tallest pure-brick towers in the world.

History

The region corresponds broadly to ancient Hyrcania, a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire. The Great Wall of Gorgan, a Sasanian-era defensive system of red brick sometimes called the "Red Snake", stretches across the province and is among the longest ancient walls in the world. Human settlement in the area dates back to the Neolithic period.

Economy

Agriculture dominates, with cotton, wheat, rice, soybeans, sunflower and canola among the main crops; the province also produces caviar and other Caspian fisheries products and has growing food-processing and tourism sectors.
