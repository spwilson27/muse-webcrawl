Port state control

Port state control (PSC) is the inspection of foreign ships in national ports by the port state's authorities to verify that the condition of the ship and its equipment comply with international regulations, and that the ship is manned and operated in compliance with those rules.

Background

Primary responsibility for a ship's standards rests with its flag state, but port state control provides a "safety net" against substandard shipping, especially for flags of convenience with weak oversight. The system was strengthened after disasters such as the 1978 Amoco Cadiz oil spill. Inspections are coordinated regionally through memoranda of understanding, notably the Paris MoU (Europe and the North Atlantic, 1982) and the Tokyo MoU (Asia-Pacific), whose members share inspection data and target high-risk ships.

Inspections

Port state control officers check certificates and documents, the ship's condition, crew certification, and compliance with conventions including SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW, and the Maritime Labour Convention. Deficiencies may require rectification before departure; seriously substandard ships can be detained or, under the Paris MoU, banned from regional ports after repeated detentions.
