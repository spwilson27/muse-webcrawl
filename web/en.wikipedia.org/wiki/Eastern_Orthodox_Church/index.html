The Eastern Orthodox Church is the second-largest Christian communion, a family of autocephalous (self-governing) churches — Greek, Russian, Serbian, Romanian, Bulgarian and others — united in faith and sacrament, with the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople holding a primacy of honour. It understands itself as the church of the seven Ecumenical Councils, preserving Byzantine liturgy, iconography and monasticism, and separated from Rome in the Great Schism of 1054 over theology (the filioque), authority and culture.

Life and theology

Orthodox theology centres on theosis — human deification by grace — and on the liturgy as heaven on earth, celebrated with icons ('windows to heaven'), incense and chant. Hesychast prayer (the Jesus Prayer) flourishes on Mount Athos, an autonomous monastic republic since Byzantine times. After communism's fall the Russian church (the largest, whose 2018 rupture with Constantinople over Ukrainian autocephaly split world Orthodoxy) regained public prominence.
