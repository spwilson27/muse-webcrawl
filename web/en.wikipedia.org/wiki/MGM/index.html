Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Studios Inc. (MGM) is an American film and television production and distribution company. Formed in 1924 by the merger of Metro Pictures, Goldwyn Pictures and Louis B. Mayer Pictures, it became the most prestigious studio of Hollywood's Golden Age, boasting "more stars than there are in heaven" and producing classics such as The Wizard of Oz, Ben-Hur and the James Bond franchise (through Eon Productions and its distribution legacy).

History

Under production chief Irving Thalberg and studio head Louis B. Mayer, MGM dominated the box office in the 1930s and 1940s with lavish productions and stars including Greta Garbo, Clark Gable, Judy Garland and Spencer Tracy. The studio system declined after the 1948 antitrust decrees and the rise of television; MGM passed through a succession of owners, including Kirk Kerkorian, Ted Turner (who acquired and then sold back much of the company, retaining its pre-1986 film library for Turner Entertainment) and a Sony-led consortium.

After a bankruptcy restructuring in 2010, MGM continued producing film and television, including the James Bond films, the Rocky/Creed series and the television series The Handmaid's Tale and Fargo. In 2022 Amazon completed its acquisition of MGM for about $8.45 billion, folding it into what became Amazon MGM Studios.
