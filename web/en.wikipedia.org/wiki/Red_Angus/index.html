Red Angus

Red Angus is a breed of reddish-brown-coloured beef cattle similar in origin to the Aberdeen Angus. It is registered in most countries as a separate breed from the black Angus. Red Angus cattle are a naturally polled (hornless) breed known for their maternal ability, calving ease, docility, and marbling.

History

The red colour of Red Angus cattle derives from the red recessive gene carried by the original Aberdeen Angus stock of north-eastern Scotland. In the United Kingdom, red animals were historically excluded from the Angus herd book, which favoured black cattle. In the United States, breeders who valued the red animals in their herds — believing colour had no bearing on quality — formed the Red Angus Association of America in 1954, the first performance registry for beef cattle in the United States.

Because red is recessive to black in Angus genetics, two black Angus parents both carrying the red gene can produce a red calf. Only red calves are eligible for registration as Red Angus, while black offspring of Red Angus parents are not registered in the main herd book in many jurisdictions.

Characteristics

Red Angus are medium-sized, naturally polled cattle with solid red pigmentation that protects against sunburn and eye problems such as cancer eye. They are recognised for fertility, easy calving, good mothering ability, and the production of well-marbled beef. The breed is widely used in crossbreeding programmes, and red-hided crossbred females are prized in commercial herds.
