The Conspicuous Gallantry Medal, the second-highest British award for gallantry available to other ranks.

The Conspicuous Gallantry Medal (CGM) was a United Kingdom decoration awarded to recognise conspicuous gallantry by non-commissioned personnel of the British armed forces in the presence of the enemy. It ranked as the second-level gallantry award for other ranks, immediately below the Victoria Cross, and recipients were entitled to the post-nominal letters CGM.

History

The medal was instituted in 1855, during the Crimean War, to reward ratings and Royal Marines for gallantry at sea — its first awards went to men of the Naval Brigades before Sevastopol. A separate Conspicuous Gallantry Medal (Flying) was created in 1942 for RAF and aircrew personnel, reflecting the medal's restriction otherwise to naval service; awards to Army and RAF personnel for gallantry not in the air were not provided for by the CGM (the Distinguished Conduct Medal and Distinguished Flying Medal filled those roles). Altogether only a few hundred CGMs were awarded across all conflicts, including both world wars, making it one of the rarer British gallantry decorations.

Discontinuation

Following the 1993 review of the honours system, which removed rank distinctions from gallantry awards, the CGM was discontinued and replaced by the all-ranks, all-services Conspicuous Gallantry Cross, which retains the same position in the order of wear below the Victoria Cross. The medal itself was a circular silver piece bearing the monarch's effigy and the words "FOR CONSPICUOUS GALLANTRY", suspended from a ribbon that for the naval medal was white with dark blue edges and for the flying version light blue with dark blue edges.
