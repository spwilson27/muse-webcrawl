Guillaume de Machaut (c. 1300 – April 1377) was a French composer and poet of the ars nova style, regarded as the most famous and influential musician of the 14th century. He was also the last great poet-composer of the trouvère tradition, equally celebrated for his lyric poetry and his music.

Life

Educated probably at Reims, Machaut entered the service of John of Luxembourg, King of Bohemia, around 1323, travelling with his court across Europe. He later served Bonne of Luxembourg and Charles of Navarre, and spent his last decades as a canon of Reims Cathedral, where he survived the plague and the Hundred Years' War sieges. Late in life he conducted a famous epistolary romance with a young admirer, Péronne d'Armentières, documented in his Livre du voir dit ("Book of the True Story", c. 1361–1365).

Works

Machaut's output is unusually well preserved in lavish complete-works manuscripts he supervised himself. His sacred masterpiece, the Messe de Nostre Dame, is the earliest known complete setting of the Mass Ordinary by a single composer. He composed 42 ballades, 33 virelais, 23 rondeaux, motets (many isorhythmic), lais, and a hoquet. His narrative poems (dits) include Remède de Fortune and the Jugement poems. Machaut set the standard for the fixed poetic forms of French lyric and influenced poets such as Eustache Deschamps and Geoffrey Chaucer.
