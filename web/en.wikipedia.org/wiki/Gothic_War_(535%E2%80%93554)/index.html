Gothic War (535–554)

Gothic War
Date: 535–554 AD
Location: Italy and Dalmatia
Result: Eastern Roman victory
Combatants: Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire vs Ostrogothic Kingdom

The Gothic War between the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire and the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy took place from 535 until 554. It was part of Emperor Justinian I's attempt to reconquer the western provinces lost in the 5th century, and is described in detail by the historian Procopius.

Course of the war

The casus belli was the murder of the Ostrogothic regent Amalasuntha. General Belisarius landed in Sicily in 535, took Naples and Rome, and after a long siege received the surrender of King Witiges at Ravenna in 540. Gothic resistance revived under Totila, who recaptured most of Italy, including Rome twice. Justinian sent the eunuch general Narses with a large army in 552; Totila was defeated and killed at Taginae, and his successor Teia fell at Mons Lactarius in 552/553. Remaining Gothic strongholds and a Frankish-Alamanni invasion were defeated by 554.

Consequences

The war devastated Italy: Rome's population collapsed, aqueducts were cut, and the countryside suffered famine and plague. Justinian's Pragmatic Sanction of 554 reorganized Italy under imperial rule, but within fifteen years much of the peninsula fell to the Lombards (from 568).
