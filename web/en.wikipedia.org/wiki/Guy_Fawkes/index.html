Guy Fawkes

Guy Fawkes (13 April 1570 – 31 January 1606), also known as Guido Fawkes while fighting for the Spanish, was a member of a group of provincial English Catholics who was involved in the failed Gunpowder Plot of 1605. He was born and educated in York; his father died when Fawkes was eight years old, after which his mother married a recusant Catholic.

Early life

Fawkes was born in 1570 in Stonegate, York, the second of four children of Edward Fawkes, a proctor and advocate of the consistory court at York. He was educated at St Peter's School in York, where one of his contemporaries was John Wright, later also a Gunpowder Plot conspirator. As a young man Fawkes converted to Catholicism and, discontent with the religious settlement in England, left for the continent in 1591 to fight for Catholic Spain in the Low Countries during the Eighty Years' War, adopting the Italian name Guido.

The Gunpowder Plot

In 1604 Fawkes was recruited by Robert Catesby into a conspiracy to assassinate King James I and restore a Catholic monarch to the throne. The plotters rented an undercroft beneath the House of Lords and, by May 1605, had stored 36 barrels of gunpowder there. Fawkes, an experienced soldier, was given charge of the explosives and was to light the fuse during the State Opening of Parliament on 5 November 1605. An anonymous letter to Lord Monteagle warning him to stay away prompted a search of the cellars; Fawkes was discovered shortly after midnight on 4–5 November with matches and touchwood, and arrested.

Trial and execution

Under interrogation in the Tower of London, Fawkes at first gave his name as John Johnson, but under torture — authorized personally by the king — he revealed his identity and implicated his fellow conspirators. He was tried at Westminster Hall on 27 January 1606 alongside the other surviving plotters and found guilty of high treason. On 31 January 1606 he was hanged, drawn and quartered at Westminster; as he jumped (or fell) from the gallows ladder his neck broke, sparing him the full horrors of the sentence.

Legacy

Fawkes's capture is commemorated in Britain each year on Bonfire Night, 5 November, when effigies of "the Guy" are burned on bonfires amid fireworks. The tradition began with the Observance of 5th November Act 1605, which mandated an annual public day of thanksgiving for the plot's failure. In modern culture, the stylised Guy Fawkes mask popularised by the graphic novel and film V for Vendetta has been adopted by the hacktivist collective Anonymous and by protest movements worldwide.
