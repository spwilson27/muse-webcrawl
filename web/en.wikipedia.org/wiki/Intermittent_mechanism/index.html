Intermittent mechanism

An intermittent mechanism or intermittent movement is a device or movement that advances an object, such as film in a projector, regularly but intermittently, alternating motion with dwell periods. It is essential to many processes in both mechanics and film projection.

Geneva mechanism

The most common intermittent mechanism is the Geneva drive (Maltese cross), in which a rotating pin engages slots in a star wheel, advancing it by a fixed angle once per revolution and locking it in place between steps. It is used in watches, film projectors and indexing machinery.

In cinema

In film projectors and cameras, mechanisms such as the claw and registration pin pull each frame into the gate, hold it still for exposure or projection, then advance to the next frame, typically 24 times per second. Designs like the Lumière Cinématographe's claw mechanism were pivotal in early cinema technology.
