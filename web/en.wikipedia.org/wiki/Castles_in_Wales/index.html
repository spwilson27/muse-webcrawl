Castles in Wales

Wales has more castles per square mile than almost any other country in Europe: over 600 have been identified, of which about 100 remain standing in some form. They were built by Norman and English invaders, by the Welsh princes of Gwynedd, Powys and Deheubarth, and by Marcher lords along the contested border.

History

After 1066 Norman lords pushed into south Wales, building motte-and-bailey castles at Chepstow, Pembroke, and elsewhere; Chepstow's stone keep, begun in 1067, is among the earliest in Britain. Welsh rulers responded with their own stone castles such as Dolwyddelan and Castell y Bere. Edward I's conquest of 1282–83 was consolidated by a ring of great castles and walled towns — Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech, and Beaumaris — designed by Master James of St George and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site as the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd.

Later castles

Later medieval building included the great lordship castles of Raglan and Powis. Many Welsh castles were ruined in the Glyndŵr rising and the Civil War. Today most are in the care of Cadw, the Welsh historic environment service, and form a major part of Welsh tourism and national identity.
