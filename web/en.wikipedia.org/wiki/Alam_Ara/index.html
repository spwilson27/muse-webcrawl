Alam Ara

Alam Ara (Hindi: आलम आरा)
Directed by: Ardeshir Irani
Starring: Master Vithal, Zubaida, Prithviraj Kapoor
Release date: 14 March 1931
Language: Hindi-Urdu (Hindustani)
Status: Lost film

Alam Ara (transl. The Light of the World) is a 1931 Indian film directed by Ardeshir Irani. It was the first Indian sound film (talkie), released on 14 March 1931 at the Majestic Cinema in Bombay. The film, a costume drama about a prince and a gypsy girl, was made in Hindi-Urdu using the Tanar sound-on-film system and featured seven songs, including "De de khuda ke naam par", sung by Wazir Mohammed Khan — the first playback-era song of Indian cinema.

Production and release

Irani, inspired by the American film Show Boat (1929), raced to complete India's first talkie ahead of rivals. The premiere drew enormous crowds; the film ran for weeks to packed houses and established song-and-dance as a defining feature of Indian popular cinema. Master Vithal, a star of silent films, was cast as the hero, with Zubaida as the heroine.

Legacy

No copy of Alam Ara is known to survive; the last known print was reportedly destroyed by fire, and the film is considered lost. It remains a landmark in Indian film history, marking the transition from silent cinema to sound. Google commemorated its 80th anniversary with a doodle in 2011.
