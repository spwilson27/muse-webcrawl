The Queens Public Library (QPL), also known historically as the Queens Library, is the public library system for the borough of Queens in New York City. It is one of three separate and independent public library systems serving the city, alongside the New York Public Library (serving the Bronx, Manhattan, and Staten Island) and the Brooklyn Public Library.

The Central Library in Jamaica, Queens, the main branch of the system.

History

The system traces its origins to 1896, when the Long Island City Public Library was chartered and several community libraries in the area that became Queens were consolidated. The library grew with the borough, absorbing independent village libraries, and its Central Library building in Jamaica opened in 1914. In 2019 the system rebranded itself as Queens Public Library, replacing the "Queens Library" name adopted in the 1990s.

Services

With more than sixty locations, QPL serves one of the most ethnically diverse populations of any library system in the United States, offering collections and programming in dozens of languages, extensive English-language learning classes, job readiness services, and the "Library Card for All" initiatives. Its circulation and attendance figures consistently rank among the highest of American urban library systems.
