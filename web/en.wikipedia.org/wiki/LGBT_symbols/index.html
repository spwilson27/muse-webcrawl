LGBT symbols

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LGBT symbols are the flags, colours, icons and emblems used by lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender communities to express pride, solidarity and identity. Because homosexuality was criminalised or stigmatised for much of the twentieth century, many of these symbols began as discreet codes understood within the community before becoming public emblems of the modern pride movement.

Rainbow flag

The best-known LGBT symbol is the rainbow flag, designed by artist Gilbert Baker in 1978 for the Gay Freedom Day parade in San Francisco. Baker's original design had eight stripes, each assigned a meaning: hot pink for sexuality, red for life, orange for healing, yellow for sunlight, green for nature, turquoise for art, indigo for harmony and violet for spirit. The pink and later the turquoise stripes were dropped for practical reasons, leaving the familiar six-stripe flag (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet). Later variants include the Progress Pride Flag, which adds a chevron of black, brown, light blue, pink and white stripes.

Triangles

The pink triangle (German: Rosa Winkel) was originally a Nazi concentration camp badge imposed on men imprisoned for homosexuality. In the 1970s gay liberation activists reclaimed it as a symbol of remembrance and resistance, most famously in the ACT UP "Silence = Death" poster, which inverted the triangle to point upward. The black triangle was reclaimed by some lesbian and feminist groups, although its use in the camps was associated with "asocial" prisoners generally.

Lambda

The Greek letter lambda (λ) was adopted by the Gay Activists Alliance of New York in 1970 and confirmed as an international gay and lesbian symbol at the International Gay Rights Congress in Edinburgh in 1974. Its precise intended meaning was never fixed; suggestions include liberation, community and energy.

Other symbols

  • Labrys – a double-headed axe associated with lesbian feminism, drawn from Minoan and Amazons imagery.
  • Green carnation – worn by Oscar Wilde and his circle in the 1890s as a coded identifier.
  • Violets – linked with Sappho and with lesbian identity in the early twentieth century.
  • Transgender pride flag – designed by Monica Helms in 1999, with light blue, pink and white stripes.
  • Bisexual pride flag – designed by Michael Page in 1998, with magenta, lavender and blue stripes.
  • Asexual pride flag – black, grey, white and purple stripes, adopted in 2010.
