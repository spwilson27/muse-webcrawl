Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) is an Indian public sector aerospace and defence company headquartered in Bengaluru. Governed by the Indian Ministry of Defence, it is one of the oldest and largest aerospace and defence manufacturers in the world, principally engaged in the design, manufacture, overhaul, and upgrade of aircraft, helicopters, aero-engines, avionics, and accessories for the Indian Armed Forces.

History

The company was founded as Hindustan Aircraft Limited in 1940 by industrialist Walchand Hirachand in association with the princely state of Mysore, with William D. Pawley of the Intercontinental Aircraft Corporation; the Government of India became a shareholder in 1942 and took management control during the Second World War, when the facility overhauled Allied aircraft. It was renamed Hindustan Aeronautics Limited in 1964 upon merger with Aeronautics India Limited.

Products

HAL's indigenous designs include the HF-24 Marut fighter-bomber (developed with a team led by Kurt Tank), the HJT-16 Kiran and HPT-32 Deepak trainers, the Dhruv advanced light helicopter, and the HAL Tejas light combat aircraft, which entered Indian Air Force service in the 2010s. It has also licence-produced numerous foreign types, including the MiG-21, Jaguar, and Su-30MKI — the latter forming the backbone of the IAF's fighter fleet. Current programmes include the Tejas Mk2, the AMCA stealth fighter study, the Prachand light combat helicopter, and the LUH light utility helicopter.
