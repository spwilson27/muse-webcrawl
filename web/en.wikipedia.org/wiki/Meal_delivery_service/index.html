Meal delivery service

A courier delivering a prepared meal ordered through an online platform

A meal delivery service is a service that delivers prepared meals, or ingredients for meals, to customers at their homes or workplaces. The term covers several distinct business models: restaurant food delivery arranged through online platforms, meal kit services that send pre-portioned ingredients with recipes, and prepared-meal subscription services that ship ready-to-heat dishes.

Types

Restaurant delivery platforms

Online platforms such as DoorDash, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, and Meituan aggregate menus from many local restaurants and coordinate couriers — often gig-economy workers travelling by car, bicycle, or scooter — to deliver orders placed through a website or mobile app. These platforms typically charge restaurants a commission and customers delivery and service fees.

Meal kits

Meal kit services, such as HelloFresh and Blue Apron, deliver boxes of pre-measured ingredients together with recipe cards on a subscription basis, allowing customers to cook dishes at home without planning or shopping for ingredients.

Prepared meal services

Prepared meal services deliver fully cooked, often chilled or frozen, meals that need only reheating. Some target health or dietary niches; others, such as Meals on Wheels, are charitable services delivering food to elderly or homebound people.

History

Restaurants have delivered food locally by telephone order since the mid-20th century, with pizza delivery becoming especially widespread. The growth of the internet in the 2000s and smartphones in the 2010s transformed the sector: online ordering platforms expanded from order-taking into full logistics networks. Demand surged in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, when restaurant closures and lockdowns drove consumers to delivery in large numbers.

Criticism and issues

The industry has been criticised over the treatment and classification of couriers, high commission rates charged to restaurants, packaging waste, and the economics of heavily subsidised growth. Regulators in several jurisdictions have introduced fee caps, worker-protection rules, and requirements on tipping practices.
