Lazar of Serbia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lazar Hrebeljanović (Serbian Cyrillic: Лазар Хребељановић; c. 1329 – 15 June 1389) was a medieval Serbian ruler who created the largest and most powerful state on the territory of the disintegrated Serbian Empire. He held the titles of prince (knez) and autocrat, and is commonly known as Tsar Lazar in Serbian epic poetry.

Reign

Lazar rose from the court of Tsar Stefan Uroš V, becoming the most powerful regional lord after the empire fragmented following Uroš's death in 1371. From his capital at Kruševac, he rebuilt Serbian political unity by diplomacy and dynastic marriages, reconciled the Serbian Church with the Patriarchate of Constantinople (1375), and patronised monasteries, including his endowment of Ravanica. He repelled Ottoman raiding forces at Dubravnica (1381) and Pločnik (c. 1386).

Battle of Kosovo and death

On 15 June 1389 (Vidovdan), Lazar led the Serbian army against the invading forces of Sultan Murad I at the Battle of Kosovo. Both rulers were killed, and the battle became the defining event of Serbian national mythology. Lazar was canonised by the Serbian Orthodox Church as Saint Lazar, and his relics, long kept at Ravanica, became objects of pilgrimage; his cult and the Kosovo cycle of epic poems shaped Serbian identity for centuries.
