Pen-y-ghent

Pen-y-ghent is a fell in the Yorkshire Dales, England. At 694 metres (2,277 ft) it is the smallest of the Yorkshire Three Peaks, the others being Ingleborough (723 m) and Whernside (736 m). The name is of Cumbric origin and is generally interpreted as "hill on the border" or "head of the winds".

Geology and routes

The hill shows classic stepped profiles of alternating limestone and gritstone strata, with millstone grit capping the summit. The most popular ascent is from Horton in Ribblesdale, often as the first leg of the Three Peaks Challenge walk of about 24 miles. The Pennine Way passes over the summit. Near the route lie the potholes Hunt Pot and Hull Pot, the latter one of the largest collapsed cavern entrances in England.
