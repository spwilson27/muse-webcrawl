Telford and Wrekin is a borough and unitary authority in Shropshire, England. It is named after the new town of Telford, which forms its largest settlement, and The Wrekin, a prominent hill west of the town. The council is based in Telford, and the borough also includes the older towns of Wellington, Newport, Oakengates, Dawley, Madeley and Ironbridge.

History

The district was created in 1974 under the name The Wrekin, covering the designated new town of Telford (designated in 1963 as Dawley New Town and renamed Telford in 1968 after the engineer Thomas Telford) together with surrounding districts. On 1 April 1998 it became a unitary authority, independent of Shropshire County Council, and in 2000 it adopted its present name.

Geography

The borough encompasses the eastern Shropshire coalfield and part of the Ironbridge Gorge, a UNESCO World Heritage Site regarded as a birthplace of the Industrial Revolution, where Abraham Darby first smelted iron with coke and where the world's first iron bridge was erected in 1779. The Wrekin, at 407 metres, topped by an Iron Age hill fort, is a Shropshire landmark.

Governance and economy

Telford and Wrekin Council provides all local services. The area sends MPs to the Telford and The Wrekin constituencies. Telford's economy centres on manufacturing, distribution and public services, with large business parks around the M54 motorway, while tourism is significant around Ironbridge's museums.
