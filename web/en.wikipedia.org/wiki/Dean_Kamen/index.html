Dean L. Kamen (born April 5, 1951) is an American engineer, inventor and entrepreneur. He is best known as the inventor of the Segway personal transporter and as the founder of FIRST (For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology), the organisation that runs international robotics competitions for school students. He holds more than a thousand patents worldwide, many of them for medical devices.

Early life and career

Kamen grew up on Long Island, New York, the son of Jack Kamen, an illustrator for science-fiction and horror magazines. As a teenager he was already being paid to design sound and light systems for museums and bands. He attended Worcester Polytechnic Institute but left before graduating to pursue his inventions. His first major success, developed while still an undergraduate, was the AutoSyringe, a wearable portable insulin pump for diabetics; he sold the company manufacturing it to Baxter International in 1982.

DEKA and the Segway

In 1982 Kamen founded DEKA Research & Development in Manchester, New Hampshire. DEKA developed the HomeChoice dialysis system, a portable dialysis machine, and the iBOT, a powered wheelchair capable of climbing stairs and raising its user to eye level, using self-balancing gyroscopic technology.

That balancing technology produced the Segway PT, unveiled in December 2001 after months of speculation about a secret invention code-named "Ginger". The two-wheeled, self-balancing electric vehicle was hailed as a transportation revolution, but high cost and regulatory hurdles limited its sales; production ended in 2020. Kamen later revived the iBOT programme and continued work on water purification, including the Slingshot vapour-compression distiller, and on regenerative medicine through ARMI (Advanced Regenerative Manufacturing Institute), which he founded in 2016.

FIRST

Kamen founded FIRST in 1989 to inspire young people's interest in science and engineering. Its flagship FIRST Robotics Competition, launched in 1992, pairs high-school teams with professional engineers to build large robots in a few weeks; the organisation's programmes, including FIRST LEGO League and FIRST Tech Challenge, now reach hundreds of thousands of students in more than a hundred countries. Kamen received the National Medal of Technology in 2000 and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2005.
