Illness anxiety disorder

Illness anxiety disorder (IAD), sometimes called health anxiety or health phobia, is a mental disorder characterized by excessive worry about having or developing a serious, undiagnosed medical condition. It was introduced in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), and replaced much of what had previously been diagnosed as hypochondriasis.

People with illness anxiety disorder are preoccupied with the idea that they are seriously ill, based on a misinterpretation of normal bodily sensations or minor symptoms. The preoccupation persists despite appropriate medical evaluation and reassurance. Unlike somatic symptom disorder, individuals with illness anxiety disorder have few or no somatic symptoms; their distress comes primarily from anxiety about the meaning, significance and cause of symptoms rather than from the symptoms themselves.

Signs and symptoms

Sufferers may repeatedly check their body for signs of illness, frequently request reassurance from doctors, family and friends, or spend excessive amounts of time researching diseases online — a pattern sometimes termed "cyberchondria". Others avoid medical care entirely for fear of receiving bad news. The anxiety can significantly impair social, occupational and other areas of functioning.

Diagnosis

DSM-5 criteria require a preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness lasting at least six months, high levels of anxiety about health, excessive health-related behaviours or maladaptive avoidance, and symptoms not better explained by another mental disorder such as somatic symptom disorder, panic disorder or generalized anxiety disorder. The DSM-5 distinguishes a "care-seeking type" from a "care-avoidant type".

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is the most strongly supported treatment, helping patients challenge catastrophic interpretations of bodily sensations and reduce checking and reassurance-seeking behaviours. Antidepressant medications, particularly selective serotonin re-uptake inhibitors (SSRIs), may also be helpful, especially where comorbid anxiety or depression is present.
