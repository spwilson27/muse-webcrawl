Lund University (Swedish: Lunds universitet) is a public research university in Lund, Scania, Sweden. Founded in 1666, it is one of Sweden's oldest and largest universities and is consistently ranked among the world's top 100 universities. The university has around 46,000 students and more than 8,000 staff across eight faculties, with additional campuses in Malmö and Helsingborg.

History

Lund had been a centre of learning since the studium generale founded in 1438. After Scania was ceded by Denmark to Sweden in the Treaty of Roskilde (1658), the university was founded in 1666—partly to integrate the province—and inaugurated in 1668 in Lund Cathedral. It closed during wars with Denmark but was revived under Charles XI, and grew through the 18th and 19th centuries; the landmark main building, designed by Helgo Zettervall, opened in 1882.

Organisation and research

The university comprises faculties including engineering (LTH), medicine, law, science, humanities and theology, social sciences, economics and management, and fine and performing arts. Lund hosts major research infrastructure, including the MAX IV synchrotron light source and, nearby, the European Spallation Source (ESS). Student life is organised around thirteen historic student "nations" and the Academic Society, and traditions such as the doctoral conferment ceremony in the cathedral date back centuries.
