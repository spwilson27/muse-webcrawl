Karwendel

The Karwendel is a mountain range of the Northern Limestone Alps, lying mostly in the Austrian state of Tyrol with its northern part in Bavaria, Germany, between the Inn valley and Innsbruck to the south and the Isar river to the north. It is one of the largest contiguous, largely unspoilt mountain areas in the Eastern Alps, characterised by four long parallel limestone chains running east–west, deep side valleys, and extensive forests and alpine meadows.

Geography

The highest summit is the Birkkarspitze at 2,749 metres. Other notable peaks include the mountains of the Gleirsch-Halltal chain overlooking Innsbruck's Nordkette, and summits popular with climbers such as the Laliderer Spitze and the peaks around the Großer Ahornboden, a plateau famous for its centuries-old sycamore maples. Rivers including the Isar rise in the Karwendel.

Conservation and tourism

Most of the Austrian part of the range is protected as the Alpenpark Karwendel, founded in 1928 and Austria's largest nature park, home to golden eagles, chamois, ibex and marmots. The range is crossed by hiking routes including stages of the Via Alpina and the Eagle Walk (Adlerweg), and mountain railways such as the Karwendelbahn near Mittenwald serve visitors. The name Karwendel derives from an old personal name, Karwendil or Gerwendil.
