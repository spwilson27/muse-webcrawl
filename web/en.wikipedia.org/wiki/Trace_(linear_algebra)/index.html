Trace (linear algebra)

In linear algebra, the trace of a square matrix A, denoted tr(A), is the sum of the elements on its main diagonal. It is defined only for square matrices and equals the sum of the matrix's eigenvalues (counted with algebraic multiplicity).

Properties

  • Linearity: tr(A + B) = tr(A) + tr(B), and tr(cA) = c·tr(A).
  • Cyclic property: tr(AB) = tr(BA) whenever both products are defined, and more generally the trace is invariant under cyclic permutations of a product.
  • Similarity invariance: tr(P−1AP) = tr(A), so the trace of a linear operator is independent of the choice of basis.
  • tr(A) = tr(AT), and for a nilpotent matrix the trace is zero.

Applications

The trace appears in the definition of the Frobenius inner product ⟨A, B⟩ = tr(ATB), in character theory of group representations (a character is the trace of a representation matrix), and in physics, where traces over operator spaces occur in quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics. For a 2×2 matrix, the trace and determinant together determine the eigenvalues.
