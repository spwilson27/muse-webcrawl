Conker

[Image placeholder: conker in split husk]

A conker is the hard, glossy brown seed of the horse chestnut tree (Aesculus hippocastanum). The seed is roughly spherical, 2–4 cm across, with a pale scar (hilum) where it was attached inside its fruit. Conkers ripen and fall in autumn, splitting from their spiky green husks.

Uses

Conkers are best known as the playing pieces of the children's game of conkers, popular in Britain and Ireland, in which a conker threaded on a string is swung at an opponent's conker until one breaks. The inedible seeds contain aescin and are mildly poisonous to humans, though deer and other animals eat them. Conkers were collected on a large scale during both World Wars as a source of acetone for cordite production, and folk belief holds that placing conkers in a room deters spiders — a claim without scientific support. The name is also used for the character Conker the Squirrel in video games.
