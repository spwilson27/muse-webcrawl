Paul Miki (Japanese: パウロ三木; c. 1562 – 5 February 1597) was a Japanese Catholic evangelist and Jesuit seminarian who was martyred in Nagasaki under the regime of Toyotomi Hideyoshi. He is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church and is the best known of the Twenty-six Martyrs of Japan.

Life

Miki was born in Kyoto into a wealthy Japanese family; his father, Miki Handayu, was a military leader in the service of Oda Nobunaga. He was baptised at the age of five and educated at the Jesuit seminary at Azuchi. He joined the Society of Jesus in 1580 and became renowned as an eloquent and effective preacher, credited with bringing many of his countrymen to the Catholic faith. At the time of his arrest he had completed his studies for the priesthood but had not yet been ordained, owing to the absence of a bishop in Japan.

Martyrdom

In 1587 Hideyoshi issued an edict expelling missionaries, but enforcement was initially sporadic. In December 1596, amid suspicion that Christians were preparing the way for Iberian conquest — inflamed by the wreck of the Spanish galleon San Felipe — Miki and twenty-five other Christians, including Franciscan missionaries, Jesuits, and laypeople (among them three young boys), were arrested in Kyoto and Osaka. Part of each prisoner's left ear was cut off, and they were marched some 600 miles through the winter to Nagasaki. On 5 February 1597 they were crucified and pierced with spears on Nishizaka Hill, overlooking Nagasaki Bay. From his cross, Miki is said to have preached a final sermon forgiving his executioners.

Veneration

The twenty-six martyrs were beatified in 1627 and canonised by Pope Pius IX on 8 June 1862. Their feast day is observed on 6 February. Paul Miki is a patron saint of Japan, and a church and museum at the site of the martyrdom in Nagasaki commemorate the group.
