The Mansion House is the official residence of the Lord Mayor of London. Located in the City of London, opposite the Bank of England, it is a Grade I listed Palladian building used for official City functions, including the annual dinner at which the Chancellor of the Exchequer traditionally delivers the "Mansion House Speech" on the state of the economy.

History

Before the Mansion House was built, lord mayors had used livery company halls or private houses for official business. After the financial disruption of the South Sea Bubble and pressure for a permanent civic residence, the City purchased a site at Stocks Market, and the foundation stone was laid in 1739. The building, designed by George Dance the Elder, the City surveyor, was first occupied by Lord Mayor Sir Crisp Gascoyne in 1752, though work continued until about 1758.

Architecture

The exterior is dominated by a hexastyle Corinthian portico raised on a rusticated ground floor, originally approached from the market square. Behind the portico lies the Egyptian Hall, a large reception room named not for Egyptian decoration but for its arrangement of columns in the manner Vitruvius described as an "Egyptian hall"; it hosts banquets for up to several hundred guests. Other notable interiors include the saloon, the drawing rooms, and the former courtroom and cells: the Mansion House historically contained the Lord Mayor's court, and suffragettes were among those held in its cells in the early twentieth century.

Collections

The house holds civic plate, including the great mace and sword, and the Harold Samuel Collection of Dutch and Flemish seventeenth-century paintings, one of the finest such collections formed in Britain in the twentieth century, bequeathed to the City in 1987.

Events

Besides the Chancellor's speech in June, Mansion House hosts the Lord Mayor's Banquet in November, at which the Prime Minister traditionally speaks, and the Easter Banquet for the diplomatic corps, hosted by the Foreign Secretary.
