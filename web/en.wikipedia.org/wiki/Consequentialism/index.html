Consequentialism

Consequentialism
FieldNormative ethics
Core claimThe morality of an action depends only on its consequences
Best-known formUtilitarianism
[Image: A railway switch (points) at a junction, recalling the trolley problem]

Consequentialism is the family of ethical theories holding that the consequences of an action — and only they — determine its moral status. Its best-known member, Utilitarianism, evaluates outcomes by aggregate well-being; other forms value equality, knowledge or preference-satisfaction. Consequentialism contrasts with deontology (duty- and rights-based) and virtue ethics (character-based), though hybrids abound.

Debates

Famous tests include the trolley problem and Bernard Williams's integrity objection (a theory that makes you a 'channel' for outcomes alienates you from your own projects). 'Rule consequentialism' evaluates rules by the optimific results of their general acceptance, hoping to recover common-sense morality; critics call the collapse into act-consequentialism or rule-worship its dilemma.
