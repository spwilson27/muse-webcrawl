K computer

LocationRIKEN Center for Computational Science, Kobe, Japan
DevelopersRIKEN and Fujitsu
Active2011–2019
Peak performance10.51 petaFLOPS
SuccessorFugaku

The K computer, named after the Japanese word kei (京), meaning 10 quadrillion, was a supercomputer developed by RIKEN and Fujitsu at the RIKEN Center for Computational Science in Kobe, Japan. In June 2011 it became the first computer to exceed 10 petaFLOPS and topped the TOP500 list as the world's fastest supercomputer.

Architecture

The system used more than 88,000 SPARC64 VIIIfx processors, totalling over 700,000 cores, interconnected by Fujitsu's Tofu six-dimensional mesh/torus network. It was notable for its energy-efficient design and for achieving high sustained performance on real applications.

Applications and legacy

The K computer was used for climate modelling, disaster prevention, materials science, and medical research, including large-scale earthquake and weather simulations. It was retired in 2019 and succeeded at the same site by Fugaku, which again topped the TOP500 in 2020.
