Electronic drum

An electronic drum kit is a percussion instrument in which sounds are generated electronically rather than acoustically. The player strikes pads fitted with sensors (triggers), which send signals to a sound module ("brain") that plays back sampled or synthesised drum and percussion sounds through an amplifier or headphones.

History

The first commercial electronic drums appeared in the 1970s, notably the Pollard Syndrum (1976). The Simmons SDS-V of 1981, with its distinctive hexagonal pads, became an icon of 1980s pop. Early kits used synthesised sounds; from the 1990s, sampling-based modules from Roland (the V-Drums line), Yamaha, Alesis and others offered realistic acoustic drum sounds, mesh playing heads with natural rebound, and positional sensing.

Advantages and use

Electronic kits are much quieter than acoustic drums (a major advantage for home practice, especially with headphones), occupy less space, and offer hundreds of different kit sounds from a single setup, as well as easy recording via MIDI and USB. They are used in teaching, home studios, theatre pits and live performance, often in hybrid acoustic-electronic setups. Criticisms traditionally centred on feel and expressiveness, which modern mesh-head kits have largely addressed.
