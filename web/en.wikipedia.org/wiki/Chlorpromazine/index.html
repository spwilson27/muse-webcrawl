Overview

Chlorpromazine, sold under trade names including Thorazine and Largactil, is a phenothiazine antipsychotic medication. Developed in France and introduced into psychiatry in 1952, it was the first effective drug treatment for schizophrenia and other psychoses, and its success helped launch the modern era of psychopharmacology.

History

Chemists at Rhone-Poulenc synthesised chlorpromazine in 1950 while seeking antihistamines; surgeon Henri Laborit noted its calming effects, and psychiatrists Jean Delay and Pierre Deniker demonstrated its antipsychotic action at Sainte-Anne Hospital in Paris. Its widespread adoption in the 1950s transformed asylum care and is credited with contributing to deinstitutionalisation, though its benefits came with significant side effects.

Pharmacology and uses

Chlorpromazine works primarily by blocking dopamine D2 receptors, with additional antihistaminic, anticholinergic and antiadrenergic effects that account for sedation, dry mouth, orthostatic hypotension and extrapyramidal symptoms including tardive dyskinesia with long-term use. It remains on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines and is also used for intractable hiccups, nausea and as a sedative.
