Ramsey County is a county in the U.S. state of Minnesota. As of the 2020 census, the population was 552,352, making it the second-most populous county in the state after Hennepin County. Its county seat and largest city is Saint Paul, the state capital. Ramsey County is the smallest county in Minnesota by land area and the most densely populated, lying entirely within the Minneapolis–Saint Paul metropolitan area.

History

Ramsey County was one of the nine original counties established by the Minnesota Territory legislature on October 27, 1849. It is named for Alexander Ramsey, the first territorial governor of Minnesota and later a U.S. senator and Secretary of War. The original county was far larger than today; its boundaries shrank as new counties were organized, reaching its present extent by the 1880s.

Geography

The county covers about 170 square miles, of which roughly 152 square miles are land and 18 square miles are water. The Mississippi River forms much of its southern and western boundary. Major suburbs include Roseville, Maplewood, Shoreview, New Brighton, Mounds View, White Bear Lake, and Little Canada. The county is governed by a seven-member Board of Commissioners elected by district.

Demographics

Ramsey County is one of the most diverse counties in Minnesota, with significant Hmong, Somali, Latino, and other immigrant communities, particularly in Saint Paul. Major institutions include the Minnesota State Capitol, the University of St. Thomas, Hamline University, and Macalester College.
