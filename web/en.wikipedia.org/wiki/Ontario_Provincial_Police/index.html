Ontario Provincial Police

The Ontario Provincial Police (OPP) is the provincial police service of Ontario, Canada. One of the largest police services in North America, it provides policing to most of the province's rural areas and small communities, patrols provincial highways, and supplies specialized investigative and support services. In French it is known as the Police provinciale de l'Ontario.

History

Provincial policing in Ontario grew from nineteenth-century constables and magistrates' forces. The OPP was formally organized in 1909. It expanded significantly with the growth of the provincial highway system and took on responsibility for policing many municipalities under contract, as well as for Ontario's provincial parks and government property.

Role and structure

The OPP is headed by a Commissioner appointed by the provincial government. Its general headquarters is in Orillia. The service is organized into regions and detachments, and includes specialized units such as the highway safety division, tactical and rescue units, canine units, aviation services, and investigative teams for organized crime and anti-rackets work. Many smaller municipalities purchase OPP policing by contract rather than maintaining their own forces.

Scope

OPP officers patrol most of Ontario's provincial highway network, including the 400-series highways outside jurisdictions served by municipal services, and police vast areas of northern and rural Ontario, including services in many First Nations communities under various agreements.
