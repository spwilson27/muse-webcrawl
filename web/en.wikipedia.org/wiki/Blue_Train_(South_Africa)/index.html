The Blue Train is a luxury passenger train service in South Africa that travels between Pretoria and Cape Town, a journey of about 1,600 kilometres (990 mi) that is regarded as one of the world's most luxurious rail journeys. It features butler service, en-suite suites, fine dining and an observation car, and is operated as an icon of South African tourism.

History

The service descends from the Union Limited (Cape Town to Johannesburg) and Union Express introduced in 1922 by the South African Railways to connect with the mail ships sailing to Southampton. In 1937 new air-conditioned, all-steel coaches painted in a distinctive blue livery were introduced, and the trains became informally known as "those blue trains". After wartime suspension, the service resumed in 1946 under the official name Blue Train. New train sets built by Union Carriage & Wagon entered service in the 1970s and again, in a comprehensive refurbishment programme, in the late 1990s.

Route and services

The standard itinerary runs between Pretoria and Cape Town with a stop at Kimberley, where passengers may tour the Big Hole and the Kimberley Mine Museum; southbound journeys have included an excursion at Matjiesfontein. The train takes roughly 27 to 31 hours including stops. Suites are configured as daytime sitting rooms and converted to bedrooms at night, all with private bathrooms; the train includes lounge and club cars, a dining car serving South African cuisine and wines, and an observation car at the rear. A second route to Hoedspruit, serving safari lodges near the Kruger National Park, has operated in some seasons.

Recognition

The Blue Train has repeatedly been voted the world's leading luxury train at the World Travel Awards. It has hosted presidents, royalty and celebrities, and is marketed alongside journeys such as the Orient Express and the Trans-Siberian.
