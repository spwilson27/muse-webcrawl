Peter Norbeck (1870–1936) was an American politician from South Dakota, of Norwegian immigrant stock, who served as the state's ninth governor and then as a United States senator from 1921 until his death. A progressive Republican and conservationist, he is remembered as the political father of Custer State Park and of the Mount Rushmore memorial.

Career

Norbeck prospered in the well-drilling business before entering politics, serving in the state senate — where he championed progressive measures — and as lieutenant governor. As governor (1917–1921) he pushed state-owned enterprises unusual for the era, including a state cement plant, coal mine, and a rural credits (farm loan) programme.

Conservation and Mount Rushmore

Norbeck secured the creation of Custer State Park in the Black Hills and designed its scenic Norbeck Scenic Byway, laying out routes such as the Needles Highway and Iron Mountain Road with their pigtail bridges and single-lane tunnels framing views of Mount Rushmore. In the Senate he worked with sculptor Gutzon Borglum and historian Doane Robinson to obtain federal authorisation and funding for the Mount Rushmore carving, begun in 1927. He also supported federal wildlife refuges and migratory-bird protection legislation.
