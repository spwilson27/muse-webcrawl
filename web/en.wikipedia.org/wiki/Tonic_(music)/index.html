Tonic (music)

In music theory, the tonic is the first scale degree (the root note) of a diatonic scale and the tonal centre or final resolution tone of a piece of music in the tonal system. The tonic is the most stable and central pitch of a key; the triad built upon it, the tonic chord, likewise functions as the harmonic home base toward which other chords tend to resolve.

Function

In the major scale the degrees are named tonic, supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant and leading tone (or subtonic). The dominant chord (V) conventionally resolves to the tonic chord (I), a progression that articulates the key and provides closure at cadences. Melodies typically begin on or gravitate toward the tonic and end upon it. In modern popular music, tonic is commonly synonymous with the keynote or tonal centre, and discussions of song "key" refer to its tonic pitch and mode.

The term is also used more loosely in modal and non-Western contexts for the central pitch of a mode or raga. In solfège, the tonic of the major scale is sung as do. Extended compositions establish the tonic so firmly that departure from it — modulation to other keys — supplies much of the drama of tonal form, before an eventual return confirms it.
