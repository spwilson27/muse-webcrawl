New Order are an English rock band formed in Salford in 1980 by Bernard Sumner, Stephen Morris, and Peter Hook, the remaining members of Joy Division following the death of lead singer Ian Curtis. Keyboardist Gillian Gilbert joined shortly afterward. The band integrated post-punk with electronic and dance music, becoming one of the most critically acclaimed and influential bands of the 1980s.

Career

After the somber debut album Movement (1981), New Order embraced synthesizers and sequencers. Their 1983 single "Blue Monday" became the best-selling 12-inch single of all time and a landmark of electronic dance music. Albums such as Power, Corruption & Lies (1983), Low-Life (1985), and Technique (1989), recorded partly in Ibiza, defined their sound. They were central to the Madchester scene through the Haçienda nightclub, which they co-owned with Factory Records.

The band split and re-formed several times; Hook departed acrimoniously in 2007. A later lineup including Tom Chapman released Music Complete in 2015.
