Comfort food

CharacteristicsNostalgic, high-calorie, simple, filling

Term in printBy the 1960s–1970s in the United States

Comfort food is food that provides a nostalgic or sentimental value to someone and is often characterised by high caloric content and simple preparation associated with childhood or home cooking. The nostalgia may be specific to the individual or to a culture. Comfort foods are frequently eaten during times of stress or unhappiness, and research suggests that consuming them can modestly improve mood through learned associations.

Regional examples

Comfort foods vary widely by culture. In the United States they include macaroni and cheese, chicken soup, fried chicken, pot roast and ice cream. British examples include bangers and mash, shepherd's pie and bread and butter pudding. Japanese comfort foods include miso soup, curry rice and ramen; in Italy, pasta dishes and polenta serve similar roles; in India, khichdi and dal with rice are common. What unites them is familiarity and emotional resonance rather than any shared ingredient.

Psychology and nutrition

Studies of "emotional eating" find that comfort-food choices often mirror foods served by caregivers in childhood. While such foods tend to be energy-dense and high in fat, sugar or salt, psychologists caution that the comfort effect derives mainly from social and emotional associations — even the memory or smell of the food can be soothing.
