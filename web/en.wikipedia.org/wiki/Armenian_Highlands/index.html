The Armenian Highlands (Armenian: Հայկական լեռնաշխարհ, Haykakan leṙnašxarh) is a mountainous plateau in West Asia that lies mainly in eastern Turkey, the whole of Armenia, southern Georgia, western Azerbaijan and northwestern Iran. It is the historic homeland of the Armenian people and one of the most elevated inhabited regions of the Near East, with an average altitude of around 1,500 to 2,000 metres.

Geography

The highlands form a rugged plateau of volcanic origin, broken by mountain ranges and deep river valleys. The region is sometimes called the "Armenian knot" because several great mountain systems meet there and because it is the watershed from which major rivers flow in different directions: the Euphrates, the Tigris, the Aras, the Kura and the Çoruh. Large high-altitude lakes include Lake Van, Lake Sevan and Lake Urmia.

The highest summit is the volcanic cone of Mount Ararat (5,137 m), the traditional resting place of Noah's Ark and the national symbol of Armenia. Other major peaks include Mount Aragats (4,090 m), the highest point in the modern Republic of Armenia, and Mount Süphan in Turkey.

Climate

The climate is continental, with severe, snowy winters and hot, dry summers. Because of the elevation, much of the plateau is steppe used for grazing, with agriculture concentrated in irrigated valley floors and around the great lakes.

History

The highlands were home to the Bronze Age kingdom of Urartu (9th–6th centuries BC), centred on Lake Van, and later to the successive Armenian kingdoms of the Orontid, Artaxiad and Arsacid dynasties. The region's position between the empires of Rome and Persia, and later Byzantium, the caliphate, the Ottomans, Safavid Persia and Russia, made it a perennial battleground. The Armenian population of the western highlands was destroyed in the Armenian genocide of 1915. Today the eastern part of the highlands forms the Republic of Armenia and parts of neighbouring states.
