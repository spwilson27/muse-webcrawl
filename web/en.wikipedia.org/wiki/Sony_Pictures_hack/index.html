Sony Pictures hack
Date revealed24 November 2014
TargetSony Pictures Entertainment
Perpetrator"Guardians of Peace"; attributed by the FBI to North Korea
TriggerThe comedy film The Interview

On November 24, 2014, a hacker group identifying itself as the Guardians of Peace (GOP) leaked confidential data from the film studio Sony Pictures Entertainment, including employee personal information, internal emails, salary data, and copies of then-unreleased films. The attack used destructive malware to wipe Sony's computer systems. The attackers demanded that Sony cancel the planned release of The Interview, a comedy about a plot to assassinate North Korean leader Kim Jong-un.

Course of events

Over the following weeks the leaks embarrassed studio executives — co-chair Amy Pascal resigned in February 2015 after racist remarks in her emails were published — and exposed the Social Security numbers of tens of thousands of employees. After the hackers threatened violence against cinemas showing the film, major chains cancelled screenings and Sony briefly shelved the release, a decision criticised by President Barack Obama; Sony ultimately released The Interview online and in limited theatres on 25 December 2014.

Attribution and response

On 19 December 2014 the FBI formally attributed the attack to North Korea, citing similarities to previous North Korean malware; some security researchers initially expressed scepticism, but subsequent reporting and the 2018 US indictment of North Korean programmer Park Jin-hyok — also linked to the WannaCry ransomware and the Bangladesh Bank heist — strengthened the attribution. The United States imposed additional sanctions on North Korea in January 2015. The incident became a landmark case in debates over cyberwarfare, corporate security, and free expression.
