Federal Election Commission

The Federal Election Commission (FEC) is an independent regulatory agency of the United States charged with administering and enforcing the Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA), which governs the financing of federal elections.

FormedOctober 15, 1974
HeadquartersWashington, D.C.
Commissioners6
AuthorityFederal Election Campaign Act

History

Congress created the FEC in the 1974 amendments to FECA following the Watergate scandal. In Buckley v. Valeo (1976) the Supreme Court struck down its original appointment structure and some spending limits, reshaping campaign finance law.

Duties

The Commission discloses campaign finance information, enforces contribution limits and prohibitions, oversees political action committees (PACs) and party committees, and administers public financing of presidential elections — a system major candidates have declined since 2008.

Structure and gridlock

Six commissioners, no more than three from one party, must reach four votes for most enforcement actions. Critics charge that the 3–3 structure produces deadlock; periods without a quorum, notably in 2019–2020, halted enforcement entirely.
