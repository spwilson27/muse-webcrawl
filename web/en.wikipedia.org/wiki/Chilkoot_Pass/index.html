Chilkoot Pass

The Chilkoot Pass is a high mountain pass through the Boundary Ranges of the Coast Mountains on the border between the U.S. state of Alaska and the Canadian province of British Columbia. At about 1,067 metres (3,501 ft), it was the principal overland route used by prospectors during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98 to reach the goldfields near Dawson City, Yukon.

History

The pass had long been a trade route of the Tlingit people, who controlled passage between the coast and the interior and carried goods such as eulachon oil inland. During the gold rush, stampeders landed at Dyea, Alaska, and hauled their outfits up the Chilkoot Trail. Canadian authorities required each prospector to bring a year's supply of food — roughly a ton of gear — which had to be relayed over the pass in loads of about 50 pounds each.

The Golden Stairs

The final ascent, a slope of about 35 degrees, was climbed via some 1,500 steps cut into the ice and snow, nicknamed the "Golden Stairs". A continuous line of climbers ascended the steps, unable to stop without blocking those behind. On 3 April 1898, a series of avalanches on the pass killed more than 60 people, the deadliest single incident of the gold rush.

Decline and legacy

Aerial tramways were built over the pass in 1897–98, but the completion of the White Pass and Yukon Route railway in 1900 made the Chilkoot route obsolete almost overnight. Today the Chilkoot Trail is a popular multi-day hiking route managed as part of the Klondike Gold Rush International Historical Park, and the pass area is a National Historic Site of Canada.
