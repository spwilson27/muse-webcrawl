Gruyères

Country: Switzerland

Canton: Fribourg

District: Gruyère

Landmark: Château de Gruyères (castle)

Gruyères (French: Gruyères; German: Greyerz) is a town in the district of Gruyère in the canton of Fribourg in Switzerland. Its well-preserved medieval old town, closed to cars, sits on a hill topped by the Château de Gruyères, the former seat of the counts of Gruyère.

Sights

The castle, whose oldest parts date to the 13th century, houses a museum of the region's history and art. The town is also home to the H. R. Giger Museum, devoted to the Swiss artist who designed the creature in the film Alien, and to a museum of Tibetan art. Nearby, the demonstration cheese dairy La Maison du Gruyère in Pringy shows how Gruyère cheese is made.

History

Nineteen counts of Gruyère ruled from the castle between the 11th and 16th centuries. The last count, Michael, fled his creditors in 1554 and the lands passed to Fribourg and Bern.
