Battle of Scheveningen

The Battle of Scheveningen was fought on 31 July 1653 (New Style; 21 July Old Style) off the Dutch coast near Scheveningen and Texel, and was the final naval battle of the First Anglo-Dutch War. The English fleet under George Monck defeated the Dutch fleet under Maarten Tromp, who was killed early in the action. In Dutch it is known as the Slag bij Ter Heijde, after the village near which Tromp's flagship fought.

The battle

Tromp sailed to relieve the English blockade of the Dutch coast and free the fleet trapped in the Texel. In hard fighting over a full day, Tromp was killed by a musket shot aboard his flagship Brederode; his flag was kept flying to hold the fleet's morale. The Dutch were driven back into the Texel, having inflicted serious damage but failing to break the blockade decisively.

Aftermath

The battle ended major fleet operations in the war. With both countries exhausted and Oliver Cromwell seeking terms, negotiations produced the Treaty of Westminster in 1654, confirming English gains under the Navigation Acts.
