High school

A high school is a school that provides secondary education, situated after primary school and before or as part of the final stage of compulsory education in many countries. The term is most common in the United States and Canada, where high schools typically serve students from about ages 14 to 18, in grades 9 through 12.

North America

In the United States, public high schools are operated by local school districts and funded largely through property taxes. Students follow a curriculum of required and elective courses, earning credits toward a high school diploma. The standard course of study includes English, mathematics, science, and social studies, supplemented by foreign languages, arts, and vocational subjects. Standardised examinations such as the SAT and ACT are used in college admissions. Extracurricular activities — sports, music, and clubs — are a prominent part of American high school culture.

Equivalent terms

Countries use differing structures and names for the same stage: "secondary school" in the United Kingdom (11–16 or 18), Gymnasium in Germany, lycée in France, and "senior high school" in Japan (ages 15–18). In some systems the term "high school" is used for only the upper part of secondary schooling, following a junior high or middle school.

History

The first public high school in the United States, the English Classical School (later English High School) in Boston, opened in 1821. The high school movement of the early 20th century transformed American education, raising attendance from a small minority of teenagers to a large majority by 1940.
