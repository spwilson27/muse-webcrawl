Overview

The mountains classification in the Tour de France determines the race's best climber. Points are awarded to the first riders over categorised summits, with the leader wearing the polka dot jersey, one of the most recognisable garments in cycling. The rider who finishes the Tour with the most points is crowned King of the Mountains.

Points system

Climbs are ranked Category 4, 3, 2, 1 and hors catégorie (HC). Points scale steeply with difficulty: an HC summit awards 20 points to the first rider (then 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 down to eighth place), down to a single point for the first rider over a Category 4 hill. Points are doubled at summit finishes on the hardest climbs in recent editions, a rule intended to reward the leading overall contenders rather than breakaway specialists; the exact scale has been revised several times.

History

The Tour began naming a meilleur grimpeur (best climber) in 1933, when Vicente Trueba earned the title. A formal points system dates from 1947. The polka dot jersey itself was introduced in 1975, sponsored by the chocolate maker Poulain, whose wrappers were white with red spots; Lucien Van Impe was its first wearer and won the competition six times. Gino Bartali, Fausto Coppi and Federico Bahamontes (six wins, including seven summit-firsts in 1959) are among the celebrated early winners. Richard Virenque won seven titles between 1994 and 2004, the outright record. In recent years overall winners such as Tadej Pogačar have also taken the polka dot jersey, as climbing strength has increasingly overlapped with general classification strength.

Notable records

Bahamontes and Van Impe each won six times. Luis Herrera in 1985 became the first Colombian winner, heralding the era of the escarabajos (beetles), the Colombian climbing specialists. The classification has sometimes been a consolation for riders beaten in the general classification and sometimes the main ambition of climbers attacking day after day in the high mountains.
