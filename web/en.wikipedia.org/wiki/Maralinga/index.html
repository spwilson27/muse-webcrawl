Maralinga

LocationGreat Victoria Desert, South Australia
Known forBritish nuclear weapons tests, 1956–1963
Traditional ownersMaralinga Tjarutja (Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara peoples)

Maralinga, in the remote western areas of South Australia, was the site, measuring about 3,300 square kilometres, of British nuclear weapons tests during the 1950s and early 1960s. The site was developed with the agreement of the Australian government of Robert Menzies, and the tests were conducted in secrecy under the condition that the land would eventually be returned in a safe condition.

The tests

Seven major atomic explosions, codenamed Operation Buffalo (1956) and Operation Antler (1957), were detonated at Maralinga, with yields ranging from about one kiloton to about 27 kilotons. In addition, hundreds of "minor trials" — experiments involving the dispersal of radioactive materials including plutonium, uranium and beryllium by explosive, fire and impact — were carried out at Emu Field and Maralinga until 1963. These trials, particularly the Vixen B series at Taranaki, scattered plutonium over a wide area and caused long-term contamination.

Impact and clean-up

The tests displaced the Maralinga Tjarutja people from their traditional lands, and Aboriginal people, Australian and British service personnel were exposed to radiation, effects investigated by the McClelland Royal Commission (1984–85), which was highly critical of the conduct of the trials. A major remediation program in the 1990s, costing over A$100 million, buried contaminated debris and soil in trenches. In 2009 the last section of the site was formally handed back to the Maralinga Tjarutja, though some areas remain restricted due to residual plutonium contamination.
