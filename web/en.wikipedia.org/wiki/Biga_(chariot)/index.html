Biga (chariot)

A biga (Latin, plural bigae) is a two-horse chariot used in ancient Rome for racing, warfare, and ceremonial purposes. It is distinguished from the quadriga, drawn by four horses, and the triga, drawn by three. The word derives from bi-iuga, "two-yoked."

[Image: A Roman relief showing a biga, a light chariot drawn by two horses]

Use

In the Roman circus, biga races featured in the games (ludi), though the four-horse quadriga was the prestige event. Two-horse chariots were also used in triumphal and religious processions, and the type had earlier been used in warfare by the Etruscans and other Italic peoples; by the historical period the Romans employed chariots in battle only rarely. Roman coinage frequently depicted a biga, sometimes carrying deities such as Luna or Victoria, and the imagery passed into triumphal art generally.

Construction

The biga was a light, open-backed car of wood, bronze, and leather, with two wheels, standing room for a single driver (the auriga in racing), and a central pole to which the yoke for the two horses was attached. Reins passed from the driver to both horses; in racing, the driver wound the reins around his body for control and carried a knife to cut himself free in a crash.
