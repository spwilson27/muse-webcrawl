Hats and Caps

Hats and Caps were the names of two political factions — later effectively parties — in Sweden during the Age of Liberty (1719–1772). The Caps (Swedish: mössorna) favoured cautious, peace-oriented policies and close relations with Russia and Britain, while the Hats (hattarna) pursued a more assertive foreign policy, revival of great-power status, and friendship with France.

Party conflict in the Riksdag dominated Sweden's Age of Liberty.

Origins

The derisive nicknames arose in the 1730s: the peace party of Arvid Horn's supporters were mocked as "nightcaps" for their supposedly sleepy diplomacy, and their opponents adopted the martial "hat" as a badge. At the Riksdag of 1738–39 the Hats, led by figures such as Carl Gustaf Tessin and subsidised by French money, drove Horn from power and took control of the council.

Alternation in power

Hat governments launched the disastrous war against Russia in 1741–43 and the Pomeranian War against Prussia (1757–1762), both costly failures. The Caps gained power in 1765, instituted economic reforms and passed the landmark Freedom of the Press Act of 1766, one of the world's earliest. Party strife, foreign bribery on both sides, and constitutional deadlock between the Riksdag and the crown ended abruptly when Gustav III seized power in the coup of 1772, closing the Age of Liberty.
