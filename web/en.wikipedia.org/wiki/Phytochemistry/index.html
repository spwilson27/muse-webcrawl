Phytochemistry

Phytochemistry is the study of phytochemicals — chemicals derived from plants. Phytochemists study the structures, biosynthesis, distribution and biological functions of plant compounds, which include the primary metabolites needed for growth and the vast array of secondary metabolites used in defence, pollination and signalling.

Extracting and analysing plant constituents in the laboratory.

Major classes of compounds

  • Alkaloids — nitrogen-containing compounds such as caffeine, nicotine and morphine.
  • Terpenoids — built from isoprene units; includes essential oils, carotenoids and steroids.
  • Phenolics — including flavonoids, tannins and lignin.
  • Glycosides — compounds with a sugar bound to another functional group, such as cardiac glycosides and glucosinolates.

Methods

Plant material is extracted with solvents and fractionated, guided by bioassays. Compounds are separated by chromatography (TLC, HPLC, GC) and identified by spectroscopy — mass spectrometry, NMR, UV and infrared. Modern work combines metabolomics with genomics to map biosynthetic pathways.

Importance

Many medicines originate in plant chemistry, including aspirin (from willow salicylates), morphine, digoxin, quinine, paclitaxel and artemisinin. Phytochemistry also underpins agriculture, food science, natural dyes, pesticides and the study of plant ecology and evolution.
