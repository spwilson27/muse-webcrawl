Jilin

Jilin (Chinese: 吉林; pinyin: Jílín) is a province of China in the northeast of the country, bordering North Korea and Russia. Its capital is Changchun, and its largest cities include Jilin City and Siping. The province is known for its long, cold winters, its rime ice on the Songhua River, and its automobile and agricultural industries.

Geography

Jilin lies in the central part of Northeast China, between Heilongjiang to the north, Liaoning to the south and Inner Mongolia to the west. The Changbai Mountains in the southeast contain the volcanic Heaven Lake (Paektu) on the Korean border, and the province's rivers include the Songhua, which flows through Jilin City and Harbin.

History

The region was inhabited by Tungusic peoples and was part of the Manchu homeland. In the Qing dynasty it was administered as part of Jilin General's territory; the province of Jilin was formally established in 1907. During the 1930s the area was part of the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo, with Changchun (then Hsinking) as its capital. After 1949 it became a major centre of heavy industry under the People's Republic.

Economy and culture

Jilin's economy is based on automobile manufacturing (the FAW Group in Changchun), petrochemicals, agriculture — notably corn and rice — and forestry. The province is home to the Korean ethnic minority, and Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture in the east is a centre of Korean culture in China. Winter tourism, centred on Changbai Mountain skiing and the Songhua rime, is a growing industry.
