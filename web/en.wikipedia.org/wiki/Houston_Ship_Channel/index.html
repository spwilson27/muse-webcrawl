Houston Ship Channel

Houston Ship Channel
Location: Harris County, Texas
Length: about 50 miles (80 km)
Opened: 1914 (deep-water channel)
Connects: Port of Houston to Galveston Bay / Gulf of Mexico

The Houston Ship Channel is a 50-mile (80 km) waterway in Harris County, Texas, linking the Port of Houston to Galveston Bay and the Gulf of Mexico. Dredged through Buffalo Bayou and the bay, it transformed landlocked Houston into one of the busiest ports in the United States and anchored one of the world's largest petrochemical complexes along its banks.

History

Navigation interests sought a deep-water route to Houston from the nineteenth century; after the 1900 Galveston hurricane devastated the island port, federal and local investment accelerated. The channel officially opened in 1914 when President Woodrow Wilson fired a cannon by remote button from Washington. Successive widening and deepening projects — most recently "Project 11", deepening the channel to about 46.5 feet and widening it — have kept pace with growing ships.

Industry

Refineries and chemical plants of companies such as ExxonMobil (Baytown), LyondellBasell, and Shell line the channel, making the Houston area the centre of the U.S. petrochemical industry. The channel's terminals handle foreign tonnage ranking the Port of Houston first in the United States by that measure in recent years, and the waterway supports hundreds of thousands of jobs in the region.
