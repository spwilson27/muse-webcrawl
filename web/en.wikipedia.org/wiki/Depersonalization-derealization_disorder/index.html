Depersonalization-derealization disorder (DPDR) is a mental disorder in which the person has persistent or recurrent feelings of depersonalization or derealization. Depersonalization is described as feeling disconnected or detached from one's self — for example, feeling like an outside observer of one's own thoughts, body or actions. Derealization is a perception that the external world is strange, dreamlike, foggy or unreal. Crucially, reality testing remains intact: the person knows the experiences are not real.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Diagnosis under DSM-5 requires persistent or recurrent episodes of depersonalization, derealization, or both, causing significant distress or functional impairment, and not better explained by another condition or substance. Transient episodes of depersonalization are common in the general population, especially under stress, fatigue or danger, but the disorder is diagnosed only when symptoms are persistent. Prevalence is estimated at around 1–2% of the population.

Causes and treatment

Onset is often associated with severe stress, trauma, anxiety, depression, or cannabis and hallucinogen use. No medication is specifically approved for DPDR; treatment focuses on psychotherapy (including cognitive behavioural approaches tailored to the condition), treatment of comorbid anxiety or depression, and grounding techniques that counter the sense of detachment. SSRIs and lamotrigine have been studied with mixed results.
