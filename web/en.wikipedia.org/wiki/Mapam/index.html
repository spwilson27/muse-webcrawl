Mapam (Hebrew: מפ"ם, an acronym for Mifleget HaPoalim HaMeuhedet, מִפְלֶגֶת הַפּוֹעֲלִים הַמְּאוּחֶדֶת, lit. "United Workers' Party") was a Marxist-Zionist political party in Israel and one of the forerunners of the modern Meretz party. Founded in January 1948 by the merger of the Hashomer Hatzair Workers' Party and the left of Ahdut HaAvoda–Poale Zion, it was the second-largest party in the first Knesset and the principal party of the kibbutz movement's left wing.

History

In Israel's first elections in 1949 Mapam won 19 seats, becoming the main opposition to David Ben-Gurion's Mapai. It combined commitment to a socialist, kibbutz-based economy with a pro-Soviet foreign policy orientation and — unusual among Zionist parties — significant Arab membership and advocacy of Arab–Jewish equality. Moshe Sneh's pro-Communist faction split away in the early 1950s amid the Slánský trial and Doctors' Plot crises in the Eastern bloc, which shattered Mapam's faith in Stalinism; Ahdut HaAvoda also seceded in 1954, sharply reducing the party.

From 1955 Mapam joined governing coalitions with Mapai, and in 1969 it entered the Alignment (Ma'arach) with the Labour Party, remaining in that alliance until 1991. During this long partnership it moderated its positions while holding portfolios including Health and Housing. Disillusioned with Labour after the 1991 Madrid Conference era, Mapam left the Alignment and in 1992 formed the Meretz alliance with Ratz and Shinui, winning 12 seats; the three parties formally merged into Meretz in 1997, ending Mapam's separate existence after nearly fifty years.

Ideology

Mapam espoused "constructive socialism": the kibbutz and moshav as the vanguard of a socialist society, public ownership, and close ties to the Histadrut trade-union federation. Its youth movement, Hashomer Hatzair, and its kibbutz federation, Kibbutz HaArtzi, formed a semi-autonomous social world — the "Mapam republic" — with its own newspaper, Al HaMishmar (1943–1995), publishing house and settlement movement.

Electoral history

Mapam's best results came early: 19 seats in 1949 and 15 in 1951. Thereafter its standalone vote declined (9 seats in 1955, 7 in 1965), and from 1969 it contested elections as part of the Alignment before the Meretz merger. Prominent figures included Meir Ya'ari, Ya'akov Hazan, Mordechai Bentov, Yair Tzaban and Haim Oron.
