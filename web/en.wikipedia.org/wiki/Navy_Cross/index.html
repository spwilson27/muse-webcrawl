Navy Cross

Type: Military decoration
Awarded for: Extraordinary heroism in combat
Presented by: United States Department of the Navy
Established: 4 February 1919
Precedence: Second only to the Medal of Honor for the naval services

The Navy Cross is the United States naval services' second-highest military decoration awarded for valour in combat, ranking below only the Medal of Honor. It is awarded to members of the United States Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard (when operating under the Navy) for extraordinary heroism in action against an enemy of the United States.

History

The decoration was established by an act of Congress on 4 February 1919, initially as the Navy's third-highest award, below the Medal of Honor and the Navy Distinguished Service Medal. It could be awarded for heroism or distinguished service, and many early awards recognised First World War service. In August 1942 Congress elevated the Navy Cross above the Distinguished Service Medal and restricted it to combat heroism, giving it its present standing.

Description

The medal is a bronze cross pattée with laurel leaves in the re-entrant angles; its centre medallion depicts a sailing ship under way, and the reverse bears crossed anchors with the initials "USN". It hangs from a ribbon of navy blue with a narrow white centre stripe. Additional awards are denoted by gold or silver stars worn on the ribbon.

Notable recipients

Recipients include Marine Corps legends such as Lewis B. "Chesty" Puller, who received five Navy Crosses — the most of any Marine — and Sergeant Major Dan Daly; Navy aviator and later senator John McCain received one for his conduct as a prisoner of war in Vietnam. More than 6,300 Navy Crosses have been awarded in total, the majority for the Second World War.
