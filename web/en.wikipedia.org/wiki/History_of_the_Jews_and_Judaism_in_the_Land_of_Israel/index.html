History of the Jews and Judaism in the Land of Israel

The history of the Jews and Judaism in the Land of Israel begins with the ancient Israelites and spans roughly 3,000 years, from the kingdoms of ancient Israel and Judah through Roman rule, a long period of diaspora during which a Jewish presence nonetheless continued, and the modern return that culminated in the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948.

Ancient period

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Israelites settled Canaan after the Exodus and formed the united monarchy of Israel under Saul, David and Solomon, which later divided into the kingdoms of Israel and Judah. Archaeology broadly confirms an Israelite presence from the Iron Age; the Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BC) is the earliest extrabiblical reference to Israel. The First Temple in Jerusalem was destroyed by Babylon in 586 BC and the Second Temple, built after the return from exile, was destroyed by Rome in 70 AD following the Great Revolt. The Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135 AD) ended large-scale Jewish political presence, though Jewish communities persisted, especially in the Galilee, where the Mishnah and the Jerusalem Talmud were compiled.

Medieval and Ottoman periods

Under Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule, Jewish communities continued in Jerusalem, Safed, Tiberias and Hebron — the "Old Yishuv". Safed became a centre of Kabbalah in the 16th century under figures such as Isaac Luria and Joseph Karo. Immigration (aliyah) of religious Jews continued in modest numbers for centuries.

Zionism and the Mandate

From 1882 the Zionist movement organised waves of immigration that built new towns such as Tel Aviv (1909) and agricultural settlements. The Balfour Declaration (1917) and the British Mandate for Palestine (1922) recognised a Jewish national home. Mass immigration in the 1930s, driven by Nazi persecution, and the Holocaust transformed the demographic and moral landscape.

State of Israel

Following the UN Partition Plan of 1947, David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the State of Israel on 14 May 1948. The ensuing war with neighbouring Arab states established Israel within expanded armistice borders and produced a large Palestinian refugee population. Israel subsequently absorbed Jewish immigrants from Europe, the Middle East, North Africa, the Soviet Union and Ethiopia, becoming the world's only Jewish-majority state, with roughly half of world Jewry now living in the Land of Israel.
