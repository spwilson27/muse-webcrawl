Term (logic)

In mathematical logic, a term is a well-formed expression of a formal language that denotes an object of the domain of discourse. Terms are built recursively: every variable and every constant symbol is a term, and if f is an n-ary function symbol and t₁…tₙ are terms, then f(t₁,…,tₙ) is a term.

Ground and open terms

A term containing no variables is a ground term (closed term); a ground term of a theory denotes a definite element under any interpretation that fixes the symbols. Terms containing variables are open terms. A term with no function symbols is simply a variable or constant.

Role in first-order logic

Terms are the noun-phrases of first-order languages: atomic formulas are formed by applying predicate symbols to terms (P(t₁,…,tₙ)) or by equations between terms. Substitution of terms for variables, and the notion of a term being free for a variable, are defined by recursion on term structure. In term rewriting and computer science, terms model syntax trees, and unification of terms underlies logic programming such as Prolog.
