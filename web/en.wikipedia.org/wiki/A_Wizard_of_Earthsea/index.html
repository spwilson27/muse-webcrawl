A Wizard of Earthsea is a fantasy novel by American author Ursula K. Le Guin, first published in 1968 by Parnassus Press. It is the first book in the Earthsea cycle and is regarded as a classic of children's and fantasy literature.

Plot

The novel follows Ged, a proud and gifted boy from the island of Gont — known by his childhood nickname Sparrowhawk — who is sent to the school of wizardry on Roke Island. In a duel of pride with a rival student, Ged attempts a powerful spell and accidentally releases a shadow creature into the world. The shadow hunts him across the archipelago of Earthsea; after first fleeing it, Ged turns and pursues it, finally confronting and naming it with his own name, recognising it as the dark side of himself, and becoming whole.

Themes

The book is a coming-of-age story shaped by Taoist ideas of balance and by the power of names: in Earthsea, knowing a thing's true name in the Old Speech gives power over it. Le Guin also subverted the racial conventions of fantasy of the time, making most of her characters people of colour in a sea-world of islands.

Reception

The novel won the Boston Globe–Horn Book Award in 1969 and was named one of the most important children's books of the twentieth century. It was followed by five more Earthsea books, beginning with The Tombs of Atuan (1971). Adaptations include a 2004 television miniseries and the 2006 Studio Ghibli film Tales from Earthsea, both received coolly by Le Guin.
