Göreme National Park

Göreme National Park (Turkish: Göreme Millî Parkı) is a national park in central Turkey, in the Nevşehir Province within the historical region of Cappadocia. It was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985 together with the Rock Sites of Cappadocia.

The park is famous for its 'fairy chimney' rock formations—tall, thin spires of soft volcanic rock—and for rock-hewn churches, monasteries and dwellings carved into the tuff by early Christians, many decorated with Byzantine frescoes.

Geology

The landscape was formed by erosion of ash deposits from ancient eruptions of Mount Erciyes and Mount Hasan. The soft rock was easily carved but also erodes readily, so preservation is an ongoing concern.

The Göreme Open Air Museum, within the park, contains some of the finest rock-cut churches, including the Dark Church (Karanlık Kilise).
