Sailing at the Summer Olympics

Sailing (formerly called yachting) has been contested at the Summer Olympic Games since the second modern Games in Paris in 1900. It was not held in 1904, but has been part of every Summer Olympics since 1908. The sport is governed internationally by World Sailing (formerly the International Sailing Federation, ISAF).

History

The first Olympic regatta, in 1900, used tonnage-based handicap classes for large yachts. Over the following decades the programme shifted toward one-design classes in which all competitors use identical boats. Classes have changed many times: famous long-serving classes include the Star (1932–2012), the Finn (1952–2020) and the Laser/ILCA dinghies (introduced 1996). Women's events were added beginning in 1988, and the sport was once notable for allowing men and women to compete together in open classes.

Format

Modern Olympic sailing events are fleet races held over a series of races on courses near the host venue. Competitors accumulate points (low score wins), discarding their worst result, and the top boats advance to a double-points medal race. Equipment in recent Games has included the ILCA 6 and 7, 470, 49er and 49erFX skiffs, Nacra 17 catamaran, iQFOiL windsurfing, and Formula Kite kiteboarding, the last added for Paris 2024.

Medallists and nations

Great Britain is the most successful sailing nation in Olympic history, and British sailor Ben Ainslie, with four gold medals and one silver between 1996 and 2012, is the most successful Olympic sailor. Other strong sailing nations include the United States, France, Australia, Denmark, Spain and the Netherlands.
