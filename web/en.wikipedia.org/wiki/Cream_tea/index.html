Cream tea
Alternative namesDevonshire tea, Cornish cream tea
TypeLight meal
Place of originEngland (Devon and Cornwall)
Main ingredientsTea, scones, clotted cream, strawberry jam

A cream tea (also known as a Devon cream tea, Devonshire tea, or Cornish cream tea) is a form of afternoon tea: a light meal consisting of tea taken with a combination of scones, clotted cream, and jam. Cream teas are sold in tea rooms throughout England, especially in Devon and Cornwall, and in other parts of the Commonwealth.

History

The tradition is claimed to have originated at Tavistock in Devon in the 11th century, where monks at Tavistock Abbey are said to have served bread with clotted cream and strawberry preserves to workers restoring the abbey. The modern cream tea, with scones replacing bread, developed in the Victorian era as afternoon tea became fashionable.

The jam–cream debate

A long-running and good-humoured dispute concerns the order in which the toppings are applied, dividing the two counties: in the Devon method, the clotted cream is spread on the scone first, then topped with jam; in the Cornish method, the jam goes on first and is topped with cream. Advocates of each claim theirs is the only correct way; the Cornish method was granted protected status discussion in 2010 when a campaign sought PDO protection for the Cornish cream tea. The related question of whether the scone should be split or a traditional split (a soft white roll, historically used in Cornwall) used is equally contested.

Variations

A savoury counterpart, with cheese scones and chutney, is sometimes offered. In Australia and New Zealand the meal is often called a "Devonshire tea". A related but grander meal is the full afternoon tea, which adds sandwiches and cakes.
