Battle of Pylos

The Battle of Pylos was fought in 425 BC during the Peloponnesian War between Athens and Sparta. An Athenian force under Demosthenes fortified the promontory of Pylos in Messenia, in Sparta's home territory; Spartan attacks failed, and when the fighting spilled onto the island of Sphacteria, an Athenian victory there captured 292 Spartan hoplites, including about 120 full Spartiates — an unprecedented blow to Sparta's prestige.

Background

In 425 BC an Athenian fleet weather-bound at Pylos fortified the headland, intending to raid Spartan territory and encourage desertion among the Messenian helots. Sparta recalled its army from Attica and assaulted the position by land and sea; in the naval fighting in the bay, the Spartan commander Brasidas was wounded and the attack repulsed.

Sphacteria

Spartan hoplites stranded on Sphacteria were blockaded for weeks. In Athens, the politician Cleon, goaded by the Assembly, promised to finish the business in twenty days and, with Demosthenes, landed light troops and Messenian auxiliaries who overwhelmed the hoplites with missile fire until they surrendered — a shocking event, since Greeks expected Spartans to die rather than yield.

Aftermath

The prisoners became Athens's shield against the annual Spartan invasions of Attica. Pylos remained an Athenian outpost raiding Laconia until 409 BC. The affair deepened the war's unpopularity in Sparta and led toward the Peace of Nicias (421 BC).
