An e-reader, also called an e-book reader, is a mobile electronic device designed primarily for reading digital e-books and periodicals. Most e-readers use an electronic paper (e-ink) display that mimics the appearance of ink on paper, is readable in bright sunlight, consumes very little power, and allows battery life measured in weeks rather than hours.

History

Dedicated reading devices appeared in the late 1990s, including the Rocket eBook (1998) and SoftBook. Sony's Librie (2004, Japan) and Reader (2006) pioneered E Ink screens, but the category took off with the Amazon Kindle in November 2007, which sold out within hours and coupled the device to a vast online store with wireless delivery (Whispernet). Competing ecosystems followed: the Barnes & Noble Nook (2009) and Kobo (2010).

Technology and formats

E Ink displays form images with charged pigment particles and draw power only when the page changes. E-readers typically support the EPUB format (except Kindle devices, which historically favoured Amazon's proprietary formats, adding EPUB support later), PDF, and plain text, and offer adjustable fonts, built-in dictionaries, front lighting, and storage for thousands of books. Many include Wi-Fi or cellular connectivity for direct purchase and library lending.

Impact

E-readers drove the commercial growth of e-books in the 2010s and influenced publishing, self-publishing and library lending models, though print sales proved resilient and many readers use phones and tablets for e-books as well.
