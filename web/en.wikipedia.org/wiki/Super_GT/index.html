Super GT

Super GT is a grand touring car racing series that began in 1993 in Japan. Known originally as the All Japan Grand Touring Car Championship (JGTC), it adopted its present name in 2005 and is the top level of sports car racing in Japan, contested primarily at circuits such as Fuji Speedway, Suzuka, Motegi and Okayama.

Classes

Super GT features two classes racing simultaneously. GT500, the premier class, is contested by factory-backed silhouette prototypes built to regulations shared loosely with the DTM's Class One concepts, currently fielded by Toyota (GR Supra), Honda (Civic Type R-GT) and Nissan (Z Nismo), with engines restricted to turbocharged 2.0-litre four-cylinder units producing over 600 horsepower. GT300 is a mixed class of FIA GT3 cars and Japanese-made "mother chassis" and JAF-GT cars, attracting large, diverse grids.

Format and handicaps

Races are endurance events of 250–500 km, usually with two drivers per car sharing the driving. A signature feature is the success-weight handicap system, in which championship points translate into physical ballast and, for GT500, fuel-flow restrictions, keeping competition close throughout the season.

History

The series grew out of the JGTC, founded in 1993, and survived Japan's economic downturns to become one of the best-attended racing series in Asia. International rounds have been held in Malaysia and Thailand. Its distinctive combination of factory rivalry, permissive aerodynamics and evening "twilight" races has made it a cult favourite among global motorsport fans.
