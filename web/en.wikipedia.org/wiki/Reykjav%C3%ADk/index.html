Reykjavík is the capital and largest city of Iceland. Located on the country's south-western coast, it is the world's northernmost capital of a sovereign state. The wider Capital Region contains roughly two-thirds of Iceland's population.

History

According to tradition, the Norse settler Ingólfr Arnarson founded his farm at Reykjavík ("smoky bay", from the steam of its hot springs) around 874 AD, making it the first permanent settlement in Iceland. It remained a small farming and trading site until the 18th century, when it grew as a centre of the wool industry, and became the seat of government as Iceland moved toward independence, achieved in 1944.

Cityscape and culture

Landmarks include the Lutheran church Hallgrímskirkja, the Harpa concert hall on the harbour, and the Sun Voyager sculpture. The city is powered almost entirely by geothermal and hydroelectric energy, and is known for its music scene, museums, and nightlife.
