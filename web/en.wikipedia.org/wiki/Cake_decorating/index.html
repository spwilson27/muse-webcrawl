Cake Decorating

Cake decorating is the art of making a cake visually appealing and thematically appropriate for an occasion, using icings, edible decorations, and sculptural techniques. It is practised professionally by pastry chefs and specialist decorators, and widely as a hobby.

Materials and techniques

Common coverings include buttercream, royal icing (used for fine piping and the intricate stringwork of the Australian method), fondant (rolled sugar paste giving a smooth, porcelain finish), ganache, and marzipan. Decorators use piping bags with shaped nozzles for borders, shells, rosettes, and writing; palette knives and combs for texture; airbrushes for colour gradients; and modelling tools to sculpt flowers and figures from gum paste or modelling chocolate. Techniques such as Lambeth overpiping, brush embroidery, mirror glazing, and wafer-paper work define distinct styles.

History

Elaborate decorated cakes developed in 17th- and 18th-century Europe alongside refined sugar; Victorian Britain made the white-iced, multi-tiered wedding cake an institution, and Joseph Lambeth popularized ornate piped styles in the early 20th century. Modern cake decorating expanded with television competitions and social media, producing trends from minimalist Korean-style buttercream cakes to hyper-realistic sculpted "illusion" cakes.
