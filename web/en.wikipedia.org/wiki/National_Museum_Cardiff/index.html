National Museum Cardiff (Welsh: Amgueddfa Genedlaethol Caerdydd) is a museum and art gallery in Cardiff, Wales. It is part of Amgueddfa Cymru — Museum Wales, the Welsh national museum network, and admission is free.

Building and history

The National Museum of Wales received its royal charter in 1907, and the Cardiff museum's foundation stone was laid in 1912 in the civic centre of Cathays Park, though construction was interrupted by the First World War and the building opened in 1927. Further extensions, including science and education facilities, were added later in the twentieth century.

Collections

The museum combines natural history displays — including the Evolution of Wales gallery, dinosaurs and Welsh wildlife — with major art collections. Its art holdings are particularly strong in Impressionist and Post-Impressionist painting, including works by Monet, Renoir, Cézanne, Van Gogh and Rodin, many deriving from the bequest of the Davies sisters, Gwendoline and Margaret Davies of Gregynog, who assembled one of Britain's finest collections of French art.
