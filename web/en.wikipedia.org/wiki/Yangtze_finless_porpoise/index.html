Yangtze finless porpoise

Scientific nameNeophocaena asiaeorientalis asiaeorientalis
HabitatYangtze River and connected lakes, China
StatusCritically Endangered (IUCN)

The Yangtze finless porpoise is a freshwater subspecies of the narrow-ridged finless porpoise, endemic to the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River and its adjoining Poyang and Dongting lakes in China. It is one of the very few cetaceans adapted entirely to fresh water and, following the functional extinction of the baiji, the only cetacean remaining in the Yangtze.

Description

Like other finless porpoises, it lacks a true dorsal fin, having instead a low ridge along the back. Adults are pale grey, about 1.3–1.7 m long, with a rounded head and a slightly upturned mouth line that gives the animal a "smiling" appearance in popular media.

Conservation

Surveys estimated about 1,012 individuals in 2017, down from roughly 2,700 in the early 1990s, due to habitat degradation, vessel traffic, pollution, sand mining, and depletion of prey fish. Conservation measures include nature reserves, ex-situ populations in oxbow lakes such as Tian'ezhou, captive breeding, and the ten-year fishing ban imposed on the Yangtze from 2021. The 2022 survey reported a recovery to about 1,249 animals.
