Overview

The Grand Duchy of Lithuania was a European state that lasted from the 13th century until 1795. Formed by Baltic Lithuanians under Mindaugas (crowned king in 1253), it expanded under Gediminas and Algirdas deep into the Slavic lands of former Kievan Rus', so that most of its population and chancery language was Ruthenian (Old Belarusian), while the dynasty remained Lithuanian and, for a time, pagan — Lithuania was Europe's last pagan state, accepting Christianity in 1387.

Union with Poland

In 1385–86 Grand Duke Jogaila married Poland's Jadwiga and took the Polish crown (as Władysław II Jagiełło), beginning a dynastic union that ripened into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth (Union of Lublin, 1569). Under Vytautas (1392–1430) the Grand Duchy reached its zenith, crushing, with Poland, the Teutonic Order at Grunwald (1410). The Commonwealth was partitioned by Russia, Prussia and Austria, disappearing in 1795. The Grand Duchy is a shared inheritance claimed by modern Lithuania, Belarus and, in part, Ukraine and Poland.
