Broadway is a major street in the U.S. state of New York. It runs 33 miles (53 km) from Bowling Green at the southern tip of Manhattan, north through the borough, across the Harlem River into the Bronx, and on through Yonkers and other Westchester County communities to Sleepy Hollow, where it forms part of U.S. Route 9. It is the oldest north–south main thoroughfare in New York City, and because it largely predates — and cuts diagonally across — Manhattan's street grid, its intersections create many of the city's famous squares, including Union Square, Madison Square, Herald Square, Times Square and Columbus Circle.

History

Broadway follows the route of the Wickquasgeck Trail, a Native American path through the forests and swamps of Manhattan island. The Dutch of New Amsterdam widened it as the Heere Straat ("Gentlemen's Street" or "High Street"), running from the fort to the wall at present-day Wall Street; the British renamed it Broadway for its unusual width. In the 18th and 19th centuries the street moved steadily northward with the city: by the early 1800s it was the fashionable promenade and shopping street ("Ladies' Mile"), and theatres migrated up it from the Bowery and lower Broadway, eventually clustering near Longacre Square — renamed Times Square in 1904 — which gave "Broadway" its worldwide meaning as a synonym for American commercial theatre. The nickname "the Great White Way" dates from the street's pioneering electric signage of the 1890s–1900s.

Theater District and Canyon of Heroes

The Theater District, roughly from West 40th to West 54th Streets, contains most of the 41 large professional "Broadway theatres" whose productions (the category is defined by theatre size and contracts, not strictly by address) dominate American commercial theatre and the Tony Awards. The lower stretch of Broadway from Bowling Green to City Hall Park is known as the Canyon of Heroes, the traditional route of New York's ticker-tape parades honouring astronauts, heads of state, championship teams and returning soldiers; granite strips in the sidewalks commemorate each parade.

Features

Because Broadway predates the Commissioners' Plan of 1811, its diagonal course generates triangular plots and some of the city's landmark buildings, including the Flatiron Building at 23rd Street, the Woolworth Building near City Hall, and the Ansonia and other grand apartment hotels on the Upper West Side. North of Manhattan, Broadway passes Van Cortlandt Park in the Bronx and becomes the Albany Post Road corridor through Westchester.
