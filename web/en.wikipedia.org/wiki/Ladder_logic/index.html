Ladder logic

Ladder logic is a graphical programming language used to program programmable logic controllers (PLCs) in industrial automation. Originally developed to document relay logic circuits used for machine control, its diagrams resemble the rungs of a ladder, with two vertical power rails and horizontal rungs of logic.

Structure

Each rung expresses a rule: input conditions on the left, drawn as contacts (normally open or normally closed), determine whether an output on the right, drawn as a coil, is energized. Modern ladder languages add timers, counters, arithmetic, and function blocks, and form one of the standard languages defined by IEC 61131-3.

History

Ladder logic arose when PLCs replaced hard-wired relay panels in the late 1960s, beginning with General Motors' specifications that led to the Modicon 084. Representing control logic in the familiar relay-diagram form allowed electricians to adopt the new technology without learning conventional programming.
