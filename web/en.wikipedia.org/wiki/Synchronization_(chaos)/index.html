Synchronization of chaos

Synchronization of chaos is a phenomenon in which two or more chaotic systems adjust a given property of their motion to a common behavior, despite the sensitivity to initial conditions that characterizes chaos. Because chaotic systems are unpredictable in isolation, the fact that coupled chaotic systems can evolve in lockstep was surprising when demonstrated systematically around 1990.

Types

Complete synchronization occurs when two identical chaotic systems, suitably coupled, converge to exactly the same trajectory. Phase synchronization means only the phases of the oscillators lock while their amplitudes remain uncorrelated. In generalized synchronization, the state of one system becomes a function of the other, and in lag synchronization the systems follow the same trajectory with a constant time delay.

History

The idea traces to Christiaan Huygens' 1665 observation of pendulum clocks synchronizing on a shared wall. For chaotic systems, Fujisaka and Yamada analyzed synchronization in the early 1980s, and Pecora and Carroll's 1990 work on synchronizing chaotic circuits made the phenomenon widely known, spurring proposals for secure communications using chaotic carriers.

Applications

Synchronization of chaos has been studied in lasers, electronic circuits, chemical oscillators, and biological systems such as neurons and cardiac tissue, as well as in proposed schemes for chaos-based communication.
