The Hammersmith & City line is a line of the London Underground, running between Hammersmith in west London and Barking in east London. It is printed in pink on the Tube map and is sometimes informally known as the "pink line". The route is 25.5 kilometres (15.8 miles) long and serves 29 stations.

Although presented as a separate line, much of its route is shared with other lines: between Hammersmith and Aldgate it runs over tracks also used by the Circle line, and east of Aldgate it shares tracks with the District line as far as Barking. Most of the route is just below the surface in shallow cut-and-cover tunnels or on the surface.

History

The western part of the route dates from the opening of the Metropolitan Railway in 1863, the world's first underground railway, which ran between Paddington and Farringdon. Services to Hammersmith were added in 1864 over a branch built jointly with the Great Western Railway. The eastern section to Barking was completed in stages, with through services to Barking beginning in 1936 as part of the Metropolitan line.

For much of the twentieth century the route was operated as a branch of the Metropolitan line. It was given its own identity as the Hammersmith & City line in 1988, and was shown in pink on Tube maps from 1990, helping passengers distinguish the Hammersmith–Barking service from other Metropolitan line trains.

Services and stations

Trains run roughly every ten minutes between Hammersmith and Barking. Key interchange stations include Hammersmith, Paddington, Baker Street, King's Cross St Pancras, Liverpool Street, Aldgate East, Whitechapel, Mile End and Barking. The line provides an important cross-London link between the West End, the City and the East End, and serves Whitechapel station, an interchange with the Elizabeth line.

Rolling stock

The line is worked by S7 Stock trains, introduced from 2012 as part of the subsurface lines' upgrade. The seven-car trains feature air conditioning, walk-through carriages and level boarding at many stations.
