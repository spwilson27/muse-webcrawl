Joseph Liouville

Born24 March 1809, Saint-Omer, France
Died8 September 1882, Paris, France
FieldsMathematics

Joseph Liouville (24 March 1809 – 8 September 1882) was a French mathematician and physicist who worked in a wide variety of fields in both pure and applied mathematics, including complex analysis, differential geometry, number theory and statistical mechanics. He is remembered for Liouville's theorem in complex analysis, for constructing the first proven transcendental numbers, and for founding a major mathematical journal.

Life and career

Liouville graduated from the École Polytechnique in 1827 and, after appointments at several institutions, became professor at the Collège de France in 1851. In 1836 he founded the Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées — still known as the "Journal de Liouville" — which became one of France's leading mathematical periodicals; as its editor he published the pioneering work of Évariste Galois in 1846.

Contributions

In complex analysis, Liouville's theorem states that every bounded entire function must be constant; it gives a short proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra. In 1844 he showed that transcendental numbers exist by constructing the Liouville constant, a number whose rational approximations are "too good" for an algebraic number. With Jacques Charles François Sturm he developed Sturm–Liouville theory for second-order differential equations, fundamental to mathematical physics. His name is also attached to Liouville's theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics, Liouville's formula for Wronskians, the Liouville function in number theory, and, in physics, the Liouville equation governing phase-space density.

Liouville was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1839 and to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences; the lunar crater Liouville is named after him.
