Romance scam

A romance scam is a confidence trick in which a perpetrator creates a fake romantic relationship with a victim, usually online, in order to extract money or personal information. It is a form of advance-fee fraud and one of the most financially damaging types of online fraud.

Method

Scammers typically use stolen photographs to create attractive profiles on dating sites and social media, often posing as military personnel stationed overseas, aid workers, or professionals working abroad — personas that explain why they cannot meet in person. After weeks or months of affectionate messaging ("love bombing"), the scammer invents an emergency — medical bills, customs fees, business trouble, or the cost of travel to visit — and asks the victim for money, usually by wire transfer, gift cards or cryptocurrency, which makes recovery difficult.

Scale and impact

Law-enforcement agencies such as the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center report losses from romance and confidence fraud in the hundreds of millions of dollars annually in the United States alone, with actual losses believed to be far higher because many victims do not report the crime out of embarrassment. Beyond financial loss, victims often suffer significant emotional harm, and in some cases are used as money mules or drawn into further criminal activity.

Prevention

Advice to avoid romance scams includes reverse image searches of profile pictures, suspicion of anyone who avoids video calls or in-person meetings, and never sending money to someone met only online.
