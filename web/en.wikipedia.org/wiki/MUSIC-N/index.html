MUSIC-N

MUSIC-N refers to a family of computer music programs developed at Bell Telephone Laboratories by Max Mathews and his colleagues from the late 1950s onward. The first of these, MUSIC (later MUSIC I), was written in 1957 and is widely regarded as the first computer program for generating digital audio waveforms through direct synthesis.

Development

Successive versions — MUSIC II (1958), MUSIC III (1960), MUSIC IV, and MUSIC V (1968) — progressively refined the concept of the "unit generator", software modules such as oscillators and filters that could be connected in a text-based score to form virtual instruments. MUSIC V, written in Fortran, became portable across machines and spread the model to universities worldwide.

Legacy

The MUSIC-N paradigm directly influenced nearly all subsequent computer music languages, including Csound, the MUSIC 360/511 systems, cmusic, and the design of Max/MSP. Mathews is often called the "father of computer music" for this work.
