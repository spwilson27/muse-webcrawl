Trans-Mongolian Railway

The Trans-Mongolian Railway (Mongolian: Транс-Монголын төмөр зам) is the main railway line of Mongolia and its principal connection between Russia and China. It follows an ancient trade route between Siberia and China and runs from Ulan-Ude on the Trans-Siberian Railway in Russia, through Mongolia's capital Ulaanbaatar, to Jining in the Inner Mongolia region of China.

Route

The line enters Mongolia at Sükhbaatar, passes south through Darkhan and Ulaanbaatar, crosses the Gobi Desert through Sainshand, and reaches the Chinese border at Zamyn-Üüd, where it meets the Chinese network at Erenhot. The total length is approximately 1,110 km (690 mi) within Mongolia.

History

Railway development in Mongolia was delayed by the country's sparse population and landlocked position. The narrow-gauge line from Ulaanbaatar to the coal mines at Nalaikh opened in 1938. The main broad-gauge Trans-Mongolian line was built with Soviet assistance between 1947 and 1961: the section from the Soviet border to Ulaanbaatar opened in 1949–1950, and the line was completed to the Chinese border in 1955, with through traffic beginning in 1956.

Gauge and operation

Mongolia uses the Russian gauge of 1,520 mm. At the Chinese border (Erenhot/Zamyn-Üüd), bogies must be exchanged or cargo transshipped because China uses standard gauge (1,435 mm). The railway is operated by Ulaanbaatar Railway (UBTZ), a joint Mongolian–Russian company. It is mostly single-track and non-electrified, and carries the great majority of Mongolia's freight and international trade.

Passenger services

The international passenger train between Moscow and Beijing via Mongolia travels over the line, as do trains between Irkutsk and Ulaanbaatar and between Ulaanbaatar and Beijing. The route is popular with tourists travelling the Trans-Siberian corridor.
