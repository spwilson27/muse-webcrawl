Public utility

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A public utility company (usually just utility) is an organisation that maintains the infrastructure for a public service (often also a state-owned or municipal body providing that service). Public utilities are typically natural monopolies — electricity grids, water supply and sewerage, natural gas distribution, and formerly telephone networks — because duplicating their physical networks would be wasteful.

Sectors

Common utility sectors include electricity generation, transmission and distribution; piped water and wastewater; natural gas; district heating; and waste management. Telecommunications were historically treated as a regulated utility monopoly in many countries before deregulation. Utilities may be publicly owned (municipal or state enterprises such as the Tennessee Valley Authority), investor-owned companies, or customer-owned cooperatives (common for rural electricity in the United States).

Regulation

Because utilities are natural monopolies, they are usually subject to economic regulation by a public utilities commission, which approves rates ("tariffs") intended to allow recovery of prudent costs plus a fair rate of return on invested capital — the basis of the rate-of-return or "cost-plus" model established in U.S. case law such as FPC v. Hope Natural Gas Co. (1944). Alternatives include price-cap (RPI−X) regulation, pioneered in Britain, which caps prices to encourage efficiency. From the 1980s many countries restructured utilities: vertically unbundling competitive generation and retail from monopoly networks, privatising state enterprises, and creating wholesale electricity markets, with mixed results.

Duties

Utilities typically bear an obligation to serve all customers within their territory on non-discriminatory terms, to maintain reliability standards, and to plan for future demand — historically under an "integrated resource planning" framework. Contemporary issues include decarbonising electricity supply, accommodating distributed generation, and funding replacement of ageing water infrastructure.
