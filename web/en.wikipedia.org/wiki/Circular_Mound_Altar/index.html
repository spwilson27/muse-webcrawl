The Circular Mound Altar (Chinese: 圜丘壇; pinyin: Huánqiū Tán) is an outdoor empty circular platform on three levels of marble stones, located in the southern part of the Temple of Heaven complex in Dongcheng District, Beijing. It was the site at which the emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties performed the annual winter solstice rites of worship of Heaven, the most solemn ceremony of the imperial Chinese state cult.

History and design

The altar was built in 1530 during the reign of the Jiajing Emperor and was rebuilt and given its present form in 1749 under the Qianlong Emperor. It consists of three concentric circular terraces of white marble, reached by staircases and surrounded by carved balustrades, standing at the centre of a square walled enclosure — the circle symbolising Heaven and the square the Earth, in accordance with the traditional cosmology of "round Heaven and square Earth".

Symbolism of the number nine

The altar's dimensions and fittings are governed by the number nine and its multiples, nine being the supreme yang number associated with the emperor and Heaven. The top terrace is paved with fan-shaped stones arranged in nine concentric rings around the central Heaven's Heart Stone; the numbers of balusters and steps likewise follow multiples of nine. Standing on the central stone, a speaker's voice is said to be reinforced by echoes from the surrounding balustrade, an acoustic effect much remarked by visitors — the emperor's prayers were believed to be amplified toward Heaven.

Use

At the winter solstice the emperor, after fasting and ritual purification in the Palace of Abstinence, processed to the altar to report to Heaven on the year's governance and to offer sacrifices including a bullock, jade, and silk on the open-air platform, accompanied by prescribed music and dance. The ceremonies ended with the fall of the Qing dynasty; a single later performance was staged by Yuan Shikai in 1914 during his attempt to assume the imperial title. The altar is now open to the public as part of the Temple of Heaven park, inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site in 1998.
