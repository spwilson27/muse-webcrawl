Spiral galaxy

A spiral galaxy is a type of galaxy originally described by Edwin Hubble in his 1936 work The Realm of the Nebulae. Spiral galaxies consist of a flat, rotating disk containing stars, gas and dust, and a central concentration of stars known as the bulge, surrounded by a faint halo and often exhibiting spiral arms extending outward.

Spiral galaxy
Examples: Milky Way, Andromeda Galaxy, Whirlpool Galaxy
Hubble types: Sa, Sb, Sc, SBa, SBb, SBc

Structure

Components include the disk, spiral arms, galactic bulge, halo, and a supermassive black hole at the centre. Arms are regions of higher density where star formation is concentrated, marked by young blue stars and H II regions — explaining why arms appear bluer than the disk.

Formation of arms

The density wave theory, proposed by C. C. Lin and Frank Shu in the 1960s, explains arms as long-lived density waves; other models invoke transient, self-propagating star formation and differential rotation winding arms.

Classification

In the Hubble sequence, spirals are denoted S, with barred spirals SB, subdivided a–c by tightness of arms and size of bulge. The Milky Way is a barred spiral, type SBbc.
