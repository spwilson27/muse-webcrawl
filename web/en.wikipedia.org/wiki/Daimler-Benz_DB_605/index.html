Daimler-Benz DB 605

The Daimler-Benz DB 605 was a German aircraft engine built during World War II. Developed from the earlier DB 601, it powered many of the Luftwaffe's principal fighter aircraft, most notably the Messerschmitt Bf 109 in its later variants, as well as the Bf 110 heavy fighter and the Me 210/410 series.

Design and development

The DB 605 was developed to extract more power from the DB 601 by increasing the bore from 150 mm to 154 mm, raising displacement from 33.9 to 35.7 litres. The engine retained the inverted V-12 layout, fuel injection, and single-stage supercharger of its predecessor, though with strengthened components. Compression ratio was initially limited because Germany lacked sufficient high-octane fuel, so early versions relied on 87-octane B4 fuel; later models could use methanol-water (MW 50) injection for emergency power.

Variants

The DB 605A was the initial production model. Later variants included the DB 605AS with a larger supercharger for altitude performance, the DB 605D with higher compression and MW 50 injection, and the coupled DB 610, two DB 605s joined to drive a single propeller shaft, used in the Heinkel He 177.
