Gaëtan Henri Alfred Edouard Léon Marie Gatian de Clérambault (2 July 1872 – 17 November 1934) was a French psychiatrist best known for describing erotomania, a delusional disorder in which a person believes that another person is in love with them.

Career

De Clérambault worked for many years at the special infirmary of the Paris police prefecture, where he examined large numbers of detained patients and developed his influential descriptions of chronic hallucinatory psychosis and mental automatism. Jacques Lacan was among the psychiatrists influenced by his teaching.

Erotomania

In his 1921 paper on the "passionate psychoses", de Clérambault described erotomania: typically a woman who is convinced that a man of higher social status secretly loves her, interpreting neutral events as coded declarations. The eponym "de Clérambault's syndrome" remains in use.

Later life

Also a painter and photographer of draped fabrics, de Clérambault suffered from failing eyesight and chronic pain; he died by suicide in 1934.
