Saatchi & Saatchi is a global advertising agency network founded in London in 1970 by brothers Maurice and Charles Saatchi. Through a famous campaign for the Conservative Party in the 1979 British general election and a decade of rapid acquisitions, the firm became — briefly — the largest advertising agency group in the world, before financial turmoil in the 1990s led to the founders' departure and the agency's eventual sale to the French Publicis Groupe in 2000.

History

The Saatchi brothers, sons of Iraqi Jewish immigrants, built the agency on bold, high-profile creative work and aggressive expansion, buying major agencies including Ted Bates Worldwide. The 1978 poster "Labour Isn't Working", showing a dole queue, became one of the most famous political advertisements in Britain and helped Margaret Thatcher's Conservatives win the 1979 election. Overexpansion and the early-1990s recession drove the share price down; shareholders forced Maurice Saatchi out in 1994–95, and the brothers founded a rival agency, M&C Saatchi. The remaining company renamed and restructured before being acquired by Publicis in 2000.

Work and philosophy

The agency has created notable campaigns for clients including Toyota, Procter & Gamble brands such as Ariel and Head & Shoulders, and Visa. Under long-time worldwide creative chief Kevin Roberts it promoted the concept of "Lovemarks" — brands that inspire loyalty beyond reason. The network operates dozens of offices worldwide and organises the New Creators' Showcase at the Cannes Lions festival.
