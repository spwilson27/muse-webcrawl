The Attack class was a planned class of twelve conventionally powered submarines for the Royal Australian Navy, to be derived from the French Shortfin Barracuda design by Naval Group. Selected in 2016 under Australia's SEA 1000 programme, they were intended to replace the Collins-class submarines from the 2030s.

Programme

France's DCNS (later Naval Group) won the competitive evaluation against Japanese and German bids with a conventional variant of its nuclear Barracuda-class design. The boats were to be built at Osborne, South Australia. The programme's cost grew substantially over time — from about A$50 billion to around A$90 billion in out-turned estimates — and schedules slipped amid disputes over local industry content and design work.

Cancellation and AUKUS

On 15–16 September 2021, Australia announced the AUKUS security partnership with the United Kingdom and the United States and cancelled the Attack-class contract in favour of acquiring nuclear-powered submarines with British and American assistance. The cancellation strained relations with France, which recalled its ambassadors, and Australia later agreed a €555 million settlement with Naval Group.
