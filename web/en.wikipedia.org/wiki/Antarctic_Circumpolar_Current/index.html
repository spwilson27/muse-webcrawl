Antarctic Circumpolar Current

The Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC) is an ocean current that flows clockwise (as seen from the South Pole) from west to east around Antarctica. An alternative name is the West Wind Drift. The ACC is the dominant circulation feature of the Southern Ocean and has a mean transport estimated at 100–150 million cubic metres per second (sverdrups), making it the largest ocean current.

Structure

The current is driven by the strong westerly winds over the Southern Ocean and is not blocked by any continent, allowing it to circle the globe. It consists of several sharp fronts, including the Subantarctic Front and the Polar Front, across which temperature and salinity change abruptly. The ACC connects the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans and enables global exchange of water masses.

History and climate role

The current developed after Antarctica separated from South America and the Drake Passage opened, roughly 30–40 million years ago, thermally isolating Antarctica and contributing to its glaciation. The ACC plays a central role in global ocean circulation, upwelling nutrient- and carbon-rich deep water around Antarctica.
