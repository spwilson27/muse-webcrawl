A designated marksman rifle bridges the gap between service rifles and sniper rifles.

A designated marksman rifle (DMR) is a modern scoped, high-precision rifle used by infantrymen in the designated marksman (DM) role. A DMR generally fills the engagement range gap between a service rifle and a dedicated sniper rifle, at ranges of roughly 300–800 metres. DMRs are distinguished from dedicated sniper rifles by being semi-automatic weapons — capable of higher rates of fire — and typically having larger magazine capacities (10, 20, or 25 rounds depending on the firearm and operational role) to support sustained fire alongside the rest of the squad.

The DM role differs significantly from that of a specially trained sniper. Whereas snipers are often deployed to engage targets from concealed positions at long range, the designated marksman operates as a regular member of the infantry squad, providing accurate fire at ranges beyond those of the standard rifleman while continuing to fight in close combat with the unit.

Characteristics

DMRs are usually built on existing service-rifle or battle-rifle platforms that have been accurized with match-grade barrels, improved triggers, free-floating handguards, and variable-power telescopic sights. Ammunition issued may be standard ball or match-grade cartridges. Because the marksman fights as part of the squad, the rifle retains semi-automatic fire and standard magazines, unlike bolt-action sniper systems.

Examples

  • SVD Dragunov (USSR/Russia) — the archetypal squad-level precision rifle, chambered in 7.62×54mmR.
  • M14-based systems (United States) — including the Mk 14 Enhanced Battle Rifle and M39 EMR.
  • M110 Semi-Automatic Sniper System and the later M110A1 (United States).
  • Heckler & Koch G28 / HK417 variants (Germany).
  • L129A1 Sharpshooter rifle (United Kingdom), based on the LMT LW308MWS.
  • SIG Sauer 716 (used by India and others).

Comparison with sniper rifles

Sniper rifles are typically optimised for maximum precision at long ranges, often with manual bolt actions, specialised ammunition, and dedicated two-person teams. A DMR trades some of that ultimate precision and range for rate of fire, magazine capacity, and commonality with the squad's other weapons, reflecting the marksman's primary role as a rifleman with enhanced capability rather than a detached specialist.
