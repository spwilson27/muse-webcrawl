"Bayin" (ဘုရင်) is also the Burmese word for "king"; this article concerns the sovereign best known by the shortened form of his name.

Bayin is a shortened form of Bayinnaung Kyawhtin Nawrahta (1516–1581), king of the Toungoo dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1550 to 1581. During his 31-year reign, Bayinnaung assembled the largest empire in the history of Southeast Asia, encompassing much of present-day Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, and parts of China and India (Manipur). In Myanmar he is remembered as one of the three greatest kings, alongside Anawrahta and Alaungpaya, and is celebrated in Thailand as "the Conqueror of the Ten Directions".

Rise to power

Born Ye Htut, he was a commoner raised at court alongside Prince Tabinshwehti, whose sister he married and whom he served as chief general. Together they reunified Burma after the collapse of the Ava kingdom, capturing Pegu, Prome and Toungoo. When Tabinshwehti was assassinated in 1550, the kingdom fragmented; Bayinnaung spent two years reconquering it before his coronation.

Conquests

Bayinnaung then campaigned almost continuously. He subdued the Shan states, conquered the Lan Na kingdom (Chiang Mai) in 1558, and took Ayutthaya, capital of Siam, in 1564 and again decisively in 1569, deporting its king and court. In the 1570s he annexed Lan Xang (Laos) after protracted jungle campaigns and pushed his authority toward the borders of China and Bengal. His empire, held together by personal loyalty and garrisons, largely disintegrated within a few years of his death under his son Nanda Bayin.

Legacy

Bayinnaung was a devout Theravada Buddhist who built and restored pagodas, banned animal sacrifice in conquered territories, and standardised weights, measures and law across his empire. His statue stands in front of Myanmar's National Museum, and his campaigns remain central to Burmese national historiography — and, in Thailand, to memories of national humiliation he inflicted on Ayutthaya.
