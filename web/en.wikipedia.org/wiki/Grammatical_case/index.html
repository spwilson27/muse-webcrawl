Grammatical case

Cases mark the grammatical function of nouns

Grammatical case is a category of nouns and noun modifiers (determiners, adjectives, participles and numerals) which corresponds to one or more potential grammatical functions for a nominal group in a wording or clause. The marking of case typically occurs through inflection: a change in the form of the noun or modifier itself, most often by the addition of a suffix or ending.

Common cases

Languages with rich case systems, such as Latin, Ancient Greek, Sanskrit, Russian and Finnish, distinguish cases including the nominative (for the subject), the accusative (for the direct object), the dative (for the indirect object), the genitive (for possession), and cases marking location, movement and instrument such as the locative, ablative and instrumental. Finnish and Hungarian are famous for having fifteen or more cases.

Case in English

English has largely lost its historical case system; word order and prepositions do most of the work instead. Remnants survive in the pronouns: I/me/my, he/him/his, she/her, we/us, they/them, and in the possessive suffix -'s.

Alignment

How cases map subjects and objects relates to morphosyntactic alignment: in nominative–accusative languages the subject of a transitive verb shares the case of an intransitive subject, while in ergative–absolutive languages it is the object that patterns with the intransitive subject.
