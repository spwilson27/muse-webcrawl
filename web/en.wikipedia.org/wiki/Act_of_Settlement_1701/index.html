Act of Settlement 1701

The Act of Settlement 1701 is an Act of the Parliament of England that settled the succession to the English and Irish crowns on Protestants only. Its main provisions remain in force in the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms. The Act settled the crown on Sophia, Electress of Hanover — granddaughter of James I and VI — and her Protestant descendants, bypassing more than fifty Catholic relatives with stronger hereditary claims.

Background and provisions

After the death of Queen Anne's last surviving child, the Duke of Gloucester, in 1700, Parliament acted to prevent a Catholic restoration. The Act required the monarch to join in communion with the Church of England, barred those who married Roman Catholics (a disqualification removed by the Succession to the Crown Act 2013), required parliamentary consent for the monarch to leave the realm or go to war for foreign possessions, and strengthened judicial independence by providing that judges hold office during good behaviour and can be removed only by both Houses of Parliament.

Consequences

Sophia died weeks before Anne in 1714, so her son succeeded as George I, founding the Hanoverian dynasty. The Act remains a cornerstone of the British constitution and, through the Statute of Westminster and the Perth Agreement reforms of 2011–2015 (which also ended male-preference primogeniture), continues to govern succession across the Commonwealth realms, subject to their own laws.
