Inclusive design

Inclusive design is a design philosophy and methodology that aims to create products, services, environments and experiences that are usable by as many people as reasonably possible, without the need for special adaptation or specialised design. It overlaps closely with universal design and accessibility, but typically emphasises inclusion across a broader range of human diversity — including age, disability, gender, language, culture and socio-economic status.

Principles

The British Design Council and the Cambridge Inclusive Design Toolkit describe inclusive design as a process that involves users throughout, recognises diversity and difference, and offers choice where a single design solution cannot suit everyone. Microsoft's Inclusive Design toolkit popularised the idea of designing for permanent, temporary and situational impairments — for example, a curb cut benefits wheelchair users, parents with strollers and travellers with luggage alike.

Related concepts

Inclusive design differs from accessibility in that accessibility often concerns compliance with standards for people with disabilities, while inclusive design is a broader human-centred approach. It is also related to design for all, barrier-free design, and participatory design.
