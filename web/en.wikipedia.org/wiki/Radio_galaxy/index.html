Radio galaxy

A radio galaxy is a galaxy with giant regions of radio emission extending well beyond its visible structure. These energetic radio lobes are powered by jets of charged particles emanating from the galaxy's active galactic nucleus. Radio galaxies are a type of active galaxy, hosted almost always by massive elliptical galaxies containing supermassive black holes.

Emission processes

The radio emission is synchrotron radiation from relativistic electrons spiralling in magnetic fields. In the Fanaroff–Riley classification, FR I sources are brightest near the nucleus and fade outward, while FR II sources have bright hotspots at the far ends of their lobes. Famous examples include Cygnus A, Centaurus A, Virgo A (Messier 87) and Fornax A.

Life cycle

Jets inflate lobes of plasma that can span millions of light-years, making radio galaxies among the largest known structures associated with a single galaxy. Their activity is episodic, with phases of jet activity lasting tens of millions of years.
