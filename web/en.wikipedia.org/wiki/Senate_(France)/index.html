The Senate (French: Sénat) is the upper house of the Parliament of France. It represents the territorial collectivities of the Republic — communes, departments and regions — and French citizens living abroad. Senators are not chosen by direct universal suffrage but by an electoral college of locally elected officials, and the Senate is not subject to dissolution.

Composition and election

The Senate has 348 members. They are elected for six-year terms, with half of the seats renewed every three years. The electors, known as grands électeurs, number about 162,000 and consist overwhelmingly of municipal councillors, together with departmental and regional councillors and members of the National Assembly. Voting is by majority ballot in smaller departments and by proportional representation in larger ones. Candidates must be at least 24 years old. Until a reform of 2003, senators served nine-year terms renewed by thirds.

Role

Under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic, legislation normally shuttles between the Senate and the National Assembly until both agree on an identical text; if disagreement persists, the government may give the National Assembly the final word, except on constitutional revision and certain laws concerning the Senate itself, which require the Senate's consent. The Senate's President is second in the order of precedence and serves as acting President of the Republic if the presidency falls vacant — a role performed by Alain Poher in 1969 and again in 1974. Because of its indirect election and staggered renewal, the Senate historically leaned to the right; the left won a majority for the first time in the Fifth Republic in 2011, holding it until 2014.
