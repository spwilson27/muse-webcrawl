Biblical Magi

The Biblical Magi (Greek: μάγοι, magoi), also called the Three Wise Men or Three Kings, were distinguished visitors who, according to the Gospel of Matthew, followed a star to Bethlehem to worship the infant Jesus, bringing gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh. Matthew does not specify their number, names, or royal status; Western tradition settled on three and named them Caspar (or Gaspar), Melchior and Balthazar.

Matthew's account

Matthew 2 relates that magi "from the east" arrived in Jerusalem asking for the newborn king of the Jews, that Herod the Great directed them to Bethlehem, and that they were warned in a dream not to return to Herod. Their visit is linked to the Massacre of the Innocents and the Flight into Egypt. The term magos originally referred to a Persian priestly caste associated with astrology and dream interpretation, and the visitors are often understood as astrologers.

Traditions

Later Christian tradition elaborated their origins — variously Persia, Arabia, India or Ethiopia — and their relics were said to have been brought to Cologne Cathedral, where a famous shrine houses them. Their feast is Epiphany (6 January), and in many Spanish-speaking countries the Three Kings (Reyes Magos) bring children gifts. The Magi are venerated as saints in both Western and Eastern Christianity, and their journey is a staple of Nativity plays and art.
