Railroad track

A railroad track (also railway track, or simply track) is the permanent way structure on which trains run. It consists of two parallel steel rails set at a fixed distance apart (the track gauge), fixed to sleepers (ties) which are embedded in ballast — a bed of crushed stone — or mounted on a concrete slab. The rails guide the flanged wheels of trains and transfer their loads into the track foundation.

Standard gauge (1,435 mm or 4 ft 8½ in) is the most common worldwide, but broad and narrow gauges are widely used. Rails are produced in standardized profiles and steel grades, joined by fishplates or, in modern continuously welded rail, by welding into long strings that reduce joint impacts. Sleepers may be of timber, concrete, or steel, and rails are attached with spikes, screws, or elastic fastening systems.

Components and maintenance

The track structure comprises the rails, fasteners, sleepers, ballast, and subgrade; switches and crossings allow trains to change tracks. Maintenance includes tamping the ballast to restore geometry, grinding rails to correct profile and remove fatigue defects, and inspection for broken rails and geometry faults. Slab track (ballastless track) is used on many high-speed lines and in tunnels for reduced maintenance.
