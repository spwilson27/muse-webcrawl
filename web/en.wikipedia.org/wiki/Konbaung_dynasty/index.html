Konbaung dynasty

The Konbaung dynasty (Burmese: ကုန်းဘောင်ခေတ်) was the last dynasty that ruled Burma (Myanmar), from 1752 to 1885. It created the second-largest empire in Burmese history and continued the administrative reforms begun by the Taungoo dynasty, laying the foundations of the modern state of Burma. The reforms proved insufficient, however, to stem the advance of the British, who waged three wars against the Burmese Empire over six decades (1824–1885) and finally annexed Burma in January 1886.

History

The dynasty was founded by Alaungpaya, chief of the village of Shwebo, who reunified Burma after the collapse of Taungoo rule and the brief Mon ascendancy of the Restored Hanthawaddy Kingdom. His successors conquered Arakan (1784), invaded Siam repeatedly, repelled four Qing invasions in the Sino-Burmese War (1765–1769), and pushed westward into Assam and Manipur — moves that brought Burma into collision with the British East India Company. The First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–1826) cost Burma Arakan and Tenasserim under the Treaty of Yandabo; the Second (1852) cost Lower Burma. King Mindon (r. 1853–1878) modernised the state, moved the capital to Mandalay (founded 1857), and convened the Fifth Buddhist Council. His successor Thibaw was deposed after the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885) and exiled to Ratnagiri, India.

Culture and administration

Konbaung kings patronised Burmese literature, theatre, and orthodox Theravada Buddhism. The dynasty carried out censuses and tax inquests, standardised weights and coinage under later kings, and maintained a court culture centred on the royal capitals of the upper Irrawaddy. The Glass Palace Chronicle (Hmannan Yazawin), commissioned in 1829, is a major historical product of the era.
