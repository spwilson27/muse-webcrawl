Te Rauparaha (c. 1768 – 27 November 1849) was a Māori rangatira (chief) of the Ngāti Toa iwi who led his people from their traditional home at Kawhia to conquer and settle the Kāpiti Coast and the top of the South Island of New Zealand during the Musket Wars of the 1820s and 1830s.

Life and campaigns

Driven south by Waikato and Ngāti Maniapoto pressure, Te Rauparaha led the great migration (Te Heke Mai Raro) to the Cook Strait region, established a stronghold on Kapiti Island, and extended his authority over the Wairau, Nelson and Banks Peninsula areas through alliance and conquest, trading flax and provisions with European whalers and ships for muskets. He is credited with composing the famous haka "Ka Mate", now performed worldwide by the All Blacks rugby team, after escaping pursuers by hiding in a food pit.

Later years

Te Rauparaha signed the Treaty of Waitangi in 1840 but clashed with the New Zealand Company over land, including in the Wairau Affray of 1843. In 1846 Governor George Grey had him arrested without charge and detained for two years. He died at Ōtaki in 1849. His son Tāmihana Te Rauparaha wrote a major biography of him, a foundational source for Ngāti Toa history.
