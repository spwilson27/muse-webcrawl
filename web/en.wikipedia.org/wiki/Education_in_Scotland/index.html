Education in Scotland

Education in Scotland is organised distinctly from the rest of the United Kingdom, reflecting Scotland's separate legal and educational traditions. Responsibility lies with the Scottish Government and local authorities. State schooling is free, and Scotland has a long tradition of broad general education administered through comprehensive schools.

School system

Children attend primary school for seven years (P1–P7) and secondary school for up to six years (S1–S6). Pupils take National qualifications and then Highers — the main university entrance qualification — in S5, with Advanced Highers available in S6. The national curriculum framework is the Curriculum for Excellence, introduced from 2010. Gaelic-medium education is offered in parts of Scotland, particularly in the Highlands and Islands.

Universities

Scotland's universities include four ancient universities — St Andrews, Glasgow, Aberdeen and Edinburgh — founded in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, and a range of later institutions. Scottish-domiciled students studying in Scotland do not pay tuition fees, which are covered by the Student Awards Agency Scotland. The school leaving age is 16.
