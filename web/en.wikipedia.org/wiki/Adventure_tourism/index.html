Adventure tourism

Adventure tourism is a type of tourism involving travel to remote or exotic locations in order to take part in physically demanding and often risky outdoor activities. The Adventure Travel Trade Association defines adventure travel as travel containing at least two of three elements: physical activity, natural environment, and cultural immersion.

Types

Activities commonly classed as adventure tourism include mountaineering, trekking, white-water rafting, scuba diving, paragliding, caving, mountain biking, surfing, and safaris. "Hard" adventure involves significant risk and specialized skill, while "soft" adventure — such as hiking or guided wildlife viewing — involves moderate exertion and lower risk.

Market and impacts

Adventure tourism has grown rapidly since the 1990s and is a significant source of income in countries such as Nepal, Costa Rica, New Zealand, and Peru. The sector is frequently promoted as environmentally sustainable, though growth brings concerns about safety standards, rescue provision, cultural disruption, and the environmental footprint of travel to fragile ecosystems.
