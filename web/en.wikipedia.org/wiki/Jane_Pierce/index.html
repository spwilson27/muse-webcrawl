Jane Means Pierce (née Appleton; March 12, 1806 – December 2, 1863) was the wife of Franklin Pierce, the 14th president of the United States, and served as First Lady from 1853 to 1857. Shy, devout, and in fragile health, she was one of the most reluctant occupants of the role in American history.

Early life and marriage

Jane Appleton was born in Hampton, New Hampshire, the daughter of Jesse Appleton, a Congregational minister who became president of Bowdoin College. She met Franklin Pierce, then a young lawyer and Bowdoin graduate, and they married on November 19, 1834, when she was 28. She disliked politics and Washington society and hoped her husband would leave public life; his advocacy of her views was limited, and the couple's marriage was strained by his career and drinking and by the deaths of their children.

Tragedy and the White House

All three of the Pierces' sons died young. Their last surviving child, Benjamin "Benny" Pierce, aged eleven, was killed in a train wreck at Andover, Massachusetts, on January 6, 1853, before his parents' eyes, two months before the inauguration. Jane Pierce blamed the accident on God's displeasure at her husband's political ambitions, spent much of the first two years of his presidency in mourning seclusion upstairs in the White House, writing letters to her dead son, and only began receiving guests in 1855.

Later life

After leaving Washington in 1857, the Pierces travelled in Europe and the Caribbean seeking better health for Jane. She died of tuberculosis at her sister's home in Andover, Massachusetts, in 1863, and was buried in Concord, New Hampshire. Her husband died six years later.
