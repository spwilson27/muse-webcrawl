Game mechanic

Game mechanics are the rules and procedures that guide the player and the game's response to the player's moves or actions. Along with the game's content, mechanics define the essential structure of any game, from the roll of dice in backgammon to the jump button in a platformer.

Definition

Definitions vary, but game mechanics are generally understood as the methods by which players interact with the game state — the verbs of a game. A single game usually combines several individual mechanics, and there exist complete games that are based solely on only one mechanic. The "core mechanic" is the action a player repeats most, around which the rest of the design is built.

Common mechanics

  • Turns and action points — players alternate moves, spending limited action budgets.
  • Dice rolling and randomness — uncertainty resolved by dice, cards or random number generation.
  • Resource management — gathering, spending and converting limited resources.
  • Capture/elimination — removing opponents' pieces, as in chess.
  • Set collection — acquiring groups of matching items, as in poker hands.
  • Auction/bidding — competing valuations of items or privileges.
  • Worker placement — assigning limited tokens to shared action spaces, popularised by modern Eurogames.
  • Deck-building — constructing a personal deck during play, begun by Dominion (2008).
  • Platforming mechanics — jumping, double-jumping, wall-running in video games.
  • Experience points and levelling — character growth imported from role-playing games into almost every genre.

Mechanics vs. dynamics

Designers distinguish mechanics (the rules as written) from dynamics (the behaviour that emerges when rules meet players — alliances, kingmaking, grinding) and from aesthetics (the feelings produced). Much of game design consists of predicting dynamics that cannot be read directly off the mechanics.
