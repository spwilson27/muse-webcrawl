Sodium hydroxide (NaOH), commonly caustic soda or lye, is a white, strongly alkaline solid that is extremely corrosive and dissolves in water with release of much heat. It is one of the world's ten largest-volume industrial chemicals.

Caustic soda saponifies fats to make soap, digests wood pulp for paper, refines alumina and petroleum, and unblocks drains by dissolving grease and hair. It is produced by the chloralkali electrolysis of brine, together with chlorine and hydrogen.

[Image: White pellets of sodium hydroxide in a glass dish]

Safety and handling

NaOH pellets rapidly absorb water and carbon dioxide from air. Its danger lies in dissolving tissue proteins and fats, painlessly at first in the eyes; laboratory rules require eye protection even for dilute solutions.

Chemistry

As a strong base it dissociates completely into Na⁺ and OH⁻, neutralising acids to salt and water and precipitating many metal hydroxides — the basis of classic qualitative analysis.
