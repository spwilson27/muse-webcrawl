Harwich is a town in Essex, England, and one of the Haven ports on the North Sea coast. It lies at the mouths of the rivers Stour and Orwell, opposite Felixstowe in Suffolk, and its natural harbour has made it an important naval and ferry port for centuries.

History

The name Harwich derives from the Old English for "army settlement". The town received its charter in 1238. Its harbour sheltered naval fleets during the medieval and early modern wars with France, Spain, and the Netherlands, and Christopher Jones, captain of the Mayflower, was a Harwich man. In both world wars Harwich was a major naval base, and it was where the German U-boat fleet was surrendered in 1918.

The port today

Harwich International Port operates ferry services to the Hook of Holland in the Netherlands and formerly to Esbjerg in Denmark. Together with Felixstowe and Ipswich it forms the Haven ports complex. The old town retains many historic buildings, including the Electric Palace Cinema, one of the oldest purpose-built cinemas in Britain, and the Redoubt Fort built in 1808 against Napoleonic invasion.
