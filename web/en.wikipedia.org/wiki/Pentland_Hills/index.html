The Pentland Hills are a range of hills running for about 20 miles (32 km) south-west of Edinburgh, Scotland, from near the city boundary at Fairmilehead towards Biggar and the upper Clyde valley. Much of the range lies within the Pentland Hills Regional Park, established in 1984 and covering about 90 km².

Geography

The hills are of volcanic origin, formed in the Devonian period. The principal summits include Scald Law (579 m), the highest point, Carnethy Hill (573 m), Turnhouse Hill, Black Hill, Allermuir Hill and Caerketton Hill, the last two overlooking Edinburgh. The range is cut by several reservoirs — including Glencorse, Loganlea, Threipmuir and Harlaw — built in the nineteenth century to supply Edinburgh with water. The artificial ski slope at Hillend, on the northern slopes, is one of the largest dry ski centres in Europe.

History

The hills contain prehistoric hillforts, notably at Caerketton and Castle Law. In 1666 the Battle of Rullion Green, the only significant battle of the Pentland Rising, was fought at the foot of the hills, ending in defeat for the Covenanter rebels. The writer Robert Louis Stevenson's family summered at Swanston, on the northern slopes, and the hills feature in his writing.

Recreation

The Pentlands are heavily used for hillwalking, mountain biking, fishing and skiing, with waymarked routes including the cross-range paths between Balerno, Currie, Penicuik and Carlops. The area is managed by a ranger service under the regional park authority.
