Intensivist

An intensivist, also known as a critical care physician, is a physician who specializes in the care of critically ill patients, most often in the intensive care unit (ICU). Intensivists can be internists or surgeons who have completed a fellowship in critical care medicine, or anaesthesiologists, pulmonologists, emergency physicians or paediatricians with additional critical-care training.

Role

Intensivists direct the multidisciplinary team caring for ICU patients, managing mechanical ventilation, haemodynamic support, sedation, nutrition, renal replacement therapy and the complications of multi-organ failure. They coordinate the contributions of nursing staff, respiratory therapists, pharmacists and other specialists, and they lead discussions with patients and families about goals of care, prognosis and end-of-life decisions. In a "closed" ICU the intensivist takes primary responsibility for all admitted patients; in an "open" ICU they act as consultants.

Training

Training pathways vary by country. In the United States, critical care is entered through fellowships following residency in internal medicine, surgery, anaesthesiology, emergency medicine or paediatrics, with board certification through bodies such as ABIM. In Australia and New Zealand, training is administered by the College of Intensive Care Medicine; in the United Kingdom, intensive care medicine became a standalone specialty in the 2010s under the Faculty of Intensive Care Medicine. Evidence generally associates dedicated intensivist staffing — particularly round-the-clock coverage — with lower ICU mortality and shorter lengths of stay.
