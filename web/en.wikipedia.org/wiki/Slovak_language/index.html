Slovak language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Slovak (slovenčina) is a West Slavic language of the Czech–Slovak group, spoken primarily in Slovakia, where it is the official language. It is closely related to Czech, with which it shares a high degree of mutual intelligibility, and is also related to Polish and other Slavic languages.

History

The Slovak literary language was codified in the nineteenth century. Anton Bernolák produced the first codification in 1787, based on western dialects, while Ľudovít Štúr's codification of 1843, founded on the central dialect, became the basis of modern standard Slovak after later reforms by Martin Hattala and others.

Phonology

Slovak has a distinctive rhythmic law limiting the length of adjacent syllables, and possesses long and short vowels as well as the diphthongs ia, ie, iu, ô. Uniquely among Slavic languages it has the vowel ä. Stress normally falls on the first syllable.

Grammar

Slovak is a highly inflected language with six or seven grammatical cases, three genders, and verbal aspect. It is written in a Latin alphabet with diacritics such as č, š, ž, ľ, ň, ť, ď and á.

Speakers

Slovak is spoken by roughly five million people in Slovakia and by diaspora communities in the United States, the Czech Republic, Serbia (Vojvodina) and elsewhere.
