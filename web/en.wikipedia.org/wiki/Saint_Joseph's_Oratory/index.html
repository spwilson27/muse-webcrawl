Saint Joseph's Oratory

Saint Joseph's Oratory of Mount Royal (French: Oratoire Saint-Joseph du Mont-Royal) is a Roman Catholic basilica and national shrine on Mount Royal in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It is the largest church in Canada and the country's tallest, its dome rising above the city, and it is dedicated to Saint Joseph, the patron saint of Canada.

The Oratory's dome dominates the western slope of Mount Royal.

History

The shrine began in 1904 as a tiny chapel built by Brother André Bessette, a humble Holy Cross brother and doorkeeper at Collège Notre-Dame, whose devotion to Saint Joseph and reputation for healing drew crowds of the sick and poor. As donations poured in, successively larger churches were planned; work on the present basilica began in 1924, though construction halted during the Great Depression and the church was only completed in 1967.

Architecture and features

The basilica is built in an Italian Renaissance style with a copper-clad dome that is among the largest of its kind in the world; the cross at its summit, 283 metres above sea level, is the highest point in Montreal. Pilgrims climb the 283 steps to the entrance, the middle flight traditionally reserved for climbing on one's knees. Inside, the shrine preserves Brother André's heart as a relic, his original small chapel, and a votive chapel where thousands of crutches left by cured pilgrims hang from the walls. Brother André was canonised by Pope Benedict XVI in 2010.
