Road of Life

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Road of Life (Russian: Доро́га жи́зни) was an ice road across the frozen Lake Ladoga that provided the only land access to the besieged city of Leningrad during the winters of 1941–42 and 1942–43 in the Second World War. Trucks carried food and supplies into the city and evacuated civilians on the return journey, saving hundreds of thousands of lives during the 872-day siege.

LocationLake Ladoga, near Leningrad (St Petersburg)
OperatedNovember 1941 – March 1943 (winter seasons)
LengthAbout 30 km across the ice
ContextSiege of Leningrad, Eastern Front

Operation

When German and Finnish forces completed the encirclement of Leningrad in September 1941, the lake remained the city's only link to unoccupied Soviet territory. In November 1941, as the ice thickened, the first horse-drawn sledges and then truck convoys crossed from Kokkorevo on the western shore to Kobona in the east. Drivers often travelled at night with doors removed so they could escape if the ice broke under shellfire or the weight of the load.

Impact

Over the first winter more than 360,000 tons of supplies, mostly food and fuel, were delivered across the ice, and about 550,000 people were evacuated; in total, roughly 1.4 million residents were evacuated over the route's existence. A fuel pipeline and power cable were also laid under the lake. Although deliveries fell far short of the city's needs and starvation continued, the Road of Life kept Leningrad from capitulating. A memorial complex and museum at Lake Ladoga commemorate the route today.
