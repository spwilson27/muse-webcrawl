Les Eyzies is a commune in the Dordogne department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It was established on 1 January 2019 by the merger of the former communes of Les Eyzies-de-Tayac-Sireuil, Manaurie, and Saint-Cirq. Les Eyzies-de-Tayac-Sireuil is the commune's administrative seat and the reason for its fame: the village, cradled beneath limestone cliffs in the Vézère valley, styles itself the "world capital of prehistory".

Prehistory

The Vézère valley contains one of the world's densest concentrations of Palaeolithic sites. In 1868 railway workers at the Cro-Magnon rock shelter near the village uncovered burials of early anatomically modern humans, giving the name "Cro-Magnon" to the first modern Europeans. Nearby sites include the caves and shelters of Font-de-Gaume—one of the few French caves with polychrome Palaeolithic paintings still open to the public—Les Combarelles, the Abri Pataud, La Micoque, and Le Moustier, the type site of the Mousterian culture of the Neanderthals. Fifteen of these sites were inscribed in 1979 on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Prehistoric Sites and Decorated Caves of the Vézère Valley". Lascaux lies some 25 km away.

Sights

The village hosts France's National Museum of Prehistory (Musée national de Préhistoire), founded in 1918 and housed partly within the cliffside Château de Tayac; a colossal statue of a Neanderthal man by Paul Dardé, erected in 1931, looms over the village from the cliff above. The museum holds one of the world's great collections of Palaeolithic stone tools and portable art. The commune is a popular base for visiting the Dordogne's caves, castles, and river valleys.
