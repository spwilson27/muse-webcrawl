Jantar Mantar

A Jantar Mantar is a collection of architectural astronomical instruments built by Maharaja Jai Singh II of Jaipur between 1724 and 1735. Five such observatories were constructed at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura and Varanasi, featuring masonry instruments for measuring time, predicting eclipses, tracking stars and determining celestial coordinates. The name derives from yantra mantra, "instrument of calculation."

Jantar Mantar
Built: 1724–1735
Builder: Maharaja Jai Singh II
Locations: Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura, Varanasi
Purpose: Astronomical observation

History

Jai Singh II, a Mughal courtier and astronomer influenced by Ulugh Beg's Samarkand observatory, found existing tables inaccurate and small brass instruments unreliable. He built large-scale masonry instruments of stone and marble, some reaching 27 metres high, based on Ptolemaic and Islamic astronomy as well as European data supplied by Jesuit missionaries.

Instruments

Principal instruments include the Samrat Yantra (a giant sundial), Jai Prakash (hemispherical bowl instruments), Ram Yantra (altitude-azimuth), Misra Yantra and Shasthansa Yantra. The instruments measure the time to an accuracy of a few seconds, the sun's declination, and planetary positions for casting horoscopes and fixing the calendar.

The five observatories

The Delhi observatory (1724) was first; the Jaipur complex is the largest and best preserved, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010. The Mathura observatory has disappeared. The sites remain emblematic of late pre-telescopic astronomy in India. See also Jaipur.
