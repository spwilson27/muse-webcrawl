Syphax

Syphax (died c. 203/202 BC) was a king of the Masaesyli tribe of western Numidia (present-day Algeria) during the last quarter of the 3rd century BC. His story is told in Livy's Ab Urbe Condita and other Roman sources concerning the Second Punic War between Rome and Carthage.

Life

Syphax ruled the Masaesyli, the western of the two great Numidian peoples. Early in the Second Punic War he was courted by both Rome and Carthage for control of North Africa. In 213 BC he entered into an alliance with Rome against Carthage, and Roman officers, including Quintus Statorius, were sent to train his army. Syphax attacked the Carthaginians and their eastern Numidian ally Gala, father of Masinissa.

Relations shifted when Syphax married Sophonisba, the beautiful daughter of the Carthaginian general Hasdrubal Gisco, in about 206 BC. The marriage sealed an alliance with Carthage and Syphax became one of Rome's principal enemies in Africa. He sent envoys to the Roman commander Scipio Africanus warning him against invading Africa.

Defeat and capture

When Scipio invaded Africa in 204 BC, Syphax and Hasdrubal Gisco combined against him. In 203 BC Scipio attacked and burned their camps near Utica in a famous night assault. Syphax raised a new army but was defeated and captured at the Battle of Cirta by Masinissa and the Roman commander Gaius Laelius. Cirta, his capital, surrendered, and Sophonisba — who had been married to Masinissa after the city's fall — took poison to avoid being paraded in a Roman triumph.

Syphax was taken to Italy as a prisoner and died at Tibur (modern Tivoli), in 203 or 202 BC. His kingdom was absorbed into that of Masinissa, Rome's ally.

Legacy

The city of Sfax in modern Tunisia is traditionally said to take its name from Syphax. His dramatic fall, and the story of Sophonisba, became popular subjects in later literature and art.
