Ecotage

Ecotage is a form of sabotage carried out for environmental reasons, in which property or machinery is damaged or disabled to obstruct activities considered harmful to the natural environment, such as logging, development, animal testing or fossil-fuel extraction. The word combines "eco-" with "sabotage". Tactics associated with ecotage include tree spiking, arson of construction sites and sport-utility vehicles, disabling bulldozers, and blockades.

History

The term gained currency in the United States in the 1970s and 1980s through the radical environmental movement. The novel The Monkey Wrench Gang (1975) by Edward Abbey popularised the idea of environmental sabotage; the group Earth First!, founded in 1980, adopted "monkeywrenching" as a tactic, and the Earth Liberation Front (ELF) and Animal Liberation Front (ALF) later claimed responsibility for arson attacks. Dave Foreman's 1985 manual Ecodefense: A Field Guide to Monkeywrenching codified techniques.

Response and debate

Law-enforcement agencies in the United States have treated some ecotage as "eco-terrorism", and the FBI at one time described it as a leading domestic-terrorism threat; in the mid-2000s "Operation Backfire" prosecuted a network of ELF and ALF activists. Supporters counter that actions target property, not people, and characterise ecotage as civil disobedience or self-defence of ecosystems, while mainstream environmental organisations generally condemn property destruction as counterproductive. Laws such as the US Animal Enterprise Terrorism Act increased penalties for such offences.
