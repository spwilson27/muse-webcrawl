Mardi Gras throws

Mardi Gras throws are the strings of beads, doubloons, cups, plush toys and other trinkets tossed from parade floats to spectators during Mardi Gras in New Orleans and other Gulf Coast carnival celebrations. Shouted appeals of "Throw me something, Mister!" accompany the custom, which has become the most famous visual symbol of the festival.

History

Twelfth Night Revelers began handing out favours — including sugar-coated almonds — in the 1870s, and glass beads (from Bohemia, later Czechoslovakia) were thrown in the early twentieth century. Plastic beads, imported mainly from Taiwan and later China, became standard after the 1960s. Aluminium doubloon coins were introduced by Rex in 1960. Certain krewes developed signature throws: Zulu's hand-painted coconuts, Muses' decorated shoes, and Nyx's purses are among the most coveted.

Types and significance

Throws in the official carnival colours of purple (justice), green (faith) and gold (power) predominate. Krewe members purchase their own throws, spending collectively tens of millions of dollars each season; a rider on a large float may throw over a thousand strands of beads in a single parade. The city collects many tons of leftover beads after carnival, and recycling programmes such as ArcGNO's have grown in response to environmental concerns about plastic waste and clogged storm drains.
