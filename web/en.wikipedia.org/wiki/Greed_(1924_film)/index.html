Greed (1924 film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greed is a 1924 American silent psychological drama film written and directed by Erich von Stroheim, based on the 1899 Frank Norris novel McTeague. It stars Gibson Gowland as John McTeague, ZaSu Pitts as Trina, and Jean Hersholt as Marcus Schouler.

The film tells the story of McTeague, a San Francisco dentist, whose wife Trina wins a lottery prize of $5,000. Her growing obsession with money corrodes their marriage, and betrayed by his friend Marcus, McTeague loses his practice and descends into poverty, alcoholism and murder, culminating in a famous climactic confrontation in Death Valley.

Production and cutting

Von Stroheim, obsessive about realism, shot on location and produced an original cut of roughly nine hours. The studio, Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, took the film away from him and reduced it first to about four hours and finally to about two hours and twenty minutes. The excised footage is lost, and the original version is often cited as one of the great "lost" works of cinema.

Legacy

Although a commercial failure on release, Greed is now regarded as one of the greatest films ever made, a landmark of cinematic realism. A 1999 reconstruction using surviving stills and the original continuity script restored the running time to about four hours.
