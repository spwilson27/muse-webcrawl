Hoop (rhythmic gymnastics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A gymnast performing a hoop routine

The hoop is one of the five apparatus used in rhythmic gymnastics, alongside the ball, clubs, ribbon, and (historically) the rope. It is one of the original apparatus of the sport and has been contested at the Olympic Games since rhythmic gymnastics became an Olympic discipline in 1984.

Specifications

According to the norms of the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG), the hoop is made of plastic or wood and must have an interior diameter of 80 to 90 centimetres and a minimum weight of 300 grams. It may be plain or covered with coloured adhesive tape, often decorated to match the gymnast's leotard.

Technique

The fundamental body movements used with the hoop include rolls over the body and on the floor, rotations around the hand or other body parts, swings, circles, figures of eight, passes through and over the hoop, and throws with catches in varied planes. The hoop's large size and rigid frame allow gymnasts to pass their whole body through it, spin it on the floor like a coin, and roll it across the back and arms. Under the Code of Points, routines must show mastery of the apparatus combined with difficulty elements such as leaps, balances, and rotations, performed to music in routines of 75 to 90 seconds for individuals.

History

Hoop exercises developed from the natural gymnastics and dance traditions of the early twentieth century and were incorporated into the Soviet rhythmic sport system. The hoop was part of the first World Championships in 1963 and remains a fixture of individual and group competition.
