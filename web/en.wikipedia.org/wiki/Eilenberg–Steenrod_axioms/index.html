Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms

FieldAlgebraic topology
Introduced bySamuel Eilenberg and Norman Steenrod (1945)
SubjectAxiomatic characterisation of homology and cohomology theories

The Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms are a set of properties, formulated by Samuel Eilenberg and Norman Steenrod around 1945, that a homology theory (or cohomology theory) on topological spaces should satisfy. They axiomatise what it means to extract algebraic invariants from spaces in a way shared by singular, simplicial, and cellular homology.

The axioms

For a homology theory assigning groups Hn(X, A) to pairs of spaces, the axioms are: homotopy (homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism), excision (removing a subset whose closure lies inside the interior of A does not change the homology), exactness (each pair gives a long exact sequence), additivity (the homology of a disjoint union is the direct sum), and the dimension axiom (the homology of a point is concentrated in degree zero, giving the coefficient group).

Consequences

Eilenberg and Steenrod proved a uniqueness theorem: any two theories satisfying the axioms agree on finite CW complexes. Dropping the dimension axiom leads to generalised (extraordinary) homology theories such as K-theory and cobordism. The axioms were foundational for the modern, categorical style of algebraic topology presented in their 1952 book Foundations of Algebraic Topology.
