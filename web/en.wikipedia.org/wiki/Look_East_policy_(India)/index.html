India's Look East policy was an effort to cultivate extensive economic and strategic relations with the nations of Southeast Asia and East Asia in order to bolster India's standing as a regional power and to counterbalance the strategic influence of China. It was launched in 1991–92 by the government of Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao, coinciding with India's economic liberalisation and the end of the Cold War, and was pursued vigorously by his successors Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Manmohan Singh.

Development

In its first phase the policy concentrated on trade and investment with ASEAN. India became a sectoral dialogue partner of ASEAN in 1992 and a full dialogue partner in 1996, joined the ASEAN Regional Forum, and later signed free-trade agreements in goods (2009, in force 2010) and in services and investment (2014) with the bloc. In a second phase the engagement was widened geographically to include China, Japan, South Korea, Australia and the Pacific, and broadened from economics to security cooperation, naval exercises and connectivity projects through Myanmar to India's north-east.

Act East

In November 2014 the government of Narendra Modi announced that the policy had been upgraded to an Act East policy, emphasising more active, time-bound engagement — including infrastructure links such as the India–Myanmar–Thailand Trilateral Highway and deeper defence partnerships.

Assessment

Trade between India and ASEAN grew from about $2 billion in the early 1990s to tens of billions annually, though commentators note that India's economic footprint in the region remains smaller than China's and that connectivity with north-east India has progressed slowly.
