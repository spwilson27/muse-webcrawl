Tallinn Song Festival Grounds

LocationPirita, Tallinn, Estonia
Opened1960
ArchitectAlar Kotli
CapacityAbout 70,000–100,000 spectators

The Tallinn Song Festival Grounds (Estonian: Lauluväljak) are an open-air venue in Tallinn, Estonia, best known as the home of the Estonian Song Festival, held every five years, in which tens of thousands of singers perform simultaneously on its great arched stage.

Architecture

The grounds were laid out in 1959–1960 to a design by architect Alar Kotli. The stage is covered by a distinctive hyperbolic paraboloid shell — an acoustic "singing arc" — that projects the sound of a choir of up to 25,000–30,000 singers toward the audience on the sloping field. A 42-metre lighthouse-like tower with a viewing platform stands beside the stage.

History

The grounds played a central symbolic role in Estonia's Singing Revolution of 1988, when mass song gatherings drew hundreds of thousands of people demanding independence from the Soviet Union. Today the venue also hosts rock and pop concerts by international artists.
