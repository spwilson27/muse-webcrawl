Egas Moniz

António Egas Moniz

Born: November 29, 1874, Avanca, Portugal

Died: December 13, 1955 (aged 81), Lisbon, Portugal

Fields: Neurology, neurosurgery

Known for: Cerebral angiography; prefrontal leucotomy (lobotomy)

Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1949)

António Caetano de Abreu Freire Egas Moniz (29 November 1874 – 13 December 1955), generally known as Egas Moniz, was a Portuguese neurologist and the developer of cerebral angiography. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern psychosurgery, having developed the surgical procedure leucotomy — better known as lobotomy — for which he received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1949, an award that later became highly controversial.

Early life and politics

Moniz was born in Avanca in northern Portugal and studied medicine at the University of Coimbra, later training in neurology in Bordeaux and Paris. Alongside medicine he pursued a political career, serving in parliament, as Portugal's ambassador to Madrid, and as minister of foreign affairs, leading the Portuguese delegation at the Paris Peace Conference of 1919.

Angiography

In the 1920s Moniz developed cerebral angiography, a technique for visualising the blood vessels of the brain by injecting radiopaque substances, which for the first time allowed the localisation of brain tumours and vascular abnormalities on X-ray images. The technique became a standard diagnostic tool for decades.

Leucotomy

In 1935, with surgeon Almeida Lima, Moniz performed the first prefrontal leucotomies, severing connections to the prefrontal cortex in patients with severe mental illness. The procedure was widely adopted internationally in modified form as the lobotomy, performed on tens of thousands of patients, before falling into disrepute because of its severe and irreversible effects on personality, and being largely abandoned with the arrival of antipsychotic drugs in the 1950s.

Later life

In 1939 Moniz was shot several times by a patient and spent the rest of his life using a wheelchair. He died in Lisbon in 1955. His house in Avanca is now the Casa-Museu Egas Moniz.
