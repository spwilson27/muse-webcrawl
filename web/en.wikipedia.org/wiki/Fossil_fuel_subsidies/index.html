Fossil fuel subsidies

Fossil fuel subsidies are energy subsidies on fossil fuels. They may be tax breaks or direct subsidies on oil, natural gas or coal, or subsidies on electricity generated from fossil fuels. They also include the failure to price in externalities such as climate change and air pollution — so-called implicit subsidies.

Estimates

Estimates differ greatly by methodology. Explicit subsidies — government support that lowers the price of fossil fuels below their supply cost — were estimated by the International Energy Agency at around US$1 trillion in 2022, a year of exceptionally high energy prices. The International Monetary Fund, counting unpriced externalities, put total subsidies in 2022 at about US$7 trillion, or roughly 7 per cent of global GDP.

Effects

Subsidies lower the cost of fossil fuel production and consumption, help keep consumer prices low — benefiting poor households who spend a large share of income on energy — but also encourage wasteful consumption, crowd out investment in renewable energy and impose large fiscal costs on governments. Analysis by the IMF suggests that phasing out subsidies, coupled with targeted transfers, could raise revenues and substantially cut global carbon dioxide emissions.

Reform efforts

In 2009 the G20 committed to phase out "inefficient" fossil fuel subsidies, a pledge repeated at subsequent summits and at COP26 in Glasgow in 2021. Progress has been uneven, and the energy price shocks of 2022 led many governments to expand consumer support measures.
