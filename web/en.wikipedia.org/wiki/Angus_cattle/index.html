Angus cattle

Angus cattle are a breed of beef cattle originating in north-east Scotland, particularly Aberdeenshire and Angus. They are naturally polled, meaning they lack horns, and are usually solid black or red. The black form is widely known as Aberdeen Angus.

Characteristics

Angus animals are valued for hardiness, calving ease, maternal ability and meat quality, especially intramuscular fat or marbling. Breeders in the nineteenth century, including Hugh Watson and William McCombie, helped standardise the type. Herdbooks were established in Scotland and later in the United States, Australia, Canada, Argentina and other beef-producing countries.

Use

The breed is used both purebred and in crossbreeding to improve carcass quality and remove horns. Branded beef programmes, notably Certified Angus Beef in the United States, market products meeting breed-influence and quality specifications.
