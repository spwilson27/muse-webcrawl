Charlottenburg Palace (German: Schloss Charlottenburg) is a Baroque palace in Berlin, the largest palace in the city and the only royal residence in Berlin to survive from the era of the Hohenzollern kings of Prussia. It is located in the Charlottenburg district, which takes its name from the palace. The palace complex includes formal gardens on the banks of the River Spree, a mausoleum, a belvedere, and a pavilion.

History

The palace was commissioned by Sophie Charlotte, wife of Elector Frederick III, who in 1701 crowned himself Frederick I, King in Prussia. The original building, designed by Johann Arnold Nering beginning in 1695, was called Lietzenburg; after Sophie Charlotte's early death in 1705, Frederick renamed it Charlottenburg in her memory. Johann Friedrich Eosander von Göthe greatly expanded the building, adding the distinctive central dome and the long side wings, creating a palace modeled in part on Versailles.

Successive monarchs altered the interiors: Frederick the Great commissioned the Rococo New Wing from Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff in the 1740s, while Frederick William II added the Neoclassical Belvedere tea house in 1788 and Frederick William III built the Mausoleum in 1810 for Queen Louise. The palace was badly damaged by bombing in 1943 but was painstakingly reconstructed after the Second World War.

The palace today

Administered by the Prussian Palaces and Gardens Foundation, the palace houses museums displaying porcelain, paintings, and the Prussian crown jewels, including Frederick the Great's snuffbox collection. Highlights include the Porcelain Cabinet, the White Hall, and the Golden Gallery. The baroque gardens, laid out in 1697 in the French style and later partly remodeled in the English landscape manner, are free to the public and among Berlin's most popular parks.
