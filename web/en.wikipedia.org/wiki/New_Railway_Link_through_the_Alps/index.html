The New Railway Link through the Alps (NRLA; German: Neue Eisenbahn-Alpentransversale, NEAT) is a Swiss infrastructure project that built fast north–south rail links across the Swiss Alps, centred on the Gotthard Base Tunnel, the Lötschberg Base Tunnel and the Ceneri Base Tunnel.

Background

Swiss voters approved the NRLA in referendums in 1992 and a financing package in 1998, funding the project largely through a heavy-vehicle fee, mineral oil tax and VAT. The policy goal was to protect the Alpine environment by moving freight traffic from trucks to trains.

Components

The Lötschberg Base Tunnel opened in 2007 on the western axis. The Gotthard Base Tunnel, at 57 km the world's longest railway tunnel, opened on 1 June 2016. The Ceneri Base Tunnel, opened in 2020, completed the flat rail route through the Alps, allowing heavy freight trains to cross with a single locomotive and cutting Zurich–Milan passenger journey times.
