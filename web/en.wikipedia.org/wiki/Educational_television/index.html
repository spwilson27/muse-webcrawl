Educational television

Educational television is television programming designed to educate its audience, including instructional broadcasts for schools, children's educational programming, documentaries, and distance-learning courses.

History

In the United States, non-commercial educational stations emerged in the 1950s after the Federal Communications Commission reserved channels for education. This developed into the Public Broadcasting Service (PBS) in 1970. In the United Kingdom, the Open University pioneered televised university courses with the BBC from 1971.

Children's programming

Programmes such as Sesame Street (from 1969) demonstrated that carefully designed television could teach literacy and numeracy to preschool children, influencing educational broadcasting worldwide. Research on such programming has generally found positive effects on school readiness.
