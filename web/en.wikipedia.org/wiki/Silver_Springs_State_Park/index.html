Silver Springs State Park, formerly known as Silver River State Park, is a Florida state park located on the Silver River in Marion County between Ocala and Silver Springs. The park comprises approximately 5,000 acres surrounding one of the largest artesian spring formations in the world.

History

The original Silver River State Park, established with land purchased by the state in 1985, protected the river's east side with trails, a pioneer cracker village, and the Silver River Museum. In October 2013, the state park merged with the historic Silver Springs attraction — Florida's oldest commercial tourist attraction, famous since the 1870s for its glass-bottom boats — when the lease of the private operator ended and the Florida Park Service took over the attraction grounds.

Description

The combined park includes the spring head and gardens of the former attraction, glass-bottom boat tours, paddling on the Silver River, hiking and equestrian trails, a campground, and the Silver River Museum and Environmental Education Center. Wildlife commonly seen includes alligators, manatees, turtles, otters, and a population of feral rhesus macaques descended from monkeys released in the 1930s by tour-boat operator Colonel Tooey.

Ecology

Silver Springs discharges from the Floridan aquifer; long-term declines in flow and rising nitrate levels have led to restoration and monitoring efforts by the state and the St. Johns River Water Management District.
