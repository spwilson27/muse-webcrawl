Power (mathematics)

In mathematics, a power is the result of exponentiation: the expression bn, read "b to the power of n", is the product of multiplying the base b by itself n times. The exponent n is also called the power to which the base is raised.

Integer powers

For a positive integer exponent, bn = b × b × … × b (n factors). By convention b1 = b and, for b ≠ 0, b0 = 1. Negative exponents give reciprocals: b−n = 1/bn. Familiar examples include squares (n = 2) and cubes (n = 3).

Rational and real powers

Fractional exponents correspond to roots: b1/n is the n-th root of b. Extending by continuity, real exponents are defined for positive bases via bx = ex ln b. Power functions of the form f(x) = xa are studied throughout algebra and calculus.

Other meanings

The term "power" is also used in several related senses in mathematics: the power set of a set is the set of all its subsets; the cardinality of a set is sometimes called its power; and a power series is an infinite series of terms involving successive powers of a variable.
