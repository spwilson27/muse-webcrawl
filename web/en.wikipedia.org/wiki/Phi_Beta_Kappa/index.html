Phi Beta Kappa

The Phi Beta Kappa Society (ΦΒΚ) is the oldest academic honor society in the United States. It was founded on December 5, 1776, at the College of William & Mary in Williamsburg, Virginia. It has chapters at 290 colleges and universities in the United States, and more than 500,000 living members. Widely considered the nation's most prestigious honor society, Phi Beta Kappa aims to promote and advocate excellence in the liberal arts and sciences, and to induct the most outstanding students of arts and sciences at selected American colleges and universities.

History

Phi Beta Kappa was founded by five students at William & Mary, led by John Heath, as a secret literary and debating society with Greek letters, a motto, and a distinctive badge. During the Revolutionary War the college closed and the founders dispersed, granting charters before they left; chapters formed at Yale (1780) and Harvard (1781). In the 19th century, amid a broader anti-secrecy movement, the Harvard chapter disclosed the society's secrets in 1831, and Phi Beta Kappa transformed into an honor society recognising academic achievement. Women were first admitted in 1875 at the University of Vermont.

Membership

Students are elected during their junior or senior year on the basis of broad academic achievement in the liberal arts and sciences, typically from the top 10 percent of their graduating class. Chapters themselves are granted only to institutions with strong liberal arts programs and are reviewed periodically. Notable members include 17 U.S. presidents, 42 Supreme Court justices, and more than 150 Nobel laureates.

Insignia and publications

The society's badge is a gold key bearing the Greek letters ΦΒΚ (for philosophia biou kubernetes, "love of learning is the guide of life"), three stars, and a pointing hand. The society publishes The American Scholar, a quarterly magazine of literature, history, science, and public affairs founded in 1932, and The Key Reporter.

Activities

Nationally, the society sponsors awards including the Phi Beta Kappa Book Awards, a Visiting Scholar Program, and advocacy for the liberal arts and sciences in American higher education.
