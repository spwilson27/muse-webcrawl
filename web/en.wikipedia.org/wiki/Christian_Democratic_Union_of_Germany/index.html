Christian Democratic Union of Germany

Christian Democratic Union (CDU)
Founded: 1945
Ideology: Christian democracy, liberal conservatism
Leader (2022–): Friedrich Merz
Sister party: CSU (Bavaria)
European affiliation: European People's Party

The Christian Democratic Union of Germany (German: Christlich Demokratische Union Deutschlands, CDU) is a Christian-democratic and conservative political party in Germany. With its Bavarian sister party, the CSU, it forms the CDU/CSU grouping ('the Union') in the Bundestag.

History

Founded after the Second World War, the CDU under Konrad Adenauer, West Germany's first chancellor (1949–1963), anchored the Federal Republic in the West and promoted the social market economy under Ludwig Erhard. The party later led governments under Kurt Georg Kiesinger, Helmut Kohl — chancellor at German reunification in 1990 — and Angela Merkel (2005–2021), Germany's first female chancellor.

Positions and organisation

The CDU supports a social market economy, European integration, and a strong transatlantic partnership. After losing power in 2021 it went into opposition; Friedrich Merz became leader in 2022 and led the Union to victory in the 2025 federal election.
