Saint Sarah

Saint Sarah, also known as Sara-la-Kâli ("Sara the Black"; Romani: Sara e Kali), is the patron saint of the Romani people. She is venerated at Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer in the Camargue region of southern France, where thousands of Romani pilgrims gather each May for her feast. The centre of her veneration is the Church of the Saintes Maries de la Mer, where her statue is kept in the crypt.

Tradition

According to Provençal tradition, Sarah was the servant (in some versions an Egyptian or a local inhabitant) who received the Three Marys — Mary Magdalene, Mary Salome and Mary of Clopas — when they arrived by boat on the Camargue coast after fleeing persecution in the Holy Land. Some versions make Sarah a charitable local woman who begged for the exiles and joined them; later Romani tradition elaborated her story, sometimes describing her as a Romani woman who guided the saints ashore.

Pilgrimage

Each year on 24–25 May, Romani families from across Europe converge on Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer. The statue of Saint Sarah is carried in procession from the crypt, through the town and down to the sea, accompanied by music, horsemen of the Camargue, and the statues of the two Marys. Devotees dress the statue in new layers of cloth and leave petitions and offerings. The pilgrimage has been documented since the nineteenth century and is one of the most significant gatherings in Romani religious life.

Interpretations

Saint Sarah is not recognised in the official Roman martyrology, and her veneration is a matter of folk devotion. Her name "the Black" and her dark statue have led some scholars to compare her with the Hindu goddess Kali — whose name she shares in Romani — possibly reflecting the Indian origins of the Romani people.
