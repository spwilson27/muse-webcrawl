Prague school

The Prague Linguistic Circle met in Prague from 1926 and reshaped modern linguistics

The Prague school, or Prague linguistic circle, was a group of linguists and literary theorists founded in Prague in 1926, centred on the Czech scholar Vilém Mathesius and including Russian émigrés such as Roman Jakobson and Nikolai Trubetzkoy, along with Bohuslav Havránek, Jan Mukařovský, and others. Drawing on the structuralism of Ferdinand de Saussure, the circle developed a functional and structural approach to language that profoundly influenced twentieth-century linguistics, phonology, and literary theory.

Ideas

The circle's programme was set out in the theses presented to the First International Congress of Slavists in 1929. Its key concepts include:

  • Language as a functional system: linguistic elements are understood by the work they do in communication, not merely by their history.
  • Phonology and markedness: Trubetzkoy's Principles of Phonology (1939) founded modern phonology, analysing sounds through oppositions and distinguishing marked from unmarked members.
  • Functional sentence perspective: Mathesius's study of theme and rheme — what an utterance is about versus what it says — which fed into later theories of information structure.
  • Centre and periphery, and the idea of language as a system in dynamic equilibrium, always tending toward stability yet perpetually changing.
  • Poetics: Mukařovský and Jakobson applied structural analysis to literature, developing ideas of foregrounding and the aesthetic function that shaped Czech structuralism and semiotics.

Legacy

Dissolved as an organisation under the Nazi occupation and the Stalinist period, the circle's ideas travelled west with Jakobson, who carried structuralism to the United States and influenced anthropology and literary studies through figures such as Claude Lévi-Strauss. Prague-school functionalism underlies much modern work on phonology, discourse analysis, and the standardisation of literary Czech, which the circle actively promoted. A revived Prague Linguistic Circle was established after 1989.
