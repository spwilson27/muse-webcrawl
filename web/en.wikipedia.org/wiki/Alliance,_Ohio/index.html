Alliance is a city in Stark and Mahoning counties in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Ohio. The population was 21,672 at the 2020 census. Alliance is known as the "Carnation City", and is home to the private University of Mount Union.

History

Alliance was formed in 1854 by the merger of three villages — Williamsport, Liberty and Freedom — near the crossing of two railroad lines; the name celebrated the junction, or "alliance", of the Cleveland and Pittsburgh Railroad and the Ohio and Pennsylvania Railroad. The town grew as a rail and manufacturing centre, producing cranes, steel products and machinery. In 1893 local physician Levi Lamborn's propagated scarlet carnation was presented to William McKinley, tying the flower to the city; the carnation later became Ohio's state flower, and Alliance celebrates an annual Carnation Festival.

Geography

The city lies in northeastern Ohio, roughly 55 miles southeast of Cleveland and west of Youngstown, on the upper Mahoning River drainage near the headwaters of the river. Downtown Alliance is in Stark County, with the eastern edge extending into Mahoning County.

Education and culture

The University of Mount Union, founded in 1846 as Mount Union College, is a prominent private liberal-arts institution whose Purple Raiders football team is a perennial power in NCAA Division III. Landmarks include the Glamorgan Castle, an eccentric mansion built in 1904–05, the Haines House, an Underground Railroad site, and the Feline Historical Museum. The city's Rodman Public Library serves the surrounding area.

Transportation

Alliance station is served by Amtrak's Capitol Limited between Chicago and Washington, D.C., and the city remains a junction for freight railroads. U.S. Route 62 and several state highways serve the area.
