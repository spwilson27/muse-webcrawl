Irish Guards

The Irish Guards (IG) is one of the Foot Guards regiments of the British Army and is part of the Guards Division. Together with the Royal Irish Regiment, it is one of the two Irish infantry regiments remaining in the British Army. The regiment was formed in April 1900 by order of Queen Victoria in recognition of the services of Irish regiments in the Second Boer War.

History

The regiment was raised on 1 April 1900. During the First World War it served on the Western Front, fighting at Mons, the Marne, Ypres, Loos and the Somme. In the Second World War the guardsmen served in Norway, North Africa and North-West Europe. More recently the regiment has deployed to Iraq and Afghanistan.

The Irish Guards' mascot is an Irish wolfhound. The regiment recruits in Northern Ireland and among Irish communities in Britain. Its motto is Quis Separabit? ("Who shall separate us?"), an order shared with the Order of St Patrick.

Role

The regiment alternates between public duties in London and operational infantry roles. Its soldiers wear the distinctive bearskin cap and a shamrock is presented to members of the Royal Family and guardsmen each St Patrick's Day.
