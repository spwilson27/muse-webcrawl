Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Pashto: خیبر پښتونخوا; Urdu: خیبر پختونخوا), often abbreviated KP or KPK and formerly known as the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP), is a province of Pakistan in the country's northwest. Its capital and largest city is Peshawar. The province borders Afghanistan to the west and north and contains the historic Khyber Pass; in 2018 the Federally Administered Tribal Areas were merged into it. It is the third-largest province by population, home to roughly 40 million people (2023 census), predominantly ethnic Pashtuns.

Geography

The province ranges from the high mountains of the Hindu Kush and Karakoram in the north — including the Chitral and Swat valleys — to the fertile Peshawar basin and the arid hills of the south. The Indus River forms part of its eastern boundary, and the Tarbela Dam, one of the world's largest earth-filled dams, stands on the Indus in the province.

History

The region was part of ancient Gandhara and was ruled in turn by Persians, Greeks, Mauryas, Kushans, Mughals and Durrani Afghans before Sikh and then British annexation in the 19th century. Britain administered it as the North-West Frontier Province from 1901, while the tribal agencies along the Durand Line were governed indirectly. After 1947 the province joined Pakistan; the NWFP was renamed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa by the 18th Amendment in 2010 to reflect its Pashtun identity. The province was heavily affected by the Afghan wars, hosting millions of Afghan refugees, and by militancy and military operations in the 2000s–2010s. In May 2018 the tribal areas (FATA) were formally merged into the province.

Economy

Agriculture, remittances, marble, gems, tourism in the northern valleys, and hydropower are major economic activities. Peshawar is the commercial hub, connected by motorway to Islamabad and by the historic Grand Trunk Road.
