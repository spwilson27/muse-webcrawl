Malcolm IV of Scotland

Reign: 24 May 1153 – 9 December 1165

Predecessor: David I

Successor: William I (the Lion)

Born: 23 April – 24 May 1141

Died: 9 December 1165, Jedburgh

Malcolm IV (Scottish Gaelic: Máel Coluim mac Eanric; 1141 – 9 December 1165), known in later tradition as Malcolm the Maiden, was King of Scots from 1153 until his death. The grandson and successor of David I, he came to the throne at the age of twelve.

Reign

Malcolm's accession faced immediate challenges: Donald MacHeth claimed the throne with support from Somerled, lord of Argyll, whose fleet invaded the Clyde in 1153. The MacHeth rebellion was not fully suppressed until 1157, and Somerled remained a power in the west until his death invading Scotland in 1164.

In 1157 Malcolm met Henry II of England at Chester and surrendered the northern English counties of Northumberland, Cumberland, and Westmorland that his grandfather had held, receiving instead the earldom of Huntingdon and swearing fealty to Henry for it. The concession angered many Scots, and revolts in Galloway, Moray, and the west punctuated the reign alongside the continuing "Davidian" reforms of burghs, sheriffdoms, and reformed monastic orders that Malcolm maintained.

Malcolm served Henry II in France in 1159, being knighted by him at Toulouse, and campaigned in Wales. Unmarried and in chronically poor health, he died at Jedburgh in December 1165, aged twenty-four, and was buried at Dunfermline Abbey. He was succeeded by his brother William the Lion.

Reputation

The epithet "the Maiden" reflected his chastity and youthful piety; contemporary chroniclers praised his devotion while sometimes faulting his generosity to the English king. Modern historians view him as a capable continuator of David I's programme under difficult circumstances.
