Tahiti

Tahiti is the largest island in French Polynesia's Windward group, an autonomous overseas collectivity of France in the South Pacific, located in the Society Islands archipelago. It is formed from two volcanic mountain ranges joined at the isthmus of Taravao. Its capital, Papeete, is also the capital of French Polynesia; the island's population is about 190,000.

Tahiti became world-famous in Europe through the voyages of Wallis (1767), Bougainville (1768) and Cook (1769), whose accounts of a sensuous paradise — and the mutiny on the Bounty (1789) off its shores — shaped Western imagination. Paul Gauguin painted his most famous works here in the 1890s. The island is the centre of French Polynesia's economy and its black-pearl and vanilla trades; nearby Moorea and Bora Bora (technically separate islands) complete the classic image of the South Seas.
