Liverpool Street station, also known as London Liverpool Street, is a major central London railway terminus and connected London Underground station in the north-eastern corner of the City of London, in the ward of Bishopsgate Without. It is the terminus of the Great Eastern Main Line to Norwich, the West Anglia Main Line to Cambridge, and local and regional services across north-east London and the East of England, including the Stansted Express airport link. With the opening of the Elizabeth line platforms in 2022 it became one of the busiest stations in Britain.

History

The station was built by the Great Eastern Railway on the site of the Bethlem Royal Hospital (the original "Bedlam") and opened in stages in 1874–1875, designed by the railway's engineer Edward Wilson with a Gothic Revival trainshed and frontage buildings. The adjoining Underground station on the Metropolitan and Circle lines had opened in 1875, with Central line platforms following in 1912 and Northern City line platforms thereafter. Liverpool Street is remembered as the arrival point of the Kindertransport in 1938–39, when some 10,000 mainly Jewish children fleeing Nazi persecution reached Britain; a memorial sculpture, Für das Kind, stands outside the station. The station was extensively remodelled between 1985 and 1992 in a scheme that unified the two trainsheds and added the Broadgate office development above, while retaining the Victorian façade. Deep-level Elizabeth line platforms beneath the station opened in May 2022, linked to Moorgate.
