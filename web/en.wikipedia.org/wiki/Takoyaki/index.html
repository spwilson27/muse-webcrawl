Takoyaki

Takoyaki (たこ焼き)

Place of originJapan (Osaka)
Created1935, attributed to Tomekichi Endo
Serving temp.Hot
Main ingredientsWheat batter, octopus, tempura scraps (tenkasu), pickled ginger, green onion

Takoyaki (たこ焼き or 蛸焼) is a ball-shaped Japanese snack made of a wheat-flour-based batter cooked in a special moulded pan. It is typically filled with minced or diced octopus (tako), tempura scraps (tenkasu), pickled ginger and green onion. The balls are brushed with takoyaki sauce (similar to Worcestershire sauce) and mayonnaise, then sprinkled with green laver (aonori) and shavings of dried bonito (katsuobushi). It is a signature street food of Osaka.

History

Takoyaki was invented in Osaka in 1935 by street vendor Tomekichi Endo, building on an earlier battered snack called radioyaki (filled with beef and konjac) and the egg-rich akashiyaki from nearby Akashi. Endo's substitution of octopus, abundant in local waters, proved popular, and the dish spread across the Kansai region and then throughout Japan, becoming one of the country's most recognisable festival and street foods.

Preparation

A seasoned wheat batter is poured into a cast-iron pan with hemispherical moulds. A piece of octopus and the other fillings are added to each mould, and the cook uses picks to turn the balls continuously, forming crisp, round exteriors around soft, creamy centres. A serving typically consists of six or eight balls on a small boat-shaped tray, eaten with toothpicks or chopsticks. Dedicated takoyaki pans are also common household items in the Kansai region, and make-your-own takoyaki parties are a popular social activity.

Variations and culture

Fillings and toppings vary widely, including cheese, shrimp, sausage and mochi, and regional versions differ in texture: Akashi's akashiyaki is softer and dipped in dashi broth. In Osaka, takoyaki is so emblematic that it features on souvenirs, mascots and even a dedicated museum on the Universal Studios Japan entertainment district.
