Billy Graham

William Franklin Graham Jr. (November 7, 1918 – February 21, 2018) was an American evangelist and an ordained Southern Baptist minister who became well known internationally in the late 1940s. He was a prominent evangelical Christian figure and, according to one biographer, was "among the most influential Christian leaders of the 20th century."

Billy Graham
Born: 7 November 1918, Charlotte, North Carolina
Died: 21 February 2018, Montreat, North Carolina
Occupation: Evangelist
Spouse: Ruth Bell Graham

Ministry

Graham held large indoor and outdoor rallies called crusades from 1947 until his retirement in 2005, preaching to live audiences totalling nearly 215 million people in more than 185 countries, and to many more via radio and television. He founded the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association (BGEA) in 1950.

Public life

He was a spiritual adviser to many US presidents, from Harry Truman to Barack Obama, supported civil rights by insisting on integrated seating at revivals, and helped popularise evangelical Christianity worldwide. His honours included the Presidential Medal of Freedom (1983).

Later life

Graham died at his home in Montreat, North Carolina, aged 99, and lay in honour in the US Capitol rotunda.
