Gatling gun

TypeRapid-fire multi-barrel weapon
DesignerRichard Jordan Gatling
Designed1861; patented 1862
Feed systemGravity hopper / drum magazine

The Gatling gun is a rapid-fire multiple-barrel firearm invented in 1861 by Richard Jordan Gatling. It was the first successful machine gun concept to see military service: turning a hand crank rotates a cluster of barrels, each of which is loaded, fired and unloaded in sequence, allowing sustained rates of fire far beyond those of single-shot rifles while distributing heat and wear among the barrels.

Design

Early models had six to ten barrels arranged around a central shaft. As the crank turns, a cam mechanism drives each bolt forward to chamber a cartridge, fires it at the front of the cycle, and extracts the spent case as rotation continues. Ammunition was fed by gravity from a hopper or top-mounted magazine. Rates of fire rose from about 200 rounds per minute in the Civil War-era guns to some 1,200 rounds per minute in improved models with electric or mechanical drives.

History

Gatling demonstrated his gun during the American Civil War; a few were purchased privately by Union commanders and used at Petersburg, and the U.S. Army formally adopted it in 1866. Gatling guns saw service in the Spanish–American War — notably supporting the assault on San Juan Hill in 1898 — and were used by many armies and navies in colonial campaigns. The type was eclipsed after 1900 by recoil- and gas-operated single-barrel machine guns such as the Maxim.

Modern descendants

The rotary principle was revived in the mid-20th century with externally powered electric motors: the M61 Vulcan 20 mm cannon, the GAU-8 Avenger and the M134 Minigun all use the Gatling configuration, achieving rates of up to 6,000 rounds per minute in aircraft, naval close-in weapon systems and helicopter armament.
