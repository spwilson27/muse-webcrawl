Argyria

Argyria
Specialty: Dermatology
Symptoms: Blue-grey discoloration of the skin
Causes: Exposure to silver compounds

Argyria or argyrosis is a condition caused by excessive exposure to chemical compounds of the element silver, or to silver dust. The most dramatic symptom of argyria is that the skin becomes blue or blue-grey coloured. It may take the form of generalized argyria or local argyria. Argyria is a form of heavy metal poisoning related to the element silver.

Causes

Argyria occurs in people who ingest or inhale silver in large quantities over a long period (several months to many years). People who work in factories that manufacture silver can also breathe in silver or its compounds. Historically, colloidal silver preparations were widely used as medicines, and their prolonged use was a common cause of the condition. In recent decades, argyria has most often been reported in association with the ingestion of colloidal silver dietary supplements.

Pathophysiology

Silver is deposited in the skin and other tissues, particularly in the basement membranes of sweat glands, the dermis, and around hair follicles. In the skin the deposited silver granules react with sunlight in a process similar to that used in photography, producing the characteristic slate-grey or blue-grey pigmentation. The discoloration is most marked on sun-exposed areas such as the face and hands.

Treatment and prognosis

Argyria is generally irreversible, although laser therapy and dermabrasion have been reported to improve the cosmetic appearance in some cases. The discoloration itself is not usually associated with serious internal disease in otherwise healthy people, though very high silver burdens can affect the kidneys and nervous system. Prevention consists of avoiding excessive silver exposure, including the consumption of colloidal silver products, which health authorities do not recognise as safe or effective for treating any disease.

Notable cases

One widely publicised modern case was Paul Karason, an American man whose skin turned blue after years of ingesting colloidal silver and applying silver preparations to his face; he was frequently referred to in the media as "Papa Smurf". Historically, argyria was observed among silversmiths and workers in silver mines.
