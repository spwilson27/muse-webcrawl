Spirograph

Spirograph is a geometric drawing device that produces mathematical roulette curves of the variety technically known as hypotrochoids and epitrochoids. It consists of plastic rings and toothed wheels (gears) with holes for a pen; a smaller wheel rolls around inside or outside a fixed ring, and the pen traces intricate, symmetric patterns on paper held beneath.

History

Devices for drawing such curves predate the toy: the mathematician and instrument maker Bruno Abdank-Abakanowicz developed curve-drawing instruments in the late nineteenth century, and geometric lathes were used to engrave security patterns (guilloche) on banknotes. The Spirograph as a toy was developed by British engineer Denys Fisher, introduced at the 1965 International Toy Fair in Nuremberg, and marketed by his company; Kenner acquired the United States rights in 1966, where it became a best-selling toy and won major toy-industry awards. Kahootz Toys relaunched the brand in 2013.

Mathematics

The curves drawn with the wheel inside the ring are hypotrochoids, described parametrically by the ring radius R, the wheel radius r, and the distance d of the pen hole from the wheel's centre. The pattern repeats with a period determined by the ratio of the numbers of teeth on the ring and wheel; the curves are closely related to hypotrochoids, epitrochoids, and the patterns of harmonographs.
