The Carnival of Venice

"The Carnival of Venice" (Il Carnevale di Venezia) is a traditional folk tune from Naples, Italy, based on the Neapolitan song "O mamma, mamma cara." It became famous as a showpiece for virtuoso variations, most notably in Niccolò Paganini's variations for violin, Op. 10, composed in 1829.

The tune

The melody is a simple, lilting 6/8 tune long associated with the carnival season in Venice, a festival of masks and licence preceding Lent. Its catchy, repetitive character made it an ideal vehicle for displays of instrumental virtuosity, and it was taken up by performers across Europe in the nineteenth century.

Paganini's variations

Paganini's Il Carnevale di Venezia, Op. 10, presents the theme followed by a series of variations exploiting harmonics, left-hand pizzicato, rapid bowing and other hallmarks of his technique. The piece became one of his signature works and helped cement the tune's international fame.

Other settings

The Danish composer J. P. E. Hartmann and, later, the French cornet virtuoso Jean-Baptiste Arban wrote celebrated variations on the theme; Arban's version is a staple of the trumpet and cornet repertoire. The tune also appears in popular culture — its melody is shared with the children's song "My Hat, It Has Three Corners" and the American folk song "The Hat My Father Wore."
