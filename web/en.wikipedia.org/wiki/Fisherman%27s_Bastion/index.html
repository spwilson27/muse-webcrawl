Fisherman's Bastion
LocationCastle Hill, Budapest, Hungary
Built1895–1902
ArchitectFrigyes Schulek
StyleNeo-Gothic / Neo-Romanesque

The Fisherman's Bastion (Hungarian: Halászbástya) is one of the best known monuments in Budapest, located near Buda Castle in the Várkerület (Castle District). It is one of the most important tourist attractions due to the unique panorama of Pest and the Danube that can be seen from its Neo-Romanesque terraces.

History

The Bastion was built between 1895 and 1902, on the plans of architect Frigyes Schulek, as part of the celebrations of the 1000th birthday of the Hungarian state. Its seven towers represent the seven Magyar tribes that founded Hungary in 895. The monument takes its name from the guild of fishermen who defended this part of the city walls in the Middle Ages, and from the fish market that stood nearby in medieval times.

Description

The structure is built in white stone with stairways, arcades and balustrades in a mix of Neo-Gothic and Neo-Romanesque styles. Between the Bastion and Matthias Church stands an equestrian statue of Stephen I of Hungary, erected in 1906. The Bastion was badly damaged in the Second World War and was restored in the following decades; further renovations took place in the 21st century.
