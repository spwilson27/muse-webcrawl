ITU prefix

The ITU prefix is the set of letters and/or digits at the beginning of radio call signs assigned by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) to each country. The ITU allocates blocks of call sign prefixes — officially "international series of call signs" — and national administrations assign individual call signs within their allocated blocks. From the prefix, a listener can identify the country (and often the region) of an amateur radio, broadcast, maritime, or aeronautical station.

Allocation

Prefixes are allocated in the ITU Radio Regulations as blocks such as AAA–ALZ (United States), GAA–GZZ (United Kingdom), and DAA–DRZ (Germany). Because historical demand exceeded the supply of two-letter combinations, allocations mix letters and digits, e.g. 2AA–2ZZ for the United Kingdom or 3DA–3DZ for Eswatini. Some series are also reserved for special purposes, such as the Q codes used in radio procedure (not call signs).

Examples

Well-known prefixes include K, N, W, and AA–AL for the United States; VE and VA for Canada; JA–JS for Japan; VK for Australia; F for France; and I for Italy. Amateur operators often collect contacts by prefix as part of awards such as the Worked All Prefixes (WPX) award.

History

Informal prefixes were used by operators before the First World War; they were internationalized beginning with the 1912 London Radiotelegraph Convention and progressively codified at later conferences, with the modern table taking shape after 1947.
