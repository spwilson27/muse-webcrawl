The Fender Esquire is a solid-body electric guitar manufactured by the Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. Introduced in 1950, it was the first solid-body Spanish-style electric guitar to be produced in quantity and is the single-pickup sibling of the two-pickup Telecaster.

History

Designed by Leo Fender and George Fullerton, the Esquire was shown in Fender's 1950 catalogue and shipped that spring. Early examples had pine bodies and no truss rod. Within a year the two-pickup version, first called the Broadcaster and then the Telecaster, overshadowed it, but the Esquire remained in the catalogue until 1970 and has been reissued repeatedly.

Electronics

Despite having only a bridge pickup, the Esquire's three-way switch gives three distinct voices: a bright setting bypassing the tone control, a conventional setting with the tone control active, and a dark preset with a fixed capacitor that rolls off treble, intended to imitate a bass. Famous players include Bruce Springsteen, whose modified 1953 Esquire appears on the cover of Born to Run, and Jeff Beck, who played a 1954 Esquire with the Yardbirds.
