The Calder Memorial Trophy is an annual award given by the National Hockey League (NHL) to the player selected as the most proficient in his first year of competition — the league's rookie of the year. It is voted on by members of the Professional Hockey Writers' Association at the end of the regular season.

The trophy is named for Frank Calder, the first president of the NHL (1917–1943), who presented a rookie award annually from 1932–33; after his death the league made the Calder Memorial Trophy permanent.

Eligibility

To qualify, a player cannot have played more than 25 games in any single preceding season, nor six or more games in two preceding seasons, in any major professional league, and must not have reached age 26 by September 15 of the season. The age rule was added after 31-year-old Sergei Makarov won in 1990.

Notable winners

Winners include Terry Sawchuk, Bobby Orr, Wayne Gretzky (ineligible in 1979 due to his WHA season — he never won it), Mario Lemieux, Martin Brodeur, Alex Ovechkin, Auston Matthews, and Connor Bedard. The Toronto Maple Leafs have produced the most winners.

Selection

Writers submit ranked ballots, and the trophy is presented at the NHL Awards ceremony. The winner receives the trophy, and each NHL team also selects its own rookie honourees, though only one Calder is awarded league-wide.
