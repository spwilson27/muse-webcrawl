Golden Globe Race

The Sunday Times Golden Globe Race was a non-stop, single-handed, round-the-world yacht race held in 1968–69, the first of its kind. Sponsored by the British Sunday Times newspaper, it offered a trophy for the first finisher and a £5,000 prize for the fastest. Of nine entrants, only one completed the course.

The race

Inspired by Francis Chichester's one-stop circumnavigation of 1966–67, the race allowed entrants to start from any port between June and October 1968 and sail around the world via the great capes, without outside assistance. Robin Knox-Johnston, sailing the 32-foot Suhaili, completed the voyage in 312 days, arriving at Falmouth on 22 April 1969 — the sole finisher and winner of both prizes.

Drama and tragedy

The race is remembered for its extraordinary human stories. Donald Crowhurst, whose trimaran was unprepared, faked his position reports while drifting in the Atlantic; descending into mental breakdown, he is believed to have taken his own life, his logbooks revealing his deception. French star Bernard Moitessier, in a position to win, rejected the commercialism of the race, turned away from the finish and sailed on to Tahiti — "because I am happy at sea." Others, including Nigel Tetley, whose trimaran broke up while he led, were forced to retire.

Legacy

The race founded the sport of solo ocean racing; its modern successors include the Vendée Globe. A retro revival, the Golden Globe Race, was held in 2018 and 2022, restricting entrants to 1968-era technology and long-keeled production yachts, starting and finishing at Les Sables-d'Olonne, France.
