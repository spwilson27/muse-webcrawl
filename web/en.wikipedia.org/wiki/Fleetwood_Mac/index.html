Fleetwood Mac

Fleetwood Mac are a British-American rock band formed in London in 1967 by guitarist and singer Peter Green. Green named the band by combining the surnames of drummer Mick Fleetwood and bassist John McVie, who have remained with the band throughout its many line-up changes.

History

The band began as a British blues outfit, achieving a UK number-one single with the instrumental "Albatross" in 1968. After Green's departure in 1970, the band underwent several changes of personnel, gradually moving toward a more pop-oriented sound. Christine McVie joined in 1970, and in 1975 the band recruited American duo Lindsey Buckingham and Stevie Nicks.

The new line-up's self-titled 1975 album reached number one in the United States, and its follow-up, Rumours (1977), became one of the best-selling albums of all time, with over 40 million copies sold worldwide. Rumours produced four US top-ten singles — "Go Your Own Way", "Dreams", "Don't Stop", and "You Make Loving Fun" — and won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year.

Subsequent albums included Tusk (1979), Mirage (1982), and Tango in the Night (1987). Buckingham left in 1987 and Nicks in 1991, but the classic line-up reunited in 1997 for the live album The Dance. Christine McVie departed in 1998 and returned in 2014. Buckingham was dismissed in 2018 and replaced by Mike Campbell and Neil Finn. Christine McVie died in November 2022, after which Fleetwood stated the band was effectively finished.

Legacy

Fleetwood Mac have sold more than 120 million records worldwide, making them one of the best-selling bands of all time. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1998 and received the Brit Award for Outstanding Contribution to the British Music Industry the same year.
