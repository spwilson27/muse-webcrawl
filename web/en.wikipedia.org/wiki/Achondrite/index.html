Achondrite

An achondrite is a stony meteorite that does not contain chondrules. It consists of material similar to terrestrial basalts or plutonic rocks and has been differentiated and reprocessed to a lesser or greater degree due to melting and recrystallisation on or inside meteorite parent bodies. As a result, achondrites have distinct textures and mineralogies indicating igneous processes.

Classification

Achondrites comprise about 8% of meteorites overall. They are divided into primitive achondrites (such as acapulcoites and lodranites, which retain some chemical features of chondrites) and evolved achondrites formed on differentiated bodies with a core, mantle and crust. Major groups include the HED meteorites (howardites, eucrites and diogenites), believed to come from the asteroid 4 Vesta, and the shergottites, nakhlites and chassignites (SNC meteorites) from Mars, as well as lunar meteorites.

Origins

Because they derive from melted parent bodies, achondrites record the geological history of planetesimals and planets. Oxygen isotope ratios and trapped gases allow many groups to be matched to their source: SNC meteorites contain gases identical in composition to the Martian atmosphere measured by the Viking landers.
