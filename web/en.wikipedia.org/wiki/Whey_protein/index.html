Whey protein

Whey protein is a mixture of proteins isolated from whey, the liquid material created as a by-product of cheese production. The proteins consist of α-lactalbumin, β-lactoglobulin, serum albumin and immunoglobulins. Whey protein is commonly marketed as a dietary supplement, typically sold in powdered form for mixing into drinks.

Production

After cheese curds are separated, the remaining liquid whey is pasteurised and dried, or fractionated by membrane filtration. Whey protein concentrate is typically 70–80% protein, with the balance lactose and fat; whey protein isolate is processed to exceed 90% protein with minimal lactose and fat; hydrolysate is pre-digested into peptides for faster absorption. Whey accounts for roughly 20% of the protein in milk, casein making up the other 80%.

Uses and evidence

Whey protein is popular among athletes and bodybuilders because it is a complete protein rich in the branched-chain amino acid leucine, which stimulates muscle protein synthesis. Research supports a modest benefit for muscle mass and strength when combined with resistance training and sufficient total protein intake. Whey is also used in infant formula, clinical nutrition and as a functional food ingredient. Possible adverse effects are mainly gastrointestinal, particularly in people with lactose intolerance or milk allergy; health claims about many other conditions remain unproven.
