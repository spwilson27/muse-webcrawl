Ethernet crossover cable

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An Ethernet crossover cable is a type of twisted pair Ethernet cable used to connect computing devices together directly, where normally one of the devices would connect through an intermediary such as a network switch. The crossover cable reverses the transmit and receive pairs, so that the output of one device is wired to the input of the other and vice versa.

Overview

The 10BASE-T and 100BASE-TX Ethernet standards were designed so that one device (an MDI, such as a computer's network interface) transmits on one pair of wires and receives on another, while an intermediary device (an MDI-X, such as a switch port) has the pairs reversed internally. When two like devices are connected, a crossover cable performs the necessary reversal in the cable itself. Most modern equipment implements Auto MDI-X, automatically detecting and adjusting to the cable type, which has made dedicated crossover cables largely unnecessary.

Pinouts

A crossover cable for 10/100 Mbit/s Ethernet is typically wired to the T568A standard at one end and T568B at the other, which swaps the orange and green pairs:

PinT568AT568B
1white/greenwhite/orange
2greenorange
3white/orangewhite/green
6orangegreen

Gigabit Ethernet (1000BASE-T) uses all four pairs bidirectionally, so a full crossover also swaps the blue and brown pairs, although automatic pair-swapping in gigabit PHYs usually makes this moot.

Other crossover cables

The crossover concept also applies to other interfaces: null modem cables cross the transmit and receive lines of RS-232 serial connections, and crossover variants exist for other signalling standards.
