OPS+

OPS+ is a baseball statistic that combines a player's on-base percentage (OBP) and slugging percentage (SLG) — together known as OPS — and adjusts the result for the player's league and ballpark, scaling it so that 100 represents the league average. A player with an OPS+ of 120 has produced at a rate 20 percent better than league average after park adjustment, while 80 is 20 percent below average.

Calculation

The formula is OPS+ = 100 × [(OBP / league OBP) + (SLG / league SLG) − 1], with the league figures adjusted for the player's home park using park factors. Unlike raw OPS, OPS+ allows fairer comparison across eras and stadiums: because the league averages are built into the measure, a 130 OPS+ in a low-scoring era represents the same dominance relative to peers as a 130 in a high-scoring era.

Interpretation

An OPS+ around 100 is average; 110–120 marks an above-average regular; 140 and above is All-Star calibre; and the greatest seasons in history exceed 200 — Barry Bonds reached 268 in 2002, and Babe Ruth's career OPS+ of 206 is the highest of all time. The statistic is published by Baseball Reference and is widely used in modern player evaluation, though it weights OBP and SLG equally whereas analysis suggests OBP contributes somewhat more to run scoring.
