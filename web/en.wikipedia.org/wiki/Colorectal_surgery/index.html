Colorectal surgery

Colorectal surgery is a field in medicine dealing with disorders of the rectum, anus, and colon. The field is also known as proctology, but this term is now used infrequently in medicine and is most often employed to identify practices relating to the anus and rectum in particular.

Colorectal surgeons are medical doctors who specialize in the diagnosis and treatment of diseases of the lower digestive tract, including colorectal cancer, diverticular disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and anorectal conditions.

Scope of practice

Colorectal surgical disorders include:

  • Varicosities or swelling, and inflammation of veins in the rectum and anus (hemorrhoids)
  • Unnatural cracks or tears in the anus (anal fissures)
  • Abnormal connections or passages between the rectum or other anorectal area to the skin surface (fistulas)
  • Severe constipation conditions
  • Fecal incontinence
  • Protrusion of the walls of the rectum through the anus (rectal prolapse)
  • Birth defects such as imperforate anus
  • Treatment of severe colic disorders, such as Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis
  • Cancer of the colon and rectum
  • Diverticulitis

Procedures

Common procedures include colectomy (removal of part or all of the colon), hemicolectomy, proctectomy, colostomy and ileostomy formation, hemorrhoidectomy, and sphincter-preserving rectal cancer operations such as low anterior resection. Many operations are now performed laparoscopically or with robotic assistance, reducing recovery times. Diagnostic work includes colonoscopy, anorectal manometry, and endorectal ultrasound.

Training

In the United States, colorectal surgery is a subspecialty requiring completion of a general surgery residency followed by a fellowship, with certification by the American Board of Colon and Rectal Surgery. In the United Kingdom, colorectal surgery is a subspecialty of general surgery within the NHS consultant training pathway.
