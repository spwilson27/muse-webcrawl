The Politburo Standing Committee (PSC) is the highest decision-making body of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and, in practice, of China itself. Its members — five to nine top leaders, seven since 2012 — each oversee a major portfolio (party affairs, government, legislature, discipline inspection, ideology, and so on) and collectively decide all major state policy. Membership is formally elected by the CCP Central Committee but in reality determined through elite bargaining before each five-yearly Party Congress.

The Standing Committee introduced to the press after a Party Congress.

Composition and ranking

Members are ranked in strict protocol order. The current committee, elected at the 20th Party Congress in October 2022, comprises Xi Jinping (General Secretary, state president, and military commission chairman), Li Qiang (premier), Zhao Leji (legislature chairman), Wang Huning (CPPCC chairman), Cai Qi (Secretariat first secretary), Ding Xuexiang (executive vice-premier), and Li Xi (discipline inspection chief).

Functioning

The PSC meets regularly, conventionally weekly, and decisions are nominally by consensus. Since the reform era, unwritten norms — retirement ages and term limits for the state presidency — constrained tenure, but the abolition of presidential term limits in 2018 and the composition of the 2022 committee signalled a decisive centralisation under Xi Jinping, now in a third term.
