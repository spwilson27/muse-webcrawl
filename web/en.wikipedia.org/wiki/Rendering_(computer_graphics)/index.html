Rendering (computer graphics)

Rendering
TypeImage synthesis from a 3D or 2D model
Main approachesRasterization, ray tracing, path tracing
UsesFilm, video games, architecture, simulation, design visualisation

Rendering or image synthesis is the process of generating a photorealistic or non-photorealistic image from a 2D or 3D model by means of a computer program. The model is a description of three-dimensional objects in a strictly defined language or data structure, including geometry, viewpoint, textures, lighting and shading information. The resulting image is a digital image or raster graphics image.

Approaches

Rasterization

Rasterization projects 3D geometry onto the image plane triangle by triangle and determines, for each pixel, which surface is visible (typically using a z-buffer). It is extremely fast and underlies real-time graphics in video games, usually computed on a graphics processing unit (GPU). Shading models such as Phong shading approximate the interaction of light with surfaces.

Ray tracing

Ray tracing instead follows rays of light from the camera through each pixel into the scene, computing intersections and recursively tracing reflected and refracted rays. It naturally produces accurate shadows, reflections and refractions, but at greater computational cost.

Path tracing and global illumination

Path tracing extends ray tracing by sampling many random light paths per pixel, solving the rendering equation (formulated by James Kajiya in 1986) by Monte Carlo integration. It simulates global illumination effects such as colour bleeding, caustics and soft shadows, and is the standard for high-end film production, often combined with denoising.

The rendering pipeline

A typical pipeline prepares scene geometry, applies materials and textures, positions cameras and lights, computes the image, and composites the result (often in layers or passes). Pre-rendered graphics may take seconds to hours per frame on render farms; real-time rendering must sustain 30–120 frames per second.

Software

Well-known renderers include RenderMan, Arnold, V-Ray, Cycles (in Blender), and the real-time engines Unreal Engine and Unity. Application programming interfaces such as OpenGL, DirectX and Vulkan mediate GPU rendering.
