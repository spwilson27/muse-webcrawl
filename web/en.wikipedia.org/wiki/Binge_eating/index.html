Binge eating

Binge eating involves consuming a large amount of food in a short period with a sense of loss of control.

Binge eating is a pattern of disordered eating characterised by consuming a large amount of food in a discrete period of time while experiencing a sense of loss of control over eating. Episodes are typically accompanied by eating more rapidly than normal, eating until uncomfortably full, eating when not physically hungry, eating alone because of embarrassment, and feelings of disgust, guilt or depression afterwards.

Binge eating disorder

When binge eating occurs recurrently — on average at least once a week for three months — and is not regularly accompanied by the compensatory behaviours (such as purging) seen in bulimia nervosa, it is classified in the DSM-5 as binge eating disorder (BED). BED is the most common eating disorder in the United States, affecting an estimated 2–3% of adults, and is associated with obesity, metabolic syndrome, depression and anxiety.

Causes

The causes are multifactorial: genetic predisposition, dieting and weight stigma, psychological traits such as negative affect and impulsivity, and environmental cues all contribute. Binge eating is not simply a lack of willpower; brain reward circuitry and stress responses are implicated.

Treatment

First-line treatment is psychological therapy, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), which helps patients regularise eating, challenge food- and shape-related thoughts, and manage triggers. Interpersonal psychotherapy and dialectical behaviour therapy are alternatives. The medication lisdexamfetamine is approved in some countries for moderate-to-severe BED in adults, and antidepressants may help some patients.
