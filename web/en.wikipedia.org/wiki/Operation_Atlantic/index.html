Operation Atlantic

Date: 18–20 July 1944

Location: Caen, Normandy, France

Part of: Battle for Caen, Second World War

Belligerents: Canada vs. Germany

Result: Partial Canadian success

Operation Atlantic (18–20 July 1944) was an offensive undertaken by the II Canadian Corps during the Battle of Caen in the Normandy campaign of the Second World War. The operation, launched in conjunction with the British Operation Goodwood, was intended to capture the remaining German-held areas of Caen on the south bank of the Orne River and secure the suburbs of Colombelles and Faubourg de Vaucelles.

Background

Caen had been a D-Day objective on 6 June 1944 but remained partly in German hands for over a month. After Operation Charnwood (8–9 July) captured the city north of the Orne, Canadian forces under Lieutenant-General Guy Simonds were tasked with clearing the southern bank as part of a broader push to draw German armoured reserves away from the American sector in preparation for Operation Cobra.

The operation

The 2nd Canadian Infantry Division attacked Faubourg de Vaucelles, while the 3rd Canadian Infantry Division was to seize Colombelles and the high ground beyond. The assault began on 18 July following heavy artillery and air support. Canadian troops cleared Vaucelles and Colombelles after hard fighting, but attacks toward the Bourguébus–Verrières ridge were checked by German defences, including elements of Waffen-SS formations, in difficult weather that turned the ground to mud on 20 July.

Aftermath

Operation Atlantic secured the southern bank of the Orne and completed the capture of Caen, at significant Canadian cost, but the ridge positions remained in German hands and were the objective of the subsequent Operation Spring (25 July), one of the costliest Canadian actions of the campaign.
