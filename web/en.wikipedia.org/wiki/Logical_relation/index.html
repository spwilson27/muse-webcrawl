Logical relation

A logical relation is a family of relations between terms (or values) of a programming language, indexed by types, used in the theory of programming languages to prove properties of programs by induction on their types. Logical relations were introduced by Gordon Plotkin in the 1970s, building on the concept of logical predicates used by J. Howard for the Curry–Howard correspondence, and were developed further by John Reynolds in his work on parametric polymorphism.

Definition

Given two terms M and M' of a typed language, one defines a relation at each type by recursion on the type's structure. At a base type (such as integers or booleans), the relation typically says the terms evaluate to equal — or otherwise observably equivalent — values. At a function type σ → τ, two functions f and f' are related if they map related arguments at type σ to related results at type τ. The fundamental lemma (or basic lemma) states that every well-typed term is related to itself; consequences such as program equivalence, representation independence and termination then follow by choosing the base relations appropriately.

Applications

  • Parametricity — Reynolds's abstraction theorem: logical relations prove that polymorphic programs behave uniformly for all types, yielding "free theorems" (Wadler) about functions from their types alone.
  • Representation independence — showing that clients of an abstract data type cannot distinguish between two implementations.
  • Compiler correctness and program equivalence — proving that source and compiled programs, or two optimisations, are contextually equivalent.
  • Termination and type soundness — defining logical predicates over a single program to show it cannot diverge or get stuck.
  • Step-indexed logical relations — used to handle recursive types, higher-order state and general references, where the relation is additionally indexed by a number of computation steps.

Relation to other concepts

Logical relations generalise the technique of strong-normalisation proofs by Tait (reducibility candidates) for the simply typed lambda calculus. In modern mechanised proofs (for example in the Coq and Agda proof assistants), logical relations and their step-indexed variants are a standard tool in programming-language semantics, including large developments such as verified compilers and foundational type soundness proofs.
