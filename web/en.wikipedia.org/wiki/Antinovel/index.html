Antinovel
GenreExperimental fiction
Associated movementNouveau roman

An antinovel (French: anti-roman) is a type of experimental fiction that deliberately avoids or subverts the conventional elements of the novel — plot, character development, narrative chronology and psychological depth.

History

The term was used by Jean-Paul Sartre in his 1948 preface to Nathalie Sarraute's Portrait of a Man Unknown. The form is closely associated with the French nouveau roman of the 1950s, practised by writers such as Alain Robbe-Grillet, Sarraute, Michel Butor and Claude Simon, who emphasised objects, surfaces and perception over story.

Characteristics

Antinovels may fragment narrative, repeat scenes with variations, refuse resolution, or dwell minutely on physical detail, challenging the reader's expectations of what a novel should do.
