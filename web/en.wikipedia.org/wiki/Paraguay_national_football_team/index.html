The Paraguay national football team, nicknamed La Albirroja ("the white-and-red") for its striped shirt, represents Paraguay in men's international football and is controlled by the Paraguayan Football Association (APF). Paraguay is a member of CONMEBOL and has long been regarded as one of South America's competitive second-tier sides, traditionally noted for a strong defence, physical play, and dangerous counterattacks.

History

Paraguay played its first international match in 1919 and joined FIFA in 1925. It participated in the inaugural World Cup in 1930 and won its first major title at the 1953 South American Championship in Peru. The team's golden era of continental success came with the Copa América triumph of 1979, won with a side featuring players such as Romerito.

World Cup record

Paraguay has qualified for the FIFA World Cup nine times (1930, 1950, 1958, 1986, 1998, 2002, 2006, 2010, and 2026 qualifying cycle aside). After a long absence, the team reached four consecutive tournaments from 1998 to 2010 under generations led by goalkeeper-captain José Luis Chilavert and defender Carlos Gamarra. Its best finish came at the 2010 World Cup in South Africa, where Paraguay reached the quarter-finals for the first time, losing 1–0 to eventual champions Spain. Paraguay failed to qualify for the 2014, 2018, and 2022 tournaments.

Copa América

Paraguay has won the Copa América twice (1953 and 1979) and finished runner-up six times, most recently in 2011, when it reached the final in Argentina without winning a single match outright — advancing through the knockout rounds on penalty shootouts before losing the final to Uruguay.

Players

Notable Paraguayan internationals include Arsenio Erico, one of the greatest goalscorers in Argentine league history; José Luis Chilavert, the free-kick-taking goalkeeper; Roque Santa Cruz, the team's long-serving striker; and all-time top scorer and most-capped player Roque Santa Cruz. The national team's home ground is the Estadio Defensores del Chaco in Asunción.
