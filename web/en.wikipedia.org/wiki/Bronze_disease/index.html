Bronze disease
Type: corrosion of copper alloys
Cause: chlorides + moisture + oxygen
Appearance: bright green powdery spots

Bronze disease is an irreversible corrosion process affecting bronze and other copper-alloy artefacts. It appears as powdery, light-green spots (basic copper chlorides such as atacamite and paratacamite) that grow in the presence of moisture and oxygen, driven by chloride contamination in the metal.

Chemistry

Cuprous chloride trapped within corrosion layers reacts with water and oxygen to form copper chlorides and hydrochloric acid, which attacks fresh metal and regenerates the chloride — a self-sustaining cycle that can pit and eventually destroy an object.

Treatment

Conservators treat bronze disease by removing chlorides through prolonged soaking in solutions such as sodium sesquicarbonate, mechanically cleaning the active spots, and applying corrosion inhibitors such as benzotriazole followed by a protective lacquer or wax, together with storage at low relative humidity.
