Emperor Tenmu

Emperor Tenmu (Japanese: 天武天皇, c. 631 – 1 October 686) was the 40th emperor of Japan, reigning from 673 to 686. Born Prince Ōama, he seized the throne from his nephew Emperor Kōbun in the Jinshin War of 672, one of the few succession wars of ancient Japanese history.

Jinshin War

After the death of his brother Emperor Tenji, Prince Ōama withdrew to Yoshino, ostensibly renouncing the succession. When the court at Ōmi moved against him, he raised forces in the eastern provinces, defeated the imperial army, and drove Kōbun to suicide. He was enthroned in 673 at the new capital of Asuka Kiyomihara.

Reforms

Tenmu strengthened imperial authority over the aristocracy, promoted Buddhism while also elevating the Ise Shrine and native cults, reformed court ranks, and advanced the ritsuryō project of governing through Chinese-style law codes, completed later under his successors. He is also associated with the compilation of Japan's earliest national histories, a project that eventually produced the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. He is credited with introducing the title tennō ("heavenly sovereign") for the Japanese monarch.
