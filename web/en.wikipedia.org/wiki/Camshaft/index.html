Camshaft

Camshaft
Function: operates engine intake and exhaust valves
Speed: half crankshaft speed (four-stroke)
Layouts: OHV, SOHC, DOHC

A camshaft is a shaft carrying a series of shaped lobes (cams) that open the intake and exhaust valves of a piston engine in correct sequence and for controlled durations. In a four-stroke engine the camshaft rotates at half the speed of the crankshaft, driven by a timing belt, chain, or gears in a fixed relationship called valve timing.

Construction and operation

Each lobe has a base circle and a raised profile; as the shaft turns, the lobe lifts a follower — a tappet, bucket, or rocker arm — against valve spring pressure. Lobe shape determines valve lift, duration, and overlap, and hence much of the engine's character: mild profiles favour low-speed torque and economy, aggressive profiles high-rpm power.

Configurations and variable timing

Camshafts may sit in the block, driving overhead valves through pushrods (OHV), or in the cylinder head (SOHC, or DOHC with separate intake and exhaust shafts). Modern engines often add variable valve timing — phasing the cam relative to the crank, and sometimes varying lift — to broaden performance and reduce emissions.
