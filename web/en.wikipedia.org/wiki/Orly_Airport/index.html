Orly Airport (French: Aéroport de Paris-Orly, IATA: ORY, ICAO: LFPO) is an international airport located partially in Orly and partially in Villeneuve-le-Roi, 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) south of Paris, France. It serves as a secondary hub for domestic and overseas territories flights of Air France and as the homebase for Transavia France. Flights operate to destinations in Europe, Middle East, Africa, Caribbean, North America and Southeast Asia.

Before the opening of Charles de Gaulle Airport in 1974, Orly was the main airport of Paris. Even with the shift of most international traffic to Charles de Gaulle, Orly remains the busiest French airport for domestic traffic and the second busiest French airport overall in passenger traffic.

History

The site began as a military airfield during the First World War. In 1932 it opened as a civil airport, Villeneuve–Orly. After the Second World War it was used by the United States Air Force before being returned to French control. The celebrated modernist South Terminal (Orly Sud), designed by Henri Vicariot, opened in 1961 and became an icon of the jet age; the West Terminal (Orly Ouest) followed in 1971. In 2019 a new junction building joined the terminals, and the airport adopted a single naming scheme of Orly 1, 2, 3 and 4 in place of the old South and West designations.

Transport links

Orly is connected to Paris by the Orlyval automated shuttle to Antony station on RER line B, by tramway line T7 to Villejuif–Louis Aragon on Métro line 7, and by road via the A106 motorway. Since June 2024, Paris Métro Line 14 serves the airport directly at Aéroport d'Orly station, providing a fast connection to central Paris.
