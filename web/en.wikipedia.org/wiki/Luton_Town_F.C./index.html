Luton Town Football Club is a professional football club based in Luton, Bedfordshire, England. Nicknamed "The Hatters", after the town's historic hat-making industry, the club was founded in 1885 and has played at Kenilworth Road since 1905. Luton were the first club in southern England to turn professional, in 1890, and joined the Football League in 1897.

History

The club's first golden era came under David Pleat in the 1980s: promoted to the First Division in 1982, Luton stayed in the top flight for a decade, famous for their artificial pitch at Kenilworth Road, a ban on away supporters introduced in 1986, and the 1988 League Cup triumph, when Brian Stein scored twice in a 3–2 win over Arsenal at Wembley — still the club's only major trophy. Luton were FA Cup finalists in 1959, losing to Nottingham Forest.

After relegation from the First Division in 1992, just before the Premier League era, the club declined through financial crises, administration and points deductions; relegated from the Football League in 2009 after a 30-point penalty, Luton spent five seasons in the Conference before beginning a celebrated climb back. Under managers including John Still, Nathan Jones, Mick Harford and Rob Edwards, the club rose from non-League football to the Premier League in 2023 — the first club to travel from the top division down to the fifth tier and back again. Luton were relegated after one Premier League season (2023–24) despite widespread admiration for their competitiveness on a small budget, and have since played in the EFL Championship. The club plans to move to a new stadium at Power Court in central Luton.

Colours and crest

Luton's colours are orange, navy blue and white; the distinctive orange shirts were adopted in the 1970s. The club crest features a straw boater hat, a bee and the town's arms, with the motto Si sapis sis apis ("if you are wise, be a bee").

Rivalries

The club's fiercest rivalry is with Watford, contested as the Beds–Herts derby. Other rivalries include Queens Park Rangers and Millwall, whose 1985 visit to Kenilworth Road was marred by a notorious riot.
