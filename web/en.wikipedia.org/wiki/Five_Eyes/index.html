Five Eyes

Five Eyes (FVEY)
MembersAustralia, Canada, New Zealand, United Kingdom, United States
TypeIntelligence alliance
Established1946 (UKUSA Agreement)

The Five Eyes (FVEY) is an intelligence alliance comprising Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States. These countries are parties to the multilateral UKUSA Agreement, a treaty for joint cooperation in signals intelligence.

Origins

The alliance traces its origins to informal wartime cooperation between British and American codebreakers at sites such as Bletchley Park during the Second World War. The UKUSA Agreement was signed in 1946 between the United Kingdom and the United States; Canada joined in 1948, and Australia and New Zealand in 1956. For decades the existence of the agreement was not officially acknowledged; it was publicly released in 2010.

Member agencies

The principal signals-intelligence agencies of the Five Eyes are the National Security Agency (NSA) of the United States, the Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) of the United Kingdom, the Communications Security Establishment (CSE) of Canada, the Australian Signals Directorate (ASD), and the Government Communications Security Bureau (GCSB) of New Zealand.

Activities and disclosure

The partners share intercepted communications and cooperate on global interception infrastructure, including the long-rumoured ECHELON system. Public knowledge of the alliance's scope expanded greatly following the 2013 disclosures by Edward Snowden, which described mass surveillance programmes and cooperation among the agencies. Related groupings, sometimes styled "Nine Eyes" and "Fourteen Eyes", include additional NATO and allied partners with more limited sharing arrangements.
