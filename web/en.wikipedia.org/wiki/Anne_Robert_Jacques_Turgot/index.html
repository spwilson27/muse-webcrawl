Anne Robert Jacques Turgot, Baron de l'Aulne (10 May 1727 – 18 March 1781) was a French economist and statesman. As intendant of Limoges (1761–1774) he applied liberal reforms, and as Controller-General of Finances under Louis XVI from 1774 he attempted sweeping free-market reforms.

Reforms

Turgot's programme included free trade in grain, abolition of the corvée (forced road labour), suppression of guild privileges, and his famous Six Edicts of 1776. His slogan — "no bankruptcy, no tax increases, no borrowing" — aimed at fiscal discipline. Fierce opposition from the parlements, the court and the clergy led Louis XVI to dismiss him in May 1776, after which most reforms were reversed.

Economic thought

His Reflections on the Formation and Distribution of Wealth (written 1766) anticipated ideas of Adam Smith, including diminishing returns and the role of capital. Turgot is regarded as a leading figure of the physiocratic school and of economic liberalism.
