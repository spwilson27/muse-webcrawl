Wallace and Gromit

Wallace and Gromit is a British claymation comedy franchise created by Nick Park and produced by Aardman Animations. The series centres on Wallace, a good-natured, eccentric inventor who loves cheese (especially Wensleydale), and Gromit, his loyal and intelligent anthropomorphic beagle, who does not speak but communicates through facial expressions and body language.

Films

The pair first appeared in the short film A Grand Day Out (1989), in which they build a rocket to travel to the Moon in search of cheese. It was followed by the Oscar-winning shorts The Wrong Trousers (1993), featuring the villainous penguin Feathers McGraw, and A Close Shave (1995), which introduced Shaun the Sheep. A series of ten short "Cracking Contraptions" followed in 2002.

The feature film Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit (2005) won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. The short A Matter of Loaf and Death (2008) was among the most-watched television programmes of its year in the UK. A second feature, Wallace & Gromit: Vengeance Most Fowl (2024), brought back Feathers McGraw.

Style and legacy

The films are made in stop-motion with plasticine models, a painstaking process in which a few seconds of footage may take a day to shoot. Wallace is voiced by Peter Sallis (until 2010) and subsequently Ben Whitehead. The characters have become icons of British culture, and their charity appeals have raised substantial sums for children's hospitals through Wallace & Gromit's Children's Charity.
