Hub and spoke

Hub and spoke
Type: Network topology / distribution model
Applications: Airlines, logistics, telecommunications, computing
Contrast: Point-to-point network

The spoke–hub distribution paradigm, or hub and spoke model, is a form of transport topology optimization in which traffic routes are organized as a series of "spokes" connecting outlying points to a central "hub." Instead of travelling directly point-to-point, journeys route through the hub, allowing many more destinations to be served economically.

Airline industry

After US airline deregulation in 1978, carriers such as Delta (Atlanta), United, and American built hub-and-spoke networks: passengers from smaller cities fly into a hub and connect onward, filling larger aircraft and increasing frequency. FedEx pioneered the model for freight, sorting packages overnight at its Memphis superhub. Criticisms include vulnerability to hub delays, longer travel times, and congestion.

Other uses

The model describes logistics distribution centres, telecommunications and computer networks, hospital referral systems, and franchising. In computing, hub-and-spoke is a common cloud network architecture connecting virtual networks through a central hub.
