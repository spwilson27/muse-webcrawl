Rusyn (русиньскый язык, rusyňskyj jazyk) is an East Slavic language variety, or group of varieties, spoken by the Rusyns (also called Carpatho-Rusyns or Ruthenians) in the Carpathian region of Central and Eastern Europe, and by Rusyn diaspora communities in Serbia's Vojvodina province and North America. Its status is debated: it is officially recognised as a minority language in several countries, while in Ukraine it has historically been treated as a dialect of Ukrainian.

Distribution and codification

Rusyn is spoken in Slovakia's Prešov Region, Poland's Lemko region, Ukraine's Zakarpattia Oblast, and adjacent parts of Hungary and Romania. Distinct codified standards exist: a Slovak Rusyn standard (codified in 1995), a Lemko standard in Poland, and the older Pannonian Rusyn standard used in Vojvodina, Serbia, where Rusyn has been an official provincial language for decades and is used in education and media.

Features

Written in Cyrillic, Rusyn shares much vocabulary and grammar with Ukrainian but shows significant influence from neighbouring West Slavic languages (Slovak and Polish) and from Hungarian, reflecting centuries of contact in the Carpathians. Characteristic features include fixed stress on the penultimate syllable in some varieties and distinctive phonological developments that set it apart from standard Ukrainian.

History

A Rusyn literary language began to develop in the 19th century with figures such as Alexander Dukhnovych, author of the Rusyn national anthem. During the Soviet period, Rusyn identity and language were suppressed in Ukraine, where Rusyns were classified as Ukrainians. Since 1989, cultural organisations across the Carpathians have promoted the language through newspapers such as Narodny Novynky, theatre, and university programmes. Rusyn was recognised as a distinct language under the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages by several states; Ukraine's recognition of Rusyns as a separate nationality remained politically contentious.
