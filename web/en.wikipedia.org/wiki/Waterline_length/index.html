Waterline length

Waterline length (LWL) is the length of a ship's hull where it meets the water surface.

Symbol: LWL or LWL

The waterline length (abbreviated LWL) is the length of a ship or boat at the level where it sits in the water — the waterline — measured from the foremost point of the hull at that level to the aftmost point. It differs from the length overall (LOA), which includes overhangs of the bow and stern above the water, and from the registered length used for official documentation.

Significance

LWL is a key determinant of a displacement vessel's performance. A hull moving through water generates a wave system, and the practical maximum speed of a displacement hull — its "hull speed" — depends mainly on waterline length, often approximated as 1.34 times the square root of the LWL in feet, giving speed in knots. Longer waterlines therefore generally permit higher speeds before wave-making resistance rises steeply. LWL also features in yacht racing rating rules and handicap systems, and in the calculation of tonnage and stability characteristics.
