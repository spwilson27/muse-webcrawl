Anna Sacher (née Fuchs; 8 January 1859 – 25 February 1930) was an Austrian hotelier and the proprietor of Vienna's famous Hotel Sacher, which under her management became one of the great meeting places of European aristocracy, diplomacy, and high society in the decades before the First World War.

Life

The daughter of a butcher, Anna Fuchs married Eduard Sacher in 1880. Eduard was the son of Franz Sacher, creator of the Sachertorte, and founder of the hotel that opened in 1876 opposite the Vienna State Opera. After her husband's early death in 1892, Anna took over the hotel at the age of 33 and ran it for nearly four decades.

Known for her cigars, her French bulldogs (the "Sacher-Dackel" was in fact a bulldog she adored), her generosity, and her iron discipline with staff, she made the hotel's private salons famous as venues for discreet political and romantic encounters. Her legendary private cabinets séparés allowed aristocratic guests to dine unobserved.

Later years and legacy

The hotel declined after the collapse of the Austro-Hungarian monarchy, and it went bankrupt shortly after her death in 1930. Anna Sacher has been portrayed in film and television, including the 2016 Austrian series Das Sacher.
