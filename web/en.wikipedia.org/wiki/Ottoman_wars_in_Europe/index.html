Ottoman wars in Europe
Datec. 1355 – 1913
LocationThe Balkans, Hungary, the Mediterranean, the Black Sea steppe
CombatantsOttoman Empire against the Balkan states, Hungary, Venice, the Habsburgs, Poland–Lithuania and Russia

The Ottoman wars in Europe were a series of military conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and a shifting set of European powers, fought from the mid-14th century to the early 20th century. Beginning with the Ottoman crossing into Thrace, they carried Turkish rule across the Balkans to the gates of Vienna, and their slow reversal from the late 17th century created the "Eastern Question" that preoccupied European diplomacy until the First World War.

Expansion, 1354–1453

The seizure of Gallipoli in 1354 gave the Ottomans a permanent foothold in Europe. Victories at the Maritsa (1371) and at Kosovo (1389) broke Serbian and Bulgarian power, while the crusades of Nicopolis (1396) and Varna (1444) failed to dislodge them. The expansion culminated in Mehmed II's conquest of Constantinople in 1453, followed by the absorption of Serbia, Bosnia, Albania (despite Skanderbeg's resistance) and the Morea.

High tide, 1453–1683

Ottoman armies campaigned against Venice in the Aegean and Dalmatia, against Hungary—destroyed as a great power at Mohács in 1526—and against the Habsburgs in Hungary and the Mediterranean, as well as against Poland and, increasingly, Russia on the steppe frontier. The failure of the second siege of Vienna in 1683 marked the empire's furthest advance in Europe.

Retreat, 1683–1913

The Great Turkish War and the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) began the long Ottoman retreat from Hungary. In the 18th and 19th centuries Russia advanced to the Black Sea and the Balkans, Serbia and Greece won independence, and the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 freed most of the Balkans. The Balkan Wars of 1912–1913 stripped the empire of nearly all its remaining European territory except eastern Thrace.
