Takeda Shingen (武田 信玄, 1 December 1521 – 13 May 1573), born Takeda Harunobu, was a powerful Japanese daimyō of Kai Province during the Sengoku period. Known as the "Tiger of Kai", he is remembered as one of the era's most brilliant military strategists and administrators.

Rise to power

In 1541 Shingen deposed his own father, Takeda Nobutora, and took control of the clan. He expanded Takeda power through the conquest of Shinano Province in a long series of campaigns, reorganised the governance and taxation of his domains, promoted flood control and gold mining, and enacted the law code known as the Kōshū Hatto.

Rivalry with Uesugi Kenshin

Shingen is most famous for his five battles against Uesugi Kenshin of Echigo at Kawanakajima (1553–1564), a legendary rivalry in Japanese history; the fourth battle (1561) was among the bloodiest of the Sengoku period. He also fought the Murakami, Ogasawara, Hōjō, and Imagawa, and maintained shifting alliances sealed by marriages.

Death and legacy

In 1572 Shingen launched a great offensive against Tokugawa Ieyasu and Oda Nobunaga, defeating Ieyasu at the Battle of Mikatagahara, but he died in 1573 during the campaign — the cause, illness or wounds, remains debated. His death was reportedly concealed for three years. Under his son Katsuyori the clan's fortunes collapsed after the Battle of Nagashino (1575). Shingen's banner carried the famous maxim from Sun Tzu: "Swift as the wind, silent as a forest, fierce as fire, immovable as a mountain" (Fūrinkazan).
