Japan Post Bank Co., Ltd. (株式会社ゆうちょ銀行, Kabushiki-gaisha Yūcho Ginkō) is a Japanese bank headquartered in Tokyo. It is a corporation held by Japan Post Holdings, in which the government of Japan has a majority stake. It is one of the largest banks in the world by deposits, a position it inherited from the postal savings system established in 1875.

History

Postal savings were introduced in Japan in 1875, modelled on the British system. For more than a century the deposits gathered at post offices across the country were channelled through the Fiscal Investment and Loan Program to fund public works and government-affiliated agencies. In 2005–2007, under the postal privatization reforms of Prime Minister Junichiro Koizumi, the old Japan Post was split into separate companies, and Japan Post Bank began operating as a distinct bank on 1 October 2007.

In November 2015, Japan Post Bank was listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange together with Japan Post Holdings and Japan Post Insurance, in one of the largest initial public offerings in Japanese history.

Operations

The bank offers savings accounts, remittances, and investment products through roughly 24,000 post offices nationwide, giving it the largest branch network of any Japanese bank. Its balance sheet is dominated by Japanese government bonds and other securities, reflecting restrictions on its lending activities that were designed to avoid unfair competition with private banks.
