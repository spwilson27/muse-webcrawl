Sønderborg (German: Sonderburg) is a Danish town in the Region of Southern Denmark. It is the seat of Sønderborg Municipality, on the island of Als and the mainland of Jutland, which it straddles across the strait of Als Sund; the two parts are connected by the King's Bridge (Kongebroen, 1930) and the Alssund Bridge (1981). The town grew up around Sønderborg Castle, founded before 1200 as a fortification against the Wends.

History

Sønderborg received town rights in the medieval period and prospered as a trading port. From 1460 it belonged to the Duchy of Schleswig; the castle famously held the deposed King Christian II as a prisoner from 1532 to 1549. After the Second Schleswig War of 1864 — when Prussian forces crossed to Als after the Battle of Dybbøl just west of the town — Sønderborg became part of Prussia and then the German Empire. Following the Schleswig plebiscites of 1920, the town voted to reunite with Denmark, an event central to Danish national memory in the border region.

Economy and education

The town is associated with the Danfoss engineering group, headquartered at nearby Nordborg, and with Linak and other electronics and heating-technology firms, giving the area one of Denmark's highest concentrations of engineering employment. Sønderborg hosts a campus of the University of Southern Denmark (SDU), known for engineering and for its cross-border Danish–German studies, and the town has declared an ambition — ProjectZero — to make its energy system carbon-neutral.

Landmarks

  • Sønderborg Castle — museum of the history of Southern Jutland and Schleswig.
  • Dybbøl Mill and the Dybbøl battlefield of 1864, on the mainland side.
  • St Mary's Church and the old town's merchant houses along the harbour.
