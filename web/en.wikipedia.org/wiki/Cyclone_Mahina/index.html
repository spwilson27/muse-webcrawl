Cyclone Mahina was a tropical cyclone that struck Bathurst Bay, Queensland, Australia, on 4–5 March 1899, causing the deaths of more than 300 people. It is Australia's deadliest recorded tropical cyclone and one of the deadliest in the Southern Hemisphere.

Impact

The cyclone struck the pearling fleet anchored in Bathurst Bay near Cape Melville, in Princess Charlotte Bay. A storm surge — reported at up to about 13 metres (43 ft) in some accounts — swept through the fleet and ashore, drowning sailors and pearl luggers' crews, many of them Japanese, Pacific Islander, and Aboriginal workers, and carrying vessels far inland; dolphins were reportedly found stranded on cliffs. The death toll is commonly given as 307, with some estimates exceeding 400. The local Aboriginal people assisted survivors, and a memorial was later erected at Cape Melville.

Pressure readings

A barometer aboard the schooner Olive recorded a pressure fall to 985 hPa and the instrument's scale limit was passed; a figure of 914 hPa has often been quoted from later estimates, though modern reassessments suggest the true central pressure may have been higher (around 950 hPa) and the surge estimates debated.
