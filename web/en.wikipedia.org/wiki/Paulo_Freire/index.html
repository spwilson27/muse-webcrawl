Paulo Reglus Neves Freire (19 September 1921 – 2 May 1997) was a Brazilian educator and philosopher who was a leading advocate of critical pedagogy. His 1968 work Pedagogy of the Oppressed is one of the most cited books in the social sciences and a foundational text of critical pedagogy, arguing that education should help the oppressed achieve conscientização — critical consciousness of their social reality — through dialogue and problem-posing rather than the mere depositing of information.

Early life

Freire was born in Recife to a middle-class family impoverished by the Great Depression; hunger in his childhood, he later wrote, taught him the connection between poverty and schooling. He studied law at the University of Recife and worked as a secondary-school teacher of Portuguese, then directed adult education programs in the state of Pernambuco. In the early 1960s his literacy method — teaching peasants to read in as little as 40 hours by starting from "generative words" drawn from their own lives — was applied in celebrated campaigns, including the 1963 experiment at Angicos, where some 300 sugarcane workers became literate in about a month.

Exile

After the 1964 Brazilian military coup, Freire was imprisoned for 70 days as a "traitor" and exiled. He worked in Chile on agrarian reform literacy programs, held a visiting professorship at Harvard, and in the 1970s advised literacy campaigns from Geneva for the World Council of Churches, influencing liberation movements in Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Nicaragua. Pedagogy of the Oppressed, published in Portuguese in 1968 and in English in 1970, introduced his famous critique of the "banking concept of education," in which students are treated as empty accounts to be filled by the teacher, and proposed instead a dialogical pedagogy in which teacher and student learn together.

Return to Brazil

Freire returned to Brazil in 1980 after amnesty, taught at the University of Campinas and the Pontifical Catholic University of São Paulo, and in 1989 was appointed Secretary of Education for the city of São Paulo under the Workers' Party administration, where he reformed curricula, raised teacher pay and expanded adult literacy. He wrote further influential works including The Politics of Education, Pedagogy of Hope and Pedagogy of Freedom. He died of heart failure in São Paulo in 1997. In 2012 Brazil officially declared him the patron of Brazilian education.
