Predicative expression

A predicative expression (also called a predicate in some traditions) is a word or phrase that occurs in the predicate of a clause and describes a property of, or a state relating to, the subject. In the sentence "The sky is blue", the adjective phrase blue is a predicative expression (a subject complement). Predicative expressions are contrasted with attributive expressions, which modify a noun inside a noun phrase, as in "the blue sky".

Types

Predicative expressions can be adjective phrases ("She is very happy"), noun phrases ("He became a doctor"), and prepositional phrases ("The keys are on the table"). In many languages, including English, predicative adjectives follow a linking verb (copula) such as be, seem or become. Some adjectives in English are used only predicatively, such as asleep and afraid.

Secondary predication

The term also covers secondary predicates in syntax: in "He ate the meat raw", raw is a predicative expression describing the object during the event. Generative grammar analyses predicative expressions in terms of small clauses and predication relations.
