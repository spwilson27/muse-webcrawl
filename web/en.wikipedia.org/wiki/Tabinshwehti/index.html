Tabinshwehti

Reign: 1530–1550

Predecessor: Mingyi Nyo

Successor: Bayinnaung

Born: 1516, Toungoo

Died: 1550, near Pantanaw

Tabinshwehti (1516–1550) was king of Toungoo from 1530 to 1550, and the founder of the First Toungoo Empire in present-day Myanmar. His reign is considered the starting point of the reunification of Burma after the collapse of the Pagan Empire in 1287.

Early campaigns

Tabinshwehti succeeded his father Mingyi Nyo at the age of fourteen. Surrounded by stronger neighbours — the Shan Confederation in the north, Hanthawaddy in the south — he chose to move against the wealthy but poorly led Mon kingdom of Hanthawaddy. After several campaigns he captured its capital, Bago (Pegu), in 1538–39, and by 1541 had taken the port of Martaban, giving Toungoo control of Lower Burma's trade and access to Portuguese mercenaries and firearms.

Later reign

Crowned in the style of both Burmese and Mon kings, Tabinshwehti campaigned against Prome and, unsuccessfully, Arakan (1546–47) and Ayutthaya (1548–49). During the Siamese campaign his forces failed to take the capital, partly owing to the strength of its defences and Portuguese gunners in Siamese service.

In his last years the king fell under the influence of a Portuguese adventurer and neglected government while searching for legendary treasure. In 1550 he was assassinated by retainers of Smim Sawhtut while on an elephant hunt near Pantanaw. The empire briefly fragmented before being reunified by his brother-in-law and deputy, Bayinnaung.
