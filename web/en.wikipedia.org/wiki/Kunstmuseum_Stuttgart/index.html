The Kunstmuseum Stuttgart is a contemporary and modern art museum in central Stuttgart, Germany, located on the Kleiner Schlossplatz at the top of the Königstraße shopping street. The museum opened on 5 March 2005 as successor to the Galerie der Stadt Stuttgart, and its striking glass-cube building has become a landmark of the city centre.

Building

Designed by the Berlin firm Hascher Jehle, the museum presents two faces: by day a minimalist glass cube roughly 26 metres high; by night, the illuminated travertine-clad core and exhibition levels glow through the glass shell. Of the museum's roughly 5,000 m² of exhibition space, much is set underground in a converted road tunnel beneath the square, keeping the above-ground volume compact. A restaurant and terrace on the upper floor overlook Schlossplatz and the Stuttgart basin.

Collection

The municipal collection spans modern and contemporary art, with a notable strength in the work of Otto Dix, whose paintings, watercolours, and prints form the world's most extensive single holding; Dix spent formative years teaching in Dresden but was born in Gera and is strongly represented in Stuttgart thanks to civic collecting. Other strengths include the circle around Adolf Hölzel at the Stuttgart Academy, Willi Baumeister, Joseph Kosuth, Dieter Roth, and substantial contemporary photography and installation works. The museum also manages the private collection of the city archive's art holdings displayed in the adjoining buildings.

Exhibitions and recognition

The museum mounts several large special exhibitions a year, ranging from monographic retrospectives to thematic contemporary shows, and became one of Germany's most-visited municipal art museums. In 2021 it was named Museum of the Year by the German section of the International Association of Art Critics (AICA), recognising its programme and collection development.
