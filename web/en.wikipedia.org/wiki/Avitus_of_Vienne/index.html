Alcimus Ecdicius Avitus (c. 450 – c. 518/525) was a Latin poet and bishop of Vienne in Gaul. Coming from a prominent Gallo-Roman senatorial family—he was the son of Hesychius, also bishop of Vienne—Avitus succeeded his father in the see around 490 and became one of the leading churchmen of the Burgundian kingdom.

Life and career

Avitus laboured to reconcile the Arian Burgundian court with Catholic Christianity; he is credited with influencing King Sigismund's conversion to Catholicism. He presided over the Council of Epaon (517), corresponded with popes and with the Frankish king Clovis—whose baptism he praised in a famous letter—and opposed Arian and other heresies in his writings.

Works

His principal poetic work, De spiritalis historiae gestis ("On the events of spiritual history"), is a five-book epic in hexameters treating the Creation, the Fall, the expulsion from Paradise, the Flood and the crossing of the Red Sea; it is regarded as a forerunner of Milton's Paradise Lost. He also wrote a poem in praise of virginity addressed to his sister Fuscina, a collection of some eighty letters of great value for the history of the period, and homilies, of which only fragments and a few complete sermons survive.
