PEGI

The Pan European Game Information (PEGI) system is a European video game content rating system established to help consumers make informed decisions when buying video games. It was developed by the Interactive Software Federation of Europe (ISFE) and came into use in April 2003, replacing many national rating systems with a single European system.

Age ratings

RatingMeaning
PEGI 3Suitable for all ages; no bad language, no frightening scenes.
PEGI 7May contain mild violence or scenes that could frighten young children.
PEGI 12Moderate violence, mild bad language, or gambling references may be present.
PEGI 16More realistic violence, stronger language, sexual references, or depictions of alcohol and tobacco use.
PEGI 18Adult classification: gross violence, glamorisation of drugs, or graphic sexual content.

Content descriptors

In addition to the age label, game packaging carries descriptors indicating the type of content present, including violence, bad language, fear, gambling, sex, drugs, discrimination, and in-game purchases. The in-game purchases descriptor was added in 2018 in response to concerns about loot boxes.

Use and legal status

PEGI is used in more than 35 countries. In some, such as the United Kingdom, the ratings for physical game sales are legally enforceable; in others the system is voluntary or supported by industry codes. The system is administered by two independent bodies, NICAM in the Netherlands and the Video Standards Council in the UK. Since 2020, PEGI ratings have also been applied to games distributed through participating digital storefronts in cooperation with the International Age Rating Coalition (IARC).
