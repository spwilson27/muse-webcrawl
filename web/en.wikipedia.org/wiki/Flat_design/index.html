Flat design

Flat design is a design language and style of interface design that emphasises minimalist use of simple elements, typography, and flat colours, in contrast to skeuomorphism, which attempts to imitate real-world materials and textures. Flat design eschews three-dimensional effects such as drop shadows, gradients, bevels, and reflections, favouring two-dimensional illustrations, bright colour palettes, and clean typography.

History

The style has antecedents in the International Typographic Style (Swiss Style) of mid-20th-century graphic design, Bauhaus, and modernism. In digital interfaces it rose to prominence in the 2010s as a reaction against the richly textured skeuomorphic interfaces popularised by early iPhone software. Microsoft's Metro design language, introduced with Windows Phone in 2010 and Windows 8 in 2012, was an early large-scale implementation; Apple's iOS 7 (2013) abandoned skeuomorphism in favour of a flat aesthetic, and Google's Material Design (2014) developed a related approach that reintroduced subtle shadows to imply layered "paper" surfaces while remaining essentially flat.

Characteristics and criticism

Typical characteristics include simple shapes and icons, vivid solid colours, sans-serif typography, generous whitespace, and the absence of decorative effects. Advantages claimed for flat design include clarity, fast loading, and easy scaling across screen sizes. Usability researchers have criticised extreme flatness for removing "signifiers" of clickability, making it harder for users to identify buttons and links; later evolutions — sometimes called "flat 2.0" or semi-flat design — reintroduced restrained shadows and depth cues.
