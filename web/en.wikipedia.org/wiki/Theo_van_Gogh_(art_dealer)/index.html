Theo van Gogh (art dealer)

Theodorus "Theo" van Gogh (1 May 1857 – 25 January 1891) was a Dutch art dealer and the younger brother of the artist Vincent van Gogh. His unstinting financial and emotional support made Vincent's work possible, and their correspondence — hundreds of letters — is a primary source for the artist's life and thought.

Life

Born in Zundert, the son of a pastor, Theo joined the art dealers Goupil & Cie in The Hague at sixteen and later managed the firm's Paris branch on the Boulevard Montmartre. From Paris he championed the Impressionists and Post-Impressionists — Monet, Degas, Pissarro, Gauguin, Toulouse-Lautrec and Seurat — mounting exhibitions when academic taste still resisted them, and introduced Vincent to the Parisian avant-garde. He financed Vincent throughout his decade as an artist, sending money, paints and encouragement in exchange for canvases. Theo married Johanna Bonger in 1889; their son, born in 1890, was named Vincent Willem. After Vincent shot himself and died in Theo's arms at Auvers-sur-Oise in July 1890, Theo's health — physical and mental — collapsed; he died six months later in Utrecht, aged 33, probably of syphilis and its complications. In 1914 his remains were moved beside Vincent's in Auvers.

Legacy

Theo's widow Johanna preserved and promoted Vincent's art and published his letters, laying the foundation of his posthumous fame. The collected correspondence, The Letters of Vincent van Gogh, documents one of the most famous brotherhoods in art history.
