G-Unit

G-Unit (short for Guerilla Unit) is an American hip hop group formed in New York City, in 1999, by rappers 50 Cent, Lloyd Banks, and Tony Yayo. The group coalesced around childhood friends from South Jamaica, Queens, and its founding members are 50 Cent, Lloyd Banks, Tony Yayo, Young Buck, and The Game — although Young Buck and Game joined later.

The group, along with its founder's Nine Inch-type hustle and massive mixtape presence, created a movement in the music industry which saw the group sell millions of albums worldwide.

History

50 Cent, Lloyd Banks, and Tony Yayo grew up together in Queens and rapped together in the late 1990s. When 50 Cent was signed to Interscope and Aftermath in 2002, the group rode his rise, releasing a series of popular mixtapes. Their debut album, Beg for Mercy, was released in 2003 and has sold over 4 million copies worldwide, followed by T.O.S. (Terminate on Sight) in 2008.

Yayo was incarcerated during much of the first album's recording, and Young Buck was recruited from Nashville. The Game was placed in the group by Dr. Dre and Jimmy Iovine in 2004, but left in 2005 amid a feud with 50 Cent, launching the G-Unot campaign. Various later reconfigurations included Kidd Kidd, and the group reunited in 2014, releasing the EPs The Beauty of Independence and The Beast Is G-Unit.

G-Unit Records

50 Cent founded G-Unit Records in 2003 as an imprint of Interscope, signing the group's members as solo artists as well as artists such as Mobb Deep, MOP, and others. The G-Unit brand also extended to clothing and other ventures.
