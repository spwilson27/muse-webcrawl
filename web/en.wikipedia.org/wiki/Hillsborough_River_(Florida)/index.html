Hillsborough River (Florida)

Hillsborough River
Length: about 59 miles (95 km)
Source: Green Swamp, Florida
Mouth: Hillsborough Bay, Tampa

The Hillsborough River is a 59-mile-long river in west-central Florida. It rises in the Green Swamp near the junction of Hillsborough, Pasco and Polk counties and flows south-westward through Hillsborough River State Park and the city of Tampa, emptying into Hillsborough Bay, part of Tampa Bay.

History and use

The river takes its name from Wills Hill, Earl of Hillsborough, a British Secretary of State for the Colonies. It has supplied drinking water to Tampa since the early 20th century; the Tampa Bypass Canal was built for flood control after flooding in 1960. Fort Foster, a Second Seminole War fort, stood on its banks.

Ecology

The river corridor supports floodplain swamp, hardwood hammocks and wildlife including alligators, river otters and wading birds. Much of its upper course is protected within state park and water-management lands.
