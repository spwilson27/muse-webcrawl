Protein purification

Key methods: Chromatography, centrifugation, precipitation, electrophoresis
Common tags: His-tag, GST, FLAG
Purity check: SDS-PAGE

Protein purification is a series of processes intended to isolate one or a few proteins from a complex mixture, usually cells, tissues, or fermentation fluid. Purified proteins are needed for structural studies, enzymology, antibody production, and biopharmaceutical manufacturing.

Strategy

A purification scheme exploits differences between the target protein and contaminants in size, charge, hydrophobicity, solubility, and binding specificity. Typical steps are cell lysis, clarification by centrifugation or filtration, a capture step, intermediate purification, and a final polishing step, with yield and purity monitored at each stage.

Chromatography

Column chromatography dominates: ion-exchange separates by charge, size-exclusion (gel filtration) by size, hydrophobic interaction by surface hydrophobicity, and affinity chromatography by specific binding — for example nickel-affinity capture of histidine-tagged recombinant proteins or Protein A capture of antibodies.

Analysis

Purity is most often assessed by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry, and activity by functional assays. Industrial purification of therapeutic proteins must additionally clear viruses, endotoxins, and host-cell proteins to regulatory standards.
