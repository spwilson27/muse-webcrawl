Boy's surface

In geometry, Boy's surface is an immersion of the real projective plane in three-dimensional space found by Werner Boy in 1901. It was discovered on assignment from David Hilbert to prove that the projective plane cannot be immersed in three-dimensional Euclidean space.

Boy's surface is discussed in Geometry and the Imagination by Hilbert and Cohn-Vossen. It has three-fold symmetry and can be built by gluing a Möbius strip to a disc, with the requirement that the resulting surface has no self-intersections of certain types; in fact, Boy's surface has self-intersections but no singularities.

Symmetry

Boy's surface has 3-fold symmetry. This means that it has an axis of discrete rotational symmetry: any 120° turn about this axis will leave the surface unaltered. The surface can be split into three congruent pieces.

Model at Oberwolfach

A large model of Boy's surface was constructed and donated in 1991 to the Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach. In 1987 Rob Kusner and Robert Bryant found a parametrization of Boy's surface with minimal total Willmore energy.
