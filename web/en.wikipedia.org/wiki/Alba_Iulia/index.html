Alba Iulia is a city in west-central Romania, the capital of Alba County, located on the Mureș River in the region of Transylvania. It is one of the oldest settlements in Romania and has been an important political, religious and military centre for two millennia.

History

The site was the location of the Roman city of Apulum, the largest castrum in Roman Dacia, established after Trajan's conquest of the Dacian kingdom. In the Middle Ages the town was known in Hungarian as Gyulafehérvár and served as a seat of the voivodes and princes of Transylvania, as well as of a Roman Catholic bishopric.

In 1599–1600, Michael the Brave, voivode of Wallachia, briefly united Wallachia, Moldavia and Transylvania under his rule and was crowned in Alba Iulia, making the city a potent symbol of Romanian unity. On 1 December 1918, the Great National Assembly of Alba Iulia, attended by 1,228 delegates, proclaimed the union of Transylvania with the Kingdom of Romania — an event commemorated annually as Romania's Great Union Day.

Landmarks

The city's most prominent feature is the Alba Carolina Citadel, a massive star-shaped Vauban-style fortress built between 1715 and 1738 under Habsburg rule. Within it stand the Roman Catholic St. Michael's Cathedral (13th century, containing the tomb of John Hunyadi) and the Orthodox Reunification Cathedral, built in 1921–1922 for the coronation of King Ferdinand I and Queen Marie of Romania, which took place in the city in October 1922.
