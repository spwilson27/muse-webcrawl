Alternative fuel vehicle
Examples: battery electric vehicles, hydrogen fuel cell vehicles, flex-fuel vehicles, natural gas vehicles

An alternative fuel vehicle is a motor vehicle that runs on alternative fuels rather than traditional petroleum fuels such as petrol or diesel. The term is commonly used to refer to vehicles powered by electricity, hydrogen, natural gas, ethanol, biodiesel, or liquefied petroleum gas, as well as hybrid vehicles that combine an internal combustion engine with an electric motor.

Types

Battery electric vehicles (BEVs) store energy in rechargeable battery packs and are driven by electric motors. Hybrid electric vehicles combine a conventional engine with an electric propulsion system, while plug-in hybrids can be charged from an external source. Fuel cell vehicles generate electricity onboard by reacting hydrogen with oxygen. Flex-fuel vehicles are designed to run on more than one fuel, typically petrol blended with ethanol.

Advantages and challenges

Alternative fuel vehicles are promoted for reducing greenhouse gas emissions, improving urban air quality, and decreasing dependence on imported oil. Challenges include higher purchase costs, limited refuelling or charging infrastructure, and the carbon intensity of the electricity or hydrogen used to power them.
