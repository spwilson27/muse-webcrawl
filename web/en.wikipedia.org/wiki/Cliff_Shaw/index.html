Cliff Shaw

John Clifford Shaw (February 23, 1922 – February 9, 1991) was an American systems programmer at the RAND Corporation and a pioneer of artificial intelligence. He is best known as the co-creator, with Allen Newell and Herbert A. Simon, of the Logic Theorist and the General Problem Solver, two of the earliest artificial intelligence programs.

Career

Shaw worked at RAND in Santa Monica, California, where he collaborated with Carnegie Tech researchers Allen Newell and Herbert Simon. Shaw was the skilled programmer of the trio: he wrote the code that ran on the JOHNNIAC computer, while Newell and Simon developed the theory.

Contributions

The Logic Theorist (1956), often called the first artificial intelligence program, proved theorems in the propositional calculus of Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica, in one case finding a more elegant proof than the published one. The team also created the Information Processing Language (IPL), an early list-processing language, and the General Problem Solver (1957), an influential model of human problem solving built on means–ends analysis.

Recognition

Although Simon and Newell received most of the public honours, including the Turing Award, historians of computing regard Shaw's programming as indispensable to the partnership's achievements. He spent most of his career at RAND and did not seek an academic profile.
