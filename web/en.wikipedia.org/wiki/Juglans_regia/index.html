Juglans regia

KingdomPlantae
FamilyJuglandaceae
GenusJuglans
SpeciesJ. regia
Common namesEnglish walnut, Persian walnut, common walnut

Juglans regia, the Persian walnut or English walnut, is a deciduous tree in the walnut family Juglandaceae, native to a region stretching from the Balkans and southwest Asia to the Himalayas and China. It is the most widely cultivated walnut species, grown for its timber and edible nuts.

Description

The tree grows 10–40 metres tall, with a broad crown and pinnate leaves of five to nine leaflets. It is monoecious, bearing male catkins and small female flowers. The fruit is a green husk surrounding a hard, wrinkled shell that encloses the edible seed, the walnut.

Cultivation and uses

Walnuts are a significant commercial crop, with China and the United States among the largest producers. The kernels are eaten raw or cooked and are rich in polyunsaturated fats. The wood, known as European walnut, is prized for furniture, veneer and gunstocks. Roots, leaves and husks contain the compound juglone, which can inhibit the growth of nearby plants.

History

Walnut cultivation spread westward from Persia through the Greek and Roman worlds; the Latin name Juglans derives from Jovis glans, "Jupiter's acorn". Alexander the Great and Roman traders are credited with introducing improved varieties into Europe.
