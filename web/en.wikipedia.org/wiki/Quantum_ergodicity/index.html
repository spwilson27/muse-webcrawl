Quantum ergodicity

Quantum ergodicity is a property of eigenfunctions of a quantum system whose classical counterpart is ergodic. Roughly, it states that in the semiclassical (high-energy) limit, the eigenfunctions of a quantised ergodic Hamiltonian become uniformly distributed over the energy surface, apart from a possible subsequence of density zero.

Theorem

The quantum ergodicity theorem, proved by Schnirelman in 1974 and developed by Yves Colin de Verdière and Steve Zelditch, concerns a compact Riemannian manifold whose geodesic flow is ergodic with respect to Liouville measure. For an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian with eigenvalues tending to infinity, there is a subsequence of full density whose associated probability measures converge weakly to the normalised Liouville measure on the unit cotangent bundle.

Quantum unique ergodicity

The stronger property of quantum unique ergodicity (QUE) asserts that the exceptional subsequence does not exist: every sequence of eigenfunctions equidistributes. QUE was conjectured by Rudnick and Sarnak in 1994. It has been proved for arithmetic surfaces by Lindenstrauss (with complementary work by Soundararajan), but counterexamples exist for certain quantised maps, where "scarring" on periodic orbits produces non-uniform limits.

Significance

Quantum ergodicity is a central result in quantum chaos, describing how classical chaos manifests in the statistical behaviour of quantum eigenstates.
