Pteranodon is a genus of pterosaur that included some of the largest known flying reptiles, with wingspans of over 7 metres (23 feet) in the largest males. They lived during the Late Cretaceous geological period of North America in present-day Kansas, Alabama, Nebraska, Wyoming, and South Dakota. More fossil specimens of Pteranodon have been found than any other pterosaur, with about 1,200 known to science, many of them well preserved.

The name Pteranodon means "toothless wing" (from Greek pteron, wing, and an-odon, toothless), referring to its complete lack of teeth — a marked contrast with earlier pterosaurs such as Pterodactylus. Its most distinctive feature is the cranial crest, which in males of P. longiceps projected far back from the skull; the crest probably served primarily display functions in courtship and species recognition.

Discovery and history

The first Pteranodon fossils were collected in 1870–1871 from the Smoky Hill Chalk of Kansas by Othniel Charles Marsh's expedition and were first assigned to species of Pterodactylus. In 1876 Marsh recognised the toothlessness of the animal and erected the new genus Pteranodon. Fossils come chiefly from the Niobrara Formation, deposited when the Western Interior Seaway covered central North America.

Paleobiology

Pteranodon was a fish-eater that foraged over the inland sea, as shown by fish remains found in fossil stomach contents and in the throat pouches of specimens. Its long, toothless beak resembles that of modern fish-eating seabirds. On the ground it walked quadrupedally. Strong sexual dimorphism has been documented: large males with big crests and wide pelvises differ from smaller females with smaller crests and wider birth canals, and the two forms were once mistaken for separate species.
