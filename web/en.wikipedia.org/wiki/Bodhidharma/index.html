Bodhidharma was a semi-legendary Buddhist monk who lived during the 5th or 6th century CE and is traditionally credited with transmitting Chan Buddhism (Japanese Zen) to China. He is regarded as the first Chinese patriarch of Chan. Early sources describe him as a monk from "the Western Regions" — variously Central Asia, Persia or South India — who arrived in China and had an audience with Emperor Wu of Liang.

Legends

Famous anecdotes depict Bodhidharma's blunt interview with Emperor Wu ("Vast emptiness, nothing holy"), his crossing of the Yangtze standing on a reed, and nine years of silent wall-gazing meditation at the Shaolin Temple on Mount Song. Later tradition associates him with the physical exercises that became Shaolin kung fu and with treatises such as the Yijin Jing, though scholars regard these attributions as later developments. Texts such as the "Two Entrances and Four Acts" are traditionally ascribed to him.

Legacy

Bodhidharma is one of the most recognisable figures in East Asian Buddhism, depicted in art as a fierce-looking, bearded foreigner. In Japan he is the model for the Daruma doll, a symbol of perseverance. His teaching emphasised direct insight: "a special transmission outside the scriptures; not founded upon words and letters."
