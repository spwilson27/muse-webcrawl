Kowloon City

Kowloon City today, with the Walled City Park on the site of the former enclave

Kowloon City (Chinese: 九龍城) is an area of New Kowloon in Hong Kong, within Kowloon City District, lying north of the old Kai Tak Airport. The district takes its name — "Nine Dragons City" — from the eight peaks of Kowloon plus, in tradition, the last boy emperor of the Southern Song dynasty who took refuge here. It is best known internationally as the former site of the Kowloon Walled City.

History

The area was a Song-dynasty salt-production and coastal lookout point, and a Qing-dynasty garrison town was walled here in 1847. When Britain leased the New Territories in 1898, the walled city was nominally reserved for Qing officials; the resulting jurisdictional limbo let it grow after the Second World War into a virtually lawless enclave of interlocked high-rise buildings housing an estimated 33,000–50,000 people on 2.6 hectares — for a time the most densely populated place on Earth. The enclave was cleared and demolished in 1993–94 and replaced by the Kowloon Walled City Park, opened in 1995, preserving the old yamen building and carved gate plaques.

The district today

Modern Kowloon City is a largely residential neighbourhood celebrated for food: it is Hong Kong's "Little Thailand", with Thai groceries and restaurants serving the long-established Thai community, alongside Chiu Chow (Teochew) eateries, old-style cha chaan teng, and dessert shops. The closure of Kai Tak Airport in 1998 removed building-height restrictions and spurred redevelopment; the area is served by Sung Wong Toi and To Kwa Wan stations on the Tuen Ma line, opened in 2021.
