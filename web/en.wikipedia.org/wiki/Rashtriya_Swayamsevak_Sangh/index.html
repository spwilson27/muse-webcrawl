The Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (abbr. RSS; lit. "National Volunteer Organisation") is an Indian Hindu nationalist volunteer paramilitary organisation. It is widely regarded as the parent organisation of the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), and forms the centre of the Sangh Parivar, a family of affiliated organisations in education, labour, student politics, religion and social service. The RSS is one of the largest volunteer organisations in the world.

History

The RSS was founded in Nagpur in 1925 by Keshav Baliram Hedgewar, a physician and former Congress activist who had become disillusioned with what he saw as the passivity of Hindu society and the failures of the independence movement's communal politics. Hedgewar designed the organisation around the shakha, a daily local meeting combining physical exercise, games, drill and ideological discussion, intended to build character, discipline and unity among Hindus.

Hedgewar was succeeded on his death in 1940 by M. S. Golwalkar, under whom the organisation expanded nationwide. The RSS was banned in 1948 after the assassination of Mahatma Gandhi by Nathuram Godse, a former member; the ban was lifted in 1949 after the organisation adopted a written constitution and disavowed violence. It was banned twice more, during the Emergency of 1975–77 and in the aftermath of the demolition of the Babri Masjid in 1992, with both bans subsequently lifted.

Ideology

The RSS's ideology rests on Hindutva — the idea, articulated by V. D. Savarkar, that India is fundamentally a Hindu nation defined by a shared culture, history and sacred geography — and on the goal of organising Hindu society. It formally espouses integral humanism as elaborated by Deendayal Upadhyaya and presents itself as a cultural rather than political organisation. Critics, in India and abroad, have accused the RSS of fostering majoritarianism and of being implicated in communal violence; the organisation denies these charges and points to its extensive social service and disaster relief work.

Organisation and affiliates

Members (swayamsevaks) meet in shakhas, which by the organisation's own figures number in the tens of thousands across India. The Sangh Parivar affiliates include the BJP (political party), the Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP), the Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (student organisation, ABVP), the Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh (trade union), and the Vidya Bharati network of schools, as well as service organisations such as Seva Bharati. Many leading BJP figures, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi, began their public careers as RSS pracharaks (full-time workers).
