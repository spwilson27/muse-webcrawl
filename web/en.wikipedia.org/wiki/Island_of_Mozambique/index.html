Island of Mozambique

LocationNampula Province, northern Mozambique
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (1991)
Former roleCapital of Portuguese East Africa (until 1898)

The Island of Mozambique (Ilha de Moçambique) is a small coral island off northern Mozambique, about 3 km long, connected to the mainland by a bridge built in the 1960s. It gave its name to the whole country and was the capital of Portuguese East Africa until the capital moved to Lourenço Marques (Maputo) in 1898.

History

A Swahili trading port dealing in gold, ivory and slaves, the island was visited by Vasco da Gama in 1498. The Portuguese seized it and built the Chapel of Nossa Senhora do Baluarte (1522, among the oldest European buildings in the southern hemisphere) and the massive Fortress of São Sebastião (1558–1583). It became the key Portuguese base on the route to India, resisting Dutch attacks in the 1600s, and later a centre of the slave trade to Brazil and the French Indian Ocean colonies.

Stone Town

The northern half is a stone town of Portuguese colonial and Swahili-influenced buildings in coral stone and lime, including the Palace of São Paulo (1670), now a museum; the southern half is a makuti (palm-thatch) town. UNESCO listed the island as a World Heritage Site in 1991 for this unified architectural ensemble.

Today

Population is roughly 14,000. Fishing, tourism and heritage restoration sustain the island, which suffered damage from Cyclone Gombe in 2022.
