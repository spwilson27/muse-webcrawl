The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ) is an American independent, nonprofit organisation based in New York City that promotes press freedom and defends the rights of journalists worldwide. It was founded in 1981 in response to the harassment of Paraguayan journalist Alcibiades González Delvalle.

Work

CPJ documents attacks on the press — killings, imprisonments, abductions, censorship, and legal harassment — and maintains a widely cited database of journalists killed in connection with their work. It publishes annual reports, including its prison census of jailed journalists and the Attacks on the Press survey, and issues its Global Impunity Index ranking countries where journalists' killers go unpunished.

Advocacy and awards

The organisation campaigns for the release of imprisoned journalists, sends protest letters and fact-finding missions, and provides emergency assistance to journalists at risk. Each year it presents the International Press Freedom Awards, honouring journalists who have shown courage in defending press freedom despite attacks, threats, or imprisonment.
