Epidermal growth factor receptor

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR; also ErbB-1 or HER1 in humans) is a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that is activated by binding members of the epidermal growth factor family of protein ligands, including epidermal growth factor (EGF) and transforming growth factor α (TGFα). In humans it is encoded by the EGFR gene on chromosome 7.

Function

Upon ligand binding, EGFR dimerises and autophosphorylates tyrosine residues in its intracellular kinase domain, triggering downstream signalling pathways such as RAS–MAPK, PI3K–AKT, and STAT. These pathways regulate cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, and migration. EGFR signalling is essential in embryonic development; knockout mice show defects in skin, lung, brain, and gastrointestinal organs.

Role in cancer

Mutations and amplification of EGFR are common in human cancers, notably non-small-cell lung cancer, glioblastoma, and colorectal cancer. Activating mutations in the kinase domain sensitise lung tumours to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as gefitinib, erlotinib, afatinib, and osimertinib. Monoclonal antibodies against the extracellular domain, including cetuximab and panitumumab, are used in colorectal and head-and-neck cancers.
