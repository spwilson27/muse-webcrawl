Wang Geon (Korean: 왕건; January 31, 877 – July 4, 943), posthumously known by his temple name Taejo ("Grand Progenitor"), was the founder of the Goryeo dynasty, which ruled Korea from 918 to 1392. He unified the Korean Peninsula's Later Three Kingdoms in 935–936, ending the fragmentation that followed the decline of Silla.

Statue of Wang Geon (Taejo) in Daegu

Rise to power

Wang Geon was born in Songak (modern Kaesong) into a powerful merchant family that traded with China. He entered the service of Gung Ye, the ruler of the breakaway kingdom of Taebong, and distinguished himself as a naval commander in campaigns against Silla and Later Baekje. As Gung Ye grew tyrannical, Wang's generals deposed him in 918 and raised Wang Geon to the throne. He renamed the kingdom Goryeo — a revival of the name Goguryeo — and moved the capital to his home city of Songak.

Unification

Through diplomacy and warfare, Wang absorbed the ailing Silla kingdom, whose last king surrendered peacefully in 935, and destroyed Later Baekje in 936 after the decisive Battle of Illicheon. He pursued conciliatory policies toward former enemies, rewarded local strongmen (hojok), married into their families — he had 29 queens — and promoted Buddhism, notably ordering the construction of temples and the founding of the capital rituals. His "Ten Injunctions" (Hunyo Sipjo) advised his successors on governing, including respect for Buddhism, geomancy, and caution toward the powerful families of the northwest.

Legacy

Wang Geon's unification created a single Korean state whose name gave rise to the Western name "Korea". He died in 943 and was succeeded by his eldest son, Hyejong.
