The Xiph.Org Foundation is a non-profit organisation that produces free multimedia formats and software tools. Its projects are dedicated to providing publicly available, patent-free (royalty-free) alternatives to proprietary multimedia standards, and its work is released under permissive open-source licences.

Projects

The foundation's best-known products are the Ogg container format and the Vorbis audio codec, developed after Fraunhofer IIS announced licensing fees for MP3 in the late 1990s. Other major projects include FLAC (lossless audio, developed by Josh Coalson and adopted by Xiph), the Theora video codec (derived from On2's VP3), the Speex speech codec, the Icecast streaming server, and the cdparanoia CD-ripping library. Xiph was also a central contributor, along with Mozilla, Skype/Microsoft, Google, and others in the IETF working group, to the Opus audio codec, standardised in 2012 and now ubiquitous in web and voice communication. Later research included the Daala video codec, whose techniques fed into the AV1 format developed by the Alliance for Open Media.

Name

The name "Xiph" is short for Xiphophorus helleri, the green swordtail fish, reflected in the foundation's fish logo.
