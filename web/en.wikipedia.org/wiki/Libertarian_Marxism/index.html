Libertarian Marxism

Libertarian Marxism is a current of Marxist thought that emphasizes the anti-authoritarian and libertarian aspects of Karl Marx's ideas, stressing working-class self-emancipation and self-management rather than leadership by a vanguard party or a centralized state. It is closely related to council communism and overlaps with left communism and elements of autonomism.

Principles

Libertarian Marxists draw on Marx's dictum that "the emancipation of the working classes must be conquered by the working classes themselves", and on writings such as The Civil War in France, praising the Paris Commune's democratic, federal structure. They oppose both capitalism and the bureaucratic state socialism of the Soviet model, arguing that state ownership without workers' control merely substitutes a new ruling class. Preferred organizational forms include workers' councils, federated communes, and free association of producers.

Historical currents

Figures and movements associated with libertarian Marxism include Rosa Luxemburg, whose critiques of both Bolshevik authoritarianism and reformist social democracy emphasized mass spontaneity; the council communists Antonie Pannekoek, Otto Rühle, and Herman Gorter; Karl Korsch; the Johnson–Forest Tendency of C. L. R. James and Raya Dunayevskaya; Cornelius Castoriadis and the Socialisme ou Barbarie group; the Situationist International; and Italian workerism and autonomism. Daniel Guérin, who coined much of the terminology, sought a synthesis of Marxism and anarchism.

Relation to other traditions

Libertarian Marxism shares anarchism's hostility to the state and hierarchy but retains a Marxist analysis of capital, class, and historical materialism. It differs from Leninism in rejecting the vanguard party, and from democratic socialism in rejecting parliamentarism as the road to socialism, though positions vary among its currents.
