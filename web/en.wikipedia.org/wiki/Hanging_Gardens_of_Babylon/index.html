Overview

The Hanging Gardens of Babylon are described in classical sources as a remarkable feat of engineering with a series of tiered gardens on a great structure in Babylon, watered by screw-like or chain pumps raising Euphrates water, and counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Tradition credits Nebuchadnezzar II with building them for his homesick Median wife Amytis, who missed the green hills of her homeland.

The mystery

Unlike the other Wonders, the Gardens' very existence is unproven: no contemporary Babylonian text mentions them, and excavations at Babylon (notably Koldewey's vaulted structure) found no agreed remains. The accounts come from later Greek and Roman writers. Assyriologist Stephanie Dalley has argued the gardens were real but at Nineveh, built by Sennacherib a century earlier — whose inscriptions describe a palace garden, aqueducts (Jerwan) and water-raising screws — and conflated with Babylon by classical authors. The debate remains open and is a staple of ancient-world documentaries.
