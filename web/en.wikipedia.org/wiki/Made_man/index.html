Made man

A made man is a formally inducted member of the Mafia — in the Sicilian Mafia and the Italian-American Mafia. To become "made," an associate must be of Italian descent (in many families, full Italian ancestry on the paternal side was historically required), be sponsored by another made man, and take the oath of omertà, the code of silence.

Induction ceremony

In the traditional induction ceremony described by turncoats such as Joseph Valachi and Sammy Gravano, the inductee's trigger finger is pricked and his blood smeared on a picture of a saint, which is then burned in his hands while he swears that he will burn likewise if he betrays the organization. After induction, the made man — also called a soldier, or soldato — is entitled to the protection and privileges of his crime family, and he cannot be harmed by another made man without the permission of his family's boss.

Status

Made status traditionally protected a member from being killed on the orders of a non-member and guaranteed a share in the family's rackets. Books on prospective members were periodically "closed" (no new inductions permitted); the American Mafia's books were famously closed from 1957 until 1976. The term has passed into popular culture through films and television series such as Goodfellas and The Sopranos.
