Domiati

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Domiati (Arabic: جبنة دمياطي, gibna Domiati), also known as Gibna beyda ("white cheese"), is a soft white brined cheese originating in Damietta in northern Egypt. It is the most widely consumed cheese in Egypt, accounting for a large share of national production, and is eaten throughout the Middle East.

Production

Domiati is unusual among cheeses because salt is added directly to the milk — typically from cow, buffalo, or a mixture — before renneting, at levels of 5 to 14 percent. The curd is ladled into wooden moulds or tins, drained, and sold fresh as a soft, salty cheese, or stored in its own whey brine for several months to develop a firmer texture and deeper flavour. Aged Domiati kept in brine for a year or more develops into a strong-flavoured cheese related to mish.

Characteristics and use

Fresh Domiati is white, soft, and distinctly salty, with no rind. It is eaten at breakfast with fava beans (ful medames), tomatoes, and bread, fried in samna, or used in pastries and in dishes where it softens but holds its shape when heated.
