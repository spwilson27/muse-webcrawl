Sony Music Publishing

IndustryMusic publishing
Founded1995 (as Sony/ATV Music Publishing)
HeadquartersNew York City, United States
ParentSony Music Group

Sony Music Publishing is the largest music publishing company in the world, administering millions of copyrights for songwriters including the catalogues associated with the Beatles (through its historical administration), Queen, and many other artists. It was known as Sony/ATV Music Publishing from 1995 until 2021, when it rebranded to Sony Music Publishing.

History

The company's roots include Associated Television (ATV), whose Northern Songs catalogue held the Lennon–McCartney songwriting catalogue. Michael Jackson purchased ATV Music in 1985; in 1995 he merged it with Sony's publishing arm to form Sony/ATV, retaining a half share. Sony bought out the Jackson estate's stake in 2016, taking full ownership. In 2012 a Sony-led consortium acquired EMI Music Publishing, whose administration further enlarged the catalogue.

Operations

The company signs songwriters, administers copyrights, collects royalties worldwide, and licenses music for recordings, film, television, advertising, and stage productions. It operates offices in dozens of countries and represents writers across pop, rock, country, Latin, hip hop, and other genres.
