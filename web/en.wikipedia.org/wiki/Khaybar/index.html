Khaybar (Arabic: خيبر) is an oasis town in the Madinah Province of northwestern Saudi Arabia, about 150 kilometres (93 mi) north of Medina. Set among volcanic lava fields (harrat), it was historically one of the principal Jewish settlements of the Hejaz, famous for its fortresses, date palms, and springs, and is best known as the site of the Battle of Khaybar in 628 CE.

History

Settlement at Khaybar is ancient; tradition and epigraphy connect the oasis to early Arabian Jewish communities, and in the pre-Islamic period it was dominated by Jewish tribes, notably the Banu Nadir, Banu Qurayza-related clans, and the Banu Qaynuqa exiles who joined them. The oasis was divided into fortified strongholds such as al-Qamus, held by the Banu Abi'l-Huqayq family, whose leaders—Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq and his nephew Kinana ibn al-Rabi—were prominent opponents of Muhammad.

Battle of Khaybar

In 628 CE (7 AH) Muhammad led an army of about 1,600 men against Khaybar. The fortresses fell one by one over several weeks; tradition especially celebrates Ali ibn Abi Talib's role at the fortress of al-Qamus and the surrender arranged through negotiation. Under the settlement the Jews of Khaybar remained on their land as tenants, surrendering half their produce to Medina—a model later applied elsewhere. The surrender also brought the celebrated prisoner Safiyya bint Huyayy, who became one of Muhammad's wives. During the caliphate of Umar the Jewish community was expelled from Khaybar, ending its ancient presence, though small communities persisted elsewhere in Arabia for centuries.

Geography and today

The modern town lies among palm groves watered by dams and springs on the edge of the Harrat Khaybar lava field, an area also notable for prehistoric desert structures visible from the air. Ruins of the old fortresses and the historic Jewish quarter remain tourist attractions in a region once closed to non-Muslim visitors.
