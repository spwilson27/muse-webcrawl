Harbin

Harbin (Chinese: 哈尔滨; pinyin: Hā'ěrbīn) is the capital and largest city of Heilongjiang province in northeastern China, as well as the largest city in Northeast China by urban population. Located on the Songhua River, Harbin is renowned for its bitterly cold winters and is known as China's "Ice City"; it hosts the Harbin International Ice and Snow Festival, the world's largest winter festival. The city also carries a distinctive Russian architectural heritage from its origins as a hub of the Chinese Eastern Railway.

History

Harbin was founded in 1898 as a camp for engineers of the Russian-built Chinese Eastern Railway and rapidly grew into a cosmopolitan boomtown with large Russian, Jewish and other foreign communities. Its landmark Saint Sophia Cathedral, a Russian Orthodox church completed in 1932, remains a symbol of the city. During the Second Sino-Japanese War the area was occupied by Japan, whose notorious Unit 731 biological warfare research facility was based in the city's Pingfang district. After 1949 Harbin became a major centre of heavy industry in the People's Republic, aided by Soviet assistance in the 1950s.

Geography and climate

Harbin lies in southern Heilongjiang on the south bank of the Songhua River, on a flat alluvial plain. It has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate with very cold, dry winters — January means are around −18 °C (0 °F) — and warm, rainy summers. The long, freezing winters underlie the city's ice and snow culture, including the annual festival held since 1985 (with a forerunner in 1963) featuring enormous illuminated ice sculptures on Sun Island and at Ice and Snow World.

Economy and culture

Harbin is an industrial base for power-generation equipment, aircraft (the Harbin Aircraft Industry Group), pharmaceuticals and food processing, and serves as a trade gateway to Russia. It is a regional centre of education, home to the Harbin Institute of Technology, one of China's leading science and engineering universities. The city's cuisine, beer culture (Harbin Beer, founded 1900, is among China's oldest breweries), and Western-style architecture along Zhongyang Street reflect its mixed heritage. UNESCO named Harbin a City of Music in 2010.
