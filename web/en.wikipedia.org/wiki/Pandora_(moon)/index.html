Pandora is an inner satellite of Saturn, discovered in 1980 from photographs taken by the Voyager 1 probe and provisionally designated S/1980 S 26. It was named in 1985 after Pandora of Greek mythology. Pandora orbits just outside the F ring.

Orbit and the F ring

Long described as a shepherd satellite of the narrow F ring, together with Prometheus on the inner edge, Pandora's influence proved more complicated: Cassini observations showed Prometheus does most of the shepherding and periodically disturbs the ring, while Pandora's own orbit is chaotic owing to a series of resonances with Prometheus. Pandora has a mean radius of about 40 km and is heavily cratered, with craters up to 30 km across — large relative to its size, suggesting an ancient surface. Its low density and high albedo indicate a porous body of mostly water ice.

Exploration

The best images come from the Cassini spacecraft, which made its closest approach to Pandora (about 2,700 km) in its final "Ring-Grazing Orbits" phase in late 2016, revealing a smoother, dust-mantled surface in some regions.
