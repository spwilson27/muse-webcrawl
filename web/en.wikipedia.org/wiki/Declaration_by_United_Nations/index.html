Declaration by United Nations

Signed1 January 1942, Washington, D.C.
Original signatories26 nations
PartiesThe Allies of World War II

The Declaration by United Nations was the main treaty that formalised the Allies of World War II. It was signed on 1 January 1942 in Washington, D.C., by representatives of 26 nations, pledging each government to employ its full resources against the Axis powers and not to make a separate armistice or peace.

Content and drafting

The declaration endorsed the principles of the Atlantic Charter of August 1941. The name "United Nations" for the Allied coalition was suggested by President Franklin D. Roosevelt — reportedly derived from Byron's poem Childe Harold's Pilgrimage — and the first signatories were the "Big Four" (the United States, United Kingdom, Soviet Union and China) followed by 22 other nations; more countries adhered over the following years.

Legacy

The declaration established the wartime alliance and provided the name and conceptual basis for the modern United Nations, founded in 1945; wartime documents often used "United Nations" simply to mean the Allies.
