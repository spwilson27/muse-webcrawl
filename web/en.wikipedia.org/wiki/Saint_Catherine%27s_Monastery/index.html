Saint Catherine's Monastery

Saint Catherine's Monastery, officially the Sacred Monastery of Saint Catherine, is a Christian monastery located in the Sinai Peninsula of Egypt, at the foot of Mount Sinai. Founded between 548 and 565 AD by order of the emperor Justinian I, it is one of the oldest working Christian monasteries in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

LocationSinai Peninsula, Egypt
Founded548–565 AD
FounderJustinian I
DenominationEastern Orthodox (Church of Sinai)

Library and treasures

The monastery's library preserves the second-largest collection of early codices and manuscripts in the world, exceeded only by the Vatican Library. The Codex Sinaiticus, one of the oldest complete Bibles (4th century), was held here until the 19th century. The site is sacred to Christianity, Islam and Judaism, being associated with Moses and the Burning Bush, and contains a mosque built within its walls.
