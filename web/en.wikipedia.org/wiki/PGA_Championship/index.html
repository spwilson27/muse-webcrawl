The PGA Championship is one of the four major championships in professional golf, organised by the PGA of America and first played in 1916. Uniquely among the majors, it fields only professional golfers — no amateurs — and reserves places for club professionals who qualify through their own championship. The winner receives the Wanamaker Trophy, the largest in major golf, and a lifetime exemption into the event. Since 2019 the championship has been held in May, as the second major of the season between the Masters and the U.S. Open.

History

The championship began as a match-play event, in which Walter Hagen won five titles in the 1920s, and switched to stroke play in 1958. Jack Nicklaus matched Hagen's five wins, and Tiger Woods won four, including back-to-back titles in 1999–2000 and 2006–2007. The event has a reputation for breakthrough winners: John Daly's victory as the ninth alternate in 1991, and Y. E. Yang's defeat of Woods in 2009 — the first major won by an Asian-born male player — are among its defining stories. Recent hosts have included Valhalla, Quail Hollow and Oak Hill.

Format

The PGA Championship is a 72-hole stroke-play tournament with a field of about 156 players, drawn from the world rankings, recent form, past champions and 20 PGA club professionals. Its slogan in earlier decades, "Glory's Last Shot", reflected its old August position as the season's final major.
