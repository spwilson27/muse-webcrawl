Trinitatis Church

Trinitatis Church (Danish: Trinitatis Kirke) is a 17th-century Lutheran church in central Copenhagen, Denmark. It forms part of the Trinitatis Complex, built for Christian IV, which also includes the Round Tower observatory and a university library, uniting a church, astronomy, and learning in a single building programme.

History

Construction began in 1637 under master builder Hans van Steenwinckel the Younger, and the church was consecrated on Trinity Sunday 1656. The complex was intended to serve students and professors of the University of Copenhagen. The church escaped the great Copenhagen fire of 1728 but was damaged during the British bombardment of 1807; the university library in the loft above the nave survived only in part.

Architecture and interior

The church is a long brick building in Dutch Baroque style with a bright, high nave. The library loft above the church — a hall about 80 metres long — now hosts exhibitions and concerts. The organ, altarpiece, and the astronomical clock are among its notable features.
