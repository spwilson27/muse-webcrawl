Flag of Pakistan

The flag of Pakistan (Urdu: پرچمِ ستارہ و ہلال, "flag of the star and crescent") is a dark green field bearing a white crescent moon and a five-pointed star in its centre, with a vertical white stripe at the hoist side equal to one quarter of the flag's length. It was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 11 August 1947, three days before Pakistan's independence from British rule, and is based on the flag of the All-India Muslim League.

Symbolism

The dark green represents the Muslim majority of the country, while the white stripe represents Pakistan's religious minorities and their equal place in the state — a symbolism stressed by Muhammad Ali Jinnah in his address to the Constituent Assembly on the day of adoption. The crescent stands for progress and the five-pointed star for light and knowledge. The flag's proportions are 2:3.

History

The design descends from the Muslim League flag of 1906, which was all green with the crescent and star; the white stripe was added to form the national flag in 1947. The flag is frequently credited to Amiruddin Kidwai, who adapted the League's banner. Pakistan's flag protocol governs its flying on national days — including Pakistan Day (23 March), Independence Day (14 August) and Quaid-e-Azam Day (25 December) — and at half-mast on days of mourning. In 2004 a monumental Pakistani flag at Wagah and other giant flags at border posts and in Islamabad became points of national display; the flag raised at the Pakistan Monument and the 400-foot flag at Wagah are among the largest in South Asia.

National anthem and pledge

The flag is honoured in a short flag anthem performed at flag-hoisting ceremonies, distinct from the national anthem, "Qaumi Taranah". Schoolchildren across Pakistan traditionally begin the day with the flag raised and the anthem sung.
