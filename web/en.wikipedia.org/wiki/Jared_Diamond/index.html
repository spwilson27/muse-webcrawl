Jared Diamond

Jared Diamond lecturing at a university

Jared Mason Diamond (born September 10, 1937) is an American scientist, historian, and author known for popular books that seek broad environmental and geographical explanations for the differing fates of human societies. A professor of geography at the University of California, Los Angeles, he trained as a physiologist and also works in ornithology and evolutionary biology.

Career

Diamond earned his doctorate in physiology from Cambridge in 1961 and became a leading authority on the birds of New Guinea, leading many expeditions there. His scientific work spans membrane physiology, evolutionary biology, and biogeography. He has written for general audiences since the 1980s, contributing columns on science and field experience.

Books

His book The Third Chimpanzee (1991) examined human evolution and won the Royal Society's Rhone-Poulenc Prize. Guns, Germs, and Steel (1997) argued that geography — the availability of domesticable plants and animals, continental axes, and disease pools — explains why Eurasian societies came to dominate; it won the Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction in 1998 and was adapted into a television documentary. Critics, including many anthropologists and geographers, have charged the work with environmental determinism and factual oversimplification, debates that have followed his later books as well.

Collapse (2005) studied how societies choose to fail or succeed in the face of environmental problems; The World Until Yesterday (2012) compared modern states with traditional societies; and Upheaval (2019) examined how nations respond to crisis. Diamond has received the National Medal of Science (1999) and a MacArthur Fellowship (1985).
