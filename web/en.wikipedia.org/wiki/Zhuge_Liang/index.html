Zhuge Liang (181–234), courtesy name Kongming, nicknamed "Crouching Dragon", was a Chinese statesman, strategist and inventor who served as Chancellor and regent of the state of Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms period. He is recognised as the most accomplished strategist of his era and is often compared to Sun Tzu; his reputation as an erudite, noble and loyal minister has made him an enduring cultural icon in China.

Career

Living in seclusion as a farmer-scholar, Zhuge Liang was recruited by the warlord Liu Bei after the famous "Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage". His Longzhong Plan proposed the tripartite division of China that came to pass. He engineered the Sun–Liu alliance that defeated Cao Cao at the Battle of Red Cliffs (208). After Liu Bei founded Shu Han, Zhuge Liang governed as chancellor, administered the conquest of the southwest, and — after Liu Bei's death — ruled as regent for his son Liu Shan.

Northern Expeditions and death

Between 228 and 234 Zhuge Liang launched five Northern Expeditions against the rival state of Cao Wei, seeking to restore the Han dynasty. Despite logistical ingenuity — he is credited with inventions including the wooden ox transport device and the sky lantern (Kongming lantern) — the campaigns failed to break Wei. He died of illness at the Wuzhang Plains in 234 during the final expedition.

Legacy

Zhuge Liang was deified in popular culture, most famously in the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which magnifies his wisdom to near-supernatural levels. His memorials, such as his Chu Shi Biao (Memorials on Dispatching Troops), remain classics of Chinese prose.
