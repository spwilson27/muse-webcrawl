Queen (Snow White)

The Evil Queen
First appearance: Snow White (1812)
Created by: Brothers Grimm
Also known as: The Evil Queen; Queen Grimhilde (Disney)

The Queen, often referred to as the Evil Queen or the Wicked Queen, is the main antagonist of the German fairy tale "Snow White", first published by the Brothers Grimm in 1812. She is Snow White's stepmother, a vain and jealous sorceress who cannot bear that her stepdaughter's beauty surpasses her own.

Role in the tale

The Queen owns a magic mirror which she asks daily, "Mirror, mirror on the wall, who is the fairest of them all?" When the mirror names Snow White, the Queen orders a huntsman to kill the girl and bring back her lungs and liver as proof. The huntsman spares Snow White, and the Queen, eating the organs of a boar in the belief they are Snow White's, learns from the mirror that the girl still lives.

Disguising herself, the Queen makes three attempts on Snow White's life: with suffocating bodice laces, a poisoned comb, and finally a poisoned apple, which lodges in Snow White's throat and leaves her in a deathlike sleep. When Snow White is revived and marries a prince, the Queen is invited to the wedding, where she is forced to dance in red-hot iron shoes until she falls down dead. In the first edition of 1812 the villain was Snow White's own mother; later editions made her a stepmother.

Disney version

In Walt Disney's Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) the Queen, given the name Grimhilde in later publications, transforms herself into an old hag to deliver the poisoned apple, and dies falling from a cliff while fleeing the dwarfs. She was voiced by Lucille La Verne and was the first Disney villain in a feature film, setting the pattern for later antagonists.

Legacy

The character is one of the archetypal fairy-tale villains and has been reinterpreted in many retellings, films, and stage adaptations, sometimes sympathetically. Her magic mirror and the "fairest of them all" formula are among the most widely quoted motifs from the Grimms' collection.
