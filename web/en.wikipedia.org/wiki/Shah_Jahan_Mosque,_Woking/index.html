Shah Jahan Mosque, Woking

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Shah Jahan Mosque (also known as the Woking Mosque) on Oriental Road in Woking, England, is the first purpose-built mosque in the United Kingdom and the first in Northern Europe. It was built in 1889 by the Orientalist Gottlieb Wilhelm Leitner and largely financed by the Begum Shah Jahan, the female ruler of the princely state of Bhopal in India, after whom it is named. It is a Grade I listed building.

History

Leitner, a linguist who had founded an institute for Indian civil service candidates at Woking, commissioned the architect William Isaac Chambers to design the mosque in an Indo-Saracenic style, with an onion dome and minarets, in Bath and Bargate stone. After Leitner's death in 1899 the mosque fell into disuse and was nearly demolished. In 1912–13 the Indian lawyer Khwaja Kamal-ud-Din, supported by the Woking Muslim Mission, restored and reopened it; it became a centre of Islam in Britain, publishing the journal The Islamic Review and hosting Eid prayers attended by Muslims from across the country.

Significance

For much of the early twentieth century the Woking mosque was the best-known Islamic institution in Britain, attracting converts and visiting dignitaries, and its imams included figures such as Marmaduke Pickthall, translator of the Quran. Today it remains an active place of worship serving the local community and is managed by the Sunni Muslim community of Woking.
