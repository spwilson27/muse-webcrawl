Roberto Canessa


Born: January 17, 1953, Montevideo, Uruguay
Occupation: Paediatric cardiologist, motivational speaker
Known for: Survivor of Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571

Roberto Jorge Canessa Urta (born January 17, 1953) is a Uruguayan paediatric cardiologist and former rugby player, known internationally as one of the sixteen survivors of Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571, which crashed in the Andes in October 1972.

The Andes disaster

Canessa, then a 19-year-old medical student travelling with the Old Christians rugby team, survived the crash and the 72 days that followed in the wreckage at over 3,500 metres. With Nando Parrado, he made the ten-day trek westward over the mountains into Chile in December 1972, a feat of endurance that brought rescuers back to the remaining survivors. During the ordeal he provided much of the group's improvised medical care.

Later life

Canessa completed his medical studies and became a leading paediatric cardiologist and foetal medicine specialist in Uruguay, recognised for work on prenatal diagnosis of congenital heart disease. He ran for president of Uruguay in 1994. A frequent motivational speaker, he told his story in the memoir I Had to Survive (2016). He was portrayed in the films Alive (1993) and Society of the Snow (2023).
