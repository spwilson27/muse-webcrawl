Locksport

Locksport is the sport or recreation of defeating locking systems. Its practitioners study, compete in, and enjoy the hobby of picking locks, typically in timed competitions, and advocate awareness of the vulnerabilities of mechanical locks in order to improve physical security. The locks used in locksport events are generally standard commercial locks deliberately manipulated by the participants' lock picks and tension tools.

History

Recreational lock picking has roots in 19th-century contests publicised by lock makers such as Joseph Bramah and Alfred Charles Hobbs, whose public defeat of supposedly unpickable locks at the Great Exhibition of 1851 shaped the modern debate about disclosure of security flaws. Organised modern locksport groups include the German Sportsfreunde der Sperrtechnik (SSDeV, founded 1997), the Dutch TOOOL (The Open Organisation Of Lockpickers, founded 2002), and US groups such as Locksport International and regional TOOOL chapters, which run the popular "Lockpick Village" at DEF CON and similar hacker conventions.

Ethics

Locksport communities promote a strict code of ethics: members may only pick locks they own or have explicit permission to manipulate, and never locks in use or relied upon for real security. The community draws a clear distinction between the hobby and illicit entry, and has cooperated with lock manufacturers on responsible disclosure of vulnerabilities.

Competitions

Competitions include timed individual and team picking of pin-tumbler, wafer, and lever locks; impressioning contests (filing a working key from a blank); safe-cracking events; and "freestyle" categories. German SSDeV championships and events at conventions such as LockCon codify rules, tool restrictions, and scoring.
