Santana (band)

Santana
Origin: San Francisco, California, U.S.
Formed: 1966
Genres: Latin rock, blues rock, jazz fusion
Leader: Carlos Santana

Santana is an American rock band formed in San Francisco in 1966 by Mexican-born guitarist Carlos Santana. The band pioneered a fusion of rock and Latin American jazz, built around Santana's melodic, sustain-laden guitar lines over Latin and Afro-Cuban percussion rhythms.

History

Santana shot to fame with its performance at the Woodstock festival in 1969, followed by hit albums Santana (1969), Abraxas (1970) — featuring "Black Magic Woman" and "Oye Cómo Va" — and Santana III (1971). Through the 1970s the band moved toward jazz fusion on albums such as Caravanserai (1972). After a commercial decline, Santana enjoyed a spectacular comeback with Supernatural (1999), whose singles "Smooth" (with Rob Thomas) and "Maria Maria" topped the charts; the album won nine Grammy Awards, including Album of the Year, and ranks among the best-selling albums of all time.

Legacy

With a frequently changing line-up around Carlos Santana, the band has sold more than 100 million records worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1998.
