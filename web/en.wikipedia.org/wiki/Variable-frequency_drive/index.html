Variable-frequency drive

Also calledVFD, adjustable-speed drive, inverter drive
FunctionControls AC motor speed and torque
ApplicationsPumps, fans, conveyors, HVAC, machine tools

A variable-frequency drive (VFD) is a type of motor drive that controls the speed and torque of an AC electric motor by varying the frequency and voltage of its power supply. VFDs are widely used in industry to match motor speed to process demand, saving large amounts of energy in pumps and fans.

Operation

A VFD first rectifies incoming AC power to DC in a converter section, smooths it in a DC link (usually with capacitors), then uses an inverter — typically IGBT transistors switching with pulse-width modulation — to synthesise a variable-frequency, variable-voltage AC output. Because an induction motor's speed is set by supply frequency, varying the frequency varies the speed, while the voltage is adjusted to keep the voltage-to-frequency ratio (and hence flux and torque) roughly constant.

Benefits and issues

Energy savings follow the affinity laws: reducing a fan or centrifugal pump's speed by 20% can cut power use by nearly half. VFDs also provide soft starting, precise process control, and reduced mechanical wear. Drawbacks include harmonic distortion of the supply, electromagnetic interference, and bearing currents or voltage reflections that can damage motors on long cable runs; filters and proper installation mitigate these.
