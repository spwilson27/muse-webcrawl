The London Borough of Southwark (/ˈsʌðərk/) is an Inner London borough on the south bank of the River Thames, facing the City of London across the river. It was created in 1965 from the former metropolitan boroughs of Southwark, Bermondsey, and Camberwell. Southwark (the name derives from the Old English for "southern defensive work") is one of the oldest parts of London, a bridgehead settlement since Roman times and, in Shakespeare's day, the home of Bankside's theatres and entertainments outside the City's jurisdiction.

Districts and landmarks

The borough's riverside includes London Bridge, Borough Market, Southwark Cathedral, Shakespeare's Globe, and the Tate Modern gallery in the former Bankside power station; the 310-metre Shard, the tallest building in the United Kingdom, stands at London Bridge station. Further inland lie Elephant and Castle, Walworth, Peckham, Camberwell, and the affluent village-like suburb of Dulwich, home to Dulwich Picture Gallery and Dulwich College. The Imperial War Museum, the Old Vic theatre, and much of the South Bank cultural quarter around Waterloo also fall within or on the edge of the borough.

Southwark has a population of around 307,000 and is ethnically diverse, with long-established Black African and Caribbean communities (particularly in Peckham and Camberwell), a Latin American community around Elephant and Castle, and rapidly gentrifying districts along the river. Southwark Council, Labour-controlled since 2010, is a major social housing landlord, and the borough contains both the City-facing office district of London Bridge and some of inner London's largest council estates.
