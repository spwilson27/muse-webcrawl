Parthenia (music)

Title page of Parthenia, or the Maydenhead of the first musicke that ever was printed for the Virginalls (1612/13).

Parthenia, or The Maydenhead of the first musicke that ever was printed for the Virginalls, is a collection of keyboard music published in London in 1612/13. It was the first printed collection of music for the virginals (a type of harpsichord) in England, engraved on copper plates by William Hole.

Contents

The collection contains 21 pieces by the three leading English keyboard composers of the day: William Byrd, John Bull and Orlando Gibbons. The pieces — pavans, galliards, preludes and variations — were composed for the virginals and represent the culmination of the Elizabethan and Jacobean virginalist school. The title, from the Greek parthenos (maiden/virgin), puns on the instrument's name and the collection's status as the "maidenhead" (first) of printed virginal music.

Significance

Parthenia was presented in connection with the marriage of Princess Elizabeth, daughter of James I, to Frederick V, Elector Palatine, in February 1613. It was reprinted several times during the seventeenth century, and a companion volume, Parthenia In-Violata, for viol and virginals, appeared around 1625. Modern editions have made the collection a cornerstone of the early keyboard repertoire.
