Oakland is a city in the U.S. state of California, the county seat of Alameda County, and a major West Coast port. Located across San Francisco Bay from San Francisco, it is the third-largest city in the Bay Area and the eighth-most populous city in California, with a population of about 440,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Ohlone people. Oakland was incorporated in 1852 and grew rapidly as the western terminus of the transcontinental railroad (1869) and after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, when many displaced residents moved across the bay. The Port of Oakland, one of the busiest container ports in the United States, and the city's manufacturing and shipbuilding boomed during the Second World War, drawing large numbers of African American migrants; Oakland became a centre of West Coast blues, civil rights activism, and the Black Panther Party, founded there in 1966.

Geography and culture

The city rises from the bayshore flatlands into the Oakland Hills. Landmarks include Lake Merritt, a tidal lagoon ringed by parkland in the city centre; Jack London Square, named for the author who grew up in Oakland; and the historic Paramount and Fox theatres. Oakland was long home to three major-league sports teams — the Raiders (NFL), Athletics (MLB), and Warriors (NBA) — all of which departed between 2019 and 2025. The city is noted for its large artist community, its restaurants, and its diverse neighbourhoods.
