Cot–caught merger

The cot–caught merger, also known as the low back merger, is a sound change in English in which the vowels in words such as cot /kɒt/ and caught /kɔːt/ are pronounced identically, making the words homophones.

Distribution

The merger is complete or nearly complete in most of Canada, in western New England historically, and in much of the western United States; it is spreading in the Midland and parts of the South. It remains absent in the traditional accents of the Northeast, the Great Lakes region and the United Kingdom.

Effects

With the merger, pairs such as Don/dawn, stock/stalk and collar/caller become homophones, and the phonemic inventory of English loses the /ɔ/ vowel in those dialects.
