Coming of age

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A debutante ball, one of many coming-of-age rituals

Coming of age is a young person's transition from childhood to adulthood, marked in many cultures by a rite of passage. The point at which a person legally becomes an adult — the age of majority, usually 18 — is only one aspect; religious ceremonies, social debuts and traditional ordeals also confer or celebrate adult status. The concept supplies the theme of the Bildungsroman and of "coming-of-age" stories in literature and film, which trace a protagonist's growth from youth to maturity.

Religious and traditional rites

  • Bar and Bat Mitzvah (Judaism) — at 13 (boys) and 12–13 (girls), a child becomes responsible for observing the commandments.
  • Confirmation (many Christian churches) — affirmation of baptismal vows in adolescence.
  • Seijin no Hi / Coming of Age Day (Japan) — a national holiday honouring those who have reached 18 (formerly 20).
  • Quinceañera (Latin America) — a girl's 15th birthday celebration marking passage into womanhood.
  • Walkabout (Aboriginal Australian tradition) and vision quests (some Indigenous North American cultures) — solitary journeys or ordeals marking male maturity.
  • Debutante balls and cotillions — formal presentation of young women to society.

Legal coming of age

The age of majority, the voting age (commonly 18, but 16 in a few countries), ages of consent, criminal responsibility, and the minimum ages for driving, military service, alcohol and marriage vary widely between and within countries. Historically 21 was the common age of majority in the English-speaking world, lowered to 18 in most jurisdictions during the late 20th century.

In fiction

The coming-of-age narrative, or Bildungsroman (e.g. Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship), is a major literary form: examples include Dickens's Great Expectations, Salinger's The Catcher in the Rye and Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird. Coming-of-age films form a recognised genre, from Rebel Without a Cause and Stand by Me to Lady Bird and Moonlight.
