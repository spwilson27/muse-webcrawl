Malayan Communist Party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Malayan Communist Party (MCP), officially the Communist Party of Malaya (CPM; Malay: Parti Komunis Malaya), was a political party in Malaya and Singapore founded in 1930. It laid the foundation for an armed resistance against Japanese occupation and later against British colonial rule.

History

The party emerged from the South Seas Communist Party under the influence of the Comintern. During the Japanese occupation (1942–1945), the MCP organised the Malayan People's Anti-Japanese Army (MPAJA), which received Allied support. After the war, the party's secretary-general Lai Teck disappeared with party funds in 1947 and was succeeded by Chin Peng.

Malayan Emergency

In June 1948, following a campaign of strikes and assassinations, the British declared a State of Emergency. The MCP's armed wing, the Malayan National Liberation Army, waged a guerrilla war from jungle bases until the Emergency was declared over in 1960. The party continued a lower-level insurgency from sanctuaries along the Malaysia–Thailand border.

Dissolution

On 2 December 1989, Chin Peng signed a peace agreement with the governments of Malaysia and Thailand at Hat Yai, formally ending the insurgency, and the MCP was dissolved.
