Marc Bolan (born Mark Feld; 30 September 1947 – 16 September 1977) was an English singer, songwriter, musician, and poet. He was a pioneer of the glam rock movement in the early 1970s as the founder, guitarist, and frontman of the band T. Rex, whose hits — "Ride a White Swan," "Hot Love," "Get It On (Bang a Gong)," "Telegram Sam," "Metal Guru," "Children of the Revolution," and "20th Century Boy" — made him one of Britain's biggest pop stars of the era, spawning the hysteria dubbed "T. Rextasy."

Career

Feld took up guitar as a skiffle-mad schoolboy, briefly modelled, and recorded unsuccessful solo singles for Decca and EMI in the mid-1960s. After a stint in the proto-punk band John's Children (1967), he formed the acoustic duo Tyrannosaurus Rex with percussionist Steve Peregrin Took, singing whimsical, Tolkien- and mythology-saturated songs; with Mickey Finn replacing Took, the duo won underground acclaim. In 1970 Bolan electrified, shortened the name to T. Rex, and with producer Tony Visconti crafted the boogie-driven glam sound: "Ride a White Swan" reached No. 2 in the UK, and the albums Electric Warrior (1971) and The Slider (1972) defined glam rock's glitter, satin, and platform-booted image, influencing punk and Britpop artists for decades.

Decline, revival, and death

By the mid-1970s Bolan's commercial fortunes waned amid musical experiments and personal difficulties, but his 1977 TV series Marc and a punk-friendly comeback were under way when, on 16 September 1977, he was killed as a passenger in a car crash in Barnes, London, two weeks before his 30th birthday. The site, "Bolan's Rock Shrine," remains a place of pilgrimage. T. Rex was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2020.
