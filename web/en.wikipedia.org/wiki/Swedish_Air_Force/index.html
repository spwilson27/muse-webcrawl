The Swedish Air Force (Swedish: Svenska flygvapnet) is the air force branch of the Swedish Armed Forces. Formed in 1926 from the aviation units of the army and navy, it grew during the Cold War into one of the world's largest air forces, reflecting Sweden's policy of armed neutrality. Uniquely among Western air forces, it has operated an almost unbroken line of indigenously designed combat aircraft built by Saab, from the piston-engined B 17 to the JAS 39 Gripen.

History

During the Second World War the air force expanded rapidly to guard Swedish neutrality. The Cold War brought large fleets of Swedish jets — the Saab 29 Tunnan (the first European swept-wing fighter in service), the Saab 32 Lansen, and the distinctive double-delta Saab 35 Draken — supported by the Bas 60 and later Bas 90 systems of dispersed wartime road bases designed to survive Warsaw Pact attack. At its 1950s peak the force fielded roughly 1,000 combat aircraft. The multirole Saab 37 Viggen served from 1971 to 2005, when the canard-delta JAS 39 Gripen became the sole fighter type.

Post-Cold War reductions shrank the force to two front-line Gripen wings (F 7 Såtenäs and F 21 Luleå, with F 17 Kallinge), but Sweden's accession to NATO in 2024 — after two centuries of non-alignment — has brought renewed investment, including the Gripen E, GlobalEye airborne early-warning aircraft, and a planned return to a larger wartime organisation with reactivated bases. Swedish Gripens have deployed abroad, including reconnaissance missions over Libya in 2011 under Operation Unified Protector, and the air force contributes combat controllers and transport aircraft (Tp 84 Hercules) to international operations.

Organisation and equipment

The Air Force comprises fighter wings, a helicopter wing (based at Linköping and Luleå, operating HKP 14/NH90 and HKP 16/Black Hawk), air surveillance and command-and-control units, and the Air Force Ranger and base-security companies. Roundel: three golden crowns on a blue field — a symbol dating to the 1930s.
