Superga air disaster

The Superga air disaster occurred on 4 May 1949, when a Fiat G.212 airliner carrying the Torino football team crashed into the retaining wall at the rear of the Basilica of Superga on a hill overlooking Turin, Italy. All 31 people on board died, including 18 players of the celebrated Grande Torino team, club officials, coaches, journalists, and the flight crew.

Background

The Grande Torino was the dominant team in Italian football, having won five consecutive Serie A championships (with an interruption for the Second World War) and supplying the core of the Italian national team. The squad was returning from Lisbon, Portugal, where it had played a friendly match against Benfica arranged as a testimonial for the Portuguese captain Francisco Ferreira.

The crash

On approach to Turin in thick fog, low cloud, and strong winds, the aircraft descended below its safe altitude. At around 17:03 it struck the rear wall of the Basilica of Superga, situated at about 675 metres above sea level. Poor visibility, a faulty altimeter reading, and wind drift have all been cited as contributing factors. There were no survivors.

Aftermath

The disaster devastated Italian football. Torino was awarded the 1948–49 scudetto at the request of its rivals, completing the season with its youth team, and its opponents fielded youth sides in respect. The Italian national team, which lost many of its finest players — including captain Valentino Mazzola — was deeply weakened ahead of the 1950 World Cup; thereafter the national side famously travelled to tournaments by ship for some years.

Legacy

A memorial at the Basilica of Superga commemorates the victims, and annual ceremonies on 4 May draw Torino supporters. The phrase "Grande Torino" remains a byword for one of the finest club sides in football history, and the tragedy is often compared with the later Munich air disaster of 1958.
