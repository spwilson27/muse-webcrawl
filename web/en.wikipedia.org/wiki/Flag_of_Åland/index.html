The flag of Åland is the flag of the autonomous Swedish-speaking Finnish region of Åland. It is a Nordic cross flag combining the colours of its neighbours: a red cross on a blue field, with the cross outlined (fimbriated) in yellow. The design symbolises Åland's dual heritage — the blue field and yellow come from the flag of Sweden, reflecting the islands' Swedish language and culture, while the red cross comes from the arms and flag of Finland, the state to which Åland belongs.

History

Åland's autonomy within Finland was settled by the League of Nations in 1921, but the islands did not initially possess their own flag; an unofficial blue-yellow-blue horizontal tricolour dating from 1922 was used at song festivals and was later prohibited by Finnish authorities in 1935. A commission appointed by the Åland provincial government proposed designs in the early 1950s, and the present flag, based on a proposal associating the Swedish and Finnish colours in a Nordic cross, was adopted in 1954 and first officially raised on 3 April 1954 in Mariehamn. The flag is regulated by provincial law, and its flag day is the last Sunday in April.

Usage

The Åland flag may be flown freely by residents, and Åland-registered ships use it as an ensign. It flies alongside the Finnish flag on official buildings in the islands, particularly on Åland Autonomy Day, 9 June.
