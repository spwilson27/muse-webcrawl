Energy content of biofuel

UnitsMJ/kg, MJ/L

The energy content of biofuel is the amount of chemical energy released when a given quantity of biofuel is burned. It is typically expressed in megajoules per kilogram or per litre, and allows biofuels such as ethanol, biodiesel, and biogas to be compared with each other and with fossil fuels.

Typical values

Ethanol contains roughly 21 MJ per litre (about 27 MJ/kg), noticeably less than petrol at roughly 34 MJ per litre, which is why ethanol-blended fuels generally deliver poorer fuel economy per litre. Biodiesel, at roughly 33–37 MJ per litre, is close to petroleum diesel. Dry wood and other solid biomass fuels fall around 15–20 MJ/kg, while methane-rich biogas derives its value from its methane fraction.

Measurement

Energy content is measured calorimetrically as a higher heating value (including latent heat recovered from condensed water) or a lower heating value (excluding it); reported figures vary with feedstock, moisture content, and processing. Net energy assessments of biofuels also subtract the energy used in cultivation, fertiliser production, and refining.
