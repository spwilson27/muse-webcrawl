Jodie Foster

Born: Alicia Christian Foster, 19 November 1962, Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Education: Yale University (BA)

Occupation: Actress, filmmaker

Years active: 1965–present

Alicia Christian "Jodie" Foster (born November 19, 1962) is an American actress and filmmaker. Regarded as one of the greatest actresses of her generation, she has won two Academy Awards for Best Actress, for The Accused (1988) and The Silence of the Lambs (1991), in which she played FBI trainee Clarice Starling.

Career

Foster began acting as a small child in commercials and television, and gained critical attention at age twelve as a child prostitute in Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver (1976), earning an Oscar nomination. After graduating magna cum laude from Yale University in 1985, she moved into adult roles with acclaimed performances in Little Man Tate (1991), which she also directed, Contact (1997), and Panic Room (2002). Later work includes The Mauritanian (2021), Nyad (2023), and the television series True Detective: Night Country (2024), for which she won an Emmy Award.

Personal life

Foster is known for guarding her privacy. In 1981 she was the object of obsession of John Hinckley Jr., who attempted to assassinate President Ronald Reagan to impress her. She married photographer Alexandra Hedison in 2014 and has two sons.
