In music theory, the term mode or modus refers to a type of musical scale coupled with a set of characteristic melodic and harmonic behaviours. The modern Western modes consist of the seven rotations of the diatonic scale: starting on successive degrees of the major scale yields the Ionian (major), Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian (natural minor), and Locrian modes, each with a distinctive pattern of whole and half steps and a characteristic sound.

The seven modern modes derived from the diatonic scale.

The diatonic modes

  • Ionian — identical to the major scale; bright and resolved.
  • Dorian — a minor scale with a raised sixth; common in jazz, folk, and rock (e.g. "So What" by Miles Davis).
  • Phrygian — a minor scale with a lowered second; associated with Spanish flamenco colour.
  • Lydian — a major scale with a raised fourth; dreamy or floating quality.
  • Mixolydian — a major scale with a lowered seventh; pervasive in blues, rock, and folk.
  • Aeolian — identical to the natural minor scale.
  • Locrian — built on a diminished tonic triad; unstable and rarely used as a tonic mode.

History

The concept descends from the tonoi and harmoniai of ancient Greek music theory, whose names (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, and others) were later attached — imperfectly — to the church modes of medieval Gregorian chant. Medieval theorists recognised eight modes (four authentic and four plagal, distinguished by their range and final). In the mid-16th century, the theorist Henricus Glarean expanded the system to twelve modes in his Dodecachordon (1547), adding what became the Ionian and Aeolian. As functional tonality developed in the Baroque era, the modes were largely superseded by the major–minor key system.

Modal music in the modern era

Composers of the late 19th and 20th centuries — Debussy, Vaughan Williams, Bartók — revived modal writing, often drawing on folk melodies. Modal jazz, exemplified by Miles Davis's Kind of Blue (1959), built improvisations on modes rather than rapid chord changes. Modes also underpin much folk, rock, metal, and film music, and modal systems elsewhere in the world include the Arabic maqam, the Indian raga, and the Persian dastgah.
