Chanter

Chanter
ClassificationWind instrument, aerophone
Related instrumentsBagpipes, practice chanter, shawm

The chanter is the part of the bagpipe upon which the player creates the melody. It is a cylindrical or slightly conical pipe with finger holes, fitted with a reed, and is usually made of wood (often African blackwood) or plastic. The chanter is open-ended, so there is no easy way for the player to stop the pipe sounding; bagpipes therefore have a constant, unbroken sound, and musical effects such as grace notes are used to articulate between notes.

Types

Chanters vary between bagpipe traditions. The Great Highland bagpipe chanter is conical, sounds roughly a semitone higher than written pitch in modern instruments, and has a range of nine notes. The uilleann pipes chanter can play two octaves and can be stopped against the knee to silence it. Border pipes, Scottish smallpipes, and the French musette each have their own chanter designs, as do Galician gaita and Breton biniou traditions.

Practice chanter

The practice chanter is a quiet, recorder-like instrument with a narrow cylindrical bore, used by learners to practise fingering and tunes without the volume of the full pipes. It is an essential first step in Highland piping tuition.
