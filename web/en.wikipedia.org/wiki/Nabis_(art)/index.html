Nabis (art)

Les Nabis
Years activec. 1888–1900
LocationParis, France
Key figuresPaul Sérusier, Pierre Bonnard, Édouard Vuillard, Maurice Denis, Ker-Xavier Roussel, Paul Ranson, Aristide Maillol
InfluencesPaul Gauguin, Synthetism, Japanese prints

Les Nabis (from the Hebrew navi, "prophet") were a group of young French post-impressionist avant-garde artists who worked in Paris in the 1890s. The group formed around Paul Sérusier's painting The Talisman (1888), produced under Paul Gauguin's guidance at Pont-Aven, and met at the Académie Julian and Paul Ranson's studio.

Style and ideas

Following Gauguin's Synthetism, the Nabis rejected naturalistic illusion in favour of flat areas of bold colour, expressive distortion, and decorative pattern, drawing also on Japanese woodblock prints. Maurice Denis framed their credo in 1890: "a picture — before being a battle horse, a nude woman, or an anecdote — is essentially a plane surface covered with colours assembled in a certain order". Members experimented across media: easel painting, posters, book illustration, stained glass, ceramics and theatre sets for the Théâtre de l'Œuvre.

Members and legacy

Core members included Sérusier, Denis, Pierre Bonnard, Édouard Vuillard, Ker-Xavier Roussel, Paul Ranson, Henri-Gabriel Ibels, Jan Verkade and the sculptor Aristide Maillol. A more spiritual wing (Denis, Sérusier) moved toward religious art, while Bonnard and Vuillard developed intimate domestic scenes ("intimism"). The group dispersed around 1900, but the work of Bonnard and Vuillard in particular carried Nabi principles deep into 20th-century painting.
