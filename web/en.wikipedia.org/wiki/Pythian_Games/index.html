The Pythian Games were one of the four Panhellenic Games of ancient Greece. They were held in honour of Apollo at his sanctuary at Delphi every four years, in the third year of each Olympiad, ranking second in prestige only to the Olympic Games. According to myth, Apollo founded the games to commemorate his slaying of the serpent Python.

History

The games were reorganised around 582 BC, following the First Sacred War, under the administration of the Delphic Amphictyony. Unlike the Olympics, the Pythian Games always included musical and artistic contests, reflecting Apollo's role as god of music. The original competition was a hymn to Apollo sung to the accompaniment of the kithara; later additions included aulos playing, singing, and eventually contests in poetry, drama and painting.

Events

Athletic events closely mirrored those at Olympia: foot races (stadion, diaulos, dolichos), wrestling, boxing, the pankration, the pentathlon, and equestrian events including chariot and horse racing, held in the stadium and hippodrome at Delphi. Victors received a wreath of laurel from the Vale of Tempe, and were celebrated with statues and commemorative odes — several of Pindar's victory odes were composed for Pythian champions. The games continued until they were suppressed under the Christian Roman emperors in the late 4th century AD.
