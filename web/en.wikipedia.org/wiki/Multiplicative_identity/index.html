Multiplicative identity

In mathematics, the multiplicative identity of a set equipped with multiplication is an element that, when multiplied by any element of the set, leaves that element unchanged. For ordinary numbers it is the number 1, since a × 1 = 1 × a = a for every number a.

In algebraic structures

The multiplicative identity is one of the defining axioms of a monoid, ring and field. In a ring, the multiplicative identity is usually denoted 1 and is required to differ from the additive identity 0 in a nontrivial ring. For matrices, the multiplicative identity is the identity matrix, and for functions under composition it is the identity function.
