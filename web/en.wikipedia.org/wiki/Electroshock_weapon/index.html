An electroshock weapon is an incapacitating weapon that delivers an electric shock to disrupt voluntary control of muscles, producing temporary neuromuscular incapacitation or pain compliance, without — under intended conditions — causing significant injury. It is a type of electrical weapon. The best-known brand is the TASER, manufactured by Axon Enterprise, a conducted energy device that fires two small barbed probes connected by wires to deliver the charge at a distance.

Types

Stun guns require direct contact: two electrodes on the device itself are pressed against the subject, and the shock mainly produces pain. Conducted energy devices (CEDs) launch probes over distances of several metres; when both probes strike and a circuit is completed, the pulses override voluntary muscle control so long as the charge is applied. Related devices include electrified shields, stun belts used on prisoners, and electric cattle prods.

Mechanism

The devices use circuitry to transform a low battery voltage into pulses of very high voltage — tens of thousands of volts to establish an arc and overcome resistance — but with very low average current and short pulse duration, limiting delivered energy. The pulses are designed to stimulate motor nerves at a rate that produces involuntary muscle contractions ("neuromuscular incapacitation").

Use and controversy

Electroshock weapons are widely issued to police as a less-lethal alternative to firearms. Their use is controversial: human-rights organisations and medical studies have examined deaths in custody following CED deployments, and the extent to which the shock, as opposed to factors such as drug intoxication, restraint, or pre-existing heart conditions, contributes remains debated. Manufacturers and police bodies maintain the devices reduce injuries to officers and suspects overall. Legal status for civilian possession varies widely between countries and between U.S. states.

History

The first TASER was developed by NASA researcher Jack Cover in the late 1960s and 1970s; the name is an acronym honouring the 1911 novel Tom Swift and His Electric Rifle. Air Taser and later Taser International (renamed Axon in 2017) commercialised successive models, including the M26, X26, and later the TASER 7 and TASER 10.
