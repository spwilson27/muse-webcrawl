Ground (Dzogchen)

In Dzogchen, the Ground (Tibetan: gzhi; Sanskrit: āśraya) is the primordial state of reality — the basis from which all phenomena arise and into which they dissolve. It is described as originally pure (ka dag), empty in essence, clear in nature, and all-pervasive in compassionate energy. The classic formulation comes from the Kunjed Gyalpo and the Seventeen Tantras of the Nyingma tradition.

Three aspects

The Ground is characterized by three wisdoms or aspects: its essence is primordial purity and emptiness; its nature is spontaneous presence (lhun grub) and luminosity; and its energy or compassion (thugs rje) is unobstructed and manifests as the play of appearances. Realizing these three as inseparable in one's own awareness (rigpa) is the essence of the path.

Ground, path and fruition

Dzogchen teaching is organized as Ground, Path, and Fruition. Beings fail to recognize the Ground and wander in samsara through the arising of ma rigpa (ignorance). The path — including the practices of trekchö and tögal — introduces the practitioner directly to the Ground's nature, and the fruition is stabilization of that recognition: the same basis, now fully realized, manifesting as the three bodies (kayas) of a Buddha.

Relation to other concepts

The Ground is sometimes compared with the ālaya (storehouse consciousness) of Yogācāra, but Dzogchen distinguishes the pure Ground (gzhi) from the impure "ground-of-all" (kun gzhi) that functions as the basis of deluded experience. Longchenpa's Treasury of the Basic Space of Phenomena gives the most celebrated philosophical exposition.
