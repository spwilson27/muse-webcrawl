John Zuccotti

John Eugene Zuccotti (June 23, 1937 – November 19, 2015) was an American businessman and public official. He served as first deputy mayor of New York City and as chairman of the New York City Planning Commission, and later became a prominent real estate developer and chairman of Brookfield Properties. The Lower Manhattan park that became the site of the Occupy Wall Street encampment in 2011, Zuccotti Park, is named for him.

Career

Born in Manhattan to Italian immigrant parents, Zuccotti studied at Princeton University and Yale Law School. After working in law and federal housing administration in Washington, he returned to New York, where Mayor Abraham Beame appointed him chairman of the City Planning Commission in 1973 and first deputy mayor in 1975, during the city's fiscal crisis.

Real estate

In the private sector Zuccotti led major development and finance firms, serving as president of Olympia & York Companies (USA) and later as chairman of Brookfield Financial Properties, overseeing properties including the World Financial Center. Zuccotti Park, a privately owned public space owned by Brookfield adjacent to Ground Zero, was renamed in his honour in 2006 after its post-9/11 rebuilding.
