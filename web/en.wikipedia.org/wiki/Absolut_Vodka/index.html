Absolut Vodka

Absolut Vodka is a Swedish brand of vodka produced near Åhus in southern Sweden. Made from winter wheat and deep-well water, and distilled using a continuous distillation process, it is one of the largest spirits brands in the world. The brand is owned by the French group Pernod Ricard, which acquired it from the Swedish state in 2008.

History

The brand traces its origins to Lars Olsson Smith, the 19th-century "King of Vodka," who in 1879 introduced "Absolut Rent Brännvin" (absolutely pure vodka) using a new continuous distillation method that removed impurities more thoroughly than pot stills. The modern brand was launched in 1979 by the Swedish state alcohol company Vin & Sprit, initially for export to the United States.

Marketing

Absolut's advertising is among the most celebrated in the industry. The bottle — shaped like an old Swedish apothecary bottle, with text printed directly on the glass and no paper label — became the centrepiece of the long-running "Absolut ___" campaign launched in 1981 with "Absolut Perfection," in which the bottle silhouette was depicted in endlessly varied artistic settings. The brand commissioned works from artists including Andy Warhol, Keith Haring and many others, building a close association with contemporary art and design.

Production and range

All Absolut production takes place in and around Åhus: winter wheat is sourced locally, water comes from a deep well, and distillation and bottling occur in a single facility, a model the company markets as "one source." Beyond the original blue-label vodka, the range includes numerous flavoured variants (such as Citron, launched 1988, Peppar, Kurant and Vanilia) and limited-edition city and artist bottles.
