South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club

The South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club was a private club in western Pennsylvania whose members included some of America's wealthiest industrialists. The club owned the South Fork Dam, whose failure on 31 May 1889 caused the Johnstown Flood, killing 2,209 people.

Founding

The club was founded in 1879 by Benjamin Franklin Ruff, who bought the abandoned South Fork Dam and its reservoir, Lake Conemaugh, on the Little Conemaugh River above Johnstown. Members included Henry Clay Frick, Andrew Carnegie, Andrew Mellon, Henry Phipps and Philander Knox. The club built a large clubhouse and cottages around the lake for summer recreation.

The dam

The dam, originally built for a canal reservoir, was repaired cheaply: its discharge pipes had been sold for scrap, its crest was lowered to widen the roadway, and a fish screen was placed across the spillway. Club engineer Daniel J. Morrell warned of the danger, but the repairs were not made to proper standards.

The flood and aftermath

After exceptionally heavy rain, the dam failed on 31 May 1889, sending some 20 million tons of water down the valley into Johnstown. Despite the scale of the disaster, lawsuits against the club and its members failed, courts treating the flood as an act of God, and no compensation was paid by the club, though individual members contributed to relief efforts. The club was abandoned and dissolved in the flood's aftermath.
