Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener (24 June 1850 – 5 June 1916) was a British Army officer and colonial administrator. Kitchener came to prominence in 1898 for winning the Battle of Omdurman and securing control of the Sudan, for which he was made Baron Kitchener of Khartoum. As Chief of Staff (1900–1902) in the Second Boer War he played a key role in Lord Roberts' conquest of the Boer Republics, then succeeded Roberts as commander-in-chief – by which time Boer forces had taken to guerrilla fighting and British forces imprisoned Boer and African civilians in concentration camps. His term as Commander-in-Chief (1902–1909) of the Army in India saw him clash with the Viceroy, Lord Curzon, who eventually resigned. Kitchener then returned to Egypt as British Agent and Consul-General (de facto administrator).

First World War

In 1914, at the start of the First World War, Kitchener became Secretary of State for War, a Cabinet Minister. One of the few to foresee a long war, lasting at least three years and requiring huge new armies, he organised the largest volunteer army that Britain had ever seen, overseeing a significant expansion of materials production to fight on the Western Front. His commanding image, appearing on recruiting posters demanding "Kitchener wants you", became iconic of the war effort.

Death

Kitchener was killed in June 1916 when the armoured cruiser HMS Hampshire, carrying him on a diplomatic mission to Russia, struck a German mine off the Orkney Islands and sank with heavy loss of life. His body was never recovered.

Legacy

Kitchener remains a controversial figure: celebrated for his imperial victories and wartime mobilisation, he has also been criticised for the concentration camps of the Boer War and for the scorched-earth policies employed under his command in South Africa.
