Accessibility

Accessibility is the design of products, devices, services and environments so that they are usable by people with disabilities, and more broadly by as many people as possible. It encompasses physical access to buildings and transport, access to information and communications technology, and cognitive and sensory access.

History and law

The modern accessibility movement grew out of post-war rehabilitation and the disability rights movements of the twentieth century. Landmark legislation includes Section 504 of the US Rehabilitation Act (1973), the Americans with Disabilities Act (1990), the UK Equality Act (2010), and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006), which frames access as a human right. Barrier-free design standards, such as ANSI A117.1 in the United States, specify requirements for ramps, door widths, signage and sanitary facilities.

Digital accessibility

Web accessibility is governed by the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), developed by the World Wide Web Consortium, organised around four principles: content must be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust. Techniques include text alternatives for images, keyboard navigation, captions and sufficient colour contrast. Assistive technologies such as screen readers, switch devices and voice control mediate access.

Universal design

Accessibility is closely related to universal design — designing for the widest range of users from the outset — whose benefits often extend beyond disabled users, as with kerb cuts used by travellers with wheeled luggage.
