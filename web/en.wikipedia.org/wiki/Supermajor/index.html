Supermajor

Supermajor is a term used to describe the largest oil and gas companies in the world. The term, analogous to Big Oil, was popularised in the late 1990s following a wave of mergers and acquisitions that consolidated the industry after a period of low oil prices. The supermajors are generally considered to be the group of integrated, publicly traded companies that dominate global petroleum production, refining and marketing.

The six companies most commonly classed as supermajors

The companies

The companies most frequently identified as supermajors are:

  • ExxonMobil (United States) — formed by the 1999 merger of Exxon and Mobil
  • Shell (United Kingdom) — Royal Dutch Shell, redomiciled to the UK in 2022
  • BP (United Kingdom) — expanded through mergers with Amoco and ARCO
  • Chevron (United States) — merged with Texaco in 2001
  • TotalEnergies (France) — formed from Total, Petrofina and Elf Aquitaine
  • ConocoPhillips (United States) — sometimes included, though primarily an upstream producer since its 2012 downstream spin-off

Characteristics

Supermajors are vertically integrated, operating across exploration and production, refining, petrochemicals and retail. Their market capitalisations and revenues place them among the largest corporations in the world, and their capital spending decisions influence global energy supply. In recent years they have faced pressure from climate policy and the energy transition, with the European supermajors generally announcing larger low-carbon investments than their American counterparts.
