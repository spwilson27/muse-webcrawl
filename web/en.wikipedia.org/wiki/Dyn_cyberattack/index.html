Dyn cyberattack

Date21 October 2016
TypeDistributed denial of service (DDoS)
TargetDyn DNS infrastructure
MethodMirai botnet

The 2016 Dyn cyberattack was a series of distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks on 21 October 2016 against the Domain Name System (DNS) provider Dyn. The attack made dozens of major websites and services unavailable across the United States and Europe, including Twitter, Reddit, GitHub, Netflix, Spotify, Amazon and the websites of major news outlets.

Attack

The attack was carried out using the Mirai botnet, which had infected large numbers of poorly secured Internet of Things devices such as IP cameras and home routers with default passwords. The malware had been released publicly weeks earlier. Waves of malicious DNS lookups overwhelmed Dyn's servers in three distinct attacks during the day, the first beginning shortly after 11:00 UTC.

Investigation

Although speculation initially focused on state actors or hacktivist groups such as Anonymous and New World Hackers, criminal proceedings in the United States later attributed the Mirai botnet to three young men — Paras Jha, Josiah White and Dalton Norman — who pleaded guilty in 2017 to computer fraud offences connected with the botnet, which they had originally built for use in the Minecraft server hosting and DDoS-for-hire market.

Impact

The attack became a landmark demonstration of the systemic risk posed by a single widely used DNS provider and by insecure consumer IoT devices. It prompted greater attention to DNS redundancy, anycast infrastructure, and device security regulation.
