Jam session

A jam session is a relatively informal musical event in which musicians, typically instrumentalists, play improvised solos and vamp on tunes, songs, and chord progressions. Jam sessions are used by musicians to develop new material, find suitable collaborators, and simply to practice and socialize. They are especially associated with jazz, but also occur in rock, blues, bluegrass, hip hop, and electronic music.

History

The jazz jam session flourished in the 1930s and 1940s, notably in after-hours sessions at clubs such as Minton's Playhouse in Harlem, where players like Thelonious Monk, Charlie Parker, and Dizzy Gillespie developed bebop through competitive "cutting contests" on standards. Amateur jam sessions are usually organized around shared repertoire — jazz standards, the twelve-bar blues, or modal vamps — with participants taking turns to solo over the chord changes.

Formats

Sessions may be private gatherings, open club nights where audience members can "sit in," or organized events at festivals and conferences. In rock music, the term also describes bands' exploratory live improvisation, as in the extended jams of groups such as the Grateful Dead and Phish, from which the "jam band" genre takes its name.
