Chita Rivera

Born: Dolores Conchita Figueroa del Rivero, 23 January 1933, Washington, D.C., U.S.

Died: 30 January 2024 (aged 91), New York City, U.S.

Occupation: Actress, dancer, singer

Awards: Two Tony Awards; Kennedy Center Honor; Presidential Medal of Freedom

Chita Rivera (born Dolores Conchita Figueroa del Rivero; January 23, 1933 – January 30, 2024) was an American actress, dancer and singer best known for her work in musical theatre. Over a career spanning more than six decades, she originated several landmark Broadway roles and was celebrated as one of the theatre's greatest triple-threat performers.

Career

Born in Washington, D.C., to a Puerto Rican father and a mother of Scottish-Italian descent, Rivera trained in ballet and moved to New York as a teenager. After touring and chorus work, she achieved stardom as Anita in the original Broadway production of West Side Story (1957) — a role later played on screen by Rita Moreno. She went on to star in Bye Bye Birdie (1960), Bajour (1964), and as Velma Kelly in the original production of Chicago (1975).

Later roles and honours

Rivera won Tony Awards for Best Actress in a Musical for The Rink (1984) and Kiss of the Spider Woman (1993), and was nominated ten times in total. Her later roles included Nine (2003) and The Visit (2015). In 1986 she was seriously injured in a car accident that broke her leg in many places, yet she returned to the stage — a comeback often cited as emblematic of her determination. She received the Kennedy Center Honor in 2002, the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2009, and a Tony Award for Lifetime Achievement in 2018.

Death

Rivera died in New York City on 30 January 2024, aged 91, shortly after her birthday.
