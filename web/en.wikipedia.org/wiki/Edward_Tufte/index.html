Edward Rolf Tufte (born March 14, 1942) is an American statistician and professor emeritus of political science, statistics and computer science at Yale University. He is noted for his writings on information design and as a pioneer in the field of data visualization. The New York Times has described him as the "Leonardo da Vinci of data".

Work

His self-published book The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983) introduced key concepts including the data-ink ratio and chartjunk — superfluous decoration that obscures data — and championed Charles Joseph Minard's 1869 chart of Napoleon's Russian campaign as perhaps the best statistical graphic ever drawn. Follow-up volumes Envisioning Information (1990), Visual Explanations (1997) and Beautiful Evidence (2006) developed ideas such as sparklines, small multiples, and layering and separation.

Tufte is a famous critic of Microsoft PowerPoint's cognitive style, arguing in his essay The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint (2003) that bullet-point slideware contributed to the information failures behind the Space Shuttle Columbia disaster. In 2010 he was appointed by President Barack Obama to the Recovery Independent Advisory Panel overseeing stimulus spending.
