Toleration Act 1688

The Toleration Act 1688 (1 Will. & Mar. c. 18), also known as the Act of Toleration, was an Act of the Parliament of England passed in 1689 following the Glorious Revolution. It granted freedom of worship to Nonconformist Protestants who took oaths of allegiance and supremacy and declared belief in the Trinity.

It exempted Protestant dissenters such as Baptists and Congregationalists from penalties for non-attendance at Church of England services, provided their meeting houses were registered. It did not extend toleration to Roman Catholics or Unitarians, and dissenters remained excluded from public office by the Test Acts.
