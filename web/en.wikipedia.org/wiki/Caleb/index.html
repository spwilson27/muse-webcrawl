Caleb (Hebrew: כָּלֵב, Kaleb) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, noted for his faith in God when the Israelites hesitated to enter the Promised Land. He was the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite and represented the tribe of Judah among the twelve spies sent by Moses into Canaan (Numbers 13).

Biblical narrative

Of the twelve spies, only Caleb and Joshua son of Nun brought back a favourable report and urged the people to trust God's promise; the other ten spread fear of the land's inhabitants. As a result, God decreed that the exodus generation would die in the wilderness, with Caleb and Joshua alone permitted to enter the land. In the Book of Joshua, Caleb, at the age of 85, asks for and receives Hebron as his inheritance, and drives out the Anakim who lived there. His daughter Achsah is given to Othniel, who captured Kiriath-Sepher.

Name and interpretation

The name Caleb is often connected with the Hebrew word kelev ("dog"), and commentators have read it as symbolising faithful, dog-like loyalty and wholehearted devotion; the Bible repeatedly says Caleb "wholly followed the LORD". Rabbinic tradition also identifies Caleb as the husband of Miriam.
