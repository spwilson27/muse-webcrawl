Hydronephrosis

Hydronephrosis
SpecialtyUrology, nephrology

CausesUrinary tract obstruction: stones, tumours, prostate enlargement, congenital defects
DiagnosisUltrasound, CT scan

Hydronephrosis is the swelling of a kidney due to a build-up of urine. It occurs when urine cannot drain from the kidney into the bladder because of a blockage or obstruction somewhere in the urinary tract, causing the renal pelvis and calyces to dilate. It may affect one kidney (unilateral) or both (bilateral).

Causes

In adults, common causes include kidney stones, cancers of the urinary tract or pelvis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, pregnancy, and scarring from infection or surgery. In children it is often congenital — for example ureteropelvic junction obstruction or vesicoureteral reflux — and is frequently detected on prenatal ultrasound, where most mild cases resolve spontaneously.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Hydronephrosis itself may cause no symptoms, or may present with flank pain, nausea, fever (if infected), and changes in urination. Diagnosis is usually by ultrasound, which shows the dilated collecting system; CT or nuclear medicine scans (such as a MAG3 renogram) identify the site and severity of obstruction and how much function each kidney retains.

Treatment

Treatment aims to relieve the obstruction and drain the kidney — by ureteral stent, nephrostomy tube, stone removal or surgery — and to treat any infection, which in an obstructed kidney is a urological emergency. Prolonged untreated obstruction can cause permanent loss of kidney function.
