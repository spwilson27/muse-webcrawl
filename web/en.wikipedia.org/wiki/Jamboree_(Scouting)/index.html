Jamboree (Scouting)

In Scouting, a jamboree is a large gathering of Scouts who rally at a national or international level. The word is believed to have been coined by Robert Baden-Powell, founder of the Scout movement, who used it for the rally held at Olympia, London, in 1920.

World Scout Jamboree

The World Scout Jamboree, organised by the World Organization of the Scout Movement, is held roughly every four years and brings together tens of thousands of young people aged 14 to 17 from around the world. The first was held in London in 1920; later jamborees have taken place in countries including Denmark, France, Canada, Australia, Korea, Japan and the United States. The 24th World Scout Jamboree in 2019, held at the Summit Bechtel Reserve in West Virginia, drew more than 45,000 participants.

National jamborees

Many national Scout associations hold their own national jamborees, such as the National Scout Jamboree of the Boy Scouts of America, held at the Summit Bechtel Reserve since 2013, and national jamborees in countries such as Australia and the Netherlands.

Other jamboree types

Related events include jamborettes (smaller local gatherings), the internet-based Jamboree on the Air and Jamboree on the Internet (JOTA/JOTI), and moots for older members.
