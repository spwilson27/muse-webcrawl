Unsolved Mysteries is an American mystery documentary television series, created by John Cosgrove and Terry Dunn Meurer, that documents cold cases and paranormal phenomena through reenactments, interviews, and updates. The program was hosted by Robert Stack from its premiere in 1987 until 2002, and became famous for its distinctive theme music, dramatic reenactments, and viewer tip line, which the producers credited with helping solve a substantial number of the cases profiled.

History

The series began as a series of television specials on NBC in 1987, the first hosted by Raymond Burr, followed by specials hosted by Karl Malden before Robert Stack took over. It became a weekly series in 1988 and ran on NBC until 1997, then moved to CBS until 1999. Lifetime produced new episodes from 2000 to 2002. After Stack's death in 2003, the series was revived on Spike TV in 2008 with Dennis Farina as host, presenting remastered segments from the original run.

Netflix revival

In 2020, Netflix revived the series in a new format, produced by Cosgrove-Meurer Productions and 21 Laps Entertainment (the production company behind Stranger Things). The revival dispenses with a host and on-screen narrator, devoting each episode to a single case in a more documentary style. New volumes of the revival were released in 2020, 2022, and 2024, including cases such as the mysterious death of Rey Rivera.

Format and impact

Segments traditionally covered categories such as unexplained deaths, missing persons, fugitives, lost loves, amnesia, UFOs, ghosts, and miracles. Many broadcasts ended with "updates" reporting resolutions; the show maintained a telecenter and later a website for viewer tips. The series is credited with helping to solve hundreds of cases, reuniting families, and contributing to the capture of numerous fugitives, and it is regarded as a foundational example of the true-crime genre on television.
