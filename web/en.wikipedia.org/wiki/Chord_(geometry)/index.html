Chord (geometry)

Chord
DefinitionLine segment joining two points on a curve
Longest chordThe diameter
Length formula2R sin(θ/2)
Related linesSecant, tangent

A chord of a curve is a line segment whose two endpoints both lie on the curve. Most commonly the term refers to a chord of a circle: the segment joining any two points of the circumference. A chord that passes through the centre of the circle is a diameter, the longest possible chord. A line that intersects the circle at two points but extends beyond the segment is a secant line, while the region of the disk bounded by a chord and an arc is a circular segment.

Properties

  • The perpendicular line from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • Chords of equal length are the same distance from the centre, and vice versa.
  • Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre, and equal arcs.
  • The inscribed angle theorem: an angle inscribed in a circle standing on a chord is half the central angle standing on the same chord.
  • Intersecting chords theorem: when two chords cross inside a circle, the products of the lengths of their segments are equal.

Length of a chord

For a circle of radius R and a chord subtending a central angle θ, the chord length is 2R sin(θ/2). If d is the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord, the length is 2√(R² − d²).

History in trigonometry

Chords were the foundation of the earliest trigonometry. Hipparchus is credited with a table of chords in the 2nd century BC, and Ptolemy's Almagest tabulates chord lengths for a circle of radius 60, effectively a table of sines at half the angle, since crd(θ) = 2R sin(θ/2). The modern sine function descends from this chord tradition via Indian and Arabic mathematics.

Chords of other curves

The term also applies to other curves: the chord of a conic section, or the line joining two points of a general curve, used for example in the secant method of numerical analysis and in definitions of convexity.
