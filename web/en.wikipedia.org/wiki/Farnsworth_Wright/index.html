Farnsworth Wright

Farnsworth Wright (29 July 1888 – 12 June 1940) was an American editor best known as the editor of the pulp magazine Weird Tales during its golden age, from 1924 until shortly before his death in 1940.

Career

Wright worked as a music critic for the Chicago Herald and Examiner before joining Weird Tales. When founding editor Edwin Baird departed in 1924, Wright took over and shaped the magazine into the leading venue for horror, fantasy and science fiction of its era. He published and nurtured H. P. Lovecraft, Robert E. Howard and Clark Ashton Smith — sometimes called the magazine's "big three" — and also gave early exposure to writers such as Robert Bloch, Seabury Quinn and August Derleth.

Wright was a demanding and sometimes erratic editor: he rejected some of Lovecraft's best-known later stories, including At the Mountains of Madness, yet his taste for atmospheric horror defined the magazine. He also edited the short-lived Oriental Stories (later The Magic Carpet Magazine). Suffering from Parkinson's disease, he was replaced by Dorothy McIlwraith in 1940 and died later that year. Wright also wrote fiction of his own, some under pen names, and poetry.
