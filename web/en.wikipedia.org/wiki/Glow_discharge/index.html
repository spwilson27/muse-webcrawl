Glow discharge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Glow discharge
TypeElectric discharge in gas / plasma
Typical pressure0.1 – 10 Torr
ApplicationsNeon signs, fluorescent lamps, plasma processing

A glow discharge is a plasma formed by the passage of electric current through a gas at low pressure. It is created by applying a voltage between two electrodes in a glass tube containing the gas. When the voltage exceeds a certain value (the breakdown voltage), the gas ionizes, becoming a conducting plasma, and emits coloured light — the "glow" of the discharge.

Structure

A positive-column glow discharge displays a characteristic sequence of bright and dark regions between cathode and anode: the Aston dark space, cathode glow, cathode (Crookes/Hittorf) dark space, the bright negative glow, the Faraday dark space, the long uniform positive column, and anode regions. The colour of the light depends on the gas: neon glows red-orange, argon lavender, and mercury vapour bluish-green.

Operating regimes

As current increases, a glow discharge passes from a dark (Townsend) discharge into the normal glow, where voltage stays nearly constant while the glowing area grows, then the abnormal glow, where voltage rises with current. At still higher currents it can transition to an arc discharge. In the normal glow the cathode is not fully covered; the constant current density in this regime is exploited in voltage-regulator tubes.

Applications

Glow discharges are the working principle of neon signage, fluorescent and gas-discharge lamps, and Nixie tubes. In industry they are used for sputtering and plasma etching in semiconductor manufacture, plasma nitriding of steel, and analytical techniques such as glow-discharge mass spectrometry.
