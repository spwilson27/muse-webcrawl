Curl (mathematics)

Curl
Curl measures the local rotation of a vector field
Notation∇ × F, also rot F
InputA vector field in three dimensions
OutputA (pseudo)vector field
Related operatorsDivergence, gradient, Laplacian

In vector calculus, the curl is a vector operator that describes the infinitesimal rotation of a vector field in three-dimensional Euclidean space. At each point of the field, the curl is a vector whose direction is the axis of rotation and whose magnitude is the strength of that rotation. If the field represents the velocity of a flowing fluid, the curl at a point measures twice the angular velocity of a tiny paddle wheel placed at that point — equivalently, the circulation density of the flow.

Definition

For a vector field F = (Fx, Fy, Fz), the curl is written with the del operator ∇ as a formal cross product:

∇ × F = ( ∂Fz/∂y − ∂Fy/∂z,   ∂Fx/∂z − ∂Fz/∂x,   ∂Fy/∂x − ∂Fx/∂y )

Formally and coordinate-free, the curl is defined as the limiting value of the circulation around a small loop divided by the loop's area, as the area shrinks to zero, with the direction given by the right-hand rule.

Interpretation and examples

A field with zero curl everywhere is called irrotational; on a simply connected region such a field is the gradient of a scalar potential (a conservative field). Uniform rigid rotation has constant curl: for a body rotating with angular velocity ω, the velocity field v = ω × r has ∇ × v = 2ω. In fluid dynamics the curl of the velocity field is the vorticity. The curl appears centrally in Maxwell's equations — the curl of the electric field equals the negative rate of change of the magnetic field (Faraday's law), and the curl of the magnetic field is given by Ampère's law — and in the Navier–Stokes equations of fluid motion.

Identities

  • The divergence of a curl is always zero: ∇ · (∇ × F) = 0.
  • The curl of a gradient is zero: ∇ × (∇f) = 0.
  • Stokes' theorem relates the surface integral of the curl to the line integral of the field around the surface's boundary.
