Overview

Chinese martial arts, commonly known in the West as kung fu and in modern China as wushu, comprise the hundreds of fighting styles developed over centuries in China. They are traditionally classified into northern and southern styles, and into 'external' styles emphasising athletic technique and 'internal' styles emphasising breath, alignment and relaxed power.

History

Chinese fighting arts have roots in military training and folk practice of great antiquity. The Shaolin Monastery became famous for martial monks by the Tang and Ming dynasties, and during the Ming and Qing periods lineages of boxing, weapons and qigong proliferated, giving rise to styles such as taijiquan (tai chi), baguazhang and xingyiquan. In the twentieth century the Republic and People's Republic standardised wushu as a performance and competition sport, while traditional lineages spread worldwide, amplified by Hong Kong cinema and stars such as Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan.

Practice

Training typically combines stances, forms (taolu) — choreographed sequences of techniques — conditioning, weapons practice and, in many schools, sparring or push hands. Chinese martial arts are practised for self-defence, health, sport and cultural heritage, and tai chi in particular is studied for balance and wellbeing benefits in older adults.
