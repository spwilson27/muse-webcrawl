Mad Hatter (DC Comics)

The Mad Hatter (Jervis Tetch) is a supervillain appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, commonly as an adversary of the superhero Batman. Created by writer Bill Finger and artist Lew Sayre Schwartz, he first appeared in Batman #49 (October 1948). Modelled on the Hatter from Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, Tetch is a deranged neuroscientist who uses mind-control technology, often concealed in hats, to commit crimes.

Fictional character biography

Jervis Tetch grew up obsessed with Lewis Carroll's Alice books and fixated on finding his own "Alice". As an adult he became a neuroscientist at Wayne Biomedical Laboratories, where he developed technology capable of inducing trance-like states and controlling the minds of others, typically delivered through microchips hidden in the band of a hat placed on the victim's head. After his first defeat he became a recurring inmate of Arkham Asylum, frequently quoting and misquoting Carroll and becoming enraged when others fail to understand his references.

The impostor Mad Hatter

During the Silver Age, a second, unnamed Mad Hatter appeared — a portly impostor with a handlebar moustache who collected hats, including an ambition to add Batman's cowl to his collection. The original Tetch eventually returned, and the impostor was retconned as one of Tetch's mind-controlled pawns.

In other media

The character appeared in the 1966 Batman television series, played by David Wayne, voiced by Roddy McDowall in Batman: The Animated Series, and portrayed by Benedict Samuel in the series Gotham. He has also featured in the Batman: Arkham video games, voiced by Peter Macon.
