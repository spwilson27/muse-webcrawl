Fish hook

A fish hook or fishhook is a tool for catching fish, by impaling the fish in the mouth or, more rarely, by snagging the body. Fish hooks have been employed for thousands of years and come in an enormous variety of sizes, shapes, and designs.

Anatomy

A typical hook consists of an eye (to which the line is attached), a shank, a bend, a point, and often a barb — a backward-facing projection that prevents the hook from slipping out. The gape is the distance between the point and the shank. Barbless hooks are required in some catch-and-release fisheries to reduce injury.

History

The oldest known fish hooks, made from snail shells, were found in a cave on Okinawa Island and date to about 23,000 years ago; bone hooks from East Timor date to roughly 16,000 years ago. Hooks were historically made from bone, shell, horn, wood, and stone before bronze and then steel became standard. Modern hooks are usually made of high-carbon steel or stainless steel, often chemically sharpened and coated against corrosion.

Types

Common patterns include bait hooks, fly hooks, treble hooks (with three points, used on lures), circle hooks, jig hooks, and double hooks. Hook sizes for most patterns run on a scale in which larger numbers mean smaller hooks, with "aught" sizes (e.g. 1/0, 2/0) denoting still larger hooks.
