Szemerédi's theorem

FieldCombinatorics, ergodic theory
Conjectured byPaul Erdős and Pál Turán (1936)
Proved byEndre Szemerédi (1975)

Szemerédi's theorem is a landmark result in additive combinatorics stating that any subset of the integers with positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. It settled a conjecture of Paul Erdős and Pál Turán from 1936.

Statement and history

Formally: for every positive real δ and every positive integer k, there exists N such that every subset of {1, …, N} of size at least δN contains an arithmetic progression of length k. Special cases came first: Roth's theorem (1953) proved the case k = 3 using Fourier analysis, and Szemerédi proved k = 4 in 1969 before the full combinatorial proof in 1975, which introduced what became known as the Szemerédi regularity lemma. Hillel Furstenberg gave an ergodic-theoretic proof in 1977, establishing the Furstenberg–Katznelson multiple recurrence theorem, and Timothy Gowers later gave a proof using higher-order Fourier analysis with greatly improved bounds.

Significance

The theorem is a cornerstone of additive combinatorics and arithmetic Ramsey theory. Its circle of ideas led directly to the Green–Tao theorem (2004), which shows that the prime numbers contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Quantitative bounds on the theorem — how large N must be — remain an active area of research.
