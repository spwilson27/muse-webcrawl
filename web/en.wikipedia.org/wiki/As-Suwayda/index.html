As-Suwayda (Arabic: ٱلسُّوَيْدَاء, romanised as as-Suwaydāʾ) is a city in southwestern Syria, the capital of the As-Suwayda Governorate, close to the border with Jordan. It lies on the western slopes of the Jabal al-Druze (Jabal Hauran) highlands and is the principal urban centre of Syria's Druze community.

History

The area was inhabited in antiquity — the nearby Roman-era town of Dionysias formed part of the province of Arabia Petraea — and the modern city grew under Ottoman rule. During the French Mandate, as-Suwayda was a centre of the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925, led by Sultan al-Atrash. The surrounding volcanic plateau is noted for its black basalt architecture, vineyards and Roman ruins at nearby Shahba and Qanawat.

Demographics

The majority of inhabitants are Druze, with Greek Orthodox and other Christian minorities and a smaller Sunni population. The city and governorate were deeply affected by the Syrian civil war, including the 2018 Islamic State attacks on villages east of the city.
