Augsburg

Augsburg is a city in the south-west of Bavaria, Germany, at the confluence of the Lech and Wertach rivers. With a population of roughly 300,000, it is the third-largest city in Bavaria after Munich and Nuremberg, and one of the oldest cities in Germany, founded in 15 BC as a Roman military camp named Augusta Vindelicum after the emperor Augustus.

History

Augsburg became a free imperial city in 1276 and one of the great commercial centres of early modern Europe. The banking families of the Fuggers and Welsers made it a hub of international finance; Jakob Fugger, "the Rich", financed emperors and founded the Fuggerei in 1521, the world's oldest social housing settlement still in use. The city played a central role in the Reformation: the Augsburg Confession was presented at the Diet of Augsburg in 1530, and the Peace of Augsburg of 1555 established the principle that rulers could determine the religion of their territories. Severely damaged in the Thirty Years' War and again by bombing in the Second World War, the city was rebuilt and its Renaissance core — including the town hall (1615–1624) and the Perlachturm — restored.

Water management system

Augsburg's historic water management system, with its canals, waterworks and fountains, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019. The city's network of Lech canals powered centuries of craft and industry and supplied drinking water separated from waste water from an early date.

Economy and education

Modern Augsburg is a centre for aerospace, mechanical engineering, printing technology and IT, and hosts the University of Augsburg (founded 1970) and a university of applied sciences. The city is the birthplace of Rudolf Diesel's engine experiments, the playwright Bertolt Brecht and the composer Leopold Mozart.
