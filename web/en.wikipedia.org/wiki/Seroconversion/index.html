Seroconversion

Seroconversion is the period during which a specific antibody develops and becomes detectable in the blood after infection or immunisation. Before seroconversion a person is said to be seronegative for that antibody; afterwards, seropositive.

Mechanism

When the immune system first encounters a pathogen, it takes days to weeks to produce measurable quantities of antibodies. IgM antibodies typically appear first, followed by longer-lasting IgG. The interval between infection and detectable antibodies is called the window period, during which an infected person may test negative despite being infectious.

Clinical importance

Seroconversion is a key concept in the diagnosis of infections such as HIV, hepatitis, and many viral diseases, and in evaluating vaccine responses. A fourfold rise in antibody titre between acute and convalescent samples is often used as evidence of recent infection.
