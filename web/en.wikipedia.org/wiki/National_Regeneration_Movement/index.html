The National Regeneration Movement (Spanish: Movimiento Regeneración Nacional), commonly known by its acronym Morena, is a major left-wing political party in Mexico. It was founded by Andrés Manuel López Obrador after his second presidential campaign, first as a civil association in 2011 and registered as a political party in 2014. Morena won the presidency and congressional majorities in the 2018 general election, ending decades of dominance by the PRI and PAN.

Rise to power

López Obrador, widely known as AMLO, formed Morena after breaking with the Party of the Democratic Revolution. Campaigning against corruption and inequality under the banner of the "Fourth Transformation" (Cuarta Transformación), he won the presidency in 2018 with about 53 percent of the vote, and Morena and its allies took control of both houses of Congress and most state governorships over the following years.

Succession

In the 2024 general election, Morena's candidate Claudia Sheinbaum — former head of government of Mexico City — won the presidency by a wide margin, becoming Mexico's first woman president, and the party's coalition secured supermajorities enabling constitutional reforms, including an overhaul of the judiciary. Morena remains by far Mexico's largest party, though critics raise concerns about the concentration of power and the weakening of autonomous institutions.
