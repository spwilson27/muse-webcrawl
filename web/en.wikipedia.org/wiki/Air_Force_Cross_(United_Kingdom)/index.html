The Air Force Cross (AFC) is a military decoration awarded to officers, and since 1993 to other ranks, of the British Armed Forces, and formerly also to members of Commonwealth air forces, for acts of gallantry while flying in active operations against the enemy. It is the third-level gallantry award for flying, ranking below the Distinguished Flying Cross in the order of wear in some contexts and equivalent in level to the Distinguished Service Cross and the Military Cross for operations at sea and on land.

History

The cross was instituted on 3 June 1918, shortly after the formation of the Royal Air Force, and was originally awarded for acts of valour, courage, or devotion to duty while flying, though not necessarily in active operations against the enemy. Awards were announced in the London Gazette. Between 1919 and 1932 the cross was also available for award to civilians, a provision used on a small number of occasions. During the Second World War it was awarded widely to aircrew and to personnel of Allied air forces.

1993 reforms

Following a review of the honours system, the 1993 reforms ended the distinction between awards to officers and other ranks, and the Air Force Cross became available to all ranks. Purely gallantry-based awards were restructured: the AFC has since been awarded for gallantry in flying in active operations, while distinguished flying service not in the face of the enemy is recognized by other means. Recipients are entitled to the post-nominal letters "AFC". Bars may be awarded for further acts of gallantry.

Description

The medal is a silver cross formed of aircraft propeller blades, surmounted by a winged Hermes design, suspended from a ribbon of alternating white and red (later blue) diagonal stripes. The design was by Bertram Mackennal. The name and year of award are engraved on the reverse. Notable recipients include pioneering aviators of the inter-war years, test pilots, and wartime aircrew of the Royal Air Force, Royal Navy Fleet Air Arm, and Army Air Corps.
