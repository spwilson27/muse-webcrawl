Privately owned public space

Placeholder image: Zuccotti Park, New York City

A privately owned public space (POPS) is a public space, such as a plaza, arcade, or atrium, that is legally required to be open to the public but is owned and maintained by a private developer, typically in exchange for zoning concessions such as additional floor area.

New York City

New York's 1961 Zoning Resolution pioneered incentive zoning: developers could build taller than otherwise permitted if they provided plazas or arcades. The programme produced more than 500 spaces at over 380 buildings, though a comprehensive study led by Jerold Kayden with the Municipal Art Society found that many were poorly designed, hidden, or effectively privatised with restrictions. In 2007 the city reformed its design standards for new plazas.

Legal and political issues

Because POPS sit between public and private law, disputes arise over security rules, photography, and protest. Zuccotti Park became famous during the 2011 Occupy Wall Street encampment, whose clearing highlighted ambiguities in owners' rule-making. Other cities — including San Francisco, Seattle, London, and Tokyo — have adopted similar incentive schemes, with comparable debates about access and accountability.
