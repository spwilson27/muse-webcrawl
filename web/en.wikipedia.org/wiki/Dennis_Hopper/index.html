Dennis Lee Hopper (May 17, 1936 – May 29, 2010) was an American actor, filmmaker, photographer, and artist. He made his first television appearance in 1954, and soon after appeared in two of the films that made James Dean famous, Rebel Without a Cause (1955) and Giant (1956). He made a name for himself as the director and co-star of Easy Rider (1969), a landmark of the New Hollywood era.

Hopper around the time of Easy Rider (1969), which he directed and co-wrote.

Career

After years of supporting roles and a reputation for being difficult, Hopper co-wrote, directed, and starred in Easy Rider with Peter Fonda; the low-budget biker film became a cultural phenomenon and won the Best First Work award at Cannes. His follow-up, The Last Movie (1971), was a commercial failure that stalled his directing career. He rebuilt his standing with performances in Apocalypse Now (1979), as the drug-addled photojournalist, and reached a new peak with Blue Velvet (1986), playing the terrifying Frank Booth, and Hoosiers (1986), which earned him an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actor.

Later work

In the 1990s and 2000s Hopper became a familiar villain in films such as Speed (1994), True Romance (1993), and Waterworld (1995), and voiced roles in animation and video games. He was also a respected photographer and painter, exhibiting from the 1960s onward.

Personal life and death

Hopper was married five times, including a famously brief marriage to Michelle Phillips of The Mamas & the Papas. He struggled for years with drug and alcohol addiction before achieving sobriety in the 1980s. He died of prostate cancer at his home in Venice, Los Angeles, on 29 May 2010, shortly after receiving a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
