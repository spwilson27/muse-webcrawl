Gulbenkian Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Calouste Gulbenkian Museum (Portuguese: Museu Calouste Gulbenkian) is an art museum in Lisbon, Portugal, housing the private collection of the Armenian-British oil magnate and collector Calouste Gulbenkian (1869–1955). Opened in 1969, it holds one of the most important private art collections in the world, some 6,000 pieces spanning 5,000 years from ancient Egypt to the early 20th century; of these, about 1,000 are on permanent display, and a complementary modern collection occupies the Centro de Arte Moderna across the gardens.

The collection

The Founder's Collection is arranged chronologically and geographically: Egyptian, Greco-Roman and Mesopotamian antiquities; Armenian manuscripts; Islamic art including Iznik ceramics, Persian carpets and Syrian glass; Chinese and Japanese art; and European painting and decorative arts from the 15th to the 19th centuries. Masterpieces include works by Rogier van der Weyden, Rubens, Rembrandt — including the Portrait of an Old Man bought from the Hermitage sales — Frans Hals, Gainsborough, Turner, Monet, Renoir and Degas. A celebrated gallery is devoted to the Art Nouveau jeweller René Lalique, whose works Gulbenkian bought directly from the artist.

The museum and foundation

Gulbenkian, who settled in Lisbon in 1942, left his fortune and collection to a foundation chartered in 1956 for charitable, artistic, educational and scientific purposes. The museum building, set in landscaped gardens, won Portugal's Valmor Prize for architecture. The foundation also maintains an orchestra, choir, library and research institutes, and its grounds form one of Lisbon's principal cultural complexes.
