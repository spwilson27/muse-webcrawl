Telehandler

A telehandler, short for telescopic handler, is a versatile lifting machine used in agriculture and construction. It combines features of a forklift and a crane: a telescopic boom mounted on a wheeled chassis can extend forwards and upwards, carrying loads on forks or other attachments to heights and reaches that a conventional forklift cannot achieve. It is also known as a teleporter, boom lift (in some regions), or cherry picker in informal usage.

Design

The operator sits in a cab offset to one side, with the boom mounted low on the opposite side or centre-rear of the chassis to preserve visibility. Most telehandlers have four-wheel drive and multiple steering modes (front-wheel, four-wheel, and crab steer) for manoeuvring on rough terrain. Typical machines lift 2.5 to 6 tonnes to heights of 6 to 18 metres; heavy-duty and rotating ("roto") models lift considerably more and can slew the boom assembly like a crane.

Attachments

A quick-attach carriage allows the forks to be swapped for buckets, muck grabs, bale spikes, lifting jibs, winches, and personnel work platforms. This interchangeability makes the telehandler the principal materials-handling machine on many farms and building sites, where it loads trailers, stacks bales, places roof trusses, and moves palletised materials.

Safety

Because the load capacity falls sharply as the boom extends, telehandlers are fitted with load charts and moment indicators, and stability is the governing safety concern. Operators are licensed or certified under national schemes, and the use of telehandlers to lift people is restricted to purpose-designed platforms and procedures.

History

The first telescopic handler is generally credited to the British company Matbro in the 1970s; JCB's Loadall, launched in 1977, popularized the concept. Manufacturers today include JCB, Manitou, Merlo, Bobcat, Caterpillar, and JLG.
