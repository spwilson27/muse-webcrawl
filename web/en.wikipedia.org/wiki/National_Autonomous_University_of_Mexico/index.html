The National Autonomous University of Mexico (Spanish: Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, UNAM) is a public research university in Mexico. It is the largest university in Latin America and consistently ranks among the top universities in the Spanish-speaking world.

History

The university traces its origins to the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico, founded in 1551. The modern university was founded in 1910 by educator and philosopher Justo Sierra. In 1929 the university was granted autonomy from direct government control, reflected in its present name. Its main campus, Ciudad Universitaria (University City), was built in the 1950s and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007; it is noted for its murals by artists such as Juan O'Gorman, Diego Rivera, and David Alfaro Siqueiros.

Academics

UNAM enrolls well over 300,000 students across its faculties, institutes, and high schools, making it one of the largest universities in the world. Three Nobel laureates have been associated with the university: Alfonso García Robles (Peace), Octavio Paz (Literature), and Mario J. Molina (Chemistry).
