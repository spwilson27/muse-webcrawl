Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers were an American rock band formed in Gainesville, Florida, in 1976. The band originally comprised lead singer and rhythm guitarist Tom Petty, lead guitarist Mike Campbell, keyboardist Benmont Tench, drummer Stan Lynch and bassist Ron Blair. In 1982 Blair left amid the rigours of touring and was replaced by Howie Epstein; Blair returned in 2002. Lynch left in 1994 and was replaced by Steve Ferrone.

Career

The band's self-titled debut album (1976) produced the singles "Breakdown" and "American Girl". Breakthrough success came with Damn the Torpedoes (1979), featuring "Refugee" and "Don't Do Me Like That". Subsequent albums included Hard Promises (1981, with "The Waiting"), Southern Accents (1985), Into the Great Wide Open (1991) and Echo (1999). Songs such as "I Won't Back Down", "Free Fallin'" (also on Petty's solo debut) and "Mary Jane's Last Dance" became staples of classic rock radio.

Over four decades the band sold more than 80 million records worldwide, played the Super Bowl XLII halftime show in 2008, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002.

End of the band

The band's 40th-anniversary tour concluded in September 2017; Tom Petty died on 2 October 2017, a week after the tour ended, effectively bringing the Heartbreakers to a close.
