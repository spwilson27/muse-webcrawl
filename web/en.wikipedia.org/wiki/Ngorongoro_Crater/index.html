Ngorongoro Crater

Ngorongoro Crater is a large volcanic caldera in the Ngorongoro Conservation Area of northern Tanzania, within the Crater Highlands region of the Great Rift Valley. It is the world's largest inactive, intact, and unfilled volcanic caldera, formed when a huge volcano collapsed on itself roughly two to three million years ago. The crater floor covers about 260 square kilometres (100 square miles), and its walls rise some 600 metres (2,000 ft) above the floor.

Wildlife

The crater supports one of the densest concentrations of wildlife in Africa, with an estimated 25,000 large animals, including wildebeest, zebras, gazelles, elephants, hippos, and one of Africa's densest populations of lions. It is also a critical refuge for the endangered black rhinoceros. The Lerai Forest, Lake Magadi — a shallow alkaline lake on the crater floor — and several springs provide varied habitats within the caldera.

Conservation and human history

The Ngorongoro Conservation Area, established in 1959, is a multiple-use area where Maasai pastoralists coexist with wildlife conservation; it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979. Nearby Olduvai Gorge, one of the most important paleoanthropological sites in the world, lies within the conservation area and has yielded fossils of early hominins such as Paranthropus boisei.
