Critical pedagogy

Critical pedagogy emphasises dialogue and problem-posing rather than rote instruction.

Critical pedagogy is a philosophy of education and a social movement that developed and applied concepts from critical theory and related traditions to teaching and the study of culture and schooling. Its founding figure is the Brazilian educator Paulo Freire, whose Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968, English 1970) argued that education should enable learners to critically examine ("conscientise") the social conditions that oppress them and to act to transform those conditions.

Principles

Freire opposed the "banking model" of education, in which teachers deposit knowledge into passive students, proposing instead a dialogical, problem-posing education in which teacher and students learn from each other. Later proponents — including Henry Giroux, Peter McLaren, bell hooks and Ira Shor — extended the approach to analyse how schooling reproduces inequalities of class, race and gender, and to advocate teaching as a form of cultural politics aimed at democratic social change. Related currents include feminist pedagogy, anti-racist education and "critical literacy".

Criticism

Critics contend that critical pedagogy can substitute political advocacy for academic content, that its jargon obscures limited empirical evidence of effectiveness, and that classroom power imbalances make genuinely equal dialogue unrealistic. Defenders respond that all education is political, and that critical pedagogy makes those commitments explicit and examinable.
