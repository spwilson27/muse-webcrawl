Enterovirus

FamilyPicornaviridae
GenomePositive-sense single-stranded RNA
TransmissionFecal–oral, respiratory

Enterovirus is a genus of positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses associated with several human and mammalian diseases. The genus includes the polioviruses, coxsackieviruses, echoviruses, and the rhinoviruses under modern classification.

Transmission and disease

Enteroviruses are transmitted primarily by the fecal–oral route and can survive the acidic environment of the stomach. Most infections are asymptomatic or cause mild illness, but some types cause hand, foot and mouth disease, herpangina, viral meningitis, myocarditis, and acute flaccid myelitis. Poliovirus, the cause of poliomyelitis, is the most historically significant member.

Structure

Enteroviruses are small, non-enveloped viruses with an icosahedral capsid about 30 nm in diameter surrounding a genome of roughly 7.5 kilobases.
