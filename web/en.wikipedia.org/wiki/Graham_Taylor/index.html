Graham Taylor OBE (15 September 1944 – 12 January 2017) was an English football player, manager, pundit and chairman of Watford Football Club. He was the manager of the England national football team from 1990 to 1993, and also managed Lincoln City, Watford, Aston Villa and Wolverhampton Wanderers.

Playing career

Taylor played as a full-back for Grimsby Town and Lincoln City in the Football League, making over 300 appearances before injury ended his playing career in 1972.

Management career

Taylor became the youngest manager in the Football League when he took over at Lincoln City in 1972, leading them to the Fourth Division title in 1976. In 1977 he joined Watford, owned by Elton John, and guided the club from the Fourth Division to the First Division in five seasons, finishing as league runners-up in 1983 and reaching the FA Cup final in 1984.

He moved to Aston Villa in 1987, winning promotion and finishing second in the First Division in 1990. That success led to his appointment as England manager after the 1990 World Cup. His tenure was difficult: England performed poorly at Euro 92 and failed to qualify for the 1994 World Cup, and Taylor resigned in November 1993. He was famously lampooned by the tabloid press, including the Sun's "turnip" headline.

Later life

Taylor returned to Watford in 1996 and led them to the Premier League in 1999. He later became chairman of Watford and worked as a respected football pundit. He died of a suspected heart attack on 12 January 2017, aged 72.
