Let's Move! was a public health campaign in the United States, led by First Lady Michelle Obama, dedicated to reducing childhood obesity. The campaign was announced on February 9, 2010, and aimed to encourage healthier food choices in schools and homes, promote physical activity among children, and make nutritious food more accessible and affordable for families.

History

The initiative was launched alongside an executive memorandum signed by President Barack Obama creating the Task Force on Childhood Obesity, which was charged with developing an interagency action plan. The campaign's stated ambition was to solve the problem of childhood obesity within a generation, so that children born in the 2010s would reach adulthood at a healthy weight.

Program components

  • Healthier school meals: Support for the Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010, which set new nutrition standards for school lunches and vending machines.
  • Chefs Move to Schools: A program pairing professional chefs with local schools to help serve healthier, affordable meals.
  • Let's Move! Cities, Towns and Counties: A call to local elected officials to adopt goals such as expanding access to fresh food and increasing opportunities for physical activity.
  • Access to healthy food: Efforts, in partnership with grocery retailers, to bring supermarkets to underserved "food deserts".
  • Physical activity: Promotion of the guideline that children get 60 minutes of play with moderate to vigorous activity every day.

Reception and legacy

The campaign was widely covered in the media and was credited with raising national attention to childhood obesity rates, which had roughly tripled in the United States since the 1970s. Critics debated the proper role of government in food policy, while supporters pointed to changes in food industry practices, such as clearer front-of-package nutrition labelling, that followed the initiative.
