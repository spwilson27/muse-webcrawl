Anthony Perkins

Anthony Perkins (April 4, 1932 – September 12, 1992) was an American actor, director, and singer. He is best known for playing Norman Bates in Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho (1960) and its sequels, a role that defined his career. The son of actor Osgood Perkins, he was nominated for an Academy Award for Friendly Persuasion (1956).

Born: April 4, 1932, New York City, U.S.
Died: September 12, 1992 (aged 60), Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, director, singer
Years active: 1953–1992

Career

Perkins made his film debut in The Actress (1953) and won a Golden Globe for New Star of the Year. After Friendly Persuasion, he appeared in Fear Strikes Out (1957) and The Tin Star (1957). His performance as Norman Bates in Psycho made him internationally famous but also typecast him in disturbed or villainous roles. He worked extensively in Europe, winning the Best Actor award at Cannes for Goodbye Again (1961), and starred in Orson Welles's The Trial (1962).

Later work and death

Perkins co-wrote The Last of Sheila (1973) with Stephen Sondheim, reprised Bates in Psycho II (1983), Psycho III (1986, which he also directed), and Psycho IV (1990). He was married to photographer Berry Berenson. Perkins died of AIDS-related pneumonia in 1992.
