Drum kit

A drum kit (also called a drum set or trap set) is a collection of drums, cymbals, and other percussion instruments set up on stands and played by a single player, the drummer, using drumsticks held in both hands and pedals operated by the feet. The kit developed in the early 20th century in the United States, when theatre and dance-band drummers combined instruments that had previously been played by several percussionists into one playable set-up.

Components

A typical kit consists of:

  • A bass drum (kick drum), played with a foot pedal;
  • A snare drum on a stand, the main source of backbeat accents;
  • One or more tom-toms (rack toms and a floor tom);
  • A hi-hat, a pair of cymbals on a pedal-operated stand;
  • One or more crash cymbals and a larger ride cymbal.

Optional additions include china cymbals, splash cymbals, cowbells, tambourines, octobans, roto-toms, and electronic pads.

History

The drum kit emerged from New Orleans marching and jazz bands around 1900. The invention of practical bass drum pedals (notably by Ludwig in 1909) and the hi-hat stand in the 1920s allowed one drummer to cover bass, snare, and cymbal parts simultaneously. The standard kit format was consolidated in the swing era and expanded by rock drummers in the 1960s and 1970s, some of whom used enormous multi-tom and double-bass-drum set-ups.

Playing

Drummers coordinate all four limbs: typically the right hand plays the hi-hat or ride cymbal, the left hand the snare, the right foot the bass drum pedal, and the left foot the hi-hat pedal. Common grips include matched grip and traditional grip. Styles range from jazz and rock to metal, funk, and Latin music, each with characteristic patterns.
