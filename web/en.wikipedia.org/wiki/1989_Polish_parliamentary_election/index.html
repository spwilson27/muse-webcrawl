1989 Polish parliamentary election

1989 Polish parliamentary election
Date4 June 1989 (first round); 18 June 1989 (second round)
CountryPolish People's Republic
ChambersSejm (460 seats) and Senate (100 seats)
OutcomeSolidarity won all freely contested seats

Parliamentary elections were held in Poland on 4 June 1989, with a second round on 18 June. They were the first elections in Poland since the Second World War that were not fully controlled by the communist authorities, following the Round Table Agreement between the government and the Solidarity opposition.

Under the agreement, only 35 percent of the seats in the Sejm, the lower house, were freely contested; the remaining 65 percent were reserved for the Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR) and its satellite parties. All 100 seats in the newly restored Senate were freely contested.

Results

The result was a landslide for the Solidarity Citizens' Committee, which won all 161 freely contested Sejm seats and 99 of the 100 Senate seats. The governing coalition failed to win even most of its reserved seats in the first round, and many unopposed regime candidates failed to reach the required 50 percent threshold.

The election marked the beginning of the end of communist rule in Poland and helped trigger the Revolutions of 1989 across Central and Eastern Europe. In August 1989 Tadeusz Mazowiecki became the first non-communist prime minister in the region since the late 1940s.
