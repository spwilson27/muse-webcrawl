The American Society for Psychical Research (ASPR) is the oldest psychical research organisation in the United States, founded in Boston in 1885 as the American counterpart of the British Society for Psychical Research. Its stated purpose is the scientific investigation of claimed paranormal phenomena such as telepathy, clairvoyance, apparitions, and survival of consciousness after death.

History

The society was founded with the support of distinguished academics, most notably Harvard philosopher and psychologist William James, who served as a vice-president and later president. Early members investigated mediums, haunted houses, and thought-transference, publishing their findings in the society's Proceedings and Journal. Boston physician Richard Hodgson, its first full-time investigator, was celebrated and criticised for his investigations of mediums such as Leonora Piper, whose trance communications Hodgson came to regard as evidence of telepathy or survival.

After Hodgson's death in 1905, infighting led James Hervey Hyslop to reorganise the society in New York in 1906, where it has remained. In the 20th century the ASPR supported laboratory research, including the dream-telepathy studies conducted by Montague Ullman and Stanley Krippner at Maimonides Medical Center in the 1960s and 1970s. It maintains a library and archives of case reports and offers lectures and publications on parapsychology. The scientific mainstream generally regards its research subjects as unproven, and parapsychology remains a controversial field.
