National Front (Colombia)

The National Front (Spanish: Frente Nacional) was a political agreement between Colombia's Liberal and Conservative parties, in force from 1958 to 1974, under which the two parties alternated the presidency every four years and shared all government posts equally. The pact ended the military dictatorship of Gustavo Rojas Pinilla and sought to close the period of bipartisan civil conflict known as La Violencia.

Background

La Violencia, unleashed after the 1948 assassination of Liberal leader Jorge Eliécer Gaitán, killed an estimated 200,000 or more Colombians. After Rojas Pinilla seized power in 1953 and drifted toward dictatorship, Liberal leader Alberto Lleras Camargo and Conservative leader Laureano Gómez negotiated the Pact of Benidorm (1956) and the Pact of Sitges (1957), laying down the terms of the Front. A plebiscite on 1 December 1957 approved the arrangement and, incidentally, first granted women the vote in Colombia.

Operation

Four presidents governed under the Front: Alberto Lleras Camargo (Liberal, 1958–62), Guillermo León Valencia (Conservative, 1962–66), Carlos Lleras Restrepo (Liberal, 1966–70), and Misael Pastrana Borrero (Conservative, 1970–74). Ministries, governorships, and legislative seats were divided equally ("paridad") regardless of election results. The disputed 1970 election, in which Rojas Pinilla's populist ANAPO movement was narrowly defeated, was widely regarded as fraudulent and fuelled the formation of the M-19 guerrilla movement.

Legacy

The Front stabilised Colombia and restored civilian rule, but by excluding all political forces outside the two parties it is blamed for entrenching clientelism and for contributing to the rise of insurgencies — including the FARC and ELN — whose grievances drew on peasant self-defence enclaves attacked during the period. Formal parity ended in 1974, though power-sharing habits persisted in Colombian politics for years afterward.
