Yamunotri

LocationUttarkashi district, Uttarakhand, India
Elevation3,293 m (10,804 ft)
DeityYamuna (goddess)
SignificanceFirst stop of the Chota Char Dham pilgrimage; near the source of the Yamuna River

Yamunotri is a Hindu pilgrimage town in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, India, sacred to the goddess Yamuna. It is the westernmost shrine of the Chota Char Dham circuit and the traditional starting point of the Char Dham Yatra, the pilgrimage that continues to Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath.

The temple

The Yamunotri temple, dedicated to the goddess Yamuna, stands at an altitude of about 3,293 metres on the flank of the Bandarpoonch massif. The present temple was built in the 19th century by Maharani Gularia of Jaipur (an earlier temple attributed to the Tehri royal house was damaged by weather and rebuilt). The sanctum houses an idol of the goddess carved from black marble. The temple opens each year on Akshaya Tritiya (April–May) and closes for winter around Diwali, when the deity is moved down to the village of Kharsali.

Geography and hot springs

The actual source of the Yamuna is the Champasar Glacier on the Kalind mountain, about a kilometre above and beyond the temple, accessible only by a difficult trek. Near the temple are hot springs, notably Surya Kund, where pilgrims cook rice and potatoes tied in cloth as offerings; Divya Shila, a rock pillar, is worshipped before entering the shrine.

Access

Pilgrims reach Yamunotri by road to Janki Chatti or Hanuman Chatti and then a trek of roughly 5–6 kilometres on foot, or by pony or palanquin. The shrine draws hundreds of thousands of devotees each season.
