Postal code

A postal code (also known locally in various English-speaking countries as a postcode, post code, PIN, or ZIP Code) is a series of letters or digits, or both, sometimes including spaces or punctuation, included in a postal address for the purpose of sorting mail.

History

Postal districts were introduced in large cities in the nineteenth century — London was divided into districts in 1857 — but modern national postal code systems spread after the Second World War. Ukraine introduced an early system in 1932, Germany launched a four-digit system in 1941, Argentina followed in 1958, the United Kingdom began rolling out postcodes in 1959, and the United States introduced the ZIP Code in 1963.

Formats

Most systems are numeric, ranging from three to ten digits. Others are alphanumeric: Canadian postal codes alternate letters and digits (e.g., K1A 0B1), and British postcodes combine letters and digits in outward and inward parts (e.g., SW1A 1AA). Codes usually identify progressively smaller areas: a region, a city or district, and finally a delivery route, street segment, or group of addresses.

Uses

Beyond mail sorting, postal codes are widely used in logistics, e-commerce address validation, geodemographic analysis, insurance rating, and mapping. Because they often correspond to geographic areas, they are frequently used as small-area units in statistics and market research, even though their boundaries are designed for delivery rather than administration.
