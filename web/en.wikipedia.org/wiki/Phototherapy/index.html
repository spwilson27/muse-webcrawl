Phototherapy
SpecialtyDermatology, neonatology, psychiatry
UsesNeonatal jaundice, psoriasis, seasonal affective disorder, eczema, vitiligo

Phototherapy, or light therapy, is the use of light — visible, ultraviolet or laser — to treat medical conditions. It is applied in several distinct fields for quite different purposes.

Neonatal jaundice

Phototherapy is the standard treatment for neonatal jaundice. Blue-green light (around 460–490 nm) shone on the infant's skin converts bilirubin into water-soluble isomers that can be excreted without conjugation in the liver, preventing the neurotoxicity (kernicterus) that high bilirubin can cause. Treatment is delivered by overhead lamps, fibre-optic blankets ("bili-blankets") or LED devices, with the eyes shielded. The treatment was discovered in the 1950s, famously after a nurse at Rochford General Hospital in England noticed that jaundiced babies near a window faded faster in sunlight.

Dermatology

Ultraviolet phototherapy is used for skin diseases including psoriasis, atopic eczema and vitiligo. Narrowband UVB (311 nm) is now the commonest modality; PUVA combines the photosensitising drug psoralen with UVA. Treatments are given in graded exposures in a light cabinet, balancing benefit against burning and the cumulative risk of skin cancer.

Light therapy for mood and sleep

Bright light therapy — typically 10,000 lux of white light for about 30 minutes in the morning — is a first-line treatment for seasonal affective disorder and is also used to stabilise circadian rhythms in sleep disorders and as an adjunct in non-seasonal depression. Light acts through intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells to suppress melatonin and reset the body clock.
