V. O. Key Jr.

Valdimer Orlando Key Jr.
Born: 13 March 1908, Austin, Texas
Died: 4 October 1963
Field: Political science
Institutions: Johns Hopkins University; Harvard University
Known for: Southern Politics (1949); The Responsible Electorate (1966)

Valdimer Orlando Key Jr. (13 March 1908 – 4 October 1963) was an American political scientist, one of the most influential scholars of American politics of the 20th century. He pioneered the behavioural study of voting and of state and regional politics, and his work reshaped how scholars understood elections, parties and public opinion.

Career

Key was educated at the University of Texas and took his PhD at the University of Chicago in 1934. He worked briefly for federal agencies before teaching at Johns Hopkins University, where he built a celebrated graduate programme, and from 1951 at Harvard University, where he held the Jonathan Trumbull Chair. He served as president of the American Political Science Association in 1958.

Major works

Southern Politics in State and Nation (1949) remains the classic study of the one-party South. Based on extensive field research, it showed how the region's politics were structured around race and the legacy of disfranchisement, and famously divided the South into distinct political subcultures — the "Black Belt" counties, the hill country, and the urban areas.

With Milton C. Cummings Jr., Key also wrote The Responsible Electorate: Rationality in Presidential Voting 1936–1960, published posthumously in 1966. Challenging the picture of voters as uninformed and impressionable, Key argued that "voters are not fools": switchers between parties generally moved for intelligible reasons connected to candidates' records and issues. His other major works include Public Opinion and American Democracy (1961) and Politics, Parties, and Pressure Groups (1942).

Legacy

Key's dictum that the "perennial debate" of American politics turns on the relationship between public opinion and government policy remains a touchstone of the discipline. The American Political Science Association's V. O. Key Award honours the best book on state politics.
