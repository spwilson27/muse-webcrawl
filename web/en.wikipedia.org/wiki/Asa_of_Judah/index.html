Asa of Judah

Reign: c. 911/910 – 870/869 BC
Predecessor: Abijam
Successor: Jehoshaphat
House: House of David

Asa ruled Judah for 41 years

Asa (Hebrew: אסא) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the third king of the Kingdom of Judah and a descendant of David. He reigned for 41 years, conventionally dated to about 911–870 BC, succeeding his father Abijam.

Reign in the biblical account

The books of Kings and Chronicles portray Asa as a reforming king who removed idols, foreign altars and high places, and deposed his grandmother Maacah as queen mother for making an image of Asherah. He defeated an invading army under "Zerah the Ethiopian" at Mareshah and fortified Judah's cities in peacetime. When Baasha of Israel blockaded Judah at Ramah, Asa sent temple treasure to Ben-Hadad of Aram-Damascus, who broke his alliance with Israel — an act the prophet Hanani rebuked. In his old age Asa was afflicted in his feet and, Chronicles notes critically, sought physicians rather than God; he was succeeded by his son Jehoshaphat.
