Christiansborg Palace

Christiansborg Palace
Location: Slotsholmen, Copenhagen, Denmark
Construction started: 1733 (first palace); current palace 1907–1928
Architect (current): Thorvald Jørgensen
Style: Neo-Baroque

Christiansborg Palace (Danish: Christiansborg Slot) is a palace and government building on the islet of Slotsholmen in central Copenhagen, Denmark. It is the seat of the Danish Parliament (the Folketing), the Danish Prime Minister's Office, and the Supreme Court of Denmark. Several parts of the palace are also used by the Danish monarch, including the Royal Reception Rooms, the Palace Chapel, and the Royal Stables.

The palace is thus home to the three supreme powers of Denmark: the executive power, the legislative power, and the judicial power — a unique arrangement among government buildings worldwide.

History

The site has been occupied by castles since Bishop Absalon built his castle there in 1167. The first Christiansborg Palace, a lavish Rococo palace built for Christian VI by Elias David Häusser, was completed in the 1740s but burned down in 1794. The second Christiansborg, designed by Christian Frederik Hansen in the Neoclassical style, was destroyed by another fire in 1884.

The third and present Christiansborg was built between 1907 and 1928 to a Neo-Baroque design by Thorvald Jørgensen, reusing granite from buildings across Denmark in its facade. Beneath the palace, visitors can see the excavated ruins of Absalon's Castle and Copenhagen Castle.

The Royal Reception Rooms

The King's (now Monarch's) Reception Rooms, located on the first floor of the north wing, are used for official functions and include the Throne Room, where foreign ambassadors present their credentials, and the Great Hall, hung with Bjørn Nørgaard's tapestries depicting 1,000 years of Danish history, a gift for Queen Margrethe II's 50th birthday in 1990.
