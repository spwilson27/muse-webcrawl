AstraZeneca plc is a British-Swedish multinational pharmaceutical and biotechnology company headquartered in Cambridge, England. It was formed in 1999 by the merger of Sweden's Astra AB and the British Zeneca Group (itself demerged from Imperial Chemical Industries in 1993). It is one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies.

Products and research

AstraZeneca's portfolio concentrates on oncology, cardiovascular, renal and metabolism diseases, and respiratory and immunology. Major products have included the lung cancer drug Tagrisso (osimertinib), the PARP inhibitor Lynparza (olaparib), the diabetes drug Farxiga (dapagliflozin), the asthma inhaler Symbicort, and earlier blockbusters Crestor and Nexium. In 2021 it acquired the rare-disease company Alexion Pharmaceuticals for $39 billion.

COVID-19 vaccine

With the University of Oxford, AstraZeneca developed the adenovirus-vector COVID-19 vaccine Vaxzevria, supplied on a not-for-profit basis during the pandemic and administered in billions of doses worldwide. Its rollout was complicated by rare blood-clotting side effects (thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome), which led several countries to restrict its use by age, and the vaccine was withdrawn globally in 2024 amid surplus of newer vaccines.
