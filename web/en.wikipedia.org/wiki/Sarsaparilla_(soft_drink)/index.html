Sarsaparilla (soft drink)

Sarsaparilla is a soft drink originally made from the roots of the sarsaparilla vine (Smilax ornata and related species) native to Central and South America. In the United States it developed as a distinctive drink of the nineteenth-century frontier, closely related to root beer, and in Australia and parts of Asia it remains a popular carbonated beverage, usually dark reddish-brown in colour.

History

Sarsaparilla root was introduced to Europe in the sixteenth century as a supposed treatment for syphilis and as a general tonic or "blood purifier." By the 1800s American pharmacies served carbonated sarsaparilla drinks from soda fountains, and bottled versions were sold as patent medicines before becoming purely refreshments. Because sassafras and safrole were later restricted, modern recipes rely on artificial flavourings or safe extracts, often combined with wintergreen, liquorice, vanilla and molasses-like notes.

Regional varieties

American sarsaparilla is similar to root beer but typically emphasises the sarsaparilla flavour over sassafras. Australian sarsaparilla, produced by brewers such as Bundaberg, has a stronger liquorice and aniseed character. In Taiwan, Hong Kong and Southeast Asia, HeySong Sarsaparilla and A&W's sarsaparilla variants are common, the latter marketed in Malaysia after root beer was rebranded for religious sensitivities.
