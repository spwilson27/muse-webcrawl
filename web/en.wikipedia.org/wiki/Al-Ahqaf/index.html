Al-Ahqaf

Al-Aḥqāf (Arabic: الأحقاف)
Classification: Meccan
Number: 46th surah
Verses: 35
Position: Juz' 26

Al-Ahqaf (Arabic: الأحقاف, al-aḥqāf; meaning: "The Wind-Curved Sandhills", "The Dunes") is the 46th chapter (surah) of the Qur'an with 35 verses (ayat). The chapter is named after the "Ahqaf", the sand dunes in the desert where the ancient people of ʿĀd lived, and where the Prophet Hud is said to have preached to them. It is a Meccan surah, believed to have been revealed in Mecca before the Hijrah.

Summary

The surah opens with the mysterious letters Ḥā Mīm and affirms the revelation of the Book from God. It warns those who worship beings that can neither hear nor respond, and describes the fate of the people of ʿĀd, who rejected Hud despite the signs given to them and were destroyed by a devastating wind. It also recounts the story of a company of jinn who listened to the Qur'an, believed in it, and returned to warn their people. The chapter closes by exhorting the Prophet Muhammad to patience, as the messengers of strong will were patient before him.

Themes

The themes of Al-Ahqaf include the unity of God, the consequences of disbelief illustrated by the punishment of ʿĀd, the duty of kindness to parents, and the endurance required of the prophets. The destruction of ʿĀd at al-Ahqaf — traditionally located in the Rub' al Khali region of southern Arabia — serves as the chapter's central warning example.
