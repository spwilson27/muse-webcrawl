Leap second

A leap second is a one-second adjustment that is occasionally applied to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) in order to keep it close to universal time (UT1), the time standard based on the rotation of the Earth. Because the Earth's rotation is irregular and gradually slowing — chiefly due to tidal friction — atomic time (TAI) and astronomical time drift apart. Leap seconds are inserted (a negative leap second is possible in principle but has never been used) at the end of 30 June or 31 December UTC, announced about six months in advance in Bulletin C of the International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS).

Mechanism

UTC is derived from International Atomic Time (TAI) but differs from it by an integer number of seconds. When the difference between UT1 and UTC approaches 0.9 seconds, IERS schedules a leap second: the minute 23:59 UTC gains an extra second labelled 23:59:60, making that day 86,401 seconds long. Leap seconds are applied simultaneously worldwide — in local time the insertion may fall at different clock times depending on the time zone.

History

The leap second system was introduced in 1972, replacing earlier "rubber seconds" and step adjustments used since UTC began in 1960. Ten seconds were added to the initial offset, and 27 further leap seconds have been inserted since, all at the ends of June or December, with December insertions more common. The insertion rate has slowed over the decades as the Earth's rotation has, on average, sped up relative to earlier 20th-century rates; between 1999 and 2005 no leap seconds were needed.

Problems and future

Leap seconds cause difficulties for computing and telecommunications systems, which must handle a minute with 61 seconds: various software failures have been attributed to leap second events, including outages in 2012 and issues around the 2016 insertion. Workarounds include "leap smearing", in which the extra second is spread over many hours. Because of these problems, international bodies debated abolishing leap seconds for years; in November 2022 the General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) resolved that the maximum allowable difference between UT1 and UTC will be increased in or before 2035, effectively suspending leap second insertions for at least a century.
