Colorado School of Mines

The Colorado School of Mines (informally Mines) is a public research university in Golden, Colorado, United States, specialising in science and engineering, particularly in fields related to earth resources, energy, and the environment. Founded in 1874, it enrolls several thousand undergraduate and graduate students and is known for its selective admissions and rigorous curriculum.

History

Mines was founded as the Territorial School of Mines under the auspices of the Episcopal Church and Bishop George Maxwell Randall, opening in 1874 shortly before Colorado statehood. The school responded to the needs of the mining industry in the Rocky Mountain region. Its campus features the large whitewashed "M" emblem on Mount Zion, maintained by students since 1908. In 2019 the university announced a long-term strategy ("MINES@150") and dropped the usage of the longer name in most branding, though its legal name remains Colorado School of Mines.

Academics

The university offers degrees in engineering and applied science fields including petroleum engineering, geological engineering, metallurgical and materials engineering, mining engineering, geophysics, mechanical, civil, electrical, chemical, and computer science, as well as applied mathematics, economics, and chemistry. Mines is consistently ranked among the top engineering schools in the United States and its graduates are heavily recruited by the energy and resources industries.

Campus and traditions

Landmarks include Guggenheim Hall, with its distinctive cupola; the Arthur Lakes Library; and the Geology Museum, which houses one of the moon rocks collected by Apollo 17 as well as extensive mineral collections. Student traditions include E-Days (Engineering Days), a spring festival featuring events such as the ore cart pull to downtown Denver, and the senior practice of carrying a rock from one's home state to add to the "M" on Mount Zion.

Athletics

The Mines Orediggers compete in NCAA Division II as members of the Rocky Mountain Athletic Conference. Football, played at Marv Kay Stadium, has been a particular strength, and the school is known for the academic demands placed on its student-athletes.
