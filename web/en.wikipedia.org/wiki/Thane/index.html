Thane

Country: India
State: Maharashtra
District: Thane
Population (2011 census): approx. 1.84 million
Nickname: City of Lakes

Thane (Marathi: ठाणे, also historically called Tannah or Thana) is a metropolitan city in the state of Maharashtra, India, located on the north-western side of the state just outside Mumbai. It forms part of the Mumbai Metropolitan Region and is the headquarters of Thane district. Known as the "City of Lakes", it contains more than thirty lakes, and is bounded by the Yeoor hills of Sanjay Gandhi National Park and by Thane Creek.

History

Thane is one of the oldest settlements in the region; it is mentioned in works of the Greek geographer Ptolemy and was a noted port (Sristhanaka) under the Shilahara dynasty, trading with Arabia and the west coast of India. It came under Portuguese control in the 16th century — the Portuguese called it Tana — passing later to the Marathas and, in 1817, to the British.

On 16 April 1853 the first railway in India ran from Bori Bunder (Mumbai) to Thane, a distance of 34 kilometres, hauled by the locomotives Sahib, Sindh and Sultan. Thane station remains one of the busiest in the Mumbai Suburban Railway network.

Geography and demography

The city is split by Thane Creek into a western portion and an eastern portion along the creek's bank. Its lakes include Masunda Lake (Talao Pali), Upvan Lake and Kacharali Lake, and the Kopineshwar temple is one of its oldest shrines, dedicated to Shiva. The 2011 census counted about 1.84 million residents, up sharply from earlier decades as Thane grew into a residential extension of Mumbai.

Economy and transport

Thane has industrial estates in the Wagle and Balkum areas and a large commercial and retail sector centred on Ghodbunder Road. It is connected to Mumbai by the Eastern Express Highway, Ghodbunder Road, and Central Railway suburban services; the under-construction Mumbai Metro lines 4 and 5 are planned to serve the city.
