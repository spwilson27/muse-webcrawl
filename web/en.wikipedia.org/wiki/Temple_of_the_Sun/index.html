Temple of the Sun

Temple of the Sun may refer to several structures dedicated to solar deities. Best known is the Inca Qorikancha in Cusco, Peru, the richest temple of the Inca Empire, dedicated to the sun god Inti, whose walls were once covered in gold. Other examples include the temple of the sun god Surya at Konark, India (the Konark Sun Temple), and the Temple of the Sun at the Mexica (Aztec) sacred precinct in Tenochtitlan.

Qorikancha

Qorikancha ("golden enclosure") in Cusco housed the mummies of past emperors and a golden image of the sun. After the Spanish conquest much of its stonework was reused in the Dominican convent of Santo Domingo built atop it; surviving Inca walls are famed for their precision masonry.

Other sun temples

Sun temples also exist in Egypt (Abu Gorab), China (the Temple of the Sun in Beijing, an altar rather than a hall), and at numerous sites in Mesoamerica and the Andes, such as Machu Picchu's Temple of the Sun with its solstice windows.
