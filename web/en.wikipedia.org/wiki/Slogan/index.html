Slogan

A slogan is a memorable motto or phrase used in a clan, political, commercial, religious, or other context as a repetitive expression of an idea or a purpose, with the goal of persuading members of the public or a more defined target group. The Oxford Dictionary of English defines a slogan as "a short and striking or memorable phrase used in advertising." A slogan usually has the attributes of being memorable, very concise and appealing to the audience.

Etymology

The word slogan is derived from sluagh-ghairm, which is Scottish Gaelic for "battle cry" or "war cry" (or sluagh-gairm in Irish). Slogans vary from the written and the visual to the chanted and the vulgar.

Usage

Slogans are used in marketing to help a company create an image that makes it stand out from competitors, and in political campaigns to summarize a candidate's message. George Orwell wrote in his essay "Politics and the English Language" that slogans are often used to manipulate opinion.

Likeness

Leaving a deeper impact on a potential customer, a good slogan has several characteristics: it is simple, memorable, and focused on the benefit of the product or idea. Some famous examples include "Just Do It" (Nike), "Think Different" (Apple), and "I'm Lovin' It" (McDonald's).
