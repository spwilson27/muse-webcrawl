ISO 14001 is an international standard that specifies requirements for an environmental management system (EMS) that an organization can use to enhance its environmental performance. It is the principal standard of the ISO 14000 family and the only one in the family against which organizations can be certified by third parties.

Overview

The standard provides a framework following the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle: establishing an environmental policy, identifying environmental aspects and compliance obligations, setting objectives, implementing operational controls, monitoring and measuring performance, auditing, and management review aimed at continual improvement. The 2015 revision adopted the Annex SL high-level structure shared with ISO 9001, added leadership and "context of the organization" requirements, and introduced a life-cycle perspective and risk-based thinking.

Adoption

ISO 14001 is one of the most widely used management standards in the world, with several hundred thousand certificates issued across more than 170 countries. Certification is often sought to demonstrate environmental responsibility to customers, regulators and supply chains. Critics note the standard certifies the management system rather than any specific level of environmental performance.
