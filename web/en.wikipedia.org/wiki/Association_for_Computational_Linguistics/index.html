The Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL) is the principal international scientific society for researchers working on computational linguistics and natural language processing (NLP). Founded in 1962 as the Association for Machine Translation and Computational Linguistics, it adopted its present name in 1968.

Activities

The ACL organises the Annual Meeting of the ACL, the flagship conference of the field, along with regional chapters and sister conferences including EMNLP (Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing), the North American chapter conference (NAACL), and the European chapter conference (EACL). It publishes the journals Computational Linguistics and Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics (TACL), and maintains the ACL Anthology, an open-access archive of over 100,000 papers from its conferences and workshops.

Special interest groups

Dozens of special interest groups (SIGs) organise workshops on topics such as machine translation (whose WMT workshop administers shared tasks), semantics (SemEval), dialogue, summarisation and low-resource languages. The ACL's conferences grew enormously with the rise of statistical and then neural NLP; annual meetings now attract several thousand submissions and attendees.
