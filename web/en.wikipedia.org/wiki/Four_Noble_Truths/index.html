Four Noble Truths

The Four Noble Truths (Pali: cattāri ariyasaccāni) are regarded in Buddhism as the central teaching of the Buddha, delivered in his first sermon, the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, at Sarnath after his enlightenment. They diagnose the human condition and prescribe its cure, in the manner of an ancient Indian medical formula.

The truths

  1. Dukkha: there is suffering / unsatisfactoriness — birth, ageing, sickness, death, and impermanence generally are marked by dukkha.
  2. Samudaya: suffering arises from craving (taṇhā) — desire for sensual pleasure, for existence, and for non-existence — together with ignorance.
  3. Nirodha: suffering can cease — the complete fading of craving is nirvana.
  4. Magga: the path leading to the cessation of suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path.

Interpretation

The truths are to be understood, abandoned, realised and developed respectively, according to the Buddha's own account in the Sacca-vibhanga Sutta. Different Buddhist traditions emphasise them differently: they are foundational in Theravada, while Mahayana schools embed them within broader doctrines such as emptiness and Buddha-nature.
