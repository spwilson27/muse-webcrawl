Restoration (England)

Restoration
Date1660
MonarchCharles II
Preceded byThe Protectorate; Interregnum

The Restoration of the Stuart monarchy in the kingdoms of England, Scotland and Ireland took place in 1660, when Charles II returned from exile following the English Civil War, the execution of Charles I in 1649, and the republican Commonwealth and Protectorate under Oliver Cromwell.

Settlement

General George Monck's intervention and the Declaration of Breda paved the way for the Convention Parliament to proclaim Charles king in May 1660. The settlement combined a general pardon (the Indemnity and Oblivion Act) with punishment of the regicides, restoration of bishops and the prayer book, and a land settlement that left many purchasers of confiscated estates in possession.

Culture

The Restoration reopened the theatres — with actresses on stage for the first time — and names an era of literature spanning Restoration comedy, John Dryden, and the poetry and diaries of the period, including Samuel Pepys's account of the Great Plague (1665) and Great Fire of London (1666).
