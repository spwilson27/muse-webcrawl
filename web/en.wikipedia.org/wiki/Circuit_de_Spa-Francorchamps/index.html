The Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, frequently referred to as Spa, is a motor-racing circuit in the Ardennes forest near the village of Francorchamps in Belgium. It is the venue of the Belgian Grand Prix of Formula One and of the Spa 24 Hours endurance race, and is regarded by many drivers as one of the finest and most demanding circuits in the world.

History

The original triangular circuit, laid out on public roads in 1921, was 14.9 km long and fearsomely fast. The modern permanent circuit, opened in 1979 and modified several times since (notably the Bus Stop chicane complex and pit area), retains the old course's character: sweeping elevation changes of about 100 metres, the famous climbing Eau Rouge–Raidillon sequence, the long Kemmel straight and La Source hairpin.

Characteristics

At 7.004 km, Spa is the longest lap on the current Formula One calendar. Its length and Ardennes setting mean weather can differ sharply around the lap — rain on one section and sunshine on another is a classic Spa scenario. The circuit also hosts World Endurance Championship racing (the 6 Hours of Spa) and numerous national series.
