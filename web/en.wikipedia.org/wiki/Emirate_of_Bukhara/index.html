Emirate of Bukhara

The Emirate of Bukhara (Persian: امارت بخارا; Uzbek: Buxoro amirligi) was a Central Asian polity that existed from 1785 to 1920 in the territory of present-day Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and parts of Turkmenistan and Kazakhstan. It was the successor of the Khanate of Bukhara and was ruled by the Manghit dynasty, whose head, Shah Murad, assumed the title of Emir in 1785. The capital, Bukhara, was a major centre of Islamic learning and Silk Road trade.

History

Under Shah Murad and his successors, the emirate consolidated control over the Zeravshan valley and campaigned against the neighbouring Khanate of Khiva and Khanate of Kokand, and against Persia over Merv and Herat. Emir Nasrullah (r. 1827–1860) was notorious for imprisoning and executing the British officers Charles Stoddart and Arthur Conolly in 1842 — an episode of the Anglo-Russian "Great Game."

Russian expansion in Central Asia reached the emirate in the 1860s: after defeats at Irjar (1866) and the fall of Samarkand (1868), Emir Muzaffar signed a treaty making Bukhara a Russian protectorate in 1873. The emirate kept internal autonomy — the emirs ruled with their own administration, army, and coinage — while Russia controlled foreign relations. Bukhara was a major cotton supplier to Russia, and the Trans-Caspian Railway reached its territory in the 1880s.

Society and culture

The population combined sedentary Persian-speaking Tajiks and Turkic Uzbeks, with Turkmen, Arabs, Jews (the ancient Bukharan Jewish community), and others. Bukhara's madrasas drew students from across Central Asia, though by the 19th century reformist Jadid intellectuals criticized the emirate's conservatism; slavery persisted in Bukhara and Khiva into the protectorate period despite Russian pressure.

End of the emirate

After the Russian Revolution, the reformist Young Bukharans allied with the Red Army. In September 1920, forces under Mikhail Frunze stormed Bukhara; the last Emir, Mohammed Alim Khan, fled to Afghanistan. The Bukharan People's Soviet Republic was proclaimed on 8 October 1920, and in 1924–25 its territory was divided among the Uzbek and Tajik Soviet republics.
