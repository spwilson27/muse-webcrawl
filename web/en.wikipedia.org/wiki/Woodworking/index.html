Woodworking

Woodworking is the skill of making items from wood, including furniture, cabinetry, carving, joinery and turning. Along with stone, mud and animal parts, wood was one of the first materials worked by early humans, and it remains one of the most versatile materials in use today.

History

Ancient Egyptian, Chinese, Greek and Roman craftsmen developed advanced woodworking techniques, including veneering, inlay and complex joinery. Medieval guilds organised the trades, and the Industrial Revolution brought power machinery — saws, planers and lathes — that transformed production. Fine woodworking survives both as an industry and as a popular hobby.

Techniques and tools

Core operations include measuring and marking, cutting (with hand saws or machine saws), shaping with planes, chisels and routers, joining with joints such as the mortise and tenon, dovetail and dowel, gluing and clamping, and finishing with sanding, staining, varnish or oil. Modern workshops add CNC routers and laser cutters to traditional hand tools.

Materials

Woodworkers use softwoods such as pine and cedar and hardwoods such as oak, maple, walnut and mahogany, as well as manufactured boards — plywood, MDF and particleboard — chosen for cost, stability and appearance.
