Geometric mean

In mathematics, the geometric mean is a mean or average that indicates a central tendency of a finite set of positive real numbers by means of their product. The geometric mean of a data set {a1, a2, ..., an} is defined as the n-th root of the product of the numbers: (a1 · a2 · ... · an)1/n.

Properties

The geometric mean is related to the arithmetic mean through logarithms: it equals the exponential of the arithmetic mean of the logarithms of the values. By the AM–GM inequality, the geometric mean of a set of non-negative numbers is never greater than their arithmetic mean, with equality only when all the numbers are equal.

Applications

The geometric mean is appropriate for averaging ratios, percentages and growth rates, such as compound annual growth rates in finance and population growth. It is also used in geometry — where it arises in the altitude of a right triangle — in signal processing, and in the construction of indices such as the Human Development Index's component aggregation.

Geometric construction

The geometric mean of two lengths can be constructed with compass and straightedge: it is the length of the altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose other segments on the hypotenuse have the two given lengths.
