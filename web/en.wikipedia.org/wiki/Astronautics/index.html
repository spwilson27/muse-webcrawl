Astronautics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A rocket lifting off at dawn, trailing fire and smoke]
A rocket lifting off at dawn, trailing fire and smoke

Astronautics is the theory and practice of travel beyond Earth's atmosphere — the design, construction and operation of spacecraft, launch vehicles and space systems. Its theoretical foundations were laid by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, Robert H. Goddard and Hermann Oberth; its practical history runs from the V-2 (1942) and Sputnik 1 (1957) through the Moon landings to today's commercial launch industry and robotic exploration of the solar system.

2. Practice

Foundations

Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation (1903) governs all spaceflight: mass ratios and exhaust velocity set what rockets can do. Staging, guidance, orbital mechanics (Hohmann transfer orbit), life support, thermal and radiation protection, and communications form the discipline's pillars, taught within aerospace engineering alongside its atmospheric twin, Aeronautics.

Practice

Astronautics today spans launch vehicles (Falcon 9, Space Launch System), human spaceflight to the International Space Station and (in planning) the Moon and Mars, satellite constellations for communication and Earth observation, and deep-space probes. Professional bodies (the AIAA) and the term 'cosmonautics' in the Russian tradition reflect its Cold War dual parentage.
