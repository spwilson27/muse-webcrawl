Politics of Puerto Rico

The politics of Puerto Rico are shaped above all by the island's relationship with the United States. Puerto Rico is an unincorporated, organised territory of the United States, self-governing under its 1952 constitution as a "Commonwealth" (Estado Libre Asociado), but subject to the plenary power of the US Congress under the Territorial Clause. Puerto Ricans are US citizens but cannot vote in presidential elections and are represented in Congress only by a non-voting Resident Commissioner.

The status question

Local party politics are organised primarily around the status issue rather than left–right ideology. The New Progressive Party (PNP) favours statehood; the Popular Democratic Party (PPD) historically defends an enhanced commonwealth; the Puerto Rican Independence Party (PIP) advocates independence. A series of non-binding plebiscites (1967, 1993, 1998, 2012, 2017, 2020 and 2024) have produced contested results, with statehood winning pluralities or majorities in the most recent votes, but Congress has not acted to change the status.

Government

The Governor heads the executive; a bicameral Legislative Assembly (Senate and House of Representatives) enacts local law. Since 2016, the federal fiscal oversight board created by the PROMESA Act has exercised strong control over the island's budget amid a major public-debt crisis. Newer parties such as the Citizen's Victory Movement (MVC) and Project Dignity have challenged the traditional two-party dominance.
