The Ohio Senate is the upper house of the Ohio General Assembly, the state legislature of the U.S. state of Ohio; the other house is the Ohio House of Representatives. The Senate meets at the Ohio Statehouse in Columbus, Ohio.

The Senate consists of 33 members, each representing a district composed of three contiguous House districts and roughly 350,000 residents. Senators serve four-year terms, staggered so that about half the chamber is elected every two years. Under term limits adopted in 1992, senators may serve no more than two consecutive terms (eight years). The chamber is led by the Senate President, and it confirms many gubernatorial appointments, as provided by the state constitution.

History

The Ohio Senate was established by the state constitution of 1802 and first met in 1803. The modern 33-district structure, with each Senate district comprising three House districts, dates from the 1966 constitutional amendment on legislative apportionment.
