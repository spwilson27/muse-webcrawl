John the Apostle

John the Apostle (c. 6 AD – c. 100 AD) was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus according to the New Testament. Son of Zebedee and brother of James the Great, fishermen on the Sea of Galilee, he is traditionally identified as the "Beloved Disciple", the author of the Gospel of John, the three Epistles of John, and the Book of Revelation — identifications debated by modern scholars.

In the New Testament

John, with his brother James and Peter, formed Jesus's inner circle, witnessing the Transfiguration, the raising of Jairus's daughter, and the Agony in the Garden. At the Crucifixion the Beloved Disciple stood by the cross and took Mary into his home. Church tradition places his later ministry at Ephesus and exile on Patmos, where Revelation was received.

Authorship debate

Ancient tradition unified John the Apostle, John the Evangelist, and John of Patmos. Many scholars now distinguish them, dating the Fourth Gospel's final form to c. 90–110 AD and treating its author as an anonymous member of a Johannine community, while acknowledging early testimony from Irenaeus linking it to John.

Legacy

John is patron saint of love, loyalty, friendships, and authors. The Basilica of St. John in Ephesus marks his traditional tomb. His symbol, the eagle, reflects the soaring theology of his Gospel.
