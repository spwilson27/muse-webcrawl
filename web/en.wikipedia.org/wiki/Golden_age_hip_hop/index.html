Golden age hip hop

Golden age hip hop is a name given to a period in hip hop music from the mid-1980s to the early 1990s, characterised by its diversity, quality, innovation, and influence within the mainstream popular-music scene. It was a period when hip hop transitioned from the old-school sound of disco-influenced party rap into a new, complex form built on dense sampling, intricate lyricism, and wide-ranging subject matter.

Characteristics

Golden age production relied heavily on sampling, especially of funk and jazz records, using samplers such as the E-mu SP-1200 and the Akai MPC. Lyricists developed multisyllabic rhyme schemes, political and Afrocentric themes, and storytelling; albums from the era — Public Enemy's It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988), De La Soul's 3 Feet High and Rising (1989), and Eric B. & Rakim's Paid in Full (1987) — are routinely ranked among the greatest in the genre.

Themes and scenes

The period encompassed New York's Native Tongues collective (A Tribe Called Quest, De La Soul, Jungle Brothers), politically charged acts such as Public Enemy and Boogie Down Productions, and the emergence of West Coast gangsta rap with N.W.A's Straight Outta Compton (1988). Legal rulings on uncleared samples in the early 1990s raised production costs and helped bring the era to a close.
