Transudate

Transudate is extravascular fluid with low protein content and a low specific gravity (< 1.012). It is essentially an ultrafiltrate of blood plasma that results from increased fluid pressure or decreased colloid osmotic forces in the blood, rather than from vessel injury or inflammation — the mechanism that produces an exudate.

Causes and characteristics

Transudates are characteristically clear, pale yellow, and contain few cells. Common causes include congestive heart failure (increased hydrostatic pressure), cirrhosis with portal hypertension and hypoalbuminemia, nephrotic syndrome (loss of plasma protein), and severe malnutrition. In the pleural space, transudative effusions are distinguished from exudative ones using Light's criteria, comparing fluid and serum protein and lactate dehydrogenase levels.

The distinction guides diagnosis: a transudate points toward systemic conditions such as heart failure, while an exudate suggests local disease such as infection or malignancy.
