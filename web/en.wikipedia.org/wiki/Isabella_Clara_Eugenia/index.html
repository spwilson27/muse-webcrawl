Isabella Clara Eugenia

Born12 August 1566, Segovia, Spain
Died1 December 1633 (aged 67), Brussels
ReignSovereign of the Spanish Netherlands (with Albert VII), 1598–1621; Governor 1621–1633
SpouseAlbert VII, Archduke of Austria

Isabella Clara Eugenia (12 August 1566 – 1 December 1633), sometimes referred to as Isabel Clara Eugenia, was a Spanish infanta, daughter of King Philip II of Spain and his third wife Elisabeth of Valois. With her husband, Archduke Albert VII of Austria, she was co-sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands, and after his death she ruled as governor until her own death.

Life

Isabella was Philip II's favourite daughter and acted as his confidante and secretary in his last years; through her mother she was also a claimant to the French throne during the French Wars of Religion. In 1598 Philip settled the Spanish Netherlands on Isabella as her dowry on her marriage to Albert, with the condition that the provinces would revert to Spain if the couple had no children — as proved the case.

Rule in Brussels

The reign of the Archdukes Albert and Isabella brought relative peace and a Catholic revival to the southern Netherlands after decades of war: the Twelve Years' Truce with the Dutch Republic (1609) allowed recovery, and their court at Brussels became a centre of Baroque culture, patronising Peter Paul Rubens, who served as court painter and diplomat. The long Siege of Ostend (1601–1604) marked the beginning of their reign. After Albert's death in 1621, Isabella — who took the habit of the Poor Clares — governed as regent for Philip IV, contending with the resumption of war with the Dutch. She died in Brussels in 1633.
