Felipe Ángeles International Airport

Felipe Ángeles International Airport
IATA: NLU · ICAO: MMSM
Location: Santa Lucía, Zumpango, State of Mexico
Opened: 21 March 2022

Felipe Ángeles International Airport (Spanish: Aeropuerto Internacional Felipe Ángeles, AIFA) is an international airport serving Mexico City, located at the Santa Lucía Air Force Base in Zumpango, State of Mexico, about 45 km north-east of the capital's centre. It was built to relieve congestion at the older Mexico City International Airport.

History

The airport was the flagship infrastructure project of President Andrés Manuel López Obrador, who cancelled the partially built Texcoco airport (NAIM) after a public consultation in 2018 and opted instead to convert the Santa Lucía military base. Construction, carried out by the Mexican Army's engineers beginning in October 2019, produced a new passenger terminal, two commercial runways and a control tower while retaining a military runway. The airport was inaugurated on 21 March 2022.

It is named after General Felipe Ángeles, a Revolutionary-era artillery officer and ally of Pancho Villa.

Facilities and operations

The terminal was designed with capacity for around 20 million passengers annually in its first phase, with expansion plans toward 80 million. Early operations were modest, but cargo flights were directed to AIFA by decree in 2023, making it a major freight hub, and passenger numbers have grown as more domestic and some international routes (including to the United States, Cuba, Venezuela and Colombia) were added. Ground access relies on highways and the Mexibús; a suburban rail extension from Buenavista station has been under construction.

Criticism

Critics questioned the airport's distance from the city, its simultaneous operation with the existing airport (raising airspace concerns), and the cost of cancelling the Texcoco project. Supporters credit its rapid, on-budget construction under military management.
