Roman–Etruscan Wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Roman–Etruscan Wars, also known as the Etruscan Wars, were a series of wars fought between ancient Rome (in both its Regal and early Republican periods) and the Etruscan city-states, stretching over centuries. The Etruscans gave Rome much of its early culture — tradition held that Rome's last three kings were Etruscan — and Rome's expansion northward was resisted by the Etruscan League.

Major conflicts

Legendary wars under the kings were fought against Veii and Fidenae. After the expulsion of the Tarquins (509 BC), the exiled king Tarquin the Proud enlisted Etruscan aid, leading to the Battle of Silva Arsia and Lars Porsena's siege of Rome. The Battle of Aricia (504 BC) weakened Etruscan power in Latium. Rome's longest Etruscan war was against Veii, ending with the city's ten-year siege and capture by Camillus in 396 BC.

Decline of Etruria

Further wars in the 4th and 3rd centuries BC, including the Battle of Lake Vadimo (310 and 283 BC), broke Etruscan resistance. Caught between Rome, the Gauls, and the Samnites, the Etruscan cities were absorbed one by one; Volsinii was destroyed in 264 BC. By the 1st century BC the Etruscans had been fully Romanised, though their influence on Roman religion, engineering, and symbols — such as the fasces — endured.
