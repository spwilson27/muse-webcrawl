Hakra River

The Hakra River (also known as the Ghaggar-Hakra in its upper Indian reaches) is a largely dry, ephemeral river channel that flows through northwestern India and eastern Pakistan. Its palaeochannel runs from the foothills of the Himalayas through Haryana, Rajasthan, and the Cholistan Desert of Punjab and Sindh, roughly parallel to and east of the Indus.

Course and history

The modern Ghaggar River is a seasonal stream that disappears into the sands of the Thar Desert; downstream its continuation is traced as the Hakra channel through Cholistan toward the Rann of Kutch region. In ancient times the system likely carried substantially more water, fed in part by rivers that have since been captured by the Yamuna and the Sutlej. Numerous archaeological sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation lie along its banks, and the river has frequently been identified with the Sarasvatī River of the Rigveda, though the extent and dating of its ancient flow remain debated among geologists and archaeologists.
