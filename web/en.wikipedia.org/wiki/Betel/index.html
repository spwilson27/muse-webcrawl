Betel

Piper betle
FamilyPiperaceae
GenusPiper
Native rangeSoutheast Asia
Used forLeaf for paan (betel quid)

Betel (Piper betle) is a climbing vine in the pepper family, Piperaceae, cultivated across South and Southeast Asia for its heart-shaped, glossy leaves. The leaf is the essential wrapping of the betel quid — in India, paan — a preparation of areca nut (often called "betel nut", though it comes from a different plant, the areca palm), slaked lime and flavourings that has been chewed as a mild stimulant by hundreds of millions of people.

Cultivation

The plant is a dioecious evergreen perennial propagated by cuttings, trained on supports in shaded, humid enclosures (in Bengal called boroj). It requires rich soil, frequent watering and protection from sun and frost. Leaves are graded by quality; famous regional varieties include Banarasi and Magahi paan from India and the leaves of Bangladesh, a major exporter.

The betel quid

A typical quid folds a betel leaf around slices of areca nut and a smear of slaked lime (calcium hydroxide), often with catechu, tobacco, cardamom, cloves or sweeteners. Chewing releases alkaloids from the nut, chiefly arecoline, producing mild euphoria and alertness, copious red saliva that chewers spit out, and characteristically stained teeth. The practice dates back thousands of years — areca-stained teeth appear in burials over 4,000 years old in Southeast Asia — and betel has deep ceremonial, social and medicinal roles, from hospitality to weddings.

Health effects

The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies the betel quid, with or without tobacco, and the areca nut itself as Group 1 carcinogens. Chronic chewing is strongly associated with oral cancer and oral submucous fibrosis, and governments across Asia run campaigns against the habit even as it remains culturally entrenched.
