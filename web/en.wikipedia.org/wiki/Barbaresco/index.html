Barbaresco

Barbaresco is an Italian wine made with the Nebbiolo grape. It is produced in the Piedmont region in the communes of Barbaresco, Neive, Treiso, and part of San Rocco Seno d'Elvio, and was awarded Denominazione di Origine Controllata (DOC) status in 1966 and Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG) status in 1980. Along with its neighbor Barolo, it is considered among Italy's greatest red wines.

History

Although Nebbiolo wines had long been made in the area—historically sweet and lightly sparkling—the modern dry style of Barbaresco is credited to Domizio Cavazza, director of the Royal Enological School of Alba, who founded the Cantina Sociale di Barbaresco in 1894 and began producing a dry red wine under the Barbaresco name. Production declined in the mid-twentieth century as growers sold grapes to Barolo producers, until a new generation in the 1960s and 1970s, led by figures such as Angelo Gaja, revived estate bottling and raised the wine's international profile. The Produttori del Barbaresco cooperative, founded in 1958, also played a central role.

Regulations and style

Barbaresco must be made from 100% Nebbiolo and aged a minimum of two years, at least nine months in wood; the Riserva designation requires four years of aging. Compared with Barolo, Barbaresco is often described as more elegant and approachable in youth, a difference attributed to the slightly warmer climate and the moderating influence of the Tanaro river, though styles vary widely between traditional long macerations and modern shorter extractions.
