Lapland (Finland)

Lapland (Finnish: Lappi)
Region of Finland
Capital: Rovaniemi
Area: about 98,984 km² (largest region)
Population: about 177,000 (lowest density in Finland)

Lapland (Finnish: Lappi; Northern Sami: Sápmi) is the largest and northernmost region of Finland. Its capital is Rovaniemi, situated on the Arctic Circle. The region covers roughly a quarter of Finland's area but contains only about three percent of its population, giving it by far the lowest population density in the country. Finnish Lapland forms the southern part of the wider transnational region of Sápmi, the homeland of the indigenous Sámi people, which extends across northern Norway, Sweden and Russia's Kola Peninsula.

Geography

The landscape consists of vast coniferous forests, bogs and treeless fells (tunturi), including Finland's highest point, Halti (1,324 m), on the Norwegian border. Major rivers include the Kemijoki, Finland's longest river, and the Tornio and Muonio rivers forming the Swedish border. In the far north the region experiences polar night in midwinter and the midnight sun in midsummer.

Economy and tourism

Traditional livelihoods include reindeer herding—practised by Sámi and Finns alike—forestry, and mining (notably the Kemi chromite mine and gold mining at Kittilä). Tourism is a mainstay: the ski resorts of Levi, Ylläs, Saariselkä and Ruka (just south of the region), Santa Claus Village at Rovaniemi, glass-roofed aurora hotels and wilderness activities draw visitors year-round, especially in winter.

Administration and history

Historically a province (lääni) of Finland from 1938, Lapland became one of the country's self-governing regions (maakunta) in the regional reforms of the 1990s and 2010. During the Second World War, northern Finland was the theatre of the Lapland War (1944–45), in which Finnish forces expelled German troops, who destroyed much of Rovaniemi and other settlements as they retreated. The region's municipalities include Inari, the centre of Finnish Sámi culture and seat of the Sámi Parliament of Finland.
