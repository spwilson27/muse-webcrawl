Service manual

A service manual is a document — sometimes called a workshop manual, repair manual, or technical manual — that provides instructions and specifications for the inspection, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair of a product, machine, or vehicle. Unlike a user manual (owner's manual), which explains routine operation for the end user, a service manual is written for trained technicians and contains detailed technical information.

Contents

Typical contents include:

  • Technical specifications, tolerances, torques, capacities, and calibration data
  • Exploded diagrams, schematics, and wiring diagrams
  • Disassembly and reassembly procedures
  • Diagnostic charts and troubleshooting flowcharts keyed to symptoms or error/fault codes
  • Special tool requirements and part numbers
  • Maintenance schedules and adjustment procedures

Automotive manuals

Motor vehicle manufacturers publish factory service manuals for their dealer networks. Independent publishers such as Haynes and Chilton produce condensed repair manuals for popular cars and motorcycles aimed at owners and independent workshops. Since the 1990s many manuals have moved to electronic formats (CD-ROM, DVD, and online subscriptions such as manufacturer technical information systems).

Right to repair

Access to service manuals has been a central issue in the right-to-repair movement, which argues that manufacturers should make manuals, parts, and diagnostic tools available to independent repairers and owners on fair terms. Legislation in the European Union and in several US states, and the 2012 Massachusetts automotive data-access law, has required broader availability of repair information.
