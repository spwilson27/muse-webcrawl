London Review of Books
Type: Literary magazine
Frequency: Fortnightly (24 issues/year)
Founded: 1979
Editor: Mary-Kay Wilmers (1979–2021); Jean McNicol and Alice Spawls (from 2021)
Based: Bloomsbury, London

The London Review of Books (LRB) is a British literary magazine published twice monthly that features articles and essays — usually lengthy, sometimes running to 10,000 words or more — on literature, politics, history, philosophy and the arts. It was founded in 1979, during the year-long lock-out of The Times, and its founding editor was Karl Miller.

History

The LRB was founded by Karl Miller, former editor of the New Statesman and The Listener, together with Mary-Kay Wilmers and Susannah Clapp. It first appeared as a paper insert in The New York Review of Books during the suspension of The Times in 1979, and became independent in 1980. Mary-Kay Wilmers became editor in 1992, editing the paper until 2021, when she was succeeded by Jean McNicol and Alice Spawls.

Content

The magazine is known for its long review essays, in which books serve as the occasion for broader reflection. Contributors have included Perry Anderson, Tariq Ali, John Lanchester, Hilary Mantel, Amia Srinivasan, Alan Bennett (whose diaries became a fixture), Terry Eagleton and Christopher Hitchens. Its personal advertisements column became famous enough to be collected in book form (They Call Me Naughty Lola).

Bookshop

The London Review Bookshop opened in Bloomsbury in 2003, and a café followed in 2007; the magazine also runs a podcast and an online archive dating back to the first issue.
