Pál Turán (Hungarian: [ˈpaːl ˈturaːn]; 18 August 1910 – 26 September 1976) was a Hungarian mathematician who worked primarily in extremal combinatorics, graph theory and analytic number theory. He was a long-time collaborator of Paul Erdős, with whom he published 28 papers.

Work

Turán's theorem (1941) on the maximum number of edges in a graph not containing a complete subgraph is a founding result of extremal graph theory, and the Turán graph is the extremal structure. His "power sum method" gave new proofs and results in number theory, and with Erdős he founded probabilistic number theory, introducing the Erdős–Turán theorem on the distribution of prime divisors.

Life

Turán received his Ph.D. from Eötvös Loránd University in 1935 under Lipót Fejér. Because of anti-Jewish laws he could not obtain a university post for years and survived the Second World War in a labour camp. After the war he became a professor in Budapest and a member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.
