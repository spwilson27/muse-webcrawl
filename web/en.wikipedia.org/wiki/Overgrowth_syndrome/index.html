Overgrowth syndrome

Overgrowth syndromes are a group of genetic disorders characterised by abnormally increased growth of the whole body or of particular parts, typically present from before birth or early childhood. Affected individuals may show macrosomia at birth, macrocephaly, and advanced bone age.

Examples

Well-known overgrowth syndromes include Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome, Sotos syndrome, Weaver syndrome, and Simpson–Golabi–Behmel syndrome. Segmental overgrowth conditions such as Proteus syndrome and the PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum involve patchy, mosaic growth rather than uniform enlargement.

Causes and management

Many of these conditions arise from dysregulation of growth-signalling pathways, including imprinting defects on chromosome 11p15 in Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome and variants in NSD1 in Sotos syndrome. Several carry an increased risk of embryonal tumours in childhood, so management often includes regular tumour screening, monitoring of growth and development, and genetic counselling.
