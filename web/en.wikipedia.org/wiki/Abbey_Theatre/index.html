The Abbey Theatre (Irish: Amharclann na Mainistreach), also known as the National Theatre of Ireland (Irish: Amharclann Náisiúnta na hÉireann), in Dublin, Ireland, is one of the country's leading cultural institutions. Founded by W. B. Yeats and Lady Gregory in 1904 as part of the Irish Literary Revival, it opened on 27 December 1904 and has remained a central stage for Irish drama.

Early years

The Abbey grew out of the Irish Literary Theatre and the Irish National Theatre Society, with Annie Horniman funding the conversion of a former morgue and music hall on Abbey Street. Its early repertoire — plays by Yeats, Lady Gregory, and J. M. Synge — helped define an Irish national drama. The 1907 premiere of Synge's The Playboy of the Western World provoked the famous "Playboy riots". Seán O'Casey's Dublin trilogy followed in the 1920s; The Plough and the Stars (1926) also caused riots. In 1925 the Abbey became the first state-subsidised theatre in the English-speaking world.

Later history

The original building burned down in 1951; the company performed at the Queen's Theatre until a new theatre, designed by Michael Scott, opened on the site in 1966, incorporating the smaller Peacock Theatre. The Abbey has premiered works by Brian Friel, Tom Murphy, Marina Carr, and Conor McPherson, and tours internationally. Long-standing plans exist for redevelopment or relocation of the theatre.
