Divisor (algebraic geometry)

In algebraic geometry, a divisor is a formal linear combination of codimension-one subvarieties of an algebraic variety. Weil divisors are formal sums of irreducible hypersurfaces with integer coefficients; on a smooth variety every Weil divisor can locally be described by a single equation. Cartier divisors are defined by collections of rational functions on an open cover, and always give rise to line bundles; on smooth varieties the two notions coincide.

Divisor class group and Picard group

Modulo linear equivalence (divisors of rational functions), Weil divisors form the divisor class group Cl(X), while Cartier divisors modulo equivalence give the Picard group Pic(X). Divisors are central to the study of projective embeddings, the Riemann–Roch theorem and intersection theory.
