Ragusa, Sicily

Ragusa Ibla, the historic lower town.

Ragusa (Sicilian: Rausa) is a city and comune in southern Sicily, Italy, capital of the province of Ragusa. It lies in the Hyblaean Mountains and has a population of about 73,000. Together with seven other towns in the Val di Noto, it is listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site for its late Baroque architecture.

History

The hilltop site was inhabited in antiquity as Hybla Heraea. After the devastating earthquake of 1693, Ragusa was rebuilt in two parts: Ragusa Superiore, laid out on a regular grid on the plateau, and Ragusa Ibla, rebuilt on the medieval street plan in the gorge below, filled with Baroque churches and palazzi, including the Cathedral of San Giorgio with its monumental staircase.

Economy and culture

Ragusa is one of the most prosperous cities in Sicily, with an economy based on agriculture, food processing, and banking — it was the home of Banca Agricola Popolare di Ragusa. The area also produces the DOP Ragusano cheese. The city and surrounding countryside served as filming locations for the television series Inspector Montalbano. See also Val di Noto and Sicily.
