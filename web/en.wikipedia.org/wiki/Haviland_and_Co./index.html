Haviland & Co.

Haviland & Co. is a French manufacturer of porcelain, founded in Limoges in the 1840s by David Haviland, an American china importer from New York. The company became one of the most famous producers of Limoges porcelain, supplying tableware to heads of state and becoming synonymous with fine French dinnerware in the American market.

Hand-painted Haviland porcelain from Limoges.

History

David Haviland moved to Limoges in 1842 after failing to persuade French makers to produce the whiteware patterns American customers wanted, deciding instead to manufacture and decorate porcelain himself. Haviland & Co. grew rapidly, combining local kaolin-based porcelain with industrial decoration techniques. Under his son Charles Edward Haviland, the firm became a leading exporter, producing state services for several United States presidents — including commissions associated with the Lincoln, Grant, Hayes, and Theodore Roosevelt administrations — and employing noted artists such as Félix Bracquemond in its Paris decoration atelier.

Legacy

The company's backstamps are a standard reference point for collectors of antique tableware, and thousands of patterns are catalogued. The Haviland name continues in porcelain production at Limoges, and the firm is recognised as part of the heritage of the Limoges porcelain industry alongside houses such as Bernardaud and Raynaud.
