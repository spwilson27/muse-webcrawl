Cleeve Hill

Cleeve Hill
Elevation: 330 m (1,083 ft)
Prominence: 242 m
Location: Gloucestershire, England
Range: Cotswolds
Designation: Highest point of the Cotswolds; SSSI

Cleeve Hill is the highest point in the Cotswolds hill range and in the county of Gloucestershire, England, at 330 metres (1,083 ft) above sea level. It lies on Cleeve Common, a limestone plateau north-east of Cheltenham, near the village of Cleeve Hill.

Geography

The summit, known as Cleeve Cloud, commands wide views westward over Cheltenham, the Vale of Evesham and the Malvern Hills, and on a clear day as far as Wales. The hill is part of the Cotswold escarpment, formed of Jurassic oolitic limestone. A trig point and a toposcope mark the summit, close to the ramparts of an Iron Age hill fort.

History and archaeology

Cleeve Common preserves evidence of human activity from the Neolithic period onward, including the Belas Knap long barrow nearby, Iron Age hill forts at Cleeve Cloud and Nottingham Hill, and a Roman temple site. In the medieval period the common was grazed and quarried for building stone.

Recreation

The common is crossed by the Cotswold Way national trail and hosts one of England's oldest golf courses, founded in 1891, reputedly the highest golf course in England. The area is a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its limestone grassland flora and butterflies, and is managed as common land with grazing sheep.
