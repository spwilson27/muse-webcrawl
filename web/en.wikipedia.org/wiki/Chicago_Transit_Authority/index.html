Chicago Transit Authority

The Chicago Transit Authority (CTA) is the operator of mass transit in the City of Chicago and some of its suburbs, and the second-largest transit system in the United States. An independent governmental agency created in 1945, it began operating in 1947, taking over the city's elevated railways, subway, streetcars (later buses) from private companies. The CTA operates the famous "L" rapid transit system and an extensive bus network.

Services

The CTA's rail system — the "L", short for "elevated" — comprises eight colour-named lines (Red, Blue, Brown, Green, Orange, Pink, Purple and Yellow) radiating from the elevated Loop downtown, with subways through the central area and extensions to both O'Hare and Midway airports. The bus system operates more than 120 routes. Together the CTA provides roughly a million rides on an average weekday in normal times.

History

The CTA was created by the Illinois legislature in 1945 and purchased the Chicago Rapid Transit Company and the Chicago Surface Lines in 1947 for about $75 million. Streetcars were converted to buses by 1958. Major milestones include the opening of the Milwaukee–Dearborn subway, the Dan Ryan and Kennedy extensions built in expressway medians, the Orange Line to Midway (1993), and the transition to the Ventra fare card system in the 2010s. The long-planned Red Line extension to 130th Street is among its largest current projects.
