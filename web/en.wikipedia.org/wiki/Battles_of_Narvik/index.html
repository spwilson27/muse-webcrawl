The Battles of Narvik were fought from 9 April to 8 June 1940 as a naval battle in the Ofotfjord and as a land battle in the mountains surrounding the town of Narvik in northern Norway, during the Norwegian campaign of the Second World War. Narvik was the ice-free port through which Swedish iron ore was shipped to Germany, making it a key objective of the German invasion of Norway.

Naval battles

On 9 April 1940 ten German destroyers landed mountain troops under General Eduard Dietl and seized Narvik. On 10 April a British destroyer flotilla under Captain Bernard Warburton-Lee attacked in a snowstorm, sinking two German destroyers and several merchant ships before Warburton-Lee was killed in the withdrawal. On 13 April a British force centred on the battleship HMS Warspite returned and destroyed the eight remaining German destroyers, whose crews joined Dietl's force ashore.

Land battle

Allied forces — British, French Foreign Legion, Polish mountain troops and Norwegian units — gradually pressed Dietl's outnumbered force, reinforced by shipwrecked sailors, back toward the Swedish border. On 28 May Allied troops captured Narvik, the first significant Allied land victory of the war. However, the German invasion of France forced the Allies to evacuate Norway; the troops were withdrawn by 8 June and Dietl reoccupied the town.

Aftermath

The destruction of ten destroyers cost the Kriegsmarine half its destroyer strength, a loss felt for the rest of the war. The Allied evacuation and the loss of the carrier HMS Glorious during the withdrawal marred the Narvik success.
