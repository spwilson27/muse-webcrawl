Giblets

Giblets (pronounced "jiblets") is a culinary term for the edible offal of a fowl, typically including the heart, gizzard, liver, and neck. The term derives from the Old French gibelet, originally referring to a game stew made with these parts. Giblets are commonly sold packaged inside whole poultry such as chickens and turkeys in many countries.

Composition

The parts usually included as giblets are:

  • Heart — the muscular heart of the bird
  • Gizzard — the thick-walled grinding stomach
  • Liver — the bird's liver
  • Neck — often sold in the package although strictly it is not offal

Kidneys, which occur naturally within the body cavity of some poultry, may also be included. In waterfowl such as geese and ducks, the feet are sometimes considered a delicacy separately.

Culinary use

Giblets are used to make stock and gravy — most famously the giblet gravy served with roast turkey at Thanksgiving and Christmas dinners. They are also minced and incorporated into stuffing or dressing, fried as a snack, or cooked in pies and stews. In many cuisines, giblets are prized in their own right: gizzards are simmered, grilled on skewers in Japanese yakitori, or confited in southwest France, where duck giblet salad (salade de gésiers) is a regional specialty. Chicken livers are made into pâté and chopped liver.

Food safety

Because giblets spoil quickly and are often packed in a paper or plastic bag inside the bird, cooks are cautioned to remove the package before roasting; modern packaging increasingly uses heat-resistant bags, but removal remains standard practice. Giblets should be cooked to the same safe internal temperature as the rest of the bird when eaten directly.
