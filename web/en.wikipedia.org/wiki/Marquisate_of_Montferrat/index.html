Marquisate of Montferrat

The Marquisate of Montferrat was a frontier march of the Kingdom of Italy in the Middle Ages and a state of the Holy Roman Empire, located in what is now Piedmont in northwestern Italy. It took its name from the hill country of Montferrat, between the Po and the Ligurian Apennines, with its chief towns at Casale Monferrato, Acqui Terme, and Alba.

History

The march was established in the 10th century and from 967 was ruled by the Aleramici dynasty, founded by Aleramo, invested by Emperor Otto I. The marquises played a notable part in the Crusades: William V of Montferrat was the father of William of Tyre's patrons and of Conrad of Montferrat, briefly King of Jerusalem, while Boniface I led the Fourth Crusade and founded the Kingdom of Thessalonica in 1204.

After the Aleramici line failed in 1305, the marquisate passed by marriage to a cadet branch of the Byzantine Palaiologos family, who ruled until 1533. It then came to the Gonzaga of Mantua, was raised to a duchy in 1574, and was gradually absorbed by the Duchy of Savoy during the 17th and 18th centuries.

Legacy

Montferrat today is known for its wines — Barbera, Asti Spumante, and Barolo country lies at its edges — and its castle-dotted hills are a UNESCO World Heritage landscape together with the Langhe and Roero.
