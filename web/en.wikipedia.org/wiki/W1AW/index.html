W1AW is the call sign of the Hiram Percy Maxim Memorial Station, the amateur radio station operated by the American Radio Relay League (ARRL) at its headquarters in Newington, Connecticut. It is one of the most famous call signs in amateur radio, known worldwide for its scheduled bulletin and code practice transmissions.

History

The call sign originally belonged to Hiram Percy Maxim, co-founder and first president of the ARRL, whose own station operated under W1AW. After Maxim's death in 1936, the League preserved his call sign for its headquarters station. The Maxim Memorial Station building on the ARRL campus in Newington was dedicated in 1938, and a recreation of the station, with both vintage and modern equipment, continues to operate from there. The station's distinctive pre-war transmitter, known as "Old Betsy", is preserved as part of the site.

Transmissions and activities

W1AW transmits bulletins of interest to radio amateurs — including propagation forecasts, satellite information, and emergency and legislative news — on a published schedule across the HF bands and on VHF, using CW (Morse code), digital modes and voice. Its slow-speed and fast-speed code practice sessions have helped generations of amateurs build Morse proficiency. Visiting licensed amateurs may operate the station as guest operators, and it is heavily active during contests and special events, as well as during emergencies when it supports traffic handling.
