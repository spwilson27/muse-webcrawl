Aranese (Occitan: aranés) is the variety of the Gascon dialect of the Occitan language spoken in the Val d'Aran, a small valley in the north-west corner of Catalonia, Spain, on the northern slopes of the Pyrenees. Because the valley drains northward into France via the Garonne river, its speech developed with the Gascon dialects across the border rather than with Catalan, although the valley has belonged politically to the Catalan–Aragonese sphere since the Middle Ages.

Status and use

Aranese was recognised in the Statute of Autonomy of Catalonia of 1979 as the valley's own language. The 2006 Statute made Occitan (under the name Aranese in the Val d'Aran) official throughout Catalonia, and a 2010 law of the Catalan Parliament confirmed its official status and preferential use in the valley. It is taught in schools in the Val d'Aran, used in local administration by the Conselh Generau d'Aran, and heard in local media, including broadcasts by Catalunya Ràdio and TV3's Aranese news service.

Surveys suggest roughly 4,700 people can speak Aranese; a majority of the valley's inhabitants understand it, while Spanish and Catalan are also widely used, especially in the tourist and service economies.

Features

Aranese shares the defining Gascon traits: initial Latin F- becoming h- (e.g. hilh, "son", from FILIU), loss of intervocalic -N-, and the "enunciative" particle system. Its standardised orthography, codified in the late 20th century and maintained by the Institut d'Estudis Aranesi (founded 2008 as the valley's language academy), follows the classical Occitan norm adapted to Gascon phonology. Notable local vocabulary includes hèsta (festival) and guah (ford, spring).
