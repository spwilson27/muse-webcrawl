Accordion

Accordion
ClassificationFree-reed aerophone
Invented1820s (patents by Friedrich Buschmann, 1822; Cyrill Demian, 1829)
TypesPiano accordion, button accordion (chromatic and diatonic)
Related instrumentsConcertina, bandoneon, harmonium, melodeon

An accordion is a box-shaped musical instrument of the bellows-driven free-reed aerophone family. The instrument is played by compressing or expanding the bellows while pressing buttons or keys, causing pallets to open and air to flow across reeds that vibrate to produce sound. The right hand typically plays the melody on a piano-style keyboard or buttons, while the left hand operates bass and chord buttons.

History

The accordion was developed in the early 19th century: Friedrich Buschmann built an early bellows instrument in Berlin around 1822, and Cyrill Demian patented the name "Accordion" in Vienna in 1829. Its portability and loud, self-accompanying sound made it popular across Europe and the Americas, and it became central to many folk traditions, including French musette, German and Alpine music, Cajun and zydeco, norteño and vallenato.

Construction

A typical piano accordion has 41 treble keys and 120 bass buttons arranged in the Stradella system. Reed blocks inside the casing hold steel reeds tuned in registers selected by switches; the bellows, traditionally of pleated cardboard and cloth corners, supply the air. Chromatic button accordions, common in Russia (the bayan) and France, use buttons for both hands.
