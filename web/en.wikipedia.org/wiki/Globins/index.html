Globin

The globins are a superfamily of heme-containing globular proteins involved in binding and transporting oxygen. They share a characteristic three-dimensional fold — the globin fold — of eight alpha helices surrounding a heme group that can bind molecular oxygen reversibly.

Members

Well-known globins include haemoglobin, the tetrameric oxygen carrier in vertebrate red blood cells; myoglobin, the monomeric oxygen store in muscle; neuroglobin and cytoglobin in vertebrate tissues; and leghemoglobin in the root nodules of leguminous plants, where it supplies oxygen to nitrogen-fixing bacteria. Plant non-symbiotic hemoglobins and truncated globins in bacteria and archaea extend the family across all kingdoms of life.

Evolution

Sequence and structural similarities indicate that all globins descend from a common ancestral protein, making the family a classic example of evolution by gene duplication and divergence.
