DJ mix

A DJ mix (also DJ mixset or mixtape in some contexts) is a sequence of musical tracks typically mixed together to appear as one continuous track. DJ mixes are usually performed live in clubs or recorded for broadcast, podcasts, and online platforms, and are a central format of electronic dance music culture.

Techniques

Mixing relies on matching the tempo (beatmatching) and often the key of successive tracks, and blending them with a DJ mixer's crossfader and equalizers so transitions are seamless. Techniques include long blends, cuts, effects, loops, and harmonic mixing. Traditionally performed with two turntables or CDJs, mixes are now also created with DJ software and controllers, which offer sync functions and waveforms.

History and formats

The continuous mix emerged in 1970s disco and was developed by club DJs such as Larry Levan and Frankie Knuckles, and by radio and studio mixers. Mixed compilation albums became commercially significant in the 1990s with series such as Ministry of Sound's The Annual and Global Underground, and radio shows like BBC Radio 1's Essential Mix. Today, mixes are distributed via streaming platforms, SoundCloud, Mixcloud, and YouTube, and recorded sets from festivals are a major way listeners discover DJs.

Legal issues

Because mixes incorporate many copyrighted recordings, their distribution has raised licensing questions; platforms have adopted content-recognition and licensing arrangements, while officially released mix albums license each track.
