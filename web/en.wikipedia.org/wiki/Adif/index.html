Adif (from Administrador de Infraestructuras Ferroviarias) is Spain's state-owned railway infrastructure manager, responsible for building, maintaining and administering most of the country's rail network, including track, signalling and stations, and for allocating capacity to train operators. It was created on 1 January 2005 under Spain's Railway Sector Law, which split the former state railway RENFE into Adif (infrastructure) and Renfe Operadora (train operations), in line with European Union requirements separating infrastructure management from services.

Network

Adif manages on the order of 15,000 km of lines in three gauges: the Iberian-gauge conventional network, the standard-gauge high-speed network — among the longest in the world, radiating from Madrid to Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, Málaga, Galicia, the Basque Country and elsewhere — and the metre-gauge network of northern Spain, taken over from FEVE in 2013. A sister entity, Adif Alta Velocidad (Adif High Speed), created in 2013, handles the high-speed lines and their financing. Adif owns and runs Spain's major stations, including Madrid Atocha and Chamartín, Barcelona Sants and Seville Santa Justa.

Operations and liberalisation

Adif charges operators access fees for using the network and coordinates timetables and traffic control. Since the liberalisation of Spanish long-distance passenger services in 2021, its high-speed corridors also host Renfe's low-cost Avlo services and competitors Ouigo España and Iryo. Adif has been involved in major projects including new high-speed lines, the standard-gauge Mediterranean Corridor, and station redevelopments, and it was the infrastructure manager of the line involved in the 2013 Santiago de Compostela derailment, after which safety procedures were reviewed.
