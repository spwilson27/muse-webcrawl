White elephant

A white elephant is a possession that is useless or troublesome, especially one that is expensive to maintain or difficult to dispose of. The term derives from the sacred white elephants kept by Southeast Asian monarchs.

Origin

In Thailand, Burma and Cambodia, genuinely white (albinistic) elephants were sacred: they belonged to the king, could not be put to work, and had to be maintained in luxury. According to a popular story, a king wishing to ruin a disfavoured courtier would present him with a white elephant — an honour that was also a financial catastrophe, since the animal could not be sold, worked or refused. Though the literal story may be apocryphal, the phrase entered English in the 19th century.

Modern usage

The term is applied to costly construction projects that fail to deliver value — grand stadiums, airports or dams built for prestige — and to any burdensome asset whose upkeep exceeds its usefulness. "White elephant" gift exchanges, popular at parties in North America, take the name from the idea of giving away amusingly useless presents.
