Securities Act of 1933

Securities Act of 1933
Enacted: May 27, 1933, signed by Franklin D. Roosevelt
Nicknames: “Truth in Securities” Act
Codified: 15 U.S.C. §§ 77a–77aa

The Securities Act of 1933, also known as the 1933 Act or the “truth in securities” law, was enacted during the Great Depression after the stock market crash of 1929. It was the first major federal legislation to regulate the offer and sale of securities.

Objectives

The act aims to require that investors receive financial and other significant information about securities offered for public sale, and to prohibit deceit, misrepresentations, and fraud in their sale.

Registration

Unless an exemption applies, securities offered to the public must be registered, with a prospectus disclosing the issuer's business, management, and audited financial statements. The Securities and Exchange Commission, created by the companion Securities Exchange Act of 1934, enforces the act.

Exemptions and liability

Exempt offerings include private placements under Regulation D, Regulation A, and crowdfunding. Section 11 imposes liability for material misstatements in registration statements.
