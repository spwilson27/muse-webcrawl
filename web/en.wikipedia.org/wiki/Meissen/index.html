Meissen

Meissen (German: Meißen) is a town of approximately 28,000 inhabitants in the Free State of Saxony, Germany, on the banks of the River Elbe. It is renowned worldwide as the home of Meissen porcelain, the first European hard-paste porcelain, produced there since 1710. The town's skyline is dominated by the Albrechtsburg Castle and Meissen Cathedral, perched on a hill above the old town.

History

Meissen grew out of a Slavic settlement and the fortress founded around 929 by King Henry I (Henry the Fowler) as a German stronghold against the Slavs; it became the seat of the Margraves of Meissen, ancestors of the Wettin dynasty that later ruled Saxony. The town received its charter in the 12th century and prospered as a trading and manufacturing centre, known for its cloth and, later, its wines from the Elbe valley.

Porcelain

In 1708–1710, under Augustus the Strong, Elector of Saxony, the alchemist Johann Friedrich Böttger and the scientist Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus discovered the secret of hard-paste porcelain manufacturing. Production began in 1710 at the Albrechtsburg and moved in 1863 to a purpose-built factory in the Triebisch valley. The crossed-swords trademark of Meissen porcelain is one of the oldest trademarks still in use.

Sights

  • Albrechtsburg Castle, a late-Gothic palace regarded as the oldest residential castle in Germany
  • Meissen Cathedral (Meißner Dom), begun around 1260
  • The porcelain manufactory's visitor centre and museum
  • The historic market square and Frauenkirche with its porcelain carillon
