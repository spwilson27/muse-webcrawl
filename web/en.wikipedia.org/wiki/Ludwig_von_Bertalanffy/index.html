Karl Ludwig von Bertalanffy (19 September 1901 – 12 June 1972) was an Austrian biologist known as one of the founders of general systems theory (GST). His work sought general principles applying to systems in biology, psychology, the social sciences and other fields, based on the idea that organisms are open systems exchanging matter and energy with their environment. He is also known for the von Bertalanffy growth function (published in 1934), a model of organismal growth still widely used in fisheries science and oncology.

Biography

Bertalanffy was born and raised in the small village of Atzgersdorf (now part of Vienna) in Austria-Hungary. He studied history and philosophy, then biology, at the universities of Innsbruck and Vienna, receiving his doctorate in 1926. He became a professor at the University of Vienna and published his first major work on theoretical biology in the 1930s. After the Second World War he left Austria, working at the University of London, the University of Montreal, the University of Ottawa and the University of Alberta, before finishing his career at the State University of New York at Buffalo, where he died in 1972.

General system theory

Bertalanffy proposed that the classical laws of thermodynamics, formulated for closed systems, do not apply to living organisms, which are open systems maintaining themselves in a steady state (Fliessgleichgewicht) far from equilibrium. His General System Theory (1968) summarised decades of work arguing for a unified science of "wholeness", emphasising concepts such as equifinality, feedback, hierarchy and isomorphism of laws across disciplines. He was a co-founder in 1954 of the Society for General Systems Research, together with Kenneth Boulding, Ralph Gerard and Anatol Rapoport.

Legacy

His ideas influenced cybernetics, ecology, family therapy, management science and systems engineering. The von Bertalanffy growth equation, L(t) = L∞ (1 − e^(−K(t−t0))), remains a standard tool for modelling the growth of fish and other organisms.
