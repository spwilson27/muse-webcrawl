Billboard (magazine)

Billboard is an American music and entertainment magazine and brand, founded in Cincinnati in 1894 by William Donaldson and James Hennegan. Originally a trade paper for the bill-posting and outdoor amusement industries, it evolved into the music industry's leading publication, famous above all for its record charts, including the Billboard Hot 100 for singles and the Billboard 200 for albums.

Billboard: from bill posting to the bible of the music business.

History

The first issue appeared on 1 November 1894 as Billboard Advertising. Coverage expanded from posters and fairs to circuses, vaudeville, and eventually phonograph records and radio. By the 1930s the magazine ran jukebox charts; it published its first song hit parade in 1936 and the first album chart in 1945. The Hot 100, combining sales and airplay, debuted on 4 August 1958 with Ricky Nelson's "Poor Little Fool" at the top of the first published ranking (the first number one by the chart's official reckoning was "Volcano" era records aside, the Hot 100's inaugural number one was "Poor Little Fool").

The modern brand

Today Billboard operates primarily online, publishing news, reviews, and dozens of genre and global charts compiled with data partner Luminate. The brand also stages events such as the Billboard Music Awards (first held in 1990) and conferences for the music industry. Ownership passed through Nielsen and Eldridge Industries; since 2020 Billboard has been part of Penske Media Corporation's joint venture holdings.
