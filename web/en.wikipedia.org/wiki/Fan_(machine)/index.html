Fan (machine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fan is a powered machine used to create a flow of air. A fan consists of a rotating arrangement of vanes or blades, generally made of wood, plastic, or metal, which act on the air. The rotating assembly of blades and hub is known as an impeller, rotor, or runner. Usually, it is contained within some form of housing or case, both for safety and to direct the airflow.

Most fans are powered by electric motors, but other sources of power may be used, including hydraulic motors, hand cranks, and internal combustion engines. Fans cool people not by lowering air temperature, but by increasing evaporation of sweat and speeding convective heat loss; in very hot, humid conditions this benefit diminishes, and fan use above certain temperatures is debated in public-health guidance.

History

Hand fans date back thousands of years in Egypt, China, and Japan. The punkah, a swinging ceiling panel, was used in India from around 500 BCE and was later operated by servants in colonial households. Rotary mechanical fans appeared in the 1830s; the first commercially successful electric fans were developed in the 1880s, notably by Schuyler Wheeler and Philip Diehl, who adapted a sewing-machine motor to drive a fan.

Types

  • Axial-flow fans — air moves parallel to the shaft: desk, pedestal, ceiling, and window fans; propeller aircraft.
  • Centrifugal fans (blowers) — air is flung outward by a squirrel-cage impeller; used in HVAC systems and leaf blowers.
  • Cross-flow fans — long cylindrical rotors used in air conditioners and air curtains.
  • Bladeless fans — use an air-multiplier ring to draw in and amplify a stream of air without exposed blades.

Applications

Beyond personal cooling, fans provide ventilation, drive air conditioning and heating systems, cool electronic equipment (computer fans), supply combustion air, convey materials in industry, and power wind tunnels. Reversing a fan's motor can turn it into an exhaust device.
