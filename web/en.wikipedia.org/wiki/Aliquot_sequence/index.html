Aliquot sequence

In mathematics, an aliquot sequence is a sequence of positive integers in which each term is the sum of the proper divisors of the previous term. If the sequence reaches the number 1, it terminates, since the sum of the proper divisors of 1 is 0. Aliquot sequences that never terminate or enter a cycle are the subject of the unresolved Catalan–Dickson conjecture.

Outcomes

An aliquot sequence can end in one of several ways:

  • Termination at a prime (whose aliquot sum is 1), then 1 and 0.
  • A perfect number, whose aliquot sum equals itself — a cycle of length 1, e.g. 6, 28, 496.
  • An amicable pair, a cycle of length 2, e.g. 220 and 284.
  • A sociable cycle of length 3 or more.
  • Open-ended growth: sequences such as the one starting at 276, whose ultimate fate remains unknown despite computation to very large terms.

The sequence from 276

The aliquot sequence starting with 276 is the smallest whose behaviour is unknown. It has been computed for thousands of terms, growing to numbers with well over 200 digits before partially descending again, and is tracked by volunteer computing projects such as Aliquot Sequences and yoyo@home. Other famous open starting values include 552, 564, 660 and 966 — the so-called "Lehmer five" and related families.
