A scissor lift — the most common type of aerial work platform

An aerial work platform (AWP), also known as an elevating work platform or mobile elevating work platform (MEWP), is a device that lifts workers, tools, and light materials to a working position in the air and can be moved around a site. An aerial lift in the broad sense; the platform form is distinguished from vehicle-mounted bucket trucks mainly by being self-propelled or towable rather than fixed to a truck chassis.

Types

The scissor lift carries a rectangular deck on criss-crossed pantograph arms and moves only vertically; slab models with electric drive are standard in warehouses and indoor construction, while rough-terrain diesel models with outriggers work outdoors. Boom lifts — telescopic and articulating — trade deck size for height and outreach, placing one or two workers in a basket at up to about 56 metres. Mast lifts and push-around personnel lifts serve low-level access, and trailer-mounted lifts can be towed behind a vehicle and set up on jacks.

Operation and safety

Platforms are controlled from the basket (with ground override), and modern machines incorporate load sensing, tilt alarms, pothole protection, and secondary guarding against crushing. Operator training and certification regimes — such as the IPAF PAL Card — are widely required by rental companies and safety regulators. The rental market, dominated by firms such as United Rentals and Sunbelt Rentals, supplies most AWPs used in North America and Europe.

History

Mechanised platforms appeared in the mid-twentieth century as alternatives to scaffolding and ladders; manufacturers JLG (founded 1969) and Genie (founded 1966) popularised the scissor and boom forms and lent their names generically to the machines in parts of the industry.
