New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival

GenreJazz, blues, gospel, R&B, rock, heritage music
LocationFair Grounds Race Course, New Orleans, Louisiana, United States
Founded1970
AttendanceRoughly 400,000–475,000 over seven days
OrganiserNew Orleans Jazz & Heritage Foundation

The New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival, commonly called Jazz Fest, is an annual celebration of the music and culture of New Orleans and Louisiana held at the Fair Grounds Race Course in New Orleans. Founded in 1970, the festival takes place over seven days across two weekends in late April and early May, presenting hundreds of acts on multiple stages along with Louisiana food, crafts and cultural demonstrations.

History

The festival was established in 1970 by the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Foundation, with George Wein, the founder of the Newport Jazz Festival, as its producer. The first event in Congo Square and the Municipal Auditorium drew only a few hundred attendees but featured legends including Mahalia Jackson, Duke Ellington and Fats Domino; a famous moment saw Jackson hand a microphone to a parading Mardi Gras Indian procession.

In 1972 the festival moved to the Fair Grounds, the third-oldest racetrack in the United States, where it has grown into one of the country's largest music festivals.

Programming

Despite its name, Jazz Fest programming extends far beyond jazz to blues, gospel, rhythm and blues, Cajun and zydeco, funk, rock and international headliners, reflecting the breadth of Louisiana musical heritage. Stages include the main Acura and Gentilly stages and tents dedicated to gospel, blues, traditional jazz and heritage presentations.

The festival is also celebrated for its food: dozens of vendors serve regional dishes such as crawfish bread, cochon de lait po'boys and alligator pie, many of them unavailable elsewhere.

Cultural role

Proceeds support the non-profit New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Foundation's year-round programmes, including community radio station WWOZ, music education and cultural documentation. The festival is considered a cornerstone event of New Orleans's cultural calendar alongside Mardi Gras.
