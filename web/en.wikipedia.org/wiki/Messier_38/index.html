Messier 38 (M38), also known as NGC 1912 or the Starfish Cluster, is an open star cluster in the constellation Auriga. It was discovered by Giovanni Battista Hodierna before 1654 and independently found by Guillaume Le Gentil in 1749; Charles Messier added it to his catalogue in 1764.

Characteristics

M38 lies roughly 3,500 to 4,200 light-years from Earth and spans some 20 light-years. Its brightest stars form an oblique cross or the Greek letter pi, giving rise to its starfish nickname. The cluster contains several hundred stars, including a yellow giant of about magnitude 7.9, and is estimated to be a few hundred million years old.

Observation

M38 is one of a trio of bright Messier open clusters in Auriga, together with M36 and M37, which sweep past the meridian on winter evenings in the Northern Hemisphere. At apparent magnitude 7.4 it is a binocular object and resolves into individual stars in a small telescope. The fainter, more compact cluster NGC 1907 sits about half a degree to its south and can be seen in the same low-power field.
