Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit

The Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit, also known as the Stealth Bomber, is an American heavy strategic bomber featuring low-observable stealth technology designed to penetrate dense anti-aircraft defences. A subsonic flying wing with a crew of two, the B-2 is capable of deploying both conventional and thermonuclear weapons, including up to eighty 500-pound class bombs or sixteen B83 nuclear bombs.

Development

Developed in secrecy during the 1980s under the Advanced Technology Bomber programme, the B-2 was first publicly displayed in November 1988 and first flew on 17 July 1989. Spiralling costs — at roughly $2.1 billion per aircraft including development, it is the most expensive aircraft ever built — led production to be cut from a planned 132 to just 21 aircraft.

Design

The tailless flying-wing configuration, radar-absorbent materials and carefully contoured surfaces give the B-2 a very small radar cross-section, allowing it to strike heavily defended targets at altitudes up to 50,000 feet. Four General Electric F118 turbofans are buried within the wing. Its two weapons bays can carry approximately 23,000 kg (50,000 lb) of ordnance.

Operational history

The B-2 entered service in 1997 and is operated exclusively from Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri, with forward deployments to Guam and Diego Garcia. It flew its first combat missions during the Kosovo War in 1999 and has since seen combat in Afghanistan, Iraq and Libya, flying intercontinental round-trip missions of up to 44 hours. One B-2 was lost in a crash on Guam in 2008. The type is planned to be replaced by the B-21 Raider in the 2030s.
