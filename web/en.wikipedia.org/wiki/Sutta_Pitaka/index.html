Sutta Pitaka

The Sutta Piṭaka (Pali for "Basket of Discourses") is the second of the three divisions of the Pali Canon of Theravada Buddhism, the other two being the Vinaya Piṭaka (rules for monastics) and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka (systematic philosophy). It contains more than 10,000 suttas — discourses attributed to the Buddha and his close disciples.

Contents

The Sutta Pitaka is organised into five collections called Nikāyas. The Dīgha Nikāya contains 34 long discourses; the Majjhima Nikāya 152 middle-length discourses; the Saṃyutta Nikāya several thousand short discourses grouped by topic; the Aṅguttara Nikāya thousands of discourses arranged numerically; and the Khuddaka Nikāya a miscellaneous collection of fifteen books including the Dhammapada, the Udāna, the Sutta Nipāta, and the Jātaka tales of the Buddha's past lives.

Origins

According to tradition, the discourses were recited at the First Buddhist Council shortly after the Buddha's death and preserved orally for centuries before being committed to writing in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE. Parallel collections exist in Chinese translation as the Āgamas, preserved by other early Buddhist schools.
