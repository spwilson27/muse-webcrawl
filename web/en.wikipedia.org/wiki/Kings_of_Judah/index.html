The kings of Judah were the monarchs who ruled the Kingdom of Judah, the southern of the two Israelite kingdoms that, according to the Hebrew Bible, emerged after the division of the united monarchy of Solomon around 931 BC. Twenty kings and one reigning queen (Athaliah) ruled from Jerusalem until the kingdom's destruction by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon in 587/586 BC.

The royal line

With the exception of Athaliah, the usurping queen who seized the throne for six years, all the kings of Judah belonged to the Davidic dynasty, claiming descent from David. The sequence begins with Rehoboam, son of Solomon, and includes Asa, Jehoshaphat, Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amon and Josiah — the last remembered in the biblical account for his religious reforms following the discovery of a "book of the law" in the Temple. After Josiah's death at Megiddo in 609 BC, the last four kings — Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin and Zedekiah — ruled as Egyptian and then Babylonian vassals.

Sources and chronology

The principal sources are the biblical Books of Kings and Books of Chronicles, supplemented by Assyrian and Babylonian royal inscriptions that mention kings such as Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh and Jehoiachin. Scholars including William F. Albright and Edwin R. Thiele have proposed influential chronologies for the reigns, which generally place the divided monarchy between the late 10th and early 6th centuries BC. The Babylonian captivity that followed Zedekiah's defeat ended the Davidic monarchy, though the line's descendants remained significant in Jewish tradition.
