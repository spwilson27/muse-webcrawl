Renewable energy in Australia

Renewable energy in Australia is based mainly on biomass, solar, wind, and hydroelectric generation. It is a growing resource, and the country has some of the best renewable energy resources in the world, including abundant sunshine and strong coastal winds. Australia's renewable sector has expanded rapidly since the 2010s, driven by the national Renewable Energy Target, state government policies, falling technology costs, and one of the world's highest rates of rooftop solar installation.

Sources

Hydroelectricity, historically dominated by the Snowy Mountains Scheme and Tasmania's hydro system, was long Australia's largest renewable source. Wind power has grown strongly in South Australia, Victoria, and New South Wales, while large-scale and rooftop solar have become major contributors. South Australia has at times met all of its electricity demand from wind and solar alone, supported by grid-scale batteries such as the Hornsdale Power Reserve.

Policy

The Renewable Energy Target (RET) scheme, begun in 2001 and expanded in 2010, required electricity retailers to source a proportion of power from renewables, with a large-scale target of 33,000 GWh by 2020 that was met ahead of schedule. The Australian Renewable Energy Agency (ARENA) and the Clean Energy Finance Corporation (CEFC) support investment in new technologies, including green hydrogen and energy storage.
