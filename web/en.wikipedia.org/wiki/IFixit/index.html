iFixit

iFixit is an American e-commerce and how-to website that sells repair parts and publishes free wiki-like online repair guides for consumer electronics and gadgets. Founded in 2003 in San Luis Obispo, California, by Kyle Wiens and Luke Soules while they were students at California Polytechnic State University, the company is a prominent advocate of the right to repair movement.

Business and activities

iFixit is best known for its product teardowns—step-by-step photographic disassemblies of new devices such as iPhones and game consoles—each concluding with a "repairability score" from 1 to 10 that rates how easy the device is to service. The site hosts tens of thousands of community-contributed repair manuals covering electronics, appliances, vehicles, and more, translated into multiple languages. It also sells precision toolkits and replacement parts.

Advocacy

The company campaigns for right-to-repair legislation, co-founded the Repair Association, and has criticised manufacturer practices such as parts pairing and glued-in batteries. Its repair guides were used in the European Fixing the Future initiatives, and iFixit has partnered with manufacturers including Google, Samsung, and Valve to supply genuine parts and documentation.
