Gun control in the United States

Gun control in the United States refers to the set of laws and policies that regulate the manufacture, sale, transfer, possession, modification, and use of firearms by civilians. It is one of the most enduringly contentious issues in American politics, shaped by the Second Amendment to the United States Constitution, which protects an individual right to keep and bear arms as interpreted by the Supreme Court in District of Columbia v. Heller (2008).

Federal law

Major federal statutes include the National Firearms Act (1934), the Gun Control Act (1968), the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act (1993), which established background checks on licensed dealer sales, and the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (2022). Federal law prohibits certain categories of people — including felons and people convicted of domestic violence — from possessing firearms.

State law

Regulation varies enormously by state: some states require permits to purchase, universal background checks, and licences for carrying, while others allow permitless ("constitutional") carry. This patchwork is a central feature of the American debate.

Debate

Supporters of stricter regulation argue it reduces gun deaths, which number over 40,000 annually in the United States, including a large share of suicides and homicides. Opponents argue that gun ownership is a constitutional right, deters crime, and that regulation burdens lawful owners without stopping criminals. Public opinion polling shows support for specific measures such as universal background checks alongside deep partisan division.
