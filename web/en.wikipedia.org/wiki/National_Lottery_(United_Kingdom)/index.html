National Lottery (United Kingdom)

The National Lottery is the state-franchised national lottery in the United Kingdom. It was established in 1994 under the government of Prime Minister John Major. The lottery is regulated by the Gambling Commission and is operated under licence by a private operator; proceeds are divided between prize winners, the government (through lottery duty), retailers, and "good causes", including arts, sport, heritage, health, education, and the environment, distributed through bodies such as the National Lottery Heritage Fund and Sport England.

History

The National Lottery Act 1993 provided the statutory basis for the lottery. The first draw took place on 19 November 1994, watched by millions on BBC television hosted by Noel Edmonds. The operator Camelot Group held the licence for the first three licence periods. In 2022, the Gambling Commission awarded the fourth licence to Allwyn Entertainment, which took over operation in February 2024.

Games

The flagship game is Lotto, in which players choose six numbers from 1 to 59. Other games include EuroMillions (run jointly with lotteries in other European countries), Thunderball, Set For Life, and scratchcards sold in shops. Players must be 18 or over, a threshold raised from 16 in 2021.

Good causes

Since 1994 the National Lottery has raised tens of billions of pounds for good causes, funding thousands of projects from village halls and sports facilities to major venues used for the London 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Roughly a quarter of ticket revenue is allocated to good causes.
