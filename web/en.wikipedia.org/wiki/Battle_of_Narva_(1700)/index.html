Battle of Narva (1700)

The Battle of Narva (Russian: Битва при Нарве; Swedish: Slaget vid Narva) was an early battle in the Great Northern War, fought on 30 November 1700 (Swedish calendar) near the city of Narva in present-day Estonia. A relief force of about 8,000–10,000 Swedes under King Charles XII decisively defeated a much larger Russian army that was besieging the Swedish garrison of Narva.

Background

Russia, allied with Denmark and Saxony-Poland, invaded Swedish Ingria and Estonia in 1700 and laid siege to Narva. Charles XII, having knocked Denmark out of the war, landed at Pernau and marched to relieve the fortress through autumn mud and rain.

The battle

Attacking in a driving snowstorm that blinded the Russian defenders, the outnumbered Swedes broke through the fortified siege lines in two places. The Russian army disintegrated: many troops drowned when a bridge over the Narva River collapsed, and thousands surrendered, though the elite Preobrazhensky and Semyonovsky guards held out briefly on the flank.

Aftermath

Narva was one of the most spectacular victories of the age and established Charles XII's military reputation across Europe. Peter the Great, however, used the respite to modernise his army; Russia returned to capture Narva in 1704, and the war turned decisively at Poltava in 1709.
