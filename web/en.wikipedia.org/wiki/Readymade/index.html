Readymade

In modern art, a readymade (or found object) is an ordinary manufactured object that the artist selected and modified, sometimes minimally, presenting it as art. The term was coined by Marcel Duchamp in 1915–16 to describe his works of this kind.

Readymade
Artist: Marcel Duchamp
Key works: Bicycle Wheel (1913), Bottle Rack (1914), Fountain (1917)
Movement: Dada, conceptual art precursor

Duchamp's readymades

Duchamp's first readymades — a bicycle wheel mounted on a stool, a bottle rack — preceded the term. Fountain (1917), a porcelain urinal signed "R. Mutt" and submitted to the Society of Independent Artists exhibition, was rejected/hidden and became the most influential artwork of the 20th century in many polls, challenging definitions of art and authorship.

Categories

Duchamp distinguished pure readymades, assisted readymades, and "reciprocal" readymades. Replicas were authorised in the 1960s (Galleria Schwarz editions) since most originals were lost.

Influence

Readymades paved the way for conceptual art, Pop, assemblage and appropriation, shifting emphasis from craft to idea and context.
