Positive reinforcement

In operant conditioning, positive reinforcement is the process of presenting a rewarding stimulus after a behaviour, thereby increasing the likelihood that the behaviour will occur again in the future. The concept was developed within behaviourism, particularly in the work of B. F. Skinner.

Definition and examples

A reinforcer is positive when its presentation strengthens the behaviour: praise following good work, a food pellet delivered to a rat after a lever press in a Skinner box, or a salary bonus for meeting a target. It is contrasted with negative reinforcement, in which behaviour is strengthened by removing an aversive stimulus, and with punishment, which decreases behaviour.

Schedules of reinforcement

Reinforcement may be continuous or intermittent. Intermittent schedules — fixed or variable, ratio or interval — produce characteristic patterns of responding, and variable-ratio schedules produce particularly high, steady response rates resistant to extinction.
