Richard Bartle

Richard Allan Bartle
Born: 10 January 1960, Ripon, England
Occupation: Writer, game researcher
Known for: Co-creating MUD; Bartle taxonomy of player types

Richard Allan Bartle (born 10 January 1960) is a British writer, professor and game researcher, best known as the co-creator, with Roy Trubshaw, of MUD (Multi-User Dungeon), the first multi-user dungeon and a forerunner of modern massively multiplayer online games, and for the Bartle taxonomy of player types.

MUD

In 1978, while a student at the University of Essex, Bartle co-wrote MUD with Roy Trubshaw on a DEC PDP-10. The text-based virtual world allowed multiple players to explore, fight and interact together over a network, laying the conceptual foundations for later MUDs, MMORPGs and online worlds generally. Bartle later worked on MUD2 and commercial online games.

Bartle taxonomy

In his 1996 paper "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs," Bartle proposed that virtual-world players fall into four types according to their interests: Achievers (diamonds), Explorers (spades), Socialisers (hearts) and Killers (clubs). The taxonomy became a standard tool in game design and was later expanded into an eight-type model in his book Designing Virtual Worlds (2003), which is regarded as a foundational text of the field.

Career

Bartle has taught game design at the University of Essex, consulted for numerous game companies, and written widely on the ethics and design of virtual worlds. In 2010 he received the Game Developers Choice Award for "The Pioneer Award," recognising his role in creating online worlds.
