Mozambican Civil War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A destroyed bridge on the road north from Maputo, emblem of the war's devastation]
A destroyed bridge on the road north from Maputo, emblem of the war's devastation

The Mozambican Civil War (1977–1992) was a conflict between Mozambique's ruling FRELIMO government and the rebel movement RENAMO, created and sustained by Rhodesian and later South African security services to destabilise the frontline state. One of Africa's most destructive wars, it killed perhaps a million people, displaced millions more, and destroyed much of the country's infrastructure before the Rome General Peace Accords of October 1992 and UN-supervised elections in 1994.

Origins

Independent in 1975 under Samora Machel's FRELIMO, Mozambique sheltered Zimbabwean liberation forces and enforced sanctions on Rhodesia; Salisbury's intelligence services assembled RENAMO from ex-Portuguese units and dissidents to retaliate. After Zimbabwe's independence (1980), apartheid South Africa — pursuing its own destabilisation doctrine — took over RENAMO's sponsorship, supplying and directing a war of extraordinary brutality against civilians, schools, clinics and transport.

Course and cost

RENAMO sabotaged the railways to the sea, massacred villagers (Homoine, 1987, was the worst single atrocity), and abducted thousands of child soldiers; FRELIMO's forced villagisation and its own abuses compounded the misery, and drought in 1983–84 turned war into famine. The 1984 Nkomati Accord with South Africa failed to stop support. Machel's death in a suspicious 1986 plane crash brought Joaquim Chissano, who liberalised the economy and constitution and negotiated the end.

Settlement

Talks in Rome, mediated by the lay Catholic Community of Sant'Egidio with Italian government support, produced the 1992 accords; the UN operation ONUMOZ demobilised both armies and ran the 1994 elections, which FRELIMO won and RENAMO accepted — a rare unambiguous success for 1990s peacekeeping, threatened only by RENAMO's partial remobilisation in 2013–19.
