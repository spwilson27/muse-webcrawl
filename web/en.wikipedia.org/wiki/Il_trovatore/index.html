Il trovatore

Il trovatore
Composer: Giuseppe Verdi
Librettist: Salvadore Cammarano (completed by Leone Emanuele Bardare)
Premiere: 19 January 1853, Teatro Apollo, Rome

Il trovatore ("The Troubadour") is an opera in four acts by Giuseppe Verdi to an Italian libretto largely written by Salvadore Cammarano, based on the Spanish play El trovador (1836) by Antonio García Gutiérrez. It premiered in Rome in 1853 and quickly became one of the most popular operas of the nineteenth century.

Plot

In fifteenth-century Spain, the troubadour Manrico and the Count di Luna are rivals in love and war, unaware that they are brothers. The gypsy Azucena, seeking revenge for her mother's execution, has raised Manrico as her own. Leonora, loved by both men, takes poison rather than marry the Count; Manrico is executed, and Azucena reveals too late that the Count has killed his own brother.

Music

Famous numbers include the "Anvil Chorus", Leonora's "Tacea la notte placida", Manrico's "Di quella pira" and Azucena's "Stride la vampa". The opera is the middle work of Verdi's popular trilogy alongside Rigoletto and La traviata.
