Sadler's Wells Theatre is a major performing arts venue in Islington, London, and one of the world's leading dance theatres. The present building, opened in 1998, is the sixth theatre on a site where entertainment has been presented since 1683, when Richard Sadler opened a "musick house" beside newly discovered chalybeate wells whose waters were said to have medicinal properties.

History

Through the 18th and 19th centuries Sadler's Wells presented pantomime, melodrama, burletta and spectacle; under manager Samuel Phelps in the mid-19th century it mounted notable Shakespeare productions, and the clown Joseph Grimaldi made it famous for pantomime. In the 20th century, Lilian Baylis revived the theatre in 1931 as a northern counterpart to the Old Vic, and it became the cradle of British ballet: Ninette de Valois's Vic-Wells Ballet, the ancestor of The Royal Ballet, was based there, and the Sadler's Wells Opera company later became English National Opera.

Today

Since the 1998 reopening, led artistically for many years by Alistair Spalding, Sadler's Wells has positioned itself as a producing house for dance in all forms — contemporary dance, flamenco, hip-hop theatre (including the annual Breakin' Convention festival), tango and ballet — with associate artists and companies such as Akram Khan, Hofesh Shechter and BalletBoyz. It also operates the smaller Lilian Baylis Studio and the Peacock Theatre in the West End, and opened a further venue, Sadler's Wells East, in the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park in Stratford.
