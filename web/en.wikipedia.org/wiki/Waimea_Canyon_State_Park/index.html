Waimea Canyon State Park

Waimea Canyon State Park
Location: Kauaʻi, Hawaii, US
Area: ~750 ha
Canyon length: ~16 km
Depth: up to ~900 m

Waimea Canyon State Park is a state park on the western side of the island of Kauaʻi in Hawaii, encompassing Waimea Canyon, often called the "Grand Canyon of the Pacific" — a large canyon about 16 km long and up to 900 m deep.

Geology

The canyon was carved by the Waimea River and erosion from the slopes of Mount Waiʻaleʻale, one of the wettest places on Earth. Its colourful red, green and brown strata come from weathered volcanic lava flows of the ancient Kauaʻi shield volcano, whose partial collapse formed the canyon's depression.

Recreation

The park offers lookouts along Waimea Canyon Drive, views of Waipoʻo Falls, and hiking trails into the canyon and neighbouring Kōkeʻe State Park. Writers have long praised it — the "Grand Canyon of the Pacific" nickname is popularly attributed to Mark Twain, though he never visited Kauaʻi.
