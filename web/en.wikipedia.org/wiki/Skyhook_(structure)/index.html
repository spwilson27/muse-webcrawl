Skyhook (structure)

Skyhook
TypeProposed momentum-exchange tether structure
PurposeReduce cost of placing payloads into orbit
Related conceptsSpace elevator, space tether, rotovator

A skyhook is a proposed structure — a type of momentum exchange tether — intended to reduce the cost of placing payloads into low Earth orbit. A long, strong cable extends from a station in orbit toward (but not reaching) the upper atmosphere; payloads are attached to the lower end and flung into higher orbits, exchanging momentum with the station. Variants include the rotating skyhook or rotovator, whose cable rotates about its centre of mass so that its tip dips into the atmosphere and can pick up hypersonic payloads.

Concepts

The non-rotating skyhook, sometimes called a space elevator for practical purposes, would hang from geostationary orbit. The rotating design, proposed in the 1960s–70s by engineers including Yuri Artsutanov and Hans Moravec, uses a tether perhaps hundreds of kilometres long; a climbing aircraft or suborbital vehicle rendezvouses with the descending tip, and the rotation lifts the payload into orbit while a returning payload can replenish the system's momentum.

Challenges

No skyhook has been built. Practical obstacles include the need for materials with very high strength-to-weight ratios, tether survival against debris and micrometeoroids, and the precision guidance required for rendezvous with a tether tip moving at kilometres per second.
