Michael Pollan

Michael Kevin Pollan (born February 6, 1955) is an American journalist, author, and professor who writes about food, agriculture, and more recently psychedelic drugs. He is best known for his books exploring the socio-cultural impacts of the industrial food chain, including The Omnivore's Dilemma (2006), and is a leading voice of the food movement.

Career

Pollan was born on Long Island, New York, in 1955, the son of author and financial consultant Stephen Pollan and columnist Corky Pollan; his sister is actress Tracy Pollan. He studied at Bennington College and received a master's degree in English from Columbia University. He has been a contributing writer to The New York Times Magazine and taught science and environmental journalism at the University of California, Berkeley, Graduate School of Journalism; he later joined Harvard University as a professor of the practice of non-fiction.

Works

  • Second Nature: A Gardener's Education (1991)
  • A Place of My Own (1997)
  • The Botany of Desire: A Plant's-Eye View of the World (2001) — tracing four plants (apple, tulip, marijuana, potato) and their co-evolution with human desires.
  • The Omnivore's Dilemma: A Natural History of Four Meals (2006) — tracing four food chains from soil to plate; named one of the ten best books of the year by The New York Times.
  • In Defense of Food: An Eater's Manifesto (2008) — opening with his famous summary: "Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants."
  • Food Rules: An Eater's Manual (2009)
  • Cooked: A Natural History of Transformation (2013)
  • How to Change Your Mind (2018) — on the history and renaissance of research into psychedelic drugs such as LSD and psilocybin.
  • This Is Your Mind on Plants (2021)

His work has been adapted for television and streaming, including the Netflix series Cooked (2016) and How to Change Your Mind (2022).
