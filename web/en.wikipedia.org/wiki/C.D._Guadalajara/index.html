C.D. Guadalajara

Full name: Club Deportivo Guadalajara S.A. de C.V.
Nickname: Chivas, Rebaño Sagrado
Founded: 8 May 1906
Ground: Estadio Akron, Zapopan
League: Liga MX

Club Deportivo Guadalajara, commonly known as Chivas, is a Mexican professional football club based in Guadalajara, Jalisco. Founded in 1906, it is one of the oldest and most popular teams in Mexico, and one of only two clubs — along with Club América — never to have been relegated from the top flight.

History

The club was founded by Belgian merchant Edgar Everaert and originally named Club Unión. It adopted the name Guadalajara in 1908. The club's most successful era was the Campeonísimo period of the late 1950s and 1960s, when it won seven league titles in nine seasons. Chivas won its twelfth league championship in the Clausura 2017 tournament under coach Matías Almeyda, and won the CONCACAF Champions League in 2018.

Identity

Chivas is famous for its policy of fielding only Mexican players, a tradition maintained since the early twentieth century, which has made it a symbol of Mexican footballing identity. Its red and white vertical stripes and blue shorts are among the most recognizable kits in Mexican sport. The club's fiercest rivalry is the Súper Clásico against Club América, and it also contests the Clásico Tapatío against Atlas, its Guadalajara neighbours.

Honours

Guadalajara has won 12 Liga MX titles, 4 Copa MX titles, and 2 CONCACAF Champions Cup/Champions League titles, among other trophies.

Stadium

Since 2010 the club has played at the Estadio Akron (formerly Estadio Omnilife and Estadio Chivas) in Zapopan, a stadium with a capacity of around 48,000 that was also a venue shortlisted for the 2026 FIFA World Cup.
