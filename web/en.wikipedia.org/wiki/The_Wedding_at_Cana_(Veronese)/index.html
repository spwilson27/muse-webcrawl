The Wedding at Cana (Veronese)

The Wedding at Cana

Artist: Paolo Veronese

Year: 1562–63

Medium: Oil on canvas

Dimensions: 677 cm × 994 cm

Location: Musée du Louvre, Paris

The Wedding at Cana (Italian: Le Nozze di Cana) is a monumental oil painting by the Italian Renaissance artist Paolo Veronese, painted in 1562–63 for the refectory of the Benedictine monastery of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice. Measuring roughly 6.8 by 9.9 metres, it is the largest painting in the collection of the Louvre, where it hangs opposite the Mona Lisa.

Subject and description

The picture depicts the wedding feast at Cana described in the Gospel of John, at which Jesus performed his first miracle, turning water into wine. Christ sits at the centre of a vast banqueting table set within classical loggias modelled on the architecture of Palladio. Around 130 figures crowd the scene, including portraits said to represent contemporary rulers and artists — tradition identifies Veronese himself, Titian, Tintoretto and Bassano among the musicians in the foreground.

History

The canvas was taken from Venice by Napoleon's troops in 1797 and brought to Paris, where it was cut in two for transport and later rejoined. A facsimile produced by Factum Arte in 2007 was installed in the original refectory on San Giorgio Maggiore, restoring the painting's intended architectural setting.
