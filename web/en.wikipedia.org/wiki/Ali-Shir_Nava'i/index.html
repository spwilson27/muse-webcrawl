Ali-Shir Nava'i (9 February 1441 – 3 January 1501), also known as Nizomiddin Mir Alisher Navoi, was a Timurid poet, writer, statesman, linguist, Hanafi Maturidi mystic and painter who was the greatest representative of Chagatai Turkic literature. He came to be regarded by many in the Muslim world as the founder of early Turkic literature.

Nava'i believed that Turkic languages were superior to Persian for literary purposes, an unusual view at the time, and defended his belief in his work Muhakamat al-Lughatayn ("The Comparison of the Two Languages"), completed in December 1499. He was a close friend and adviser of Sultan Husayn Bayqara, ruler of Herat, under whom the city became a celebrated centre of art and learning.

Works

Under the pen name Nava'i ("the weeper" or "melodious"), he composed a Khamsa (quintet) of five epic poems in imitation of Nizami, beginning with Hayrat al-abrar ("Wonders of Good People", 1483) and including versions of Layla and Majnun and Farhad and Shirin. He also compiled four divans (collections) of lyric poetry in Chagatai and one in Persian under the pen name Fani. His prose works include the biographical anthology Majalis al-nufais, an early history of Turkic poets.

Legacy

Nava'i is a national hero in Uzbekistan, where the city of Navoi, the National Library, and the Alisher Navoi Theatre of Opera and Ballet in Tashkent bear his name. His works remained influential across Central Asia, and his advocacy established Chagatai as a major literary language, used by figures such as Babur, founder of the Mughal Empire.
