Infection prevention and control (IPC)

Focus: Preventing healthcare-associated infections

Key measures: Hand hygiene, isolation precautions, sterilization, surveillance

Infection prevention and control (IPC) is the discipline concerned with preventing the spread of infections within healthcare settings and the community. It addresses healthcare-associated infections such as surgical site infections, bloodstream infections, and pneumonia.

Core practices

Key elements include hand hygiene (promoted by the WHO's "Five Moments"), standard and transmission-based precautions, cleaning and sterilization of equipment, antimicrobial stewardship, vaccination of staff and patients, and surveillance of infection rates.

History

Foundations were laid by Ignaz Semmelweis, who showed in 1847 that hand disinfection drastically reduced puerperal fever, and by Florence Nightingale and Joseph Lister's antiseptic practice.
