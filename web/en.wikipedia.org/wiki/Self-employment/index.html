Self-employment

Placeholder image: a self-employed person working independently.

Self-employment is the state of working for oneself rather than as an employee of another person or organisation. Self-employed people include sole traders, freelancers, independent contractors, and gig workers, and are common in trades, professions, agriculture, retail, and creative industries. They generally find their own work, invoice clients directly, and bear responsibility for their own taxes, insurance, and retirement saving.

Characteristics and trends

Rates of self-employment vary widely between countries — higher in developing economies with large agricultural and informal sectors, and around 10–15 percent of employment in many advanced economies. People choose self-employment for autonomy and flexibility, or are pushed into it by lack of alternatives; earnings and job security tend to be more variable than in employment. The growth of platform-mediated "gig work" has blurred the boundary, prompting legal disputes in many jurisdictions over whether platform workers should be classed as employees or genuinely self-employed.

Taxation and regulation

Self-employed workers typically pay both the employer and employee shares of social insurance contributions (for example, the self-employment tax in the United States) and file taxes as businesses, deducting allowable expenses. Misclassification of employees as independent contractors is a persistent enforcement issue because it shifts costs and risks onto workers.
