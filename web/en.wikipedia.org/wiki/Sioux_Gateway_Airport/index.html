Sioux Gateway Airport (IATA: SUX, ICAO: KSUX), also known as Brigadier General Bud Day Field, is a public and military airport about six miles southeast of downtown Sioux City, in Woodbury County, Iowa. It serves the tri-state Siouxland region of Iowa, Nebraska and South Dakota and is jointly used by the Iowa Air National Guard's 185th Air Refueling Wing, which flies KC-135 tankers from the field.

History

The airport was built during the Second World War as Sioux City Army Air Base, a training field for B-17 and later B-29 bomber crews. After the war it passed to civilian control while retaining an Air National Guard presence. In 1989 the airport became known nationwide as the site of the crash landing of United Airlines Flight 232, a DC-10 that lost all hydraulic flight controls after an engine failure; the crew's partial-control landing on runway 22 (since closed) saved 184 of the 296 people aboard and became a landmark case in crew resource management. A memorial to the flight stands near the airport entrance. The airfield's secondary name honours Sioux City native George "Bud" Day, a Medal of Honor recipient and former prisoner of war in Vietnam.

Facilities and service

The airport has two asphalt runways, the longest of about 9,000 feet, and a small passenger terminal. Commercial service, long supported by the federal Essential Air Service programme, has typically consisted of regional flights to hubs such as Chicago O'Hare and Denver, operated in recent years by United Express and American Eagle at various times. General aviation, cargo and military operations make up the remainder of traffic.
