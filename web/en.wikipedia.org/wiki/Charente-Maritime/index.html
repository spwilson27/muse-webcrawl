Charente-Maritime (French pronunciation: [ʃaʁɑ̃t maʁitim]) is a department in the French region of Nouvelle-Aquitaine, on the Atlantic coast. It takes its name from the Charente River and its maritime character. Until 1941 it was called Charente-Inférieure. Its prefecture is La Rochelle, and other important towns include Rochefort, Saintes, Royan, and Jonzac.

Geography

The department fronts the Bay of Biscay and the Gironde estuary, and includes the islands of Ré, Aix, Madame, and Oléron — Île d'Oléron being the second-largest island of metropolitan France. The coast is famous for oyster farming (Marennes-Oléron oysters), salt marshes, and seaside resorts such as Royan and La Palmyre.

History

Created during the French Revolution in 1790 from the former provinces of Aunis and Saintonge, the department has a rich maritime history: La Rochelle was a Huguenot stronghold besieged by Richelieu in 1627–28; Rochefort was founded by Colbert as a naval arsenal; and the Fort Louvois and the citadel of Brouage defended the coast. The area also lies partly within the Cognac-producing region.

Tourism

Charente-Maritime is one of France's most visited departments, drawing visitors to La Rochelle's old port and aquarium, the Hermione frigate replica at Rochefort, the Roman amphitheatre at Saintes, Fort Boyard (the offshore fort famous from the television show), and the beaches of Île de Ré and Île d'Oléron.
