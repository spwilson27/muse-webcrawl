Oblasts of Russia

The oblasts of Russia are a type of federal subject of the Russian Federation. An oblast is a first-level administrative division analogous to a province, governed by a governor and a regional legislature. There are 48 oblasts, making them the most numerous category of Russian federal subject, alongside republics, krais, federal cities, an autonomous oblast, and autonomous okrugs.

Overview

Most oblasts are named after their administrative centre (capital city) — for example, Moscow Oblast surrounds but does not include the federal city of Moscow, and Leningrad Oblast retains its Soviet-era name although its capital, St. Petersburg, was renamed in 1991. Oblasts vary enormously in area and population: Tyumen Oblast in Siberia is among the largest federal subjects, while Kaliningrad Oblast is an exclave on the Baltic Sea separated from the rest of Russia.

History

The oblast structure was largely inherited from the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic, which organised much of its territory into oblasts and krais (the distinction between them being mostly historical — krais traditionally contained autonomous okrugs or lay on the frontier). With the adoption of the 1993 Constitution, the oblasts became equal federal subjects of the Russian Federation. A series of mergers in the 2000s reduced the total number of federal subjects, converting some autonomous okrugs into parts of oblasts and krais.
