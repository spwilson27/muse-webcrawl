Sleeping Beauty is a 1959 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney and based on Charles Perrault's fairy tale "Sleeping Beauty." It is the 16th Disney animated feature film. The voices include Mary Costa as Princess Aurora, Bill Shirley as Prince Phillip, and Eleanor Audley as the evil fairy Maleficent.

Plot

Princess Aurora is cursed by Maleficent to die on her sixteenth birthday by pricking her finger on a spinning wheel's spindle; the good fairy Merryweather weakens the curse to a deep sleep broken by true love's kiss. Raised in hiding as Briar Rose by the three good fairies, Aurora meets Prince Phillip in the forest, falls under the spell at sixteen, and is saved when Phillip, aided by the fairies, defeats Maleficent — who has transformed into a dragon — and kisses her awake.

Production

The film's distinctive, angular art direction by Eyvind Earle was inspired by medieval art and tapestries. Its score, by George Bruns, adapts Tchaikovsky's ballet music, including the famous "Once Upon a Dream." Shot in Super Technirama 70, it was among the most expensive animated films of its time at about $6 million.

Reception and legacy

Initial box-office returns disappointed and reviews were mixed, but re-releases and home media established it as a classic; in 2019 it was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry. Maleficent is regarded as one of Disney's greatest villains and headlined two live-action films (2014, 2019). Sleeping Beauty Castle is the icon of Disneyland.
