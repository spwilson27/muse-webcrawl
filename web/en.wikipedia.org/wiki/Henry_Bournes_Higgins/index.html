Henry Bournes Higgins

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Bournes Higgins KC (30 June 1851 – 13 January 1929) was an Australian lawyer, politician, and judge. He served on the High Court of Australia from 1906 until his death in 1929 and was president of the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration from 1907 to 1920, where he delivered the landmark Harvester judgment establishing the basic wage in Australia.

Early life and politics

Higgins was born in Newtownards, County Down, Ireland, and emigrated with his family to Melbourne in 1870. He studied at the University of Melbourne and was admitted to the Victorian bar. He entered the Victorian Legislative Assembly in 1894 and was elected to the first federal Parliament in 1901 as the member for Northern Melbourne. He served as Attorney-General in Chris Watson's short-lived Labor government of 1904, the first Labor government at the federal level.

The Harvester judgment

Appointed to the High Court in 1906, Higgins also presided over the federal arbitration court. In the 1907 Harvester case (Ex parte H.V. McKay), he determined that a "fair and reasonable" wage for an unskilled labourer was seven shillings per day — enough for a man, his wife, and three children to live in "frugal comfort". This established the basic wage concept that underpinned Australian industrial relations for decades.

Later life

Higgins resigned from the arbitration court in 1920 in frustration at government interference but remained on the High Court until his death at Dromana, Victoria, in 1929. The federal electoral Division of Higgins in Melbourne is named after him.
