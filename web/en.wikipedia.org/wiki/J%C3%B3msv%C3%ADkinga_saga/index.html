Jómsvíkinga saga

The Jómsvíkinga saga is an Old Norse saga composed in Iceland in the late 12th or early 13th century. It tells the story of the Jómsvíkingar, a legendary brotherhood of Viking warriors said to have been based at Jómsborg, a stronghold somewhere on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea.

Summary

The saga relates the founding of the order by Palnatoke, a Danish chieftain, and describes the strict laws governing the brotherhood: members had to be between eighteen and fifty years old, were forbidden to flee from an enemy of equal strength, and could not show fear. The saga culminates in the Battle of Hjörungavágr (c. 986), in which the Jómsvíkingar were defeated by the Norwegian jarls Håkon Sigurdsson and his son Eirik.

Manuscripts and sources

The saga survives in several manuscripts, including AM 291 4to and the version incorporated into the Flateyjarbók. It draws on earlier traditions also reflected in Fagrskinna and in the writings of Saxo Grammaticus. Although the Jómsvíkingar are generally considered largely legendary, the saga remains an important source for medieval Scandinavian conceptions of honour and warrior society.
