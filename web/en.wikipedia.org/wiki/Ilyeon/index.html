Ilyeon (Korean: 일연; Hanja: 一然; 1206–1289) was a Buddhist monk and historian of Goryeo-dynasty Korea. He is best remembered as the compiler of the Samguk yusa ("Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms"), one of the two great surviving sources for early Korean history, legend, and folklore, alongside Kim Bu-sik's more official Samguk sagi.

Ilyeon spent his final years at Ingaksa temple in present-day Gunwi.

Life

Born Kim Gyeong-myeong in 1206, he entered the Buddhist priesthood as a boy and became a monk at Muryangsa temple. He passed the monastic examinations and rose through the hierarchy of the Seon (Chan/Zen) school during the turbulent decades of the Mongol invasions of Korea. In his later years he was appointed State Preceptor (guksa), the highest monastic rank, by King Chungnyeol. He retired to Ingaksa temple, where he completed the Samguk yusa in the 1280s, and died there in 1289.

Samguk yusa

Compiled in five volumes, the Samguk yusa gathers foundation myths of the ancient kingdoms — including the famous Dangun legend of Korea's founding — biographies of monks, records of temples, pagodas, and Buddhist miracles, and folktales omitted from the Confucian-style official history. Because it preserves mythic and religious material the Samguk sagi excluded, it is indispensable for the study of early Korean religion and folklore, even where its accounts are legendary rather than strictly historical.

Legacy

The temple of Ingaksa, associated with Ilyeon's final years, preserves monuments connected to him, and modern editions and translations of the Samguk yusa — including Ha Tae-hung's English translation — have made his compilation a cornerstone text for Korean studies.
