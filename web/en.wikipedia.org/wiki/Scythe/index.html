Scythe

A scythe is a hand agricultural tool with a long, curved blade attached at an angle to a long wooden handle (the snath), used for mowing grass or harvesting crops. Unlike the shorter sickle, the scythe is wielded with both hands in a sweeping motion while standing upright, cutting a wide swath with each stroke.

History

Scythes appeared in Europe around the 12th–13th centuries and gradually replaced the sickle for haymaking, since a mower with a scythe could cut roughly a hectare in a day. The blade must be kept sharp by honing with a whetstone carried in the field and periodically re-shaped by peening. Fitted with a cradle of wooden fingers, the tool became the grain cradle for harvesting cereals.

Decline and revival

Mechanical mowers and combine harvesters displaced the scythe in industrialised agriculture during the 19th and 20th centuries, but it remains in use on small farms, in steep or wet terrain, and in conservation mowing, and has seen a revival among enthusiasts and in competitive mowing. In art and folklore the scythe is the attribute of Chronos and of the personified Death, the Grim Reaper.
