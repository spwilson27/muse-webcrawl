Herbertsmithite

Herbertsmithite is a mineral with chemical structure ZnCu3(OH)6Cl2. It was named in 2004 for the mineralogist George Frederick Herbert Smith (1872–1953) and is prized by physicists because its copper ions form a perfect kagome lattice, making it a leading candidate material for hosting a quantum spin liquid state.

Occurrence

The mineral was first found in the Los Tres Presidentes mine in the Sierra Gorda district of Chile, and has since been reported from other copper districts including Iran and Kazakhstan. It is closely related to the polymorphs paratacamite and clinoatacamite, and is the zinc endmember of the paratacamite group.

Properties

Herbertsmithite forms rhombohedral crystals of a vivid emerald to blue-green colour, with a hardness of about 3–3.5 on the Mohs scale. In the structure, magnetic Cu2+ ions occupy a two-dimensional kagome lattice of corner-sharing triangles separated by non-magnetic zinc layers.

Quantum spin liquid research

Because magnetic frustration on the kagome lattice prevents conventional ordering, herbertsmithite's spins remain disordered down to very low temperatures. Experiments beginning in the 2000s found no magnetic order down to 50 mK, and neutron scattering studies on synthetic single crystals have been interpreted as evidence of fractionalised spinon excitations characteristic of a quantum spin liquid.
