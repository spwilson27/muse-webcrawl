Leipzig University (German: Universität Leipzig) is a public research university in Leipzig, Saxony, Germany. Founded on 2 December 1409 by Frederick I, Elector of Saxony, and his brother William II, it is one of the world's oldest universities and the second-oldest in Germany by continuous operation. Its foundation followed the exodus of German masters and students from Charles University in Prague after the Decree of Kutná Hora.

History

The university grew with the city, a centre of trade and printing. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz was born in Leipzig and studied there, as did Goethe, who took a law degree; later students included Nietzsche, Wagner, and Angela Merkel, who studied physics. Wilhelm Wundt founded the first laboratory for experimental psychology at Leipzig in 1879, an event often taken as the birth of modern psychology, and the university's Institute for Physics hosted Werner Heisenberg's early career. During the East German era the university was renamed Karl Marx University (1953–1991) and its baroque main church, the Paulinerkirche, was demolished in 1968 — a decision reversed symbolically after reunification by the construction of the Paulinum, a combined chapel and assembly hall recalling the lost church, opened in 2017.

Present day

Leipzig University today comprises 14 faculties, from theology and law to medicine, life sciences, and mathematics and computer science, teaching roughly 31,000 students. The university library, the Bibliotheca Albertina, holds major manuscript collections including the Papyrus Ebers. Nine Nobel laureates are associated with the university, and it anchors the research landscape of the Leipzig–Halle region alongside Max Planck and Leibniz Association institutes.
