Air-cooled engine

An air-cooled engine is an internal combustion engine that rejects waste heat directly to the surrounding air, rather than to a liquid coolant circulated through a radiator. Air-cooled engines have finned cylinders and heads to increase surface area, and often use a fan or vehicle motion to drive air over the hot surfaces.

Advantages and disadvantages

Air cooling eliminates the radiator, water pump, hoses, and coolant, making engines lighter, simpler, and immune to freezing or boiling of coolant — virtues in aircraft, small equipment, and military vehicles. The disadvantages are less precise temperature control, which historically limited power output and emissions performance, and greater noise, since there is no water jacket to dampen sound and fans are often needed.

Applications

Famous examples include the Volkswagen Beetle's flat-four, the Porsche 356 and early 911 engines, the Citroën 2CV, motorcycle engines (notably classic Harley-Davidson and BMW twins), many small piston aircraft engines from Lycoming and Continental, and countless lawnmowers and generators. Emissions regulations and noise standards have pushed most mass-market cars to liquid cooling, but air cooling remains common in light aircraft and small machinery.
