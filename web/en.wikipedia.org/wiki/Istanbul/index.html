Istanbul

Istanbul is the largest city in Turkey and the country's economic, cultural and historic centre, straddling the Bosphorus strait between Europe and Asia. With a population of roughly 16 million (approximate), it is among the world's largest cities. It was successively the capital of the Roman/Byzantine and Ottoman empires as Constantinople, and was officially renamed Istanbul in general usage in 1930.

The historic peninsula holds Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapi Palace and the Grand Bazaar; the modern city spreads across both continents, linked by three Bosphorus bridges and tunnels. Istanbul is Turkey's financial and media capital and one of the world's most visited tourist cities, though Ankara has been the political capital since 1923.

History in brief

Founded as Byzantium (c. 657 BC), refounded as Constantinople (330), conquered by Mehmed II (1453) and capital of the Ottoman Empire until its dissolution, the city kept its cosmopolitan, multi-religious character — mosques, churches and synagogues side by side — into the modern republic.
