Hyōgo Prefecture (兵庫県, Hyōgo-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Kobe. The prefecture stretches from the Sea of Japan in the north to the Seto Inland Sea and Awaji Island in the south, making it one of the few prefectures with coastlines on two seas.

Geography and cities

Major cities include Kobe, Himeji — home to the UNESCO World Heritage Himeji Castle — Nishinomiya, Amagasaki and Akashi. Northern Hyōgo is rural and mountainous, known for the Kinosaki hot springs and the Tajima cattle from which Kobe beef is raised.

History

The prefecture was formed from the former provinces of Harima, Tajima, Awaji and parts of Tamba and Settsu. On 17 January 1995 the Great Hanshin earthquake, centred near Kobe, killed more than 6,400 people and devastated the region, prompting major advances in Japanese disaster preparedness.
