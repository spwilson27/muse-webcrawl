Northrop Corporation

The Northrop Corporation was an American aircraft manufacturer founded in 1939 by John Knudsen "Jack" Northrop, headquartered in Hawthorne, California. Best known for its pioneering flying-wing designs and later for the B-2 Spirit stealth bomber, the company merged with Grumman in 1994 to form Northrop Grumman.

History

Jack Northrop had earlier co-founded the Lockheed Aircraft Corporation and run two previous companies bearing his name before founding the Northrop Corporation in 1939. During the Second World War the company produced the P-61 Black Widow night fighter, the first American aircraft designed specifically for night interception, and built components for other manufacturers.

Northrop devoted much of his career to the flying wing, an all-wing aircraft without a conventional fuselage or tail. The wartime XB-35 and jet-powered YB-49 bombers proved unstable and were cancelled, but the concept was vindicated decades later when Northrop won the contract for the B-2 Spirit stealth bomber, first flown in 1989 — one of the most expensive and secretive aircraft ever built. Other successful products included the F-5 Freedom Fighter and T-38 Talon trainer, exported widely, the F-89 Scorpion interceptor, and the Snark intercontinental cruise missile. The company's F-20 Tigershark failed to find buyers, and its YF-23 lost the Advanced Tactical Fighter competition to the YF-22.

Merger

Following the end of the Cold War and consolidation of the U.S. defence industry, Northrop acquired Grumman in 1994 for about $2.17 billion, creating Northrop Grumman, which continued the B-2 program and became one of the world's largest defence contractors.
