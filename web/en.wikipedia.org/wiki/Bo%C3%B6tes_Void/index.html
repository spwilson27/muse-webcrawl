The Boötes Void (colloquially the Great Nothing) is an enormous, roughly spherical region of space containing very few galaxies. Located in the vicinity of the constellation Boötes, it is one of the largest known voids in the observable universe.

Discovery and properties

The void was reported in 1981 by Robert Kirshner and colleagues as part of a survey of galactic redshifts. Its centre lies roughly 700 million light-years from Earth, and it spans about 330 million light-years across. Whereas a region of that size would be expected to contain thousands of galaxies, only a few dozen have been found within it, many arranged in a tube-like filament running through the void.

Formation

Voids are thought to grow as gravity pulls matter toward the dense filaments and walls of the cosmic web, evacuating the regions between them. The Boötes Void may have formed from the merger of smaller voids.
