The Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S are home video game consoles developed by Microsoft, released worldwide on 10 November 2020 as the fourth generation of the Xbox family and the successors to the Xbox One. The Series X is the high-end model targeting 4K gaming, while the smaller, cheaper, all-digital Series S targets 1440p with otherwise similar features.

Hardware

Both consoles use a custom AMD system-on-chip combining a Zen 2 CPU with an RDNA 2 GPU supporting hardware-accelerated ray tracing, along with a fast NVMe SSD and the Xbox Velocity Architecture, which enables features such as Quick Resume (suspending several games at once) and greatly reduced load times. The Series X ships with 16 GB of GDDR6 memory and a 1 TB SSD and includes an optical disc drive; the Series S has 10 GB of memory, a 512 GB SSD, and no disc drive. Storage can be expanded with proprietary expansion cards or standard USB drives for older titles.

Software and services

The consoles run an interface evolved from Xbox One and support backward compatibility with thousands of Xbox One, Xbox 360, and original Xbox games, many enhanced by FPS Boost and Auto HDR. Microsoft's strategy emphasises the Xbox Game Pass subscription service, day-one releases of first-party games, Smart Delivery (buying a game once for both generations), and the xCloud game-streaming service, which can stream Series X-level gameplay to the consoles.

Sales and reception

The Series X and S launched in 40 countries amid pandemic-era supply shortages and were praised for performance, quiet operation, and Game Pass value. Combined estimates placed sales above 28 million units by 2024, trailing Sony's competing PlayStation 5, as Microsoft increasingly shifted toward a services- and platform-agnostic publishing strategy.
