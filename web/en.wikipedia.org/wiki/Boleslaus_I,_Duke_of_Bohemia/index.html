Boleslaus I, Duke of Bohemia

Boleslaus I (Czech: Boleslav I. Ukrutný, "Boleslaus the Cruel"; c. 915 – 967/972), a member of the Přemyslid dynasty, was Duke of Bohemia from 935 (or 929) until his death. He is notorious for the murder of his elder brother, Duke Wenceslaus — later canonised as Saint Wenceslaus — yet as ruler Boleslaus proved an effective state-builder who consolidated Přemyslid rule, subdued the Bohemian magnates, founded churches and monasteries, and checked Hungarian incursions, standing with Otto I at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955.

Accession and the murder of Wenceslaus

According to tradition, Boleslaus invited his brother Wenceslaus to a feast at Stará Boleslav and had him murdered at the door of the church there, traditionally dated to 28 September 935 (some historians prefer 929). The motive was likely political: Wenceslaus's submissive policy toward East Francia and his alliances with Saxony clashed with Boleslaus's independent stance. Wenceslaus was soon venerated as a martyr and became Bohemia's patron saint; Boleslaus himself later had his brother's remains translated to St Vitus's Church in Prague, an early example of a ruler legitimising power through a dynastic saint's cult.

Reign

Boleslaus waged a fourteen-year war with Otto I before submitting in 950, thereafter becoming an ally: Bohemian troops fought alongside Otto against the Magyars at Lechfeld (955) and against the Slavic tribes on the Recknitz. Within Bohemia, he broke the power of rival dukes, extended control over Moravia and, briefly, parts of Silesia and Lesser Poland, and organised an early territorial administration of strongholds. He supported the Church, founding the Benedictine convent of St George at Prague Castle and the monastery at Břevnov is also credited to his era of church building; his daughter Doubravka's marriage to Mieszko I of Poland in 965 helped bring Christianity to Poland, and his daughter Mlada sought a bishopric for Prague, achieved shortly after his death.
