Eccleston Square

Eccleston Square is a garden square in Pimlico, in the City of Westminster, London. Laid out in the 1830s as part of Thomas Cubitt's development of the Grosvenor Estate lands south of Belgravia, it takes its name from Eccleston in Cheshire, a village associated with the Grosvenor family. The square's white stucco terraces are characteristic of the grand mid-Victorian domestic architecture of Pimlico.

History and residents

The square was built to Cubitt's masterplan for the district and its central garden was planted for the private use of residents, with tennis courts added later; the garden is noted for a notable collection of plants and won awards in London garden-square competitions. Notable past residents include a young Winston Churchill, who lived at 33 Eccleston Square in the years before the First World War, and the neighbourhood later hosted the offices and meetings connected with the early Labour Party. The square's houses are now largely divided into flats and include hotels on its southern side near Victoria station.

Layout

Eccleston Square is one of a sequence of grand squares in Pimlico, alongside Warwick Square and St George's Square, which together form a protected conservation area. Most of its buildings are Grade II listed. The central garden remains private to residents, opening to the public on occasional open-garden days.
