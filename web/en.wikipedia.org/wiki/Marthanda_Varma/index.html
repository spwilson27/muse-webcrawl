Anizham Thirunal Marthanda Varma (1705 – 7 July 1758) was the Maharaja of Travancore from 1729 until his death. Regarded as the "maker of modern Travancore," he transformed the small principality of Venad in present-day southern Kerala into one of the most powerful states in 18th-century India, expanding its territory, reforming its administration and army, and famously dedicating his kingdom to the deity Padmanabha in 1750.

Rise and conquests

Succeeding as ruler of Venad in 1729, Marthanda Varma suppressed the powerful Ettuveetil Pillamar (the "Lords of the Eight Houses") and the Ettara Yogam who had constrained royal authority, and centralised the administration. Through a series of campaigns between 1731 and the 1750s he annexed neighbouring chiefdoms — including Attingal, Kollam (Quilon), Kayamkulam, Kottarakkara, Elayadath Swarupam, Madhupur and others — and defeated the raja of Kochi's allies, greatly enlarging Travancore.

Battle of Colachel

His most celebrated victory was the Battle of Colachel (1741), in which Travancore's forces defeated a Dutch East India Company expedition under Captain Eustachius De Lannoy — a rare defeat of a European power by an Asian state. De Lannoy, taken prisoner, entered Travancore service and modernised its army on European lines, becoming its commander (the "Valiya Kapithan"). Travancore's pepper trade diplomacy and treaties with the English East India Company balanced Dutch influence.

Dedication of the kingdom

On 3 January 1750 Marthanda Varma performed the Thrippadidanam, formally dedicating the entire kingdom to Sri Padmanabha, the presiding deity of the Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram, and thereafter ruled as Padmanabha Dasa ("servant of Padmanabha") — a title borne by all subsequent Travancore maharajas. He also undertook major temple works, including renovation of the Padmanabhaswamy Temple and its great gopuram, and instituted the Murajapam and Bhadra Deepam festivals.

Administration and legacy

Marthanda Varma reorganised revenue administration, built roads and irrigation works, encouraged trade, and made Padmanabhapuram his capital. He was succeeded in 1758 by his nephew Karthika Thirunal Rama Varma, known as Dharma Raja. Marthanda Varma's state-building is considered foundational to the later prosperity of Travancore and modern Kerala.
