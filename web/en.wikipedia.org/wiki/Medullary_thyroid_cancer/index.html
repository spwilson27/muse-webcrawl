Medullary thyroid cancer

Medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) is a form of thyroid carcinoma that originates in the parafollicular cells (C cells) of the thyroid gland, which produce the hormone calcitonin. Medullary tumours account for approximately 3–4% of all thyroid cancers. Unlike the more common papillary and follicular thyroid cancers, MTC does not arise from follicular cells and does not take up radioactive iodine.

Signs and symptoms

MTC usually presents as a solitary thyroid nodule or neck mass, sometimes with enlarged cervical lymph nodes. Because the tumour secretes calcitonin (and occasionally other peptides), very high levels can cause flushing and diarrhoea. Metastasis commonly occurs to cervical and mediastinal lymph nodes, liver, lung, and bone.

Causes and genetics

About 75% of cases are sporadic, while about 25% are hereditary, caused by germline activating mutations in the RET proto-oncogene. Hereditary MTC occurs as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 syndromes (MEN 2A and MEN 2B) or as familial MTC. Genetic testing for RET mutations and prophylactic thyroidectomy in childhood are central to managing affected families; MEN 2 patients must also be screened for pheochromocytoma and hyperparathyroidism.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on fine-needle aspiration cytology, imaging, and markedly elevated serum calcitonin (and carcinoembryonic antigen, CEA), which also serve as tumour markers for follow-up. Histology characteristically shows amyloid deposits derived from calcitonin in the tumour stroma, staining with Congo red.

Treatment and prognosis

The mainstay of treatment is total thyroidectomy with central neck lymph-node dissection; radioactive iodine is ineffective. For advanced disease, targeted therapies including the multikinase inhibitors vandetanib and cabozantinib, and the selective RET inhibitors selpercatinib and pralsetinib, are used. Prognosis depends strongly on stage and on post-operative calcitonin levels; localised disease has good long-term survival, while distant metastasis worsens outcomes.
