Evander of Pallantium

Evander (Greek: Εὔανδρος, "good man") is a figure in Roman mythology, an Arcadian hero who was said to have founded the settlement of Pallantium on the Palatine Hill, at the future site of Rome, some sixty years before the Trojan War. He was credited with introducing the Greek alphabet, laws, and the worship of Greek gods such as Pan to Italy.

In the Aeneid

Evander plays a prominent role in Book 8 of Virgil's Aeneid. Having fled Arcadia after accidentally killing his father, he rules a humble settlement on the Tiber. When Aeneas arrives seeking allies against Turnus and the Rutulians, Evander welcomes him, shows him the sights of the primitive site of Rome, and entrusts his son Pallas to fight beside the Trojans. Pallas is killed by Turnus, whose wearing of Pallas's sword-belt later seals his own fate at Aeneas's hands in the poem's final scene.

Cult

Roman tradition associated Evander with the Lupercal and the festival of the Lupercalia, and with an altar to his mother Carmenta at the Porta Carmentalis. Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Livy both recount his story as part of Rome's prehistory.
