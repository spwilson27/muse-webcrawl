Copper Canyon

StateChihuahua, Mexico
DepthUp to ~1,880 m
AccessEl Chepe railway
[Image: The Urique Canyon from Divisadero]
The Urique Canyon from Divisadero

Copper Canyon (Barrancas del Cobre) is a system of six canyons in the Sierra Madre Occidental of Chihuahua, Mexico — together larger and parts of it deeper than the Grand Canyon — named for the coppery-green colour of its walls.

Overview

The canyons are the homeland of the Rarámuri (Tarahumara), famed for endurance running in sandals across this vertical country. The Chihuahua al Pacífico railway ("El Chepe", completed 1961) climbs from Los Mochis on the coast through 86 tunnels and 37 bridges to Creel and Divisadero, where the rim views drop 1,800 m to the Urique River. Adventure tourism (a park with one of the world's longest zip-lines, cable cars and rim trails) centres on Divisadero; the canyon bottoms are subtropical while snow dusts the rims.
