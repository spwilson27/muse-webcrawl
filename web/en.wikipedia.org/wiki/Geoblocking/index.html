Geoblocking

Geoblocking (also spelled geo-blocking) is the practice by which access to Internet-based content is restricted based on the user's geographical location. Providers determine a user's location most commonly from the IP address assigned to their connection, and then allow or deny access to media, services, software or websites according to licensing agreements, regulatory requirements or pricing policies.

Mechanisms

Geoblocking relies on geolocation databases that map IP address ranges to countries or regions. Techniques used to enforce it include checking the IP address against such databases, inspecting GPS or device locale settings on mobile devices, examining the billing address of a payment method, and analyzing network latency. Users sometimes circumvent geoblocking with virtual private networks (VPNs), proxy servers, or the Tor anonymity network, prompting some services to actively block known VPN endpoints.

Uses

The most visible use of geoblocking is in online distribution of films, television and sports broadcasts, where rights are typically sold territory by territory. Streaming services such as Netflix and the BBC iPlayer therefore present different catalogues — or no access at all — depending on the viewer's country. It is also used for regional pricing of software and video games, compliance with export controls and sanctions, gambling regulation, and enforcement of national laws restricting certain content.

Criticism and regulation

Geoblocking is criticised by consumer groups as a barrier to the free movement of goods and services in digital markets. The European Union adopted the Geo-blocking Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2018/302), applicable from December 2018, which prohibits unjustified geoblocking of goods and certain services within the EU internal market, although copyright-protected audiovisual content was largely excluded. In Australia, a 2015 parliamentary inquiry described circumvention of geoblocking for personal use as not necessarily unlawful, and the practice there attracted attention because of higher local prices for software, a phenomenon nicknamed the "Australia tax".
