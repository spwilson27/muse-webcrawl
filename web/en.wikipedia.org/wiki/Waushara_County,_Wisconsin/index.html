Waushara County is a county located in the U.S. state of Wisconsin. As of the 2020 census, the population was 24,520. Its county seat is Wautoma.

Waushara County is known for its many lakes, trout streams and recreational tourism. The name "Waushara" is believed to come from a Native American word meaning "good land". Much of the county lies on sandy soils left by glacial outwash, and agriculture — especially potatoes, beans and other vegetables grown under irrigation — is a major part of the local economy.

History

Waushara County was created in 1851 from territory that had been part of Marquette County. Settlement increased after the land cessions of the Menominee, and the county developed as a farming and lumbering region. Berlin, in the southeastern part of the county, was a centre of the fur and glove industries, earning it the nickname "the fur and glove capital of the world".

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of 637 square miles (1,650 km²), of which 626 square miles (1,620 km²) is land and 11 square miles (28 km²) (1.8%) is water. The county is drained by tributaries of the Fox River and Wolf River systems and contains numerous small glacial lakes popular for fishing and cottages.

Communities

The county's cities include Berlin and Wautoma; villages include Hancock, Lohrville, Plainfield, Redgranite and Wild Rose. Numerous unincorporated communities and townships make up the rest of the county.
