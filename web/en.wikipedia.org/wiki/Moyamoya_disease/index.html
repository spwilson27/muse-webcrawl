Moyamoya disease

SpecialtyNeurology, neurosurgery
SymptomsStroke, transient ischemic attacks, seizures, headache
Usual onsetChildhood or young adulthood
CausesOften genetic (RNF213); idiopathic

Moyamoya disease is a rare, progressive cerebrovascular disorder in which the internal carotid arteries and their main branches within the skull become narrowed or blocked. To compensate, the brain develops a network of tiny collateral vessels at its base. On angiography this network resembles a "puff of smoke", which in Japanese is moyamoya — hence the name.

Signs and symptoms

Reduced blood flow can cause ischemic strokes and transient ischemic attacks, especially in children, while fragile collateral vessels may rupture and cause hemorrhagic stroke, more common in adults. Seizures, headaches, and cognitive decline also occur.

Cause and diagnosis

The condition is most prevalent in East Asia. Variants in the RNF213 gene strongly predispose to the disease. Diagnosis is made by cerebral angiography or magnetic resonance angiography showing the characteristic stenosis and collateral network.

Treatment

There is no cure, but surgical revascularization — direct bypass such as superficial temporal artery to middle cerebral artery anastomosis, or indirect procedures such as encephaloduroarteriosynangiosis — can improve blood flow and reduce stroke risk.
