The Gulbarg Society massacre took place on 28 February 2002, during the 2002 Gujarat riots in India. A mob attacked the Gulbarg Society, a residential enclave of Muslim families in the Chamanpura area of Ahmedabad, killing 69 people. Among the dead was Ehsan Jafri, a former Congress Member of Parliament who had sheltered neighbours in his house and repeatedly telephoned officials for help before being killed.

Background and attack

The massacre occurred the day after the burning of a train at Godhra killed 59 Hindu pilgrims, which sparked statewide communal violence. On the morning of 28 February a large crowd surrounded the Gulbarg Society compound, populated mainly by affluent Muslim families. Over several hours the attackers breached the boundary wall, set houses on fire, and killed residents with swords, petrol bombs and burning tyres, while survivors' calls for police assistance went largely unanswered. By evening 69 people, including Jafri, were dead; his body was never fully recovered.

Investigations and trial

The case became one of the most closely watched of the riot cases. The Supreme Court of India transferred investigations of major riot cases to a Special Investigation Team (SIT) in 2008. Jafri's widow, Zakia Jafri, filed a complaint alleging a wider conspiracy involving senior state officials; the SIT found insufficient prosecutable evidence against the then chief minister, and the Supreme Court upheld the closure in 2022. In the massacre trial itself, a special court in Ahmedabad convicted 24 accused in June 2016 — 11 for murder, sentenced to life imprisonment — and acquitted 36 others, ruling that the killings were not part of a pre-planned conspiracy.

Legacy

The abandoned, burnt-out housing society stands as a memorial to the riots; survivors and activists have resisted its redevelopment, and the site is visited each year on the anniversary.
