Apparent magnitude

Apparent magnitude (m)
Sun: −26.74
Moon (full): −12.74
Sirius: −1.46
Faintest naked eye: ~+6.5

Apparent magnitude is a measure of the brightness of a star or other astronomical object as seen from Earth. Lower (more negative) values are brighter.

Scale

The logarithmic scale originates with Hipparchus and was formalised by Pogson (1856): a difference of 5 magnitudes is a factor of 100 in brightness, so 1 magnitude ≈ 2.512×.

Relation to absolute magnitude

Absolute magnitude is apparent magnitude at 10 parsecs. Distance modulus m − M = 5 log10(d) − 5 (d in parsecs), corrected for extinction.

Examples

Venus reaches −4.6, Jupiter −2.9, Vega is defined near 0. Hubble can detect objects to about +31.
