Marree Man

The Marree Man, also known as Stuart's Giant, is a geoglyph depicting an Aboriginal man hunting birds or wallabies with a throwing stick. It is located on a plateau near Marree, South Australia, about 60 km west of the township. Discovered by chance from the air in 1998, it is one of the largest geoglyphs in the world, measuring 4.2 km long.

Discovery and description

The figure was first noticed in June 1998 by Trevor Wright, a charter pilot flying between Marree and Coober Pedy. The lines were created by ploughing and were up to 35 metres wide and 30 cm deep, though they have since eroded. The figure is drawn in the style of an Aboriginal man with a woomera or throwing stick, wearing a headband, and was probably made with satellite positioning equipment and a grader.

Authorship and controversy

Who made the Marree Man remains unknown. The work's ownership and any legal rights were never claimed, and local Indigenous groups have criticised the figure as offensive, arguing it was created without consultation and depicts a sacred being in an inappropriate way. In 2016 the South Australian government, with the support of the Arabana people, restored the lines with fresh ploughing. The figure is periodically re-ploughed as the lines fade, and it can only be seen clearly from the air.
