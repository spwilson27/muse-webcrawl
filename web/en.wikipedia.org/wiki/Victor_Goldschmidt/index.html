Victor Goldschmidt

Born27 January 1888, Zürich, Switzerland
Died20 March 1947, Oslo, Norway
FieldsGeochemistry, mineralogy, crystallography

Victor Moritz Goldschmidt (27 January 1888 – 20 March 1947) was a Swiss-born Norwegian mineralogist and petrologist who is considered, along with Vladimir Vernadsky, to be the founder of modern geochemistry and crystal chemistry. He developed the Goldschmidt classification of the elements, dividing them into lithophile, siderophile, chalcophile, and atmophile groups according to their affinity for the Earth's major phases.

Born in Zürich to a family of German-Jewish academics, Goldschmidt moved with his family to Norway in 1901. He studied at the University of Kristiania (Oslo) under Waldemar Brøgger and received his doctorate in 1911 for work on contact metamorphism in the Oslo region. In 1929 he became professor at Göttingen, but as a Jew he was forced to resign under the Nazi regime in 1935 and returned to Norway. After the German occupation he was arrested and narrowly escaped deportation; he spent the war years in Britain, working on raw materials for the war effort, before returning to Oslo in 1946.

Contributions

Goldschmidt used X-ray diffraction to determine the crystal structures of hundreds of compounds, establishing ionic radii for the elements and formulating the empirical rules of crystal chemistry known as Goldschmidt's rules. His classification of elements by geochemical affinity explained their distribution among the Earth's core, mantle, crust, and atmosphere. In his later years he studied the abundances of the elements in meteorites and cosmic matter, and he investigated the use of trace elements in soils for agriculture. The Goldschmidt Conference, the principal annual meeting of geochemists, is named in his honor.
