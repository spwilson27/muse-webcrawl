Flag of Manitoba

Proportion: 1:2
Adopted: May 12, 1966
Design: A Red Ensign with the shield of the provincial coat of arms in the fly

The flag of Manitoba is a variation of the Red Ensign which bears the shield of the provincial coat of arms. This flag was approved by the Royal Proclamation of Queen Elizabeth II on October 27, 1965, and officially proclaimed on May 12, 1966. The decision to adopt the flag was made after the Canadian Parliament voted in the Great Flag Debate to replace the Canadian Red Ensign with the Maple Leaf flag.

Design

The flag is a British Red Ensign: a red field with the Union Jack in the canton. In the fly is the shield from the coat of arms of Manitoba, granted in 1905, which shows a bison standing on a rock on a green field, beneath a red Cross of St George on a white chief. The bison recalls the vast herds that once roamed the Manitoba prairies and were central to the lives of Indigenous peoples and settlers alike.

History

Manitoba had no official provincial flag until the 1960s. Following the federal decision of 1964–1965, Manitoba's premier Duff Roblin introduced legislation to adopt a defaced Red Ensign so that the former Canadian ensign would remain in use provincially. Ontario adopted a similar flag in the same period. The Manitoban flag was first raised over the provincial legislature in Winnipeg on May 12, 1966.

Periodic proposals to replace the flag with a more distinctive design have been raised, but as of the present the 1966 flag remains in use. In a 2001 survey by the North American Vexillological Association, the flag ranked in the lower half of US and Canadian state and provincial flags.
