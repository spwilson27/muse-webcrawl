Cliff diving is the sport of jumping or diving from high cliffs into water. Divers hit the water at speeds of up to 85 km/h (53 mph) from the men's 27-metre platform, making technique on entry essential to avoid injury. Organised competition is governed under the banner of high diving, which has been contested at the World Aquatics Championships since 2013.

History

Diving from cliffs has ancient roots: the Kahekili, Hawaiian chiefs, practised lele kawa (feet-first leaps) from cliffs as a test of bravery, and divers at Acapulco's La Quebrada in Mexico have performed for tourists since the 1930s. The modern competitive era began with the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series, founded in 2009.

Technique and safety

Unlike springboard or platform diving, high divers enter the water feet-first, with arms protecting the head and body tensed to withstand the impact. A team of scuba divers and jet skis monitors the landing zone, and water depth, currents, and underwater obstacles are checked before every session.

Competition

At the World Aquatics Championships, men dive from 27 m and women from 20 m, performing a series of dives judged on take-off, position, and entry. The Red Bull series tours natural and urban venues, including dives from the Stari Most bridge in Mostar and the Copenhagen Opera House.
