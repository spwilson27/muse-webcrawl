Portuguese India

Portuguese India (Estado da Índia)
Established: 1505
Capital: Cochin, then Goa (1530)
Ended: 1961, annexation by India

Portuguese India, officially the State of India (Estado Português da Índia), was the Portuguese colonial complex on the Indian subcontinent, founded after Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498 and organised as a viceroyalty in 1505.

History

Afonso de Albuquerque captured Goa in 1510, making it the capital of Portuguese Asia, and Malacca in 1511. At its height the Estado controlled forts and factories from East Africa to Macau and Timor. Its power declined with Dutch and English competition in the 17th century, leaving the enclaves of Goa, Daman and Diu, plus Dadra and Nagar Haveli.

End

After Indian independence in 1947, Portugal refused to cede the territories. In December 1961 India annexed them in Operation Vijay; Portugal recognised Indian sovereignty only after 1974. Goa became a state of India in 1987.

Legacy

Four and a half centuries left Roman Catholic communities, Indo-Portuguese creole languages, architecture — the churches and convents of Goa are a UNESCO site — and a distinctive Goan diaspora.
