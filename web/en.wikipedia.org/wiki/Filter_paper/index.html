Filter paper

Filter paper folded in a funnel for gravity filtration
MaterialCellulose fibres (cotton or wood pulp)
UsesLaboratory filtration, coffee filters, air and oil filters
Pore sizesRoughly 0.1–30 μm depending on grade

Filter paper is a semi-permeable paper barrier placed perpendicular to the flow of a liquid or gas, used to separate fine solid particles from liquids or gases. It is most familiar as a laboratory consumable in analytical chemistry and as the filter in coffee makers.

Properties and grades

Filter papers are made from cellulose fibres and are characterised by pore size, particle retention, flow rate, wet strength, and ash content. Qualitative filter papers are used for routine separations and identification work, while quantitative ("ashless") papers are manufactured to leave minimal residue on ignition and are used in gravimetric analysis. Common qualitative grades are classed as fast, medium, and slow according to their pore size and retention. Hardened papers treated with resins offer greater wet strength and chemical resistance.

Use

In the laboratory, a circular sheet is folded into a cone and placed in a filter funnel for gravity filtration, or used flat in a Büchner funnel under vacuum. Beyond the laboratory, filter media based on the same principles are used for coffee and tea, engine air and oil filters, and industrial separations.
