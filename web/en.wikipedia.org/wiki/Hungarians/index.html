Hungarians

Hungarians (Hungarian: magyarok), also known as Magyars, are a nation and ethnic group native to Hungary, who share a common culture, history, ancestry and language. Hungarian is a Uralic language, unlike the Indo-European languages of Hungary's neighbours. The Hungarian population in Hungary is close to 10 million; worldwide there are about 14–15 million ethnic Hungarians and people of Hungarian descent.

Origins and history

The Magyars originated in the Ural region and migrated westward across the steppe. Under Árpád, seven Hungarian tribes conquered the Carpathian Basin in 895–896 (the Honfoglalás, "conquest of the homeland"). King Stephen I founded the Christian Kingdom of Hungary in 1000/1001, integrating the Magyars into Latin Christendom.

Diaspora and minorities

After the Treaty of Trianon (1920), large Hungarian communities remained in territories ceded to Romania, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and Austria. Significant emigrant communities exist in the United States, Canada, Germany and elsewhere, augmented by emigration after 1956 and EU accession.

Culture

Hungarian culture includes distinctive folk music and dance traditions, cuisine noted for paprika-seasoned dishes such as goulash, and contributions to music (Liszt, Bartók, Kodály), literature, film, science and sport.
