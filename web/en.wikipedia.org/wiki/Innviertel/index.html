Innviertel

Country: Austria
State: Upper Austria
Area: ~2,250 km²
Main towns: Braunau am Inn, Ried im Innkreis, Schärding

The Innviertel (literally "Inn Quarter") is a traditional region of north-western Austria, one of the four historic quarters of the state of Upper Austria, alongside the Hausruckviertel, Traunviertel and Mühlviertel. It takes its name from the River Inn, which forms its western border with Bavaria, Germany.

Geography

The Innviertel comprises the districts of Braunau am Inn, Ried im Innkreis and Schärding. Its landscape is made up of rolling hills and fertile farmland between the Inn and the Hausruck forest. Principal towns include Braunau am Inn — known as the birthplace of Adolf Hitler — the baroque town of Schärding on the Inn, and Ried im Innkreis, the regional trading centre.

History

For centuries the Innviertel belonged to the Duchy (later Electorate) of Bavaria, unlike the rest of Upper Austria. It was first ceded to Austria in the Treaty of Teschen (1779), which ended the War of the Bavarian Succession; the region was then briefly called the "Innbaiern". During the Napoleonic Wars it returned to Bavaria (1810–1816), before the Congress of Vienna and the Treaty of Munich (1816) confirmed its final transfer to the Austrian Empire. The Innviertel dialect and many local customs still show strong Bavarian influence.

Culture

The region is known for its folk music, brass bands, and dishes shared with Bavarian cuisine. Schärding's pastel-painted Silberzeile row of merchants' houses along the Inn is a well-known landmark, and the area's many breweries reflect its beer culture.
