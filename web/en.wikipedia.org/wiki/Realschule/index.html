Realschule

Realschule (German: [ʁeˈaːlʃuːlə]) is a type of secondary school in Germany, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. It has also existed in Croatia (realna gimnazija), the Austrian Empire, the German Empire, Denmark and the Russian Empire. In the German education system, the Realschule stands between the Hauptschule (lowest) and the Gymnasium (highest) in terms of academic level.

Germany

Pupils typically enter the Realschule at the age of ten, after four years of primary school (Grundschule), and attend for six years, completing at about age sixteen with the Mittlere Reife (intermediate school-leaving certificate). The curriculum emphasises modern languages, mathematics, natural sciences and practical subjects, preparing students for vocational training, technical colleges, or, with sufficiently good grades, transfer to a Gymnasium upper level to take the Abitur.

The first Realschulen emerged in the eighteenth century as an alternative to the classical Gymnasium, offering instruction in "real" (practical and scientific) subjects rather than Latin and Greek. In some German states the traditional three-tier system has been restructured, with Realschulen merged into Oberschulen or Sekundarschulen.
