Pyongyang (Korean: 평양) is the capital and largest city of North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea). It is located in the west-central part of the country, on the Taedong River about 109 kilometres upstream from its mouth on the Yellow Sea. Pyongyang is the political, industrial and transport centre of North Korea, and residence in the city is restricted to citizens deemed loyal to the state.

History

According to North Korean state mythology, Pyongyang is the site of the ancient capital Asadal of Gojoseon, founded by the legendary Dangun. The city later became the capital of Goguryeo in 427 AD, making it one of the oldest continuously significant cities on the Korean Peninsula. It was destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly through wars, including during the Imjin War and the First Sino-Japanese War.

During the Korean War (1950–1953) the city was almost completely destroyed by United Nations bombing. It was rebuilt with Soviet and Chinese assistance in a monumental socialist style, with broad boulevards, large public squares and grand monuments such as the Arch of Triumph, the Juche Tower, and the vast Kim Il-sung Square.

Landmarks

Notable structures include the unfinished 105-storey Ryugyong Hotel, the Rungrado 1st of May Stadium — among the largest stadiums in the world by capacity — and the Pyongyang Metro, one of the deepest metro systems in the world, whose ornate stations double as bomb shelters.

Administration

Pyongyang is a directly governed city (chikhalsi), administered separately from any province. It is divided into 19 districts (guyok) and several counties. The city hosts the Supreme People's Assembly, the Workers' Party of Korea headquarters, and the official residences of the country's leadership.
