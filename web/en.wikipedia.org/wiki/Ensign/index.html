Ensign

Ensign (late 14th century, from Old French enseigne, "mark, symbol, signal or flag", from Latin insignia) has several related meanings, principally a flag or banner and a junior commissioned officer rank in some armed forces.

Flag

As a flag, an ensign is flown at the stern of a ship or vessel to indicate nationality, or used by military and governmental bodies. British usage distinguishes the Red Ensign (civil), Blue Ensign (government), and White Ensign (Royal Navy); many former British colonies based their national flags on these ensigns, often with the national arms or a badge in the fly. Other nations, including the United States, use the national flag itself as a naval ensign, carried aboard ship and styled the "ensign" in regulations.

Military rank

Historically, an ensign was the officer who carried the colours of an infantry regiment — the most junior commissioned rank in the British infantry until it was replaced by second lieutenant in 1871 (the rank lingered longest in the Foot Guards, where "ensign" remains the traditional title of the equivalent rank). In the United States Navy and Coast Guard, ensign (pay grade O-1) is the most junior commissioned officer rank, above chief warrant officer and below lieutenant (junior grade); newly commissioned officers from the Naval Academy and ROTC programmes normally enter service at this rank. Some other navies and armies retain ensign or its cognates as a rank for officer cadets or junior officers.

Other uses

"Ensign" has also been used for a badge or emblem of office generally, for a sign or token, and as a given name and surname. In fiction, the rank is familiar as the entry-level officer grade of Starfleet in the Star Trek franchise.
