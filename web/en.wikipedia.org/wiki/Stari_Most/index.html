Stari Most

Location: Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina
Crosses: Neretva River
Design: Single-span stone arch bridge
Span: 28.7 m; height about 24 m above the river
Built: 1557–1566 (original); rebuilt 2001–2004
Architect: Mimar Hayruddin

Stari Most (Bosnian for "Old Bridge") is a historic Ottoman bridge spanning the Neretva River in the city of Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina. Built in the 16th century, it stood for 427 years before being destroyed during the Bosnian War in 1993, and was rebuilt using original materials and techniques, reopening in 2004. The bridge and the surrounding Old Town are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and an enduring symbol of Mostar — and of both the destruction of the war and post-war reconciliation.

History

The bridge was commissioned by the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and designed by Mimar Hayruddin, a pupil of the great architect Sinan. Completed in 1566, its single elegant limestone arch — 28.7 metres across and rising some 24 metres above the summer water level — was a technical marvel of its age, replacing an earlier wooden suspension bridge. It connected the two halves of Mostar and gave the city its name (mostari means "bridge-keepers").

Destruction and reconstruction

During the Croat–Bosniak War, the bridge was shelled by Bosnian Croat (HVO) forces and collapsed into the Neretva on 9 November 1993 — an act later addressed in war-crimes proceedings at The Hague. After the war, an international effort coordinated by UNESCO, the World Bank and partners rebuilt the bridge stone by stone, recovering original blocks from the riverbed, reopening quarries used in the 16th century, and employing traditional Ottoman construction methods. The reconstructed Stari Most was inaugurated on 23 July 2004.

Diving tradition

For centuries, local divers have leapt from the bridge into the cold Neretva — a rite of passage said to date back to the bridge's early years. The annual diving competition, held each summer, continues today, and the bridge is the venue of a Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series stop.
