Ventricular system

The ventricular system is a set of four interconnected cavities in the brain in which cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is produced and circulated. It is continuous with the central canal of the spinal cord.

Components

The two lateral ventricles, the largest, lie within the cerebral hemispheres. Each connects through an interventricular foramen (of Monro) to the midline third ventricle in the diencephalon. The cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius) leads to the fourth ventricle between the pons, medulla and cerebellum. CSF exits through the median and lateral apertures into the subarachnoid space.

Cerebrospinal fluid

CSF is produced mainly by the choroid plexus lining the ventricles, at about 500 mL per day, cushioning the brain, removing waste and maintaining chemical stability. Total CSF volume is about 150 mL at any time.

Clinical significance

Obstruction of CSF flow causes hydrocephalus — enlargement of the ventricles and raised intracranial pressure — treated with shunts or endoscopic third ventriculostomy. Enlarged ventricles also appear in neurodegenerative disease as brain tissue is lost.
