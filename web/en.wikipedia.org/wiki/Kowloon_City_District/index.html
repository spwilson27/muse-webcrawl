Kowloon City District (Chinese: 九龍城區) is one of the 18 districts of Hong Kong. It is located in the centre of Kowloon and had a population of about 419,000 in 2021, making it one of the more densely populated districts in the territory. The district includes the neighbourhoods of Kowloon City, Ho Man Tin, Hung Hom, To Kwa Wan, Ma Tau Kok, and the former Kai Tak airport area.

History and features

The district takes its name from Kowloon City, a historic area associated with the famous Kowloon Walled City, which was demolished in 1993–94 and replaced by the Kowloon Walled City Park. Song Wong Toi, an important historical relic, commemorates the last emperors of the Song dynasty, who took refuge in the area in the 13th century. The former Kai Tak International Airport, which closed in 1998, lay within the district; its site has been redeveloped with the Kai Tak Cruise Terminal, sports facilities, and housing.

Character

Kowloon City is noted for its concentration of Thai restaurants and shops—Hong Kong's "Little Thailand"—and for the Chiu Chow (Teochew) community. Ho Man Tin is a predominantly residential area with many schools, while Whampoa Garden in Hung Hom is one of Hong Kong's largest private housing estates. The neighbourhoods are served by the MTR Kwun Tong line extension (Ho Man Tin and Whampoa stations, opened 2016) and the Tuen Ma line stations at To Kwa Wan and Sung Wong Toi (opened 2021).
