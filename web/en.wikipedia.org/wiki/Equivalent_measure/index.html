Equivalent measure

Equivalent measures
FieldMeasure theory, probability theory
DefinitionTwo measures with exactly the same null sets
Notationμ ~ ν (mutually absolutely continuous)

In measure theory, two measures μ and ν on the same measurable space are called equivalent if they have exactly the same sets of measure zero — that is, each is absolutely continuous with respect to the other (μ ≪ ν and ν ≪ μ). Equivalent probability measures therefore agree about which events are possible and which are impossible, even though they assign different probabilities.

Radon–Nikodym derivative

If ν is equivalent to μ, the Radon–Nikodym theorem guarantees a positive measurable density f = dν/dμ with ν(A) = ∫A f dμ, and dμ/dν = 1/f.

Applications

Equivalence of measures is central to mathematical finance: the equivalent martingale measure (risk-neutral measure) underlies arbitrage-free asset pricing in the fundamental theorem of asset pricing. The Girsanov theorem in stochastic calculus describes how Brownian motion changes under an equivalent change of measure.
