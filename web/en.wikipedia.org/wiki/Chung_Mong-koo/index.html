Chung Mong-koo

Chung Mong-koo (Korean: 정몽구; born 19 March 1938) is a South Korean business magnate and the honorary chairman of the Hyundai Motor Group, the world's third-largest automobile manufacturer by sales volume. He is the second son of Chung Ju-yung, the founder of the Hyundai conglomerate, and led the group's automotive businesses — Hyundai Motor Company and Kia — for two decades from 2000.

Career

Chung joined Hyundai in 1970 and worked across the group's service, engineering, and steel businesses before taking control of its motor operations. After the Hyundai conglomerate was broken up in the wake of the Asian financial crisis, he became chairman of the newly independent Hyundai Motor Group in 2000, shortly after the group acquired control of Kia Motors. Under his leadership Hyundai and Kia transformed their reputations in overseas markets, emphasising quality improvement, design, and extended warranties, and expanded production with factories in the United States, Europe, India, and China.

Legal issues

In 2007 Chung was convicted in South Korea of embezzlement and breach of trust in connection with slush funds; he received a suspended prison sentence and was later pardoned by President Lee Myung-bak in 2008, with the government citing the importance of business continuity and his role in supporting the country's bid for the 2018 Winter Olympics.

Succession

In October 2020, Chung stepped down as chairman in favour of his son, Chung Eui-sun, and assumed the title of honorary chairman. He has also been a prominent figure in Korean sport administration, heading the Korea Archery Association for decades and serving in Olympic-related roles.
