Hypersplenism

Hypersplenism is a disorder in which an enlarged and overactive spleen removes blood cells from the circulation prematurely and excessively, producing anaemia, leukopenia (low white cell counts), thrombocytopenia (low platelets), or combinations of these, even though the bone marrow is functioning normally and often compensates by producing more cells.

Causes

Hypersplenism most often accompanies splenomegaly (enlargement of the spleen). Common causes include portal hypertension from liver cirrhosis, which congests the spleen; haematological conditions such as thalassaemia, hereditary spherocytosis and myelofibrosis; infections including malaria and visceral leishmaniasis; and infiltrative or storage diseases. The classic diagnostic criteria combine an enlarged spleen, one or more cytopenias, a compensatorily hypercellular bone marrow, and correction after splenectomy.

Symptoms and treatment

Patients may experience abdominal fullness or pain from the enlarged spleen, easy bruising or bleeding from low platelets, fatigue from anaemia, and recurrent infections from low white cells. Treatment addresses the underlying cause where possible; in severe or refractory cases, splenectomy (surgical removal of the spleen) or partial splenic embolisation can relieve the cytopenias, though removal of the spleen carries a lifelong increased risk of overwhelming infection, mitigated by vaccination.
