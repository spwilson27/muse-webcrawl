Nana Sahib

Nana Sahib (born Dhondu Pant, 19 May 1824 – disappeared 1859) was an Indian aristocrat and a leading figure of the Indian Rebellion of 1857. The adopted son of the exiled Maratha peshwa Baji Rao II, he led the uprising at Cawnpore (Kanpur).

Background

Nana Sahib was adopted by the last peshwa in 1827 and raised at Bithoor. When Baji Rao died in 1851, the British East India Company refused to continue his pension to his adopted heir, citing the doctrine of lapse; Nana Sahib's appeals, including an envoy sent to London, failed, leaving him with a lasting grievance.

The rebellion at Cawnpore

When sepoy regiments mutinied at Cawnpore in June 1857, Nana Sahib placed himself at their head and besieged the British entrenchment under General Wheeler. After the garrison surrendered on terms, many were killed at the Satichaura Ghat during evacuation by boat, and most of the surviving women and children were massacred at the Bibighar on 15 July as British relief forces approached — events that became infamous in Britain as the "Cawnpore massacres", though Nana Sahib's personal responsibility for them remains debated.

Defeated by Havelock's force, he fled toward Nepal. His fate is unknown: he is generally believed to have died in the Nepalese Terai by 1859, though rumours of his survival persisted for decades, and he became a folk hero in India as an early leader of resistance to British rule.
