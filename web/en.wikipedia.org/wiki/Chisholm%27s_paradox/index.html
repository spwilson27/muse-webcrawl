Chisholm's paradox

Chisholm's paradox, also known as the contrary-to-duty paradox, is a problem in deontic logic posed by philosopher Roderick Chisholm in 1963. It concerns obligations that apply when a primary obligation has been violated.

The puzzle

The paradox arises from a set of seemingly consistent statements: a man ought to help his neighbours; if he helps, he ought to tell them he is coming; if he does not help, he ought not to tell them; and in fact he does not help. Standard deontic logic struggles to formalise these without deriving contradictions or unwanted conclusions, such as that he ought to tell them he is coming.

Significance

The paradox motivated the development of dyadic deontic logic and remains a benchmark problem for theories of conditional obligation.
