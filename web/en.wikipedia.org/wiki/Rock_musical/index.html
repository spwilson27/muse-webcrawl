Rock musical

A rock musical is a musical theatre work with rock music. The genre grew out of the 1960s counterculture and, after the landmark success of Hair, became a recurring strand of Broadway and West End theatre, ranging from original rock-scored shows to rock operas and adaptations of rock albums. Cast albums of rock musicals have repeatedly crossed over into the pop charts.

Precursors

Musicals had flirted with rock and roll since the 1950s — Bye Bye Birdie (1960) satirised the Elvis phenomenon — but scores remained largely conventional show music. The first full-blooded rock score for the stage arrived with Hair, created by James Rado, Gerome Ragni and composer Galt MacDermot, which opened Off-Broadway in 1967 and on Broadway in 1968, complete with nudity, profanity and anti-Vietnam War protest. Its songs "Aquarius" and "Let the Sunshine In" became pop hits.

Rock operas and concept works

Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice brought the rock opera to the theatre with Jesus Christ Superstar (album 1970; Broadway 1971), following the model of the Who's Tommy (1969). The Rocky Horror Show (1973) turned rock pastiche and B-movie horror into a cult phenomenon. Later decades added Chess (1984), the Who's Tommy on Broadway (1993) and Jonathan Larson's Rent (1996), a Pulitzer- and Tony-winning update of La bohème set amid the AIDS crisis.

Twenty-first century

The 2000s and 2010s saw major successes including Hedwig and the Angry Inch, Spring Awakening (2006), Green Day's American Idiot (2010), Once (2011) and Alanis Morissette's Jagged Little Pill (2019), while rock idioms also infused shows such as Hamilton (2015). Rock musicals remain a bridge between popular music and mainstream theatre audiences.
