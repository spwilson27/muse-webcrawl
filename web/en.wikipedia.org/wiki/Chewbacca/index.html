Chewbacca

Chewbacca (/tʃuːˈbɑːkə/), nicknamed "Chewie", is a fictional character in the Star Wars franchise. He is a Wookiee — a tall, hairy, highly intelligent species from the planet Kashyyyk — and the loyal friend and co-pilot of the smuggler Han Solo aboard the starship Millennium Falcon.

Appearances

Chewbacca first appeared in Star Wars (1977) and returned in The Empire Strikes Back (1980) and Return of the Jedi (1983). In the prequel film Revenge of the Sith (2005) he is shown helping Jedi Master Yoda escape during the Battle of Kashyyyk. He reappeared in the sequel trilogy — The Force Awakens (2015), The Last Jedi (2017) and The Rise of Skywalker (2019) — and as a younger character in Solo: A Star Wars Story (2018), which depicts his first meeting with Han Solo.

Character

Chewbacca communicates in Shyriiwook, the Wookiee language of growls and roars, which his companions understand even though he also understands Galactic Basic without speaking it. He carries a bowcaster, the traditional Wookiee weapon, and wears only a bandolier. According to series background, Chewbacca owes Han Solo a "life debt" after Solo rescued him from Imperial slavery, and he extended this loyalty to Solo's friends and family.

George Lucas was inspired to create Chewbacca by his Alaskan Malamute dog, Indiana, who sat in the passenger seat of his car. The name derives from the Russian word sobaka (собака), meaning "dog". The character's voice, created by sound designer Ben Burtt, mixes recordings of bears, walruses, lions and other animals.

Portrayal

The 7-foot-3-inch (2.21 m) actor Peter Mayhew played Chewbacca in the original trilogy, the prequel Revenge of the Sith, and The Force Awakens, where Finnish actor and former basketball player Joonas Suotamo served as his double; Suotamo took over the role fully from The Last Jedi onward and played the character in Solo.

Cultural impact

In 1997, Chewbacca received an MTV Movie Award for lifetime achievement, presented by Carrie Fisher. The "Chewbacca defense" — a satirical legal strategy of deliberately confusing the jury — was popularised by the television series South Park. The character remains one of the most recognisable figures of the franchise.
