Date17 July 1945
AuthorLeó Szilárd
Signatories70 scientists (Manhattan Project, Chicago and Oak Ridge)
Addressed toPresident Harry S. Truman

The Szilard petition, drafted by physicist Leó Szilárd and signed by 70 scientists working on the Manhattan Project, was a July 1945 appeal to U.S. President Harry S. Truman arguing against using the atomic bomb on Japan without first giving the Japanese an opportunity to surrender after the weapon's effects had been demonstrated.

Content

The petition argued that the United States, having developed the bomb, bore a moral responsibility to consider the consequences of its use: an attack on Japan without warning would sacrifice American moral authority and likely precipitate a nuclear arms race, particularly with the Soviet Union. It urged that the bomb first be demonstrated, or its power described, and that Japan be given a chance to surrender before atomic weapons were used against populated targets.

Fate

The petition circulated at the Chicago Metallurgical Laboratory and Oak Ridge, gathering some 70 signatures by 17 July 1945—though at Los Alamos, Edward Teller was dissuaded from circulating it. It was forwarded through military channels to Washington but was classified and delayed; it never reached President Truman before the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. The document was declassified in 1961. It followed the June 1945 Franck Report, which had raised similar arguments.
