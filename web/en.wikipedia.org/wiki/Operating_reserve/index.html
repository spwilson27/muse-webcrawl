Operating reserve

In electricity networks, the operating reserve is the generating capacity that is available to the system operator within a short interval of time to meet demand in case a generator goes down or there is another disruption to the supply. Most power systems are designed so that, under normal conditions, the operating reserve is always at least the capacity of the largest supplier plus a fraction of the peak load.

Types of operating reserve

  • Spinning reserve: extra generating capacity available from generators already connected (synchronized) to the grid, which can be activated by increasing the power output of those generators.
  • Non-spinning reserve: extra generating capacity not currently connected to the grid but which can be brought online within a short period, typically from fast-start units such as gas turbines or hydroelectric plants.
  • Supplemental reserve: capacity that can be made available over a longer period, used to restore spinning and non-spinning reserves after an event.

Operation

System operators continuously balance supply and demand. Operating reserves allow the grid to survive the sudden loss of a major generator or transmission line (the "N−1" criterion) without shedding load, maintaining frequency within acceptable limits while replacement capacity is brought online.
