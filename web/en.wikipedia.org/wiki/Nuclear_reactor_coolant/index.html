Nuclear reactor coolant

A nuclear reactor coolant is a fluid that removes heat from a reactor core and transfers it to electrical generators or other uses.

Types

Common coolants include light water (in PWRs and BWRs), heavy water (CANDU), gases such as carbon dioxide and helium, liquid metals such as sodium and lead, and molten salts. An ideal coolant has high heat capacity, low neutron absorption, chemical stability and low induced radioactivity. Loss of coolant is a severe accident scenario requiring emergency core cooling.
