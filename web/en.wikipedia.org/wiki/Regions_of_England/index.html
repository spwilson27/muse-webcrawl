Regions of England

The regions of England, formerly known as the government office regions, are the highest tier of sub-national division in England. Between 1994 and 2011, nine regions had officially devolved functions within the UK Government. While they no longer fulfil this role, they continue to be used for statistical and some administrative purposes, notably as the basis for the NUTS 1 statistical regions and for regional statistics published by the Office for National Statistics.

History

The regional structure has roots in the regional planning boards of the 1960s and the standard regions used for statistics. In 1994 the Major government established integrated Government Offices for the Regions, each bringing together the regional offices of several departments. Under the Blair government, regional development agencies were created in 1998, and regional assemblies were established. Following the 2010 general election, the coalition government abolished the government offices and regional development agencies, with functions reverting to departments or local bodies by 2011.

Subdivisions

Each region is subdivided into metropolitan and non-metropolitan counties, unitary authorities and, in London's case, the London boroughs and the City of London. Combined authorities headed by directly elected mayors have since been created across several regions, restoring a measure of regional-level governance.
