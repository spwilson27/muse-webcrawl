Fractional ownership of aircraft

Fractional ownership of aircraft is a method of owning a private aircraft in which several unrelated parties each purchase a share of the aircraft and share the costs of crewing, maintenance and operation. A management company operates a fleet and guarantees each owner access, typically to any aircraft of the same type in the fleet rather than the specific airframe they part-own.

History

The modern fractional model was launched in 1986 with Executive Jet Aviation's NetJets programme, devised by Richard Santulli, who applied timeshare concepts from real estate to business jets. The model expanded rapidly in the 1990s, with competitors such as Flexjet and Flight Options, and was later extended to turboprops and helicopters.

Structure

Owners buy shares (commonly 1/16 to 1/2) corresponding to an annual allotment of flight hours. Costs include the purchase price, a monthly management fee and an occupied hourly rate. In the United States such programmes operate under Part 91 Subpart K of the Federal Aviation Regulations, a framework adopted in 2003 specifically for fractional ownership operations.
