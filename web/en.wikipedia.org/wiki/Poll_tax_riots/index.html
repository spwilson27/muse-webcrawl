Poll tax riots

Date: 31 March 1990

Location: London, England

Cause: Introduction of the Community Charge ("poll tax")

The poll tax riots were a series of protests and a major riot in central London on Saturday 31 March 1990, directed against the Community Charge, the new per-adult local taxation system — universally called the "poll tax" — introduced by the Conservative government of Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher.

Background

The Community Charge replaced domestic rates, which were based on the notional rental value of homes, with a flat charge on every adult, regardless of income. It was introduced in Scotland in 1989 and in England and Wales in 1990. The tax was deeply unpopular: millions refused to pay, local Anti-Poll Tax Federations organised a mass non-payment campaign, and "Can't Pay, Won't Pay" became the movement's slogan.

The march and riot

On 31 March 1990 a demonstration organised by the All Britain Anti-Poll Tax Federation drew an estimated 200,000 people to central London. After confrontations around Whitehall and Downing Street, parts of the crowd moved into Trafalgar Square, where fighting with police continued into the evening. Shops and cars in the West End were attacked and set alight. Hundreds of people were injured and more than 300 arrested.

Aftermath

The riot was among the most serious civil disturbances in 20th-century Britain. Although the government initially stood firm, the tax's unpopularity contributed to the Conservative Party's decision to replace Thatcher as leader in November 1990. Her successor, John Major, announced the abolition of the Community Charge; it was replaced in 1993 by the Council Tax, a property-based tax with a personal element.
