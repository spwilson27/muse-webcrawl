Opéra cake

Opéra cake
Course: Dessert
Place of origin: France
Main ingredients: Almond sponge (joconde), coffee syrup, ganache, coffee buttercream, chocolate glaze

Opéra cake (French: gâteau opéra) is a French cake made with layers of almond sponge (known as joconde in French) soaked in coffee syrup, layered with ganache and coffee French buttercream, and covered in a chocolate glaze.

History

The cake's origins are disputed. The Parisian pâtisserie Dalloyau popularised it in 1955, when it was created by Cyriaque Gavillon and named — according to the house account — by his wife after the Opéra Garnier, whose stage the layered cake evoked. Gaston Lenôtre also claimed to have invented a similar cake in 1960. An earlier precursor, the "Clichy cake", was created by Louis Clichy in the early twentieth century.

Composition

A classic opéra is built as a precise rectangular slab, typically of ten thin layers: three sheets of joconde sponge brushed with strong coffee syrup, two layers of coffee buttercream, two of dark chocolate ganache, and a mirror-smooth chocolate glaze on top, sometimes decorated with gold leaf and the word "Opéra" written in chocolate. The cake is cut with clean edges to display the strata, and is served chilled in small slices because of its richness.
