Chromaffin cell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chromaffin cells, also called pheochromocytes, are neuroendocrine cells found mostly in the medulla of the adrenal gland in mammals. They are also found in other tissues, such as the paraganglia near sympathetic ganglia. Chromaffin cells are named for their affinity for chromium salts, which turn their stored catecholamines brown in histological preparations (the chromaffin reaction).

Function

Under the control of the sympathetic nervous system, chromaffin cells release the catecholamines adrenaline (epinephrine) and noradrenaline (norepinephrine) directly into the bloodstream from secretory granules called chromaffin granules. Roughly 80 percent of the output is adrenaline. These hormones drive the fight-or-flight response, increasing heart rate, blood pressure and blood glucose. The cells also release enkephalins, chromogranins and other peptides.

Development and pathology

Chromaffin cells derive embryologically from the neural crest, making them modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons that have lost their axons. Tumors of these cells include pheochromocytoma (in the adrenal medulla) and paraganglioma (elsewhere), which can cause episodic hypertension from catecholamine excess.
