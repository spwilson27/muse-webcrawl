Mohamed Al-Fayed

Mohamed Al-Fayed (27 January 1929 – 30 August 2023) was an Egyptian businessman whose residence and chief business interests were in the United Kingdom from the mid-1960s. His business interests included ownership of the Hôtel Ritz Paris, the department store Harrods in London, and Fulham Football Club.

Early life and career

Al-Fayed was born Mohamed Fayed in the Roshdy district of Alexandria, Egypt, the eldest son of an Egyptian primary school teacher. He worked in various jobs in Egypt before moving into business, working with the Saudi arms dealer Adnan Khashoggi and later in shipping in Genoa and the United Kingdom. In 1974 he added "Al-" to his name. Together with his brothers he bought the House of Fraser group in 1985, including Harrods, in a takeover that led to a long-running feud with the businessman Tiny Rowland and a Department of Trade and Industry inquiry.

Business interests

Al-Fayed bought the Ritz Hotel in Paris in 1979 and Fulham F.C. in 1997, funding the club's rise to the Premier League. He sold Harrods to Qatar Holdings in 2010 for a reported £1.5 billion, and Fulham to Shahid Khan in 2013.

Dodi Fayed and later life

His son Dodi Fayed was killed in a car crash in Paris in 1997 alongside Diana, Princess of Wales. Al-Fayed maintained that the couple had been murdered in a conspiracy involving the British establishment, claims rejected by the official French investigation and by the 2008 inquest verdict of unlawful killing by the grossly negligent driving of Henri Paul and the pursuing paparazzi. Despite decades of residence, Al-Fayed was repeatedly refused British citizenship. He died in London on 30 August 2023, aged 94.
