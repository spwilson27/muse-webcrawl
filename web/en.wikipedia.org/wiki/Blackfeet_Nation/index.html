The Blackfeet Nation, officially the Blackfeet Tribe of the Blackfeet Indian Reservation of Montana, is a federally recognized Native American tribe in the northwestern United States. The tribe comprises principally the South Piegan (Piikani) division of the Blackfoot Confederacy (Niitsitapi), whose peoples historically ranged over the northern Great Plains of present-day Montana and Alberta.

Reservation

The Blackfeet Indian Reservation, established by treaty and executive agreements in the nineteenth century, covers about 1.5 million acres in Glacier and Pondera counties, bordered by Glacier National Park to the west and Canada to the north. The tribal headquarters and largest community is Browning. Chief Mountain (Nínaiistáko), on the reservation's western boundary, is of central spiritual significance. The tribe operates Blackfeet Community College, ranching and tourism enterprises, and the Museum of the Plains Indian.

History

The Blackfeet were a powerful bison-hunting people who resisted encroachment through the fur trade era. The Marias massacre of 1870, in which U.S. cavalry killed some 200 Piegan, most of them women, children and elders, and the starvation winter of 1883–84 following the collapse of the bison herds, devastated the tribe. The reservation was reduced by land cessions, including the 1895 agreement that ceded the mountainous western strip later incorporated into Glacier National Park. The tribe reorganised under a constitution adopted after the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934 and is governed by an elected nine-member Tribal Business Council.

Culture

The tribe works to revitalise the Blackfoot language, taught through immersion programmes such as the Piegan Institute's schools. Annual events include North American Indian Days in Browning, one of the largest powwows in the region.
