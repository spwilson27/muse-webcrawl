Korean is the official language of both North and South Korea, spoken by about 82 million people. It is written in Hangul, the featural alphabet promulgated in 1443–44 by King Sejong the Great in the document Hunminjeongeum — a script whose letter shapes encode the articulation of the sounds they represent, and which is widely praised as one of the most scientific writing systems ever devised.

Structure

Korean is agglutinative and verb-final (SOV), marking grammatical relations with particles and stacking suffixes onto verb stems. An elaborate system of speech levels encodes politeness and social relations — a dimension of grammar central to Korean life. The vocabulary layers a native stock with a large Sino-Korean stratum (roughly half or more of the lexicon) and modern loanwords, especially from English. The language's genetic affiliation is debated: it forms the small Koreanic family with Jeju, and the old hypothesis linking it to an "Altaic" group is no longer widely accepted.

History and varieties

Old Korean is attested through Chinese-character transcriptions; Middle Korean, the language of the Hangul texts, had a pitch-accent system since lost in Seoul speech. The peninsula's division has let the standard languages drift apart in vocabulary and spelling — South Korea's Hangugeo against North Korea's Joseonmal — though mutual intelligibility remains high. Regional dialects (satoori) persist alongside the standards.
