Zhang Yimou

Zhang Yimou (Chinese: 张艺谋)
Born: 2 April 1950, Xi'an, Shaanxi, China
Occupation: film director, cinematographer, producer
Movement: Fifth Generation of Chinese cinema

Zhang Yimou (born 2 April 1950) is a Chinese filmmaker who is considered a leading figure of the Fifth Generation of Chinese cinema. He first worked as a cinematographer before his directorial debut, Red Sorghum (1987), won the Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival and brought Chinese cinema to wide international attention.

Career

Zhang's early collaborations with actress Gong Li — Ju Dou (1990), Raise the Red Lantern (1991), The Story of Qiu Ju (1992), To Live (1994) and Shanghai Triad (1995) — won major prizes at Cannes, Venice and Berlin, though several were initially restricted in China. He later turned to large-scale wuxia epics, including Hero (2002), House of Flying Daggers (2004) and Curse of the Golden Flower (2006); Hero became a worldwide box-office success. More recent work includes The Great Wall (2016), Shadow (2018) and Full River Red (2023), one of China's highest-grossing films.

Other work

Zhang directed the acclaimed opening and closing ceremonies of the 2008 Beijing Olympic Games, and he directed the ceremonies again for the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics, making him the first person to direct Summer and Winter Olympic ceremonies in the same host city era. He has also staged large-scale outdoor and opera productions, including Puccini's Turandot in the Forbidden City.
