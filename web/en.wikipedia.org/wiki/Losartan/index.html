Losartan

Losartan, sold under the brand name Cozaar among others, is a medication used mainly to treat high blood pressure (hypertension). It is also used for diabetic kidney disease, heart failure and left ventricular enlargement. It is taken by mouth and may be combined with other antihypertensive agents such as hydrochlorothiazide.

Medical uses

Losartan was the first of the angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs, also called sartans) to be marketed, approved in the 1990s. It blocks the binding of angiotensin II to the AT1 receptor, causing blood vessels to relax. It is used to lower blood pressure, to slow the progression of kidney disease in patients with type 2 diabetes, and to reduce the risk of stroke in patients with hypertension and left ventricular hypertrophy.

Pharmacology and side effects

Losartan is a prodrug partly converted in the liver to a more potent active metabolite, EXP3174. Common side effects include dizziness, headache, upper respiratory infection and high blood potassium. Like other drugs acting on the renin–angiotensin system, it must not be used during pregnancy because of the risk of harm to the fetus. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is widely available as a generic drug. See also Angiotensin II receptor blocker.
