Andhra Pradesh (Telugu: ఆంధ్ర ప్రదేశ్) is a state on the southeastern coast of India, bordered by Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, and the Bay of Bengal, with which it has one of India's longest coastlines (about 974 km). Its capital is Amaravati and its largest city the port of Visakhapatnam. The state is a major producer of rice — often called the "rice bowl of India" — and is known for the pilgrimage city of Tirupati.

The Eastern Ghats meet the Bay of Bengal near Visakhapatnam.

History

The region was ruled in antiquity by the Satavahanas and later by dynasties including the Ikshvakus, Eastern Chalukyas, and the Kakatiyas, and it formed part of the Vijayanagara Empire. Andhra State was created in 1953 from the Telugu-speaking districts of Madras State after the fast-unto-death of Potti Sreeramulu; in 1956 it merged with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of Hyderabad State to form a united Andhra Pradesh, the first Indian state formed largely on linguistic lines. On 2 June 2014, under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, Telangana became a separate state, and Andhra Pradesh retained the residuary territory, with Hyderabad serving as joint capital for a transitional period.

Geography and economy

The Krishna and Godavari rivers cross the state, their deltas forming fertile agricultural plains; major dams include Nagarjuna Sagar and Polavaram (under construction). The economy combines agriculture (rice, chillies, mangoes, tobacco), aquaculture, pharmaceuticals, information technology in Visakhapatnam and the capital region, ports, and tourism. Tirumala Venkateswara Temple at Tirupati is among the world's most-visited and wealthiest religious sites. Kuchipudi, one of India's classical dance forms, originates from the village of Kuchipudi in the state.
