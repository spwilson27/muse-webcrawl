Marjory Stoneman Douglas (7 April 1890 – 14 May 1998) was an American journalist, author, women's suffrage advocate and conservationist best known for her defence of the Florida Everglades. Her landmark book The Everglades: River of Grass (1947) redefined public understanding of the Everglades as a precious river ecosystem rather than a worthless swamp, and she campaigned for its protection for five decades, earning the title "Grande Dame of the Everglades".

Early life and career

Douglas was born in Minneapolis and raised in Taunton, Massachusetts, after her parents separated. She graduated from Wellesley College in 1912, briefly married (and divorced) Kenneth Douglas, and in 1915 moved to Miami, where her father Frank B. Stoneman was the first publisher of the newspaper that became the Miami Herald. She worked as a reporter and editor there, served in the Red Cross in Europe during the First World War, and became a popular columnist and short-story writer for magazines such as the Saturday Evening Post.

River of Grass and conservation

Commissioned to write about the Miami River for the "Rivers of America" series, Douglas expanded her research into a five-year study of the whole Everglades watershed. Published in 1947 — the year Everglades National Park was dedicated, an outcome she had lobbied for since the 1920s — the book's opening sentence called the Everglades a "river of grass" flowing imperceptibly from Lake Okeechobee to Florida Bay. It galvanised public support for conservation and remains a classic of environmental literature.

Douglas never retired: at 79 she founded Friends of the Everglades (1969) to fight a proposed jetport in the Big Cypress Swamp, and she campaigned against drainage, sugar-industry pollution and Army Corps of Engineers water projects well into her second century. President Bill Clinton awarded her the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1993. She died in 1998 aged 108; her ashes were scattered in the Everglades, and the vast Marjory Stoneman Douglas Wilderness within the national park bears her name, as does Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School in Parkland, Florida.
