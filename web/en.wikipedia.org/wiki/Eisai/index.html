Myōan Eisai (明菴栄西, 1141–1215), also called Eisai Zenji, was a Japanese Buddhist priest credited with introducing the Rinzai school of Zen Buddhism and tea culture from China to Japan.

Life

Ordained on Mount Hiei in the Tendai tradition, Eisai travelled twice to Song-dynasty China (1168 and 1187–1191), studying Tiantai and Chan (Zen), receiving transmission in the Linji (Rinzai) lineage. On returning he promoted Zen despite opposition from the Tendai establishment, founding Shōfuku-ji in Hakata — Japan's first Zen temple — and Kennin-ji in Kyoto (1202), which combined Zen with Tendai and Shingon practice.

Tea and writings

Eisai brought tea seeds and powdered-tea preparation to Japan, planting tea on Kyushu and near Kyoto. His Kissa Yōjōki ("Drinking Tea for Health", 1211) praised tea as a medicine preserving health — presented to the shogun Minamoto no Sanetomo. His Kōzen Gokoku Ron defended Zen as protection for the nation.

Legacy

Eisai is honoured as founder of Japanese Rinzai Zen and the "father of tea" in Japan; his disciple Dōgen went on to found the Sōtō school.
