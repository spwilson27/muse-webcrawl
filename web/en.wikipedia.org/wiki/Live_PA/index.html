Live PA

Live PA (short for "live public address") is a term used in electronic dance music culture for a live performance by an artist using electronic music equipment — synthesizers, drum machines, samplers, sequencers and effects — rather than DJing with pre-recorded tracks. The term originated in the rave scene of the late 1980s and early 1990s, when promoters listed acts as "DJ" or "live PA" to distinguish them on flyers.

Practice

In a Live PA, the performer triggers, sequences and manipulates the components of tracks in real time: muting and unmuting parts, tweaking filter cut-offs, improvising arrangements and adding effects. Historically this required transporting large amounts of hardware — acts such as The Prodigy, Underworld, Orbital and The Chemical Brothers built their reputations on live hardware sets. Modern live PAs often centre on a laptop running software such as Ableton Live (its name a nod to the practice) combined with MIDI controllers.

Debate

Because a Live PA can range from fully improvised performance to simply pressing play on a pre-arranged set, the term has been debated within dance music culture; some artists have been criticised for presenting essentially pre-recorded performances as "live". The ambiguity has driven interest in transparent performance formats, such as performances on modular synthesizers where the live generation of sound is visible to the audience.
