FlixTrain is a German open-access long-distance passenger rail operator and a subsidiary of Flix, the company best known for the FlixBus intercity coach network. FlixTrain entered the market in 2018, initially by taking over services that had been operated by the insolvent Hamburg-Köln-Express (HKX) and the Berlin-based Locomore start-up, both of which had attempted to compete with Deutsche Bahn on Germany's main intercity corridors.

History

FlixMobility announced its entry into rail in 2017 after the collapse of two independent operators. The first FlixTrain services began on 24 March 2018 on the Hamburg–Cologne route, followed by Stuttgart–Berlin services using refurbished former Interregio coaches hauled by leased locomotives. The company expanded rapidly, adding routes linking Berlin, Cologne, Hamburg, Munich, Frankfurt and other major cities, and in May 2021 it launched its first international services in Sweden, between Stockholm and Gothenburg.

Services were heavily reduced during the COVID-19 pandemic but were progressively restored. In 2022 FlixTrain announced an order for new Talgo-built trainsets, signalling a shift from hired-in rolling stock toward a dedicated fleet for future expansion.

Services and business model

Like FlixBus, FlixTrain operates an asset-light model: train operations are provided with partner companies and leased rolling stock, while FlixTrain handles marketing, pricing, network planning and customer service. Tickets are sold primarily online with dynamic pricing, and the trains are painted in the company's distinctive green livery. FlixTrain competes on price with Deutsche Bahn's ICE and Intercity services, typically offering slower journeys with fewer stops served by locomotive-hauled trains.
