Two-spirit

Two-spirit (also spelled two spirit or 2S) is a modern pan-Indian, umbrella term used by some Indigenous North Americans to describe Native people who fulfil a traditional third-gender or gender-variant ceremonial and social role in their cultures.

Terminology and history

The term was coined at the third Annual Inter-tribal Native American, First Nations, Gay and Lesbian American Conference in Winnipeg in 1990, as an English rendering of the Anishinaabemowin phrase niizh manidoowag ("two spirits"), to replace the anthropological term berdache. Many nations have their own older words for such roles — for example lhamana among the Zuni and nádleehi among the Navajo — and the roles historically carried recognised social and spiritual functions.

Contemporary use

Two-spirit identity today may encompass gender, sexuality and cultural identity, and is distinct from mainstream LGBTQ categories because it is rooted in specific Indigenous traditions. The term is increasingly included in the extended initialism 2SLGBTQ+, especially in Canada.
