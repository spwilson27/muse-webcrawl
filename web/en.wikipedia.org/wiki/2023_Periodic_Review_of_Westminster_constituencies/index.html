2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies

The 2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies was a redrawing of the boundaries of parliamentary constituencies in the United Kingdom, carried out by the four national Boundary Commissions under the Parliamentary Constituencies Act 2020. The review kept the House of Commons at 650 seats, and its final recommendations were published in June 2023 and took legal effect later that year. The new boundaries were first used at the 2024 general election.

Review details

Conducted by: Boundary Commissions for England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
Legal basis: Parliamentary Constituencies Act 2020
Total seats: 650 (unchanged)
Electorate quota: 73,393 (range 69,724–77,062)
First election: 2024 general election

Rules

Each constituency (apart from five protected island seats, including Ynys Môn, Na h-Eileanan an Iar, Orkney and Shetland, and two on the Isle of Wight) had to have an electorate within 5 percent of the UK electoral quota of 73,393. Population shifts since the previous boundaries — mostly dating from 2000 data — meant substantial change across much of the country.

Seat changes between nations

England gained ten seats (533 to 543), Scotland lost two (59 to 57), Wales lost eight (40 to 32) and Northern Ireland remained at 18. Only 65 of the 650 constituencies emerged entirely unchanged. The reforms followed two aborted reviews in 2013 and 2018, which would have reduced the Commons to 600 seats.
