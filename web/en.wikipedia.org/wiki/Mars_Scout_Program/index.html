Mars Scout Program

The Mars Scout Program was a NASA initiative for competitively selected, low-cost missions to Mars, supporting the agency's "faster, better, cheaper" philosophy with a cost cap of roughly US$485 million.

Missions

The first selected Scout mission was Phoenix, a lander that touched down in Mars's far north in May 2008 and confirmed the presence of water ice just below the surface. The second was MAVEN, an orbiter launched in 2013 to study the Martian upper atmosphere and how its loss contributed to climate change.

Legacy

The programme was later wound down as NASA's Mars exploration planning moved to other lines, but the Scout model of principal-investigator-led missions influenced the broader Discovery Program approach to planetary science.
