Nordic Council Literature Prize

[Image: Prize ceremony]

The Nordic Council Literature Prize is an annual award for a work of literature written in one of the languages of the Nordic countries, administered by the Nordic Council. First awarded in 1962, it is regarded as the most prestigious literary prize in the Nordic region and is given during the Nordic Council's autumn session.

Selection

Each member country — Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden — together with the autonomous territories and language areas (the Faroe Islands, Greenland, Åland, and the Sámi language area) nominates works, typically two per country. A Nordic adjudication committee chooses the winner. The prize money is DKK 300,000.

Notable laureates

Early winners included Eyvind Johnson (1962) and William Heinesen (1965). Later laureates include Kjartan Fløgstad, Herbjørg Wassmo, Einar Már Guðmundsson, Sofi Oksanen for Purge (2010), Merethe Lindstrøm, Kim Leine for The Prophets of Eternal Fjord (2013), Jon Fosse (2015), Auður Ava Ólafsdóttir (2018), Jonas Eika (2019), Monika Fagerholm (2020), Niviaq Korneliussen (2021), Solvej Balle (2022), and Joanna Rubin Dranger (2023), the first graphic novel to win.

Purpose

The prize aims to raise interest in the literature and languages of neighbouring Nordic countries. A companion award, the Nordic Council Children and Young People's Literature Prize, was established in 2013.
