The Low Tatras or Low Tatra (Slovak: Nízke Tatry) is a mountain range of the Inner Western Carpathians in central Slovakia. It is located south of the High Tatras, from which it is separated by the valley of the Váh River. Much of the range is protected as the Low Tatras National Park (NAPANT), founded in 1978, the largest national park in Slovakia.

Geography

The range runs roughly east–west for about 80 km. Its highest summit is Ďumbier (2,043 m), followed by Chopok (2,024 m), which is developed as the Jasná ski resort, the largest in Slovakia, served by cable cars from both its northern and southern slopes. Other prominent peaks include Kráľova hoľa (1,946 m) at the eastern end, a mountain of strong symbolic importance in Slovak culture.

Nature

The Low Tatras preserve extensive spruce and mixed forests, dwarf pine at higher elevations, and wildlife including brown bears, lynx, wolves and chamois. The range contains some of Slovakia's best-known caves, notably the Demänovská Cave of Liberty and the Demänovská Ice Cave in the Demänovská valley, and the Bystrianska Cave.

History

During the Slovak National Uprising of 1944, the mountains were a stronghold of partisan resistance, and numerous memorials mark the fighting. The ridge carries a popular long-distance hiking trail, the Heroes of the Slovak National Uprising trail (Cesta hrdinov SNP), which crosses the whole range.
