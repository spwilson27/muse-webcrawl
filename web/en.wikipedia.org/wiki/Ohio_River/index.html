The Ohio River is a 981-mile (1,579 km) long river in the United States. It is located at the boundary of the Midwestern and Southern United States, flowing southwesterly from western Pennsylvania to its mouth on the Mississippi River at the southern tip of Illinois. It is the third largest river by discharge volume in the United States and the largest tributary by volume of the north–south flowing Mississippi River.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers at Pittsburgh. It forms the borders of Ohio, Indiana and Illinois with West Virginia and Kentucky. Major tributaries include the Tennessee, Cumberland, Wabash, Kanawha, Big Sandy and Licking rivers. The Falls of the Ohio at Louisville, the river's only natural obstruction, are bypassed by the McAlpine Locks and Dam.

History

The name "Ohio" comes from a Seneca word meaning "good river" or "great river". The river was the principal route of westward expansion in the early republic, carried enormous steamboat commerce in the 19th century, and formed part of the boundary between free and slave states — crossing it to Ohio meant freedom for many enslaved people escaping via the Underground Railroad. Today the river carries more freight tonnage than the Panama Canal, much of it coal and bulk cargo moved by barges through a system of locks and dams maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.
