Pole of Cold

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Poles of Cold are the places in the southern and northern hemispheres where the lowest air temperatures have been recorded. In the Southern Hemisphere, the Pole of Cold is at the Russian Vostok Station in Antarctica, where a temperature of −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F) was recorded on 21 July 1983 — the lowest natural air temperature ever directly measured on Earth. Satellite observations have since detected surface temperatures as low as about −98 °C on the East Antarctic Plateau.

Northern Hemisphere

In the Northern Hemisphere the title is contested between two localities in the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) of Siberia:

LocalityRecorded minimumDate
Verkhoyansk−67.8 °C (−90.0 °F)5 and 7 February 1892
Oymyakon−67.7 °C (−89.9 °F)6 February 1933

Both lie in deep river valleys surrounded by highlands, where intense winter temperature inversions allow frigid air to pool. Oymyakon, a village of a few hundred inhabitants, is often described as the coldest permanently inhabited place on Earth; an unofficial reading of −71.2 °C from 1924 is displayed on a monument there but is not officially recognised. In 2020 the World Meteorological Organization also recognised a Northern Hemisphere record of −69.6 °C measured at Klinck station in Greenland in December 1991 — the coldest temperature recorded by an automatic weather station in the hemisphere.

Life at the Pole of Cold

Residents of Oymyakon and Verkhoyansk contend with frozen plumbing (indoor plumbing is impractical, so outhouses are common), vehicles that must be kept running or garaged heated, batteries that lose charge, and ground so frozen that burials require thawing the soil with fires. The short summers, by contrast, can be surprisingly warm, giving Verkhoyansk one of the largest temperature ranges of any place on Earth.
