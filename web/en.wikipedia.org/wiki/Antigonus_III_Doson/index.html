Antigonus III Doson

Antigonus III Doson (Greek: Ἀντίγονος Δώσων, 263–221 BC) was king of Macedon from 229 BC until his death in 221 BC. A member of the Antigonid dynasty, he was the son of Demetrius the Fair and a nephew of Antigonus II Gonatas. His epithet Doson, "the one who will give", was said to refer to his habit of promising but not delivering.

Reign

Antigonus became regent for the infant Philip V after the death of Demetrius II in 229 BC, and subsequently took the throne himself while continuing to guard his ward's interests. Early in his reign he campaigned against the Dardanians and recovered Thessaly. In 224 BC he intervened in Greece at the invitation of the Achaean League under Aratus against Cleomenes III of Sparta, refounding the Hellenic League (League of Corinth) under Macedonian leadership.

Sellasia and death

In 222 BC Antigonus defeated Cleomenes at the Battle of Sellasia and occupied Sparta — the first time the city fell to a foreign army. He died the following year, reportedly of a haemorrhage after shouting in battle against the Illyrians, and was succeeded by Philip V. His reign restored Macedonian power and stability after decades of decline.
