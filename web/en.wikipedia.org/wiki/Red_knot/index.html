Scientific nameCalidris canutus
FamilyScolopacidae

The red knot (Calidris canutus) is a medium-sized shorebird (wader) that breeds in tundra and the Arctic Cordillera in the far north of Canada, Europe, and Russia. It is a large Calidris sandpiper, second only to the great knot, and undertakes some of the longest migrations of any bird.

Description

In breeding plumage the face, breast, and belly are a rich rufous red, while in winter the bird becomes pale grey above and white below. Adults are 23–26 cm long with a wingspan of 47–53 cm.

Migration

Subspecies migrate between Arctic breeding grounds and wintering coasts as far away as Tierra del Fuego, South Africa, and Australasia. The American subspecies C. c. rufa, famous for its reliance on horseshoe crab eggs in Delaware Bay, was listed as threatened under the US Endangered Species Act in 2014.
