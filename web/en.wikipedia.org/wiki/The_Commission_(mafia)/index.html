The Commission (mafia)

Founded1931
FounderCharles "Lucky" Luciano
TypeGoverning body of the American Mafia
MembershipBosses of the Five Families, the Chicago Outfit, and other families
PurposeApproving new bosses, sanctioning murders of made men, settling disputes

The Commission is the governing body of the American Mafia, established in 1931 by Charles "Lucky" Luciano following the Castellammarese War and the murders of the old-guard "Mustache Petes." It replaced the single capo di tutti capi ("boss of all bosses") with a board of directors modeled, ironically, on legitimate corporate governance, and it worked alongside the broader National Crime Syndicate.

Structure and powers

The Commission originally comprised the bosses of New York's Five Families — Luciano (later Genovese), Profaci, Mangano, Bonanno, and Gagliano (later Lucchese) — together with Al Capone's Chicago Outfit and Buffalo boss Stefano Magaddino. Its functions included approving new family bosses, regulating the making of new members, mediating disputes between families, and sanctioning (or forbidding) the murder of "made men," especially bosses. Meetings were held roughly every five years, with emergency sessions as needed; the ill-fated 1957 Apalachin meeting in upstate New York, raided by state police, exposed its existence to the public.

Decline

Federal prosecutions under the RICO Act culminated in the Mafia Commission Trial of 1985–1986, in which the heads of the Five Families — including Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo, Carmine Persico, and Anthony Salerno — were convicted of operating the Commission as a criminal enterprise, receiving 100-year sentences. The convictions, built partly on the testimony of informants, effectively ended the Commission as a functioning national body, though investigators believe informal consultations among family bosses continue.
