Law & Order

Law & Order is an American police procedural and legal drama television series created by Dick Wolf, which premiered on NBC on 13 September 1990. Set and filmed in New York City, the series follows a two-part approach: the first half-hour is a police investigation of a crime (usually murder) by NYPD detectives, and the second half depicts the prosecution of the defendant by the Manhattan District Attorney's office. Its stories are often "ripped from the headlines", loosely inspired by real cases.

Format

Episodes open with a teaser in which a crime is discovered, followed by the iconic voiceover: "In the criminal justice system, the people are represented by two separate yet equally important groups: the police, who investigate crime; and the district attorneys, who prosecute the offenders. These are their stories." Scene changes are marked by the famous two-tone "doink-doink" sound, created by composer Mike Post. The show famously avoids the characters' personal lives, focusing on the case at hand, and its ensemble cast rotated frequently over the long run.

Cast and history

Long-serving cast members included Jerry Orbach as the wisecracking Detective Lennie Briscoe (1992–2004), Sam Waterston as Executive ADA (later District Attorney) Jack McCoy, S. Epatha Merkerson as Lieutenant Anita Van Buren, Steven Hill as DA Adam Schiff, Fred Dalton Thompson as DA Arthur Branch, and Angie Harmon, Carey Lowell, and Elisabeth Röhm among the assistant district attorneys. With 20 seasons, the original run tied Gunsmoke as the longest-running live-action primetime drama in American television at the time of its cancellation in 2010. NBC revived the series in 2022 with a 21st season, with Waterston returning briefly.

Franchise and influence

Law & Order launched one of television's most successful franchises, including Law & Order: Special Victims Unit (1999–present, the longest-running live-action primetime series in US history), Law & Order: Criminal Intent, Law & Order: Trial by Jury, Law & Order: LA, Law & Order True Crime, and Law & Order: Organized Crime, as well as international adaptations. The series won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series in 1997 and remains in heavy syndication worldwide.
