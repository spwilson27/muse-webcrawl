Hastinapura (Sanskrit: हस्तिनापुर) is a city in the Meerut district of Uttar Pradesh, India, on the right bank of the river Ganges. In Hindu tradition it is famous as the capital of the Kuru Kingdom and the setting of much of the Sanskrit epic the Mahabharata.

In the Mahabharata

According to the epic, Hastinapura was founded by King Hastin, an ancestor of the Kuru clan. It was the capital from which the blind king Dhritarashtra ruled, and the seat of the Kauravas, whose rivalry with their cousins the Pandavas led to the Kurukshetra War. The city is described in the epic as wealthy and splendid, with great palaces, gardens, and treasuries.

The epic also records that Hastinapura was eventually abandoned after a flood of the Ganges, during the reign of Nichakshu, a descendant of the Pandavas, who moved the capital to Kaushambi.

Archaeology

Excavations at Hastinapur carried out by B. B. Lal in 1951–52 for the Archaeological Survey of India uncovered evidence of several occupational periods, including Painted Grey Ware associated with the early first millennium BC, and a flood layer in the early levels, which Lal connected with the epic tradition of the city's abandonment. Though archaeologists debate any direct identification with the epic, the excavations established Hastinapur as an important early historic site.

Modern significance

Today Hastinapur is a town of religious importance for both Hindus and Jains. Jain tradition holds that the first Tirthankara, Rishabhanatha, received his first alms here, and the town contains several ancient Jain temples, making it a place of pilgrimage.
