Bent Pyramid

The Bent Pyramid is an ancient Egyptian pyramid at Dahshur, about 40 kilometres south of Cairo, built under the Old Kingdom pharaoh Sneferu (c. 2613–2589 BC). Uniquely, the pyramid's slope changes partway up: the lower portion rises at a steep angle of about 54 degrees, while the upper portion continues at a shallower 43 degrees, giving the monument its distinctive "bent" profile. It represents a transitional stage between the step pyramid and the true smooth-sided pyramid, and retains much of its outer casing of polished Tura limestone — the best-preserved casing of any Egyptian pyramid.

Construction

Sneferu's builders began the pyramid at a steep angle; cracks in the internal corridors suggest structural stress, and midway through construction the angle was reduced, which lightened the mass. The pyramid has two entrances — in the north and west faces — leading to two separate chamber systems, another unique feature. A small satellite pyramid stands to the south, and a valley temple connected by causeway was excavated nearby.

Significance

Sneferu went on to build the Red Pyramid at the shallower angle throughout, the first successful true pyramid, and his son Khufu later built the Great Pyramid of Giza. The Bent Pyramid's interior was opened to visitors in 2019 after long closure. Its ancient name was "Sneferu Shines in the South".
