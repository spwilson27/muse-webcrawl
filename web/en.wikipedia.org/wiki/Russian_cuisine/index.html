Russian cuisine

Russian cuisine is a collection of the cooking traditions of Russia and the Russian people, shaped by a cold northern climate, a vast territory, and centuries of exchange with neighbouring peoples. Its foundations are the hearty foods of the peasant table: rye bread, grains, root vegetables, cabbage, mushrooms, fish, and preserved foods for long winters.

Characteristic dishes

Soups are central: shchi (cabbage soup), borscht (beet soup with sour cream, shared with Ukraine), solyanka, ukha (fish soup), and cold summer okroshka on kvass. Pelmeni are small meat dumplings of Siberian origin; blini are thin pancakes served with sour cream, caviar, or jam; pirozhki are filled baked or fried buns. Kasha — porridge of buckwheat or other grains — is a staple, as are pickled cucumbers, cabbage, and mushrooms. From the eighteenth century, French-influenced imperial cooking added elaborate dishes such as beef Stroganoff and Olivier salad.

Drinks and customs

Tea, brewed strong and traditionally heated in a samovar, is the national drink; kvass, a lightly fermented rye-bread drink, is a summer staple, and Russia is famed (and stereotyped) for vodka. Orthodox fasting rules, which restricted animal products on many days of the year, fostered a rich repertoire of fish and mushroom dishes.
