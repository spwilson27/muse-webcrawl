Tax law

Tax law: the body of rules by which governments impose financial charges

Tax law or revenue law is the area of law governing the imposition and collection of taxes by public authorities. It covers the statutes, regulations and case law that define what is taxed, at what rates, on whom, and through what procedures, as well as the rights of taxpayers in disputes with tax administrations. Tax law is generally divided between direct taxes (such as income and corporate taxes) and indirect taxes (such as value-added tax, sales tax and excise duties), and between domestic and international tax law.

Sources and structure

In most jurisdictions, tax law is primarily statutory: constitutions grant legislatures the power to tax, and detailed codes — for example, the United States Internal Revenue Code, the United Kingdom's Income Tax Act and Corporation Tax Acts, or Germany's Abgabenordnung (Fiscal Code) — set out the operative rules. Administrative agencies such as the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) in the United States or HM Revenue and Customs in the United Kingdom issue regulations and guidance, assess and collect tax, and conduct audits. Specialist tribunals and courts resolve disputes, usually with a right of appeal on points of law.

Principal subjects

Major branches include income taxation of individuals (wages, business income, capital gains), corporate taxation, payroll and social security taxes, consumption taxes (VAT/GST and sales taxes), excise taxes on specific goods, customs duties, property and wealth taxes, and inheritance and gift taxes. Cross-cutting doctrines include residence and source rules for jurisdiction to tax, deductions and credits, anti-avoidance rules (such as general anti-avoidance rules and controlled foreign corporation regimes), and transfer pricing for transactions within multinational groups.

International tax law

International tax law is built largely on a network of bilateral double taxation treaties, mostly following the OECD Model Tax Convention, which allocate taxing rights between countries and provide mechanisms — credits, exemptions and mutual agreement procedures — to relieve double taxation. Recent developments include the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) project and the agreement on a global minimum corporate tax ("Pillar Two") adopted by many jurisdictions in the 2020s.

Practice

Tax law is practised by specialist lawyers, accountants and advisers, spanning compliance, transactional planning, and litigation. Because tax rules change frequently through annual budgets and finance acts, it is among the most rapidly evolving fields of law.
