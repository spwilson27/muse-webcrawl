PSR B1913+16 (also known as the Hulse–Taylor pulsar) is a binary star system composed of a pulsar and a neutron star companion, discovered in 1974 by Russell Alan Hulse and Joseph Hooton Taylor Jr. using the Arecibo Observatory. It was the first binary pulsar found.

Test of general relativity

Precise timing of the pulsar's 59-millisecond pulses revealed that the pair's orbit is shrinking and its periastron advancing. The rate of orbital period decay matches, to within a fraction of a percent, the energy loss predicted by Einstein's general theory of relativity through the emission of gravitational waves — the first indirect evidence for gravitational radiation. The system also exhibits relativistic time dilation and Shapiro delay, providing stringent tests of gravity.

Recognition

Hulse and Taylor received the 1993 Nobel Prize in Physics "for the discovery of a new type of pulsar, a discovery that has opened up new possibilities for the study of gravitation."
