Discounted cash flow in capital budgeting

Capital budgeting is the planning process used by companies to determine whether their long-term investments — such as new machinery, replacement machinery, new plants, new products, and research and development projects — are worth funding through the firm's capitalization structure. The discounted cash flow (DCF) method is a central tool in this process.

Discounted cash flow methods

DCF methods, including net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR), account for the time value of money by discounting projected future cash flows back to their present value using a discount rate that reflects the project's risk, often the firm's weighted average cost of capital.

Decision rules

Under the NPV rule, a project should be accepted if its net present value is positive. Under the IRR rule, a project is accepted if its internal rate of return exceeds the required rate of return. Conflicting rankings between the two methods can arise for mutually exclusive projects of different scale or timing.

Limitations

The accuracy of DCF-based capital budgeting depends heavily on forecasts of future cash flows and on the chosen discount rate, both of which can be difficult to estimate.
