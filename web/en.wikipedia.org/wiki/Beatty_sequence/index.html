In mathematics, a Beatty sequence (or homogeneous Beatty sequence) is the sequence of integers found by taking the floor of the positive multiples of a positive irrational number. Beatty sequences are named after Samuel Beatty, who wrote about them in 1926.

Definition

A Beatty sequence for the irrational number r > 1 is the sequence of integer parts of r, 2r, 3r, and so on. For example, the Beatty sequence for the golden ratio (about 1.618) begins 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 16.

Beatty's theorem

Beatty's theorem states that if r and s are positive irrational numbers satisfying 1/r + 1/s = 1, then the Beatty sequences for r and s partition the positive integers: every positive integer belongs to exactly one of the two sequences. For the golden ratio and its square, this gives the complementary lower and upper Wythoff sequences. Beatty sequences arise naturally in the analysis of Wythoff's game and in Sturmian words.
