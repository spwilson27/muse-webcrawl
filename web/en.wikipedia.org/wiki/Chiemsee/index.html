Chiemsee

Chiemsee
Location: Bavaria, Germany
Surface area: 79.9 km²
Max. depth: 72.7 m
Surface elevation: 518 m
Islands: Herrenchiemsee, Frauenchiemsee, Krautinsel

Chiemsee is a freshwater lake in Bavaria, Germany, between the cities of Rosenheim and Salzburg. It is often called the "Bavarian Sea" (Bayerisches Meer). Formed at the end of the last ice age about 10,000 years ago, it is the largest lake in Bavaria and the third-largest in Germany, with a surface area of about 79.9 square kilometres. The rivers Tiroler Achen and Prien flow into the lake, and it is drained by the River Alz.

Islands

The lake's principal islands are Herreninsel (Herrenchiemsee), the largest, home to the Herrenchiemsee palace built by King Ludwig II of Bavaria from 1878 as a homage to Versailles; Fraueninsel (Frauenchiemsee), home to a Benedictine convent founded in 782; and the small, uninhabited Krautinsel ("herb island"). The municipality of Chiemsee, centred on the islands and lakeshore communities, is the smallest municipality in Bavaria by population.

Surroundings

The surrounding region, the Chiemgau, is a popular recreational area known for sailing, windsurfing and cycling. In August 1948 the island of Herrenchiemsee hosted the constitutional convention (Verfassungskonvent) whose draft formed the basis of the Basic Law of the Federal Republic of Germany.
