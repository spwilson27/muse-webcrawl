Italia turrita

Italia turrita (Italian: [iˈtaːlja turˈriːta], "turreted Italy") is the national personification of Italy, depicted as a woman wearing a mural crown shaped like the walls and towers of Italian cities, often accompanied by the Stella d'Italia, a five-pointed star.

History

The image derives from Roman coinage showing a turreted female figure, and was revived during the Risorgimento as a symbol of Italian unity and of the communal freedoms of medieval Italian cities. Italia turrita appears on Italian coins, stamps, monuments and official emblems, sometimes holding a cornucopia or the national flag.

Symbolism

The mural crown represents the many walled cities of Italy and, by extension, the nobility of the Italian communes; the star is one of the oldest Italian national symbols, dating to ancient Greek and Roman associations with Venus and Italy.
