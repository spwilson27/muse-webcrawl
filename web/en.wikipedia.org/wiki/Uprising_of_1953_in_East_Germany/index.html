Uprising of 1953 in East Germany

Demonstrators faced Soviet tanks in Berlin on 17 June 1953

The uprising of 1953 in East Germany (German: Volksaufstand vom 17. Juni 1953) was a nationwide revolt against the government of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) that began on 16 June 1953 with a strike by Berlin construction workers and erupted into widespread protests across the country the following day.

Events

The trigger was a government decree raising work quotas (production norms) by 10 percent without increased pay. Protests spread to hundreds of towns and cities and took on political demands, including free elections and the resignation of the government. The uprising was suppressed by Soviet occupation troops, with martial law declared and tanks deployed in Berlin and elsewhere. At least 55 people were killed in the uprising and its aftermath, and thousands were arrested.

Legacy

In West Germany, 17 June was made a national holiday, the "Day of German Unity", until reunification in 1990. The street Unter den Linden axis in Berlin was renamed Straße des 17. Juni in commemoration.
