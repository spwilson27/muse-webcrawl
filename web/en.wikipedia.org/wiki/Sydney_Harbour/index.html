Sydney Harbour

Sydney Harbour, officially Port Jackson, is a natural harbour on Australia's east coast around which the city of Sydney is built. It is widely regarded as one of the world's finest harbours, framed by the Sydney Opera House and the Sydney Harbour Bridge.

Geography

The harbour extends inland from the Tasman Sea between North Head and South Head to its western reaches near Parramatta. It is a drowned river valley (ria) with numerous bays, inlets and islands, and includes the separate inlets of Middle Harbour and the Lane Cove and Parramatta rivers.

History

The harbour was the site of the first British settlement in Australia in 1788, at Sydney Cove, and has long been home to the Eora peoples, including the Gadigal. It remains a working port, ferry network hub and recreational waterway, and hosts famous New Year's Eve fireworks.
