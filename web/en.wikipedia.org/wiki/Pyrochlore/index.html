Pyrochlore

Pyrochlore ((Na,Ca)2Nb2O6(OH,F)) is a mineral and a group of related minerals belonging to the pyrochlore supergroup. It is an important ore of niobium and also contains tantalum, titanium and rare-earth elements.

Properties

Pyrochlore crystallizes in the isometric (cubic) system, typically forming octahedral crystals. Its colour ranges from brown and reddish-brown to black, with a resinous to submetallic lustre. It has a hardness of 5–5.5 on the Mohs scale. The name derives from the Greek pyr (fire) and chloros (green), referring to its tendency to turn green on ignition.

Crystal structure

The pyrochlore structure (space group Fd-3m) consists of corner-sharing octahedra forming a network of tetrahedra. Pyrochlore-structure oxides are studied in materials science for their ionic conductivity, frustrated magnetism, and potential use in immobilizing radioactive waste.

Occurrence

Pyrochlore occurs in carbonatites, pegmatites and alkaline igneous rocks. Notable deposits include Araxá in Brazil, the Oka carbonatite in Quebec, Canada, and occurrences in Norway, where it was first described in 1826.
