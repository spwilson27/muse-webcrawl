iMovie

iMovie is a video editing application developed by Apple Inc. for macOS, iOS, and iPadOS devices. It was originally released in 1999 as a Macintosh application bundled with the first iMac DV. iMovie allows users to edit their own home movies, applying titles, transitions, effects, and music, with simple drag-and-drop tools aimed at consumers rather than professionals.

History

iMovie debuted alongside FireWire-equipped Macs, making it easy to import footage from DV camcorders. It became part of the iLife suite of consumer media applications. A major rewrite, iMovie '08, simplified the interface around a skimming-based library but removed features, prompting Apple to keep the prior version available; later releases gradually restored and extended its capabilities. Versions for iPhone and iPad followed in 2010 and 2011.

Features

Current versions support 4K video, trailers with pre-built templates, themes, green-screen and split-screen effects, picture-in-picture, speed changes, and stabilization. Projects can be started on iPhone or iPad and finished on a Mac, and finished movies can be shared to online video platforms or exported for Apple's professional editor, Final Cut Pro.

Availability

iMovie is free and comes preinstalled on new Apple devices, distributed through the App Store.
