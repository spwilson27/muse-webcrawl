Kirtland is a city in Lake County, Ohio, United States. It is best known as the early headquarters of the Latter Day Saint movement in the 1830s, and as the site of the Kirtland Temple, the first temple built by the movement. The population was about 6,900 at the 2020 census.

History

Kirtland was named for Turhand Kirtland, a director of the Connecticut Land Company and an early landowner in the Connecticut Western Reserve. In 1831 Joseph Smith moved his followers to Kirtland, where the church grew rapidly; the Kirtland Temple was dedicated in 1836, and much early Latter Day Saint scripture and organisation dates to this period. Financial collapse in the Kirtland Safety Society banking scandal of 1837 fractured the community, and Smith and most of his followers left for Missouri in 1838.

The temple passed eventually to the Community of Christ (formerly the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints), which maintained it as a historic site for over a century; in 2024 ownership of the temple and other historic properties was transferred to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Modern Kirtland is also home to Holden Arboretum, one of the largest arboreta in the United States.
