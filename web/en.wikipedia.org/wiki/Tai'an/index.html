Tai'an (Chinese: 泰安; pinyin: Tài'ān) is a prefecture-level city in central Shandong province, China. It lies at the southern foot of Mount Tai (Tai Shan), one of China's Five Sacred Mountains, and exists largely in the mountain's cultural and touristic shadow. The city's name combines tai (from Mount Tai) with an, meaning peace or stability, expressing the traditional hope "as stable as Mount Tai" — the mountain having for millennia symbolized permanence and imperial legitimacy.

Mount Tai

Mount Tai's summit, the Jade Emperor Peak, rises 1,545 metres (5,069 ft) above sea level. For more than 3,000 years emperors ascended the mountain to perform the Feng and Shan sacrifices, announcing their rule to Heaven and Earth. The mountain's slopes are covered with temples, steles, and inscriptions — over 1,800 carved texts — including the Dai Temple (Dai Miao) at its base in Tai'an, one of the largest ancient temple complexes in China. Mount Tai was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987 for both its cultural and natural significance, and pilgrims and tourists traditionally climb its roughly 6,000-plus stone steps, often overnight, to watch the sunrise from the summit.

History and administration

The area of Tai'an has been settled since the Neolithic Dawenkou culture, whose type site lies within the prefecture. The modern prefecture-level city administers two districts (Taishan and Daiyue), two county-level cities (Xintai and Feicheng), and two counties (Ningyang and Dongping). Its population is approximately 5.5 million, with about 1.7 million in the urban districts.

Economy and transport

Tourism centered on Mount Tai dominates the urban economy, supplemented by manufacturing, agriculture (the area is noted for peaches from Feicheng and chestnuts), and mining in outlying counties. Tai'an is served by the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (Tai'an station), which puts Beijing about two hours away, and by expressways linking it with the provincial capital Jinan, roughly 60 km to the north.
