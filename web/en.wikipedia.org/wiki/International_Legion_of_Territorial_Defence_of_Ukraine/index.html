The International Legion of Territorial Defence of Ukraine, or simply the International Legion, is a formation of the Armed Forces of Ukraine composed of foreign volunteers. It was announced by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on 27 February 2022, three days after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine, as part of an appeal for foreign citizens to join the defence of Ukraine. Volunteers sign contracts with the Ukrainian military and serve alongside Ukrainian units.

Formation

Following the announcement, Ukraine temporarily lifted visa requirements for foreign volunteers, and thousands of applications were reported from dozens of countries. Ukrainian officials stated in early March 2022 that around 20,000 people from over 50 countries had expressed interest. Recruits were processed through Ukrainian embassies and a dedicated recruitment structure; many had prior military experience, though screening standards were tightened over time to require combat experience for infantry roles.

Composition and units

The Legion was initially organised as battalions within the Territorial Defence Forces and later reorganised, with several of its units transferred to the Ground Forces and other structures. It has included nationally or regionally identified units, such as Georgian, Belarusian (including the Kastuś Kalinoŭski Regiment, which later became a separate formation), Chechen and Russian-volunteer groups; Russian citizens opposed to the Kremlin formed separate formations such as the Freedom of Russia Legion and the Russian Volunteer Corps, the latter outside the Legion structure.

Operations

Legion units have fought in major engagements of the war, including the defence of Kyiv in 2022, fighting around Irpin, the Kharkiv counteroffensive, and battles in the east around Severodonetsk, Lysychansk and Bakhmut. Foreign volunteers have also been among the casualties of the war, and their deaths have been reported in their home countries.

Legal status

Members of the Legion, as contracted members of Ukraine's armed forces, are entitled to combatant status and prisoner-of-war protections under the Geneva Conventions, although Russia has characterised foreign fighters as mercenaries; some captured volunteers were tried in Russian-controlled courts in 2022, drawing international condemnation. The laws of volunteers' home countries vary: some states prohibit or discourage enlistment in foreign armies, while others have taken no action against returning volunteers.
