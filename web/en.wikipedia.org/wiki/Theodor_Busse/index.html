Overview

Theodor Busse (15 December 1897 - 21 October 1986) was a German officer who served in both world wars, rising to the rank of General of the Infantry in the Wehrmacht. He is best known as chief of staff to Field Marshal Erich von Manstein on the Eastern Front and, in the final months of the Second World War, as commander of the German Ninth Army.

Second World War

Busse served as chief of staff of the 11th Army and then of Army Group Don and Army Group South under Manstein, helping plan operations including the relief attempt at Stalingrad and the 1943 Kharkov counteroffensive. Promoted to command I Corps and later the Ninth Army in January 1945, he led that army in the defence of the Oder front.

In April 1945 the Ninth Army was encircled by Soviet forces in the Halbe pocket southeast of Berlin; Busse led a breakout westward in which a remnant of his army reached the Elbe and surrendered to American forces, while many soldiers and civilians were killed. After the war he worked in West German civil defence and wrote on the final battles in the east.
