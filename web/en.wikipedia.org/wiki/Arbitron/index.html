Arbitron was an American consumer research company founded in 1949 as the American Research Bureau. For decades it was the dominant provider of radio audience ratings in the United States, and its name became generic for radio ratings. Its survey reports told stations and advertisers the size and demographics of their audiences, expressed in measures such as average quarter-hour (AQH) persons and cumulative ("cume") audience.

History

The American Research Bureau began by measuring television audiences with diaries and set meters, competing with the Nielsen ratings. It withdrew from television measurement in the 1960s to concentrate on radio, taking the name Arbitron in the following decade. For most radio markets Arbitron's diary method — in which sampled listeners recorded their listening in a paper booklet for one week — remained the standard methodology into the 21st century.

Portable People Meter

In the 2000s Arbitron rolled out the Portable People Meter (PPM), a pager-sized electronic device that passively detects inaudible codes embedded in broadcast audio, replacing diaries in the largest markets. The PPM frequently showed different listening patterns — more stations sampled, shorter average listening spans — than diaries had, prompting controversy and, in some states, government scrutiny over the representation of minority audiences.

Acquisition

In December 2012, Nielsen Holdings announced it would acquire Arbitron for about $1.26 billion; the deal closed on September 30, 2013. Arbitron was renamed Nielsen Audio, and its ratings remain the currency of the American radio advertising market.
