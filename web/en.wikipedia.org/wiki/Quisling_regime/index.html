Quisling regime

The Quisling regime was the collaborationist government of Norway headed by Vidkun Quisling during the German occupation of Norway in the Second World War. From 1 February 1942 Quisling served as Minister President of a national government composed of his Nasjonal Samling party, though real power lay with the German Reichskommissar Josef Terboven.

Background

On 9 April 1940, the day Germany invaded Norway, Quisling proclaimed a coup government in a radio broadcast. The king and legitimate government rejected him and fled to London. The Germans initially installed an Administrative Council, then Terboven's Reichskommissariat, before elevating Quisling in 1942 in the hope of giving the occupation a Norwegian façade.

Rule

The regime attempted to nazify Norwegian institutions, introducing compulsory membership of a teachers' organisation and a labour service, persecuting Jews — about 770 were deported, most murdered at Auschwitz — and suppressing the resistance. Its measures met widespread civil disobedience from teachers, clergy, and sports organisations. After Germany's surrender in May 1945, Quisling was arrested, tried for treason, and executed on 24 October 1945. His surname became a common noun meaning a traitor who collaborates with an occupying enemy.
