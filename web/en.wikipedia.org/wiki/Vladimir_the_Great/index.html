Vladimir the Great

Vladimir I Sviatoslavich (c. 958 – 15 July 1015), also known as Vladimir the Great and Saint Vladimir, was a Varangian prince of Novgorod and Grand Prince of Kiev, ruling Kievan Rus' from 980 to 1015. He is best known for the Christianisation of Kievan Rus' in 988, for which he is venerated as a saint and called the "Apostle of the Rus'".

Rise to power

Vladimir was the youngest son of Sviatoslav I by his housekeeper Malusha. Made prince of Novgorod in 970, he fled to Scandinavia during the fraternal war after Sviatoslav's death, returned with a Varangian army, seized Polotsk and Kiev, and had his half-brother Yaropolk killed, becoming sole ruler in 980. He consolidated the realm through campaigns against neighbouring tribes and initially promoted Slavic paganism, erecting a pantheon of idols in Kiev.

Conversion

According to the Primary Chronicle's famous "testing of the faiths", Vladimir's envoys examined Islam, Judaism, Latin Christianity and Byzantine Christianity, reporting that in Constantinople's Hagia Sophia "we knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth". In 988 Vladimir seized the Byzantine city of Chersonesus in Crimea, was baptised, and married Anna, sister of Emperor Basil II. Returning to Kiev, he ordered the mass baptism of the population in the Dnieper River and the destruction of the pagan idols.

Legacy

The conversion bound Rus' to Byzantine civilisation, brought literacy and church architecture, and remains a foundational event claimed by both Russian and Ukrainian national traditions. Vladimir was canonised by the Orthodox Church; his feast is 15 July, and a colossal statue of him overlooks the Dnieper in Kyiv.
