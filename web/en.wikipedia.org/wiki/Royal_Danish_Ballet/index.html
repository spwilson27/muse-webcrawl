The Royal Danish Ballet (Danish: Den Kongelige Ballet) is an internationally renowned classical ballet company based at the Royal Danish Theatre in Copenhagen, Denmark. Founded in 1748, it is among the oldest ballet companies in the world.

History

The company's identity was shaped above all by August Bournonville (1805–1879), its ballet master from 1830 to 1877, whose choreographic style — light, joyful and technically demanding, with unusually prominent roles for male dancers — remains the cornerstone of its repertory. The Bournonville tradition is preserved through the Bournonville Schools, a graded syllabus still taught to company dancers.

Repertory

Signature works include Bournonville's La Sylphide, Napoli and A Folk Tale, alongside a modern repertory by choreographers such as John Neumeier, Jiří Kylián and Christopher Wheeldon. A Bournonville Festival is held periodically in Copenhagen, most recently celebrating the legacy of the company's defining choreographer.

School

The Royal Danish Ballet School, founded in 1771, trains dancers from childhood and supplies most of the company's performers.
