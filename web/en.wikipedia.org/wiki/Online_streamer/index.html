Online streamer

An online streamer or live streamer is a type of social-media influencer who broadcasts themselves online through a live stream to an audience. Streaming emerged in the mid-2000s and grew rapidly in the 2010s with platforms such as Twitch, YouTube Live, and later Kick and TikTok Live.

Content

Streamers broadcast a wide range of content, most prominently video game playthroughs, commentary, esports, speedrunning, "just chatting" and in-real-life (IRL) streams, music, art, cooking, and study sessions. Audiences interact in real time through text chat, which many streamers read and respond to on air, creating a participatory, community-driven medium.

Platforms and careers

Twitch, launched in 2011 from Justin.tv, became the dominant platform for game streaming, and streamers such as Ninja, Pokimane, and Shroud built audiences of millions. Revenue comes from subscriptions, advertising, donations, sponsorships, and platform partner programs. Successful streamers often employ moderators and editors and treat streaming as a full-time career, while most broadcasters stream to small audiences as a hobby.

Culture and issues

Streaming culture has developed its own language, emotes, and norms. The profession has also drawn attention to issues including burnout from long broadcast hours, harassment, copyright strikes for music played on stream, gambling sponsorships, and platform moderation. Virtual streamers (VTubers), who perform through animated avatars, form a major subculture originating in Japan.
