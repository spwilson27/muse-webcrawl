April Laws

The April Laws, also called the March Laws, were a set of laws legislated by the Diet of the Kingdom of Hungary in the spring of 1848 and sanctioned by King Ferdinand V on 11 April 1848. They were the legislative foundation of the Hungarian Revolution of 1848 and transformed Hungary from a feudal estate society into a modern constitutional state with a parliamentary system.

Provisions

The laws, drafted under the leadership of Lajos Kossuth and Ferenc Deák, established an independent and responsible Hungarian ministry (government) accountable to an elected parliament, abolished serfdom and the tax privileges of the nobility, introduced equal taxation and universal male suffrage for property holders, guaranteed freedom of the press and of religion, and proclaimed the union of Transylvania with Hungary. The palatine, Archduke Stephen, headed the first responsible ministry under Count Lajos Batthyány as prime minister.

Aftermath

The Vienna court initially accepted the laws under revolutionary pressure but worked to undermine them as imperial arms recovered in Italy and Croatia. Conflict over the laws' implementation, the status of Croatia under Ban Josip Jelačić, and Hungary's military and fiscal autonomy escalated into open war in late 1848. After the revolution was crushed in 1849, the laws were annulled; however, they remained the legal reference point for Hungarian constitutionalism, and many of their provisions were restored in substance by the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867.
