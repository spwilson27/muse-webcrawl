Fort William, Scotland

Fort William (Scottish Gaelic: An Gearasdan, "The Garrison") is a town in Lochaber in the Scottish Highlands, on the eastern shore of Loch Linnhe. It is the largest town in the Highlands after Inverness and a major centre for outdoor tourism, lying in the shadow of Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles.

History

The first fort on the site was a wooden stronghold built by Oliver Cromwell's forces in 1654, called the Garrison of Inverlochy. After the Glorious Revolution, a stone fort was rebuilt in 1690 and named Fort William for William III; the settlement that grew around it was called Maryburgh and later Gordonsburgh before becoming Fort William. In 1746 the fort withstood a Jacobite siege during the final rising. The Victorian railway reached the town in 1894, and the West Highland Line still terminates here; the famous Jacobite steam train runs to Mallaig over the Glenfinnan Viaduct.

Tourism

Fort William markets itself as the "Outdoor Capital of the UK." It is the southern terminus of the West Highland Way and the Great Glen Way, hosts mountain biking World Cup events at Aonach Mòr, and serves walkers climbing Ben Nevis via the Mountain Path from Glen Nevis.
