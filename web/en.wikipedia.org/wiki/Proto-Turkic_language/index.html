Proto-Turkic language

The Proto-Turkic language is the reconstructed common ancestor of the Turkic languages, spoken by the Proto-Turks before the family's diversification. It is generally dated to roughly 2,000 years ago (with estimates varying) and located in the eastern Central Asian steppe, in or near present-day Mongolia and the Altai region.

Reconstruction and features

Proto-Turkic is reconstructed from the modern Turkic languages and the earliest written records, the 8th-century Old Turkic inscriptions in Orkhon script. Reconstructed features include vowel harmony, agglutinative suffixing morphology, verb-final (SOV) word order, and a system of eight (or nine) vowels distinguished by front/back quality and length. A long-running scholarly debate concerns the "r/z" and "l/sh" correspondences: the divergent Oghur branch (whose only surviving member is Chuvash) shows /r/ and /l/ where Common Turkic shows /z/ and /š/, which is central to debates about the earliest split in the family and to the (disputed) Altaic hypothesis linking Turkic with Mongolic and Tungusic.

Descendants

The family diversified into the Oghur branch and Common Turkic, the latter giving rise to the Karluk (Uzbek, Uyghur), Kipchak (Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Tatar), Oghuz (Turkish, Azerbaijani, Turkmen), and Siberian (Yakut, Tuvan) branches, among others. Early expansions carried Turkic languages across Eurasia, from Anatolia to Siberia.
