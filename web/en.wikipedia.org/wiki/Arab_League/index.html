Arab League

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Arab League, formally the League of Arab States, is a regional organisation of 22 Arab states in the Middle East and North Africa, founded in Cairo on 22 March 1945 by Egypt, Iraq, Transjordan, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia and Syria (with Yemen joining shortly after). Its aims are to strengthen relations among member states, coordinate policies, and protect their independence and sovereignty, in the spirit of pan-Arabism tempered by the member regimes' own state interests.

History

The League coordinated positions on Palestine (its members fought the 1948 Arab–Israeli war), boycotts and, later, peace diplomacy: Egypt's separate peace with Israel (1979) led to its suspension and the headquarters' move to Tunis (returning to Cairo in 1990). The Arab Peace Initiative (Beirut summit, 2002) offered normalisation with Israel in return for withdrawal and a Palestinian state. During the Arab Spring, the League suspended Libya (2011) and requested the no-fly zone that preceded NATO intervention, and suspended Syria (2011–2023) over its civil war — Syria's readmission in 2023 marked a regional normalisation trend also visible in the Abraham Accords era.

Assessment

Scholars judge the League more effective at regime solidarity and agenda-setting than at integration or conflict resolution; unanimity rules and rivalries (notably the 2017–21 Qatar blockade by fellow members) limit it, yet it remains the Arab world's principal multilateral forum, with summits, a secretariat and specialised councils.
