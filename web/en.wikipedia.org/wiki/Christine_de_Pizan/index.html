Christine de Pizan

Christine de Pizan
Born: 11 September 1364, Venice, Republic of Venice
Died: c. 1430, Poissy, France
Occupation: Writer, poet, philosopher
Notable works: The Book of the City of Ladies (1405)

Christine de Pizan (born Cristina da Pizzano; 11 September 1364 – c. 1430) was an Italian-born French court writer and poet at the court of King Charles VI of France. Widowed at the age of 25 with three children to support, she turned to writing professionally and became one of the most prolific and versatile authors of the Middle Ages — often described as Europe's first professional woman writer.

Life

Born in Venice, Christine moved to Paris as a small child when her father, Tommaso di Benvenuto da Pizzano, became astrologer and physician to Charles V. She married Étienne du Castel, a royal secretary, in 1379; his death from plague around 1389, followed by protracted legal disputes over his estate, forced her to support her family by her pen.

Works

Her best-known work, The Book of the City of Ladies (Le Livre de la Cité des Dames, 1405), constructs an allegorical city built from the stories of famous women of history and mythology, refuting misogynist claims about women's nature. Its companion, The Book of the Three Virtues, offers practical advice to women of all social ranks. Earlier, she had entered the literary debate over the Roman de la Rose, challenging Jean de Meun's portrayal of women — one of the earliest literary controversies in French letters, the Querelle du Roman de la Rose.

Her later works include political writings on good government and warfare (The Book of Deeds of Arms and of Chivalry) and, in her retirement at the abbey of Poissy, a poem celebrating Joan of Arc (1429), the only French-language work celebrating Joan written during her lifetime.
