William Guy

Born: 1810
Died: 1885
Occupation: Physician, medical statistician, forensic medicine specialist
Known for: Guy's method in forensic odontology; statistical work

William Augustus Guy (1810 – 10 September 1885) was a British physician and medical statistician, remembered for his work in forensic medicine, public health statistics, and the study of occupational disease.

Career

Guy was educated at Christ's Hospital and Guy's Hospital in London — the latter founded by his ancestor Thomas Guy's foundation — and at Heidelberg and Paris. He became professor of forensic medicine at King's College London and physician to King's College Hospital, and served as dean of its medical faculty.

Contributions

Guy edited the journal of the Statistical Society of London and contributed important studies on the health and life expectancy of workers in different occupations, arguing for the value of statistics in medical and social questions. In forensic odontology, "Guy's method" refers to his approach of estimating a deceased person's age from changes in the teeth. He also gave expert testimony in prominent poisoning trials of the era and wrote widely on prison health and sanitation.

Later life

Guy served as president of the Statistical Society in the 1870s and remained active in public health advocacy until his death in 1885.
