L-1 visa

The L-1 visa is a non-immigrant visa category in the United States that allows a foreign company to transfer an employee to a related U.S. company, or to establish a new office in the United States. It is commonly known as the intracompany transferee visa.

Categories

The L-1 visa is divided into two subcategories. The L-1A visa is for executives and managers, who may initially be admitted for up to three years and can extend their stay to a maximum of seven years. The L-1B visa is for employees with specialized knowledge of the organization's products, services, or procedures; its maximum total stay is five years. Spouses and unmarried children under 21 are admitted in L-2 status, and L-2 spouses are generally authorized to work incident to their status.

Requirements

To qualify, the employee must have worked for the foreign affiliate, parent, branch, or subsidiary for at least one continuous year within the three years preceding the application, in an executive, managerial, or specialized-knowledge capacity. The U.S. and foreign entities must have a qualifying relationship and both must be doing business. Employers may file individual petitions with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services or use a blanket L petition if the organization meets size and activity thresholds.

New offices and dual intent

Petitions to open a new U.S. office are initially approved for only one year, after which the employer must show the office is active and viable. Unlike many other non-immigrant categories, the L-1 visa is recognized as allowing dual intent, meaning the holder may pursue permanent residence without jeopardizing the visa.
