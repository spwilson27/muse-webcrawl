Politics of Belgium

The politics of Belgium take place within a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. The King is head of state and the Prime Minister is head of government, in a multi-party system in which no single party can govern alone and coalition cabinets are the norm.

Federal structure

Belgium is divided both into three Regions — Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels-Capital — with powers over territory and the economy, and three Communities — Flemish, French and German-speaking — with powers over culture, education and language. Successive state reforms (1970–2011) steadily transferred powers from the federal level to these entities.

Parties and elections

Because party systems are split along linguistic lines, there are effectively two party systems: Flemish parties competing in Dutch-speaking Belgium and francophone parties in the south. Major families include Christian democrats, socialists, liberals, greens and nationalists; the Flemish nationalist N-VA and the far-right Vlaams Belang have been prominent in Flanders, while the PS has long been strong in Wallonia. Federal elections use proportional representation, and voting is compulsory for citizens over 18.

Government formation

Belgium is famous for lengthy coalition negotiations. After the 2010 election the country went 541 days without an elected government, a world record for a democracy, before Elio Di Rupo formed a cabinet. The linguistic divide, especially the status of the Brussels-Halle-Vilvoorde constituency (split in 2012), has historically been the chief fault line of Belgian politics.
