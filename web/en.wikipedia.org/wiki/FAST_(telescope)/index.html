The Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope (FAST; Chinese: 五百米口径球面射电望远镜), nicknamed Tianyan (天眼, "Heavenly Eye" or "Eye of Heaven"), is a radio telescope in a natural karst depression in Pingtang County, Guizhou province, southwest China. It is the world's largest filled-aperture radio telescope, surpassing the Arecibo Observatory in Puerto Rico, and one of the most sensitive radio telescopes ever built. Its chief scientist and founding project leader was the astronomer Nan Rendong.

Construction

Proposed in the 1990s as China's contribution to an international next-generation radio telescope, the project was approved in 2007. Construction began in 2011 in the Dawodang depression, whose natural bowl shape suited the design; more than 9,000 residents were relocated from the surrounding area to preserve radio quietness. The last of the reflector's roughly 4,450 triangular panels was installed on 3 July 2016, and the telescope achieved first light on 25 September 2016. After a period of testing and commissioning — during which it already discovered pulsars — FAST was formally accepted in January 2020 and opened to Chinese astronomers, with time later offered to international observers.

Design

Unlike Arecibo's fixed spherical dish, FAST's reflector is an active surface: its metal panels rest on a network of cables and more than 2,000 actuators that deform the surface into a paraboloid aimed at the current source, illuminating a 300 m aperture. The feed cabin, weighing about 30 tonnes, is suspended from six towers by cables and positioned with millimetre precision, carrying multi-beam receivers including a 19-beam L-band array.

Science

FAST's principal programmes include surveys for pulsars (it has discovered several hundred, including millisecond and binary pulsars), mapping of neutral hydrogen in the Milky Way and nearby galaxies, studies of fast radio bursts, molecular spectroscopy, and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) in commensal observing modes.
