Kerosene lamp

A kerosene lamp (also known as a paraffin lamp in some countries) is a type of lighting device that uses kerosene as a fuel. Kerosene lamps have a wick or mantle as the light source, protected by a glass chimney or globe; they may be used as table lamps or hand-held lanterns. They were the principal source of artificial light in homes and rural areas from the mid-19th century until the spread of electric lighting.

History

The modern kerosene lamp followed the commercialisation of kerosene refining in the 1850s by Abraham Gesner and Ignacy Łukasiewicz, who built an early kerosene lamp in Lviv in 1853. Designs improved with the flat-wick burner with a glass chimney, which produced a bright, steady flame, and with central-draught burners using round wicks. Later, incandescent mantle lamps such as the Aladdin lamp produced a much brighter, whiter light by heating a rare-earth mantle to incandescence.

Types

Main types include flat-wick lamps (including "hurricane" lanterns with wire guards, designed to stay lit in wind), central-draught (round-wick) lamps, and pressure lamps, in which kerosene is pressurised and vaporised before burning — designs such as the Petromax lantern that produce very bright light. Dead-flame lanterns have air supplied from above, while hot-blast and cold-blast tubular lanterns differ in how air is delivered to the flame.

Use today

Kerosene lamps remain in use in parts of the world without reliable electricity and are collected as antiques and used for emergency lighting. They carry risks: kerosene lamps are a cause of fires and burns, contribute to indoor air pollution, and accidental ingestion of kerosene stored in drink bottles is a hazard to children. Solar lamps are increasingly promoted as safer replacements.
