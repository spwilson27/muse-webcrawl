Ninth generation of video game consoles

The ninth generation of video game consoles began in November 2020 with the release of Microsoft's Xbox Series X and Series S and Sony's PlayStation 5. The generation followed the eighth generation's PlayStation 4, Xbox One, and Nintendo Switch (2017) — the Switch itself being variously counted as a late eighth-generation system or an early ninth-generation one.

Characteristics

The ninth-generation home consoles emphasised solid-state drives to eliminate loading times, hardware-accelerated ray tracing, support for 4K resolution at up to 120 frames per second, and near-full backward compatibility with the previous generation's library. Both flagship machines use AMD Zen 2 CPUs and RDNA 2 GPUs. Subscription services — Xbox Game Pass and PlayStation Plus — and digital-only console variants became central to platform strategy.

Hardware

The PlayStation 5 launched on 12 November 2020 in select markets, with a custom 825 GB SSD; a slimmer revision followed in 2023 and the upgraded PlayStation 5 Pro in November 2024. The Xbox Series X, billed as the most powerful Xbox, and the cheaper, smaller Series S launched on 10 November 2020. Nintendo's Nintendo Switch 2 launched in June 2025 and is generally considered part of the same generation.

Market

Early sales were constrained by semiconductor shortages during the COVID-19 pandemic, and many major games continued to be released cross-generationally for years. Handheld gaming PCs such as Valve's Steam Deck (2022) blurred the category of console hardware, while cloud gaming services remained a minor part of the market.
