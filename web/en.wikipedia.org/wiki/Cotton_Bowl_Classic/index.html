Cotton Bowl Classic

[Image: Cotton Bowl Classic]

The Cotton Bowl Classic is an American college football bowl game played annually in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. First played in 1937 at the Cotton Bowl stadium in Dallas, the game moved to AT&T Stadium in Arlington in 2010. It is one of the New Year's Six bowl games and periodically hosts College Football Playoff semifinals.

History

The game was founded by Dallas oilman J. Curtis Sanford, who financed the first game out of his own pocket. It traditionally hosted the champion of the Southwest Conference until that league's dissolution in 1996, and featured famous contests including the 1954 game in which Rice's Dicky Moegle was tackled from the bench by Alabama's Tommy Lewis, and the 1979 "Chicken Soup" game in which Joe Montana led Notre Dame's comeback against Houston.

Since 2014 the Cotton Bowl has been part of the College Football Playoff's New Year's Six rotation, hosting national semifinals in the 2015, 2018, 2021 and 2024 seasons. It is sponsored by Goodyear and officially known as the Goodyear Cotton Bowl Classic.

Venue

The original Cotton Bowl stadium in Fair Park, Dallas, hosted the game from 1937 through 2009. Since the January 2010 game, it has been played at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, home of the Dallas Cowboys.
