Female athlete triad

The female athlete triad is a syndrome in physically active women and girls characterised by three interrelated components: low energy availability (with or without an eating disorder), menstrual dysfunction, and low bone mineral density. It is most commonly seen in sports that emphasise leanness or aesthetic appearance, such as gymnastics, ballet, distance running and figure skating.

Components

Energy deficiency, whether from dietary restriction or high training loads, suppresses reproductive hormones and can lead to amenorrhea. Reduced oestrogen in turn impairs bone formation, increasing the risk of stress fractures and, over time, osteoporosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Early recognition is important because some bone loss may not be fully reversible. Treatment centres on restoring energy balance through increased nutritional intake and, where necessary, reduced training, supported by a multidisciplinary team. The concept has since been broadened by the International Olympic Committee into the model of Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S), which applies to athletes of all sexes.
