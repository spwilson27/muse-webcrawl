Federalist No. 51

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Federalist No. 51, titled “The Structure of the Government Must Furnish the Proper Checks and Balances Between the Different Departments”, is an essay by James Madison (published 6 February 1788 under the name Publius). It is the classic defence of the Separation of powers and Checks and balances in the United States Constitution.

Madison argues that each department must have a will of its own (different modes of election and tenure), that members of each should have as little agency as possible in appointing members of the others, and that “ambition must be made to counteract ambition”: the interest of the man connected with the constitutional rights of the place. In the essay's most quoted line: “If men were angels, no government would be necessary. If angels were to govern men, neither external nor internal controls on government would be necessary.” Because dependence on the people is the primary control but insufficient, the Constitution adds “auxiliary precautions” — the compound republic's double security (federalism plus separation of powers) protecting both the rights of minorities against factious majorities (the theme of Federalist No. 10) and society against its rulers.
