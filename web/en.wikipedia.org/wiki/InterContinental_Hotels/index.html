InterContinental Hotels & Resorts is a luxury hotel brand founded in 1946 by Juan Trippe, president of Pan American World Airways, at the request of U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt, to provide quality accommodation for air crews and passengers in international destinations. The first hotel, the Grande Hotel in Belém, Brazil, opened in 1949.

History

Inter-Continental grew as a Pan Am subsidiary, often opening the first international luxury hotel in emerging destinations. Notable properties include the Phoenicia in Beirut, the Willard in Washington, D.C., and the InterContinental Paris Le Grand. Pan Am sold the chain in 1981; after several owners, it became part of Bass plc and then, from 2003, of IHG Hotels & Resorts (InterContinental Hotels Group), headquartered in the United Kingdom.

Brand

InterContinental is IHG's flagship luxury brand, with more than 200 hotels in over 65 countries, and its loyalty scheme is the IHG One Rewards program together with the InterContinental Ambassador paid membership. The brand's marketing emphasizes local knowledge and "Insider Experiences."
