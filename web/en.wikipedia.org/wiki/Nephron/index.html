Nephron

The nephron is the minute structural and functional unit of the kidney. It is composed of a renal corpuscle and a renal tubule. The renal corpuscle consists of a tuft of capillaries called a glomerulus and a cup-shaped Bowman's capsule. The tubule extends into the proximal convoluted tubule, the loop of Henle, the distal convoluted tubule and the collecting duct system. Each human kidney contains roughly one million nephrons.

Function

Nephrons filter blood at the glomerulus, then reabsorb water, ions, glucose and amino acids while secreting wastes and excess ions along the tubule. The loop of Henle establishes the medullary osmotic gradient that lets the kidney concentrate urine. Hormones such as antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and aldosterone fine-tune these processes, regulating blood volume, pressure and pH.

Types

Cortical (superficial) nephrons have short loops of Henle, while juxtamedullary nephrons have long loops reaching deep into the medulla and are essential for producing concentrated urine.

Clinical significance

Loss of nephron function underlies chronic kidney disease; glomerular filtration rate (GFR), estimated from nephron function, is the standard measure of kidney health.
