Skirmisher

Skirmishers are light infantry or light cavalry soldiers deployed in a loose formation, often ahead of the main line of battle, in a skirmish line. Their roles include screening the main body, harassing the enemy with ranged fire, scouting, delaying an enemy advance, and protecting flanks. Unlike line infantry, skirmishers fight in open order, take advantage of terrain and cover, and exercise more individual initiative.

Ancient warfare

Greek armies employed peltasts — javelin-armed light infantry — and slingers and archers as skirmishers, while Roman armies used velites in front of the legion and later a variety of auxiliary light troops. In late antiquity and the medieval period, archers, crossbowmen and mounted archers frequently performed skirmishing roles, and the feigned retreats and harassment tactics of steppe horse archers, such as the Mongols, were skirmishing raised to a strategic system.

Gunpowder era

With the spread of firearms, skirmishing grew in importance. In the 18th century, armies raised dedicated light troops — German and Austrian Jäger, French chasseurs, British light infantry and rifle regiments armed with the Baker rifle — to fight in open order. The French Revolutionary and Napoleonic wars saw extensive use of tirailleurs. In the American Civil War, regiments routinely deployed a portion of their strength as skirmishers, and specialist units such as Berdan's Sharpshooters operated in that role.

Modern usage

The increasing lethality of modern weapons dissolved the distinction between line and light infantry: by the First World War, effectively all infantry fought dispersed. The term survives in the name of the skirmish line used in dismounted patrolling, in military simulations, and in gaming. In wargaming and video games, "skirmishers" typically denote mobile, lightly armoured units that excel at harassment but cannot hold ground against heavier troops.
