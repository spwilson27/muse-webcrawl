Roman civil wars

The Roman civil wars were a series of internal conflicts fought between rival factions, generals and claimants to power in the Roman Republic and Empire. The civil wars of the late Republic destroyed the republican constitution and led to the establishment of the Roman Empire under Augustus; civil wars recurred throughout imperial history whenever the succession was disputed.

Late Republic

  • Social War (91–88 BC) — Rome against its Italian allies.
  • Sulla's civil wars (88–80 BC) — Sulla marched on Rome and defeated the supporters of Marius, becoming dictator.
  • Sertorian War (80–72 BC) — Sertorius's revolt in Hispania.
  • Caesar's civil war (49–45 BC) — Julius Caesar crossed the Rubicon and defeated Pompey and the Senate's forces at Pharsalus, Thapsus and Munda.
  • War of Mutina, Perusine War and the Liberators' civil war (44–42 BC) — Octavian, Antony and Lepidus (the Second Triumvirate) defeated Caesar's assassins at Philippi.
  • Final War of the Roman Republic (32–30 BC) — Octavian defeated Mark Antony and Cleopatra at Actium, leaving him master of the Roman world as Augustus.

Empire

Major imperial civil wars include the Year of the Four Emperors (AD 69), the Year of the Five Emperors (193), the conflicts of the Crisis of the Third Century, the wars between Constantine and his rivals, and the wars of the Tetrarchy. Contested successions remained a structural weakness of the Principate and Dominate.
