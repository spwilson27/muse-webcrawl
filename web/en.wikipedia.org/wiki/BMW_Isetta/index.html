BMW Isetta

The BMW Isetta is an Italian-designed microcar built under licence in a number of different countries, including Spain, Belgium, France, Brazil, Germany, and the United Kingdom. Produced in the aftermath of World War II, when cheap, short-distance transportation was most needed, it became one of the most successful and influential designs from that era.

The Isetta was originally designed by the Italian company Iso Autoveicoli S.p.A. BMW acquired the licence and redesigned the car extensively, retaining the distinctive egg shape and single front-opening door but fitting its own single-cylinder motorcycle engine. The BMW Isetta 250 was launched in April 1955, followed by the more powerful Isetta 300. Between 1955 and 1962, some 161,728 Isettas were built in Germany, making it for a time the best-selling single-cylinder car in the world.

Design

The entire front end of the car is hinged at the side, allowing the single door to swing out, with the steering wheel and instrument cluster swinging out with it. The car is only 2.29 metres long and 1.37 metres wide, seating two adults in tandem-adjacent comfort. Its bubble-like canopy and tiny dimensions made it ideally suited to crowded post-war European cities.

Legacy

The Isetta's commercial success is widely credited with rescuing BMW from near-bankruptcy in the 1950s, providing the funds that allowed the company to develop the larger cars that secured its future. The design has since become an icon of 1950s motoring, and BMW later paid homage to it with the all-electric Microlino-inspired concepts and the BMW i3's urban philosophy.
