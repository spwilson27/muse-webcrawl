Level (video games)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In video games, a level (also called a map, stage, area, or mission, depending on genre) is a discrete portion of the game world or a segment of gameplay that the player must complete before progressing. Levels typically introduce new challenges, mechanics, enemies, or environments, and completing one usually unlocks access to the next. The organization of a game into levels is one of the oldest structures in game design.

Characteristics

A level provides the space in which gameplay occurs: terrain, obstacles, items, enemies, and an objective or exit condition. In action games a level may end when the player reaches a goal point; in puzzle games, when the puzzle is solved; in many role-playing games the related concept is divided into areas, dungeons, and quests. Difficulty generally increases across a game's sequence of levels, and designers use levels to teach skills progressively — a principle sometimes called "teaching through design" — introducing a mechanic in a safe setting before demanding mastery of it.

Terminology

Different genres and eras favoured different names: arcade and platform games use "stage" or "level"; first-person shooters often use "map" (also used for multiplayer arenas); strategy games use "scenario" or "mission"; driving games use "track." In tabletop and video role-playing games, "level" can also refer to character advancement or to a floor of a dungeon, a meaning inherited from early dungeon-crawl games.

Level design

Level design is a specialized discipline within game development. Level designers build environments using level editors, balancing layout, pacing, lighting, and visual guidance so that players intuitively understand where to go. Many games ship with editors that let players create and share their own levels, a practice that produced influential community works — the tower-defence and MOBA genres both trace their origins to user-created maps for StarCraft and Warcraft III. Procedural generation, in which algorithms assemble levels from components, is used to create effectively unlimited level variety in games such as roguelikes.
