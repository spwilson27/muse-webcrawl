Paper terrorism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Paper terrorism is a neologism referring to the use of false filings, bogus liens, frivolous lawsuits, and other fraudulent or vexatious legal documents to intimidate, harass, or retaliate against public officials, private individuals, and institutions. The term is associated particularly with adherents of the sovereign citizen movement in the United States, though similar tactics are used by other groups and individuals.

Methods

Practitioners file large volumes of documents with courts and recording offices, including fabricated liens against the property of judges, police officers, prosecutors, and private citizens; spurious "arrest warrants" and judgments issued by self-proclaimed courts; and lawsuits demanding enormous damages. Although the filings usually have no legal effect, victims may have to spend time and money having fraudulent liens removed, and court and clerks' offices can be burdened by the volume of material.

History and responses

The tactic emerged in the 1970s and 1980s with tax protester and Posse Comitatus circles and spread with the growth of the sovereign citizen movement in the 1990s and 2000s. The FBI and other law-enforcement bodies have described paper terrorism as a common retaliatory tactic of sovereign citizen extremists.

In response, many U.S. states have enacted statutes authorizing officials to reject or expunge fraudulent filings and imposing penalties — including criminal liability — for filing false liens against public servants. Courts may declare habitual filers vexatious litigants and require prior judicial approval for new filings.
