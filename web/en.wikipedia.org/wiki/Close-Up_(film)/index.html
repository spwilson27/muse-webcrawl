Close-Up (film)

Directed by: Abbas Kiarostami

Written by: Abbas Kiarostami

Starring: Hossain Sabzian, Mohsen Makhmalbaf, Hassan Farazmand

Release date: 1990

Running time: 98 minutes

Country: Iran

Language: Persian

Close-Up (Persian: کلوزآپ، نمای نزدیک, Klūzāp, nemāye nazdīk) is a 1990 Iranian docufiction film written and directed by Abbas Kiarostami. It tells the story of the real-life trial of a man who impersonated the filmmaker Mohsen Makhmalbaf, conning a family into believing they would star in his new film. The film features the people involved, acting as themselves, and blends documentary footage of the trial with re-enactments of the events.

Plot

Hossain Sabzian, a poor, unemployed film lover, is introduced by chance to Mrs. Ahankhah on a bus, where he is reading the published screenplay of Makhmalbaf's The Cyclist. Pretending to be Makhmalbaf himself, Sabzian visits the family repeatedly, flattering them with promises of roles in his supposed new film. Suspicious, the family alerts the authorities, and journalist Hassan Farazmand accompanies police to arrest Sabzian. Kiarostami obtained permission to film the trial, in which Sabzian explains that he acted out of his love of cinema and the respect the role of "director" afforded him. The film ends with Sabzian's release and his meeting with the real Makhmalbaf.

Reception

Close-Up has been met with widespread critical acclaim and is regarded as one of the greatest films of Iranian cinema and of the 1990s. It placed highly in the 2012 Sight & Sound critics' poll of the greatest films of all time and has been praised for its blurring of documentary and fiction, and for its humane treatment of its protagonist.
