Six factor formula

The six factor formula is used in nuclear engineering to determine the multiplication of a nuclear chain reaction in a non-infinite medium. The formula gives the effective neutron multiplication factor keff as the product of six factors: k = η ε p f PFNL PTNL, where η is the reproduction factor, ε the fast fission factor, p the resonance escape probability, f the thermal utilisation factor, and the two final terms are the fast and thermal non-leakage probabilities.

It is an extension of the four factor formula, which treats an infinite medium. The six factor version was developed to model finite reactors, accounting for neutrons that leak from the system. It is closely related to the theory of the nuclear reactor and to neutron transport.

Factors

The reproduction factor η counts neutrons produced per neutron absorbed in fuel; the fast fission factor ε accounts for extra neutrons from fast fission, chiefly in uranium-238; p is the probability a neutron slows to thermal energies without resonance capture; f is the fraction of thermal neutrons absorbed in fuel rather than in moderator or structure; and the non-leakage probabilities account for escape during slowing down and after thermalisation.
