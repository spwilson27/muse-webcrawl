The Toronto Argonauts (officially the Toronto Argonaut Football Club and colloquially known as the Argos) are a professional Canadian football team competing in the East Division of the Canadian Football League (CFL). Based in Toronto, Ontario, the team was founded in 1873 by the Argonaut Rowing Club, from which it takes its name; it is the oldest existing professional sports team in North America still using its original name.

History

The Argonauts played in the Ontario Rugby Football Union and the Interprovincial Rugby Football Union (the "Big Four") before becoming a founding member of the CFL in 1958. The club won its first Grey Cup in 1914 and triumphed regularly through the 1930s and 1940s. After a 31-year drought, Toronto won the famous 1983 Grey Cup, ending decades of frustration.

The team won back-to-back championships in 1996 and 1997 with quarterback Doug Flutie, and surprised the league with a home-soil victory in the 100th Grey Cup in 2012 at the Rogers Centre. Further titles followed in 2017, 2022 (the 109th Grey Cup, beating Winnipeg) and 2024 (the 111th Grey Cup, again over Winnipeg), taking Toronto's total to a league-record 19 Grey Cups.

Stadium

The Argonauts have played at several Toronto venues, including Varsity Stadium, Exhibition Stadium, and the SkyDome/Rogers Centre. Since 2016 the team has played at BMO Field.

Rivalries

The club's fiercest rivalry is with the Hamilton Tiger-Cats, contested in the annual Labour Day Classic. Historic rivalries also exist with the Montreal Alouettes and Ottawa Redblacks.
