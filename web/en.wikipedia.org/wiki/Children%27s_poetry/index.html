Children's poetry

GenrePoetry for young readers
FormsNursery rhymes, nonsense verse, narrative poems
Notable poetsEdward Lear, Lewis Carroll, Shel Silverstein, Roald Dahl, Michael Rosen

Children's poetry is poetry written for, or appropriate for, children. It ranges from traditional nursery rhymes and lullabies to nonsense verse and contemporary collections written specifically for young readers.

History

Early children's verse was heavily didactic, such as Isaac Watts's Divine Songs (1715). In the 19th century, Edward Lear's A Book of Nonsense (1846) and the verse of Lewis Carroll popularized playful, humorous poetry. Robert Louis Stevenson's A Child's Garden of Verses (1885) treated childhood experience seriously and lyrically.

Modern children's poetry

Twentieth-century writers such as Shel Silverstein, Jack Prelutsky, Roald Dahl and Michael Rosen made children's poetry hugely popular, often using humor, rhyme and performance. Poetry collections and anthologies remain a staple of schools and libraries.
