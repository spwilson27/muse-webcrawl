Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics

The Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics was a school of mathematics and astronomy founded by Madhava of Sangamagrama in Kerala, South India, which flourished between the 14th and 16th centuries.

Achievements

Members of the school, including Parameshvara, Nilakantha Somayaji and Jyeshthadeva, developed infinite series expansions for trigonometric functions — including series for π, sine, cosine and arctangent — centuries before their rediscovery in Europe. Nilakantha's Tantrasamgraha (1501) revised the planetary model, and Jyeshthadeva's Yuktibhāṣā (c. 1530) presented proofs and rationales for these results, an early text on the methods of calculus-like reasoning.

Decline and transmission

The school faded in the later 16th century. Whether its results were transmitted to Europe via Jesuit missionaries in Cochin remains debated among historians.
