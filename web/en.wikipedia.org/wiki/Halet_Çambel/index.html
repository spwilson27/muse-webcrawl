Halet Çambel (27 August 1916 – 12 January 2014) was a Turkish archaeologist and Olympic fencer. She was the first Muslim woman to compete in the Olympic Games, representing Turkey in fencing at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, and went on to become one of Turkey's most celebrated archaeologists.

Early life and sport

Çambel was born in Berlin, where her father was serving as a military attaché, and educated in Istanbul and at the Sorbonne in Paris. At the 1936 Olympics she competed in the women's foil event; she famously declined a personal invitation to meet Adolf Hitler on political grounds.

Archaeology

After earning her doctorate at Istanbul University in 1940, Çambel joined the faculty and worked on Hittite studies. In 1946 she began, with Helmuth Theodor Bossert, the excavation of Karatepe, a late Hittite fortress in southern Turkey. The bilingual Phoenician–Hieroglyphic Luwian inscriptions found there provided the key that finally allowed the decipherment of Hieroglyphic Luwian. She established an open-air museum at Karatepe — the first of its kind in Turkey — and campaigned successfully to protect the site from flooding by a proposed dam. Among many honours, she received the Prince Claus Award in 2010 for her lifetime contribution to cultural heritage.
