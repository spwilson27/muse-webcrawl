Code of ethics

A code of ethics is a document adopted by an organisation, profession, or public body that sets out the values, principles, and standards of conduct expected of its members or employees. Codes of ethics may be aspirational (stating ideals), educational (guiding judgement), or regulatory (laying down enforceable rules backed by sanctions). The related term code of conduct usually denotes a more specific, rules-based document governing behaviour in concrete situations, though the two phrases are often used interchangeably.

Professional codes

Professions have maintained ethical codes since antiquity; the Hippocratic Oath, sworn by physicians, is the most famous early example. Modern professional codes include the legal profession's rules of professional conduct (covering confidentiality, conflicts of interest, and duties to the court), engineering codes requiring practitioners to hold paramount the safety and welfare of the public, and journalism ethics codes dealing with accuracy, independence, and minimising harm. Professional bodies typically enforce their codes through disciplinary procedures that can lead to suspension or loss of licence.

Corporate codes

Companies adopt codes of ethics to govern employee behaviour in areas such as bribery and corruption, conflicts of interest, gifts, insider trading, confidentiality, and treatment of colleagues. In the United States, the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002 required publicly traded companies to disclose whether they had adopted a code of ethics for senior financial officers. Corporate codes are usually supplemented by ethics training, compliance departments, and whistleblower hotlines; critics note that a code without enforcement or ethical leadership can become a formality, as in the case of Enron, which had an elaborate code of ethics before its collapse in 2001.

Public sector and research

Governments impose ethical codes on officials and civil servants, addressing gifts, outside employment, lobbying, and the use of public resources. In research, ethical codes and review boards govern the treatment of human and animal subjects, informed consent, fabrication and plagiarism, building on instruments such as the Nuremberg Code, the Declaration of Helsinki, and the Belmont Report.
