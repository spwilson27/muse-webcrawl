Trajan's Column

LocationTrajan's Forum, Rome, Italy
Completed113 CE
Heightc. 30 m (98 ft) shaft; c. 38 m with pedestal
MaterialCarrara marble

Trajan's Column (Italian: Colonna Traiana) is a Roman triumphal column in Rome, Italy, that commemorates Roman emperor Trajan's victory in the Dacian Wars. It was completed in 113 CE and stands in Trajan's Forum, north of the Roman Forum.

Description

The column is famous for its spiral bas relief, which artistically represents the wars between the Romans and Dacians (101–102 and 105–106 CE). The frieze winds 23 times around the shaft and is about 200 metres long, depicting some 155 scenes and roughly 2,500 figures; Trajan himself appears dozens of times. Inside the shaft, a spiral staircase of 185 steps leads to a viewing platform. The inscription on the pedestal, lettered in what became known as Trajan type, is considered a masterpiece of Roman epigraphy.

History

Trajan's ashes were placed in a golden urn in a chamber at the base of the column. The original statue of Trajan atop the column was replaced in 1587 by a bronze statue of St Peter, which still crowns it. The column became the prototype for later commemorative columns in Rome and elsewhere, including the Column of Marcus Aurelius and Nelson's Column in London.
