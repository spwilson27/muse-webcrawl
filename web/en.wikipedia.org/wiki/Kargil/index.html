Kargil is a town in the Indian union territory of Ladakh and the administrative headquarters of Kargil district. It lies on the banks of the Suru River at an elevation of about 2,676 metres (8,780 ft), roughly halfway along National Highway 1 between Srinagar and Leh, and is one of the coldest inhabited places in India, with winter temperatures dropping below −30 °C.

History

Kargil was historically an important trading post on the caravan routes linking Kashmir, Ladakh, Baltistan and Central Asia. The population is predominantly Shia Muslim, with cultural ties to Baltistan. The name is said to derive from words meaning a place between many forts, or, in local tradition, from "Gar" and "Khil"—between kingdoms.

The town gave its name to the Kargil War of 1999, when Pakistani soldiers and militants occupied strategic heights across the Line of Control in the Kargil sector; India recaptured the positions after heavy fighting, much of it around the nearby town of Drass and features such as Tololing and Tiger Hill.

Geography and economy

Kargil district is known for apricots and for the Suru Valley, which leads south to the Zanskar region over the Pensi La pass. The town is a staging point for treks into Zanskar and for visits to the giant rock-cut Maitreya Buddha statues at Mulbekh and Kartse Khar.
