Grettis saga

Grettis saga Ásmundarsonar
LanguageOld Icelandic
DateEarly 14th century (events c. 1000)
GenreSaga of Icelanders (Íslendingasaga)

Grettis saga Ásmundarsonar (Old Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈɡrɛhtɪs ˈsaɣa ˈausˌmʏntarˌsɔnar]), also known as Grettla or the Saga of Grettir the Strong, is one of the sagas of Icelanders. It tells the life of Grettir Ásmundarson, an Icelandic outlaw of extraordinary strength and ill fortune.

Contents

The saga traces Grettir from his wilful childhood through his youthful adventures in Norway and Iceland — including his wrestling with the revenant Glámr, whose curse condemns Grettir to fear darkness and to never grow stronger — to his outlawry. Sentenced to full outlawry (skóggangur) for a killing, Grettir survives nearly twenty years as a fugitive, longer than any other Icelandic outlaw, hiding in remote parts of the country and performing feats of strength.

His final years are spent on the island fortress of Drangey in Skagafjörður with his brother Illugi and the servant Glaumr. There he is overcome by illness brought on by a witch's curse laid into a piece of driftwood, and is killed by his enemies, led by Þorbjörn öngull, in 1031. A shorter sequel, Spes þáttr, tells of the vengeance taken for him in Constantinople.

Authorship and influence

The saga was probably composed in the early fourteenth century; its final redaction is sometimes attributed to Sturla Þórðarson. It draws on oral tradition, earlier written works and the lost saga of Grettir's ancestor. It remains one of the most beloved of the Icelandic sagas and has influenced later literature, including works by J.R.R. Tolkien.
