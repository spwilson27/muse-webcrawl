Phryne


Phryne (c. 371 – after 316 BC) was a famous ancient Greek hetaira (courtesan) of Athens, celebrated for her beauty and wealth, and the model for Praxiteles' Aphrodite of Knidos.

Phryne (Ancient Greek: Φρύνη, "toad"; born Mnesarete, c. 371 BC) was an ancient Greek hetaira, one of the most celebrated courtesans of classical Athens. Born at Thespiae in Boeotia, she amassed such wealth that she is said to have offered to rebuild the walls of Thebes (destroyed by Alexander the Great) on condition that they bear the inscription "Destroyed by Alexander, restored by Phryne the hetaira" — an offer the Thebans declined.

Model for Aphrodite

Phryne is famed as the model for the sculptor Praxiteles, her lover, whose Aphrodite of Knidos (c. 350 BC) was the first monumental female nude in Greek art; she also posed for the painter Apelles' Aphrodite Anadyomene. Ancient sources describe her beauty as so great that judges and artists were struck dumb by it.

Trial

Put on trial for impiety (asebeia) — in some accounts a capital charge — Phryne was defended by the orator Hypereides, also her lover. According to the celebrated anecdote (preserved by Athenaeus and Pseudo-Plutarch), when the speech seemed to fail, Hypereides bared her breast before the judges, who, in awe of her beauty as a "priestess of Aphrodite", acquitted her. The story is likely embellished, but it became a favourite subject of later art, notably Jean-Léon Gérôme's 1861 painting Phryne before the Areopagus.
