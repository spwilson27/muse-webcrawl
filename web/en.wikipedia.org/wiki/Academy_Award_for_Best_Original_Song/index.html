Academy Award for Best Original Song

The Academy Award for Best Original Song is one of the awards given annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) to the songwriter(s) of the best song written specifically for a film. It was introduced at the 7th Academy Awards in 1934, when it was won by "The Continental" from The Gay Divorcee.

Rules

To be eligible, a song must consist of words and music, both of which must be original and written specifically for the film. It must be used within the film itself or as the first piece of music in the end credits. The award is presented to the songwriters, not to the performers, unless the performer also received songwriting credit.

Notable winners

Famous winning songs include "Over the Rainbow" from The Wizard of Oz (1939), "Moon River" from Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), "My Heart Will Go On" from Titanic (1997), "Lose Yourself" from 8 Mile (2002) and "Shallow" from A Star Is Born (2018). Lyricist Sammy Cahn won the award four times, as did composer Alan Menken, who holds the record for the most wins in the category by a composer.

Performances

Nominated songs are traditionally performed live during the Academy Awards ceremony, a practice that has made the category one of the most visible parts of the broadcast.
