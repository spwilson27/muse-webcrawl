Amboseli National Park, formerly Maasai Amboseli Game Reserve, is a national park in Kajiado County, Kenya, near the border with Tanzania. The park is famous for its large elephant herds and for its views of Mount Kilimanjaro, the highest free-standing mountain in the world, which rises just across the border in Tanzania.

Geography and ecology

The park protects part of the Amboseli ecosystem, a semi-arid landscape of savanna, woodland, and swamps fed by underground water from Kilimanjaro's melting snows. The dried-up bed of Lake Amboseli covers much of the park. Permanent swamps such as Enkongo Narok support elephants, buffalo, hippos, and abundant birdlife.

Wildlife

Amboseli is one of the best places in Africa to view large herds of African bush elephants at close range, and its elephant population has been studied for decades by researchers including Cynthia Moss and the Amboseli Elephant Research Project, founded in 1972. Other wildlife includes lions, cheetahs, giraffes, zebras, wildebeest, and over 400 bird species.

History

The area was set aside as the Southern Reserve for the Maasai in 1906 and became a game reserve in 1948. It was declared a national park in 1974. In 1991 it was designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
