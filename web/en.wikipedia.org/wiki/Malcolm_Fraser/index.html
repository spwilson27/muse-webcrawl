John Malcolm Fraser (21 May 1930 – 20 March 2015) was an Australian politician who served as the 22nd prime minister of Australia from 1975 to 1983. He led the Liberal Party and came to power following the dismissal of Gough Whitlam's Labor government by Governor-General Sir John Kerr in the 1975 constitutional crisis.

Early life

Fraser was born in Toorak, Melbourne, into a wealthy pastoral family of Scottish descent, and educated at Melbourne Grammar School and Magdalen College, Oxford, where he read philosophy, politics and economics. He was first elected to the House of Representatives for the Division of Wannon in 1955, aged 25, and held ministerial office under Harold Holt, John Gorton and William McMahon, including as Minister for Defence.

Prime ministership

After challenging Billy Snedden for the Liberal leadership in March 1975, Fraser blocked supply in the Senate, precipitating the crisis that ended with Whitlam's dismissal on 11 November 1975; Fraser was installed as caretaker prime minister and won the ensuing elections of 1975, 1977 and 1980 by large margins. His government pursued economic restraint, established the Special Broadcasting Service (SBS) and the Aboriginal land rights legislation for the Northern Territory, supported multiculturalism, and took a strong stance against apartheid and on Rhodesia/Zimbabwe independence. He lost the 1983 election to Bob Hawke's Labor Party and resigned the leadership and then parliament.

Later life

In later years Fraser became a prominent elder statesman and human-rights commentator, at times sharply critical of his own party's direction; he resigned his Liberal Party membership in 2009. He died in Melbourne in 2015 and was given a state funeral.
