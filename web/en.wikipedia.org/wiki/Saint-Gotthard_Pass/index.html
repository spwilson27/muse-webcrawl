Saint-Gotthard Pass

The Saint-Gotthard Pass (German: Gotthardpass; Italian: Passo del San Gottardo) is a mountain pass in the Swiss Alps at an elevation of 2,106 metres (6,909 ft), traversing the Saint-Gotthard Massif and connecting northern and southern Switzerland between Göschenen in the canton of Uri and Airolo in Ticino. It has been one of the most important north–south routes across the Alps since the Middle Ages.

The Tremola road climbing the Saint-Gotthard Pass.

History

Although known in Roman times, the pass became a major trade route only after the construction of bridges across the Schöllenen Gorge — including the Devil's Bridge — in the early 13th century. A hospice at the summit, run by Capuchin monks, sheltered travellers for centuries. The famous cobbled Tremola road on the southern side, with its tight hairpin bends, dates from the 19th century.

Tunnels

The pass is paralleled by three of the world's great transport tunnels: the Gotthard railway tunnel (opened 1882), the Gotthard Road Tunnel (1980), and the Gotthard Base Tunnel (2016), at 57 kilometres the world's longest railway tunnel. The summit road remains open seasonally and is a popular route for motorists and cyclists.
