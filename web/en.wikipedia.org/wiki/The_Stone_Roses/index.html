The Stone Roses

OriginManchester, England
Formed1983
MembersIan Brown, John Squire, Mani (Gary Mounfield), Reni (Alan Wren)
GenresMadchester, alternative rock, indie rock

The Stone Roses were an English rock band formed in Manchester in 1983. One of the pioneering groups of the Madchester movement of the late 1980s and early 1990s, the band's classic line-up consisted of vocalist Ian Brown, guitarist John Squire, bassist Mani and drummer Reni.

Debut album and peak

The band released their debut album, The Stone Roses, in 1989, to widespread critical acclaim. With songs such as "Waterfall", "Made of Stone", "She Bangs the Drums" and the epic "I Am the Resurrection", the album fused 1960s-influenced guitar pop with the dance rhythms of the emerging rave culture and came to be regarded as one of the greatest British albums of all time. A celebrated open-air concert at Spike Island in May 1990 drew some 27,000 fans and cemented the band's legend.

Second Coming and breakup

Legal battles with their label Silvertone delayed the follow-up. The second album, Second Coming (1994), leaned toward blues rock and received mixed reviews. Reni left in 1995 and Squire in 1996; the band dissolved after a disastrous Reading Festival performance in August 1996.

Reunion

The classic line-up reunited in 2011, announcing a world tour headlined by three homecoming shows at Manchester's Heaton Park in 2012, then among the fastest-selling rock concerts in UK history. New singles "All for One" and "Beautiful Thing" followed in 2016 before the band again became inactive, with a final show in Glasgow in 2017 widely treated as farewell.
