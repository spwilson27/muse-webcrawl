Sharecropping

Sharecropping is a system of agriculture in which a landowner allows a tenant to farm the land in return for a share of the crops produced. It has existed worldwide in various forms and became widespread in the Southern United States after the Civil War.

History in the United States

After emancipation, freed African Americans and poor whites often lacked land, tools and credit. Landowners supplied land, housing, seed and equipment — often on credit at high interest through crop-lien systems — while sharecroppers supplied labour and received typically half the crop. Debt frequently bound families to the land in conditions critics likened to peonage.

Decline

Mechanisation, the Great Migration, and New Deal-era changes largely ended US sharecropping by the mid-20th century, though share tenancy persists elsewhere in the world.
