National String Instrument Corporation

Founded: 1927, Los Angeles, California
Founders: John Dopyera, George Beauchamp, Adolph Rickenbacker
Products: Resonator guitars, lap steels, mandolins
Fate: Merged into Valco (1940s)

The National String Instrument Corporation was an American manufacturer of stringed instruments, famous for inventing and popularising the resonator guitar. It was founded in Los Angeles in 1927 by Slovak immigrant luthier John Dopyera, his business partner George Beauchamp, and Adolph Rickenbacker.

The resonator guitar

Beauchamp, a vaudeville steel guitarist, wanted a louder guitar that could be heard over dance bands before electric amplification existed. Dopyera designed a guitar with spun aluminium resonator cones — modelled on a loudspeaker — inside a metal body. The first instruments, the Tricone models (1927), used three small cones; cheaper single-cone "biscuit bridge" models followed. National's gleaming metal-bodied guitars became iconic in blues, Hawaiian and country music.

Later history

Dopyera left in 1929 after disputes and, with his brothers, founded Dobro, a direct competitor. National moved into electric instruments in the 1930s and during the Second World War merged with Dobro and the Chicago manufacturer Supro to form Valco, which made guitars under the National name into the 1960s. Vintage National resonator guitars are highly collectable; a modern company, National Reso-Phonic Guitars (founded 1989), revives the designs.
