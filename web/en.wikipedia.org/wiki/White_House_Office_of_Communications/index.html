White House Office of Communications

The White House Office of Communications is a unit of the Executive Office of the President of the United States responsible for developing and disseminating the administration's message and communicating it to the media and the public. It is headed by the White House communications director, a title distinct from the White House press secretary, who works closely with the office.

History

The office was created by President Richard Nixon in 1969, with journalist Herbert G. Klein as its first director. It consolidated media outreach, speechwriting coordination and long-range communications planning, functions previously scattered among White House staff. Successive administrations have expanded the office to encompass the press office, speechwriting, media affairs, and, from the 2000s, digital strategy.

Functions

The office plans the president's communications calendar, coordinates announcements across federal agencies, supervises the speechwriting staff and organises media events, while the press secretary conducts the daily briefings and serves as the administration's on-camera spokesperson.
