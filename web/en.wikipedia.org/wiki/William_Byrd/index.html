William Byrd

William Byrd (c. 1540 – 4 July 1623) was an English composer of the Renaissance. He is widely regarded as among the greatest composers of his era, producing sacred and secular polyphony, keyboard music and consort music of exceptional quality, and he was a pupil of Thomas Tallis.

Life

Byrd sang in the Chapel Royal under Mary I and became organist of Lincoln Cathedral in 1563. In 1572 he became a Gentleman of the Chapel Royal, sharing the organist post with Tallis. In 1575 Elizabeth I granted Tallis and Byrd a monopoly on music printing; their Cantiones Sacrae of that year was the first publication under the patent.

Despite remaining a Catholic in Protestant England — and repeatedly fined as a recusant — Byrd enjoyed royal protection. He composed Anglican services and anthems as well as Latin Masses (including the Masses for three, four and five voices) and the two books of Gradualia for the Catholic community.

Works and legacy

His output includes motets, madrigals in the Psalmes, Sonets and Songs (1588), virginal music in My Ladye Nevells Booke and the Fitzwilliam Virginal Book, and fantasias for viol consort. Byrd taught a generation of composers including Thomas Morley, Peter Philips and Thomas Tomkins.
