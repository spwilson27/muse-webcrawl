Georges de La Trémoille

Georges de La Trémoille (c. 1382 – 6 May 1446) was Count of Guînes and a powerful French courtier during the reign of Charles VII of France. A member of the La Trémoille family, he rose to become Grand Chamberlain of France and the king's chief favourite, dominating the court during the later phase of the Hundred Years' War.

Rise to power

La Trémoille accumulated great wealth and influence through advantageous marriages and royal favour. After the fall of Pierre de Giac and the execution of Le Camus de Beaulieu, he became the king's principal adviser in 1427. He used his position to enrich himself and his family, and became notorious for his intrigues against the Constable Arthur de Richemont and other rivals at court.

Joan of Arc

La Trémoille is remembered in the story of Joan of Arc as one of the courtiers most hostile to her influence. He sought to limit her role, favoured negotiations and truces with Burgundy over Joan's aggressive strategy, and was accused by her supporters of undermining the campaigns of 1429–30. After Joan's capture he did nothing to secure her ransom or release.

Fall and later life

In 1433 La Trémoille was overthrown in a coup organised by his enemies, wounded and briefly imprisoned; he was forced from court. He retired to his estates at Sully-sur-Loire, from where he continued to scheme and was implicated in later plots, including the Praguerie of 1440. He died in 1446.
