The River Teith is a river in Scotland, a tributary of the River Forth. It is formed at Callander by the confluence of two headstreams: the Garbh Uisge, flowing from Loch Venachar, and the Eas Gobhain, flowing from Loch Lubnaig.

Course

From Callander the Teith flows south-east through the broad valley of Strathgartney, passing the village of Deanston — where a cotton mill once powered by the river now houses a whisky distillery — and the burgh of Doune, famous for Doune Castle. It is joined by the Keltie Water and the Ardoch Burn before meeting the Forth near Drip, a few miles west of Stirling.

Fishing and wildlife

The Teith is noted for its runs of Atlantic salmon and sea trout, and is regarded as one of the Forth system's most productive salmon rivers. The river valley also supports otters, dippers, and goosanders. The Callander to Doune stretch follows the route of a former railway, now part of National Cycle Route 7.

History

The river's name is of uncertain origin, possibly from a Gaelic or Brittonic word associated with quietness or warmth. The crossing of the Teith at Doune and the nearby Roman fort of Bochastle — part of the Gask Ridge frontier — testify to its longstanding importance as a route into the Highlands.
