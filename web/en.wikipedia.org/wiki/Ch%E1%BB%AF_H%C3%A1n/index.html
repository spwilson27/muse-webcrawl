Chữ Hán

Chữ Hán (𡨸漢, "Han characters") are the Chinese characters used to write Classical Chinese and, historically, formal Vietnamese. For more than a thousand years, while Vietnam was under Chinese rule and long after independence, chữ Hán was the script of government, scholarship, and literature; Vietnamese Confucian scholars composed official documents, histories, and poetry in Literary Chinese written with these characters.

History and use

Alongside chữ Hán, Vietnamese scholars developed chữ Nôm, a script adapting Chinese characters to write the Vietnamese vernacular. Chữ Hán remained dominant in official writing until the early 20th century, when the Latin-based Vietnamese alphabet (chữ Quốc ngữ) was promoted under French colonial rule and eventually became standard. Today chữ Hán survives mainly in calligraphy, temple inscriptions, traditional festivals, and scholarly study of pre-modern texts.
