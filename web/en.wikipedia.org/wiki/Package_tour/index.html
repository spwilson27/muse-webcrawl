Package tour

A package tour, or package holiday, is a vacation arranged by a tour operator in which transport and accommodation — and often other elements such as meals, transfers, excursions and insurance — are sold together at a single inclusive price.

History

Thomas Cook is credited with pioneering organised tours in the 1840s and 1850s. The modern package holiday began after the Second World War: Vladimir Raitz's Horizon Holidays offered the first air-based package trips from Britain to Corsica in 1950, and through the 1960s and 1970s charter flights and new Mediterranean resorts made foreign holidays affordable to mass markets in northern Europe.

Characteristics and regulation

Operators bulk-buy airline seats and hotel rooms, achieving prices below those available to individual travellers. In the European Union the Package Travel Directive gives consumers rights to information, price protection and repatriation if an operator fails; in the United Kingdom, ATOL protection covers air packages. Dynamic packaging, in which customers assemble their own combinations online, has blurred the boundary with independent travel since the 2000s.
