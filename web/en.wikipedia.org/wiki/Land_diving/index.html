Land diving (Naghol)

Land diving from a tower on Pentecost Island

LocationPentecost Island, Vanuatu
SeasonApril–June (yam harvest)
Tower height20–30 metres

Land diving (Sa: naghol, or nanggol) is a ritual performed by the men of the southern part of Pentecost Island, Vanuatu. Divers leap from tall wooden towers, some 20 to 30 metres (66 to 98 ft) high, with vines tied to their ankles as a kind of safety rope. The ritual is the precursor of modern bungee jumping.

Land diving is performed each year during the yam harvest season (roughly April to June). For the islanders the ritual is bound up with ensuring a good yam harvest and with demonstrating masculinity and courage; the ideal dive brings the diver's shoulders or head close enough to brush the specially tilled earth.

Description

A tower of wooden scaffolding is built in the village clearing, its front supported by lopped trees. Each diver has two vines selected and measured to the height of his platform; the vines are elastic when freshly cut, and getting their length right is a matter of expert local knowledge. The ground below is dug up to soften it. Divers jump head-first in silence, to the singing and stamping of men below, while women and children watch from behind.

Boys may begin diving from low platforms as a rite of passage; injuries, though not unknown, are rare, and the ritual has been held annually for centuries by local tradition.

Origin legend

Local legend tells of a woman who fled an abusive husband by climbing a banyan tree; when he followed her up and jumped after her as she leapt, she survived because she had secretly tied vines to her ankles, while he fell to his death. The dives are said to commemorate the story and to ward off its repetition.

Influence on bungee jumping

Footage of land diving shown in David Attenborough's BBC documentaries brought the ritual to world attention, and in 1979 members of the Oxford University Dangerous Sports Club cited the Pentecost dives as inspiration for the first modern bungee jumps from the Clifton Suspension Bridge in Bristol.
