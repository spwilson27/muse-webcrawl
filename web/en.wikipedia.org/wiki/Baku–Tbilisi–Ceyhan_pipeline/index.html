The Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan (BTC) pipeline is a 1,768-kilometre crude oil pipeline from the Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli oil field in the Caspian Sea to the Mediterranean Sea. It connects the Sangachal Terminal near Baku, Azerbaijan, via Tbilisi, Georgia, to the Ceyhan marine terminal on Turkey's Mediterranean coast, and was the second-longest oil pipeline in the former Soviet sphere when completed.

History

Conceived in the 1990s as a route for Caspian oil that would bypass both Russia and Iran, the project was strongly supported by the United States and Turkey. The intergovernmental agreements were signed in Istanbul in 1999; construction began in 2003 and the line was filled with oil in 2005. The first tanker loaded at Ceyhan in June 2006, and the pipeline was formally inaugurated on 13 July 2006. It cost approximately US$4 billion.

Route and operation

The pipeline crosses mountainous terrain in the Caucasus, reaching elevations above 2,800 metres, and passes through Georgia's Borjomi region, prompting environmental controversy and extensive mitigation measures. Eight pumping stations move crude along the route. Operator BP leads the BTC Pipeline Company consortium, whose shareholders include SOCAR, Chevron, and others. Capacity is about one million barrels per day; in addition to Azerbaijani crude, the line has carried Kazakh and Turkmen oil delivered across the Caspian by tanker.

Significance

BTC gave Azerbaijan a direct export route to world markets independent of Russian pipelines, strengthening the economies of the transit states through transit fees and anchoring the "East–West energy corridor" together with the South Caucasus gas pipeline. In August 2008 an explosion and fire on the Turkish section temporarily shut the line, an attack claimed by Kurdish separatists.
