Toroidal inductors and transformers

Toroidal inductors and transformers are passive electronic components whose windings are wrapped around a closed ring-shaped (torus, or doughnut) magnetic core of ferrite, powdered iron or laminated steel. The closed magnetic path confines nearly all the flux inside the core.

Advantages and disadvantages

Because the core forms a complete loop with no air gap (unless deliberately introduced), toroidal devices achieve high inductance per volume, low stray magnetic field and low electromagnetic interference, and they radiate and pick up little hum — making them prized in audio equipment, medical devices and precision instrumentation. Their drawbacks are higher winding cost (wire must be threaded through the ring, usually by shuttle machines), difficulty of adding an adjustable gap, and higher inter-winding capacitance in some designs.

Construction and calculation

Inductance scales with the square of the number of turns and with the core's cross-section and permeability, divided by the mean path length around the ring. Cores are often coated with epoxy or parylene for insulation. Toroidal transformers commonly serve as mains transformers in high-end amplifiers and as current transformers clamped around conductors.
