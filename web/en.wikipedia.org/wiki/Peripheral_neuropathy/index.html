Peripheral neuropathy is damage to, or disease affecting, the nerves of the peripheral nervous system, which may impair sensation, movement, gland or organ function depending on which nerve fibres are involved. It most often affects the longest nerves first, producing symptoms in the feet and hands in a characteristic "stocking-glove" pattern.

Signs and symptoms

Sensory symptoms include numbness, tingling ("pins and needles"), burning or shooting neuropathic pain, and loss of vibration and position sense, which can impair balance. Motor involvement causes weakness, cramps, fasciculations and muscle wasting. Autonomic involvement may cause orthostatic hypotension, digestive disturbance, bladder dysfunction and abnormal sweating.

Classification

Neuropathies are classified by the number and distribution of nerves affected — mononeuropathy (one nerve, e.g. carpal tunnel syndrome), mononeuritis multiplex (several individual nerves), or polyneuropathy (diffuse, length-dependent) — and by the fibre type involved and the underlying pathology: axonal degeneration or demyelination, distinguished by nerve conduction studies and electromyography.

Causes

Diabetes mellitus is the commonest cause in developed countries. Other causes include chronic alcohol use, vitamin B12 deficiency, hypothyroidism, kidney failure, autoimmune diseases (Guillain–Barré syndrome, CIDP, vasculitis), infections (HIV, leprosy, Lyme disease, shingles), hereditary disorders (Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease), toxins, and many drugs — notably chemotherapy agents such as vincristine and platinum compounds. In a substantial minority of cases no cause is found (idiopathic).

Treatment

Management targets the underlying cause where possible (glycaemic control, B12 replacement, stopping an offending drug). Neuropathic pain is treated with duloxetine, pregabalin, gabapentin or tricyclic antidepressants rather than simple analgesics. Physiotherapy, foot care (especially in diabetes, to prevent ulceration and amputation) and occupational therapy help preserve function.
