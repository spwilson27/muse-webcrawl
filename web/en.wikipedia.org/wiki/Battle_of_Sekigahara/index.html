The Battle of Sekigahara, fought on 21 October 1600 in present-day Gifu Prefecture, was the decisive battle of Japan's Sengoku period. Tokugawa Ieyasu's Eastern Army destroyed the Western Army loyal to the Toyotomi heir, clearing Ieyasu's path to found the Tokugawa shogunate three years later; Japanese tradition calls it the realm-dividing battle (tenka wakeme no tatakai).

Background

On the death of Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1598, government devolved to five regents for his infant son Hideyori. Ieyasu's growing power alarmed the administrator Ishida Mitsunari, who in the summer of 1600 raised the western daimyo in the Toyotomi name while Ieyasu was campaigning in the east. Ieyasu turned back, and the armies converged on the strategic pass town of Sekigahara after Ieyasu's vanguard seized Gifu Castle and Mitsunari's allies failed to hold the Nakasendō road.

The battle

The armies deployed in fog on the morning of 21 October. The fighting opened with a charge by Fukushima Masanori and Ii Naomasa's "Red Devils" against Ukita Hideie. Though the Western Army was numerically stronger and held high ground, much of it never engaged: the Mōri and Chōsokabe contingents stayed inactive, and in the afternoon the battle turned when Kobayakawa Hideaki — who had secretly promised Ieyasu his support — charged down Mount Matsuo into the flank of Ōtani Yoshitsugu, triggering a cascade of defections. By late afternoon the Western Army had collapsed; Mitsunari fled and was captured, and was executed in Kyoto on 6 November.

Aftermath

Ieyasu redistributed the lands of 87 defeated houses among his followers, reduced the Toyotomi to an ordinary daimyo house in Osaka, and in 1603 accepted the title of shōgun, inaugurating over 260 years of Tokugawa rule.
