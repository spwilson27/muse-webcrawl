The Second City

Address1616 N. Wells Street, Chicago, Illinois
TypeImprovisational comedy theatre and training centre
OpenedDecember 16, 1959

The Second City is an improvisational comedy enterprise based in Chicago, best known as the first ever ongoing improvisational theatre troupe in the United States. Founded on December 16, 1959, by Paul Sills, Howard Alk and Bernie Sahlins, it grew out of the Compass Players and the Playwrights Theatre Club at the University of Chicago.

History and influence

The troupe pioneered the sketch-comedy revue format built from audience suggestions and improvisational games developed by Viola Spolin. Its name derives from a dismissive 1952 New Yorker article by A. J. Liebling about Chicago, the "second city" to New York; the company embraced the label ironically.

The Second City has launched the careers of many of North America's most successful comedians, including Alan Alda, John Belushi, Dan Aykroyd, Gilda Radner, Bill Murray, Mike Myers, Tina Fey, Stephen Colbert, Steve Carell, Amy Poehler, Keegan-Michael Key and Jordan Peele. A Toronto company, founded in 1973, produced the television series SCTV. The company's training centre is the largest school of improvisation and sketch comedy in the world.
