Great Yarmouth

Great Yarmouth, often known simply as Yarmouth, is a seaside town and borough in Norfolk, England. It is located at the mouth of the River Yare, on the North Sea coast, about 20 miles (32 km) east of Norwich. The town has been a seaside resort since 1760 and is the gateway to the Broads from the sea. Historically it was a major fishing port, famous for herring, and its economy now depends on tourism and service to the offshore oil, gas, and renewable energy industries.

History

A settlement grew near the Roman fort of Gariannonum at Burgh Castle. Great Yarmouth received a charter from King John in 1208. Its medieval town walls, built from the 13th century, survive in large part, and the town retains a distinctive grid of narrow lanes known as "Rows." For centuries the herring fishery dominated; in its early-20th-century peak, huge autumn fleets of Scottish fisher girls followed the herring south to Yarmouth to gut and pack the catch. The town suffered bombing in both world wars and severe flooding in the North Sea flood of 1953.

Landmarks and economy

St Nicholas Minster, founded in 1101, is one of the largest parish churches in England. Other landmarks include the Britannia Pier, the Wellington Pier and Winter Gardens, the Time and Tide Museum (housed in a Victorian herring curing works), and the Nelson Monument (Britannia Monument) of 1819. The town's two piers and seafront "Golden Mile" anchor its tourism trade. Since the 1960s Great Yarmouth has served North Sea oil and gas; its outer harbour, opened in 2009, supports offshore wind projects in the southern North Sea.
