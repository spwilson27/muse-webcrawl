Fractionation

Fractionation is a separation process in which a certain quantity of a mixture (of gases, liquids or solids) is divided during a phase transition into a number of smaller quantities (fractions) in which the composition varies according to a gradient. Fractions are collected based on differences in a specific property of the individual components.

Methods

Common fractionation techniques include fractional distillation, in which components are separated by boiling point; fractional crystallization, based on solubility; and fractionation of natural oils and fats by controlled cooling, as in the production of cocoa butter equivalents and palm oil fractions (olein and stearin).

Applications

Fractionation is fundamental to petroleum refining, where crude oil is separated into fuels and feedstocks. In medicine, blood fractionation splits donated blood plasma into products such as albumin, immunoglobulins and clotting factors (the Cohn process). Isotope fractionation describes the enrichment of one isotope relative to another by physical, chemical or biological processes, which geochemists exploit as a tracer. In radiotherapy, fractionation refers to dividing a total radiation dose into smaller doses given over time.
