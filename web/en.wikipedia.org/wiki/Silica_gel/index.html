Silica gel

Silica gel is an amorphous and porous form of silicon dioxide (silica) consisting of irregular granules with nanoscale pores. Despite its name it is a hard solid, not a gel in the everyday sense. It is widely used as a desiccant — a substance that absorbs moisture from its surroundings.

Properties

Silica gel has a very high internal surface area — around 800 square metres per gram — and can adsorb up to about 40% of its own weight in water. It can be regenerated by heating to about 120 °C. The familiar small packets labelled "do not eat" are included in packaging for electronics, leather goods, pharmaceuticals and foods to control humidity.

Other uses

Silica gel is used as a stationary phase in chromatography, in cat litter, as a food additive (anti-caking agent), and in museum and archive storage to stabilise relative humidity. Some varieties are treated with a moisture indicator, such as cobalt(II) chloride, that changes colour as the gel becomes saturated.
