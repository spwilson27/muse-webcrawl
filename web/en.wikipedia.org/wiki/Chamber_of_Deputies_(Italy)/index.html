Chamber of Deputies (Italy)

The hemicycle of the Chamber of Deputies in Palazzo Montecitorio, Rome.

The Chamber of Deputies (Italian: Camera dei Deputati) is the lower house of the bicameral Italian Parliament, the upper house being the Senate of the Republic. Under Italy's system of "perfect bicameralism", the two houses have equal powers: legislation must be approved in identical form by both, and the government must hold the confidence of both. The Chamber meets in the Palazzo Montecitorio in Rome.

Composition

Following a constitutional reform approved by referendum in 2020, the Chamber has 400 deputies (reduced from 630), first elected at the 2022 general election. Of these, 392 are elected in Italy and 8 by Italians residing abroad. Deputies are elected under a mixed system (the Rosatellum): roughly three-eighths of seats by first-past-the-post in single-member districts and the remainder proportionally.

History

The Chamber descends from the Chamber of Deputies of the Kingdom of Sardinia created under the Statuto Albertino of 1848, which became the lower house of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861 and moved to Montecitorio in 1871. During the Fascist period it was replaced in 1939 by the appointed Chamber of Fasci and Corporations. The present Chamber dates from the republican constitution of 1948, which first elected women as deputies and set the chamber's size and powers. The oldest deputy traditionally presides at the first sitting until a President of the Chamber is elected.

Functions

Beyond legislation and confidence, the Chamber elects, together with the Senate and regional delegates, the President of the Republic; it can bring impeachment charges against the President before the Constitutional Court; and its President is one of the highest offices of the state. Bills may originate in either house and shuttle between them until an identical text is agreed.
