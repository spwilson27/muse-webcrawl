Indonesian cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An Indonesian rijsttafel-style spread with satay and rendang]

The cuisine of Indonesia spans more than 6,000 inhabited islands and hundreds of ethnic groups, unified by rice, coconut, chillies, peanuts, fermented shrimp paste (terasi) and spice pastes (bumbu). Signature dishes include nasi goreng (fried rice, often called the national dish), satay (sate) of skewered grilled meats with peanut sauce, rendang — the slow-braised spiced beef of the Minangkabau, voted top of CNN reader polls of world foods — soto soups, gado-gado vegetable salad with peanut dressing, and tempeh, the fermented soybean cake invented in Java.

Trade made the archipelago the original Spice Islands; Indian, Chinese, Arab, Portuguese and Dutch (rijsttafel) influences layered onto indigenous cooking. Meals are usually eaten with spoon and fork, or by hand, and sambal chilli condiments — hundreds of kinds — accompany everything.

1. Regional note

Regional note

Sumatran food is rich and fiery; Javanese food is sweeter; Balinese food uses base gede spice paste and lawar; Sulawesi and Papua cook with sago and seafood. Pork appears in Hindu Bali and Christian regions, while most of the country eats halal.
