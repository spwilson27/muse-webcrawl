The Alcmaeonids (Greek: Ἀλκμαιωνίδαι) were a powerful aristocratic family of ancient Athens, descended from Alcmaeon, the great-grandson of Nestor. For two centuries they were among the most prominent and controversial families in Athenian politics, remembered both for a hereditary curse and for founding Athenian democracy.

The curse

Around 632 BC, Megacles, archon of Athens, was held responsible for the killing of the supporters of Cylon, who had sought sanctuary at an altar of Athena. The sacrilege brought a curse (agos) on the family, which their enemies — notably Sparta — invoked for generations to demand the expulsion of Alcmaeonid descendants, a charge still raised against Pericles on the eve of the Peloponnesian War.

Exile, wealth and Delphi

The Alcmaeonids opposed the tyrant Peisistratus and spent much of the 6th century BC in exile, amassing wealth abroad. While contracted to rebuild the Temple of Apollo at Delphi after a fire, they completed the facade in expensive Parian marble; tradition held that they used their influence to persuade the Delphic oracle to urge Sparta to liberate Athens from the Peisistratid tyranny, which Sparta did in 510 BC.

Cleisthenes and democracy

After the tyranny fell, the Alcmaeonid Cleisthenes outmanoeuvred his rival Isagoras by allying with the Athenian people, and in 508/7 BC enacted the reforms that created the democratic constitution — reorganising citizens into ten new tribes and founding the Council of Five Hundred. The family's descendants through the female line included Pericles and the flamboyant Alcibiades.
