Sanctus

The Sanctus (Latin: "Holy") is a hymn of praise sung or recited in Christian liturgy, forming part of the Eucharistic Prayer (anaphora) in both Eastern and Western traditions. Its text combines the song of the seraphim in Isaiah 6:3 — "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory" — with the acclamation of the crowds at Christ's entry into Jerusalem from Matthew 21:9, "Hosanna in the highest. Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord."

Text

The Latin text reads:

Sanctus, Sanctus, Sanctus
Dominus Deus Sabaoth.
Pleni sunt caeli et terra gloria tua.
Hosanna in excelsis.
Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini.
Hosanna in excelsis.

In English: "Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of hosts. Heaven and earth are full of your glory. Hosanna in the highest. Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord. Hosanna in the highest." The portion beginning Benedictus is sometimes treated as a separate movement in musical settings.

Liturgical use

In the Roman Rite Mass the Sanctus follows the Preface and introduces the Canon (Eucharistic Prayer). In Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox liturgies it appears as the epinikios hymn ("victory hymn"). Anglican, Lutheran, and many other Western liturgies retain it in the same position.

Musical settings

As one of the fixed texts of the Mass Ordinary, the Sanctus has been set to music by countless composers, from Gregorian chant and Renaissance polyphony (Palestrina, Byrd) to the great concert masses of Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, and Verdi's Requiem. In chant, numerous melodies survive in the Liber Usualis.
