Pinocchio is a 1940 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney and based on the 1883 Italian children's novel The Adventures of Pinocchio by Carlo Collodi. It was the second animated feature film produced by Disney, made after the success of Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937).

Pinocchio with Jiminy Cricket, his appointed conscience

Plot

A kindly woodcarver named Geppetto creates a wooden puppet, Pinocchio, and wishes upon a star that he might become a real boy. The Blue Fairy gives the puppet life and tells him he can become real if he proves himself brave, truthful and unselfish, with Jiminy Cricket appointed as his conscience. On his way to school Pinocchio is lured by the fox Honest John into Stromboli's puppet show, escapes, and is later taken to Pleasure Island, where disobedient boys are transformed into donkeys. Escaping with only donkey ears and a tail, he learns Geppetto has been swallowed by the monstrous whale Monstro; Pinocchio rescues him at the risk of his own life, and the Blue Fairy transforms him into a real boy.

Production and reception

The production was a landmark in animation, noted for its detailed hand-drawn effects — water, rain, splashing waves and sparkling fairy dust — achieved with the multiplane camera. Initially a box-office disappointment because the Second World War cut off European markets, the film was re-released repeatedly and came to be regarded as one of the greatest animated films ever made. Its song "When You Wish Upon a Star", sung by Cliff Edwards as Jiminy Cricket, won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and later became the signature tune of The Walt Disney Company; the film also won the Oscar for Best Original Score.
