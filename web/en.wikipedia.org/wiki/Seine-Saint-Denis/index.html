Seine-Saint-Denis (French pronunciation: [sɛn sɛ̃ d(ə)ni]) is a department of France located in the Île-de-France region, immediately northeast of Paris. Numbered 93, it is colloquially known as le quatre-vingt-treize or simply le 9-3. Created on 1 January 1968 from parts of the former Seine and Seine-et-Oise departments, it forms part of the petite couronne, the inner ring of suburbs around the capital.

Geography and communities

The department covers 236 square kilometres and includes 40 communes, among them Saint-Denis, Montreuil, Aubervilliers, Aulnay-sous-Bois, Drancy, Noisy-le-Grand, Pantin, Bondy, and Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine. Its prefecture is Bobigny. The Basilica of Saint-Denis, burial place of the kings of France, stands in Saint-Denis, and the department hosts the Stade de France (built for the 1998 FIFA World Cup) and Le Bourget airport, site of the Paris Air Show. Saint-Denis and neighbouring communes hosted major venues and the Olympic Village for the Paris 2024 Olympic Games.

Society

Seine-Saint-Denis is one of France's most ethnically diverse and youngest departments, shaped by successive waves of immigration to its industrial suburbs, and it has historically been a stronghold of the French Communist Party — part of the old "red belt" around Paris. It also has the highest poverty rate of any department in metropolitan France and faces persistent challenges in unemployment, housing, and public services, issues that came to national attention during the 2005 riots, which began in Clichy-sous-Bois. At the same time, the department is a centre of French rap and urban culture, and significant redevelopment — media and film studios at La Plaine Saint-Denis, university campuses, and Grand Paris Express metro lines — is reshaping its economy.
