Women's marathon

The women's marathon is a long-distance running event over 42.195 kilometres (26 miles 385 yards) contested by women at the Olympic Games, World Championships and major city marathons. For much of the twentieth century women were officially excluded from marathon racing on the grounds that endurance running was believed harmful to them; the event was added to the Olympic programme only in 1984.

History

Pioneers such as Bobbi Gibb, who ran the Boston Marathon unofficially in 1966, and Kathrine Switzer, who entered officially in 1967, challenged the exclusion. The first women's Olympic marathon was held at Los Angeles in 1984 and won by Joan Benoit Samuelson. Paula Radcliffe's time of 2:15:25, set in London in 2003, stood as the world record for sixteen years; it was broken by Brigid Kosgei in 2019, and Tigst Assefa ran 2:11:53 in Berlin in 2023.

Major races

The World Marathon Majors circuit includes Tokyo, Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago and New York City, along with the Olympic and World Championship marathons.
