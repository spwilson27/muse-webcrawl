The Charlie Ramsay Round, commonly known as the Ramsay Round, is a Scottish hill-running and mountaineering challenge which takes in 24 summits around Lochaber in the Scottish Highlands, starting and finishing at Glen Nevis near Fort William. Like its English counterpart the Bob Graham Round, it is a circuit of roughly 60 miles with about 28,500 feet of ascent, to be completed within 24 hours. It is regarded as one of the classic "big rounds" of British fell running.

The Mamores, crossed by the Ramsay Round

The route

The round takes in 23 Munros (Scottish peaks over 3,000 feet) and one Corbett, Sgùrr Innse. From Glen Nevis it crosses the Mamores ridge, the remote hills around Loch Treig, and the Grey Corries, finishing over Aonach Beag, Aonach Mòr, Càrn Mòr Dearg and finally Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in Britain, before descending to the finish. Both clockwise and anticlockwise traversals are recognised.

History

The round grew out of an extension of Philip Tranter's earlier 24-hour Lochaber traverse of 19 peaks. On 9 July 1978 Charlie Ramsay, a postal worker and accomplished fell runner, completed the extended 24-peak circuit in 23 hours 58 minutes — two minutes inside the limit — establishing the round that bears his name. Completions remain relatively few compared with the Bob Graham Round, reflecting the remoteness of the Lochaber hills; successful runners are recorded on the round's official list, and the fastest times have been brought well under 15 hours by leading mountain runners.
