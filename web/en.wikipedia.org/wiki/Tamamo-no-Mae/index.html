Tamamo-no-Mae

Tamamo-no-Mae (玉藻前) is a legendary figure in Japanese mythology — a beautiful and intelligent courtesan serving Emperor Toba who was in truth a nine-tailed fox spirit (kitsune). She is counted among the Nihon San Dai Aka Yōkai, the three most notorious evil monsters of Japan, alongside Shuten-dōji and Emperor Sutoku.

Legend

According to the otogi-zōshi tale, Tamamo-no-Mae was supremely beautiful and learned, able to answer any question at court. Emperor Toba fell in love with her, but soon fell ill. The astrologer Abe no Yasuchika divined that she was a nine-tailed fox working to seize the throne, and she fled to the plains of Nasu in Shimotsuke Province.

The emperor sent warriors — Kazusa-no-suke and Miura-no-suke — with 80,000 men to hunt the fox. After a long chase she was killed, and her body became the Sessho-seki ("Killing Stone"), which emitted poisonous gas and killed anything that approached. The stone was later shattered by the monk Genno Shinsho in the Nanboku-chō period. In March 2022 the real Sessho-seki stone at Nasu split in two, prompting wide media and social-media attention.

Background

The story draws on older tales of fox spirits seducing rulers in China (Daji, consort of King Zhou of Shang) and India, transmitted to Japan. Tamamo-no-Mae appears in Noh and Kabuki plays, and remains a popular character in modern manga, anime, and games such as the Fate series.
