Emperor Nintoku (Japanese: 仁徳天皇, Nintoku-tennō) was the 16th legendary Emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. He is traditionally placed in the late 4th century CE, though historians regard the details of his life as semi-legendary: no firm dates can be assigned, and his reign as recorded in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki chronicles is dated 313–399.

Legendary narrative

Nintoku was a son of Emperor Ōjin. The chronicles relate that he yielded precedence to his elder half-brother for several years before accepting the throne. He is remembered as a virtuous and frugal ruler: in a famous anecdote, seeing no cooking smoke rising from the people's houses — a sign of poverty — he suspended taxation for three years, letting his own palace fall into disrepair; when prosperity returned, he refused to resume repairs until the people had recovered, and is said to have died content that the smoke now rose thickly from every village.

Tomb

Nintoku's attributed tomb, the Daisenryō Kofun in Sakai, Osaka Prefecture, is one of the largest tombs in the world: a keyhole-shaped mound about 486 metres long, surrounded by triple moats, covering some 32 hectares and built in the 5th century during the Kofun period. It forms part of the Mozu–Furuichi Kofun Group, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019. The Imperial Household Agency administers it as an imperial mausoleum and forbids excavation, so its occupant has never been archaeologically confirmed.
