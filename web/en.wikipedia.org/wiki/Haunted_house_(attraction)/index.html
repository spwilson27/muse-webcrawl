Haunted house (attraction)

A haunted house, spook house or ghost house is a commercial attraction designed to thrill and frighten visitors, typically operated around Halloween. Guests walk a dark route through themed rooms populated by costumed actors ("scareactors"), animatronics, special effects and staged tableaux drawn from horror films and folklore.

History

Haunted attractions have roots in nineteenth-century illusion shows and fairground ghost houses such as those in Madame Tussaud's Chamber of Horrors. In the United States, community organisations including the Jaycees popularised charity haunted houses from the 1960s and 1970s. Theme parks then raised production values: Knott's Berry Farm began its Halloween Haunt in 1973, and Universal Studios' Halloween Horror Nights, launched in 1991, became a benchmark for large-scale events.

Industry

The modern industry ranges from small charity "haunts" and farm-based hayride attractions to elaborate professional productions and extreme "full-contact" attractions that have attracted controversy. Elaborate narrative design, film-quality sets and trained actors are typical of major attractions. Safety and building codes — covering sprinklers, emergency exits and actor conduct — were tightened after incidents such as the 1984 Haunted Castle fire at Six Flags Great Adventure, in which eight teenagers died.

Related attractions

Escape rooms, zombie experiences and immersive theatre productions share many design techniques with haunted houses, and permanent haunted attractions operate year-round in tourist districts in several countries.
