Glass-bottom boat

A glass-bottom boat is a boat with sections of glass, or other transparent material, below the waterline allowing passengers to observe the underwater environment from within the vessel. The effect is similar to looking through a diving mask, but without getting wet.

Uses

Glass-bottom boats are widely used for tourism over coral reefs, kelp forests, shipwrecks, and other shallow-water attractions, for example at Silver Springs in Florida, on the Great Barrier Reef, in the Red Sea, and around Mediterranean and Caribbean islands. They also serve in surveying, salvage inspection, and marine research where a clear view of the bottom is useful.

Design

Viewing panels are usually set into a well in the hull of a catamaran or monohull. Modern panels use toughened laminated glass or acrylic. Some vessels are semi-submersibles, in which passengers sit in a submerged viewing chamber beneath a floating hull.
