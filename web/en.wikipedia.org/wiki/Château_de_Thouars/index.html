The Château de Thouars is a large 17th-century château in the town of Thouars in the Deux-Sèvres department of western France. Set on a terrace above the river Thouet, it is one of the great classical residences of the Loire region, distinguished by the extraordinary length of its main façade — some 110 metres — and by its orangery, among the longest in France.

History

A medieval fortress stood on the site from the early Middle Ages, guarding a strategic crossing of the Thouet; the viscounts of Thouars were among the great feudal lords of Poitou. The medieval castle was replaced beginning in 1635 by Marie de La Tour d'Auvergne, wife of Henri de La Trémoille, duke of Thouars. Construction continued for decades, producing a vast classical palace in the local golden tuffeau stone.

During the French Revolution the château was seized and used for various public purposes, and in the 19th century it served for a time as a prison. After a fire in 1849 it was restored, and in the 20th century the building housed a school (the collège Marie de La Tour d'Auvergne) for many years. It remains a protected historic monument (monument historique) and a symbol of the town.

Architecture

The château is built in the restrained classical style of the reign of Louis XIII, with three storeys of regularly spaced windows, pavilions at either end and steep slate roofs. The terrace before the façade commands a wide view over the Thouet valley. The orangery, built against the hillside in the 18th century, is famed for its length and for its collection of citrus trees; the grounds also preserve the remains of the ducal stables and gardens laid out in the French formal style.
