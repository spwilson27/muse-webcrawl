Holy orders

Holy orders
TypeSacrament (Catholic, Orthodox, some Anglican traditions)
OrdersBishop, priest, deacon
RiteLaying on of hands and prayer of consecration

Holy orders is the sacrament or rite by which men (and, in some churches, women) are ordained as bishops, priests, or deacons in the Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, Oriental Orthodox churches, and parts of the Anglican Communion. Ordination confers a sacramental character and authorizes the ordained to preach, celebrate the sacraments, and govern the faithful.

The three orders

Since the early centuries, ordained ministry has had three degrees: the episcopate (bishops), the presbyterate (priests), and the diaconate (deacons). In Catholic teaching, bishops possess the fullness of the sacrament; priests are their co-workers, and deacons are ordained for service.

Theology and history

The rite centers on the laying on of hands by the bishop and the prayer of consecration, understood as continuing the ministry of the apostles (apostolic succession). The Latin Church requires celibacy of priests (with limited exceptions), while Eastern churches permit married men to be ordained as priests and deacons but choose bishops from monastics.

Other traditions

Many Protestant churches practice ordination but do not count it among the sacraments. The ordination of women is practiced in many Anglican, Lutheran, and other churches, while the Catholic and Orthodox churches restrict ordination to men—a continuing subject of theological debate.
