The Shwezigon Pagoda or Shwezigon Paya (Burmese: ရွှေစည်းခုံ ဘုရား) is a Buddhist stupa located in Nyaung-U, a town near Bagan, in Myanmar. A prototype of Burmese stupas, it consists of a circular gold leaf-gilded stupa surrounded by smaller temples and shrines. Construction of the Shwezigon Pagoda began during the reign of King Anawrahta (r. 1044–1077), the founder of the Pagan Empire, in 1059–1060 and was completed in 1102 AD, during the reign of his son King Kyansittha.

The pagoda is believed to enshrine a bone and tooth of Gautama Buddha. It is one of the most famous pagodas in Myanmar and an important pilgrimage site, and its design strongly influenced later Burmese stupa architecture, including the Shwedagon Pagoda in Yangon.

History

According to Burmese chronicles, construction began under Anawrahta after he converted to Theravada Buddhism. Legend holds that the site was chosen when a white elephant carrying a relic of the Buddha stopped at the spot; the name Shwezigon derives from the Pali jeyyabhūmi, "ground of victory." Anawrahta died before the pagoda was finished, and his son Kyansittha completed it in 1102.

The pagoda was damaged by earthquakes over the centuries, most notably the 1975 Bagan earthquake, and has been repeatedly repaired and regilded through donations.

Architecture

The stupa rises on three receding terraces and follows a bell-shaped (anda) profile that became the standard for Burmese stupas. The terraces are decorated with glazed terracotta plaques depicting scenes from the Jataka tales. Four bronze statues of the Buddhas of the present age stand in shrines at the cardinal points, and the compound includes nat (spirit) shrines, including images of the 37 nats.

Festival

The Shwezigon Pagoda Festival, held during the Burmese month of Nadaw (November–December), draws pilgrims from across the country and was historically a major trading fair for the region.
