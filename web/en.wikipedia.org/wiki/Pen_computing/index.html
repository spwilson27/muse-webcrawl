Pen computing

Pen computing refers to a computer user interface that uses a pen (or stylus) and a graphics tablet or touch-sensitive display, rather than devices such as a keyboard or mouse, as the primary means of interaction. Pen-based systems typically support handwriting recognition, allowing users to write naturally on the screen and have their writing converted to digital text.

The concept dates back to early systems such as the RAND Tablet of the 1960s. Pen computing gained commercial attention in the early 1990s with devices such as the GO Corporation's PenPoint OS machines, the Apple Newton MessagePad (1993), and Microsoft's Windows for Pen Computing. Although early handwriting recognition was often unreliable, the paradigm found lasting niches in signature capture, field data collection, note-taking, and digital art.

Modern pen computing is embodied in tablet computers and convertible laptops with active styluses, such as the Microsoft Surface Pen, Apple Pencil, and Samsung S Pen, which support pressure sensitivity, palm rejection, and tilt detection.
