Flood barrier

A flood barrier is a structure designed to prevent flooding of a protected area by storm surge, high tide, or river water. Barriers may be permanent installations — levees, dikes, seawalls, and floodwalls — or large movable engineering works, such as storm surge barriers that close estuaries and river mouths only when dangerous water levels threaten. Temporary and demountable barriers, from sandbag walls to inflatable dams, are deployed ahead of individual floods to protect buildings and infrastructure.

Major movable barriers

  • Thames Barrier (London, England) — completed in 1982, a series of rotating steel gates spanning the River Thames that rise from the riverbed to protect central London from tidal surges.
  • Maeslantkering (Netherlands) — two enormous swinging arms in the Nieuwe Waterweg near Rotterdam, part of the Delta Works; when closed, it protects Rotterdam's port and hinterland. Its twin arms are among the largest moving structures on Earth.
  • Oosterscheldekering (Netherlands) — a 9 km storm surge barrier with sluice gates between the islands of the Eastern Scheldt, built after the North Sea flood of 1953.
  • MOSE (Venice, Italy) — a system of mobile gates at the inlets of the Venetian Lagoon designed to isolate the lagoon from exceptional high tides (acqua alta), first operated in 2020.
  • St. Petersburg Dam (Russia) — a barrier complex across the Gulf of Finland protecting St. Petersburg, completed in 2011.

Design considerations

Surge barriers must close reliably under storm conditions, withstand wave and current loads, and allow navigation and ecological exchange when open — partial barriers with sluice gates, as in the Oosterscheldekering, were chosen partly to preserve tidal ecosystems and shellfish fisheries. Designers size barriers for a chosen standard of protection (for example, a 1-in-10,000-year event in the most critical Dutch defenses), and climate-change-driven sea level rise is forcing reassessment of those standards and of barrier raising schedules.

Temporary barriers

At property scale, flood barriers include demountable aluminium plank systems fitted to doorways, water-filled or air-filled tube dams, self-closing flood barriers that rise with the water itself, and simple sandbags. Such measures protect individual buildings but depend on timely deployment and warning.
