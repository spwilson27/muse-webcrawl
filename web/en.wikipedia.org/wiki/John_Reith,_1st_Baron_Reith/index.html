John Reith, 1st Baron Reith

John Charles Walsham Reith, 1st Baron Reith (20 July 1889 – 16 June 1971) was a Scottish broadcasting executive who established the tradition of independent public service broadcasting in the United Kingdom. He became the first general manager of the British Broadcasting Company in 1922 and the first director-general of the BBC when it became a corporation in 1927, a post he held until 1938.

Early life

Reith was born in Stonehaven, Kincardineshire, the son of a Presbyterian minister. He trained as an engineer, served with distinction in the First World War, and was shot through the cheek by a sniper. After the war he worked in engineering management in Glasgow before applying — almost on impulse — for the BBC post.

The BBC and the Reithian mission

Reith defined the BBC's purpose as being to "inform, educate and entertain." He insisted on the corporation's independence from both government and commercial pressure, resisted sensationalism, and built a national institution of centralised, paternalistic character. Under his leadership the BBC began television broadcasts and the Empire Service (now the World Service).

Later career

After leaving the BBC, Reith served as chairman of Imperial Airways and, during the Second World War, as Minister of Information and in other government posts. He was raised to the peerage in 1940. His diaries, published posthumously, revealed the difficult, driven personality behind the public image.
