Vegas Vic is a neon sign depicting a 75-foot-tall (23 m) cowboy that has stood above Fremont Street in downtown Las Vegas, Nevada, since 1951. It is one of the most famous neon signs in the world and a symbol of the city.

History

The sign was designed by Pat Denner and built by the Young Electric Sign Company (YESCO) for the Pioneer Club casino. Vegas Vic greeted visitors with a waving arm, a moving cigarette and a recorded voice saying "Howdy, partner!" every 15 minutes. The voice was silenced in 1966 — reportedly after actor Lee Marvin, staying nearby, threatened to shoot the sign if it was not quieted.

The waving arm stopped moving in 1991 and was later restored. When the Fremont Street Experience canopy was built in 1994, Vic's hat had to be trimmed to fit beneath it. In 1998 the sign's ownership passed with the Pioneer Club building, which by then operated as a souvenir shop.

Cultural references

Vegas Vic has appeared in numerous films and television programmes set in Las Vegas, including the James Bond film Diamonds Are Forever and Honeymoon in Vegas. A female counterpart, "Vegas Vickie", was created in 1980 for the Glitter Gulch casino across the street; the two were ceremonially "married" in 1994, and Vickie was restored and re-erected inside the Circa Resort & Casino in 2020.
