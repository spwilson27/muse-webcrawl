Curry County, New Mexico


Curry County Courthouse in Clovis

Curry County is a county located in the U.S. state of New Mexico. As of the 2020 census, the population was 48,430. Its county seat is Clovis. The county is named in honour of George Curry, territorial governor of New Mexico from 1907 to 1910. Curry County is the sole county in the Clovis Micropolitan Statistical Area, and its economy is strongly influenced by Cannon Air Force Base, located between Clovis and Portales.

Geography

Curry County lies on the High Plains of eastern New Mexico along the Texas border, part of the Llano Estacado plateau. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of about 1,408 square miles (3,650 km²), of which a small fraction is water. The terrain is flat to gently rolling prairie, historically shortgrass rangeland, now extensively farmed and used for dairy production.

History

The county was created in 1909 from parts of Quay and Roosevelt counties, shortly before New Mexico statehood in 1912. Clovis, founded as a rail town in 1906, became famous in archaeology for the nearby Blackwater Draw site, which in the 1930s gave the Clovis culture and its distinctive Clovis points their name — some of the earliest evidence of human habitation in North America.

Communities and economy

Communities include Clovis (the largest city), Texico on the Texas line, Melrose, Grady, and Ranchvale. Agriculture — cattle feeding, dairy farming, wheat, sorghum, and peanuts — and Cannon Air Force Base, home to the 27th Special Operations Wing, are the principal employers.
