Juliana (30 April 1909 – 20 March 2004) was Queen of the Netherlands from 1948 until her abdication in 1980. The only child of Queen Wilhelmina, she presided over the postwar reconstruction of the Netherlands, the loss of the Dutch East Indies, and the transformation of the country into a prosperous welfare state, and was much loved for her informality and common touch.

Early life and exile

Juliana was born at Noordeinde Palace in The Hague and studied law at Leiden University, receiving an honorary doctorate. In 1937 she married Prince Bernhard of Lippe-Biesterfeld; the couple had four daughters, Beatrix, Irene, Margriet and Christina. During the German occupation she lived in exile in Canada, where Princess Margriet was born in Ottawa in 1943 — the maternity ward being temporarily declared Dutch territory so the princess would hold only Dutch citizenship.

Reign

Acting as regent during her mother's final illness, Juliana became queen on 4 September 1948. In December 1949 she signed the transfer of sovereignty to the Republic of Indonesia. Her reign saw the North Sea flood of 1953, after which she toured the devastated areas, the founding membership of the Netherlands in the European Communities, and growing social liberalisation. Her deep interest in spirituality and her reliance on the faith healer Greet Hofmans caused a constitutional crisis in 1956, and in 1976 the Lockheed bribery scandal involving Prince Bernhard nearly forced her abdication.

Abdication and later life

On 30 April 1980, her 71st birthday, Juliana signed the act of abdication in Amsterdam and was succeeded by her daughter Beatrix. As Princess Juliana she lived quietly at Soestdijk Palace until her death in 2004, aged 94 — then the longest-lived former Dutch monarch.
