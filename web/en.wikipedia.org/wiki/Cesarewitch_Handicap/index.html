Cesarewitch Handicap

Cesarewitch Handicap
Location: Newmarket Racecourse, England
Race type: Flat / Thoroughbred
Distance: 2 mi 2 f (3,621 m)
Inaugurated: 1839
Surface: Turf

The Cesarewitch Handicap is a flat handicap horse race in Great Britain open to horses aged three years or older. It is run at Newmarket over a distance of 2 miles and 2 furlongs (3,621 metres) each October, during the Future Champions Festival.

History

The race was founded in 1839 and named in honour of Tsesarevich Alexander Nikolaevich, the future Tsar Alexander II of Russia, who had donated £300 to the Jockey Club. Together with the Cambridgeshire Handicap, run in the same period, it forms the historic "Autumn Double", a famous betting double for punters. The Cesarewitch is one of the longest and most valuable staying handicaps in Britain, attracting large fields of up to 34 runners.

Records

Few horses have won the race twice, and it has often been won by horses that also compete over hurdles in National Hunt racing, reflecting the extreme stamina the distance demands.
