Russian Americans

Russian Americans are Americans of full or partial Russian ancestry. The term can also apply to residents of the United States who immigrated from the Russian Empire or the Soviet Union. Around 2.4 million Americans claimed Russian ancestry in recent census estimates, with substantial communities in New York City (notably Brighton Beach in Brooklyn), Los Angeles, Chicago, San Francisco and other metropolitan areas.

History

Russian presence in North America began with the fur trade in Alaska in the 18th and 19th centuries, including settlements such as Sitka (then New Archangel). Large-scale immigration came in waves: in the late 19th and early 20th centuries many Jews and others fled persecution and pogroms in the Russian Empire; after 1917, "White" émigrés fled the Revolution and Civil War; and after the Second World War and again following the collapse of the Soviet Union, further waves arrived, including many Russian Jews admitted as refugees in the 1970s–1990s.

Culture

Russian American cultural life centres on Orthodox parishes, Russian-language newspapers, schools and shops in neighbourhoods such as Brighton Beach ("Little Odessa"). Notable Russian Americans include composer Irving Berlin, inventor Igor Sikorsky and authors Ayn Rand and Vladimir Nabokov (who became a US citizen).
