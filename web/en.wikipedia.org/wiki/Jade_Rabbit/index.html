Jade Rabbit

The Jade Rabbit (Chinese: 玉兔; pinyin: Yùtù), also known as the Moon Rabbit, is a figure in East Asian folklore that lives on the Moon and is seen in the markings of the lunar surface (pareidolia). The rabbit is depicted pounding medicine with a mortar and pestle, often as the companion of the moon goddess Chang'e.

Legend

In Chinese tradition the Jade Rabbit pounds the elixir of life on the Moon for Chang'e, the goddess who flew to the Moon after consuming an elixir of immortality. Related tales appear in Japanese folklore (tsuki no usagi) and Korean folklore, where the rabbit pounds ingredients for rice cakes or medicine. A famous Buddhist Jataka tale tells of a rabbit that offered its own body as food to a disguised god and was rewarded by being placed on the Moon — a story popular across Asia and echoed in other cultures' lunar folklore.

Cultural influence

The Jade Rabbit is a familiar motif of the Mid-Autumn Festival and appears widely in art, literature, and modern media across East and Southeast Asia. In modern times, China named its lunar rovers Yutu ("Jade Rabbit"): Yutu was carried to the Moon by the Chang'e 3 lander in December 2013, becoming the first soft landing on the Moon since 1976, and Yutu-2, delivered by Chang'e 4 in January 2019, made the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon.
