Expenditure minimization problem

In microeconomics, the expenditure minimization problem is the problem of how much income a consumer must spend, at given factor prices, to achieve a given level of utility. Formally, the consumer chooses a consumption bundle to minimise total expenditure p·x subject to a utility constraint u(x) ≥ u*.

The solution to the problem gives the Hicksian (compensated) demand functions, named after John Hicks, and the minimum value of the problem is the expenditure function e(p, u*). The problem is the dual of the utility maximization problem: under standard conditions the two problems yield equivalent information about consumer behaviour, connected by identities such as Shephard's lemma.
