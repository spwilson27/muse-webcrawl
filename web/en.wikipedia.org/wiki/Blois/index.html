Blois is a commune and the prefecture of the Loir-et-Cher department in central France, situated on the banks of the Loire River between Orléans and Tours. It is famous for the Château de Blois, a residence of several French kings, and for its well-preserved old town of steep streets and half-timbered houses.

History

Blois was the seat of powerful medieval counts who rivalled the early Capetian kings. In 1429 Joan of Arc was blessed in the château chapel before setting out to relieve Orléans. Under Louis XII and Francis I the château became a principal royal residence, rebuilt in successive styles from Gothic to Renaissance. In 1588 the Duke of Guise was assassinated there by order of Henry III during the Wars of Religion.

Château de Blois

The château, built around a single courtyard, displays four wings in four architectural styles — medieval fortress, Gothic, Renaissance (with the famous external spiral staircase of the Francis I wing), and classical — making it a summary in stone of French architecture. It now houses museums and hosts a son-et-lumière show.

Other sights

Sights include Blois Cathedral, the Maison de la Magie (a museum of magic honouring the illusionist Jean-Eugène Robert-Houdin, a native of the town), and the Denis Papin staircase in the old town. Blois is a gateway to the châteaux of the Loire Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage landscape.
