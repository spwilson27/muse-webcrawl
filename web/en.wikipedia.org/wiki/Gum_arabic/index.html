Gum arabic

Source: Acacia (senegal and seyal) trees

Main producer: Sudan

E number: E414

Uses: Food stabiliser, watercolour binder, emulsifier

Gum arabic, also known as acacia gum, is a natural gum consisting of hardened sap obtained from two species of acacia tree, Senegalia senegal and Vachellia seyal, which grow across the Sahel region of Africa. It is a complex mixture of glycoproteins and polysaccharides and dissolves readily in water.

Uses

Gum arabic is used in the food industry as a stabiliser and emulsifier (E414) in soft drinks, confectionery and chewing gum; in art as the traditional binder of watercolour paint; and in printing, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals and postage-stamp adhesive.

Production

Sudan has historically supplied the majority of the world market; the gum is harvested by tapping the trees and collecting the dried exudate nodules by hand.
