Overview

A distilled beverage, spirit, or liquor is an alcoholic drink produced by distillation of grains, fruits, vegetables or sugar that have already gone through alcoholic fermentation. Distillation concentrates ethanol and flavour compounds, producing drinks typically of 35–60% alcohol by volume — far stronger than fermented-only drinks such as beer and wine.

History and types

Distillation spread from alchemical practice in the medieval Islamic world and European monasteries to aqua vitae medicines and then convivial spirits: brandy (wine), whisky and vodka (grain and potato), rum (sugarcane and molasses), gin (juniper-flavoured), tequila and mezcal (agave), baijiu (sorghum) and shōchū. Ageing in oak defines many brown spirits. Excise taxation and temperance shaped the industry; moonshine and Prohibition lore are its folklore. Spirits are the basis of cocktails and of liqueurs (sweetened, flavoured distillates).
