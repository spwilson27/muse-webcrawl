Orbis Pictus

Orbis Sensualium Pictus
Author: John Amos Comenius
Published: 1658, Nuremberg
Language: Latin and German (later many languages)
Subject: Children's textbook

Orbis Sensualium Pictus ("The Visible World in Pictures") is a textbook for children written by the Czech educator John Amos Comenius and published in Nuremberg in 1658. It is widely regarded as the first picture book specifically created for children, and it remained influential in European education for two centuries.

Contents

The book contains about 150 chapters, each with an engraved or woodcut picture and bilingual text in Latin and the learner's vernacular. Each item in the picture is numbered and keyed to a word or short description in the text, so the child learns vocabulary, reading and knowledge of the world simultaneously — from God, the elements, animals and the human body through trades, crafts, games and moral subjects. It embodies Comenius's pedagogical principle that learning should begin with the senses and things themselves rather than with abstract grammar.

Influence

Orbis Pictus was translated into most European languages and was used in schools from Hungary to Sweden and colonial America; the first English edition appeared in 1659, translated by Charles Hoole. It is considered a landmark in the history of education and of illustrated books, and gives its name to the Orbis Pictus Award for outstanding nonfiction for children.
