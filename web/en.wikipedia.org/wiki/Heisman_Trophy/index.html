Heisman Trophy

The Heisman Memorial Trophy (usually called the Heisman Trophy or simply the Heisman) is awarded annually since 1935 to the top player in college football in the United States. It is considered the most prestigious award in college football and is presented by the Heisman Trophy Trust in early December, before the postseason bowl games. The trophy depicts a football player executing a stiff-arm — the iconic "Heisman pose" — and was modelled on 1934 NYU player Ed Smith.

The trophy's stiff-arm pose is one of the most recognisable images in American sport.

History

The award was created by New York's Downtown Athletic Club in 1935 as the DAC Trophy; the first recipient was University of Chicago halfback Jay Berwanger. After the death in 1936 of the club's athletic director John Heisman — a pioneering player and coach — the award was renamed in his honour. Ohio State running back Archie Griffin remains the only player to win twice (1974 and 1975). Running backs dominated early decades, but quarterbacks have won the great majority of 21st-century awards.

Notable winners include Roger Staubach, O. J. Simpson, Barry Sanders, Tim Tebow, Cam Newton, Johnny Manziel, Lamar Jackson, Joe Burrow and Caleb Williams. USC has produced the most winners by school (with Reggie Bush's 2005 award vacated in 2010 amid NCAA sanctions and later reinstated in 2024).

Voting

The electorate of roughly 930 voters combines media members across six regions, past winners, and one fan vote. Voters name three players, weighted 3–2–1 points. Regional biases and the advantage given to players on highly ranked teams, especially quarterbacks, are perennial topics of debate.
