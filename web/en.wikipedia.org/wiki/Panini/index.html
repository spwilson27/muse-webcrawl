Panini

A panini (from the Italian panino, "small bread roll"; plural panini) is a sandwich made with Italian bread — such as ciabatta, rosetta, or michetta — usually served warm after pressing or grilling on a two-sided grill, often called a panini press. Unlike a simple toastie, a panini is defined by its bread rather than by sliced sandwich bread.

History

References to filled bread rolls appear in Italian sources from the sixteenth century onward. Panini became fashionable in Milanese bars called paninoteche in the 1970s and 1980s, when the paninari youth subculture took its name from the sandwich bars its members frequented. The style spread to the United States and other countries in the 1990s and 2000s, where "panini" came to denote almost any pressed sandwich.

Preparation

Typical fillings include cured meats such as prosciutto, salami, and mortadella, cheeses like mozzarella and provolone, grilled vegetables, and spreads such as pesto. The assembled sandwich is pressed between heated ridged plates, compressing it, crisping the crust, melting the cheese, and leaving characteristic grill marks.
