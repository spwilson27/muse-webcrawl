Battle of Halbe
Date24 April – 1 May 1945
LocationHalbe, Brandenburg, Germany
ResultSoviet victory; partial German breakout to the west
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs Nazi Germany
CommandersIvan Konev, Alexander Luchinsky vs Theodor Busse, Walther Wenck

The Battle of Halbe (German: Kessel von Halbe, the Halbe pocket) was fought from 24 April to 1 May 1945 in the forests southeast of Berlin during the final weeks of the Second World War in Europe. The German Ninth Army under General Theodor Busse, encircled by forces of Marshal Ivan Konev's 1st Ukrainian Front, attempted to break out westward to link up with the Twelfth Army and surrender to the Americans on the Elbe rather than to the Soviets.

Background

During the Battle of the Seelow Heights and the Soviet encirclement of Berlin, the Ninth Army was cut off in the Spreewald region around Halbe. Trapped with it were tens of thousands of civilians fleeing the Soviet advance. Hitler's orders to hold fast gave way to a desperate breakout plan toward the lines of General Walther Wenck's Twelfth Army near Beelitz.

The breakout

Beginning on the night of 25–26 April and continuing in repeated waves, German groups attacked westward through Soviet blocking positions around Halbe and the surrounding forest roads. The small town of Halbe changed hands amid savage fighting, and the roads through the pine forests became choked with destroyed vehicles, dead soldiers, and civilian casualties. Of perhaps 200,000 soldiers and civilians in the pocket, a substantial remnant — estimates vary widely — broke through to Wenck's lines and crossed the Elbe to surrender to the US Army; many thousands were killed or captured.

Aftermath

The destruction of the Ninth Army removed the last major German formation southeast of Berlin. The Halbe forest war cemetery (Waldfriedhof Halbe) is one of the largest war cemeteries in Germany, holding more than 20,000 dead, many of them unidentified victims of the pocket.
