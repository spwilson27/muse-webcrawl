Finnic languages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Distribution of the Finnic languages around the Baltic Sea.

The Finnic or Baltic Finnic languages constitute a branch of the Uralic language family spoken around the Baltic Sea by the Finnic peoples, mainly in Finland and Estonia, by about 7 million people. The major Finnic languages are Finnish and Estonian, both official languages of their respective states; other languages include Karelian, Veps, Votic, Ingrian, Livonian, and Võro.

Classification

The Finnic languages descend from a Proto-Finnic language spoken around the Gulf of Finland roughly two thousand years ago. They are conventionally divided into a southern group (Estonian, Võro, Livonian, Votic) and a northern group (Finnish, Ingrian, Karelian, Veps). The Finnic branch is closely related to the Sámi languages, together forming a western branch of Uralic, though the exact higher-level grouping is debated.

Features

Finnic languages share characteristic features including vowel harmony (weakened or lost in some languages such as Estonian and Livonian), consonant gradation, a rich case system (Finnish has about 15 cases), no grammatical gender, and no articles. Several of the smaller languages are severely endangered; the last native speaker of Livonian died in 2013, though revival efforts continue.
