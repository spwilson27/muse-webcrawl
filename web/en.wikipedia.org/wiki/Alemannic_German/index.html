Alemannic German (Alemannisch) is a group of Upper German dialects spoken in the southwestern German-speaking world: German-speaking Switzerland, Baden-Württemberg in Germany, Alsace in France, Vorarlberg and Liechtenstein in the Alps, and small Walser colonies in northern Italy. The name derives from the Alemanni, the Germanic tribal confederation that settled the region in late antiquity.

Varieties

Linguists divide Alemannic into Swabian (in Württemberg), Low Alemannic (along the Upper Rhine, Basel and Alsace), High Alemannic (most of German-speaking Switzerland and southern Baden), and Highest Alemannic, the archaic dialects of the Walser people in high Alpine valleys. The varieties participated fully in the High German consonant shift, and Highest Alemannic preserves sounds lost elsewhere.

Status and use

In Switzerland, Alemannic dialects (Schwyzerdütsch) are the everyday spoken language of all social classes, while Standard German is used for most writing — a classic diglossia. In Germany and Alsace the dialects have receded more strongly under pressure from the standard language, though Swabian retains a strong regional identity. Alemannic has no single official orthography; writing in dialect is common in literature, carnival culture, texting and social media. There are Alemannic editions of Wikipedia (als.wikipedia.org), which also serve neighbouring dialects.
