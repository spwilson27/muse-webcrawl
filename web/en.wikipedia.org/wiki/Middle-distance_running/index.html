Middle-distance running

Middle-distance running events are track races longer than sprints and shorter than long-distance races. The standard Olympic middle-distance events are the 800 metres and 1500 metres; the mile run is also commonly contested, and events such as the 1000 metres and 3000 metres are sometimes included in the category.

Events

The 800 metres, run over two laps of a standard track, combines speed and endurance and is often described as the longest sprint. The 1500 metres, the metric counterpart of the mile, rewards tactical positioning and a strong finishing kick. Races are run from a standing start without blocks, with runners breaking for the inside lane after the first bend in the 800 m.

Physiology and training

Middle-distance performance depends on both aerobic capacity and anaerobic speed reserve. Training typically blends interval work, tempo runs, and speed sessions, and elite athletes often specialise in one event or double across the 800 m and 1500 m.
