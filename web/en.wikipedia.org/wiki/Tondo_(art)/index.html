Tondo (art)

A tondo (plural tondi) is a Renaissance term for a circular work of art, either a painting or a sculpture. The word derives from the Italian rotondo, meaning "round".

History

Circular formats appear in ancient art, including Roman medallions and the roundels of medieval manuscripts, but the tondo flourished in fifteenth-century Florence. Artists such as Fra Angelico, Botticelli and Michelangelo produced celebrated examples, frequently depicting the Madonna and Child or the Adoration of the Magi.

Characteristics

The circular format challenged artists to compose figures within a round field, often using the frame itself as part of the design. Tondi were commonly commissioned for domestic interiors, palaces and churches.

Famous examples

  • The Doni Tondo by Michelangelo (Uffizi, Florence)
  • Botticelli's Madonna of the Pomegranate
  • Raphael's Alba Madonna
