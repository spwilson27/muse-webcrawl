Arbroath smokie

Arbroath smokies — hot-smoked haddock with a distinctive coppery colour.

The Arbroath smokie is a type of hot-smoked haddock associated with the town of Arbroath in Angus, Scotland. It holds Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) status under European law, meaning that only haddock smoked in the traditional manner within a defined area around Arbroath may be sold under the name.

Preparation

Haddock are headed, gutted, salted, and tied in pairs by the tail. They are then hung over a triangular-bottomed barrel containing a hardwood fire — traditionally oak or beech — and covered with damp jute sacking. The fish are hot-smoked for roughly half an hour, emerging cooked, with a deep coppery-brown skin and a moist, creamy flesh with an intense smoky flavour. Unlike cold-smoked fish, smokies are ready to eat, though they are often grilled with butter or used in soups such as cullen skink.

Origins

Local tradition attributes the smokie to the nearby fishing village of Auchmithie: one account says a cottage fire accidentally cooked barrels of salted haddock, and the result proved so good that the method was adopted deliberately. The method moved to Arbroath in the late 19th century as the town's fishing industry grew. Iain R. Spink is credited with championing the product and obtaining its protected status in 2004.
