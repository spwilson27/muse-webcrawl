Premillennialism

Premillennialism, in Christian eschatology, is the belief that Jesus will physically return to the Earth (the Second Coming) before the Millennium, the golden age or "thousand years" of peace mentioned in the Book of Revelation, and that the Millennium will be a literal reign of Christ on Earth. The doctrine is called premillennialism because it holds that Jesus's return will occur before the Millennium. It stands in contrast to postmillennialism, in which Christ returns after a (figurative or literal) thousand-year age of righteousness produced by the spread of the gospel, and to amillennialism, which interprets the thousand years symbolically as the present church age.

Biblical basis

Premillennialists rest their case principally on Revelation 20:1–6, which describes Satan bound for a thousand years while resurrected saints reign with Christ, and on prophecies in Daniel, Ezekiel 37–40, Isaiah, and Zechariah 14, read as promises of a future earthly kingdom centred on Israel. They argue that a "plain" or literal reading of these texts places the Second Coming before the kingdom.

History

Much premillennialism in the early church, sometimes called chiliasm, is found in writers such as Papias, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, and Tertullian. Justin, for example, affirmed a future resurrection and a thousand-year reign in a rebuilt Jerusalem, while noting that other orthodox Christians disagreed. From the time of Augustine, who spiritualized the Millennium as the age of the church, amillennial interpretation dominated Western theology, though millenarian movements recurred throughout the medieval and Reformation periods, notably among some Anabaptists.

Modern forms

Historic premillennialism

Historic (or covenantal) premillennialism expects a single, visible return of Christ after a period of tribulation, followed by the Millennium; it does not sharply separate God's plans for Israel and the Church. Figures such as George Eldon Ladd popularized it in the 20th century.

Dispensational premillennialism

Dispensational premillennialism, systematized by John Nelson Darby in the 19th century and spread by the Scofield Reference Bible (1909), distinguishes Israel from the Church and typically teaches a pre-tribulation rapture of the Church, followed by a seven-year tribulation, the return of Christ, and a literal thousand-year kingdom. It became highly influential in American evangelicalism and fundamentalism, in prophecy conferences, and in popular culture through works such as Hal Lindsey's The Late Great Planet Earth and the Left Behind novels.
