Vänern

Vänern (Swedish pronunciation: [ˈvɛ̂ːnɛɳ]) is the largest lake in Sweden, the largest lake in the European Union, and the third-largest lake in Europe after Lake Ladoga and Lake Onega in Russia. It is located in the provinces of Västergötland, Dalsland, and Värmland in the southwest of the country.

Geography

Vänern covers about 5,650 square kilometres with a maximum depth of 106 metres. The lake is divided by peninsulas and islands into a western basin (Dalbosjön) and an eastern basin (Värmlandssjön). Its principal inflow is the Klarälven, and its outflow is the Göta älv, which drains to the Kattegat at Gothenburg, forming part of the Göta Canal route across Sweden. Towns on its shores include Karlstad, Lidköping, Mariestad, and Vänersborg.

Geology and ecology

The basin was scoured by glacial erosion and freed from ice about 10,000 years ago; isostatic rebound has been slowly raising its southern shore relative to the outlet. The lake contains some 22,000 islands and skerries and supports important commercial fisheries for vendace, salmon, and pike-perch, including prized vendace roe (löjrom).
