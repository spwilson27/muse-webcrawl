The Battle of Mohács of 1687, sometimes called the Battle of Nagyharsány or the Second Battle of Mohács, was fought on 12 August 1687 between the forces of the Ottoman Empire under Grand Vizier Sari Süleyman Pasha and the Habsburg Imperial army commanded by Charles V, Duke of Lorraine, during the Great Turkish War. It was a decisive victory for the Habsburg forces.

The second Battle of Mohács, fought near Nagyharsány in 1687.

Background

After the relief of Vienna in 1683 and the recapture of Buda in 1686, the Imperial army campaigned down the Danube in 1687 to complete the reconquest of Hungary. The Ottoman army under Sari Süleyman Pasha took up a fortified position near Villány and Nagyharsány, close to the field where, in 1526, the first Battle of Mohács had destroyed the medieval Kingdom of Hungary.

The battle

On 12 August the Ottoman forces attacked the Imperial left wing, but the assault was contained. Charles of Lorraine then launched a general counter-attack; Imperial cavalry and infantry broke into the Ottoman fortified camp, and the Ottoman army collapsed into rout. Ottoman losses were severe — contemporary estimates ran to many thousands killed, along with the loss of the camp, artillery, and treasury — while Imperial casualties were comparatively light.

Consequences

The victory opened the way for the Habsburg occupation of Transylvania and Slavonia. In its aftermath, the Hungarian Diet at Pressburg (1687) recognised the hereditary right of the Habsburgs to the Hungarian crown. The defeat also provoked a mutiny in the Ottoman army and contributed to the deposition of Sultan Mehmed IV later that year.
