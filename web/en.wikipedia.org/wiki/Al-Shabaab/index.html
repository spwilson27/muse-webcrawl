Al-Shabaab

Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen (Arabic: حركة الشباب المجاهدين), commonly known as al-Shabaab, is a Sunni Islamist militant organisation based in Somalia and active in East Africa. It emerged in the mid-2000s as the youth wing of the Islamic Courts Union, which controlled Mogadishu in 2006 before being ousted by Ethiopian forces.

History

Al-Shabaab waged an insurgency against the Somali federal government and African Union missions (AMISOM and its successors), at its peak controlling much of south-central Somalia including parts of Mogadishu. In 2012 it formally pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda. Long led by Ahmed Abdi Godane until his death in a US airstrike in 2014, it was subsequently led by Ahmed Diriye (Abu Ubaidah).

Activities

The group has carried out numerous bombings and attacks in Somalia, including the October 2017 Mogadishu bombing, and cross-border attacks in Kenya, including the Westgate shopping mall attack (2013) and the Garissa University attack (2015). It is designated a terrorist organisation by many countries, funds itself through taxation and extortion in areas under its control, and continues to contest territory with Somali and African Union forces despite offensives since 2022.
