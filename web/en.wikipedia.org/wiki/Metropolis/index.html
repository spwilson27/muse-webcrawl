Metropolis

A metropolis is a large city or conurbation that is a significant economic, political, and cultural centre for a country or region, and an important hub for regional or international connections and communications. The word comes from the Greek mētropolis (μητρόπολις), "mother city," the city from which settlers founded a colony.

Definitions and usage

There is no single population threshold; a metropolis may be defined by the size of its metropolitan area (frequently a million inhabitants or more), by its functions, or by administrative usage. In some countries the term has a legal meaning: in Japan, Tokyo is designated a to (metropolis) prefecture; in Greek and other administrative traditions, a metropolis may head a metropolitan municipality. In ecclesiastical usage, a metropolis is the see of a metropolitan bishop.

Global cities

The largest metropolises anchor metropolitan regions of tens of millions, such as Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, São Paulo, and Mexico City. Urban scholars distinguish the subset of "global cities" that command disproportionate influence in finance and culture. Fritz Lang's 1927 film Metropolis gave the word an enduring science-fiction resonance, as did the fictional Metropolis of Superman comics.
