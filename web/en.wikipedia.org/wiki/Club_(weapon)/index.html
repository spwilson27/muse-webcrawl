Club (weapon)

A club (also known as a cudgel, baton, bludgeon, truncheon, or nightstick) is a short staff or stick, usually made of wood, wielded as a weapon since prehistory. A club is among the simplest of all weapons: it consists of a shaft with a grip at one end and a solid, heavier or rounded end for striking. Most clubs are small enough to be swung with one hand, although larger two-handed clubs exist.

History

The club is one of the oldest human weapons, attested in prehistoric art and archaeology and used by cultures worldwide. Because it requires no metalworking, it remained common long after edged weapons appeared, and ceremonial maces derive from it. Many cultures developed distinctive forms: the Irish shillelagh, the Fijian ula, the Māori mere and wahaika, and the knobkerrie of southern Africa, among others.

Types

  • Baton / truncheon / nightstick — short clubs carried by police and security forces.
  • Mace — a club with a metal head, often flanged, developed for use against armour.
  • Morning star — a club or mace whose head bears spikes.
  • Baseball bat and similar sporting implements, occasionally classified as improvised clubs in law.

Law enforcement

Clubs in the form of batons remain standard less-lethal equipment for police in many countries, used for strikes, blocks, and control holds. Their use is regulated by law and departmental policy in most jurisdictions.
