Larnite

Larnite is a calcium silicate mineral with the formula Ca2SiO4. It is the natural form of belite, one of the principal phases in Portland cement clinker. The mineral was first described in 1929 from Scawt Hill, near Larne, County Antrim, Northern Ireland, from which it takes its name.

Occurrence

Larnite forms in high-temperature, silica-undersaturated contact metamorphic rocks, where limestone or chalk has been heated by basaltic intrusions. Besides the type locality, it occurs at sites in Israel (the Hatrurim Formation), Russia, Germany and elsewhere, typically associated with minerals such as spurrite, gehlenite and wollastonite. It is rare, in part because it alters readily in the presence of water.

Properties

Larnite is monoclinic, colourless to grey, with a Mohs hardness of about 6. The β polymorph is the phase of interest in cement chemistry because it reacts hydraulically with water, contributing to the later strength of concrete; the transformation to the γ form, with a large volume increase, is responsible for "dusting" of slags and refractories.
