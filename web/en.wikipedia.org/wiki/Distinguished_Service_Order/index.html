The Distinguished Service Order (DSO) is a military decoration of the United Kingdom, and formerly of other parts of the Commonwealth, awarded for meritorious or distinguished service by officers of the armed forces during wartime, typically in actual combat. Instituted by Queen Victoria on 6 September 1886, it filled a gap: at the time the Order of the Bath was given mainly to senior officers, and there was no decoration to reward junior and field officers for distinguished service in action.

Eligibility and grades

The DSO is normally awarded to officers of the rank of major (or equivalent) and above, and in its early decades it was a purely military award given for service in war. Recipients are styled Companions of the Distinguished Service Order and may use the post-nominal letters DSO. Further awards are denoted by bars to the medal; a small number of officers have received the DSO with three bars (i.e. four awards in total). Famous recipients include T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia") and the First World War flying ace and infantry officer Bernard Freyberg.

1993 reform

Following a 1993 review of gallantry awards, the DSO's role changed: it became an award for distinguished leadership in operations, open in principle to all ranks in command appointments, while gallantry by officers became recognised instead by the Conspicuous Gallantry Cross, which replaced the DSO's gallantry function and the Distinguished Conduct Medal.

Description

The badge is a gold cross pattée enamelled white, with a crown of laurel in green enamel; the obverse centre bears the imperial crown and the reverse the royal cypher, suspended from a crimson ribbon. It is worn after the Order of the British Empire in the United Kingdom order of precedence.
