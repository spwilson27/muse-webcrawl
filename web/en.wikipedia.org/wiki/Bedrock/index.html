Bedrock

Bedrock exposed at the surface where soil and regolith have been stripped away.

Bedrock is the solid rock that lies beneath loose surface material such as soil, sand, clay or gravel. In geology, bedrock is the consolidated rock underlying the regolith; the boundary between the two is an important factor in engineering, hydrology and landscape evolution.

Formation and exposure

Bedrock may be exposed at the surface as an outcrop, or buried under metres of weathered material. Its upper surface is often weathered into a fractured, partially decomposed zone called saprolite. The type and depth of bedrock are determined by the geological history of an area — bedrock may be igneous, sedimentary or metamorphic in origin.

Engineering and resources

Engineers seek to found the foundations of large structures, such as skyscrapers, bridges and dams, on or in bedrock to ensure stability. Bedrock also controls the flow of groundwater as an aquitard or fractured aquifer, hosts most hard-rock mining for metals and aggregates, and influences the course of rivers and the shape of valleys. Mapping bedrock beneath cover is a major task of geophysical surveying.
