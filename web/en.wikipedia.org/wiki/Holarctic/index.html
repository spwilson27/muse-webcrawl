Holarctic

The Holarctic is a biogeographic realm comprising most of the habitats found throughout the northern continents of the world. It is commonly divided into the Palearctic (Europe, North Africa, and northern and central Asia) and the Nearctic (North America down to central Mexico). The Holarctic is the largest of the world's biogeographic realms.

Flora and fauna

Because the northern landmasses were repeatedly connected — most recently by the Bering land bridge — many plant and animal taxa are shared between the Old and New Worlds. Characteristic biomes include boreal coniferous forest (taiga), tundra, temperate broadleaf forest, steppe, and Mediterranean scrub. Widespread Holarctic animals include the brown bear, gray wolf, red fox, reindeer (caribou), and moose (elk).

History

During the Pleistocene ice ages, glaciation and shifting climate zones repeatedly reshaped Holarctic ecosystems, driving migration, isolation, and speciation in glacial refugia.
