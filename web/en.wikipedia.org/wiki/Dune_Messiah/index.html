Dune Messiah

Dune Messiah is a science fiction novel by American writer Frank Herbert, the second in his six-novel Dune series. It was originally serialized in Galaxy magazine in 1969 and published in hardcover the same year. Set twelve years after the events of Dune, it follows Emperor Paul Atreides as he confronts a conspiracy against his throne and the terrible human cost of the jihad waged in his name.

Plot

Paul "Muad'Dib" Atreides rules as Emperor while his Fremen legions wage jihad across the known universe, killing billions. A conspiracy uniting the Bene Gesserit, the Spacing Guild and the Bene Tleilax plots his overthrow, presenting him with a ghola — a cloned reincarnation — of his dead friend Duncan Idaho. Blinded by an atomic stone burner attack, Paul navigates by prescience alone. After the death of his beloved Chani in childbirth, Paul, following Fremen custom for the blind, walks into the desert, leaving his twin children, Leto II and Ghanima, and the empire to his sister Alia.

Themes

Herbert wrote the novel to deconstruct the heroic mythos of the first book, warning against charismatic leaders and showing the corrupting machinery of empire and religion. It is shorter and more political than Dune, focusing on court intrigue.
