Serology


An ELISA plate, a common serological assay (placeholder image)

Serology is the scientific study of serum and other body fluids, in practice usually meaning the diagnostic identification of antibodies in serum. Such antibodies are typically formed in response to an infection (against a microorganism), against other foreign proteins (for example in response to a mismatched blood transfusion), or to one's own proteins (in autoimmune disease).

Serological tests

Common techniques include the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), agglutination assays, complement fixation, precipitation and fluorescent antibody tests, and Western blotting. Serology is used to diagnose infectious diseases such as hepatitis, HIV, syphilis and measles, to determine blood type, and to investigate immunity after infection or vaccination.

Serosurveillance

In epidemiology, serological surveys ("serosurveys") sample a population's blood to estimate how many people carry antibodies to a pathogen, revealing the true spread of infections — including asymptomatic ones — and the level of population immunity. Such surveys were widely used during the COVID-19 pandemic. Interpretation must account for antibody waning, test sensitivity and specificity, and cross-reactivity.
