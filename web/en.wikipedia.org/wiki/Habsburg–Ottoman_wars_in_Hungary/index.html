Habsburg–Ottoman wars in Hungary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Frontier warfare between the Habsburg and Ottoman empires dominated Hungary for over 150 years.

The Habsburg–Ottoman wars in Hungary were a series of conflicts between the Habsburg monarchy and the Ottoman Empire fought over the territory of the Kingdom of Hungary between 1526 and 1683, forming the Hungarian theatre of the wider Ottoman–Habsburg wars. They began with the Ottoman victory at the Battle of Mohács in 1526 and ended with the Habsburg reconquest confirmed at the Treaty of Karlowitz in 1699.

Background

The death of King Louis II of Hungary at Mohács (29 August 1526) extinguished the Jagiellonian dynasty's Hungarian line. The kingdom split three ways: the central plains fell under direct Ottoman rule as the Eyalet of Buda (from 1541), the east became the tributary Principality of Transylvania, and a rump "Royal Hungary" in the north and west passed to the Habsburg archduke Ferdinand I. Hungary thus became the frontier zone between two empires.

Course of the wars

The Little War (1530–1552)

Suleiman the Magnificent twice marched on Vienna (1529 and 1532) and Ottoman armies consolidated their hold on central Hungary, taking Buda in 1541. Famous episodes of this period include the Siege of Szigetvár (1566), where Nikola Šubić Zrinski's garrison died to a man and Suleiman himself died during the siege. The Truce of Adrianople (1568) fixed an uneasy frontier along which both sides built chains of border fortresses (végvár).

The Long War (1593–1606)

The Long Turkish War saw large-scale campaigning in Hungary and Croatia, including the battles of Sisak (1593) and Mezőkeresztes (1596), but ended in stalemate with the Peace of Zsitvatorok (1606), which for the first time treated the Habsburg emperor and the Ottoman sultan as equals.

Final phase and reconquest

War resumed in 1663–64 (Battle of Saint Gotthard, Peace of Vasvár) and again in 1683, when the Ottoman Grand Vizier Kara Mustafa besieged Vienna. The relief of Vienna by a Polish-Imperial army under John III Sobieski (12 September 1683) turned the tide. The Holy League reconquered Hungary: Buda was retaken in 1686, and Prince Eugene of Savoy's victory at Zenta (1697) led to the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), which transferred most of Hungary to the Habsburgs.

Consequences

A century and a half of warfare devastated central Hungary: towns were destroyed, the plains were depopulated, and the ethnic and religious geography of the kingdom was transformed. The frontier military border system (Militärgrenze) and the magnate-led defence of Royal Hungary shaped Hungarian politics well into the eighteenth century, while memories of the "Turkish yoke" remain central to Hungarian national historiography.
