Duesenberg was an American manufacturer of racing cars and automobiles based in Indianapolis, Indiana. The company was founded by brothers Fred and August Duesenberg in 1913 and became renowned for building some of the most luxurious and technically advanced automobiles of the interwar period.

History

The Duesenberg brothers began by building racing engines. Their cars were successful in the Indianapolis 500, winning the race in 1924, 1925, and 1927. In 1921 a Duesenberg became the first American car to win the French Grand Prix.

The company was purchased by Errett Lobban Cord in 1926, who tasked the brothers with building a car to surpass established luxury marques. The result was the Duesenberg Model J, introduced in 1928, powered by a 6.9-litre straight-eight engine producing 265 horsepower — an extraordinary figure for the time. Supercharged versions, the SJ, produced 320 horsepower.

Legacy

Production ended in 1937 following the collapse of Cord's business empire. Duesenberg cars are among the most valuable collectible automobiles in the world, and the phrase "it's a doozy" is popularly said to derive from the marque's name, though this etymology is disputed.
