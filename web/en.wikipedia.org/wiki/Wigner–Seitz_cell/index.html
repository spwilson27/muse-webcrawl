Wigner–Seitz cell
TypePrimitive cell of a lattice
Named afterEugene Wigner; Frederick Seitz
Introduced1933
Equivalent conceptVoronoi cell of a lattice point

The Wigner–Seitz cell, named after physicists Eugene Wigner and Frederick Seitz, is a type of primitive cell — containing exactly one lattice point — used in the study of crystalline materials. It is defined as the set of points in space that are closer to a given lattice point than to any other, making it the Voronoi cell of the lattice.

Construction

To construct the cell, choose a lattice point and draw lines to all nearby lattice points; then draw the perpendicular bisector planes (or lines, in two dimensions) of each; the smallest enclosed volume around the point is its Wigner–Seitz cell. For a body-centred cubic (BCC) lattice the cell is a truncated octahedron; for face-centred cubic (FCC) it is a rhombic dodecahedron.

Applications

The construction applied to the reciprocal lattice yields the first Brillouin zone, central to band-structure theory of solids. Wigner and Seitz introduced the cell in their 1933 paper on the electronic structure of sodium, using it as the region in which to solve the Schrödinger equation approximately — the basis of the cellular method of band calculations. The same geometry appears in physics as the model of a neutral "Wigner–Seitz" sphere in electron-gas theory.
