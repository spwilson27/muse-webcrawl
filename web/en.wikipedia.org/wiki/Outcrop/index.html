Outcrop

An outcrop or rocky outcrop is a visible exposure of bedrock or ancient superficial deposits on the surface of the Earth, not covered by soil or vegetation. Outcrops occur naturally where erosion, glaciation or uplift strips away the covering material — on mountain slopes, in river valleys, quarries, road cuttings and along rocky coastlines.

Because outcrops expose rock in place, they are fundamental to geological mapping: geologists examine the rock type, bedding, folding, faulting and fossil content of outcrops to reconstruct the geological history of an area and to locate mineral and hydrocarbon resources. Where bedrock is mostly hidden, as in heavily vegetated or deeply weathered terrain, features such as tors and crags may provide the only direct evidence of the underlying geology.

Prominent natural outcrops can also be ecological habitats in their own right, supporting specialised plants and animals able to tolerate thin soils and exposure.
