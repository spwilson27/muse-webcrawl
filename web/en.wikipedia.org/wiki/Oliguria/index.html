Oliguria

Oliguria
DefinitionUrine output < 400 mL/day in adults (or < 0.5 mL/kg/h)
SpecialtyNephrology; critical care
RelatedAnuria (< 100 mL/day); acute kidney injury

Oliguria is the low output of urine, defined in adults as less than about 400 millilitres per day, or less than 0.5 mL/kg/h over a sustained period. It is a common sign of acute kidney injury and forms one of the KDIGO staging criteria. The near-complete absence of urine output (below 50–100 mL/day) is termed anuria.

Causes

Causes are conventionally grouped as prerenal (reduced renal perfusion from hypovolemia, heart failure or sepsis), intrinsic renal (acute tubular necrosis, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis) and postrenal (urinary tract obstruction, e.g. prostatic enlargement or bilateral ureteric obstruction).

Diagnosis

Evaluation includes measurement of urine output, serum creatinine and electrolytes, urinalysis, fractional excretion of sodium, and renal ultrasound to exclude obstruction. A bladder scan or catheterisation helps distinguish retention from true oliguria.

Management

Treatment targets the underlying cause: restoration of circulating volume and perfusion in prerenal states, withdrawal of nephrotoxic drugs, relief of obstruction, and, where indicated, renal replacement therapy for refractory hyperkalemia, acidosis, fluid overload or uremic complications.
