Paperboard

Paperboard
MaterialThick paper-based board
ThicknessGenerally over 0.30 mm
Main usesPackaging: cartons, boxes, tubes

Paperboard is a thick paper-based material, generally thicker than 0.30 mm (0.012 in), distinguished from ordinary paper by its higher stiffness and grammage. While there is no sharp dividing line, paperboard is used chiefly for packaging — folding cartons, rigid boxes, beverage carriers, and blister cards — rather than for printing or writing.

Types

Major grades include solid bleached sulfate (SBS), a premium white board used for cosmetics and pharmaceuticals; solid unbleached sulfate (SUS) for beverage carriers; folding boxboard (FBB) with a mechanical-pulp middle layer; white lined chipboard (WLC) made largely from recycled fibre; and containerboard — linerboard and corrugating medium — used to make corrugated fibreboard.

Manufacture

Paperboard is produced on multi-ply paper machines that form several layers of pulp in a single pass, often with mineral coatings (such as kaolin clay) on the printing surface. It is highly recyclable and is one of the most recovered packaging materials worldwide.
