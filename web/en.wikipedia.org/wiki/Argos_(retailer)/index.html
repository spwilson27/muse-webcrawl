Argos is a British catalogue retailer operating in the United Kingdom and Ireland, owned by the supermarket group Sainsbury's since 2016. Founded in 1972 by Richard Tompkins — who rebranded his Green Shield Stamps business — Argos became famous for its thick printed catalogues from which customers ordered by number and collected goods at a counter.

History

The first Argos stores opened in 1973, and the company grew rapidly; it was acquired by BAT Industries in 1979 and later demerged, joining the GUS group and then Home Retail Group. Sainsbury's acquired Argos in 2016 for about £1.4 billion, progressively integrating Argos outlets into supermarkets as collection points and closing many standalone stores.

Model and operations

Argos sells general merchandise — electronics, furniture, appliances, toys, sports goods — through its website, app, and remaining stores, with same-day Fast Track collection and delivery a signature service. The printed catalogue, once a fixture in millions of homes, was discontinued after the 2020 edition as shopping shifted online.
