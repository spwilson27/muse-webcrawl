The eight-spoked dharma wheel symbolises the path.

The Noble Eightfold Path (Pali: Ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga) is an early summary of the path of Buddhist practice leading to liberation from samsara, the cycle of rebirth. It is the fourth of the Buddha's Four Noble Truths — the truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering — and was set out in his first sermon at Sarnath. The eight factors are practised together rather than sequentially, and the path is symbolised by the eight spokes of the dharma wheel.

The eight factors

GroupFactorMeaning
Wisdom (paññā)Right viewUnderstanding the Four Noble Truths and karma
Right intentionResolve toward renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness
Ethical conduct (sīla)Right speechNo lying, divisive, harsh or idle talk
Right actionNo killing, stealing or sexual misconduct
Right livelihoodEarning a living without harming others
Meditation (samādhi)Right effortCultivating wholesome states, abandoning unwholesome ones
Right mindfulnessAttention to body, feelings, mind and phenomena
Right concentrationDeep meditative absorption (jhāna)

Interpretation

In the Theravada tradition the path is systematised in Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga as the training in morality, concentration and wisdom. Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions also honour the eightfold path while setting it within the bodhisattva ideal. "Right" translates sammā, meaning correct, complete or perfect — the factors are not commandments but skills to be developed until the path culminates in insight and release.
