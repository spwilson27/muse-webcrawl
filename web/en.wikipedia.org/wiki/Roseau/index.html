Roseau (Dominican Creole: Wozo) is the capital and largest city of Dominica, an island country in the Caribbean Sea. It lies on the island's leeward (western) coast at the mouth of the Roseau River, in Saint George Parish, and is the country's political, commercial and port centre, with a population of roughly 15,000 — about a fifth of the national total.

History

The site was originally a Kalinago settlement called Sairi. French colonists established a town in the early 18th century, naming it for the reeds (roseaux) growing along the river. Roseau passed between French and British hands with the island until Britain retained Dominica in 1783; French influence survives in the grid of streets, the Roman Catholic cathedral built of volcanic stone, and French Creole place names. Fires repeatedly destroyed the town — notably in 1805 and 1979 — and hurricanes have also caused severe damage, most recently Hurricane Maria in 2017, which devastated Dominica and left Roseau largely without power and water for an extended period.

Geography and sights

The city is compact, mixing Georgian and French colonial architecture with modern buildings, and is notable for its largely wooden houses with overhanging balconies and jalousie shutters. Landmarks include the Old Market (a former slave market, now a craft market), the Dominica Museum, the Botanic Gardens — famous for an African baobab and for a school bus crushed by a fallen tree during Hurricane David in 1979 — Fort Young (now a hotel), and the viewpoint of Morne Bruce above the city. Roseau is the gateway for excursions to Trafalgar Falls, the Boiling Lake in Morne Trois Pitons National Park, and the island's whale-watching grounds.

Economy

Roseau hosts the government, the deep-water port at Woodbridge Bay, and the headquarters of the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank's agency for Dominica. Tourism (including cruise ships), bananas and other agricultural exports, and services dominate the economy.
