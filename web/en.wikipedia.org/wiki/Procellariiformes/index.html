Procellariiformes

Albatrosses, the largest members of the order, are masters of dynamic soaring.
OrderProcellariiformes
FamiliesDiomedeidae (albatrosses), Procellariidae (petrels and shearwaters), Hydrobatidae (storm petrels), Pelecanoididae (diving petrels)
Common nameTubenoses

Procellariiformes is an order of seabirds that comprises four families: the albatrosses, the petrels and shearwaters, the storm petrels, and the diving petrels. Members of the group are often collectively called petrels or "tubenoses," after the prominent tube-shaped nostrils on their bills.

Characteristics

Procellariiformes are among the most oceanic of birds, spending most of their lives over the open sea and returning to land only to breed, usually on remote islands. They share a number of adaptations: a keen sense of smell — unusual among birds — used to locate food and their home colonies; salt glands that excrete excess salt through the nasal tubes; and stomach oils, an energy-rich substance produced in the proventriculus that can be sprayed at predators and fed to chicks.

Biology

All species lay a single egg per breeding attempt, and both parents share incubation and chick-rearing duties that can last for months. Many species are long-lived and slow to mature, making them vulnerable to introduced predators at breeding colonies and to mortality in longline fisheries. The order includes both the largest flying seabirds, the great albatrosses with wingspans over three metres, and the smallest, the least storm petrel, weighing only about 20 grams.

Distribution

The group is found over all the world's oceans, but the greatest diversity occurs in the Southern Hemisphere, particularly around New Zealand and the subantarctic islands. Several species undertake some of the longest migrations known in the animal kingdom.
