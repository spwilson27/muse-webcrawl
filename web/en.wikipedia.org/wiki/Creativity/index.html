Creativity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Creativity is the ability to produce ideas, artefacts or solutions that are both novel (original, unexpected) and valuable (useful, appropriate or meaningful within a context). It is studied in psychology, cognitive science, education, business and the arts, and is involved in domains from scientific discovery and invention to art, music, humour and everyday problem-solving. Most definitions converge on the two criteria of originality and effectiveness — sometimes called the "standard definition" of creativity.

Creativity involves generating ideas that are both new and useful.

Approaches to study

J. P. Guilford's 1950 presidential address to the American Psychological Association is usually credited with launching modern creativity research, distinguishing divergent thinking (generating many varied ideas) from convergent thinking (finding the single correct answer). Research is often organised by the "four Ps": the creative person, process, product and press (environment).

The creative process

Graham Wallas (1926) proposed a classic four-stage model: preparation, incubation (unconscious processing during a break), illumination (the sudden "aha" insight) and verification (testing and elaborating the idea). Later models include Geneplore (generation and exploration) and Amabile's componential model, which emphasises domain skills, creativity-relevant processes and intrinsic motivation. Techniques for enhancing creative output include brainstorming, lateral thinking (Edward de Bono), synectics and TRIZ.

Measurement

Creativity is assessed by divergent-thinking tests (such as the Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking and the Alternative Uses Task), by expert judgement of products (Amabile's Consensual Assessment Technique), and by biographical measures distinguishing everyday ("little-c") creativity from eminent ("Big-C") achievement; Kaufman and Beghetto add "mini-c" (personal insight) and "Pro-c" (professional) levels.

Creativity, intelligence and mental health

Creativity correlates modestly with intelligence up to a point (the debated "threshold hypothesis") and with personality traits such as openness to experience. Research has explored links between creativity and mood disorders, largely in eminent creators, though findings are mixed. Neuroscience studies associate creative cognition with cooperation between the default mode and executive control networks.

In organisations and education

Creativity is prized in innovation-driven economies; organisations attempt to foster it through psychological safety, autonomy, diverse teams and tolerance of failure, while schools debate whether creativity can be taught as a general skill or only within domains. Artificial intelligence has recently raised new questions about machine creativity and the evaluation of AI-generated work.
