Travel

Travel is the movement of people between distant geographical locations. Travel can be done by foot, bicycle, automobile, train, boat, bus, airplane, ship or other means, with or without luggage, and can be one way or round trip. Travel can also include relatively short stays between successive movements, as in the case of tourism.

Purposes

People travel for many reasons: pleasure and tourism, recreation, holidays, research and education, business, trade, commuting, migration, religious pilgrimage, medical treatment, and flight from war or disaster. Motivations studied by tourism scholars include relaxation, exploration, cultural experience, social interaction, and prestige.

History

Travel for pleasure has ancient roots — the Romans visited spas and coastal resorts, and pilgrimage routes such as the Camino de Santiago drew travellers throughout the Middle Ages. The aristocratic Grand Tour of the 17th–19th centuries made travel part of education. Steamships and railways in the 19th century, and organised tours pioneered by Thomas Cook, opened travel to the middle classes; the jet age and low-cost airlines after the mid-20th century made international travel a mass activity. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, international tourist arrivals reached about 1.4–1.5 billion per year.

Safety and impact

Modern travel raises issues of safety, public health, and environmental impact — air travel is a significant source of greenhouse gas emissions — as well as economic effects on destinations, both positive (income and employment) and negative (overtourism).
