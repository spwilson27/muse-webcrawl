Philip I of Castile (22 July 1478 – 25 September 1506), known as Philip the Handsome (Felipe el Hermoso), was the first Habsburg king of Castile. The son of Maximilian I, Holy Roman Emperor, and Mary of Burgundy, he inherited the Burgundian Netherlands as a child and became King of Castile in 1504 through his marriage to Joanna, heir of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile.

Life

Philip grew up in the Burgundian court at Mechelen under his step-grandmother Margaret of York. In 1496 he married the Spanish infanta Joanna in a double marriage alliance between the Habsburgs and Spain. He ruled the Netherlands in his own right and governed with Flemish advisers, which later caused friction in Spain.

On Isabella's death in 1504 Joanna inherited Castile, and Philip was recognised as king jure uxoris alongside her under the Concord of Salamanca (1505), though her father Ferdinand contested their rule and Joanna's mental state was increasingly questioned. After a brief period of joint rule and the Concord of Villafáfila, in which Ferdinand withdrew to Aragon, Philip died suddenly at Burgos in September 1506, aged 28 — officially of typhoid fever, though poisoning was rumoured.

Legacy

Philip's marriage united the Burgundian, Austrian and Spanish inheritances in his children. His eldest son became Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor and King of Spain; his younger son became Ferdinand I. His daughters included Eleanor, Queen of France, and Mary, Queen of Hungary. Joanna, declared mad, remained nominal queen of Castile until her death in 1555 but never governed again.
