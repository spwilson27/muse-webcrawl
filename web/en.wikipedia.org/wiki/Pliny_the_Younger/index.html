Pliny the Younger

Gaius Plinius Caecilius Secundus, better known as Pliny the Younger (61 – c. 113 AD), was a lawyer, author and magistrate of Ancient Rome. His hundreds of surviving letters are a valuable historical source.

Pliny the Younger
Born61 AD, Comum, Italia
Diedc. 113 AD, Bithynia
OccupationLawyer, magistrate, author
RelativePliny the Elder (uncle)

Life

Born in Comum (Como), he was adopted by his uncle Pliny the Elder. He served as consul in 100 AD and governor of Bithynia-Pontus under Trajan.

Letters

His Epistulae include the famous eyewitness account of the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD, in which his uncle died, and correspondence with Trajan on Christians — an early pagan reference to early Christianity.
