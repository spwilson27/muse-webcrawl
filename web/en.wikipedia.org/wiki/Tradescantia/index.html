Tradescantia is a genus of 85 species of herbaceous perennial wildflowers in the family Commelinaceae, native to the Americas from southern Canada to northern Argentina, with the centre of diversity in Mexico and Central America. The genus was named by Carl Linnaeus in honour of the English naturalists John Tradescant the Elder and John Tradescant the Younger. Common names include spiderwort and inchplant (or wandering Jew, a name now often avoided because of its antisemitic origin).

Description

Species are weakly upright to scrambling perennials, growing 30–60 cm (12–24 in) tall, often rooting at the nodes. The leaves are long, thin and blade-shaped to lanceolate, sometimes purple beneath. The flowers, which usually last only a day, have three petals in shades of white, pink, mauve or blue and three hairy stamens — the easily observed stamen hairs of Tradescantia virginiana are a classic school demonstration of living cell cytoplasm. Many species flower in the morning and close by afternoon.

Cultivation and uses

Tradescantias are popular houseplants and ground cover. Tradescantia pallida (purple heart), T. zebrina and T. fluminensis are grown for their colourful trailing foliage, while T. virginiana and the Andersoniana hybrids are hardy garden spiderworts. They are exceptionally easy to propagate from cuttings, a stem section rooting readily in water or soil — hence "inchplant". T. pallida is also used as a bioindicator of air pollution, its stamen hairs changing colour in response to mutagens and radiation.

Weediness

In warm climates several species have become invasive weeds. Tradescantia fluminensis (small-leaf spiderwort) smothers forest understories in New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, and the southeastern United States; T. zebrina and T. spathacea (oyster plant, now sometimes placed in Tradescantia) are similarly invasive in some regions. Because fragments regenerate, mechanical removal must be thorough.

Species

Selected species include T. bracteata, T. brevifolia, T. buckleyi, T. cerinthoides, T. commelinoides, T. crassula, T. fluminensis, T. gigantea, T. hirsuticaulis, T. occidentalis, T. ohiensis, T. ozarkana, T. pallida, T. pinetorum, T. spathacea, T. subaspera, T. virginiana and T. zebrina.
