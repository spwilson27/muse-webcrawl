Maurice Marie Émile Leblanc (11 December 1864 – 6 November 1941) was a French novelist and writer of short stories, known primarily as the creator of the fictional gentleman thief and detective Arsène Lupin, often described as a French counterpart to Arthur Conan Doyle's creation Sherlock Holmes.

Biography

Leblanc was born in Rouen, Normandy, the son of a shipowner. He was educated at the Lycée Pierre Corneille in Rouen and studied law in Paris, but soon abandoned a legal career to become a journalist and writer. He moved to Paris and initially wrote short stories and novels that attracted critical praise but limited sales, encouraged by writers such as Guy de Maupassant, whose work he admired.

His breakthrough came in 1905, when the editor of the magazine Je sais tout commissioned a series of adventure stories. The result was "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin", published on 15 July 1905, which introduced the gentleman burglar who would make Leblanc famous throughout France and abroad. Lupin appeared in a long series of novels and story collections over the following decades, including Arsène Lupin, Gentleman Burglar (1907), Arsène Lupin versus Herlock Sholmès (1908), The Hollow Needle (1909), 813 (1910), The Crystal Stopper (1912) and The Countess of Cagliostro (1924).

Leblanc was awarded the Légion d'honneur for his services to French literature. He bought a house in Étretat, Normandy, whose setting inspired several Lupin stories; it is now a museum, the Clos Lupin. Leblanc died of pneumonia in Perpignan in 1941.

Arsène Lupin

Lupin is a master of disguise and a burglar of great ingenuity who robs the rich and corrupt while remaining, in his own phrase, a "gentleman thief". Unlike Doyle's Holmes, Lupin operates on the other side of the law, though he frequently outwits criminals and foreign spies as well as the police, represented by his long-suffering pursuer Inspector Ganimard. The character's popularity in France rivals that of Holmes in the English-speaking world, and the stories have been adapted many times for stage, film and television, including the Japanese manga and anime franchise Lupin III and the 2021 Netflix series Lupin, starring Omar Sy.
