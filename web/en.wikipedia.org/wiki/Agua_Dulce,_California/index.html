Agua Dulce (Spanish for "sweet water") is an unincorporated community and census-designated place in Los Angeles County, California, United States. It lies in the Sierra Pelona Valley northeast of Santa Clarita, at an elevation of roughly 2,500 feet (770 m), and had a population of about 3,400 at the 2020 census. The rural community is known for its equestrian character, wineries, and the dramatic tilted rock formations of Vasquez Rocks.

Geography and history

Agua Dulce sits along State Route 14 (the Antelope Valley Freeway) between Santa Clarita and Palmdale, in a high-desert valley of the western Mojave fringe. The area was inhabited by the Tataviam people before Spanish colonization; the name derives from the Agua Dulce ("sweet water") springs and creek. Ranching and small-scale gold mining occupied the area in the nineteenth century, and the community developed as a rural residential and agricultural area in the twentieth.

Vasquez Rocks

The community's most famous landmark is Vasquez Rocks Natural Area Park, a county park of striking sandstone formations uplifted along the San Andreas Fault system. The rocks are named for Tiburcio Vásquez, a Californio bandit who used them as a hideout in the 1870s. The site is a celebrated filming location, appearing in numerous Westerns and famously in the Star Trek episode "Arena", in which Captain Kirk fights the Gorn; it has also appeared in films such as Blazing Saddles and The Flintstones.

Other features

Agua Dulce is home to several boutique wineries and the Agua Dulce Winery, as well as animal sanctuaries and film ranches. The Pacific Crest Trail passes through the area near Vasquez Rocks.
