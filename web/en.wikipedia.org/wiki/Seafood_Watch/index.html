Seafood Watch is a program of the Monterey Bay Aquarium that assesses the environmental sustainability of fisheries and aquaculture operations and publishes recommendations to help consumers, chefs and businesses choose ocean-friendly seafood. Founded in 1999, it became one of the most influential sustainable-seafood guides in North America, distributed as colour-coded pocket guides and a smartphone app, and through partnerships with retailers, food-service companies and chefs.

Method

Seafood Watch scientists assess wild-capture fisheries against criteria including stock status, bycatch and discards, habitat and ecosystem impacts, and management effectiveness; farmed seafood is assessed on factors such as feed use, escapes, disease and parasite transfer (including sea lice), pollution and chemical use. Each seafood item — specific to species, region and fishing or farming method — is rated "Best Choice" (green), "Good Alternative" (yellow) or "Avoid" (red). Seafood certified by bodies such as the Marine Stewardship Council or Aquaculture Stewardship Council is recognised as at least a Good Alternative equivalent.

Influence

The program has been credited with shifting procurement policies of major buyers (including large retailers and food service firms) and popularising the traffic-light model of seafood labelling used by similar guides worldwide. It has also drawn criticism at times from fishing communities who dispute individual assessments, and participates in the broader sustainable seafood movement alongside certification and fishery-improvement projects.
