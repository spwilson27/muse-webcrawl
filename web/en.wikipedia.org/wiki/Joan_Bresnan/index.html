Joan Wanda Bresnan (born August 22, 1945) is an American linguist and the Sadie Dernham Patek Professor in Humanities Emerita at Stanford University. She is best known as one of the architects, together with Ronald Kaplan, of lexical-functional grammar (LFG), a non-transformational theory of grammar developed from the late 1970s.

Bresnan received her PhD from MIT in 1972, where she initially worked within transformational grammar before becoming one of its prominent critics. Her work on passive constructions and on the relationship between syntax and lexical structure helped motivate the lexicalist approach of LFG.

Research

Her research spans theoretical syntax, the syntax of Bantu and Australian Aboriginal languages, and probabilistic and corpus-based approaches to grammar. She has studied the English dative alternation using statistical methods and worked extensively on the Austronesian language Chamorro and on Malagasy.

Honours

Bresnan is a fellow of the Linguistic Society of America and served as its president in 1999.
