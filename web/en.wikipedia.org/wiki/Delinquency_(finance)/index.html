Delinquency (finance)

In finance, delinquency is a situation in which a borrower fails to make a payment on a debt — such as a mortgage, credit card, or other loan — by its due date. An account is typically described as delinquent in stages measured in days past due (30, 60, 90 days and beyond). Prolonged delinquency can lead to default, foreclosure, repossession, or assignment to a debt collection agency.

Measurement

Lenders and regulators track delinquency rates — the percentage of loans in a portfolio that are past due — as an indicator of credit quality and economic conditions. Statistics published by bodies such as the Federal Reserve and mortgage industry associations recorded sharp rises in delinquency during the subprime mortgage crisis of 2007–2009.

Consequences

Delinquency usually results in late fees, penalty interest, and negative reporting to credit bureaus, lowering the borrower's credit score. Under agreements such as credit card contracts, a payment typically must be more than a set number of days late before different consequences apply, and consumer-protection law in many jurisdictions regulates collection practices for delinquent debts.
