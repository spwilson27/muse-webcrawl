Sotos syndrome

Sotos syndrome
Also called: Cerebral gigantism
Cause: Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in NSD1 (5q35)
Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (usually de novo)
Features: Childhood overgrowth, macrocephaly, learning disability, advanced bone age

Sotos syndrome is a rare genetic disorder characterised by excessive physical growth during the early years of life. It was described by Juan Sotos and colleagues in 1964 and is sometimes called cerebral gigantism.

Signs and symptoms

Children with Sotos syndrome grow rapidly, are significantly taller than peers in early childhood, and have a large head (macrocephaly) with a characteristic facial appearance: a prominent forehead, high hairline, long narrow face and pointed chin. Development is typically delayed, with learning disabilities ranging from mild to severe, low muscle tone and speech delay. Bone age is advanced, so final adult height often falls within the normal range as growth decelerates after puberty. Associated features can include scoliosis, seizures, heart and kidney anomalies, and a slightly increased risk of certain tumours.

Genetics and management

Most cases result from new (de novo) heterozygous mutations or deletions affecting NSD1 on chromosome 5q35, a gene encoding a histone methyltransferase. Diagnosis is clinical and confirmed by genetic testing. There is no cure; management is supportive, with early intervention, educational support and surveillance for complications.
