The Kingdom of Württemberg (German: Königreich Württemberg) was a German state that existed from 1806 to 1918, located in the area that is now Baden-Württemberg. It grew out of the Duchy of Württemberg, which was elevated first to an electorate (1803) and then, under Napoleon's patronage, to a kingdom on 1 January 1806.

Napoleonic expansion

Duke Frederick II, as Frederick I, more than doubled his territory through the mediatisation of imperial cities and ecclesiastical lands, joining Napoleon's Confederation of the Rhine and supplying troops for French campaigns, including the disastrous Russian campaign of 1812. Switching sides in 1813 (Treaty of Fulda), he kept his gains at the Congress of Vienna, and Württemberg joined the German Confederation.

Constitutional monarchy

William I (r. 1816–1864) granted a constitution in 1819 that, after a struggle with the estates, created one of the more liberal constitutional frameworks in Germany, with a bicameral Landtag. The kingdom industrialised along the Neckar, promoted education, and in 1845 began railway construction; emigration was heavy in years of hardship. During the 1866 Austro-Prussian War Württemberg sided with Austria; defeated, it paid an indemnity and entered a secret military alliance with Prussia.

German Empire and end

Württemberg troops fought in the Franco-Prussian War, and the kingdom entered the German Empire in 1871, retaining reserved rights (Reservatrechte) such as its own army corps in peacetime, postal service, and railway administration. Under William II (r. 1891–1918), a popular and relatively liberal monarch, Stuttgart grew as an industrial and cultural centre. William abdicated on 30 November 1918 during the German revolution, and Württemberg became the Free People's State of Württemberg in the Weimar Republic.
