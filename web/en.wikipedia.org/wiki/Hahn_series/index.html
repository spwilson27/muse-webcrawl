Hahn series

In mathematics, a Hahn series is a formal infinite series with coefficients in a field and exponents drawn from an ordered abelian group, subject to the condition that the set of exponents occurring with non-zero coefficient (the support) is well-ordered. Hahn series generalise formal power series, Laurent series and Puiseux series, and were introduced by Hans Hahn in 1907 in the course of his work on ordered groups and non-Archimedean fields.

Definition

Let K be a field and G an ordered abelian group. A Hahn series is a formal sum ∑γ∈G aγ Tγ with aγ ∈ K, such that the support {γ : aγ ≠ 0} is a well-ordered subset of G. The well-ordering condition guarantees that addition and multiplication of such series are well-defined, and the resulting object forms a field, denoted K[[TG]].

Properties

The field of Hahn series carries a natural valuation whose value group is G, given by the least element of the support. If K is algebraically closed and G is divisible, then the Hahn series field is algebraically closed. Hahn series fields are important in valuation theory and model theory of valued fields, and the field of surreal numbers can be described in terms of Hahn series. The special case in which G is the group of rational numbers yields series closely related to the Levi-Civita field.
