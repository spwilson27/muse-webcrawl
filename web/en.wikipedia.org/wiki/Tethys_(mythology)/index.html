Tethys (mythology)

Tethys, Titan goddess of fresh water and mother of the rivers and Oceanids.

In Greek mythology, Tethys (Ancient Greek: Τηθύς, romanized: Tēthýs) is a Titan daughter of Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth). She is a sea goddess and the wife of Oceanus, her brother. Together they are the parents of the great rivers of the world and of the three thousand Oceanid nymphs.

Tethys plays almost no active part in Greek myth and had no established cult; her importance lies in her position in the divine genealogy as mother of the waters of the world. In the Iliad, she and Oceanus are described as the origin of the gods, and Hera visits them at the ends of the earth during the Trojan War.

Offspring

By Oceanus, Tethys bore the Potamoi — the river gods, including Nilus (the Nile), Alpheus, and Eridanos — and the Oceanids, among them Styx, Dione, Metis, and Electra. Through this vast progeny she became the grandmother of many figures of Greek myth.

Name and legacy

The ancient Tethys Ocean of geology is named for her, as is Saturn's moon Tethys, discovered by Giovanni Domenico Cassini in 1684. In late antique art, Tethys appears in mosaics as a personification of the sea, sometimes with wings on her forehead and a ship's rudder.
