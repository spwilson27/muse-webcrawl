Zakarpattia Oblast

Zakarpattia Oblast (Ukrainian: Закарпатська область), also known as Transcarpathia, is an oblast (province) in western Ukraine, mostly coterminous with the historical region of Carpathian Ruthenia. Its administrative center is the city of Uzhhorod. The oblast has an area of about 12,800 km² and a population of roughly 1.24 million.

Geography

The oblast lies on the southwestern slopes of the Carpathian Mountains and in the adjacent lowlands of the Pannonian Plain. It borders Poland, Slovakia, Hungary and Romania, as well as the Ukrainian oblasts of Lviv and Ivano-Frankivsk. Mount Hoverla, the highest peak in Ukraine at 2,061 metres, stands on its border with Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast.

History

For centuries the region was part of the Kingdom of Hungary. After the First World War it was incorporated into Czechoslovakia as Subcarpathian Ruthenia, was briefly independent as Carpatho-Ukraine in March 1939, and was annexed by Hungary before being ceded to the Soviet Union in 1945, becoming part of the Ukrainian SSR. It has been part of independent Ukraine since 1991.

Demographics

The population is predominantly Ukrainian, with significant Hungarian, Romanian, Roma, Russian, Slovak and Rusyn minorities. The region is notable for its ethnic and religious diversity and for the Rusyn population, whose identity and language status remain subjects of discussion.
