Kruskal–Wallis test

The Kruskal–Wallis test (also called the Kruskal–Wallis H test or one-way ANOVA on ranks) is a non-parametric method for testing whether samples originate from the same distribution. It is used for comparing two or more independent samples of equal or different sample sizes. It extends the Mann–Whitney U test, which is used for comparing only two groups. The parametric equivalent of the Kruskal–Wallis test is the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA).

The test is named after William Kruskal and W. Allen Wallis, who described it in a 1952 paper in the Journal of the American Statistical Association.

Method

All individual observations from the k groups are pooled and ranked, with average ranks assigned in the case of ties. Let N be the total number of observations, ni the number of observations in group i, and Ri the sum of ranks in group i. The test statistic is

H = (12 / (N(N+1))) · Σ (Ri² / ni) − 3(N+1).

Under the null hypothesis that the groups have the same distribution, H is approximately chi-squared distributed with k − 1 degrees of freedom, provided the sample sizes are not too small. A correction factor is applied when there are many ties.

Interpretation and follow-up

A significant Kruskal–Wallis test indicates that at least one sample stochastically dominates another sample, but does not identify which pairs differ. Post-hoc comparisons are usually performed with Dunn's test or pairwise Mann–Whitney tests with an appropriate adjustment for multiple comparisons. Strictly, the test compares mean ranks; it is a test of medians only under additional assumptions about the shapes of the distributions.
