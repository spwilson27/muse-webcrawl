Atorvastatin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Atorvastatin, sold under the brand name Lipitor among others, is a statin medication used to prevent cardiovascular disease in those at high risk and to treat abnormal lipid levels. It is one of the most widely prescribed drugs in the world and appears on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines. Pfizer's Lipitor became the best-selling pharmaceutical in history, with peak annual sales exceeding US$12 billion before its patent expired in 2011.

Medical uses

Atorvastatin is used for the treatment of dyslipidaemia, lowering LDL cholesterol and triglycerides while modestly raising HDL cholesterol, and for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular events including myocardial infarction and stroke in people with risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes or smoking.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include headache, nausea, constipation and muscle pain. Serious but rare effects include myopathy and rhabdomyolysis, and liver enzyme elevations. Risk of muscle symptoms increases with interacting drugs that inhibit CYP3A4.

Mechanism of action

Like other statins, atorvastatin competitively inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol biosynthesis in the liver. Reduced hepatic cholesterol upregulates LDL receptors, increasing clearance of LDL particles from the blood.

History

Atorvastatin was synthesised at Parke-Davis under the direction of a team including Bruce Roth, and first described in a 1990 patent. It was marketed by Pfizer after its acquisition of Warner-Lambert in 2000.
