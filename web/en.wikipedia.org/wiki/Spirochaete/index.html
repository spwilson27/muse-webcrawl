Spirochaete

Spirochaetes
DomainBacteria
PhylumSpirochaetota
ClassSpirochaetia

A spirochaete (also spelled spirochete) is a member of the phylum Spirochaetota, a group of distinctive Gram-negative bacteria characterised by long, helically coiled cells and a unique mode of motility. Their flagella, called endoflagella or axial filaments, run lengthwise between the cell membrane and an outer membrane, rotating the cell body like a corkscrew and allowing movement through viscous media such as mucus and connective tissue.

Notable genera

Several spirochaetes are important human pathogens. Treponema pallidum causes syphilis; Borrelia burgdorferi and related species cause Lyme disease, and other Borrelia cause relapsing fever; Leptospira species cause leptospirosis. Many other spirochaetes are free-living in aquatic environments or live harmlessly in the guts of animals, including termites, where they assist in digestion.

Characteristics

Spirochaetes range from about 3 to 500 micrometres in length. They are chemoheterotrophic and may be aerobic or anaerobic. Their unusual morphology and internal flagellar arrangement distinguish them from other bacterial groups, and their corkscrew motility helps pathogenic species penetrate host tissues.
