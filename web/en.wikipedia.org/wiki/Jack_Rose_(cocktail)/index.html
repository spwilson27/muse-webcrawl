Jack Rose (cocktail)

Jack Rose

Type: Cocktail

Base spirit: Applejack (apple brandy)

Served: Straight up, in a cocktail glass

Ingredients: Applejack, lemon or lime juice, grenadine

IBA official cocktail: Yes

The Jack Rose is a classic cocktail containing applejack, lemon or lime juice, and grenadine. It is an official cocktail of the International Bartenders Association. Popular in the 1920s and 1930s, it is named — depending on the account — for the pink ("rose") color given by the grenadine, for the "jacqueminot" rose, for an infamous gambler, or for a New Jersey bartender; its exact origin is disputed, though it is often associated with New York's Harvey's restaurant in the early twentieth century.

Preparation

The IBA recipe calls for 6 parts applejack, 1 part lemon juice, and a dash of grenadine, shaken with ice and strained into a chilled cocktail glass. The quality of the drink depends heavily on real grenadine made from pomegranate and on a robust barrel-aged apple brandy such as Laird's, the historic New Jersey applejack.

History and cultural references

The Jack Rose rose to fame during Prohibition, when applejack was widely produced in New Jersey and New York. It was a favorite of John Steinbeck, appears in Ernest Hemingway's The Sun Also Rises (1926), where Jake Barnes drinks Jack Roses at the Crillon bar in Paris, and is mentioned by Raymond Chandler. The drink faded after mid-century as applejack production declined, but the craft-cocktail revival of the 2000s restored it to bar menus, helped by renewed production of quality American apple brandy.
