Atatürk's reforms

Atatürk's reforms (Turkish: Atatürk İnkılâpları) are a series of political, legal, cultural, social and economic policy changes that were designed to convert the Republic of Turkey into a secular, modern nation-state, implemented under the leadership of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk in accordance with Kemalist ideology. His reforms involved the complete abolition of the Ottoman Empire's ancien régime and the establishment of the modern Turkish state.

Political and legal reforms

The Turkish Grand National Assembly abolished the Ottoman sultanate on 1 November 1922 and the caliphate on 3 March 1924, ending the Ottoman dynasty's six centuries of rule. The Republic of Turkey had been proclaimed on 29 October 1923, with Ankara as its capital. A new constitution followed in 1924. Swiss-style civil law, an Italian-based penal code, and German-influenced commercial law replaced the sharia-based legal system, and in 1926 the Gregorian calendar and Western numerals were adopted.

Social and cultural reforms

The Hat Law of 1925 promoted Western-style dress; religious orders and dervish lodges were closed; and the Surname Law of 1934 required all citizens to adopt family names, with the Assembly bestowing the surname "Atatürk" ("Father of the Turks") on Mustafa Kemal. In 1928 the Arabic-based Ottoman Turkish alphabet was replaced by a Latin-based Turkish alphabet, and a nationwide literacy campaign followed. Women's rights advanced markedly: women gained the right to vote in local elections in 1930 and in national elections in 1934, earlier than in several European countries.

Educational and economic reforms

The Law of Unification of Education (1924) placed all schools under the Ministry of Education and closed the religious madrasas. University reform in 1933 modernised Istanbul University with the help of émigré academics from Europe. Economically, the reforms combined initial liberal policies with etatism (state-led industrialisation) in the 1930s, building railways, factories, and a national banking system.

Legacy

The reforms laid the foundations of modern Turkey and remain central to the country's political identity; their principles are embodied in the Turkish constitution. They have also been debated ever since, particularly regarding secularism, the role of religion in public life, and the treatment of ethnic minorities.
