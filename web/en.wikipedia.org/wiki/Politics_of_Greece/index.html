Politics of Greece

Polity type: Unitary parliamentary republic

Constitution: Constitution of Greece (1975)

Head of state: President of the Hellenic Republic

Head of government: Prime Minister of Greece

The politics of Greece take place within the framework of a parliamentary republic. The modern Greek state was founded in 1830, and the current constitutional order dates to 1975, following the fall of the military junta and the abolition of the monarchy by referendum in 1974.

Constitution

The Constitution of 1975, revised several times (notably in 1986, 2001 and 2019), establishes Greece as a parliamentary republic. Amendments in 1986 curtailed the powers of the president in favour of the prime minister and parliament.

Executive branch

The President of the Republic is head of state, elected by parliament for a five-year term. Executive power is exercised chiefly by the Prime Minister and the government. The president appoints the prime minister — normally the leader of the party controlling a majority in parliament — and formally appoints and dismisses ministers on the prime minister's advice.

Legislature

The Hellenic Parliament is a unicameral legislature of 300 members elected for four-year terms through a system of reinforced proportional representation, in which the winning party historically received a seat bonus. Greece has a multi-party system dominated in recent decades by the centre-right New Democracy, the centre-left PASOK, and, since the debt crisis, the left-wing Syriza.

Judiciary

The judiciary is independent and organised into civil, criminal and administrative courts, headed respectively by the Court of Cassation (Areios Pagos) and the Council of State.
