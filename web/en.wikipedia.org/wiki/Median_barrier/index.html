Median barrier

A concrete Jersey barrier used as a median barrier on a divided highway

A median barrier is a longitudinal barrier placed in the median (central reservation) of a divided highway to prevent vehicles from crossing into oncoming traffic. Median barriers are a key road safety device: head-on collisions resulting from median crossovers are among the most severe crash types, and barriers greatly reduce their frequency.

Types

Concrete barriers, such as the Jersey barrier and F-shape profiles, are rigid and deflect very little on impact, making them suitable for narrow medians. Their sloped faces are designed to redirect vehicles back toward the roadway and reduce the likelihood of rollover or vaulting. Metal guardrail (W-beam or thrie-beam) is semi-rigid and absorbs energy by deforming, requiring more deflection room. Cable barriers, consisting of steel wire ropes mounted on weak posts, are flexible systems that capture or redirect vehicles with low deceleration forces and are relatively inexpensive, but require wide medians and must be repaired after impacts.

Placement and warrants

Highway agencies decide whether to install a median barrier based on median width and traffic volume: wide, low-volume medians may be left unprotected, while narrow medians on busy freeways almost always receive barriers. Guidance such as the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials' Roadside Design Guide provides warrants relating average daily traffic to median width. Movable concrete barrier systems, transferred by a zipper machine, are used in some corridors to vary lane allocation by time of day.

Effectiveness

Studies consistently find that median barriers reduce cross-median fatal crashes dramatically, though they increase the total number of (much less severe) barrier strikes. Cable barriers in particular have been credited with preventing the large majority of would-be crossover fatalities where installed.
