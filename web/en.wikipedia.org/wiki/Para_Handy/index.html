Para Handy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Para Handy, the anglicised Gaelic form of Pàra Thapaidh ("Peter the clever"), is a fictional character created by Scottish writer Neil Munro. He is the captain of the Vital Spark, a Clyde puffer plying the waters of the Firth of Clyde and the west coast of Scotland, and the central figure of the much-loved "Para Handy" stories first published in the Glasgow Evening News from 1905.

The stories

The tales, written under Munro's pen name "Hugh Foulis", are narrated in a dry, laconic style. Para Handy — whose real name is Peter Macfarlane — is a shrewd, silver-tongued Highland skipper who constantly schemes to avoid work, outwit the boat's owner, and obtain cargoes (and whisky) on his own terms. His long-suffering crew consists of the innocent engineer Dan Macphail, the gloomy mate Dougie (the "Tar"), and the ship's boy, Sunny Jim.

The stories were collected in The Vital Spark (1906), In Highland Harbours (1911), and Hurricane Jack of the Vital Spark (1923). They gently satirise Highland and island life, temperance campaigns, bureaucracy, and the pretensions of the yacht-owning classes, and remain classics of Scottish comic writing.

Adaptations

The stories have been adapted many times for stage, radio, and television. The BBC Scotland television series The Vital Spark (1965–1967) starred Roddy McMillan as Para Handy, and a further series, Para Handy – Master Mariner, ran in 1994 with Gregor Fisher in the title role. A 1954 film, The Maggie, directed by Alexander Mackendrick for Ealing Studios, drew on the puffer tales, with Paul Douglas as an American businessman outwitted by a puffer crew.

Legacy

Para Handy and the Vital Spark have become enduring symbols of Clyde maritime heritage. A restored puffer named Vital Spark is preserved at Inveraray Maritime Museum, and the character is commemorated in place-names and pub names across the west of Scotland.
