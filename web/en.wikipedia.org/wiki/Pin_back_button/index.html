A pin-back button is a button or badge worn on clothing, attached by a safety pin formed from the button's backing. Pin-back buttons are typically round, flat, and bear a design or slogan, and are used for political campaigning, advertising, identification, and personal expression.

History

Political campaign buttons date to the era of George Washington, whose supporters wore brass clothing buttons engraved with slogans. The modern pin-back button was created in the 1890s by the Whitehead & Hoag Company of Newark, New Jersey, which patented a design in which a printed paper image was protected by a transparent celluloid cover and held in a metal shell with a pin back. The inexpensive buttons were produced in huge numbers for the 1896 US presidential campaign of William McKinley and William Jennings Bryan.

Uses

Pin-backs became a staple of political campaigns, protest movements, and advertising, and later of fan and subculture fashion. Notable examples include the smiley face buttons of the 1970s, anti-war buttons of the Vietnam era, and punk and band merchandise. Lithographed tin buttons, in which the design is printed directly on the metal, were also widely produced.

Collecting

Vintage campaign and advertising buttons are widely collected, and rare early examples can command high prices. Large collections are held by museums and by the American Political Items Collectors association.
