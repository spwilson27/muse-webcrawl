Canadian soccer league system

The Canadian soccer league system, also sometimes called the Canadian soccer pyramid, is a series of professional, semi-professional, and amateur leagues for men's association football clubs in Canada. The system is overseen by the Canadian Soccer Association. Unlike most national league systems, Canada does not have a system of promotion and relegation, reflecting the franchise-based structure shared with the United States, whose leagues sanction Canadian teams at the top levels.

Structure

Three Canadian clubs — Toronto FC, CF Montréal, and Vancouver Whitecaps FC — play in Major League Soccer, which is sanctioned as Division I in the United States. Canada's own Division I league is the Canadian Premier League (CPL), founded in 2017 and launched in 2019 with clubs such as Forge FC, Cavalry FC, and Pacific FC. Below the CPL, the semi-professional level is represented by regional leagues including League1 Ontario, League1 British Columbia, and the Première ligue de soccer du Québec, which feed into League1 Canada. University and college soccer operates under U Sports and the CCAA, while a broad base of provincial and local amateur leagues completes the pyramid.

Cup competitions

The Canadian Championship, contested since 2008, is the premier cup competition open to professional clubs from MLS, the CPL, and League1 Canada; its winner lifts the Voyageurs Cup and qualifies for the CONCACAF Champions Cup. The Interprovincial Championship serves the semi-professional leagues.

Women's soccer

Women's league soccer in Canada historically ran through US-based leagues and League1 competitions. The Northern Super League, a professional women's league founded by former national team player Diana Matheson, launched in 2025 with six clubs, providing Canada with a domestic professional women's top flight.
