The Imperial Japanese Army (IJA; Japanese: 大日本帝国陸軍, Dai-Nippon Teikoku Rikugun) was the official ground-based armed force of the Empire of Japan from 1871 to 1945. It was controlled by the Imperial Japanese Army General Staff Office and the Ministry of the Army, and was nominally under the authority of the Emperor of Japan as supreme commander.

History

The army was founded during the Meiji Restoration as Japan modernised its military along Western lines, initially with French and later German advisers. Conscription was introduced in 1873, ending the samurai monopoly on military service. The young army suppressed the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877 and went on to win the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) against Qing China and the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) against the Russian Empire, establishing Japan as a great power.

During the 1930s the army dominated Japanese politics; the semi-autonomous Kwantung Army staged the Mukden Incident of 1931 and invaded Manchuria, and from 1937 Japan waged full-scale war in China in the Second Sino-Japanese War, during which Japanese forces committed major atrocities including the Nanjing Massacre.

World War II

In December 1941 Japan entered World War II against the United States and its allies. The army overran Southeast Asia and much of the Pacific in the war's opening months, but from 1942 onward it was progressively driven back in campaigns including Guadalcanal, Burma, the Philippines, Iwo Jima and Okinawa. The IJA was also responsible for widespread war crimes across Asia, including the use of chemical and biological weapons by Unit 731 and the abuse of prisoners of war and civilian labourers. Following Japan's surrender in September 1945, the Imperial Japanese Army was dissolved; its successor in the postwar era is the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force, established in 1954.

Organisation

At its peak the army numbered over six million men, organised into area armies, armies, divisions and independent units, with its own air service, the Imperial Japanese Army Air Service. The army competed for resources and strategic priority with the Imperial Japanese Navy, a rivalry that significantly shaped Japanese strategy.
