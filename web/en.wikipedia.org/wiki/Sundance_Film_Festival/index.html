Sundance Film Festival

The Sundance Film Festival is an annual film festival organized by the Sundance Institute in Utah, United States. Held each January in Park City and Salt Lake City, it is the largest independent film festival in the United States and one of the most prestigious film festivals in the world. The festival showcases new work from American and international independent filmmakers, with competitive sections for dramatic and documentary films, shorts, and experimental work in its New Frontier section.

The Egyptian Theatre on Main Street in Park City is an iconic Sundance venue.

History

The festival began in 1978 in Salt Lake City as the Utah/US Film Festival, founded by Sterling Van Wagenen and others to showcase American-made films and the potential of independent cinema. In 1981 it moved to Park City, and in 1984 the Sundance Institute, founded by Robert Redford, took over management. The festival was renamed after Redford's character, the Sundance Kid, from Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid.

Significance

Sundance has launched the careers of many prominent directors, including Quentin Tarantino, Steven Soderbergh, the Coen brothers, Kevin Smith, and Damien Chazelle. Acclaimed films that premiered at the festival include sex, lies, and videotape (1989), Reservoir Dogs (1992), Little Miss Sunshine (2006), Whiplash (2014), and CODA (2021), which went on to win the Academy Award for Best Picture. The festival is also a major marketplace where distributors acquire independent films.

Organization

The festival is a program of the nonprofit Sundance Institute, which also runs year-round labs and fellowships for filmmakers, including the Directors Lab and Screenwriters Lab founded in 1981. In 2025 the Institute announced the festival would relocate to Boulder, Colorado, beginning in 2027, after four decades in Utah.
