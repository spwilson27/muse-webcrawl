Agos (Armenian: Ակօս, "furrow") is a bilingual Turkish–Armenian weekly newspaper published in Istanbul, Turkey. Founded on 5 April 1996 by the journalist Hrant Dink and colleagues, it was the first newspaper in the Republic of Turkey to publish in both Turkish and Armenian, and it became a prominent independent voice on minority rights, democratisation and Turkish–Armenian relations.

History

Agos addressed Turkey's Armenian community in Turkish as well as Armenian, since most Turkish Armenians speak Turkish as their first language, and it sought to open a dialogue with wider Turkish society on taboo subjects including the Armenian genocide, discrimination and the condition of minority foundations. Under Dink's editorship the paper gained a wide readership and international attention, but Dink was prosecuted for "insulting Turkishness" over his writing and became a target of nationalist hostility.

After Hrant Dink

Dink was assassinated outside the paper's offices in Şişli on 19 January 2007 by a young nationalist gunman; his funeral drew more than 100,000 mourners to Istanbul's streets in a landmark moment of public solidarity. The newspaper continued under successive editors, including Etyen Mahçupyan and Yetvart Danzikyan, remaining a small but influential part of Turkey's independent media landscape.
