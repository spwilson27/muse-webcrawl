Tiberius Sempronius Gracchus (c. 163 – 133 BC) was a Roman politician best known for his agrarian reform law, the Lex Sempronia agraria, which sought to redistribute the ager publicus to poor citizens.

Background

Son of the censor Tiberius Gracchus the Elder and Cornelia, daughter of Scipio Africanus, he served with distinction in the Third Punic War and in Spain, where his negotiated treaty saved a Roman army but was repudiated by the Senate.

Tribunate

As tribune in 133 BC he proposed limiting holdings of public land and distributing the surplus in small allotments. When fellow tribune Marcus Octavius vetoed the bill, Gracchus had him deposed—an unprecedented breach of custom. Standing for re-election, he and some 300 supporters were clubbed to death by senators led by his cousin Scipio Nasica, the first political bloodshed in Rome in centuries.

Legacy

With his brother Gaius Gracchus, Tiberius inaugurated the age of the Gracchi: the populares–optimates conflict and the use of the tribunate against the Senate that culminated in the fall of the Roman Republic.
