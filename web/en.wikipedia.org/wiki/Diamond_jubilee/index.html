Diamond jubilee

A diamond jubilee is a celebration held to mark a 60th anniversary. Traditionally, the term has been applied to the anniversary of a monarch's accession to the throne, though it is also used for other long-running institutions and events. In Britain the diamond wedding anniversary of 60 years of marriage is likewise recognised.

Monarchs

Only a small number of monarchs have reigned long enough to celebrate a diamond jubilee. Queen Victoria celebrated her Diamond Jubilee in 1897, the first such celebration for a British monarch, marked by a procession through London and services of thanksgiving across the Empire. Queen Elizabeth II celebrated her Diamond Jubilee in 2012 with a pageant on the River Thames, a concert at Buckingham Palace and the lighting of thousands of beacons. Other diamond jubilee monarchs have included King Bhumibol Adulyadej of Thailand in 2006 and Emperor Hirohito of Japan, whose sixtieth year of reign was marked in 1986 without the term jubilee being used in the British sense.

Other uses

The term is also used for the 60th anniversary of organisations, events and institutions. In wedding anniversary traditions in many countries, the 60th anniversary is the diamond anniversary; in earlier tradition the diamond anniversary was the 75th, and some systems still use diamond for 75 years.
