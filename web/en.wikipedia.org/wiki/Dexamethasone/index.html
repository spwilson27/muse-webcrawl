Dexamethasone is a fluorinated glucocorticoid medication used to treat rheumatic problems, a number of skin diseases, severe allergies, asthma, chronic obstructive lung disease, croup, brain swelling, eye pain following eye surgery, superior vena cava syndrome (a complication of some forms of cancer), and, along with antibiotics, tuberculosis. In adrenal insufficiency, it may be used in combination with a mineralocorticoid medication such as fludrocortisone. In preterm labor, it may be used to improve outcomes in the baby.

Dexamethasone was first synthesised in 1957 by Philip Showalter Hench and was approved for medical use in 1961. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. It is available as a generic medication. In 2020, results from the RECOVERY trial showed that dexamethasone reduced mortality in hospitalised patients with severe COVID-19 who required oxygen or ventilation.

Medical uses

Dexamethasone is used to treat many inflammatory and autoimmune conditions, such as rheumatoid arthritis and bronchospasm. It is also used as a chemotherapy adjunct to prevent nausea and vomiting, to reduce cerebral edema associated with brain tumors, and in high-altitude cerebral edema. Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, a decrease in numbers of platelets due to an immune problem, responds to 40 mg daily for four days.

Anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressant effects

As a glucocorticoid, dexamethasone has strong anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressant activity — roughly 25–30 times that of hydrocortisone — with minimal mineralocorticoid (salt-retaining) activity. It is therefore a preferred agent when fluid retention must be avoided, as in cerebral edema.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include mood changes, increased appetite, insomnia, elevated blood glucose, and increased risk of infection. With prolonged use, Cushing's syndrome, osteoporosis, adrenal suppression, hypertension, and cataracts may develop. Abrupt withdrawal after long-term therapy can cause adrenal crisis, so doses are tapered.

Mechanism of action

Dexamethasone binds to the cytosolic glucocorticoid receptor; the complex translocates to the nucleus and modulates transcription of genes controlling inflammation, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines and mediators while upregulating anti-inflammatory proteins.
