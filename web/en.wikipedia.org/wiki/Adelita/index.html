Adelita

TypeCorrido (folk ballad)
OriginMexican Revolution (1910–1920)
SubjectSoldaderas (women soldiers/camp followers)

La Adelita is one of the most famous corridos (folk ballads) of the Mexican Revolution. It tells the story of a young woman who follows a regiment during the war, and the song became an emblem of the soldaderas, the women who accompanied, fed, and sometimes fought alongside the revolutionary armies. The name "Adelita" itself became a generic term for such women.

Lyrics and theme

The corrido describes a sergeant who falls in love with Adelita, a brave and beautiful woman in the camp. He declares that if Adelita were to leave with another man, he would follow her by land and by sea — "by sea in a warship, by land in a military train." If he dies in battle, he asks that she weep for him and that his body be buried with the bullets of the enemy. The song blends romance, bravado, and the fatalism of soldiers at war.

Historical background

The soldaderas were essential to the revolutionary armies of leaders such as Pancho Villa and Emiliano Zapata: they foraged, cooked, carried supplies, nursed the wounded, and in some cases took up arms. One figure often associated with the song's inspiration is Adela Velarde Pérez, a nurse with the Constitutionalist army who was later recognized as a veteran of the Revolution. Whatever its precise origin, "La Adelita" endures as a symbol of the women's role in the conflict and of Mexican revolutionary culture.

Legacy

The song remains a standard of mariachi and norteño repertoire, performed at celebrations of Mexican heritage. The image of the Adelita — wearing cartridge belts crossed over her chest — is an iconic visual of the Revolution, reproduced in murals, films, and Day of the Dead imagery.
