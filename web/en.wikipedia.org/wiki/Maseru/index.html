Overview

Maseru is the capital and largest city of Lesotho, located in the northwest of the country on the Caledon River (Mohokare), which forms the border with South Africa's Free State province. Its name means 'place of red sandstone' in Sesotho.

Moshoeshoe I, founder of the Basotho nation, made Maseru his base in 1869 during the era of the Free State–Basotho wars, after which Lesotho became a British protectorate. It remained a small colonial administrative town until independence in 1966, when it became the capital; most of Lesotho's urban population and formal employment are concentrated here.

Modern city

Maseru is Lesotho's commercial and transport hub, linked by the Maseru Bridge border post to Ladybrand in South Africa and served by Moshoeshoe I International Airport. Notable buildings include the conical, straw-hat-shaped Basotho Hat craft centre — its design echoes the traditional mokorotlo hat that appears on Lesotho's flag — and the Royal Palace.
