Christian prayer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christian prayer is an important activity in Christianity, and there are several different forms used for this practice. Christian prayers are diverse: they can be completely spontaneous, or read entirely from a text, such as from a breviary. The most common prayer among Christians is the Lord's Prayer, which according to the gospel accounts is how Jesus taught his disciples to pray.

Forms of prayer

Tradition distinguishes several purposes of prayer, often summarised as adoration, contrition, thanksgiving and supplication (ACTS). Forms include intercessory prayer for others, prayers of petition, thanksgiving, and praise. Liturgical traditions use set prayers at fixed hours — the Liturgy of the Hours in Roman Catholicism and Morning and Evening Prayer in Anglicanism — while evangelical and Pentecostal traditions emphasise extempore prayer.

Devotional practices

Well-known devotions include the Rosary in Catholic practice, centred on the Hail Mary; the Jesus Prayer in Eastern Christianity ("Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"), repeated with a prayer rope; grace said before meals; and contemplative or centering prayer, a form of silent meditation. Novenas — prayers repeated over nine days — and prayers for the dead are practised by Catholics and Orthodox Christians.

Theological views

Christian theologians from Origen and Augustine to Karl Barth have written extensively on prayer as communion with God. Many traditions teach that the Holy Spirit assists believers in prayer (Romans 8:26). Views on the efficacy of petitionary prayer, and on whether it can change outcomes, vary among denominations.
