Conveyor belt

A conveyor belt is the carrying medium of a belt conveyor system, consisting of a continuous loop of material — commonly rubber reinforced with fabric or steel cords — stretched between pulleys, one of which is driven. Belt conveyors are among the most widely used machines for transporting bulk materials and packaged goods over fixed paths in mining, manufacturing, agriculture, warehousing, and airports.

Structure

A typical belt conveyor includes a drive pulley, a tail pulley, idlers supporting the belt along its length, a frame, and a drive unit. Belts are characterized by their carcass material (textile plies or steel cord), cover compounds resistant to abrasion, heat, oil, or flame, and surface profiles such as chevron patterns for inclined transport. Belt widths range from a few centimeters for small parts to more than three meters for high-capacity mining installations; single conveyors can extend for many kilometers, and overland systems have exceeded 10 km in a single flight.

History

Primitive conveyor belts appeared in the late 18th century, and by the late 19th century steel-cable and rubber belts were transporting coal and ore. Henry Ford's moving assembly line of 1913 adapted conveyor principles to manufacturing. Modern systems incorporate belt scales, metal detectors, rip detection, and automated controls.

Applications

  • Bulk handling of coal, ore, grain, and aggregates
  • Assembly lines and packaging operations
  • Baggage handling in airports
  • Checkout counters, escalators' relatives (moving walkways), and sushi restaurants' conveyor-belt dining
