Searchlight (magazine)

Searchlight is a British magazine, founded in 1975, dedicated to researching and campaigning against fascism, racism and the far right. It grew out of an earlier news-sheet of the 1960s produced by Labour MPs and anti-fascist activists.

History

The original Searchlight bulletin was launched in 1964–65 with the involvement of Reg Freeson and Joan Lestor; it folded after a few years. The magazine was relaunched in 1975, with Maurice Ludmer as its first editor. After Ludmer's death in 1981, Gerry Gable became editor and the magazine became known for investigative exposure of far-right groups such as the National Front and the British National Party, and of Combat 18 in the 1990s.

Work

Searchlight's research has fed into journalism, television documentaries and anti-racist campaigning. Its associated educational trust has published books and training materials. The organisation has also faced controversy and legal challenges arising from its use of informants within far-right movements.
