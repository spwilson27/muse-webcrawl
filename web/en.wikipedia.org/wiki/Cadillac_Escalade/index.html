The Cadillac Escalade is a full-size luxury SUV manufactured by General Motors and marketed by its Cadillac division. Introduced for the 1999 model year, it was Cadillac's first major entry into the SUV market, developed in response to the success of the Lincoln Navigator, and is built alongside the Chevrolet Tahoe, Chevrolet Suburban, and GMC Yukon on GM's full-size truck platforms.

History

The first-generation Escalade (1999–2000) was a lightly restyled GMC Yukon Denali. The second generation (2002–2006) gave the model distinctive Cadillac styling and established it as a cultural icon, heavily featured in music videos and popular culture. The third (2007–2014) and fourth (2015–2020) generations added hybrid and Platinum variants, while the fifth generation, launched for 2021, introduced independent rear suspension, a 38-inch curved OLED dashboard display, and available Super Cruise hands-free driving.

Variants

The line-up has included the Escalade ESV, a long-wheelbase version based on the Suburban; the Escalade EXT sport utility truck (2002–2013); and the high-performance Escalade-V (2023), powered by a supercharged 6.2-litre V8 producing 682 horsepower. The all-electric Escalade IQ, built on GM's Ultium platform, entered production for 2025.

Powertrains

Petrol V8 engines have dominated the range, most recently a 6.2-litre V8, with a 3.0-litre Duramax turbo-diesel six-cylinder offered on the fifth generation. Ten-speed automatic transmissions are standard.

Cultural impact and sales

The Escalade became one of Cadillac's most profitable and recognisable models and a status symbol in American popular culture. It is assembled primarily in Arlington, Texas.
