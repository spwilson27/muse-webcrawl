Jump blues

Jump blues is an up-tempo style of blues, popular in the 1940s, usually played by small bands featuring saxophone, guitar, rhythm section, and a charismatic vocalist. It was a bridge between big-band swing and the rhythm and blues and rock and roll that followed.

Characteristics

Jump blues combines the twelve-bar blues form with swinging rhythms, honking saxophone solos, and humorous, energetic lyrics designed for dancing. As wartime economics shrank big bands, smaller combos took their place in clubs and juke joints.

Key artists

The style's greatest star was Louis Jordan, whose Tympany Five racked up hits such as "Caldonia" and "Choo Choo Ch'Boogie." Other leading figures included Roy Brown, Wynonie Harris, Big Joe Turner, T-Bone Walker, Lionel Hampton, and Jay McShann. Many jump musicians recorded for independent labels like Atlantic, Savoy, and Specialty.

Influence

Jump blues directly shaped rhythm and blues and, through artists like Bill Haley — whose "Shake, Rattle and Roll" covered Big Joe Turner — early rock and roll. A revival in the 1990s, associated with the swing dance scene, renewed interest in the style.
