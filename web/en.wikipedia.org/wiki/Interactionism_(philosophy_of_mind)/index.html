Interactionism (philosophy of mind)

Interactionism is a position in the philosophy of mind holding that mental and physical states causally influence one another. It is a form of mind–body dualism most famously associated with René Descartes, who held that the immaterial mind interacts with the physical body, suggesting the pineal gland as the point of interaction.

The principal objection to interactionism, raised by Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia in correspondence with Descartes, is how an immaterial substance can causally affect a material one, and modern critics add that it appears to conflict with the causal closure of the physical world and conservation laws.

The philosopher Karl Popper and neuroscientist John Eccles defended a modern version of interactionism in the twentieth century.
