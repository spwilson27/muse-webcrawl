Yatagarasu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Yatagarasu (Japanese: 八咫烏) is a legendary three-legged crow in Japanese mythology, regarded as a messenger of the gods and a symbol of divine guidance and the sun. It appears in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, the earliest chronicles of Japan.

Mythology

According to the chronicles, the sun goddess Amaterasu — or in some accounts the thunder god Takemikazuchi acting on heaven's behalf — sent Yatagarasu to guide the legendary first emperor, Jimmu, on his eastern expedition through the mountains of the Kii Peninsula to Yamato. The great crow flew ahead of Jimmu's forces and showed them the way, so Yatagarasu is venerated as a guide deity, particularly in the Kumano region, where the three Kumano grand shrines honour it.

Three legs

The crow's three legs are variously interpreted: as representing heaven, earth, and humanity; or the three virtues of wisdom, benevolence, and valour; or the three powerful Kumano clans. Three-legged sun crows also appear in Chinese (sanzuwu) and Korean (samjok-o) mythology, where the bird is said to live in the sun.

Modern symbolism

Yatagarasu is the emblem of the Japan Football Association and appears on the crest of the Japan national football team, symbolising guidance toward victory. It also features on the badge of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force and in numerous shrine and pop-culture contexts.
