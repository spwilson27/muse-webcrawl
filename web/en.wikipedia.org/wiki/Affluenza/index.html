Affluenza

Affluenza is a portmanteau of affluence and influenza, used to describe a supposed psychological condition arising from the pursuit of wealth and material possessions. Popularized by the 1997 PBS documentary Affluenza and the 2001 book Affluenza: The All-Consuming Epidemic by John de Graaf, David Wann, and Thomas H. Naylor, the term describes the stress, debt, and dissatisfaction associated with consumer culture and "keeping up with the Joneses".

Meaning

Writers on affluenza describe it as a "painful, contagious, socially transmitted condition of overload, debt, anxiety, and waste resulting from the dogged pursuit of more". Proposed remedies include voluntary simplicity, downshifting, and reduced consumption. The concept is a critique rather than a recognized medical diagnosis.

Legal notoriety

The word entered wide public discussion in 2013, when a psychologist testified in the Texas trial of Ethan Couch, a teenager who killed four people while driving drunk, that Couch suffered from "affluenza" due to his wealthy parents' failure to set limits. The defence helped Couch receive probation rather than prison, provoking national controversy; he later fled to Mexico, was returned, and served jail time for probation violations.
