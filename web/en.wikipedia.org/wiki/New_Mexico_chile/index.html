New Mexico chile

New Mexico chile or New Mexican chile is a group of cultivars of the chile pepper (Capsicum annuum) grown in the US state of New Mexico, and the foundation of New Mexican cuisine. The peppers are used green — harvested unripe, then roasted — or red, harvested ripe and dried, and are made into the state's signature red and green chile sauces.

History and cultivation

Chiles were cultivated in the Rio Grande valley by Pueblo peoples and Spanish settlers for centuries. Modern cultivars trace to the work of horticulturist Fabián García at New Mexico State University, whose 1913 release 'New Mexico No. 9' standardized a milder, more consistent pod. The town of Hatch, in the Hatch Valley, is famous for its chiles, and the harvest season each late summer is marked by roasting in rotating steel drums outside grocery stores.

In New Mexican cuisine

Chile sauce is served over enchiladas, burritos, huevos rancheros and countless other dishes, and ordering "Christmas" means asking for both red and green. Heat levels are officially graded from mild to extra-hot. In 1999 New Mexico adopted an official state question: "Red or Green?"
