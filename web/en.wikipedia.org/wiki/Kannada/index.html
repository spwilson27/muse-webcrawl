Kannada

Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ, kannaḍa) is a Dravidian language spoken predominantly in the Indian state of Karnataka in southwestern India. It is one of the scheduled languages of India and has roughly 44 million native speakers, with many more second-language speakers. Kannada is written in its own script, derived from the Kadamba script, and has a literary tradition spanning well over a thousand years.

History

Kannada is one of the oldest Dravidian languages, diverging from Proto-Tamil–Kannada around the early centuries CE. Inscriptions in Kannada date to about 450 CE (the Halmidi inscription). The earliest extant literary work is the Kavirajamarga (c. 850 CE), a treatise on rhetoric attributed to the Rashtrakuta king Amoghavarsha I. The classical period produced the "three gems" of Kannada poetry — Pampa, Ponna and Ranna — in the 10th century.

Phonology and grammar

Kannada has a phonemic inventory of about 49 sounds, including retroflex consonants typical of Dravidian languages. It is agglutinative, with words built from roots plus suffix chains marking case, number, person and tense. The basic word order is subject–object–verb, and the language distinguishes inclusive usage patterns and three genders in the singular.

Literature and culture

Kannada literature has received the most Jnanpith Awards of any Indian language, with eight laureates including Kuvempu and U. R. Ananthamurthy. The Vachana movement of the 12th century, led by Basava and Akka Mahadevi, produced a distinctive devotional prose-poetry. Kannada cinema, centred in Bengaluru and nicknamed "Sandalwood", is a major regional film industry.
