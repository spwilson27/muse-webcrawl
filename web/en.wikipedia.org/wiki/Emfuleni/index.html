Emfuleni

Emfuleni Local Municipality is a local municipality in the Sedibeng District of Gauteng province, South Africa. Its name means "by the river" in Zulu, referring to the Vaal River, which forms its southern boundary. The municipality covers the industrial Vaal Triangle towns of Vanderbijlpark and Vereeniging, together with townships including Sebokeng, Sharpeville, Boipatong, Bophelong, Evaton and Tshepiso.

History and economy

The area developed around steel production and heavy industry: Vereeniging grew with coal mining and the Union Steel Corporation, while Vanderbijlpark was laid out in the 1940s–50s as a planned town for the Iscor (later ArcelorMittal) steel works. Sharpeville was the site of the Sharpeville massacre of 21 March 1960, when police killed 69 anti-pass protesters, an event commemorated in South Africa as Human Rights Day.

Governance problems

In the democratic era Emfuleni has been governed by the African National Congress but has become known for chronic financial distress, debt to Eskom and Rand Water, failing infrastructure and sewage pollution of the Vaal River system. The provincial and national governments have repeatedly intervened in the municipality's administration under section 139 of the Constitution, and in 2024 Eskom moved to attach the municipality's bank accounts over unpaid electricity debt, before a court suspended the action after payment arrangements.

Geography

The municipality covers about 987 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 720,000 at the 2011 census, growing thereafter. Its seat is Vanderbijlpark.
