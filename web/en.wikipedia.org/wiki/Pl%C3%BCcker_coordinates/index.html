Plücker coordinates

In geometry, Plücker coordinates, introduced by Julius Plücker in the 19th century, are a way to assign six homogeneous coordinates to each line in projective 3-space, ℝℙ3. Because lines satisfy a quadratic constraint — the Plücker relation — they lie on a quadric hypersurface, the Klein quadric, in projective 5-space.

Definition

Given two points p and q on a line, with homogeneous coordinates (p0, p1, p2, p3) and (q0, q1, q2, q3), the Plücker coordinates are the six 2×2 determinants Pij = piqj − pjqi. Equivalently, a line can be described by a direction vector and a moment vector, as in screw theory.

The Plücker relation

The six coordinates are not independent: they satisfy P01P23 + P02P31 + P03P12 = 0. Conversely, any point of the Klein quadric in ℝℙ5 corresponds to a unique line in ℝℙ3, a bijection known as the Klein correspondence.

Uses

Plücker coordinates are used in ray tracing and computer vision to represent rays symmetrically, in robotics and screw theory to describe rigid-body motion, and in line geometry, the study of families and complexes of lines.
