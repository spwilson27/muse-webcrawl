Hyperthermia

Hyperthermia
DefinitionCore body temperature elevated above the normal range due to failed thermoregulation
SpecialtyEmergency medicine
Severe formHeat stroke (core temperature > 40 °C with CNS dysfunction)
OppositeHypothermia

Hyperthermia results when heat gain exceeds the body's ability to lose heat.

Hyperthermia, also known simply as overheating, is a condition in which an individual's body temperature is elevated beyond the normal range because of a failure of thermoregulation: the body produces or absorbs more heat than it can dissipate. It is distinct from fever, in which the hypothalamic temperature set-point itself is raised. When extreme, hyperthermia becomes a medical emergency requiring immediate treatment to prevent disability or death.

Heat illnesses

The spectrum of heat-related illness runs from minor conditions — heat cramps, heat rash and heat exhaustion (weakness, nausea, heavy sweating, temperature normal or moderately raised) — to heat stroke, the most severe form, defined by a core temperature above about 40 °C together with central nervous system dysfunction such as confusion, seizures or coma. Heat stroke is classically divided into classic (passive) heat stroke, affecting vulnerable people during heat waves, and exertional heat stroke, affecting athletes, soldiers and labourers generating large amounts of metabolic heat.

Causes

Environmental heat and humidity, strenuous exercise, dehydration, heavy clothing and certain drugs (stimulants, anticholinergics) are common contributors. Distinct hyperthermic syndromes include malignant hyperthermia, a genetic reaction to some anaesthetics; neuroleptic malignant syndrome; and serotonin syndrome.

Treatment

The priority is rapid cooling: cold-water immersion is the most effective method for exertional heat stroke ("cool first, transport second"), supplemented by ice packs, fanning and evaporative cooling. Antipyretics such as paracetamol are ineffective because the set-point is not raised. Supportive care treats complications including rhabdomyolysis, kidney failure and disseminated intravascular coagulation.
