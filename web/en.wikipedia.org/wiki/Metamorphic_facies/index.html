Metamorphic facies

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A metamorphic facies is a set of mineral assemblages in metamorphic rocks formed under similar pressures and temperatures. The assemblage is typical of what is formed in conditions corresponding to an area on a two-dimensional graph of temperature vs. pressure, so the facies concept lets geologists infer the conditions under which a rock metamorphosed from the minerals it contains, regardless of its bulk composition. The concept was introduced by the Finnish geologist Pentti Eskola in 1915 and refined in his 1920 paper "The Mineral Facies of Rocks".

Principal facies

  • Zeolite facies — lowest grade; burial metamorphism of sediments.
  • Prehnite–pumpellyite facies — very low grade, common in buried volcanic sequences.
  • Greenschist facies — low grade; characteristic minerals chlorite, actinolite, albite and epidote.
  • Amphibolite facies — medium grade; hornblende and plagioclase dominate mafic rocks.
  • Granulite facies — high grade, high temperature; orthopyroxene-bearing, largely dry assemblages.
  • Blueschist facies — high pressure, low temperature; glaucophane and lawsonite, typical of subduction zones.
  • Eclogite facies — very high pressure; garnet + omphacite, from deeply subducted crust.
  • Hornfels facies — low pressure, high temperature contact metamorphism around intrusions (subdivided into albite–epidote, hornblende, pyroxene and sanidinite hornfels facies).
  • Sanidinite facies — extreme temperature at very low pressure, e.g. xenoliths in lavas.

Facies series

Sequences of facies encountered along regional pressure–temperature gradients are grouped into facies series associated with tectonic settings: the high-pressure blueschist–eclogite series of subduction zones, the intermediate Barrovian series of collisional mountain belts (named for George Barrow's Scottish Highlands zones), and the low-pressure Buchan / Abukuma series of volcanic arcs and contact aureoles. Miyashiro's paired metamorphic belts in Japan — a high-pressure belt beside a low-pressure belt — became classic evidence for plate tectonics.
