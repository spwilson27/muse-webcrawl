Māui (mythology)

Māui is a demigod and culture hero in Polynesian mythology, famed across the Pacific for his trickery, daring and supernatural feats. Tales of Māui are told from New Zealand to Hawaiʻi, Tahiti, Samoa, Tonga, the Cook Islands and elsewhere, making him one of the most widely known figures in Oceanic tradition.

Feats

Among Māui's celebrated exploits are fishing up islands from the sea — in Māori tradition he hauled up the North Island of New Zealand, Te Ika-a-Māui ("the fish of Māui"), using a fishhook made from his grandmother's jawbone and his own blood as bait. He is said to have snared the sun with ropes to slow its passage across the sky, giving people longer days. He also stole fire from the goddess Mahuika, whose fingernails held the flames, and brought it to humankind.

Death

Māui met his end attempting to win immortality for humanity. He tried to pass through the body of Hine-nui-te-pō, the goddess of night and death, entering while she slept, but a fantail (pīwakawaka) burst into laughter, waking the goddess, who crushed him. Thus death came to humankind permanently.

In Māori tradition

In New Zealand, Māui-tikitiki-a-Taranga was a mischievous youngest brother, cast into the sea at birth wrapped in his mother's topknot (tikitiki), and raised by ancestors. His brothers appear in many tales, sometimes as foils to his cleverness. Māori place names throughout New Zealand commemorate his deeds.

In Hawaiian tradition

In Hawaiʻi, Maui is one of the kupua (demigods); the island of Maui bears his name. His feats include pushing up the sky, capturing the sun at Haleakalā, and obtaining fire through the ʻalae birds.
