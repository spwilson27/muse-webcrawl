Tasman Peninsula

LocationSouth-east Tasmania, Australia
Administered byTasman Council
Known forPort Arthur Historic Site, sea cliffs

The Tasman Peninsula is a peninsula in south-east Tasmania, Australia, roughly 75 km from Hobart. It is connected to the Forestier Peninsula by the narrow isthmus of Eaglehawk Neck and is part of the Tasman National Park area, noted for some of the tallest sea cliffs in the Southern Hemisphere.

History

The peninsula is best known for Port Arthur, established in 1830 as a penal settlement for repeat convict offenders transported to Van Diemen's Land. Guarded by a line of chained dogs across Eaglehawk Neck, the settlement became infamous for its harsh discipline. Port Arthur is now a major heritage tourism site and part of the UNESCO-listed Australian Convict Sites.

Geography and tourism

Dramatic dolerite coastal formations — including the Tasman Arch, the Blowhole and the Tessellated Pavement — draw visitors, and the Three Capes Track offers multi-day walking along the cliff tops. In April 1996 the peninsula was the scene of the Port Arthur massacre, in which 35 people were killed.
