A trophy truck, also known as a trick truck in some series, is a vehicle used in high-speed off-road racing. It is the fastest and most powerful class of off-road race vehicle, purpose-built for desert races such as the SCORE Baja 1000 in Mexico and the Mint 400 in Nevada. Trophy trucks bear only a superficial resemblance to the production pickup trucks they are styled after; beneath their fibreglass bodies they are purpose-built tube-chassis machines costing several hundred thousand dollars or more.

Design

Trophy trucks are typically two-wheel drive (with all-wheel drive versions increasingly competitive since the late 2010s), powered by large-displacement V8 engines producing roughly 700 to 1,000 horsepower. Their defining feature is enormous suspension travel — around 24 to 26 inches at the front and 32 to 34 inches at the solid-axle rear — allowing them to cross deep whoops, rocks, and jumps at speeds exceeding 100 mph. Tyres are typically 39 to 40 inches in diameter, and the trucks carry huge fuel cells for long desert stages between pits. A driver and co-driver crew the truck, the latter navigating by GPS and course notes and monitoring the vehicle's systems.

Competition

The class was formalised by the SCORE International series in 1994 (as "Trophy Truck"), with other sanctioning bodies using names such as "Trick Truck" for legally distinct but equivalent classes. Trophy trucks compete for the overall victory at the Baja 1000, Baja 500, San Felipe 250, and Mint 400, and headline stadium-style events such as Robby Gordon's Stadium Super Trucks in modified form. Notable drivers include Ivan "Ironman" Stewart, Rob MacCachren, B.J. Baldwin, and Bryce Menzies.
