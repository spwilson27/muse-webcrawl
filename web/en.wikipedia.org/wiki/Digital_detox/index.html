Digital detox
DefinitionVoluntary period of abstaining from digital devices and media
Typical targetsSmartphones, social media, email, news feeds
Dictionary recognitionTerm popularised 2010s; campaigns include National Day of Unplugging
Setting devices aside is the core of a digital detox

A digital detox is a period of time during which a person voluntarily refrains from using digital devices such as smartphones, computers and social media platforms, in order to reduce stress, improve focus, sleep and social interaction, or counter perceived overuse and compulsive checking habits. The term emerged in the early 2010s as concerns about the attention economy and heavy smartphone use entered mainstream discussion.

Practices

Detoxes range from a few hours to weeks and include switching off notifications, "phone-free" meals and bedrooms, app-limiting software, holidays at off-grid retreats, and organised events such as the National Day of Unplugging. Commercial "detox" camps and resorts market device-free stays. More moderate, sustainable versions emphasise restructuring habits rather than total abstinence — for example scheduled checking windows and removing social-media apps from the phone's home screen.

Evidence and criticism

Research on the benefits is mixed: some studies report small improvements in well-being, mood and sleep after abstinence from social media, while others find negligible or short-lived effects, and researchers note that self-selected participants and expectancy effects complicate interpretation. Critics argue that the detox framing individualises a problem created by persuasive platform design, and that complete disconnection is impractical for people whose work and social lives are digitally mediated.
