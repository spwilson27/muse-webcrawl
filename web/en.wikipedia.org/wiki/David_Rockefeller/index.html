David Rockefeller (June 12, 1915 – March 20, 2017) was an American banker and statesman who served as chairman and chief executive of Chase Manhattan Bank. The youngest child of John D. Rockefeller Jr. and the grandson of Standard Oil co-founder John D. Rockefeller, he was the last surviving grandchild of the original Rockefeller fortune's founder and, at his death at age 101, was widely regarded as the family's patriarch.

Early life and education

Rockefeller was born in New York City and raised in the family's Manhattan mansion. He graduated from Harvard University in 1936 and earned a PhD in economics from the University of Chicago in 1940, writing his dissertation on unused capacity and corporate profits. During World War II he served in the U.S. Army in North Africa and France, working in military intelligence.

Banking career

Rockefeller joined Chase National Bank in 1946. He became president in 1961 and chairman and chief executive in 1969, leading the bank—renamed Chase Manhattan—through a period of major international expansion. He cultivated relationships with heads of state around the world and was a central figure in global finance, helping to found institutions and forums that linked business and government. He retired in 1981.

International affairs and philanthropy

Rockefeller was a founder of the Trilateral Commission in 1973 and a longtime participant in the Bilderberg meetings. He was closely associated with the Council on Foreign Relations and supported the Museum of Modern Art, which his mother Abby Aldrich Rockefeller helped found. His philanthropy included major gifts to Harvard University and Rockefeller University. He was a notable art collector and an entomologist who amassed a large beetle collection.

Personal life and death

He married Margaret "Peggy" McGrath in 1940; she died in 1996. They had six children. Rockefeller published his memoirs in 2002, the first by a member of the family. He died in his sleep of congestive heart failure on March 20, 2017, at his home in Pocantico Hills, New York.
