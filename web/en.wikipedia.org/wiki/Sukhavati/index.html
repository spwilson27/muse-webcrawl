Sukhavati (Sanskrit: सुखावती, "possessed of ease; Land of Bliss") is the western pure land of the Buddha Amitābha in Mahāyāna Buddhism. It is the central object of devotion in Pure Land Buddhism, the most widely practised form of Buddhism in East Asia, whose adherents aspire to be reborn there through faith in Amitābha and recitation of his name (the nianfo / nembutsu).

Description in the sutras

The Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, the Shorter Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, and the Amitayurdhyana Sūtra describe Sukhavati as a realm created by Amitābha's forty-eight vows: a land of jewelled trees, lakes of fragrant water covered in lotuses, and celestial music, where beings are born from lotus buds, never fall back into lower rebirths, and progress surely toward enlightenment under Amitābha's teaching, attended by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta.

Interpretation

Traditions differ over how literally to take Sukhavati: as an actual world located far to the west, as a state of mind ("the pure land is not far from here"), or as both. Rebirth there is held to depend on Amitābha's "other-power" rather than solely on the practitioner's own efforts, a doctrine developed by teachers such as Hōnen and Shinran in Japan.
