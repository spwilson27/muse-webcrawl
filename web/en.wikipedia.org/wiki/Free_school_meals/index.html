Free school meals

A typical school meal served to pupils.

Free school meals are meals provided to children at school at no cost to their families, typically funded by government programmes. They aim to ensure that children from low-income households receive adequate nutrition during the school day, supporting health, concentration, and educational attainment.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, eligibility for free school meals has historically been linked to receipt of certain means-tested benefits such as Income Support and Universal Credit. Since September 2014, all infant school children in England (Reception, Year 1 and Year 2) have been entitled to a free school lunch under the Universal Infant Free School Meals policy. Scotland and Wales have moved toward universal provision in primary schools. Campaigns by figures including footballer Marcus Rashford during the COVID-19 pandemic drew attention to holiday food provision for eligible children.

United States

In the United States, the National School Lunch Program, established in 1946, provides free or reduced-price lunches to children from low-income families. During and after the COVID-19 pandemic, several states, including California and Maine, adopted universal free school meal policies for all public school students.

Effects

Research generally associates free school meal provision with improved nutrition, reduced food insecurity, and modest positive effects on attainment and attendance, though effects vary by context and implementation.
