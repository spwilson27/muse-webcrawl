Agloe, New York

Agloe is a fictional hamlet in Colchester, Delaware County, New York, that became an actual landmark after being invented as a copyright trap — a fictitious entry placed on a map to catch plagiarists. It is a well-known example of a "paper town".

History

In the 1930s, Otto G. Lindberg, founder of the General Drafting Company, and his assistant Ernest Alpers placed Agloe — an anagram of their initials — at the intersection of NY 206 and Morton Hill Road near Roscoe, on their road maps of New York. When the name later appeared on a Rand McNally map, Lindberg's company prepared to sue, but Rand McNally pointed to county records showing an Agloe General Store at the site: a business had taken the name from the map itself, making the fictional place real. The store later closed, but Agloe continued appearing on maps for decades and briefly reappeared in Google Maps in 2014 before being removed.

In popular culture

Agloe figures prominently in John Green's 2008 novel Paper Towns and its 2015 film adaptation, where it serves as a clue in the protagonist's search for a missing friend.
