Bob Bullock (July 10, 1929 – June 18, 1999) was an American politician who served as the 38th Lieutenant Governor of Texas from 1991 to 1999, during the governorship of George W. Bush and earlier Ann Richards. Widely regarded as one of the most powerful politicians in modern Texas history, Bullock was known for bipartisan dealmaking, an encyclopedic knowledge of state government, and blunt, profane candor. Before becoming lieutenant governor he served sixteen years as Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts, where he modernized the state's tax administration.

Early life and career

Bullock was born in Hillsboro, Texas, and grew up in Corsicana. He served in the United States Air Force during the Korean War era, then earned a degree from Texas Tech University and a law degree from Baylor University in 1958, after which he entered private practice. He won election to the Texas House of Representatives in 1956 and 1958, serving two terms, and later worked in state government posts including a position on the staff of Governor John Connally and as assistant attorney general under Crawford Martin.

Bullock was appointed Secretary of State of Texas in 1971 by Governor Preston Smith. In 1974 he was elected Comptroller of Public Accounts, taking office in January 1975. As comptroller he transformed a sleepy revenue office into a formidable agency, aggressively auditing businesses for unpaid taxes and building a professional staff that made him a fixture of Texas politics.

Lieutenant governor

Elected lieutenant governor in 1990, Bullock took office in January 1991. Under the Texas system the lieutenant governor presides over the state senate and is often considered more powerful than the governor. Bullock worked closely with Democratic Governor Ann Richards and, after 1995, with Republican Governor George W. Bush, who credited Bullock's cooperation as essential to his legislative program. Bullock declined to seek reelection in 1998 and was succeeded by Rick Perry. His health, long affected by alcoholism in his earlier years — a struggle he discussed publicly — declined, and he died on June 18, 1999.

Legacy

The Bob Bullock Texas State History Museum in Austin, which opened in 2001, is named for him. Bullock is remembered for institutionalizing professional revenue estimating in Texas and for a style of leadership that combined hardball tactics with genuine cross-party friendship.
