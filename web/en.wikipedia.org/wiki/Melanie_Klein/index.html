Melanie Klein

Melanie Klein (née Reizes; 30 March 1882 – 22 September 1960) was an Austrian-British author and psychoanalyst known for her work in child analysis. She was the primary figure in the development of object relations theory and pioneered the play technique for analysing young children, extending psychoanalytic theory to the earliest months of infancy.

Life and work

Born in Vienna, Klein was analysed by Sándor Ferenczi in Budapest, who encouraged her to analyse children, and later by Karl Abraham in Berlin. She moved to London in 1926 at the invitation of Ernest Jones. Her theories — including the paranoid-schizoid and depressive positions, and concepts such as projective identification, envy, and the early Oedipus complex — diverged from orthodox Freudian views and led to the "controversial discussions" within the British Psychoanalytical Society during the 1940s, opposing her followers to those of Anna Freud.

Legacy

Kleinian theory profoundly influenced later psychoanalysis, including the work of Wilfred Bion, Donald Winnicott (in part) and Hanna Segal, and remains one of the major schools of psychoanalytic thought.
