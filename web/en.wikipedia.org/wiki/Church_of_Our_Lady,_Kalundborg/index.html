Church of Our Lady, Kalundborg

The distinctive five-towered church at Kalundborg

The Church of Our Lady (Danish: Vor Frue Kirke) is a historical building in Kalundborg, on the west coast of Zealand in Denmark. Built around 1220, it is famous for its unique plan: a central tower and four smaller corner towers rising above a Greek-cross layout, making it one of the most distinctive medieval churches in Northern Europe.

Architecture

The church was constructed in granite and brick, traditionally attributed to Esbern Snare, the elder brother of Bishop Absalon. The central octagonal tower is supported inside by four granite columns. In 1827 the central tower collapsed; it was rebuilt by 1871, restoring the church's characteristic silhouette. Each of the five towers is topped with a spire, and the four outer arms of the cross end in apses.

Interior

The interior preserves medieval frescoes in the eastern apse and a baroque altarpiece dating from 1650, carved by Lorentz Jørgensen, as well as a granite Romanesque baptismal font. The church's plan may have been inspired by descriptions of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem, reflecting crusading-era influences in Danish church building.
