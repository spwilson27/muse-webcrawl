Toyota 86

The Toyota 86 is a series of light, front-engine, rear-wheel-drive sports cars jointly developed by Toyota and Subaru. The first generation, introduced in 2012, was sold as the Toyota 86 (GT86 in Europe), the Subaru BRZ and, in North America, the Scion FR-S. A second generation, the Toyota GR86, arrived in 2021 alongside a new Subaru BRZ.

The car was designed as an affordable, balanced driver's car inspired by the classic Toyota AE86 Corolla of the 1980s, from which it takes its name. It uses a Subaru-derived flat-four ("boxer") engine mounted low in the chassis for a low centre of gravity.

First generation (2012–2020)

The first-generation car is powered by a 2.0-litre naturally aspirated flat-four producing about 200 PS (147 kW), coupled to six-speed manual or automatic transmissions. Its near 50:50 weight distribution, limited-slip differential and deliberately modest grip were praised by motoring journalists for making the car enjoyable at road speeds.

Second generation (2021–present)

The GR86 uses a larger 2.4-litre flat-four with about 235 PS, a stiffer and slightly larger body, and updated styling. It continues to be built alongside the Subaru BRZ at Subaru's Gunma plant.
