Candlestick pattern

In technical analysis, a candlestick pattern is a movement in prices shown graphically on a candlestick chart that some believe can help predict future market direction. A single candlestick summarises the open, high, low and close of a security over a chosen period: the body spans the open and close, while the wicks (shadows) mark the extremes.

History

Candlestick charting originated in 18th-century Japan, where rice traders such as Munehisa Homma used it to track prices at the Dojima Rice Exchange. The method was introduced to Western traders largely through Steve Nison's book Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques (1991).

Common patterns

  • Doji – open and close nearly equal; signals indecision.
  • Hammer and hanging man – small body at the top of the range with a long lower wick.
  • Engulfing patterns – a candle whose body fully covers the previous candle's body, bullish or bearish depending on direction.
  • Morning star and evening star – three-candle reversal patterns.
  • Shooting star and inverted hammer – small body with a long upper wick.
  • Marubozu – a long body with almost no wicks, showing strong one-sided pressure.

Assessment

Traders usually seek confirmation from context — trend, support and resistance, volume and subsequent candles — rather than treating a pattern as a signal on its own. Academic studies of candlestick rules have produced mixed results, and many researchers regard most patterns as having little or no standalone predictive power.
