Lewis Baltz (September 12, 1945 – November 22, 2014) was an American visual artist and photographer, a leading figure of the New Topographics movement of the 1970s. His cool, deadpan photographs of suburban and industrial landscapes — tract houses, warehouses, office parks and construction sites in the American West — examined the impact of human development on the landscape.

Career

Baltz studied at the San Francisco Art Institute and the Claremont Graduate School. His early series The Tract Houses (1971) and The New Industrial Parks Near Irvine, California (1974) presented serial, typological views of anonymous architecture. In 1975 his work was included in the landmark exhibition New Topographics: Photographs of a Man-Altered Landscape at the George Eastman House, alongside Robert Adams, Bernd and Hilla Becher and Stephen Shore.

Later projects, including Park City (1980), San Quentin Point (1985) and Candlestick Point (1989), extended his survey of development at the edges of cities. From the 1990s Baltz lived mainly in Europe, principally Paris, and his later work turned toward surveillance, technology and the mediated image.
