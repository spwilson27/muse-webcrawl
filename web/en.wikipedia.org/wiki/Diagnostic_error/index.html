Diagnostic error

A diagnostic error is a failure to establish an accurate and timely explanation of a patient's health problem, or to communicate that explanation to the patient. The United States National Academy of Medicine's 2015 report Improving Diagnosis in Health Care concluded that most people will experience at least one diagnostic error in their lifetime, sometimes with devastating consequences.

Causes

Diagnostic errors arise from both system-related factors — such as fragmented care, poor test-result follow-up and inadequate communication — and cognitive factors in clinicians, including premature closure, anchoring bias and availability bias. Many errors involve both.

Frequency and harm

Studies suggest diagnostic errors affect roughly 5% of outpatient encounters in the United States each year, and they are a leading category in medical malpractice claims. Commonly missed or delayed diagnoses include cancer, cardiovascular events and infections.

Prevention

Proposed remedies include better feedback to clinicians, diagnostic checklists, decision-support tools, improved access to second opinions, and involving patients and families as partners in the diagnostic process.
