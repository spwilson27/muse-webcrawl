The Annunciation, in which the angel Gabriel announces to Mary that she will bear Jesus, is a central subject of Christian art.

The Annunciation (from Latin annuntiatio) is, according to the Gospel of Luke, the announcement by the archangel Gabriel to the Virgin Mary that she would conceive and bear a son through a virgin birth, and become the mother of Jesus Christ. Gabriel told Mary to name him Jesus, "for he will save his people from their sins."

Biblical account

In Luke 1:26–38, Gabriel is sent to Nazareth in Galilee in the sixth month of Elizabeth's pregnancy. He greets Mary with the words rendered in Latin as Ave Maria, "Hail, Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee." Troubled by the greeting, Mary is told she has found favour with God and will conceive a son who will inherit the throne of David. When she asks how this can be, Gabriel replies that the Holy Spirit will come upon her, and concludes, "for nothing will be impossible with God." Mary answers: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word" — her consent, known as the fiat.

Feast day

The Feast of the Annunciation is celebrated on 25 March, nine months before Christmas. In England it was known as Lady Day and traditionally marked the start of the legal year until 1752. The feast is one of the twelve Great Feasts of the Orthodox Church, and in the Catholic Church it ranks as a solemnity. The Angelus prayer, commemorating the Annunciation, is traditionally recited morning, noon and evening.

In art

The Annunciation is among the most frequently depicted subjects in Western art, treated by Fra Angelico, Leonardo da Vinci, Botticelli, Jan van Eyck and many others. Conventional elements include the lily (symbol of Mary's purity), the dove of the Holy Spirit, a ray of light, and Mary reading at a lectern or prie-dieu, often in a walled garden (hortus conclusus). In Islamic tradition, the annunciation to Maryam is recorded in the Quran (Surah Maryam and Surah Al Imran), where the angel announces a son named Isa.
