Coventry City F.C.

Coventry City Football Club is a professional football club based in Coventry, West Midlands, England. Nicknamed the Sky Blues, the club was founded in 1883 as Singers F.C. and currently plays in the EFL Championship, the second tier of English football.

History

The club joined the Football League in 1919 and played at Highfield Road for over a century. Under manager Jimmy Hill in the 1960s the "Sky Blue Revolution" carried Coventry to the First Division in 1967, beginning an unbroken 34-year stay in the top flight. The club's greatest triumph came in 1987, when, managed by John Sillett and George Curtis, it beat Tottenham Hotspur 3–2 in a celebrated FA Cup final at Wembley.

Relegation from the Premier League in 2001 began a long decline. The club left Highfield Road in 2005 for the new Coventry Building Society Arena (formerly the Ricoh Arena) but became embroiled in rent disputes with the arena's owners, ground-sharing at Northampton Town and later Birmingham City, and dropped as far as League Two in 2017. Under manager Mark Robins the club won the EFL Trophy in 2017 and earned three promotions, returning to the Championship in 2020, losing the 2023 play-off final on penalties, and reaching an FA Cup semi-final in 2024.

Honours

  • FA Cup: 1986–87
  • EFL Trophy: 2016–17
  • Football League Second Division: 1966–67
  • League One: 2019–20
