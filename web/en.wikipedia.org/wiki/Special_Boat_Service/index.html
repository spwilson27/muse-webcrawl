Special Boat Service

The Special Boat Service (SBS) is the special forces unit of the United Kingdom's Royal Navy. The SBS can trace its origins back to the Second World War when the Army Special Boat Section was formed in 1940. After the war, the Royal Navy formed the Special Boat Company, which in 1987 finally adopted the name Special Boat Service. The SBS is the sister unit to the British Army's 22 Special Air Service (SAS), with both falling under the operational command of the Director Special Forces.

History

Early wartime boat sections carried out raids and reconnaissance in the Mediterranean, including canoe operations by men such as the "Cockleshell Heroes" of the Royal Marines Boom Patrol Detachment. Postwar, the unit saw service in Korea, Borneo, Oman and Aden. In 1982 the SBS played a major role in the Falklands War, conducting reconnaissance ahead of the landings at San Carlos. Since 2001 the SBS has deployed heavily to Afghanistan and Iraq as part of UK special forces task groups.

Role and selection

The SBS specialises in operations in and around water: maritime counter-terrorism (including protection of North Sea oil installations), beach and hydrographic reconnaissance, and clandestine insertion by submarine, boat and parachute. Most recruits come from the Royal Marines, and candidates pass the joint UK Special Forces selection course shared with the SAS before completing SBS-specific swimmer-canoeist training. The unit is based at RM Poole in Dorset and maintains UK national counter-terrorism standby duties in rotation with the SAS.
