Flatbrød

Flatbrød (Norwegian for "flat bread") is a traditional Norwegian dry flatbread made from barley flour, salt, and water, sometimes also containing potatoes and sour milk. It is rolled extremely thin and baked on a large griddle (takke), producing a crisp bread that keeps for long periods.

TypeFlatbread / crispbread
Place of originNorway
Main ingredientsBarley flour, salt, water

History

Flatbrød was historically the staple bread of Norwegian households. Because it could be stored for months or even years without spoiling, large batches were baked only a few times a year — traditionally in the autumn — and kept in storehouses. The thinness of the bread and the careful drying process were essential to its keeping qualities.

Preparation and serving

The dough is rolled out with a grooved or spiked rolling pin into large, paper-thin rounds, then baked briefly on a hot griddle. Today flatbrød is commonly eaten with cured meats, cheese, herring, or soups, and is a standard accompaniment to traditional dishes such as rakfisk and sodd. In Norway it is produced commercially as well as in homes.
