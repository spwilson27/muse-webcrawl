Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II

The Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II was the international celebration held in 2002 marking the 50th anniversary of the accession of Queen Elizabeth II to the thrones of the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms. It was intended by the Queen to be both a commemoration of her reign and an opportunity for her to officially and personally thank her people in her role as head of state and as the "head of the nation" in the United Kingdom and other realms.

Despite the deaths of Princess Margaret, Countess of Snowdon and Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother, in February and March 2002 respectively, and predictions in the media that the anniversary would be a non-event, the jubilee was marked with large-scale and popular events throughout London in June of the same year, and was bookended by events throughout the Commonwealth realms. The Queen and the Duke of Edinburgh also toured the United Kingdom and several Commonwealth countries during the year.

Preparations

Planning for the Golden Jubilee began in 2000, with a Golden Jubilee Office established within the Department for Culture, Media and Sport. Because of cost and security concerns, the celebrations were designed to be community-focused rather than dominated by a single large national event. Buckingham Palace stressed that the jubilee should be a celebration for the whole country rather than an expensive spectacle.

Celebrations in the United Kingdom

The main celebrations were concentrated in an extended bank holiday weekend from 1 to 4 June 2002. Events included the Party at the Palace, a pop and rock concert in the gardens of Buckingham Palace attended by 12,000 guests, and the Prom at the Palace, a classical concert. Attendees at the pop concert had been selected by a national ballot.

On 3 June, an estimated one million people gathered in The Mall and around the Queen Victoria Memorial to watch a carnival-style parade, and in the evening the Queen lit the National Beacon at the memorial, the last in a chain of 2,006 beacons lit throughout the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth. On 4 June, a national service of thanksgiving was held at St Paul's Cathedral, followed by a lunch at the Guildhall and a procession down The Mall. The day ended with a flypast over Buckingham Palace, which included Concorde and the Red Arrows.

Street parties were held across the country; more than 25,000 applications were made to local councils to close roads for parties, recalling the celebrations of the Silver Jubilee in 1977.

Tours

During 2002 the Queen and the Duke of Edinburgh undertook an extensive tour of the United Kingdom, visiting every region of England as well as Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Internationally, the couple visited Jamaica, New Zealand, Australia and Canada, where further jubilee events were staged.

Commemoration

Commemorative items issued for the jubilee included coins, stamps and a wide range of souvenirs bearing the official emblem. The Golden Jubilee was followed a decade later by the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II in 2012 and, twenty years later, by the Platinum Jubilee in 2022.
