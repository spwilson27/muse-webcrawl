Ogata Kōrin

Born1658, Kyoto, Japan
Died2 June 1716, Kyoto, Japan
NationalityJapanese
Known forPainting, lacquerware, textile design
MovementRinpa school

Ogata Kōrin (1658 – 2 June 1716) was a Japanese painter, lacquerer, and designer of the Rinpa school. Born into a wealthy Kyoto textile merchant family, he squandered his inheritance before turning seriously to art, and went on to become one of the most celebrated artists of the Edo period.

Career

Kōrin studied under Yamamoto Soken and absorbed the influence of his Rinpa predecessors Hon'ami Kōetsu and Tawaraya Sōtatsu, whose style he revived and transformed. Working with his younger brother Kenzan, a pioneering potter, he produced paintings, lacquerware, and kimono designs of striking boldness and decorative abstraction.

Major works

His masterpieces include the screen paintings Red and White Plum Blossoms, with its sinuous river rendered in swirling tarashikomi technique, the Irises at Yatsuhashi screens, and Waves at Matsushima. These works are designated National Treasures of Japan.

Legacy

The very name "Rinpa" derives from the final syllable of Kōrin's name ("rin") plus "school" ("pa"). His art profoundly influenced later Japanese design and, through Japonisme, European modernists such as the Vienna Secession painters.
