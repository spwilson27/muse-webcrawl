Potaro-Siparuni

Potaro-Siparuni (Region 8)
Country: Guyana
Regional capital: Mahdia
Area: about 20,051 km²
Population: about 11,000 (2012)

Potaro-Siparuni (Region 8) is a region of Guyana. It is named for the Potaro and Siparuni rivers and borders the regions of Cuyuni-Mazaruni to the north, Upper Demerara-Berbice and East Berbice-Corentyne to the east, Upper Takutu-Upper Essequibo to the south, and Brazil to the west. The regional capital is Mahdia.

Geography

Much of the region forms part of the Guiana Shield highlands. It is dominated by the Pakaraima Mountains, and includes Kaieteur Falls — one of the world's most powerful single-drop waterfalls, with a sheer drop of about 226 metres — on the Potaro River, protected within Kaieteur National Park. The Iwokrama Forest reserve partly lies within the region, and much of the land is tropical rainforest.

Economy and population

The population is small and dispersed, with significant Indigenous (Amerindian) communities, including Patamona, Akawaio, and Macushi peoples. Gold and diamond mining, both large-scale and by small "pork-knocker" prospectors, are major activities around Mahdia and the Konawaruk area; logging and ecotourism also contribute. Venezuela claims a large part of western Guyana, including territory west of the Essequibo River, as Guayana Esequiba.
