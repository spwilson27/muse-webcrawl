Castel Gandolfo is a town located 25 kilometres (16 mi) southeast of Rome, in the Italian region of Lazio. Situated on a height in the Alban Hills overlooking Lake Albano, Castel Gandolfo has a population of approximately 9,000 residents and is considered one of Italy's most scenic towns. It is best known internationally as the location of the Papal Palace of Castel Gandolfo, the traditional summer residence of the Pope, which enjoys extraterritorial status under the Lateran Treaty of 1929.

History

The site is traditionally identified with Alba Longa, the ancient Latin city said to have been founded by Ascanius, son of Aeneas. The modern town takes its name from a castle of the Genoese Gandolfi family, built in the 12th century and later acquired by the Savelli family. In 1596 the Apostolic Camera purchased the property, and Pope Urban VIII commissioned a palace on the site in the 1620s, designed by Carlo Maderno. Successive popes enlarged the complex, which includes the palace, gardens, the Villa Barberini and an astronomical observatory, the Vatican Observatory's historical home.

Papal residence

From the 17th century the palace served as the popes' summer retreat. Pope Pius XII died there in 1958, as did Paul VI in 1978. Benedict XVI spent his first months after resigning the papacy at Castel Gandolfo in 2013. Pope Francis chose not to use it as a residence, and in 2016 the palace was opened to the public as a museum, though the estate remains an extraterritorial property of the Holy See.

Geography

The town stands on the western rim of the volcanic crater occupied by Lake Albano, the deepest lake in Lazio. During the 1960 Summer Olympics in Rome, the lake hosted the rowing and canoeing events.
