Dungeness Lighthouse

Dungeness Lighthouse
LocationDungeness, Kent, England
First lit1615 (first tower); 1961 (current tower)
OperatorTrinity House

Dungeness Lighthouse stands at the tip of the Dungeness headland in Kent, England, guiding shipping through the English Channel. There have been five lighthouses on the site, reflecting the continual growth of the shingle spit, which periodically left earlier towers too far inland to be effective.

History

The first lighthouse was built in 1615, a wooden tower with a coal fire. It was replaced in 1635 and again in 1792 by a brick tower built by Samuel Wyatt, whose design resembled the Eddystone Lighthouse. In 1904 a new tower, 43 metres high, was completed; this "Old Lighthouse" still stands and is open to the public as a visitor attraction.

The modern lighthouse

The present lighthouse, commissioned in 1961, was required because the construction of Dungeness nuclear power station obscured the light of the 1904 tower from some bearings. The new tower is notable for its experimental construction from precast concrete rings and for its distinctive flashing light pattern, designed to distinguish it from the power station's lighting. It was one of the first lighthouses converted to automatic operation, in 1991, and is monitored from the Trinity House operations centre at Harwich.
