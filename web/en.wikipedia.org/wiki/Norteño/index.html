Norteño

Norteño or música norteña ("northern music") is a genre of Mexican folk music from northern Mexico. Its defining instruments are the button accordion and the bajo sexto (a twelve-string bass guitar), and its repertoire includes corridos, rancheras, polkas, waltzes, and redowas.

Norteño
Origin: Northern Mexico, early 20th century
Instruments: Accordion, bajo sexto, tololoche, drums
Related: Corrido, banda, tejano

Origins

The genre arose in the late 19th and early 20th centuries along the Mexico–Texas border, blending Mexican corrido traditions with the polka and waltz rhythms brought by German, Czech, and Polish immigrants to northern Mexico and Texas. The accordion, introduced by German settlers, became its signature sound.

Development

Pioneers such as Narciso Martínez ("El Huracán del Valle") popularised the conjunto sound in the 1930s. Los Tigres del Norte, formed in the late 1960s, became the genre's most famous act, famous for narrative corridos about immigration, work, and border life. Modern norteño is closely related to Tejano/conjunto music in Texas and has influenced narcocorridos and regional Mexican pop.
