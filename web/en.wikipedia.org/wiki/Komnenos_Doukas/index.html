Komnenos Doukas

Coinage of the Komnenos Doukas emperors of Thessalonica

The Komnenos Doukas (Greek: Κομνηνός Δούκας), often simply the Doukai of Epirus, was a Byzantine Greek noble family that founded and ruled the Despotate of Epirus from 1205 to 1318, and for a time ruled Thessalonica as rival emperors of the Romans. The house descended from the marriage of Alexios Komnenos, a grandson of Emperor Alexios I Komnenos, and a granddaughter of Emperor John Doukas, combining the names of two great Byzantine dynasties.

Origins and Epirus

The founder of the family's fortunes was Michael I Komnenos Doukas, an illegitimate cousin of the Angeloi emperors. After the Fourth Crusade captured Constantinople in 1204, Michael established himself at Arta in Epirus, building one of the main Greek successor states resisting the Latin Empire. His half-brother Theodore Komnenos Doukas succeeded in 1215, conquered Thessalonica in 1224, and was crowned emperor there, briefly making Epirus the leading candidate to recover Constantinople before his defeat and capture by the Bulgarians at Klokotnitsa in 1230.

Later history

After 1230 the family split into two branches: one at Thessalonica, which submitted to the Empire of Nicaea and faded, and one at Arta, which continued to rule Epirus as despots. The Epirote branch intermarried with the Italian Orsini family, into whose hands the despotate passed in 1318, ending a century of Komnenos Doukas rule. A junior line governed Thessaly from Neopatras, where John I Doukas of Thessaly and his sons were among the most troublesome rivals of the restored Byzantine Empire under Michael VIII Palaiologos. The family name remained prestigious: later Epirote rulers of other houses continued to use "Komnenos Doukas" in their titulature.

Notable members

Members include Michael I Komnenos Doukas (founder of the Epirote state), Theodore Komnenos Doukas (emperor of Thessalonica), Manuel Komnenos Doukas, Michael II Komnenos Doukas, Nikephoros I Komnenos Doukas, Thomas I Komnenos Doukas (last of the line at Arta), John I Doukas of Thessaly, and the celebrated patroness Anna Komnene Doukaina's namesake Anna of Epirus, queen consort of Naples.
