Peter Lalor (5 February 1827 – 9 February 1889) was an Irish-born Australian politician and leader of the gold miners at the Eureka Rebellion in Ballarat, Victoria, in 1854, an uprising against the colonial administration's mining licence system. He later became a member of parliament and Speaker of the Victorian Legislative Assembly.

Early life and emigration

Lalor was born at Raheen, Queen's County (now County Laois), Ireland, into a politically active family; his father Patrick Lalor was a tenant farmer and sometime MP who opposed tithes. Peter trained as a civil engineer and worked on railway construction in Ireland. He emigrated to Australia in 1852 during the Victorian gold rush and worked the goldfields at the Ovens district and then Ballarat with his brother James.

Eureka Rebellion

Resentment among miners over the expensive monthly licence fee, police "licence hunts", and lack of political representation led to the formation of the Ballarat Reform League. Lalor, previously uninvolved in agitation, was elected leader on 30 November 1854 and led the miners in swearing allegiance to the Eureka flag and building a stockade. In the dawn attack on 3 December 1854, government troops stormed the stockade; Lalor was wounded, lost his left arm by amputation, and hid while a reward was offered for his capture. Charges against the captured rebels were dropped after acquittals at trial, and Lalor was never prosecuted.

Political career

The rebellion hastened reform: the licence was replaced by a cheaper miner's right, and goldfields representation was granted. Lalor was elected to the Legislative Council in 1855 and later to the Legislative Assembly, representing South Grant for many years. Initially a radical, he grew more conservative, opposing payment of members and democratic reforms he had once championed. He served as a minister in 1875 and again in the 1880s, and as Speaker of the Legislative Assembly from 1880 to 1887. He was offered a knighthood but declined it.

Legacy

Lalor is commemorated by the Melbourne suburb of Lalor, a statue in Ballarat, and the federal electoral Division of Lalor. The Eureka Rebellion is regarded as a defining moment in the development of Australian democracy.
