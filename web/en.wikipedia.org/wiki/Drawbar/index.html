Drawbar

A drawbar is a solid coupling between a hauling vehicle and its hauled load. Drawbars are in common use with rail transport, road trailers, tractors and agricultural implements, and are a fundamental component of the towing connection between a towing vehicle and a trailer.

TypeVehicle coupling
Used withTractors, trailers, rail vehicles
RelatedTow hitch, fifth-wheel coupling

Agricultural drawbars

On a farm tractor, the drawbar is a steel bar attached to the rear of the tractor, to which implements such as ploughs, mowers and trailers are hitched with a pin. Drawbar horsepower is a standard measure of a tractor's pulling performance, determined in official tests such as those at the Nebraska Tractor Test Laboratory. Swinging and fixed drawbars allow some lateral movement of the implement behind the tractor.

Railway use

In rail transport, a drawbar permanently or semi-permanently connects two rail vehicles, transmitting pulling and braking forces between them. Drawbars are used on articulated railcars and permanently coupled freight wagons, replacing individual couplers and reducing slack action between cars.

Road trailers

On road trailers, the drawbar (or tongue) is the A-frame or straight bar extending forward from the trailer to the tow vehicle's hitch. Full trailers use a pivoting drawbar connected to a pintle hook or ball coupling on the towing vehicle.
