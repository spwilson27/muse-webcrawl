Refreshable braille display

A refreshable braille display or braille terminal is an electro-mechanical device for displaying braille characters, usually by means of round-tipped pins raised through holes in a flat surface. Blind computer users who cannot use a visual monitor can use it to read text output, typically in combination with a screen reader.

Mechanism

Each braille cell contains eight pins (arranged as two columns of four), driven by piezoelectric actuators; the combination of raised pins forms the braille character. Displays commonly present 20, 32, 40 or 80 cells, corresponding to a partial or full line of screen text. The display refreshes as the user navigates the screen, and many models include braille input keys and cursor-routing buttons above each cell.

Use

Braille displays connect to computers and smartphones by USB or Bluetooth and are used with screen readers such as JAWS, NVDA and VoiceOver. They provide access to spelling, punctuation and formatting that speech output conveys poorly, and are especially important for deafblind users. High cost, driven by the piezo cells, remains a barrier, motivating research into cheaper actuator technologies.
