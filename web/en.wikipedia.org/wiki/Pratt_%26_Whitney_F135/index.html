The Pratt & Whitney F135 is an American afterburning turbofan engine developed for the Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II family of stealth strike fighters. Derived from the F119 engine of the F-22 Raptor, it is the most powerful fighter engine in service, producing around 43,000 pounds of thrust with afterburner.

Variants

The F135-PW-100 powers the conventional F-35A; the F135-PW-400 powers the carrier-based F-35C; and the F135-PW-600, combined with the Rolls-Royce LiftSystem, gives the short take-off and vertical landing F-35B its hover capability through a shaft-driven lift fan.

Development and service

The engine was selected as part of the Joint Strike Fighter programme in 2001 and entered service with the F-35's initial operating capability in the mid-2010s. It has attracted attention both for its performance and for sustainment costs and durability issues in hot-section components, which Pratt & Whitney has addressed through upgraded core programmes.
