Abd al-Karim Qasim Muhammad Bakr al-Qasim (Arabic: عبد الكريم قاسم; 21 November 1914 – 9 February 1963) was an Iraqi brigadier and nationalist who seized power in the 14 July Revolution of 1958, overthrowing the Hashemite monarchy and proclaiming the Republic of Iraq. He served as Prime Minister and de facto ruler until he was overthrown and executed in the Ramadan Revolution of February 1963.

Early life and military career

Qasim was born in Baghdad to a poor family of mixed Arab-Kurdish background and raised in Suq al-Shuyukh and Baghdad. He worked as a schoolteacher before entering the military college, graduating as an officer in 1934. He fought in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, where his battalion distinguished itself at Kafr Qasim and Jenin, and rose to command the 19th Brigade, based at Jalawla.

The 14 July Revolution

As a leader of the clandestine Free Officers movement, Qasim used a troop movement toward Jordan as cover to march on Baghdad on 14 July 1958. King Faisal II and the regent Abd al-Ilah were killed, Prime Minister Nuri al-Said was caught and killed the following day, and the monarchy was abolished. Qasim headed a Sovereignty Council and took the premiership himself, sidelining his fellow plotter Abdul Salam Arif within months.

Rule

Qasim withdrew Iraq from the Baghdad Pact, legalised political activity briefly, issued a landmark agrarian reform law, raised living standards, began the giant public-housing project later known as Sadr City, and in 1961 issued Public Law 80 reclaiming most of the Iraq Petroleum Company's unexploited concession area. He relied increasingly on the Iraqi Communist Party, then turned against it after the Mosul revolt and Kirkuk violence of 1959. His claim to Kuwait in 1961 alarmed Arab neighbours and Britain, and a Kurdish rebellion under Mustafa Barzani began the same year. In October 1959 he survived an assassination attempt by a Ba'athist hit team that included a young Saddam Hussein.

Overthrow and death

On 8 February 1963 a Ba'athist–Nasserist coalition seized power. After a day of fighting around the Ministry of Defence, Qasim surrendered, was given a brief televised trial, and was executed by firing squad on 9 February 1963; his body was displayed on television. He was 48.
