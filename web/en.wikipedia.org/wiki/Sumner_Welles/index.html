Sumner Welles

Benjamin Sumner Welles (October 14, 1892 – September 24, 1961) was an American government official and diplomat in the administration of President Franklin D. Roosevelt. As Under Secretary of State from 1937 to 1943, he was one of the most influential figures in American foreign policy, a principal architect of the Good Neighbor policy toward Latin America, and the author of the Welles Declaration of 1940, by which the United States refused to recognise the Soviet annexation of the Baltic states.

Born: October 14, 1892, New York City
Died: September 24, 1961, Bernardsville, New Jersey
Office: Under Secretary of State, 1937–1943

Early life and career

Welles was born into a wealthy New York family, educated at Groton School and Harvard University, and entered the Foreign Service in 1915 at Roosevelt's suggestion. He specialised in Latin American affairs, serving in Argentina and as chief of the State Department's Division of Latin American Affairs, and played a key role in relations with the Dominican Republic and Cuba.

Under Secretary of State

Roosevelt appointed Welles Assistant Secretary of State in 1933 and Under Secretary in 1937. His close relationship with the President allowed him to bypass Secretary of State Cordell Hull, with whom he feuded bitterly. Welles undertook a celebrated fact-finding mission to Europe in early 1940, issued the July 1940 declaration on the Baltic states that governed US policy for fifty years, and helped plan the post-war international order, contributing to the drafting that led to the United Nations. He resigned in 1943 amid scandal and pressure from Hull's allies, and spent his remaining years writing on foreign policy.
