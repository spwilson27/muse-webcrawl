Law of Jersey

The law of Jersey is the legal system of the Bailiwick of Jersey, a Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands. It is a mixed legal system rooted in Norman customary law, overlaid with statutes enacted by the States Assembly and influences from English common law and, in some areas, French civil law.

Sources

Jersey law derives from several sources: the customary law of Normandy, preserved in medieval commentaries; legislation passed by the island's legislature; and case law of the Royal Court. Historically, legal proceedings and many documents were conducted in French, and Norman French terminology survives in Jersey legal usage. Acts of the United Kingdom Parliament apply to Jersey only when expressly extended to it.

Courts

The principal court is the Royal Court, presided over by the Bailiff, sitting with elected Jurats who act as judges of fact. Appeals lie to the Jersey Court of Appeal and ultimately to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London. The island's legal system underpins its substantial financial services industry, particularly in trusts, company, and funds law.
