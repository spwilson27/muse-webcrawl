Storage ring

A storage ring is a type of circular particle accelerator in which a beam of charged particles — typically electrons, positrons, protons or ions — is kept circulating at constant energy for minutes to hours. Bending magnets hold the beam on its closed orbit, focusing magnets control its cross-section, and radio-frequency cavities replace the energy the particles lose as synchrotron radiation.

Storage rings are used as colliders in high-energy physics, where beams of particles and antiparticles are accumulated and then brought into collision at interaction points. Milestones include the SPEAR ring at SLAC, where the J/ψ particle and the tau lepton were discovered, and CERN's LEP, the largest electron–positron collider ever built.

Electron storage rings are also the technology behind dedicated synchrotron light sources, where the synchrotron radiation produced by the circulating beam is extracted at beamlines for experiments in physics, chemistry, biology and materials science. Examples include the ESRF in Grenoble, the Advanced Photon Source in the United States and Diamond Light Source in the United Kingdom.
