Romantic comedy


Genre: Comedy, romance
Media: Film, theatre, television, literature
Subgenres: Screwball comedy, rom-com drama ("dramedy")

Romantic comedy (often shortened to rom-com) is a genre of fiction — in film, theatre, television, and literature — that centres on lighthearted, humorous plot lines focused on romantic ideas, such as the belief that true love can overcome most obstacles. The typical structure follows two people who meet, are separated by circumstance, misunderstanding, or their own flaws, and are ultimately united, often closing with a declaration of love or a wedding.

History

The genre's roots run from Greek New Comedy and Roman comedy through Shakespeare's romantic comedies (Much Ado About Nothing, Twelfth Night) and Restoration comedy to the novels of Jane Austen, whose Pride and Prejudice remains a template for the form. In cinema, the screwball comedies of the 1930s and 1940s — It Happened One Night (1934), Bringing Up Baby (1938), The Philadelphia Story (1940) — set the pattern of witty sparring between equals.

Modern era

Woody Allen's Annie Hall (1977) and the films of Nora Ephron (When Harry Met Sally..., 1989; Sleepless in Seattle, 1993) revived the genre for a more sceptical age. The 1990s and 2000s were a commercial golden age: Pretty Woman (1990), Four Weddings and a Funeral (1994), Notting Hill (1999), and the works of Richard Curtis defined the British rom-com, while stars such as Julia Roberts, Hugh Grant, Meg Ryan, and Tom Hanks became synonymous with the genre. After a theatrical decline in the 2010s, romantic comedies found a second life on streaming platforms.

Conventions

Common devices include the "meet-cute" (a charming or comic first encounter), the confidant friend, the last-minute dash to airport or wedding, the public declaration of love, and obstacles supplied by unsuitable rival partners or class and lifestyle differences. Critics have debated the genre's gender politics and its idealisation of persistence in courtship, while defenders praise its wit, its focus on women's perspectives, and its durable comic architecture.
