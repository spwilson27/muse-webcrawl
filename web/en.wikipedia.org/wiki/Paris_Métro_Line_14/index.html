Paris Métro Line 14 is one of the sixteen lines of the Paris Métro rapid transit system. Opened in 1998, it was the first Paris Métro line built from the outset for fully driverless operation, and the first entirely new line in the city since the 1930s. It runs diagonally from the northwest to the southeast of Paris. In June 2024, as part of the Grand Paris Express project and in time for the Paris Olympic Games, the line was extended north to Saint-Denis–Pleyel and south to Orly Airport, roughly tripling its length and making it the longest line of the network.

An MP 14 train at a Line 14 platform, fitted with screen doors — the line's distinctive feature.

History

Line 14 first opened between Madeleine and Bibliothèque François Mitterrand on 15 October 1998, under the project name Météor (Métro Est-Ouest Rapide). It was extended to Saint-Lazare in 2003 and south to Olympiades in 2007. The line was designed with large, cathedral-like stations, generous headways, and platform screen doors — a novelty in Paris — for safety on the driverless system. A first northern extension to Mairie de Saint-Ouen opened in December 2020. The major 2024 extension added stations including Saint-Denis–Pleyel (a major Grand Paris interchange), Villejuif–Gustave Roussy, and Aéroport d'Orly, integrating the line into the region's expanding automated network.

Technical features

Trains run on rubber tyres on concrete pads, following Paris tradition for newer lines, and are operated automatically (GoA4) from a central control centre. The original MP 89 CA trains were transferred and supplemented by MP 05 and eight-car MP 14 trains as the line lengthened. Station spacing is greater than on historic lines, giving higher commercial speeds.
