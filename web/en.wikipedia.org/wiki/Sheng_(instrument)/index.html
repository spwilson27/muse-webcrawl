Sheng (instrument)

Sheng
Chinese笙 (pinyin: shēng)
ClassificationFree-reed aerophone
Related instrumentsKhaen, shō, hulusi

The sheng (Chinese: 笙; pinyin: shēng) is a Chinese mouth-blown polyphonic free-reed instrument consisting of a number of vertical bamboo pipes set into a wind chamber with a mouthpiece. One of the oldest Chinese instruments, it is traditionally said to imitate the shape of a phoenix with folded wings, and it is unusual among wind instruments in producing chords and sounding on both exhalation and inhalation.

History

Free-reed mouth organs are depicted in Chinese sources from more than 3,000 years ago, and the sheng was an essential instrument of court ritual music (yayue) and later of regional ensembles and Chinese opera. Its free reed, transmitted westward in the late 18th century, inspired the European development of the harmonica, accordion and harmonium. Related instruments include the Laotian and Thai khaen and the Japanese shō used in gagaku.

Construction and playing

Each bamboo pipe contains a small tuned metal free reed at its base and a finger hole; covering the hole while blowing or drawing air causes the pipe to sound. Traditional sheng had 13 to 19 pipes, with 17 common. Modern "improved" sheng developed in the 20th century have 21, 24, 36 or 37 pipes, chromatic range, and sometimes key mechanisms and resonators, allowing the instrument to play as a solo and orchestral instrument in the modern Chinese orchestra, which also uses alto, tenor and bass sheng.

Repertoire

The sheng accompanies kunqu opera and appears in Jiangnan sizhu and other regional ensembles. Virtuoso soloists such as Hu Tianquan and Wu Wei have expanded its repertoire, and contemporary composers worldwide have written concertos for it.
