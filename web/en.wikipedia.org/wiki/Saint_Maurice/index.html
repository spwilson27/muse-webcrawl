Saint Maurice

Saint Maurice (also Moritz, Morris, or Mauritius; d. c. 287) was an Egyptian military leader and the commander of the legendary Theban Legion of the Roman army. According to tradition, he and his legion of Christian soldiers were martyred at Agaunum (present-day Saint-Maurice, Switzerland) after refusing orders they believed contrary to their faith. He is the patron saint of armies, soldiers and the Swiss Guard, and has historically been depicted as a Black African saint, especially in German art.

Legend

The earliest account of Maurice comes from Eucherius of Lyon in the fifth century. It relates that the Theban Legion, some 6,600 Christian soldiers recruited from Thebes in Egypt, was summoned to Gaul by the emperor Maximian. Ordered to take part in sacrifices or to attack fellow Christians, Maurice and his men refused; after two decimations failed to break their resolve, the whole legion was put to death. The site of the martyrdom, Agaunum, became a major pilgrimage centre, and the abbey founded there in 515 is among the oldest continuously occupied monasteries in the West.

Veneration

Maurice's cult spread widely through the Holy Roman Empire. The Holy Lance carried by the emperors was associated with him, and he became a patron of the Ottonian dynasty. In medieval German sculpture — most famously the statue of about 1240 in Magdeburg Cathedral — he is shown as a Black African knight in contemporary armour, one of the earliest realistic depictions of an African in European art. His feast is kept on 22 September, and he remains patron of the Swiss town and abbey of Saint-Maurice in Valais.
