Cities Service Company

The Cities Service Company was an American petroleum and utility holding company founded in 1910 by Henry Latham Doherty. It grew out of Doherty's Denver Gas and Electric Company and expanded into a vast empire of natural gas, oil and electric utilities across the United States.

History

Doherty built Cities Service by acquiring local utility and oil properties, financing the expansion by selling shares to customers — an early populist approach to utility ownership. The Public Utility Holding Company Act of 1935 forced the company to choose between its utility and oil businesses; it retained petroleum, divesting its electric utilities by the 1940s.

The company's service stations used the Cities Service name and its green-and-white branding. In 1965 the marketing name was shortened to Citgo, combining "Cities" with "go"; the famous Citgo sign in Boston descends from a Cities Service sign first erected in 1940. Cities Service was acquired by Occidental Petroleum in 1982, and Citgo was later sold on, eventually passing to Venezuela's state oil company PDVSA.

Legacy

The company's New York headquarters, the Cities Service Building at 70 Pine Street (now the American International Building), is an Art Deco landmark skyscraper completed in 1932.
