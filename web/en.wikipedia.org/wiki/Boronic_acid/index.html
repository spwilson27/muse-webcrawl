Boronic acid

A boronic acid is an organoboron compound containing an alkyl- or aryl-substituted boronic acid group, with the general formula R–B(OH)2. Boronic acids are mild Lewis acids and are notable for reversibly forming covalent bonds with diols, a property exploited in sensing and synthesis. The first boronic acid, ethylboronic acid, was reported by Edward Frankland in 1860.

Applications

Boronic acids are key reagents in the Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling reaction, which links them to aryl or vinyl halides to form carbon–carbon bonds—a transformation recognized by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. They are also used in Chan–Lam coupling, in saccharide sensors for glucose monitoring research, and as pharmaceutical agents; bortezomib, a boronic-acid-based proteasome inhibitor, is an approved anticancer drug, and tavaborole is an antifungal agent.

Properties

Most boronic acids are stable, crystalline, and easy to handle, though they can dehydrate to cyclic anhydrides called boroxines. Their aqueous pKa values typically lie between 8 and 10, reflecting ionization to tetrahedral boronate anions.
