Westwood is a commercial and residential neighbourhood in the western part of Los Angeles, California. It is best known as the home of the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), whose campus occupies the northern part of the neighbourhood.

History

Westwood was developed in the 1920s by the Janss Investment Company on land formerly part of Rancho San José de Buenos Ayres. Westwood Village, the Mediterranean-style shopping district designed around pedestrian courts and landmark movie theatres such as the Fox Village Theatre (1931) and the Bruin Theatre, opened in 1929. UCLA moved to its Westwood site in 1929. After the Second World War, high-rise office and condominium towers rose along Wilshire Boulevard, including the Wilshire Corridor to the east.

Geography and institutions

Westwood is bordered by Brentwood to the west, Bel-Air to the north, Beverly Hills to the east and West Los Angeles to the south. Institutions include the Hammer Museum, the Geffen Playhouse, the Armand Hammer Museum of Art, the Los Angeles California Temple of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and the Tehran-Angeles district along Westwood Boulevard, a centre of the Iranian diaspora.
