A Passage to India is a 1924 novel by the English author E. M. Forster set against the backdrop of the British Raj and the Indian independence movement in the 1920s. It was selected as one of the 100 great works of 20th-century English literature and won the 1924 James Tait Black Memorial Prize for fiction.

Plot

The novel centres on Dr Aziz, a young Indian Muslim physician in the fictional city of Chandrapore, and his relationships with the English schoolmaster Cyril Fielding and the visiting Englishwomen Adela Quested and Mrs Moore. During a visit to the Marabar Caves, Adela believes she has been assaulted by Aziz in one of the caves; the ensuing trial polarises the colonial community and ends Aziz's friendships with the English, reflecting the deep divisions of British India.

Themes

The book explores the possibility — or impossibility — of friendship between coloniser and colonised, the nature of truth and ambiguity (symbolised by the echo of the Marabar Caves), and the failure of liberal humanism under colonial conditions. Forster drew on his own visits to India in 1912–13 and 1921–22.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted for the stage by Santha Rama Rau and for film in 1984 by David Lean, in his final feature film.
