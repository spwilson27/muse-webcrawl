Saṃsāra (Jainism)

In Jainism, saṃsāra is the worldly cycle of birth and rebirth through which every soul (jīva) wanders, driven by karma, until liberation (mokṣa) is attained. Jain texts describe saṃsāra as beginningless: souls have transmigrated through innumerable lives since eternity, taking birth in the four states of existence according to the karmic matter bound to them.

The Jain universe (loka), within which souls cycle among the heavenly, human, animal and hellish realms.

The four destinies

Rebirth occurs in one of four gatis (destinies or states):

  • Deva — celestial beings in the heavens, whose lives are pleasurable but finite.
  • Manuṣya — human beings; only from a human birth can liberation be achieved.
  • Nāraki — hell beings, born in the hells below the earth to suffer the fruits of heavy negative karma.
  • Tiryañca — animals, plants, and elemental beings (earth-, water-, fire- and air-bodied beings and nigodas), possessing from one to five senses.

Karma and transmigration

Unlike the more metaphorical treatment in some Indian traditions, Jain doctrine holds karma to be a subtle form of matter (pudgala) that physically adheres to the soul, weighing it down and colouring its innate qualities of infinite knowledge, perception, bliss, and energy. Activities of body, speech, and mind — especially those driven by the passions (kaṣāya): anger, pride, deceit, and greed — cause influx (āsrava) and bondage (bandha) of karmic particles, determining the soul's next birth, lifespan, body, and experiences. The fourteen guṇasthānas map the soul's progressive purification from delusion to omniscience (kevala jñāna).

Liberation

Through right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct — the ratnatraya — a soul stops new influx (saṃvara) and sheds accumulated karma (nirjarā), through austerities and equanimity. When all karma is destroyed, the soul rises to the top of the universe, to Siddhaloka, where it abides forever in omniscience and bliss, never to return to saṃsāra. Such liberated souls are the siddhas, while those who attain omniscience while still embodied are kevalins; the ford-makers among them are the twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras.
