Chevron (insignia)

Chevron
ShapeV-shaped or inverted-V stripe
Used byArmies, navies, police, fire services
DenotesRank (esp. non-commissioned officers), length of service
Origin of wordFrench chevron, "rafter"

A chevron is a V-shaped stripe used as an insignia of rank or service in military and paramilitary uniforms, and as a motif in heraldry. The word derives from the French chevron, meaning a rafter, after the shape's resemblance to roof beams.

Military use

Chevrons are worn on the sleeve or shoulder to denote non-commissioned officer rank in many armed forces: a corporal typically wears two, a sergeant three, with additional rockers or stars for senior grades in the United States forces. In British and Commonwealth armies, chevrons are also awarded as long-service and good-conduct badges. Historically, the orientation varied — British chevrons point downward, while American NCO chevrons point upward.

History

Chevron-like badges appeared in Napoleonic-era uniforms to mark veterans' service. The British Army adopted rank chevrons in the early 19th century, and the device spread through colonial and allied forces. Naval ratings use chevrons combined into "crow's foot" patterns on rating badges.

Heraldry and other uses

In heraldry a chevron is an ordinary (geometric charge) shaped like an inverted V. Police and fire services worldwide use chevrons for rank and years-of-service marks, and the motif appears widely in corporate logos.
