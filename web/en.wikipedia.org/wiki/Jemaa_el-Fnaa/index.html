Jemaa el-Fnaa (Arabic: ساحة جامع الفناء, also romanised Jamaa el Fna and Jemaa el-Fna) is a square and market place in Marrakesh's medina quarter (old city). It remains the main square of Marrakesh, used by locals and tourists alike, and is one of the best-known squares in Africa. The square is the heart of the old city, lying at the junction of the Koutoubia Mosque, the royal kasbah district, and the labyrinth of souks.

The name's meaning is disputed — often glossed as "assembly of the dead" or "mosque of annihilation," possibly referring to a ruined Almoravid mosque on the site or, according to tradition, to public executions displayed there. The square's cultural space was proclaimed by UNESCO in 2001 as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, the first designation of its kind.

History

Marrakesh was founded around 1070 by the Almoravid emir Yusuf ibn Tashufin, and the open ground south of the Koutoubia became the city's public gathering place. Under the Almohads the square adjoined the kasbah and its congregational mosque; in later centuries it hosted markets, public ceremonies, military parades and, reputedly, executions of criminals and rebels whose heads were displayed there. The 1931 description by Elias Canetti in The Voices of Marrakesh, and the advocacy of Spanish writer Juan Goytisolo, helped draw international attention to preserving the square's oral traditions; Goytisolo's campaign contributed to UNESCO's recognition.

On 28 April 2011, a bomb in the Argana Café overlooking the square killed 17 people, mostly foreign tourists — the deadliest attack in Morocco since the 2003 Casablanca bombings.

Description

During the day the square is occupied by orange juice stalls, water sellers in traditional costume with brass cups, snake charmers, Barbary ape handlers, henna tattooists, dentists, and storytellers and acrobats performing before gathered circles (halqa). Around the square are hotels, cafés (including the Café de France), the covered souks to the north, and the gardens and minaret of the Koutoubia Mosque dominating the western skyline.

Night market

As dusk falls, the square transforms into one of the world's largest open-air food markets: more than a hundred stalls are erected nightly, each with benches and grills serving harira soup, grilled meats and kebabs, sheep's head, snails in spiced broth, fried fish, couscous, and mint tea, accompanied by Gnawa musicians, drummers, and crowds of diners. Stallholders compete vigorously, sometimes theatrically, for customers.

Cultural heritage

The "Cultural space of Jemaa el-Fna Square" was among the first entries on UNESCO's intangible heritage lists, recognising the square's storytellers (hlaykiya), musicians, and popular traditions as a living heritage threatened by urban development and tourism pressures. The square forms part of the Medina of Marrakesh World Heritage Site inscribed in 1985.
