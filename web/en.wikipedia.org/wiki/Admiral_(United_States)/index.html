Admiral (United States)

Image placeholder: insignia of a four-star admiral, US Navy

Admiral is a four-star commissioned officer rank in the United States Navy, the United States Coast Guard, and the United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps. Its pay grade is O-10 and it is the highest rank that can be routinely attained in those services; it ranks above vice admiral and below the special wartime grade of fleet admiral. The corresponding rank in the other uniformed services is general.

Service branches: US Navy, US Coast Guard, USPHS
Pay grade: O-10
NATO code: OF-9
Abbreviation: ADM

History

The United States Navy had no officer ranked above captain until 1862, when Congress created the grade of rear admiral during the Civil War. David Farragut became the first vice admiral in 1864 and the first full admiral in 1866; David Dixon Porter and, by special act, George Dewey followed. The five-star grade of fleet admiral was created in 1944 for the Second World War and has not been awarded since.

Appointment and limits

Admirals are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. By statute, four-star billets are tied to specific positions, such as the Chief of Naval Operations, the Vice Chief of Naval Operations, commanders of combatant commands, and the Commandant of the Coast Guard. The number of four-star officers is capped by law, and admiral normally carries a mandatory retirement after a set period in grade or age.

Insignia

A US Navy admiral wears four silver stars on shoulder boards and four sleeve stripes — one broad band surmounted by three narrower stripes — while Coast Guard admirals wear a similar pattern on a Coast Guard blue uniform.
