Orc (Middle-earth)

Orcs

Created by: J. R. R. Tolkien

First appearance: The Hobbit (1937)

Homeland: Mordor, Misty Mountains, Isengard

Language: Black Speech, Orkish dialects, Westron

Orcs are a fictional race of humanoid creatures in the works of J. R. R. Tolkien, most prominently in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings. They are the principal foot soldiers of the Dark Lords Morgoth and Sauron, and are depicted as ugly, brutish, quarrelsome beings, fond of fighting and cruelty, who shun sunlight and live in mountain caves and the land of Mordor.

Tolkien's orcs served as a convenient enemy the heroes could slaughter without moral qualms, but their origin and moral status troubled him throughout his life. In The Silmarillion he suggested that orcs were bred by Morgoth from captured Elves, corrupted and twisted into a mockery of their form — a notion he later doubted, since it implied that orcs were irredeemably evil and ensouled, a problem for his Catholic theology. Other drafts proposed that they were corrupted Men, beasts given speech, or fallen Maiar.

Characteristics

Orcs vary in size and shape, from small snaga ("slaves") to the large fighting Uruk-hai bred by Saruman and Sauron. They are generally described as shorter than Men, with long arms, bowed legs, fanged teeth and dark or sallow skin. They hate the Sun and prefer darkness, though the Uruk-hai could endure daylight. Orcs are organised in tribes with chiefs, speak debased dialects of the Black Speech or the Common Speech, and are skilled in making weapons and machines of war while having little love of beauty.

History within the legendarium

Orcs first appear in the First Age as Morgoth's servants in the wars against the Elves of Beleriand. After Morgoth's defeat they survived in the Misty Mountains, where — called "goblins" in The Hobbit — they killed Bilbo's companions' ponies and fought the Battle of Five Armies. In the Third Age Sauron gathered them in Mordor, and Saruman bred his own Uruk-hai at Isengard. Following Sauron's final defeat in the War of the Ring the orcs scattered and faded.

Derivation and influence

Tolkien took the word from Old English orc, a demon or ogre, found in Beowulf, ultimately from Latin Orcus, god of the underworld. His portrayal drew on earlier fantasy such as George MacDonald's goblins. Tolkien's orcs became the template for the orcs of modern fantasy literature, tabletop games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games, though later works have often softened the portrayal considerably.
