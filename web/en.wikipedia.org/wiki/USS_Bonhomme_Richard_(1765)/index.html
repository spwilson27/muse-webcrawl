USS Bonhomme Richard (1765)

Bonhomme Richard, originally the French East Indiaman Duc de Duras, was a warship of the Continental Navy commanded by John Paul Jones during the American Revolutionary War. Built in 1765 at Lorient, France, the merchantman was placed at Jones's disposal by King Louis XVI in 1779 and renamed in honor of Benjamin Franklin — "Bonhomme Richard" being the French rendering of "Poor Richard," Franklin's pen name.

The Serapis battle

On 23 September 1779, off Flamborough Head on the Yorkshire coast, Bonhomme Richard engaged the Royal Navy frigate HMS Serapis. In one of the most famous single-ship actions of the age of sail, the ships lashed together and fought at point-blank range for over three hours. When called upon to surrender, Jones is traditionally quoted as replying, "I have not yet begun to fight!" Despite his own ship being shattered, burning, and sinking, Jones captured Serapis.

Fate

Bonhomme Richard was so badly damaged that she sank on 25 September 1779, two days after the battle, while Jones sailed his prize to the Texel in the Netherlands. Her wreck has never been conclusively located despite numerous searches in the North Sea off Flamborough Head. The battle made Jones a legend in America and France, and the name Bonhomme Richard has since been borne by several United States Navy ships, including aircraft carriers and an amphibious assault ship.
