The monarchy of the Netherlands is a constitutional monarchy whose head of state is the King or Queen of the Netherlands, a member of the House of Orange-Nassau. The present monarch is King Willem-Alexander, who ascended the throne on 30 April 2013 following the abdication of his mother, Queen Beatrix.

Constitutional role

Under the constitution, the monarch is head of state and, together with the ministers, forms the government; ministers, not the monarch, are politically responsible for acts of government. The monarch formally appoints ministers after elections, delivers the annual Speech from the Throne (written by the government) on Prinsjesdag, signs acts of parliament and represents the kingdom abroad. The role is therefore largely ceremonial and symbolic, though the monarch retains a right to be consulted and to encourage and warn.

History

The kingdom dates to 1815, when William I was proclaimed king after the Napoleonic Wars, though the House of Orange had supplied stadtholders to the Dutch Republic for centuries before. Three queens regnant—Wilhelmina, Juliana and Beatrix—reigned from 1890 to 2013, and abdication rather than death has become the normal mode of succession for Dutch monarchs. The heir bears the title Prince or Princess of Orange.
