Rigoletto

Rigoletto is an opera in three acts by Giuseppe Verdi. The Italian libretto was written by Francesco Maria Piave based on the 1832 play Le roi s'amuse by Victor Hugo. Despite serious initial problems with the Austrian censors who had control over northern Italian theatres at the time, the opera had a triumphant premiere at La Fenice in Venice on 11 March 1851 and is considered by many to be the first of the operatic masterpieces of Verdi's middle-to-late career.

Synopsis

The tragedy revolves around the licentious Duke of Mantua, his hunch-backed court jester Rigoletto, and Rigoletto's beautiful daughter Gilda. The opera's original title, La maledizione (The Curse), refers to a curse placed on both the Duke and Rigoletto by a courtier whose daughter the Duke has seduced with Rigoletto's encouragement. The curse comes to fruition when Gilda falls in love with the Duke and eventually sacrifices her life to save him from the assassin hired by her father.

Music

Famous numbers include the Duke's cynical aria "La donna è mobile" ("Woman is fickle"), Gilda's "Caro nome", and the quartet "Bella figlia dell'amore", which Hugo himself admired. Verdi guarded "La donna è mobile" closely before the premiere, keeping it from the cast until the last moment because he knew it would instantly become popular.

Reception and legacy

Verdi himself considered Rigoletto among his finest works. Along with Il trovatore and La traviata, both of which followed within two years, it established his international fame, and it remains one of the most frequently performed operas worldwide.
