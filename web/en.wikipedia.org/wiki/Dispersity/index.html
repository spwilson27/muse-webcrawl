Dispersity

In polymer chemistry, dispersity (symbol Đ, formerly called the polydispersity index, PDI) is a measure of the distribution of molecular mass in a given polymer sample. It is defined as the ratio of the weight-average molar mass (Mw) to the number-average molar mass (Mn):

Đ = Mw / Mn

Interpretation

Dispersity is always greater than or equal to 1. A perfectly uniform polymer, in which all chains have the same length, has Đ = 1 and is called monodisperse; such materials are rare in synthetic polymers but occur in many biopolymers such as proteins. Typical synthetic polymers have dispersities from about 1.05 (for controlled polymerisations) to 2 or more (for conventional step-growth or free-radical polymerisation).

Measurement

Average molar masses are measured by techniques such as size-exclusion chromatography (gel permeation chromatography), light scattering, and osmometry. IUPAC recommends the term "dispersity" in place of "polydispersity index".
