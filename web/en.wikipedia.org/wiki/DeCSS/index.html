DeCSS

DeCSS is one of the first free computer programs capable of decrypting content on a commercially produced DVD video disc. It decrypts the Content Scramble System (CSS), the encryption scheme used on DVDs. DeCSS was released in October 1999, based on reverse engineering of a Xing software DVD player whose player keys had not been properly protected.

Legal controversies

The Motion Picture Association of America sued websites that distributed DeCSS under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act. In Universal City Studios v. Corley (2001), the Second Circuit upheld an injunction against 2600 magazine. In Norway, teenage contributor Jon Lech Johansen was prosecuted but acquitted. In California, the trade-secret case against Andrew Bunner ultimately ended in favor of publication.

Legacy

DeCSS became a symbol in debates over digital rights and free speech; its short source code was printed on T-shirts, written into poems and songs, and studied as a test case for the legality of reverse engineering. The more capable libdvdcss library later superseded it in practice.
