Girevoy sport

Girevoy sport (Russian: гиревой спорт), also known as kettlebell sport or kettlebell lifting, is a competitive weightlifting discipline originating in Russia and the Soviet Union, in which athletes perform as many repetitions as possible of standard kettlebell lifts within a set time, traditionally ten minutes, without setting the bells down.

Events

The classical programme for men is the biathlon: the two-kettlebell jerk, followed after a rest by the one-kettlebell snatch (one hand switch permitted). A separate event, the long cycle (clean and jerk with two kettlebells), is also widely contested. Women compete in the snatch and, increasingly, in one-arm jerk and long cycle events. Competition kettlebells are colour-coded steel bells of identical dimensions regardless of weight — elite men typically lift 32 kg bells, elite women 24 kg — and athletes are divided by bodyweight class. Repetitions must meet technical standards judged from the platform, with fixation of the bell overhead required before each rep counts.

History

Kettlebell lifting grew out of Russian strongman and circus traditions of the 18th–19th centuries; organised competition dates from the late 19th century, and the sport was formalised in the USSR, where it was recognised as a national sport in 1985. International federations — notably the International Union of Kettlebell Lifting (IUKL) — were founded after the Soviet collapse, and world championships have been held since the 1990s. The sport demands exceptional muscular endurance, grip strength and technique rather than maximal single lifts.
