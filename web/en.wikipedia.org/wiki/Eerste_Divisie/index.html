The Eerste Divisie (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈeːrstə diˈvizi]; English: First Division) is the second-highest tier of football in the Netherlands. It is organised by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB) and sits directly below the top-flight Eredivisie. For sponsorship reasons the league has been known as the Keuken Kampioen Divisie since the 2018–19 season.

The Eerste Divisie is the second tier of Dutch professional football.

History

The Eerste Divisie was founded in 1956, two years after the introduction of professional football in the Netherlands. For much of its history it was divided into two parallel groups, before being consolidated into a single league. From the 1971–72 season until 2008–09 the league operated without relegation to amateur football, making it effectively a closed second tier; a system of promotion and relegation with the amateur leagues was later reintroduced in a limited form.

Format

The league normally consists of 20 clubs, including a number of reserve ("Jong") teams of Eredivisie clubs, such as Jong Ajax, Jong PSV, Jong AZ and Jong FC Utrecht. The champions and runners-up are promoted directly to the Eredivisie, while a further group of clubs contest play-offs for the remaining promotion place. The season is divided into periods, and the winners of each period qualify for the promotion play-offs, a distinctive feature of the competition.

Notable aspects

  • Reserve teams cannot be promoted and are ineligible for the play-offs.
  • The league is known for developing young players who later move to the Eredivisie and abroad.
  • Matches are traditionally played on Friday evenings.
