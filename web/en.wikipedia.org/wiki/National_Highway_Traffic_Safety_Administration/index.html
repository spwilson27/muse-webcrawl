The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is an agency of the United States Department of Transportation responsible for reducing deaths, injuries and economic losses from road traffic crashes. It sets and enforces vehicle safety standards, investigates defects, administers vehicle recalls, and works with states on driver behaviour such as drunk driving, seat-belt use and distracted driving.

Functions

NHTSA issues the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards that govern everything from brakes and lights to crashworthiness. Its New Car Assessment Program (NCAP) publishes the familiar five-star safety ratings based on frontal, side and rollover crash tests. The agency also sets corporate average fuel economy (CAFE) standards, maintains the Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS) census of fatal crashes, runs public campaigns such as "Click It or Ticket" and "Drive Sober or Get Pulled Over", and operates the safercar.gov recall database and consumer hotline.

History

The agency was created by the Highway Safety Act of 1970, in the wake of Ralph Nader's Unsafe at Any Speed (1965) and the first federal vehicle safety laws of 1966. U.S. road deaths per mile travelled have fallen by roughly three-quarters since its founding, an achievement attributed to safer vehicles, graduated licensing, enforcement and public campaigns — though annual fatalities remain around 40,000, and recent rises in pedestrian deaths and questions over driver-assistance and automated-driving systems have kept the agency under close scrutiny.
