An able seaman (AB) is a seaman and member of the deck department of a merchant ship with more than two years' experience at sea and considered "well acquainted with his duty". An AB performs watchkeeping, butting, cleaning, and maintenance duties and often steers the ship while underway.

Duties

An able seaman must be able to splice wire and fibre rope, work aloft and over the side, operate deck equipment such as winches and cranes, secure cargo, and handle mooring lines. Able seamen also stand lookout and steering watches and typically serve as helmsmen during manoeuvres directed by a pilot or officer.

Certification

Under the STCW Convention, able seafarers (deck) must demonstrate competence and documented sea service. In the United States, the Coast Guard issues credentials for able seaman grades, which traditionally required a specified number of days of deck service.

Naval rating

In the Royal Navy, "Able Seaman" (AB) is also a junior rank — ordinary seamen are promoted to able rate after training and service. It is the second-lowest enlisted rank in several Commonwealth navies.
