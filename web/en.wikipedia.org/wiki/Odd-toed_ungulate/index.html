Odd-toed ungulate

Odd-toed ungulates (order Perissodactyla) are mammals with hooves and an odd number of toes on their hind feet — usually one or three — with the axis of the foot passing through the enlarged middle (third) toe. Living members are horses, rhinoceroses and tapirs, about 17 species.

Biology

Perissodactyls are hindgut fermenters, generally large herbivores with simple stomachs and enlarged caeca. Horses have a single toe; rhinos three; tapirs four on front feet, three on hind.

Evolution

The order arose in the early Eocene and was once far more diverse, including chalicotheres and giant Paraceratherium, one of the largest land mammals. Diversity declined as even-toed ungulates (artiodactyls) radiated; many living rhinos and tapirs are endangered.
