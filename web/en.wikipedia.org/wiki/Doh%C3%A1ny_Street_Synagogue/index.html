Dohány Street Synagogue

LocationDohány Street, Budapest, Hungary
Completed1859
Capacity2,964

The Dohány Street Synagogue (Hungarian: Dohány utcai zsinagóga), also known as the Great Synagogue, is a historical building in Erzsébetváros, the 7th district of Budapest, Hungary. It is the largest synagogue in Europe and one of the largest in the world, seating 2,964 people.

Architecture

Built between 1854 and 1859 in the Moorish Revival style, to designs by the Viennese architect Ludwig Förster, the synagogue is notable for its twin towers with onion domes, rose window and opulent interior. Its organ was played by Franz Liszt and Camille Saint-Saëns.

History

Theodor Herzl, founder of modern political Zionism, was born in a house on the site now occupied by the adjacent Jewish Museum. During World War II the synagogue stood within the Budapest Ghetto; its grounds contain a memorial and the mass graves of Jews who died there. The building was restored in the 1990s.
