National symbols of Scotland

The national symbols of Scotland are the symbols used to represent Scotland and Scottish culture. They include official emblems such as the national flag and royal arms, together with long-established unofficial symbols like the thistle and the unicorn.

Flag and arms

The national flag is the Saltire, or St Andrew's Cross — a white diagonal cross on a blue field, associated with Scotland's patron saint, Saint Andrew. The Royal Arms of Scotland feature a red lion rampant on a gold field within a double border decorated with fleurs-de-lis, supported by two unicorns. The unicorn is Scotland's national animal, appearing in Scottish heraldry since the 12th century.

Floral emblem

The thistle is Scotland's floral emblem. Legend holds that a barefoot invading Norseman stepped on a thistle and cried out, alerting Scottish defenders. The thistle appears on coinage, in the Order of the Thistle (Scotland's order of chivalry), and in the motto Nemo me impune lacessit ("No one attacks me with impunity").

Other symbols

Other symbols include tartan and the kilt, the Great Highland bagpipe, heather, the Scots pine (named national tree in 2014), and the golden eagle (national bird). Scotland has no official national anthem; "Flower of Scotland", "Scotland the Brave" and "Scots Wha Hae" are all used on different occasions. The Stone of Scone (Stone of Destiny), used in coronations, is another potent historic symbol.
