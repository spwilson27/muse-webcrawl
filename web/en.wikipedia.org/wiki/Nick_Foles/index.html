Nicholas Edward Foles (born January 20, 1989) is an American former professional football quarterback who played in the National Football League (NFL) for 11 seasons. A member of the Philadelphia Eagles for most of his career, he is best known for leading the Eagles to victory in Super Bowl LII over the New England Patriots, earning Super Bowl MVP honors — the franchise's first Super Bowl championship.

College career

Foles began his college career at Michigan State before transferring to the University of Arizona, where he became the Wildcats' starting quarterback and set numerous school passing records.

Professional career

Drafted by the Eagles in the third round of the 2012 NFL draft, Foles took over as starter during the 2013 season under coach Chip Kelly and produced one of the most efficient seasons in league history, throwing 27 touchdowns against only 2 interceptions and tying the NFL record with seven touchdown passes in a single game against the Oakland Raiders. He was traded to the St. Louis Rams in 2015 and later spent a season with the Kansas City Chiefs before returning to Philadelphia in 2017 as the backup to Carson Wentz.

When Wentz suffered a season-ending knee injury late in the 2017 season, Foles stepped in and led the Eagles through the playoffs. In Super Bowl LII, Foles threw for 373 yards and three touchdowns and caught a touchdown pass on the famous "Philly Special" trick play, defeating Tom Brady and the Patriots 41–33. He was named Super Bowl MVP. A statue of Foles and coach Doug Pederson commemorating the Philly Special stands outside Lincoln Financial Field.

Foles later played for the Jacksonville Jaguars, Chicago Bears, and Indianapolis Colts before announcing his retirement in 2024, signing a one-day contract to retire as an Eagle.
