Black Tuesday

Black Tuesday
DateOctober 29, 1929
LocationNew York Stock Exchange, New York City
EventStock market crash

Black Tuesday was October 29, 1929, the day the New York Stock Exchange collapsed in the most devastating stock market crash in the history of the United States. It followed Black Thursday (October 24) and Black Monday (October 28) and marked the culmination of the Wall Street Crash of 1929, which signalled the beginning of the decade-long Great Depression.

Events

On Black Tuesday, panicked sellers traded a record 16.4 million shares, overwhelming the ticker tape system, which ran hours behind and compounded the panic. Attempts by leading bankers to support prices, including large purchases by Richard Whitney on Black Thursday, failed to restore confidence. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell roughly 12% on the day, following a similar drop the previous day, and continued falling into November and, with interruptions, until 1932, by which time it had lost nearly 90% of its peak value.

Causes and aftermath

The crash followed a speculative bubble inflated by margin buying and exuberant expectations during the Roaring Twenties. Its aftermath included widespread bank failures, collapsing demand and mass unemployment, and led to regulatory reforms such as the creation of the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission and the Glass–Steagall Act. The term "Black Tuesday" has since been applied to other severe single-day market declines.
