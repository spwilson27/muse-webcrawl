Richard E. Byrd

Richard Evelyn Byrd Jr.
Born: 25 October 1888, Winchester, Virginia
Died: 11 March 1957, Boston, Massachusetts
Rank: Rear Admiral, US Navy
Known for: Polar exploration by air

Richard Evelyn Byrd Jr. (25 October 1888 – 11 March 1957) was an American naval officer, aviator and polar explorer. He claimed the first flights over the North Pole (1926) and South Pole (1929), led five major expeditions to Antarctica, and received the Medal of Honor.

North Pole flight

On 9 May 1926, Byrd and pilot Floyd Bennett flew from Spitsbergen in the Fokker trimotor Josephine Ford, claiming to have reached the North Pole and returned in under sixteen hours. The flight made Byrd an international hero and earned him the Medal of Honor, although later analysis of his diary and records has led many historians to doubt that the aircraft actually reached the pole.

Transatlantic and Antarctic flights

In 1927 Byrd's team flew the Atlantic from New York to France, crash-landing off Normandy. On 28–29 November 1929, with Bernt Balchen at the controls, he flew over the South Pole from the Little America base on the Ross Ice Shelf, dropping a US flag at the pole — the undisputed first flight over it.

Antarctic expeditions and later life

Byrd led further expeditions in 1933–35, 1939–41, 1946–47 (the massive naval Operation Highjump) and 1955–56 (Operation Deep Freeze). In 1934 he spent nearly five months alone at an advance weather station in the Antarctic interior, nearly dying of carbon monoxide poisoning — an ordeal recounted in his memoir Alone (1938). He died in Boston in 1957, the year of the International Geophysical Year he had helped inspire.
