Date: 1125–1234
Location: Northern and central China
Result: Jin conquers northern China; Song survives as Southern Song until the Mongol conquest

Jin–Song wars

The Jin–Song wars were a series of conflicts between the Jurchen-led Jin dynasty and the Han Chinese Song dynasty in the 12th and 13th centuries.

Fall of Northern Song

The Song and Jin initially allied against their common enemy, the Liao dynasty, under the Alliance Conducted at Sea (1120). After the Liao fell, relations collapsed, and Jin armies invaded in 1125, capturing the Song capital Kaifeng in the Jingkang incident of 1127 and taking the emperors Huizong and Qinzong captive. The Song court fled south and re-established itself at Hangzhou as the Southern Song.

Continued warfare and settlement

Fighting continued for decades along the Huai River frontier. Song generals such as Yue Fei won notable victories but were checked by a peace faction at court; the Treaty of Shaoxing (1141) fixed the border at the Huai, required the Song to pay annual tribute, and was accompanied by Yue Fei's execution. Later campaigns — the Jin prince Hailing's invasion of 1161, the Song's Kaixi Northern Expedition (1206–1208), and fighting during the Mongol rise (1217–1224) — ended in stalemate. The Song eventually allied with the Mongols, and Jin fell to them in 1234.
