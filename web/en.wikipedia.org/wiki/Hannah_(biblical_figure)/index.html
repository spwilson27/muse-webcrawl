Hannah (biblical figure)

Hannah (Hebrew: חַנָּה, Ḥannāh, "favour" or "grace") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the wife of Elkanah and the mother of the prophet Samuel. Her story is told in the first two chapters of the First Book of Samuel.

Narrative

Elkanah had two wives: Hannah, who was childless, and Peninnah, who had children and provoked Hannah over her barrenness. On an annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, Hannah prayed silently and fervently for a son, vowing to dedicate him to God's service. The priest Eli, initially mistaking her silent prayer for drunkenness, blessed her. Hannah conceived and bore Samuel, whom she presented at Shiloh after weaning, leaving him to be raised in the sanctuary.

Hannah's prayer of thanksgiving (1 Samuel 2:1–10) is a celebrated poem of praise celebrating God's reversal of fortunes — exalting the humble and bringing down the proud — and is often seen as a literary model for the Magnificat in the Gospel of Luke. The narrative states that Hannah later bore three more sons and two daughters. Because of her dedication of Samuel, Hannah is regarded in Jewish and Christian tradition as a model of prayer and devotion.
