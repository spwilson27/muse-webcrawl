Guru (rapper)

Keith Edward Elam (July 17, 1961 – April 19, 2010), better known by his stage name Guru (an acronym for "Gifted Unlimited Rhymes Universal"), was an American rapper, producer and actor. He was a founding member of the hip hop duo Gang Starr alongside DJ Premier, and was acclaimed for his smooth, monotone delivery and thoughtful, jazz-inflected lyricism.

Career

Born in the Roxbury neighborhood of Boston, Elam graduated from Morehouse College in Atlanta before moving to New York, where he formed Gang Starr in 1987. With DJ Premier, the duo released a string of critically acclaimed albums including Step in the Arena (1991), Daily Operation (1992), Hard to Earn (1994) and Moment of Truth (1998), blending jazz samples with streetwise, philosophical rhymes.

As a solo artist, Guru pioneered the fusion of hip hop and live jazz with his Jazzmatazz series, beginning with Jazzmatazz, Vol. 1 (1993), which featured collaborations with musicians such as Donald Byrd, Roy Ayers and Branford Marsalis, and vocalists including N'Dea Davenport and MC Solaar. He released further volumes in 1995, 2000 and 2007, as well as the solo albums Version 7.0: The Street Scriptures (2005) and Guru 8.0: Lost and Found (2009).

Death and legacy

Guru died on April 19, 2010, from complications of multiple myeloma. He is remembered as one of hip hop's most literate and distinctive voices; his baritone delivery and commitment to merging jazz with rap influenced generations of artists. Gang Starr's catalogue was posthumously celebrated with the 2019 album One of the Best Yet.
