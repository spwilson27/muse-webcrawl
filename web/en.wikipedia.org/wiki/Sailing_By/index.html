Sailing By

Composer: Ronald Binge
Composed: 1963
Genre: Light music
Known for: Theme preceding the BBC Shipping Forecast

"Sailing By" is a piece of light music composed by Ronald Binge in 1963, performed by the Alan Perry/William Gardner Orchestra (the BBC Midland Light Orchestra recording). It is best known in the United Kingdom as the music broadcast on BBC Radio 4 in the minutes before the late-night Shipping Forecast, where it has been played since 1967.

Use on Radio 4

The tune is broadcast each night shortly before 00:48, preceding the Shipping Forecast on Radio 4's longwave and FM frequencies. Its purpose was practical as well as evocative: playing the same piece in full before the forecast allowed sailors and listeners to tune in and adjust their radios in time for the start of the bulletin, and to find the station in the dark. The music's gentle, rolling character has made it beloved by many land-based listeners, for whom it has become a kind of lullaby and a symbol of Radio 4's nighttime schedule.

Composer

Ronald Binge (1910–1979) was a British composer and arranger of light music who had worked with Mantovani, originating the "cascading strings" effect. His other well-known works include "Elizabethan Serenade" (1951) and "The Watermill".

Cultural status

Repeated proposals within the BBC to drop or shorten "Sailing By" have provoked strong public campaigns to retain it, and it remains an iconic part of the British broadcasting landscape, closely associated with the Shipping Forecast itself.
