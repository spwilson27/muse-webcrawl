Dahmane El Harrachi

Dahmane El Harrachi (Arabic: دحمان الحراشي; 7 July 1926 – 31 August 1980), born Abderrahmane Amrani, was an Algerian singer-songwriter and a master of Algerian chaabi music. His songs, written in vivid colloquial Algerian Arabic and often accompanied by the mandole and banjo, chronicled the lives of emigrants and the urban poor. He is best known as the composer of "Ya Rayah" ("You who are leaving"), the most famous song about the pain of exile in the Arabic-speaking world.

Life

El Harrachi was born in El Biar, Algiers, into a family originally from the village of Djellal in the Chaoui region; his father was a muezzin at the Great Mosque of Algiers. The family moved to El Harrach, the working-class suburb from which he took his stage name. He began working as a fitter and turner and later as a tramway conductor, performing music in his spare time. In 1949 he emigrated to France, working in factories in Lille, Marseille and Paris while establishing himself in the cafés of the North African immigrant community, where chaabi flourished in exile. He recorded extensively, eventually returning to Algeria, where he died in a car accident on the road west of Algiers on 31 August 1980.

"Ya Rayah" and legacy

"Ya Rayah", written in the 1970s, addresses the emigrant who leaves home in search of fortune, only to find hardship, and warns him that he will grow tired of wandering and return to his country. The song became a standard in Algeria and across the diaspora; in 1997 the French-Algerian raï singer Rachid Taha recorded a cover version that brought it to a worldwide audience, reaching the French charts and becoming an anthem of the immigrant experience. El Harrachi’s complete catalogue, of more than 500 songs, addressed themes of morality, homesickness, faith and social conduct in plain language that endeared him to ordinary listeners.
