Der Judenstaat (The Jewish State, or more precisely The State of the Jews) is a pamphlet written by Theodor Herzl and published in February 1896 in Leipzig and Vienna by M. Breitenstein's Verlags-Buchhandlung. It is considered one of the most important texts of early Zionism. Its full German title is Der Judenstaat. Versuch einer modernen Lösung der Judenfrage ("The Jewish State: Proposal of a modern solution for the Jewish question").

Contents

Herzl argued that antisemitism in Europe could not be cured by assimilation, and that the "Jewish question" was a national question requiring a political solution: the creation of a Jewish state, sanctioned by international law, in a territory to be granted — he considered both Palestine and Argentina as possibilities, favouring Palestine for its historical resonance. He proposed two organisations to carry out the project: a Society of Jews to negotiate politically, and a Jewish Company, modelled on chartered companies, to organise finance, land purchase and the orderly transfer of Jewish property and emigration.

Herzl imagined a modern, tolerant, technologically advanced state, with a seven-hour working day, a multilingual population, and separation of religion and state. He famously wrote that if the project were realised, "if you will it, it is no dream."

Impact

The pamphlet electrified Jewish communities, particularly in Eastern Europe, and led directly to Herzl convening the First Zionist Congress at Basel in 1897, which founded the World Zionist Organization and adopted the Basel Program calling for a Jewish home in Palestine secured by public law.
