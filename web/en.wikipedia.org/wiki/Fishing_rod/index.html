Fishing rod

A fishing rod is a long, thin rod used by anglers to catch fish by manipulating a line ending in a hook, generally attached to a lure or bait. The rod's length varies from about 0.6 m (2 ft) for ice fishing rods to as much as 4 m (13 ft) for surf and spey rods. At its simplest, a fishing rod is a stick or pole with a line fastened to one end; modern rods are sophisticated casting tools fitted with line guides and a reel seat for mounting a fishing reel.

History

Fishing rods date back to ancient Egypt, China and Greece. Early rods were made of wood, bamboo and later split cane. Fibreglass rods became common after the Second World War, and carbon fibre (graphite) composites, introduced in the 1970s, now dominate because of their light weight and sensitivity.

Design

A modern rod consists of a tapered blank, a handle of cork or EVA foam, a reel seat, and a series of line guides that distribute stress along the blank. Rods are classified by power (the amount of force needed to bend them) and action (where along the length the rod bends). Taper designs and materials determine casting distance, accuracy and the ability to fight fish.

Types

Spinning rods — used with fixed-spool reels mounted below the rod; guides are relatively large.

Casting rods — used with revolving-spool baitcasting reels mounted above the rod; often fitted with a trigger grip.

Fly rods — designed to cast a weighted line, rated by line weight from 0 to 16.

Telescopic, tenkara, surf, boat and ice rods — specialised forms for particular fisheries and methods.
