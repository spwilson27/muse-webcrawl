Roller screw

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A roller screw, also known as a planetary roller screw or satellite roller screw, is a low-friction precision screw-type actuator, a mechanical device for converting rotational motion to linear motion, or vice versa. Unlike ball screws, roller screws use threaded rollers arranged planetarily around a threaded shaft inside a threaded nut.

Design and performance

The rollers mesh simultaneously with the screw shaft and the nut, providing many more contact points than a ball screw of similar size. This gives roller screws very high load capacity, stiffness, and durability, together with high efficiency and the ability to achieve fine leads and rapid linear speeds. In the common planetary design, the rollers do not recirculate; in inverted and recirculating designs the arrangement differs to suit space and lead requirements.

Applications

Because of their cost, roller screws are used where their performance justifies it: electromechanical actuators in aerospace flight controls, industrial presses, injection moulding machines, medical equipment, robotics, and military systems such as missile fin actuators. They were patented in the mid-20th century and commercialised notably by the Swedish company SKF and the Swiss firm Rollvis.
