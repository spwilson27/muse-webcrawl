James Connolly

James Connolly (Irish: Séamas Ó Conghaile; 5 June 1868 – 12 May 1916) was an Irish republican, socialist and trade union leader. Born in Edinburgh to Irish parents, he became one of the leaders of the Easter Rising of 1916 and a signatory of the Proclamation of the Irish Republic. He was executed by the British Army after the Rising.

James Connolly
Born: 5 June 1868, Cowgate, Edinburgh, Scotland
Died: 12 May 1916, Kilmainham Gaol, Dublin
Occupation: Trade unionist, socialist theorist
Known for: Easter Rising, Irish Citizen Army

Early life

Connolly left school at eleven, worked as a printer's devil and enlisted in the British Army underage, serving in Ireland. In Edinburgh he joined the socialist movement, influenced by John Leslie and the Social Democratic Federation, and became secretary of the Scottish Socialist Federation.

Ireland and America

Moving to Dublin in 1896, he founded the Irish Socialist Republican Party and the newspaper The Workers' Republic. From 1903 to 1910 he lived in the United States, organising for the Socialist Labor Party and the Industrial Workers of the World, and writing Labour in Irish History (1910), a Marxist reinterpretation of Irish history.

Trade unionism and the Citizen Army

Returning to Ireland, he became organiser for the Irish Transport and General Workers' Union under James Larkin. During the Dublin Lock-out of 1913 he co-founded the Irish Citizen Army to protect strikers. As acting general secretary of the ITGWU and commandant of the Citizen Army, he pushed the Irish Volunteers toward insurrection during the First World War, arguing England's difficulty was Ireland's opportunity.

Easter Rising and execution

Connolly was military commander of the Dublin forces in Easter Week, occupying the General Post Office with Patrick Pearse. Badly wounded, he was carried on a stretcher and shot tied to a chair at Kilmainham Gaol. His writings remain foundational for Irish republican socialism.
