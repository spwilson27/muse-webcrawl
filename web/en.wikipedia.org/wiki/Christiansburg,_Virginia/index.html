Christiansburg is a town in Montgomery County, Virginia, United States, and the county seat. The population was 23,348 at the 2020 census. It is part of the Blacksburg–Christiansburg metropolitan area and lies along the Eastern Continental Divide in the New River Valley of southwestern Virginia.

History

The town was established in 1792 and named for Colonel William Christian, an early settler, soldier, and member of the Virginia legislature — and brother-in-law of Patrick Henry. Christiansburg developed along the Wilderness Road, a principal route for settlers moving westward through the Cumberland Gap, and served as a stopping point for travellers with inns and taverns. During the American Civil War, the town saw nearby fighting in connection with raids on the Virginia and Tennessee Railroad.

In the twentieth century, Christiansburg grew with the expansion of the regional retail and services economy along U.S. Route 460 and Interstate 81, and with the growth of nearby Virginia Tech in Blacksburg.

Geography

Christiansburg is located at an elevation of roughly 2,100 feet (640 m) in the Ridge-and-Valley Appalachians. Crab Creek flows through the town. The climate is humid subtropical at lower elevations transitioning to a cooler highland character, with four distinct seasons.

Economy and education

The town is home to the New River Valley Mall and a regional retail corridor. Montgomery County Public Schools serve the town, including Christiansburg High School. The Christiansburg Institute, founded in 1866 to educate formerly enslaved people, operated in the town until 1966 and is remembered as an important African-American educational institution of the Reconstruction era.
