The Gold Democrats, formally the National Democratic Party, were a conservative splinter faction of the United States Democratic Party that broke away in 1896 over the party's adoption of free silver at the 1896 Democratic National Convention, where William Jennings Bryan won the nomination with his "Cross of Gold" speech.

The 1896 campaign

Led by supporters of outgoing President Grover Cleveland — the "Bourbon Democrats" — the Gold Democrats held their own convention in Indianapolis in September 1896 and nominated former Union general and Illinois governor John M. Palmer for president and former Confederate general Simon Bolivar Buckner of Kentucky for vice president, a ticket symbolising sectional reconciliation. Their platform defended the gold standard, a tariff for revenue only, and limited government. The ticket won about 1% of the popular vote (some 134,000 votes) but may have drawn enough support from Republican William McKinley in Kentucky to carry that state.

Aftermath

After McKinley's victory, prosperity and the Gold Standard Act of 1900 defused the silver issue. In 1900 most Gold Democrats quietly returned to the regular party or supported McKinley; the faction dissolved. Its members are often seen as the last organised expression of 19th-century classical-liberal Bourbon Democracy within the party.
