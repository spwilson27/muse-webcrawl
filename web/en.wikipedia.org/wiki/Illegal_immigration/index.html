Illegal immigration

Illegal immigration is the migration of people into a country in violation of that country's immigration laws, or the continued residence of people without legal authorisation to reside. Illegal immigration tends to be financially upward, from poorer to richer countries. People who migrate illegally are variously described as illegal immigrants, undocumented immigrants, or irregular migrants.

Causes

Motivations for illegal immigration include seeking better economic opportunities and living standards, escaping poverty, persecution, conflict or environmental disaster, and reuniting with family members. Where legal migration channels are limited or slow, people may resort to irregular routes, sometimes using smugglers.

Effects and debates

Illegal immigration is a politically contentious issue in many countries. Debates concern its effects on labour markets, public services, crime, and social cohesion, as well as humanitarian obligations toward migrants and asylum seekers. Estimates of illegal immigrant populations are inherently uncertain; the United States, for example, has been estimated to have around 11 million unauthorised residents in recent decades.

Enforcement

Governments respond with border enforcement, employer sanctions, detention, and deportation (removal), as well as periodic regularisation or amnesty programmes. International law recognises the right to seek asylum, and the principle of non-refoulement prohibits returning people to countries where they face persecution.
