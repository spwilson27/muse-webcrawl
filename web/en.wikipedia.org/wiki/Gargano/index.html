Gargano

The Gargano (Italian: Promontorio del Gargano) is a historical and geographical region in the province of Foggia, in the Apulia (Puglia) region of south-eastern Italy. It consists of a wide promontory of limestone massif jutting into the Adriatic Sea, often called the "spur" on the "boot" of Italy. Its highest point is Monte Calvo at 1,065 metres.

Geography

The Gargano is largely covered by the Gargano National Park (Parco Nazionale del Gargano), established in 1991, which protects the ancient Foresta Umbra beech forest, coastal wetlands and the Tremiti Islands offshore. The coastline alternates between sandy beaches and dramatic white limestone cliffs, sea caves and rock arches, particularly around Vieste, Peschici and Mattinata. Inland lie the plateau towns of Monte Sant'Angelo, San Giovanni Rotondo and San Marco in Lamis.

History and pilgrimage

The shrine of Monte Sant'Angelo, a cave sanctuary of Saint Michael the Archangel, has been a major pilgrimage site since the early Middle Ages; tradition dates apparitions of the Archangel to 490–493 AD. The sanctuary is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the Longobards in Italy. In the 20th century, San Giovanni Rotondo became one of the most visited Catholic pilgrimage destinations in the world as the home of Saint Pio of Pietrelcina (Padre Pio).

Tourism

The Gargano is a popular summer holiday area, known for its resort towns of Vieste, Peschici, Rodi Garganico and Mattinata, its trabucchi (traditional wooden fishing platforms), olive groves and citrus gardens around Rodi, famed for its oranges and lemons.
