Molar attenuation coefficient

Symbolε (epsilon)
SI / customary unitL·mol−1·cm−1 (M−1 cm−1)
Governing lawBeer–Lambert law: A = εlc
Also calledMolar absorptivity; molar extinction coefficient (older usage)

The molar attenuation coefficient, also known as the molar absorptivity (and in older literature the molar extinction coefficient), is a physical constant that measures how strongly a chemical species absorbs, and thereby attenuates, light at a given wavelength. It is an intrinsic property of the substance under specified conditions (solvent, temperature and pH) and is denoted by the Greek letter ε (epsilon).

Beer–Lambert law

The coefficient appears in the Beer–Lambert law relating the absorbance A of a solution to concentration c and path length l:

A = ε l c

With path length in centimetres and concentration in mol/L, ε has units of L·mol−1·cm−1. The term "attenuation" replaced "extinction" and "absorption" in the preferred IUPAC terminology because measured attenuation can include light scattering as well as true absorption; in most spectrophotometric practice the two are treated as equivalent.

Typical values and use

Values of ε range from near zero for substances transparent at a wavelength to more than 105 L·mol−1·cm−1 for strongly absorbing dyes and conjugated chromophores; intensely allowed electronic transitions give large values, while "forbidden" transitions (such as the d–d bands of many transition-metal complexes) give small ones, often below 10. Standard examples include NADH at 340 nm (ε ≈ 6,220 L·mol−1·cm−1), widely used in enzyme assays, and the nucleic-acid value at 260 nm used to estimate DNA and RNA concentrations (commonly via the average coefficient of about 0.020 μg−1·mL·cm−1 for double-stranded DNA). In protein chemistry, ε at 280 nm can be computed from a sequence's content of tryptophan, tyrosine and cystine residues (the Gill–von Hippel / Edelhoch method, as implemented in tools such as ProtParam), letting researchers measure concentration from a simple absorbance reading.

Mass attenuation coefficient

For mixtures or substances of unknown molar mass, the related mass attenuation coefficient is used: absorbance divided by path length and mass concentration, with units such as L·g−1·cm−1. In the physics of ionising radiation the same name denotes a different quantity — the fraction of a photon beam attenuated per unit areal density — defined for X-rays and gamma rays.
