The 7.5×54mm French (7.5 French, or 7.5×54mm MAS) is a rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridge developed by France in the 1920s as its standard military rifle round. It evolved from the short-lived 7.5×58mm cartridge of 1924, whose similarity to the German 7.92×57mm Mauser caused dangerous confusion, prompting a shortening of the case to 54 mm in 1929.

History and use

The cartridge chambered the MAS-36 bolt-action rifle, the MAS-49 and MAS-49/56 semi-automatic rifles, the FM 24/29 light machine gun, and the MAC 1950-era automatic weapons. It served as France's principal rifle cartridge through the Second World War, the First Indochina War, and the Algerian War, before being superseded by the 5.56×45mm and 7.62×51mm NATO rounds.

Performance

Ballistics are broadly comparable to the 7.62×51mm NATO, with a spitzer bullet of about 9 grams fired at roughly 820 m/s from a full-length rifle barrel.
