Quarterback

PositionsOffense
SportAmerican football, Canadian football
AbbreviationQB

The quarterback (QB) is a position in gridiron football. Quarterbacks are members of the offensive team and line up directly behind the offensive line, receiving the snap from the center at the start of each play. The quarterback is usually the on-field leader of the offense: in modern play, they relay the plays called by the coaching staff, may change the play at the line of scrimmage (an "audible"), and begin most plays by throwing a forward pass, handing the ball to a running back, or running with it themselves.

Role

The quarterback is the most visible and often the most important player on the field. A successful quarterback combines arm strength and accuracy, field vision, decision-making under pressure, mobility, and leadership. Passing statistics — completions, yards, touchdowns, and interceptions — are combined in measures such as passer rating.

History

The position evolved from the rugby-style halfback role; the name reflects the player's position a quarter of the way back from the line in early formations. The legalization of the forward pass in 1906 transformed the quarterback into the offense's primary ball distributor. In the modern NFL, quarterbacks dominate the Most Valuable Player award and command the highest salaries.

In Canadian football

Quarterbacks play a similar role in the CFL, with a wider field and three downs placing an even greater premium on passing.
