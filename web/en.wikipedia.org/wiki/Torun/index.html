Toruń (spelled Torun without the diacritic in plain ASCII text; German: Thorn) is a city in north-central Poland, on the Vistula River, in the Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship. It is one of the oldest cities in Poland and is celebrated for its remarkably preserved Gothic Old Town, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997, and as the birthplace of the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus (1473).

History

Toruń originated as a settlement of the Teutonic Knights, who built a castle there in the 1230s at a crossing of the Vistula. The Old Town received city rights in 1233 and the New Town in 1264; the city joined the Hanseatic League and prospered from river trade. After the Thirteen Years' War, the Second Peace of Thorn (1466) transferred the city to the Kingdom of Poland, under which it flourished as a wealthy, largely autonomous trading centre. Toruń later passed to Prussia in the partitions of Poland, returned to Poland after the First World War, survived the Second World War with its historic centre largely intact, and is today a university and cultural city.

Landmarks

  • The medieval Old Town, with its grid of Gothic streets largely unchanged since the Middle Ages.
  • Toruń Cathedral (Cathedral of SS. John the Baptist and John the Evangelist), one of the largest brick churches in Poland.
  • The Old Town Hall (Ratusz), a major example of Gothic civic architecture, now the District Museum.
  • The Copernicus House, traditionally identified as the astronomer's birthplace, and his monument in the Old Town Market Square.
  • The Leaning Tower of Toruń, a medieval defensive tower tilting from the vertical.
  • Ruins of the Teutonic castle, destroyed by the townspeople in 1454.

Culture

Toruń is famous for its gingerbread (pierniki), baked in the city since the Middle Ages. Nicolaus Copernicus University, founded in 1945, is one of Poland's largest universities. The city hosts numerous festivals and is a regional centre of the voivodeship, sharing regional-capital functions with nearby Bydgoszcz.
