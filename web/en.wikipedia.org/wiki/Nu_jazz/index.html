Nu jazz

Nu jazz (also written nu-jazz or Nujazz) is a genre of music that blends jazz elements with other musical styles, such as funk, soul, electronic music and free improvisation. The term was coined in the late 1990s to refer to music that mixes traditional jazz instrumentation with electronic textures. Bands in the genre typically perform with a DJ or incorporate electronic instruments such as samples, synthesizers and sequencers alongside live performers.

Characteristics

Nu jazz often features improvisation, but unlike earlier forms of jazz it frequently uses a loop-based or groove-oriented structure drawn from club and dance music. Musicians may play trumpet, saxophone, keyboards, bass and drums over programmed beats, or producers may sample classic jazz recordings and rework them into downtempo tracks. The genre overlaps with acid jazz, jazztronica, electro-jazz and broken beat.

History

The roots of nu jazz lie in the 1970s experiments of artists such as Miles Davis and Herbie Hancock, who fused jazz with rock, funk and electronic instruments. In the 1990s, producers and groups such as St Germain, Jazzanova, Kruder & Dorfmeister, Bugge Wesseltoft and the Norwegian jazz scene associated with Nils Petter Molvær helped define the sound. Labels such as Compost Records and the British broken-beat scene around IG Culture also contributed. By the 2000s, acts like The Cinematic Orchestra, Bonobo and Nujabes had brought related styles to a wide audience, while the rise of streaming-era jazz artists kept the electronic-jazz crossover alive.
