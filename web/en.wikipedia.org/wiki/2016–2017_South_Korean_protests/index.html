2016–2017 South Korean protests

The 2016–2017 South Korean protests, also known as the Candlelight Demonstrations, were a series of mass protests held across South Korea from October 2016 to March 2017 demanding the resignation and impeachment of President Park Geun-hye. The protests followed revelations of a corruption scandal involving Park's confidante Choi Soon-sil, and drew cumulative crowds estimated in the tens of millions.

Candlelight Demonstrations

Date: 26 October 2016 – 2017
Location: South Korea (mainly Seoul)
Cause: Park Geun-hye–Choi Soon-sil scandal
Outcome: Impeachment and removal of Park Geun-hye

Background

In October 2016, media reports revealed that Choi Soon-sil, a close friend of Park with no official position, had received classified government documents and used her influence to extort money from major conglomerates and interfere in state affairs. The revelations triggered nationwide outrage; weekly Saturday rallies at Gwanghwamun Square in Seoul grew rapidly, with the 3 December demonstration drawing an estimated 2.3 million people nationwide.

Impeachment

On 9 December 2016 the National Assembly voted to impeach Park by 234 votes to 56. Prime Minister Hwang Kyo-ahn became acting president. The Constitutional Court upheld the impeachment on 10 March 2017, permanently removing Park from office — the first democratically elected South Korean president to be removed in this way. Park was arrested later that month and in 2018 was sentenced to 24 years in prison for corruption and abuse of power.

Aftermath

A presidential election was held on 9 May 2017 and won by Moon Jae-in of the Democratic Party. The candlelight movement is widely credited with demonstrating the maturity of South Korean democracy; it remained overwhelmingly peaceful throughout.
