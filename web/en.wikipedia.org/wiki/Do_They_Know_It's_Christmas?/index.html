Do They Know It's Christmas?

"Do They Know It's Christmas?" is a charity song written in 1984 by Bob Geldof and Midge Ure in response to the 1983–85 famine in Ethiopia. It was recorded on 25 November 1984 by Band Aid, a supergroup of leading British and Irish pop musicians assembled by Geldof and Ure, and released on 7 December 1984. The single became the UK Christmas number one, outsold all other records in the chart combined, and raised millions of pounds for famine relief.

Background and recording

Geldof was moved to act after seeing Michael Buerk's BBC news report on the Ethiopian famine in October 1984. He and Ure wrote the song quickly and recruited performers including Paul Young, Boy George, Sting, Simon Le Bon, George Michael, Jody Watley, Bono, and members of Status Quo, Spandau Ballet, and Duran Duran. Phil Collins played drums. The recording was completed in a single day.

Impact and legacy

The song sold over a million copies in its first week in the UK and more than 11 million copies worldwide by 1989. It inspired the American "We Are the World" and the Live Aid concerts of July 1985. New versions were recorded as Band Aid II (1989), Band Aid 20 (2004), and Band Aid 30 (2014, for the Ebola crisis), each reaching number one in the UK. The song has also drawn criticism for its portrayal of Africa and lyrical inaccuracies, a debate renewed in later decades.
