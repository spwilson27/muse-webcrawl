The Governor of South Dakota is the head of government of the U.S. state of South Dakota. The governor is the chief executive of the state government, commander-in-chief of the South Dakota National Guard when not called into federal service, and is responsible for seeing that the laws of the state are faithfully executed. The governor's office is located in the State Capitol in Pierre.

Election and term

The governor is elected by popular vote for a four-year term and, under the state constitution, may serve no more than two consecutive terms. Elections are held in even-numbered years that are not presidential-election years (midterm years), concurrent with elections to the South Dakota Legislature. To be eligible, a candidate must be at least 21 years old, a United States citizen, and a resident of South Dakota for at least two years before the election.

In the event of a vacancy, the lieutenant governor succeeds to the office. This happened in 1978, when Governor Richard F. Kneip resigned to become ambassador to Singapore and was succeeded by Lieutenant Governor Harvey Wollman, and in 1993 after Governor George S. Mickelson died in a plane crash and was succeeded by Walter Dale Miller.

Powers

The governor signs or vetoes bills passed by the legislature, may call the legislature into special session, delivers the annual State of the State address, prepares and submits the executive budget, and grants pardons and commutations. The governor also appoints the heads of state agencies, many members of boards and commissions, and judges to fill vacancies in the state's courts, generally subject to confirmation requirements set by law.

History

Before statehood, the Dakota Territory was administered by federally appointed territorial governors, beginning with William Jayne in 1861. South Dakota entered the Union on 2 November 1889, and Arthur C. Mellette — the last territorial governor — became the first state governor. South Dakota governors have included Charles N. Herreid, Peter Norbeck (who championed state-owned enterprises such as the cement plant and hail insurance fund), William J. Bulow (later a U.S. senator), and novelist-era figures of the populist and progressive movements. One of the state's best-known governors, George S. Mickelson, died in an airplane crash near Dubuque, Iowa, in April 1993, an event that remains a defining moment in the state's modern political memory.

Recent governors

  • Walter Dale Miller (1993–1995) — succeeded Mickelson.
  • Bill Janklow (1995–2003) — previously served 1979–1987.
  • Mike Rounds (2003–2011).
  • Dennis Daugaard (2011–2019).
  • Kristi Noem (2019–2025) — the state's first woman governor; left office on confirmation as U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security.
  • Larry Rhoden (2025– ) — lieutenant governor who succeeded Noem.
