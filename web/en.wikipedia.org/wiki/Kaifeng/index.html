Kaifeng

Kaifeng (開封) is a prefecture-level city in east-central Henan province, China. It is one of the Eight Ancient Capitals of China, having served as the capital of the Northern Song dynasty (960–1127), when it was among the largest cities in the world with a population of over a million.

History

Founded as Daliang in the Warring States period, Kaifeng was capital successively to several dynasties including the Later Liang, Later Jin, Later Han and Later Zhou of the Five Dynasties period. Under the Northern Song it became a brilliant centre of commerce and culture, immortalised in the handscroll Along the River During the Qingming Festival. The Jurchen Jin captured the city in 1127 and later made it their southern capital. The city was repeatedly devastated by Yellow River floods, most catastrophically in 1642, when the river was deliberately unleashed during the Ming–Qing transition. Kaifeng is also famed for its historic Jewish community, whose synagogue stood for centuries.

Landmarks

Notable monuments include the Iron Pagoda (built 1049, glazed-brick), the Dragon Pavilion, the reconstructed Qingming Riverside Landscape Garden, and the ancient city wall. Kaifeng is also known for its chrysanthemum festival and its night markets.
