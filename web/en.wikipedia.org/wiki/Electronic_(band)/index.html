Electronic were an English alternative dance supergroup formed by singer/guitarist/keyboardist Bernard Sumner (of New Order and Joy Division) and guitarist Johnny Marr (of the Smiths). They co-wrote the majority of their output between 1989 and 1998, collaborating with Neil Tennant and Chris Lowe of Pet Shop Boys on three tracks, and with former Kraftwerk member Karl Bartos on one album.

History

Sumner and Marr began working together after the Smiths split, combining Marr's guitar playing with Sumner's synthesisers and the dance rhythms of the Madchester era. Their debut single, "Getting Away with It" (1989), featured Pet Shop Boys and reached the UK top 20 and became a US alternative hit. The self-titled debut album Electronic (1991) sold over a million copies and included the singles "Get the Message" and "Feel Every Beat".

The second album, Raise the Pressure (1996), was made with Karl Bartos and featured a more guitar-led sound; it produced the singles "Forbidden City" and "For You". A third album, Twisted Tenderness, followed in 1999, after which the group wound down as Sumner returned to New Order and Marr pursued other projects.

Legacy

Electronic are remembered as one of the defining collaborations of the British indie-dance crossover, uniting two of the most influential guitarists and songwriters of the post-punk generation. "Getting Away with It" remains their best-known song.
