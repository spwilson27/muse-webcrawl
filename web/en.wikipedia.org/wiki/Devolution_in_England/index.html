Devolution in England

Devolution in England is the transfer of powers from the Government of the United Kingdom to regional or local levels within England. Unlike Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, England has no devolved parliament or assembly of its own and is governed directly by the UK Parliament, giving rise to the "West Lothian question".

London

The Greater London Authority, comprising the Mayor of London and the London Assembly, was established in 2000 following a referendum, with powers over transport, policing oversight, fire, planning and economic development. Ken Livingstone became the first mayor.

Regional assemblies and combined authorities

Plans for elected regional assemblies in other regions collapsed after voters in the North East rejected the proposal by 78% in a 2004 referendum. Instead, from 2014 the government pursued "devolution deals" creating combined authorities led by directly elected "metro mayors" — including Greater Manchester (2017), the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, Tees Valley, South Yorkshire and the West of England — with powers over transport, skills, housing and health integration.

Debate

Proposals for an English parliament, regional assemblies, "English votes for English laws" (used 2015–2021), and further city-region devolution continue to be debated, alongside wider questions of the UK's constitutional settlement.
