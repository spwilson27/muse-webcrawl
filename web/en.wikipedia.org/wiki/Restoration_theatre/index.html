Restoration theatre

Restoration theatre is the English theatre of the Restoration period, roughly from 1660 to 1710. After the closure of the theatres during the English Civil War and the Interregnum (1642–1660), the reopening under Charles II led to a flowering of theatrical culture distinct from the earlier Elizabethan and Jacobean drama.

Reopening of the theatres

On his restoration in 1660, Charles II granted patents to two companies — Thomas Killigrew's King's Company and Sir William Davenant's Duke's Company — giving them a duopoly on spoken drama in London. The patent theatres featured moveable painted scenery, elaborate stage machinery, and music.

Characteristics

Restoration drama is best known for the Restoration comedy, a witty, sexually explicit genre satirizing the manners of the aristocracy, written by dramatists such as William Wycherley (The Country Wife), William Congreve (The Way of the World), George Etherege, and Aphra Behn. Heroic drama and adaptations of Shakespeare were also popular.

Actresses

The Restoration stage introduced professional actresses to England for the first time; previously, female roles had been played by boys or men. Actresses such as Nell Gwyn became celebrities, and the novelty of women on stage was itself a major attraction, reflected in the era's famous "breeches roles".
