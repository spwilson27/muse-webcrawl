Samadhi

Samadhi (Sanskrit: समाधि, samādhi) is a state of meditative consciousness in Indian religions including Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. In Hindu yoga traditions it is the eighth and final limb of Patanjali's ashtanga yoga, a state of intense concentration in which the meditator's awareness merges with the object of meditation. In Buddhism, samadhi (right concentration, sammā-samādhi) is the last of the Noble Eightfold Path's eight factors and is developed through the practice of the jhanas (meditative absorptions).

Etymology

The word derives from the Sanskrit roots sam- ("together") and ā-dhā ("to place, put"), giving the sense of "joining, putting together" — a state in which the mind is collected and unified. Related usages include the term for a Hindu saint's tomb or memorial shrine, so named because the saint is understood to have entered final samadhi at death (mahasamadhi).

In Hinduism and yoga

In the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, samadhi follows dharana (concentration) and dhyana (meditation): when meditation deepens so that only the object shines forth and awareness of self is lost, samadhi occurs. Classical commentators distinguish samprajnata ("with cognition") samadhi, in stages from gross to subtle objects, from asamprajnata ("without cognition") samadhi, and later traditions speak of savikalpa and nirvikalpa samadhi — the latter a state without mental modification, associated in Advaita Vedanta with recognition of the identity of atman and Brahman. In bhakti traditions samadhi can denote absorption in God.

In Buddhism

Buddhist texts develop samadhi through serenity meditation (samatha), culminating in the four form jhanas and four formless attainments, characterised by collectedness, joy, equanimity and one-pointedness. Combined with insight (vipassana), samadhi supports the realisation of nirvana. Theravada commentaries distinguish momentary, access and absorption concentration. In Mahayana Buddhism, samadhi also names particular meditative states and texts (samadhi sutras), and "samadhi" can refer to the meditative power of a Buddha or bodhisattva.
