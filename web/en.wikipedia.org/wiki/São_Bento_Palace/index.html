São Bento Palace (Portuguese: Palácio de São Bento, "Saint Benedict's Palace") is a neoclassical palace in Lisbon, Portugal, which has been the seat of the Portuguese parliament — the Assembly of the Republic — since 1834.

History

The palace was originally built in 1598 as a Benedictine monastery, the Monastery of São Bento da Saúde. After the extinction of the religious orders in Portugal in 1834, following the Liberal Wars, the building was taken over by the state and adapted to house the parliament, then known as the Palácio das Cortes.

The building suffered fires during the 19th and 20th centuries and was repeatedly remodelled. The present neoclassical façade, with its grand staircase and pediment supported by columns, was designed in the late 19th century; architect Ventura Terra won the competition for the remodelling, and the new plenary hall (Sala das Sessões) was inaugurated in 1903. The monumental exterior staircase, flanked by lion sculptures — known as the "lionesses" — was completed in the 1940s.

The palace today

São Bento Palace houses the Assembly of the Republic, Portugal's unicameral parliament of 230 deputies. Behind the palace stands the São Bento Mansion (Palacete de São Bento), built in 1877, which serves as the official residence of the Prime Minister of Portugal. The palace contains important works of Portuguese art, a parliamentary museum and archive, and the historic Sessions Chamber where the constituent assembly drafted the 1976 constitution after the Carnation Revolution.
