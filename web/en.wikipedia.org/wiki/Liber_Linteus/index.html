Liber Linteus

The Liber Linteus Zagrabiensis (Latin for "Linen Book of Zagreb", also rarely known as Liber Agramensis) is the longest Etruscan text and the only surviving linen book. Dating to the third century BC, it contains a ritual calendar prescribing offerings to various deities on particular dates, and is written in black ink on linen.

Discovery

The linen had been cut into strips and used as burial wrappings for an Egyptian mummy. The mummy was bought in Alexandria in 1848 by the Croatian official Mihajlo Barić and was later given to what is now the Archaeological Museum in Zagreb, Croatia. The significance of the text was not recognised until 1891, when the Egyptologist Jacob Krall identified the writing as Etruscan.

Content

About 1,330 words survive, written in twelve columns. Because knowledge of Etruscan vocabulary remains limited, only part of the text is understood, but its formulaic calendar structure — names of gods, dates, and sacrificial instructions — has been broadly deciphered. The fabric itself was probably produced in southern Etruria before being reused in Ptolemaic Egypt.
