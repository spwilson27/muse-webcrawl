Reservoir-induced seismicity

TypeInduced seismicity
CauseStress changes from large water reservoirs
Notable caseKoyna Dam, India (1967)

A large reservoir behind a dam

Reservoir-induced seismicity (RIS) refers to earthquakes triggered by the filling of large artificial reservoirs behind dams. The weight of the impounded water and the diffusion of water pressure into underlying rock can alter stresses on faults, sometimes bringing them to failure.

Mechanism

Two effects contribute: the direct elastic loading of the crust by the mass of water, and the increase in pore pressure in fractured rock as water penetrates, which reduces the effective normal stress clamping faults shut. Seismicity often begins soon after impoundment and may continue for decades.

Examples

The best-known case is the 1967 magnitude 6.3 earthquake near the Koyna Dam in India, which killed around 180 people. Other documented sites include Lake Kariba on the Zambezi, the Aswan High Dam in Egypt, and the Zipingpu Dam in China, whose reservoir has been debated in connection with the 2008 Sichuan earthquake. Most reservoirs, however, produce at most small tremors.
