Awareness ribbon

FormShort length of ribbon folded into a loop, or a representation thereof
PurposeSymbol of support or awareness for a cause

Awareness ribbons are symbols consisting of a short length of ribbon folded into a loop, or representations of such a loop, worn or displayed to signify support for, or awareness of, a social cause or health condition. Different colours — and sometimes patterns — denote different causes; because colours are reused, the meaning of a ribbon usually depends on context.

History

The practice grew out of older traditions of wearing ribbons and bows as tokens, including the yellow ribbons associated with waiting loved ones popularized in the United States in the 1970s and during the Iran hostage crisis. The modern awareness ribbon emerged in the early 1990s: the red ribbon, created by the Visual AIDS Artists Caucus in 1991, became the international symbol of AIDS awareness, and the pink ribbon was popularized in 1991–1992 by the Susan G. Komen Foundation and Self magazine for breast cancer awareness. The format quickly spread to hundreds of causes.

Use and criticism

Ribbons are distributed at charity events, worn on lapels, and reproduced on products, vehicles, and websites; digital versions appear as overlays and emoji-like icons. Critics have described some ribbon campaigns as "slacktivism" — conspicuous displays that substitute for substantive action — and have questioned the commercialization of certain causes, notably the "pinkwashing" debate around breast cancer products. Supporters argue ribbons open conversations and normalize discussion of stigmatized conditions.
