Santa Ana River

The Santa Ana River is the largest river entirely within Southern California in the United States. It rises in the San Bernardino Mountains and flows for about 96 miles (154 km) through San Bernardino, Riverside, and Orange counties before emptying into the Pacific Ocean near Huntington Beach and Newport Beach.

Course

The river begins in the San Bernardino National Forest from tributaries such as Bear Creek and the South Fork. It passes through the Inland Empire cities of San Bernardino and Riverside, then through the Santa Ana Canyon between the Santa Ana Mountains and the Chino Hills, and across the Orange County coastal plain.

Watershed and management

The watershed covers roughly 2,650 square miles (6,900 km²). Major dams include Seven Oaks Dam and Prado Dam, built for flood control after the devastating 1938 flood of the Santa Ana River, one of the worst natural disasters in Southern California history. Much of the lower river is channelized, though restoration and recreational projects such as the Santa Ana River Trail run along its banks.

Ecology

Despite urbanization, the river supports riparian habitat important for birds and for the endangered Santa Ana sucker fish. Groundwater recharge operations along the river supply a large share of Orange County's drinking water.
