Patroclus

Patroclus (Greek: Πάτροκλος, Pátroklos, "glory of his father") is a hero of Greek mythology, best known as the closest companion of Achilles in Homer's Iliad and a central figure of the Trojan War.

In the Iliad

Patroclus, son of Menoetius, came to Phthia as a boy after accidentally killing a playmate, and was raised alongside Achilles. At Troy, when Achilles withdrew from battle in anger at Agamemnon, the Greeks suffered heavy losses. Patroclus, moved by the wounded, persuaded Achilles to let him lead the Myrmidons wearing Achilles' armour. He drove back the Trojans and killed the Lycian hero Sarpedon, but, defying Achilles' instruction to return, pressed on to the walls of Troy, where Apollo struck him and Hector killed him.

Aftermath

Achilles, devastated by grief, returned to battle to avenge Patroclus, killing Hector. The funeral games of Patroclus occupy Book 23 of the Iliad. The intense bond between Achilles and Patroclus has been interpreted variously as deep friendship, kinship loyalty, or romantic love, from classical antiquity through modern scholarship.

Later tradition

Patroclus appears in later Greek tragedy, in Shakespeare's Troilus and Cressida, and in modern retellings such as Madeline Miller's novel The Song of Achilles (2011).
