American Viticultural Area

An American Viticultural Area (AVA) is a designated wine grape-growing region in the United States, providing an official appellation for the mutual benefit of wineries and consumers. Winemakers frequently want their consumers to know about the geographic pedigree of their wines, as wines from a particular area can possess distinctive characteristics. Consumers often seek wines from specific AVAs, and certain wines of particular pedigrees can claim premium prices and loyal followings. Wine labels are required to identify the source of the grapes used to make the wine.

Regulation

The system is administered by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB) of the United States Department of the Treasury. The legal framework was established in 1978, and the first AVA — Augusta, Missouri — was approved in 1980, slightly ahead of the better-known Napa Valley AVA in California, which followed in 1981.

For a wine to carry an AVA name on its label, at least 85% of the grapes used to make the wine must have been grown within the boundaries of that AVA. By contrast, a wine labelled with only a state or county appellation requires 75% of its grapes from that jurisdiction. AVA boundaries are defined by distinctive geographic, climatic and soil features rather than by political boundaries, and AVAs may overlap or be nested inside one another — for example, the Oakville AVA lies entirely within the larger Napa Valley AVA.

Establishing an AVA

Anyone may petition the TTB to establish a new AVA. The petition must demonstrate that the proposed name is locally or nationally known as referring to the area, that the boundaries are legitimate, and that the growing conditions — such as climate, soil, elevation and physical features — distinguish the area from surrounding regions. The approval process, involving public notice and comment, often takes several years.

Distribution

California has the most AVAs of any state, with well over 100, including Napa Valley, Sonoma County's many sub-appellations, Paso Robles and Santa Barbara County regions. Other significant AVA states include Oregon (Willamette Valley), Washington (Columbia Valley, Yakima Valley, Walla Walla Valley), New York (Finger Lakes, Long Island), Virginia, Texas (Texas Hill Country, Texas High Plains) and Missouri. The largest AVA by area is the Upper Mississippi River Valley AVA, covering some 29,900 square miles across four states.
