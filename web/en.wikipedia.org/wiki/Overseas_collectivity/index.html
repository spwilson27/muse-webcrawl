Overseas collectivity

An overseas collectivity (French: collectivité d'outre-mer, COM) is a first-order administrative division of France with a status distinct from that of the overseas departments and regions. The category was created by the constitutional reform of 28 March 2003.

List

The overseas collectivities are French Polynesia, Saint Pierre and Miquelon, Wallis and Futuna, Saint Martin, and Saint Barthélemy. Each has a degree of autonomy defined by an organic law; French Polynesia is designated an "overseas country" within the Republic and enjoys the broadest autonomy of the group.

Status

Unlike overseas departments, the collectivities are not fully integrated into the French legal system: statutes passed in Paris do not automatically apply, and several have their own tax and customs regimes. All are represented in the French Parliament, and their inhabitants are French and European Union citizens, though most COMs have the status of overseas countries and territories (OCTs) rather than being part of the EU itself.
