AZERTY

AZERTY is a specific layout for the characters of the Latin alphabet on typewriter keys and computer keyboards. It takes its name from the first six letters of the top alphabetic row. The layout is used principally in France and Belgium, and in other francophone regions, having been adapted from the English QWERTY layout to accommodate characters needed for the French language, such as accented letters (é, è, à, ù) and the cedilla (ç).

Differences from QWERTY

Compared with QWERTY, the AZERTY layout swaps the positions of A and Q, swaps Z and W, moves M to the right of L, and places digits on the shifted positions of the number row (so unshifted keys produce punctuation and accented characters instead). Numbers therefore require the Shift key, a frequently criticised ergonomic feature of the French layout.

Variants

The Belgian AZERTY layout differs slightly from the French one (for example in the placement of some punctuation and the = and § keys). In 2019 the French standards body AFNOR published a voluntary standard (NF Z71-300) defining an improved AZERTY layout that makes all French characters and symbols easier to type, alongside the BÉPO layout, an alternative designed from scratch for French.

Usage elsewhere

AZERTY-derived layouts are also used in parts of francophone Africa and in Luxembourg in combination with other layouts. Most operating systems allow AZERTY to be selected as an input method regardless of the physical legends printed on the keys.
