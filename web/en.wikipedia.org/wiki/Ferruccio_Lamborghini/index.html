Ferruccio Lamborghini

Born: 28 April 1916, Renazzo, Italy

Died: 20 February 1993, Perugia, Italy

Known for: Founder of Lamborghini

Ferruccio Lamborghini (28 April 1916 – 20 February 1993) was an Italian industrialist who founded the luxury sports car manufacturer Automobili Lamborghini in 1963. Before that, he had built a successful business manufacturing tractors from surplus military equipment in post-war Italy.

Career

Lamborghini founded Lamborghini Trattori in 1948, which became one of Italy's largest agricultural equipment manufacturers. Wealthy and a keen collector of sports cars, he established his automobile company in Sant'Agata Bolognese — by popular account after a dispute with Enzo Ferrari over a clutch problem in a Ferrari he owned. The company's first cars, the 350 GT and the mid-engined Miura, quickly established its reputation.

Later life

Ferruccio sold controlling interests in his companies during the economic turmoil of the 1970s and retired to his estate near Lake Trasimeno, where he produced wine. He died in 1993. His company survives today as a subsidiary of Audi within the Volkswagen Group.
