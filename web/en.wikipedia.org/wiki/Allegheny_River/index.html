The Allegheny River is a 325-mile (523 km) river in the eastern United States, the principal headwater of the Ohio River. Rising in north-central Pennsylvania, it flows northwest into New York, turns southwest back into Pennsylvania, and joins the Monongahela River at Pittsburgh to form the Ohio.

Course and history

The river's name derives from a Lenape word, possibly meaning "fine river". It was a vital corridor for Native American peoples and later for the French and British contesting the Ohio Country; Fort Duquesne and its successor Fort Pitt stood at its confluence. In 1859 Edwin Drake's well near Titusville on Oil Creek, a tributary, launched the petroleum industry, and barges of oil floated down the Allegheny to Pittsburgh. The Kinzua Dam, completed in 1965, created the Allegheny Reservoir, flooding lands of the Seneca Nation — a controversial taking that breached the 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua.

Navigation and recreation

A series of locks and dams makes the lower 72 miles navigable to East Brady. Much of the upper river runs through the Allegheny National Forest, and several middle stretches are designated National Wild and Scenic River segments, popular for paddling and fishing.
