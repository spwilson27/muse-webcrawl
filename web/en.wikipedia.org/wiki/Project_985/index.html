Project 985

Project 985 (Chinese: 985工程) was a project announced by the Chinese government in 1998 to develop a system of world-class universities in China by providing major funding to a select group of leading institutions. It is named after the date of its announcement — May 1998 — by then-President Jiang Zemin in a speech at Peking University marking its centenary.

History

Jiang Zemin declared that China should have a number of world-class universities, and initial funding went to nine universities, which formed the informal C9 League, sometimes called China's Ivy League. The project was expanded in phases, ultimately covering 39 public universities, which received billions of yuan in additional funding from central and local governments for faculty recruitment, research facilities, and international exchange.

Member institutions

The 39 member universities included Peking University, Tsinghua University, Fudan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Zhejiang University, Nanjing University, the University of Science and Technology of China, Harbin Institute of Technology, and Xi'an Jiaotong University, among others. Admission to these universities is among the most competitive in China through the gaokao examination system.

Successor

Together with Project 211, Project 985 was superseded in 2015–2017 by the Double First-Class Initiative, which reorganized funding around building world-class universities and disciplines, though the "985" designation remains a widely recognized mark of prestige in China.
