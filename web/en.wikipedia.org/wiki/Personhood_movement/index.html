Personhood movement

The personhood movement is an effort, primarily within the United States anti-abortion movement, to secure legal recognition that human life — and full legal personhood — begins at fertilisation, so that embryos and fetuses would be entitled to constitutional protection. Supporters aim to achieve this through state constitutional amendments, legislation, and litigation.

History and strategy

The strategy traces to dissatisfaction with incremental abortion regulation and to language in Roe v. Wade (1973) stating that if the fetus were a person under the Fourteenth Amendment, abortion rights would collapse. Groups such as Personhood USA (founded 2008) promoted state ballot measures defining "person" to include every human being from fertilisation. Voters rejected such measures in Colorado (2008, 2010, 2014) and Mississippi (2011), among others, and similar bills have been introduced in many state legislatures.

Debate

Supporters argue the position follows from the biological beginning of a new organism and universal human rights. Critics — including some within the broader anti-abortion movement — warned the measures could endanger in vitro fertilisation, some forms of contraception, and treatment of ectopic pregnancy, and questioned the electoral strategy. After Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022) overturned Roe, personhood arguments have appeared in state-level litigation, including a 2024 Alabama Supreme Court ruling treating frozen embryos as children under a wrongful-death statute, which prompted protective legislation for IVF providers.

Related uses

The term "personhood" also appears in separate debates over animal rights, artificial intelligence, and environmental legal rights, which are distinct movements.
