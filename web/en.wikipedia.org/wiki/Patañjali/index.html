Patañjali

Patañjali (Sanskrit: पतञ्जलि, IAST: Patañjali) was the name of one or more ancient Indian authors and sages. The best known is the compiler of the Yoga Sutras, the foundational text of classical yoga, and tradition also credits a Patañjali with the Mahābhāṣya, a major commentary on Pāṇini's grammar. Whether the grammarian and the yoga author were the same person is disputed among scholars.

The Yoga Sutras

The Yoga Sutras consist of 195 (or 196) short aphorisms (sutras) organised into four chapters. They define yoga as "the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind" (yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ) and describe an eightfold path (ashtanga) including ethical restraints, postures (asana), breath control (pranayama), and meditation culminating in samadhi. The text, usually dated between the 2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE, profoundly influenced later Indian philosophy and modern global yoga practice.

The Mahābhāṣya

The grammarian Patañjali, usually dated to the mid-2nd century BCE, wrote the Mahābhāṣya ("Great Commentary") on Kātyāyana's notes to Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī. The work is a landmark of Sanskrit linguistics and Indian philosophy of language, and contains incidental remarks that help date its author to the period of the Śuṅga Empire.

Legacy

Patañjali is revered in the yoga tradition as an incarnation of the serpent Śeṣa, and his image — half human, half serpent — is common in yoga schools. The modern yoga organisation founded by B. K. S. Iyengar popularised a widely used invocation to Patañjali. Tradition also attributes a medical work on Ayurveda to a Patañjali, though this attribution is likewise uncertain.
