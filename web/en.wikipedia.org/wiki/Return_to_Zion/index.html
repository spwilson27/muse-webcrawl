Return to Zion

The Return to Zion (Hebrew: Shivat Tziyon) refers to the return of the Jewish exiles from Babylonian captivity to the Land of Israel following the decree of Cyrus the Great in 538 BCE, and to the rebuilding of Jerusalem and the Second Temple. The events are described in the biblical books of Ezra and Nehemiah, and in the prophecies of Haggai, Zechariah and others.

Background and waves of return

After Nebuchadnezzar II destroyed Jerusalem and the First Temple in 586 BCE, much of Judah's population was deported to Babylonia. When Cyrus conquered Babylon, his edict permitted subject peoples to return and rebuild their temples. Tradition counts several waves of return: the first under Sheshbazzar and then Zerubbabel, who laid the foundations of the Second Temple (completed in 516/515 BCE); a later wave under Ezra the scribe (458 BCE, in the reign of Artaxerxes I), who re-established Torah observance; and the mission of Nehemiah (445 BCE), who rebuilt Jerusalem's walls. Not all exiles returned — a large Jewish community remained in Babylonia for centuries.

Significance

The return reshaped Jewish identity around the Torah and the Temple and began the Second Temple period. In modern Hebrew usage, "Return to Zion" also became a poetic and political term for Jewish immigration to the Land of Israel in the Zionist era.
