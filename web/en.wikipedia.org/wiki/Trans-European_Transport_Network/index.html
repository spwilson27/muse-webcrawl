Trans-European Transport Network

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) is a planned network of roads, railways, airports and water infrastructure in the European Union, established by EU policy to connect the continent and improve the functioning of the single market. The TEN-T guidelines define a dual-layer structure: a core network of the most important strategic links, to be completed by 2030, and a wider comprehensive network feeding into it, to be completed by 2050.

Structure and corridors

The core network is organised around nine core network corridors, multimodal routes crossing several member states — among them the Rhine–Alpine corridor (from the North Sea ports through the Rhineland and Switzerland to Genoa), the Scandinavian–Mediterranean corridor, the Atlantic corridor, the North Sea–Baltic corridor, and the Orient/East–Med corridor. Each corridor is overseen by a European Coordinator and a work plan addressing cross-border bottlenecks, missing links, and interoperability — for rail, notably deployment of the ERTMS signalling system.

Policy and funding

TEN-T policy originates in the Maastricht Treaty (1992) and has been revised several times, most significantly by Regulation (EU) No 1315/2013, which introduced the corridor structure, and by a further revision adopted in 2024 that merges the core corridors into "European Transport Corridors", adds extended core network deadlines of 2040, and strengthens requirements on rail capacity, military mobility and connections with neighbouring countries such as Ukraine and Moldova. EU co-funding is provided principally through the Connecting Europe Facility, with additional support from cohesion funds and the European Investment Bank.

Aims and standards

The network sets technical standards for infrastructure — for example, core rail lines must generally be electrified, equipped with ERTMS, and capable of 160 km/h operation for passenger trains and 100 km/h for freight, and core roads must be motorways or express roads. Policy goals include shifting freight and passengers from road to rail and waterways, reducing transport emissions, removing cross-border bottlenecks, and ensuring that all major urban nodes and ports are connected to the network.
