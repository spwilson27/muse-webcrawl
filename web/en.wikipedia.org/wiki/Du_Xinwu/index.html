Du Xinwu (杜心五, 1869–1953) was a Chinese martial artist, the most famous practitioner and lineage holder of Ziranmen (自然門, "Natural School" or "Spontaneous Boxing"), a Taoist-influenced internal martial art. Small in stature and famously lean, he became a legendary figure of the late Qing and Republican eras, remembered as a bodyguard, anti-opium campaigner, and one of the great eccentrics of the martial arts world.

Early life and training

Du was born in Sichuan (his family had military connections and he later settled in Hunan) and began martial training young. As a boy he sought out the reclusive Taoist master Xu Ai, said to be a dwarf-like hermit known as "Xu the Dwarf," and endured years of harsh foundational training—including weighted running, kicking practice against trees, and qigong exercises—before being taught the full Ziranmen method, which emphasizes softness, evasion, and deceptively casual movement rather than fixed forms. He supplemented this with study of other styles and with modern schooling, reportedly studying in Japan for a time.

Career

Du travelled widely, worked as a security guard for officials and as a bodyguard—including, by tradition, protecting revolutionary circles around the young Sun Yat-sen's movement—and built a fearsome reputation in challenge matches. He was an outspoken enemy of opium: he had himself been addicted in youth, cured himself, and campaigned against the trade, at one point (in the famous anecdote) publicly confronting corrupt dealers in Hunan. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he refused collaboration and withdrew from public life, living in Changsha, where he died in 1953.

Legacy

His best-known disciple, Wan Laisheng (1903–1989), popularized Ziranmen through the Guangdong and Hunan Guoshu institutes and books such as Wushu Huizong, preserving Du's art for modern students. Du Xinwu remains a stock character in Chinese martial-arts folklore and fiction—the small, wiry master whose apparently aimless wandering conceals devastating skill.
