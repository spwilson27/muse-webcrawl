Operation Manna

Date: 29 April – 7 May 1945

Location: German-occupied Netherlands

Forces: Royal Air Force, Royal Canadian Air Force, Royal Australian Air Force

Result: Over 6,600 tons of food delivered

Operation Manna was the delivery of food by RAF, RCAF and RAAF bombers to the starving population of the German-occupied western Netherlands in the closing days of the Second World War in Europe. Between 29 April and 7 May 1945, Lancasters and Mosquitos flew more than 3,300 sorties and dropped over 6,600 tons of food. The American counterpart, using B-17 Flying Fortresses, was called Operation Chowhound.

Background

During the "Hunger Winter" of 1944–45, a German embargo on food transport to the western Netherlands, imposed in reprisal for a Dutch railway strike supporting Operation Market Garden, combined with a harsh winter to produce famine that killed an estimated 18,000–22,000 Dutch civilians. By April 1945 daily rations in the cities had fallen to a few hundred calories.

Negotiations

Prince Bernhard and Allied officials negotiated with Reichskommissar Arthur Seyss-Inquart for a truce permitting food drops. German anti-aircraft batteries were ordered not to fire on the aircraft, which flew unarmed at very low altitude — as low as 50 feet — over agreed drop zones marked with white crosses.

The drops

On 29 April 1945, after a test flight days earlier, RAF Lancasters began dropping food over Rotterdam, The Hague, Leiden and other cities. Crowds gathered on rooftops waved to the crews. The American Operation Chowhound followed from 1 May, delivering a further 4,000 tons. Food distribution by truck under the truce continued with Operations Faust.

Legacy

The operations are still commemorated in the Netherlands; many food parcels carried the message "From your friends across the sea". The low-level flights are depicted in monuments at Terbregge and elsewhere, and veterans were long honoured guests at Dutch liberation celebrations.
