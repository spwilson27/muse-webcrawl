Robert's Rules of Order

AuthorHenry Martyn Robert
First published1876
Current editionRobert's Rules of Order Newly Revised, 12th edition (2020)
SubjectParliamentary procedure

Robert's Rules of Order, often simply called Robert's Rules, is a manual of parliamentary procedure originally written by U.S. Army officer Henry Martyn Robert and first published in 1876. Robert developed the rules after being asked to preside over a church meeting and realising he did not know how to run it; he based his work on the procedures of the United States Congress, adapted for ordinary societies.

Contents

The book sets out how meetings are conducted: motions and their precedence, debating and voting, the roles of chair and secretary, quorum, agendas, committees, bylaws and disciplinary procedures. Its guiding principles are the rights of the majority to decide, of the minority to be heard, and of individual members and absentees to fair process. The current authorised edition is Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised (RONR), maintained by the Robert's Rules Association.

Use

Robert's Rules is the most widely adopted parliamentary authority in the United States, used by clubs, unions, churches, homeowners' associations, student governments and many local bodies whose bylaws name it as their rulebook.
