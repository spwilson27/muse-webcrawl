The Leduc 0.10 was a research aircraft built in France, one of the world's first aircraft to fly powered solely by a ramjet. It was designed by René Leduc, who had been developing ramjet propulsion since the 1930s and had patented a design in 1933. Because a ramjet produces no static thrust, the 0.10 had to be carried aloft and released by a carrier aircraft before its engine could be ignited.

Design and development

The aircraft was a short, barrel-shaped monoplane whose fuselage was double-walled: the inner shell formed the duct of the ramjet, and the pilot sat inside the nose of the duct itself, within the intake. Air entered through the annular space between the shells, was compressed by the shape of the duct, mixed with fuel and burned at the tail. Construction was undertaken by Breguet at Villacoublay. The chosen carrier aircraft was the SNCASE SE.161 Languedoc, a four-engined airliner, from which the 0.10 was launched in a parasitic configuration on top of the fuselage.

Testing

Glide flights began on 21 October 1947. After a period of unpowered testing, the first powered flight took place on 21 April 1949, piloted by Jean Gonord. The aircraft demonstrated speeds in excess of 680 km/h (about 420 mph) and climb rates that exceeded those of the best jet fighters of the day, although endurance was very limited. A second example, designated 0.16, incorporated a small turbojet to allow some self-powered operation. The programme led on to the larger Leduc 0.21 research aircraft and ultimately to the planned Leduc 0.22 interceptor prototype, which was cancelled before completion in the late 1950s.

Survivors

The 0.10 is preserved and displayed at the Musée de l'Air et de l'Espace at Le Bourget, Paris.
