Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal is a term relating to hot water, especially water heated by the Earth's internal heat. Hydrothermal processes are responsible for a wide range of geological phenomena, including the formation of many ore deposits, hot springs, geysers and deep-sea hydrothermal vents.

Hydrothermal circulation

Hydrothermal circulation occurs when water penetrates the Earth's crust, is heated by magma or by the geothermal gradient, and rises again, often carrying dissolved minerals. At mid-ocean ridges, seawater circulates through the hot oceanic crust, emerging at hydrothermal vents as superheated, mineral-laden fluid that supports unique ecosystems independent of sunlight.

Hydrothermal ore deposits

Many economically important deposits of gold, silver, copper, lead, zinc and other metals form when hot aqueous fluids deposit minerals in veins and fractures as they cool or react with surrounding rock. Epithermal and mesothermal gold deposits, porphyry copper deposits and volcanogenic massive sulfide deposits are all products of hydrothermal activity.

Hydrothermal synthesis

In chemistry and materials science, hydrothermal synthesis refers to techniques of crystallising substances from high-temperature aqueous solutions at high vapour pressures. It is used to grow single crystals such as quartz and to produce nanomaterials with controlled properties. See also geothermal energy.
