Independence Day

An Independence Day is an annual event commemorating the anniversary of a nation's independence or statehood, usually after ceasing to be part of another nation or state, or after the end of a military occupation. Many countries mark the occasion as a national holiday with parades, fireworks, ceremonies and flag displays.

Around the world

Examples include the United States (July 4, 1776, independence from Great Britain), India (August 15, 1947, from British rule), Pakistan (August 14, 1947), Mexico (September 16, the 1810 Cry of Dolores), Brazil (September 7, 1822), Indonesia (August 17, 1945), Nigeria (October 1, 1960) and many others. Some countries have no independence day because they were never formally colonised, celebrating instead a national day marking unification, a constitution or a founding event.

United States

In the United States, Independence Day — commonly the Fourth of July — is a federal holiday commemorating the adoption of the Declaration of Independence in 1776. It is strongly associated with fireworks, parades, barbecues, baseball games and patriotic displays.
