Four-gradient

In differential geometry, the four-gradient is the four-vector analogue of the gradient in three-dimensional space, used in the theory of special relativity and in relativistic formulations of classical and quantum field theory. It is written as ∂μ and consists of the partial derivatives with respect to the components of the four-position.

Definition

In flat spacetime with metric signature (+, −, −, −), the four-gradient is defined as ∂μ = (∂/∂t, −∇), that is, the time component is the partial derivative with respect to time (with c = 1) and the spatial components are the negative of the ordinary gradient operator. Its contravariant counterpart ∂μ has the opposite signs. The contraction ∂μ∂μ gives the d'Alembertian operator □, which appears in the Klein–Gordon equation and the wave equation.

Applications

The four-gradient acts on scalar fields to produce four-vectors, and combinations of four-gradients of the electromagnetic four-potential yield the electromagnetic field tensor. It is also central to expressing conservation laws, such as the continuity equation ∂μJμ = 0 for the four-current.
