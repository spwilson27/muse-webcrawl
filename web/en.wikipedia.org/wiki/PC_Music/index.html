PC Music was a British record label and art collective based in London. It was founded by producer A. G. Cook in 2013 and released music by Cook and artists including Hannah Diamond, GFOTY, Danny L Harle, EASYFUN, Namasenda and Planet 1999. The label's surreal, exaggerated take on pop music — often described with terms such as "bubblegum bass" and, later, "hyperpop" — made it one of the most talked-about and divisive forces in 2010s electronic and pop music.

History

Cook launched PC Music in 2013 while studying at Goldsmiths, University of London, releasing tracks online by a rotating cast of often pseudonymous projects. The label drew attention in 2014 with releases such as Hannah Diamond's "Pink and Blue" and Cook's own "Beautiful", and through its association with the producer Sophie, whose singles "Bipp" (2013) and "Lemonade" / "Hard" (2014) shared the label's aesthetic. In 2015 PC Music announced a partnership with Columbia Records and staged showcases including a widely covered event at SXSW and the "Pop Cube" multimedia event in New York and London.

Style and reception

PC Music tracks typically combined the song structures and vocal tropes of mainstream dance-pop, Eurodance, happy hardcore and J-pop/K-pop with pitch-shifted vocals, metallic and rubbery synthesizer textures, extreme compression and abrupt editing. Lyrics frequently played with consumerism, branding and internet culture. Critics were split: some hailed the collective as the future of pop, others dismissed it as ironic hipster parody. Cook rejected the idea that the music was merely satire, describing it as a sincere attempt to push pop's own logic to an extreme.

Legacy

Members and associates of the label went on to shape mainstream pop: Cook became a close collaborator and executive producer for Charli XCX, Danny L Harle worked across pop production, and Sophie's solo work received wide acclaim before her death in 2021. The "hyperpop" label that coalesced around playlists in 2019 drew heavily on the PC Music sound. In June 2023 Cook announced that 2023 would be PC Music's final year of new releases, with the label continuing thereafter as an archive and reissue imprint; a run of farewell "PC Music Forever" events followed.
