Dharug

The Dharug (also spelled Darug or Dharuk) are an Aboriginal Australian people who are the traditional custodians of much of what is now the Sydney region of New South Wales, Australia. Their country extends across the Cumberland Plain and surrounding areas, roughly bounded by the Hawkesbury River to the north-west, the Nepean River to the west, and extending south towards the Georges River.

Language

The Dharug language, sometimes called the Sydney language together with the closely related Dharawal, was spoken across the Sydney basin at the time of European colonisation in 1788. The language ceased to be spoken as a first language by the late nineteenth century, though it has been partially reconstructed from historical records, including the notebooks of early settlers and the wordlists collected by figures such as William Dawes, who learned from the young Dharug woman Patyegarang. Revival efforts continue today.

History

Dharug people have occupied the Sydney region for many thousands of years. Following the arrival of the First Fleet in 1788, the Dharug suffered catastrophic losses from smallpox and dispossession. Notable Dharug figures include Pemulwuy, who led resistance against the colony in the 1790s, and his son Tedbury.
