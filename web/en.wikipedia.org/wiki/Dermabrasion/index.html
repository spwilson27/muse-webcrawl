Dermabrasion

Dermabrasion is a type of surgical skin planing, typically performed in a professional medical setting by a dermatologist or plastic surgeon. It uses a rapidly rotating abrasive instrument — traditionally a wire brush or diamond fraise (burr) — to remove the outer layers of skin, allowing new, smoother skin to grow in their place.

Uses

Dermabrasion is used to improve the appearance of acne scars, fine wrinkles (especially around the mouth), sun-damaged skin, rhinophyma, and some precancerous keratoses and tattoos. The procedure is usually performed under local anesthesia with sedation or general anesthesia, and the treated skin takes days to weeks to re-epithelialize.

Risks and alternatives

Possible complications include changes in pigmentation, scarring, infection, and prolonged redness. Dermabrasion differs from microdermabrasion, a gentler, non-surgical exfoliation of only the superficial epidermis. Chemical peels and laser resurfacing are common alternatives that have partly supplanted dermabrasion in modern practice.
