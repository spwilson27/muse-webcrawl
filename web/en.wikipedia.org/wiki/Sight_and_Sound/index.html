Sight and Sound (stylised Sight & Sound) is a monthly film magazine published by the British Film Institute (BFI). Founded in 1932, it is one of the oldest and most respected English-language film journals, known for serious criticism, interviews and coverage of world cinema. An earlier quarterly, it became a monthly and absorbed the BFI's Monthly Film Bulletin in 1991. Long-serving editors include Gavin Lambert (1949–55) and Penelope Houston (1956–90); more recent editors include Nick James and Mike Williams.

The critics' poll

Sight and Sound is best known internationally for its decennial poll of critics to determine the greatest films of all time, conducted since 1952. Bicycle Thieves topped the first poll; Citizen Kane held first place from 1962 through 2002; Vertigo displaced it in 2012. In the 2022 poll—greatly expanded to over 1,600 voters—Chantal Akerman's Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du commerce, 1080 Bruxelles (1975) became the first film directed by a woman to top the list. A parallel directors' poll has been held since 1992.

Content and criticism

The magazine combines reviews of new releases, festival reports, interviews, and essays on film history and theory. Pauline Kael's famous 1963 essay "Circles and Squares" attacked the magazine's taste and the auteur theory associated with its writers. In 2020 the BFI relaunched the title in an expanded format, and it continues to publish in print and digital editions alongside its website's criticism and the BFI's other publishing.
