Holy sites in Islam are places of special sanctity for Muslims, chief among them the cities of Mecca and Medina in Arabia and Jerusalem. The three holiest sites are the Masjid al-Haram in Mecca, the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, and the Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem.

Mecca

Mecca is Islam's holiest city, containing the Masjid al-Haram and the Kaaba, toward which Muslims direct their prayers (the qibla). The Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca, required once in a lifetime of every able Muslim, draws millions annually. Only Muslims may enter the city.

Medina

Medina, the city to which Muhammad migrated in 622 CE (the Hijra), holds the Prophet's Mosque (Al-Masjid an-Nabawi), beneath whose Green Dome Muhammad is buried. Medina is the second holiest city in Islam.

Jerusalem

Jerusalem is revered as the site of the Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock on the Haram al-Sharif (Temple Mount), associated with Muhammad's Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj). Jerusalem was the first qibla before Mecca.

Other sites

Shia Muslims additionally revere sites such as Karbala and Najaf in Iraq and Mashhad in Iran, associated with the Imams. Tombs of prophets and saints across the Muslim world are also pilgrimage destinations, though some traditions discourage shrine veneration.
