Alvis Edgar "Buck" Owens Jr. (August 12, 1929 – March 25, 2006) was an American musician, singer, songwriter, and bandleader. With his band the Buckaroos, he pioneered the Bakersfield sound — a bright, twangy, electrified strain of honky-tonk country music that stood in deliberate contrast to the lush Nashville sound. Owens recorded 21 number-one hits on the Billboard country chart and co-hosted the television variety show Hee Haw from 1969 to 1986.

Early life

Owens was born in Sherman, Texas, to a sharecropping family; he took the name "Buck" from the family mule. The family migrated west during the Dust Bowl, settling in Mesa, Arizona. Owens left school young, worked as a truck driver and farm labourer, and played in honky-tonks. In 1951 he moved to Bakersfield, California, becoming a fixture of its club scene and a session guitarist in Los Angeles who played on records by Wanda Jackson, Tommy Sands, and others.

Career

Signing with Capitol Records, Owens broke through in 1963 with "Act Naturally" — later recorded by the Beatles with Ringo Starr singing lead — beginning a run of consecutive number ones including "Love's Gonna Live Here", "My Heart Skips a Beat", "Together Again", "I've Got a Tiger by the Tail", and "Waitin' in Your Welfare Line". His band, the Buckaroos, featuring guitarist and harmony singer Don Rich, defined the sound with Fender Telecasters, steel guitar, and tight harmonies. Rich's death in a 1974 motorcycle accident devastated Owens, who largely withdrew from recording for a decade. In 1988 a duet re-recording of "Streets of Bakersfield" with Dwight Yoakam returned him to number one, reviving interest in his catalogue.

Business and legacy

A shrewd businessman, Owens owned radio and television stations, music publishing, and the Crystal Palace, a Bakersfield concert venue and museum opened in 1996, where his car hangs above the bar. He negotiated an unusually favourable Capitol contract that returned ownership of his master recordings to him. Owens was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1996. He died in his sleep on March 25, 2006, hours after performing at the Crystal Palace.
