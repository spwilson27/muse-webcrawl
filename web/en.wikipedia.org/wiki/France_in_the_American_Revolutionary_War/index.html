France in the American Revolutionary War

France in the American Revolutionary War describes the involvement of the Kingdom of France in the American War of Independence (1775–1783), first through covert supply of arms and money, and from 1778, after the Treaty of Alliance, as an open belligerent against Great Britain.

Intervention

Seeking revenge for its defeat in the Seven Years' War, France under Louis XVI supplied the American rebels via front companies organised by Beaumarchais. The American victory at Saratoga in 1777 persuaded France to sign treaties of commerce and alliance in February 1778, bringing its navy and an expeditionary army under Rochambeau into the war.

Yorktown and aftermath

French sea power proved decisive at the Battle of the Chesapeake in 1781, enabling the Franco-American victory at Yorktown that effectively ended the war. The Peace of Paris (1783) confirmed American independence, but the cost of the war deepened France's fiscal crisis, contributing to the calling of the Estates-General in 1789.
