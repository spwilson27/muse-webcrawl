Vector (molecular biology)

Vector
Schematic of a typical plasmid cloning vector

In molecular biology, a vector is a DNA molecule used as a vehicle to artificially carry foreign genetic material into another cell, where it can be replicated and/or expressed. Vectors are central tools of molecular cloning and genetic engineering. A vector containing foreign DNA is termed recombinant DNA.

Characteristics

Most vectors share several essential features: an origin of replication allowing autonomous replication inside the host cell; a selectable marker, typically an antibiotic resistance gene, allowing cells carrying the vector to be identified; and one or more unique restriction enzyme sites, often grouped in a multiple cloning site (polylinker), into which foreign DNA can be inserted. Expression vectors additionally carry promoters and other regulatory sequences that drive transcription and translation of the inserted gene in the host.

Types of vector

  • Plasmid vectors – small circular DNA molecules, the most common cloning vectors, typically carrying inserts of up to about 10–15 kilobases.
  • Viral vectors – modified viruses (such as adenoviruses, retroviruses and lentiviruses) used to deliver DNA into cells, widely used in gene therapy research.
  • Bacteriophage vectors – based on phages such as lambda, able to carry larger inserts than plasmids.
  • Cosmids and fosmids – hybrid vectors combining plasmid and phage features for inserts of roughly 35–45 kb.
  • Artificial chromosomes – bacterial (BAC), yeast (YAC) and human (HAC) artificial chromosomes capable of carrying very large inserts, up to megabases.
  • Shuttle vectors – vectors that can propagate in two different host species, containing an origin of replication for each.

Applications

Vectors underpin the production of recombinant proteins such as insulin, the construction of genomic and cDNA libraries, functional genomics studies, and the delivery of therapeutic genes in gene therapy. The choice of vector depends on the size of the insert, the host organism and whether expression of the cloned gene is required.
