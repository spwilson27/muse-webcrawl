Kaminarimon

LocationAsakusa, Taitō, Tokyo, Japan
Part ofSensō-ji temple complex

The Kaminarimon (雷門, "Thunder Gate") is the outer of two large entrance gates leading to the Sensō-ji Buddhist temple in Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan. It is one of the most famous landmarks of Tokyo and a symbol of both Asakusa and the city itself.

Description

The gate stands 11.7 metres tall and spans 11.4 metres. Two protective statues flank the entrance: Fūjin, the god of wind, on the east side, and Raijin, the god of thunder, on the west. Hanging in the centre of the gate is a giant red paper lantern (chōchin), 3.9 metres tall and 3.3 metres in circumference, weighing about 700 kilograms, painted with the gate's name "Fūraijinmon" on the front and "Kaminarimon" on the back. Beyond the gate stretches the Nakamise shopping street, one of Japan's oldest, leading to the inner Hōzōmon gate and the temple hall.

History

A gate was first built on this site in 941 by the military commander Taira no Kinmasa. It burned down repeatedly over the centuries; the present structure was donated in 1960 by Matsushita Kōnosuke, founder of Panasonic, whose company also funded the current lantern, renewed periodically — most recently in 2023. The lantern is collapsed during typhoons and festivals to protect it.
