Budweiser Budvar (Czech: Budvar) is a brewery in the Czech city of Ceske Budejovice (German: Budweis), best known internationally for its pale lager of the same name. The brewery was founded in 1895 as the Czech Joint Stock Brewery, established by Czech-speaking citizens of the city in response to the older German-owned Citizens' Brewery (founded 1795), whose beer was also marketed as Budweiser. Budvar remains unusual among large breweries in being owned by the Czech state.

History

Brewing in Budweis dates back to the 13th century, and the city's name became attached to the "Budweiser" style of lager. The Czech Joint Stock Brewery brewed its first batch on 7 October 1895. The brewery survived nationalisation under communist Czechoslovakia, when it became a state enterprise, and unlike most Czech breweries it was not privatised after 1989, remaining in state hands. Its flagship lager is brewed with Moravian malt, Saaz hops, and soft water drawn from deep artesian wells, and is lagered for around 90 days — much longer than most industrial lagers.

Beer

The flagship product, Budweiser Budvar Original, is a pale lager of about 5.0% alcohol by volume in the Czech tradition. The range also includes a dark lager, a stronger premium lager, and a non-alcoholic beer. In many markets the beer is sold under the names "Czechvar" or simply "Budvar" because of the trademark dispute described below.

Trademark dispute

Budvar has been engaged in a trademark dispute with the American brewer Anheuser-Busch (now part of AB InBev) over the name "Budweiser" for more than a century, one of the longest-running trademark conflicts in history. Anheuser-Busch began selling its Budweiser in 1876, naming it after Budweis. Agreements in 1907 and 1939 divided some markets between the two companies, but litigation has continued in dozens of jurisdictions, with outcomes varying by country. In the European Union, "Budweiser Budvar" also benefits from protected geographical indication status for beer from Ceske Budejovice.
