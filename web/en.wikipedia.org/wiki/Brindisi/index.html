Brindisi is a city and port in the region of Apulia in southern Italy, capital of the province of Brindisi, on the Adriatic coast. Its natural harbour has made it a trading and ferry port for over two thousand years, historically the principal point of departure from Italy for Greece and the eastern Mediterranean.

History

Ancient Brundisium was a Messapian settlement conquered by Rome in 267 BC. It became the southern terminus of the Via Appia — a Roman column still standing by the harbour marks the end of the road — and a vital naval base and emporium. The poet Virgil died here in 19 BC on his return from Greece. In the Middle Ages the port was a staging point for Crusaders; it later passed through Swabian, Angevin, Venetian and Spanish hands. After Italian unification, the opening of the Suez Canal (1869) made Brindisi the mail port for India via the P&O "Valigia delle Indie" route, and it briefly hosted the seat of the Italian government in 1943–44.

Economy and transport

Today Brindisi's economy rests on its commercial and ferry port (with services to Greece and Albania), petrochemical and energy industries, agriculture and wine production in the surrounding plain, and a growing cruise and tourism trade. The city is served by Brindisi Airport and by the Adriatic railway line.

Main sights

  • The Roman column marking the end of the Via Appia
  • The Aragonese (Swabian) Castle and the Alfonsino Castle on the harbour island
  • The Cathedral and the church of San Giovanni al Sepolcro
  • The monument to Italian sailors (Monumento al Marinaio d'Italia)
