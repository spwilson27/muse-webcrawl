Temple Society

The Temple Society (German: Tempelgesellschaft) is a religious society founded in 1861 in Württemberg, Germany, by Christoph Hoffmann and Georg David Hardegg. Its members, known as Templers (German: Tempelgesellschaft, not to be confused with the medieval Knights Templar), sought to put into practice their interpretation of biblical prophecy concerning the ingathering of God's people in the Holy Land. The society established several colonies in Ottoman Palestine beginning in 1868, whose well-planned settlements in Haifa, Jaffa, Jerusalem and elsewhere became notable centres of agriculture, industry and commerce.

History

Christoph Hoffmann (1815–1885), a Lutheran theologian from Ludwigsburg, developed the idea of gathering a "people of God" in Palestine to prepare for the Second Coming. Together with Georg David Hardegg he founded the Society of the Friends of Jerusalem in 1854; after a break with the Lutheran Church the movement was formally constituted as the Temple Society in 1861. Hoffmann's writings, including his 1855 work Occident und Orient, set out the society's theology.

The first Templer colony in Palestine was founded in 1868 at the foot of Mount Carmel in Haifa. Colonies followed at Jaffa (1869), Sarona near Jaffa (1871), Jerusalem (1873), and later Bethlehem of Galilee, Waldheim, Wilhelma and others. The colonists introduced modern agricultural methods, hotels, workshops and transport services, and their settlements remained German-speaking Protestant communities for several generations.

During the Second World War many Templers of German nationality were interned by the British Mandate authorities as enemy aliens; hundreds were deported to Australia in 1941, where the community continued at Tatura and elsewhere. After 1948 the remaining Palestine Templers largely emigrated to Australia or Germany. The Temple Society survives today with congregations in Germany and Australia, with its German headquarters at Stuttgart-Degerloch.
