Game mechanics


Game mechanics are the rules and procedures that guide play and the player's interaction with a game.

Game mechanics are the constructs of rules or methods designed for interaction with the game state, thus providing gameplay. All games use mechanics; different games have different sets, but the mechanics define what actions players can take, what happens when they act, and what conditions determine success or failure. In video games, mechanics include the systems governing movement, combat, resources, and progression.

Common mechanics

Frequently encountered mechanics include: turns and action points; dice rolls and random draws introducing chance; resource management and economic engines; capture/elimination of opposing pieces; set collection, tile placement, and area control in board games; and in video games, jumping and traversal, shooting, health and damage systems, leveling and skill trees, crafting, and quest structures. Emergent gameplay arises when simple interacting mechanics produce complex, unanticipated situations.

Core mechanics and game design

Designers often speak of a game's core mechanic — the activity players repeat most, such as jumping in a platformer or matching tiles in a puzzle game — around which other systems are built. Game balance tunes mechanics' numerical parameters to keep a game challenging but fair. The academic study of mechanics is part of game design and game studies; related concepts include the "magic circle," game feel, and the MDA framework (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics), which analyzes how mechanics generate player experience.
