Skua

The skuas are a group of predatory seabirds comprising the genus Stercorarius in the family Stercorariidae. In North America the smaller species are often called jaegers. They are powerful fliers of polar and temperate seas.

Family: Stercorariidae
Species: 7
Habitat: Coastal tundra and open ocean

Description and behaviour

Skuas are generally dark-plumaged with hooked bills and strong talons. They are famous for kleptoparasitism — harassing gulls, terns and even gannets in mid-air until they drop or regurgitate their catch — but also hunt rodents, eggs, chicks and fish, and scavenge. On the breeding grounds (Arctic tundra and Antarctic coasts) they fiercely defend their nests.

Species

The group includes the great skua, brown skua, South polar skua, Chilean skua, pomarine jaeger, parasitic jaeger (Arctic skua) and long-tailed jaeger. Several are long-distance migrants; the Arctic skua winters off South America and southern Africa.
