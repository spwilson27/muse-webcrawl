St Catherine's Lighthouse

LocationNiton, Isle of Wight, England
Built1838–1840
OperatorTrinity House

St Catherine's Lighthouse stands at St Catherine's Point, the southernmost point of the Isle of Wight. A light was first requested here after the wreck of a ship in 1314 led Walter de Godeton to build a chantry and oratory with a beacon; the medieval tower, known locally as the "Pepperpot", still stands on St Catherine's Down above the point.

The modern lighthouse

The present lighthouse was built by Trinity House between 1838 and 1840 after the disastrous wreck of the sailing barque Clarendon in 1836, in which 11 people died. Fog rendered the original tall tower ineffective, so it was reduced in height in 1875. The station was automated in 1997. A nearby stone tower, the "Salt Pot", was begun as a second light but never completed.
