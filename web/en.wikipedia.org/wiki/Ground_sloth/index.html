Ground sloth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ground sloths are a diverse group of extinct sloths in the mammalian superorder Xenarthra. Most ground sloths were much larger than modern tree sloths; the largest, such as Megatherium and Eremotherium, reached the size of elephants, standing up to 6 metres (20 ft) tall on their hind legs and weighing several tonnes. Ground sloths lived in the Americas from the early Oligocene until the late Pleistocene and earliest Holocene, going extinct roughly 10,000 years ago.

Evolution and distribution

Ground sloths evolved in South America, where sloths first appeared more than 30 million years ago. After the formation of the Isthmus of Panama around 3 million years ago, several lineages migrated north during the Great American Interchange, reaching as far as Alaska and the Yukon. Major families include the Megatheriidae, Mylodontidae, Nothrotheriidae, and Megalonychidae.

Description

Ground sloths were quadrupedal herbivores with powerful forelimbs tipped with large claws. Many species could rear up on their hind legs and pelvis — supported by a short, stout tail — to pull down branches and strip leaves. Their teeth grew continuously and lacked enamel, an adaptation to grinding abrasive vegetation. Some mylodonts had skin embedded with small bony ossicles (osteoderms), forming a kind of natural armour.

Extinction

Ground sloths survived the last glacial maximum but disappeared at the end of the Pleistocene, around 11,000–10,000 years ago, in an extinction event that also eliminated mammoths, mastodons, and other megafauna. The causes remain debated: human hunting, rapid climate warming, or a combination of both are the leading hypotheses. Cut-marked bones and kill sites suggest humans hunted at least some species, and a population of dwarf ground sloths survived on Caribbean islands until roughly 4,400 years ago.

Notable genera

Megatherium ("great beast") was among the largest land mammals of its time. Mylodon is famous from remarkably preserved skin and dung found in caves in Patagonia. Megalonyx, the genus of Jefferson's ground sloth, was named with the involvement of Thomas Jefferson, who presented a paper on its fossils to the American Philosophical Society in 1797.
