Civil Rights Division

The U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division is a division of the United States Department of Justice whose responsibilities include enforcing federal statutes prohibiting discrimination on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, disability, family status, and national origin. It was established in 1957 by the Civil Rights Act of 1957, and its first head was Assistant Attorney General W. Wilson White. The division is headed by the Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights.

Responsibilities

The division enforces, among other laws, the Civil Rights Acts of 1957, 1960, 1964, and 1991; the Voting Rights Act of 1965; the Fair Housing Act; the Americans with Disabilities Act; and statutes protecting the rights of institutionalised persons, students, and military voters. Its sections handle voting, employment litigation, housing and civil enforcement, disability rights, educational opportunities, criminal matters (including prosecutions of hate crimes and official misconduct), and appellate work.

History

The division grew out of a smaller Civil Rights Section created in 1939. During the 1960s its attorneys, working under leaders such as John Doar and Burke Marshall, brought landmark voting and desegregation cases across the South. Later decades saw major work on police pattern-or-practice investigations, disability rights following the ADA (1990), and enforcement of the Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act (2009).
