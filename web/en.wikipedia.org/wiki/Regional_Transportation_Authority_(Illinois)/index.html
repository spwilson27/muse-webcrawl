Regional Transportation Authority (Illinois)

The Regional Transportation Authority (RTA) is the financial oversight and regional planning body for public transportation in the Chicago metropolitan area in Illinois. It oversees three service boards: the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA), Metra commuter rail, and Pace suburban bus.

History

The RTA was created by referendum in 1974 amid the financial collapse of private transit operators in the region. A 1983 restructuring established the three service boards, which operate services, while the RTA provides funding, budgeting oversight and regional planning. The authority is funded primarily by a dedicated sales tax levied in Cook County and the five collar counties (DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry and Will).

Governance

The RTA is governed by a 16-member board of directors appointed by regional officials including the Mayor of Chicago, the Cook County Board President and the collar-county board chairs. Together the system it funds provides roughly two million rides per weekday (pre-pandemic figures) across a six-county region.
