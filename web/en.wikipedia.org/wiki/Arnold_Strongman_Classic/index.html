The Arnold Strongman Classic is an annual strongman competition held in Columbus, Ohio, as part of the Arnold Sports Festival founded by Arnold Schwarzenegger and Jim Lorimer. First held in 2002, it is considered one of the most prestigious titles in strength sports and is renowned for extremely heavy, static events that favour brute strength over speed.

History

The contest was created in 2002 as the strongman arm of the expanding Arnold Sports Festival. Its first champion was American Mark Henry, later a professional wrestler, who won the inaugural event against a field including Svend Karlsen. Lithuanian Žydrūnas Savickas went on to dominate the competition, winning eight titles between 2003 and 2016. Iceland's Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson won three consecutive titles from 2018 to 2020, while Martins Licis, Mitchell Hooper and others have won in the 2020s. Sister Arnold Strongman Classic events are now staged in other countries, including the United Kingdom and Spain.

Events

Signature events have included the Apollon's Axle press, the Austrian Oak log lift, the Hummer Tire Deadlift with extra-long bar, heavy timber and frame carries, the Bale Tote yoke, the Stone Shoulder, and lifts of the replica Thomas Inch dumbbell and the Cyr dumbbell. The emphasis on maximal weight — rather than repetitions against the clock — distinguishes the Arnold from the World's Strongest Man contest.

Champions

Alongside Savickas's eight victories, multiple titles have been won by Brian Shaw (three) and Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson (three); Derek Poundstone won twice. The event's prize fund grew to be among the largest in strongman, with the 2024 champion's purse exceeding $80,000.
