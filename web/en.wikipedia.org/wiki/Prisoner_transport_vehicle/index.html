Prisoner transport vehicle

Also known as: Prison van, prison bus, "paddy wagon" (informally)

Operators: Police forces, correctional services, court services

A prisoner transport vehicle, informally known as a "Sally Port" van, prison van or (in the United States) paddy wagon, is a specially designed or retrofitted vehicle used to transport prisoners from one secure location, such as a jail or prison, to another, such as a courthouse or a different facility.

Design

Transport vehicles are typically fitted with a segregated, secure compartment separate from the driver, steel mesh or bars over the windows, interior surfaces that are easy to clean and difficult to damage, and locking mechanisms controlled from outside the prisoner compartment. Large prison buses can carry dozens of restrained inmates on bench seating, while vans used by police for short journeys divide detainees into individual or group cells.

Operation

Prisoners are usually searched and restrained with handcuffs and other restraints before loading. In the United States, specialised carriers such as the U.S. Marshals Service's Justice Prisoner and Alien Transportation System (JPATS) move prisoners by air and road. Court security and police services in the United Kingdom, Australia and other Commonwealth countries use cellular prison vans, sometimes nicknamed "sweatboxes" for the cramped individual cells.

Safety concerns

The transport of restrained prisoners has raised safety issues, including injuries from sudden braking when passengers cannot brace themselves, which has led to litigation and design changes in some jurisdictions.
