Administrative divisions of Taiwan

The administrative divisions of Taiwan (the Republic of China) are structured in two principal levels: first-level divisions consisting of provinces and special municipalities, and second-level divisions consisting of districts and administrative districts. Following administrative streamlining in the late 1990s, the provincial governments were largely frozen, and day-to-day governance is exercised chiefly by the special municipalities and by county and city governments.

First-level divisions

Taiwan has six special municipalities — Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Taichung, Tainan, and Kaohsiung — which are directly administered by the central government. The remaining territory is nominally divided between Taiwan Province and Fujian Province (which administers the Kinmen and Lienchiang islands), although the provincial governments were streamlined in 1998–1999 and their functions transferred to the Executive Yuan.

Second-level divisions

Under the provinces are counties and provincial cities. Counties are subdivided into county-administered cities, urban townships, and rural townships; special municipalities and provincial cities are subdivided into districts. Townships and districts are further divided into villages and neighborhoods, the lowest units of local administration.

History

The modern structure descends from the administrative system imposed during Japanese rule (1895–1945), reorganized after the Republic of China government relocated to Taiwan in 1949. Taipei became a special municipality in 1967 and Kaohsiung in 1979; further municipalities were created in 2010 and 2014 as counties merged with cities to reach the population thresholds required for direct central administration.
