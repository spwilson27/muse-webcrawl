Primer (paint)

Primer being applied to a bare surface before painting.

A primer is a preparatory coating applied to materials before painting. Priming ensures better adhesion of paint to the surface, increases paint durability, and provides additional protection for the material being painted. A primer consists of a synthetic resin binder, solvents, and additive agents, and often — unlike paint — contains little or no pigment.

PurposeAdhesion, sealing, corrosion protection, stain blocking
TypesOil-based, latex (water-based), shellac-based
Applied toWood, drywall, metal, masonry, plastic

Functions

  • Adhesion: primer bonds tightly to difficult substrates (glossy, smooth, or chalky surfaces) and gives the topcoat a surface it can grip.
  • Sealing: porous materials such as new drywall, bare wood, and masonry absorb paint unevenly; primer seals them for a uniform finish.
  • Stain blocking: shellac and oil-based primers seal in water stains, smoke damage, tannins, and knots.
  • Corrosion protection: metal primers contain rust-inhibiting pigments such as zinc phosphate (historically red lead and zinc chromate).
  • Colour transition: tinted primers help when making a drastic colour change, reducing the number of topcoats needed.

Types

Oil-based (alkyd) primers excel at stain blocking and adhesion to wood but have strong odours and slow drying times. Latex primers are water-cleanup, flexible, and fast-drying, suited to drywall and softwoods. Shellac primers are the most powerful stain and odour blockers and adhere to nearly any surface. Specialised primers exist for galvanised metal, PVC, melamine, and previously painted glossy surfaces ("bonding primers").

Use

Primer is usually applied to new, unpainted surfaces, repaired patches, and bare spots. Self-priming paints combine primer and topcoat but are not substitutes for a dedicated primer on bare or problem substrates.
