Neutral point

In aeronautics, the neutral point of an aircraft is the longitudinal position of its aerodynamic centre — the point at which the pitching moment coefficient does not vary with lift coefficient (angle of attack), as if all aerodynamic forces acted there. It is a key concept in longitudinal static stability.

Definition and significance

The neutral point can be thought of as the aerodynamic centre of the complete aircraft configuration, combining the contributions of the wing, fuselage and tailplane. If the centre of gravity lies ahead of the neutral point, the aircraft is statically stable: a disturbance in angle of attack generates a restoring pitching moment. If the centre of gravity lies behind it, the aircraft is unstable.

Static margin

The distance between the centre of gravity and the neutral point, expressed as a fraction of the mean aerodynamic chord, is called the static margin. A positive static margin (centre of gravity ahead of the neutral point) indicates positive static stability. Conventional aircraft are designed with a modest positive static margin; relaxed-static-stability designs, flown with computer control, may have a negative margin.
