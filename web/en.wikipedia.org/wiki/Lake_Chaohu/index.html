Lake Chaohu
Location: Anhui, China
Surface area: ~780 km²
Average depth: ~2.7 m
Primary outflow: Yuxi River to the Yangtze

Lake Chaohu and Laoshan Island

Lake Chaohu (Chinese: 巢湖, Cháohú) is one of the five largest freshwater lakes in China, situated in Anhui Province between the cities of Hefei and Chaohu. Covering about 780 square kilometres, the shallow lake drains eastward through the Yuxi River into the Yangtze River. Its name means "Nest Lake", referring to its shape.

Geography and use

The lake's main island, Laoshan, is a popular tourist site with temples and hot springs. Chaohu has long supported fisheries — famed for its whitebait, shrimp and crabs — and supplies irrigation water and drinking water to surrounding farmland and cities. A ship lock built at the lake's outlet in the twentieth century ended the lake's natural connection with the Yangtze for migratory fish.

Pollution

Rapid urban and industrial growth around Hefei made Chaohu notorious for severe eutrophication: nutrient runoff produced vast algal blooms from the 1980s onward, threatening drinking water supplies. Large-scale treatment and wetland restoration programmes since the 2010s have gradually improved water quality, though the lake remains one of China's most closely monitored polluted water bodies.
