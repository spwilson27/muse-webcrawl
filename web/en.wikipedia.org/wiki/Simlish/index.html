Simlish

Simlish is a fictional language featured in The Sims series of life-simulation video games developed by Maxis and published by Electronic Arts. Sims (the characters in the game) speak Simlish through improvised, emotionally expressive gibberish rather than any real language, accompanied in the games by thought and speech bubbles bearing pictographic icons that indicate the topic of conversation.

Development

The language was devised during the development of the original The Sims (2000). Lead designer Will Wright and the development team wanted the characters to communicate without the expense and repetition problems of recording real dialogue in many languages. Voice actors Stephen Kearin and Gerri Lawlor improvised the first Simlish performances, drawing loosely on fragments of languages including Ukrainian, French, Latin, Finnish, English and Tagalog; later lore within the series even jokes that the language descends from the ramblings of an eccentric inventor.

Simlish has no fixed vocabulary or grammar; it is improvised gibberish shaped by emotion, intonation and context. A written form also appears in the games, using a playful alphabet of invented glyphs on books, signs and screens; dedicated fans have mapped these glyphs to Latin letters and use them to write messages.

In popular culture

Several real recording artists have re-recorded their songs in Simlish for inclusion in the games, a tradition that has included acts such as Katy Perry, Paramore, Depeche Mode, Lily Allen and My Chemical Romance. Familiar phrases recur across the series, such as the greeting "sul sul" and the farewell "dag dag". Simlish has become one of the most recognisable fictional languages in gaming, and its style of expressive non-verbal speech influenced later simulation and virtual-world games.
