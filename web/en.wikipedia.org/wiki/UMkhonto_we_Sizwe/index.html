uMkhonto weSizwe (Xhosa pronunciation: [um̩ˈkʰonto we ˈsizwe]; abbreviated MK; "Spear of the Nation") was the paramilitary wing of the African National Congress (ANC), founded by Nelson Mandela in the wake of the Sharpeville massacre of 21 March 1960. Its mission was to fight against the ruling National Party and its apartheid policies in South Africa.

Formation and sabotage campaign

After the ANC was banned in 1960 and the government refused to consider a negotiated settlement, Mandela and others concluded that continued adherence to non-violence alone would not end apartheid. MK was launched on 16 December 1961 with a series of sabotage attacks on government installations, power plants, and military targets, accompanied by a manifesto pledging to avoid loss of life. Mandela was arrested in 1962, and much of MK's leadership, including Walter Sisulu and Govan Mbeki, was captured in the Liliesleaf Farm raid of July 1963, leading to the Rivonia Trial.

Exile and armed struggle

During the 1970s and 1980s MK operated from bases in neighbouring African states, especially Angola and Zambia, training cadres and infiltrating guerrillas back into South Africa. Its campaigns included attacks on the Sasol oil refinery, the Koeberg nuclear power station, and military installations. Chris Hani became chief of staff in 1987. MK fighters also fought alongside Zimbabwean ZIPRA forces in the Wankie and Sipolilo campaigns of 1967–68.

Disbandment

The ANC suspended the armed struggle in 1990 as part of the negotiations to end apartheid, and MK was formally disbanded in 1991. In 1994, its remaining personnel were integrated into the new South African National Defence Force. In December 2021, the name was adopted by a splinter political veterans' association, and in December 2023 former president Jacob Zuma announced the formation of a new political party, uMkhonto weSizwe Party (MK Party), which contested the 2024 general election.
