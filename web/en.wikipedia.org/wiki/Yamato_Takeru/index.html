Yamato Takeru

Yamato Takeru
Japanese: ヤマトタケル
Era: Legendary (traditionally 1st–2nd century AD)
Father: Emperor Keikō
Known for: Legendary conquests; the sword Kusanagi

Yamato Takeru (ヤマトタケル), originally Prince Ōsu, is a legendary prince of the Yamato dynasty in Japanese mythology, son of the legendary Emperor Keikō. His exploits are recorded in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, and he became one of the archetypal tragic heroes of Japanese legend.

Legend

Fearing the prince's violent strength — he had killed his elder brother — Keikō sent him on dangerous campaigns instead. In the west he defeated the Kumaso chieftains, reportedly disguising himself as a woman at a banquet to assassinate their leader, from whom he received the name Yamato Takeru ("the brave of Yamato"). Sent east against the Emishi and other peoples, he received the sacred sword Kusanagi from his aunt Yamatohime at Ise; with it he escaped a fire trap by cutting the burning grass, an episode behind the sword's name ("grass cutter"). On his final campaign he offended the god of Mount Ibuki, fell ill, and died at Nobono in Ise; legend says his soul became a great white bird and flew away.

Legacy

Shrines dedicated to Yamato Takeru are found across Japan, and his story has been retold in Noh, kabuki, literature, and modern media. His figure embodies the hero whose strength and melancholy serve the imperial line.
