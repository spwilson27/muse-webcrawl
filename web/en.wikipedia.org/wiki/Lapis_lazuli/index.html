Lapis lazuli

Lapis lazuli is a deep-blue metamorphic rock used as a semi-precious stone that has been prized since antiquity for its intense colour. It is composed chiefly of the mineral lazurite, with calcite, pyrite (which gives golden flecks) and other minerals. The finest material comes from mines in the Badakhshan province of north-eastern Afghanistan, worked for more than 6,000 years.

Uses

Lapis was used in jewellery, seals, mosaics and sculpture in Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Indus Valley; the funeral mask of Tutankhamun is inlaid with it. Ground and purified, lapis provided the pigment ultramarine, the most expensive blue of medieval and Renaissance painting, reserved for the robes of the Virgin Mary and other important figures until synthetic ultramarine appeared in the nineteenth century.

Properties

Lapis has a Mohs hardness of about 5–6 and takes a good polish. Besides Afghanistan, it is mined in Chile and Russia, though Afghan material is generally considered the finest.
