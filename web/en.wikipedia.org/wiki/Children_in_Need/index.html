Children in Need

BBC Children in Need is the BBC's UK charity appeal, held annually since 1980. Its flagship event is a telethon broadcast each November on BBC One, which raises money for disadvantaged children and young people across the United Kingdom. The appeal's mascot is Pudsey Bear, a yellow teddy bear wearing a spotted bandage over one eye.

History

The BBC's first broadcast appeal for children took place in 1927, but the modern Children in Need telethon began in 1980, hosted for many years by Terry Wogan alongside presenters such as Sue Cook and Gaby Roslin. The first telethon raised about £1 million. Pudsey Bear was introduced in 1985, designed by Joanna Lane, and was named after her grandfather's home town of Pudsey in West Yorkshire.

The telethon

The annual show combines entertainment, musical performances, sketches from popular BBC programmes, and short films showing how donations are used. Fundraising events take place across the country, including sponsored activities, fancy dress, and the Countryfile Ramble. In 2016, following the death of long-time host Terry Wogan, the appeal paid tribute to his decades of involvement.

Distribution of funds

Money raised is distributed as grants to charities and community projects working with children facing poverty, disability, illness, distress, abuse, or neglect. Children in Need states that every penny of public donations goes to charitable projects, with administration costs covered by investment income and other sources.
