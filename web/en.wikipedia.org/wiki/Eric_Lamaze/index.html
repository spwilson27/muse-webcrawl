Eric Lamaze (born 17 April 1968) is a Canadian retired show jumper and Olympic champion. He won individual gold and team silver at the 2008 Beijing Olympics riding the celebrated stallion Hickstead, and individual bronze at the 2016 Rio Olympics. Together, Lamaze and Hickstead formed one of the most successful partnerships in the history of the sport.

Career

Lamaze grew up in Montreal and overcame a troubled youth to reach the top of equestrian sport. His career was interrupted by drug suspensions in the 1990s, but he returned to become Canadian champion repeatedly and a fixture on Nations Cup teams. With Hickstead, a Dutch Warmblood stallion owned by Torrey Pines Stable, he won the 2007 Pan American team silver and individual bronze, the 2008 Olympic titles, and the $1 million CN International at Spruce Meadows in 2007 and 2011.

Hickstead's death and later years

Hickstead collapsed and died of an aortic rupture after completing a round at Verona, Italy, in November 2011, a loss that shook the equestrian world. Lamaze continued competing with other horses, winning individual bronze at Rio 2016 aboard Fine Lady 5. He was diagnosed with a brain tumour in 2017 but continued to compete for several years before announcing his retirement from competition in 2022, moving into a chef d'équipe and coaching role with the Canadian team.
