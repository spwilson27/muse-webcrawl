Mississippi Valley-type deposit

Mississippi Valley-type (MVT) deposits are a major class of lead–zinc ore deposits hosted in carbonate (limestone and dolomite) sedimentary rocks. They are named for the classic districts of the Mississippi River valley in the United States, including the Tri-State district, southeast Missouri, and the Upper Mississippi Valley district.

MVT deposits form from warm (roughly 75–200 °C), highly saline basinal brines that migrate through aquifers and precipitate sphalerite and galena where they encounter reducing conditions, hydrogen sulfide, or structural and stratigraphic traps such as solution-collapse breccias and reef margins.

Characteristics

Common features include epigenetic (post-depositional) timing, low-temperature hydrothermal mineralogy (sphalerite, galena, dolomite, calcite, fluorite, barite, marcasite), an absence of associated igneous activity, and regional-scale fluid flow often linked to mountain-building (orogenic) events that drive brines from foreland basins into platform carbonates.

Examples

Notable MVT districts include Pine Point in Canada's Northwest Territories, the Lennard Shelf of Western Australia, the Irish Midlands, and Silesia in Poland. MVT and similar carbonate-hosted deposits have historically supplied a large share of the world's zinc and lead.
