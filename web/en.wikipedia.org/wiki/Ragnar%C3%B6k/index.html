Ragnarök

In Norse mythology, Ragnarök (Old Norse "fate of the gods") is a prophesied series of events — a great battle, natural disasters and the submersion of the world in water — in which many gods, including Odin, Thor, Týr, Freyr, Heimdall and Loki, will die. Afterwards the world resurfaces renewed and fertile, and surviving and returning gods meet; humanity is repopulated from two survivors, Líf and Lífþrasir.

Sources

The events are described chiefly in the Poetic Edda, compiled in the 13th century from earlier oral tradition — especially the poems Völuspá and Vafþrúðnismál — and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

The events

Signs include the Fimbulvetr, a winter lasting three seasons; the wolf Fenrir breaks free and devours Odin before being killed by Víðarr; the serpent Jörmungandr rises and is slain by Thor, who walks nine steps before dying from its venom; and the fire jötunn Surtr sets the world ablaze. The earth sinks into the sea and rises again, green.

Influence

Ragnarök has been a rich subject in art, literature, music and popular culture, from Wagner's Götterdämmerung — the German rendering of the name — to modern film and games.
