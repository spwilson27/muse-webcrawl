Jacobite rising of 1689

DateMarch 1689 – February 1692
LocationScotland
ResultGovernment victory
Jacobite commanderJohn Graham, 1st Viscount Dundee

The Jacobite rising of 1689 was an attempt to restore James VII of Scotland (James II of England), deposed in the Glorious Revolution of 1688, led in Scotland by John Graham of Claverhouse, Viscount Dundee. It was the first of the Jacobite risings.

Course of the rising

When the Convention of Estates in Edinburgh recognised William and Mary, Dundee withdrew and raised the Highlands for James. On 27 July 1689 his force defeated a government army under Hugh Mackay at the Battle of Killiecrankie, but Dundee himself was killed in the moment of victory. His successors were less capable: the Jacobites were checked at Dunkeld in August 1689 and decisively defeated at Cromdale in May 1690. Organised resistance ended, and in early 1692 the remaining chiefs took the oath of allegiance — the deadline whose enforcement led to the Massacre of Glencoe.

Aftermath

Although the rising failed, it established the pattern of later Jacobite attempts in 1715, 1719 and 1745, and led the government to build roads and garrisons, including Fort William, to control the Highlands.
