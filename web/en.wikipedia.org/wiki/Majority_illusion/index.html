Majority illusion

The majority illusion is a phenomenon in social networks in which individuals systematically overestimate how common an attribute or behaviour is among the population, because the attribute appears prevalent within their local neighbourhood of the network even though it is rare globally.


In some networks, a minority attribute can appear to almost every node as if held by the majority of its neighbours.

Description

The effect was described by Kristina Lerman, Xiaoran Yan and Xin-Zeng Wu in 2016. It arises when a small number of high-degree nodes (hubs) possess an attribute: because hubs are connected to many others, most nodes see the attribute among many of their contacts and infer it is widespread, although only a small fraction of the whole population has it.

The majority illusion is related to the friendship paradox — the observation that most people have fewer friends than their friends have — and can help explain the rapid spread of social norms, fashions, rumours and misinformation, as well as pluralistic ignorance in online communities.
