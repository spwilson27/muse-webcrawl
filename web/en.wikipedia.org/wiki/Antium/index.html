Antium

Antium was an ancient coastal city in Latium, Italy, corresponding to the modern towns of Anzio and Nettuno, about 58 km south of Rome. Originally a stronghold of the Volsci, it was long a thorn in Rome's side before being conquered in 338 BC, after which its warships' beaks (rostra) were carried to Rome to adorn the speakers' platform in the Forum.

History

Under the Empire, Antium became a fashionable resort of villas; both Caligula and Nero were born there, and Nero built a celebrated villa and harbour on the site. Famous artworks found at Antium include the Apollo Belvedere (attributed to the area) and the Farnese Bull. The city declined in late antiquity as malaria and Saracen raids emptied the coast, and its population eventually regrouped at Nettuno.

Modern site

The modern port of Anzio, founded under Innocent XII in the 17th century, was the site of the Allied amphibious landings of January 1944 (Operation Shingle) during the Second World War.
