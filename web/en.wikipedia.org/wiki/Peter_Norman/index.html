Peter George Norman (15 June 1942 – 3 October 2006) was an Australian track athlete. He won the silver medal in the 200 metres at the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico City in a time of 20.06 seconds, which still stands as the Australian national record. He is best known for his role in the famous 1968 Olympics Black Power salute.

1968 Olympics protest

In the 200 m final on 16 October 1968, Norman finished second between Americans Tommie Smith (gold) and John Carlos (bronze). On the medal podium, Smith and Carlos raised black-gloved fists and stood shoeless in protest against racial injustice in the United States. Norman, who knew of the plan and supported it, wore an Olympic Project for Human Rights badge on his tracksuit and stood with the two Americans; it was Norman who suggested that Smith and Carlos share the one pair of gloves they had when Carlos forgot his.

Aftermath

Smith and Carlos were expelled from the Games, and Norman was also ostracised by Australian sporting authorities despite his record performance; he was not selected for the 1972 Munich Olympics even though he had run qualifying times many times over, and he received no invitation to take part in the 2000 Sydney Olympics ceremonies. He continued competing, worked as a teacher and coach, and later struggled with injuries and depression.

Recognition

Norman's reputation was rehabilitated over time. Smith and Carlos remained close friends with him and were pallbearers at his funeral in 2006; a statue of the podium protest at San Jose State University includes Norman's place at his own insistence that visitors can stand in his position. In 2012 the Australian Parliament formally apologised to Norman, and in 2018 the Australian Olympic Committee posthumously awarded him the Order of Merit.
