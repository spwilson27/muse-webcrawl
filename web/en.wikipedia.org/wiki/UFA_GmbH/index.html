UFA GmbH, shortened from Universum-Film Aktiengesellschaft, is a German film and television production company. Founded in 1917 during the First World War with covert government backing, it became one of the most important film studios in the world during the Weimar Republic, and today is a major producer of German television as part of Fremantle.

Weimar era

UFA was created by consolidating several German film companies, partly to counter Allied propaganda films. In the 1920s its Babelsberg studios produced masterpieces of German Expressionist and Weimar cinema, including The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, F. W. Murnau's The Last Laugh, Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927) and M, and The Blue Angel (1930), which made Marlene Dietrich a star. The near-bankrupting cost of Metropolis led to a rescue by the nationalist press magnate Alfred Hugenberg in 1927.

Nazi era and aftermath

After 1933 the studio was progressively brought under state control and in 1942 was fully nationalised as part of UFA-Film GmbH (UFI), serving the propaganda apparatus of Joseph Goebbels. After the Second World War the Babelsberg studios passed to the East German state studio DEFA, while a revived UFA operated in the West. In 1964 Bertelsmann acquired the company.

Modern company

Today's UFA, headquartered in Potsdam, is among Germany's largest television producers, making series, serial dramas, entertainment formats and feature films through units such as UFA Fiction, UFA Serial Drama and UFA Show & Factual.
