Shwebo

Shwebo (Burmese: ရွှေဘိုမြို့) is a city in Sagaing Region, Myanmar. It is located 113 km northwest of Mandalay on the eastern bank of the Chindwin River. Shwebo was the royal capital of Burma from 1752 to 1760 during the early Konbaung dynasty, founded by King Alaungpaya, who was born in the village of Moksobo on the site.

History

Shwebo began as the village of Moksobo, home of the headman Aung Zeya, who in 1752 proclaimed himself King Alaungpaya and founded the Konbaung dynasty after resisting the Mon kingdom of Hanthawaddy. He renamed his capital with five auspicious names, of which Shwebo ("Golden Chief") became standard. Alaungpaya built a palace, city walls, and a moat, traces of which remain.

Alaungpaya unified Burma by 1757 but died in 1760; his son Naungdawgyi briefly reigned from Shwebo before the capital moved to Sagaing and then Ava. The city remained a significant regional town. Shwebo's surrounding plain was historically an important rice-growing and irrigation region, served by canals from the Mu River.

Geography and economy

Shwebo lies in the dry zone of central Myanmar between the Irrawaddy and Chindwin rivers. It is a trading and administrative centre for the surrounding agricultural district, known for rice, beans, and sesame. The Shwebo–Myitkyina road and rail links pass through the town. Nearby attractions include the reconstructed Shwebo Palace, Alaungpaya's tomb, and pagodas of the Konbaung period.

1947 incident

The city is also associated with nearby Kyaukmyaung and the wider Sagaing area's role in modern Myanmar history; the region suffered considerable fighting during the Second World War and again during recent conflicts following the 2021 military coup.
