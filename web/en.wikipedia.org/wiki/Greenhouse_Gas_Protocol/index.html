The Greenhouse Gas Protocol (GHG Protocol) is a set of global standardized frameworks to measure and manage greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from private and public sector operations, value chains, products, cities, and policies. It is the world's most widely used greenhouse gas accounting standard.

The GHG Protocol was developed through a partnership between the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD), convening representatives from businesses, governments, and environmental groups around the world.

Corporate Standard

The GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard, first published in 2001 and revised in 2004, provides requirements and guidance for companies and other organizations preparing a corporate-level GHG emissions inventory. It classifies emissions into three "scopes":

  • Scope 1 – direct emissions from owned or controlled sources (e.g., fuel combustion, company vehicles).
  • Scope 2 – indirect emissions from purchased energy, such as electricity, steam, heating, and cooling.
  • Scope 3 – all other indirect emissions in the value chain, both upstream and downstream, including business travel, purchased goods, and use of sold products.

Other standards

The Protocol's suite of standards includes the GHG Protocol for Project Accounting (2005), the Product Life Cycle Accounting and Reporting Standard (2011), the Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Accounting and Reporting Standard (2011), the Global Protocol for Community-Scale Greenhouse Gas Emission Inventories (GPC, 2014), and the Policy and Action Standard. Guidance on land sector and removals accounting has also been developed.

Use

GHG Protocol standards form the basis for many other initiatives, including the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi), the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), CDP reporting, and ISO 14064. In 2016, 92% of Fortune 500 companies responding to CDP used the GHG Protocol directly or indirectly.
