Thonburi Kingdom

The Thonburi Kingdom was a Siamese kingdom that existed from 1767 to 1782, founded by King Taksin the Great after the destruction of the Ayutthaya Kingdom by Burmese invaders. Its capital, Thonburi, lay on the west bank of the Chao Phraya River opposite modern Bangkok.

Founding

After the fall of Ayutthaya in April 1767, the general Phraya Tak gathered followers, seized Chanthaburi, and liberated the Chao Phraya basin within months. Crowned king on 28 December 1767, he chose Thonburi as capital because of its access to the sea and defensible position.

Reunification and expansion

Taksin spent most of his fifteen-year reign campaigning. He defeated rival regional lords, reunified Siam by 1770, and extended Siamese power over Lanna (Chiang Mai, taken from Burma in 1774–1775), the Lao kingdoms of Vientiane and Luang Prabang (1778), and Cambodia, making Siam the dominant mainland Southeast Asian power. He promoted trade with Qing China, encouraged immigration, revived Buddhism and the arts, and compiled the Traiphum cosmology.

End of the kingdom

In his later years Taksin's behaviour became erratic; in 1782 a rebellion led to his deposition and execution. His general Chao Phraya Chakri ascended the throne as Rama I, founded the Chakri dynasty, and moved the capital across the river to Bangkok, beginning the Rattanakosin era.
