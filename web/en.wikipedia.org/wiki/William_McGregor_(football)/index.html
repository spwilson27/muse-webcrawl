William McGregor (football)

Born: 13 April 1846, Braco, Perthshire, Scotland
Died: 20 December 1911, Birmingham, England
Occupation: Football administrator; draper
Known for: Founding the Football League (1888)

William McGregor (13 April 1846 – 20 December 1911) was a Scottish association football administrator and a director of Aston Villa, best known as the founder of the Football League in 1888, the world's first national football league competition.

Background

McGregor moved from Perthshire to Birmingham, where he ran a draper's shop near Aston Villa's ground. He became involved in the club's administration and served on the Football Association's committee, becoming an influential figure in the professionalisation of the game in the Midlands.

Founding the League

Frustrated by the fixture chaos caused by cup ties and friendlies being cancelled at short notice, McGregor wrote to leading professional clubs in March 1888 proposing a league of clubs playing each other home and away on a fixed schedule, modelled partly on American baseball leagues. The Football League was founded that year with twelve member clubs, Aston Villa among them, and its format spread worldwide.

Legacy

McGregor served as the League's president and chairman and is honoured by a statue outside Villa Park. He is often called the father of league football.
