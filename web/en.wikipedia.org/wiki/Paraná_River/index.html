The Paraná River (Spanish: Río Paraná; Portuguese: Rio Paraná) is a river in south-central South America, running through Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina for some 4,880 kilometres (3,030 mi). Among South American rivers, it is second in length only to the Amazon River. It merges with the Paraguay River and then farther downstream with the Uruguay River to form the Río de la Plata and empties into the Atlantic Ocean.

The name Paraná is an abbreviation of the phrase para rehe onáva, which comes from the Tupi language and means "like the sea" (that is, "as big as the sea").

Course

The course is formed at the confluence of the Grande and Paranaíba rivers in southern Brazil. From there it flows generally south, forming the border between Brazil and Paraguay, then between Paraguay and Argentina. Along its upper course the Paraná passes through the Itaipu Dam, one of the largest hydroelectric power stations in the world, shared between Brazil and Paraguay.

The river receives the Paraguay River at Paso de la Patria, Argentina, and continues south through the Argentine Mesopotamia region, passing the cities of Corrientes, Paraná, and Rosario, before reaching the Río de la Plata estuary near Buenos Aires.

Uses

Together with its tributaries, the Paraná forms a massive drainage basin that covers much of the south central part of the continent. The river is heavily used for commercial navigation, hydroelectric generation (including the Yacyretá Dam), fisheries, and irrigation.

Ecology

The Paraná supports one of the most productive freshwater ecosystems in the world, including the Iberá Wetlands and the vast Paraná Delta, which extends over some 14,000 km² before the river reaches the Río de la Plata.
