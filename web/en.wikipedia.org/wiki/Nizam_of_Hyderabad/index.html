Nizam of Hyderabad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Mir Osman Ali Khan, the last reigning Nizam

The Nizams were the rulers of Hyderabad State, the largest and wealthiest princely state in India, from 1724 until 1948. The title Nizam derives from the Arabic Niẓām, "order" or "administrator", from the honorific Nizam-ul-Mulk ("governor of the realm"). The dynasty, known as the Asaf Jahi dynasty, was founded by Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan, a Mughal viceroy of the Deccan who asserted de facto independence from the Mughal Empire in 1724.

The dynasty

  1. Asaf Jah I (Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan), 1724–1748
  2. Nasir Jung, 1748–1750
  3. Muzaffar Jung, 1750–1751
  4. Salabat Jung, 1751–1762
  5. Asaf Jah II, 1762–1802
  6. Asaf Jah III, 1803–1829
  7. Asaf Jah IV, 1829–1857
  8. Asaf Jah V, 1857–1869
  9. Asaf Jah VI (Mahbub Ali Khan), 1869–1911
  10. Asaf Jah VII (Mir Osman Ali Khan), 1911–1948

Under British paramountcy

Hyderabad entered a subsidiary alliance with the British East India Company in 1798 and remained a loyal ally through the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and both world wars, its ruler styled "Faithful Ally of the British Crown". The state covered some 215,000 km² — roughly the size of Great Britain — with a population of over 16 million. The seventh Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan, was reputed to be among the richest men in the world, famed for the Jacob Diamond and for patronising institutions such as Osmania University.

End of the state

At the partition of India in 1947, the Nizam sought independence or accession on special terms rather than joining India. The standoff, a communist-led peasant uprising in Telangana, and communal violence ended with the Indian Army's "police action", Operation Polo, in September 1948, which annexed Hyderabad to the Indian Union. Mir Osman Ali Khan served as Rajpramukh (governor) of the successor state until 1956, and the princely order was formally abolished in 1971.
