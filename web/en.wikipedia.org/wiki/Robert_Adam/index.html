Robert Adam (3 July 1728 – 3 March 1792) was a British neoclassical architect, interior designer, and furniture designer. He was a son of the Scottish architect William Adam and younger brother of John Adam. Working also with his younger brother James Adam, he developed the Adam style, a light, elegant interpretation of classical architecture and interiors that became dominant in Britain and influenced design in Europe and North America in the late 18th century.

Life and career

Born in Kirkcaldy, Fife, Adam was educated at the Royal High School and the University of Edinburgh. In 1754 he embarked on a Grand Tour of France and Italy, studying classical ruins and working with the French architect Charles-Louis Clérisseau and the artist Giovanni Battista Piranesi. Returning to Britain in 1758, he set up practice in London with his brothers and won major commissions for country houses, including Harewood House, Kedleston Hall, and Syon House, where his redesigned interiors became famous.

From 1768 the brothers developed the Adelphi, a speculative terrace of houses on the Thames in London; financial difficulties with the scheme were resolved by a lottery authorized by Parliament in 1774. Adam published The Works in Architecture of Robert and James Adam (1773–1779) to promote their designs.

The Adam style

The Adam style unified architecture, interior decoration, and furniture into a single harmonious scheme. It drew on Roman, Etruscan, and Renaissance sources, using delicate classical motifs—urns, swags, griffins, and grotesques—in shallow relief and pastel colour schemes. Adam's interiors at Kedleston, Syon, Osterley, and Culzean Castle, and public works such as Register House in Edinburgh and the University of Edinburgh's Old College, exemplify the style. He is regarded as the leading British architect of the later 18th century.
