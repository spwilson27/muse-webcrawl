Pileus (mycology)

The pileus is the technical name for the cap, or cap-like part, of a basidiocarp or ascocarp (fungal fruiting body) that supports a spore-bearing surface, the hymenium. It is characteristic of agarics (gilled mushrooms), boletes, and some polypores and tooth fungi.

Morphology

Pileus shape is an important identification feature in mycology and is described by terms such as campanulate (bell-shaped), conical, convex, depressed, flat, infundibuliform (funnel-shaped), umbonate (with a central bump), and umbilicate (with a central depression). The cap surface may be smooth, scaly, fibrillose, or viscid, and its margin may be striate or inrolled when young.

Function

The pileus protects the developing hymenium on its underside — gills, pores, or teeth — from rain and desiccation and elevates the spores for effective dispersal by wind once released.
