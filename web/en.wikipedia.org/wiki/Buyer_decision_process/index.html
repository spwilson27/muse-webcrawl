Buyer decision process

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The buyer decision process (also called the consumer decision-making process) describes the stages a consumer passes through when making a purchase. The classic five-stage model, widely taught in marketing, comprises need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase decision, and post-purchase behaviour.

The five stages

Need recognition occurs when a buyer perceives a difference between their actual state and a desired state. In the information search stage, the buyer gathers information from personal, commercial, public, and experiential sources. Evaluation of alternatives involves weighing attributes and beliefs about competing brands. The purchase decision may still be altered by the attitudes of others and by unexpected situational factors. Finally, post-purchase behaviour determines satisfaction, repeat purchase, and word of mouth.

Variations

Not every purchase passes through all stages: routine, low-involvement purchases may skip stages, while high-involvement and organisational buying decisions involve more participants and formalised steps. Marketers use the model to target communications at each stage of the journey.
