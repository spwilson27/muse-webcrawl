Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Seoul

Archdiocese of Seoul
Established: 1831 (vicariate apostolic); 1962 (archdiocese)
Cathedral: Myeongdong Cathedral
Country: South Korea

The Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Seoul (Latin: Archidioecesis Seulensis) is a metropolitan archdiocese of the Latin Church of the Catholic Church in South Korea. The archdiocese is centred on Seoul and includes parts of the capital region; its cathedral is Myeongdong Cathedral, dedicated to Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception.

History

Catholicism reached Korea in the late 18th century through Korean envoys to Beijing who encountered Jesuit missionaries and brought back Christian texts. The Vicariate Apostolic of Korea was erected in 1831 under the Paris Foreign Missions Society, and Korean Christians endured repeated persecutions during the 19th century, producing many martyrs who were canonised by Pope John Paul II in 1984. The vicariate became the Archdiocese of Seoul in 1962, when the Korean hierarchy was formally established.

Territory and leadership

The archdiocese is the metropolitan see of an ecclesiastical province with several suffragan dioceses in northern South Korea. Its archbishop, who also traditionally serves as apostolic administrator of Pyongyang, has frequently been a prominent public voice in South Korean society; Cardinal Stephen Kim Sou-hwan, the first Korean cardinal, served as archbishop from 1968 to 1998.
