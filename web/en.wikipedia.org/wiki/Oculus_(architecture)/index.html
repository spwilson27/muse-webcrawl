Oculus (architecture)

An oculus (Latin for "eye"; plural oculi) is a circular opening in the center of a dome or in a wall. Originating in antiquity, it is a feature of Byzantine and Neoclassical architecture. It is also known as an oeil-de-boeuf from the French, or simply a "bull's-eye window".

History

The oculus was used by the Romans, one of the best known being that in the dome of the Pantheon in Rome. Open to the weather, it allows rain and air to enter and fall to the floor, where it is carried away by drains. Though often intended as a sun, in the Pantheon it also serves as a cooling and ventilation method.

Uses

Oculi have been used in architecture from antiquity through Neoclassical revivals. In domes they reduce weight and admit light. In later periods they became decorative elements in walls and pediments.
