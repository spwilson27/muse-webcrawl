Oskar Ryszard Lange (27 July 1904 – 2 October 1965) was a Polish economist and diplomat. He is best known for advocating the use of market pricing mechanisms in socialist systems of economic allocation, a position he set out most fully in his essay On the Economic Theory of Socialism (1936–1937). Along with Abba Lerner, he is associated with the market-socialist model often called the Lange–Lerner solution to the economic calculation problem.

Early life and education

Lange was born in Tomaszów Mazowiecki, then part of Russian-ruled Congress Poland, the son of a textile manufacturer of German descent. He studied law and economics at the Jagiellonian University in Kraków, where he received his doctorate in 1928. From 1931 to 1935 he lectured at Kraków, and in 1934 he travelled to the United States on a Rockefeller Foundation fellowship, working at Harvard and other universities.

Career in the United States

Unable to return safely to Poland as war approached, Lange remained in America, teaching at the University of Michigan and then, from 1938, at the University of Chicago, where he became a professor of economics. He took United States citizenship in 1943. During this period he wrote extensively on price theory, monetary economics, and econometrics, including Price Flexibility and Employment (1944), and engaged in a celebrated debate with Friedrich Hayek and Ludwig von Mises over whether rational economic calculation was possible under socialism.

Lange's proposal was that a central planning board could simulate a market by setting prices parametrically and adjusting them in response to shortages and surpluses, while managers of publicly owned firms followed rules to produce at minimum cost and to equate price with marginal cost.

Return to Poland

At the end of the Second World War, Lange broke with the Polish government-in-exile in London and supported the Soviet-backed administration in Warsaw. He renounced his American citizenship and returned to Poland in 1945, serving as ambassador to the United States and as Poland's delegate to the United Nations Security Council. He later became a deputy chairman of the Polish Council of State and chairman of the Economic Council (1957–1962), and a professor at the University of Warsaw, where he helped re-establish Polish economics after Stalinism.

Later work and death

In his later years Lange wrote on cybernetics and systems theory, arguing in Wholes and Parts (1962) that systems concepts of feedback could unify economic analysis. His multi-volume Political Economy remained unfinished. Lange died in London on 2 October 1965 while undergoing medical treatment. One of Poland's leading economics universities, the Wrocław University of Economics, now bears his name.
