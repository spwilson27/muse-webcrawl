Blackford Hill

Blackford Hill is a hill in Edinburgh, Scotland, rising to about 164 metres (538 feet) south of the city centre, between Blackford, Morningside, and the Braid Hills. It is topped by the Royal Observatory, Edinburgh, and forms part of the Blackford Hill and Hermitage of Braid Local Nature Reserve, offering panoramic views over the city to Arthur's Seat and the Firth of Forth.

Geography and nature

The hill's crags are of volcanic origin, sculpted by ice moving eastward, leaving a crag-and-tail form. At its foot lies Blackford Pond, a former quarry turned ornamental pond popular for waterfowl. The adjoining Hermitage of Braid follows the Braid Burn through wooded gorge, and the combined reserve supports gorse scrub, woodland, and grassland wildlife. An ancient hill fort site and a Roman-era find, as well as the Agassiz Rock — where Louis Agassiz identified glacial striations in 1840, key evidence for ice-age theory — are among its features.

Royal Observatory

The Royal Observatory moved to Blackford Hill from Calton Hill in the 1890s, its twin domes becoming a landmark. It remains home to the UK Astronomy Technology Centre and the Institute for Astronomy of the University of Edinburgh, with a visitor centre and public astronomy evenings.

Recreation

Paths from Blackford, Morningside, and the Hermitage connect the summit with the Braid Hills golf courses and Braidburn Valley Park. The hill is used by walkers, runners, and orienteering clubs.
