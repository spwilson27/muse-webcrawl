A matador (Spanish for "killer") is the principal performer in a bullfight, or corrida de toros, who works closest to the bull and is responsible for killing it with a sword thrust in the final stage of the fight. The full title is matador de toros, "killer of bulls". A matador is also known more generally as a torero, although strictly speaking that term covers all the participants in the bullfight who face the bull in the ring.

Training and career

Matadors typically begin their careers as novilleros, fighting younger and smaller bulls called novillos. After a period of apprenticeship and a required number of novilladas, a novillero may take the alternativa, a formal ceremony in which a senior matador presents him with the muleta and estoque (sword), graduating him to the rank of matador de toros. The ceremony is later confirmed in Madrid's Las Ventas ring for Spanish matadors seeking full recognition.

The fight

The corrida is divided into three parts, or tercios. The matador first observes and studies the bull, then works with the large cape (capote) during the opening passes. In the final tercio, the tercio de muerte, the matador performs a faena with the smaller red cloth (muleta) draped over the sword, drawing the bull into a series of passes before delivering the estocada, the sword thrust intended to kill the bull cleanly between the shoulder blades.

Costume

The matador wears the traje de luces, the "suit of lights", a richly embroidered silk costume adorned with sequins and gold or silver thread. The outfit includes a short jacket, a waistcoat, knee breeches, pink stockings, flat shoes, and the montera, a distinctive two-pointed hat. The costume is traditionally handmade and can be extremely costly.

Famous matadors

Celebrated matadors include Juan Belmonte, credited with revolutionising the art by standing still and working the bull close; Manolete, who was killed by the bull Islero in Linares in 1947; and El Cordobés, the flamboyant star of the 1960s. Women matadors, such as Cristina Sánchez, have also achieved prominence despite historical bans on female bullfighters in Spain.
