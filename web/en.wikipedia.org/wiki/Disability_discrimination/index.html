Disability discrimination

Disability discrimination occurs when a person is treated less favourably, or put at a disadvantage, because of their disability. It can occur in employment, education, access to goods and services, housing, and transport. Many jurisdictions have enacted laws prohibiting such discrimination and requiring reasonable accommodations or adjustments.

Forms of discrimination

Disability discrimination may take several forms, including:

  • Direct discrimination — treating a disabled person worse than others because of the disability itself.
  • Indirect discrimination — a policy or practice that applies to everyone but disproportionately disadvantages disabled people.
  • Failure to make reasonable adjustments — not removing barriers, such as providing step-free access or auxiliary aids.
  • Harassment and victimisation related to disability.

Legislation

In the United States, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA) prohibits discrimination in employment, public services, and public accommodations. In the United Kingdom, the Equality Act 2010 consolidated earlier laws including the Disability Discrimination Act 1995. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006) sets international standards.
