At San Quentin

At San Quentin
Artist: Johnny Cash
Released: June 16, 1969
Recorded: February 24, 1969
Venue: San Quentin State Prison, California
Label: Columbia

At San Quentin is the second live prison album by American singer Johnny Cash, released on Columbia Records on June 16, 1969. Recorded before an audience of inmates at California's San Quentin State Prison on February 24, 1969, it was Cash's second concept album recorded in a prison, following the success of At Folsom Prison (1968).

The album was certified gold in August 1969, platinum and double platinum in November 1986, and triple platinum in March 2003 by the RIAA. It was nominated for a number of Grammy Awards, including Album of the Year, and won Best Male Country Vocal Performance for "A Boy Named Sue".

Recording

The concert was filmed by Britain's Granada Television, and the famous photograph of Cash giving the middle finger to the camera — actually aimed in jest at the film crew — comes from this show, though it only became iconic decades later. Cash performed several songs that he had not released before, including "San Quentin" itself, which he wrote for the occasion and performed twice at the inmates' request, and Shel Silverstein's comic novelty song "A Boy Named Sue", which became Cash's biggest pop hit, reaching number two on the Billboard Hot 100.

Legacy

At San Quentin spent 20 weeks at number one on the Billboard country chart and four weeks at number one on the pop albums chart, becoming one of the best-selling live albums of its era. Together with its predecessor, it cemented Cash's image as the "Man in Black" championing prison reform.
