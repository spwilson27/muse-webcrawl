Mumming

Mumming is the practice of performing traditional folk plays, known as mummers' plays, by troupes of amateur actors, traditionally all male, who visit houses and pubs during festive seasons such as Christmas, New Year, Plough Monday and Easter. Mumming is found across the British Isles and in communities settled from Britain, including Newfoundland, where the custom of "mummering" or "janneying" — visiting neighbours in disguise — survives.

The plays

Although texts vary locally, most mummers' plays follow a hero-combat pattern: a champion such as Saint George (or King George) fights and kills an opponent, often the Turkish Knight or Bold Slasher, whereupon a comic Doctor enters and revives the slain man with a magic cure. Other stock figures include Father Christmas, who introduces the play, and characters such as Beelzebub and Little Devil Doubt who collect money from the audience. Performers often wear costumes made of ribbons, rags or strips of paper, and blacken or disguise their faces.

Origins and survival

The documented history of the plays reaches back to the 18th century in their surviving form, though disguised seasonal visiting customs are much older; claims of pagan or medieval origins are common but difficult to substantiate. Mumming declined in the 20th century but was revived by folk enthusiasts, and teams now perform across England, Ireland and beyond. In Philadelphia, the New Year's Day Mummers Parade, with its elaborate string bands, is a famous descendant of related traditions.
