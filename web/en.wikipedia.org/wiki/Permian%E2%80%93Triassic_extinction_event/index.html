Permian–Triassic extinction event

The Permian–Triassic extinction event, colloquially the Great Dying, was Earth's most severe mass extinction, occurring about 251.9 million years ago at the boundary between the Permian and Triassic periods.

Permian–Triassic extinction
Date: ~251.9 million years ago
Severity: ~81–90% of marine species lost
Leading cause: Siberian Traps volcanism

Scale

Estimates suggest roughly 81% (older estimates up to 96%) of marine species and about 70% of terrestrial vertebrate species went extinct, along with the last trilobites and many insect groups. Recovery of ecosystems took millions of years — far longer than after other mass extinctions.

Causes

The leading explanation is massive volcanism from the Siberian Traps flood basalts, releasing carbon dioxide and methane that drove extreme global warming, ocean anoxia and acidification, and possibly ozone destruction. Coal combustion by intruding sills and methane release may have amplified warming to equatorial sea temperatures near 40°C.
