Corinth is a city in and the county seat of Alcorn County, Mississippi, United States. It lies in the northeastern corner of the state, near the Tennessee border, and has a population of roughly 15,000. The city was founded in 1853 as Cross City, so named because it was the crossing point of the Mobile & Ohio and Memphis & Charleston railroads; it was soon renamed Corinth after the Greek city that likewise sat at a crossroads of trade routes.

Civil War history

Because of its two railroads, Corinth was one of the most strategically important points in the western theater of the American Civil War. After the Battle of Shiloh in April 1862, Union forces under Major General Henry Halleck advanced slowly on the city in the Siege of Corinth; Confederate General P. G. T. Beauregard evacuated Corinth at the end of May 1862, faking reinforcements with empty trains and cheering troops to mask the withdrawal. In October 1862, Confederate forces under Earl Van Dorn attacked the Union garrison in the Second Battle of Corinth but were repelled with heavy losses. The Corinth National Cemetery and the Shiloh National Military Park's Corinth unit preserve this history; the city contains one of the best-preserved collections of Civil War earthworks in the country.

Geography and economy

Corinth sits at the junction of U.S. Routes 45 and 72. Its economy has historically included manufacturing, rail transport, and distribution; a Caterpillar remanufacturing facility has been a major employer. The downtown historic district retains many nineteenth-century buildings, and the Crossroads Museum documents the region's railroad and Civil War heritage.
