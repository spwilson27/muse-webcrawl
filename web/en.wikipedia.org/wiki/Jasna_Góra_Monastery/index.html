Jasna Góra Monastery (Polish: Jasna Góra, "Bright Mount") in Częstochowa, Poland, is a famous Polish shrine to the Virgin Mary and one of the country's places of pilgrimage. The image of the Black Madonna of Częstochowa, also known as Our Lady of Częstochowa, to which miraculous powers are attributed, is one of Jasna Góra's most precious treasures.

History

The monastery was founded in 1382 by Pauline monks who came from Hungary at the invitation of Władysław, Duke of Opole. According to tradition, the icon of the Black Madonna was brought to Jasna Góra in 1384. In 1430 the shrine was attacked and plundered by Hussite raiders, during which the icon was damaged — the slashes on the Madonna's cheek date from this attack.

The Deluge

In 1655, during the Swedish invasion of Poland known as the Deluge, the small garrison of the fortress-monastery successfully resisted a Swedish siege. The defence was credited to the protection of the Virgin Mary, and in 1656 King John II Casimir proclaimed Our Lady of Częstochowa Queen of Poland. The event became a lasting symbol of Polish national resistance.

Pilgrimage

Jasna Góra is one of the world's most visited Catholic shrines, receiving several million pilgrims a year, many of whom walk in organised pilgrimages to Częstochowa for the Feast of the Assumption on 15 August.
