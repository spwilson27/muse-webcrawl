Estadio Centenario is a stadium in the Parque Batlle neighbourhood of Montevideo, Uruguay, used primarily for association football. It was built between 1929 and 1930 to host the inaugural 1930 FIFA World Cup, and to commemorate the centenary of Uruguay's first constitution. It is listed by FIFA as one of the football world's classic stadiums.

History

The stadium was inaugurated on 18 July 1930 with a World Cup group match in which Uruguay defeated Peru 1–0. Twelve days later it staged the World Cup Final, in which Uruguay beat Argentina 4–2 to become the first world champions. Its most recognisable feature is the Torre de los Homenajes (Tower of Homage), an Art Deco tower rising above the main stand, designed by architect Juan Antonio Scasso.

The Centenario has hosted four editions of the Copa América (1942, 1956, 1967 and 1995), all of which Uruguay won, reinforcing the stadium's legendary status in Uruguayan football culture. It has also been the traditional home ground of the Uruguay national team and a frequent venue for the Montevideo clubs Peñarol and Nacional.

Museum

Beneath the Olympic stand, the stadium houses the Museo del Fútbol Uruguayo, opened in 1975, which displays trophies, photographs and memorabilia relating to Uruguay's Olympic gold medals of 1924 and 1928 and its World Cup triumphs of 1930 and 1950.
