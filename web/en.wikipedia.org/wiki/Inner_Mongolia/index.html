Inner Mongolia

The Hulunbuir grassland in northeastern Inner Mongolia

Inner Mongolia, officially the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, is an autonomous region of the People's Republic of China, located in the north of the country along its border with Mongolia and Russia. Its capital is Hohhot, and its largest city is Baotou. With an area of about 1.18 million square kilometres, it is the third-largest provincial-level subdivision of China, stretching in a long arc from the Greater Khingan mountains in the northeast to the deserts of the Alxa League in the west. Its population is about 24 million, of whom ethnic Mongols form roughly a sixth; Han Chinese are the majority.

Geography

Much of Inner Mongolia is a high plateau, averaging over 1,000 metres in elevation, covered by the grasslands for which the region is famous—among them the Hulunbuir, Xilingol and Ordos steppes—while deserts including the Badain Jaran, Tengger and parts of the Gobi occupy the west. The Yellow River makes a great bend through the fertile Hetao plain, an irrigated agricultural zone, and the Greater Khingan range in the east carries extensive forests. The climate is continental and generally dry, with cold winters and strong winds that make the region a major source of wind power.

History

The region formed part of the Mongol Empire of Genghis Khan and later of the Yuan dynasty; its name derives from the Manchu and Chinese administrative usage distinguishing the southern Mongol lands, brought under Qing rule in the 17th century, from "Outer" Mongolia to the north. Large-scale Han settlement followed in the late Qing and Republican periods. The autonomous region was established in 1947, two years before the People's Republic itself, under the Mongol communist leader Ulanhu.

Economy

Inner Mongolia is a leading producer of coal, rare earth elements (from the Bayan Obo mine), natural gas and cashmere, and has major dairy companies such as Yili and Mengniu. Grassland livestock farming, energy and heavy industry drive its economy, while tourism draws visitors to the steppes, the mausoleum of Genghis Khan in Ordos, and desert landscapes.
