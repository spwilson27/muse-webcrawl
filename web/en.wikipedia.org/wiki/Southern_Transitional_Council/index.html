Southern Transitional Council

Southern Transitional Council
Arabic: المجلس الانتقالي الجنوبي
Founded: 11 May 2017
Headquarters: Aden, Yemen
President: Aidarus al-Zoubaidi
Aim: restoration of an independent South Yemen

The Southern Transitional Council (STC; Arabic: المجلس الانتقالي الجنوبي, al-Majlis al-Intiqālī al-Janūbī) is a political organization and governing body in southern Yemen. Formed in 2017, it seeks the restoration of an independent state in the territory of the former People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (South Yemen), which unified with North Yemen in 1990. The STC is a major party to the Yemeni civil war and has administered Aden and other southern areas, in alliance — at times strained — with the internationally recognized Yemeni government.

Formation and history

The STC was announced on 11 May 2017 by Aidarus al-Zoubaidi, the former governor of Aden, after President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi dismissed him. It drew on the Southern Movement (al-Hirak), which had agitated for southern autonomy or independence since 2007, and was backed by the United Arab Emirates, which trained and equipped its affiliated Security Belt and Southern Armed Forces.

In January 2018 and again in August 2019, STC-aligned forces clashed with government troops and seized control of Aden. The Saudi-brokered Riyadh Agreement (November 2019) provided for power-sharing, and in April 2022 the STC joined the new Presidential Leadership Council, with al-Zoubaidi as a vice-president, though the Council continued to assert its separatist goal and its separate military command.
