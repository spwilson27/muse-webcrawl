Track bicycle

A track bicycle or track bike is a bicycle optimized for racing at a velodrome or an outdoor track. Unlike road bicycles, track bikes have a single fixed gear and no freewheel — the pedals turn whenever the rear wheel turns — and they have no brakes, since braking is unnecessary and potentially dangerous in a closely packed field; riders slow by resisting the pedals. They are also known as "fixies" in their street-ridden form.

Design

Track frames have steep geometry, a high bottom bracket for pedal clearance on banked tracks, and tight clearances. The rear fork ends are horizontal dropouts ("track ends") that allow chain tension adjustment. Wheels are often deep-section or disc designs for aerodynamics, with high-pressure tubular or clincher tires. Because the drivetrain is fixed, the rider's legs act as the brake; skidding and track stands are characteristic techniques.

Use in racing

Track bikes are used in all velodrome disciplines — sprint, keirin, pursuit, points race, madison, scratch race and time trials — under UCI regulations that mandate the fixed gear and absence of brakes. The same basic design is used by bicycle messengers and urban riders; fixed-gear bicycles became a prominent part of urban cycling culture in the 2000s, though street use without brakes is restricted in some jurisdictions.

History

Track racing was one of the most popular spectator sports of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and the track bicycle's essential form — diamond frame, single fixed gear — was established early and has changed only incrementally since.
