Thomas Howard, 2nd Duke of Norfolk

Thomas Howard, 2nd Duke of Norfolk KG PC (1443 – 21 May 1524), styled Earl of Surrey from 1483 to 1514, was an English nobleman, soldier and statesman who served four kings: Edward IV, Richard III, Henry VII and Henry VIII. He is best known for commanding the English army that defeated the Scots at the Battle of Flodden in 1513, after which he was restored to the dukedom of Norfolk that his father had lost at Bosworth.

Early career

Howard served Edward IV at the Battle of Barnet (1471) and was made a Knight of the Bath. Under Richard III he fought at the Battle of Bosworth (1485), where his father the 1st Duke was killed and Howard himself was captured and attainted by Henry VII. Gradually restored to favour, he was created Earl of Surrey in 1489 and served as the king's lieutenant in the north, suppressing the Yorkshire rebellion of 1489.

Flodden and restoration

As Earl Marshal and lieutenant-general of the north, Howard, aged about seventy, led the English army to victory over James IV of Scotland at Flodden on 9 September 1513; the Scottish king was killed on the field. In reward, on 1 February 1514 Henry VIII restored him as Duke of Norfolk.

Later years

Howard remained a leading counsellor into his eighties, serving as Lord High Treasurer and guardian of the realm during Henry VIII's absences in France. He was grandfather to two queens consort of Henry VIII, Anne Boleyn and Catherine Howard. He died at Framlingham Castle in 1524 and was buried at Thetford Priory.
