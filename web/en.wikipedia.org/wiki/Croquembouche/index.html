Croquembouche

A croquembouche (French: [kʁɔ.kɑ̃.buʃ]) or croque-en-bouche is a French dessert consisting of choux pastry puffs piled into a cone and bound with threads of caramel. In Italy and France it is often served at weddings, baptisms and first communions.

Croquembouche

Course: Dessert
Place of origin: France
Main ingredients: Choux pastry, crème pâtissière, caramel

History

The name comes from the French phrase croque en bouche, meaning '(something that) crunches in the mouth'. The invention is often attributed to Antonin Carême in the late 18th / early 19th century, who included it in his patisserie works.

Preparation

Profiteroles filled with pastry cream are dipped in caramel and stacked — traditionally around a cone mould — then decorated with spun caramel, sugared almonds, flowers or ribbons.
