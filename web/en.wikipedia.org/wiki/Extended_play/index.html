Extended play

An extended play (EP) is a musical recording that contains more tracks than a single but fewer than an album or LP record. Contemporary EPs often contain up to six tracks, and they generally feature less than 30 minutes of music or have a total playing time of less than 30 minutes. On rare occasions, an EP may have a playing time of more than 30 minutes. Some artists prefer to refer to these as mini-albums.

Extended plays were originally 7-inch vinyl records with more than the usual two songs and less than the usual number of songs on a full album. The format was introduced by RCA Victor in 1952 and widely used in the 1950s before declining in popularity as albums became dominant. EPs enjoyed a revival with the punk rock movement and later became popular in hip hop and electronic music.

History

Extended plays were released in various sizes in different eras. The earliest multi-track records, issued around 1919 by Grey Gull Records, were vertically cut 78 rpm discs known as "2-in-1" records. These had finer grooves than typical 78s, allowing longer play times per side.

RCA Victor introduced the EP in early 1952, just as it had introduced the LP by Columbia a few years earlier. The first EPs were seven-inch records, played at 45 rpm and typically lasting eight to ten minutes. In the mid-1950s, record companies began issuing double EPs, with playing times of 15 minutes or more.

Definition

The definition of an EP varies by chart authority. The United Kingdom's Official Charts Company defines a release with more than three tracks or a total of 25 minutes. In the United States, the Recording Industry Association of America defines an EP as having three to five tracks and less than 30 minutes total. Grammy eligibility and many streaming platforms use similar thresholds.

Modern usage

EPs are used by artists as a lower-cost way to release music, promote upcoming albums, or showcase new material. In hip hop, EPs are often used for debut releases, and in electronic music they remain a standard format for a handful of tracks and remixes.
