The economy of South Africa is the most industrialised and diversified in Africa, with deep financial markets, a major mining sector, and significant manufacturing and services. It is an upper-middle-income country of about 60 million people. Since the end of apartheid in 1994 the economy has been transformed politically but has struggled with slow growth, very high unemployment, electricity shortages and among the world's highest levels of income inequality.

Mining

Mining built the modern South African economy: gold from the Witwatersrand from 1886, diamonds from Kimberley, and later coal, platinum group metals (of which South Africa holds the world's largest reserves), iron ore, chromium and manganese. Mining's share of GDP has fallen to well under 10 percent but minerals still dominate export earnings.

Finance and services

Johannesburg's financial sector — the JSE, major banks such as Standard Bank and FirstRand, and large insurers — serves the whole region. Retail, telecommunications and business services are well developed by continental standards, and tourism draws on the country's wildlife, scenery and cities such as Cape Town.

Energy crisis

The state utility Eskom, which generates most of the country's electricity mainly from coal, fell into crisis from 2007 onward, imposing years of rolling blackouts known locally as "load shedding" that severely constrained growth. Reforms opening generation to private and renewable producers, and improved plant performance, eased the crisis in the mid-2020s.

Unemployment and inequality

Official unemployment has hovered around 30 percent — among the highest in the world — with youth unemployment far higher. The legacy of apartheid is reflected in persistent racial disparities in income, wealth and land ownership; Black Economic Empowerment policies aim to broaden participation in the economy. A large social grants system supports millions of households.

Trade

South Africa is a member of BRICS and the Southern African Customs Union. Its main trading partners include China, the European Union, the United States and its African neighbours. Growth since the 2010s has averaged around 1 percent a year, below population growth, making per-capita incomes stagnant or falling.
