Signal booster

A signal booster is a device that increases the strength of a received signal so that it can be used in an area or by equipment where the original signal is too weak. The term is most commonly applied to cellular signal boosters (also called cell phone repeaters), which amplify mobile-phone signals inside homes, offices, and vehicles, and to television and radio antenna amplifiers. A cellular booster system consists of an external (donor) antenna, an amplifier unit, and an internal (broadcast) antenna, connected by coaxial cable.

How cellular boosters work

The outside antenna receives the downlink signal from a distant cell tower; the amplifier boosts it — typically by 50 to 70 dB in consumer systems — and the inside antenna rebroadcasts it locally. In the reverse (uplink) direction, signals from phones inside are amplified and sent back toward the tower. Wideband boosters amplify all carriers in a band, while carrier-specific systems are provisioned by an operator. Proper separation between the antennas is required to prevent oscillation, and modern amplifiers include automatic gain control to shut down or reduce power if feedback or a strong nearby signal is detected.

Regulation

Because boosters retransmit on licensed spectrum, they are regulated. In the United States, the Federal Communications Commission adopted rules in 2013 requiring consumer signal boosters to meet technical standards, be sold as complete kits, and be registered with and used with the consent of the wireless carrier. Similar approval regimes exist in Canada, the UK (where Ofcom licenses certain mobile repeaters), and Australia.

Other types

Television masthead amplifiers boost weak broadcast signals before cable losses; Wi-Fi "boosters" or range extenders rebroadcast wireless networks (as repeaters or mesh nodes rather than true amplifiers); and bidirectional amplifiers are used in public-safety distributed antenna systems (DAS) to ensure in-building radio coverage for emergency responders, which is mandated by fire codes in many jurisdictions.
