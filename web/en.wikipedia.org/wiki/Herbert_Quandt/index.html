Herbert Quandt

Herbert Werner Quandt (22 June 1910 – 2 June 1982) was a German industrialist of the Quandt family who inherited and greatly expanded a business empire spanning batteries (VARTA), chemicals, textiles, and automobiles. He is best remembered for rescuing BMW from near-bankruptcy and a planned takeover by Daimler-Benz in 1959, staking his own fortune on the carmaker's independent future.

Life and business

Quandt, who was nearly blind from a childhood retinal condition, joined his father Günther Quandt's firms in the 1930s and took a leading role at the battery maker AFA (later VARTA). After the Second World War he rebuilt the family's holdings. His decisive intervention at BMW's 1959 annual meeting blocked the sale of the company, and under his control BMW's "New Class" cars restored its fortunes; the Quandt family remains BMW's largest shareholder bloc.

Legacy

His children by his third marriage, Stefan Quandt and Susanne Klatten, inherited major stakes in BMW and other companies and rank among Germany's wealthiest people. Later historical research documented the family's businesses' use of forced labour under the Nazi regime, prompting the family to commission and publish an independent study in 2011.
