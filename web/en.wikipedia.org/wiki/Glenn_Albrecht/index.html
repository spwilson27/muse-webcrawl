Glenn Albrecht

Glenn Anthony Albrecht (born 1953) is an Australian environmental philosopher known for coining the term solastalgia — the distress caused by environmental change to one's home environment — and for developing a vocabulary of "psychoterratic" (psyche–Earth) emotions. He was formerly professor of sustainability and director of the Institute for Sustainability and Technology Policy at Murdoch University in Western Australia.

Solastalgia

Albrecht developed the concept of solastalgia in the early 2000s while studying communities in the Upper Hunter Valley of New South Wales affected by open-cut coal mining and drought. Unlike nostalgia — homesickness for a place one has left — solastalgia is the pain experienced when the place one still inhabits is transformed beyond recognition: "the homesickness you have when you are still at home." The concept has been widely taken up in environmental psychology, public health, climate-change research, and the arts.

Other concepts and work

In his book Earth Emotions: New Words for a New World (2019), Albrecht proposed a suite of new terms — including endemicity, terrafurie, tierratrauma, and the positive state of eutierria, a feeling of oneness with the Earth — and argued for a hopeful transition from the Anthropocene to a new era he calls the Symbiocene, characterized by mutually beneficial relationships between humans and the rest of life. His research has also addressed the ethics of mining, bioregionalism, and the mental-health dimensions of climate change.
