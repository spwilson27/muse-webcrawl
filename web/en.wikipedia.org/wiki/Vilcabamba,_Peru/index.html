Vilcabamba (in Quechua, Willkapampa) was a city founded by the Inca ruler Manco Inca Yupanqui in about 1539 in the remote jungle region of modern Peru. It served as the capital of the Neo-Inca State, the last independent remnant of the Inca Empire, until its capture by Spanish forces in 1572.

History

After Manco Inca's rebellion against the Spanish failed at Cusco and Ollantaytambo, he withdrew into the mountainous forest region northwest of Cusco and established Vilcabamba as a refuge capital. His successors — Sayri Túpac, Titu Cusi Yupanqui and Túpac Amaru — ruled from there, alternating diplomacy and resistance. In 1572 Viceroy Francisco de Toledo launched a final campaign; the city was taken and burned, and Túpac Amaru was captured and executed in Cusco, ending Inca sovereignty.

Identification and archaeology

The site was lost for centuries. Hiram Bingham visited the ruins at Espíritu Pampa in 1911 but identified Machu Picchu with the "lost city" instead; later research, notably by Gene Savoy in the 1960s and subsequent archaeologists, established Espíritu Pampa as the true Vilcabamba. The sprawling ruins, largely overgrown by forest, include hundreds of buildings. A separate colonial town, Vilcabamba la Nueva, also exists in the district.
