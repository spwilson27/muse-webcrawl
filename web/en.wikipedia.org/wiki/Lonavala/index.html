Lonavala

Lonavala
State: Maharashtra, India
District: Pune
Elevation: 624 m
Population: approx. 58,000 (2011)

Lonavala (ISO: Loṇāvaḷā) is a hill station town and a Municipal Council in the Pune district, Maharashtra, India. It is about 64 km (40 mi) west of Pune and 96 km (60 mi) to the east of Mumbai. It is known for its production of the hard candy chikki and is also a major stop on the railway line connecting Mumbai and Pune.

History

The area around Lonavala was part of the territory of the Maratha Empire. Lord Elphinstone, Governor of Bombay Presidency from 1853 to 1860, is credited with the discovery of Lonavala as a hill station in 1811. Nearby are the ancient Buddhist rock-cut caves of Karla and Bhaja, dating to the 2nd century BCE.

Tourism

Lonavala and the neighbouring town of Khandala form a popular monsoon getaway from Mumbai and Pune. Attractions include Rajmachi Point, Tiger Point (Tiger's Leap), Bhushi Dam, Lonavala Lake, and the forts of Lohagad and Visapur. The town lies in the Sahyadri range (Western Ghats) and is surrounded by dense greenery during the rains.

Transport

Lonavala is served by the Mumbai–Pune Expressway and the old Mumbai–Pune highway, and is a station on the Mumbai–Pune suburban and long-distance railway line. The steep Bhor Ghat section of the railway climbs from Karjat to Lonavala.
