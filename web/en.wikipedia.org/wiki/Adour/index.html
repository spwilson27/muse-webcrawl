The Adour (Basque: Aturri; Occitan: Ador) is a river of south-western France, about 308 kilometres long, rising in the Pyrenees and flowing into the Atlantic Ocean (Bay of Biscay). Its basin drains much of Gascony. The name is thought to derive from a pre-Latin word for water or river, akin to the source of many European river names.

Course

The Adour rises at La Mongie on the slopes of the Pic du Midi de Bigorre in the High Pyrenees, formed by the union of three torrents (the Adour de Payolle, de Gripp and de Lesponne). It flows north through the Campan valley and Bagnères-de-Bigorre, then turns west across the plains of Bigorre and Béarn, passing Tarbes, Riscle and Aire-sur-l'Adour. In its lower course it receives major tributaries — the Midouze, the Luy, and the Gaves réunis (the combined Gave de Pau and Gave d'Oloron) — and flows past Dax and Bayonne. It reaches the sea between Anglet and Tarnos, just north of Bayonne.

History and use

The river's mouth was notoriously unstable: in the Middle Ages it discharged near Vieux-Boucau, far north of its present outlet, before shifting; engineers of the sixteenth century (under Louis de Foix) diverted it to its present course at Bayonne, enabling the port to flourish. The Adour was long used for floating timber and for salmon fishing, and its lower reaches remain navigable; its estuary port serves Bayonne's trade in timber, chemicals and steel. The river gives its name to the commune of Aire-sur-l'Adour and to the surrounding Landes de Gascogne country.
