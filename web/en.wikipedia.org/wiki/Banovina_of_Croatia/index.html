The Banovina of Croatia (Croatian: Banovina Hrvatska) was an autonomous province (banovina) of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia between 1939 and 1941. It was created by the Cvetković–Maček Agreement of 26 August 1939, the negotiated settlement between Yugoslav prime minister Dragiša Cvetković and Vladko Maček, leader of the Croatian Peasant Party, intended to resolve the long-running "Croatian question" within the Yugoslav state.

Territory and government

The banovina united the former Sava and Littoral banovinas with additional districts of Bosnia and Herzegovina having Croat majorities, including parts of the Vrbas, Drina, and Zeta banovinas. It covered roughly 66,000 square kilometres with about 4.4 million inhabitants, of whom some three-quarters were Croats, alongside substantial Serb and Bosnian Muslim minorities. The banovina had its own diet (the Sabor, though it never convened), a government in Zagreb responsible for most internal affairs, and a ban — Ivan Šubašić — appointed by the Crown. Belgrade retained defence, foreign policy, and parts of finance and trade.

Dissolution

The settlement came too late to stabilise Yugoslavia. Following the Axis invasion of April 1941, the banovina ceased to exist: most of its territory was incorporated into the puppet Independent State of Croatia, while Dalmatian coastal areas were annexed by Italy. After the war, Croatia re-emerged as a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia within broadly similar borders.
