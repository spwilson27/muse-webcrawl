Dosage form

Dosage forms (also called unit doses) are pharmaceutical drug products in the form in which they are marketed for use, with a specific mixture of active ingredients and inactive components (excipients) in a particular configuration and apportioned into a particular dose.

Dosage form
Field: Pharmaceutics
By route: Oral, topical, parenteral, inhaled
Examples: Tablet, capsule, syrup, injection

Classification

By route of administration: oral forms include tablets, capsules, solutions, suspensions and powders; parenteral forms include injections and infusions; topical forms include creams, ointments, gels and transdermal patches; inhaled forms include metered-dose inhalers and nebulizer solutions; others include suppositories, implants and eye drops. By physical form they may be solid, liquid, semi-solid or gaseous.

Design considerations

The dosage form affects bioavailability, stability, patient acceptability and adherence. Modified-release forms — extended-release, delayed-release (enteric-coated) — control where and how fast the drug is liberated. Combination products place several actives in one unit dose.
