Dilemma

A dilemma forces a choice between two alternatives, both of which have significant drawbacks.

A dilemma is a problem offering at least two solutions or possibilities, of which none is completely acceptable in a classical sense — a person is placed "on the horns of a dilemma" when caught between two equally unfavourable options. The term comes from Greek di- (twice) and lemma (premise).

In logic and rhetoric

In formal argument, a dilemma is an argument of the form: if A then B, and if C then D; either A or C; therefore either B or D. A constructive dilemma draws the two consequents, while a destructive dilemma works by denying them. A false dilemma (or false dichotomy) is a fallacy that presents only two options when more exist.

Responses

Traditional rhetorical responses to a dilemma include grasping the horn (denying one of the conditional premises), escaping between the horns (denying that the two options are exhaustive), and counter-dilemma (constructing a parallel argument reaching the opposite conclusion).

Ethical dilemmas

In ethics, a moral dilemma is a situation in which an agent must choose between two courses of action, each of which violates some moral requirement. Famous examples include the trolley problem and Heinz's dilemma, used by Lawrence Kohlberg in his theory of moral development. The prisoner's dilemma in game theory is a related but distinct concept, analysing how two rational actors might fail to cooperate even when cooperation benefits both.
