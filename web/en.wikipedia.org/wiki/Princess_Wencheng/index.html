Princess Wencheng

Princess Wencheng (628 – 680/682), surnamed Li, was a member of a minor branch of the royal clan of the Tang dynasty of China who married King (Tsenpo) Songtsen Gampo of the Tibetan Empire in 641. The marriage was arranged as part of a peace settlement between the Tang and Tibet, and she is remembered in both Chinese and Tibetan traditions.

Marriage

After military clashes between Tang forces and the Tibetan empire — including the Tibetan attack on Songzhou in 638 — Emperor Taizong agreed to a marriage alliance (heqin). Wencheng travelled to Tibet with a large dowry, said in later accounts to include a gold statue of the Buddha, the Jowo, which remains enshrined in the Jokhang Temple in Lhasa, as well as books, artisans, seeds, and craftsmen.

Legacy

In Tibetan tradition Wencheng is revered as a consort of the king and an emanation of the goddess Tara (the "White Tara"); she is credited with introducing Chinese agricultural techniques, sericulture, calendrical knowledge, and Buddhist texts to Tibet. Together with Songtsen Gampo's Nepalese wife, Bhrikuti, she is honoured as a founder of Tibetan Buddhism. A famous statue of the Jowo brought by the princess is considered the most sacred image in Tibetan Buddhism, and her journey is commemorated in temples and folk tales across the plateau.

She died in Lhasa around 680–682 and was given a grand funeral by the Tibetan court.
