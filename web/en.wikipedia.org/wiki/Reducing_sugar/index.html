Reducing sugar

A reducing sugar is any sugar that is capable of acting as a reducing agent. In its open-chain form, a reducing sugar has a free aldehyde group or a free ketone group: all monosaccharides (such as glucose and fructose) are reducing sugars, as are many disaccharides, including lactose and maltose.

Chemistry

The aldehyde group of an aldose can be oxidised to a carboxylic acid while reducing another substance. Sucrose is the classic non-reducing disaccharide, because its glycosidic bond joins the anomeric carbons of glucose and fructose, leaving no free reducing end.

Tests

Reducing sugars are detected by reagents such as Benedict's solution and Fehling's solution, in which blue copper(II) ions are reduced to red copper(I) oxide on heating. Tollens' reagent similarly deposits a silver mirror. The Maillard reaction between reducing sugars and amino acids is responsible for much of the browning and flavour of cooked food.
