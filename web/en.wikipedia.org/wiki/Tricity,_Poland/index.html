Tricity (Polish: Trójmiasto, Kashubian: Trzëgard) is a metropolitan area in northern Poland consisting of three coastal cities on the Gulf of Gdańsk: Gdańsk, Gdynia, and Sopot. Together with surrounding towns the wider agglomeration has well over a million inhabitants and forms one of Poland's largest urban areas and Baltic port complexes.

The three cities

Gdańsk, the oldest and largest, is a historic Hanseatic port and shipbuilding city, birthplace of the Solidarity movement in its Lenin Shipyard in 1980. Sopot, between the other two, is a spa and resort town famous for its long wooden pier (the longest in Europe) and the Forest Opera. Gdynia, built from a fishing village in the 1920s as Poland's new Baltic port after Gdańsk (Danzig) became a Free City, is noted for its modernist architecture and naval and maritime museums.

Transport and economy

The three cities are linked by a continuous built-up strip and by the SKM (Szybka Kolej Miejska) commuter railway, the backbone of local transport since the 1950s, supplemented by the Pomeranian Metropolitan Railway (PKM, opened 2015) connecting to Gdańsk Lech Wałęsa Airport. The ports of Gdańsk (including the deep-water DCT container terminal) and Gdynia together handle a major share of Poland's seaborne trade. Shipbuilding, petrochemicals (the Gdańsk refinery), logistics, IT, and tourism anchor the economy; the Ergo Arena straddles the Gdańsk–Sopot boundary.

Cooperation

Although administratively separate, the cities cooperate through metropolitan associations and jointly market the Tricity brand; discussions of a formal metropolitan union recur in Polish politics. The area is also a centre of Kashubian culture, with bilingual signage in parts of the region.
