The government of California is the governmental structure of the U.S. state of California as established by the Constitution of California. It is composed of three branches: the executive, headed by the Governor; the legislative, consisting of the bicameral California State Legislature; and the judicial, headed by the Supreme Court of California.

Executive branch

The Governor of California is elected to a four-year term (limited to two terms) and heads the executive branch. California has a plural executive: voters separately elect the Lieutenant Governor, Attorney General, Secretary of State, State Controller, State Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner, and Superintendent of Public Instruction, as well as the members of the State Board of Equalization.

Legislative branch

The California State Legislature consists of the 40-member Senate and the 80-member Assembly. Members are subject to term limits of 12 years in the legislature.

Direct democracy

California's constitution provides for ballot initiatives, referendums, and recalls, a legacy of the Progressive Era reforms of Governor Hiram Johnson in 1911. Voters have used these tools to enact major measures such as Proposition 13 (1978), which limited property taxes.

Judiciary

The judiciary includes the Supreme Court of California, courts of appeal, and superior courts in each of the state's 58 counties.
