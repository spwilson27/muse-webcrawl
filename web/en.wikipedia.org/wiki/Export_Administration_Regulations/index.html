Export Administration Regulations

Image placeholder: U.S. Department of Commerce

The Export Administration Regulations (EAR) are a set of regulations found at 15 CFR §730 et seq., administered by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) of the United States Department of Commerce. They govern the export, re-export, and transfer of commercial and dual-use items — goods, software, and technology that have both civilian and potential military applications — for reasons of national security, foreign policy, and short supply.

Scope

The EAR apply to U.S.-origin items wherever located, foreign-made items containing more than a de minimis amount of controlled U.S. content, and certain foreign-made direct products of U.S. technology or software. Items specifically designed or modified for military use generally fall instead under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) administered by the Department of State, while nuclear-related exports are controlled by other agencies.

Classification and licensing

Items subject to the EAR are classified either with an Export Control Classification Number (ECCN) on the Commerce Control List or as "EAR99", a basket category for items not otherwise listed. Whether a license is required depends on the item's classification, destination country (organised on the Commerce Country Chart), end user, and end use. The EAR also maintain denied-party lists, including the Entity List and the Denied Persons List, and regulate "deemed exports" — the release of controlled technology to foreign nationals within the United States. Violations can result in substantial civil and criminal penalties.
