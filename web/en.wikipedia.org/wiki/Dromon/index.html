Dromon

The dromon (Greek: δρόμων, "runner") was a type of galley and the most important warship of the Byzantine navy from the 5th through the 12th centuries. It evolved from the liburnian vessels of the Roman Empire and eventually gave place to Italian-style galleys.

Design

Dromons were oared warships, typically with one or two banks of oars and lateen sails. Later types were larger, with two masts and crews of up to a few hundred men. They were armed with a ram at the waterline, marines armed with bows and spears, and famously with siphons projecting Greek fire, the incendiary weapon that gave Byzantine fleets a decisive advantage.

Variants

Smaller types included the pamphylos and the fast chelandion; the term "dromon" eventually became a generic word for warships in medieval sources.
