2020–2023 global chip shortage

The 2020–2023 global chip shortage was a period of severe worldwide undersupply of integrated circuits that began in early 2020 and affected more than 169 industries. Demand for semiconductors surged during the COVID-19 pandemic as remote working drove purchases of computers and electronics, while supply was disrupted by factory shutdowns, natural disasters and geopolitical tensions. The shortage is estimated to have cost the global economy hundreds of billions of dollars, with the automotive industry alone losing an estimated $210 billion in revenue in 2021.

Global chip shortage

Period: 2020 – 2023
Cause: COVID-19 demand shifts, supply disruptions, underinvestment
Most affected: Automotive, consumer electronics, gaming
Estimated auto revenue loss (2021): ~$210 billion

Causes

At the start of the pandemic, automakers cancelled chip orders in anticipation of falling sales, while demand from electronics firms soared. When vehicle demand rebounded faster than expected, carmakers found themselves at the back of the queue at foundries such as TSMC, which were already at capacity. Additional shocks included the February 2021 Texas winter storm that idled fabs in Austin, a fire at a Renesas plant in Japan, drought in Taiwan, and later Russia's invasion of Ukraine, which affected supplies of neon gas and other inputs.

Effects

Car manufacturers idled assembly lines worldwide; an estimated 10.5 million vehicles were cut from production in 2021. Consumers faced long waits and higher prices for cars, games consoles such as the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, graphics cards and appliances. Governments responded with industrial policy, including the United States' CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 and the European Chips Act, subsidising domestic fabrication capacity.

Resolution

By late 2022 and 2023, shortages eased as consumer electronics demand cooled and new capacity came online; some segments swung into glut, though automotive-grade chips remained tight for longer.
