Cold dark matter

Cold dark matter (CDM) is a hypothetical type of dark matter whose particles move slowly compared with the speed of light (the 'cold' in CDM) and interact very weakly with ordinary matter and radiation. It is the cornerstone of the standard ΛCDM model of cosmology.

Theory

Because cold particles cluster early and on small scales, CDM predicts bottom-up structure formation: small halos merge into galaxies and clusters, matching the observed cosmic web and the cosmic microwave background's power spectrum. Hot dark matter, by contrast, erases small-scale structure and is ruled out as the dominant component.

Challenges

Simulations predict more dwarf satellite galaxies and cuspier halo centres than some observations suggest (the missing-satellites and cusp–core problems), though baryonic feedback may reconcile many discrepancies. No CDM particle — neither WIMPs nor axions — has yet been directly detected, keeping alternatives such as self-interacting or warm dark matter under study.
