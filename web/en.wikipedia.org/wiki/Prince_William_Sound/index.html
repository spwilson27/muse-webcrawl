Glaciers and islands of Prince William Sound, Alaska

Prince William Sound is a sound of the Gulf of Alaska on the south coast of the U.S. state of Alaska, lying east of the Kenai Peninsula. It is bounded by the Chugach Mountains and the dense Chugach National Forest, and contains numerous glacier-carved fjords and about 150 glaciers, including the Columbia and Harriman tidewater glaciers. Its principal ports are Valdez, Cordova and Whittier.

History

The sound was named by Captain James Cook in 1778 for Prince William, the third son of King George III and later King William IV; the region had been explored earlier by Vitus Bering's 1741 expedition for Russia, and the indigenous population is the Chugach Alutiiq (Sugpiaq) people. The 1964 Good Friday earthquake, centred in the sound, devastated Valdez and other communities and generated tsunamis across the Pacific.

Economy and the Exxon Valdez spill

Valdez is the southern terminus of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, and tankers carry crude oil through the sound. On 24 March 1989, the tanker Exxon Valdez ran aground on Bligh Reef, spilling roughly 11 million gallons (about 260,000 barrels) of crude oil — the largest oil spill in U.S. waters until the Deepwater Horizon spill of 2010. The disaster devastated fisheries and wildlife, led to the U.S. Oil Pollution Act of 1990 and requirements for double-hulled tankers and escort tugs, and its ecological effects were studied for decades.

Wildlife

The sound supports rich marine life — salmon, herring, sea otters, orcas, humpback whales and seabird colonies — and is a major destination for kayaking, fishing and glacier tourism.
