Fast Carrier Task Force

The Fast Carrier Task Force (TF 38 under the Third Fleet, TF 58 under the Fifth Fleet) was the main striking force of the United States Navy during the second half of the Pacific War of World War II. Task Groups within the force numbered up to four, each centered on fleet aircraft carriers screened by fast battleships, cruisers, and destroyers.

Organization

The force alternated designations when command rotated between Admiral William Halsey's Third Fleet (as TF 38) and Admiral Raymond Spruance's Fifth Fleet (as TF 58), a rotation that gave staffs time to plan. It was commanded at sea for most of its existence by Vice Admiral Marc Mitscher. At its peak, in early 1945, the force included over a dozen fleet and light carriers with more than 1,000 aircraft.

Operations

Formed in late 1943, the Fast Carrier Task Force supported the island-hopping campaigns across the Central Pacific, including the Gilbert and Marshall Islands, the Marianas (where its air groups won the lopsided "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot" during the Battle of the Philippine Sea in June 1944), Iwo Jima, and Okinawa. It struck Japanese bases across the Pacific, fought in the Battle of Leyte Gulf, and launched air raids on the Japanese home islands in 1945.

Legacy

The task force demonstrated the dominance of carrier aviation over the battleship and became the model for postwar US Navy carrier strike groups. Its mobility was enabled by an advanced underway-replenishment logistics train that let it remain at sea for months.
