Venta Icenorum

Venta Icenorum, literally "the marketplace of the Iceni", was a civitas capital of Roman Britain and the principal town of the Iceni tribe, on the River Tas near present-day Caistor St Edmund in Norfolk, about 5 miles south of Norwich.

History

The town was established after the Boudican revolt of AD 60/61, in which the Iceni under Boudica rebelled against Roman rule. Laid out on a grid plan in the later 1st century, Venta had a forum and basilica, public baths, temples, an amphitheatre outside the walls, and workshops and houses along its streets. Stone defences were added in the 3rd century, enclosing about 35 acres. Occupation continued into the Anglo-Saxon period, after which settlement shifted to Norwich and the site reverted to farmland — making it one of the few major Romano-British towns not buried beneath a modern successor.

Archaeology

Aerial photographs taken in 1928 famously revealed the street plan in cropmarks. Excavations, notably by the University of Nottingham from 2009, have investigated the forum, defences and suburbs. The site is a scheduled ancient monument, largely in private farmland with public footpath access.
