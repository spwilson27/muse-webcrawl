Newton polygon

In mathematics, the Newton polygon is a tool for understanding the behaviour of polynomials over local fields, particularly the valuations and factorisation of polynomials over the p-adic numbers. It is named after Isaac Newton, who used related ideas in his work on plane algebraic curves and power series (Puiseux series).

Construction

Given a polynomial with coefficients in a valued field,

P(x) = anxn + an−1xn−1 + ⋯ + a0

one plots the points (i, v(ai)) for each non-zero coefficient ai, where v is the valuation (for the p-adic valuation, vp(ai) is the exponent of p in ai). The Newton polygon is the lower convex hull of these points: the sequence of line segments joining the lowest points from left to right.

Properties

The key theorem states that the slopes of the segments of the Newton polygon give the valuations of the roots of the polynomial in an algebraic closure: a segment of horizontal length ℓ and slope m corresponds to exactly ℓ roots (counted with multiplicity) of valuation −m. Consequently, if the polygon has a segment of length 1, the polynomial has a root in the base field with that valuation, and the polygon's shape determines how the polynomial factors over the local field.

A classical application is Dumas's criterion for irreducibility: if the Newton polygon of a polynomial over the p-adic numbers consists of a single segment whose slope, in lowest terms, has denominator equal to the degree, the polynomial is irreducible over ℚp. Eisenstein's criterion is a special case.

Generalisation

In tropical geometry, Newton polygons and their higher-dimensional analogues, Newton polytopes, play a central role: the Newton polytope of a polynomial is the convex hull of its exponent vectors, and its structure governs the geometry of the associated hypersurface.
