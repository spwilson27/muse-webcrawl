Wagon

A wagon (also spelled waggon) is a heavy four-wheeled vehicle pulled by draught animals or, occasionally, humans, used for transporting goods, commodities, agricultural materials, supplies and sometimes people. It differs from a cart, which has two wheels, and from a carriage, which is primarily for passengers.

History

Wheeled vehicles appeared in the 4th millennium BC. Wagons were central to farming, trade and migration for millennia: ox-drawn farm wagons, stage and freight wagons, and canvas-topped "prairie schooners" such as the Conestoga wagon used by settlers crossing North America in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Construction

A traditional wagon has a wooden bed, four large spoked wheels (rear wheels larger), a tongue or shafts for the team, and a brake. The driver often walked beside the team or rode the left rear animal.

Modern usage

The term survives in "station wagon" (estate car), railway freight wagons, and children's pull wagons. Horse-drawn wagons remain in use in agriculture, tourism and among Amish communities.
