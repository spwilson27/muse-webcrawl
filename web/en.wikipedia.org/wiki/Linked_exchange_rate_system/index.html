Linked exchange rate system

The linked exchange rate system is the currency board system that pegs the Hong Kong dollar to the United States dollar at a rate of HK$7.80 to US$1. It was established on 17 October 1983 by the Hong Kong government amid a currency crisis and loss of confidence during negotiations over Hong Kong's future, and it continues under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA).

Mechanism

Under the system, Hong Kong's three note-issuing banks (HSBC, Standard Chartered and Bank of China) may issue banknotes only against US dollars surrendered to the Exchange Fund at the fixed rate, and the monetary base is fully backed by US dollar reserves. Since 2005 the HKMA has maintained a Convertibility Zone: it undertakes to buy US dollars at HK$7.75 per dollar and to sell at HK$7.85, keeping the market rate within the band.

Effects and debate

The peg gives Hong Kong monetary stability and credibility, but imports US monetary policy regardless of local economic conditions, which critics blamed for deflation after the Asian financial crisis. Proposals to repeg to a basket or to the renminbi are periodically debated, but the HKMA has consistently reaffirmed the link.
