Severance package

A severance package is pay and benefits that employees receive when they leave employment at a company, typically upon involuntary termination such as a layoff or restructuring, and sometimes on retirement or by mutual agreement. Severance is usually offered in exchange for the departing employee's agreement, in a severance or separation agreement, to release the employer from certain legal claims.

Components

A package may include additional pay beyond the employee's final wages (commonly one or two weeks of salary per year of service), continuation of health insurance coverage, outplacement assistance, payment for unused leave, and retention of bonuses, stock options or pension benefits. Senior executives often have severance terms written into their employment contracts; "golden parachutes" for chief executives can be worth tens of millions of dollars and may require shareholder disclosure.

Legal status

In the United States there is no general federal law requiring severance pay; the Fair Labor Standards Act does not mandate it. It may, however, be required by a contract, collective bargaining agreement, or company policy, and the WARN Act requires advance notice (or pay in lieu) for large-scale layoffs and plant closings. In many European countries, statutory redundancy payments are prescribed by law and scaled to length of service.
