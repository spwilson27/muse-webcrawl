John of Nepomuk (Czech: Jan Nepomucký; c. 1345 – 20 March 1393) was a priest in Prague who was drowned in the Vltava River on the orders of King Wenceslaus IV of Bohemia. He is the most famous Czech saint and a national symbol of Bohemia.

Life and death

John studied at the University of Prague and at Padua, and rose to become vicar-general of the Archdiocese of Prague under Archbishop Jan of Jenštejn. His conflict with Wenceslaus IV arose from a bitter dispute between king and archbishop, most directly over the appointment of an abbot of Kladruby, which John confirmed against the king's wishes. In March 1393 John was arrested, tortured, and thrown from the Charles Bridge into the Vltava, where he drowned.

Legend

Later tradition transformed John into a martyr of the confessional: in the beloved legend, he was the confessor of Queen Joanna and was killed for refusing to reveal her confession to the jealous king. When his tomb in St Vitus Cathedral was opened in 1719, the relic identified as his tongue was found apparently preserved — a discovery that spurred his canonisation by Pope Benedict XIII in 1729.

Veneration

John's statue on Charles Bridge (1683) became the model for countless Nepomuk statues on bridges throughout Central Europe. He is invoked against floods and drowning and as patron of the seal of confession. Historians regard the confessor legend as a later construction, but his cult — promoted during the Counter-Reformation and embraced by Czech national sentiment — remains central to Czech religious identity; his feast is celebrated with the Navalis festival in Prague.
