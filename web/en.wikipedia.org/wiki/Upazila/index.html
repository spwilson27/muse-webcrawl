Upazila

An upazila (Bengali: উপজেলা, literally "sub-district") is a mid-level administrative division of Bangladesh. Upazilas are the second-lowest tier of administrative government, situated below districts (zila) and above union councils. There are nearly 500 upazilas in the country. The equivalent unit in metropolitan cities is the thana, though rural upazilas and thanas function similarly at the local level.

History

The upazila system was introduced in 1982–1983 under the government of Hussain Muhammad Ershad, which converted the country's former thanas (police station areas) into upazilas as part of a decentralisation programme. Elected upazila councils (upazila parishads) were created to bring development administration closer to rural citizens. The system was abolished in 1991 and later revived; elections to upazila councils resumed in 2009 and have been held periodically since.

Administration

Each upazila is administered by an Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO), a civil servant of the Bangladesh Civil Service (Administration) cadre, who acts as the chief executive officer. The elected upazila parishad is led by a chairman and includes vice-chairmen and the chairmen of the constituent union councils as members. Local government functions such as primary education oversight, health services, agriculture extension, and rural infrastructure are coordinated at the upazila level, although central government ministries retain substantial control through their field officers.

Demographics and geography

An average upazila covers roughly 280 square kilometres and has a population of several hundred thousand people, though both figures vary widely between the densely populated deltaic plains and the hill districts of the southeast. Each upazila is subdivided into union councils — typically around ten — and, in some areas, municipalities (pourashavas).
