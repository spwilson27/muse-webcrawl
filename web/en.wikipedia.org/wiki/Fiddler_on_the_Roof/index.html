Fiddler on the Roof is a musical with music by Jerry Bock, lyrics by Sheldon Harnick, and book by Joseph Stein, set in the fictional Jewish village of Anatevka in the Pale of Settlement of Imperial Russia around 1905. It is based on Tevye and His Daughters and other tales by Sholem Aleichem. The story centres on Tevye, a poor Jewish milkman, and his five daughters, as he struggles to maintain his Jewish and cultural traditions as outside influences encroach upon the family's life.

Plot

Tevye explains the precarious life of the villagers through the metaphor of a fiddler on the roof ("Tradition"). His three eldest daughters each challenge the custom of arranged marriage: Tzeitel marries the tailor Motel instead of the wealthy butcher Lazar Wolf; Hodel falls in love with the revolutionary student Perchik and follows him to Siberia; and Chava marries the Russian Fyedka, a marriage outside the faith that Tevye cannot accept. At the end, an edict from the Tsar forces the Jews of Anatevka to leave their village, dispersing the family to America and Palestine.

Productions and film

The original Broadway production, directed and choreographed by Jerome Robbins and starring Zero Mostel as Tevye, won nine Tony Awards, including Best Musical, and became the first musical in history to surpass 3,000 performances. The 1971 film adaptation, directed by Norman Jewison and starring Chaim Topol, won three Academy Awards. The title was inspired by Marc Chagall's paintings of a fiddler, including The Fiddler (1912–13) and Green Violinist (1923–24).
