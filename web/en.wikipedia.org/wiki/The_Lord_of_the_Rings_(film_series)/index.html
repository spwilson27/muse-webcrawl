The Lord of the Rings is a trilogy of epic fantasy adventure films directed by Peter Jackson, based on the novel The Lord of the Rings by J. R. R. Tolkien. The films are subtitled The Fellowship of the Ring (2001), The Two Towers (2002) and The Return of the King (2003). Produced by New Line Cinema and WingNut Films and filmed simultaneously in New Zealand, they follow the hobbit Frodo Baggins and the Fellowship as they quest to destroy the One Ring and defeat the Dark Lord Sauron.

Production

Jackson shot all three films back-to-back over roughly fifteen months of principal photography, with extensive pick-ups in following years — an unprecedented undertaking. Weta Workshop created the armour, creatures and miniatures ("bigatures"), while Weta Digital developed the MASSIVE crowd software for the battle scenes and the motion-capture performance of Andy Serkis as Gollum. Howard Shore's score, drawing on leitmotifs for each culture of Middle-earth, became one of the most celebrated in film.

Reception

The trilogy grossed nearly $2.9 billion worldwide and won 17 Academy Awards from 30 nominations. The Return of the King won all 11 of its nominations, including Best Picture — the first fantasy film to do so — equalling the records of Ben-Hur and Titanic. The extended editions released on home media are regarded as definitive versions by many viewers.

Legacy

The films revitalised the fantasy genre, transformed New Zealand's film and tourism industries, and were followed by Jackson's Hobbit trilogy (2012–2014) and further screen adaptations of Tolkien's world.
