Newport, Rhode Island

Newport is a seaside city on Aquidneck Island in Rhode Island, United States. Founded in 1639, it was a major colonial port and a centre of religious tolerance; in the late 19th century it became the summer playground of America's wealthiest families, whose "cottages" — the great Gilded Age mansions along Bellevue Avenue — remain its best-known landmarks.

History

Newport was settled by followers of Anne Hutchinson and became one of the five capitals of the Colony of Rhode Island. Its merchants grew rich in the colonial rum and slave trades. The city was occupied by the British during the American Revolutionary War and later hosted the French fleet. In the Gilded Age, families such as the Vanderbilts and Astors built palatial summer houses including The Breakers (1895), Marble House and Rosecliff, most now preserved by the Preservation Society of Newport County.

Attractions and events

Newport hosted the America's Cup yacht races from 1930 to 1983 and is home to the International Tennis Hall of Fame, where the first U.S. National Championships were played in 1881. The Newport Folk Festival (founded 1959) and Newport Jazz Festival (1954) remain major annual events. The U.S. Naval War College and Naval Station Newport are major institutions; the population was about 25,000 at the 2020 census.
