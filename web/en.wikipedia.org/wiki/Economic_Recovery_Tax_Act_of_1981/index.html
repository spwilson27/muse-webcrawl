Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981

Long titleAn Act to amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1954 to encourage economic growth through reduction of the tax rates for individual taxpayers, acceleration of capital cost recovery of investment in plant, equipment, and real property, and incentives for savings, and for other purposes.
Enacted byThe 97th United States Congress
SignedAugust 13, 1981, by President Ronald Reagan
Public lawPub. L. 97–34

The Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 (ERTA), also known as the Kemp–Roth Tax Cut, was a major United States federal tax law enacted in 1981 as a centrepiece of President Ronald Reagan's economic programme, often called Reaganomics. It sharply reduced individual income tax rates and liberalised depreciation rules for business investment.

Provisions

The Act phased in a 23% reduction in individual income tax rates over three years, cutting the top marginal rate from 70% to 50%, and provided for indexation of tax brackets against inflation beginning in 1985. It also introduced the Accelerated Cost Recovery System (ACRS), which substantially shortened depreciation schedules for plant and equipment, expanded Individual Retirement Accounts, and reduced estate taxes and the tax on capital gains.

Aftermath

Supporters credited the Act with helping spur the long economic expansion of the 1980s, while critics linked it to growing federal budget deficits. Concern over deficits led Congress to pass the Tax Equity and Fiscal Responsibility Act of 1982, which rolled back some of ERTA's business provisions, and later reforms — notably the Tax Reform Act of 1986 — restructured the system the Act had created.
