Manresa (Catalan pronunciation: [mənˈrɛzə]) is a city in central Catalonia, Spain, capital of the comarca of Bages. It lies on the river Cardener, a tributary of the Llobregat, near the mountain of Montserrat, and serves as an important industrial and commercial centre for the surrounding region.

History

The area has been inhabited since prehistoric times and was settled in Roman times. Manresa is best known as the place where Saint Ignatius of Loyola stayed in 1522–1523 after his pilgrimage to Montserrat. Living in a cave beside the Cardener (the Cova de Sant Ignasi), he experienced the spiritual insights that led to his Spiritual Exercises; the city remains an important Jesuit pilgrimage destination.

During the Peninsular War, Manresa was burned by French troops in 1811. In the 19th century it became a significant textile-manufacturing centre, and the surrounding canal network (the Séquia de Manresa, built in the 14th century) still supplies water to the city.

Main sights

  • Basilica of Santa Maria de la Seu, a Gothic church overlooking the old town, begun in the 14th century.
  • Cave of Saint Ignatius and the adjoining sanctuary and museum.
  • Pont Vell (Old Bridge) over the Cardener, of Romanesque origin.
  • Casa de la Ciutat and other modernista buildings from the city's industrial era.
