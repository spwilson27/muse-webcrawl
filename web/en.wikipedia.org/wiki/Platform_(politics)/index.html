Party platform

A political party platform, party program, or party manifesto (in British usage) is a formal set of principal goals supported by a political party or individual candidate, for the purpose of appealing to the general public, on matters such as social and political issues and the economy. The individual elements of the platform are called planks — a metaphor from a wooden platform made of boards.

Function

Platforms serve several purposes: they signal a party's commitments to voters, coordinate the party's own candidates and activists, and provide a benchmark against which a government's performance can later be judged. Research on pledge fulfilment in parliamentary democracies finds that governing parties implement a substantial majority of their specific pledges, though figures vary by country and by how pledges are counted.

Development

In the United States, national party platforms are adopted at the quadrennial national conventions, following drafting by a platform committee that holds hearings around the country. In the United Kingdom and other parliamentary systems, the manifesto is drawn up by the party leadership and, under doctrines of collective responsibility, is treated as the programme the party will carry out if elected; the Salisbury convention in the House of Lords reflects the manifesto's constitutional weight.

Criticism

Critics note that platforms are often long, little-read documents produced to satisfy internal factions, and that candidates in candidate-centred systems may distance themselves from unpopular planks. Others defend them as the clearest statement a party offers of its intentions, and a basis for democratic accountability.
