Hangzhou (Chinese: 杭州; pinyin: Hángzhōu) is the capital and most populous city of Zhejiang province in East China. It lies at the head of Hangzhou Bay on China's southeastern coast, at the southern end of the Grand Canal where it meets the Qiantang River. Celebrated for its natural beauty — above all West Lake — Hangzhou has been one of China's most renowned and prosperous cities for the last millennium, and was the capital of the Southern Song dynasty.

History

The area was the heartland of the Neolithic Liangzhu culture (c. 3400–2250 BC). Hangzhou rose to prominence after the completion of the Grand Canal's southern section under the Sui dynasty. After the fall of Kaifeng to the Jin dynasty in 1127, the Song court fled south and made Hangzhou (then called Lin'an) its capital; by the 13th century it was among the largest and richest cities in the world, described admiringly by Marco Polo, who called it the finest and most splendid city in the world.

Economy

Hangzhou is a centre of the silk and tea trades — the surrounding hills produce Longjing (Dragon Well) green tea — and in recent decades has become a hub of China's digital economy. It is the home of the e-commerce company Alibaba Group and numerous technology firms, and hosted the 2016 G20 summit and the 2022 Asian Games (held in 2023).

Geography and climate

The city has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, hot humid summers and cool damp winters. The Qiantang River is famous for its tidal bore, among the largest in the world, watched by crowds at the Mid-Autumn Festival.
