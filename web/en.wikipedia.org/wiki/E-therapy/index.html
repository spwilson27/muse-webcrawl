E-therapy

E-therapy
Also called: Online therapy, telepsychology
Delivery: Internet, video, text, email

E-therapy, also known as online therapy, cybertherapy or telepsychology, is the delivery of mental health counselling and psychological support over the internet. Sessions may take place by videoconferencing, voice calls, live chat, email or messaging, either synchronously or asynchronously.

History

Computer-mediated psychological help dates to early internet advice services of the 1990s, such as Ivan Goldberg's online depression support and fee-based question-and-answer sites. Growth accelerated with broadband video and, dramatically, during the COVID-19 pandemic, when most in-person practice moved online.

Effectiveness and issues

Research generally finds video-based therapy, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy, comparably effective to face-to-face treatment for many common conditions including depression and anxiety disorders. Concerns include privacy and data security, licensure across jurisdictions, verifying client identity and location in a crisis, and suitability for severe conditions or emergencies. Professional bodies have issued telepractice guidelines and ethics standards for remote work.
