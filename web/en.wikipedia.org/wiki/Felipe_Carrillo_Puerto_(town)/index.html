Felipe Carrillo Puerto is a town in the Mexican state of Quintana Roo, on the Yucatán Peninsula. It is the municipal seat of the municipality of the same name and one of the most important centres of Maya culture in the state.

History

The town was founded in 1850, during the Caste War of Yucatán, as Chan Santa Cruz, the capital and religious centre of the independent Maya state of Chan Santa Cruz. Its inhabitants, known as the Cruzob, maintained a theocratic polity centred on the cult of the "Talking Cross", and resisted Mexican authority for more than half a century. Mexican federal forces occupied the town in 1901, effectively ending the Caste War.

In 1930 the town was renamed in honour of Felipe Carrillo Puerto, the socialist governor of Yucatán who was executed in 1924 and who had been a prominent defender of Maya rights.

Geography and demography

The town lies in the south-central part of Quintana Roo, in the region known as the Zona Maya. A large proportion of its inhabitants speak Yucatec Maya as a first language, and the town is regarded as a stronghold of living Maya culture, with community museums, traditional medicine practitioners and Maya-language radio.

Transport

Felipe Carrillo Puerto is crossed by Federal Highway 184 and lies near the route of Federal Highway 307, which connects Cancún with Chetumal. The Tren Maya railway project includes a station serving the town.
