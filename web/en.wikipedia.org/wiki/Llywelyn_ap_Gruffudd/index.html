Llywelyn ap Gruffudd (c. 1223 – 11 December 1282), sometimes written in English as Llewelyn ap Gruffydd and known as Llywelyn the Last (Welsh: Llywelyn Ein Llyw Olaf, "Llywelyn, Our Last Leader"), was the last Welsh prince of the Principality of Wales before its conquest by Edward I of England. He was the grandson of Llywelyn the Great.

Rise to power

Llywelyn was the second son of Gruffudd ap Llywelyn, the eldest but illegitimate son of Llywelyn the Great. After the death of his uncle Dafydd ap Llywelyn in 1246 and the division of Gwynedd, Llywelyn defeated his brothers Owain and Dafydd at the Battle of Bryn Derwin in 1255 and became sole ruler of Gwynedd west of the Conwy.

Expansion and recognition

Exploiting the baronial wars in England, Llywelyn expanded his power across Wales, capturing the Perfeddwlad and much of Powys and the Marches. In 1267 Henry III recognised him as Prince of Wales by the Treaty of Montgomery, in return for homage and a payment of 25,000 marks.

Conflict with Edward I

Relations deteriorated after Edward I came to the throne in 1272. Llywelyn repeatedly refused to render homage, and in 1276 Edward declared him a rebel. In the war of 1276–77 Llywelyn was forced to accept the Treaty of Aberconwy, which reduced his principality to Gwynedd west of the Conwy. In 1278 he married Eleanor de Montfort at Worcester Cathedral.

Death and legacy

In March 1282, Llywelyn's brother Dafydd began a new revolt, drawing Llywelyn reluctantly into a final war. On 11 December 1282 Llywelyn was killed at the Battle of Orewin Bridge near Builth Wells, separated from his army — according to tradition after being lured to a meeting. His head was sent to Edward and displayed at the Tower of London. Dafydd was captured and executed in 1283, and the Statute of Rhuddlan (1284) annexed the principality to the English crown. Llywelyn is remembered in Wales as a national hero; the site of his death at Cilmeri is marked by a memorial.
