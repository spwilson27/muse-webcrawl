Home Children

Home Children were child migrants sent from the United Kingdom to Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa under child emigration schemes run by charities and religious organisations from 1869 until the mid-20th century. Over 100,000 children were sent to Canada alone.

Scheme

Organisations such as Barnardo's and the Fairbridge Society placed children, many from poor or orphaned backgrounds, with farming families as labourers or domestic servants. Some thrived; others suffered neglect, abuse and loss of identity, and many were told incorrectly that their parents were dead.

Recognition

The Australian government apologised in 2009 and the British government in 2010 for the schemes. Canada designated 2010 the Year of the British Home Child, and descendants' organisations preserve records and memorials.

References: Parliamentary reports; national archives of Canada and Australia.
