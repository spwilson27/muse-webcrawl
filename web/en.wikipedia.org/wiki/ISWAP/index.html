The Islamic State – West Africa Province (ISWAP) is a militant Islamist group and a regional branch ("province") of the Islamic State, operating primarily in northeastern Nigeria and the Lake Chad basin. It emerged from the Nigerian insurgent group Boko Haram.

History

In March 2015, Boko Haram leader Abubakar Shekau pledged allegiance to the Islamic State, and the group was accepted as its West Africa province. In 2016 the Islamic State appointed Abu Musab al-Barnawi, son of Boko Haram founder Mohammed Yusuf, as governor (wali), rejecting Shekau. The movement split: al-Barnawi's ISWAP faction, based around Lake Chad, and Shekau's faction (JAS), based in the Sambisa Forest. The factions fought each other as well as Nigerian and regional forces.

In May 2021 ISWAP attacked Shekau's stronghold; Shekau died during the assault, reportedly by detonating an explosive vest, and many of his fighters were absorbed by ISWAP, making it the dominant jihadist group in the region.

Activities

ISWAP has attacked military bases, convoys, aid workers and civilians, and has kidnapped for ransom. It has also pursued a "hearts and minds" strategy in areas it controls — taxing, providing some services and digging wells — in contrast to Shekau's indiscriminate violence against Muslim civilians. It remains in conflict with the Nigerian military and the Multinational Joint Task Force, and is designated a terrorist organisation by numerous states and the United Nations.
