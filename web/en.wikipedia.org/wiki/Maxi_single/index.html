Maxi single

A maxi single (often just maxi) is a music single release containing more than the usual two tracks — typically an A-side and B-side — often including remixes, extended versions, or additional songs. The term is applied to releases on 12-inch vinyl, cassette, and compact disc.

History

The format grew out of the 12-inch disco singles of the 1970s, which offered extended mixes for club DJs. In the 1980s and 1990s the CD maxi single became a standard commercial format, especially in Europe, where singles commonly bundled three to five tracks: the radio edit, one or more remixes, and bonus material. Prominent dance and pop acts released elaborate maxi packages, and remix culture flourished around the format.

Formats and decline

Maxi singles appeared as 12" vinyl (45 rpm), CD maxi (distinct from the smaller 3-inch CD single), and cassette maxi. Chart rules in various countries limited the number of tracks and playing time for a release to qualify as a single. With the rise of digital downloads and streaming in the 2000s, the physical maxi single declined, though the concept survives in digital "single" bundles and remix EPs.
