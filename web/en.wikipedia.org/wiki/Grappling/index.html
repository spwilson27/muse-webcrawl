Grappling is a set of hand-to-hand combat techniques used at close range to gain a physical advantage over an opponent, such as improving relative position, escaping, or submitting the opponent. Grappling techniques include clinch fighting, throws and takedowns, joint locks, chokeholds, and pinning holds. They form the core of many martial arts and combat sports and contrast with striking techniques such as punches and kicks.

Techniques

Stand-up grappling centers on the clinch and on throws or takedowns that bring the fight to the ground. Ground grappling emphasizes positional control — positions such as guard, mount and side control — and transitions between them, leading to submission holds: joint locks that hyperextend or rotate a limb joint, and strangles that compress the neck's blood vessels or airway. Practitioners signal submission by "tapping out".

Arts and sport

Grappling is ancient and global: Greek pankration and wrestling, Japanese judo and jujutsu, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, Russian sambo, Mongolian wrestling and many folk styles all specialize in it. Modern submission grappling competitions (e.g. ADCC) and mixed martial arts have made grappling proficiency — especially Brazilian jiu-jitsu and wrestling — a core requirement for fighters. Gi grappling uses the jacket as a gripping surface, while no-gi grappling relies on body locks and underhooks.
