Outage

Crews restoring service after an outage

An outage is the interruption or loss of a service or system. The term is used in several fields:

Types of outage

  • Power outage — the loss of electric power to an area, also called a blackout, power cut, or power failure. Outages may be brief or long-lasting and can result from faults at power stations, damage to transmission lines, short circuits, or overloading of the grid. Planned outages (rolling blackouts) may be imposed to manage supply shortages.
  • Network or service outage — in computing and telecommunications, the unavailability of a network, website, or online service, often measured as downtime and governed by service-level agreements specifying uptime.
  • Planned outage — in the power and process industries, a scheduled shutdown of a plant or unit for maintenance, refuelling (in the case of nuclear power plants), inspection, or upgrades, as opposed to a forced outage caused by equipment failure.

Measurement

Utilities commonly report reliability using indices such as SAIDI (average interruption duration per customer) and SAIFI (average interruption frequency per customer). In information technology, outage impact is often expressed in terms of downtime minutes per month and the availability percentage ("nines") achieved.
