Exchange Fund (Hong Kong)

The Exchange Fund of Hong Kong is a fund established under the Exchange Fund Ordinance of 1935. Its primary purpose is to affect, directly or indirectly, the exchange value of the Hong Kong dollar; it is also used to maintain the stability and integrity of Hong Kong's monetary and financial systems. The Fund is managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) under the direction of the Financial Secretary, advised by the Exchange Fund Advisory Committee.

History

The Fund originally backed the issue of Hong Kong dollar banknotes after Hong Kong left the silver standard in 1935. In 1983, under the linked exchange rate system, the Hong Kong dollar was pegged to the US dollar at HK$7.80 = US$1.00, a rate around which the HKMA maintains a convertibility zone of 7.75–7.85. During the Asian financial crisis of 1998 the government used the Fund to buy HK$118 billion of Hong Kong stocks to deter speculators; those holdings were later disposed of through the Tracker Fund of Hong Kong (TraHK).

Structure

The Fund is divided into a Backing Portfolio, which fully backs the monetary base, and an Investment Portfolio, with a smaller Strategic Portfolio holding the TraHK. Its assets, amounting to several trillion Hong Kong dollars, make it one of the world's larger official reserve pools.
