Maurice Sendak

Maurice Bernard Sendak (10 June 1928 – 8 May 2012) was an American illustrator and writer of children's literature, widely regarded as the most important children's book artist of the 20th century. His best-known book is Where the Wild Things Are (1963), which won the Caldecott Medal and transformed the possibilities of the picture book by treating children's anger and fantasy with psychological honesty.

Early life and career

Sendak was born in Brooklyn, New York, to Polish-Jewish immigrant parents. A sickly child, he spent much time indoors reading and drawing; the kidnapping of the Lindbergh baby, the deaths of relatives in the Holocaust, and the lively immigrant relatives who visited his home all marked his imagination. After working as a window display designer and illustrating comic books, he began illustrating children's books for Harper & Row under editor Ursula Nordstrom, producing art for more than a hundred books, including Else Holmelund Minarik's Little Bear series, before writing his own.

Major works

Where the Wild Things Are tells of Max, sent to bed without supper, who sails to an island of monstrous Wild Things, becomes their king, and returns home to find his supper waiting, "and it was still hot." Initially controversial and even banned by some librarians, it became a classic, selling tens of millions of copies, and was adapted into an opera and a 2009 film directed by Spike Jonze. Sendak's other celebrated books form a loose trilogy: In the Night Kitchen (1970), a dreamlike homage to his childhood, and Outside Over There (1981). He also designed sets and costumes for operas, including Mozart's The Magic Flute and Prokofiev's Love for Three Oranges, and co-created the television series Seven Little Monsters.

Legacy

Sendak received the Hans Christian Andersen Award (1970), the National Medal of Arts (1996), and the inaugural Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award (2003). He died in Danbury, Connecticut, in 2012. His papers and original art are held by the Rosenbach Museum & Library in Philadelphia.
