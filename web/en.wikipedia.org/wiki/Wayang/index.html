Wayang

OriginJava, Indonesia
MediumShadow puppetry, rod puppets, dance-drama
RecognitionUNESCO Masterpiece of Intangible Heritage (2003)

Wayang is a traditional form of puppet theatre art found in Indonesia and other parts of Southeast Asia, wherein a dramatic story is told through shadows thrown by puppets and sometimes combined with human characters. The term derives from the Indonesian word for "shadow" or "imagination".

Forms

The best-known form, wayang kulit, uses flat puppets carved from buffalo leather, manipulated by a single master puppeteer, the dalang, behind a backlit cotton screen, accompanied by a gamelan orchestra. Other forms include wayang golek (three-dimensional wooden rod puppets, from West Java) and wayang wong, in which human dancers perform the roles. Stories are drawn largely from the Hindu epics Ramayana and Mahabharata, blended with local myths, Islamic-era tales and contemporary commentary improvised by the dalang.

Cultural significance

In 2003 UNESCO proclaimed Indonesian wayang a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. Performances traditionally last all night and serve religious, educational and entertainment purposes.
