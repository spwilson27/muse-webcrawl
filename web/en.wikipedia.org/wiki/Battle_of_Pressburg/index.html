Battle of Pressburg

The Battle of Pressburg (Hungarian: Pozsonyi csata; German: Schlacht bei Pressburg) was a three-day engagement, 4–6 July 907, in which the forces of the East Frankish (Bavarian) margrave Luitpold of Bavaria were annihilated by the army of the Hungarians (Magyars) near Pressburg (present-day Bratislava, Slovakia).

Background

After occupying the Carpathian Basin in the Hungarian conquest (c. 895), the Magyars raided deep into East Francia. In 907 Margrave Luitpold assembled a large Bavarian host, with church contingents under Archbishop Theotmar, to destroy the Hungarians and recover Pannonia.

The battle

Operating in several columns on both banks of the Danube, the Bavarians were defeated in detail over three days. The Hungarians destroyed the Bavarian fleet on the river, then overwhelmed the northern army; Luitpold, Theotmar, and Prince Sieghard were among the dead. The young king Louis the Child fled to Passau.

Aftermath

Pressburg secured Hungarian control of Pannonia and the middle Danube for generations; the East Frankish frontier fell back to the Enns River. Hungarian raids into Germany, Italy and France continued for another half-century until the victory of Otto I at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955.
