Tissue paper


Tissue paper is produced on high-speed paper machines

Tissue paper or simply tissue is a lightweight paper or, sometimes, light toilet card. Tissue can be made from recycled paper pulp or virgin fibre on a paper machine. It is characterized by low basis weight, softness, absorbency, and a creped surface that gives it stretch and bulk.

Properties and manufacture

Tissue papers weigh as little as 10–35 g/m². They are produced on Fourdrinier-type machines, often with a large heated Yankee dryer against which the sheet is pressed and then scraped off by a doctor blade — a process called creping that produces the characteristic softness and absorbency. Key qualities include softness, strength (including when wet), and the ability to disintegrate in water for sanitary products.

Uses

The principal products made from tissue are toilet paper, facial tissues, paper towels, napkins, and wrapping tissue. The global tissue business is one of the most consumer-facing branches of the pulp and paper industry, with a small number of very large machines supplying regional markets.
