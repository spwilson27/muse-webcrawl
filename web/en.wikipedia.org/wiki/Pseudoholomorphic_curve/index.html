Pseudoholomorphic curve

In mathematics, specifically in topology and geometry, a pseudoholomorphic curve (or J-holomorphic curve) is a smooth map from a Riemann surface into an almost complex manifold satisfying a generalised Cauchy–Riemann equation. Introduced by Mikhail Gromov in a foundational 1985 paper, pseudoholomorphic curves revolutionised symplectic geometry by supplying a rigorous way to "count" curves in symplectic manifolds.

Definition

Let (M, J) be an almost complex manifold and Σ a Riemann surface with complex structure j. A map u : Σ → M is pseudoholomorphic if its differential intertwines the two structures, du ∘ j = J ∘ du — that is, u is holomorphic with respect to J even when J is not integrable. When J comes from an ordinary complex structure, these are ordinary holomorphic curves.

Applications

Gromov showed that, with a compatible J, moduli spaces of pseudoholomorphic curves are well behaved: sequences of curves either converge or develop "bubbles" in a controlled way (Gromov compactness). Counting curves through specified points yields the Gromov–Witten invariants, central to enumerative geometry and string theory, and pseudoholomorphic curves underlie Floer homology and much of modern symplectic topology, including proofs that symplectic rigidity phenomena (such as Gromov's non-squeezing theorem) hold.
