Mixtec languages

The Mixtec languages are a group of closely related languages of the Oto-Manguean family spoken by the Mixtec people in the La Mixteca region of the Mexican states of Oaxaca, Puebla, and Guerrero, and by migrant communities elsewhere. Estimates of the number of distinct languages range widely (from about 20 to over 50) because many varieties are not mutually intelligible. Total speakers number in the hundreds of thousands.

Characteristics

Mixtec languages are tonal, with two or three contrastive tones, nasalisation, and complex pronoun systems. Before the Spanish conquest the Mixtecs produced famous pictographic codices such as the Codex Zouche-Nuttall. Colonial-era grammars, notably by Francisco de Alvarado (1593), documented the language of Teposcolula.

Status

All varieties are to some degree endangered under pressure from Spanish, though Mixtec is among the most widely spoken indigenous language groups in Mexico.
