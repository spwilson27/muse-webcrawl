Business rules engine

Placeholder image: schematic of a rule engine's working cycle.

A business rules engine is a software system that executes one or more business rules in a runtime production environment. The rules might come from legal regulation, company policy, or other sources; the engine separates this decision logic from the rest of an application's code so that policies can be changed without redeploying the application. Rules engines are a key component of business rule management systems (BRMS).

How it works

A rule is typically expressed as a condition–action pair ("if–then"). Facts about a case — a customer's credit score, an order value — are placed in working memory. The engine's inference engine matches facts against rule conditions and fires the rules whose conditions are satisfied, performing their actions, which may add or modify facts and trigger further rules. This cycle is called forward chaining; some engines also support backward chaining from a goal.

Algorithms

Many production-rule engines use the Rete algorithm, developed by Charles Forgy, which trades memory for speed by caching partial matches between facts and rule conditions. Well-known implementations include Drools (Java) and CLIPS.

Uses

Rules engines are used in pricing, insurance underwriting, credit decisioning, fraud detection, compliance checking and configuration, where decision criteria change frequently and must be auditable.
