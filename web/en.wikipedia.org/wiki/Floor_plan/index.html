Floor plan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In architecture and building engineering, a floor plan is a technical drawing to scale, showing a view from above of the relationships between rooms, spaces, traffic patterns and other physical features at one level of a structure. Dimensions are usually drawn between the walls to specify room sizes and wall lengths. Floor plans may also include details such as fixtures, cabinetry, doors and windows, and are one of the most fundamental documents in the design and construction of buildings.

Overview

A floor plan is an orthographic projection of a horizontal section through a building, conventionally cut about 1.2 metres (4 feet) above floor level so that doors, windows and other openings are shown. Walls are usually drawn with thick lines, openings indicated by breaks, and door swings shown as arcs. Symbols communicate stairs, plumbing fixtures, appliances and furniture layout. Similar plans exist for each storey of a building, and related drawings include site plans, reflected ceiling plans and structural framing plans.

History

Some of the oldest surviving floor plans are carved plans of buildings from ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt. The Roman architect Vitruvius described ground plans (ichnographia) as one of the three basic forms of architectural drawing. During the Renaissance, architects such as Andrea Palladio published systematic plans of villas and palaces, and by the 19th century the floor plan had become the central tool of domestic design and building regulation. Computer-aided design has since largely replaced hand drafting, and building information modelling (BIM) generates plans directly from three-dimensional models.

Floor plan design

Designing a floor plan involves arranging spaces to suit function, circulation, privacy, light and ventilation, structure and building codes. Open-plan layouts, which combine kitchen, dining and living areas, became common in residential design from the mid-20th century, contrasting with older cellular plans of separate rooms.
