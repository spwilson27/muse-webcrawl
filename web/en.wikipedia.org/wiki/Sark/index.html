Sark

Sark (French: Sercq) is an island in the Channel Islands, part of the Bailiwick of Guernsey, lying about 80 miles (130 km) south of mainland England. Roughly 3 miles long and 1.5 miles wide, with a population of around 500–600, it comprises Great Sark and Little Sark, joined by the narrow isthmus of La Coupée. Sark is famous for its car-free roads — transport is by bicycle, horse-drawn carriage and tractor — and in 2011 became the world's first designated Dark Sky Island.

Government

Sark was the last feudal state in Europe. From 1565, when Elizabeth I granted the island as a fief to Helier de Carteret, it was ruled by a hereditary Seigneur (or Dame) presiding over the Chief Pleas, its parliament of tenant landowners. Constitutional reforms in 2008 replaced the feudal legislature with a democratically elected Chief Pleas of 18 conseillers, though the Seigneur retains a ceremonial and residual role. The current Seigneur is Christopher Beaumont.

History

The island had early monastic settlement and was raided by pirates before the de Carteret colonisation. During the Second World War Sark was occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945, an experience chronicled by its Dame, Sibyl Hathaway. In 1990 the island repelled a whimsical one-man "invasion" by an unemployed French physicist, André Gardes.

Life on the island

Sark's economy rests on tourism and finance. There are no street lights, contributing to its dark-sky status, and the island holds an annual sheep race and summer festivals. The Barclay brothers, owners of the Telegraph newspapers, built a castle residence on the neighbouring island of Brecqhou, leading to long-running political and legal disputes with islanders.
