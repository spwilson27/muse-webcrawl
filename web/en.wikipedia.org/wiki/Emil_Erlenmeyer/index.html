Richard August Carl Emil Erlenmeyer (28 June 1825 – 22 January 1909) was a German chemist known for contributing to the early development of the theory of chemical structure and for devising the Erlenmeyer flask, a type of conical laboratory flask that bears his name.

Life and career

Erlenmeyer studied medicine and then pharmacy at Giessen, where he came under the influence of Justus von Liebig, and later worked in Heidelberg with August Kekulé and in Bonn. In 1868 he became professor of chemistry at the Polytechnic School in Munich, a post he held until his retirement in 1883.

Contributions

Erlenmeyer was among the first to adopt and promote Kekulé's structural formulae, using them in his influential textbook teaching. He proposed that naphthalene consists of two fused benzene rings — a structure soon confirmed — and formulated the Erlenmeyer rule, which states that alcohols with a hydroxyl group directly attached to a double-bonded carbon (enols) tautomerise to the corresponding aldehyde or ketone. In 1861 he designed the conical flask that made his name familiar in every chemistry laboratory.
