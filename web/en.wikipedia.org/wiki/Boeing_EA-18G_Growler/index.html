Boeing EA-18G Growler

The Boeing EA-18G Growler is an American carrier-based electronic warfare aircraft, a specialized version of the two-seat F/A-18F Super Hornet. It replaced the Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowler in service with the United States Navy. The EA-18G's electronic warfare systems were developed mainly by Northrop Grumman; the aircraft is produced by Boeing and entered service in 2009, first deploying operationally with squadron VAQ-132 in Iraq in 2010–2011.

Design and equipment

The Growler shares over 90% commonality with the Super Hornet. Its gun is replaced by electronics bays, and its wingtip missile rails carry AN/ALQ-218 receiver pods. The centerpiece of its mission fit is the AN/ALQ-99 tactical jamming system pods (being supplemented and partly replaced by the Next Generation Jammer, AN/ALQ-249), along with the AN/ALQ-227 communications countermeasures set, satellite communications, and an interference cancellation system allowing the crew to communicate while jamming. The two-person crew — a pilot and an electronic countermeasures officer — can conduct escort and standoff jamming, suppression of enemy air defenses (SEAD), and signals surveillance. The aircraft retains the APG-79 AESA radar and can carry AIM-120 AMRAAMs for self-defense and AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missiles.

Operators and service

The U.S. Navy operates Growlers in electronic attack (VAQ) squadrons embarked on carriers and in expeditionary squadrons deployed ashore, including in support of operations in Iraq, Syria (against ISIS), and the Red Sea. Australia, the only export customer, acquired 11 EA-18Gs (plus a twelfth to replace one lost in an accident) operated by No. 6 Squadron RAAF from Amberley, receiving them from 2017. US procurement totaled about 160 aircraft. The Growler's first combat use was during Operation Odyssey Dawn over Libya in 2011.

Specifications

Crew: 2. Length: 17.0 m (56 ft); wingspan: 13.6 m (44 ft 8.5 in); height: 4.9 m (16 ft). Maximum takeoff weight: 29,964 kg (66,000 lb). Powerplant: 2 × General Electric F414-GE-400 turbofans, 22,000 lbf (98 kN) each with afterburner. Maximum speed: Mach 1.8 (1,915 km/h). Range: 2,346 km (1,458 mi); combat radius about 722 km. Service ceiling: 15,000 m (50,000 ft). Armament: up to 13,700 lb of stores on 9 hardpoints, typically jamming pods, AGM-88 HARM, AIM-120, and external fuel tanks.
