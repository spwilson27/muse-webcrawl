Kingdom of Jerusalem

The Kingdom of Jerusalem, also known as the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem, was a Crusader state established in the Levant after the First Crusade in 1099. It lasted in some form until the fall of Acre in 1291. At its height in the mid-12th century it stretched from Lebanon in the north to the Sinai and Transjordan in the south.

Its first ruler was Godfrey of Bouillon, styled Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre; his brother Baldwin I was the first crowned king. Successive kings Baldwin II, Fulk, Baldwin III, Amalric and the leper king Baldwin IV defended the kingdom against Muslim neighbours. After Saladin's victory at Hattin in 1187 Jerusalem itself was lost, though the Third Crusade restored a coastal kingdom centred later on Acre.

Society

The kingdom was ruled by a Frankish elite over a diverse population of local Christians, Muslims, Jews and Eastern Christians, governed through feudal institutions, the Haute Cour, and military orders such as the Knights Templar and Hospitallers.
