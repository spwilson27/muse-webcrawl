Heijō-kyō

Heijō-kyō (平城京, also Heijōkyō) was the capital city of Japan during most of the Nara period, from 710 to 740 and again from 745 to 784. The palace site, in what is now the city of Nara, is a listed UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara". It is one of the Historical Sites of Japan and among the best-preserved records of an early Japanese planned capital.

Layout

The city was modelled on Chang'an, the capital of Tang-dynasty China, and laid out on a grid plan (the jōbō system) divided by the north–south Suzaku Avenue, which ran from the Suzaku Gate to the Rashōmon gate in the south. The imperial palace (Heijō Palace) occupied the northern end of the city. The eastern and western halves of the city (Sakyō and Ukyō) each had markets, temples, and aristocratic residences; the population is estimated at around 100,000–200,000 at its height.

History

Empress Genmei moved the capital from Fujiwara-kyō to Heijō-kyō in 710. The city flourished as the political and cultural centre of the ritsuryō state, with great temples such as Tōdai-ji, Kōfuku-ji, and Yakushi-ji. The capital moved several times between 740 and 745 before returning to Heijō, and in 784 Emperor Kanmu relocated it to Nagaoka-kyō and then, in 794, to Heian-kyō (Kyoto). The palace grounds were later farmed over, which preserved the site archaeologically; excavations since the twentieth century have uncovered the palace layout, and structures such as the Suzaku Gate and the Former Imperial Audience Hall have been reconstructed.
