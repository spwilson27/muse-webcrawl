Michael Clark Rockefeller (18 May 1938 – disappeared 19 November 1961; declared dead in 1964) was a member of the wealthy Rockefeller family and the youngest son of New York governor and later U.S. vice president Nelson Rockefeller. He disappeared in Dutch New Guinea while collecting art of the Asmat people; his body was never found, and his fate remains one of the 20th century's enduring mysteries.

Expedition and disappearance

After graduating from Harvard, Rockefeller joined an expedition filmed by the Peabody Museum and then returned to New Guinea to collect Asmat art for the Museum of Primitive Art in New York, founded by his father. On 17 November 1961 the catamaran carrying Rockefeller and Dutch anthropologist René Wassing capsized near the mouth of the Eilanden River. Rockefeller set out to swim to shore, several kilometres away; he was never seen again. Wassing was rescued; an enormous search found no trace.

Theories

The official conclusion was that Rockefeller drowned or was taken by a shark or crocodile. Journalist Carl Hoffman, in his 2014 book Savage Harvest, presented evidence from missionary accounts and Asmat testimony that Rockefeller reached shore and was killed by men of the village of Otsjanep, possibly in retaliation for a violent Dutch patrol action in 1958. The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Michael C. Rockefeller Collection of Asmat and other Oceanic art, now in its Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, preserves the collection he helped assemble.
