Dowlais Ironworks

The Dowlais Ironworks was a major iron and steel works at Dowlais near Merthyr Tydfil in South Wales. Founded in 1759, it grew during the Industrial Revolution into one of the largest ironworks in the world, employing around 5,000 people at its peak in the 1840s.

History

The works began with a partnership that included Isaac Wilkinson and John Guest; the Guest family became dominant owners, and under Josiah John Guest in the early 19th century Dowlais became famous for producing rails for the rapidly expanding railway networks of Britain, Europe, Russia and North America. Lady Charlotte Guest, his wife, was a noted translator of the Welsh Mabinogion and an advocate for workers' education.

Innovation

Dowlais was an early adopter of new technology: it used steam power extensively, and in the 1850s–60s it worked with the Bessemer process for steelmaking. In 1865 the company built what was then the most powerful rolling mill engine in the world. The works later moved steel production to East Moors, Cardiff, while iron production continued at Dowlais.

Decline

The works became part of Guest, Keen and Nettlefolds (GKN) in 1902 and later passed through nationalised British Steel. Production wound down over the 20th century and the site finally closed in 1987. Little survives above ground, though the Dowlais Engine House has been restored as a community centre.
