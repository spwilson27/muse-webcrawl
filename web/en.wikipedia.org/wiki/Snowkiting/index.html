Snowkiting

Snowkiting or kite skiing is an outdoor winter sport in which a person uses a large controllable kite to pull themselves across snow or ice while wearing skis or a snowboard. It is related to kitesurfing on water but adapted to frozen terrain such as lakes, fields, and mountain slopes.

Equipment

Snowkiters use foil or inflatable kites similar to those used in kitesurfing, connected by lines to a control bar and a harness worn by the rider. Because snow offers less resistance than water, smaller kites can generate sufficient pull.

Techniques

Riders can travel upwind and downwind, jump using the kite's lift, and in mountainous terrain use the kite to ascend slopes — a practice known as kitecrossing or gliding when combined with downhill descents. Speeds can be considerable on flat ice.

Safety

Hazards include sudden gusts, thin ice, obstacles hidden under snow, and the risk of being lifted unexpectedly. Helmets and knowledge of local conditions are recommended.
