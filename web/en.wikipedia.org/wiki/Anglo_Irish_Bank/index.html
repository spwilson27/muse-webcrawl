Anglo Irish Bank was an Irish bank headquartered in Dublin. Once one of Ireland's largest lenders, particularly to property developers during the Celtic Tiger boom, it was nationalised in January 2009 during the Irish financial crisis and became emblematic of the country's banking collapse.

History

Founded in 1964, the bank grew rapidly in the 1990s and 2000s under chief executive Seán FitzPatrick by specialising in commercial property lending. Its aggressive growth made its loan book heavily concentrated in Irish and British property.

Crisis and nationalisation

In 2008 a scandal over concealed directors' loans forced FitzPatrick's resignation, and the bank's share price collapsed. The Irish government nationalised the bank in January 2009. Its eventual cost to the Irish state was estimated at around €29–34 billion, a major driver of Ireland's sovereign debt crisis and EU–IMF bailout.

Liquidation

In 2011 Anglo Irish was merged with Irish Nationwide Building Society to form the Irish Bank Resolution Corporation (IBRC), which was put into special liquidation in February 2013. Several former executives were later convicted of offences including conspiracy to defraud and misleading auditors.
