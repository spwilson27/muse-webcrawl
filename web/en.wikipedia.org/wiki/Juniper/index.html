Juniper

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Juniperus communis]

Junipers are coniferous trees and shrubs in the genus Juniperus of the cypress family Cupressaceae. Depending on the taxonomy, between 50 and 67 species are recognised, widely distributed throughout the Northern Hemisphere from the Arctic to tropical Africa and Central America.

Description

Junipers vary from tall trees to low spreading shrubs. Their leaves are of two forms: juvenile needle-like leaves and adult scale-like leaves. The female cones are distinctive fleshy, berry-like structures — "juniper berries" — which are actually fused cones, taking 18 months to three years to ripen in some species.

Uses

Juniper berries are the principal flavouring of gin, and are used as a spice in European cuisine, particularly with game. The aromatic, durable wood is used for carving and for cedar chests; incense and traditional medicine also employ parts of the plant.
