Plant quarantine

Plant quarantine is the set of legal and phytosanitary measures by which governments restrict the movement of plants, plant products, soil, and related materials in order to prevent the introduction and spread of pests and diseases that are absent from, or under official control in, an area.

Practices

Quarantine measures include import permits and prohibitions, phytosanitary certificates issued by the exporting country, inspection at ports of entry, post-entry quarantine growing in isolation for observation, and treatments such as fumigation, hot-water immersion, or irradiation. Domestic quarantines likewise restrict movement out of infested regions within a country.

Framework

Internationally, plant quarantine is coordinated under the International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC), whose standards govern pest risk analysis and certification; national plant protection organizations enforce the rules. Historic failures of exclusion — such as the spread of grape phylloxera and chestnut blight — motivated the first modern plant quarantine laws in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
