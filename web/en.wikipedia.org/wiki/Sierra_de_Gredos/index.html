Sierra de Gredos

The Sierra de Gredos is a mountain range in the centre of the Iberian Peninsula, part of Spain's Sistema Central. It spans the provinces of Ávila, Cáceres, Madrid and Toledo. Its highest point is Pico Almanzor, at 2,592 metres (8,504 ft), the highest summit of the entire Central System. Much of the range is protected as the Sierra de Gredos Regional Park.

Geography

The range runs roughly east–west for about 120 km. It consists chiefly of granite, deeply sculpted by Pleistocene glaciers, which carved cirques, arêtes, and glacial lakes (lagunas), most famously the Circo de Gredos with its Laguna Grande beneath Almanzor. The northern slopes descend gently to the Duero basin, while the southern face falls steeply — more than 1,800 metres in places — to the Tiétar valley in Extremadura, giving the range one of the sharpest reliefs in Spain.

Flora and fauna

Gredos is noted for endemism, a product of its glacial refuges. Its emblematic animal is the Gredos ibex (Capra pyrenaica victoriae), a subspecies of Iberian ibex once hunted nearly to extinction but protected in the former royal hunting reserve established in 1905, and now numerous. Other fauna include griffon and black vultures, Spanish imperial eagles, roe deer, and endemic amphibians such as the Gredos toad. Mountain flora includes broom and piorno scrub, alpine meadows, and endemic species such as the Gredos snapdragon.

Recreation

The range is a popular destination for mountaineering, climbing, hiking and ski touring, with refuges such as Elola in the Circo de Gredos serving climbers attempting Almanzor. The Parador de Gredos, opened in 1928, was the first of Spain's state-run parador hotels.
