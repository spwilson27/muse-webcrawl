Jaigarh Fort

Hill fort above Amber, near Jaipur, Rajasthan, India

Jaigarh Fort
Jaigarh Fort, the "Fort of Victory", above Amber
LocationAravalli Hills, near Amber, Jaipur district, Rajasthan, India
Built1726, by Maharaja Jai Singh II
Known forJaivana cannon; cannon foundry; treasury of the Kachhwaha rulers

Jaigarh Fort is a hill fort situated on the promontory called Cheel ka Teela ("Hill of Eagles") in the Aravalli range, overlooking the Amber Fort and the Maota Lake below, near Jaipur in Rajasthan, India. Built in 1726 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II to protect Amber and its palace complex, it is known as the "Fort of Victory". Unlike the ornate Amber palace below, Jaigarh is a stern military structure of red sandstone, connected to Amber Fort by subterranean passages.

The Jaivana cannon and foundry

Jaigarh was a major centre of cannon production for the Kachhwaha Rajput rulers, with its own foundry — one of the few preserved in India — made possible by the iron-rich mines of the region. Its most famous product is the Jaivana cannon, cast in 1720, which at the time was the world's largest cannon on wheels: about 6.15 metres long and weighing some 50 tons. By legend it was fired only once, its 100 kg ball landing some 35 km away; its enormous recoil and charge required 100 kg of gunpowder.

Features and legend

The fort contains palaces, temples, gardens, granaries, an armoury museum, and one of the world's earliest giant water-harvesting systems, with vast underground tanks holding millions of litres beneath its courtyards. Legend long held that the fabulous treasure of the Kachhwaha kings was buried within Jaigarh; in 1976–77, during the Indian Emergency, the fort was famously searched by government order — an episode that remains part of its lore, though no treasure was officially found.

Today

Jaigarh is open to visitors and is toured together with Amber Fort and the neighbouring Nahargarh Fort, offering panoramic views over Jaipur and the surrounding hills.
