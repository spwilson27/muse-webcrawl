Higher Education Act of 1965

The Higher Education Act of 1965 (HEA) is a United States federal law designed to strengthen the educational resources of colleges and universities and to provide financial assistance for students in postsecondary and higher education. Signed into law by President Lyndon B. Johnson on November 8, 1965, at Southwest Texas State College (now Texas State University), it was part of Johnson's Great Society domestic agenda.

History

The act created grants, loans, and other programs to help students pay for college. It established the federal student financial aid programs that would later include the Pell Grant and federal student loans. The law is reauthorized periodically by Congress, with major reauthorizations in 1968, 1972, 1976, 1980, 1986, 1992, 1998, and 2008.

Provisions

The act is divided into titles addressing different aspects of higher education, including teacher quality, library resources, aid to developing institutions, student assistance, and facilities. Title IV, covering student financial assistance, is the largest and best-known part of the law and governs most federal student aid programs.
