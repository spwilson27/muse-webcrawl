Superstation

A superstation is a television station that is retransmitted via satellite to cable and satellite subscribers nationwide in the United States, beyond its local broadcast market. The term became common in the late 1970s and 1980s.

History

The concept was pioneered in 1976 when Ted Turner's WTCG in Atlanta (later WTBS) was uplinked to satellite and distributed to cable systems across the country, becoming the first superstation. Others followed, including WGN in Chicago, WOR/WWOR in New York, WPIX, KTVT in Dallas, and KTLA in Los Angeles.

Regulation and decline

Superstations operated under a statutory copyright licence allowing retransmission, while "syndication exclusivity" rules allowed local stations to black out duplicated programming. As national cable networks grew and satellite feeds diverged from local broadcasts, the classic superstation model faded; qualifying US superstations are now few, though the term persists in satellite television law.
