The politics of Austria take place within the framework of a federal parliamentary republic. Austria's political system has been characterised, since the re-establishment of the republic in 1945, by the principle of Proporz (proportional distribution of influence between the two traditional major parties), by strong social partnership between organised labour and business, and by coalition government.

Constitutional framework

The Federal Constitutional Law (Bundes-Verfassungsgesetz, B-VG) of 1920, revised in 1929 and restored in 1945, is the core of a fragmented body of constitutional law. Austria is a federation of nine states (Länder). The Constitutional Court (Verfassungsgerichtshof), established in 1920 on the ideas of Hans Kelsen, was the first court of its kind with the power to annul legislation on constitutional grounds.

Executive

The head of state is the Federal President (Bundespräsident), directly elected for a six-year term (renewable once). The President formally appoints the Federal Chancellor and, on the Chancellor's advice, the other ministers; in practice the President's role is largely ceremonial, though the office retains significant reserve powers. The head of government is the Federal Chancellor (Bundeskanzler), usually the leader of the largest party in the governing coalition. The federal government (Bundesregierung) is accountable to the National Council, which can dismiss it by a vote of no confidence.

Legislature

Federal legislative power is exercised by the Parliament, consisting of the National Council (Nationalrat) with 183 members elected by proportional representation for up to five years, and the Federal Council (Bundesrat), whose members are delegated by the state parliaments and whose veto over most legislation is merely suspensive. The voting age for national elections was lowered to 16 in 2007, making Austria the first EU country to adopt that threshold.

Political parties

The party system was dominated for decades by the Social Democratic Party of Austria (SPÖ) and the Austrian People's Party (ÖVP), which governed together in grand coalitions for much of the postwar era. The Freedom Party of Austria (FPÖ), founded in 1956, grew into a major force, entering government in 1983 and in 2000 (provoking EU diplomatic sanctions), and again in 2017. The Greens entered the National Council in 1986 and government in 2020; NEOS – The New Austria and Liberal Forum, founded in 2012, represents liberal positions. Other parties represented at times include the Communist Party of Austria (KPÖ), the BZÖ and the Team Stronach. The FPÖ won a National Council election for the first time in September 2024, though coalition negotiations proved difficult.

Social partnership

A distinctive feature of Austrian politics is the Sozialpartnerschaft, the institutionalised cooperation of the Chamber of Labour, the Austrian Trade Union Federation (ÖGB), the Federal Economic Chamber and the Chamber of Agriculture, which historically influenced wage bargaining, prices and social legislation.

Foreign policy

Austria regained full sovereignty by the State Treaty of 1955 and adopted permanent neutrality. It joined the European Union in 1995 but is not a member of NATO.
