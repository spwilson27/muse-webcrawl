Richard Attenborough

Richard Samuel Attenborough, Baron Attenborough (29 August 1923 – 24 August 2014) was an English actor, film director and producer. As a director he won two Academy Awards for Gandhi (1982), including Best Picture and Best Director. As an actor he is remembered for roles in Brighton Rock (1948), The Great Escape (1963), The Sand Pebbles (1966), Doctor Dolittle (1967), and as John Hammond in Jurassic Park (1993). He was the elder brother of naturalist Sir David Attenborough.

Career

Attenborough trained at RADA and made his film debut in In Which We Serve (1942). After wartime service in the RAF Film Unit, his breakthrough came playing the young gangster Pinkie Brown in Brighton Rock. With Bryan Forbes he co-founded Beaver Films and Allied Film Makers, producing films such as The Angry Silence and Whistle Down the Wind.

Directing

His directorial debut was the musical Oh! What a Lovely War (1969). He spent years raising finance for Gandhi, which won eight Academy Awards. Other directorial credits include A Bridge Too Far (1977), Cry Freedom (1987), Chaplin (1992) and Shadowlands (1993).

Honours

Attenborough was knighted in 1976 and created a life peer in 1993. He served as president of BAFTA and chairman of Channel 4 and the British Film Institute, and was a lifelong supporter of Chelsea Football Club.
