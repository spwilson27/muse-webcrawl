Open tuning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An open tuning is a tuning of a stringed instrument in which the open strings form a chord, most often a major chord. In an open tuning, a simple barré across all strings at any fret produces a new chord, and chords can often be played with one finger, which makes open tunings particularly suited to slide guitar. Open tunings are widely used in blues, folk, country, and rock music.

Common open tunings

For the six-string guitar, the most common open tunings (listed from the lowest-pitched string to the highest) include:

  • Open G — D–G–D–G–B–D. Associated with blues and with Keith Richards of the Rolling Stones, who often removed the sixth string. The traditional Russian seven-string guitar is also tuned to an open G chord.
  • Open D — D–A–D–F♯–A–D. Popular in folk and blues; closely related to open E (E–B–E–G♯–B–E), which raises the same pattern by a whole step and is used extensively in electric blues, though its higher string tension leads many players to use open D with a capo instead.
  • Open C — C–G–C–G–C–E. Used in folk and by artists such as John Fahey.
  • Open A and open E variants, historically used in early blues and Hawaiian music.

Technique and uses

Open tunings change the geometry of chords on the fretboard, making some shapes easier and others impractical. They allow full chords to be played with a slide (bottleneck) laid across the strings, a defining sound of Delta blues players such as Robert Johnson and of later rock slide guitarists like Duane Allman. They also facilitate drone strings, resonant open-string accompaniment, and fingerstyle patterns; folk musicians use them for modal and droning textures. Related tunings that do not form a full chord, such as DADGAD, are sometimes classed with open tunings because they are used in a similar spirit.
