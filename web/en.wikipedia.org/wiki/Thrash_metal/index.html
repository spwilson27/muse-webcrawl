Thrash metal

[Hero image placeholder: a thrash metal band performing live with a circle pit in front of the stage.]

Thrash metal (or simply thrash) is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music characterised by its overall aggression and fast tempo. The songs usually use fast percussive beats and low-register guitar riffs, overlaid with shredding-style lead work. Lyrical themes frequently deal with war, corruption, social injustice and violence.

History

Thrash emerged in the early 1980s, drawing on the New Wave of British Heavy Metal and the speed and ethos of hardcore punk. The most commercially successful bands are the American "Big Four" — Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth and Anthrax — alongside significant scenes in Germany (Kreator, Sodom, Destruction), Brazil (Sepultura) and the San Francisco Bay Area (Exodus, Testament, Death Angel). Landmark albums include Metallica's Master of Puppets and Slayer's Reign in Blood, both 1986.

Influence

Thrash metal was a direct precursor of death metal and black metal, and its aggressive, virtuosic style influenced groove metal, metalcore and the alternative metal of the 1990s. A revival movement in the 2000s brought a new generation of thrash bands to prominence.
