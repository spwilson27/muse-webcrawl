Chandrayaan-2

Chandrayaan-2 (Sanskrit: candra-yāna, "mooncraft") is the second lunar exploration mission of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), launched on 22 July 2019. The mission consisted of a lunar orbiter, the Vikram lander, and the Pragyan rover, aiming to explore the Moon's south polar region. The orbiter entered lunar orbit successfully and operated for years beyond its planned life, but the Vikram lander deviated from its descent trajectory on 6 September 2019 and was lost in a hard landing near the lunar south pole; the rover was destroyed with it.

Mission profile

After an initial launch postponement due to a technical snag, Chandrayaan-2 lifted off aboard India's heavy LVM3 rocket. Following a series of Earth- and Moon-bound manoeuvres, it entered a 100 km polar lunar orbit. On 2 September the Vikram lander separated from the orbiter and began its descent sequence.

During the powered descent on 6 September 2019, contact was lost when the lander was about 2.1 km above the surface; it had deviated from its planned trajectory during the fine-braking phase. In December 2019, NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter imaging team, aided by an Indian amateur observer, Shanmuga Subramanian, located the debris field and impact site. ISRO later attributed the failure to a software glitch that caused thrust and attitude deviations beyond the craft's control limits.

Orbiter science

The orbiter itself was a major success. Carrying eight instruments — including the Terrain Mapping Camera-2, a large-area soft X-ray spectrometer (CLASS), an imaging infrared spectrometer, a dual-frequency synthetic aperture radar, and the CHACE-2 mass spectrometer — it mapped the lunar surface and minerals, studied water ice in polar craters, measured the lunar exosphere, and reported evidence concerning hydroxyl and water molecules on the surface. Designed for one year, the orbiter had enough propellant to operate for several additional years and later supported Chandrayaan-3 as a communications relay.

Legacy

Despite the lander's loss, the mission was widely described as largely successful (ISRO's chairman put mission success at 95%). Lessons from Vikram's failure were incorporated into Chandrayaan-3, which achieved a successful soft landing near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to land in the south polar region.
