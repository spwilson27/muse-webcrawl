Longships Lighthouse

LocationLand's End, Cornwall, England
First lit1795 (current tower 1875)
Height35 m (115 ft)
Range11 nautical miles

Longships Lighthouse is an active 19th-century lighthouse on Carn Bras, the largest of the Longships islets off Land's End in Cornwall, England. The islands lie about 2 kilometres (1.2 miles) west of Land's End, the most westerly point of mainland England, in an area notorious for shipwrecks.

History

The first lighthouse on the site was built privately in 1795 under licence from Trinity House, designed by Samuel Wyatt. Its fixed white light was frequently obscured by heavy seas breaking over the tower, and in 1870–1875 the present, taller grey granite tower was constructed on the rock, rising 35 metres above the water. The lighthouse was automated in 1988 and is now monitored from the Trinity House operations centre at Harwich.

Character

The light shows two white flashes approximately every ten seconds and has a nominal range of about 11 nautical miles. A fog signal sounds when visibility is poor. Despite the lighthouse, the waters around the Longships have seen many wrecks, and the name is sometimes linked in local tradition to the Arthurian legend of Lyonesse.
