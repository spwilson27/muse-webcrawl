A trade association, also known as an industry trade group, business association, sector association or industry body, is an organisation founded and funded by businesses that operate in a specific industry. Trade associations engage in public relations activities such as advertising, education, publishing, lobbying and political action, but their focus is collaboration between companies and standardisation.

Activities

Nearly all trade associations are non-profit organisations. Their activities typically include providing a forum for members (including conferences and networking), publishing industry statistics and market research, setting technical and quality standards, offering training and certification, and representing the industry's interests to governments and regulators. Many also run trade fairs and publish trade journals.

History

Trade associations descend in part from medieval guilds and early chambers of commerce. They proliferated in the late 19th and 20th centuries as industrialisation created distinct sectors; in the United States alone there are several thousand national trade associations, and the American Society of Association Executives counts tens of thousands of trade and professional associations overall.

Criticism

Associations are sometimes criticised for facilitating anti-competitive behaviour, for standard-setting that entrenches incumbents, or for diluting members' individual positions through lowest-common-denominator lobbying. Coordination on price or market sharing by members can violate antitrust law, so associations usually operate under strict compliance guidelines.
