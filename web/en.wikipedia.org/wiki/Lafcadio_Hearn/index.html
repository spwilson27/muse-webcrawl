Lafcadio Hearn

Patrick Lafcadio Hearn (27 June 1850 – 26 September 1904), known in Japan as Koizumi Yakumo (小泉 八雲), was a Greek-Irish writer, translator, and teacher who introduced the culture and literature of Japan to the Western world. He is best known for his writings about Japan, including collections of Japanese legends and ghost stories such as Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things (1904).

Early life

Hearn was born on the Greek island of Lefkada to a Greek mother and an Irish army surgeon father. He was raised in Ireland, lost the sight in one eye in a school accident, and emigrated to the United States at the age of nineteen, where he worked as a journalist in Cincinnati and New Orleans.

Career

In New Orleans Hearn wrote about Creole culture and Louisiana folklore. He later spent two years in the French West Indies before travelling to Japan in 1890 as a newspaper correspondent. There he married Koizumi Setsu, took Japanese citizenship, and taught English literature, eventually lecturing at Tokyo Imperial University.

Works

  • Glimpses of Unfamiliar Japan (1894)
  • Kokoro: Hints and Echoes of Japanese Inner Life (1896)
  • Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things (1904)
  • Japan: An Attempt at Interpretation (1904)

Legacy

Hearn's books remain among the most influential English-language interpretations of Meiji-era Japan. His former residence in Matsue and the Lafcadio Hearn Memorial Museum preserve his memory in Japan, where his retellings of folk tales are still widely read.
