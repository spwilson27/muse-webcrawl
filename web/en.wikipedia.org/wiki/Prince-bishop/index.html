Prince-bishop

A prince-bishop (German: Fürstbischof) is a bishop who is also the civil ruler of some secular principality and sovereignty, as well as the spiritual head of his diocese. The principality ruled politically by a prince-bishop could wholly or largely overlap his diocese, since both were typically in the same geographic area, but his civil jurisdiction could extend further.

Holy Roman Empire

Prince-bishops were most prominent in the Holy Roman Empire, where dozens of prince-bishoprics (Hochstifte) existed, including the great ecclesiastical electorates of Mainz, Cologne and Trier, whose archbishops were prince-electors and arch-chancellors of the Empire. Others included Würzburg, Bamberg, Salzburg (a prince-archbishopric), Liège, Münster, Osnabrück and Passau. These states survived until the great secularisation of 1803, when nearly all were mediatised and absorbed by larger secular states.

Elsewhere

In England, the Bishops of Durham were "prince-bishops" or Counts Palatine, wielding extensive secular powers in the County Palatine of Durham until the 19th century. The Bishop of Andorra remains, as co-prince, a joint head of state of that country. Historically, prince-bishops also ruled Montenegro (the prince-bishops or vladikas of Cetinje) and territories in the Baltic and the Low Countries.
