LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE, commonly known as LVMH, is a French multinational luxury goods conglomerate headquartered in Paris. It is the world's largest luxury group, controlling some 75 "houses" across fashion, wines and spirits, perfumes and cosmetics, watches and jewellery, and selective retailing.

History

LVMH was formed in 1987 by the merger of fashion house Louis Vuitton (founded 1854) with Moët Hennessy, itself a 1971 union of champagne maker Moët & Chandon and cognac producer Hennessy. A boardroom battle in the late 1980s brought in Bernard Arnault, who had quietly accumulated control and became chairman in 1989 — beginning decades of acquisitions.

Arnault built the group by buying heritage brands and reviving them with star designers and tight control of distribution: Dior (via his earlier holding), Fendi, Kenzo, Bulgari (2011), Loro Piana (2013), Rimowa, and the jeweller Tiffany & Co., acquired for about $15.8 billion in 2021 after a bruising public fight. A celebrated failed episode was the 2010–2014 stealth stake in Hermès, which the founding family rebuffed.

Operations

Fashion and leather goods — dominated by Louis Vuitton and Christian Dior Couture — supply roughly half of revenue and most profit. The group operates thousands of directly run stores and trades on the strength of Asian and American demand for luxury; it periodically rivals Europe's most valuable listed companies by market capitalisation.
