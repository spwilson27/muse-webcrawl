Casting

In casting, liquid material is poured into a mould and allowed to solidify.

Casting is a manufacturing process in which a liquid material is poured into a mould containing a hollow cavity of the desired shape and allowed to solidify. The solidified part, also called a casting, is removed from the mould and may be finished by machining or other processes. Casting is one of the oldest manufacturing methods, used for millennia to produce metal objects, and it remains essential because it can form complex shapes economically, including shapes that would be difficult to machine.

Process

Metal casting typically involves melting the alloy, preparing a mould from sand, ceramic, plaster, or metal, designing runners and risers to feed the cavity and compensate for shrinkage, pouring, cooling, shakeout, and cleaning. Patterns used to form moulds are made slightly oversize to allow for contraction. Casting defects such as porosity, inclusions, and misruns are controlled through melt practice, mould design, and simulation.

Types

Common processes include sand casting, investment (lost-wax) casting, die casting, centrifugal casting, and continuous casting. Expendable-mould processes destroy the mould for each part, while permanent metal moulds are reused many times.

Materials and applications

Cast materials include iron, steel, aluminium, bronze, and brass, as well as plastics and concrete in analogous processes. Applications range from engine blocks and pipes to statues, jewellery, and turbine components.
