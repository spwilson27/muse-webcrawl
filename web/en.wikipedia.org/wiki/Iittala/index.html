Iittala

Founded: 1881, Iittala, Finland

Industry: Glassware, tableware, home design

Parent: Fiskars Group

Iittala is a Finnish design brand specialising in glassware, tableware and home furnishings, founded in 1881 as a glassworks in the village of Iittala in southern Finland. It is part of the Fiskars Group and is internationally recognised as a flagship of Scandinavian design.

History

The glass factory was established by Swedish glassblower Petrus Magnus Abrahamsson. In the 1930s the company turned decisively toward design-led production, collaborating with designers such as Alvar Aalto, whose undulating Savoy vase (1936) — the "Aalto vase" — remains an icon of Finnish modernism, and Aino Aalto, whose pressed-glass tumblers won a gold medal at the 1936 Milan Triennale.

Designers and products

Iittala's post-war golden age featured Kaj Franck, who stripped tableware down to essentials in the Kilta (later Teema) range; Timo Sarpaneva, creator of the i-linja and the Orkidea vase; Tapio Wirkkala, known for the Ultima Thule glassware inspired by melting ice; and Oiva Toikka, whose Birds by Toikka glass birds, produced from the 1970s onward, became collectible art objects. Other enduring lines include Kastehelmi (by Toikka), Kartio (Franck) and the cast-iron Sarpaneva pot. The brand's distinctive lower-case "i" logo dates from 1956.
