Personal statement

A personal statement is an essay written by an applicant as part of an application for university admission, graduate or professional school, a job, or a scholarship. Its purpose is to present the applicant's background, motivations, achievements, and suitability in their own voice, complementing grades, test scores, and transcripts.

Usage

In the United Kingdom, applicants through UCAS submit a single personal statement (limited to 4,000 characters) sent to all their chosen universities; from the 2026 entry cycle UCAS replaced the free-form statement with structured questions. In the United States, the Common Application essay and individual college supplements serve a similar role, and personal statements are central to applications for medical, law, and graduate schools. Statements of purpose, a related graduate-school document, focus more narrowly on research interests and fit with a programme.

Content and style

Advisers typically recommend explaining one's motivation for the subject, evidence of relevant study or experience, skills, and future goals, written clearly and personally rather than as a list of achievements. Integrity rules prohibit fabrication and, increasingly, institutions issue guidance on the acceptable use of editing help and AI tools.

Criticism

Research suggests personal statements can advantage applicants with access to coaching and well-resourced schools, prompting reforms — such as UCAS's move to structured questions — intended to level the field.
