Videophone

A videophone is a telephone with a video camera and display, capable of simultaneous video and audio communication with the person on the other end. It is also commonly referred to as a video telephone or a videotelephone. The concept was popularised early in the development of the television and the telephone; concepts spanned the late 19th century to the early 1930s, when both basic concepts began to be used together in earnest.

AT&T's Picturephone, demonstrated in 1964

History

AT&T developed the Picturephone, shown publicly at the 1964 New York World's Fair, and launched a commercial videophone service in Pittsburgh in 1970. The service was a commercial failure due to high cost, bulky equipment and low picture quality. Various European and Japanese systems followed, including videophones used for the deaf community via video relay services. Dedicated videophones remained a niche product for decades.

Modern development

The rise of broadband internet, webcams and smartphones transformed video calling into a mainstream feature of general-purpose devices. Services such as Skype, FaceTime, Zoom and Microsoft Teams effectively absorbed the functions of the standalone videophone. Dedicated desktop videophones are still used in some business, healthcare and accessibility contexts.

Protocols

Traditional videophones used standards such as H.320 over ISDN and H.323 or SIP over IP networks. Modern systems rely on internet protocols with codecs such as H.264 and VP8/VP9 for video compression.
