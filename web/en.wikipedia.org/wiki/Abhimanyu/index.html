Abhimanyu is a warrior in the Hindu epic Mahabharata, the son of the Pandava prince Arjuna and the Yadava princess Subhadra, sister of Krishna. Renowned for his courage and skill, he is best remembered for his heroic death on the thirteenth day of the Kurukshetra War, when he broke into the Kaurava army's discus formation, the Chakravyuha.

Legend of the Chakravyuha

According to the epic, while still in his mother's womb Abhimanyu overheard Krishna describing how to penetrate the Chakravyuha but fell asleep (or Subhadra fell asleep) before hearing how to escape it. On the thirteenth day of the war, with Arjuna drawn away by another battle, the sixteen-year-old Abhimanyu used his partial knowledge to breach the formation, fighting alone against many great warriors, including Drona and Karna.

Death

Surrounded and disarmed, Abhimanyu is said to have fought on with a chariot wheel before being killed — struck down, in the most common telling, by a mace blow from Dushasana's son Durmashana after being attacked simultaneously by many warriors, in violation of the rules of righteous warfare. His death enraged Arjuna, who vowed to kill Jayadratha, the king who had prevented the other Pandavas from following Abhimanyu into the formation.

Legacy

Abhimanyu's widow Uttarā bore his posthumous son Parikshit, through whom the Kuru line continued. Abhimanyu remains a popular symbol of youthful valour and sacrifice in Indian culture.
