Catawba Nuclear Station

Country: United States
Location: York County, South Carolina
Operator: Duke Energy
Units operational: 2 × 1,160 MW
Nameplate capacity: 2,320 MW

The Catawba Nuclear Station is a nuclear power plant located on a 391-acre (158 ha) peninsula, called "Concord Peninsula", that reaches out into Lake Wylie, in York County, South Carolina, United States. Catawba, like other nuclear plants in the region, uses the lake water for cooling.

History

Construction of the plant began in 1974. Unit 1 began commercial operation in June 1985 and Unit 2 in August 1986. The plant is operated by Duke Energy Carolinas and contains two Westinghouse pressurised water reactors, each producing about 1,160 megawatts of electricity.

Electricity production

The two units together generate roughly 19 million megawatt-hours of electricity per year, enough to power more than a million homes, and the station is one of the largest nuclear generating stations in the United States.

Surrounding population

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission defines two emergency planning zones around nuclear power plants: a plume exposure pathway zone of about 10 miles (16 km) radius, and an ingestion pathway zone of about 50 miles (80 km) radius. The city of Charlotte, North Carolina, lies about 19 miles (31 km) northeast of the station.
