Keren, Eritrea

Keren (Tigrinya: ከረን), historically known as Cheren, is the second-largest city in Eritrea. It is situated around 91 kilometres (57 mi) northwest of Asmara at an elevation of 1,390 metres (4,560 ft) above sea level, and is the capital of the Anseba Region. The city is spread over a basin surrounded by granitic mountains.

Keren is an important market town for the surrounding agricultural region, known for its Monday market and its camel market. Its population includes speakers of Tigrinya, Tigre, and Bilen, and the city has a notable Italian colonial legacy, including a railway station from the Eritrean Railway, Catholic and Orthodox churches, and a mosque.

History

Keren grew in importance in the 19th century along trade routes between Massawa and Sudan. It was occupied by Egyptian forces, then by Italy in 1889, becoming part of Italian Eritrea. During the Second World War, the Battle of Keren in early 1941 was a hard-fought engagement in which British and Commonwealth forces defeated the Italian defenders, opening the way to Asmara and Massawa. During the Eritrean War of Independence, Keren changed hands several times and was liberated by the EPLF in 1991.

Landmarks

Sights include the 19th-century Egyptian fort at Tigu, the St. Maryam Dearit shrine set in a baobab tree, and the British and Italian war cemeteries from the Second World War.
