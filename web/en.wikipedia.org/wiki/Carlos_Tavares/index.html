Carlos Tavares (born 14 August 1958) is a Portuguese business executive who served as chief executive officer of the French carmaker Groupe PSA from 2014 to 2021, and then as the first CEO of Stellantis, the group formed by the merger of PSA and Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, from 2021 until his resignation in December 2024.

Early career at Renault and Nissan

Tavares graduated in engineering from the École Centrale Paris and joined Renault in 1981. He rose through programme-management roles and, after Renault's alliance with Nissan, worked in Japan and the United States, becoming chairman of Nissan North America. In 2011 he returned to Renault as chief operating officer and the number two to Carlos Ghosn, leaving in 2013.

Groupe PSA

In 2014 Tavares became CEO of PSA Peugeot Citroën, which was then close to bankruptcy and had just been rescued with investment from the French state and China's Dongfeng Motor. His "Back in the Race" and later "Push to Pass" plans restored profitability through cost discipline and platform sharing. In 2017 PSA acquired Opel and Vauxhall from General Motors and returned them to profit. In 2019 PSA agreed to merge with Fiat Chrysler, creating Stellantis in January 2021, with Tavares as CEO and John Elkann as chairman.

Stellantis and resignation

As Stellantis CEO, Tavares pursued aggressive cost-cutting and an electrification strategy ("Dare Forward 2030"). The group's profitability peaked in 2023, but in 2024 sales and margins fell sharply, particularly in North America, amid dealer and union discontent. On 1 December 2024 the Stellantis board accepted Tavares's resignation with immediate effect.

Personal life

Tavares is an amateur racing driver who has competed in rally and circuit events, and has raced in the 24 Hours of Le Mans in historic categories.
