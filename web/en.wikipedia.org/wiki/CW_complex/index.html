A CW complex is a type of topological space that is particularly important in algebraic topology. It was introduced by J. H. C. Whitehead to meet the needs of homotopy theory. This class of spaces is broader and has some better categorical properties than simplicial complexes, but still retains a combinatorial nature that allows for computation, often with a much smaller structure.

Definition

A CW complex is built inductively by attaching cells of increasing dimension. One starts with a discrete set of points, the 0-cells, forming the 0-skeleton. The n-skeleton is then formed by attaching n-dimensional balls (n-cells) along their boundary spheres to the (n−1)-skeleton via attaching maps. The process may terminate at a finite dimension or continue indefinitely, in which case the space is given the weak topology: a set is open (or closed) exactly when its intersection with every skeleton is open (or closed).

The letters "CW" stand for the two defining properties: closure-finiteness — the closure of each cell meets only finitely many other cells — and the weak topology described above.

Examples

Every simplicial complex has the homotopy type of a CW complex, and many familiar spaces are naturally CW complexes: a sphere can be built from a single 0-cell and a single n-cell; real projective space and complex projective space have one cell in each dimension up to their real/complex dimension count; graphs are exactly the one-dimensional CW complexes.

Properties

CW complexes are locally contractible and satisfy the homotopy extension property for CW pairs, which makes them the natural setting for much of homotopy theory. Whitehead's theorem states that a weak homotopy equivalence between CW complexes is a homotopy equivalence. Their cellular chain complexes make homology and cohomology computable directly from the attaching maps.
