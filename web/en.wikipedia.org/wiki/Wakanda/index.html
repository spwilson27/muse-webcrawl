Wakanda (/wəˈkɑːndə, -ˈkæn-/) is a fictional country in East Africa appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. It is the home of the superhero Black Panther. Wakanda first appeared in Fantastic Four #52 (July 1966), and was created by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby.

Within the Marvel Universe, Wakanda is depicted as the most technologically advanced nation on Earth. Its wealth and technology derive from a vast deposit of the fictional metal vibranium, which fell to Earth as a meteorite thousands of years ago and was first mined under the leadership of the nation's early Panther kings. Wakanda concealed its true nature from the outside world for centuries, presenting itself as a poor, isolated nation while developing advanced science and medicine.

Government and culture

Wakanda is ruled by a king (or occasionally queen) who also holds the title of Black Panther, the nation's ceremonial and martial protector, empowered by a heart-shaped herb linked to the vibranium mound. The ruler is advised by a tribal council representing the nation's constituent tribes, including the River Tribe, Border Tribe, Mining Tribe, Merchant Tribe, and the Jabari (Mountain Tribe), who traditionally distance themselves from Wakanda's technology and worship the White Gorilla god Hanuman rather than the panther goddess Bast.

The king is guarded by the Dora Milaje, an order of elite female warriors. Wakanda's religion centres on a pantheon — the Orisha — including Bast, the Panther Goddess, after whom the Black Panther is styled.

In other media

Wakanda features prominently in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, debuting in a mid-credits visit in Captain America: Civil War (2016) and as the principal setting of Black Panther (2018), which became one of the highest-grossing films of all time and won three Academy Awards. Its sequel, Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022), expanded the setting's mythology. The country also appears in Avengers: Infinity War (2018), where a climactic battle is fought on its plains. The phrase "Wakanda Forever", accompanied by a crossed-arms salute, became a widely recognized cultural symbol.
