Consumer Credit Act 1974

The Consumer Credit Act 1974 (c. 39) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that significantly reformed the law relating to consumer credit, hire and hire-purchase. It implemented the recommendations of the Crowther Committee (1971), replaced a patchwork of earlier statutes such as the Moneylenders Acts and Hire-Purchase Acts, and created a licensing system for credit businesses administered first by the Office of Fair Trading and later by the Financial Conduct Authority.

Provisions

  • Regulated agreements: most credit agreements with individual debtors below prescribed limits are "regulated agreements" attracting statutory formalities.
  • Form and cancellation: agreements must be in prescribed form; debtors have cooling-off rights for agreements signed off trade premises, and lenders must send prescribed notices before enforcement or termination.
  • Section 56: representations by the dealer/negotiator are deemed made as agent of the creditor.
  • Section 75: for debtor–creditor–supplier agreements (e.g. credit card purchases), the creditor is jointly and severally liable with the supplier for misrepresentation or breach of contract — a protection unique in its breadth and widely used for purchases between £100 and £30,000.
  • Connected lender liability and extortionate credit: courts were given power to reopen extortionate credit bargains (later reframed by the 2006 Act as an "unfair relationships" test, sections 140A–140B).
  • Protected goods and time orders: restrictions on repossession of hire-purchase goods once a fraction of the price is paid, and court power to grant debtors more time to pay.

Amendments

The Consumer Credit Act 2006 extended the regime's coverage, strengthened licensing, created the Consumer Credit Jurisdiction of the Financial Ombudsman Service, and replaced the extortionate-bargain test with the broader unfair-relationships provisions. From 1 April 2014, regulation of consumer credit passed from the Office of Fair Trading to the Financial Conduct Authority under the Financial Services Act 2012.
