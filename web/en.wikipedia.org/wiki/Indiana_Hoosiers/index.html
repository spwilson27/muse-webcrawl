Indiana Hoosiers

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indiana Hoosiers are the intercollegiate sports teams and players of Indiana University Bloomington, named after the demonym "Hoosier" for people from the state of Indiana. The Hoosiers compete in NCAA Division I as members of the Big Ten Conference. The school's colours are cream and crimson.

Men's basketball

The Indiana Hoosiers men's basketball programme is the school's most storied team, with five NCAA national championships (1940, 1953, 1976, 1981, 1987). The 1975–76 team, coached by Bob Knight, remains the last men's team to complete an undefeated season. Knight, who coached from 1971 to 2000, won three titles and became famous—and controversial—for his intensity and his motion offence. The team plays at Simon Skjodt Assembly Hall, noted for its steep stands. Earlier coaches included Branch McCracken, who won the 1940 and 1953 titles.

Football

The football team plays at Memorial Stadium. Historically a weaker programme in the Big Ten, the Hoosiers have appeared in a dozen bowl games, including the 1968 Rose Bowl, and enjoyed a renaissance in the 2020s under coach Curt Cignetti, reaching the College Football Playoff. The team's traditional rivals include Purdue, contested annually for the Old Oaken Bucket.

Other sports

Indiana has a distinguished men's soccer programme with eight national championships, and its swimming and diving teams produced Olympic stars such as Mark Spitz. Women's basketball reached national prominence in the 2020s with multiple deep NCAA tournament runs. In total the school sponsors 24 varsity sports.
