Ardingly College is an independent day and boarding school for boys and girls aged 2 to 18 near the village of Ardingly in West Sussex, England. Founded in 1858 by the priest and educationalist Nathaniel Woodard, it is one of the schools of the Woodard Corporation, a group of Anglican independent schools, and is commonly counted among England's public schools.

History

Woodard founded the school as St Saviour's School at Shoreham-by-Sea in 1858, part of his project to provide sound Church of England education for the middle classes. In 1870 the school moved to its present site at Ardingly, where a campus of red-brick Gothic Revival buildings designed by R. Carpenter and William Slater rose around the chapel and quadrangle, set in about 230 acres of Sussex countryside near the Ardingly Reservoir. The school became fully co-educational in stages during the late twentieth century.

School life

Ardingly comprises a pre-prep and prep school and the senior school, with pupils divided into boarding and day houses. It offers GCSEs, A levels, and the International Baccalaureate diploma in the sixth form, has a strong tradition in hockey, cricket, and football, and hosts an annual arts programme in its theatre. The school is regularly listed among the leading IB schools in the United Kingdom by average points score. Alumni, known as Old Ardinians, include the actor Terry-Thomas, racing driver Mike Hawthorn, and novelist Neil Gaiman, who attended as a day boy.
