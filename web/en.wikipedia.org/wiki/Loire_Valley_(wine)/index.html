Loire Valley (wine)

The Loire Valley wine region includes the wine areas situated along the Loire River from the Muscadet region near the Atlantic coast to the regions of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé in north-central France. In total the region stretches roughly 1,000 kilometres along the river and its tributaries, making it France's most diverse wine region by style. Most production is white wine, from Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc and Melon de Bourgogne grapes, alongside reds from Cabernet Franc and sparkling wines second in volume only to Champagne.

Wine styles and history

Viticulture in the Loire dates to Roman times, and its wines were prized by the French and English courts in the Middle Ages and Renaissance, when the region was the heart of royal France. Today the Loire is known for fresh, food-friendly wines: steely Muscadet aged sur lie, long-lived dry and sweet Chenin from Vouvray and the Layon valley, benchmark Sauvignon Blanc from Sancerre, and elegant Cabernet Franc. Much sparkling wine is made by the traditional method and sold as Crémant de Loire. The region was also a centre of the French natural-wine and organic viticulture movements.
