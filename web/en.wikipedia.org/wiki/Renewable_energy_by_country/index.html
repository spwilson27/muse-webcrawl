Renewable energy by country

This article presents statistics on renewable energy by country, covering the share of electricity and total energy supplied by hydroelectricity, wind, solar, geothermal and biomass sources. Renewable energy deployment varies widely: some countries, such as Iceland, Norway and Paraguay, generate nearly all of their electricity from renewables thanks to abundant hydro and geothermal resources, while others are rapidly expanding wind and solar capacity from a smaller base.

Leaders in deployment

China is the world's largest producer of renewable electricity in absolute terms, with the biggest installed capacities of hydro, wind and solar power. The United States, Brazil, India, Germany and Japan are also among the largest producers. Several countries have set targets of 100% renewable electricity, and Costa Rica has repeatedly run on renewables alone for extended periods.

Growth

Solar photovoltaics and wind are the fastest-growing sources worldwide, driven by steep cost declines. The International Energy Agency has reported record annual additions of renewable capacity through the 2020s, with solar accounting for the majority of new installations.
