Wellerman

"Soon May the Wellerman Come"
Genre:Sea ballad / folk song (often called a sea shanty)
Origin:New Zealand, c. 1860s–1870s
Subject:Shore whalers awaiting supply ships of the Weller brothers
Famous recording:Nathan Evans (2021 viral version)

"Soon May the Wellerman Come", also known as "Wellerman", is a New Zealand folk ballad originating among shore whalers in the 19th century. The song refers to supply ships ("Wellermen") owned by the Weller brothers, whose Otago whaling stations provisioned whalers in exchange for their catch.

History

The Wellers were English merchants who established whaling stations in Otago in the 1830s. Shore whalers, paid not in wages but in slops, spirits and supplies, awaited the supply ships' visits. The ballad, which tells of a whaling crew whose ship the Billy o' Tea fastens onto a whale and waits for relief, was collected in New Zealand folk-song collections in the 20th century and recorded by folk singers such as the Wellington Sea Shanty Society.

Lyrics and meaning

The chorus promises that the Wellerman will bring "sugar and tea and rum", and that one day "when the tonguing is done" — cutting out the whale's tongue — the crew will take their leave. Although widely called a sea shanty, it was probably a shore ballad sung for entertainment rather than a work song timed to shipboard tasks.

2021 revival

In late 2020, Scottish singer and former postman Nathan Evans posted a rendition on TikTok, sparking the viral "Shanty Tok" phenomenon during COVID-19 lockdowns. His recording, released in 2021 with a dance remix by 220 Kid and Billen Ted, reached number one in the UK and several other countries, prompting a broad mainstream revival of sea songs.
