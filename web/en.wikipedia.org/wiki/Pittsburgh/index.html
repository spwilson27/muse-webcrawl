Pittsburgh is a city in the U.S. state of Pennsylvania, the county seat of Allegheny County, and the second-largest city in the state after Philadelphia. It stands at the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers, which join to form the Ohio River. The city is known for its steel-industry heritage, its 446 bridges, and its reinvention as a centre of healthcare, education and technology.

History

The strategic forks of the Ohio were contested by France and Britain in the French and Indian War. The French built Fort Duquesne in 1754; after several expeditions, including Braddock's defeat in 1755, the British took the site in 1758 and built Fort Pitt, named for Prime Minister William Pitt the Elder. The settlement grew into a manufacturing centre exploiting nearby coal and river transport.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Pittsburgh became the heart of American steelmaking, home to Carnegie Steel and later U.S. Steel, and to industrialists such as Andrew Carnegie, Henry Clay Frick and George Westinghouse. Heavy industry caused notorious air pollution, later reduced by post-war clean-air reforms. The collapse of the steel industry in the 1970s–80s cost the city much of its population, but its economy diversified into "eds and meds", finance, and robotics and artificial intelligence research centred on Carnegie Mellon University and the University of Pittsburgh.

Geography

The city occupies hilly terrain cut by three rivers, connected by hundreds of bridges — more than Venice, it is often said. Distinct neighbourhoods such as Oakland, Shadyside, Lawrenceville and the South Side occupy the ridges and valleys. The downtown "Golden Triangle" sits on the narrow point of land between the Allegheny and Monongahela.

Culture and sports

Pittsburgh is home to the Andy Warhol Museum, the Carnegie Museum of Art and Natural History, Phipps Conservatory, and the Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra. Its professional teams — the Steelers (NFL), Penguins (NHL) and Pirates (MLB) — all wear black and gold, the city's colours. Local institutions include the Primanti Brothers sandwich and the dialect known as "Pittsburghese".
