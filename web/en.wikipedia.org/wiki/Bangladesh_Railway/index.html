Bangladesh Railway

Bangladesh Railway is the state-owned railway operator of Bangladesh, running passenger and freight trains across a network of about 3,000 kilometres. Founded in its present form after the independence of Bangladesh in 1971, it is headquartered in Dhaka.

History

Railways in the Bengal delta date to the British colonial era, beginning in the 1860s. After the partition of India in 1947 the eastern network became part of Pakistan's railway system, and after 1971 it was reorganised as Bangladesh Railway. The opening of the Bangabandhu Bridge in 1998 connected the previously separated eastern and western networks.

Network and services

The network uses both metre gauge (1,000 mm) in the east and broad gauge (1,676 mm) in the west, with some dual-gauge track. Intercity expresses link Dhaka with Chattogram, Sylhet, Khulna, and Rajshahi, alongside mail trains, commuter services, and freight workings carrying containers from Chattogram port.

Development

Recent projects include the Padma Bridge rail link, new lines to Cox's Bazar, and the Dhaka elevated metro's separate rapid-transit system, intended to relieve severe congestion in the capital.
