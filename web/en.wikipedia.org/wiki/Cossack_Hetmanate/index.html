The Cossack Hetmanate (Ukrainian: Гетьманщина, Hetmanshchyna), officially the Zaporozhian Host, was a Cossack polity in the territory of present-day central Ukraine between 1648/49 and 1764. It was established by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky during the Khmelnytsky Uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and was recognised in the 1649 Treaty of Zboriv; its status changed with the 1654 Pereiaslav Agreement, which placed it under the protection of the Tsardom of Russia.

The Hetmanate was organised on a regimental system: the territory was divided into regiments (polky) and companies (sotni), each both a military unit and an administrative district, governed by Cossack officers (the starshyna). Its society combined the Cossack estate, townspeople, peasants and the Orthodox clergy.

The Ruin and decline

After Khmelnytsky's death in 1657, competing hetmans and foreign interventions produced the period known as the Ruin. The 1667 Truce of Andrusovo divided the Cossack lands along the Dnieper between Poland and Russia. The Left-Bank Hetmanate remained under Russian suzerainty with diminishing autonomy, despite episodes such as Ivan Mazepa's alliance with Charles XII of Sweden, crushed at the Battle of Poltava in 1709. Catherine II abolished the office of hetman in 1764 after the resignation of Kirill Razumovsky, and the regimental system was dismantled in the early 1780s; the Zaporozhian Sich had been destroyed in 1775.
