Frank O'Hara

Born27 March 1926, Baltimore, Maryland, United States
Died25 July 1966, Fire Island, New York, United States
OccupationPoet, art curator
MovementNew York School

Francis Russell "Frank" O'Hara (27 March 1926 – 25 July 1966) was an American writer, poet and art critic. A curator at the Museum of Modern Art, he was a central figure of the New York School of poets alongside John Ashbery, James Schuyler and Kenneth Koch.

Poetry

O'Hara's poems, many written during his lunch hours, mingle the everyday events of New York life with references to art, film and friends in a conversational, improvisational style. His collections include Meditations in an Emergency (1957), Lunch Poems (1964) and the posthumous The Collected Poems of Frank O'Hara (1971), which won the National Book Award.

Death

O'Hara died aged forty after being struck by a beach buggy on Fire Island in July 1966. His influence on American poetry has grown steadily since his death.
