Psychedelic drug

Psychedelics are a subclass of hallucinogenic drugs whose primary effect is to trigger non-ordinary states of consciousness (known as psychedelic experiences or "trips") via serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonism. The classic psychedelics include LSD, psilocybin (from psilocybin mushrooms), mescaline (from peyote and other cacti) and DMT. The word psychedelic — "mind-manifesting" — was coined by psychiatrist Humphry Osmond in 1956 correspondence with Aldous Huxley.

Effects

Psychedelic experiences involve altered perception (geometric visual patterns, intensified colours), changes in thought and mood, synaesthesia, and a sense of ego dissolution at high doses. Effects depend strongly on dose, "set" (mindset) and "setting" (environment). Classic psychedelics have low physiological toxicity and are not considered addictive, though adverse psychological reactions, including anxiety and, rarely, prolonged perceptual disturbance (HPPD), can occur.

History

Psychedelic plants and fungi have been used for centuries in indigenous religious and healing practices in the Americas. In the modern era, Albert Hofmann synthesised LSD in 1938 and discovered its effects in 1943 at Sandoz. In the 1950s and early 1960s psychedelics were widely researched in psychiatry; they then became emblematic of 1960s counterculture. The UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) and national laws such as the US Controlled Substances Act placed the classic psychedelics in the most restrictive schedules, and research largely halted for decades.

Research revival

Since the 2000s, clinical research has resumed at institutions including Johns Hopkins and Imperial College London, investigating psilocybin for depression and end-of-life anxiety, and psychedelic-assisted therapy for addiction and PTSD (the latter chiefly with MDMA, an empathogen rather than a classic psychedelic). Several jurisdictions have decriminalised or created regulated access programmes for psilocybin in the 2020s, while regulators have continued to require further trial evidence for medical approval.
