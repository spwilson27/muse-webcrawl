Provinces of South Korea

The provinces of South Korea are first-level administrative divisions of the Republic of Korea. South Korea is divided into nine provinces (do), along with special and metropolitan cities that have province-level status. Provincial governments are headed by elected governors and provincial councils, and local autonomy was restored in the 1990s after decades of central appointment.

Jeju is a special self-governing province with additional autonomy. Gangwon also adopted the name Gangwon State (Special Self-Governing Province) in 2023, and North Jeolla became Jeonbuk State in 2024.

History

The nine provinces derive largely from the Eight Provinces of the Joseon dynasty, several of which were split into northern and southern halves in 1896. The division of the peninsula in 1945 left five provinces wholly or partly in the North; South Korea claims the northern provinces through the Committee for the Five Northern Korean Provinces, which maintains symbolic administrations.

Province-level cities

Alongside the provinces, Seoul is a special city, six cities (Busan, Daegu, Incheon, Gwangju, Daejeon and Ulsan) are metropolitan cities, and Sejong is a special self-governing city — all with province-equivalent status.
