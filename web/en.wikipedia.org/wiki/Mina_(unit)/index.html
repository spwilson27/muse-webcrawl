Mina (unit)

The mina (Greek: μνᾶ; Akkadian: manû) was an ancient unit of weight and, by extension, of money, used throughout the Near East and the Greek world. It was subdivided into shekels or drachmas and multiplied into talents.

Values

The Babylonian mina was about 500 grams, divided into 60 shekels, with 60 minas to the talent. The Attic mina of Athens was about 436 grams, divided into 100 drachmas. As money, a mina represented that weight of silver; the "mina" of the New Testament parable of the pounds (Luke 19) corresponds to the Greek unit.
