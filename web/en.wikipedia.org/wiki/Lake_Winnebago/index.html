Lake Winnebago is a freshwater lake in east-central Wisconsin, United States. Covering about 137,700 acres in its watershed-counted extent and 131,939 acres of open water, it is the largest lake entirely within the state. It is about 30 miles (48 km) long and 10 miles (16 km) wide but very shallow, with an average depth of 15.5 feet (4.7 m). Cities on its shores include Oshkosh at the mouth of the upper Fox River, Fond du Lac at the southern end, and Neenah and Menasha at the outlet.

Geology and history

Winnebago is a remnant of Glacial Lake Oshkosh, dammed by the Green Bay lobe of the Laurentide ice sheet roughly 12,000 years ago. The lake takes its name from the Winnebago (Ho-Chunk) people of the region. French explorer Jean Nicolet passed nearby in 1634. Dams at Neenah and Menasha, first built in the 1850s for navigation and water power on the Fox–Wisconsin waterway, raised the level by about 3 feet, creating the present shoreline and flooding marshes at the margins.

Ecology and fishing

The lake anchors the Winnebago System — Lakes Poygan, Winneconne and Butte des Morts plus the Wolf and Fox rivers — one of the most studied freshwater fisheries in the Midwest. It is renowned for lake sturgeon: Winnebago hosts one of the world's largest self-sustaining lake sturgeon populations, and the February spearfishing season through the ice is a major regional tradition. Walleye, perch, white bass and drum are also abundant. The lake suffers chronic nutrient loading; algal blooms and oxygen depletion reflect its shallow, fertile character.

Culture

In winter the frozen lake supports ice fishing villages, ice-boating and the Sturgeon Spectacular festival in Fond du Lac. High Cliff State Park, on the northeastern shore atop the Niagara Escarpment, is the lake's only state park.
