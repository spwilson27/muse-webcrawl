Greensboro, North Carolina

Greensboro (locally /ˈɡriːnzbʌrə/) is a city in and the county seat of Guilford County, North Carolina, United States. It is the third-most populous city in North Carolina, after Charlotte and Raleigh, with a population of about 301,000 at the 2020 census. Greensboro is the largest city in the Piedmont Triad region, which also includes Winston-Salem and High Point.

History

Greensboro was established in 1808 as the new county seat of Guilford County, chosen for its central location, and named in honor of Major General Nathanael Greene, commander of American forces at the Battle of Guilford Court House in 1781. It grew as a transportation hub with the arrival of railroads in the 1850s.

The city became a major textile manufacturing center in the late 19th and 20th centuries, home to companies such as Cone Mills and Burlington Industries. Greensboro played a pivotal role in the American civil rights movement: on February 1, 1960, four North Carolina A&T students — Ezell Blair Jr., Franklin McCain, Joseph McNeil, and David Richmond — sat at the segregated lunch counter of the downtown Woolworth's, sparking sit-ins across the South. The site is now the International Civil Rights Center & Museum.

Geography and economy

Greensboro is located in the rolling hills of the Piedmont, roughly midway between North Carolina's mountains and coast. Its central location and highway connections make it a logistics and distribution center. Major employers include Honda Aircraft Company, the Volvo Group, VF Corporation, and large health care, education, and insurance sectors.

Education and culture

The city hosts several institutions of higher education, including the University of North Carolina at Greensboro, North Carolina A&T State University, Greensboro College, Guilford College, and Bennett College. Cultural venues include the Greensboro Coliseum Complex, the Tanger Center for the Performing Arts, and the Greensboro Science Center.
