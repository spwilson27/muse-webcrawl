The Isle of Wight is an island, English county, and unitary authority in the English Channel, 2 to 5 miles off the coast of Hampshire, across the Solent. It is the largest island in England and the second-most populous. Its diamond shape, 23 miles by 13 miles, contains a varied landscape of chalk downs, chines, and coastline long popular with holidaymakers; much of the island is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, and in 2019 it was awarded UNESCO Biosphere Reserve status.

History

The island was inhabited in the Palaeolithic and is rich in dinosaur fossils from the Cretaceous — its cliffs around Compton Bay and Yaverland are among Europe's best-known dinosaur localities. The Romans knew it as Vectis; it was later conquered by the Jutish king Cædwalla of Wessex in 686. Its strategic position made it a target of French invasions in the Middle Ages and a fortified bulwark in the Victorian "Palmerston forts" era.

Queen Victoria had her beloved holiday home, Osborne House, built at East Cowes (1845–51), where she died in 1901; the island consequently became a fashionable Victorian resort. Cowes became the world centre of yacht racing, and the original America's Cup race was sailed around the island in 1851. The Isle of Wight Festival of 1970, headlined by Jimi Hendrix, was one of the largest rock festivals of its era; the revived festival remains a major annual event.

Geography and transport

A spine of chalk downs runs east–west across the island, ending at Culver Cliff and at the Needles — three chalk stacks rising from the sea at Alum Bay, famous for its coloured sands. The island is separated from the mainland by the Solent, crossed by car and passenger ferries from Portsmouth, Southampton, and Lymington, and by hovercraft from Southsea. It has no motorway and no fixed link to the mainland; rail services run on the Island Line between Ryde and Shanklin.

Governance

The Isle of Wight is a ceremonial county and unitary authority governed by the Isle of Wight Council, and elects two Members of Parliament (Isle of Wight East and West, since 2024). Historically administered as part of Hampshire, it gained its own county council in 1890 and county status in 1974.
