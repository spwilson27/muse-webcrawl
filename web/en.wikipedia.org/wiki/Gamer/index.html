Gamer

A gamer is someone who plays interactive games, particularly video games, tabletop role-playing games or skill-based card games, usually over an extended period. The word dates to at least 1422, when the town records of Walsall refer to dice and card players, but it acquired its modern sense with the rise of gaming subcultures in the late 20th century.

Demographics

Industry surveys estimate that more than three billion people play video games worldwide. In the United States the Entertainment Software Association reports an average player age in the mid-30s, and women make up roughly half of all players, though proportions differ sharply by genre and platform. Mobile phones are the most widely used gaming platform, followed by consoles and PCs.

Types of gamer

Common informal categories include:

  • Casual gamers, who play simple games in short sessions, typically on phones.
  • Core or mid-core gamers, who play mainstream titles regularly.
  • Hardcore gamers, who devote substantial time and money to games, often competitive or difficult titles, and follow gaming news and hardware.
  • Professional gamers, who compete in esports for prize money and salaries.
  • Retro gamers, who collect and play classic systems and games.
  • Newbie ("noob") and retrogaming slang form part of a broad gamer vocabulary that has spilled into general internet culture.

Identity and culture

"Gamer" is also a cultural identity, associated with gaming conventions, streaming culture on platforms such as Twitch and YouTube, speedrunning communities and esports fandom. The identity has been contested: as gaming became mainstream entertainment, commentators debated whether the label — historically marketed toward young men — still describes the audience, a debate that flared during the Gamergate controversy of 2014. Avid players also build and maintain much of the participatory culture around games: wikis, mods, fan art and guide-writing communities such as GameFAQs.
