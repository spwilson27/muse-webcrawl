Jubilee (biblical)

The Jubilee (Hebrew: יובל, yōḇel) is the year that follows seven cycles of seven years in the Hebrew Bible — the fiftieth year — as prescribed in the Book of Leviticus (chapter 25). In the Jubilee year, enslaved Israelites were to be freed, debts forgiven, and land sold or lost since the previous Jubilee returned to its original family owners, while the fields lay fallow as in a sabbatical year.

Biblical legislation

Leviticus 25 commands that the Jubilee be proclaimed throughout the land with the sound of the shofar (ram's horn, from which "jubilee" derives) on the Day of Atonement. Its provisions include the return of ancestral land to its original family ("each of you shall return to his holding"), the release of Israelite slaves, fallowing of fields, and rules pricing land sales according to the years remaining until the Jubilee — land being, in the text's theology, God's property held in trust: "The land is mine; for you are strangers and sojourners with me."

The Jubilee extends the logic of the sabbatical year (shmita), in which the land rests every seventh year, and of debt release (Deuteronomy 15). Related measures appear in the manumission proclamations of Jeremiah 34 and the reforms of Nehemiah 5, and Isaiah 61's promise of "liberty to the captives" in "the year of the LORD's favour" echoes the Jubilee proclamation — a passage Jesus reads aloud at Nazareth in Luke 4.

Historicity and observance

Scholars debate whether the Jubilee legislation, usually dated to the Holiness Code, was ever systematically implemented in ancient Israel; rabbinic tradition held that the Jubilee applied only while all the tribes dwelt in the land, and it lapsed during or after the Babylonian exile. The Second Temple-era Book of Jubilees used 49-year "jubilee" periods as a framework for sacred chronology.

Legacy

The Jubilee concept has inspired later movements for debt relief and land reform, including the Catholic Church's Holy Years (Jubilees, beginning in 1300), campaigns for Third World debt cancellation such as Jubilee 2000, and economic proposals to counteract wealth concentration. The Liberty Bell in Philadelphia bears Leviticus 25:10: "Proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof."
