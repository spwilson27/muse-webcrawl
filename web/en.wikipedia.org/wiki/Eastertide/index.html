Eastertide

Eastertide (also known as Easter Season or the Easter Season, and in Latin Tempus paschale) is a festal season in the liturgical year of Christianity that focuses on celebrating the Resurrection of Jesus Christ. It begins on Easter Sunday and lasts for seven weeks, concluding with Pentecost — a period of fifty days, which is why it is also called the Great Fifty Days.

Observance

In the Roman Catholic Church, Eastertide is the second-longest liturgical season after Ordinary Time. The first week, the Octave of Easter, is treated as a single great day of celebration, and in the Eastern Orthodox tradition Bright Week is similarly kept with festal services. Sundays of the season are numbered as the Second, Third, and so on "Sundays of Easter." The Ascension of Jesus is celebrated on the fortieth day (or transferred to the following Sunday in many places), and the season ends with Pentecost, commemorating the descent of the Holy Spirit.

Liturgical features of the season include the Paschal candle, the singing of the Alleluia (omitted during Lent), white or gold vestments, and in some traditions the sprinkling of the congregation with holy water in place of the penitential rite.
