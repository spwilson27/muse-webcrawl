Anne of Cleves (1515 – 16 July 1557) was Queen of England from 6 January to 12 July 1540 as the fourth wife of King Henry VIII. Noted for not looking as attractive as she did in her portrait — though doubts remain about this account — and for her inability to communicate in English, Anne did not impress the king, and their marriage was never consummated. She was given the name "the King's Beloved Sister" after agreeing to an annulment, and outlived Henry and all his other wives.

Early life

Anne was born in Düsseldorf, the second daughter of John III, Duke of Cleves, and Maria of Jülich-Berg. She grew up at Schloss Burg in a conservative court, receiving an education focused on domestic skills rather than the humanist learning fashionable in England and France. In 1539, seeking an alliance with German Protestant states, Thomas Cromwell arranged for Hans Holbein the Younger to paint portraits of Anne and her sister Amalia; Henry chose Anne based on the portrait.

Marriage and annulment

Anne arrived in England at the end of December 1539 and married Henry at Greenwich Palace on 6 January 1540. The king complained he found her unattractive and unlike her portrait — later tradition gives him the (probably apocryphal) insult "Flanders mare". Within months the marriage was annulled on the grounds of non-consummation and Anne's pre-contract with Francis of Lorraine. Anne accepted the settlement gracefully, receiving a generous income and houses including Hever Castle.

Later life

Anne remained in England, becoming a wealthy and independent landowner and a familiar figure at court. She lived to see the reign of Mary I and died at Chelsea in 1557, the last surviving wife of Henry VIII. She is buried in Westminster Abbey.
