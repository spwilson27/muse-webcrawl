Restitution

FieldLaw (contract, unjust enrichment)
PurposeRestore benefits unjustly gained
MeasureDefendant's gain, not claimant's loss

Restitution restores the status quo

In law, restitution is a remedy by which a person who has been unjustly enriched at another's expense is required to give that benefit back. Unlike compensatory damages, which are measured by the claimant's loss, restitution is measured by the defendant's gain.

Unjust enrichment

Restitution is the principal remedy for unjust enrichment, arising for example where money was paid by mistake, under a void contract, or under duress. Common claims include actions for money had and received and quantum meruit for services rendered. Courts ask whether the defendant was enriched, whether the enrichment was at the claimant's expense, and whether it would be unjust to allow the defendant to keep it.

Other contexts

The term is also used for the return of looted property — notably Nazi-looted art — for reparations to victims of crime ordered by criminal courts, and in the physics sense, the coefficient of restitution measures the elasticity of collisions.
