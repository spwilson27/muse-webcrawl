Bishkek

Bishkek, formerly Pishpek and Frunze, is the capital and largest city of Kyrgyzstan, with about 1.1 million residents. It is located in the Chüy Valley in the north of the country, near the Kazakhstan border, at the foot of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too range.

Bishkek began as a Kokand fortress (1825) and Russian garrison town, laid out on a grid with tree-lined boulevards and irrigation canals — it is one of Central Asia's greenest capitals. Among its landmarks are Ala-Too Square with its honour guard and giant flagpole, the State History Museum (formerly the Lenin Museum), Osh Bazaar, and Panfilov Park. Renamed after independence in 1991 (its Soviet name, Frunze, honoured the Bolshevik general born there). It is the political centre of Kyrgyzstan's three revolutions (2005, 2010, 2020).
