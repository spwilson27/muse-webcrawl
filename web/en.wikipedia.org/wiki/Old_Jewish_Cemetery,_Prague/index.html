Old Jewish Cemetery, Prague

The Old Jewish Cemetery (Czech: Starý židovský hřbitov) in Prague, Czech Republic, is one of the largest and most historically important Jewish cemeteries in Europe. Located in Josefov, the former Jewish ghetto of Prague's Old Town, it was in use from the first half of the 15th century until 1786.

History

The oldest preserved tombstone dates to 1439 and belongs to the rabbi and poet Avigdor Kara. Because space within the ghetto was limited and Jewish law forbids the disturbance of graves, new layers of earth were repeatedly added over the centuries, and burials were stacked in as many as twelve layers. As a result, the cemetery's roughly 12,000 visible tombstones stand in a dense, uneven jumble, while the total number of people buried there is estimated at around 100,000.

Notable burials

Among the most famous graves is that of Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal of Prague, d. 1609), associated with the legend of the Golem of Prague. Others include the banker Mordecai Meisel (d. 1601) and the historian and astronomer David Gans (d. 1613).

Today

The cemetery is administered by the Jewish Museum in Prague and is one of the city's most visited historic sites. It features in Umberto Eco's novel The Prague Cemetery, which deals with the antisemitic forgery known as the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, falsely said to have been concocted in connection with the cemetery.
