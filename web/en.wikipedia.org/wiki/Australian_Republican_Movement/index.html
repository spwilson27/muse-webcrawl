Australian Republican Movement

The Australian Republican Movement (ARM) is a non-partisan organisation that campaigns for Australia to become a republic with an Australian head of state, replacing the monarchy it shares with the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms. Founded in 1991, it was the leading "Yes" organisation in the failed 1999 republic referendum and remains the principal republican advocacy group in Australia.

History

The ARM was launched on 7 July 1991, with novelist Thomas Keneally as founding chair. It grew rapidly during the prime ministership of Paul Keating, who put a republic on the national agenda. Under chair Malcolm Turnbull (1993–2000), the ARM advocated a "minimalist" model: a president appointed by a two-thirds majority of a joint sitting of Parliament, replacing the governor-general. That model was endorsed by the 1998 Constitutional Convention and put to referendum on 6 November 1999, where it was defeated nationally (54.9% No) and in every state — a result attributed both to monarchist sentiment and to division among republicans over the model, with supporters of direct election campaigning for No.

After the referendum

The movement continued at lower intensity through the 2000s. It revived public debate around model design, and in 2022 proposed the "Australian Choice Model", under which state and federal parliaments would nominate candidates and the head of state would be chosen by popular vote. Campaigning was complicated by the Albanese government's prioritisation, and later abandonment, of other constitutional referendums. The ARM is opposed by Australians for Constitutional Monarchy and the Australian Monarchist League.
