Salisbury Plain is a chalk plateau in the south-west of Wiltshire, England, covering roughly 780 km² (300 sq mi). It is famous for its rich archaeology — above all Stonehenge — and for being the British Army's principal training area, whose military use has incidentally preserved the largest expanse of unimproved chalk grassland in north-west Europe.

Archaeology

The plain was settled in Neolithic times and is dense with monuments: the Stonehenge and Avebury World Heritage Site includes Stonehenge itself, Woodhenge, Durrington Walls, the Avenue, and hundreds of Bronze Age round barrows. Later sites include Iron Age hillforts such as Battlesbury and Scratchbury, and the deserted medieval village of Imber, evacuated in 1943 for military training and still closed to the public except on occasional open days.

Military use

The War Office began buying land on the plain in the 1890s; Salisbury Plain Training Area now covers about half the plain, with garrison towns at Tidworth, Bulford, Larkhill and Warminster. The Royal School of Artillery is based at Larkhill, and the first British military aeroplane flights took place on the plain. Public access to the training area is restricted during exercises.

Wildlife

Because it is largely uncultivated, the plain supports rare chalk-grassland plants and butterflies, stone-curlews, and a reintroduced population of great bustards — the county bird of Wiltshire — released from 2004 after the species' extinction in Britain in the 19th century.
