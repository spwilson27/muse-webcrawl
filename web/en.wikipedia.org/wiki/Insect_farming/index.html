Insect farming

Insect farming is the practice of raising and breeding insects as livestock, also referred to as minilivestock or micro stock. Insects may be farmed for their products — such as silk from silkworms, honey from bees, or lac from lac insects — or themselves used as food for humans (entomophagy) and as feed for animals.

Species

Commonly farmed species include house crickets and banded crickets, mealworms (darkling beetle larvae), black soldier fly larvae, and silkworms. Black soldier flies are especially valued for converting organic waste streams into protein-rich biomass, while their frass can be used as fertiliser.

Advantages

Insects are efficient converters of feed, require relatively little land and water, and generally emit fewer greenhouse gases than conventional livestock. The FAO has promoted edible insects as a potential contribution to food security.

Regulation and challenges

Regulatory approval for insects in food and feed varies by jurisdiction; the European Union has authorized several insect species as novel foods and as processed animal protein in aquafeed and poultry and pig feed. Scaling, cost, consumer acceptance, and ensuring the safety of waste substrates remain challenges.
