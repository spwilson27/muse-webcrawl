Rarámuri (Tarahumara)
Region: Chihuahua, Mexico
Ethnicity: Rarámuri people
Language: Tarahumara (Rarámuri)
Population: approx. 70,000–110,000


Rarámuri runners in the Sierra Tarahumara.

The Rarámuri are an Indigenous people of northern Mexico, native to the state of Chihuahua. They are also widely known as the Tarahumara, a name derived from the Spanish rendering of their language. In their own language, rarámuri means "those who run fast" or "foot runners," reflecting their renowned long-distance running tradition.

History

The Rarámuri have inhabited the Sierra Madre Occidental for centuries. Following Spanish colonization in the 16th and 17th centuries, missionaries and miners pushed many Rarámuri into the remote Copper Canyon (Barrancas del Cobre) region, where their semi-nomadic way of life persisted with limited outside contact.

Culture

Rarámuri communities are organized around small settlements and communal labor. Traditional life includes subsistence agriculture (maize, beans, squash), goat herding, and seasonal migration between canyon bottoms and highland homes. The tesgüino (maize beer) gathering is a central social and religious institution.

Running

The Rarámuri are famed for endurance running over distances of 100 kilometres or more, often in handmade sandals (huaraches). Their running tradition gained international attention through Christopher McDougall's 2009 book Born to Run.

Language

The Rarámuri language belongs to the Uto-Aztecan family. It has several dialects and is still spoken by a majority of the ethnic population, though Spanish is increasingly common.
