Anna Atkins

Anna Atkins (née Children; 16 March 1799 – 9 June 1871) was an English botanist and photographer. She is often considered the first person to publish a book illustrated with photographic images, and some sources credit her as the first woman to create a photograph.

Life and work

Born in Tonbridge, Kent, she was the daughter of John George Children, a chemist and zoologist at the British Museum, through whom she met both William Henry Fox Talbot and the astronomer and chemist Sir John Herschel, inventor of the cyanotype process in 1842. Atkins was a serious botanist, a member of the Botanical Society of London, with a particular interest in algae and ferns.

In October 1843 she began issuing Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions, in which specimens were placed directly on photosensitised paper to produce white-on-blue photograms, accompanied by handwritten text. Issued in parts over a decade, the privately published work predated Talbot's The Pencil of Nature (1844). With her friend Anne Dixon she later produced cyanotype volumes on ferns and flowering plants (1853–54). Only a small number of copies survive, held by major museums and libraries.

Legacy

Atkins was largely forgotten for a century; Larry Schaaf's 1985 monograph Sun Gardens revived her reputation. Her cyanotypes are now celebrated both as scientific illustration and as art, and she is commemorated in exhibitions and in the naming of photographic awards and a Google Doodle on her 216th birthday in 2015.
