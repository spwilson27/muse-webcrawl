Baseboard

A baseboard, also known as a skirting board in British English, is a board covering the lowest part of an interior wall. Its purpose is both to protect the wall from kicks, abrasion, and furniture, and to cover the joint between the wall surface and the floor.

Construction

Baseboards are usually made of softwood or medium-density fibreboard (MDF), profiled with a decorative moulding on the upper edge and often primed or painted. They are fixed to the wall with nails, screws, or adhesive. Profiles range from simple chamfered boards to elaborate classical mouldings in period houses. Taller, more decorated baseboards are characteristic of older and grander interiors, while modern minimalist interiors may omit them or use flush "shadow gap" details.

Function

Beyond decoration, the baseboard protects plaster from chair legs, vacuum cleaners, and mops, hides uneven gaps between flooring and wall, and can conceal wiring in hollow-backed designs. In some heating systems, baseboard radiators or convectors take the place of the board itself.
