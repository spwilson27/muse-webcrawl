Paranasal sinuses

The paranasal sinuses are a group of four paired air-filled spaces that surround the nasal cavity. They are named for the bones in which they lie: the maxillary, frontal, sphenoid, and ethmoid sinuses. They are lined with respiratory epithelium and connect to the nasal cavity through small openings called ostia.

Function

Proposed functions include reducing the weight of the skull, humidifying and warming inspired air, increasing resonance of the voice, and providing a "crumple zone" to protect facial structures in trauma. Their precise biological role remains debated.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the sinuses, sinusitis, is a common condition causing facial pain, congestion, and discharge. The maxillary sinus lies close to the roots of the upper teeth, so dental infections can spread into it.
