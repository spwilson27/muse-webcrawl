Tooth eruption

Tooth eruption is the process by which a developing tooth moves from its position within the jaws through the alveolar bone and overlying soft tissue into the oral cavity, until it reaches the occlusal plane and functions in mastication.

Phases

Eruption is conventionally divided into pre-eruptive movements within the jaw, the eruptive (pre-functional) phase — intraosseous migration, penetration of the mucosa and emergence into the mouth — and the post-eruptive phase of continued movement compensating for jaw growth and tooth wear. The dental follicle orchestrates bone resorption above the crown and bone formation below the root, via signalling molecules such as CSF-1, RANKL and interleukin-1.

Timing

Primary (deciduous) teeth usually begin erupting at about six months of age — typically the lower central incisors — and the full set of 20 is complete by around age three. Permanent teeth begin with the first molars and lower incisors near age six; most are in place by age 13, with third molars (wisdom teeth) erupting, if at all, between 17 and 25.

Disorders

Delayed or failed eruption occurs in conditions such as cleidocranial dysplasia, hypothyroidism and Down syndrome, and may result from obstruction by supernumerary teeth or cysts. Teething — the emergence of infant teeth — is associated with mild irritability and gum discomfort, though severe symptoms usually have another cause.
