Women in Refrigerators

Women in Refrigerators is a literary trope and a website created in 1999 by comic book writer Gail Simone. The term refers to female characters in comic books and other fiction who are killed, maimed, depowered, raped, or otherwise harmed as a plot device intended to motivate a male hero. The name derives from Green Lantern vol. 3, #54 (1994), written by Ron Marz, in which the villain Major Force murders Kyle Rayner's girlfriend Alexandra DeWitt and stuffs her body into a refrigerator, where Rayner discovers it on returning home.

The website

Simone compiled a list of more than one hundred female comic-book characters who had suffered violent or degrading fates, and published it on a website titled "Women in Refrigerators". She has said the list was not intended as an indictment of all such stories, but as an observation about a pattern:

"If you destroy most of the female characters in an audience, you're going to lose a lot of your audience."

Many comics writers responded to Simone's list, and the resulting discussion — including responses from those who had written the stories in question — was published on the site alongside the list.

Use of the term

The phrase has spread beyond comics into general criticism of film, television, games, and literature, where "fridging" describes the killing or brutalisation of a (usually female) character solely to advance a protagonist's story arc. Scholars of comics and feminist critics have examined the trope in relation to the treatment of supporting characters, arguing that female characters disproportionately suffer permanent or sexualised harm while male characters' deaths are more often temporary or heroic.

Criticism and response

Some writers have objected that male characters are also frequently killed or injured in superhero fiction, and that the list took incidents out of context. Simone and other commentators replied that the issue is one of proportion and of the specific, often sexualised kinds of violence involved. Simone later went on to write several of the characters on her own list in titles such as Birds of Prey.
