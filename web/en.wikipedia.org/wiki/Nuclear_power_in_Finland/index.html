Nuclear power in Finland

Nuclear power in Finland supplies roughly a third of the country's electricity from five reactors at two power stations: Olkiluoto on the west coast and Loviisa on the south coast. Finland is noted for broad public acceptance of nuclear power, a system of producer-responsibility waste funding, and the Onkalo deep geological repository — the world's first permanent repository for spent nuclear fuel.

History

The Loviisa plant, built with Soviet VVER-440 reactors fitted with Western safety systems and containment, entered service in 1977 and 1980. The Swedish-designed boiling water reactors Olkiluoto 1 and 2 followed in 1978 and 1980, operated by Teollisuuden Voima (TVO). Parliament approved a fifth reactor in 2002 — the first such decision in Western Europe after the Chernobyl accident — resulting in the EPR unit Olkiluoto 3, which after long construction delays began regular electricity production in 2023 as one of the most powerful reactors in Europe, at about 1,600 MW.

Waste management

Under Finnish law, waste producers bear full responsibility for waste management, funded through the State Nuclear Waste Management Fund. Low- and intermediate-level waste is disposed of in repositories at the plant sites, and spent fuel is being placed in the Onkalo repository at Olkiluoto, excavated in bedrock and based on the Swedish KBS-3 concept of copper canisters in bentonite clay.

Cancelled plans

A planned additional unit at Hanhikivi, ordered with a Russian VVER-1200 reactor, was cancelled in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.
