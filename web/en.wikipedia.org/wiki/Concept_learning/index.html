Concept learning

Concept learning, also known as category learning, concept attainment, and concept formation, is defined by Douglas L. Medin as "the search for and testing of attributes that can be used to distinguish exemplars from non-exemplars of various categories". It is studied in psychology and cognitive science, and in machine learning as the problem of inferring a boolean-valued function from labelled training examples.

Types of concepts

Concepts may be defined by a set of necessary and sufficient features (classical view), by prototypes representing typical members, or by stored exemplars. Bruner and colleagues' classic 1956 studies examined strategies people use to attain concepts from examples. In machine learning, concept learning is formalised with frameworks such as version spaces, candidate elimination, and Mitchell's FIND-S algorithm.

In psychology and education

Concept learning is central to how children acquire categories such as "dog" or "chair" and to how students learn academic subjects. Instructional approaches include providing examples and non-examples, concept maps, and inductive teaching. Errors and biases in human concept learning inform models of cognition.
