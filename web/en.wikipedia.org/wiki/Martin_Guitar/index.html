C. F. Martin & Company, commonly known as Martin Guitar, is an American manufacturer of acoustic guitars founded in 1833 by Christian Frederick Martin, a German immigrant cabinetmaker and luthier. The company, headquartered in Nazareth, Pennsylvania, has remained family-owned for six generations and is credited with many foundational innovations in the modern steel-string acoustic guitar.

Innovations

Martin developed the X-bracing system for the guitar top in the 1850s, which allowed instruments to withstand the tension of steel strings and became the industry standard. In 1916 the company produced the first Dreadnought, a large-bodied guitar named after the British battleship, initially built for the Oliver Ditson Company and sold under the Martin name from 1931. In 1929 Martin introduced the 14-fret neck joint, moving the neck-body joint from the 12th to the 14th fret, a design that became the standard for acoustic guitars.

Models and players

Famous Martin models include the D-18, D-28, D-45, OM-28, and 000-28. Martin guitars have been played by musicians including Hank Williams, Elvis Presley, Johnny Cash, Bob Dylan, Eric Clapton, John Mayer, and Ed Sheeran. The company's museum and factory in Nazareth offer tours, and vintage Martins are among the most collectible instruments in the world.
