Aker Brygge

The waterfront promenade at Aker Brygge

Aker Brygge is a neighbourhood and commercial district in central Oslo, Norway, located on the western shore of the Pipervika inlet of the Oslofjord, just west of Oslo City Hall. Developed on the site of the former Akers Mekaniske Verksted shipyard, it is today one of Oslo's most popular areas, known for its waterfront promenades, restaurants, shops, offices and apartments, and for the views across the harbour toward Akershus Fortress.

History

The area takes its name from the Aker farm and the wharf (brygge) serving it. From the mid-19th century the site was dominated by Akers Mekaniske Verksted, one of Norway's principal shipyards and engineering works, which employed thousands of workers building ships and machinery. When the yard closed in 1982, the waterfront was redeveloped in stages through the later 1980s and 1990s, retaining some industrial brick architecture while adding new office, retail and residential buildings.

Present day

Aker Brygge attracts millions of visitors annually to its quayside restaurants and bars, marina and shopping streets. It connects westward to Tjuvholmen, home of the Astrup Fearnley Museum of Modern Art, and eastward to the City Hall square. The area also hosts offices for major Norwegian companies and is served by the Nationaltheatret station and several tram lines.
