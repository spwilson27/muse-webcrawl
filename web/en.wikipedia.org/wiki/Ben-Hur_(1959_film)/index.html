Ben-Hur (1959 film)

Director: William Wyler

Starring: Charlton Heston, Stephen Boyd, Jack Hawkins, Haya Harareet

Released: 1959

Running time: 212 minutes

Studio: Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer

Ben-Hur is a 1959 American epic religious drama film directed by William Wyler, produced by Sam Zimbalist for Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, and starring Charlton Heston as the title character. A remake of the 1925 silent film, it adapts Lew Wallace's 1880 novel Ben-Hur: A Tale of the Christ. The film premiered on 18 November 1959 and became the fastest-grossing and highest-grossing film of its time.

Plot

Judah Ben-Hur, a wealthy Jewish prince of Jerusalem, is betrayed by his childhood friend, the Roman tribune Messala, and condemned to the galleys while his mother and sister are imprisoned. Surviving slavery, Judah saves the life of the Roman consul Quintus Arrius, wins his freedom and adoption, and returns to seek revenge, culminating in a spectacular chariot race against Messala. Along the way his path crosses that of Jesus of Nazareth, and the film ends with the miraculous healing of his family and Judah's turn toward forgiveness.

Production and reception

Filmed in Italy at Cinecittà Studios with a budget of about $15 million — the largest of its era — the film employed hundreds of sets and thousands of extras. Its nine-minute chariot race, staged by Yakima Canutt and Andrew Marton, remains one of the most famous sequences in cinema. Ben-Hur won a record eleven Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor — a record later tied by Titanic (1997) and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King (2003) but never surpassed.
