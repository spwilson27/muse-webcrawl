New Holland is a borough in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, United States. Located in the heart of Pennsylvania Dutch Country, it is known for its agricultural heritage and as the historical home of the New Holland farm machinery company.

History

The area was settled in the early 18th century by German and Swiss immigrants. The community grew around farming and took the name New Holland, said to honour the Dutch homeland of some early settlers or the Dutch who financed travel to America.

In 1895 Abe Zimmerman opened a machine shop in New Holland that grew into the New Holland Machine Company, famous for farm equipment including an automatic hay baler; the brand survives today as New Holland Agriculture under CNH Industrial.

Geography and community

New Holland lies in eastern Lancaster County amid fertile farmland, within the Pequea Valley. The surrounding township of Earl is home to a large Amish and Mennonite population, and horse-drawn buggies are a common sight.

The borough hosts the annual New Holland Farmers Fair, a community tradition dating to the 1920s.
