United States Bureau of Reclamation

The United States Bureau of Reclamation, usually called Reclamation, is a federal agency within the Department of the Interior that manages water resources in the western United States. Founded in 1902 under the Reclamation Act, it built many of the large dams, reservoirs, canals and hydroelectric plants that supply irrigation and municipal water in the arid West.

History

The early Reclamation Service sought to “reclaim” arid lands for farming by storing rivers and delivering irrigation water. In the 1930s its mission expanded to hydroelectric power, flood control and Depression-era employment, producing landmarks such as Hoover Dam on the Colorado River and Grand Coulee Dam on the Columbia. Mid-century projects included the Central Valley Project in California and the Colorado River Storage Project.

Operations

Reclamation operates hundreds of dams and reservoirs and delivers water to millions of acres of farmland and tens of millions of people. It is one of the largest producers of hydroelectric power in the United States. Regional offices work with irrigation districts, tribes, states, power customers and environmental agencies, and many facilities have been transferred to local operators while remaining federally owned.

Modern mission

Since the large dam-building era ended, the agency has focused more on dam safety, water conservation, recycling and reuse, drought response, river restoration, fish and wildlife habitat, Native American water rights settlements and adaptation to climate change. Decisions over scarce western water—especially in the Colorado River Basin—remain politically and environmentally contentious.
