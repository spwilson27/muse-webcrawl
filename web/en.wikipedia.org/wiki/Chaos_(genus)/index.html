Chaos (genus)

Chaos is a genus of single-celled amoeboid organisms in the family Amoebidae. Members of the genus are among the largest known amoebae: the best-known species, Chaos carolinense, can reach 5 mm in length, large enough to be visible to the naked eye. These multinucleate amoebae move and feed by means of pseudopodia and have long been used as model organisms in cell biology, notably in studies of amoeboid movement and cytoplasmic streaming.

Taxonomy

The genus was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1767, based on the organism Rösel von Rosenhof had earlier called the "little Proteus." The naming history is tangled: Linnaeus's name Volvox chaos and subsequent combinations led to longstanding confusion with Amoeba proteus, and the familiar species name Chaos carolinensis (later corrected to the neuter carolinense) was settled only in the 20th century. Recognized species include C. carolinense, C. illinoisense, and C. nobile.

Description

Chaos species are polypodial amoebae with hundreds of nuclei distributed through a strongly granular cytoplasm. They feed by phagocytosis on bacteria, algae, other protists, and even small multicellular animals such as rotifers. Like other Amoebidae they reproduce asexually by binary fission, in which the many nuclei divide before the cell splits. Their large size and vigorous cytoplasmic streaming made them favourite subjects for classical experiments on cell motility, membrane physiology, and the effects of microsurgery on cells.
