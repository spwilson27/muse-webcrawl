Bokmål
English: "book tongue"
Native to: Norway
Type: Written standard of Norwegian
Regulated by: Language Council of Norway (Språkrådet)
Language code: nb

Bokmål (literally "book language" or "book tongue") is one of the two official written standards of the Norwegian language, alongside Nynorsk. It is used by an estimated 85–90 percent of Norway's population and is the dominant form taught to foreign students of Norwegian.

History

Bokmål descends from the Danish written language used in Norway during the centuries of the Dano-Norwegian union (1380–1814), when Danish was the official language of administration and literature. After Norway's separation from Denmark, the written language was gradually Norwegianized through a series of spelling reforms, notably those of 1907, 1917, and 1938, which brought it closer to the speech of urban eastern Norway. The standard was officially called Riksmål ("national language") until 1929, when the name Bokmål was adopted by the Storting.

Dialects and usage

Norway does not have an official spoken standard, and Bokmål can be pronounced according to any regional dialect, though it is most closely associated with the dialects of Eastern Norway and the educated Oslo speech variety sometimes called Standard Østnorsk. Spoken Danish and Bokmål are largely mutually intelligible, and Bokmål shares much of its written form with Danish, differing most in vocabulary and in some grammatical features such as three grammatical genders.
