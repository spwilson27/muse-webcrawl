Merry Cemetery

The Merry Cemetery (Romanian: Cimitirul Vesel) is a cemetery in the village of Săpânța, Maramureș County, Romania. It is famous for its brightly coloured tombstones, which are painted in vivid hues — especially a distinctive shade of blue — and decorated with naive artwork and humorous, rhyming epitaphs that describe, in a light-hearted way, the lives, occupations, and sometimes the manner of death of the people buried there.

The colourful tombstones of the Merry Cemetery

History

The tradition is attributed to the local craftsman Stan Ioan Pătraș, who began carving the cemetery's distinctive crosses in the mid-1930s. Around 1935, Pătraș started adding short, ironic poems to the crosses, written in the first person as if spoken by the deceased. Pătraș himself carved and painted over 800 such crosses before his death in 1977. His house in Săpânța is now a small museum, and his own tombstone can be seen in the cemetery.

After Pătraș's death, the work was continued by his apprentice Dumitru Pop Tincu, who maintained the style of oak crosses painted with scenes from the deceased's life and witty verses in the local dialect.

Description

Each tombstone consists of an oak cross painted predominantly in a bright blue known locally as "Săpânța blue". A panel above the cross depicts a scene from the life of the deceased — a ploughman with his horse, a weaver at her loom, a shepherd with his flock, or, in more recent times, a person with a tractor, a car, or a computer. Below the image is the epitaph, typically a short poem written in the first person, often playful and self-deprecating, but sometimes candid about the deceased's flaws, such as a fondness for drink.

The cemetery reflects a local attitude toward death influenced, some suggest, by ancient Dacian beliefs that death was a passage to a better life and should be greeted with joy rather than fear. It has become one of Romania's best-known tourist attractions and is often listed among the most unusual cemeteries in the world.
