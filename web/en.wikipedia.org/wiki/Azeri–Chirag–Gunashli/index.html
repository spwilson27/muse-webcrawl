Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli

Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli (Azerbaijani: Azəri–Çıraq–Günəşli) or ACG is a complex of oil fields in the Caspian Sea, about 120 kilometres (75 mi) off the coast of Azerbaijan. The complex comprises the Azeri, Chirag and the deepwater portion of the Gunashli fields and is the largest oilfield complex in the Azerbaijani sector of the Caspian basin.

History

The production sharing agreement for the ACG fields was signed in Baku on 20 September 1994 between Azerbaijan and a consortium of international oil companies, in what Azerbaijan has called the "Contract of the Century". The agreement was the first major Western investment in the post-Soviet Caspian region. The Azerbaijan International Operating Company (AIOC), operated by BP, manages the development.

Oil production began from the Chirag field in 1997, followed by phased development of the Azeri field (Central, West and East Azeri) and Deepwater Gunashli. Crude from ACG is exported mainly through the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline to the Turkish Mediterranean coast, as well as via the Baku–Supsa pipeline and by rail.

Production

The fields contain recoverable reserves originally estimated at more than 5 billion barrels of oil. In 2017 the production sharing agreement was extended to 2049 with revised terms, and a new platform, Azeri Central East, was sanctioned as the next development phase. ACG production has long accounted for the majority of Azerbaijan's total oil output, although production has declined from its peak of around 820,000 barrels per day in 2010.
