Bell pepper

Species: Capsicum annuum

Cultivar group: Grossum Group

Heat: 0 Scoville units (sweet)

Origin: Americas

The bell pepper (also known as sweet pepper, pepper, or capsicum) is the fruit of plants in the Grossum Group of the species Capsicum annuum. Cultivars produce fruits in colours including red, yellow, orange, green, white, and purple. Unlike most other capsicums, bell peppers contain little or no capsaicin and are therefore sweet rather than hot; on the Scoville scale they rate zero.

History

Peppers were domesticated in the Americas thousands of years ago and were brought to Europe after the voyages of Christopher Columbus, spreading from there to Asia and Africa. The blocky, mild bell pepper was developed through selective breeding in the 20th century, notably in Hungary and the United States.

Culinary use

Bell peppers are eaten raw in salads, roasted, grilled, stir-fried, or stuffed. Green peppers are unripe fruits with a more bitter flavour, while fully ripe red peppers are sweeter and richer in vitamin C and beta-carotene. Peppers are central to many cuisines, including Mexican (fajitas), Mediterranean (ratatouille, peperonata), Chinese, and Hungarian cooking; ground dried peppers produce paprika.

Nutrition

Bell peppers are an excellent source of vitamin C — a red pepper can contain roughly three times the vitamin C of an orange by weight — as well as vitamin A, vitamin B6, and antioxidants such as lycopene in red varieties.
