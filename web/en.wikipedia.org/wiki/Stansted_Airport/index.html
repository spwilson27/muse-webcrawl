London Stansted Airport (IATA: STN, ICAO: EGSS) is an international airport serving London, England. It is located near Stansted Mountfitchet, Essex, 42 mi (68 km) northeast of Central London. It is the fourth-busiest airport in the United Kingdom by passenger traffic, behind Heathrow, Gatwick, and Manchester.

History

The site began as RAF Stansted Mountfitchet, a United States Army Air Forces bomber base opened in 1943 during the Second World War. After postwar civil use, the government designated Stansted in 1985 as London's third airport. The current terminal, designed by Norman Foster and notable for its "floating" roof supported by a space-frame structure, opened in 1991. The airport was sold by Heathrow Airport Holdings to Manchester Airports Group in 2013.

Operations

Stansted is a major base for low-cost carriers, in particular Ryanair, its largest airline, and also hosts easyJet, Jet2, and cargo and long-haul services. It has a single runway and a main terminal with three satellite buildings, and is connected to London by the Stansted Express rail service to Liverpool Street.
