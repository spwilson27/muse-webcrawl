The Wills Memorial Building is a landmark neo-Gothic building of the University of Bristol, situated at the top of Park Street on Queens Road in Bristol, England. Opened in 1925, it is one of the last great Gothic buildings erected in Britain and a symbol of the city.

History

The building was commissioned by the tobacco magnates George and Henry Wills in memory of their father, Henry Overton Wills III, the university's first chancellor and a principal benefactor. Designed by Sir George Oatley in Perpendicular Gothic style and built in reinforced concrete faced with Bath stone, it was opened on 9 June 1925 by King George V and Queen Mary.

Features

Its central tower rises 65.5 metres and houses Great George, a bell of over nine tonnes that is among the largest in England. Inside are the Great Hall, used for graduation ceremonies, a council chamber, and facilities for the university's law and earth sciences departments. The building is Grade II* listed.
