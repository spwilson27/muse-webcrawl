Shimekazari

A shimekazari (注連飾り, also written しめ飾り or 締め飾り) is a Japanese New Year decoration consisting of a sacred rope (shimenawa) made of rice straw, adorned with auspicious ornaments, hung above doors and gateways during the New Year season. It serves both to welcome the toshigami (deity of the new year) and to ward off evil spirits and misfortune, marking the enclosed space as purified and sacred for the coming year.

Description

The basic form is a twisted rope of rice straw, related to the shimenawa hung at Shinto shrines and around sacred objects. Attached decorations vary by region and household budget but commonly include paper zigzag streamers (shide), a bitter orange (daidai, a pun wishing prosperity for "generation after generation"), kelp (konbu, associated with joy), a lobster (ise-ebi, symbolising longevity because of its bent "waist" like an old person), fern fronds (purity and growth), pine sprigs, and dried persimmons or other lucky motifs. Regional styles include the elaborate gohei-like forms of some areas and the ring-shaped shimewa common in others.

Customs

Shimekazari are traditionally put up in late December — many households hang them on 28 December, considered doubly auspicious because the word for "eight" connotes growth, while 29 December ("double suffering") and 31 December (mere overnight decoration) are avoided. They are kept in place through the matsu no uchi period (roughly until 7 January in Kantō, 15 January in Kansai) and then ritually burned in the dondoyaki (or sagichō) fire festival in mid-January, returning the year's luck to the deities and praying for health. Smaller versions are also placed on cars, bicycles, and business premises.
