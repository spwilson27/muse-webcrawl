Newark-on-Trent, or simply Newark, is a market town and civil parish in the Newark and Sherwood district in the county of Nottinghamshire, England. It stands on the River Trent, and was originally a centre around Newark Castle, now largely ruined. In the Middle Ages it was a major inland port and an important wool centre. At the 2021 census the parish had a population of 30,345.

History

The town stands where the Roman Fosse Way crossed the Trent. Its place-name may derive from a Celtic river name; the town appears in Domesday Book as Newerche. Newark Castle was founded in the twelfth century by Bishop Alexander of Lincoln; King John died at the castle in October 1216. During the English Civil War the royalist town withstood three sieges before surrendering on the orders of Charles I in May 1646. Newark later became a centre of malting and brewing, engineering, and the manufacture of agricultural machinery, bearings and pumps.

Landmarks

The parish church of St Mary Magdalene is noted for its tall spire, among the highest in England, and the market place is lined with Georgian buildings, including the former Saracen's Head inn. The ruins of Newark Castle overlook the river, and the town hosts the National Civil War Centre, opened in 2015. The Trent Navigation made the town a busy river port, and the River Devon joins the Trent here.

Transport

Newark has two railway stations: Newark North Gate, on the East Coast Main Line with services to London King's Cross, York and Edinburgh, and Newark Castle, on the Nottingham–Lincoln line. The A1 and A46 trunk roads meet near the town, which grew significantly in the late twentieth century as a commuter and distribution centre.
