Sterol

A sterol is any of a group of natural steroid alcohols derived from plants, animals and fungi. All sterols are derived from a tetracyclic triterpene skeleton (with modifications). Sterols are a subgroup of steroids and an important class of organic molecules. They are a type of lipid.

The best-known sterol is cholesterol, which is vital to cell membrane structure and function and a precursor to fat-soluble vitamins and steroid hormones. Sterols occur naturally in plants, animals, and fungi, and can also be found in some bacteria.

Types

Phytosterols

Phytosterols are sterols present in plants. Notable examples include campesterol, sitosterol, and stigmasterol. They are found in vegetable oils, nuts, and seeds, and can help reduce the absorption of cholesterol in the gut.

Zoosterols

Sterols present in animals are called zoosterols. Important zoosterols include cholesterol and some steroid hormones.

Mycosterols

Sterols present in fungi are called mycosterols. A common example is ergosterol, which is a target of many antifungal drugs.

Role in biochemistry

Sterols are essential components of the membranes of all eukaryotic cells. They modulate the fluidity and permeability of the lipid bilayer and serve as signaling molecules and precursors for steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D.
