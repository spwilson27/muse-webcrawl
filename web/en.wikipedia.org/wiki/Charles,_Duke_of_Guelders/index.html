Charles, Duke of Guelders

Charles, Duke of Guelders (10 November 1467 – 30 June 1538), often called Charles of Egmond, was Duke of Guelders and Count of Zutphen from 1492 until his death. The son of Adolf of Egmond and Catherine of Bourbon, he spent much of his youth as a prisoner and hostage of the Burgundian and Habsburg rulers who had seized his father's duchy, and after regaining Guelders in 1492 he spent a reign of nearly half a century resisting Habsburg attempts to absorb it, in alliance with France. He is remembered as the last effective independent ruler of Guelders before its incorporation into the Habsburg Netherlands.

Early life and captivity

Charles was captured with his father at the fall of Zutphen in 1471 and taken to the Burgundian court at Ghent after Charles the Bold conquered Guelders in 1473. After his father's death in 1477 he remained a hostage of Maximilian of Austria and campaigned with Habsburg forces in Hungary. In 1492 the cities of Guelders ransomed him, repudiated Habsburg rule, and proclaimed him duke.

Reign

Supported by French subsidies and by troops under commanders such as Maarten van Rossum, Charles waged intermittent war against the Habsburgs for control of the eastern Netherlands, periodically overrunning parts of Overijssel, Friesland, and Groningen. His forces famously sacked and raided deep into Holland. Treaties in 1528 (Gorinchem) temporarily recognized his rule, but he refused to accept Habsburg succession arrangements for his duchy, seeking instead a French or Cleves succession. He died at Arnhem in 1538, and Guelders passed briefly to William of Cleves before Emperor Charles V annexed it in 1543.
