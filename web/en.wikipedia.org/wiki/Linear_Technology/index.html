Linear Technology Corporation was an American designer and manufacturer of high-performance analog integrated circuits. Founded in 1981 in Milpitas, California, by Robert H. Swanson Jr. and Robert Dobkin, the company became one of the most profitable chipmakers in the industry, serving industrial, automotive, communications and military markets.

Business

Linear's catalogue of some 7,500 products included power management devices (voltage regulators, battery chargers, DC/DC converters), amplifiers, data converters, interface and RF products, sold under the slogan of serving "analog" functions computers could not digitise. The company famously maintained gross margins above 70 percent and a culture of engineer-led design centres across North America, Europe and Asia.

Acquisition

In July 2016 Analog Devices announced it would acquire Linear Technology for about $14.8 billion in cash and stock; the deal closed in March 2017, creating a combined analog semiconductor leader. The Linear name and its "LTC" part-number prefixes continued in use within Analog Devices' product lines.
