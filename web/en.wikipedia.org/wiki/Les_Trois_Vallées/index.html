Les Trois Vallées

Les Trois Vallées (The Three Valleys) is a ski area in the Tarentaise Valley of the French Alps, in the Savoie department, and is widely described as the largest linked ski area in the world. Since the valleys were connected by lifts and passes in 1973, a single lift pass has covered about 600 kilometres of pistes served by some 180 ski lifts.

Resorts

The three valleys are named for their rivers and comprise the resorts of Courchevel, Méribel, and Les Menuires–Saint-Martin-de-Belleville in the Belleville valley with Val Thorens — at 2,300 metres the highest ski resort in Europe — plus La Tania, Brides-les-Bains, and Orelle, which gives access from the Maurienne valley. The highest lift-served point is the Cime de Caron at 3,200 metres. Courchevel and Méribel co-hosted events of the 1992 Albertville Winter Olympics, and jointly staged the 2023 Alpine World Ski Championships.

History and tourism

The area's development began in the 1930s–40s, when British and French planners laid out Courchevel and Méribel as purpose-built resorts. The Trois Vallées attracts several million skier-days per season and is especially popular with British visitors. Much of the terrain lies within or beside the Vanoise National Park, France's first national park, founded in 1963.
