The Independent Games Festival (IGF) is an annual festival at the Game Developers Conference, the largest annual gathering of the independent video game industry. It was founded in 1998 to promote and recognise innovation in independent video game development, modelled in part on the Sundance Film Festival's role for independent film.

Awards

The festival's competition culminates in the IGF Awards. The top honour is the Seumas McNally Grand Prize, named after the programmer of the first grand-prize-winning game, Tread Marks, who died in 2000. Other categories have included Excellence in Visual Art, Excellence in Audio, Excellence in Design, Excellence in Narrative, the Nuovo Award for experimental games, Best Student Game, and the Audience Award.

Notable winners

Grand Prize winners have included Minecraft, Fez, Papers, Please, Her Story, Quadrilateral Cowboy, Night in the Woods, Return of the Obra Dinn, A Short Hike, Umurangi Generation, Inscryption, and Betrayal at Club Low. Many IGF finalists have gone on to major commercial and critical success, making the festival an influential showcase for emerging developers.

Structure

Developers submit their games months in advance and pay a small entry fee; volunteer judges drawn from the industry and press evaluate the entries and select finalists in each category. Finalists exhibit their games on the GDC show floor, and the winners are announced at the awards ceremony, traditionally held on the Wednesday evening of the conference alongside the Game Developers Choice Awards.
