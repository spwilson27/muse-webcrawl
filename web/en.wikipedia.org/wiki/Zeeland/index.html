Zeeland (Dutch: [ˈzeːlɑnt], literally "sea-land") is the westernmost and least populous province of the Netherlands. It lies in the southwest of the country, bordering Belgium, and consists of a number of islands and peninsulas in the delta of the Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt rivers, together with a strip of mainland bordering Belgium (Zeelandic Flanders). The provincial capital is Middelburg.

Geography

Much of Zeeland lies below sea level and was reclaimed from the sea by its inhabitants over the centuries. The province's islands — including Walcheren, Noord-Beveland, Zuid-Beveland, Schouwen-Duiveland, Tholen, and Sint Philipsland — are now connected by the dams and barriers of the Delta Works. Tourism is an important economic activity, particularly on the beaches of Walcheren and Schouwen-Duiveland, and the ports of Vlissingen and Terneuzen serve industry along the Ghent–Terneuzen Canal.

History

Zeeland was contested between the counts of Holland and Flanders in the Middle Ages before passing to Burgundian and Habsburg rule. It was one of the seven provinces of the Dutch Republic and a major maritime trading region; the Dutch East India Company's chamber in Middelburg was second only to Amsterdam's. On 1 February 1953, the catastrophic North Sea flood devastated the province, killing 1,836 people in the Netherlands, most of them in Zeeland — a disaster that prompted the construction of the Delta Works, including the Oosterscheldekering storm surge barrier. The province gave its name to New Zealand, named by Dutch explorer Abel Tasman after Zeeland (via the Latinised "Nova Zeelandia").
