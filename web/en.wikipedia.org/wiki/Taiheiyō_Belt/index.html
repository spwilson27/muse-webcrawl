Taiheiyō Belt

The Taiheiyō Belt (太平洋ベルト, Taiheiyō beruto), literally "Pacific Belt", also known in English as the Tokaido corridor, is the megalopolis in Japan extending from Ibaraki Prefecture in the north, along the Pacific coast of Honshu through the Greater Tokyo Area, Nagoya, Osaka and Kobe, and onward to Fukuoka Prefecture in Kyushu. The name comes from the belt's position along the Pacific (Taiheiyō) side of Japan.

Geography and population

The corridor follows the historic Tōkaidō and San'yōdō roads and the modern Tōkaidō and San'yō Shinkansen railway lines. It contains Japan's largest metropolitan areas — Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka, Kobe and Fukuoka — and the major industrial regions of Keihin, Chūkyō, Hanshin and Kitakyūshū. A large majority of Japan's population, on the order of 80 million people or more depending on definition, lives within the belt, as does most of the country's manufacturing output, port capacity and heavy industry, much of it built on reclaimed coastal land during the post-war economic boom.

Development

The term came into use as the Japanese government designated the Pacific coastal zone as the priority area for industrial development under the income-doubling plans of the 1960s. Concentration of industry in the belt produced severe pollution problems in the 1960s and 1970s, prompting later national plans that sought, with limited success, to disperse industry and population to other regions. The corridor remains the economic heart of Japan, and its transport axis is the route planned for the Chūō Shinkansen maglev line.
