Spirit possession


Possession rituals occur in many cultures.

Spirit possession is an unusual or altered state of consciousness and associated behaviours said to arise from the control of a human body by spirits, ghosts, demons, or deities. The concept is widespread across cultures and religions, and possession may be viewed as voluntary and beneficial, as in the work of mediums and shamans, or as involuntary and pathological, requiring exorcism.

Forms

Anthropologists distinguish between possession by benevolent beings, sought deliberately in ritual contexts, and possession by malevolent entities believed to cause illness or misfortune. In Afro-Brazilian Candomblé, Haitian Vodou, and many African and Asian traditions, possession by deities or ancestors is a central and valued feature of worship, during which the possessed person is treated as a vessel through whom the spirit speaks and acts.

Exorcism

Where possession is judged harmful, many traditions prescribe exorcism — ritual expulsion of the possessing entity. The Roman Catholic Church maintains a formal rite of exorcism, and similar practices exist in Orthodox Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, and Buddhism.

Medical and psychological interpretations

Modern psychiatry generally interprets claimed possession states in terms of dissociative disorders, psychosis, epilepsy, or culturally shaped trance states. Possession-form presentations are recognised in diagnostic literature as a form of dissociative identity phenomena in some cultural settings.
