Ostinato

Ostinato
EtymologyItalian: "obstinate"
TypeCompositional technique
Related formsGround bass, passacaglia, chaconne, riff, loop

In music, an ostinato (Italian: "obstinate") is a motif or phrase that persistently repeats in the same musical voice, usually at the same pitch. The repeating idea may be a rhythmic pattern, part of a tune, or a complete melody. Ostinati are a feature of music across many cultures and periods, from medieval and Baroque ground-bass forms to jazz, rock and minimalism.

Ground bass

A repeating bass line is called a basso ostinato or ground bass. Baroque forms built on ground basses include the passacaglia and chaconne: the descending tetrachord of Pachelbel's Canon is perhaps the best-known example, and ostinato basses underpin works by Purcell, Bach and many others.

In later music

The short repeating figure in popular music is usually called a riff, and repeated recorded fragments form loops in electronic and hip-hop production. Twentieth-century composers such as Stravinsky, Bartók and Orff used ostinati for rhythmic drive, and the technique is foundational in minimalism, where composers such as Steve Reich and Philip Glass build large structures from small repeating cells.
