Gene Rayburn

Gene Rayburn (born Eugene Peter Rubessa; 22 December 1917 – 29 November 1999) — American radio and television personality; best known as host of Match Game.

Gene Rayburn (born Eugene Peter Rubessa; December 22, 1917 – November 29, 1999) was an American radio and television personality. He is best remembered as the host of various editions of the American game show Match Game, which he fronted in the 1960s and, most famously, from 1973 to 1982.

Career

Born in Christopher, Illinois, Rayburn moved to New York and worked in radio before serving in the US Army Air Forces during the Second World War. He became a popular morning radio host at WNEW in New York, notably as part of the duo "Rayburn and Finch".

On television, he was the announcer and sidekick on Steve Allen's Tonight show in the 1950s, and he hosted early game shows including The Sky's the Limit and Dough Re Mi. He hosted the original Match Game on NBC (1962–1969), and the revived CBS version (1973–1979) and its syndicated continuation became ratings successes, aided by Rayburn's playful style — and by his long, thin handheld microphone, an enduring visual trademark.

Later life

Rayburn hosted later revivals of Match Game (including Match Game-Hollywood Squares Hour in 1983–84) and Break the Bank (1985). He died in 1999 in Gloucester, Massachusetts.
