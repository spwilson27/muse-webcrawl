History of Indian Americans

The history of Indian Americans covers the experiences of people of Indian origin in the United States, from the earliest recorded arrivals in the colonial period to the present day. Indian Americans are among the largest and fastest-growing immigrant groups in the country, with a population of several million concentrated in metropolitan areas such as New York, the San Francisco Bay Area, Chicago, Houston, and Los Angeles.

Early arrivals

A handful of Indians reached British North America in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, often as servants or sailors aboard East India Company ships. The first recorded Indian in the United States is generally dated to about 1790, when an Indian sailor visited Salem, Massachusetts. In the early twentieth century, Punjabi farmers and labourers settled on the Pacific coast, particularly in California's Central Valley, where many married Mexican American women and formed the Punjabi Mexican American community.

Exclusion era

Rising anti-Asian sentiment led to the Immigration Act of 1917, which created the Asiatic Barred Zone excluding immigration from most of Asia, and to the Supreme Court decision in United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind (1923), which held that Indians, though classified as Caucasian by some anthropologists, were not "white" within the meaning of naturalization law. Dozens of previously naturalized Indian Americans were denaturalized. The Luce–Celler Act of 1946 restored a small quota and naturalization rights.

Post-1965 immigration

The Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 abolished national-origins quotas and opened large-scale immigration from India. The first post-1965 wave consisted heavily of professionals — physicians, engineers, and academics — while later waves included technology workers on H-1B visas and family-sponsored immigrants. Indian Americans became one of the highest-earning and most highly educated ethnic groups in the United States, prominent in medicine, information technology, academia, hospitality, and increasingly in politics.
