Gatwick Airport

Gatwick Airport (IATA: LGW, ICAO: EGKK), also known as London Gatwick, is a major international airport near Crawley, West Sussex, England, 29.5 miles (47.5 km) south of Central London. It is the second-busiest airport by total passenger traffic in the United Kingdom, after Heathrow, and was for many years the world's busiest single-runway airport. In 2019 it handled a record 46.6 million passengers.

Gatwick's two terminals serve more than 40 million passengers in a typical year.

History

An aerodrome was established at Gatwick in the late 1920s on land beside the A23 London–Brighton road; the name derives from an estate recorded as early as 1241. After use by the Royal Air Force during the Second World War, the government selected Gatwick for development as London's second airport. Rebuilt at a cost of £7.8 million, it was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 9 June 1958. The South Terminal was the original passenger terminal; the North Terminal opened in 1988, connected by an automated people mover.

Operations

Gatwick operates with a single main runway at high intensity — up to 55 movements per hour — supplemented by a parallel taxiway that can serve as a standby runway. The airport is a major base for low-cost carrier easyJet and hosts scheduled services to over 200 destinations. Long-running debate over London airport expansion has included proposals to build a second runway at Gatwick; the airport has also pursued bringing its standby runway into routine use.

Transport links

Gatwick Airport railway station, served by the Gatwick Express to London Victoria as well as Southern, Thameslink and Great Western services, gives the airport one of the best rail connections of any UK airport, with direct trains to London, Brighton, Reading and beyond.
