3G is the third generation of wireless mobile telecommunications technology. It was launched commercially in 2001 and provided a major increase in data capacity over the preceding 2G networks, enabling mobile Internet access, video calls, and mobile television on handsets. 3G networks were specified by the International Telecommunication Union under the IMT-2000 framework, which required data rates of at least 144 kbit/s for high-mobility users and up to 2 Mbit/s in fixed environments; later enhancements greatly exceeded these figures.

Standards

Two main standards families dominated 3G deployment. UMTS (Universal Mobile Telecommunications System), the successor to GSM, used wideband CDMA (W-CDMA) as its air interface and was widely adopted in Europe, Japan, and much of the world. CDMA2000, developed from the IS-95 (cdmaOne) standard, was deployed principally in North America and parts of Asia. Both families received packet-data upgrades — HSDPA and HSUPA, collectively HSPA and HSPA+ for UMTS, and EV-DO for CDMA2000 — that raised peak download speeds into the tens of megabits per second and led some operators to market them as "3.5G" or "3.75G".

History

Research into third-generation systems began in the 1980s, and the ITU's IMT-2000 initiative sought a single global standard, an ambition only partly realised. The Japanese operator NTT DoCoMo launched the first commercial 3G service, FOMA, in October 2001. Networks spread through Europe and North America over the following decade, accompanied by new spectrum auctions that raised large sums for governments. The arrival of smartphones, beginning with the iPhone in 2007, drove rapid growth in 3G data traffic.

Features and applications

Compared with 2G, 3G offered always-on packet data, richer multimedia messaging, mobile broadband for laptops via USB modems, video telephony, and location-based services. These capabilities laid the groundwork for the app economy that expanded further under 4G.

Shutdown

From the late 2010s onward, operators began switching off 3G networks to refarm spectrum for 4G and 5G. Carriers in the United States completed their 3G shutdowns in 2022, and many other countries followed, raising concerns about legacy devices such as alarms, vehicle telematics, and early smartphones that depended on 3G.
