Kaskaskia

Kaskaskia is a village in Randolph County, Illinois, United States. Founded by French Jesuit missionaries in 1703, it was for a time the largest settlement in the Illinois Country and served as the first capital of the state of Illinois. Because the Mississippi River changed course in 1881, cutting through the old channel of the Kaskaskia River, the village now sits on the Missouri (western) side of the Mississippi, an exclave of Illinois reachable by road only from Missouri.

History

The village takes its name from the Kaskaskia people, a tribe of the Illiniwek Confederation, whose Grand Village French missionaries visited in the 1670s. In 1703 French settlers founded a mission and trading post at the mouth of the Kaskaskia River. It grew into the administrative and commercial centre of Upper Louisiana, guarded by Fort de Chartres downstream, and its population reached several thousand.

After the French and Indian War, Kaskaskia passed to Britain in 1763 and then to the United States in 1778, when George Rogers Clark captured it during the Revolutionary War. When Illinois became a state in 1818, Kaskaskia was chosen as its first capital; the first General Assembly met there before the capital moved to Vandalia in 1820.

Decline and the river

The town declined through the 19th century as river commerce shifted. In April 1881 the Mississippi broke through into the bed of the lower Kaskaskia River during a flood, eventually destroying much of the old town site and leaving the remainder west of the main channel. Repeated flooding, including the Great Flood of 1993, reduced the population to a few dozen. The Church of the Immaculate Conception (1843) and the state memorial building housing the "Liberty Bell of the West" — a bell given by France in 1741 — survive as historic sites.
