Bungay

Bungay is a market town and civil parish in the East Suffolk district of Suffolk, England. It lies in the Waveney valley, about 12 miles (19 km) south-east of Norwich and 15 miles (24 km) west of Lowestoft, near the Norfolk border. The parish had a population of around 5,100 at the 2011 census.

History

Bungay's site, in a loop of the River Waveney protected on three sides by water, was settled early; Roman remains have been found nearby. Bungay Castle was built by the Norman lord Roger Bigod and rebuilt in stone by Hugh Bigod, 1st Earl of Norfolk, in the 12th century; its gatehouse and curtain walls survive. The town was largely destroyed by fire in 1688 and rebuilt, acquiring its Butter Cross in 1689.

Bungay is famous in folklore for the legend of Black Shuck, the phantom black dog said to have attacked St Mary's Church during a violent storm on 4 August 1577, an event recorded in Abraham Fleming's pamphlet A Straunge and Terrible Wunder. The black dog remains a town emblem, appearing on the town sign and as the name of the local running club's marathon.

Amenities and culture

The town has a weekly market, the Fisher Theatre, and independent shops noted after Bungay's "town against the supermarkets" campaigns. The novelist Sir Henry Rider Haggard was born nearby at Bradenham and had lifelong ties to the area, while the Earsham Street printing firm of Clays — founded in the 19th century — became one of Britain's largest book printers. The town lies on the Angles Way long-distance path.
