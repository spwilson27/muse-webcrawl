The Confederation Bridge (French: Pont de la Confédération) is a box-girder road bridge spanning the Abegweit Passage of the Northumberland Strait between Prince Edward Island and New Brunswick, Canada. At 12.9 kilometres, it is Canada's longest bridge and the longest bridge in the world over ice-covered water.

Construction

Prince Edward Island's Terms of Union with Canada in 1873 had promised a continuous communication link with the mainland, served for over a century by ferries. After a 1988 plebiscite narrowly favoured a fixed link, the bridge was built from 1993 to 1997 as a public–private project. It consists of 62 piers supporting precast concrete segments assembled with a purpose-built launching truss; conical ice shields on the piers protect against moving sea ice. The project cost about C$1 billion.

Operation

The bridge carries the Trans-Canada Highway and replaced the ferry service between Borden-Carleton, P.E.I., and Cape Jourimain, New Brunswick. Tolls are collected only when leaving Prince Edward Island. The Mi'kmaq name for the crossing, and the strait itself, reflects the much older history of travel across the passage.
