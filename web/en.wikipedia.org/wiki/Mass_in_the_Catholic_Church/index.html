Mass in the Catholic Church

Celebration of the Eucharist, the central act of Catholic worship.

The Mass is the central liturgical service of the Catholic Church, in which the Eucharist is celebrated. The term is derived from the Latin missa, from the dismissal formula at the end of the rite, Ite, missa est ("Go, it is sent" / "Go, the Mass is ended"). Catholics believe that in the Mass the sacrifice of Christ on the cross is made present, and that the bread and wine consecrated by the priest become, by transubstantiation, the body and blood of Christ.

Structure

Since the liturgical reforms following the Second Vatican Council, the ordinary form of the Roman Rite Mass consists of two principal parts:

  1. The Liturgy of the Word — opening rites (procession, greeting, penitential act, Gloria, collect), readings from Scripture, a homily, the Creed, and the Prayer of the Faithful.
  2. The Liturgy of the Eucharist — presentation of the gifts of bread and wine, the Eucharistic Prayer including the consecration and the Lord's Prayer, the sign of peace, Holy Communion, and concluding rites ending with the dismissal.

Forms of the Roman Rite

The ordinary form is the Mass promulgated by Pope Paul VI in 1969 (the "Novus Ordo"), celebrated in the vernacular or in Latin. The extraordinary form refers to the Tridentine Mass according to the 1962 Roman Missal of John XXIII, whose celebration was restricted again by Pope Francis's 2021 motu proprio Traditionis custodes. The Eastern Catholic Churches celebrate corresponding services usually called the Divine Liturgy.

Theology

The Council of Trent (1545–1563) defined that the Mass is a true and proper sacrifice, in which the same Christ who offered himself on the cross is offered in an unbloody manner. The Church teaches the Real Presence of Christ in the Eucharist and requires Catholics to attend Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation. Frequent, ideally daily, celebration is encouraged; the faithful who are properly disposed receive Communion under the species of bread, and in many places also from the chalice.

Music and language

Gregorian chant holds pride of place in the liturgy, though congregational hymns and polyphony are also used. The Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, and Agnus Dei — the "Ordinary" of the Mass — have been set to music by countless composers, making the Mass one of the most important forms in Western art music.
