Glastonbury is a town and civil parish in Somerset, England, situated in the Somerset Levels at the foot of the Mendip Hills. Built around Glastonbury Abbey, once one of the richest and most powerful monasteries in England, the town is steeped in Christian and Arthurian legend: medieval monks claimed it as the site of the earliest church in Britain, founded by Joseph of Arimathea, and as the burial place of King Arthur and Queen Guinevere. Today it is a centre for pilgrimage, New Age spirituality and alternative culture, and lends its name to the nearby Glastonbury Festival, held at Pilton.

History

Settlement in the area dates to Neolithic times, evidenced by the Sweet Track, one of Britain's oldest engineered roads, built across the marshy Levels around 3800 BC, and by the Iron Age lake villages of Glastonbury and Meare. The abbey was founded in the early medieval period and endowed by Saxon kings; King Ine of Wessex is credited with building a stone church around 712. The monastery was rebuilt after a devastating fire in 1184. In 1191 its monks announced the discovery of the grave of Arthur and Guinevere, a claim that drew pilgrims and royal patronage. The abbey was dissolved by Henry VIII in 1539, when its last abbot, Richard Whiting, was hanged on Glastonbury Tor.

Landmarks and legend

Glastonbury Tor, a steep conical hill topped by the roofless tower of St Michael's Church, dominates the landscape and is associated in folklore with the Celtic otherworld of Annwn and with "ley lines" and the Glastonbury Zodiac of modern esoteric tradition. At its foot lie the Chalice Well and the White Spring. The town also preserves the Abbey ruins, St John's Church, the George and Pilgrims' Inn, and the Tribunal. The Holy Thorn, a hawthorn said to descend from Joseph of Arimathea's staff that flowered on Christmas Day, grows on Wearyall Hill; a sprig is traditionally sent to the monarch each Christmas.

The modern town

Glastonbury's High Street is noted for shops selling crystals, books and esoteric goods, reflecting its role as a hub of goddess spirituality, Druidry and alternative healing; the town hosts the Goddess Conference and has an elected "Mayor" alongside tongue-in-cheek ceremonial figures. The Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, founded by Michael Eavis in 1970 at Worthy Farm in Pilton, some six miles away, has made the town's name known worldwide, though the event itself takes place outside the town.
