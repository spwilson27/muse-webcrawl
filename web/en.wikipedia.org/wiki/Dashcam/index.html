Dashcam

A dashboard camera or simply dashcam, also known as a car digital video recorder (car DVR), driving recorder, or event data recorder, is an onboard camera that continuously records the view through a vehicle's front windscreen and sometimes rear or other windows. Dashcams typically record onto removable memory cards in a loop, may include GPS, accelerometers (G-sensors) that lock footage when a collision is detected, and parking-mode monitoring.

History and adoption

Police forces used dashboard video from the 1980s with bulky VHS systems. Consumer dashcams spread rapidly in the early 2010s as hardware became cheap. They became especially ubiquitous in Russia — where they served as protection against insurance fraud and disputed accident claims, and famously captured the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor — and in South Korea and China. In Britain, some insurers began offering discounts for dashcam use, and footage became routinely accepted by police through reporting portals.

Uses

Dashcam evidence helps determine fault in collisions, documents hit-and-runs and "crash for cash" scams, monitors learner and fleet drivers, and records scenic drives or incidents such as meteor falls and extreme weather. Taxi and rideshare drivers use interior-facing cameras for safety.

Legal status

Legality varies: dashcams are generally permitted for private use in the US, UK and Russia, subject to rules against obstructing the windscreen; in some European countries, notably Austria and (historically) Germany, continuous recording raises privacy-law concerns, and in Luxembourg public recording is restricted. Rules on audio recording and data protection (GDPR) also affect use and publication of footage.
