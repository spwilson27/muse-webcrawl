Succession crisis of 1580

Date: 1580
Claimants: António, Prior of Crato; Philip II of Spain; Catherine of Braganza
Result: Iberian Union under Philip II

The Portuguese succession crisis of 1580 arose after the death of King Sebastian at the Battle of Alcácer Quibir in Morocco in 1578 and the death of his successor, the aged Cardinal-King Henry, in January 1580 without a direct heir. The crisis ended with Philip II of Spain taking the Portuguese crown, beginning the Iberian Union (1580–1640).

Claimants

The principal claimants were António, Prior of Crato, an illegitimate son of Infante Louis, who enjoyed popular support; Philip II of Spain, whose mother Isabella was a Portuguese princess and who had the strongest dynastic claim among foreign candidates, backed by much of the nobility and clergy; and Catherine, Duchess of Braganza, whose claim through her father's descent was strong but who pressed it less forcefully.

War of the Portuguese Succession

António was acclaimed king in Santarém in July 1580, but Philip II sent an army under the Duke of Alba into Portugal. At the Battle of Alcântara on 25 August 1580, Alba defeated António's forces outside Lisbon. António fled and maintained resistance in the Azores, with French support, until Spanish naval victories in 1582–83 ended the challenge.

Outcome

Philip was recognised as King Philip I of Portugal by the Cortes of Tomar in 1581, promising to preserve Portuguese laws, institutions and colonial administration as separate from Spain's. The union lasted until the Portuguese Restoration War began in 1640, when John IV of the house of Braganza was proclaimed king.
