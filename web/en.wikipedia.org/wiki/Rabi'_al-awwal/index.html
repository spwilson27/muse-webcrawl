Rabi' al-awwal

Rabiʽ al-awwal (Arabic: رَبِيع ٱلْأَوَّل, "the first [month] of spring"), also known as Rabiʽ al-Awwal or Rabi I, is the third month of the Islamic lunar calendar. The name literally means "the first month of spring", recalling a time when the Islamic calendar's months aligned with the seasons.

Significance

Rabi' al-awwal holds particular significance for Muslims because it is traditionally held to be the month in which the Islamic prophet Muhammad was born. Most Sunni Muslims and some Shia Muslims celebrate the Mawlid, the birthday of Muhammad, on the 12th of Rabi' al-awwal, while many Shia Muslims observe it on the 17th of the month. The Mawlid is a public holiday in many Muslim-majority countries and is marked by processions, recitations of poetry in praise of Muhammad, charity, and gatherings.

According to traditional accounts, Muhammad's birth in the Year of the Elephant (c. 570 CE) fell in Rabi' al-awwal. The month also features in accounts of other early Islamic events and is observed by some Muslims as a time of death anniversaries of figures in Islamic history, including (in the Shia tradition) the eighth Imam's ancestors and the death of Muhammad's wife Khadija in other months' traditions; the most widely observed occasion remains the Mawlid.

Timing

Because the Islamic calendar is lunar and about eleven days shorter than the solar year, Rabi' al-awwal migrates through all the seasons over a cycle of about 33 years. The month has 30 days in some years and 29 in others, depending on the sighting of the crescent moon or on calculated calendars.
