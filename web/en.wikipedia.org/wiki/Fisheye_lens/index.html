Fisheye lens

TypeUltra wide-angle lens
Field of viewUp to 180° or more
IntroducedTerm coined 1906 (R. W. Wood)

A fisheye lens is an ultra wide-angle lens that produces strong visual distortion, mapping the scene onto a curved, hemispherical image. Fisheye lenses achieve an angle of view of up to 180 degrees or beyond, far wider than rectilinear wide-angle lenses, at the cost of bending straight lines that do not pass through the image centre.

History

The name was coined in 1906 by American physicist Robert W. Wood, who likened the view to that of a fish looking up through the water's surface (Snell's window). Early fisheye lenses were developed in the 1920s for meteorology, to record cloud cover over the whole sky. Mass-produced photographic fisheyes appeared in the 1960s.

Types

Circular fisheyes image the full hemisphere as a circle within the frame, while full-frame (diagonal) fisheyes fill the frame with an approximately 180° view across the diagonal. Projections used include equidistant, equisolid-angle and stereographic mappings. Applications range from scientific sky surveys and surveillance to action sports photography and planetarium projection.
