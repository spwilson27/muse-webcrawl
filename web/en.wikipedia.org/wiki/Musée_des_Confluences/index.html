Musée des Confluences
Location86 Quai Perrache, Lyon, France
Opened20 December 2014
ArchitectCoop Himmelb(l)au
CollectionNatural history, anthropology, science and societies

The Musée des Confluences is a museum of science and anthropology in Lyon, France, standing at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers. Designed by the Austrian firm Coop Himmelb(l)au in a dramatic deconstructivist style of steel and glass, it opened in December 2014, succeeding Lyon's old natural history museum (Musée Guimet).

Architecture

The building is composed of the "Crystal", a glass entrance volume with a swirling gravity well, and the "Cloud", an opaque steel structure housing the galleries, raised on stilts above a public plinth. The project, conceived in 2001, ran far over schedule and budget — final costs approached €255 million — but the finished building became a landmark of Lyon's southern redevelopment.

Exhibitions

The permanent exhibition is organised around four questions — Where do we come from? Who are we? What do we do? Where are we going? — in galleries named Origins, Species, Societies and Eternities. Displays mix dinosaur skeletons, a mammoth discovered in the region, Egyptian antiquities, ethnographic objects and industrial artefacts, in the cross-disciplinary spirit of Paris's Quai Branly museum and Marseille's Mucem. The museum holds more than two million objects in reserve.
