The Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey is an American multi-mission, tiltrotor military aircraft with both vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) and short takeoff and landing (STOL) capabilities. It is designed to combine the functionality of a conventional helicopter with the long-range, high-speed cruise performance of a turboprop aircraft.

Development

The program that produced the V-22 began in 1981 under the Joint-service Vertical take-off/landing Experimental (JVX) program. Bell Helicopter and Boeing Helicopters teamed up and won the development contract in 1986. The first prototype flew on 19 March 1989. Development was long and controversial: testing accidents in 1991, 1992 and two fatal crashes in 2000 (killing 23 Marines in total) forced redesigns, including of the nacelle hydraulics and flight-control software, before the aircraft entered service with the U.S. Marine Corps in 2007.

Design

The Osprey has two Rolls-Royce T406 (AE 1107C-Liberty) turboshaft engines, each driving a three-bladed proprotor on wingtip nacelles that rotate through about 97.5 degrees. In helicopter mode the nacelles are vertical; for forward flight they tilt to make the aircraft a turboprop with a cruising speed of about 275 knots (509 km/h). The wings and rotors fold so the aircraft can be stowed aboard ships. Variants include the MV-22B for the Marine Corps, the CV-22B for Air Force special operations, and the CMV-22B carrier onboard delivery aircraft for the Navy.

Operational history

Ospreys first deployed to Iraq in 2007 and later served extensively in Afghanistan, Libya and elsewhere. Japan became the first export customer, operating the type from 2020. The fleet was temporarily grounded after fatal crashes in Australia (2023) and off Yakushima, Japan (November 2023, eight killed), the latter traced to a failure in the proprotor gearbox, and operations resumed under flight restrictions.
