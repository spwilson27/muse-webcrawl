Rackets (sport)

Rackets or racquets is an indoor racket sport played in a large enclosed court with a hard ball, originating in the debtors' prisons of 18th-century London. It is a fast game, played with long wooden rackets against the walls of a court roughly 60 feet by 30 feet, and is a forerunner of squash.

History

The game developed at the King's Bench and Fleet prisons, where inmates played against the yard walls. It was taken up at Harrow School in the early 19th century and standardised there; the open court at Harrow became the model for dedicated courts. An open championship began in the 1820s, and the sport spread to other public schools and clubs.

Play

Rackets is played as singles or doubles; the small, hard ball can reach speeds of around 180 mph (290 km/h), among the fastest of any racket sport. The service must strike the front wall above the service line, and rallies proceed by rebounding the ball off the walls. Because of the ball's speed and hardness, players historically wore little protection.

Present day

Today the game survives at a small number of schools and clubs in Britain and North America, governed by the Tennis and Rackets Association. Its gentler offshoot, squash, played with a soft ball in a smaller court, became far more widespread.
