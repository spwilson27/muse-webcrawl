Škoda Works

Škoda Works Czech: Škodovy závody
Founded: 1859 (engineering works); bought by Emil Škoda in 1869
Headquarters: Plzeň, Bohemia (then Austria-Hungary)
Industry: Heavy engineering, armaments, locomotives, vehicles
Fate: Nationalised 1945; successor companies include Škoda Auto and Škoda Transportation
The Škoda Works in Plzeň, one of Europe's great heavy-industry combines.

The Škoda Works (Czech: Škodovy závody) was one of the largest industrial conglomerates of twentieth-century Europe, based in Plzeň in Bohemia. Its roots lie in the engineering works of Count Waldstein, founded in 1859 and bought in 1869 by the engineer Emil Škoda, from whom the enterprise took its name.

Under Škoda and his successors the works grew into the principal armaments supplier of Austria-Hungary, producing artillery — including the famous Škoda mortars and mountain guns — naval guns, armour plate and machinery. Between the wars, as part of independent Czechoslovakia and in partnership with the French Schneider group, it diversified into locomotives, aircraft, ships, electrical equipment and motor vehicles, acquiring the car maker Laurin & Klement in 1925, the origin of today's Škoda Auto.

Later history

During the Second World War the works were absorbed into the German war economy under Hermann Göring's Reichswerke and were heavily bombed by the USAAF in April 1945. Nationalised after the war, the Škoda enterprise became Czechoslovakia's flagship heavy-engineering exporter, building power stations, rolling stock and the Škoda trolleybuses and locomotives used across the Eastern bloc. After 1989 the conglomerate was broken up and privatised in parts; the Škoda name survives in several successor firms, most prominently the car manufacturer and Škoda Transportation.
