Overview

St Pancras railway station, officially London St Pancras International since 2007, is a central London railway terminus on Euston Road in the Borough of Camden. Opened in 1868 by the Midland Railway, it is famous for William Barlow's single-span train shed — then the largest enclosed space in the world — and for the Midland Grand Hotel fronting it, a masterpiece of Gothic Revival by George Gilbert Scott.

Revival

Threatened with demolition in the 1960s, the station was saved by a campaign led by John Betjeman (whose statue stands on the concourse). As the London terminus of High Speed 1, it was transformed for Eurostar services from November 2007, with champagne bar, the restored hotel (St Pancras Renaissance), Platform 9¾'s neighbour King's Cross adjacent, and the British Library next door. Thameslink through-services and East Midlands trains share the site.
