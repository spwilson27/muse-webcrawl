The Monterey Bay Aquarium is a nonprofit public aquarium in Monterey, California, located on the site of a former sardine cannery on Cannery Row at the edge of Monterey Bay. When it opened in 1984 it was the largest aquarium in the United States. Its mission is to inspire conservation of the ocean, and it draws roughly two million visitors a year.

History

The aquarium grew out of a proposal by marine biologists from Stanford's Hopkins Marine Station and was funded principally by David and Lucile Packard, whose daughter Julie Packard, a marine biologist, became its founding executive director. It was built on the site of the Hovden Cannery, evoking the bay's sardine-fishing heritage. A major expansion, the Outer Bay (now Open Sea) wing, opened in 1996 for pelagic species such as tuna, sharks, and ocean sunfish.

Exhibits

Signature exhibits include the Kelp Forest, one of the tallest aquarium exhibits in the world, fed by raw seawater pumped directly from the bay; the million-gallon Open Sea tank; and displays of jellyfish, sea otters, penguins, and giant Pacific octopus. The aquarium was the first to successfully exhibit great white sharks temporarily (2004–2011), releasing them after monitoring. Its seawater system circulates thousands of gallons per minute from Monterey Bay, allowing sensitive filter-feeders to thrive.

Conservation and research

The aquarium runs the Seafood Watch programme, launched in 1999, which publishes consumer guides rating the sustainability of seafood. It also works in sea otter rehabilitation through its surrogate-rearing programme, and supports ocean policy through its associated Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute (MBARI) in Moss Landing.
