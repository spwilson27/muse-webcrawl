Stalin Epigram

The Stalin Epigram is a satirical poem by the Russian poet Osip Mandelstam, written in November 1933, which directly lampooned Soviet leader Joseph Stalin. Reciting it to a small circle of acquaintances led to Mandelstam's arrest and exile, and it became one of the most famous acts of literary defiance under Stalinism.

Osip Mandelstam (1891–1938)

The poem

The sixteen-line epigram depicts Stalin as a brutal, coarse tyrant — the "Kremlin highlander" — surrounded by sycophantic henchmen, savouring executions. Its opening reads, in one English rendering:

"We live, deaf to the land beneath us,
Ten steps away no one hears our speeches."

and it mocks Stalin's fingers "fat as grubs", his "cockroach whiskers", and decrees issued "like horseshoes".

Consequences

Someone among the listeners denounced the poem. In May 1934 the NKVD arrested Mandelstam; Stalin personally intervened in the case, and the poet was exiled to Voronezh rather than executed. Rearrested in 1938 during the Great Terror, he died in a transit camp near Vladivostok that December. His widow Nadezhda Mandelstam preserved the poem by memorising it, recounting its story in her memoir Hope Against Hope.
