A digital identity is information on an entity used by computer systems to represent an external agent — a person, organisation, application or device. It typically comprises identifiers (such as usernames or ID numbers), credentials used to authenticate (passwords, keys, biometrics), and attributes (name, date of birth, entitlements) that allow access to services to be granted and audited.

Models

Identity management historically followed centralised models, in which each service kept its own accounts, then federated models (single sign-on through providers such as identity federations using SAML or OpenID Connect), in which an identity provider vouches for the user. More recently, self-sovereign identity (SSI) approaches aim to give individuals wallets of cryptographically verifiable credentials issued by authorities, which can be presented selectively. National digital identity programmes — Estonia's e-ID, India's Aadhaar biometric system, and the European Union's eIDAS framework with its European Digital Identity Wallet — are prominent large-scale implementations.

Issues

Digital identity systems raise concerns about privacy and surveillance (linkability across services), exclusion of people unable to enrol or authenticate, identity theft and account takeover, and the security of centralised biometric databases. Conversely, reliable digital ID is promoted as enabling financial inclusion and access to public services for the roughly 850 million people worldwide estimated to lack official identification.
