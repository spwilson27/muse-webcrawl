Bernard Cyril Freyberg, 1st Baron Freyberg, VC, GCMG, KCB, KBE, DSO & Three Bars (21 March 1889 – 4 July 1963) was a British-born New Zealand soldier and Victoria Cross recipient who served as commander of the 2nd New Zealand Division during the Second World War and afterwards as Governor-General of New Zealand from 1946 to 1952.

First World War

Raised in Wellington, New Zealand, Freyberg travelled to Britain at the outbreak of war and joined the Royal Naval Division. He won the Distinguished Service Order at Gallipoli, where he famously swam ashore alone at Bulair to light diversionary flares, and on 13 November 1916, at Beaucourt-sur-Ancre during the Battle of the Somme, he won the Victoria Cross for leading an attack despite being wounded four times. He ended the war as a brigadier general, reputedly the youngest in the British Army, with the DSO and three Bars and having been wounded nine times.

Second World War

In 1939 Freyberg took command of the New Zealand Expeditionary Force and its 2nd Division, leading it in Greece, Crete (where he was Allied commander during the German airborne invasion), North Africa — including the Second Battle of El Alamein and the pursuit to Tunisia, during part of which he commanded X Corps — and Italy, where his division's assaults at Cassino in 1944, including the controversial bombing of the abbey of Monte Cassino, remain debated.

Later life

Created Baron Freyberg in 1951, he served as Deputy Constable and Lieutenant Governor of Windsor Castle after his term as Governor-General. He died at Windsor in 1963 and is buried at St Martha's, Guildford. Freyberg is remembered in New Zealand as one of the country's greatest soldiers; Freyberg High School in Palmerston North and numerous streets bear his name.
