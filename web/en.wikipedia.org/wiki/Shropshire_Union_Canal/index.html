Shropshire Union Canal

The Shropshire Union Canal is a navigable canal in England, linking the River Mersey at Ellesmere Port via Chester and Nantwich to Wolverhampton and the Birmingham canal network, with branches to Shrewsbury area and to Llangollen (now the Llangollen Canal).

History

The Shropshire Union Railways and Canal Company was formed in 1846 by amalgamation of several canals including the Ellesmere and Chester Canal and the Birmingham and Liverpool Junction Canal, the latter engineered by Thomas Telford with long straight cuttings and tall embankments, including the high embankment at Shelmore. Unusually, the company remained a canal operator under railway ownership and remained profitable into the 20th century, carrying goods until the 1960s.

Route

The main line runs from Ellesmere Port through Chester, where it shares the Chester Canal section, past Nantwich, Market Drayton and Gnosall to Autherley Junction near Wolverhampton. Branches include the Middlewich Branch and the Shrewsbury Branch (largely closed). The former Ellesmere branch to Llangollen, famous for the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct, is now separately named.

Today

Now managed by the Canal & River Trust, the "Shroppie" is popular with leisure boaters for its rural scenery, cuttings, and characteristic bridges and lock-keeper's huts.
