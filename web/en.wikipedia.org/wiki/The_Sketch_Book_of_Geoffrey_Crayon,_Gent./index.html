The Sketch Book of Geoffrey Crayon, Gent. is a collection of 34 essays and short stories written by the American author Washington Irving. It was first published serially in the United States in 1819 and 1820, and later in book form in England in 1820. The collection includes two of Irving's most famous stories, "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" and "Rip Van Winkle", both adapted from German folk tales and set in the Dutch-American Hudson Valley.

Written under the pen name Geoffrey Crayon, the book was Irving's first major commercial success and established his reputation on both sides of the Atlantic. It is widely regarded as the first American work of literature to achieve international fame and as a landmark of the short story form in English.

Background

Irving had moved to England in 1815 after the failure of the family business. Writing in the persona of the genial, wandering Geoffrey Crayon, he modelled the collection loosely on the periodical essay tradition of Addison and Steele, mixing gentle observational sketches of English life with tales drawn from American and European folklore.

Contents

The collection's best-known pieces include "Rip Van Winkle" and "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow", both presented as papers found among the effects of the fictional historian Diedrich Knickerbocker. Other notable sketches include "The Voyage", "English Writers on America", "Rural Life in England", "The Broken Heart", "The Art of Book-Making", "Westminster Abbey", "Christmas", "The Stage-Coach", "Christmas Eve", "Christmas Day", "The Christmas Dinner", and "John Bull".

Influence

The Sketch Book made Irving the first American man of letters to support himself by writing and encouraged a generation of American authors. Its Christmas sketches helped popularize many customs associated with the modern Christmas celebration in the English-speaking world.
