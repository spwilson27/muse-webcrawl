Albanian (endonym: shqip) is an Indo-European language that forms its own independent branch of the family. It is the official language of Albania and Kosovo and is also spoken in parts of North Macedonia, Montenegro, Greece and southern Italy, as well as in a large worldwide diaspora.

Dialects

Albanian divides into two principal dialect groups separated by the River Shkumbin: Gheg in the north and Tosk in the south. The modern standard language, adopted in the twentieth century, is based largely on Tosk with Gheg elements. The Arbëreshë variety spoken in Italy diverged several centuries ago and preserves archaic features.

History

The earliest surviving Albanian text is a baptismal formula from 1462, and the first book, Gjon Buzuku's Meshari, appeared in 1555. At the Congress of Monastir in 1908 Albanian intellectuals agreed on a single Latin-based alphabet, replacing the mixture of Latin, Greek and Arabic scripts previously used. Linguists still debate whether Albanian descends from ancient Illyrian, Thracian, or another Paleo-Balkan language, as direct evidence is sparse.

Features

Albanian has a postposed definite article (as in Romanian and Bulgarian, a Balkan sprachbund feature), three genders including a neuter in some varieties, and extensive loanwords from Latin, Greek, Slavic languages, Turkish and Italian layered over an Indo-European core vocabulary.
