Grammatical aspect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In linguistics, aspect is a grammatical category that expresses how an action, event, or state, denoted by a verb, extends over time. Unlike tense, which locates an event relative to a reference point (past, present, future), aspect describes the event's internal temporal structure — whether it is viewed as completed, ongoing, habitual, or repeated.

Perfective and imperfective

The most widespread aspectual contrast is between the perfective aspect, which views a situation as a single, bounded whole, and the imperfective aspect, which views it from within, without reference to its boundaries. This contrast is grammaticalized in Slavic languages, where most verbs exist in perfective/imperfective pairs, and in Ancient Greek verb stems.

Other aspects

Languages variously mark the progressive (ongoing action, e.g., English "is running"), habitual, iterative, inchoative (beginning), cessative (ending), and prospective aspects, through verb morphology, auxiliary verbs, or particles. The perfect (e.g., English "has run") is sometimes treated as an aspect, sometimes as a tense or a separate category.

Aspect and Aktionsart

Aspect (viewpoint aspect) is distinguished from Aktionsart (lexical aspect): the inherent temporal properties of a verb's meaning, such as whether it denotes a state, activity, accomplishment, or achievement in Vendler's classification. The interaction of grammatical aspect with lexical aspect and tense produces the temporal interpretation of a clause.
