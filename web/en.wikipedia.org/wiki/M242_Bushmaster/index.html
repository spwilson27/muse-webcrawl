The M242 Bushmaster is a 25 mm (25×137 mm) chain-driven autocannon. It is used extensively by the United States military, as well as by NATO and other allied forces, in ground combat vehicles and naval vessels. The weapon is the primary armament of the M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicle and is also mounted on the LAV-25 and in naval close-in weapon roles.

Design

Unlike most automatic weapons, which rely on recoil or gas pressure for cycling, the Bushmaster is a "chain gun": an electric motor drives a chain that cycles the bolt and feeds ammunition. This gives the gun a selectable and controllable rate of fire and makes misfires non-disruptive, as a dud round is simply ejected. It can fire single shots or bursts, and uses dual feed paths allowing rapid switching between armour-piercing and high-explosive ammunition.

Service

The M242 entered service in the 1980s and saw combat in the Gulf War, the Iraq War and Afghanistan. An enhanced version, the M242 with improved barrel and feed systems, remains in production with Northrop Grumman's Bushmaster family, which includes larger 30 mm and 40 mm variants.
