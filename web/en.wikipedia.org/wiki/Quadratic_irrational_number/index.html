Quadratic irrational number

In mathematics, a quadratic irrational number (also known as a quadratic surd or quadratic irrational) is an irrational number that is the solution of some quadratic equation with rational coefficients. Equivalently, it is a real number of the form a + b√d, where a and b are rational, b ≠ 0, and d is a square-free integer greater than 1 — for example, the golden ratio (1 + √5)/2.

Properties

Quadratic irrationals are characterised by having eventually periodic continued fraction expansions — a theorem of Lagrange. They form a field, the simplest class of algebraic numbers of degree 2, and any irrational root of a quadratic equation with integer coefficients is a quadratic irrational.

Examples

Familiar examples are √2, √3, and the golden ratio φ. Quadratic irrationals with the same discriminant are related through the theory of binary quadratic forms and class numbers.
