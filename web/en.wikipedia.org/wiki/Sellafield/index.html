Overview

Sellafield is a large multi-function nuclear site near Seascale on the coast of Cumbria, England. Opened as Windscale in the late 1940s to produce plutonium for Britain's atomic weapons programme (its two air-cooled "Piles"), it hosted the world's first commercial-scale nuclear power station, Calder Hall (1956), and became the centre of British spent-fuel reprocessing (Magnox and THORP plants). It is operated by Sellafield Ltd under the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority.

History and cleanup

The Windscale fire of October 1957 — burning graphite in Pile No. 1 — was the worst nuclear accident in British history (INES 5), releasing radioactive material including iodine-131 (milk from surrounding farms was destroyed, though the full picture emerged only decades later). The site was renamed Sellafield in 1981 amid the BNFL era and the Irish Sea discharge controversies pressed by Ireland and Norway. Reprocessing ended with THORP's closure (2018) and Magnox reprocessing (2022); the mission is now the century-scale, tens-of-billions-of-pounds task of decommissioning and managing Britain's most hazardous nuclear legacy, including the legacy ponds and silos — among Europe's most complex cleanup challenges.
