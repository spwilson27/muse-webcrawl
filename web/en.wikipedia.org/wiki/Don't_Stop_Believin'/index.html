Don't Stop Believin'

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Don't Stop Believin'" is a song by the American rock band Journey, released in 1981 as a single from their seventh studio album, Escape. Written by Steve Perry, Neal Schon, and Jonathan Cain, it describes a "small-town girl" and a "city boy" who board a midnight train; its title phrase came from encouragement Cain's father gave him. The song reached number nine on the Billboard Hot 100 on its original release.

Structure

Unusually, the song's chorus does not arrive until near the end; most of its length builds through verses and instrumental passages over a repeating piano figure by Cain, with Schon's guitar and Perry's vocals. The lyric's reference to being "born and raised in South Detroit" has been much noted, since the area south of downtown Detroit is across the river in Windsor, Canada; Perry has said the phrase simply sang better than the alternatives.

Revival

The song returned to prominence when it scored the final scene of the television series The Sopranos in 2007, and again as a signature number of Glee in 2009. It became one of the best-selling digital tracks from the 20th century, a staple of sporting events and karaoke, and Journey's signature song. In 2022 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry.
