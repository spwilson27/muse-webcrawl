The Emsland test facility (German: Transrapid-Versuchsanlage Emsland, TVE) was a testing site for Transrapid magnetic levitation (maglev) trains in Emsland, Lower Saxony, Germany. It was the largest maglev test facility in the world and operated for more than 25 years.

History

Construction of the facility began in 1980, and the first section was completed in 1984. The track formed a single loop with a total length of 31.8 kilometres (19.8 mi), running from a test centre near Lathen on elevated guideway supported by concrete pillars. Because the line was built largely over flat farmland and moorland, long straight sections allowed test vehicles to reach very high speeds.

Transrapid vehicles tested at Emsland included the Transrapid 06, 07, 08 and 09. In 1993 the Transrapid 07 set a world speed record for maglev passenger vehicles at the facility, and later vehicles regularly operated above 400 km/h (250 mph). The Transrapid 08 was the vehicle type subsequently deployed on the Shanghai Maglev Train line in China, the only commercial Transrapid application.

2006 accident

On September 22, 2006, the facility was the scene of the first fatal accident involving a maglev train. A Transrapid 08 carrying 29 passengers collided with a wheeled maintenance vehicle that had been left on the guideway, at a speed of about 200 km/h. Twenty-three people were killed and ten seriously injured. Two maintenance workers were later convicted of negligent homicide, and the accident was attributed to human error in dispatching rather than to the maglev technology itself.

Closure

Test operations ended in 2011 after funding was withdrawn, and the facility was closed. Dismantling of parts of the site followed, although the guideway and test centre buildings remained standing for years afterward, and the site has been considered for other industrial and research uses. The closure marked the end of large-scale maglev development testing in Germany.
