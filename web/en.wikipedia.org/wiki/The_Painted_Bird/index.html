The Painted Bird is a 1965 novel by Jerzy Kosiński, a Polish-American writer. It describes the wanderings of an unnamed boy — separated from his parents at the start of the Second World War — through the villages of an unnamed Eastern European country, where he witnesses and suffers extreme cruelty and superstition.

Plot and title

Sent by his parents to a foster mother in a remote village for safety, the boy is left alone when she dies. Mistaken by the villagers, because of his dark hair and eyes, for a Jew or a Roma, he moves from village to village, surviving brutality, witnessing atrocities committed by peasants and by German and Soviet soldiers, and gradually losing his innocence — and, after a trauma, his power of speech. The title comes from an episode in which a bird-catcher paints a captured bird in bright colours and releases it; its own flock, no longer recognising it, attacks and kills it.

Controversy and reception

The novel's graphic violence shocked many readers, but it won critical acclaim, including the French Prix du Meilleur Livre Étranger. Kosiński originally presented the book as drawn from his own wartime experiences; later biographical research showed that his family had in fact survived the war together in Poland under assumed identities, and the novel's autobiographical status became the subject of lasting controversy, alongside later disputes about Kosiński's working methods. The book is nonetheless regarded as a significant work of Holocaust-era literature. It was adapted into a feature film directed by Václav Marhoul, released in 2019.
