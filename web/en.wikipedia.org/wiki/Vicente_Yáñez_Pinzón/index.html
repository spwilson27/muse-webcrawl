Vicente Yáñez Pinzón

Bornc. 1462, Palos de la Frontera, Crown of Castile
Diedc. 1514 (aged c. 52), Triana, Seville, Spain
OccupationNavigator, explorer
Known forCaptain of the Niña; first European to reach Brazil

Vicente Yáñez Pinzón (Spanish: [biˈθente ˈɟaɲeθ pinˈθon]; c. 1462 – after September 1514) was a Spanish navigator and explorer, the youngest of the Pinzón brothers. Along with his brother Martín Alonso Pinzón, who captained the Pinta, Vicente Yáñez captained the Niña on Christopher Columbus's first voyage of discovery in 1492.

Voyage to Brazil

In 1499, Pinzón sailed from Palos in command of four caravels on an independent expedition. Driven west by a storm, on 26 January 1500 he reached the northern coast of present-day Brazil near modern Fortaleza, making him the first documented European to reach Brazil — several months before Pedro Álvares Cabral's official Portuguese discovery. He named the cape Santa María de la Consolación and explored northward, becoming the first European known to explore the Amazon River estuary, which he called the Río Santa María de la Mar Dulce ("River Saint Mary of the Fresh Water Sea"). He also discovered the Oiapoque River, once known as the Vicente Pinzón River, which formed the boundary between Brazil and French Guiana.

Later career

Because Brazil lay on the Portuguese side of the line drawn by the Treaty of Tordesillas, Spain could not claim it. Pinzón was made a knight by King Ferdinand in 1501. In 1508 he sailed with Juan Díaz de Solís to Central America in search of a passage to the Pacific. He died around 1514; the exact circumstances are unknown.
