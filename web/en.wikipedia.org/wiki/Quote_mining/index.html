Quote mining

Quote mining is the fallacious tactic of taking quotations out of context in order to misrepresent the intended meaning of the original speaker, implying a meaning that was not intended or distorting it to support an opposing position. It is a form of the straw man fallacy: a short fragment is extracted, stripped of qualifying context, and presented as though it were representative of the author's views.

Quote mining selects fragments and discards the context

Usage of the term

The expression became prominent in debates over evolution and creationism, where critics charged that creationist literature mined the writings of scientists — including Charles Darwin — for sentences that, read in isolation, appeared to express doubt about evolution. A well-known example is Darwin's passage on the evolution of the eye in On the Origin of Species, which creationist works sometimes quoted only up to the words "seems absurd in the highest possible degree", omitting the reasoned argument that immediately follows.

Related practices

Related to quote mining are cherry-picking of evidence, contextomy, and the misleading use of ellipsis to omit material that changes a statement's meaning. The opposite practice — restoring and examining the full context of a quotation — is a standard step in evaluating arguments from authority.
