Fulham

Fulham is an area of the London Borough of Hammersmith and Fulham in west London, England, lying 3.6 miles (5.8 km) southwest of Charing Cross on the north bank of the River Thames. Historically a parish of Middlesex, Fulham was a market garden and rural retreat for Londoners before being developed in the 19th century. It is known for Fulham Palace, former residence of the Bishops of London, and for two professional football clubs in the wider borough area: Fulham F.C., based at Craven Cottage in Fulham, and Chelsea F.C., whose Stamford Bridge ground lies just within Fulham's boundary.

History

Settlement at Fulham is ancient: the name is thought to derive from Old English for "place of fowls" or "muddy place of Fulla". Fulham Palace was the country home of the Bishop of London from at least the 11th century until 1973, and its grounds now include a museum and botanic garden. Fulham remained largely agricultural until the arrival of the District line railway in 1880 spurred suburban development; the area also had a notable pottery, founded by John Dwight in the 1670s, producing Fulham pottery stoneware.

Geography and character

Fulham is bounded by the Thames to the south, Hammersmith to the west, Kensington to the north, and Chelsea to the east. Its main thoroughfares include Fulham Road, Fulham Broadway, and the Fulham Palace Road. The area is predominantly residential, with Victorian terraces and garden squares, and a significant French expatriate community centred near the Lycée Français.

Sport

Fulham F.C., founded in 1879, plays at Craven Cottage on the banks of the Thames, one of England's most distinctive football grounds, with the Johnny Haynes Stand a listed structure. Bishop's Park, adjoining the ground and the palace, is a popular riverside open space and lies near the start of the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race course at Putney Bridge.
