Heinrich Heine

Born13 December 1797, Düsseldorf
Died17 February 1856, Paris
OccupationPoet, writer, journalist
Notable worksBuch der Lieder; Deutschland. Ein Wintermärchen

Christian Johann Heinrich Heine (1797–1856) was a German poet, writer and literary critic, regarded as one of the most significant German poets of the 19th century. Born in Düsseldorf to a Jewish family, he converted to Protestantism in 1825 in the hope of easing his professional prospects.

Work

His early lyric poetry, collected in the Buch der Lieder (Book of Songs, 1827), was set to music by composers including Schumann and Schubert; his poem "Die Lorelei" became a famous song. Disillusioned with censorship and reaction in Germany, he moved to Paris in 1831, writing the satirical verse epic Deutschland. Ein Wintermärchen (1844) and the poetry collection Romanzero (1851). His wit, irony and political radicalism made his books targets of censorship, and in Nazi Germany his works were burned and his Jewish origins suppressed — "Die Lorelei" was long passed off as by an "unknown" author.
