Karun

The Karun (Persian: کارون, Kārūn) is Iran's most voluminous river and its only navigable one of any length. Rising in the Zagros Mountains of western Iran, it flows generally southward through Khuzestan Province for about 950 kilometres before joining the Arvand Rud (Shatt al-Arab) near Khorramshahr, on the way to the Persian Gulf. Its basin drains a large part of western Iran, and a series of major dams harness it for hydroelectric power and irrigation.

Course

The Karun rises on the slopes of Zard Kuh in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province and winds through deep gorges of the Zagros, receiving tributaries such as the Bazoft and the Dez (which joins near Shushtar). It passes the cities of Shushtar — famous for its ancient hydraulic system of mills, weirs and canals, a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and Ahvaz, the capital of Khuzestan, where a series of bridges span the river. Below Ahvaz the river splits and rejoins before reaching Khorramshahr.

Dams and development

Few rivers of its size are so heavily dammed. The Karun-1 (Shahid Abbaspour), Karun-2 (Masjed Soleyman), Karun-3 and Karun-4 dams, along with the Upper Gotvand Dam, generate a large share of Iran's hydroelectricity. The dams also store water for irrigating the Khuzestan plain, a major agricultural region producing sugarcane and cereals. Development has brought controversy, including salinity problems at Gotvand caused by salt formations in its reservoir, and the submergence of archaeological sites and villages.

History

The lower Karun valley was part of ancient Elam and later Susiana. In the 19th century the river became an artery of British commercial interest after navigation to Shushtar was opened in 1888, and the discovery of oil at Masjed Soleyman in 1908 — the first commercial oil strike in the Middle East — lay within its basin.
