Pedro II (2 December 1825 – 5 December 1891), nicknamed "the Magnanimous", was the second and last monarch of the Empire of Brazil, reigning for over 58 years. Born in Rio de Janeiro, he was the son of Emperor Pedro I and Empress Maria Leopoldina of Austria.

Early reign

Pedro became emperor at the age of five when his father abdicated in 1831, and Brazil was governed by regents during his minority. He was declared of age and crowned in 1841 at fifteen. His long reign brought stability after the turbulent regency period and saw the consolidation of Brazil's territory and institutions.

Achievements

Widely regarded as an exemplary head of state — a scholar-emperor who corresponded with scientists and writers across the world — Pedro II oversaw economic modernisation, the growth of coffee exports, railway and telegraph construction, and victory in the Paraguayan War (1864–1870). A liberal monarch who privately opposed slavery, he presided over its gradual abolition, completed with the Golden Law of 1888 signed by his daughter Princess Isabel as regent.

Overthrow and death

Abolition alienated the slave-owning planter class, and republican and military discontent culminated in a coup d'état on 15 November 1889 that proclaimed the Republic. Pedro, by then elderly and ill, refused to countenance resistance and went into exile in Europe with his family. He died in Paris on 5 December 1891. His remains, and those of Empress Teresa Cristina, were repatriated to Brazil in 1921 and now rest in the Imperial Mausoleum in Petrópolis.
