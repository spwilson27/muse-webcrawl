Panic attack

A panic attack is a sudden episode of intense fear or discomfort that reaches a peak within minutes, during which physical and cognitive symptoms occur. Panic attacks can be unexpected or situationally triggered, and while many people experience isolated attacks, recurrent unexpected attacks with persistent worry about further attacks characterise panic disorder.

Signs and symptoms

According to the DSM-5, a panic attack features four or more of the following: palpitations or accelerated heart rate; sweating; trembling or shaking; sensations of shortness of breath or smothering; feelings of choking; chest pain or discomfort; nausea or abdominal distress; dizziness or light-headedness; chills or heat sensations; paraesthesias (numbness or tingling); derealisation or depersonalisation; fear of losing control or "going crazy"; and fear of dying. Attacks typically peak within about ten minutes and subside within twenty to thirty minutes. Because of the chest pain and breathlessness, panic attacks are frequently mistaken for heart attacks, often prompting emergency evaluation.

Causes

Panic attacks involve activation of the body's fight-or-flight response and are associated with sensitivity of brain circuits involving the amygdala and neurotransmitter systems including serotonin, norepinephrine, and GABA. Risk factors include genetics, major stress, temperament, caffeine and other stimulants, certain medical conditions (such as hyperthyroidism), and other anxiety or mood disorders. Attacks may also accompany phobias, PTSD, and substance use or withdrawal.

Treatment

Effective treatments include cognitive behavioural therapy — particularly interoceptive exposure, which teaches patients that the feared bodily sensations are harmless — and medication such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs). Benzodiazepines give rapid relief but carry dependence risks and are generally reserved for short-term use. Reassurance, slow breathing, and avoidance of caffeine are commonly recommended self-management measures.
