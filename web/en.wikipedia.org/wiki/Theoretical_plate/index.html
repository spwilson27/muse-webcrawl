Theoretical plate

A theoretical plate in separation science is a hypothetical zone or stage in which two phases — such as vapour and liquid in distillation, or mobile and stationary phases in chromatography — establish an equilibrium with each other. The concept, developed by Martin and Synge, lets engineers quantify the efficiency of a separation process as if it consisted of many discrete equilibrium steps.

FieldChemical engineering, chromatography
Key measuresPlate number N, HETP
Related equationvan Deemter equation

In distillation

A fractionating column behaves as if it contained a number of plates on each of which vapour and condensed liquid equilibrate; methods such as the McCabe–Thiele diagram count these stages to design columns. The height equivalent to a theoretical plate (HETP) is the length of packing giving one plate's worth of separation.

In chromatography

Plate number N is calculated from a peak's retention time and width, N = 16(tR/W)² or N = 5.54(tR/W½)². Plate height H = L/N is described by the van Deemter equation, which balances eddy diffusion, longitudinal diffusion and mass-transfer resistance to find an optimum flow rate. Modern high-performance columns achieve tens or hundreds of thousands of plates.
