Garhwali (गढ़वळि) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by the Garhwali people in the Garhwal region of the northern Indian state of Uttarakhand. Belonging to the Central Pahari group, it is written in the Devanagari script and had roughly 2.5 million speakers in the 2011 census. Although not an official language of Uttarakhand (a status held by Hindi and Sanskrit), it is a major marker of regional identity.

Dialects

Garhwali has numerous dialects named after districts and valleys, including Srinagariya (historically the prestige dialect of the old royal capital Srinagar), Tehri, Jaunsari-influenced western varieties, and the dialects of Pauri, Chamoli, Rudraprayag and Uttarkashi. Mutual intelligibility is generally high, though the language shades into Kumaoni in the east.

Status and culture

Garhwali faces pressure from Hindi in education and administration and is classified as vulnerable by UNESCO. It nonetheless has a rich oral and literary tradition of folk ballads, theatre and songs, and a growing presence in regional cinema and music.
