ENSA

The Entertainments National Service Association (ENSA) was an organisation set up in 1939 by Basil Dean and Leslie Henson to provide entertainment for British armed forces personnel during the Second World War. ENSA operated as part of the Navy, Army and Air Force Institutes (NAAFI). It was superseded after the war by Combined Services Entertainment (CSE). Its performers joked that ENSA stood for "Every Night Something Awful".

History

ENSA was created in 1939, learning from the experience of concert parties in the First World War. By the end of the war it had grown into an enormous enterprise: it employed some 6,581 performers and staff, and its concerts, plays, variety shows, and film screenings reached an estimated 300 million attendances worldwide, with parties touring everywhere British and Commonwealth forces served — from Iceland and North Africa to India and the Far East, and to Normandy within days of D-Day.

Performers

Many famous entertainers performed for ENSA, including Gracie Fields, Vera Lynn, George Formby, Tommy Trinder, Joyce Grenfell, John Gielgud, Beatrice Lillie, Arthur Askey, Will Hay, Ivor Novello, and a young Terry-Thomas and Peter Sellers (who toured with the RAF Gang Show). Quality was variable under wartime conditions, and the organisation's reputation among troops was affectionate but satirical; nevertheless it is credited with a major contribution to wartime morale.

Legacy

ENSA's work is commemorated in films such as It's in the Air and countless memoirs. Its successor organisation, Combined Services Entertainment (part of the BFBS network), continues to provide live entertainment to deployed British forces. Basil Dean's memoir The Theatre at War documents its founding and operation.
