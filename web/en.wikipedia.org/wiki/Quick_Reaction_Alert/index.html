Quick Reaction Alert

Quick Reaction Alert (QRA) is a state of readiness maintained by air forces in which armed interceptor aircraft are kept at immediate readiness to take off ("scramble") to intercept and identify aircraft approaching or entering national airspace. QRA is maintained around the clock in many NATO countries and elsewhere, a legacy of Cold War air defence practice that continues in response to unidentified or unresponsive aircraft and to Russian long-range aviation activity.

QRA interceptors must be able to launch within minutes of an alert.

Operations

When radar or air traffic control detects an aircraft that cannot be identified, is not communicating, or deviates from its flight plan, the air defence authority may order the QRA fighters to launch. The interceptors visually identify the aircraft, photograph it, and escort it as required. In the United Kingdom, the Royal Air Force maintains QRA with Typhoon aircraft from RAF Coningsby and RAF Lossiemouth, protecting UK airspace within the NATO air policing framework.

NATO air policing

NATO members without their own fighter forces, notably the Baltic states, Iceland, Slovenia, Albania, Montenegro, and North Macedonia, are covered by allied QRA rotations under Baltic Air Policing and similar arrangements. QRA launches also occur in response to civilian airliners that lose radio contact, a routine event several times a year in European airspace.

History

During the Cold War, QRA reached its most intense form, with nuclear-armed interceptors and anti-ballistic-missile forces on minutes' notice. The concept descends from the Battle of Britain era scramble system and the American and Canadian NORAD alert posture.
