Seven-number summary

In descriptive statistics, the seven-number summary is a collection of seven summary statistics used to describe a dataset. It is an extension of the five-number summary and the three-number summary introduced in exploratory data analysis by John Tukey.

Definition

The seven numbers, in order, are:

  1. The sample minimum (smallest observation)
  2. The first octile, or 12.5th percentile (sometimes denoted E1)
  3. The lower quartile (first quartile, Q1, 25th percentile)
  4. The median (second quartile, Q2, 50th percentile)
  5. The upper quartile (third quartile, Q3, 75th percentile)
  6. The seventh octile, or 87.5th percentile (E7)
  7. The sample maximum (largest observation)

Like the five-number summary, the seven numbers together give a compact picture of the location, spread and shape of a distribution, including information about the tails provided by the two outer octiles. All seven values can be displayed together in an extended box plot.

Related summaries

  • The three-number summary uses the median and the two quartiles (or the minimum, median and maximum).
  • The five-number summary uses the minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile and maximum.
  • The Bowley skewness and other robust measures of shape can be computed from these percentile-based summaries.
