Ahvaz (Persian: اهواز) is a city in southwestern Iran and the capital of Khuzestan Province. Located on the banks of the Karun River, Iran's most effluent and only navigable river, Ahvaz is a major industrial and transportation center, best known for its role in the Iranian oil industry, its steel production, and its extremely hot summers, which regularly rank among the hottest recorded in any major city in the world.

History

The site of Ahvaz has ancient roots: it is commonly identified with a settlement rebuilt by the Sasanian king Ardashir I, who constructed a dam and irrigation works on the Karun. In the medieval Islamic period the city, known as Suq al-Ahvaz, was a prosperous center of sugar cultivation and traded widely. Its fortunes declined in later centuries through flooding, plague, and the silting of waterways. The modern city owes its revival to the opening of the Karun to commercial navigation in 1888 and, above all, to the discovery of oil at nearby Masjed Soleyman in 1908, which transformed the region's economy.

Economy and industry

Ahvaz is a hub of Iran's petroleum and petrochemical sectors, with pipelines, refineries, and the National Iranian South Oil Company headquartered in the region. It is also one of the country's principal steel-producing cities, home to the Khuzestan Steel Company and related rolling and pipe mills. The Karun and the city's Trans-Iranian Railway connection and international airport make it a transport crossroads for the southwest.

Climate

Ahvaz has a hot desert climate. Summers are exceptionally hot and dry, with afternoon temperatures frequently exceeding 45 °C (113 °F) and occasionally approaching 54 °C (129 °F); a temperature of 53.7 °C was recorded in June 2017. Winters are mild. The city has also suffered severe dust storms and air-pollution episodes, and water scarcity in Khuzestan has been a recurring source of public concern.

Demographics and culture

The population is mixed, including ethnic Persians and a large Arab community, alongside Bakhtiari and other groups; both Persian and Khuzestani Arabic are widely spoken. The city is known for its bridges over the Karun — including the White Bridge (Pol-e Sefid), a symbol of Ahvaz — and for a distinctive cuisine and music influenced by Arab culture. During the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988), Ahvaz lay close to the front lines and was a major military logistics center.
