United States budget process

The United States budget process is the framework used by Congress and the President of the United States to create and implement the federal budget. The process is regulated by the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act of 1974 and other laws.

Stages

The process begins with the President's budget request, submitted to Congress by the first Monday in February for the fiscal year beginning 1 October. The House and Senate Budget Committees then draft a concurrent budget resolution setting overall spending and revenue levels, which guides the Appropriations Committees in drafting the twelve annual appropriations bills. Authorisation committees set policy and authorise programmes, while appropriations bills provide funding. The President signs or vetoes the bills; if appropriations lapse, a government shutdown may occur unless a continuing resolution is passed.

Components

Federal spending is divided into discretionary spending, funded annually through appropriations, and mandatory spending, such as Social Security and Medicare, which flows automatically under existing law, plus net interest on the national debt.
