Tiger economy

A tiger economy is the economy of a country that undergoes rapid economic growth, usually accompanied by an increase in the standard of living. The term was first used for the Four Asian Tigers — South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore — which maintained exceptionally high growth rates and rapid industrialisation between the early 1960s and the 1990s.

Other "tigers"

The label has been extended to the "Tiger Cub Economies" of Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam), Ireland's Celtic Tiger boom of the 1990s, the Baltic Tigers, and the Anatolian Tigers of inland Turkey, among others.

Features

Tiger economies are typically characterised by export-led growth, investment in education and infrastructure, high savings rates, and initially low labour costs, though several suffered severe setbacks in the 1997 Asian financial crisis.
