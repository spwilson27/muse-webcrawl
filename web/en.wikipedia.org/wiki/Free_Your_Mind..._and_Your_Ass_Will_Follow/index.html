Free Your Mind... and Your Ass Will Follow is the second studio album by American funk rock band Funkadelic, released in July 1970 on Westbound Records. Produced by George Clinton, the album was reportedly recorded in a single day under the influence of LSD, and it pushed the band's sound further into psychedelic rock and free-form experimentation.

Background and recording

Following the group's self-titled debut earlier in 1970, Clinton encouraged the band to improvise heavily in the studio. According to accounts of the sessions, the musicians were instructed to imagine a scenario in which they had taken acid and to play as though trying to "free their minds". The title track opens the record with a swirling, feedback-laden jam built around Eddie Hazel's guitar and Bernie Worrell's keyboards.

Music

The album blends funk grooves with distorted guitars, gospel-inflected vocals and avant-garde noise. Songs such as "Friday Night, August 14th" and "Funky Dollar Bill" sit alongside the more experimental "Eulogy and Light" — a backwards-tape collage recitation derived from the Lord's Prayer — and the album's closer, "I Wanna Know If It's Good to You?".

Legacy

The album reached the lower reaches of the US charts on release and has since been regarded as a landmark of psychedelic funk, regularly listed among Funkadelic's essential early records alongside Funkadelic (1970) and Maggot Brain (1971).
