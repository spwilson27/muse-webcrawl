Battle of Mons Graupius

Battle of Mons Graupius
DateAD 83 (or 84)
LocationNortheast Scotland (exact site unknown)
BelligerentsRoman Empire vs Caledonian tribes
ResultRoman victory

The Battle of Mons Graupius was, according to the Roman historian Tacitus, a decisive Roman victory in AD 83 or 84 over the Caledonian tribes of northern Britain, won by the governor Gnaeus Julius Agricola.

Sources

The only account is Tacitus' Agricola, written by Agricola's son-in-law. Tacitus puts a famous speech in the mouth of the Caledonian leader Calgacus — including the line "they make a desert and call it peace" — though the speech is generally considered Tacitus' own composition.

The battle

Tacitus says some 30,000 Caledonians faced about 20,000 Romans. Agricola's auxiliary infantry and cavalry won the battle while the legions stood in reserve; Tacitus claims 10,000 Caledonian dead against 360 Romans, figures modern historians treat with caution.

Aftermath

Despite the victory, Rome never permanently conquered the far north of Scotland, and Agricola was soon recalled. The battle's precise location remains disputed, with sites such as Bennachie and Raedykes among the candidates.
