Controlled natural language
TypeSubset of a natural language with restricted grammar and vocabulary
UsesTechnical documentation, knowledge representation, machine translation
ExamplesASD-STE100, Attempto Controlled English, Basic English

Controlled natural languages (CNLs) are subsets of natural languages obtained by restricting grammar and vocabulary in order to reduce or eliminate ambiguity and complexity. Traditional motivations are improving readability for non-native speakers and enabling reliable machine processing, for instance in technical documentation, knowledge representation, and ontology authoring.

Maintenance documentation is a classic application of controlled languages.

Types

Following the classification of Kuhn (2014), CNLs vary along dimensions of precision, expressiveness, naturalness, and simplicity. Broadly, some CNLs are designed primarily for human readers — such as ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, used in aerospace maintenance manuals, and Caterpillar Technical English — while others are designed as formal knowledge-representation languages that happen to look like natural language, such as Attempto Controlled English (ACE), which maps deterministically to first-order logic, and Processable English used in rule systems.

History

Precursors include C. K. Ogden's Basic English (1930), with a core vocabulary of 850 words. Industrial controlled languages emerged in the 1970s and 1980s in the aerospace and heavy-machinery industries. From the 1990s, research CNLs targeted the Semantic Web, ontology engineering, and executable specification; workshops on Controlled Natural Language have run since 2009.

Characteristics

Typical restrictions include a limited approved dictionary, one approved meaning per word, short sentences, simple verb forms, active voice, and explicit rules on constructs such as pronouns, articles, and coordination. Machine-oriented CNLs additionally fix word order and provide a deterministic translation into a formal logic, sometimes using tools that flag or repair non-conforming text.
