Independent contractor

An independent contractor is a person or entity contracted to perform work for — or provide services to — another entity as a non-employee. Unlike employees, independent contractors typically control how and when the work is done, work for multiple clients, supply their own tools, and are paid by the job rather than receiving a salary. The distinction matters for taxation, labour-law protections, benefits, and liability.

Independent contractors commonly work for several clients from their own premises.

Classification tests

Because classification carries major financial consequences, legal systems use tests to distinguish contractors from employees. In the United States, the IRS considers behavioural control, financial control, and the relationship of the parties; many states apply an "ABC test" requiring, among other things, that the worker be free from the hiring entity's control and perform work outside the entity's usual business. California's AB5 law (2019) codified a strict version of the test, followed by ballot-measure exemptions for app-based drivers. In the United Kingdom, the IR35 rules target "disguised employment" through intermediaries.

Misclassification and the gig economy

Employers save on payroll taxes, benefits, and overtime obligations by classifying workers as contractors, creating incentives for misclassification, which enforcement agencies and courts police. The growth of platform-based "gig work" — ride-hailing, delivery, freelance marketplaces — has made contractor status one of the most contested questions in modern labour law, with disputes in many countries over whether such workers are employees, contractors, or an intermediate category such as the UK's "worker" status.

Tax treatment

Independent contractors in the US generally pay self-employment tax covering both the employer and employee shares of Social Security and Medicare contributions, receive Form 1099 rather than a W-2, and may deduct business expenses. They usually lack employer-provided health insurance, retirement plans, unemployment insurance, and workers' compensation coverage.
