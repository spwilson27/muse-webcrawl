The Clarence Buckingham Memorial Fountain, commonly called Buckingham Fountain, is a Chicago landmark in the centre of Grant Park, dedicated in 1927. Donated to the city by Kate Sturges Buckingham in memory of her brother Clarence, it is one of the largest fountains in the world, built in a rococo wedding-cake style modelled on the Latona Fountain at the Palace of Versailles, with allegorical sculptures by Marcel Loyau representing Lake Michigan and its four bordering states (the four pairs of seahorses).

Operation

The fountain's basin is 280 feet (85 m) in diameter and holds about 1.5 million US gallons of water. During operating hours (roughly April to October, weather permitting), its pumps recirculate water through 134 jets; on the hour, a 20-minute water display sends the central jet 150 feet (46 m) into the air. In the evening the display is accompanied by coloured lights and music. The fountain also famously marked the eastern terminus of historic U.S. Route 66.

History

Designed by architect Edward H. Bennett following Daniel Burnham's 1909 Plan of Chicago, the fountain was constructed of pink Georgia marble and dedicated on 26 August 1927. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places as part of the Grant Park historic district and underwent major restorations completed in 1994 and 2009.
