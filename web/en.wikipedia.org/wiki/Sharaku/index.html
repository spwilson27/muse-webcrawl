Sharaku

Active1794-1795
Known forUkiyo-e woodblock prints of kabuki actors
IdentityDisputed

Tōshūsai Sharaku (active 1794-1795) was a Japanese ukiyo-e designer known for strikingly expressive portraits of kabuki actors. Nothing certain is known of his identity, and his career appears to have lasted only about ten months, during which he produced roughly 140 prints.

Works

Published by Tsutaya Jūzaburō in 1794, Sharaku's large-head portraits (ōkubi-e) of actors such as Ōtani Oniji III as the servant Edobei used mica-dusted backgrounds and exaggerated features that emphasised the actor's character and expression. Contemporaries reportedly found the likenesses unflattering, and the prints sold poorly at the time.

Legacy

Rediscovered by collectors in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Sharaku is now ranked among the greatest masters of the Japanese woodblock print, and his works fetch high prices. Theories about his true identity have proposed various artists and even that "Sharaku" was the noh actor Saitō Jūrōbei.
