Canopus

Canopus is the brightest star in the southern constellation Carina and the second-brightest star in the night sky, after Sirius, with an apparent magnitude of −0.74.

Characteristics

Canopus is a bright giant roughly 71 times the Sun's radius and over 10,000 times as luminous, located about 310 light-years away. It is a navigational star, used by spacecraft star trackers because of its brightness and distance from the ecliptic. Named for the pilot of Menelaus's ship in Greek myth, it is invisible from most of Europe and the northern United States but prominent in southern skies.

Cultural significance

Known in Arabic as Suhail, the star figures in many southern-hemisphere traditions and is the brightest star that was circumpolar nowhere; it appears on the flag of Brazil among other stars.
