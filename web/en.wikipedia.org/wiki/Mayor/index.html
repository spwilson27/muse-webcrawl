Mayor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In many countries, a mayor is the highest-ranking official in a municipal government, such as that of a city or a town. The role descends from the medieval Latin maior ("greater") — the mayor of the palace (major domus) and the mayors of chartered boroughs — and today spans from purely ceremonial figures to powerful city executives governing millions.

Models

In strong-mayor systems (New York, Chicago, Paris, most French communes and German cities), the directly elected mayor heads the executive, controls budgets and appointments, and is the city's political leader. In weak-mayor / council-manager systems (common in smaller US cities and, in varied forms, the UK and Ireland historically), executive power rests with the council or a professional manager and the mayor chairs and represents. England since 2000 added directly elected executive mayors (London's mayor, created 2000, and combined-authority "metro mayors" from 2017), while many British and Commonwealth mayors remain civic figureheads distinct from council leaders. Mayors have become prominent policy actors — in climate networks (C40 Cities), housing and transit politics, and crisis management — a "local turn" in governance scholarship.
