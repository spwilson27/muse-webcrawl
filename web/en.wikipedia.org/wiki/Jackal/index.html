Jackal

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyCanidae
SpeciesGolden jackal, side-striped jackal, black-backed jackal (and relatives)

Jackals are medium-sized canids native to Africa and Eurasia. The term is most often applied to the golden jackal (Canis aureus) of south-eastern Europe, the Middle East and southern Asia, and to the black-backed jackal (Lupulella mesomelas) and side-striped jackal (Lupulella adusta) of Africa. Genetic studies have also identified the African golden wolf as a distinct relative.

Behaviour and ecology

Jackals are opportunistic omnivores, hunting small mammals, birds and reptiles and scavenging carrion. They typically live in monogamous pairs that hold territories and cooperate in raising pups, with offspring from earlier litters sometimes remaining as helpers. Their long, howling choruses at dawn and dusk are a familiar sound in savanna and scrub habitats.

In culture

Jackals appear in the folklore and religion of many regions: the Egyptian god Anubis was depicted with a jackal's head, and jackals feature as clever or cowardly tricksters in Indian and African tales. The word "jackal" has also entered English as a metaphor for a person who does menial or disreputable work for another.
