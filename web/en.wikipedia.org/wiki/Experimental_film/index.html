Experimental film

Experimental film, experimental cinema, or avant-garde film is a mode of filmmaking that rigorously re-evaluates cinematic conventions and explores non-narrative forms or alternatives to traditional narrative and methods of working.

History

Roots lie in 1920s European avant-gardes: Dada, Surrealism, and abstract film, with works by Man Ray, Fernand Léger (Ballet mécanique), Luis Buñuel and Salvador Dalí (Un Chien Andalou), and Hans Richter. In America, Maya Deren's Meshes of the Afternoon (1943) catalyzed postwar experimental cinema.

Forms and figures

Structural film, flicker films, found footage, and hand-painted film are major strands. Key artists include Stan Brakhage, Kenneth Anger, Jonas Mekas, Andy Warhol, Hollis Frampton, and Michael Snow (Wavelength). Distribution cooperatives such as Canyon Cinema and the Film-Makers' Cooperative supported the field. See Avant-garde.
