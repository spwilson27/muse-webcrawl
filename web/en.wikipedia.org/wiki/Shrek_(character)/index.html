Shrek is a fictional ogre and the title character of the Shrek franchise by DreamWorks Animation. He is based on the protagonist of William Steig's 1990 picture book Shrek!. In the films, Shrek is voiced by Mike Myers, who gave the character a Scottish accent. A large, green, physically intimidating ogre with a gruff exterior, Shrek is revealed to value his privacy and solitude, yet underneath has a kind and loyal heart — a contrast he famously explains by comparing ogres to onions: both have layers.

In the films

In Shrek (2001), the ogre's swamp is overrun by fairy-tale creatures exiled by Lord Farquaad. To reclaim his home, Shrek agrees to rescue Princess Fiona from a dragon-guarded castle, accompanied by the talkative Donkey. He falls in love with Fiona, who is herself cursed to become an ogress by night, and they marry. In Shrek 2 (2004) he meets Fiona's parents, the King and Queen of Far Far Away, and confronts the Fairy Godmother and Prince Charming. In Shrek the Third (2007) he searches for an heir to the throne while Fiona is pregnant, and in Shrek Forever After (2010) he makes a disastrous bargain with Rumpelstiltskin that erases his existence. Shrek also appears in holiday specials and as the namesake narrator figure within the Puss in Boots spin-offs' world.

Characterization

Shrek subverts the fairy-tale hero: he is neither handsome nor charming, resents being judged by his appearance, and prefers mud baths and solitude to adventure. His character arc across the series concerns accepting love, friendship, and family despite his instinct for isolation. Myers took over the voice after Chris Farley, the original choice, died in 1997; Myers recorded much of the dialogue first in a Canadian accent and then re-recorded it with the now-iconic Scottish accent.

Reception

Shrek became one of the most recognisable animated characters of the 2000s. In 2010 he received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. The character's design and catchphrases became widespread in popular culture and internet memes, and the first film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2020.
