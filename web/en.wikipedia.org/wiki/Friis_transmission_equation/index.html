Friis transmission equation

The Friis transmission equation is used in telecommunications and antenna engineering to calculate the power received by one antenna from another in free space, given the transmitted power, the antenna gains, the wavelength and the distance between the antennas. It was derived by Harald T. Friis in 1946.

The equation

In its common form, the received power Pr equals the transmitted power Pt multiplied by the transmitting and receiving antenna gains Gt and Gr and by (λ / 4πR)2, where λ is the wavelength and R the separation. The last factor expresses free-space path loss: received power density falls with the square of distance.

Assumptions and use

The equation assumes the antennas are in each other's far field, impedance-matched and polarization-aligned, with no multipath or obstructions. It underlies link-budget calculations for satellite, microwave and mobile radio systems, and is often restated in decibel form for practical engineering use.
