Inequality of arithmetic and geometric means

In mathematics, the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, or more briefly the AM–GM inequality, states that the arithmetic mean of a list of non-negative real numbers is greater than or equal to the geometric mean of the same list. Moreover, the two means are equal if and only if every number in the list is the same.

Statement

For non-negative real numbers x1, …, xn:

(x1 + … + xn)/n ≥ (x1 … xn)1/n

with equality exactly when x1 = … = xn. In particular, for two variables, (x + y)/2 ≥ √(xy).

Proofs and generalizations

The inequality can be proved by induction, a method notably developed by Augustin-Louis Cauchy using forward–backward induction. Weighted and generalized mean versions extend the result: the arithmetic mean exceeds the geometric mean, which in turn exceeds the harmonic mean. The AM–GM inequality is a fundamental tool in optimization, analysis, and the theory of inequalities.
