Meme

A meme (/miːm/ MEEM) is an idea, behavior, or style that spreads by means of imitation from person to person within a culture, and often carries symbolic meaning representing a particular phenomenon or theme. A meme acts as a unit for carrying cultural ideas, symbols, or practices, that can be transmitted from one mind to another through writing, speech, gestures, rituals, or other imitable phenomena with a mimicked theme.

Origin of the term

The term was coined by evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins in his 1976 book The Selfish Gene. Dawkins proposed it as a unit of cultural transmission analogous to the gene in biological evolution, derived from the Greek word mimema ("imitated thing") and shortened to rhyme with "gene". Examples he gave included melodies, catch-phrases, fashion, and the technology of building arches. Susan Blackmore's The Meme Machine (1999) further developed the theory of memetics.

Memetics

Memetics applies Darwinian ideas to culture: memes are replicators subject to variation, selection and heredity. Daniel Dennett and others have used the concept to explain phenomena from religion to language. Critics argue that cultural transmission lacks the discrete, high-fidelity copying of genetics, and academic memetics waned in the 2000s, though the concept remains influential in popular discussion of culture.

Internet memes

The term has become strongly associated with Internet memes — pieces of media (images, videos, hashtags, phrases) that spread virally online, often through social media, and are remixed by users. Early examples circulated by email and forums (such as the Dancing Baby, 1996); later phenomena include image macros, LOLcats, rage comics, viral videos, and TikTok trends. Scholar Limor Shifman defines Internet memes as groups of digital items sharing common characteristics of content, form or stance, created with awareness of each other and circulated by many users.
