Architectural theory is the body of written reflection on the nature, purpose, and principles of architecture — its act of thinking, discussing, and writing about buildings. From antiquity to the present, theory has served to justify design practice, to transmit design knowledge, and to debate what makes architecture good: recurring questions include the relationship between form and function, the legitimacy of ornament, the authority of historical precedent, and architecture's social and environmental responsibilities.

Antiquity and the Renaissance

The oldest surviving treatise is Vitruvius's De architectura (c. 30–15 BCE), which prescribed that buildings exhibit firmitas, utilitas, venustas — firmness, commodity, and delight. Rediscovered in the Renaissance, Vitruvius inspired Leon Battista Alberti's De re aedificatoria (1452) and Andrea Palladio's I quattro libri dell'architettura (1570), which codified the classical orders and harmonic proportions for centuries of Western building.

Enlightenment to the 19th century

Marc-Antoine Laugier's Essai sur l'architecture (1753) proposed the "primitive hut" as architecture's rational origin. The 19th century produced competing programmes: John Ruskin's moralised Gothic revival and The Seven Lamps of Architecture (1849); Eugène Viollet-le-Duc's structural rationalism; and Gottfried Semper's theory of the four elements and Bekleidung (dressing).

Modernism and after

Modernist theory inverted historical precedent: Adolf Loos's "Ornament and Crime" (1908/1913), Le Corbusier's Toward an Architecture (1923) and his Five Points, and the functionalism of the Bauhaus and CIAM dominated mid-century debate. Postmodern responses included Robert Venturi's Complexity and Contradiction in Architecture (1966), Aldo Rossi's typology, Kenneth Frampton's "critical regionalism", and Rem Koolhaas's Delirious New York (1978) and "Bigness". Contemporary theory engages parametricism, post-digital design, decolonisation of the canon, and the climate imperative of adaptive reuse and low-carbon construction.
