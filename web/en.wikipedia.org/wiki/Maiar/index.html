Maiar

The Maiar (singular: Maia) are fictional characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. They are lesser Ainur, primordial spirits of the same order as the Valar but of lower rank, who descended into the world (Arda) at its beginning to help shape it. Many Maiar serve individual Valar — for example, Ilmarë attends Varda and Eönwë is the herald of Manwë. In Tolkien's cosmology the most famous Maiar are the five Wizards (Istari) sent to Middle-earth in the Third Age, the Balrogs corrupted by Melkor, and Sauron himself.

Nature and role

Like the Valar, the Maiar are angelic beings created by Eru Ilúvatar before the world. They can take physical form at will — "raiment" for their spirits — or walk unseen. Those who remained loyal served the Valar in Valinor and watched over the peoples of Middle-earth; those corrupted by Melkor (Morgoth) became his servants, among them the fire-demons called Balrogs and, greatest of all, Sauron, originally a Maia of Aulë the Smith.

Notable Maiar

The Istari — Gandalf (Olórin), Saruman (Curumo), Radagast (Aiwendil), and the two Blue Wizards — were sent in the Third Age, clothed in the bodies of old men and forbidden to match Sauron's power with power. Melian, a Maia who wed the Elven king Thingol of Doriath, protected his realm with an enchanted border, the Girdle of Melian; her daughter Lúthien became the most celebrated figure of the First Age. Ossë and Uinen are Maiar of the sea under Ulmo, and Arien and Tilion guide the vessels of the Sun and Moon. Eagles' lord Thorondor is sometimes counted among the Maiar in Tolkien's writings, and Tom Bombadil's nature is left unexplained — a mystery Tolkien deliberately preserved.
