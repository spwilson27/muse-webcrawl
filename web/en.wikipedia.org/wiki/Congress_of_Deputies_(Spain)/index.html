The Congress of Deputies (Spanish: Congreso de los Diputados) is the lower house of the Cortes Generales, the Spanish parliament. It has 350 members elected by provincial constituencies, with terms of four years. The Congress meets in the Palacio de las Cortes in Madrid.

Composition and elections

Deputies are elected in each of Spain's 50 provinces plus the autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla, which return one deputy each. Seats are allocated to closed party lists by the D'Hondt method, with a 3% threshold in each constituency. Each province is guaranteed a minimum of two seats, and the remainder are distributed by population, a system that somewhat over-represents rural provinces. Candidates must be eligible Spanish citizens; voting is universal from age 18.

Powers

The Congress is the politically dominant chamber. It alone grants or withdraws confidence in the government: it elects the President of the Government (prime minister) by investiture vote, and can remove the government through a constructive motion of no confidence, which must name an alternative candidate. Together with the Senate, it passes laws, approves the budget, and ratifies treaties, but the Congress can ultimately override Senate vetoes and amendments. The king formally appoints the prime minister after consultation, normally the leader able to command a majority.

History

The Congress traces its origin to the Estatuto Real of 1834 and the constitutions of the 19th century; the present chamber was established by the Constitution of 1978 adopted during Spain's transition to democracy after the Franco dictatorship. The Palacio de las Cortes, built between 1843 and 1850, was famously the scene of the attempted coup of 23 February 1981, when Civil Guard officers seized the chamber during an investiture vote.

Presidency

The Congress is chaired by the President of the Congress of Deputies, the third-highest office of state after the monarch and the prime minister, who presides over debates and represents the chamber.
