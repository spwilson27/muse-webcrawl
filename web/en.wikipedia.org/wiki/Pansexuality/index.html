Pansexuality

Pansexuality is sexual, romantic, or emotional attraction towards people of all genders, or regardless of their sex or gender identity. Pansexual people may describe their attraction as gender-blind, meaning that gender is not a determining factor in their attraction to others. The term combines the Greek prefix pan- ("all") with "sexuality". Pansexuality is often considered part of the broader bisexual umbrella, though many pansexual people identify distinctly with the term.

Terminology and distinctions

The word "pansexual" was used in early 20th-century psychoanalytic writing (often as "pan-sexualism") to describe the view that the sex instinct drives much human behaviour; its modern sense of attraction to all genders developed later in the 20th century. Pansexuality is distinguished from bisexuality — attraction to more than one gender — primarily in emphasis: pansexual definitions stress attraction regardless of gender and explicitly include non-binary people. Scholars and activists debate the relationship between the labels, and many people identify with both. Related terms include omnisexuality and polysexuality.

Symbols and community

The pansexual pride flag, created around 2010, consists of three horizontal stripes: pink (attraction to women), yellow (attraction to non-binary people), and cyan (attraction to men). May 24 is observed as Pansexual and Panromantic Awareness and Visibility Day. A number of public figures have identified as pansexual, contributing to wider recognition of the identity in the 2010s and 2020s.
