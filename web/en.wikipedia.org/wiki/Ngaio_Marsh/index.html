Dame Edith Ngaio Marsh DBE (April 23, 1895 – February 18, 1982) was a New Zealand crime writer and theatre director. She was appointed a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1966. As a crime writer during the "Golden Age of Detective Fiction," Marsh is known as one of the "Queens of Crime," along with Agatha Christie, Dorothy L. Sayers, and Margery Allingham.

Career

Her 32 detective novels feature Chief Inspector (later Superintendent) Roderick Alleyn of Scotland Yard, a gentleman detective and painter. The first, A Man Lay Dead, was published in 1934; the last, Light Thickens, appeared posthumously in 1982. Many novels draw on her twin passions for painting and the theatre, including Artists in Crime (1938), Death in Ecstasy (1936), and Overture to Death (1939).

In New Zealand, Marsh transformed university and regional theatre as a producer and director, founding the Canterbury University College Drama Society productions that toured Australia. The Ngaio Marsh Theatre at the University of Canterbury bears her name. She died in Christchurch in 1982.
