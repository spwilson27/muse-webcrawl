Pittsfield is the largest city and the county seat of Berkshire County, Massachusetts, United States. It is the principal city of the Pittsfield metropolitan statistical area, which encompasses all of Berkshire County. The city sits at the confluence of the east and west branches of the Housatonic River, in the Berkshire Hills of western Massachusetts.

History

Pittsfield was first settled in 1752 as Pontoosuc Plantation and incorporated as a town in 1761, named in honour of William Pitt the Elder. In the 19th century it grew as a manufacturing center for woolens, paper, and agricultural machinery, and its civic history includes the first recorded reference to baseball in America — a 1791 town bylaw prohibiting the game near the meeting house.

General Electric developed a major presence in the city from the early 1900s, building transformers and later plastics; GE's gradual withdrawal in the late 20th century left the city to reinvent itself around healthcare, insurance, and the arts.

Culture

Pittsfield is home to the Berkshire Museum, the Barrington Stage Company, and the Colonial Theatre. Nearby in Lenox are Tanglewood, summer home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, and Herman Melville's Arrowhead, where Moby-Dick was written, lies within the city.
