St. James Theatre

The St. James Theatre, originally the Erlanger Theatre, is a Broadway theater at 246 West 44th Street in the Theater District of Midtown Manhattan in New York City. Opened in 1927, it was designed by the architectural firm of Warren and Wetmore in the neo-Georgian style of the Beaux-Arts tradition. The theater seats approximately 1,700 and is operated by Jujamcyn Theaters.

History

The theatre was built by Abraham Lincoln Erlanger, a theatrical producer, and opened on September 26, 1927, with the musical The Merry Malones. After Erlanger's death in 1930, the theatre was renamed the St. James. It briefly served as a movie house and a television studio before returning to legitimate theatre use under Jujamcyn.

Notable productions

The St. James has hosted many landmark productions, including the original Broadway runs of Oklahoma! (1943), The King and I (1951), The Pajama Game (1954), Flower Drum Song, Becket, Hello, Dolly!, The Secret Garden, The Producers (2001), which won a record 12 Tony Awards, and Something Rotten!. The theatre's stage is among the largest on Broadway, making it a favored house for large-scale musicals.
