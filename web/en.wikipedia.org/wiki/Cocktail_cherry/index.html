Cocktail cherry

Cocktail cherries used as a garnish.

A cocktail cherry is a preserved cherry, generally a maraschino cherry, used to garnish cocktails and desserts. Cocktail cherries are usually sold with their stems on, packed in syrup, and are a standard garnish for drinks such as the Manhattan, the Old Fashioned, and the Shirley Temple, as well as for ice cream sundaes and baked goods.

Production

Commercial cocktail cherries were traditionally made from light-fleshed sweet cherry varieties such as Royal Ann (Napoleon). The fruit is brined, bleached, pitted, and then soaked in flavoured, coloured syrup; the familiar bright red colour traditionally comes from food colouring, and the flavouring historically imitated the bitter-almond taste of maraschino liqueur, from which the original Dalmatian marasca cherry preserve took its name. Premium cocktail cherries, such as the Italian amarena and the Luxardo maraschino cherry, are instead preserved in their own dark cherry syrup without artificial colouring, giving them a deep burgundy colour and a richer flavour, and they have become fashionable in craft cocktail bars.

Use

Beyond garnish, cocktail cherries and their syrup ("cherry juice") are used as ingredients in drinks and desserts. A small amount of the syrup is an ingredient in some versions of the Shirley Temple and Roy Rogers, and cherries are baked into fruitcakes, Black Forest gateau, and other desserts.
