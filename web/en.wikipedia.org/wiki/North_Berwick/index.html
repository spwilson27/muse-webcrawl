North Berwick

[Image: North Berwick harbour]

North Berwick (Scottish Gaelic: Bearaig a Tuath) is a seaside town and former royal burgh in East Lothian, Scotland, situated on the southern shore of the Firth of Forth, approximately 20 miles (32 km) east-north-east of Edinburgh. It has a population of roughly 8,000 and is a popular commuter town and holiday destination.

History

The harbour was established in the 12th century for ferry traffic to Earlsferry in Fife, carrying pilgrims to St Andrews. The town was made a royal burgh by charter of William the Lion around 1373. North Berwick is remembered for the witch trials of 1590–92, in which dozens of local people were accused of conjuring storms against James VI; the trials, described in the pamphlet Newes from Scotland, influenced Shakespeare's Macbeth. In the Victorian era the town became a fashionable resort, earning the nickname "the Biarritz of the North".

Geography and attractions

The town is overlooked by North Berwick Law, a conical volcanic hill topped by a whale jawbone arch. Offshore lie the islands of Fidra, Craigleith, the Lamb, and the Bass Rock, home to one of the world's largest northern gannet colonies and watched through the Scottish Seabird Centre at the harbour. Championship golf links, including the West Links, border the town.

Transport

North Berwick railway station is the terminus of the North Berwick Line from Edinburgh Waverley, with services operated by ScotRail.
