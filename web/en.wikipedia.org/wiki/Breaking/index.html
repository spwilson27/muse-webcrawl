Breaking

Breaking, commonly known as breakdancing or b-boying/b-girling, is a dynamic style of street dance that originated among African American and Puerto Rican youth in the Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s as one of the core elements of hip-hop culture. It combines acrobatic power moves, stylised footwork, and freezes performed to the extended percussion breaks of funk and hip-hop records. Breaking became an Olympic sport at the Paris 2024 Games.

History

Breaking emerged at Bronx block parties where DJ Kool Herc pioneered the "merry-go-round" technique of looping the break sections of records; dancers who performed during the breaks were called break-boys and break-girls, or b-boys and b-girls. Crews such as the Zulu Kings and the Rock Steady Crew developed the form in the late 1970s, and media coverage in the early 1980s — including the films Wild Style and Flashdance — carried it worldwide, often under the media label "breakdancing", a term many practitioners reject. International battles such as Battle of the Year (founded 1990 in Germany) and Red Bull BC One (from 2004) professionalised competitive breaking.

Technique

Breaking is usually analysed into four elements. Toprock is dancing performed standing, used to enter the cypher (the circle of dancers). Downrock (or footwork) is performed on the floor with hands supporting the body, including the fundamental six-step. Power moves are acrobatic, momentum-driven rotations such as windmills, headspins, backspins, and flares adapted from gymnastics. Freezes are static poses in which the dancer supports the body on the hands or elbows, often stacked in improbable balances. Dancers string moves together with transitions, musicality, and personal style, and are judged in battles on technique, vocabulary, execution, musicality, and originality.

Olympic inclusion

Breaking appeared at the 2018 Summer Youth Olympics in Buenos Aires and was confirmed for the Paris 2024 Olympic programme. At Paris, Canada's Phil Wizard (Philip Kim) won the men's gold and Japan's Ami (Ami Yuasa) the women's gold. Its Olympic judging used the Trivium system, developed with the World DanceSport Federation. Breaking was not retained for the Los Angeles 2028 programme.
