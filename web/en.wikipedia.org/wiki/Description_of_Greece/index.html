Description of Greece

Description of Greece (Greek: Ἑλλάδος Περιήγησις, Hellados Periegesis) is a work by the ancient geographer and traveller Pausanias, written in the second century AD. It describes ancient Greece based on his own observations and is a crucial link between classical literature and modern archaeology.

The work comprises ten books, each dedicated to a region of mainland Greece: Attica, Corinth, Laconia, Messenia, Elis, Achaea, Arcadia, Boeotia, and Phocis (including Delphi). Pausanias describes cities, sanctuaries, monuments, works of art, local myths, rituals, and historical traditions, often pausing for extended digressions.

Long doubted, Pausanias's accuracy was vindicated by archaeological excavations in the 19th and 20th centuries, which confirmed many of his descriptions of sites such as Olympia, Delphi, and Mycenae. The work remains an indispensable source for the topography, religion, and art of ancient Greece.
