Puss in Boots

Puss in Boots (French: Le Maître chat ou le Chat botté) is a European fairy tale about an anthropomorphic cat who uses trickery and deceit to gain power, wealth and the hand in marriage of a princess for his penniless master. The tale was published by Charles Perrault in his Histoires ou contes du temps passé (1697), though earlier versions appeared in Italian literature, notably Giovanni Francesco Straparola's Costantino Fortunato (c. 1550–53) and Giambattista Basile's Cagliuso (1634). It is classified as Aarne–Thompson type 545B, "the cat as helper".

Plot

A miller dies leaving his three sons the mill, a donkey, and — to the youngest — only a cat. The cat asks for boots and a bag, and proves himself a shrewd hunter, catching rabbits and partridges which he presents to the king in the name of his master, the fictitious "Marquis of Carabas". One day the cat arranges for his master to bathe in a river along the royal route; as the king's carriage passes, the cat cries that the Marquis is drowning and his clothes stolen, and the youth is rescued and dressed in fine clothes. The cat runs ahead, threatening peasants into telling the king that all the lands belong to the Marquis of Carabas. Finally he tricks a wealthy shape-shifting ogre into turning into a mouse, which he eats; the ogre's castle is then claimed for the master. Impressed, the king gives the young man his daughter in marriage, and the cat lives as a great lord.

Analysis and legacy

Perrault appended two morals: one praising industry and know-how, another noting the power of fine clothes to win regard — a gently ironic comment on social climbing. The story has been adapted countless times: Tchaikovsky's opera-in-miniature, pantomimes, films, and in modern popular culture DreamWorks' Shrek franchise made Puss in Boots (voiced by Antonio Banderas) a star, headlining the spin-off films Puss in Boots (2011) and Puss in Boots: The Last Wish (2022). Illustrations by Gustave Doré are among the tale's most famous images.
