Ramanujan–Sato series

In mathematics, a Ramanujan–Sato series is an infinite series identity of the general form used to compute the mathematical constant π (pi) with very rapid convergence. The series are named after Srinivasa Ramanujan, who in 1914 published a family of remarkable series for 1/π, and Mikio Sato, whose work on modular equations and related structures helped explain and generalise them.

Background

Ramanujan's 1914 paper "Modular equations and approximations to π" gave 17 series for 1/π. A famous example is:

1/π = (2√2 / 9801) · Σk=0∞ [(4k)! (1103 + 26390k)] / [(k!)4 3964k]

Each term of this series adds roughly eight correct decimal digits. The formulas are connected with the theory of elliptic integrals, modular forms and singular values of the elliptic modulus.

General theory

The unifying explanation uses the theory of modular equations. Higher-level analogues, associated with work of Sato and others, give series corresponding to different levels of modular equations. The Chudnovsky algorithm, used in modern record computations of π, is based on a similar series discovered via the same circle of ideas.
