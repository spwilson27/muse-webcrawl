Amadeo I (Italian: Amedeo Ferdinando Maria di Savoia; 30 May 1845 – 18 January 1890) was King of Spain from 1870 to 1873, the only king from the House of Savoy to hold the Spanish crown. The second son of Victor Emmanuel II of Italy, he was elected king by the Spanish Cortes after the revolution of 1868 deposed Isabella II and the revolutionary government searched Europe for a suitable constitutional monarch.

Reign

Amadeo's position was undermined from the start. His chief sponsor, General Juan Prim, was assassinated just as the new king arrived in Spain in December 1870. The reign was plagued by the Third Carlist War, a continuing war in Cuba, republican agitation, hostility from the Church and the old Bourbon aristocracy, and chronic instability among the governing parties — six governments in little over two years. Conscientious and liberal, but foreign and isolated, Amadeo was nicknamed "el rey caballero" (the gentleman king) yet never won popular acceptance.

Abdication and later life

On 11 February 1873, after a crisis over the artillery corps, Amadeo abdicated, declaring Spain ungovernable, and left the country; the First Spanish Republic was proclaimed the same day. He returned to Italy as Duke of Aosta, married his niece Maria Letizia Bonaparte in 1888 after the death of his first wife, and died in Turin in 1890. His grandson Amedeo, 3rd Duke of Aosta, later became viceroy of Italian East Africa, and another descendant, Aimone, was briefly designated king of Croatia in 1941.
