The Sphere

The Sphere is a monumental cast bronze sculpture by German artist Fritz Koenig (1924–2017). Created between 1969 and 1971, it stood for three decades at the plaza of the World Trade Center in New York City as a symbol of world peace through trade. Damaged but not destroyed in the September 11 attacks, it became an iconic memorial artefact and is now installed in Liberty Park overlooking the World Trade Center site.

History

Koenig, then a professor at the Technical University of Munich, was commissioned by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey at the suggestion of architect Minoru Yamasaki. Cast in 52 bronze segments in Bremen, Germany, the 25-foot (7.6 m) tall sphere, weighing roughly 20 tons, rotated slowly on its fountain base in the Austin J. Tobin Plaza between the Twin Towers.

September 11 and after

When the towers collapsed on September 11, 2001, the sculpture was buried under debris. It was recovered largely intact — dented, punctured, and bearing a fragment of a hijacked airliner inside — and was re-erected in Battery Park in March 2002 as an interim memorial, with an eternal flame nearby. In 2017 the Port Authority moved The Sphere to Liberty Park in the rebuilt World Trade Center complex, where it was rededicated on August 16, 2017, still unrestored, its scars preserved as a testament to resilience.
