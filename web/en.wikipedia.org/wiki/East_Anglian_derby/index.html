East Anglian derby

TeamsIpswich Town and Norwich City
First meeting15 November 1902 (Norfolk and Suffolk League)
StadiumsPortman Road (Ipswich); Carrow Road (Norwich)
Nickname"Old Farm" derby

The East Anglian derby is a football rivalry between Ipswich Town and Norwich City, the two largest professional clubs in East Anglia, England. It is sometimes jokingly called the "Old Farm derby", a pun on Glasgow's Old Firm derby combined with the region's agricultural character. The fixture is one of the fiercest local rivalries in English football despite the roughly 40 miles separating the two towns.

History

The clubs first met on 15 November 1902 in the Norfolk and Suffolk League, when both were amateur sides; Norwich won 1–0. The rivalry continued through the Southern League after both turned professional and intensified once the clubs met regularly in the Football League from the 1930s onwards. Notable encounters include the two-legged 1985 League Cup semi-final, won by Norwich, who went on to lift the trophy, and the 2015 Championship play-off semi-final, also won by Norwich. Ipswich's greatest successes came under Bobby Robson, winning the FA Cup in 1978 and the UEFA Cup in 1981, honours Norwich have not matched, while Norwich have enjoyed long unbeaten runs in the derby in the modern era, including Grant Holt's hat-trick in a 4–1 win in 2010 and a sixteen-year period in which Ipswich failed to beat them between 2009 and 2025.

Statistics

The derby has been played well over 100 times in all competitions, with the head-to-head record remaining close. Meetings depend on the clubs being in the same division, so fixtures have sometimes been separated by several seasons.
