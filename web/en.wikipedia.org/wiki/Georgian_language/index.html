Georgian language

Georgian (ქართული ენა, kartuli ena) is the official language of Georgia and the country's most widely spoken Kartvelian language. It is written in its own unique alphabetic script, Mkhedruli, and has a continuous literary tradition stretching back to the 5th century AD. Around four million people speak Georgian as a first language.

Classification and history

Georgian belongs to the Kartvelian (South Caucasian) family, together with Mingrelian, Laz, and Svan. It is not demonstrably related to any other language family. The earliest surviving Georgian inscriptions date to the 5th century, shortly after Georgia's conversion to Christianity, and the language's medieval literature includes the national epic The Knight in the Panther's Skin by Shota Rustaveli (12th–13th century).

Writing system

Georgian has been written in three successive scripts: Asomtavruli, Nuskhuri, and the modern Mkhedruli, a unicameral alphabet of 33 letters with a nearly phonemic correspondence between letters and sounds. The three scripts together were inscribed by UNESCO on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016.

Grammar

Georgian is famous among linguists for its complex morphology. It is an agglutinative language with polypersonal verbs that can encode subject, direct object, and indirect object in a single word, and it uses a system of verb forms called screeves to mark tense, aspect, and mood. Alignment varies by screeve: nominative–accusative in some series, ergative in the aorist series, and dative-subject constructions in others. Nouns inflect for seven cases, and adjectives generally precede the noun. Word order is relatively free but typically subject–object–verb or subject–verb–object.

Vocabulary

Georgian has absorbed loanwords from Greek, Persian, Arabic, Turkish, Russian, and other languages, while preserving a distinctive native core, including its vigesimal (base-20) counting system for numbers between 20 and 99.
