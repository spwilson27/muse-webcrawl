United Nations Transitional Administration in East Timor

The United Nations Transitional Administration in East Timor (UNTAET) was a peacekeeping operation and transitional government that administered East Timor (now Timor-Leste) from 25 October 1999 until the territory's independence on 20 May 2002. Established by Security Council Resolution 1272 in the aftermath of the 1999 East Timorese crisis, UNTAET exercised full legislative and executive authority over the territory, an unusually broad mandate for a UN mission.

Background

After the East Timorese voted for independence from Indonesia in a UN-supervised referendum on 30 August 1999, pro-Indonesia militias launched a campaign of violence that destroyed much of the territory's infrastructure and displaced most of the population. An Australian-led multinational force, INTERFET, restored security, and administration then passed to the United Nations.

Administration

Led by Sérgio Vieira de Mello, UNTAET rebuilt government from near-scratch: it created a civil service, courts, a police force, a defence force, a central bank and currency arrangements, and organized elections to a Constituent Assembly in 2001. The mission also coordinated humanitarian relief and reconstruction. On 20 May 2002 the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste was proclaimed, with Xanana Gusmão as its first president, and a smaller successor mission, UNMISET, took over support functions.
