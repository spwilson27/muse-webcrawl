Tirumala

Tirumala is a hill town in Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh, India, situated in the Seshachalam Hills of the Eastern Ghats at about 853 metres above sea level. It is home to the Sri Venkateswara Temple, one of the most visited Hindu pilgrimage sites in the world.

The temple town

The temple, dedicated to Venkateswara (a form of Vishnu), stands on the seventh peak, Venkatadri. The town exists to serve the temple, administered by the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD), which manages accommodation, free meals (annadanam), tonsuring facilities and transport for tens of thousands of pilgrims daily.

Geography and access

Tirumala is reached from Tirupati by two ghat roads and by pedestrian footpaths, the Alipiri and Srivari Mettu routes. The surrounding hills form the Sri Venkateswara National Park, noted for red sanders trees and waterfalls such as Akasa Ganga and Papavinasam.
