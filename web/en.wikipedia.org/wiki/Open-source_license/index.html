Open-source license

An open-source license is a license for computer software and other products that allows the source code, blueprint or design to be used, modified or shared under defined terms and conditions. Licensing terms must comply with the Open Source Definition maintained by the Open Source Initiative (OSI), which approves licences as conforming.

Categories

Permissive licences, such as the MIT License, BSD licences and Apache License 2.0, impose minimal conditions — mainly attribution and preservation of notices — and allow use in proprietary software. Copyleft licences, notably the GNU General Public License (GPL), require derivative works distributed to others to be licensed under the same terms, keeping them open. A spectrum between the two includes weak-copyleft licences such as the LGPL and MPL.

Comparison and compatibility

Licence proliferation and incompatibilities (for example, between GPLv2 and Apache 2.0, resolved in GPLv3) complicate combined works. The OSI approval process, and the related concept of free software licences listed by the Free Software Foundation, guide most projects' choices.
