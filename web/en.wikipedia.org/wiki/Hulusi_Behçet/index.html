Hulusi Behçet

Hulusi Behçet
Born: 20 February 1889, Istanbul, Ottoman Empire
Died: 8 March 1948, Istanbul, Turkey
Known for: First description of Behçet's disease
Field: Dermatology

Hulusi Behçet (February 20, 1889 – March 8, 1948) was a Turkish dermatologist and scientist who first described a systemic inflammatory disease in 1937, which was later named Behçet's disease after him.

Life

Behçet was born in Istanbul and studied medicine at the Gülhane Military Medical Academy, specialising in dermatology and syphilology. During and after the First World War he served in military hospitals, then worked in Budapest and at the Charité in Berlin before returning to Istanbul, where he became the first professor of dermatology and venereal diseases at Istanbul University.

Behçet's disease

From 1924 Behçet followed patients with recurring mouth and genital ulcers and eye inflammation (uveitis), arguing they formed a single syndrome probably caused by a virus. He published the association internationally in 1937; the condition — a variable-vessel vasculitis now known to be most common along the historic Silk Road — was formally named after him in 1947. He also published extensively on syphilis, leishmaniasis (he described the "nail sign" in oriental sore) and other skin diseases, and founded Turkey's first dermatology journal.
