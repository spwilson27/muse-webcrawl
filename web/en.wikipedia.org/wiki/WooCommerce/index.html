WooCommerce

Developer: Automattic

Initial release: 27 September 2011

Written in: PHP

Platform: WordPress

Type: E-commerce

License: GPL

WooCommerce is an open-source e-commerce plugin for WordPress. It is designed for small to large-sized online merchants using WordPress. Launched on 27 September 2011, the plugin quickly became popular for its simplicity to install and customise and its free core product. WooCommerce is the most widely used e-commerce platform on the web by site count, powering several million online stores.

History

WooCommerce was developed by WooThemes, a WordPress theme company, after it hired Jigoshop developers Mike Jolley and James Koster, who forked the Jigoshop plugin to create WooCommerce. In 2015 WooThemes and WooCommerce were acquired by Automattic, the operator of WordPress.com. The first WooConf developer conference was held in San Francisco in 2014. In 2020 the hosted payment service WooCommerce Payments (later rebranded WooPayments) was introduced in partnership with Stripe, letting merchants accept cards directly from the store dashboard.

Features

The core plugin is free and open source under the GPL, and provides product management for physical and digital goods, inventory and order management, tax calculation, shipping zones, coupon systems, and built-in payment options such as bank transfer and cash on delivery, with card payments via extensions or WooPayments. A large marketplace sells hundreds of official and third-party extensions — for subscriptions, bookings, memberships, and additional payment gateways — as well as themes such as the default Storefront. WooCommerce exposes a REST API and a library of hooks and filters, and since version 7 (2022) has migrated custom order tables (High-Performance Order Storage) into the core to improve scalability. An official mobile app lets merchants manage orders on the move.

Usage

WooCommerce has consistently ranked as the most common e-commerce technology on the internet, used on roughly a fifth to a third of the top million e-commerce sites depending on the survey, and on millions of sites overall. Its popularity derives from WordPress's large installed base and the absence of licence fees or transaction percentages on the core software.
