En no Gyōja (役行者, "En the Ascetic"), also known as En no Ozunu (役小角), was a Japanese ascetic and mystic traditionally held to be the founder of Shugendō, the path of mountain asceticism combining elements of Buddhism, Shinto, and folk religion. He is traditionally dated to 634–700/707, and is said to have lived on Mount Katsuragi in Yamato Province.

Life and legend

According to the Nihon Ryōiki and later hagiographies, En no Ozunu was born in the village of Chihara in what is now Gose, Nara Prefecture. From a young age he withdrew to the mountains, practising austerities, consuming pine needles and wearing bark clothing, and was said to have attained supernatural powers (siddhi), commanding demons and deities such as Zaō Gongen. In 699 he was exiled to Izu Ōshima after being slandered at court by a rival, the sorcerer Karukuni no Muraji Hirotari, who accused him of using magic to bewitch people. Tradition holds that he escaped nightly to climb Mount Fuji. He was later pardoned, and legend says he eventually ascended to heaven from Mount Tenjō.

Legacy

En no Gyōja is venerated as the patriarch of the yamabushi mountain ascetics. Sites associated with him include Mount Ōmine, where he is said to have received the revelation of Zaō Gongen, and numerous caves and temples across the Kii Mountain Range. In 1799 he was posthumously granted the title Jinben Daibosatsu by Emperor Kōkaku.
