Bert Sakmann (born 12 June 1942) is a German physiologist. Together with Erwin Neher he developed the patch clamp technique for studying individual ion channels in cells, work for which they shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1991.

Life and career

Sakmann was born in Stuttgart and studied medicine at the universities of Tübingen, Freiburg, Munich and Paris. He completed his medical studies at the Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich and took his doctorate at Göttingen, where he joined the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry and began collaborating with Erwin Neher in the 1970s.

Patch clamp

The patch clamp technique uses a glass micropipette pressed against a tiny patch of cell membrane to record the minute electrical currents flowing through single ion channel proteins. Neher and Sakmann's refinement of the method in 1976, and their achievement of the high-resistance "gigaseal", made it possible to record currents from one channel molecule at a time. The technique transformed cellular physiology and pharmacology, enabling direct study of the channels involved in nerve and muscle function and of many drug targets. Sakmann went on to work on synaptic transmission in the brain, and served as a scientific member of the Max Planck Institute for Medical Research in Heidelberg.
