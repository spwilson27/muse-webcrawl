Biodynamic wine
BasisBiodynamic agriculture (Rudolf Steiner, 1924)
CertificationDemeter International; Biodyvin
Key practicesBiodynamic preparations (500–508), lunar/sowing calendar, no synthetic chemicals, farm as organism
Notable producersDomaine de la Romanée-Conti (partial), Nicolas Joly (Coulée de Serrant), Zind-Humbrecht, Chapoutier

Biodynamic wine is wine made from grapes grown according to the principles of biodynamic agriculture, a holistic farming system derived from Rudolf Steiner's 1924 "Agriculture Course" lectures. Biodynamic viticulture treats the vineyard as a self-sustaining organism, prohibits synthetic fertilisers and pesticides, and incorporates spiritual and cosmic elements — including timed "preparations" and work scheduled by lunar and planetary calendars — beyond the requirements of organic farming.

Practices

Central practices include nine herbal and mineral preparations (numbered 500–508), such as horn manure (preparation 500, cow manure fermented in a buried cow horn) sprayed on soil and horn silica (501) sprayed on vines; composting; integration of livestock; and planting, pruning and harvesting guided by the Maria Thun sowing calendar, distinguishing fruit, flower, leaf and root days.

Certification and producers

Certification is offered principally by Demeter International, and for wine estates by Biodyvin. Many acclaimed estates have adopted biodynamics wholly or in part, among them Nicolas Joly's Coulée de Serrant in Savennières (an early and outspoken adopter), Domaine Zind-Humbrecht in Alsace, M. Chapoutier in the Rhône, and parts of Domaine de la Romanée-Conti in Burgundy.

Assessment

Supporters report improved soil health, vine resilience and wine "expressiveness of terroir". Critics note that the esoteric elements (astrological timing, homeopathic preparations) lack scientific evidence; comparative studies generally find soil and ecological benefits similar to good organic practice, while evidence for additional effects unique to biodynamics remains contested.
