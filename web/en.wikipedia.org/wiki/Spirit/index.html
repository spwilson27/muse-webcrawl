Spirit


Spirits are commonly depicted as immaterial beings.

In folk belief, spirit is the vital principle or animating essence within humans and other living things, conceived as an incorporeal substance distinct from the material body. The concept overlaps with ideas of the soul, ghost, and various supernatural beings, and appears in religious, philosophical, and psychological traditions around the world.

Etymology

The English word derives from the Latin spiritus, meaning "breath", also "soul", "courage", and "vigour". It is related to the Latin verb spirare, "to breathe". Related concepts include the Greek pneuma, the Hebrew ruach, and the Arabic ruh.

Religious and philosophical views

In many traditions, humans are held to possess a spirit that survives bodily death. In Christian theology, the spirit is distinguished from the soul (anima) in some frameworks, while the Holy Spirit is the third person of the Trinity. Animistic traditions attribute spirits to animals, plants, places, and natural phenomena; such beings may be honoured as ancestors or nature spirits.

Philosophers have debated whether spirit is a substance, a property of matter, or a useful fiction. In German idealism, Geist (spirit/mind) became a central category, notably in Hegel's philosophy of history.

Folklore

Spirits figure widely in folklore as ghosts of the dead, household guardians, tricksters, and nature beings. Practices such as spirit possession, mediumship, and offerings at shrines reflect beliefs that spirits can interact with the living.
