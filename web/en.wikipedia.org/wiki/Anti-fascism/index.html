Anti-fascism

Anti-fascism is a political movement in opposition to fascist ideologies, groups and individuals. Beginning in European countries in the 1920s, it was most significant in Italy, Germany and Spain, where movements arose in opposition to Italian fascism, Nazism and the military rising of Francisco Franco respectively.

History

Anti-fascism emerged alongside fascism itself after the First World War. In Italy, the Arditi del Popolo and later the partisan resistance opposed Mussolini's regime; in Germany, groups such as Antifaschistische Aktion (founded 1932) opposed the Nazis, though the anti-fascist left was divided between communists and social democrats. During the Spanish Civil War (1936–39), anti-fascists from many countries volunteered in the International Brigades to defend the Spanish Republic. In the Second World War, resistance movements across occupied Europe carried on the anti-fascist struggle.

Postwar and contemporary

After 1945, anti-fascism became a founding ideology of several European states, notably East Germany, while in the West it continued in movements opposing neo-Nazism and the far right. In recent decades the term "antifa" has been applied to decentralised militant anti-fascist and anti-racist networks, particularly in Germany and the United States, whose tactics and role remain politically contested.
