Beslan school siege

LocationBeslan, North Ossetia–Alania, Russia
Date1–3 September 2004
Deaths334 hostages (including 186 children); also attackers and rescuers
PerpetratorsChechen and Ingush militants (Riyad-us Saliheen)

The Beslan school siege (also referred to as the Beslan school hostage crisis or the Beslan massacre) was a terrorist attack that started on 1 September 2004, lasted three days, and involved the imprisonment of more than 1,100 people as hostages (including 777 children) at School Number One in the town of Beslan, North Ossetia, Russia.

Events

On the first day of the school year, armed militants seized the school during the opening ceremony and herded hostages into the gymnasium, which they rigged with explosives. The attackers demanded the withdrawal of Russian forces from Chechnya. Hostages were denied food and water in worsening conditions.

On 3 September, explosions inside the gymnasium — whose origin remains disputed — triggered a chaotic storming of the building by Russian security forces, local police, and armed civilians. Fires and the collapse of the gymnasium roof caused many deaths. At least 334 hostages were killed, including 186 children, and hundreds more were injured.

Aftermath

The attack, claimed in the name of Chechen separatist leader Shamil Basayev, was the deadliest school shooting in history. It prompted major changes in Russian security policy, including the abolition of direct elections for regional governors. Official investigations and the handling of the rescue operation have remained subjects of controversy and criticism from victims' families.
