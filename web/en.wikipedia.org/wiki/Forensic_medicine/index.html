Forensic medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forensic medicine, also known as legal medicine, is the branch of medicine that applies medical and scientific knowledge to legal issues, principally in the investigation of death and injury. It encompasses forensic pathology — the determination of the cause and manner of death — as well as clinical forensic medicine, which deals with the examination of living persons, for example victims of assault.

History

The origins of forensic medicine can be traced to ancient Egypt and China; the Chinese Xi Yuan Ji Lu ("Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified"), published in 1248, is regarded as one of the earliest systematic texts on forensic medicine. In Europe, the field developed during the 16th and 17th centuries, and medico-legal autopsy became central to homicide investigation.

Practice

Forensic physicians conduct autopsies, determine cause and manner of death (natural, accident, suicide, homicide, or undetermined), estimate the time of death, identify human remains, and collect trace evidence. In many jurisdictions the role is performed by a medical examiner or coroner, and forensic practitioners frequently give expert testimony in court.

Related disciplines

Forensic medicine overlaps with forensic toxicology, forensic anthropology, forensic odontology, and forensic psychiatry, and forms part of the wider field of forensic science.
