Block upconverter

A block upconverter (BUC) is a device used in the uplink transmission of satellite signals. It converts a band of frequencies from a lower intermediate frequency (typically L-band) to a higher frequency (such as C-band, Ku-band or Ka-band), amplifies the result, and feeds it to the antenna for transmission to a satellite.

Operation

The BUC receives an intermediate-frequency signal from an indoor modem, mixes it with a stable local oscillator to translate the whole block of channels to the uplink band, and amplifies it with a solid-state power amplifier (SSPA) or, in high-power units, a gallium nitride amplifier. Typical output powers range from a few watts for small VSAT terminals to hundreds of watts for broadcast uplinks. The BUC is usually mounted outdoors at the antenna feed, next to the low-noise block downconverter (LNB) used for reception, and is powered through the same coaxial cable that carries the IF signal.

Applications

BUCs are standard components of VSAT networks, satellite news gathering trucks, maritime and military satellite terminals, and teleport uplinks. Redundant 1:1 or 1:2 BUC configurations are used where continuous service is required.
