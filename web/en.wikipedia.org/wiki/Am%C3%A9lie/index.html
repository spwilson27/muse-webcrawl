Amélie (French: Le Fabuleux Destin d'Amélie Poulain) is a 2001 French-language romantic comedy film directed by Jean-Pierre Jeunet. Written by Jeunet with Guillaume Laurant, it stars Audrey Tautou as a shy, whimsical waitress in Montmartre, Paris, who decides to improve the lives of those around her while struggling with her own isolation.

Plot

After finding a small box of childhood treasures hidden in her flat, Amélie Poulain returns it to its now-grown owner and resolves to perform anonymous good deeds. Along the way she falls for Nino Quincampoix, a quirky young man who collects discarded photo-booth pictures, and pursues him through a trail of playful clues.

Reception

The film was a major critical and commercial success, praised for Yann Tiersen's score and its distinctive visual style. It won four César Awards and was nominated for five Academy Awards, and it has since become a cult classic credited with boosting tourism to Montmartre.
