The Dabuyid dynasty was an Iranian dynasty of Zoroastrian rulers who governed Tabaristan, on the southern Caspian coast of present-day northern Iran, from the mid-7th century until 761 CE. Its rulers bore the title ispahbadh and claimed descent from the Sasanian era.

History

The dynasty was founded by Gil Gavbara (Gamshid's descendant Dabuya giving the house its name), who was installed in Tabaristan around 642–660, as the Sasanian Empire collapsed before the Arab conquests. The mountainous, forested terrain of Tabaristan allowed the Dabuyids to resist the Umayyad Caliphate for over a century, minting their own coins in the Sasanian style.

Notable rulers include Farrukhan the Great (r. c. 712–728), who expanded Dabuyid control, and Khurshid (r. 740–761), the last ruler. In 759–761 the Abbasid Caliphate invaded; Khurshid fled and ultimately died, and Tabaristan was annexed, though local dynasties such as the Bavandids continued under Muslim suzerainty.

Legacy

The Dabuyids are remembered as one of the last Iranian dynasties to preserve pre-Islamic Zoroastrian court culture after the Arab conquest of Persia.
