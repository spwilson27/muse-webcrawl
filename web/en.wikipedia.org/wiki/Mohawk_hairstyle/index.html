Mohawk hairstyle

The mohawk (also called a Mohican in British English) is a hairstyle in which both sides of the head are shaved, leaving a strip of noticeably longer hair in the centre of the head, running from the forehead to the nape. The hairstyle takes its name from the Mohawk people, an Indigenous people of North America, though the style was historically worn by several Native American groups. In contemporary culture it is most strongly associated with the punk subculture.

A spiked mohawk, a hallmark of punk fashion.

History

Hairstyles resembling the mohawk are ancient — the Clonycavan Man, a bog body from Iron Age Ireland, was found with a similar style stiffened with resin. Warriors of the Mohawk and other Haudenosaunee peoples sometimes plucked the hair to leave a narrow strip, a look popularized for wider audiences by Hollywood westerns and by World War II paratroopers of the 101st Airborne, some of whom shaved their heads into mohawks before D-Day.

Punk and popular culture

In the 1970s and 1980s the mohawk became an emblem of punk rock, often dyed in bright colours, spiked upright with soap, glue or gel, and paired with leather and studs. Variants include the fauxhawk (a shorter, unshaved imitation popularized by footballers), the deathhawk in goth circles, and the wide "Chelsea hawk". Robert De Niro's character Travis Bickle in Taxi Driver (1976) famously sports a mohawk in the film's climax.
