Codex Alimentarius

The Codex Alimentarius (Latin for "Food Code") is a collection of internationally recognised standards, codes of practice, guidelines, and other recommendations relating to food, food production, labelling, and food safety, published by the Codex Alimentarius Commission.

The Commission

The Codex Alimentarius Commission was established in 1963 by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Health Organization (WHO) to develop food standards under the Joint FAO/WHO Food Standards Programme.

Scope

Codex texts cover general standards for food additives and contaminants, maximum limits for pesticide residues, codes of hygienic practice, labelling rules (including allergen declarations), and commodity standards for foods ranging from cereals to fish products. Its General Principles of Food Hygiene underpin the HACCP system used worldwide.

Legal status

Codex standards are voluntary, but they serve as the reference point for food safety under the World Trade Organization's SPS Agreement, giving them substantial influence over national regulations and international trade disputes.
