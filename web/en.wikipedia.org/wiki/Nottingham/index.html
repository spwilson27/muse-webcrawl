Nottingham

Nottingham is a city and unitary authority in Nottinghamshire, East Midlands, England, on the River Trent. It had a population of about 323,000 in 2021, with a much larger urban area. Nottingham is famous for its association with the legend of Robin Hood, its castle, its lace-making and bicycle industries, and its large student population.

History

The settlement originated as the Anglo-Saxon town of Snotingaham; the Normans built a castle on the sandstone rock in 1068, around which the borough grew. Nottingham became a county corporate in the 15th century and a centre of the hosiery and lace trades, worked in the Lace Market district's warehouses. In the 19th century the city grew rapidly with textiles, coal, and engineering; it was also a centre of Luddite protest and reform riots, including the burning of the castle in 1831. Raleigh bicycles and John Player cigarettes were among its famous products.

Modern Nottingham

Tourist landmarks include the rebuilt Nottingham Castle, the City of Caves carved in the sandstone beneath the city, Ye Olde Trip to Jerusalem (which claims to be England's oldest inn), and Wollaton Hall. The city is home to two professional football clubs, Nottingham Forest and Notts County—among the closest rivals in England—as well as Trent Bridge cricket ground. Its economy today centres on education, life sciences (including BioCity), creative industries, and services.
