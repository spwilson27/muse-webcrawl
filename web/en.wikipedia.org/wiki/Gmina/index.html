A gmina (pronounced roughly "gmee-na") is the smallest unit of local government in Poland, comparable to a municipality or commune in other countries. Gminas form the base tier of Poland's three-level system of territorial self-government, below the powiat (county) and the voivodeship (province). There are roughly 2,477 gminas, and each is responsible for local services such as primary education, water supply, local roads, waste collection and spatial planning.

Types

There are three types of gmina: urban gminas, consisting of a single town or city; rural gminas, consisting of villages and countryside with the seat usually in a village; and urban-rural gminas, combining a town with surrounding villages. Large cities additionally hold powiat status, combining both tiers of government. Each gmina is governed by an elected council and an executive head — a wójt in rural gminas, a burmistrz (mayor) in towns, or a prezydent miasta (city president) in larger cities. Rural gminas are further subdivided into village districts called sołectwa.

History

The gmina has roots in the communal institutions of partitioned and interwar Poland and was re-established as a self-governing body in the local government reforms of 1990, the first stage of Poland's post-communist decentralisation. The present structure dates from the reforms of 1999, which introduced the three-tier system that took effect on 1 January 1999.
