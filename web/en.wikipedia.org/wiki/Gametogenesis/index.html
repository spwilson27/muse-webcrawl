Gametogenesis

Gametogenesis
Field: Developmental biology
Process: Formation of gametes (sperm and eggs)
Cell division: Meiosis
Types: Spermatogenesis, oogenesis

Gametogenesis is a biological process by which diploid or haploid precursor cells (germ cells) undergo cell division and differentiation to form mature haploid gametes. In animals, gametogenesis occurs in the gonads: in males it is called spermatogenesis and produces sperm, while in females it is called oogenesis and produces ova (egg cells).


Spermatogenesis and oogenesis compared

Process

Gametogenesis involves both mitosis, which multiplies the germ-line stem cells, and meiosis, which halves the chromosome number and generates genetic diversity through crossing over and independent assortment. In spermatogenesis, one primary spermatocyte yields four functional spermatozoa. In oogenesis, one primary oocyte yields a single ovum and smaller polar bodies, concentrating cytoplasm in the egg.

In plants

In flowering plants, gametogenesis occurs within the anthers and ovules, producing the male gametophyte (pollen grain) and female gametophyte (embryo sac) through microgametogenesis and megagametogenesis.
