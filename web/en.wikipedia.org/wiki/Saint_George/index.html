Saint George

Saint George (Greek: Γεώργιος; died 23 April 303) was a soldier in the Roman army, a Christian martyr executed under Diocletian, and one of the most venerated saints in Christianity. He is the patron saint of England, Georgia, Catalonia, and many cities, soldiers and scouts.

Life and martyrdom

Tradition holds that George, born in Cappadocia to Christian parents, served as an officer in Diocletian's guard. When the emperor ordered persecution of Christians in 303, George refused to renounce his faith, was tortured and beheaded. His tomb at Lydda (Lod) became a pilgrimage site.

The dragon legend

The famous tale of George slaying a dragon that demanded human sacrifice, rescuing a princess, appears in medieval sources such as Jacobus de Voragine's Golden Legend (13th century). It symbolises the triumph of good over evil and spread with the Crusaders.

Veneration

George's red cross on white became England's flag; Edward III made him patron of the Order of the Garter. He is also venerated in Islam-adjacent folk tradition as al-Khidr in parts of the Middle East, and St George's Day is celebrated on 23 April.
