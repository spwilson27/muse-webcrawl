Education in Quebec

Education in Quebec is administered by the provincial Ministry of Education and differs from the rest of Canada in its structure. Schooling is compulsory from ages 6 to 16, and instruction is offered in French and English networks, with French predominant. Since 1998, public school boards have been organised by language rather than by religion.

Structure

Elementary school lasts six years and secondary school five years, ending with the Secondary School Diploma. Quebec is unique in North America for the CEGEP system (collèges d'enseignement général et professionnel), created in 1967 during the Quiet Revolution: after secondary school, students attend a CEGEP for a two-year pre-university programme or a three-year technical programme leading directly to employment. University bachelor's degrees are consequently often three years for Quebec CEGEP graduates. The system also includes extensive adult education and vocational training centres.

Universities

Major institutions include McGill University and the Université de Montréal, Université Laval, Concordia University, and the Université du Québec network. Quebec resident tuition fees have historically been the lowest in Canada, a policy defended by successive governments and a focus of major student protests, notably the 2012 "Maple Spring" strike against planned fee increases.

Language

Under the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977), children of immigrants must generally attend French-language schools, a policy intended to preserve French in North America. English-language public schooling is principally reserved for children whose parents were educated in English in Canada. Quebec students perform strongly in mathematics in national and international assessments.
