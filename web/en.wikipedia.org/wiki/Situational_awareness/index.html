Situational awareness

Situational awareness (or situation awareness, SA) is the understanding of an environment, its elements, and how it changes with respect to time and space. It involves perceiving the relevant elements around oneself, comprehending their meaning, and projecting their future status, so as to support effective decision-making. Loss of situational awareness is a major contributing factor in aviation accidents, medical errors, and military friendly-fire incidents.

Endsley's model

The most widely used framework is that of Mica Endsley, who defines SA in three levels:

  1. Perception — perceiving the status, attributes, and dynamics of relevant elements in the environment (for a pilot: other aircraft, terrain, weather, aircraft systems).
  2. Comprehension — synthesising those elements into an understanding of the current situation and its significance for one's goals.
  3. Projection — anticipating the future status of those elements and how the situation will evolve.

SA is shaped by task and environmental factors (workload, stress, automation design) and by individual factors such as experience, training, and mental models. In team settings, team SA and shared SA describe how awareness is distributed and coordinated among members.

Applications

The concept originated in military aviation — fighter pilots in the First World War were already said to succeed by maintaining awareness of the enemy — and became a major human-factors research topic in the 1980s and 1990s. It is now applied in air traffic control, surgery and anaesthesia, emergency response, driving, cyber security operations, and the design of displays and autonomous systems. Measurement techniques include freeze-probe methods such as SAGAT, in which a simulation is paused and participants are quizzed about the current state.
