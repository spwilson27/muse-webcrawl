Adjunction (category theory)

In mathematics, specifically category theory, adjunction is a relationship that two functors may have, expressing a form of duality between two related categories.

Definition

Functors F : D → C and G : C → D form an adjunction if there is a natural bijection between hom-sets HomC(F(X), Y) and HomD(X, G(Y)). F is the left adjoint and G the right adjoint, written F ⊣ G. Equivalently, adjunctions can be specified by unit and counit natural transformations satisfying the triangle identities.

Examples

The free group functor is left adjoint to the forgetful functor to sets; tensor product is left adjoint to internal hom; limits and colimits arise as adjoints to the diagonal functor. Daniel Kan introduced adjoint functors in 1958.
