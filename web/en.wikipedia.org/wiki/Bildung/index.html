Bildung


Bildung refers to the German tradition of self-cultivation and formation.

Bildung (German: [ˈbɪldʊŋ], "education, formation, cultivation") refers to the German tradition of self-cultivation (as related to the German for "creation, image, shape"), wherein philosophy and education are linked in a manner that refers to a process of both personal and cultural maturation. This maturation is described as a harmonisation of the individual's mind and heart and in a unification of selfhood and identity within the broader society, as evidenced with the literary tradition of the Bildungsroman.

In this sense, the process of harmonisation of mind, heart, selfhood and identity is achieved through personal transformation, which presents a challenge to the individual's accepted beliefs. In Hegel's writings, the challenge of personal transformation in the process of Bildung arises in the individual's alienation from their immediate, natural self. In this way, the subject undergoes a re-education in which they are alienated from their prior knowledge and consciousness, which exposes the individual to internal conflict and tension between their current self and the new knowledge they encounter.

Etymology

The term Bildung is a German word meaning "education" and "formation" simultaneously, deriving from the noun Bild ("image, picture") and the verb bilden ("to shape, to form"). Its ambiguity allows it to encompass both the process of education and the resulting formed state, as well as the shaping of the human being with regard to their humanity and their intellectual and spiritual capacities.

Wilhelm von Humboldt

The educational philosopher Wilhelm von Humboldt is often considered the originator of the modern conception of Bildung. In Humboldt's writings, Bildung was conceived of as a lifelong process of human development, rather than mere training in gaining certain external knowledge or skills. Contrary to the professional training concept, Bildung becomes the process of the spiritual and cultural sensibilities of man as well as his life, personal and social, being in a process of constant expansion and growth. Bildung is seen herein not as a mere accumulation of knowledge, but as a process in which an individual's spiritual and cultural sensibilities, as well as life, personal and social skills are in a process of continual expansion and growth.

The Bildungsroman

The literary genre most closely associated with the concept is the Bildungsroman, a novel of formation in which the protagonist undergoes a process of moral and psychological growth from youth to maturity. Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship (1795–96) is the genre's founding example; other canonical works include Thomas Mann's The Magic Mountain and Hermann Hesse's Demian and Siddhartha.
