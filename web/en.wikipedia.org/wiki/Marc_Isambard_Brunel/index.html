Marc Isambard Brunel

Born: 25 April 1769, Hacqueville, France
Died: 12 December 1849, London
Occupation: Civil engineer
Known for: Thames Tunnel; block-making machinery
Children: Isambard Kingdom Brunel

Sir Marc Isambard Brunel (25 April 1769 – 12 December 1849) was a French-British engineer who is most famous for the construction of the Thames Tunnel in London and as the father of Isambard Kingdom Brunel.

Early life and career

Born in Hacqueville, Normandy, Brunel served in the French navy and emigrated to the United States in 1793, becoming chief engineer of New York City. There he designed the Capitol cannon foundry and won the competition for the United States Capitol building's construction contracts. In 1799 he moved to England, where he married Sophia Kingdom.

Block mills

Working with Henry Maudslay, Brunel designed the Portsmouth Block Mills (1802), a pioneering factory of machine tools for mass-producing wooden pulley blocks for the Royal Navy — often regarded as one of the first examples of fully mechanised production.

Thames Tunnel

In 1818 Brunel patented a tunnelling shield inspired by the shipworm Teredo navalis. Using it, he drove the Thames Tunnel between Wapping and Rotherhithe (1825–1843), the first tunnel under a navigable river — a project that ruined his finances, imprisoned him briefly for debt, and was completed under the direction of his son Isambard. He was knighted in 1841.
