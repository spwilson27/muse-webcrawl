Salad bowl (cultural idea)

The salad bowl is a metaphor for multicultural integration in which different cultures coexist within a society while retaining their distinct identities — like ingredients in a salad that keep their flavour while contributing to the whole dish. It contrasts with the melting pot, in which immigrant cultures are expected to blend and assimilate into a single homogeneous culture.

Concept

In the salad bowl model, associated with multiculturalism in Canada and with thinkers influenced by Horace Kallen's "cultural pluralism" (1915), ethnic communities preserve languages, religions and customs while participating in shared civic life. Supporters argue it respects diversity and avoids forced assimilation.

Criticism

Critics argue the model can entrench separation, weaken social cohesion and shared values, or mask inequality between groups. Others note real societies fall between the two metaphors, with assimilation in some domains (language of work) and pluralism in others (food, religion). Related metaphors include the "mosaic" used in Canada and the "gumbo" or "stew".
