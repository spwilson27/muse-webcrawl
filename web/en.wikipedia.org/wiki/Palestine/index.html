Palestine

Palestine (Arabic: فلسطين, Filasṭīn) most commonly refers to the State of Palestine, a state in the southern Levant that claims the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, with East Jerusalem as its designated capital, and whose administrative centre is Ramallah. The term also denotes the wider geographic region between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea. The State of Palestine was proclaimed in 1988 by the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO), is recognised by a large majority of United Nations member states, and has held non-member observer state status at the UN since 2012. Its claimed territory remains under Israeli occupation and its governance is divided between the Palestinian Authority in parts of the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza.

History

The region of Palestine was part of the Ottoman Empire for four centuries before the First World War. Under the British Mandate for Palestine (1920–1948), conflict between the Arab majority and the growing Jewish community intensified. The UN Partition Plan of 1947 proposed two states; its rejection by Arab leaders was followed by the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, in which the State of Israel was established and more than 700,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled from their homes — the exodus Palestinians call the Nakba ("catastrophe"). Jordan annexed the West Bank and Egypt administered Gaza.

Israel occupied both territories in the Six-Day War of 1967. The PLO, founded in 1964, became the umbrella of Palestinian nationalism under Yasser Arafat. The First Intifada (1987–93) uprising led to the Oslo Accords (1993–95), under which the Palestinian Authority was created to administer parts of the West Bank and Gaza and final-status issues — borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements — were deferred. Negotiations repeatedly failed; the Second Intifada (2000–05), Israel's 2005 disengagement from Gaza, and Hamas's 2007 takeover of Gaza left Palestinian politics split. The war that began with Hamas's attack on Israel on 7 October 2023 and Israel's ensuing campaign in Gaza caused catastrophic loss of life and destruction, alongside a renewed international push for recognition of Palestinian statehood, with a number of additional Western states extending recognition in the mid-2020s.

Politics and recognition

The State of Palestine is recognised by more than 140 of the UN's 193 member states (a number that grew during 2024–25) and is a member of bodies including UNESCO and the International Criminal Court. The Palestinian Authority, dominated by Fatah and led by President Mahmoud Abbas (in office since 2005), administers Palestinian population centres in the West Bank, while Israel retains overall security control there and maintains a blockade of Gaza, tightened during the 2023 war. Elections have not been held nationally since 2006.

Geography and demography

The West Bank (5,655 km², including East Jerusalem) is landlocked hill country east of Israel and west of the Jordan River; the Gaza Strip (365 km²) is a narrow coastal enclave on the Mediterranean bordering Israel and Egypt. Together they are home to roughly 5.4 million Palestinians, in addition to some 700,000 Israeli settlers in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. A large Palestinian diaspora and refugee population lives in Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and worldwide.
