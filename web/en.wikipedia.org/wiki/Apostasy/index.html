Apostasy
Definition: Formal renunciation of a religion
Related concepts: Heresy, blasphemy, conversion
Legal status: Varies by country

Apostasy (from Greek apostasia, "a defection or revolt") is the formal disaffiliation from, abandonment of, or renunciation of a religion by a person. In a broader sense, the term can describe renunciation of any belief or cause, including political parties and ideologies. One who undertakes apostasy is called an apostate.

In religious traditions

Different religions treat departure from the faith differently. In Christian theology, apostasy has historically referred to a total repudiation of the faith, distinguished from heresy, which involves holding erroneous beliefs while remaining within the community. In Islam, apostasy (ridda) has traditionally been treated as a serious offence in classical jurisprudence, though interpretations vary widely today. Judaism distinguishes between involuntary and voluntary apostates in rabbinic law, while traditions such as Buddhism and Hinduism generally place less institutional emphasis on formal membership.

Legal treatment

The 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights affirms the freedom to change one's religion or belief. Nevertheless, in the early twenty-first century a number of countries retained laws criminalising apostasy, in a few cases carrying the death penalty, while elsewhere the main consequences are social, such as ostracism or loss of civil status.

Sociology

Sociologists distinguish apostasy from mere switching between denominations, reserving the term for defection accompanied by rejection of the former group and sometimes active opposition to it, often involving a public narrative of the reasons for leaving.
