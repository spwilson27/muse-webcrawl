First Congo War

The First Congo War (1996–1997) was a civil war and international conflict in Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo) that culminated in the overthrow of the long-serving president Mobutu Sese Seko by rebel forces led by Laurent-Désiré Kabila. The rebels' Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo (AFDL) was heavily supported by Rwanda, Uganda and Angola, whose intervention was driven in large part by the presence in eastern Zaire of armed Hutu groups responsible for the 1994 Rwandan genocide.

Background

After the Rwandan genocide of 1994, more than a million Hutu refugees, among them the defeated Rwandan army (ex-FAR) and Interahamwe militias, fled into refugee camps in eastern Zaire. From these camps the génocidaires attacked the new Tutsi-led government in Rwanda, while Mobutu's regime did little to disarm them. Rwanda, Uganda and Angola — the last angered by Zaire's support for UNITA rebels — resolved to act, backing the AFDL, a coalition of Congolese opposition groups fronted by the veteran rebel Laurent-Désiré Kabila. Tension over the citizenship of the Banyamulenge (Congolese Tutsi) in South Kivu provided the immediate trigger in October 1996.

Course of the war

Rwandan and AFDL forces attacked the refugee camps in November 1996, sending hundreds of thousands of refugees back to Rwanda and driving others westward into the forest, where many died or were massacred. The demoralised Zairian armed forces (FAZ) offered little resistance, frequently looting as they retreated. The AFDL advanced across the vast country with remarkable speed, taking Kisangani in March 1997 and Lubumbashi in April. Western attempts to negotiate a transition failed, and Mobutu, already gravely ill with cancer, fled Kinshasa on 16 May 1997. AFDL forces entered the capital the following day, and Kabila proclaimed himself president, restoring the country's name as the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Aftermath

The war ended Mobutu's 32-year rule but did not bring peace. Relations between Kabila and his Rwandan and Ugandan backers collapsed within a year, and in August 1998 the Second Congo War began — a far larger conflict, sometimes called "Africa's World War," which drew in numerous African states and caused millions of excess deaths. Massacres of Hutu refugees during the 1996–97 campaign were later documented by the UN Mapping Report of 2010.
