Duran Duran

Duran Duran
Origin: Birmingham, England
Formed: 1978
Genres: New wave, synth-pop, pop rock, dance-rock
Members: Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes, John Taylor, Roger Taylor

Duran Duran are an English pop rock band formed in Birmingham in 1978 by singer Stephen Duffy, keyboardist Nick Rhodes, and bassist John Taylor. The band, named after the villain Dr. Durand-Durand in the film Barbarella, became leading figures of the New Romantic scene and the "second British Invasion" of the United States in the 1980s, propelled by stylish music videos in heavy rotation on MTV.

Career

With singer Simon Le Bon and drummer Roger Taylor, the classic line-up released the albums Duran Duran (1981) and Rio (1982), producing hits including "Girls on Film", "Rio", "Hungry Like the Wolf", and "Save a Prayer". Seven and the Ragged Tiger (1983) yielded "The Reflex", and the band recorded the James Bond theme "A View to a Kill" (1985), a US number one. Side projects Power Station and Arcadia occupied the mid-1980s; later successes included "Ordinary World" and "Come Undone" from the 1993 "Wedding Album".

The original five members reunited in 2001 for Astronaut (2004). Later albums include Paper Gods (2015), Future Past (2021), and the Halloween-themed Danse Macabre (2023). Duran Duran have sold over 100 million records and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.
