Stabile (sculpture)

A stabile is a type of stationary abstract sculpture, the counterpart to the mobile. The term was coined by Jean Arp in 1932 to describe the works of Alexander Calder, whose kinetic sculptures were known as mobiles.

Characteristics

Stabiles are typically made of painted sheet metal, often in bold colours such as black, red and blue. Although they do not move, their curving, biomorphic forms suggest motion and balance. Calder produced monumental stabiles for public spaces around the world, including La Grande Vitesse in Grand Rapids, Michigan, and Flamingo in Chicago.

Legacy

The stabile became a defining form of modernist public art, influencing generations of sculptors working at architectural scale.
