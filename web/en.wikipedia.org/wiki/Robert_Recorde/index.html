Robert Recorde

Robert Recorde (c. 1512 – 1558) was a Welsh physician and mathematician. He invented the "equals" sign (=) and introduced the pre-existing "+" and "−" signs to English speakers in 1557. He wrote several popular textbooks in English — unusual for the period, when scholarly works were usually in Latin — cast in the form of dialogues between master and scholar.

Life

Recorde was born in Tenby, Pembrokeshire, into a respectable family. He entered the University of Oxford around 1525 and was elected a fellow of All Souls College in 1531. He took a medical degree at Cambridge in 1545 and practised as a physician, counting members of the royal court among his patients. He also served as comptroller of the Bristol Mint and of mines in Ireland. Accused of slander by the Earl of Pembroke, he was imprisoned for debt in the King's Bench Prison, Southwark, where he died in 1558.

Works

His books include The Grounde of Artes (1543), the first English book on algebra and arithmetic; The Pathway to Knowledg (1551), on geometry; The Castle of Knowledge (1556), on astronomy, which mentioned the Copernican heliocentric theory; and The Whetstone of Witte (1557), in which the equals sign first appeared. Recorde explained that he chose "a pair of parallels... because no two things can be more equal."
