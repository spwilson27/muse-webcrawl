Serge Haroche

Serge Haroche
Born11 September 1944, Casablanca, Morocco
Known forCavity quantum electrodynamics

[Image: Photograph of Serge Haroche beside his superconducting microwave cavity apparatus]
Haroche with a cavity QED experiment.

Serge Haroche (born 11 September 1944) is a French physicist who has worked on atomic physics and quantum optics. He shared the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physics with David Wineland 'for ground-breaking experimental methods that enable measuring and manipulation of individual quantum systems'.

At the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, Haroche built cavity quantum electrodynamics: Rydberg atoms sent one by one through a superconducting microwave cavity, counting and manipulating individual photons without destroying them (quantum non-demolition measurement). His group realised a 'Schrödinger cat' of the radiation field (1996) and watched its decoherence in real time — directly observing the quantum-to-classical transition that the Copenhagen interpretation had left abstract.

Career

Haroche studied at ENS and Paris VI (PhD 1971) and held posts at Yale, Stanford, Harvard and MIT before returning to ENS, where he headed the physics department and later served as administrator of the Collège de France (2012–2015). His experiments complement Wineland's trapped ions: one masters charged particles, the other photons — the two great toolkits of quantum information science.
