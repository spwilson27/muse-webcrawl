Falkirk (Scots: The Fawkirk; Scottish Gaelic: An Eaglais Bhreac) is a town in the Central Lowlands of Scotland, historically in the county of Stirlingshire. It lies in the Forth Valley, roughly midway between Edinburgh and Glasgow, at the junction of the Forth and Clyde Canal and the Union Canal, a location that made it a centre of heavy industry during the Industrial Revolution.

History

The town's name means "speckled church", from the Middle English falkirk or Gaelic eaglais bhreac, referring to an early church of mottled stone. The Antonine Wall, the northern frontier of Roman Britain, passes just south of the town. Two major battles were fought nearby: the Battle of Falkirk (1298), where Edward I of England defeated William Wallace, and the Battle of Falkirk Muir (1746), the last Jacobite victory of the '45 rising.

Industry

From the late 18th century Falkirk became a centre of iron founding; the Carron Company, founded in 1759, was once among the largest ironworks in Europe and gave its name to the carronade naval cannon. Brewing, textiles and later chemicals and bus manufacturing (Alexander Dennis) were also significant employers.

Landmarks

Modern landmarks include the Falkirk Wheel (opened 2002), the world's only rotating boat lift, which reconnects the Forth and Clyde and Union canals, and The Kelpies (2013), two 30-metre steel horse-head sculptures by Andy Scott in The Helix park. Callendar House, a mansion with Roman and Jacobite exhibitions, stands in Callendar Park. The town's football club, Falkirk F.C., plays at the Falkirk Stadium.
