Metta Sutta

A manuscript of the Metta Sutta

The Metta Sutta, also known as the Karaṇīyametta Sutta ("Discourse on Loving-Kindness"), is a Buddhist scripture in the Pali Canon that extols mettā—loving-kindness or benevolence toward all beings. It is one of the most beloved and widely recited texts in Theravada Buddhism, chanted daily by monks and laypeople and used as a protective recitation (paritta). The sutta appears in the Sutta Nipāta (Sn 1.8) and in the Khuddakapāṭha (Khp 9).

Content

The discourse begins by describing the conduct and character of one who wishes to attain peace: the practitioner should be "capable, upright, and straightforward, easy to speak to, gentle, and not proud," contented, frugal, and with senses calmed. It then gives the famous meditation on universal goodwill: "Even as a mother protects with her life her child, her only child, so with a boundless heart should one cherish all living beings," radiating kindness above, below, and all around, without limit or hostility. The sutta concludes that one established in this practice, not falling into wrong views, will not be reborn in a womb again.

Traditional account

According to tradition, the Buddha taught this discourse to a group of monks who had gone to meditate in a forest and were frightened by local deities; after learning to radiate loving-kindness, the monks were received warmly and completed their retreat successfully. The text remains a core script for the practice of mettā meditation within the four brahmavihāras (divine abodes).
