The Tennessee Volunteers football program represents the University of Tennessee in American football. The Volunteers compete in the Football Bowl Subdivision of the NCAA and the Southeastern Conference, playing home games at Neyland Stadium in Knoxville, one of the largest stadiums in the United States with a capacity of over 100,000.

History

Tennessee fielded its first team in 1891. The program rose to prominence under coach Robert Neyland, who led the Volunteers from 1926 to 1952 (with interruptions for military service) and whose teams won national championships and built the stadium that now bears his name. His maxims — "the team that makes the fewest mistakes will win" among them — remain part of the program's identity.

Under Phillip Fulmer, Tennessee won the 1998 national championship, defeating Florida State in the Fiesta Bowl to complete a 13–0 season in the first year of the Bowl Championship Series. Notable players include Peyton Manning, Reggie White, Condredge Holloway and Johnny Majors, who also coached the team. The program claims six national titles and has produced numerous All-Americans and NFL players.

Traditions

The Volunteers run onto the field through the "Power T" formed by the Pride of the Southland Band, wear a checkerboard pattern in the end zones, and sing "Rocky Top," one of college football's best-known fight songs. The bluetick coonhound Smokey serves as mascot. Major rivalries include Alabama (the Third Saturday in October), Florida, Georgia and Vanderbilt.
