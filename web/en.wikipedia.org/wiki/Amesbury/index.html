Amesbury

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amesbury is a town and civil parish in Wiltshire, England. It is known for the prehistoric monument of Stonehenge, which is within the parish, and for the discovery of the Amesbury Archer — a well-preserved Bronze Age burial. The town is often described as one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in Great Britain, with archaeological evidence of occupation around 8820 BC.

Population: about 10,700 (2021 census)
County: Wiltshire
Region: South West England
Postcode district: SP4

History

The land around Amesbury has been settled since prehistoric times. In 2002, excavation for a new school uncovered the grave of the "Amesbury Archer," a man buried around 2300 BC with the richest collection of Bronze Age grave goods found in Britain, including some of the earliest gold and copper objects. The town's name derives from Ambrosius Aurelianus or from an Anglo-Saxon landowner. Amesbury Abbey was founded around 979 AD by Queen Ælfthryth.

Geography

The town sits in the valley of the River Avon on Salisbury Plain, about 8 miles (13 km) north of Salisbury. The A303 trunk road passes nearby, serving visitors to Stonehenge, which lies some two miles west of the town.
