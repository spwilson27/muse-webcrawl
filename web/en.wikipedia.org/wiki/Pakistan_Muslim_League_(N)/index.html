The Pakistan Muslim League (Nawaz), abbreviated as PML(N) or PML-N, is a centre-right, liberal-conservative political party in Pakistan. It is one of the country's major parties and has formed the federal government several times under Nawaz Sharif and his brother Shehbaz Sharif. The "(N)" denotes Nawaz, distinguishing it from the many other factions of the historic Muslim League.

History

The party descends from the Muslim League that founded Pakistan in 1947. In its modern form it emerged in 1988 within the Islami Jamhoori Ittehad alliance, with Nawaz Sharif as Chief Minister of Punjab and then Prime Minister (1990–1993). After Sharif's dismissal in 1993, the faction formally organised as PML(N). It won a heavy mandate in 1997, but the government was overthrown in Pervez Musharraf's 1999 coup and the Sharif family went into exile.

Returning in 2007, PML(N) came second in the 2008 elections and formed the government in Punjab. It won the 2013 general election, returning Nawaz Sharif as prime minister for a third term; he was disqualified by the Supreme Court in 2017 in the Panama Papers case. The party lost the 2018 election to Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf but returned to power in April 2022 when Shehbaz Sharif became prime minister after Imran Khan's removal in a no-confidence vote. Following the February 2024 general election, PML(N) again formed a coalition government under Shehbaz Sharif.

Policies

PML(N) is associated with market-oriented economic reform, privatisation and large infrastructure projects — including motorways, the Lahore Metro and energy projects under the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor — with a core support base among Punjab's business and trading communities.
