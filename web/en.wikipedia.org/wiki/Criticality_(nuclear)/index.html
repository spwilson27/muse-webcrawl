Criticality (nuclear)

In nuclear engineering, criticality describes the state of a system containing fissile material in which a self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction occurs. A system is described by its effective neutron multiplication factor, k: it is subcritical when k < 1, critical when k = 1, and supercritical when k > 1.

Reactor operation

A nuclear reactor is maintained exactly at criticality for steady power production, with reactivity controlled by control rods, moderators, and neutron poisons. Prompt criticality — where the reaction is sustained by prompt neutrons alone — risks rapid power excursions.

Criticality accidents

Unintended criticality in fissile-material processing has caused a number of fatal accidents, such as the 1945–46 "demon core" incidents at Los Alamos and the 1999 Tokaimura accident in Japan. Criticality safety practice uses margins on mass, geometry, moderation, and concentration to prevent recurrence.
