Nintendo Entertainment System

The Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) is an 8-bit home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. It was released in Japan in 1983 as the Family Computer (Famicom), and redesigned for international markets as the NES, launching in North America in 1985 and in Europe and Australia in 1986–1987. The best-selling gaming console of its time, it revitalised the video game industry after the North American crash of 1983 and established the model of third-party developer licensing that the industry still uses.

History

Designed under Masayuki Uemura, the Famicom launched in Japan in July 1983. After the video game crash of 1983 devastated the American market, Nintendo repositioned the machine for the United States not as a game console but as an "Entertainment System", with a VCR-like front-loading design, a robot accessory (R.O.B.), and light-gun bundled play, avoiding terms like "video game" and "cartridge". A test launch in New York in October 1985 was followed by a nationwide rollout in 1986.

Market control and licensing

To prevent the flood of poor-quality games that had contributed to the crash, Nintendo imposed strict licensing terms: third-party publishers were limited in annual releases, cartridges were manufactured only by Nintendo, and a lockout chip (the 10NES) prevented unlicensed software. The "Nintendo Seal of Quality" appeared on licensed products. These policies gave Nintendo extraordinary control of the market, and some — including the lockout system — were later challenged in litigation, notably by Atari Games (Tengen).

Games

The NES library includes some of the most influential games ever made. Super Mario Bros., bundled with the console in many territories, became the platform's signature title; The Legend of Zelda pioneered open exploration and battery-backed saving; Metroid, Mega Man, Castlevania, Final Fantasy and Dragon Quest launched long-running franchises. Officially licensed releases number over 700 titles.

Hardware

The NES uses a Ricoh 2A03 processor based on the MOS Technology 6502, a custom Picture Processing Unit generating 256×240 resolution with 25 simultaneous colours from a 54-colour palette, and cartridge-based memory mappers that extended the system's capabilities over its life. Peripherals included the Zapper light gun, R.O.B., the Power Pad, the NES Advantage arcade stick and the Power Glove. In Japan, the Famicom Disk System add-on used rewritable floppy disks.

Legacy

The NES is widely credited with reviving the home console industry and establishing Nintendo as its leader. Its controller's cross-shaped D-pad design became an industry standard. Nintendo released the NES Classic Edition miniature console in 2016, preloaded with 30 games; it sold 2.3 million units in its first run and was later reissued.
