Salsa verde

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Salsa verde]
Mexican salsa verde

Salsa verde (Spanish for "green sauce") is the name given to several distinct green sauces in world cuisines. The best known is the Mexican sauce based on tomatillos and green chillies, a staple condiment and cooking sauce. The name also applies to Italian salsa verde, a cold herb sauce of parsley, capers, and anchovies, and to various herby green sauces elsewhere in Europe, such as the German Grüne Soße of Frankfurt.

Mexican salsa verde

The Mexican version is made from tomatillos (Physalis philadelphica) husked and either boiled, roasted, or used raw, blended with onion, coriander (cilantro), garlic, salt, and green chillies such as jalapeño or serrano. A raw preparation is called salsa verde cruda; a cooked one is smoother and milder. It is served at the table with tacos and grilled meats, and is the cooking medium for dishes such as chilaquiles verdes, enchiladas verdes, and pork in salsa verde. Its origins predate the Spanish conquest: Aztec markets sold salsas ground in the molcajete, and Bernardino de Sahagún described chilli-and-tomatillo sauces in the 16th century.

Italian and other green sauces

Italian salsa verde, typical of Piedmont (where it is called bagnet verd), is a piquant cold sauce of chopped parsley, capers, anchovies, garlic, breadcrumbs, and olive oil, traditionally served with bollito misto (boiled meats). Related sauces include the French sauce verte (a herb mayonnaise) and Spain's salsa verde of parsley and garlic cooked with fish such as hake (merluza en salsa verde).
