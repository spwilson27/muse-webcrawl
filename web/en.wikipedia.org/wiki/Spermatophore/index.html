Spermatophore

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A spermatophore or sperm ampulla is a capsule or mass containing spermatozoa created by males of various animal species, especially salamanders and arthropods, and transferred in entirety to the female's ovipore during reproduction.

Function

Spermatophores offer a means of indirect sperm transfer: the male deposits the packet on the substrate or attaches it to the female, and sperm are subsequently released to fertilise the eggs. The packet often includes nutrients, constituting a 'nuptial gift' that may improve female fecundity.

Occurrence

They are characteristic of most salamanders and newts, many insects (such as bush crickets, where the spermatophore may be very large), arachnids, cephalopods (where it is passed by a modified arm, the hectocotylus), and various other invertebrates including velvet worms and some gastropods.

In cephalopods

In octopuses and squid the spermatophore is a complex structure manufactured in the male's reproductive tract and stored in Needham's sac before transfer.
