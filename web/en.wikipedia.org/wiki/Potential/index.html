Potential

Potential generally refers to a currently unrealized ability or possibility. In philosophy, the concept of potentiality, together with actuality, originates with Aristotle, who distinguished between what a thing could become (dynamis) and what it is in actuality (energeia).

In physics

In physics, a potential is a function whose gradient gives a field of force. Examples include the gravitational potential, the electric potential (voltage), the scalar and vector potentials of electromagnetism, and potential energy — the energy stored in a system by virtue of its position or configuration. A potential field in which the force depends only on position is called conservative, meaning the work done moving between two points is path-independent.

Other uses

In mathematics, potential theory studies harmonic functions and their applications. In chemistry, chemical potential measures the change in a system's free energy as particles are added. In biology, membrane potential is the voltage difference across a cell membrane, essential to nerve impulses; action potentials are the rapid signals of neurons. In everyday usage, "human potential" refers to capacities a person might develop.
