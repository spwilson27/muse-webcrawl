Tin Woodman


The Tin Woodman of Oz

The Tin Woodman, also known as the Tin Man, is a fictional character created by American author L. Frank Baum. He first appeared in Baum's classic 1900 children's book The Wonderful Wizard of Oz and is one of Dorothy's companions on the journey to the Emerald City. He seeks a heart from the Wizard, believing that lacking one he cannot love.

Character history

The Tin Woodman was born Nick Chopper, a flesh-and-blood woodman in the land of the Winkies. The Wicked Witch of the East enchanted his axe so that it successively severed his limbs, each of which a tinsmith replaced with a prosthesis of tin, until he was entirely made of tin — but the tinsmith forgot to give him a heart. Rusting in the forest after a rainstorm, he stood immobile for a year until Dorothy and the Scarecrow oiled his joints. Despite believing himself heartless, he is the most tender and emotional of Dorothy's companions, careful not to step on even an ant.

Later appearances and legacy

In later Oz books the Tin Woodman becomes Emperor of the Winkies and rules from a tin castle. He was memorably portrayed by Jack Haley in the 1939 film The Wizard of Oz. Interpretations of the Oz books have read the character as an allegory of the dehumanized industrial worker of the late nineteenth century, though such political readings remain debated among scholars.
