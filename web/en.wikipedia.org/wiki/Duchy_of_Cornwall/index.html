Duchy of Cornwall
Founded: 17 March 1337
Current holder: William, Prince of Wales
Land: approx. 52,000 hectares across 23 counties

The Duchy of Cornwall is one of two royal duchies in England, the other being the Duchy of Lancaster. It is a private estate established by charter in 1337 by Edward III for his son Edward, the Black Prince, and it provides an income for the heir apparent to the British throne.

History and rights

The dukedom of Cornwall is automatically held by the sovereign's eldest living son who is also heir apparent; since 2022 the Duke of Cornwall has been William, Prince of Wales. The Duchy enjoys special legal privileges, including bona vacantia (ownerless property) within Cornwall, rights to wrecks and royal fish, and ownership of much of the Cornish foreshore and riverbeds.

Estate

The estate comprises roughly 52,000 hectares (128,000 acres) of land in 23 counties, largely in the South West of England, including farms, residential and commercial property, the Isles of Scilly, and the Poundbury model town development in Dorset championed by Charles III during his tenure as duke. Charles managed the Duchy from 1969 to 2022, turning it into a diversified and profitable estate whose surplus funds the public and charitable work of the heir.

Governance

The Duchy is governed by the Prince's Council and is not a company or trust in the ordinary sense; its unique status is defined by statute and charter. Its accounts are published annually, and the Duke pays income tax voluntarily on the surplus.
