Committee of Public Safety

The Committee of Public Safety (French: Comité de salut public) was a committee of the National Convention that formed the provisional government and war cabinet during the Reign of Terror, a violent phase of the French Revolution. Supplementing the Committee of General Defense established in January 1793, the Committee of Public Safety was created on 6 April 1793 by the National Convention. It was charged with protecting the new republic against foreign attacks and internal rebellion, and it oversaw the executive, military, and judicial functions of government.

Rise to power

As the committee sought to contain the dangers facing France — war with much of Europe, the revolt in the Vendée, and economic crisis — it acquired increasing powers. Following a reorganization in July 1793, Maximilien Robespierre joined the committee, along with figures such as Georges Danton (an earlier member), Lazare Carnot, Bertrand Barère, and Louis Antoine de Saint-Just. Under their direction, the committee directed the levée en masse, reorganized the revolutionary armies, and supervised the Revolutionary Tribunal.

The Terror

The committee is most closely associated with the Reign of Terror (1793–1794), during which tens of thousands of people were executed or died in prison as suspected enemies of the Revolution. The Law of Suspects (September 1793) and the Law of 22 Prairial (June 1794), which stripped accused persons of legal defense, marked the height of the Terror. Divisions within the committee and the Convention eventually turned against Robespierre, who was arrested on 27 July 1794 (9 Thermidor) and executed the following day, along with Saint-Just and other allies.

Dissolution

After Thermidor, the committee's powers were sharply curtailed, its membership rotated more frequently, and it was progressively weakened by the Thermidorian Reaction. It was formally dissolved on 26 October 1795, when the National Convention itself was replaced by the Directory.
