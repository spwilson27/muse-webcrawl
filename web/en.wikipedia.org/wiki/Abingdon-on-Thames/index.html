Abingdon-on-Thames (traditionally simply Abingdon) is a historic market town in Oxfordshire, England, on the River Thames about six miles south of Oxford. It lays claim — contested with a few rivals — to being among the oldest continuously inhabited towns in Britain, and was formerly the county town of Berkshire until boundary changes moved it into Oxfordshire in 1974.

History

Settlement at the Thames–Ock confluence is ancient. Abingdon Abbey, a great Benedictine house founded in the seventh century (c. 675), dominated the town until its dissolution in 1538; St Helen's Church, with its thirteenth-century spire, remains a landmark, and little of the abbey survives beyond the gateway and some monastic buildings. Mary I granted the town its charter in 1556. The Wilts & Berks Canal (1810) and later the railway branch from Radley (1856) linked Abingdon to national trade; the celebrated County Hall (1678–1682) with its roof used for the town's peculiar bun-throwing ceremonies overlooks the market place.

Industry and science

From 1929 to 1980 Abingdon was home to the MG car factory, whose Abingdon-built sports cars became world-famous; the MG Car Club is still headquartered in the town. The nearby village of Culham hosts the Joint European Torus (JET) fusion experiment and the UK Atomic Energy Authority, and Harwell Campus lies to the south-west, drawing many scientists to live in Abingdon.

Customs and amenities

Councillors throw buns from the County Hall roof on royal and civic occasions, a custom said to date from the nineteenth century; the town also holds the traditional Michaelmas fair and one of England's oldest agricultural shows. Abingdon School, founded by 1256, is among the country's oldest independent schools. The town is a centre for rowing and Thames-side recreation.
