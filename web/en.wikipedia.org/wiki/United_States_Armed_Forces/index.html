The United States Armed Forces are the military forces of the United States. They consist of six service branches: the Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force and Coast Guard. The president of the United States is the commander-in-chief; the Department of Defense, headed by the secretary of defense, is the principal organ of military policy, while the Coast Guard is administered by the Department of Homeland Security in peacetime.

History

The armed forces trace their origins to the Continental Army, formed in 1775 during the American Revolutionary War. The modern structure emerged with the National Security Act of 1947, which created the Department of Defense and an independent Air Force. The Space Force, established in 2019, is the newest branch. The United States maintains the world's largest military budget and a global network of bases and alliances.

Structure and personnel

The forces are an all-volunteer force of about 1.3 million active-duty personnel, supplemented by reserve components including the National Guard. Operational forces are organised under unified combatant commands, such as Central Command and Indo-Pacific Command, which report through the secretary of defense to the president. The chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff is the senior military officer and principal military adviser to the president.

Role

The armed forces defend the United States, deter aggression, protect U.S. interests and allies abroad, and conduct operations ranging from humanitarian relief to major combat. The Coast Guard additionally performs maritime law-enforcement and search-and-rescue missions.
