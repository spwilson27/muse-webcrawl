Luthier

A luthier is a craftsperson who builds or repairs string instruments that have a neck and a sound box, such as violins, violas, cellos, double basses, guitars, lutes, and mandolins. The word comes from the French luthier, related to luth, the French word for lute.

History

The craft traces its roots to the workshops of Renaissance Italy, most famously in Cremona, where makers such as Andrea Amati and Antonio Stradivari established the forms of the modern violin family. Guitar making developed parallel traditions in Spain and elsewhere. Today luthiers range from individual artisans making bespoke instruments to specialists in restoration of historic instruments.

The craft

Luthiers select and season tonewoods such as spruce and maple, carve plates to graduated thicknesses, bend ribs, and apply varnish. Repair and restoration work includes regraduation, crack repair, neck resetting, and setup (adjusting bridge, soundpost, and fingerboard). Many luthiers complete apprenticeships or attend specialist violin-making schools.
