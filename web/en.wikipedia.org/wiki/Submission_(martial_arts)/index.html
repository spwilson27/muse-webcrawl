Submission (martial arts)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A submission, in combat sports and martial arts, occurs when a competitor concedes defeat to an opponent's grappling technique — typically a joint lock or a chokehold — by "tapping out": tapping the opponent, the mat, or verbally signalling surrender. Requiring an opponent to submit is the central objective of submission grappling arts such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo (in its groundwork), sambo, and catch wrestling, and one of the principal ways of winning a mixed martial arts bout.

Types of submission holds

Joint locks apply leverage to force a joint beyond its normal range of motion. Common examples include the armbar (juji-gatame), the kimura and americana (shoulder locks), and leg locks such as the heel hook and kneebar, which are restricted in some rule sets because of their injury potential.

Chokeholds and strangles restrict either airflow (chokes) or blood flow to the brain (strangles), and can render an opponent unconscious within seconds if not released. Examples include the rear-naked choke, triangle choke, guillotine choke, and various collar chokes in gi grappling. Compression locks and spinal cranks form a smaller, often prohibited category.

Tapping out and safety

The tap is the fundamental safety mechanism of grappling: it obliges the applying competitor to release the hold immediately and the referee to stop the contest. A submission may also be awarded when a competitor is rendered unconscious by a choke, or when a referee or corner stops the fight to protect a trapped competitor. Failure to release a hold after a tap is a serious foul.

In mixed martial arts

Submissions account for a substantial minority of finishes in professional MMA. Early UFC events demonstrated the effectiveness of Brazilian jiu-jitsu submission techniques against larger striking specialists, and submission proficiency remains an essential component of a complete MMA skill set.
