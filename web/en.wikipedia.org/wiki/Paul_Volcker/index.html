Paul Adolph Volcker Jr. (5 September 1927 – 8 December 2019) was an American economist who served as the 12th chairman of the Federal Reserve from 1979 to 1987. He is widely credited with ending the high inflation of the 1970s in the United States.

Federal Reserve chairmanship

Appointed by President Jimmy Carter in 1979 amid double-digit inflation, Volcker sharply tightened monetary policy. In October 1979 the Fed shifted to targeting money supply growth, allowing the federal funds rate to rise as high as 20% in 1981. The policy — the "Volcker shock" — triggered the deep recessions of 1980 and 1981–82 and pushed unemployment above 10%, but it broke inflation, which fell from a peak of about 13.5% in 1980 to roughly 3% by 1983. Volcker was reappointed by President Ronald Reagan in 1983 and served until 1987, when he was succeeded by Alan Greenspan.

Later career

Volcker chaired investigations including the inquiry into Swiss banks' handling of Holocaust-era accounts, and in 2004–05 led the Independent Inquiry Committee into the UN Oil-for-Food Programme. Under President Barack Obama he chaired the Economic Recovery Advisory Board and lent his name to the "Volcker Rule" of the Dodd–Frank Act, which restricts proprietary trading by deposit-taking banks.
