Born: 25 August 1944, Montreal, Canada
Occupation: Publisher, author, former media magnate

Conrad Black

Conrad Moffat Black, Baron Black of Crossharbour (born 25 August 1944), is a Canadian-born British former newspaper magnate, writer, and financier. Through Hollinger International he controlled newspapers including the Chicago Sun-Times, London's Daily Telegraph, and the Jerusalem Post, at its peak one of the largest newspaper groups in the world.

Career

Black built his empire from the late 1970s, acquiring Canadian newspapers and later expanding internationally. He renounced Canadian citizenship in 2001 to accept a British life peerage. He is the author of biographies of Maurice Duplessis, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and Richard Nixon, and of a history of Canada.

Fraud conviction and pardon

In 2007 Black was convicted in the United States of mail fraud and obstruction of justice in connection with unauthorized payments at Hollinger, and served about 37 months in prison; two fraud counts were later vacated on appeal. In May 2019, President Donald Trump granted him a full pardon.
