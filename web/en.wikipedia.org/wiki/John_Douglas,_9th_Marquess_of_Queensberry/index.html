Born20 July 1844, Florence, Italy
Died31 January 1900, London, England
Known forQueensberry Rules of boxing; feud with Oscar Wilde

John Sholto Douglas, 9th Marquess of Queensberry (20 July 1844 – 31 January 1900) was a Scottish nobleman, remembered for his atheism, his outspoken views, and his role in the downfall of the writer Oscar Wilde.

In 1867 Queensberry endorsed a set of twelve rules for gloved boxing drafted by John Graham Chambers; published under his name as the "Queensberry Rules", they transformed boxing into the modern sport, introducing gloves, three-minute rounds and the ten-count.

Feud with Oscar Wilde

Queensberry objected violently to the relationship between Wilde and his son, Lord Alfred Douglas. In February 1895 he left a card at Wilde's club accusing him of posing as a sodomite. Wilde sued for libel, lost, and was himself prosecuted and imprisoned for gross indecency. Queensberry died in 1900, months before Wilde.
