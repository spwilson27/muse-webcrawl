MacCarthy dynasty

The MacCarthy dynasty (Irish: Mac Cárthaigh) was a great Irish clan and royal dynasty originating in the southwest of Ireland, which for centuries ruled as Kings of Cashel and of Desmond. The family claimed descent from the Eóganachta, the ruling kindred of Munster in the early middle ages, and took its name from Carthach mac Saorbhreathach (died 1045), king of Eóganacht Chaisil.

Origins and branches

After the family's power base at Cashel was displaced, the MacCarthys moved southwest into what became the kingdom of Desmond (South Munster). The dynasty divided into several major branches:

  • MacCarthy Mór — the senior line, Kings and later Princes of Desmond, seated in County Kerry.
  • MacCarthy Reagh — Lords of Carbery in west County Cork.
  • MacCarthy of Muskerry — centred on Blarney Castle in County Cork, whose Cormac MacCarthy built much of the castle famed for the Blarney Stone.
  • MacCarthy MacDonogh — Lords of Duhallow.

History

The MacCarthys resisted the Norman invasion of Ireland, winning a notable victory at the Battle of Callann in 1261, where Fínghin Mac Cárthaigh killed John FitzGerald. For centuries they alternately warred and allied with the Anglo-Norman FitzGeralds, Earls of Desmond. In the Tudor reconquest the MacCarthy Mór title was surrendered and regranted as Earl of Clancare, but the dynasty's power declined after the Desmond Rebellions and the Nine Years' War. Donal MacCarthy, Earl of Clancarty, was a leading Jacobite commander and was attainted after the Williamite war, ending the family's great days in Ireland; many MacCarthys entered continental service in the "Wild Geese", notably in France and Spain.
