The Hôtel de la Marine (French pronunciation: [otɛl də la maʁin]) is an eighteenth-century building on the east side of the Place de la Concorde in Paris. Designed by the architect Ange-Jacques Gabriel, it was built between 1757 and 1774 as one of two palaces framing the square, and originally housed the Garde-Meuble de la Couronne, the institution responsible for the French royal furniture collection. Its twin across the square is the Hôtel de Crillon.

History

From 1774 the building displayed the Crown's furniture, tapestries and the French Crown Jewels to the public on certain days — an early form of museum. In September 1792, during the Revolution, thieves famously stole most of the Crown Jewels from the building, including the Regent and Sancy diamonds (later recovered in part).

In 1789 the Ministry of the Navy moved in, giving the building its present name; it remained the headquarters of the French naval staff for more than two centuries, until 2015, when the ministry relocated to the Hexagone Balard. During the Revolution, Marie Antoinette's secretary also lodged here, and on 16 February 1793 the building was the scene of events surrounding the monarchy's fall; it was from the square in front (then Place de la Révolution) that Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette were guillotined.

Museum

After an extensive restoration by the Centre des monuments nationaux, the Hôtel de la Marine opened to the public on 12 June 2021. Visitors can tour the restored eighteenth-century apartments of the Garde-Meuble's intendant, the nineteenth-century state salons of the Navy ministry, and the loggia overlooking the Place de la Concorde. Since November 2021 the building also hosts the Collection Al Thani, a major private collection of art and antiquities exhibited under an agreement with the French state.

Architecture

The building is a leading example of French neoclassicism: a façade of Corinthian columns and a pediment facing the square, arranged around a courtyard. Its interiors include the celebrated Salons d'Honneur and the Galerie Dorée.
