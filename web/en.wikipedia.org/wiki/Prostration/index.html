Prostration

Prostration during Islamic prayer (sujud).

Prostration is the placement of the body in a reverentially or submissively prone position as a gesture. Typically prostration is distinguished from the lesser acts of bowing or kneeling by involving a part of the body above the knee touching the ground, especially the hands and forehead.

In religion

Many world religions employ prostration as an act of submission to a deity or teacher. In Islam, prostration (sujud) is a central part of the daily prayers (salah), with the forehead, nose, hands, knees and toes touching the ground. In Buddhism, pilgrims and devotees perform full-length prostrations before images and stupas, sometimes accumulating hundreds of thousands of repetitions as a practice. In Eastern Orthodox Christianity, prostrations (metanoia) accompany penitential prayer, especially in Lent, and in Roman Catholic and Eastern rites ordinands prostrate themselves during ordination liturgies.

In secular custom

Historically, prostration signified submission to rulers: the Chinese kowtow required kneeling and knocking the forehead on the ground before the emperor, and Persian proskynesis before the king scandalised the Greeks of Alexander the Great's court, for whom the gesture was reserved for gods.

Medical usage

"Prostration" is also an older medical term for extreme physical weakness and collapse, as in "nervous prostration" or heat prostration, in which the patient is laid low and unable to stand.
