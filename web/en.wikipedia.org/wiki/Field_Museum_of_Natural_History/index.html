Field Museum of Natural History

Field Museum
Location: 1400 S. Lake Shore Drive, Chicago, Illinois
Established: 1893
Collections: ~40 million specimens and objects
Named for: Marshall Field

The Field Museum of Natural History, commonly known as the Field Museum, is a natural history museum in Chicago, Illinois, and one of the largest such museums in the world. Located on Lake Shore Drive in the Near South Side, it forms part of the Museum Campus with the Shedd Aquarium and Adler Planetarium. Its collections contain tens of millions of specimens across anthropology, botany, geology, and zoology, and it maintains an active research programme with more than a hundred scientists.

History

The museum was founded in 1893 as the Columbian Museum of Chicago, drawing on the exhibits of the World's Columbian Exposition. Its principal benefactor was the department-store magnate Marshall Field, who gave a million dollars to establish it and more in his will; the museum was renamed for him in 1905. In 1921 it moved into its present neoclassical building in Grant Park.

Collections and exhibitions

The museum is best known for Sue, the largest and most complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton ever found, acquired at auction in 1997 for $8.4 million and placed in its own gallery in 2018 alongside "Máximo", a cast of the giant titanosaur Patagotitan. Other celebrated holdings include the man-eating Lions of Tsavo, the Inside Ancient Egypt exhibition, the Grainger Hall of Gems, and the Evolving Planet gallery on the history of life. Its anthropology collections are especially strong in material from the Pacific, the Americas, and ancient Egypt.

Research

Beyond its public galleries, the Field Museum is a research institution whose staff conduct fieldwork worldwide in systematics, evolutionary biology, archaeology, and conservation. Its herbarium and zoological collections rank among the most important in North America.
