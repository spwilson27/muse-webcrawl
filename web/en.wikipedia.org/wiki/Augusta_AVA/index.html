The Augusta AVA is an American Viticultural Area located around the town of Augusta, Missouri, in St. Charles County. It was the first AVA established in the United States, designated on 20 June 1980—eight months before Napa Valley received its own AVA status in February 1981.

History

German immigrants settled the area in the 1830s and 1840s and planted vineyards on the south-facing bluffs above the Missouri River, where the river moderated the climate and the rocky soils suited wine grapes. By the mid-19th century the region was a significant wine producer, and Missouri became one of the largest wine-producing states before Prohibition devastated the industry in 1920. Revival began in the 1960s with the restoration of historic wineries such as Mount Pleasant, founded in 1859.

Geography and climate

The viticultural area covers about 15 square miles along the Missouri River. Its distinctive feature is the flood-plain and bluff topography: vineyards sit on terraces and hillsides of alluvial and loess soils above the river, with good air drainage that reduces frost risk. The area lies within the much larger Ozark Mountain AVA, designated in 1987.

Wine production

Wineries in the AVA specialise in French-American hybrid varieties and the native Norton grape (Missouri's state grape), alongside vinifera in favourable sites. The district is a popular day-trip destination from St. Louis and forms part of the Missouri Rhineland wine country along Highway 94.
