Barbara Hepworth

Born10 January 1903, Wakefield, Yorkshire, England
Died20 May 1975, St Ives, Cornwall
MovementModernism, abstract sculpture
Known forDirect carving; pierced forms in bronze and stone
HonoursDame Commander of the Order of the British Empire (1965)

Dame Jocelyn Barbara Hepworth (10 January 1903 – 20 May 1975) was an English artist and sculptor, one of the most important figures of British modernism and a leading pioneer of abstract sculpture. Her work exemplifies Modernism, particularly in the development of direct carving and the pierced form, and her influence was international.

Early life and training

Hepworth was born in Wakefield, Yorkshire, and, inspired by the landscapes of Yorkshire and a childhood encounter with Egyptian sculpture, studied at Leeds School of Art (where she met Henry Moore) and the Royal College of Art, London. A scholarship took her to Italy in 1924, where she learned marble carving and married the sculptor John Skeaping. Returning to London, she joined the circle of abstract artists around Ben Nicholson, whom she later married.

Career

In the 1930s Hepworth developed her signature vocabulary: smooth, sensuous abstract forms in wood and stone, pierced by holes—a breakthrough usually dated to Pierced Form (1932)—and enlivened with strings and colour. In 1939 she moved with Nicholson and their triplets to St Ives, Cornwall, where she lived for the rest of her life and helped make the town a centre of modern art. After the war she produced major public commissions, including Contrapuntal Forms for the Festival of Britain (1951) and the monumental Single Form (1964) at the United Nations in New York, a memorial to Dag Hammarskjöld.

Death and legacy

Hepworth died in a fire caused by a cigarette in her Trewyn Studio in St Ives in 1975. Her home and studio became the Barbara Hepworth Museum and Sculpture Garden, administered by Tate. The Hepworth Wakefield gallery, named in her honour, opened in her birthplace in 2011. She was created a Dame in 1965 and remains a defining figure of 20th-century sculpture.
