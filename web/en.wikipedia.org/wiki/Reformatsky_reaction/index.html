Reformatsky reaction

The Reformatsky reaction (sometimes spelled Reformatskii reaction) is an organic reaction which condenses aldehydes or ketones with α-halo esters using metallic zinc to form β-hydroxy-esters. It was discovered by the Russian chemist Sergey Reformatsky in 1887.

Mechanism

Zinc metal inserts into the carbon–halogen bond of the α-halo ester to form an organozinc compound — the Reformatsky reagent, a zinc enolate. This nucleophile adds to the carbonyl group of the aldehyde or ketone, and aqueous workup of the resulting alkoxide gives the β-hydroxy ester. Unlike Grignard reagents, the organozinc reagent is formed in the presence of the ester group because it is less reactive, so the reagent does not attack its own ester functionality.

Variations and scope

The reaction tolerates a range of functional groups and can be performed with aldehydes, ketones, and in variants with imines (giving β-amino esters) or nitriles (the Blaise reaction). Activated zinc (for example the Rieke zinc or acid-washed zinc with iodine or TMSCl initiation), sonication, and alternative metals such as samarium or indium improve yields. The related Reformatsky reaction performed intramolecularly is a route to ring formation.

Applications

The Reformatsky reaction has been used in natural product synthesis, including key steps in the synthesis of taxol intermediates and various macrolides, where its mild conditions and functional group tolerance are advantageous.
