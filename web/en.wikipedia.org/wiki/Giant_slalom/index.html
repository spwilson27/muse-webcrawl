Giant slalom

Giant slalom racing at the World Cup

Giant slalom (GS) is an alpine skiing and alpine snowboarding discipline. It involves skiing between sets of poles ("gates") spaced at a greater distance from each other than in slalom but less than in super-G. Giant slalom and slalom make up the "technical events" in alpine ski racing, as distinct from the "speed events" of super-G and downhill.

Course

A giant slalom course has a vertical drop of 250–450 m for men and 250–400 m for women, with gates set so that the skier must make sweeping, medium-radius turns at speeds typically between 20 and 50 km/h. Competition usually consists of two runs on different courses on the same day, with the winner decided by combined time.

History

The first giant slalom was set in 1935 on the Mottarone in Italy. It was added to the World Championships in 1950 and to the Winter Olympics in 1952. Equipment evolved towards shaped ("carving") skis in the 1990s, and regulations on ski length, sidecut and stance height have repeatedly been adjusted by the FIS to manage speed and injuries.
