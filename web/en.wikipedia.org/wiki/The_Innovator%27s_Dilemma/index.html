The Innovator's Dilemma

The Innovator's Dilemma: When New Technologies Cause Great Firms to Fail is a 1997 book by Harvard Business School professor Clayton Christensen. It introduced the influential theory of disruptive innovation, explaining how successful companies can lose market leadership by rationally ignoring low-end or emerging-market innovations.

AuthorClayton Christensen
Published1997 (Harvard Business School Press)
SubjectBusiness strategy, innovation

Argument

Christensen distinguishes sustaining technologies, which improve products along dimensions existing customers value, from disruptive technologies, which initially underperform but are cheaper, simpler or more convenient. Because established firms listen to their best customers and chase higher margins, they cede the low end to entrants, who then improve until they displace the incumbents. Christensen drew on case studies from the disk-drive, excavator and steel industries.

Influence and criticism

The book is considered one of the most influential business books of its era; Christensen later extended the theory in The Innovator's Solution (2003). Critics, including historian Jill Lepore, have questioned the theory's evidence and predictive power, and Christensen himself refined the model in response.
