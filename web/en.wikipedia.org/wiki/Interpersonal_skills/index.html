Interpersonal skills


Interpersonal skills underpin everyday communication and collaboration

Interpersonal skills, also known as people skills or social skills, are the skills a person uses to interact and communicate with others effectively. They include verbal and non-verbal communication, listening, empathy, negotiation, problem solving, teamwork, conflict resolution and assertiveness. Interpersonal skills are a category of "soft skills", as distinct from the technical or "hard" skills required for a particular job, and they are among the attributes most frequently sought by employers.

Components

  • Communication: expressing ideas clearly in speech and writing, and reading body language and tone.
  • Active listening: attending to a speaker, asking questions and paraphrasing to confirm understanding.
  • Empathy: recognising and responding appropriately to the emotions and perspectives of others; related to the broader concept of emotional intelligence.
  • Conflict resolution and negotiation: managing disagreements constructively and reaching mutually acceptable agreements.
  • Teamwork and leadership: cooperating towards shared goals, giving and receiving feedback, and motivating others.
  • Assertiveness: expressing one's own views and needs honestly while respecting others.

Development

Interpersonal skills develop from early childhood through socialisation, education and experience. Psychologists such as Daniel Goleman popularised the idea that they can be deliberately trained, and organisations invest heavily in communication and leadership training. Difficulties with interpersonal skills are a feature of some psychological conditions, and social skills training forms part of several therapeutic approaches — for example, the interpersonal effectiveness module of dialectical behaviour therapy.

Workplace significance

Surveys of employers consistently rank communication and teamwork among the most desired graduate attributes. Research in organisational psychology links supervisors' interpersonal skills to employee engagement and retention, and links poor interpersonal functioning to workplace conflict and stress. In healthcare, communication skills training is associated with better patient satisfaction and outcomes.
