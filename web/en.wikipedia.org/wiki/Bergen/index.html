Bergen is a city on Norway's southwestern coast, in Vestland county. It is Norway's second-largest city and is surrounded by mountains and fjords, including Sognefjord, the country's longest and deepest fjord.

History

Founded around 1070, Bergen was Norway's capital in the 13th century and a major trading port of the Hanseatic League. The German Hanseatic merchants' wharf, Bryggen, with its colourful wooden warehouses, is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Bergen remained Norway's largest city until the 1830s.

Geography and culture

Known as the "city of seven mountains", Bergen is famously rainy. It is a centre for aquaculture, shipping, offshore petroleum and tourism — the gateway to the fjords. Composer Edvard Grieg's home, Troldhaugen, and the annual Bergen International Festival are cultural landmarks.
