Allison Engine Company

Founded1915 (as Allison Engineering Company), Indianapolis, Indiana
FounderJames A. Allison
FateAcquired by Rolls-Royce plc in 1995; became Rolls-Royce North America
ProductsAircraft engines, gas turbines, transmissions

The Allison Engine Company was an American aircraft engine manufacturer based in Indianapolis, Indiana. Founded by James A. Allison — a co-founder of the Indianapolis Motor Speedway — as a racing-car and experimental engineering shop, it became a major supplier of military aero engines and later of gas turbines and turboprops, before its 1995 acquisition by Rolls-Royce.

History

General Motors purchased the company in 1929, developing it as the Allison Division. Its famous product was the V-1710, the only American-designed liquid-cooled V-12 to see large-scale service in the Second World War, powering the P-38 Lightning, P-39 Airacobra, P-40 Warhawk and early P-51 Mustangs; more than 70,000 were built. Postwar, Allison produced early US jet engines (the J33 and J35), then the T56 turboprop that powers the C-130 Hercules, and the Model 250 turboshaft used in countless helicopters. A sister division, Detroit Diesel Allison, built automatic transmissions for trucks and military vehicles.

Rolls-Royce era

After GM sold the business to a management buyout in 1993, Rolls-Royce acquired Allison in 1995 for $525 million, gaining a major US defence footprint; the Indianapolis operations continue as the core of Rolls-Royce North America, producing engines such as the AE 3007 and AE 2100.
