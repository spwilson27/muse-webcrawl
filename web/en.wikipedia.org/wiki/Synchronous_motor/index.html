Synchronous motor

A synchronous electric motor is an AC electric motor in which, at steady state, the rotation of the shaft is synchronized with the frequency of the supply current; the rotation period is exactly equal to an integer number of AC cycles. Synchronous motors contain electromagnets or permanent magnets on the rotor that create a field which turns in step with the rotating field of the stator.

Operation

The synchronous speed depends only on the supply frequency and the number of poles: ns = 120f/P rpm. Because the rotor locks to the stator field, speed does not vary with load (up to pull-out torque). Synchronous motors are not inherently self-starting and may use damper (amortisseur) windings, pony motors, or variable-frequency drives to start.

Uses

Large synchronous motors drive compressors, mills, and pumps where constant speed and high efficiency matter, and they can be run at leading power factor to correct a plant's power factor (as synchronous condensers). Small synchronous motors power clocks and timers. Permanent-magnet synchronous motors are widely used in electric vehicles and servo drives.
