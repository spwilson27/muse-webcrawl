Liturgy of Addai and Mari

The Liturgy of Addai and Mari (or the Holy Qurbana of Mar Addai and Mar Mari) is the eucharistic liturgy of the East Syriac Rite, used principally by the Church of the East, the Ancient Church of the East, the Assyrian Church of the East, the Chaldean Catholic Church, and the Syro-Malabar Catholic Church of India. Tradition attributes its composition to Addai (Thaddeus of Edessa) and Mari, disciples of the apostle Thomas, who evangelised Mesopotamia in the first century.

History

The anaphora of Addai and Mari is among the oldest eucharistic prayers in continuous use, with a core that many scholars date to the third century or earlier, originating in Edessa or elsewhere in Mesopotamia. Its archaic features include a structure centred on praise and thanksgiving, intercession, and the epiclesis, without the developed institution narrative found in most other anaphoras. The liturgy is celebrated in classical Syriac, with vernacular translations (Malayalam, Arabic, English, and others) used in modern practice.

Structure

The liturgy comprises a Liturgy of the Word — readings from the Old and New Testaments, psalmody, and a homily — and the Liturgy of the Eucharist proper: the preparation and presentation of the gifts, the signing of the altar, the anaphora with its prefatory dialogue, the breaking and commingling of the consecrated bread, the Lord's Prayer, and communion. Seasonal propers vary the hymns and prayers through the East Syriac liturgical year of Hudra cycles.

Absence of the institution narrative

The anaphora of Addai and Mari is famous for lacking a coherent, explicit recitation of the words of institution ("This is my body... This is my blood") found in other liturgies; the words are present only in a dispersed, allusive form within prayers of thanksgiving. This raised questions in the West about the validity of the sacrament when celebrated by the Assyrian Church of the East.

2001 recognition by the Holy See

In 2001, the Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity, with the approval of Pope John Paul II, issued guidelines admitting the Chaldean Catholic Church and the Assyrian Church of the East to eucharistic sharing, formally recognising the validity of the Anaphora of Addai and Mari. The decision held that the words of institution, though not recited as a single narrative, are present "in a dispersed euchological way" and that the anaphora's antiquity and orthodox faith guarantee its validity. The ruling is considered a landmark in ecumenical sacramental theology.

Other East Syriac anaphoras

Addai and Mari is the ordinary anaphora of the East Syriac tradition; two others, the Anaphora of Theodore of Mopsuestia and the Anaphora of Nestorius, are used on certain feasts of the year in some churches.
