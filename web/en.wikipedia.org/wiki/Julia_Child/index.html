Julia Child

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A tall, cheerful woman chef in a 1960s television kitchen with copper pans]

Julia Child (née McWilliams; 15 August 1912 – 13 August 2004) was an American chef, author and television personality recognised for bringing French cuisine to the American public with her debut cookbook Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961, with Simone Beck and Louisette Bertholle) and her television programmes, most notably The French Chef (1963–1973).

Born in Pasadena, California, Child joined the OSS during the Second World War and was posted to Ceylon and China, where she met her husband Paul. In Paris she studied at Le Cordon Bleu and taught cooking with Beck and Bertholle. Her WGBH programme made her a star: tall, warm, unflappable, dropping food and recovering with "if you're alone in the kitchen, who's going to know?" — she taught a generation that French cooking was a learnable craft, not a mystery.

1. Legacy

Legacy

Child's Cambridge, Massachusetts kitchen is preserved at the Smithsonian. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2003) and was the first woman inducted into the Culinary Institute of America's Hall of Fame. The film Julie & Julia (2009), with Meryl Streep's portrayal, renewed her fame.
