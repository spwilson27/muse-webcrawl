Goodbye to Berlin is a 1939 semi-autobiographical novel by Anglo-American writer Christopher Isherwood. Set in Berlin during the final years of the Weimar Republic (1930–1933), it comprises six loosely linked stories — among them "A Berlin Diary", "Sally Bowles", "On Ruegen Island", "The Nowaks" and "The Landauers" — narrated by a character named Christopher Isherwood, an English writer and teacher of English living in the city as the Nazi Party rises to power.

Content

The book opens with the famous passage "I am a camera with its shutter open, quite passive, recording, not thinking", establishing the narrator's detached, observational stance. Through his landlady Fräulein Schroeder, the cabaret singer Sally Bowles, the working-class Nowak family and the wealthy Jewish Landauers, Isherwood portrays the poverty, decadence and gathering menace of Berlin as democracy collapses. Together with his earlier novel Mr Norris Changes Trains (1935), it forms the Berlin Stories, later reissued in one volume.

Adaptations

The Berlin Stories were adapted by John Van Druten into the play I Am a Camera (1951), filmed in 1955, and then transformed into the Kander and Ebb musical Cabaret (1966) and Bob Fosse's celebrated film Cabaret (1972), starring Liza Minnelli as Sally Bowles.
