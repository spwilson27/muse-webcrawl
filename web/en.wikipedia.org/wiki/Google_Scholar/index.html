Google Scholar

Type of site: Bibliographic database
Owner: Google
Created by: Alex Verstak, Anurag Acharya
Launched: November 20, 2004
Current status: Active

Google Scholar is a freely accessible web search engine that indexes the full text or metadata of scholarly literature across an array of publishing formats and disciplines. Released in beta in November 2004, the Google Scholar index includes peer-reviewed online academic journals and books, conference papers, theses and dissertations, preprints, abstracts, technical reports, and other scholarly literature, including court opinions and patents.

Google Scholar uses a web crawler or web robot to identify files for inclusion in the search results. For content to be indexed in Google Scholar, it must meet certain specified criteria. An earlier statistical estimate published in PLOS ONE using the "capture-mark-recapture method" estimated approximately 160 million documents as of May 2014. A later estimate, made in 2017, placed the size at roughly 200 million articles, making it the largest academic search engine in the world.

History

Google Scholar arose out of a discussion between Alex Verstak and Anurag Acharya, both of whom were then working on building Google's main web index. Their goal was "to make the world's problem solvers 10% more efficient" by allowing easier and more accurate access to scientific knowledge. This goal is reflected in the Google Scholar motto, "Stand on the shoulders of giants," a quote adapted from Isaac Newton and displayed on the homepage.

Features and specifications

Google Scholar allows users to search for digital or physical copies of articles, whether online or in libraries. It indexes "full-text journal articles, technical reports, preprints, theses, books, and other documents, including selected Web pages that are deemed to be 'scholarly'." Results are ranked by relevance using an algorithm that weighs the full text of each article, the author, the publication, and how often and how recently the piece has been cited in other scholarly literature.

Key features include the "Cited by" link under each result, which lists subsequent articles that have cited the given work; author profiles, which aggregate an author's publications and citation metrics such as the h-index; alerts for new results; and "My library," which lets users save articles. In 2012, Google Scholar Citations profiles were made available to all scholars with a verified institutional affiliation.

Criticism

Google Scholar has been criticized for not vetting journals and for including predatory journals in its index, as well as for the opacity of its coverage and ranking. Because its inclusion criteria are automated, citation counts can be manipulated, a vulnerability demonstrated by researchers who uploaded fabricated papers that accumulated citations. Librarians and bibliometricians have also noted that, unlike curated databases such as Scopus and Web of Science, its coverage by discipline and language is uneven and difficult to determine.
