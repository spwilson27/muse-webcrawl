Lieutenant Governor of Maryland

Lieutenant Governor of Maryland
Term: Four years
Election: On a joint ticket with the Governor
Office established: 1864; re-established 1970

The Lieutenant Governor of Maryland is the second-highest executive officer of the U.S. state of Maryland. The officeholder is elected on a joint ticket with the Governor of Maryland and succeeds to the governorship if the office becomes vacant.

History

The office was first created by the Maryland Constitution of 1864 but abolished three years later. It was restored by a constitutional amendment ratified in 1970, and Blair Lee III became the first modern lieutenant governor in 1971, serving under Marvin Mandel — whom he briefly succeeded as acting governor. Unlike most states, Maryland's lieutenant governor has only the duties assigned by the governor, which have typically included portfolios such as business development, health, or transportation.

Notable officeholders

Later lieutenant governors have included Kathleen Kennedy Townsend, Michael Steele — the first African American elected to statewide office in Maryland — and Anthony Brown. By tradition the office is a stepping stone to higher office in Maryland politics.
