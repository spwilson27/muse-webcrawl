Spirit of St. Louis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Spirit of St. Louis monoplane with its name on the nose, photograph]
The Spirit of St. Louis.

The Spirit of St. Louis (Ryan NYP, registration NX-211) is the custom-built, single-engine monoplane flown by Charles Lindbergh on the first solo nonstop transatlantic flight in May 1927. Built by Ryan Aeronautical in San Diego in just 60 days, it was essentially a modified Ryan M-2 mailplane with an extended wing, a Wright Whirlwind J-5C engine, and fuel tanks placed ahead of the cockpit — leaving Lindbergh to see forward only by periscope. It now hangs in the Smithsonian\'s National Air and Space Museum.

Design

Designer Donald Hall enlarged the wing to 46 ft, added chord, and fitted five fuel tanks totalling 450 US gallons (1,705 litres); the main tank blocked the forward view. Construction used fabric over a steel-tube fuselage and wooden wing. Every gram was scrutinised: Lindbergh flew without a parachute, radio or night-flying equipment.

Career after 1927

Following the Paris flight and goodwill tours of the US and Latin America (including a famous flight to Mexico City), the Spirit was retired to the Smithsonian in 1928 after 489 flying hours. It is displayed suspended in the museum\'s Boeing Milestones of Flight Hall in Washington, DC.
