Personalized learning

Personalized learning
FieldEducational theory and practice
RelatedAdaptive learning, Blended learning

Personalized learning is an educational approach that aims to customize learning for each student's strengths, needs, skills, interests, and pace. It encompasses a variety of educational programs, instructional approaches, and academic-support strategies intended to address the distinct learning needs and aspirations of individual students, in contrast to uniform instruction delivered to a whole class at a single pace.

Approaches

Personalized learning typically combines several elements: learner profiles that record each student's strengths and goals; personal learning paths that sequence content according to demonstrated mastery; competency-based progression, in which students advance upon mastering material rather than with seat time; and flexible learning environments that vary time, place and grouping. Technology — including adaptive learning software and learning management systems — is frequently used to manage differentiation at scale, though advocates stress that personalization is a pedagogical model rather than a product.

History

Ideas behind personalized learning trace to early twentieth-century programs such as the Dalton Plan and to mastery learning models developed by Benjamin Bloom, whose research suggested that most students could achieve high levels of learning given appropriate instruction and time. The approach gained prominence in the 2010s as digital tools made individualized pacing more practical, and it was promoted by foundations and education reform organisations.

Criticism

Critics question the evidence base for large achievement effects, warn that screen-based implementations may narrow the curriculum or reduce teacher and peer interaction, and raise concerns about the collection of student data by commercial platforms.
