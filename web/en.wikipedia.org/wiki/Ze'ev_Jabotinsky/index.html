Ze'ev Jabotinsky

Ze'ev Jabotinsky (born Vladimir Yevgenyevich Zhabotinsky; 17 October 1880 – 3 August 1940) was a Russian-born Jewish writer, orator and Zionist leader. He founded the Zionist Revisionist movement, the youth movement Betar, and, during the First World War, the Jewish Legion of the British Army. He was the chief ideologue of what became Israel's right-wing nationalist tradition.

Early life

Jabotinsky was born in Odessa and raised in a middle-class, largely assimilated Jewish family. A gifted linguist and journalist, he worked as a foreign correspondent in Italy and wrote for major Russian newspapers. The Kishinev pogrom of 1903 turned him decisively toward Zionism and Jewish self-defence; he organised self-defence units in Odessa.

Jewish Legion and Palestine

During the First World War Jabotinsky campaigned successfully for the creation of Jewish units in the British Army, and served as an officer in the 38th Battalion of the Royal Fusiliers, which fought in the Jordan Valley. In 1920 he organised the defence of Jerusalem's Jewish community during the Nebi Musa riots and was imprisoned by the British in Acre fortress; his sentence was soon remitted after public protest.

Revisionist Zionism

Breaking with the mainstream Zionist leadership of Chaim Weizmann, Jabotinsky founded the Union of Zionists-Revisionists in 1925, demanding open mass Jewish immigration, a Jewish state on both banks of the Jordan, and the formation of a Jewish army. His 1923 essay "The Iron Wall" argued that Arab opposition to Zionism was natural and that settlement could proceed only behind a protective force until an accommodation became possible. He founded the Betar youth movement (1923) and later the New Zionist Organization (1935).

Death and legacy

Jabotinsky died of a heart attack in New York in 1940 while touring to promote a Jewish army to fight Nazi Germany. His remains were transferred to Jerusalem in 1964 and buried on Mount Herzl. His followers, including Menachem Begin, shaped the Herut and later Likud parties; he is remembered as one of the principal ideological founders of political Zionism's nationalist wing.
