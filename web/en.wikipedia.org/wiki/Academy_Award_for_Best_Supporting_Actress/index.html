The Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given in honor of an actress who has delivered an outstanding performance in a supporting role in a film released that year. The award is traditionally presented by the previous year's Best Supporting Actor winner.

History

The category was introduced at the 9th Academy Awards in 1937, when Gale Sondergaard won for Anthony Adverse. Initially, winners received a plaque rather than the Oscar statuette; a miniature statuette was presented instead during the Second World War years. Dianne Wiest and Shelley Winters have each won the award twice.

Selection

Nominees are chosen by the actors' branch of the Academy through nomination ballots, and the winner is selected by the full voting membership. Five performances are nominated each year.
