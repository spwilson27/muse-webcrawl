William the Lion

Born: c. 1142
Died: 4 December 1214, Stirling, Scotland
Reign: King of Scots, 1165–1214
Predecessor: Malcolm IV; Successor: Alexander II

William the Lion (c. 1142 – 4 December 1214), sometimes styled William I of Scotland, was King of Scots from 1165 to 1214, the second-longest reign in Scottish history before the Union of the Crowns. His reign saw significant developments in royal administration, burghs, and church, though it began with a disastrous intervention in England.

Accession and the revolt of 1173–74

William succeeded his brother Malcolm IV in 1165. Seeking to recover Northumbria, he joined the revolt of Henry II's sons in 1173 and invaded northern England, but was captured at Alnwick in 1174 after charging into the mist and being unhorsed.

Treaty of Falaise and Quitclaim

As Henry's prisoner, William accepted the Treaty of Falaise (1174), acknowledging Henry as his feudal overlord and surrendering key Scottish castles. Richard I, needing money for the Third Crusade, sold back Scotland's independence in the Quitclaim of Canterbury (1189) for 10,000 merks.

Later reign

William granted charters to many burghs, fostered the development of royal justice and coinage, and founded Arbroath Abbey (1178), dedicated to St Thomas Becket, where he was buried. He is associated with the red lion rampant that became the royal banner of Scotland, and was succeeded by his son Alexander II.
