Neutron economy

Neutron economy is the accounting of neutrons in a nuclear reactor or other fission system: how many neutrons are produced by fission, and how many are lost to absorption in non-fuel materials, leakage from the core, or capture without causing fission. Good neutron economy means a surplus of neutrons beyond those needed to sustain the chain reaction.

Significance

A high neutron economy allows a reactor to operate with low-enriched or natural uranium fuel, enables breeder reactors to convert fertile material (such as uranium-238 or thorium-232) into new fissile fuel, and lengthens fuel cycles. Heavy-water moderated reactors such as the CANDU are noted for excellent neutron economy because heavy water absorbs few neutrons.

Losses

Neutron losses include capture in the moderator, coolant, structural materials and fission-product poisons such as xenon-135, as well as leakage from the core, which is minimised by reflectors and larger core sizes.
