Transport coefficient

A transport coefficient, in physics and physical chemistry, is a proportionality constant relating a flux of some quantity (energy, mass, momentum, or charge) to the gradient of a driving potential, in the linear regime near equilibrium. Transport coefficients quantify how readily a system transports a quantity in response to an applied gradient.

Examples

  • Thermal conductivity κ — relates heat flux to a temperature gradient (Fourier's law).
  • Diffusion coefficient D — relates mass flux to a concentration gradient (Fick's law).
  • Viscosity η — relates momentum flux (shear stress) to a velocity gradient (Newton's law of viscosity).
  • Electrical conductivity σ — relates electric current density to an electric field (Ohm's law).

Theory

In statistical mechanics, transport coefficients can be computed from equilibrium fluctuations through the Green–Kubo relations, which express each coefficient as an integral of a time correlation function (a consequence of the fluctuation–dissipation theorem). The Onsager reciprocal relations state that the matrix of transport coefficients coupling different fluxes and forces is symmetric, a landmark result of irreversible thermodynamics. Transport coefficients can also be estimated from kinetic theory — for gases, from mean free path arguments — or from molecular dynamics simulation.
