Crossref

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crossref maintains metadata linking scholarly publications and their citations.

Crossref (formerly stylized as CrossRef) is a nonprofit, open infrastructure organization that provides scholarly metadata and services for the research community. It operates as a membership organization whose members include academic publishers, learned societies, universities, funders, and technology companies. Crossref is best known for assigning Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) to scholarly works and for maintaining a large open database of publication metadata that connects articles, books, conference papers, datasets, preprints, and other research outputs.

History

Crossref was founded in 2000 as the Crossref Initiative by a group of scholarly publishers who wanted a reliable way to link citations between online journal articles. At the time, links on the web frequently broke as publishers changed platforms or URLs. By registering a persistent identifier for each work together with a structured metadata record, Crossref made it possible for readers to follow a reference in one article to the cited work hosted by a different publisher.

The organization is based in Lynnfield, Massachusetts, in the United States, and is governed by a board elected from its membership. Over time its remit expanded beyond journal articles to include books, book chapters, conference proceedings, working papers, technical reports, dissertations, peer review reports, grants, and research datasets.

Services

Crossref's core service is DOI registration: members deposit metadata for each item they publish, and Crossref stores the record and resolves the DOI to the item's current location. Its metadata corpus, containing records for well over one hundred million works, is made available through open APIs and is widely used by discovery services, reference managers, citation indexes, libraries, and research evaluation tools.

Other services include Cited-by, which lets members see how works have been cited; Crossmark, which signals updates such as corrections and retractions; Similarity Check, a plagiarism-detection service powered by iThenticate text-matching software; and Event Data, which collects mentions of scholarly works from sources such as Wikipedia, blogs, and social media. Crossref also maintains the Funder Registry, a taxonomy of research funding organizations used to link publications to the grants that supported them.

Governance and membership

Crossref is a not-for-profit membership organization. Members pay annual fees and per-record registration fees, and in return agree to deposit metadata, link references, and display DOIs. Its board of directors is drawn from member organizations across the scholarly publishing ecosystem. Much of Crossref's technical infrastructure and metadata are openly available, reflecting its stated mission to make scholarly communications researchable, linked, and persistent.
