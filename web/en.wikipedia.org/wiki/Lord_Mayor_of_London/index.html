The Lord Mayor of London is the mayor of the City of London and the leader of the City of London Corporation. The Lord Mayor must be distinguished from the Mayor of London, who governs Greater London as a whole; the Lord Mayor's jurisdiction is only the City of London — the historic "Square Mile" — rather than all of modern London.

Role

Within the City, the Lord Mayor enjoys precedence over all but the sovereign, and presides over the Court of Common Council and the Court of Aldermen. The role is today largely ambassadorial: the Lord Mayor promotes London's financial and professional services industry around the world, making numerous overseas visits during the year of office. The Lord Mayor is also chief magistrate of the City, admiral of the Port of London, and rector of St Paul's School, among many ceremonial functions.

Election and term

The Lord Mayor is elected annually on Michaelmas (29 September) at Common Hall by the liverymen of the City's livery companies, from among the aldermen who have served as Sheriff of the City of London. The term begins in November with the "Silent Ceremony" at Guildhall, followed the next day by the Lord Mayor's Show, a public procession to the Royal Courts of Justice in Westminster, where the new Lord Mayor swears loyalty to the sovereign.

History

The office of Mayor of London dates to 1189, the first holder being Henry Fitz-Ailwin. The title "Lord Mayor" came into use after 1354. Notable holders include Richard "Dick" Whittington, who served three (in legend, four) terms in the late 14th and early 15th centuries. Since 2015 the office has used the name "Lord Mayor of London" in place of the older styling referencing the City alone, to emphasise the international role.

Residence and banquets

The official residence is Mansion House, completed in 1758. Each November the Lord Mayor hosts the Lord Mayor's Banquet, at which the Prime Minister traditionally delivers a major foreign-policy speech.
