Adyghe people

Image placeholder: Adyghe in traditional dress

The Adyghe (Adyghe: Адыгэхэр, Adyghekher) are a Circassian ethnic group native to Circassia in the North Caucasus. They speak Adyghe (West Circassian), a member of the Northwest Caucasian language family, and form the titular population of the Republic of Adygea in Russia, while large communities also live in Krasnodar Krai, Karachay-Cherkessia and the worldwide Circassian diaspora.

History

The Adyghe descend from the tribal confederations of historical Circassia, including groups such as the Abzakh, Bzhedug, Temirgoy, Shapsug, Hatuqwai and Natukhaj. Russia's conquest of the Caucasus culminated in 1864 with mass expulsion and emigration of Circassians to the Ottoman Empire — an event Circassians commemorate as a genocide and exile. Those who remained were concentrated in what became, in 1922, the Adyghe Autonomous Oblast.

Language and culture

Adyghe is written in a Cyrillic-based script in Russia and is co-official with Russian in Adygea. Traditional culture is governed by the Adyghe Khabze, a code of honour, hospitality and conduct. Distinctive elements include the cherkeska coat with cartridge pockets (gazyr), the papaha and bashlyk, the shashka sabre, polyphonic and dance music — including the lezginka-style dances — and a rich oral epic tradition, the Nart sagas, shared with neighbouring peoples.

Religion

Most Adyghe are Sunni Muslims of the Hanafi school, Islam having spread through Circassia between the 16th and 19th centuries; earlier layers of belief included indigenous religion and periods of Christianity.
