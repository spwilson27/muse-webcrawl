Flemish Giant rabbit
OriginFlanders, Belgium
WeightTypically 6.8–10 kg (15–22 lb) or more
UseMeat, fur, exhibition, pet

The Flemish Giant is a very large breed of domestic rabbit (Oryctolagus cuniculus domesticus) originating in Flanders, Belgium. It is one of the largest rabbit breeds in the world, and was historically bred for its fur and meat.

History

The breed developed from large stone rabbits raised around Ghent in the 16th and 17th centuries, possibly with input from Patagonian rabbits imported from Argentina. Flemish Giants were exported widely in the late 19th century, helping to improve the size of meat rabbit breeds in England and the United States, where the breed is recognised by the American Rabbit Breeders Association.

Characteristics

The Flemish Giant has a semi-arched body profile, heavy bone, and a broad head. Adults commonly weigh between 6.8 and 10 kilograms, and exceptional individuals exceed this. Several colour varieties are recognised, including sandy, fawn, black, blue, white, steel grey and light grey. Despite their size they are generally docile and are kept as show animals and pets; their size requires large housing and increases feed and veterinary costs.
