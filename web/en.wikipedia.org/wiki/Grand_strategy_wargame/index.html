Grand strategy wargame

A grand strategy wargame is a wargame that places the player in control of an entire nation or empire, including its military forces, economy, diplomacy, technology, and internal politics, usually over long historical periods and at a global or continental scale. The genre emphasises high-level decision-making — what to build, whom to fight, and how to allocate limited resources — over the tactical control of individual battles.

Characteristics

Grand strategy games typically feature a map divided into provinces or regions, real-time or turn-based play over decades or centuries of game time, and systems for diplomacy, trade, espionage, dynastic or ideological politics, and technological development. Victory may be defined by territorial expansion, prestige, survival to an end date, or scenario-specific objectives rather than total conquest.

History

The roots of the genre lie in board wargames such as Diplomacy (1959) and complex hex-and-counter designs of the 1970s. In video games it was popularised by Koei's Nobunaga's Ambition series and, above all, by the Swedish studio Paradox Interactive, whose titles — Europa Universalis, Crusader Kings, Hearts of Iron, Victoria, and Stellaris — define the modern form. Other notable examples include the Total War series (which combines a grand-strategy campaign map with real-time tactical battles), Civilization at the broadest scale, and Amplitude's Endless titles.
