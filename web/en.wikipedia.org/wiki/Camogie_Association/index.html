Camogie Association

The Camogie Association (Irish: Cumann Camógaíochta) is the national governing body for the sport of camogie in Ireland. Founded in 1904, it organises the game — the women's counterpart of hurling — at club, county and national level, and oversees the All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship.

History

Camogie was formalised in the early 1900s, with the first clubs formed in Dublin. The association held its first All-Ireland championship in 1932, won by Dublin. For much of its history the body operated independently of the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), though the two organisations cooperate closely and camogie finals are played at Croke Park.

Competitions

The association's flagship competition is the All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship, contested for the O'Duffy Cup. It also runs intermediate and junior championships, the National Camogie League, and underage competitions. Cork and Dublin are among the most successful counties in the sport's history.

Structure

The association is organised through county boards and provincial councils, with clubs across Ireland and among the Irish diaspora abroad. Its president serves a three-year term. In recent years there have been ongoing discussions about closer integration between the Camogie Association, the GAA and the Ladies' Gaelic Football Association.
