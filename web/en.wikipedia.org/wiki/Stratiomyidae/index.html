The Stratiomyidae, commonly called soldier flies, are a family of flies in the order Diptera, with more than 2,700 described species in over 380 genera worldwide. Many adults are brightly coloured or patterned and mimic wasps or bees, while others are metallic green or dark and inconspicuous.

Biology

Adult soldier flies are often found on flowers, where they feed on nectar and pollen. The larvae occupy diverse habitats: many are aquatic or live in wet soil, dung, decaying wood or compost, feeding on algae, detritus and microorganisms. The black soldier fly, Hermetia illucens, is widely used commercially to convert organic waste into larval biomass for animal feed and to manage manure.

Description

Adults are recognised by the characteristic venation of the wing, in which the veins are crowded toward the leading edge, and by antennae whose third segment is annulated. The family is placed in the infraorder Stratiomyomorpha, among the lower Brachycera.
