Yehudi Menuhin

Yehudi Menuhin, Baron Menuhin (22 April 1916 – 12 March 1999) was an American-born British violinist and conductor who spent most of his performing life in the United Kingdom. One of the greatest violinists of the 20th century, he was a child prodigy who matured into a musician celebrated for the warmth, spirituality, and humanism of his playing and his work in music education.

Early life and career

Born in New York to Lithuanian Jewish parents, Menuhin made his solo debut in San Francisco at age seven and his New York debut at ten. At thirteen he performed the Brahms, Beethoven, and Bach concertos in Berlin, prompting Albert Einstein's famous remark that he now believed there was a God in heaven. His early recordings, including Elgar's Violin Concerto under the composer's own direction in 1932, remain landmarks.

Later career and humanitarian work

During the Second World War Menuhin gave hundreds of concerts for Allied troops and concentration camp survivors. He championed Béla Bartók, who wrote his Solo Violin Sonata for him, and later embraced Indian music, recording with Ravi Shankar. He founded the Yehudi Menuhin School in Surrey in 1963 and the Menuhin Festival in Gstaad, Switzerland. Knighted by Britain and later made a life peer, he took British citizenship in 1985 and remained an advocate for music as a force for peace until his death while on tour in Berlin in 1999.
