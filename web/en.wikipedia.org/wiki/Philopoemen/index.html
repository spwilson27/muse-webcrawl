Philopoemen

Philopoemen
Born: 253 BC, Megalopolis, Arcadia
Died: 183 BC (aged about 70), Messene
Allegiance: Achaean League
Rank: Strategos (general) of the Achaean League, eight times
Battles: Sellasia (222 BC), Mantinea (207 BC), Tegea, Laconia campaigns

Philopoemen (Greek: Φιλοποίμην; 253–183 BC) was a skilled Greek general and statesman of the Achaean League, elected strategos (commander-in-chief) eight times. Under his leadership the League reached the height of its power, and a Roman writer later called him "the last of the Greeks".

Early career and reforms

Born at Megalopolis, Philopoemen distinguished himself at the Battle of Sellasia (222 BC). After the death of Aratus, he became the dominant figure in the League. He reformed the Achaean army on Macedonian lines, replacing light skirmishing equipment with heavier armour, the long sarissa pike and phalanx tactics, and trained the citizen infantry to fight in close order.

Wars with Sparta and expansion

Philopoemen defeated the Spartan tyrant Machanidas at Mantinea in 207 BC, where Machanidas was killed. He subsequently campaigned against Nabis of Sparta, and after Nabis's assassination he forced Sparta itself into the Achaean League in 192 BC, later punishing the city by abolishing the Lycurgan laws and exiling opponents. Under him the League came to control virtually the whole Peloponnese for the first time.

Death

In 183 BC, campaigning against rebellious Messene, the elderly Philopoemen was thrown from his horse, captured, and imprisoned. The Messenian leader Deinocrates compelled him to drink poison; he died asking whether his cavalry had escaped safely. His ashes were carried home in a grand funeral procession attended, according to Plutarch, by the historian Polybius carrying the urn. Plutarch's Life of Philopoemen is the main source for his career.
