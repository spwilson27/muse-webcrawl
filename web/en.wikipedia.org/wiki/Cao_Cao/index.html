Cao Cao (155 – 15 March 220), courtesy name Mengde, was a Chinese statesman, warlord, and poet who rose to power in the late Eastern Han dynasty and laid the foundation for the state of Cao Wei in the Three Kingdoms period. Though he served as the penultimate Imperial Chancellor of the Han dynasty and controlled the imperial court, he never formally usurped the throne; his son Cao Pi did so after his death, posthumously honouring him as Emperor Wu of Wei.

Rise to power

Cao Cao distinguished himself suppressing the Yellow Turban Rebellion and built a power base in Yan Province. In 196 he received Emperor Xian and moved the capital to Xu, allowing him to govern in the emperor's name. His victory over Yuan Shao at the Battle of Guandu (200) made him master of northern China. In 208 his southward campaign was defeated at the Battle of Red Cliffs by the allied forces of Sun Quan and Liu Bei, fixing the tripartite division of China.

Policies and legacy

Cao Cao promoted agriculture through military-agricultural colonies (tuntian), recruited talent regardless of birth, and valued ability over pedigree. He was also an accomplished poet; his sons Cao Pi and Cao Zhi were leading literary figures. Traditional historiography and the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms often portray him as a cunning villain, while modern reassessments emphasise his political and military genius. His tomb at Xigaoxue in Anyang was excavated in 2009 and confirmed as his in 2018.
