Emilio Aguinaldo

Emilio Aguinaldo y Famy (Spanish: [eˈmiljo aɣiˈnaldo]; March 22, 1869 – February 6, 1964) was a Filipino revolutionary, statesman, and military leader who is the youngest president of the Philippines (1899–1901) and became the first president of the Philippines and of an Asian constitutional republic. He led the Philippine forces first against Spain in the Philippine Revolution (1896–1898), then in the Spanish–American War (1898), and finally against the United States during the Philippine–American War (1899–1901).

Revolution against Spain

Aguinaldo joined the secret society Katipunan and became leader of its Magdalo chapter in Cavite. When the revolution broke out in 1896, his forces won notable victories at Imus and Binakayan-Dalahican. At the Tejeros Convention in March 1897 he was elected president of the revolutionary government in absentia, displacing Katipunan founder Andrés Bonifacio, who was later tried and executed by Aguinaldo's government — a lasting controversy. After the Battle of Mount Puray, Aguinaldo negotiated the Pact of Biak-na-Bato and went into exile in Hong Kong in December 1897.

First Philippine Republic

Returning aboard an American vessel in May 1898 during the Spanish–American War, Aguinaldo resumed command, proclaimed Philippine independence at Kawit on 12 June 1898, and presided over the Malolos Congress, which ratified the Malolos Constitution. He was inaugurated as President of the First Philippine Republic on 23 January 1899. War with the United States followed in February 1899; after a conventional phase and then guerrilla warfare, Aguinaldo was captured by US forces under Frederick Funston at Palanan, Isabela, on 23 March 1901, and swore allegiance to the United States on 1 April 1901.

Later life

Aguinaldo retired to private life in Kawit, later ran unsuccessfully for president of the Commonwealth in 1935 against Manuel L. Quezon, and served on the Council of State under President Elpidio Quirino. He died in Manila in 1964 at the age of 94. His ancestral home in Kawit, the Aguinaldo Shrine, is where independence was proclaimed.
