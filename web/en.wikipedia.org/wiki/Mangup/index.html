Mangup

Mangup (Ukrainian and Russian: Мангуп), also known as Mangup-Kale, is a historic mountain fortress and cave city in Crimea, located on a flat plateau about 300 metres above sea level in the Bakhchysarai Raion, southwest of Bakhchysarai. The site covers roughly 90 hectares and is the largest cave-city settlement in Crimea.

History

The plateau was settled from the early medieval period. Known in late antiquity as Doros, it became a stronghold of the Crimean Goths, who maintained a distinct Germanic-speaking Christian community in the region for centuries. In the later Middle Ages Mangup was the capital of the Principality of Theodoro, a small Byzantine successor state ruled by the Gabras family.

The Ottomans besieged and captured Mangup in 1475 after a long siege, ending the principality's independence, and held the fortress for about three centuries, garrisoning it (under the name Mangup-Kale) until the late 18th century. A Karaite community also lived at the site, and its Jewish cemetery survives on the plateau.

The site

Surviving remains include defensive walls across the plateau's narrow access points, the ruins of a large basilica, an octagonal church, a citadel, palace remains, and extensive cave complexes — dwellings, storerooms, casemates and tombs — cut into the cliff edges. The strong natural defences, with cliffs on most sides, made the plateau the chief fortress of southwestern Crimea for over a millennium.
