Craig Cameron Mello (born 18 October 1960) is an American biologist. He is the Blais University Chair in Molecular Medicine and co-director of the RNA Therapeutics Institute at the University of Massachusetts Chan Medical School. He shared the 2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Andrew Fire for the discovery of RNA interference.

Career and discovery

Mello earned his B.Sc. from Brown University in 1982 and his Ph.D. from Harvard in 1990. After a postdoctoral fellowship with James Priess in Seattle, he joined the faculty of the University of Massachusetts Medical School in 1994. His group studied embryonic development in the worm C. elegans, using RNA injection as a tool.

Experiments by Mello's group found puzzling results: both "sense" and "antisense" RNA injections silenced genes. Collaborating with Fire's team, they established in the celebrated 1998 paper in Nature that the active agent was double-stranded RNA, and that the silencing effect spread through the animal and into its offspring. Mello coined aspects of the experimental framework, and his laboratory went on to identify genes required for the RNAi machinery. Beyond the laboratory, RNA interference reshaped functional genomics and led to approved RNAi therapeutics, an application Mello has continued to champion through the RNA Therapeutics Institute.
