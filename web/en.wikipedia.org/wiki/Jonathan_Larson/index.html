Jonathan Larson

DiedJanuary 25, 1996 (aged 35), New York City, U.S.

Jonathan David Larson (February 4, 1960 – January 25, 1996) was an American composer, lyricist and playwright most famous for the rock musical Rent, which won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and the Tony Award for Best Musical. His work explored multiculturalism, addiction, AIDS and homophobia in 1990s New York.

Life and career

Larson grew up in White Plains, New York, and studied acting at Adelphi University on Long Island. Moving to New York, he supported himself for years waiting tables while writing musicals, mentored by Stephen Sondheim. His early works included Superbia, an adaptation of Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four, and the autobiographical rock monologue Tick, Tick... Boom! about turning thirty without success.

From 1991 he developed Rent, a modern retelling of Puccini's La bohème set among impoverished young artists in the East Village during the AIDS crisis. On the morning of January 25, 1996 — the day of Rent's first Off-Broadway preview — Larson died suddenly at home of an aortic dissection, later linked to undiagnosed Marfan syndrome. He never saw the production.

Legacy

Rent transferred to Broadway, ran for twelve years, and made Larson a posthumous recipient of the Pulitzer Prize and three Tony Awards. Tick, Tick... Boom! was later produced Off-Broadway and filmed in 2021, directed by Lin-Manuel Miranda with Andrew Garfield as Larson. The Jonathan Larson Performing Arts Foundation supports emerging musical theatre writers.
