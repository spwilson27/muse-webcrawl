Food loss and waste

ScaleRoughly one-third of food produced globally by weight (FAO estimates)
Related goalSDG 12.3 — halve retail/consumer food waste by 2030

Food loss and waste refers to food that is produced for human consumption but is not ultimately eaten. "Food loss" generally occurs earlier in the supply chain — during production, post-harvest handling, storage, processing and distribution — while "food waste" occurs at retail and consumption. The FAO has estimated that about 14% of the world's food is lost between harvest and retail, and UNEP estimates a further share — around 17–19% of food available to consumers — is wasted by households, food service and retailers.

Causes

In lower-income countries, losses stem largely from inadequate storage, cold chains and transport infrastructure, and from harvest timing. In higher-income countries, waste predominates at the consumer end: over-purchasing, confusion over date labels, cosmetic standards for produce, and large portion sizes all contribute.

Impacts and responses

Wasted food squanders the land, water and energy used to produce it and generates greenhouse gases — food loss and waste account for an estimated 8–10% of global emissions, with methane from landfill a notable part. Responses follow a hierarchy: prevention, redistribution to people (food banks, apps for surplus food), animal feed, industrial uses and composting or anaerobic digestion ahead of landfill. Sustainable Development Goal 12.3 targets halving consumer-level waste by 2030.
