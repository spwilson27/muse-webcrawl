Preston Robert Tisch

Preston Robert "Bob" Tisch
Born: 29 April 1926, New York City, U.S.
Died: 15 November 2005 (aged 79), New York City
Occupation: Businessman
Known for: Co-founder of Loews Corporation; co-owner of the New York Giants; Postmaster General

Preston Robert Tisch (April 29, 1926 – November 15, 2005) was an American businessman who, with his brother Laurence Tisch, co-founded the Loews Corporation. He served as United States Postmaster General from 1986 to 1988 and was a co-owner of the New York Giants football team.

Business career

Tisch was born in Bensonhurst, Brooklyn, and graduated from the University of Michigan in 1948. With his brother Laurence, he began by investing in hotels in New Jersey and Florida, then acquired the Loews Theatres chain in 1960, building it into Loews Corporation, a diversified conglomerate with interests in hotels, insurance (CNA), tobacco (Lorillard), energy, and watches (Bulova). Preston ran the hotel operations and was known as the more outward-facing of the brothers; he also led the campaign that brought the 1992 Democratic National Convention to New York and chaired the New York City Convention and Visitors Bureau.

Public service and sports

Appointed by President Ronald Reagan, Tisch served as Postmaster General from 1986 to 1988, bringing private-sector cost controls to the postal service. In 1991 he purchased a 50 percent stake in the New York Giants from the Mara family, becoming co-owner alongside Wellington Mara; the two owners died within weeks of each other in 2005. Tisch was a prominent philanthropist: New York University's Tisch School of the Arts, the Tisch Center for the Arts at the 92nd Street Y, and programs at the University of Michigan bear the family name.

Death

Tisch died of brain cancer at his Manhattan home on 15 November 2005, aged 79. His share of the Giants passed to his son, Steve Tisch, who became the team's chairman and executive vice president.
