Kościuszko Uprising

The Kościuszko Uprising, also known as the Polish Uprising of 1794, was an uprising against the Russian Empire and the Kingdom of Prussia led by Tadeusz Kościuszko in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Prussian partition in 1794. It was a failed attempt to liberate the Commonwealth from foreign influence after the Second Partition of Poland (1793) and the creation of the Targowica Confederation.

Background

The First Partition of Poland in 1772 and the adoption of the reforming Constitution of 3 May 1791 alarmed Poland's neighbours. In 1792, conservative Polish magnates formed the Targowica Confederation and invited Russian intervention; the ensuing Polish–Russian War ended in defeat for the reformers, and in 1793 Russia and Prussia carried out the Second Partition, stripping the Commonwealth of more than half its remaining territory. The reduced state was forced to accept a permanent Russian garrison and the humiliating Grodno Sejm ratified the partition under duress.

The uprising

On 24 March 1794, in the Main Square of Kraków, Tadeusz Kościuszko — a veteran of the American Revolutionary War — proclaimed an act of insurrection and assumed the powers of commander-in-chief of all Polish forces. He promised personal freedom to peasants who joined the fight and reductions in serfdom. On 4 April, his forces, including peasant volunteers armed with war scythes (the famous kosynierzy), defeated a Russian force at the Battle of Racławice. On 7 May, Kościuszko issued the Połaniec Manifesto, partially abolishing serfdom in Poland and granting peasants the protection of the government.

Warsaw rose against its Russian garrison on 17 April, and the city joined the uprising; Vilnius followed days later. However, the insurrection was defeated at Szczekociny (6 June) by combined Russian and Prussian forces. Kościuszko himself was wounded and captured at the Battle of Maciejowice on 10 October 1794, crying out, according to legend, Finis Poloniae ("Poland is ended").

Aftermath

In November 1794, Russian forces under Alexander Suvorov stormed Praga, the eastern suburb of Warsaw, massacring thousands of civilians. Warsaw capitulated, and the uprising collapsed. In 1795, Russia, Prussia and Austria carried out the Third Partition of Poland, erasing the Commonwealth from the map of Europe for 123 years. Kościuszko was imprisoned in the Peter and Paul Fortress in St Petersburg, then released in 1796 by Tsar Paul I.
