Midjourney is a generative artificial intelligence program and service created by the San Francisco–based independent research lab Midjourney, Inc. It generates images from natural language descriptions, called prompts, similar to OpenAI's DALL-E and Stable Diffusion. The program entered open beta on July 12, 2022, and was accessed primarily through a bot on the messaging platform Discord, with a dedicated web editor launched later.

History

Midjourney, Inc. was founded by David Holz, previously a co-founder of Leap Motion. The platform released successive model versions — V1 through V6 and beyond — each improving photorealism, prompt adherence and coherence of details such as hands. Founder David Holz has stated that the company is self-funded and profitable, and the team remained comparatively small (a few dozen employees) relative to its large user base.

Functionality

Users direct the Discord bot with commands such as /imagine, followed by a text prompt; the bot returns a grid of four images, which users can upscale or vary. Parameters appended to prompts control aspect ratio, stylisation and model version. A free trial was suspended in March 2023, which the company attributed to extraordinary demand and trial abuse; access thereafter required a subscription.

Reception and controversies

Midjourney images have won art competitions, most famously Jason M. Allen's Théâtre D'opéra Spatial, which took first place in the digital art category at the 2022 Colorado State Fair and sparked debate over AI-generated art. The service has been used in publishing, advertising and concept art, while critics raise concerns about training data drawn from artists' work without consent, and artists have brought copyright litigation against Midjourney and other image-generator developers. Midjourney-generated imagery has also featured in misinformation incidents, including viral fake images of public figures that prompted discussion of deepfakes and labelling.
