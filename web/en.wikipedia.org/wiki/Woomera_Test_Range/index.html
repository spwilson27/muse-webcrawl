The Woomera Test Range, officially the RAAF Woomera Range Complex, is a major Australian military and civil aerospace facility in the Far North region of South Australia. Covering about 122,000 square kilometres — roughly the size of England — it is one of the largest land-based test ranges in the world.

History

The range was established in 1947 as a joint British–Australian project for testing long-range weapons, particularly missiles, after the experience of German V-weapons during World War II. The name "Woomera" is an Aboriginal word for a spear-throwing device (atlatl). During the Cold War it supported programs including the Blue Streak missile, the Europa rocket launcher at Launch Area 6, and the WRESAT satellite in 1967, the first Australian satellite. It also hosted the Joint Defence Facility Nurrungar, a satellite ground station operated with the United States until 1999.

Operations

Today the range is managed by the Royal Australian Air Force's Air Warfare Centre. It is used for testing aircraft, missiles, unmanned aerial vehicles, and electronic warfare systems, and is available to allied nations and commercial customers. The prohibited area is guarded, with access controlled under the Defence Force Regulations; parts of the area overlap Aboriginal land and sacred sites, and native title issues, including those of the Maralinga Tjarutja people displaced by related nuclear test programs at Maralinga and Emu Field, remain sensitive.

Geography

The range lies in arid desert country northwest of Adelaide, centered on the town of Woomera, which was built to support the range and at its peak housed several thousand people; the population is now only a few hundred.
