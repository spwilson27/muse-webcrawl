Juan Pujol García

Born14 February 1912, Barcelona, Spain
Died10 October 1988, Caracas, Venezuela
CodenameGarbo (British); Arabel (German)
OccupationDouble agent

Juan Pujol García (1912–1988) was a Spanish double agent during the Second World War who worked for Britain's MI5 under the codename Garbo. He played a key role in Operation Fortitude, the deception that misled Germany about the location of the D-Day landings.

Background

Pujol developed a hatred of fascism during the Spanish Civil War. Determined to help Britain, he first offered his services to Germany, inventing a fictitious network of agents in Britain while actually operating from Lisbon. He fed the Abwehr fabricated reports assembled from guidebooks and magazines.

Work for MI5

Recruited by British intelligence in 1942, Pujol moved to London and, with his handler Tomás Harris, created an entirely fictional network of sub-agents. His messages were trusted so highly in Berlin that German intelligence relied on them in preference to other sources.

Operation Fortitude

In 1944 Pujol convinced the Germans that the main Allied invasion would strike the Pas-de-Calais rather than Normandy, and that the Normandy landings were a diversion. German forces were held back at a critical moment. He is believed to be the only person to have received both the Iron Cross from Germany and an MBE from Britain for his wartime service.

Later life

After the war Pujol faked his death and settled in Venezuela, where he ran a bookshop. He was rediscovered by researchers in the 1980s, visited the Normandy beaches in 1984, and died in Caracas in 1988.
