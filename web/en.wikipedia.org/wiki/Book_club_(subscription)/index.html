Book club (subscription)

A subscription book club is a method of selling books in which customers join a club and receive books by post on a regular basis, typically committing to purchase a minimum number of volumes in exchange for discounted introductory offers. Not to be confused with a reading group (also commonly called a book club), the subscription club is a commercial mail-order model that was a major channel of book distribution in the twentieth century.

How it works

Members traditionally received a monthly catalogue or bulletin describing a featured selection chosen by an editorial board. Under the widespread "negative option" system, the featured book was shipped automatically unless the member returned a form declining it by a deadline. Clubs recruited members with heavily discounted introductory bundles — several books for a nominal price — in return for a commitment to buy a set number of further books at club prices. Club editions were often printed specially for the club, sometimes on cheaper paper or in slightly smaller formats than trade editions, a fact of interest to collectors.

History

The model was pioneered in Germany by the Bertelsmann Lesering and in the United States by the Book-of-the-Month Club, founded in 1926 by Harry Scherman, which used a distinguished board of judges to select titles and reached millions of members. Similar clubs specialised in genres such as science fiction, mystery, military history, and children's books. The rise of discount booksellers, online retail, and e-books from the 1990s eroded the model, though curated subscription boxes and online clubs revived elements of it in the 2010s.
