Collecting

The hobby of collecting consists of acquiring, organising, cataloguing and displaying items based on a common theme or category. Collectors seek out objects such as stamps, coins, books, art, antiques, trading cards, dolls, comic books, wine, watches, militaria or natural specimens, motivated by interest in the subject, the pleasure of the hunt, investment, preservation, or social connection with other collectors.

Psychology and practice

Practitioners suggest collecting satisfies urges for mastery, completeness and order as well as nostalgia and aesthetic pleasure. Collections are built through shops, fairs, auctions, dealers and online marketplaces, and the condition and rarity of items usually determine their value. Many fields have their own vocabularies, catalogues and grading systems — philately and numismatics being among the oldest organised collecting pursuits — and terms for collectors of particular things (for example, a vexillologist collects flags; a deltiologist collects postcards).

History

Collecting is ancient: Renaissance cabinets of curiosities assembled natural and artificial wonders, and the great museum collections of Europe often began as private accumulations later bequeathed to the public. Mass production in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries created inexpensive collectables for a mass market, while some categories — fine art, rare books, classic cars — became significant alternative investments.
