Tigrinya (ትግርኛ, təgrəñña) is an Ethiopian Semitic language in the Afro-Asiatic family, spoken as a first language by the Tigrinya people of Eritrea and the Tigrayan people of northern Ethiopia. It is the most widely spoken language in Eritrea and one of the most widely spoken languages in Ethiopia. The language is sometimes referred to as Tigray in Ethiopia, after the Tigray Region.

History

Tigrinya developed from the North Ethiopian Semitic branch that also produced Ge'ez, the ancient language of the Aksumite Empire and still the liturgical language of the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo churches. The earliest known written example of Tigrinya is a text of local laws found in the district of Logo Sarda, Eritrea, dated to the 13th century. Modern written literature began in the 19th century, aided by missionaries and by printing presses in Asmara during the Italian colonial period in Eritrea.

Phonology

Tigrinya has a typical Ethiopian Semitic consonant inventory with a three-way contrast among voiced, voiceless, and ejective obstruents, plus the pharyngeals /ħ/ and /ʕ/. Its seven-vowel system includes the central vowel /ə/ (written with the first-order fidel). Gemination (consonant length) is phonemic and is important in verb morphology, though it is not indicated in the spelling.

Grammar

Tigrinya is a subject–object–verb language with postpositions. Nouns have masculine and feminine gender. The verb system is built on consonantal roots fitted into templates, with extensive use of prefixes and suffixes marking subject agreement, tense-aspect, mood, and object pronouns. A distinctive feature is the system of converbs (gerundive forms) used to chain clauses. Negation is expressed with a prefix and, in many forms, a suffix -n.

Writing system

Tigrinya uses the Ge'ez script, an abugida of more than thirty base characters, each with seven "orders" reflecting the following vowel. Unlike its ancestor Ge'ez, modern Tigrinya orthography is largely phonemic. Punctuation borrowed from European practice is common alongside traditional marks.

Status and use

In Eritrea, Tigrinya is one of the country's working languages and the principal language of education, media, and government in the highlands. In Ethiopia it is the working language of the Tigray Region and is spoken by diaspora communities worldwide, particularly in Israel, Sudan, Europe, and North America.
