Multinomial probit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multinomial probit models a choice among several alternatives with correlated unobserved utility.

In statistics and econometrics, multinomial probit is a model for discrete choice among more than two alternatives, generalizing the binary probit model. It belongs to the random utility family of models: a decision-maker chooses the alternative that yields them the highest utility, where utility is the sum of a systematic part (a function of observed characteristics) and a random error term.

Specification

The distinguishing feature of multinomial probit is that the vector of error terms across alternatives is assumed to follow a multivariate normal distribution with a (potentially) full covariance matrix. This allows the unobserved components of utility to be correlated across alternatives, so the model does not impose the independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) property that constrains the simpler multinomial logit model, in which adding or modifying one alternative draws market share proportionally from all others. In the classic "red bus / blue bus" example, probit can capture the fact that two similar options are close substitutes.

Estimation

The choice probabilities in multinomial probit are integrals of the multivariate normal density over regions defined by utility differences; beyond a few alternatives these integrals have no closed form. Estimation therefore historically lagged multinomial logit, despite probit's theoretical appeal dating to Thurstone (1927) and Marschak. Modern practice uses simulation-based maximum likelihood, especially the GHK (Geweke–Hajivassiliou–Keane) simulator, or Bayesian methods using Gibbs sampling and data augmentation (Albert and Chib, 1993), which make models with many alternatives and flexible substitution patterns routine.

Applications

Multinomial probit is widely used in transportation research (mode choice), marketing (brand choice), labour economics (occupation choice), and political science (voting among multiple candidates), wherever alternatives differ in similarity and IIA is implausible.
