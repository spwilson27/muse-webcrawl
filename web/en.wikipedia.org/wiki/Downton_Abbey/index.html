Downton Abbey is a British historical drama television series set in the early twentieth century, created and largely written by Julian Fellowes. First broadcast on ITV in the United Kingdom on 26 September 2010, the series follows the lives of the aristocratic Crawley family and their domestic servants at Downton Abbey, a fictional country estate in Yorkshire, between 1912 and 1925.

Premise

The series opens with the sinking of the RMS Titanic in April 1912, which throws the future of the estate into doubt: the heir presumptive to the Earl of Grantham is lost, and the entail means the title and estate must pass to a distant cousin, the middle-class solicitor Matthew Crawley. Over six series the drama weaves upstairs and downstairs storylines through the First World War, the Spanish influenza pandemic, the Irish War of Independence and the changing social order of the 1920s.

Cast

The ensemble includes Hugh Bonneville as Robert Crawley, Earl of Grantham; Elizabeth McGovern as his American wife Cora; Michelle Dockery, Laura Carmichael and Jessica Brown Findlay as their daughters Mary, Edith and Sybil; Dan Stevens as Matthew Crawley; Penelope Wilton as Isobel Crawley; and Maggie Smith in a widely acclaimed performance as Violet Crawley, the Dowager Countess. The servants' hall is led by Jim Carter as the butler Carson, Phyllis Logan as the housekeeper Mrs Hughes and Brendan Coyle and Joanne Froggatt as Bates and Anna.

Production and reception

The series was produced by Carnival Films and filmed principally at Highclere Castle in Hampshire, with servants' quarters built at Ealing Studios. It became a global phenomenon, airing in more than 200 countries and winning numerous awards, including Golden Globes and Primetime Emmy Awards; Maggie Smith won three Emmys for her role. It remains one of the most widely watched British costume dramas.

Films

A feature film continuing the story was released in 2019, followed by Downton Abbey: A New Era in 2022 and a third film, Downton Abbey: The Grand Finale, in 2025.
