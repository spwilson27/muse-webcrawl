List of U.S. states by minimum wage

This is a list of U.S. states by minimum wage. The federal minimum wage in the United States has been $7.25 per hour since July 24, 2009, under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Many states and localities set higher rates; where a state has no law or a lower rate, the federal rate applies to most workers.

Overview

As of the mid-2020s, 30 states and the District of Columbia have minimum wages above the federal level. Washington, California, and parts of New York and Massachusetts are among the highest, exceeding $16 per hour, while several states in the South and Midwest retain the federal rate of $7.25. Some states index their wage to inflation through automatic cost-of-living adjustments, and a number of cities and counties — including Seattle, San Francisco, and New York City — set still higher local rates.

History

The first federal minimum wage was set at 25 cents per hour in 1938. Congress raised it periodically through the twentieth century, reaching $5.15 in 1997 and $7.25 in 2009 after a three-step increase enacted in 2007. State action accelerated in the 2010s, with ballot measures and legislation in many states scheduling increases toward $15 per hour.
