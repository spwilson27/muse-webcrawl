Andreas Hofer (22 November 1767 – 20 February 1810) was a Tyrolean innkeeper and patriot who in 1809 led the Tyrolean Rebellion against the French and Bavarian occupiers during the War of the Fifth Coalition. He remains the national hero of Tyrol in Austria and Italy.

Background

Hofer was born at St. Leonhard in the Passeier valley and inherited the Sandhof inn at St. Leonhard — hence his nickname, the Sandwirt ("innkeeper of the Sand"). A cattle and wine trader who had served in the Tyrolean militia sharpshooters, he was a prosperous, deeply Catholic and conservative figure.

The rebellion of 1809

Tyrol had been ceded by Austria to Napoleon's ally Bavaria in 1805, and Bavarian religious and economic reforms provoked strong resistance. When Austria went to war in April 1809, Hofer led the Tyrolean militia in a rising that liberated Innsbruck after the first Battle of Bergisel. Tyrolean forces won further Bergisel battles in May and August 1809, and Hofer governed Tyrol from Innsbruck in the Emperor's name.

Austria's defeat at Wagram and the Treaty of Schönbrunn abandoned Tyrol, but Hofer fought on until his forces were crushed at the fourth Battle of Bergisel on 1 November 1809. Betrayed and captured in January 1810, he was court-martialled and shot at Mantua on 20 February 1810, reportedly refusing a blindfold and giving the order to fire himself.

Legacy

Hofer's remains were returned to Innsbruck in 1823 and rest in the Hofkirche near the tomb of Maximilian I. The song "Zu Mantua in Banden", telling of his death, is the anthem of the Austrian state of Tyrol.
