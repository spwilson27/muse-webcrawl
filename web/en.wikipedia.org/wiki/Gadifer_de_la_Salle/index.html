Gadifer de la Salle

Gadifer de la Salle (c. 1355 – c. 1422) was a French knight and crusader of Gascon origin who, with Jean de Béthencourt, undertook the conquest and settlement of the Canary Islands beginning in 1402, the first European conquest of Atlantic islands off Africa.

La Salle and Béthencourt's expeditions opened the European colonisation of the Canaries.

Life

La Salle served in the armies of France during the Hundred Years' War and took part in crusading expeditions, including the 1390 Barbary Crusade against Mahdia. In 1402 he joined Béthencourt's expedition, sailing from La Rochelle to the Canary Islands, where they established a foothold on Lanzarote and built the fortress of Rubicón.

Conquest of the Canaries

While Béthencourt returned to Europe to seek men, arms, and supplies from the Crown of Castile, La Salle directed operations in the islands, attempting the conquest of Fuerteventura and El Hierro. The chronicle of the expedition, Le Canarien, records their deeds — though its surviving versions reflect the later quarrel between the two leaders, each claiming the greater share of honour and rights.

Feeling wronged by Béthencourt's settlement with Castile and the distribution of lordships, La Salle returned to France to plead his case, without success. He spent his later years in Gascony and died around 1422.
