Joseph von Fraunhofer

Joseph Ritter von Fraunhofer (6 March 1787 – 7 June 1826) was a German physicist and optical lens manufacturer. He made optical glass and achromatic telescope objectives, invented the spectroscope, and discovered and mapped the dark absorption lines in the Sun's spectrum now known as Fraunhofer lines. His diffraction gratings laid foundations for spectroscopy and astrophysics.

Life

Orphaned young and apprenticed to a glassmaker in Munich, Fraunhofer was rescued from a collapsed workshop in 1801, an event that brought him patronage. He joined the Utzschneider optical institute at Benediktbeuern, becoming its director, and produced the finest telescopes of the age, including the Dorpat Great Refractor used by Friedrich Georg Wilhelm Struve.

Discoveries

In 1814 Fraunhofer mapped some 570 dark lines in the solar spectrum, designating the strongest with letters A–K still in use. He measured wavelengths with diffraction gratings he ruled himself, studied spectra of stars and flames, and improved optical glass homogeneity. He died of tuberculosis at 39, ennobled and honoured by Munich. The Fraunhofer Society (Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft), Europe's largest applied research organisation, bears his name.
