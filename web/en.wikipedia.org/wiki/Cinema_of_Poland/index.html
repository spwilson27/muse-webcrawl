Cinema of Poland

The cinema of Poland is the film industry and art of filmmaking in Poland. Although interrupted by war and censorship, Polish cinema has produced internationally acclaimed directors and films, with a history stretching back to the first screenings in Warsaw in 1896 and the first Polish feature, Niewolnica zmysłów (1914).

History

The interwar period established a popular commercial cinema. After World War II the industry was nationalised, and the post-war years produced masterpieces including The Last Stage (1948) and the "Polish Film School" movement of the 1950s–60s, centred on directors Andrzej Wajda (Ashes and Diamonds, 1958) and Andrzej Munk. The 1970s "cinema of moral anxiety" addressed the communist system through directors such as Krzysztof Zanussi, Agnieszka Holland and Krzysztof Kieślowski, whose Three Colours trilogy (1993–94) became a landmark of European cinema.

After 1989

Post-communist Poland developed a mixed model of state support (the Polish Film Institute, founded 2005) and private production. Recent international successes include Ida (2013, Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film), Cold War (2018), and the 2020 Oscar-winner The Zone of Interest, co-produced with the UK.

Key figures

Notable Polish directors include Jerzy Kawalerowicz, Roman Polanski, Jerzy Skolimowski, Wojciech Has, Paweł Pawlikowski and Małgorzata Szumowska.
