The National Football League (NFL) is a professional American football league in the United States, composed of 32 teams divided equally between the American Football Conference (AFC) and the National Football Conference (NFC). The NFL is one of the major professional sports leagues in North America and the highest professional level of American football in the world. It is the wealthiest professional sports league by revenue.

The league was formed in Canton, Ohio, in 1920 as the American Professional Football Association and took its current name in 1922. It merged with the rival American Football League in 1970, and the two leagues' champions met in the first Super Bowls from 1967. Teams play a 17-game regular season over 18 weeks, followed by single-elimination playoffs.

History and culture

The NFL's popularity surged from the 1960s on the back of television; the 1958 NFL Championship Game ("The Greatest Game Ever Played") is often credited with launching the modern era. Franchises such as the Green Bay Packers, Dallas Cowboys ("America's Team"), Pittsburgh Steelers and New England Patriots have defined successive eras. The annual college draft, Thanksgiving games and the Super Bowl halftime show are fixtures of American sporting culture. The Kansas City Chiefs of Patrick Mahomes won consecutive Super Bowls in 2023 and 2024 before losing Super Bowl LIX to Philadelphia.
