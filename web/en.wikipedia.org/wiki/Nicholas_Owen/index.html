Nicholas Owen

Bornc. 1562, Oxford, England
Died1/2 March 1606, Tower of London
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church (saint)

Saint Nicholas Owen (c. 1562 – 1 or 2 March 1606) was an English Jesuit lay brother who was the principal builder of priest holes during the reigns of Elizabeth I and James I, when Catholic priests were hunted in England. He was arrested after the Gunpowder Plot and died under torture in the Tower of London. He was canonised in 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales.

Life and work

A carpenter and mason by trade, Owen entered the service of the Jesuit priest Henry Garnet around 1588. For nearly two decades he travelled England constructing ingenious hiding places in country houses — concealed behind panelling, within chimneys, under floors and inside walls — which saved the lives of many priests.

Arrest and death

After the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, Owen surrendered himself at Hindlip Hall to protect others, hoping to pass as a servant. Recognised as Garnet's associate, he was taken to the Tower and racked repeatedly; he died of his injuries without revealing information. His feast day is 22 March.
