LED lamp

LED lamp
Type: Electric light using light-emitting diodes
Efficacy: up to ~200 lm/W
Lifespan: 25,000–50,000 hours typical

An LED lamp is an electric light that produces light using one or more light-emitting diodes (LEDs). LED lamps are far more energy-efficient and longer-lived than incandescent lamps and generally outperform fluorescent lamps.

Technology

Most white LED lamps combine blue LEDs with phosphors that convert part of the light to yellow, producing white illumination; driver electronics convert mains AC to the low-voltage DC the diodes need, and heat sinks manage thermal output. Lamps are made in standard bases (Edison screw, bayonet) as drop-in replacements.

Adoption

Falling prices in the 2010s, efficiency regulations, and incandescent phase-outs made LEDs the dominant lighting technology worldwide, cutting global electricity demand for lighting. Issues include colour quality (CRI), flicker and blue-light content. See also Incandescent light bulb.
