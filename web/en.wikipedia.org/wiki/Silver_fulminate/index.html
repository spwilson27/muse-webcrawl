Silver fulminate

Chemical formulaAgCNO
Molar mass149.89 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
ClassificationPrimary explosive

Silver fulminate (AgCNO) is the silver salt of fulminic acid. It is a highly sensitive primary explosive that has found use mainly in novelty fireworks, toy caps, and the small explosive charges known as "bang snaps" or throwdowns, in which a tiny quantity of the compound is wrapped in paper with grit so that impact or friction causes it to detonate with a sharp crack.

Properties

Silver fulminate forms white, needle-like crystals. It is isomeric with silver cyanate, a fact that played an important role in the nineteenth-century development of the concept of isomerism: Justus von Liebig's analysis of silver fulminate and Friedrich Wöhler's analysis of silver cyanate gave the same elemental composition despite the compounds' very different properties, prompting Jöns Jacob Berzelius to coin the term "isomerism".

The compound is extremely sensitive to shock, friction, heat, and static discharge. Even minute quantities can detonate violently, and larger amounts are dangerous to handle; it therefore cannot be used as a military or commercial blasting explosive. It is slightly soluble in cold water and can be recrystallised from hot water with care.

Preparation

Silver fulminate can be prepared by the reaction of silver nitrate dissolved in nitric acid with ethanol. The reaction must be carefully controlled, as the product may detonate during formation if conditions are not managed properly. It was first prepared in 1800 by Edward Charles Howard.

Uses

Historically, silver fulminate was used in percussion caps and early detonators, but its extreme sensitivity led to its replacement by mercury fulminate and later by lead azide and lead styphnate. Today its principal uses are in toy caps, Christmas crackers, and novelty "snap" fireworks, where the quantity involved is far below a milligram per item.
