Ilium (bone)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ilium (plural: ilia) is the uppermost and largest part of the hip bone, forming the prominence of the hips. Together with the ischium and the pubis it makes up the coxal (hip) bone of the pelvis. In adults the three bones are fused at the acetabulum, the cup-shaped socket that receives the head of the femur.

Structure

The ilium consists of a lower, thick body, which contributes about two-fifths of the acetabulum, and a broad, curved upper plate called the wing or ala. The wing's upper margin is the iliac crest, palpable along the waist and used as a clinical landmark (its highest point lies at the level of the L4 vertebra). Its inner surface bears the iliac fossa, while the rough posterior surface articulates with the sacrum at the sacroiliac joint. Prominent landmarks include the anterior and posterior superior iliac spines, attachment points for muscles and the inguinal ligament.

Function and clinical relevance

The ilium transmits weight from the vertebral column to the lower limbs and provides extensive attachment for the gluteal muscles, abdominal wall muscles and latissimus dorsi. The iliac crest is a common donor site for bone grafts and for diagnostic bone marrow biopsy and aspiration. Fractures of the ilium occur in high-energy pelvic trauma.
