Cayenne pepper

The cayenne pepper is a type of Capsicum annuum. It is usually a moderately hot chili pepper used to flavour dishes. Cayenne peppers are a group of tapering, 10 to 25 cm long, generally skinny, mostly red-coloured peppers, often with a curved tip and somewhat rippled skin, which hang from the bush as opposed to growing upright. Most varieties are generally rated at 30,000 to 50,000 Scoville units.

The fruits are generally dried and ground to make the powdered spice of the same name, though cayenne powder may be a blend of different types of peppers, quite often not containing cayenne peppers, and may or may not contain the seeds.

Etymology

The word cayenne is thought to be a corruption of the word quiínia (also sometimes spelled cayenne) of the Old Tupi language, once spoken in Brazil, which means pepper. It is probable that the place Cayenne in French Guiana was named after the peppers, and not vice versa, although it is commonly claimed that the pepper was named after the city.

Cultivation

Most cultivated varieties of cayenne pepper, Capsicum annuum, can be grown in a variety of locations and need around 100 days to mature. Peppers prefer moist but not waterlogged, nutrient-rich soil. Overwatering can cause fungal diseases and death of the plant. The peppers are picked while green or red, and are often dried for storage.

Culinary use

Cayenne pepper is used in cooking spicy dishes both as a powder and in its whole form. It is also used as an ingredient in hot sauces, notably those based on vinegar, such as Frank's RedHot and Tabasco-style sauces. Cayenne is a key spice in Cajun and Creole cooking, Korean gochugaru blends, Sichuan dishes, and many curries.
