Battle of Bash Abaran

The Battle of Bash Abaran was fought on 21–23 May 1918 between Armenian volunteer and regular units under Drastamat Kanayan ("Dro") and the invading Ottoman Army, near Bash Abaran (modern Aparan, Armenia), during the Caucasus Campaign of the First World War. Together with the battles of Sardarabad and Karakilisa, it halted the Ottoman advance into Eastern Armenia and made possible the proclamation of the First Republic of Armenia days later.

The Bash Abaran battlefield in the Aparan plain.

Background

Following the collapse of the Russian Caucasus front after the October Revolution, Ottoman forces advanced into the Armenian-populated provinces in the spring of 1918, driving refugees eastward. Armenian forces, hastily organised under the Armenian National Council, prepared defensive lines covering the approaches to Yerevan. At Bash Abaran, Drastamat Kanayan commanded roughly 1,000–1,500 men against a larger Ottoman force advancing from the direction of Alexandropol (Gyumri).

The battle

Fighting began on 21 May. Using the broken terrain and prepared positions, the Armenians repelled repeated attacks and counterattacked on 23 May, inflicting heavy losses and forcing the Ottomans to withdraw toward Hamamlu. Armenian casualties were comparatively light, and the victory protected the northern approach to Yerevan at the same time as the decisive stand at Sardarabad to the southwest.

Legacy

The Armenian victory at Bash Abaran is commemorated by a memorial complex near modern Aparan, erected in 1978 for the battle's sixtieth anniversary, which includes a monument to the fallen and a museum.
