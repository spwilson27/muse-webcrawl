European People's Party group

The European People's Party group (EPP Group) is a centre-right political group of the European Parliament consisting of deputies (MEPs) from the member parties of the European People's Party (EPP). It has been the largest political group in the European Parliament since 1999.

History

The group traces its origins to the Christian Democratic Group founded in the Common Assembly of the European Coal and Steel Community in 1953. In 1979, the year of the first direct elections to the European Parliament, it took the name European People's Party Group. For most of the Parliament's history it has alternated as the largest group with the Socialists, and it has held a plurality continuously since the 1999 European elections.

Between 2009 and 2021 the group also included MEPs from Hungary's Fidesz party, whose membership was suspended in 2019; the Fidesz delegation left the group in March 2021, shortly before the party's expulsion from the EPP itself. British Conservative MEPs sat in the group until 2009, when they left to form the European Conservatives and Reformists Group.

Organisation

The group is led by a chairperson elected by its members; Manfred Weber of Germany's Christian Social Union has chaired the group since 2014. Its bureau coordinates positions on legislation, and group members hold key posts across Parliament's committees. Following the 2024 European elections the group remained the largest, with roughly 188 of the 720 seats.
