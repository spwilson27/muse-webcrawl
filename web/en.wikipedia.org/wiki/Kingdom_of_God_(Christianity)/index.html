Kingdom of God (Christianity)

The Kingdom of God (Greek: βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ, basileia tou theou) is a central concept in Christianity, and the core of the teaching of Jesus according to the Synoptic Gospels. It denotes God's reign or sovereignty, both as a present spiritual reality and as a future eschatological hope. The phrase "kingdom of heaven", used in the Gospel of Matthew, is generally understood to be equivalent.

In the teaching of Jesus

Jesus begins his ministry in Mark's Gospel proclaiming: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news." Many of his parables — of the sower, the mustard seed, the leaven, the hidden treasure — describe the kingdom's paradoxical growth and hidden presence. The Lord's Prayer petitions "Thy kingdom come", and the Beatitudes promise the kingdom to the poor in spirit and the persecuted.

Interpretations

Interpretations differ among traditions and scholars. Eschatological readings stress a future, apocalyptic intervention of God; "realised eschatology" (associated with C. H. Dodd) holds that the kingdom arrived in Jesus's ministry, while the influential "already but not yet" view sees it as inaugurated but awaiting consummation at the second coming. Other interpretations identify the kingdom with the Church, with the inner spiritual life of believers ("the kingdom of God is within you", Luke 17:21), or with a social order of justice — a theme developed in liberation theology and in Ritschl's liberal Protestantism.

Related concepts

The concept continues the Old Testament theme of God as king of Israel and of the nations (as in the Psalms and Daniel) and Jewish expectations of a coming divine kingdom associated with the Messiah.
