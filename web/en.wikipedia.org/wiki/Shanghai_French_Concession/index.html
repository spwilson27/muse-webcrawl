The Shanghai French Concession (French: Concession française de Changhaï; Chinese: 上海法租界) was a foreign concession in Shanghai, China, administered by France from 1849 until 1943. Together with the British- and American-administered Shanghai International Settlement, it formed one of the treaty-port enclaves created after the First Opium War. Known for its leafy, plane-tree-lined avenues, elegant villas, cafés and cathedrals, the area long retained a distinct residential character and remains one of the city's most desirable historic districts.

History

The concession was established in 1849, when the French consul Charles de Montigny obtained an agreement from the Shanghai circuit intendant (taotai) granting France a tract of land between the British settlement to the north and the Chinese walled city to the south. It expanded westward in 1900 and again in 1914, ultimately covering much of present-day Xuhui and part of Huangpu district. Unlike the merged International Settlement, the French Concession remained under direct French administration through its municipal council and the French consul.

During the interwar years the concession became home to a large White Russian émigré community fleeing the Russian Revolution, especially along Avenue Joffre (now Huaihai Road), as well as to wealthy Chinese residents, gangsters of the Green Gang such as Du Yuesheng, and, clandestinely, early members of the Chinese Communist Party — the First Congress of the CCP was held in a shikumen house in the concession in July 1921.

In 1943, during the Second World War, the Vichy French government formally retroceded the concession to the Chinese government of Wang Jingwei, ending nearly a century of French administration, although practical transfer followed the war's end.

Character and legacy

The concession was famous for its strict building codes, which produced a wealth of Art Deco apartments, Tudor-style villas, and shikumen lane houses; for the French Municipal Council's plane trees, still a hallmark of the district; and for institutions such as the Aurora University, the Collège Saint-Ignace, and the Shanghai Conservatory of Music. Today the former concession area, centred on streets such as Wukang Road, Fuxing Road and Huaihai Road, is a protected heritage zone of historic residences, boutiques and cafés.
