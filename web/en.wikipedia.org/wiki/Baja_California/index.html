Baja California (Spanish for "Lower California") is a state in northwestern Mexico, occupying the northern half of the Baja California Peninsula. It is bordered to the north by the United States (California), to the east by the state of Sonora and the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortés), to the south by Baja California Sur, and to the west by the Pacific Ocean. Its capital is Mexicali, while Tijuana, on the border with San Diego, is its largest city.

History

The peninsula was inhabited for millennia by indigenous peoples including the Paipai, Kiliwa, Kumeyaay (Diegueño), and Cochimi. Spanish explorers reached the peninsula in the 1530s, long believing California to be an island. Jesuit missions, most famously founded from 1697 at Loreto in the south, spread through the peninsula during the 18th century. After the Mexican–American War (1848), the peninsula remained Mexican while Alta California was ceded to the United States. The northern territory of Baja California became Mexico's 29th state in 1952.

Geography

A mountainous spine, the Peninsular Ranges, runs down the state, culminating in Picacho del Diablo (3,096 m), the peninsula's highest peak. The east coast along the Gulf of California is hot and arid, while the Pacific side enjoys a Mediterranean climate in the northwest, supporting the Valle de Guadalupe wine country near Ensenada. The Mexicali Valley, irrigated by the Colorado River, is one of Mexico's most productive agricultural regions despite summer temperatures among the highest on Earth.

Economy

Baja California's economy is tightly integrated with the United States. Maquiladora assembly plants in Tijuana and Mexicali manufacture electronics, medical devices, and aerospace components for export. Tourism, wine production, fishing, and cross-border retail are also significant. The state's proximity to California makes it a major corridor for trade — and for migration and smuggling — along the Mexico–United States border.

Cities

Principal cities are Tijuana, Mexicali, Ensenada, Tecate, and Playas de Rosarito. The Tijuana–San Diego conurbation forms one of the largest cross-border metropolitan areas in the world.
