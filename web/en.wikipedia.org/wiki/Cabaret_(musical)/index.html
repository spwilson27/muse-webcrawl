Cabaret is a musical with music by John Kander, lyrics by Fred Ebb, and a book by Joe Masteroff. It is based on the 1951 play I Am a Camera by John Van Druten, itself adapted from Christopher Isherwood's semi-autobiographical 1939 novel Goodbye to Berlin, part of The Berlin Stories.

Set in 1929–1930 Berlin during the twilight of the Jazz Age as the Nazis are rising to power, the musical focuses on the hedonistic nightlife at the seedy Kit Kat Klub and revolves around young American writer Clifford Bradshaw and his relationship with English cabaret performer Sally Bowles. A subplot involves the doomed romance between German boarding-house owner Fräulein Schneider and her elderly suitor Herr Schultz, a Jewish fruit vendor. Overseeing the action is the Master of Ceremonies at the Kit Kat Klub, a figure who serves as a metaphor for the deteriorating state of Germany throughout the show.

Background

Producer and director Harold Prince acquired the rights to Van Druten's play and Isherwood's stories in 1963. Composer John Kander and lyricist Fred Ebb, together with book writer Joe Masteroff, reshaped the material: the action was moved partly into the cabaret itself, where the Emcee's numbers comment on the plot, an innovative device derived from the concept musical techniques Prince had admired in German theatre. The role of Sally, English in the stage version rather than American as in Isherwood's novel, was written with Jill Haworth in mind.

Productions

Original Broadway production (1966)

The original Broadway production, directed by Harold Prince and choreographed by Ron Field, opened on November 20, 1966, at the Broadhurst Theatre and transferred to the Imperial and Broadway theatres before closing on September 6, 1969, after 1,165 performances. The cast starred Joel Grey as the Emcee, Jill Haworth as Sally Bowles, Bert Convy as Cliff Bradshaw, Lotte Lenya as Fräulein Schneider and Jack Gilford as Herr Schultz. The production won eight Tony Awards, including Best Musical.

1968 London production

The West End production opened at the Palace Theatre on February 28, 1968, starring Judi Dench as Sally Bowles and Barry Dennen as the Emcee. It ran for 336 performances.

1987 revival

A Broadway revival, again directed by Prince and choreographed by Field, opened in 1987 with Joel Grey reprising his role as the Emcee, alongside Alyson Reed as Sally and Gregg Edelman as Cliff. It ran for 261 performances.

1998 revival

Sam Mendes directed a radically reimagined production, first at the Donmar Warehouse in London in 1993, which transferred to Broadway in 1998, co-directed and choreographed by Rob Marshall. Starring Alan Cumming as the Emcee and Natasha Richardson as Sally Bowles, it emphasised the decadence and seediness of the Klub, placed the audience at cabaret tables, and delved more frankly into Cliff's bisexuality and the Nazi threat. It won four Tony Awards, including Best Revival of a Musical, and ran for 2,377 performances, becoming one of the longest-running revivals in Broadway history.

2021 London revival and 2024 Broadway transfer

Rebecca Frecknall's production opened at the Playhouse Theatre in London in December 2021, transformed into an immersive "Kit Kat Club," starring Eddie Redmayne as the Emcee and Jessie Buckley as Sally. It won seven Olivier Awards, a record for a musical revival, and transferred to Broadway's August Wilson Theatre in 2024.

Film adaptation

The musical was adapted into the acclaimed 1972 film Cabaret, directed by Bob Fosse and starring Liza Minnelli, Michael York and Joel Grey, which substantially altered the plot and song list and won eight Academy Awards.

Themes and legacy

Cabaret is celebrated for its innovative structure — splitting the action between a naturalistic "book" world and the cabaret, whose songs comment metaphorically on the story — and for its chilling portrait of a society amusing itself while fascism takes hold. It is considered one of the landmarks of the concept musical and of American musical theatre.
