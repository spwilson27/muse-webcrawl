Mary Province (Turkmen: Mary welaýaty) is one of the five provinces (welaýatlar) of Turkmenistan. It is located in the south-east of the country, bordering Afghanistan to the south and Uzbekistan to the north-east. Its capital is the city of Mary, formerly known as Merv. The province covers an area of about 87,150 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 1.5 million.

Geography

Much of Mary Province is covered by the Karakum Desert, but the fertile Murghab River oasis supports extensive agriculture and settlement. The Karakum Canal, one of the largest irrigation canals in the world, crosses the province, bringing water from the Amu Darya to the oasis and beyond. The ancient oasis city of Merv, near modern Mary, was one of the great cities of the Islamic world and a major stop on the Silk Road; its remains are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy

The province is a major centre of Turkmenistan's natural gas industry. The Galkynysh Gas Field (formerly South Yolotan–Osman), near Ýolöten, is one of the largest gas fields in the world. Other industries include cotton ginning, food processing, and power generation at the Mary power station. Agriculture centres on cotton, grain, melons, and livestock, supported by irrigation from the Murghab and the Karakum Canal.

Districts

The province is divided into districts including Baýramaly, Ýolöten, Murgap, Sakarçäge, Tagtabazar, Türkmengala, and Wekilbazar, along with the cities of Mary and Baýramaly.
