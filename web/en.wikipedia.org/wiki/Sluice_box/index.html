Sluice box

A sluice box is a long, narrow channel with riffles in the bottom, used in placer mining to separate gold from gravel and sediment using flowing water. Material is shovelled in at the top; the current washes away light material while heavy gold particles sink and are trapped behind the riffles.

Sluice boxes were a staple of the California and Klondike gold rushes and remain in use by hobbyist prospectors and small-scale miners today. Modern versions use carpets, mats and expanded metal over riffles to improve recovery. Long industrial versions known as long toms and sluice runs processed large volumes of gravel in hydraulic mining.

Principle

The device exploits gold's high density — about 19 times that of water — so particles settle quickly in low-velocity zones created by the riffles while sand and gravel flush through. See also gold panning.
