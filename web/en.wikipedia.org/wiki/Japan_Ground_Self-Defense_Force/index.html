The Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF; Japanese: 陸上自衛隊, Rikujō Jieitai) is the land warfare branch of the Japan Self-Defense Forces. Established on 1 July 1954, it is the largest of the three service branches and is responsible for the ground defence of Japan, disaster relief, and contributions to international peacekeeping operations.

Organisation

The JGSDF is organised into five regional armies — the Northern, North Eastern, Eastern, Middle and Western Armies — together with central units including the Ground Component Command, the Amphibious Rapid Deployment Brigade (established in 2018 for the defence of Japan's remote islands), special forces and aviation units. Its headquarters is at Ichigaya in Tokyo alongside the Ministry of Defense.

History and role

Created during the Cold War primarily to counter a potential Soviet invasion of Hokkaido, the force has since reoriented towards the defence of Japan's southwestern islands. Because of Article 9 of the Japanese constitution, the Self-Defense Forces are maintained for self-defence only, though legislation enacted in 2015 permits limited collective self-defence. The JGSDF plays a prominent role in responding to earthquakes, tsunamis and other natural disasters, and has deployed abroad on peacekeeping missions in Cambodia, Iraq, South Sudan and elsewhere.
