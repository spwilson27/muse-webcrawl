Siegfried Line campaign

The Siegfried Line campaign was a phase in the Western European campaign of the Second World War, fought in the autumn and early winter of 1944 as the Western Allies advanced from France and Belgium to the German frontier and attempted to breach the Westwall — known in English as the Siegfried Line — Germany's system of frontier fortifications. The campaign, running roughly from September to mid-December 1944, encompassed the failed Operation Market Garden, the battles for Aachen and the Hürtgen Forest, the fighting in Lorraine and the Saar, and the clearance of the Scheldt estuary opening the port of Antwerp. It ended when Germany launched the Ardennes offensive (the Battle of the Bulge) on 16 December 1944.

Background

After the breakout from Normandy and the liberation of Paris and Brussels, Allied armies outran their supply lines in September 1944. Logistical exhaustion, the need to open Antwerp — whose approaches the Canadians cleared only in November — and stiffening German resistance combined to halt the advance at Germany's border. The Westwall, built in the 1930s and studded with bunkers and "dragon's teeth" anti-tank obstacles, had been partly stripped of weapons but was hastily re-manned by German reserve and fortress troops.

Major operations

Market Garden and the north

In September, Montgomery's Operation Market Garden attempted to seize Rhine crossings in the Netherlands by airborne assault; it failed at Arnhem, leaving a narrow salient. Canadian and British forces then fought the costly Battle of the Scheldt to clear the way to Antwerp, essential for Allied supply.

Aachen and the Hürtgen Forest

The US First Army encircled and captured Aachen in October 1944, the first major German city to fall to the Western Allies, after bitter urban fighting. South of Aachen, American divisions were drawn into the Battle of Hürtgen Forest, a grinding attritional struggle in dense woods and pillbox lines that caused very heavy casualties for little gain, while attempts to seize the Roer River dams were frustrated.

Lorraine and the Saar

Patton's Third Army fought through Lorraine, taking Metz after a prolonged siege of its forts, and advanced toward the Saar and the Westwall defences around Saarlautern, hampered by rain, mud and shortages of fuel and ammunition.

Outcome

By mid-December the Allies held a foothold in Germany but had not achieved a breakthrough; casualties in the autumn fighting were heavy on both sides, and both armies paused to rebuild. Germany used the respite to assemble the reserves that struck in the Ardennes on 16 December 1944, opening the last great German offensive in the west. The Siegfried Line was finally broken through in the offensives of February–March 1945.
