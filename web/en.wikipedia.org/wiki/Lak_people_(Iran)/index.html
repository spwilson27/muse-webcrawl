Lak people (Iran)

Lak people
Regions: Lorestan, Kermanshah, Ilam and Hamedan provinces, western Iran
Language: Laki
Religion: Predominantly Shia Islam; also Yarsanism (Ahl-e Haqq)
Related groups: Lurs, Kurds, other Iranian peoples

The Lak (Persian: لک) are an Iranian people living mainly in the Zagros Mountains of western Iran, particularly in the provinces of Lorestan, Kermanshah, Ilam and Hamedan. They speak Laki, a language whose classification — as a dialect of Southern Kurdish or as a distinct language closely related to Luri — remains debated among linguists.

History

The Lak historically practised transhumant pastoralism in the Zagros region and were organised in tribal confederations. During the Zand dynasty (1751–1794), founded by Karim Khan Zand, who was of Lak origin from the village of Pari near Malayer, the Lak rose to prominence in Iranian politics. Under later dynasties many Lak were forcibly settled, and tribal structures weakened during the centralisation campaigns of Reza Shah in the 1920s and 1930s.

Language and culture

Laki consists of several dialects and contains a substantial body of oral poetry and religious literature, including the sacred texts of the Yarsan (Ahl-e Haqq) faith, followed by a significant minority of Lak. The majority of Lak are Twelver Shia Muslims. Traditional Lak culture features distinctive dress, music and carpet weaving, and the Lak are often grouped culturally with their neighbours, the Lurs to the south and Kurds to the north, sharing elements of both.
