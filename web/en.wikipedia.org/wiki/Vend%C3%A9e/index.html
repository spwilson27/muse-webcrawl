Vendée (French: [vɑ̃de]) is a department in the Pays de la Loire region of western France, on the Atlantic coast. It is named after the Vendée River, a tributary of the Sèvre Nantaise, and its prefecture is La Roche-sur-Yon.

Geography

The department has about 250 km of Atlantic coastline, much of it sandy beach, together with the islands of Noirmoutier and Yeu. Inland lie the bocage — a landscape of small fields and hedgerows — and the marshlands of the Marais Poitevin in the south and the Marais Breton in the north. Other significant towns include Les Sables-d'Olonne, a major sailing port and resort, Challans, Fontenay-le-Comte and Les Herbiers.

History

The Vendée is best known historically for the War in the Vendée (1793–1796), a large royalist and Catholic counter-revolutionary uprising against the French Revolutionary government, sparked by conscription and religious persecution. The revolt and its brutal suppression, including the so-called "infernal columns", cost very large numbers of lives and remain a sensitive subject in French historical memory. The department is also the home of the Puy du Fou historical theme park and the start/finish port of the Vendée Globe solo round-the-world yacht race, held every four years from Les Sables-d'Olonne.
