Commons

Examples: Fisheries, pastures, atmosphere, knowledge commons, digital commons

The commons is the cultural and natural resources accessible to all members of a society, including natural materials such as air, water and a habitable planet. Such resources are held in common, not owned privately, or are managed collectively for the long-term benefit of a community. The term also describes the social institutions and norms by which communities govern shared resources.

History

Historically the commons referred to common land in England and similar shared pasture, forest and fisheries elsewhere, governed by customary rights. Enclosure privatised many such commons. In the 20th century, Garrett Hardin's 1968 essay "The Tragedy of the Commons" argued unmanaged open-access resources are inevitably overused; Elinor Ostrom's empirical work, recognised by a Nobel Prize in Economics in 2009, showed communities often devise durable institutions to govern common-pool resources.

Modern commons

The concept has been extended to the global commons (oceans, atmosphere, outer space, Antarctica), to knowledge and digital commons such as free and open-source software, Wikipedia and Creative Commons licensing, and to urban commons.
