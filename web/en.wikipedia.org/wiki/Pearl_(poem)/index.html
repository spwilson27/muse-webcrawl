Pearl (poem)

LanguageMiddle English (North West Midlands dialect)

The Dreamer meets the Pearl-maiden in a visionary landscape, from the Cotton Nero A.x manuscript illustrations.

Pearl is a late 14th-century Middle English alliterative poem, considered one of the finest works of medieval English literature. It survives in a single manuscript, Cotton Nero A.x, alongside Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Patience and Cleanness, and is attributed to the same anonymous author, known as the Pearl Poet or Gawain Poet.

Summary

The narrator, grieving the loss of a precious pearl—interpreted as his young daughter, who died before the age of two—falls asleep in a garden and dreams of a radiant otherworldly landscape. Across a river he sees a maiden crowned and adorned with pearls, who tells him she is a queen in heaven, a bride of Christ. In a long dialogue she teaches him about grace, salvation, and the parable of the vineyard, and shows him a vision of the New Jerusalem. When the Dreamer tries to cross the river to reach her, he wakes, consoled and reconciled to God's will.

Structure and language

The poem consists of 101 stanzas of twelve lines each, totalling 1,212 lines, combining alliteration with rhyme and intricate stanza-linking through repeated refrain words. Its elaborate numerological structure mirrors the perfection of the pearl itself.
