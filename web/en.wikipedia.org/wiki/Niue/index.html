Niue (Niuean: Niuē) is an island country in the South Pacific Ocean, about 2,400 km north-east of New Zealand. It is one of the world's largest raised coral atolls — nicknamed "The Rock" — with an area of about 260 km² and a population of around 1,700, making it one of the smallest countries in the world. Many more Niueans (over 30,000) live in New Zealand.

History

Niue was settled by Polynesian voyagers around 900 AD. Captain James Cook sighted the island in 1774 and named it "Savage Island" after hostile receptions. It became a British protectorate in 1900 and was annexed to New Zealand in 1901. In a 1974 referendum Niueans chose self-government in free association with New Zealand; Niueans hold New Zealand citizenship.

Geography and economy

The island has a central plateau ringed by limestone cliffs and a narrow reef, with no natural harbour. The economy relies on New Zealand aid, remittances, tourism, and internet services: Niue's .nu domain became unexpectedly valuable, and the island famously offered free nationwide Wi-Fi. Cyclone Heta devastated Niue in 2004, after which the capital Alofi was partly rebuilt inland.
