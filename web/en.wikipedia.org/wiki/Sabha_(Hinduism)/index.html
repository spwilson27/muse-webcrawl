Sabha (Hinduism)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sabha (Sanskrit: sabhā) is a Sanskrit term denoting an assembly, council, court or congregation. In early Vedic literature the sabha was one of the principal political institutions alongside the samiti; it appears to have been a smaller, more select body of elders and nobles that advised the king and deliberated on matters of state and justice.

In Vedic and epic tradition

The Rigveda refers to the sabha as a gathering place that also served social and recreational functions, including dicing. In the epics, the sabha is the royal court: the Mahabharata's second book, the Sabha Parva, describes the magnificent assembly hall built for the Pandavas at Indraprastha and the fateful dice game held there. The Ramayana likewise depicts royal councils deliberating on succession and war.

Celestial sabhas

Hindu cosmology describes the courts of the gods, each deity presiding over a sabha of attendants and sages: Indra's Sudharma sabha, Brahma's sabha, and the courts of Yama and Varuna are enumerated in epic and Puranic texts. The divine sage Narada is said to have visited and described these halls.

Later usage

In later Indian usage the word came to denote any organised assembly or society, and it survives in modern institutional names such as the Lok Sabha (the lower house of India's Parliament), the Rajya Sabha (the upper house), and the numerous cultural organisations called sabhas, such as the music sabhas of Chennai that host Carnatic concerts.
