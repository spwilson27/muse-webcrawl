United States Air Force Pararescue

United States Air Force Pararescue is the Air Force's elite personnel-recovery special operations force. Its members, known as Pararescuemen or PJs (from "parajumpers"), are trained to rescue and medically treat downed aircrew and other isolated personnel in hostile or denied territory, on land or at sea. Their motto, "That Others May Live", reflects the force's rescue ethos. Pararescue is one of the smallest career fields in the Air Force, with only a few hundred operators.

History

Pararescue originated in the Second World War, when flight surgeon Colonel Don Flickinger and others pioneered jumping medical personnel into remote crash sites, notably a famous 1943 rescue in Japanese-held Burma. The specialty was formally established in 1947 as the Air Force became an independent service. PJs distinguished themselves in Korea and especially Vietnam, where pararescueman William Pitsenbarger was killed aiding wounded soldiers in 1966 — posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor in 2000 — and Airman Duane Hackney became one of the most decorated enlisted men of the war. In 2000 Staff Sergeant Timothy Wilkinson's actions at Mogadishu in 1993 (depicted in Black Hawk Down) were also remembered within the community.

Training

The pararescue training pipeline, nicknamed "Superman School", lasts about two years and has very high attrition. It includes the indoctrination course, Army airborne school, combat dive qualification, survival (SERE) training, military free-fall school, underwater egress, and an intensive paramedic course culminating in certification, followed by the Pararescue Recovery Specialist Course. PJs are qualified paramedics as well as parachutists, divers and mountaineers.

Role

PJs serve in Rescue Squadrons alongside HH-60 Pave Hawk helicopters and HC-130 aircraft, and in Special Tactics Squadrons with other Air Force special warfare airmen. Beyond combat search and rescue (CSAR), they support NASA astronaut recovery, civil search and rescue, and disaster relief worldwide, including hurricane responses in the United States.
