Alpha (finance)

Alpha (α) is a measure of the excess return of an investment relative to a benchmark or to the return predicted by a model such as the capital asset pricing model (CAPM). Positive alpha indicates outperformance after adjusting for risk; negative alpha indicates underperformance.

Definition

In the CAPM regression Ri = Rf + βi(Rm − Rf) + αi + ε, alpha is the intercept — often called Jensen's alpha after Michael Jensen's 1968 study of mutual funds. It is expressed in percentage terms per period; an alpha of 0.03 means 3% annual outperformance versus the model prediction.

Interpretation

Alpha is used to judge active managers: persistent positive alpha would signal skill, but statistically distinguishing skill from luck requires long records. The efficient-market hypothesis holds that alpha should average zero before costs and be negative after fees. Alpha is distinct from beta, which measures exposure to market risk; "portable alpha" and "alpha decay" describe strategies and the erosion of excess returns as they become crowded.
