Satipaṭṭhāna (Pali; Sanskrit: smṛtyupasthāna) is a central concept in Buddhist meditation, usually translated as the "foundations" or "establishings of mindfulness". It refers to the sustained contemplation of four domains of experience — the body, feelings, the mind, and mental phenomena (dhammas) — with clear comprehension of their impermanent, unsatisfactory nature, and is presented in the early Buddhist texts as the direct path to the realisation of nibbāna.

Mindfulness of breathing is the opening exercise of the satipaṭṭhāna contemplations on the body.

The four foundations

Mindfulness of the body (kāyānupassanā) includes contemplation of breathing, postures, bodily activities, the anatomical parts, the four elements and the body's decomposition after death. Mindfulness of feelings (vedanānupassanā) is the observation of pleasant, painful and neutral feelings as they arise. Mindfulness of mind (cittānupassanā) is noticing mental states such as desire, aversion, delusion or concentration. Mindfulness of mental phenomena (dhammānupassanā) covers contemplation of the five hindrances, the five aggregates, the sense bases, the seven factors of awakening and the Four Noble Truths.

In modern practice

Satipaṭṭhāna is the scriptural foundation of the modern vipassanā (insight) meditation movements of Myanmar, Thailand and Sri Lanka, and through them of the secular mindfulness programmes now used worldwide. The principal textual source is the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta of the Pali Canon, expanded in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta of the Digha Nikaya.
