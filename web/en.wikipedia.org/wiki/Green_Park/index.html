Green Park (officially The Green Park) is one of the Royal Parks of London. Covering an area of about 19 hectares (47 acres), it lies between Hyde Park and St James's Park in the City of Westminster, immediately north of Buckingham Palace.

Unlike its neighbours, Green Park contains no lakes, no playgrounds and few monuments; it consists almost entirely of mature tree-lined grassland, making it a tranquil green space in the centre of the capital.

History

The park is said to have originated as a swampy burial ground for lepers from the nearby hospital at St James's. It was first enclosed in the 16th century by Henry VIII as part of his hunting grounds. In the 1660s Charles II had it landscaped as an upper park, laying out avenues and planting lime trees. During the 18th century it was a popular duelling ground and a venue for fireworks displays, including the celebrated 1749 performance of Handel's Music for the Royal Fireworks, for which a huge wooden pavilion known as the Temple of Peace was erected.

During the Second World War, parts of the park were dug up for allotments and used for anti-aircraft installations; the park was restored after the war.

Features and memorials

Notable features include the Canada Memorial (unveiled in 1994), commemorating the Canadians who served in the two world wars, and the Bomber Command Memorial, opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 2012, honouring the aircrews of RAF Bomber Command. The park's Constitution Hill entrance is marked by the Wellington Arch area just beyond its western edge, and the Green Park tube station on the Piccadilly, Victoria and Jubilee lines serves the park.

Flora

The park is planted with plane trees, limes and other species, and in spring its grass is covered with naturalised daffodils — around a million bulbs have been planted, so Green Park is one of the few Royal Parks where flowers are largely confined to these spring displays.
