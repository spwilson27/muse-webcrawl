Zora Arkus-Duntov

Zora Arkus-Duntov (25 December 1909 – 21 April 1996) was a Belgian-born American engineer whose work on the Chevrolet Corvette earned him the nickname "Father of the Corvette".

Early life

Born Zachary Arkus in Brussels to Russian Jewish parents, he grew up in Leningrad and Berlin, studied engineering, and raced cars and motorcycles in Europe before emigrating to the United States in 1940. During the war he co-founded Ardun Mechanical Corporation, maker of the famous Ardun hemispherical cylinder heads for Ford flathead V8 engines.

General Motors

Impressed by the 1953 Corvette show car, Arkus-Duntov wrote to Chevrolet chief engineer Ed Cole and joined GM that year. He pushed the Corvette from a mild six-cylinder cruiser toward a serious sports car: fitting the small-block V8, fuel injection, four-speed gearboxes, disc brakes, and racing development. His famous 1953 memo argued Chevrolet needed a performance image to win young buyers. Despite never holding the Corvette chief-engineer title until 1967, his influence defined the car.

Legacy

He retired in 1975. His ashes were interred at the National Corvette Museum in Bowling Green, Kentucky — the only person so honoured.
