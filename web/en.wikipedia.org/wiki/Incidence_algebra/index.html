Incidence algebra

In mathematics, the incidence algebra of a partially ordered set (poset) is an associative algebra formed from functions defined on intervals of the poset, with multiplication given by a convolution-like sum over intermediate elements. The concept was introduced by Gian-Carlo Rota in 1964 as a foundation for the theory of Möbius inversion in combinatorics.

Definition

For a locally finite poset, the incidence algebra consists of functions f(x, y) defined for x ≤ y, with addition pointwise and multiplication (f * g)(x, y) = Σ f(x, z)g(z, y), the sum running over all z between x and y. The zeta function of the poset takes the value 1 on every interval; its inverse in the algebra is the Möbius function μ, which generalises the classical number-theoretic Möbius function recovered when the poset is the positive integers ordered by divisibility.

Applications

Incidence algebras underlie combinatorial enumeration, inclusion–exclusion arguments and the study of poset topology, and they connect combinatorics with representation theory and algebraic geometry.
