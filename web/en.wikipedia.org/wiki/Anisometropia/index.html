Anisometropia

Anisometropia

SpecialtyOphthalmology, optometry
DefinitionRefractive power differing significantly between the two eyes (often ≥1–2 dioptres)
ComplicationsAmblyopia, aniseikonia, eyestrain
[Image: Spectacle lenses of markedly different powers in anisometropia]

Anisometropia is a condition in which the two eyes have unequal refractive power, so that one eye focuses light much better or worse than the other. The name derives from Greek roots meaning "not same measure eye".

Effects

Large differences can cause the brain to receive two images of different sizes (aniseikonia), making fusion difficult and causing headaches or double vision. In children, an untreated difference can lead to amblyopia ("lazy eye"), as the brain suppresses the blurrier image during visual development.

Treatment

Correction is by spectacles, contact lenses or refractive surgery. Contact lenses minimise the image-size difference; glasses with very different lens powers may be poorly tolerated. In children, prompt correction and sometimes patching of the stronger eye are used to treat amblyopia.
