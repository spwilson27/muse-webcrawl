Libreville
Country: Gabon
Province: Estuaire
Capital of Gabon
Population: about 700,000 (metro over 1 million)
Founded: 1849

Libreville is the capital and largest city of Gabon, a country in west central Africa. Located on the Komo River, near the Gulf of Guinea, the city is a port on the Komo estuary and a trade centre for the timber region. Roughly a third of Gabon's population lives in Libreville.

History

The area was inhabited by the Mpongwe people long before European contact. France acquired the area in 1839 through treaties with Mpongwe chiefs and established a naval station. In 1849, a settlement was founded for freed slaves from the captured slave ship Elizia, and was named Libreville — "free town" in French — in imitation of Freetown, Sierra Leone.

Libreville was the administrative centre of French Equatorial Africa's Gabon colony, and became the capital of independent Gabon in 1960. Oil wealth from the 1970s funded much of its modern construction.

Geography and climate

The city sprawls across rolling hills along the northern shore of the Gabon Estuary. It has an equatorial climate with a long rainy season from roughly September to May and high humidity year-round. Léon-Mba International Airport serves the city, and the Trans-Gabon Railway terminates at the port of Owendo, just south of Libreville.

Economy and culture

Timber exports, shipping and government dominate the economy. Landmarks include the Presidential Palace, the National Museum of Arts and Traditions, St. Michael's Church (built entirely of wood), and the seaside boulevard. The Université Omar Bongo, founded in 1970, is the country's principal university.
