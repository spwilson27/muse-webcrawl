Hunt–Lenox Globe

The Hunt–Lenox Globe, also known as the Lenox Globe, dating from approximately 1510, is the third-oldest known terrestrial globe, after the Erdapfel by Martin Behaim (1492) and its identical twin, the Jagiellonian Globe. It is held by the Rare Book Division of the New York Public Library.

Description

The globe is a hollow copper sphere about 112 mm (4.4 in) in diameter, made in two halves joined at the equator. It shows the world as known shortly after the voyages of Columbus and Amerigo Vespucci: the Americas appear as large islands, Asia is depicted with a greatly extended coastline, and a large island southeast of Asia represents an imprecise knowledge of the region.

"Here be dragons"

The globe is famous as the only known historical map or globe actually bearing the phrase HC SVNT DRACONES ("here be dragons"), written in Latin on the coast of eastern Asia. It was purchased in Paris in 1855 by the architect Richard Morris Hunt, who gave it to the philanthropist James Lenox, whose collection became part of the New York Public Library.
