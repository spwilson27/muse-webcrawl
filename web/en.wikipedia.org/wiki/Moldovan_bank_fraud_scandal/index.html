Moldovan bank fraud scandal

The Moldovan bank fraud scandal, often called the "theft of the billion", was a financial scandal revealed in 2014 in which approximately US$1 billion — about 12% of Moldova's gross domestic product — disappeared from three Moldovan banks: Banca de Economii, Banca Socială, and Unibank. The money was moved abroad through a coordinated scheme of fraudulent loans and shell companies.

Background and discovery

Between 2012 and 2014, the three banks issued loans to related parties and offshore entities that vastly exceeded their capital. In November 2014 the National Bank of Moldova placed them under special administration after discovering they were insolvent, and the government issued emergency loans to cover the losses, converting the hole into public debt. A confidential report by the risk firm Kroll, commissioned to trace the funds, was leaked in 2015 and named businessman Ilan Shor as a central figure in the scheme.

Consequences

The scandal triggered a currency collapse, inflation, mass street protests in 2015–2016, and a deep loss of public trust that reshaped Moldovan politics. The three banks were liquidated. Former prime minister Vlad Filat was convicted of corruption connected to the fraud in 2016; Ilan Shor was convicted in 2017, fled the country in 2019, and was sentenced in absentia to a lengthy prison term in 2023. Recovery of the stolen assets has been slow and only partial.
