Stormtrooper

The term stormtrooper may refer to several distinct subjects.

Military

Stormtroopers were specialist assault soldiers of the Imperial German Army in the final years of the First World War. Trained in infiltration tactics (often associated with General Oskar von Hutier), they used speed, surprise and light equipment — submachine guns, grenades, flamethrowers and trench mortars — to bypass and isolate enemy strongpoints rather than destroy them, leaving follow-up infantry to consolidate. These tactics premiered on a large scale in the German spring offensive of 1918. The name was later used by the Nazi Sturmabteilung (SA), the paramilitary "storm troopers" of the Nazi Party.

Star Wars

In the fictional Star Wars universe, Stormtroopers are the elite shock troops of the Galactic Empire, recognizable by their white plastoid armour and full-face helmets. Introduced in the 1977 film Star Wars, they serve as the Empire's main ground force, largely descended from the clone troopers of the prequel trilogy but later recruited from ordinary humans. Specialized variants include snowtroopers, scout troopers and sandtroopers. Running jokes about their poor marksmanship notwithstanding, stormtroopers have become one of the franchise's most iconic images.
