Spanish conquest of Peru

The Spanish conquest of Peru, also known as the Conquest of Peru, was one of the most important conquests in the Spanish colonization of the Americas. After years of preliminary exploration and encounters, Spanish soldiers led by Francisco Pizarro captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa at the Battle of Cajamarca on 16 November 1532, and over the following decades subdued the Inca Empire, though Inca resistance continued from the Neo-Inca state of Vilcabamba until 1572.

Background

Pizarro's expedition reached an empire already weakened by a devastating smallpox epidemic and a civil war between the half-brothers Huáscar and Atahualpa over succession to Huayna Capac. Earlier voyages in 1524 and 1526–1528 had confirmed the existence of a rich Andean state; at Tumbes the Spaniards saw evidence of Inca wealth.

Cajamarca and its aftermath

At Cajamarca, Pizarro's roughly 168 men ambushed Atahualpa's retinue in the plaza, killing thousands and seizing the emperor. Atahualpa offered a room filled with gold and twice with silver as ransom — one of the largest ransoms in history — but was executed in July 1533 after a summary trial. The Spaniards entered Cuzco in November 1533 and installed Manco Inca as a puppet ruler.

Resistance and consolidation

Manco rebelled in 1536, besieging Cuzco and nearly destroying the Spanish, before retreating to Vilcabamba. Civil wars among the conquistadors followed, in which Pizarro was assassinated in 1541. The Crown established the Viceroyalty of Peru in 1542, imposed the encomienda system, and finally destroyed the Vilcabamba state in 1572, executing Túpac Amaru I. Epidemic disease and forced labor in mines such as Potosí caused catastrophic population decline among Indigenous peoples.
