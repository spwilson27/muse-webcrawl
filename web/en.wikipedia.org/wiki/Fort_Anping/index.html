Fort Anping

Fort Anping (Chinese: 安平古堡; pinyin: Ānpíng Gǔbǎo), known in the 17th century as Fort Zeelandia, is a fortress in Anping District, Tainan, Taiwan. It was built between 1624 and 1634 by the Dutch East India Company (VOC) as the administrative and trading center of Dutch Formosa.

History

The Dutch chose the sandbar at Tayouan, off the coast of present-day Tainan, for their stronghold, naming it Fort Zeelandia after the Dutch province of Zeeland and the ship that carried the expedition. The fortress served as the capital of Dutch Taiwan for nearly four decades. In 1661 the Ming loyalist Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong) besieged the fort; after a nine-month siege, the Dutch governor Frederick Coyett surrendered in February 1662, ending Dutch rule on the island.

During Qing rule the site was renamed after the area, Anping. The original Dutch fort fell into ruin; the brick platform that survives today is largely a Japanese-era and later reconstruction, with remnants of the original Dutch walls visible among later structures. A small museum on the site displays artifacts of the Dutch period.

Layout

The fort originally consisted of an inner square fort with bastions and an outer town, Taiouan. Today visitors see a raised brick terrace, an observation tower added in the 1970s, and the remaining sections of the original outer wall built with Dutch bricks bonded with a mixture of glutinous rice, sugar, sand, and oyster-shell lime.
