Armenian literature

Armenian literature began in the early 5th century AD, following Mesrop Mashtots' creation of the Armenian alphabet around 405 AD. The earliest works were translations of the Bible and Greek and Syriac texts, followed by original histories and theological writings.

Golden age

The 5th century is regarded as a golden age, producing historians such as Movses Khorenatsi, author of the History of Armenia, Agathangelos and Yeghishe. Historiography remained the dominant genre through the medieval period, alongside poetry, hymns (sharakans) and epic traditions such as David of Sassoun.

Modern literature

In the 19th century a literary revival produced two standard forms of the language, Eastern and Western Armenian. Major figures include Khachatur Abovian, novelist and author of Wounds of Armenia, poets Hovhannes Tumanyan and Siamanto, and the diaspora writer William Saroyan.
