The Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC) is a political advisory body in the People's Republic of China and a central part of the Chinese Communist Party's united front system. Its members advise on major state policies and issues, though it has no legislative power.

History

The CPPCC was founded in 1949, when its first plenary session in Beijing adopted the Common Program, proclaimed the People's Republic of China, and elected the first central government. It acted as a de facto legislature until the National People's Congress was established in 1954, after which it settled into its present consultative role.

Composition

Members of the CPPCC National Committee — more than 2,000 — are drawn from the Chinese Communist Party, the eight legally recognised "democratic parties", mass organisations, ethnic minority groups, business figures, religious bodies, and representatives from Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan and overseas Chinese communities. Members are selected through consultation rather than direct public election.

Functions

The CPPCC's stated functions are political consultation, democratic supervision, and participation in and deliberation of state affairs. Its annual plenary session, held together with the National People's Congress session as the "Two Sessions", is a major event in China's political calendar, at which members submit proposals on economic, social and cultural policy. A Standing Committee handles affairs between sessions, chaired by a senior party leader.
