Magome-juku

Magome-juku (馬籠宿) was the forty-third of the sixty-nine stations of the Nakasendō, the route that connected Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto during the Edo period of Japan. It is located in the present-day city of Nakatsugawa, Gifu Prefecture, at the southern entrance of the Kiso Valley.

Description

Magome is unusual among the post towns of the Nakasendō in being built on a steep slope, with a single stone-paved main street climbing through the village, lined by restored wooden buildings, waterwheels, and latticed inns. From its heights there are views toward Mount Ena. The town was largely rebuilt after fires, notably in 1895 and 1915, but residents have preserved its Edo-period character by burying power lines and restricting signage.

Shimazaki Tōson

Magome is the birthplace of the novelist and poet Shimazaki Tōson (1872–1943), whose historical novel Before the Dawn (Yoake Mae) depicts the Kiso Valley and the Meiji Restoration through the life of his father, modeled on the town's honjin keeper. The Tōson Memorial Museum stands at the site of the former honjin, the inn reserved for high-ranking travellers.

Tourism

Together with neighbouring Tsumago-juku, reached by a popular 8 km hiking trail over the Magome Pass, Magome is a flagship example of Japan's preservation movement for historic post towns. A luggage-forwarding service lets visitors walk the old highway between the two towns as travellers once did.
