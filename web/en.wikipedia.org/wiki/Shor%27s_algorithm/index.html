Shor's algorithm

ClassQuantum algorithm
Named afterPeter Shor
Published1994

Shor's algorithm is a quantum algorithm devised by mathematician Peter Shor in 1994 for integer factorisation: given an integer N, it finds its prime factors in polynomial time on a quantum computer, exponentially faster than the best known classical algorithms.

How it works

The algorithm reduces factoring to finding the period of a modular exponential function, using quantum parallelism and the quantum Fourier transform to extract the period efficiently, followed by classical post-processing with continued fractions.

Significance

Because RSA and related public-key cryptosystems rely on the hardness of factoring, a large fault-tolerant quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could break them — a major motivation for post-quantum cryptography. Practical demonstrations so far have factored only small numbers such as 15 and 21.
