Ján Golian (26 January 1906 – 1945) was a Slovak officer, brigadier general, who was one of the main organisers and the first commander of the Slovak National Uprising, the armed insurrection of 1944 against Nazi Germany and the collaborationist Slovak regime of Jozef Tiso.

Career

Golian was born in Dombóvár in present-day Hungary and studied at the military academy in Hranice. He served in the Czechoslovak Army and, after the establishment of the Slovak State in 1939, in the Slovak Army, rising to chief of staff positions in Banská Bystrica. There, with other officers, he built the underground Military Headquarters (Vojenské ústredie) that, in cooperation with the Slovak National Council and the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London, prepared the uprising.

Slovak National Uprising

When German forces moved to occupy Slovakia, Golian issued the order "Start the defence" on 29 August 1944, launching the uprising. From Banská Bystrica — liberated "Tri Duby" airport and the Free Slovak Radio included — he commanded the insurgent 1st Czechoslovak Army in Slovakia, organising the defence of the territory against German divisions. In October 1944 he handed over command to General Rudolf Viest.

After the uprising's suppression in late October 1944, Golian and Viest were captured by German forces at Pohronský Bukovec on 3 November 1944. They were interrogated, deported to Germany and murdered at the Flossenbürg concentration camp in 1945, probably in January.

Legacy

Golian is remembered as a national hero in Slovakia; streets, a military unit and memorials bear his name, and the launch of the uprising on 29 August is a national holiday.
