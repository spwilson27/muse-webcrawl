The Art of Loving

The Art of Loving is a 1956 book by psychoanalyst and social philosopher Erich Fromm, published in the World Perspectives series. It argues that love is not primarily a feeling one "falls into" but an art requiring knowledge and effort — a skill to be learned and practised like medicine or carpentry.

Content

Fromm distinguishes romantic infatuation from mature love, which he defines as union under the condition of preserving one's integrity and individuality. He analyses forms of love — brotherly love, motherly love, erotic love, self-love and love of God — and identifies care, responsibility, respect and knowledge as the basic elements common to all of them. The book also critiques how capitalist society, oriented toward consumption and exchange, distorts people's capacity to love.

Reception

The Art of Loving became an international bestseller, translated into dozens of languages, and remains Fromm's best-known work.
