Interchangeable lens camera

An interchangeable lens camera is a camera whose lens can be removed and replaced with another lens designed for its lens mount. The category includes digital single-lens reflex cameras (DSLRs), mirrorless interchangeable-lens cameras (MILCs), and rangefinder and medium-format systems, as well as their film predecessors.

Characteristics

The ability to change lenses lets one body serve purposes from wide-angle landscapes to telephoto sports photography, and is paired with larger sensors than most fixed-lens compact cameras, giving better low-light performance and depth-of-field control. Each manufacturer uses proprietary mounts — such as Canon EF/RF, Nikon F/Z, Sony E and the shared Micro Four Thirds and L-Mount standards — so lenses are generally not compatible across brands without adapters.

Market

Interchangeable-lens cameras defined professional and enthusiast photography through the film and DSLR eras. Since the 2010s mirrorless systems have displaced DSLRs, while smartphone cameras have shrunk the overall market, pushing manufacturers toward higher-end full-frame and medium-format models.
