Teleidoscope

A teleidoscope is a type of kaleidoscope fitted with a lens and an open view, so that it forms symmetrical patterns from whatever scene it is pointed at rather than from objects contained in a chamber. It was patented by John Lyon Burnside III and is associated with the kaleidoscope revival led by Cozy Baker in the 1970s.

Design

Like a kaleidoscope, the teleidoscope uses two or more mirrors arranged at angles inside a tube. Instead of an object cell filled with beads or glass, a clear lens (often a fisheye or a transparent sphere) at the far end gathers light from the external environment, so everyday scenes are transformed into mandala-like symmetric images.

History

John Lyon Burnside III filed a patent for the teleidoscope in the 1970s. The device became popular among collectors during the kaleidoscope renaissance promoted by Cozy Baker, founder of the Brewster Kaleidoscope Society.
