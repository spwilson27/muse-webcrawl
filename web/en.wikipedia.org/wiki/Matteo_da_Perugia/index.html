Matteo da Perugia

Matteo da Perugia (fl. 1400–1416) was a Medieval Italian composer, presumably from Perugia, and one of the most significant composers of the ars subtilior and of the early 15th-century Italian school. Between 1402 and 1407 he was the first magister cappellae of Milan Cathedral, appointed under Archbishop Pietro Filargo, who later became Antipope Alexander V.

Life

Little is known of Matteo's life beyond his Milan appointment. He served as a cantor at Milan Cathedral alongside the composer Augustinus de Florentia, and his tenure there placed him at one of the most cosmopolitan musical centers of the time. His later whereabouts are uncertain.

Music

Matteo composed in a variety of genres: ballades, virelais, rondeaux, ballate, and a Gloria movement. Many of his works survive in the Modena Codex (Modena, Biblioteca Estense, α.M.5.24), a principal source of ars subtilior music. His compositions show the rhythmic complexity characteristic of the style, combined with Italian lyricism. Works attributed to him include the ballata Se je me plaing, virelais, and settings in both French and Italian forms. Thirty-one works are attributed to Matteo in modern editions, making him one of the best-represented composers of his generation.
