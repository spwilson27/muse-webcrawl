The 24th Infantry Division was an infantry division of the United States Army, originally formed from units of the Hawaiian Division. Nicknamed the "Victory Division" and the "Taro Leaf Division" for its distinctive shoulder patch, it fought in the Pacific Theatre of the Second World War, in the Korean War, and in the Gulf War of 1991.

Second World War

Elements of the division were among the first American units engaged at Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941. The division later fought in New Guinea and in the liberation of the Philippines, including the landings on Leyte and Luzon.

Korean War

The 24th was the first American division sent to Korea in June 1950. Task Force Smith, drawn from its ranks, fought the first American ground action of the war at Osan. The division endured the brutal early fighting around the Pusan Perimeter and served throughout the conflict.

Later service

Reactivated as a mechanized division at Fort Stewart, the 24th formed part of the XVIII Airborne Corps in Operation Desert Storm in 1991, making a deep armored thrust into Iraq. It was inactivated in 1996, briefly reactivated as a training formation, and finally inactivated in 2006.
