SCP Foundation

The SCP Foundation is a fictional secret organization documented in a collaborative writing project of the same name. In the project's fictional universe, the Foundation is responsible for capturing, containing, and studying anomalous, supernatural, or otherwise paranormal entities, objects, and phenomena (referred to as "SCPs"), while also keeping their existence hidden from the rest of human society.

The collaborative writing project includes elements of many genres such as horror, science fiction, and urban fantasy. The majority of works on the SCP Wiki consist of "special containment procedures": documents written in a scientific and clinical tone describing a single SCP entity and the means of keeping it contained, followed by a description of the anomaly itself. The site also contains thousands of "Foundation Tales", short stories set within the fictional universe.

History

The SCP project originated on the paranormal /x/ board of 4chan in 2007, where the first SCP file, SCP-173, was posted. The project moved to its own wiki in 2008. The community grew steadily, and the format of pseudo-scientific containment documents proved highly influential on internet horror fiction and the "creepypasta" genre.

Notable SCPs

Well-known entries include SCP-173, a statue that moves when unobserved; SCP-682, a highly adaptive and hostile reptilian creature; SCP-096, a humanoid that attacks anyone who views its face; and SCP-939 and countless others numbering in the thousands. Articles are numbered in series, with Series I covering SCP-002 through SCP-999 and later series extending the catalogue.

Influence

The SCP Foundation universe has inspired video games such as SCP – Containment Breach (2012) and SCP: Secret Laboratory, novels, and other media. The Finnish video game Control (2019) by Remedy Entertainment was heavily influenced by the SCP format and themes. In 2022, author Qntm (Sam Hughes) published the novel There Is No Antimemetics Division, based on his SCP Foundation stories.
