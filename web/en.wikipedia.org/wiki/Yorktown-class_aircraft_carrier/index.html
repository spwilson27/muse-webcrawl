Yorktown-class aircraft carrier

The Yorktown class was a class of three aircraft carriers built for the United States Navy and completed shortly before World War II: Yorktown (CV-5), Enterprise (CV-6) and Hornet (CV-8). They were designed under the constraints of the Washington and London naval treaties, displacing about 20,000 tons with an air group of roughly 90 aircraft and a top speed of about 32.5 knots.

Design

The class embodied lessons from the earlier Ranger and the converted battlecruisers Lexington and Saratoga. They featured a full-length flight deck, an island superstructure, and hangar-deck catapults that were little used. Treaty tonnage limits constrained their protection; a fourth ship, Wasp (CV-7), was built to a smaller design to use remaining treaty tonnage.

Service

All three ships fought in the critical carrier battles of 1942. Yorktown was sunk at the Battle of Midway, where aircraft from all three carriers sank four Japanese carriers. Hornet launched the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in April 1942 and was sunk at the Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands. Enterprise survived the war as the most decorated US naval vessel of World War II, participating in more major actions than any other American ship.
