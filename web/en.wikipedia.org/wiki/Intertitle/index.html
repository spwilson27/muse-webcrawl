Intertitle

In films and videos, an intertitle, also known as a title card, is a piece of filmed, printed text edited into the midst of (hence inter-) the photographed action at various points. Intertitles are used to convey narrative material (expository intertitles), to present dialogue in silent films (dialogue intertitles), or as opening and closing title sequences.

History

Intertitles appeared in films from the first years of the 20th century, as filmmakers moved beyond single-shot scenes to more complex narratives. In the silent era they carried both exposition and dialogue, often elaborately lettered and decorated, and specialist title writers worked in studios. Some filmmakers, notably in avant-garde and Soviet cinema, used intertitles sparingly or experimentally.

Use in sound film

The arrival of synchronized sound in the late 1920s made dialogue intertitles obsolete, but intertitles survive as opening and closing credits, chapter headings, location and date captions, and in stylistic revivals of silent-film conventions. In television and streaming, the term overlaps with "title card" for episode titles and introductory graphics.
