The Battle of Königgrätz (also called the Battle of Sadowa) was the decisive battle of the Austro-Prussian War, fought on 3 July 1866 near the city of Königgrätz in Bohemia. Prussia's victory ended Austrian dominance of the German states.

Background

Under Chief of Staff Helmuth von Moltke, three Prussian armies invaded Bohemia separately and converged on the Austrian Northern Army under Ludwig von Benedek, who took a defensive position between the Bystřice river and the Elbe.

The battle

The Prussian First and Elbe armies attacked across the Bystřice, where fighting in the Swiepwald was especially bloody; the Prussian Dreyse needle gun's rate of fire outmatched Austrian muzzle-loaders. The arrival of the Crown Prince's Second Army on the Austrian flank in the afternoon decided the battle, and the Austrian army retreated across the Elbe with heavy losses.

Aftermath

Austrian casualties were over 40,000 against some 9,000 Prussian. The Peace of Prague excluded Austria from German affairs and cleared the way for the Prussian-led North German Confederation and, ultimately, German unification in 1871.
