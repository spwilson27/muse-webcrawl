Thiazide

Thiazide
Class: Diuretic
Examples: Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorothiazide, chlortalidone
Uses: Hypertension, edema

Thiazide refers both to a class of organic compounds and to a class of diuretic medications based on the benzothiadiazine chemical structure. Thiazide diuretics are among the most widely prescribed drugs for the treatment of high blood pressure (hypertension) and edema associated with heart failure and other conditions.

Mechanism of action

Thiazides act on the kidneys, inhibiting the sodium-chloride symporter in the distal convoluted tubule. This reduces the reabsorption of sodium and chloride, so more water is excreted in the urine, lowering blood volume and blood pressure. They also increase calcium reabsorption and promote potassium and magnesium excretion.

History

The first thiazide, chlorothiazide, was developed by researchers at Merck in the 1950s and introduced in 1958. It was the first safe and effective oral diuretic and revolutionised the treatment of hypertension. Hydrochlorothiazide and longer-acting thiazide-like agents such as chlortalidone and indapamide followed.

Adverse effects

Side effects can include low potassium (hypokalemia), low sodium, raised blood sugar and uric acid (occasionally precipitating gout), dizziness, and increased sensitivity to sunlight.
