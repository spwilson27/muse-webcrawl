Argonauts

Argonauts
Greek: Ἀργοναῦται
Leader: Jason
Ship: Argo
Quest: The Golden Fleece in Colchis
Main source: Apollonius of Rhodes, Argonautica

In Greek mythology, the Argonauts were a band of heroes who accompanied Jason aboard the ship Argo in the quest for the Golden Fleece in the generation before the Trojan War. Their voyage is told most fully in Apollonius of Rhodes' epic Argonautica (3rd century BC).

The quest

Jason, rightful heir of Iolcus, was sent by the usurper Pelias to fetch the Golden Fleece from Colchis on the Black Sea, guarded by a dragon and King Aeëtes. The Argo, built by Argus with Athena's help and fitted with a speaking oak timber from Dodona, carried some fifty heroes — lists vary, including Heracles, Orpheus, Castor and Pollux, Peleus, Atalanta (in some accounts), and the winged Boreads.

Voyage and return

Adventures included the island of Lemnos, the loss of Hylas, the Harpies and Phineus, and passing the Clashing Rocks (Symplegades). In Colchis, Aeëtes' daughter Medea, a sorceress, betrayed her father to help Jason win the Fleece. On the return — by routes that vary by author — the Argonauts escaped Aeëtes' pursuit after Medea killed her brother Absyrtus, survived the Sirens (drowned out by Orpheus' music), and were cleansed by Circe.

Legacy

The tale, older than the Odyssey in its roots, was retold by Pindar, Euripides (in Medea), and later writers, and "Argonaut" became a byword for daring voyagers — including California gold-rush prospectors of 1849.
