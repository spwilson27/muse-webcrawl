Arius

Arius (c. 250/256 – 336) was a Cyrenaic presbyter and ascetic from Alexandria, Egypt. His teachings concerning the nature of the Trinity in Christianity gave rise to Arianism.

Teaching

Arius taught that Jesus Christ, the Son of God, was created by God the Father and was therefore subordinate and distinct — "there was a time when he was not". This contradicted the view that the Son was co-eternal and consubstantial (homoousios) with the Father.

Council of Nicaea

The controversy led Emperor Constantine to convene the First Council of Nicaea in 325, which condemned Arius and produced the Nicene Creed affirming the Son's consubstantiality. Arius was exiled, later partially rehabilitated, but died in 336 before formal restoration. Arian Christianity persisted among several Germanic peoples for centuries.
