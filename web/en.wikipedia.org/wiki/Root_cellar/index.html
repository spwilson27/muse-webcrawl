Root cellar

A root cellar is a structure, usually underground or partially underground, used for storage of vegetables, fruits, nuts and other foods. Its name reflects the traditional focus on root vegetables stored in an underground space that keeps them cool through the winter, allowing food harvested in the autumn to be eaten through the cold months.

Principles

Below the frost line the ground maintains a nearly constant temperature, typically between 0 °C and 10 °C (32–50 °F), cold enough to slow spoilage but above freezing. A well-built root cellar combines this stable cool temperature with high humidity, darkness and ventilation that removes ethylene gas given off by ripening produce, which would otherwise accelerate decay.

Construction

Root cellars may be:

  • a room in the basement of a house, often an unheated corner with an outside vent;
  • a separate excavation dug into a hillside, lined with stone or concrete and covered with earth;
  • an above-ground structure insulated with thick earth or straw bales (a "clamp" in Britain is a simpler outdoor pile of roots covered with straw and soil).

Stored foods

Potatoes, carrots, beets, parsnips, turnips, onions, garlic, winter squash, cabbages and apples are classic root-cellar crops. Different produce has different ideal conditions — apples prefer drier, colder air and give off ethylene, so they are often stored apart from vegetables.

Modern use

Before household refrigeration, the root cellar was an essential feature of rural homes in cold climates, including Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, Canada and the northern United States. Interest in root cellars has revived among gardeners and homesteaders as a zero-energy method of food storage.
