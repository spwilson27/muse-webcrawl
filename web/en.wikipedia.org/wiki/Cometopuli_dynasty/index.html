The Cometopuli dynasty was the last royal dynasty of the First Bulgarian Empire, ruling from 976 until the fall of Bulgaria under Byzantine domination in 1018. The name derives from the Greek Kometopouloi, "sons of the count" (komes), referring to the four sons of Count Nicholas: David, Moses, Aaron and Samuel.

Rise

After Byzantium and Kievan Rus' destroyed the eastern Bulgarian state in 968–971, the four brothers — probably of Armenian origin — raised the western lands in resistance. By 976 they led an open revolt. David and Moses were killed and Aaron executed by Samuel, who by 997 was crowned tsar, ruling from Ohrid and Prespa.

Samuel and the fall

Tsar Samuel waged a decades-long war against Emperor Basil II, winning at the Gates of Trajan (986) but steadily losing ground. His defeat at Kleidion in 1014, followed — according to Byzantine chroniclers — by the mass blinding of his captured army, broke the empire; Samuel died days later. His successors Gavril Radomir and Ivan Vladislav were killed by 1018, and the last claimant, Presian II, surrendered. Bulgaria remained under Byzantine rule until 1185.
