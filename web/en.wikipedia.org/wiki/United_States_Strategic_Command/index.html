United States Strategic Command

STRATCOM
Founded: 1 June 1992
Headquarters: Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska
Role: Functional combatant command

The United States Strategic Command (USSTRATCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands in the United States Department of Defense. Headquartered at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska, STRATCOM is responsible for strategic deterrence, global strike, and operating the Defense Department's Global Information Grid.

Mission

STRATCOM oversees the nation's nuclear forces: intercontinental ballistic missiles, nuclear-capable bombers, and ballistic missile submarines — the nuclear triad. Its missions also include missile defense advocacy, intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance support, and joint electronic warfare. Space operations were transferred to the re-established U.S. Space Command in 2019.

History

The command was established on 1 June 1992, succeeding the Strategic Air Command (SAC) at the end of the Cold War. In 2002 it absorbed the United States Space Command, and in 2017 it adopted responsibility for nuclear command, control, and communications modernization.

Operations

STRATCOM maintains airborne command posts (E-6B Mercury and E-4B aircraft) and the underground command center at Offutt, providing survivable command and control of nuclear forces.
