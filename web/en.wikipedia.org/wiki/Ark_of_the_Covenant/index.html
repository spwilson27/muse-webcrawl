Ark of the Covenant

Ark of the Covenant
Hebrew: אָרוֹן הַבְּרִית
Contents (tradition): Tablets of the Law; Aaron's rod; pot of manna
Material: Acacia wood overlaid with gold
Last housed: Holy of Holies, First Temple, Jerusalem
Fate: Unknown after Babylonian conquest

The Ark of the Covenant, also called the Ark of the Testimony or Ark of God, is described in the Hebrew Bible as a gold-covered wooden chest containing the tablets of the Ten Commandments, built by the Israelites at God's command to Moses on Mount Sinai and later placed in the Holy of Holies of the Temple in Jerusalem.

Biblical description

Exodus describes a chest of acacia wood overlaid with gold, about 2.5 cubits long, with rings and carrying poles, a gold "mercy seat" lid flanked by two cherubim, and contents including the tablets, a pot of manna, and Aaron's rod that budded. It was carried by Levites before the Israelites, led the crossing of the Jordan, and was borne around Jericho.

History in the narrative

Captured by the Philistines after the battle of Aphek, the Ark is said to have brought plagues on its captors and was returned. David brought it to Jerusalem, and Solomon installed it in the First Temple. After Babylon destroyed the Temple in 586 BC, the Ark disappears from the biblical record; later traditions claim it was hidden, taken to Ethiopia (the Ethiopian Orthodox Church maintains an ark rests at Axum), or carried off to Babylon or Rome.

Cultural legacy

The Ark has inspired centuries of speculation and popular works, notably the film Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981). Replica "arks" (tabots) remain central to Ethiopian Christian practice.
