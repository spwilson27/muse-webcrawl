Rolf Disch

Rolf Disch (born 1944) is a German architect, solar energy pioneer and environmental activist. He is best known for his work in solar architecture and for designing the Heliotrope, a house that rotates to follow the sun, in Freiburg im Breisgau.

Born: 1944, Schopfheim, Germany

Occupation: Architect

Known for: Solar architecture, the Heliotrope

Life and work

Disch studied architecture and became involved in the environmental movement in the 1970s, campaigning against nuclear power in the region around Freiburg. He founded his own architectural practice, Rolf Disch Solar Architecture, and became one of the leading figures in the development of energy-efficient and solar-powered buildings in Germany.

His most famous project is the Heliotrope (Heliotrop), completed in 1994 in Freiburg. The cylindrical house rotates slowly on a central column to track the sun, maximising solar gain in winter and avoiding overheating in summer. It is considered the first building in the world to produce more energy than it consumes.

Other projects

Disch also designed the Solar Settlement (Solarsiedlung) at Freiburg's Vauban district, a community of plus-energy houses whose photovoltaic roofs generate more electricity than the residents use, and the Sonnenschiff ("Sun Ship"), a mixed-use building in the same district. His work helped establish Freiburg's reputation as a "solar city".
