FR-4

FR-4 (or FR4) is a grade designation for a flame-retardant composite material composed of woven fiberglass cloth impregnated with an epoxy resin binder. It is the most common material used for printed circuit boards (PCBs).

Properties

The "FR" stands for flame retardant, indicating the material complies with the UL94 V-0 flammability standard. FR-4 sheets are electrical insulators with good strength-to-weight ratio, low water absorption, and dielectric properties suitable for most consumer and industrial electronics. Its glass transition temperature typically ranges from about 130 °C for standard grades to 170–180 °C for high-Tg grades.

Fabrication

PCB manufacturers laminate copper foil onto one or both sides of FR-4 sheet, then drill, plate, print etch resist, and etch away unwanted copper to form conductive traces. Multilayer boards stack several etched cores with prepreg (partially cured FR-4) between them.

Alternatives

Cheaper paper-based phenolic materials such as FR-2 are used in simple consumer devices; high-frequency circuits use PTFE-based laminates or materials like Rogers RO4000 series with lower dielectric loss; metal-core boards are used for LED lighting and power applications requiring heat dissipation.
