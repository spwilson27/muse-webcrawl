The Seattle Seahawks are a professional American football team based in Seattle, Washington. They compete in the National Football League as a member of the NFC West division. The Seahawks joined the NFL in 1976 as an expansion team, initially playing in the NFC before moving to the AFC in 1977 and returning to the NFC in 2002 as part of league realignment.

History

The franchise played its first seasons in the Kingdome. Under coach Chuck Knox the team reached the AFC Championship Game in the 1983 season. In 1988 businessman Ken Behring bought the team; a threatened move to California in the mid-1990s was halted, and Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen purchased the Seahawks in 1997, leading to voter approval of a new stadium, which opened in 2002 as Seahawks Stadium (later Qwest Field, CenturyLink Field, and Lumen Field).

Coached by Mike Holmgren and led by quarterback Matt Hasselbeck and running back Shaun Alexander, Seattle reached its first Super Bowl, XL, in the 2005 season, losing to the Pittsburgh Steelers. Under head coach Pete Carroll, hired in 2010, and quarterback Russell Wilson, drafted in 2012, the Seahawks built the celebrated "Legion of Boom" defense and won Super Bowl XLVIII in February 2014, defeating the Denver Broncos 43–8. They returned to Super Bowl XLIX the following season, losing narrowly to the New England Patriots.

Fan base

Seahawks fans are known collectively as the "12s" (originally the "12th Man"); the team retired the number 12 jersey in 1984 in their honor, and a flag bearing the number is raised before home games. Crowd noise at Lumen Field has twice set Guinness World Records for the loudest roar at a sports stadium.

Rivalries

The Seahawks' principal rivals include the San Francisco 49ers, Los Angeles Rams, and Arizona Cardinals within the NFC West.
