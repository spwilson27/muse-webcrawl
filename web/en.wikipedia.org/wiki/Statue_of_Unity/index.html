The Statue of Unity is the world's tallest statue, with a height of 182 metres (597 feet), located in the Indian state of Gujarat. It depicts Indian statesman and independence activist Vallabhbhai Patel (1875–1950), who was the first deputy prime minister and home minister of independent India and an adherent of Mahatma Gandhi. Patel is highly respected for playing a significant role in the political integration of India. The statue is on the Narmada River in the Kevadiya colony, facing the Sardar Sarovar Dam 100 kilometres (62 mi) southeast of the city of Vadodara.

The project was first announced in 2010, and construction started in October 2013 by Indian company Larsen & Toubro, with a total construction cost of ₹29.89 billion (US$422 million). It was designed by Indian sculptor Ram V. Sutar and was inaugurated by the Prime Minister of India, Narendra Modi, on 31 October 2018, the 143rd anniversary of Patel's birth.

History

Narendra Modi announced the project on 7 October 2013 at a press conference to mark the beginning of his tenth year as the Chief Minister of Gujarat. At the time, the project was dubbed "Gujarat's tribute to the nation". A society named the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Rashtriya Ekta Trust (SVPRET) was formed for the construction of the statue. The iron needed for the statue was collected from farmers across India in a campaign known as the Loha (iron) collection drive; by 2016, a total of 135 metric tonnes of scrap iron had been recovered, of which about 109 tonnes were used in the statue's foundation.

Design

The statue depicts Vallabhbhai Patel, known as the "Iron Man of India", wearing his characteristic simple clothing, in a walking pose. At 182 metres it is nearly twice the height of the Statue of Liberty (93 metres including the pedestal) and surpasses the previous record holder, the Spring Temple Buddha in China (128 metres). The figure stands on a star-shaped base covering an island on the Narmada River called Sadhu Bet. A museum and exhibition hall dedicated to Patel's life occupies the pedestal, and visitors can ascend to a viewing gallery at a height of about 153 metres, near the statue's chest.

Construction

The statue was built by Larsen & Toubro, which won the contract in 2014. Its core is made of reinforced concrete, with a steel frame and an outer cladding of bronze panels cast in China. The structure was engineered to withstand winds of up to 180 km/h and earthquakes measuring 6.5 on the Richter scale. Construction employed more than 3,000 workers and took 33 months to complete.

Tourism

The complex around the statue includes a museum, a memorial garden known as the Valley of Flowers, a tent city, and a jungle safari. Within its first year the statue attracted millions of visitors, and in November 2019 its daily average footfall surpassed that of the Statue of Liberty.
