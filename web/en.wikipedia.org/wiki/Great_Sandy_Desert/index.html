Great Sandy Desert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image placeholder: Long parallel dunes of the Great Sandy Desert]
Long parallel dunes of the Great Sandy Desert

The **Great Sandy Desert** is an interim Australian bioregion of about 285,000 km² in northwestern Western Australia, lying between the Pilbara and southern Kimberley and extending toward the Northern Territory border. It is the second-largest desert in Australia after the Great Victoria Desert, and consists largely of parallel red sand dunes running east–west, with salt lakes and claypans between them.

Despite its aridity (rainfall 250–300 mm, highly variable, mostly from thunderstorms and monsoon depressions), the desert supports spinifex grasslands, desert oaks and a rich lizard fauna — among the world's richest. Aboriginal peoples including the Martu and Pintupi have lived here for millennia and continue to manage country with fire. The Canning Stock Route and Gary Junction Road cross it; Rudall River (Karlamilyi) National Park protects its eastern part. Ernest Giles and other explorers crossed it in the 1870s.

Climate and landforms

Summer temperatures regularly exceed 40 °C. The dune fields belong to the great Australian dunefield that also covers the Gibson and Simpson deserts; Lake Disappointment (Kumpupintil) and Lake Dora are major salt lakes.
