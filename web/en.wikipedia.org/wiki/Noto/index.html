Noto fonts

Noto is a font family comprising more than 100 individual fonts designed by Google to cover all the scripts encoded in the Unicode standard, as well as numerous regional and historical writing systems. The name derives from the term "tofu" — the nickname for the blank rectangles (□) displayed when a device lacks a glyph — since the project's goal was "no more tofu".

Design and coverage

The family includes Noto Sans and Noto Serif in many script-specific variants (Noto Sans CJK, Noto Naskh Arabic, Noto Sans Devanagari, and so on), weights from thin to black, and companion fonts such as Noto Color Emoji. Development began around 2011 with Monotype as a partner; the fonts are released under the SIL Open Font License.

Usage

Noto is the default fallback font family on Android and ChromeOS and is widely used in digital publishing wherever multilingual coverage is needed, covering over 1,000 languages and more than 150 writing systems.
