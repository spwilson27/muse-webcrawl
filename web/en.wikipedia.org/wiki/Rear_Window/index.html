The courtyard set of Rear Window, built at Paramount Studios.

Rear Window is a 1954 American mystery thriller film directed by Alfred Hitchcock and written by John Michael Hayes, based on Cornell Woolrich's 1942 short story "It Had to Be Murder." It stars James Stewart as a photographer confined to a wheelchair in his Greenwich Village apartment, who spies on his neighbours through his rear window and becomes convinced one of them has committed murder; Grace Kelly co-stars as his girlfriend Lisa.

Plot

Professional photographer L. B. "Jeff" Jefferies, immobilized with a broken leg, passes the heatwave watching his courtyard neighbours. He grows suspicious of travelling salesman Lars Thorwald, whose bedridden wife disappears. With Lisa and his nurse Stella, Jeff investigates at growing personal risk, culminating in a confrontation in his own darkened apartment.

Production and reception

The film was shot entirely on one enormous Paramount soundstage, a complete courtyard set with working apartments. Widely considered one of Hitchcock's best films, it was nominated for four Academy Awards and was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 1997. It holds a prominent place in polls of the greatest films ever made.
