Nikaya

Nikāya is a Pāli word meaning "volume," "collection," or "assemblage." It is most commonly used in reference to the Buddhist texts of the Sutta Piṭaka of the Pāli Canon, the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism, which are divided into five nikāyas. The term is also sometimes used, though rarely in English, for the schools of early Buddhism.

The five nikāyas

  • Dīgha Nikāya — the "collection of long discourses," containing 34 long suttas.
  • Majjhima Nikāya — the "collection of middle-length discourses," containing 152 suttas.
  • Saṃyutta Nikāya — the "collection of connected discourses," grouping thousands of shorter suttas by topic.
  • Aṅguttara Nikāya — the "collection of numbered discourses," arranged by the number of items discussed, from ones to elevens.
  • Khuddaka Nikāya — the "minor collection," a miscellany including the Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Sutta Nipāta, Jātaka tales, and other works.

The first four nikāyas have close parallels in the Āgamas preserved in the Chinese Buddhist canon, reflecting their origin in the discourses of early Buddhism. Translations of the nikāyas by scholars such as Bhikkhu Bodhi have made them widely accessible in English.

Other uses

In Sri Lankan and Southeast Asian Theravāda monasticism, "nikāya" also denotes monastic orders or fraternities, such as the Siam Nikāya, Amarapura Nikāya, and Rāmañña Nikāya of Sri Lanka.
