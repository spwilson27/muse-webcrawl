The Thyssen-Bornemisza National Museum is an art museum in Madrid, Spain, located near the Prado Museum on the Paseo del Prado. It completes Madrid's 'Golden Triangle of Art' with the Prado and the Reina Sofía.

Collection

The core collection was assembled over two generations by Baron Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza and his son Hans Heinrich, rivalling the British Royal Collection as a private holding. It spans medieval panels and Italian primitives, Dutch Golden Age painting, Impressionism, Post-Impressionism, German Expressionism, Russian avant-garde, and American art—areas complementing the Prado's strengths.

History

Long housed at Villa Favorita in Lugano, the collection moved to Madrid after Spain outbid other suitors. The museum opened in 1992 in the renovated Villahermosa Palace; in 1993 the Spanish state purchased the core 775 works, and the Carmen Thyssen collection later expanded the displays.
