Manvantara

A manvantara, in Hindu cosmology, is a cyclic period of time identifying the duration, reign, or age of a Manu, the progenitor of humanity. Each manvantara is followed by a great flood (pralaya) that destroys the world, which is then recreated.

Duration

Each manvantara lasts for 306,720,000 years (852,000 divine years; 71 chaturyugas plus a transitional sandhya) and repeats seventy-one times in a thousand-year cycle. A kalpa (a single day of Brahma) consists of fourteen manvantaras, each ruled by a different Manu, and totals 4.32 billion years.

The fourteen Manus

The present kalpa, called the Shveta-Varaha Kalpa, is in its seventh manvantara, ruled by Vaivasvata Manu, the Manu associated with the flood narrative and the Matsya avatar of Vishnu. The fourteen Manus of this kalpa are Svayambhuva, Svarochisha, Uttama, Tapasa (Tamasa), Raivata, Chakshusha, Vaivasvata (current), Savarni, Daksa Savarni, Brahma Savarni, Dharma Savarni, Rudra Savarni, Deva Savarni, and Indra Savarni.

Structure

Each manvantara has its own set of seven sages (Saptarishi), chief of the gods (Indra), and avatar of Vishnu, as described in Puranic literature. The concept frames human history within an immense cyclical cosmology of creation, preservation, and dissolution.
