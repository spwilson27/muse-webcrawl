Rogers–Ramanujan identities

FieldNumber theory, combinatorics, q-series
Discovered byLeonard James Rogers (1894); independently Srinivasa Ramanujan (before 1913)
First proofRogers (1917) and Ramanujan; Issai Schur (1917, combinatorial)
ApplicationsPartition theory, statistical mechanics (hard hexagon model), representation theory

In mathematics, the Rogers–Ramanujan identities are two identities concerning infinite series and products, discovered by Leonard James Rogers in 1894 and independently rediscovered by Srinivasa Ramanujan, who could not prove them at first. They relate sums over q-Pochhammer symbols to infinite products, and they yield elegant theorems about integer partitions.

Statement

The identities state, for |q| < 1 (with (a; q)n the q-Pochhammer symbol):

Σn≥0 qn² / (q; q)n  =  Πm≥0 1 / [(1 − q5m+1)(1 − q5m+4)]

Σn≥0 qn(n+2) / (q; q)n  =  Πm≥0 1 / [(1 − q5m+2)(1 − q5m+3)]

Partition interpretation

Equating coefficients gives the celebrated combinatorial theorems: the number of partitions of an integer n into parts differing by at least 2 equals the number of partitions of n into parts congruent to ±1 (mod 5); and the number of partitions of n into parts ≥ 2 differing by at least 2 equals the number of partitions of n into parts congruent to ±2 (mod 5). Issai Schur proved these combinatorially in 1917, and the identities were generalized by Gordon (the Andrews–Gordon identities) in the 1960s–70s.

History

Ramanujan communicated the identities to G. H. Hardy, who could not prove them; Ramanujan then found Rogers's 1894 paper in back volumes of the Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society and wrote to Rogers, leading to Rogers's new proof published in 1917. MacMahon had already included the partition theorems in his Combinatory Analysis. The identities have since appeared in surprisingly diverse areas: Rodney Baxter's solution of the hard hexagon model in statistical mechanics (1980) — which won him the Boltzmann Medal — uses them centrally, and they recur in conformal field theory, affine Lie algebra representation theory, and vertex operator algebras. The Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction is a related object Ramanujan introduced in his first letter to Hardy.
