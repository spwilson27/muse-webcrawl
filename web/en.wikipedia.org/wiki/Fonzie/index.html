Arthur Herbert Fonzarelli, better known as "Fonzie" or "The Fonz", is a fictional character played by Henry Winkler in the American television sitcom Happy Days (1974–1984). Originally a secondary character — a leather-jacketed, motorcycle-riding greaser and high-school dropout — Fonzie became the breakout star of the series and one of the most recognisable characters in American television history.

Character

Fonzie is depicted as the epitome of cool: laconic, confident and effortlessly popular, he commands instant respect, famously able to start the jukebox at Arnold's diner with a tap and to summon admirers with a snap of his fingers. His catchphrases include "Ayyy" (delivered with two thumbs up) and "Sit on it." Beneath the tough image, the character developed into a loyal friend and mentor to Richie Cunningham and his family, eventually moving into the apartment above the Cunninghams' garage, earning his high-school diploma, and becoming a teacher and part-owner of Arnold's.

Development and popularity

Network standards initially restricted how the character could be shown — his leather jacket was at first permitted only in scenes with his motorcycle, so writers contrived to include the bike frequently; the jacket became his permanent costume as the character's popularity grew. Fonzie's leather jacket is now held by the Smithsonian Institution. In a 1977 episode Fonzie water-skied over a shark, giving rise to the phrase "jumping the shark" for the moment a creative work passes its peak.

In 2008 a bronze statue of the character, the Bronze Fonz, was unveiled on the Milwaukee Riverwalk.
