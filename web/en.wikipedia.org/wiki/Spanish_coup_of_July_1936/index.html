Spanish coup of July 1936

The Spanish coup of July 1936 was a military uprising against the government of the Second Spanish Republic that began on 17 July 1936 in Spanish Morocco and spread to the mainland over the following days. Its partial failure — the rebels seized only part of the country — precipitated the Spanish Civil War.

Background

The Republic's polarised politics, land and church conflicts, and the victory of the Popular Front in February 1936 convinced a group of generals, including Emilio Mola (the conspiracy's chief planner), José Sanjurjo, Gonzalo Queipo de Llano and Francisco Franco, to overthrow the government. Sanjurjo died in a plane crash on 20 July while flying to take command, leaving Franco to emerge eventually as the Nationalist leader.

The rising

The revolt began in Melilla on 17 July, a day early after the plot was discovered, and Franco proclaimed a state of war from the Canary Islands before flying to Morocco to command the Army of Africa. On the mainland the coup succeeded in much of the north and west and in Seville, but failed in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and the Basque Country, where workers' organisations, loyal police and soldiers crushed the garrisons. The government's decision to arm trade unions turned a coup into a war.

Consequences

With neither side able to win quickly, Germany and Italy airlifted Franco's colonial troops to the mainland — the first major military airlift — internationalising the conflict that lasted until April 1939 and ended with Franco's dictatorship.
