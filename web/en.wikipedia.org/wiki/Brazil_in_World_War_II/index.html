Brazil in World War II

Brazil participated in the Second World War on the side of the Allies from August 1942 until the end of the war in 1945. It was the only South American country to send combat troops overseas, deploying an infantry division — the Brazilian Expeditionary Force (FEB) — of some 25,000 men to fight in the Italian Campaign, together with a fighter squadron, while its navy and air force fought German U-boats in the Battle of the Atlantic. About 948 Brazilian military personnel were killed in action.

Road to war

Under President Getúlio Vargas, Brazil initially maintained neutrality while trading with both sides, but moved toward the United States after the 1938–41 period, granting the U.S. air bases in the north-east (notably at Natal, the "Trampoline to Victory") in exchange for American support for Brazilian industrialisation, including the Volta Redonda steel mill. In January 1942 Brazil broke diplomatic relations with the Axis. Between February and August 1942 German and Italian submarines sank numerous Brazilian merchant ships, killing hundreds of civilians; amid public outrage, Brazil declared war on Germany and Italy on 22 August 1942.

South Atlantic

The Brazilian Navy, operating with the U.S. Fourth Fleet, escorted convoys and conducted anti-submarine patrols along the Brazilian coast, while Brazilian Air Force aircraft attacked U-boats off the north-east. Brazilian forces were credited with assisting in the sinking of several German submarines, including U-590 and U-662, and possibly U-513.

Italian Campaign

The Brazilian Expeditionary Force, commanded by General Mascarenhas de Morais and attached to the U.S. IV Corps of the Fifth Army, arrived in Naples from July 1944. It fought in the Serchio Valley and the final spring offensive of 1945, taking part in the battles of Monte Castello, Castelnuovo, Montese, and Collecchio-Fornovo, where it accepted the surrender of the German 148th Infantry Division along with Italian fascist units — some 20,000 prisoners in total. The Brazilian 1st Fighter Group flew P-47 Thunderbolts in ground-attack missions under the U.S. 350th Fighter Group. The FEB's shoulder insignia — a snake smoking a pipe — mocked the pre-war prediction that Brazil would join the fighting "when snakes smoke".

Consequences

The participation of a dictatorship in a war for democracy sharpened contradictions at home: returning veterans and public pressure contributed to Vargas's removal in October 1945 and the restoration of democratic elections. Brazil's wartime contribution also cemented its close post-war alignment with the United States and its founding membership in the United Nations.
