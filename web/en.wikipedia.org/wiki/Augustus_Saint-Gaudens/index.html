Augustus Saint-Gaudens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Augustus Saint-Gaudens (March 1, 1848 – August 3, 1907) was an Irish-American sculptor of the Beaux-Arts generation who embodied the ideals of the American Renaissance. Saint-Gaudens was born in Dublin to an Irish mother and a French father and raised in New York City. He traveled to Europe for further training and artistic study, and then returned to New York, where he achieved major critical success for his monuments commemorating heroes of the American Civil War, many of which still stand. Saint-Gaudens created works such as the Robert Gould Shaw Memorial on Boston Common and grand equestrian monuments to Civil War generals. He also created the gold coinage of 1907, the $20 double eagle, considered one of the most beautiful coins ever issued by the United States.

Career

Apprenticed as a cameo cutter as a teenager, Saint-Gaudens studied at the Cooper Union and the National Academy of Design before moving to Paris in 1867, where he trained at the École des Beaux-Arts under François Jouffroy. His first major commission, a statue of Admiral David Farragut (1881) in New York's Madison Square, was a triumph that established his reputation.

Among his best-known works are the Adams Memorial (1891) in Washington, D.C., a haunting hooded figure commissioned by Henry Adams; the Diana that crowned the tower of Madison Square Garden; the Shaw Memorial (1897), depicting Colonel Robert Gould Shaw and the African American soldiers of the 54th Massachusetts Regiment; and the standing Lincoln in Chicago. At the invitation of President Theodore Roosevelt, he redesigned American gold coinage; his double eagle design, struck from 1907, is widely considered the most beautiful United States coin.

Legacy

Saint-Gaudens's home and studio in Cornish, New Hampshire — the centre of the Cornish Art Colony — are preserved as the Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park. He mentored a generation of American sculptors and helped found the American Academy in Rome.
