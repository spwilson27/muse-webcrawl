Hugh Tracey

Hugh Travers Tracey (29 January 1903 – 23 October 1977) was an English ethnomusicologist who made pioneering field recordings of indigenous music across southern and central Africa. He founded the International Library of African Music (ILAM) and the journal African Music.

Life and work

Born in Willand, Devon, Tracey went to Southern Rhodesia as a young man to farm tobacco, where he began learning the songs of his Karanga workers and realised that African music was being neither recorded nor understood on its own terms. With a grant from the Carnegie Corporation he undertook his first recording expedition in 1932. Over the following decades he travelled more than 100,000 miles through Africa, recording some 35,000 tracks of music on discs and later magnetic tape — the largest archive of its kind assembled by one person.

International Library of African Music

In 1954 Tracey established the International Library of African Music at Roodepoort, South Africa, later moved to Rhodes University in Grahamstown (Makhanda), where it remains a major research archive. He devised the Tracey system of measuring and documenting African instruments, helped popularise the mbira and kalimba (the "Hugh Tracey kalimba" is still manufactured), and recorded landmark series such as The Sound of Africa. His sons Andrew and Paul continued his work, and his recordings have been reissued and sampled widely, notably in the Deep Forest project.
