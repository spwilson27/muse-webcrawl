European XFEL

Image: European XFEL (placeholder)

The European XFEL (European X-Ray Free-Electron Laser Facility) is an X-ray research laser facility in the Hamburg area of Germany. Inaugurated in 2017, it produces extremely bright, ultrashort flashes of X-ray light — 27,000 times per second — allowing scientists to image matter at atomic resolution and film chemical reactions as they happen.

Design

The facility is 3.4 kilometres long, running mostly underground from the DESY campus in Hamburg-Bahrenfeld to the town of Schenefeld in Schleswig-Holstein. A superconducting linear accelerator accelerates electrons to energies up to 17.5 GeV; the electrons then pass through long undulator magnets, emitting coherent X-ray pulses by the self-amplified spontaneous emission (SASE) process.

Organisation and research

European XFEL is operated by an international non-profit company whose shareholders include Germany, Russia (whose participation has been suspended since 2022), France, Italy, Poland, Sweden, Switzerland, and other partner countries. Its instruments support research in structural biology, materials science, plasma physics, and chemistry, and its MHz pulse repetition rate distinguishes it from other hard X-ray free-electron lasers such as LCLS in the United States.
