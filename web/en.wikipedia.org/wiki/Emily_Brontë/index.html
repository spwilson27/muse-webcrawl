Emily Jane Brontë (30 July 1818 – 19 December 1848) was an English novelist and poet best known for her only novel, Wuthering Heights, now considered a classic of English literature. She published under the pen name Ellis Bell.

Life

Emily was the fifth of the six Brontë children, born at Thornton and raised at the parsonage in Haworth on the Yorkshire moors after her father became perpetual curate there. Her mother died in 1821, and two elder sisters died after attending the Clergy Daughters' School at Cowan Bridge. With her siblings Charlotte, Branwell and Anne, Emily created elaborate imaginary worlds — in her case the island kingdom of Gondal — that filled notebooks with prose and poetry throughout her life.

She worked briefly as a teacher at Law Hill School near Halifax and in 1842 studied in Brussels with Charlotte, returning to Haworth after their aunt's death. Intensely private and attached to the moors, she thereafter remained at home, doing much of the housework and writing.

Works

In 1846 the sisters published Poems by Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell at their own expense; Emily's poems, including "No coward soul is mine", are now regarded as the finest of the collection. Wuthering Heights appeared in December 1847 in a three-volume set with Anne's Agnes Grey. Its innovative structure, stark portrayal of passion and cruelty, and elemental moorland setting puzzled many early reviewers, but its reputation grew steadily after her death.

Death and legacy

Emily caught a chill at her brother Branwell's funeral in September 1848 and died of tuberculosis on 19 December 1848, aged 30, refusing medical help until the end. Charlotte edited a posthumous edition of Wuthering Heights in 1850. The novel is now among the most celebrated in English literature, and Emily's poetry is admired for its intensity and originality.
