3-j symbol

In quantum mechanics, the Wigner 3-j symbol is a coefficient used when coupling two angular momenta, introduced by Eugene Wigner. It is closely related to the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients but has greater symmetry, which makes it more convenient in many calculations involving angular momentum, spherical harmonics and the theory of atomic spectra.

Definition

The 3-j symbol is written as a two-row array of six numbers:

( j1   j2   j3 ;   m1   m2   m3 )

where the j values are angular momentum quantum numbers (integers or half-integers) and the m values are their projections. The symbol can be non-zero only when m1 + m2 + m3 = 0 and when the three j values satisfy the triangle inequalities |j1 − j2| ≤ j3 ≤ j1 + j2. The 3-j symbol is proportional to the corresponding Clebsch–Gordan coefficient, differing by a phase and a normalisation factor involving the square root of (2j3 + 1).

Symmetry properties

A distinguishing feature of the 3-j symbol is its high symmetry under permutation of its columns: interchanging any two columns leaves the symbol unchanged if the sum j1 + j2 + j3 is an even integer, and changes its sign if that sum is odd. In addition, changing the sign of all three m values multiplies the symbol by (−1)j1+j2+j3. These symmetries have no counterpart in the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients and greatly simplify manipulations in angular momentum algebra.

Applications

3-j symbols appear throughout quantum mechanics and mathematical physics: in the Wigner–Eckart theorem, in integrals of three spherical harmonics over a sphere (Gaunt integrals), in atomic and nuclear spectroscopy, and in the analysis of the cosmic microwave background. They are special cases of the more general 6-j and 9-j symbols used when coupling three or four angular momenta.
