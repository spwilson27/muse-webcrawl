Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor

SpecialtyOncology, endocrinology
OriginPancreatic islet (endocrine) cells
TypesFunctioning and non-functioning; graded G1–G3, plus poorly differentiated NEC

Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PNETs), also known as pancreatic endocrine tumours or islet cell tumours, are a group of uncommon neuroendocrine neoplasms arising from the hormone-producing cells of the pancreatic islets. They account for only a few percent of all pancreatic tumours and generally behave very differently from the common pancreatic adenocarcinoma, often growing more slowly. Some 5–10% occur as part of inherited syndromes such as multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) or von Hippel–Lindau disease.

Classification

PNETs are classified as functioning if they secrete hormones in excess, producing a clinical syndrome, or non-functioning if they do not. Functioning types are named for their hormone: insulinoma (insulin), gastrinoma (gastrin; causing Zollinger–Ellison syndrome), glucagonoma (glucagon), VIPoma, and somatostatinoma. Tumours are also graded by proliferative rate (Ki-67 index and mitotic count) into well-differentiated NET grades 1–3, distinct from poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), which are aggressive.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the hormone involved: insulinomas cause episodes of hypoglycaemia (Whipple's triad); gastrinomas cause severe peptic ulceration and diarrhoea; glucagonomas cause a characteristic rash (necrolytic migratory erythema), diabetes and weight loss; and VIPomas cause profuse watery diarrhoea with hypokalaemia. Non-functioning tumours, the majority, often present late with pain, jaundice or a mass, or are found incidentally on imaging.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines cross-sectional imaging (CT/MRI), endoscopic ultrasound with biopsy, hormone assays, and somatostatin-receptor imaging such as gallium-68 DOTATATE PET/CT, which most well-differentiated tumours express. Surgery is the only curative option for localised disease. For advanced disease, treatments include somatostatin analogues, targeted agents such as everolimus and sunitinib, chemotherapy (notably capecitabine/temozolomide), liver-directed therapies, and peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) with lutetium-177 dotatate.

Prognosis

Prognosis varies widely with grade, stage and tumour type; many grade 1–2 tumours progress slowly over years, whereas neuroendocrine carcinomas behave aggressively. The disease received wide public attention when Apple co-founder Steve Jobs died in 2011 of metastatic pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour.
