Girl group

A girl group is a popular music act featuring several female singers who generally harmonise together. The term usually denotes vocal groups whose members sing and perform choreography rather than playing instruments, although all-female bands are sometimes included.

History

Close-harmony sister acts such as the Andrews Sisters preceded the classic 1960s era of the Shirelles, the Supremes, and the Ronettes, whose records helped define the "girl group sound". The format revived in the 1990s with the Spice Girls, TLC, and Destiny's Child, and later became central to K-pop, with groups such as Girls' Generation, Blackpink, and Twice achieving global success.

Characteristics

Girl groups typically share lead vocals, emphasise harmonies and visual presentation, and market distinct member personas to fans.
