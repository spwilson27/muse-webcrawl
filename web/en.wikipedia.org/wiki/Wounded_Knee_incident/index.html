The occupation of Wounded Knee (also called the Wounded Knee incident) was a 71-day standoff beginning on 27 February 1973, when about 200 Oglala Lakota and followers of the American Indian Movement (AIM) seized the town of Wounded Knee on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in South Dakota.

Background

The site was chosen for its symbolism: Wounded Knee Creek was the location of the 1890 massacre of Lakota by the U.S. Army. Protesters objected to the administration of tribal chairman Richard Wilson, accused of corruption and intimidation, and to the U.S. government's failure to honour treaties with Native American nations. An attempt to impeach Wilson had failed shortly before the occupation.

The standoff

Federal marshals, FBI agents and later military advisors surrounded the town. Gunfire was exchanged intermittently; two occupiers — Frank Clearwater and Lawrence "Buddy" Lamont — were killed, and a U.S. marshal was paralysed by a wound. Journalists covered the siege extensively, bringing national attention to Native American grievances. The occupation ended on 8 May 1973 under a negotiated agreement providing for a federal investigation of tribal government and treaty issues.

Aftermath

AIM leaders Russell Means and Dennis Banks were tried on charges arising from the occupation; the case was dismissed in 1974 because of prosecutorial misconduct. Violence on Pine Ridge continued during the following years, including the 1975 shootout that led to the conviction of Leonard Peltier.
