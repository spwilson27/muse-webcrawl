USS Constellation (1797)

USS Constellation was a nominally rated 38-gun wooden-hulled, three-masted frigate of the United States Navy. She was built under the direction of David Stodder at the Joseph and Samuel Sterett shipyard on Harris Creek in Baltimore's Fell's Point maritime community, and she was launched on 7 September 1797. She was one of the original six frigates whose construction the Naval Act of 1794 had authorized.

Launched: 7 September 1797
Builder: David Stodder, Baltimore
Type: 38-gun frigate
Fate: Broken up 1853

Quasi-War

Constellation first sailed under Captain Thomas Truxtun and served in the Quasi-War with France. On 9 February 1799 she captured the French frigate L'Insurgente in the first major action of the undeclared war, earning the nickname "Yankee Racehorse" for her speed. A year later, on 1 February 1800, she fought a hard night action against the French frigate La Vengeance, badly mauling her opponent, though damage to her own rigging prevented a capture.

Barbary Wars and the War of 1812

The frigate served in the Mediterranean during the First Barbary War against Tripoli. During the War of 1812 she was blockaded in Norfolk, Virginia, and played a shore-defense role at Craney Island, helping to repel British attacks. She later made cruises to the West Indies, South America, and the Pacific.

Later service and fate

Constellation continued in service into the 1840s, including a notable cruise to China in 1841–42. She was broken up in 1853 at the Gosport (Norfolk) Navy Yard. Her name and some material were associated with a new sloop-of-war launched in 1854, also named Constellation, now preserved as a museum ship in Baltimore — long the subject of historical debate over whether the two ships were the same vessel rebuilt.
