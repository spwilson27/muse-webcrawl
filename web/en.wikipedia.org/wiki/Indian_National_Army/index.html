Indian National Army

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian National Army (INA), also known as the Azad Hind Fauj, was a collaborationist armed force formed by Indian nationalists in Southeast Asia during World War II. Its aim was to secure Indian independence from British rule. It was first formed in 1942 under Mohan Singh from Indian prisoners of war captured by Japan in the Malayan campaign and at Singapore, collapsed later that year, and was revived in 1943 under the leadership of Subhas Chandra Bose.

First INA

Following the fall of Singapore in February 1942, some 40,000 Indian prisoners of war were handed over by the Japanese to Captain Mohan Singh, an officer of the British Indian Army who, with the support of Japanese intelligence officer Major Fujiwara Iwaichi, organized the first Indian National Army. Disagreements with the Japanese over the army's role led to its disbandment in December 1942 and Mohan Singh's arrest.

Second INA under Bose

Subhas Chandra Bose, who had escaped from India in 1941 and broadcast from Germany, arrived in Japanese-held Singapore in July 1943 aboard a submarine transfer from the German U-boat U-180. He took command of the revived INA and proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind) on 21 October 1943. The INA's regiments included the Gandhi, Nehru and Azad brigades, and the all-women Rani of Jhansi Regiment commanded by Lakshmi Sahgal. Its battle cries included "Chalo Delhi" ("On to Delhi") and "Jai Hind".

Operations

The INA fought alongside the Imperial Japanese Army in the Burma campaign. In March 1944 it crossed into India during the U-Go offensive, hoisting its flag at Moirang in Manipur, but the siege of Imphal and the fighting at Kohima ended in a disastrous Japanese retreat. Bose died in August 1945 following an air crash at Taihoku (Taipei), though the circumstances of his death remain contested.

Trials and legacy

After the war, the British put INA officers on trial for treason at the Red Fort in Delhi. The first trial, of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon—a Hindu, a Muslim and a Sikh—sparked mass protests across India and a mutiny in the Royal Indian Navy in February 1946. The INA trials are widely regarded as having hastened the end of British rule. The INA's veterans were not inducted into the post-independence Indian Army, but the army is honoured in Indian public memory; "Jai Hind" remains a common patriotic salutation.
