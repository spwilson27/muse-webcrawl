Yu Gwan-sun (Korean: 유관순; 16 December 1902 – 28 September 1920) was a Korean independence activist who organised demonstrations in her home county during the March 1st Movement against Japanese colonial rule in 1919. She was arrested, tortured and died in prison at the age of seventeen. She is one of the most famous and beloved figures of the Korean independence movement, often called "Korea's Joan of Arc".

Early life

Yu was born in a village near Cheonan, in present-day South Chungcheong Province, to a Christian family. She attended Ewha Haktang (later Ewha Womans University) in Seoul on a scholarship, where she was among the students who witnessed and joined the first independence demonstrations of March 1919 after the proclamation of Korean independence by student and religious leaders.

Activism

When the Japanese authorities closed her school, Yu returned home and, with her family, organised a demonstration in the Aunae market town on 1 April 1919, drawing some 3,000 people. Japanese military police fired on the crowd, killing 19 people, including both her parents. Yu was arrested and convicted of sedition and security-law violations. Transferred to Seodaemun Prison in Seoul, she continued to protest from her cell, organising a demonstration among inmates on the first anniversary of the movement in March 1920.

Death and legacy

Severely beaten and tortured by prison guards after the anniversary protest, Yu died in Seodaemun Prison on 28 September 1920, aged seventeen. Her body was recovered by Ewha teachers and buried, though her grave was later lost during cemetery relocations; a cenotaph and memorial hall now stand in Cheonan. In 1962 she was posthumously awarded the Order of Merit for National Foundation, Independence Medal grade. Statues, a memorial shrine, films and annual commemorations honour her as a symbol of youthful, non-violent resistance to colonial rule.
