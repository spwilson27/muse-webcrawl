The September 11 attacks, commonly known as 9/11, were a series of four coordinated suicide terrorist attacks carried out by the militant Islamist network al-Qaeda against the United States on Tuesday, September 11, 2001. Nineteen terrorists hijacked four commercial airliners scheduled to travel from the East Coast to California. Two of the planes were flown into the Twin Towers of the World Trade Center in New York City, a third struck the Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia, and the fourth crashed in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after passengers attempted to retake the aircraft.

The attacks

American Airlines Flight 11 struck the North Tower at 8:46 a.m., and United Airlines Flight 175 struck the South Tower at 9:03 a.m. Both towers collapsed within two hours of being hit, the South Tower at 9:59 a.m. and the North Tower at 10:28 a.m. American Airlines Flight 77 crashed into the Pentagon at 9:37 a.m. United Airlines Flight 93, believed to be headed for Washington, D.C., crashed in Pennsylvania at 10:03 a.m. after a passenger revolt.

Casualties

The attacks killed 2,977 people (excluding the 19 hijackers) and injured thousands more. They remain the deadliest terrorist attacks in history and the single deadliest incident for firefighters and law enforcement officers in United States history, killing 343 firefighters and 72 officers.

Aftermath

The United States responded by launching the War on Terror and invading Afghanistan to depose the Taliban regime, which had harboured al-Qaeda. The USA PATRIOT Act was enacted, and the Department of Homeland Security was created. Osama bin Laden, who claimed responsibility for the attacks, was killed by U.S. forces in Pakistan in May 2011. A new World Trade Center complex was built on the site in Lower Manhattan, including the One World Trade Center skyscraper, and memorials were established at all three attack sites.
