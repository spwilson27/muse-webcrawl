Georgia (Georgian: საქართველო, Sakartvelo) is a country in the Caucasus region at the intersection of Eastern Europe and Western Asia. It is bounded by the Black Sea to the west, Russia to the north, Turkey and Armenia to the south, and Azerbaijan to the south-east. The capital and largest city is Tbilisi. Georgia is one of the world's oldest Christian nations and the homeland of one of the earliest wine-making traditions.

History

The ancient kingdoms of Colchis (on the Black Sea coast, famed in Greek myth as the land of the Golden Fleece) and Iberia dominated the region in classical antiquity. Georgia adopted Christianity in the 4th century, traditionally dated to 337 AD through the preaching of Saint Nino. The medieval Kingdom of Georgia reached its golden age under King David IV "the Builder" (r. 1089–1125) and Queen Tamar (r. 1184–1213), when it stretched across much of the Caucasus and produced the poet Shota Rustaveli and his epic The Knight in the Panther's Skin.

Mongol invasions and later Ottoman and Persian pressure fragmented the kingdom, and in 1801 the Russian Empire annexed its eastern kingdoms; the whole country was incorporated into Russia by the 1860s. Georgia declared independence as a democratic republic in 1918, but the Red Army invaded in 1921 and it became part of the Soviet Union. Georgia restored its independence on 9 April 1991 following a referendum.

The post-Soviet decades brought civil conflict and the secession, with Russian support, of Abkhazia and South Ossetia. The peaceful Rose Revolution of 2003 brought Mikheil Saakashvili to power on a pro-Western, reformist platform. In August 2008 Georgia fought a five-day war with Russia, after which Russia recognised the two breakaway regions as independent states; Georgia and most of the world regard them as Georgian territory under Russian occupation. Georgia signed an association agreement with the European Union in 2014 and was granted EU candidate status in December 2023.

Geography

Georgia is a mountainous country dominated by the Greater Caucasus range in the north — including Mount Shkhara (5,193 m), the country's highest peak — and the Lesser Caucasus in the south. The Likhi range divides the wetter, subtropical west from the drier east. Major rivers include the Mtkvari (Kura) and the Rioni. The climate ranges from humid subtropical on the Black Sea coast to alpine in the high mountains.

Culture

Georgian is written in its own unique script and forms the core of the Kartvelian language family. The country is famous for its polyphonic singing (inscribed by UNESCO), its supra feast tradition led by a toastmaster (tamada), its church architecture, and a wine culture that has used buried clay qvevri vessels for some 8,000 years. The majority of Georgians belong to the Georgian Orthodox Church.
