West Japan Railway Company


A JR West 500 series Shinkansen.

The West Japan Railway Company (西日本旅客鉄道株式会社, Nishi-Nihon Ryokaku Tetsudō Kabushiki-gaisha), more commonly known as JR West, is one of the seven constituent companies of the Japan Railways Group (JR Group). It operates passenger rail services in the western part of Honshu, covering the Kansai, Chugoku, and Hokuriku regions, including the cities of Osaka, Kyoto, Kobe, Hiroshima, and Kanazawa. Its headquarters are in Kita-ku, Osaka.

Network

JR West was created on 1 April 1987 when Japanese National Railways was divided and privatised. It operates the Sanyo Shinkansen between Shin-Osaka and Hakata and the Hokuriku Shinkansen west of Joetsumyoko, as well as an extensive conventional network including the busy Osaka Loop Line and commuter routes of the Keihanshin metropolitan area. Its ICOCA smart card is used across much of western Japan. The company also runs buses, ferries, real estate, and retail businesses around its stations.

Amagasaki derailment

On 25 April 2005, a JR West commuter train derailed on the Fukuchiyama Line at Amagasaki, killing 107 people. Investigations criticised the company's safety culture and its harsh "day-shift education" retraining regime for drivers; the accident led to major safety reforms across the company.
