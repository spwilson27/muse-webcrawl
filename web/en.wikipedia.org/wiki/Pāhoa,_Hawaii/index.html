Pāhoa, Hawaii

State: Hawaii

Pāhoa is a census-designated place and unincorporated village in the District of Puna on the Island of Hawaii, the largest island in the Hawaiian chain. The name derives from a type of dagger, pāhoa. The town serves as the commercial hub of lower Puna, a rural district known for rainforest, papaya farming, and an alternative-lifestyle community.

2014 lava flow

Pāhoa came to international attention in 2014 when a lava flow from the Puʻu ʻŌʻō vent of Kīlauea advanced on the town. The June 27 flow crossed the village cemetery, burned one house, and forced the closure of the main road and relocation of services before stalling; the town's transfer station was protected by emergency measures. The episode highlighted the exposure of Puna's subdivisions, many built on young lava flows in Lava Hazard Zones 1 and 2.

2018 eruption

During the 2018 lower Puna eruption, fissures opened in the Leilani Estates subdivision near Pāhoa, destroying hundreds of homes; Pāhoa served as the relief and shelter centre for displaced residents, and its geothermal plant (PGV) was shut down as lava approached.
