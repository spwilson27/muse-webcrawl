Dean (Christianity)

The dean is the senior cleric of a cathedral chapter

A dean, in an ecclesiastical context, is a cleric holding certain positions of authority within a religious hierarchy. The title is derived from the Latin decanus, originally the leader of a group of ten soldiers or monks. In modern church usage the term covers several distinct offices, most prominently the head of the chapter of a cathedral, and, in the Anglican tradition, the rural or area deans who supervise clergy groups within a diocese.

Cathedral deans

In the Roman Catholic Church, the dean of a cathedral chapter is the senior canon; in many places the role is filled by the cathedral's rector or a dean of the chapter elected by the canons. In the Church of England, the dean is the head of the foundation of a cathedral — for example the Dean of St Paul's or the Dean of Canterbury — chairing the cathedral chapter, which is responsible for the governance of the cathedral, its worship and its mission, in cooperation with the bishop, who has his or her seat (cathedra) there. English cathedral deans are appointed by the Crown or the bishop depending on the foundation, and since the Cathedrals Measure 2021 they lead chapters with strengthened governance structures. In the Scottish Episcopal Church and the (Presbyterian) Church of Scotland, related usages survive: the Dean of the Chapel Royal in Scotland is a royal chaplaincy office.

Deans of colleges and peculiars

Historically, some collegiate churches and chapels royal in England were headed by deans, such as the Dean of Westminster, who presides over Westminster Abbey — a "royal peculiar" outside diocesan jurisdiction — and the Dean of St George's Chapel, Windsor. The dean and canons of such foundations report directly to the monarch.

Rural and area deans

In the Church of England, a rural dean (increasingly styled area dean) is a parish priest appointed by the bishop to exercise a measure of pastoral oversight and administrative leadership over the clergy and parishes of a deanery, convening the deanery synod and chapter and acting as a channel between parishes and the diocesan administration. Equivalent offices exist in several other Anglican provinces and, as vicars forane or deans, in Roman Catholic deaneries, where a dean coordinates the parishes of a subdivision of a diocese.

Dean of the College of Cardinals

The most senior dean in the Roman Catholic Church is the Dean of the College of Cardinals, the first among the cardinal bishops, who presides over the College, performs functions such as announcing the election of a new pope, and ordains the pope to the episcopate if necessary. The office is elected by the cardinal bishops and approved by the pope.
