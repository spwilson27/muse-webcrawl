Minister of Health (United Kingdom)

The Minister of Health was a Cabinet-level position in the government of the United Kingdom, heading the Ministry of Health from its creation in 1919 until the post was abolished in 1968, when its responsibilities were merged into the new office of Secretary of State for Health and Social Services. The office is historically significant because its holder Aneurin Bevan oversaw the establishment of the National Health Service (NHS) in 1948.

History

The Ministry of Health Act 1919 created the ministry, taking over public health and local government functions from the Local Government Board and health insurance functions from other bodies. The first Minister was Christopher Addison. The ministry was responsible for public health, housing, and local government in England and Wales.

In 1942 the Beveridge Report proposed comprehensive social reform, and after the Second World War the Labour government of Clement Attlee appointed Aneurin Bevan as Minister of Health in 1945. Bevan steered the National Health Service Act 1946 through Parliament, and the NHS began operating on 5 July 1948, providing healthcare free at the point of use. Bevan also held responsibility for the post-war council housing programme.

Later years and abolition

Housing was split off into the Ministry of Local Government and Planning in 1951 (later the Ministry of Housing and Local Government), leaving the Minister of Health focused on health services. Notable later holders include Iain Macleod and Enoch Powell. In 1968 the ministry merged with the Ministry of Social Security to form the Department of Health and Social Security under a Secretary of State. The present-day successor in health matters is the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care.
