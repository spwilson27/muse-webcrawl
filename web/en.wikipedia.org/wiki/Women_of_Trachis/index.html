Women of Trachis

Women of Trachis (Ancient Greek: Τραχίνιαι, Trachiniai) is an Athenian tragedy by Sophocles. It tells the story of Deianira, the wife of Heracles, who accidentally kills her husband by sending him a poisoned robe. The play takes its name from the women of Trachis who make up the chorus.

Plot

At Trachis, Deianira anxiously awaits the return of Heracles from his labours and campaigns. When a herald announces his victory, captive women are led in, among them Iole, with whom Heracles has fallen in love. Fearing the loss of her husband, Deianira sends him a robe smeared with the blood of the centaur Nessus, which the dying centaur had told her would act as a love-charm. In fact the blood is poisoned with the venom of the Lernaean Hydra. Heracles dons the robe and is consumed by agony; learning what she has done, Deianira kills herself, and Heracles, borne to Mount Oeta, orders his own funeral pyre.

Date

The date of the play is uncertain. Stylistic arguments have led many scholars to place it relatively early in Sophocles' career, perhaps in the 450s or 440s BC, though others date it later. It is one of the seven Sophoclean tragedies to survive complete.

Themes

The play explores the suffering caused by ignorance, the destructiveness of erotic passion, and the gulf between human intention and outcome. Deianira is notable among tragic heroines for her gentleness and good faith; her fatal act is an attempt to preserve her marriage.
