The Embassy of the United States in Beirut is the diplomatic mission of the United States of America in the Republic of Lebanon. The embassy is located in Awkar, a suburb north of the capital Beirut, in the Metn District. Diplomatic relations between the United States and Lebanon were established in 1942, and a legation was raised to embassy status in 1952.

History

The embassy is historically notable for the attacks it suffered during the Lebanese Civil War era. On 18 April 1983, a suicide car bomber detonated at the embassy's chancery in West Beirut, killing 63 people, including 17 Americans. The attack was attributed to the Islamic Jihad Organization with links to Hezbollah and Iran. A second suicide bombing struck the embassy annex in Aukar (Awkar) on 20 September 1984, killing 24 people. Following these attacks, the embassy's operations were consolidated at the Awkar site, which has since been heavily fortified.

New embassy compound

In the 2010s, the United States began construction of a large new embassy compound in Awkar, one of its largest diplomatic complexes worldwide, intended to replace existing facilities. The project has attracted attention for its scale and cost, and construction continued into the 2020s.
