Justice League: The Flashpoint Paradox is a 2013 American animated superhero film directed by Jay Oliva and written by Jim Krieg. It is an adaptation of the 2011 comic book crossover storyline Flashpoint by Geoff Johns and Andy Kubert. The film was released by Warner Bros. Animation on July 30, 2013, and is the first film in the DC Animated Movie Universe continuity.

Plot

Barry Allen, the Flash, wakes up in an altered timeline after travelling back in time to prevent his mother's murder. In this new reality, his powers are gone, his mother is alive, and the world stands on the brink of annihilation: Aquaman and the Atlanteans are at war with Wonder Woman and the Amazons, a conflict that has devastated Western Europe. Bruce Wayne died in Crime Alley instead of his parents; his father Thomas Wayne became a brutal, gun-wielding Batman, while Martha Wayne was driven insane and became the Joker. Superman's rocket landed in Metropolis and he was captured by the government as an infant.

With Batman's help, Barry regains his speed through a dangerous recreation of the accident that gave him his powers, and the two assemble a resistance to stop the war. Barry ultimately confronts his nemesis, Professor Zoom (Eobard Thawne), and realises he must let his mother die to restore the timeline — delivering a letter from Thomas Wayne to Bruce in the restored reality.

Voice cast

Justin Chambers stars as Barry Allen / The Flash, with C. Thomas Howell as Professor Eobard Thawne / Reverse-Flash, Kevin McKidd as Thomas Wayne / Batman, Michael B. Jordan as Victor Stone / Cyborg, Cary Elwes as Aquaman, Vanessa Marshall as Wonder Woman, Nathan Fillion as Hal Jordan / Green Lantern, and Kevin Conroy in a brief appearance as Bruce Wayne / Batman.

Reception

The film received generally positive reviews, praised for its faithful adaptation, darker tone, and action sequences. It launched a shared continuity of animated DC films that continued through Justice League: War (2014) and concluded with Justice League Dark: Apokolips War (2020).
