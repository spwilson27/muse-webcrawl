The Byzantine–Bulgarian wars were a series of conflicts fought between the Byzantines and Bulgarians that began when the Bulgars first settled in the Balkan Peninsula in the 7th century and continued, with interruptions, for nearly seven centuries, ending only with the Ottoman conquest of both states.

First Bulgarian Empire

Khan Asparuh's victory at Ongal (680) forced Constantinople to recognise a Bulgarian state south of the Danube. Khan Krum crushed Emperor Nikephoros I at Pliska in 811, killing the emperor. Under Tsar Simeon I (893–927) Bulgaria reached its greatest extent and Simeon aspired to the imperial throne, but failed before Constantinople's walls. After his death Bulgaria weakened; Emperor Basil II — the "Bulgar Slayer" — systematically conquered it, his victory at Kleidion (1014) being followed, legend holds, by the blinding of thousands of captured soldiers. By 1018 Bulgaria was annexed; see the Cometopuli dynasty for its last rulers.

Second Bulgarian Empire

The uprising of Peter and Asen (1185) restored Bulgarian independence. Tsar Kaloyan defeated the Latin Crusaders at Adrianople (1205), capturing Emperor Baldwin I, and Ivan Asen II made Bulgaria again the dominant Balkan power after Klokotnitsa (1230). Later wars with a revived Byzantium and, increasingly, with Serbia and the Ottomans eroded both empires; the Ottomans conquered Bulgaria by 1396 and Constantinople itself in 1453.
