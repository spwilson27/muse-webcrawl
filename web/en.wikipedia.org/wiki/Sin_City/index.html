Sin City is a series of neo-noir comics by American writer-artist Frank Miller, first published by Dark Horse Comics in 1991. Set in the fictional Basin City — "Sin City" to its inhabitants — the stories follow an ensemble of criminals, vigilantes, corrupt officials, and prostitutes in a violent, morally black-and-white world rendered in Miller's stark black-and-white artwork. The series is considered a landmark of 1990s comics.

Miller's art uses extreme contrasts of black and white with minimal use of colour.

Stories

The first story, later collected as The Hard Goodbye (1991–92), follows the brute Marv as he avenges the murder of Goldie, a prostitute who showed him kindness. Subsequent volumes include A Dame to Kill For, The Big Fat Kill, That Yellow Bastard — notable for its single spot colour, the yellow of its villain — and the collections Booze, Broads & Bullets, Family Values, and Hell and Back. Recurring characters include Dwight McCarthy, the ex-cop John Hartigan, and Nancy Callahan.

Style and themes

Miller drew on hard-boiled crime fiction and film noir, writing in terse first-person narration and drawing with heavy black fields and slashing white highlights, often using silhouette alone to define figures. The stories explore corruption, vengeance, and codes of honour among outcasts in a city where the police, the church, and the Roark crime family are all rotten.

Adaptations

Robert Rodriguez and Miller co-directed the film adaptation Sin City (2005), which reproduced the comics' panels with unusual fidelity using digital backlot techniques; Quentin Tarantino guest-directed one scene. A sequel, Sin City: A Dame to Kill For, followed in 2014. The first film was a critical and commercial success and influenced the wave of stylised comic-book adaptations that followed.
