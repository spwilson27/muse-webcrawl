Venera

The Venera programme (Russian: Венера, "Venus") was a series of space probes developed by the Soviet Union between 1961 and 1984 to explore Venus. The Venera probes were the first human-made objects to enter the atmosphere of another planet, to make a soft landing, to return images from its surface, and to map it by radar.

History

Early probes failed en route or on contact. Venera 4 (1967) analysed the atmosphere by parachute; Venera 7 survived landing, transmitting weakly for about 23 minutes; Venera 8 measured surface light. The heavier second-generation landers Venera 9–14 returned black-and-white and colour panoramas, soil analyses and weather data, surviving up to about two hours in 460°C heat and ~90-atmosphere pressure.

Orbiters and mapping

Venera 15 and 16 (1983) mapped the northern hemisphere by synthetic-aperture radar. Two Venera-design Vega probes in 1984–86 dropped balloons into the Venusian atmosphere en route to Halley's Comet.

Legacy

Venera remains the only programme to have transmitted from the Venusian surface, providing ground truth for temperature, pressure, chemistry and geology used by all later Venus missions.
