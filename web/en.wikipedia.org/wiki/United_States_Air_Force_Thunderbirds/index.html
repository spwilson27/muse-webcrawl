The United States Air Force Thunderbirds are the official air demonstration squadron of the United States Air Force. Formally the U.S. Air Force Air Demonstration Squadron, the team was activated in 1953 at Luke Air Force Base, Arizona, and is based at Nellis Air Force Base, Nevada. Its name derives from the Thunderbird of Native American legend in the southwestern United States.

Performances

The squadron performs at air shows across the United States and abroad, flying formations of six F-16 Fighting Falcons — four in a diamond formation plus two solo aircraft performing opposing manoeuvres — painted in a red, white and blue scheme. The show mixes formation passes demonstrating the precision of Air Force pilots with high-speed solo routines. Team members serve two-year tours; the squadron comprises officers, enlisted maintainers and support staff, and a narrator.

History

Since 1953 the Thunderbirds have flown a succession of aircraft, including the F-84 Thunderjet, the supersonic F-100 Super Sabre, the F-105 Thunderchief, the F-4 Phantom II and the T-38 Talon, before converting to the F-16 in 1982. The team's history includes the 1982 "Diamond Crash" at Indian Springs, in which four pilots were killed during practice — an accident that led to reforms in training and safety. The Thunderbirds are the Air Force counterpart to the U.S. Navy's Blue Angels.
