Docusoap

A docusoap is a genre of television programming that combines elements of documentary filmmaking with those of the soap opera. The term, a portmanteau of "documentary" and "soap opera," is used to describe reality-style programmes that adopt the narrative conventions of serialized drama — continuing storylines, character arcs, cliffhangers and voiceover narration — while following real people in real settings, typically a workplace or institution.

Development

The genre flourished in British television in the late 1990s, with series such as Airport (following staff at Heathrow), Driving School, Paddington Green and The Cruise, which made a star of singer Jane McDonald. Docusoaps typically feature a fixed workplace, a recurring cast of real employees or residents, and editing that heightens interpersonal drama. The format proved cheap to produce and highly rated, spawning imitators worldwide.

Criticism

Media scholars have criticized docusoaps for blurring fact and fiction: although participants are real, situations are frequently staged or prompted by producers, storylines are shaped in the edit, and participants may play up to the cameras. The genre is considered a precursor of later structured reality television formats.
