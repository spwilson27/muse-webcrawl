Antonio Negri

Antonio Negri (1 August 1933 – 16 December 2023) was an Italian political philosopher known as a leading theorist of autonomist Marxism (operaismo). He co-authored, with Michael Hardt, the influential book Empire (2000) and its sequels Multitude and Commonwealth.

Born1 August 1933, Padua, Italy
Died16 December 2023, Paris, France
OccupationPhilosopher, academic
Known forAutonomist Marxism; Empire (with Michael Hardt)

Life and work

Negri was a professor of political science at the University of Padua and a founder of the Potere Operaio group. In 1979 he was arrested and accused of involvement with the Red Brigades and the kidnapping of Aldo Moro; although the most serious charges collapsed, he was convicted on lesser charges and spent years in prison and exile in France. There he developed the ideas, drawn from Spinoza and Marx, that informed Empire, which analysed a new form of global sovereignty and proposed the "multitude" as a revolutionary subject. He returned to Italy in 1997 to serve the remainder of his sentence, and later lived in Paris and Venice.
