Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority

HFEA
Formed: 1 August 1991
Type: Non-departmental public body
Parent: Department of Health and Social Care

The Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority (HFEA) is the United Kingdom's independent regulator of fertility treatment and research using human embryos. It was the world's first statutory body of its type.

History

The HFEA was established by the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act 1990, following the Warnock Report of 1984, and began work on 1 August 1991. The 1990 Act, substantially amended in 2008, legalized and regulated IVF, donor insemination and embryo research under licence, including the rule that embryos generally may not be kept or used beyond 14 days after creation.

Functions

The Authority licenses and inspects fertility clinics and research centres, maintains a code of practice, keeps the statutory register of treatments and donors (donor-conceived people may obtain identifying information about donors registered after 2005 on turning 18), sets licence fees, and publishes clinic success-rate data for patients through its "Choose a Fertility Clinic" service.

Policy areas

The HFEA has overseen controversial developments including preimplantation genetic testing, the licensing of mitochondrial donation techniques (legalized in 2015, making the UK the first country to permit them), and reviews of add-on treatments and gamete donor compensation.
