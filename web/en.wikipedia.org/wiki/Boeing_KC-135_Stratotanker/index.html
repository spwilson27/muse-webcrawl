The Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker is an American military aerial refuelling tanker aircraft that was developed from the Boeing 367-80 prototype, alongside the Boeing 707 airliner. It is the predominant variant of the C-135 Stratolifter family of transport aircraft. The KC-135 was the United States Air Force's first jet-powered refuelling tanker and replaced the piston-engined KC-97 Stratotanker.

The KC-135 was initially tasked with refuelling strategic bombers, but it was used extensively in the Vietnam War and later conflicts such as Operation Desert Storm to extend the range and endurance of US tactical fighter and bomber aircraft. The type has operated in the strategic aerial refuelling role for more than six decades and is supplemented by the larger KC-10 Extender; it is itself being replaced in part by the KC-46 Pegasus.

Design and development

Like its sibling, the commercial Boeing 707 jetliner, the KC-135 was derived from the Boeing 367-80 "Dash 80" demonstrator of 1954. The KC-135 has a narrower fuselage than the 707 and a flying boom as its primary fuel transfer method, operated by a boom operator lying prone in the rear fuselage; a drogue adapter can be fitted for probe-and-drogue refuelling, and some aircraft carry wing-mounted multipoint refuelling pods.

Most surviving aircraft were re-engined with CFM56 engines as the KC-135R, greatly improving fuel efficiency and reducing noise. The fleet has also received avionics upgrades under programs such as Pacer-CRAG and Block 45, and the USAF expects some KC-135s to serve for up to a century after their manufacture.
