Order of the Engineer

The Order of the Engineer is an association for graduate and professional engineers in the United States that emphasizes pride and responsibility in the engineering profession. It was inspired by the Canadian Calling of an Engineer ceremony, in which graduates accept an obligation and wear an iron ring.

History

The first American ceremony was held on 4 June 1970 at Cleveland State University, organised by students and faculty who had learned of the Canadian ritual developed in the 1920s with the involvement of Rudyard Kipling. The Order has since inducted hundreds of thousands of engineers through local sections called "links" at universities and professional societies across the United States.

The Obligation

Inductees sign and recite an Obligation — a creed akin to the Hippocratic Oath — pledging to practise integrity and fair dealing, to tolerate and respect diversity, and to give the public their best professional judgment. They then receive a plain stainless steel Engineer's Ring, worn on the little finger of the working (dominant) hand so that it drags against the writing surface as a constant reminder.

Eligibility

Membership is open to engineering graduates of accredited programmes, licensed professional engineers, and certain senior engineering students near graduation. The Order is independent of licensing bodies; its purpose is ethical and symbolic rather than regulatory.
