Solicitor General for Scotland

The Solicitor General for Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Àrd-neach-lagha a' Chrùin an Alba) is the deputy of the Lord Advocate, one of the two Law Officers of the Crown in Scotland. Together with the Lord Advocate, the Solicitor General heads the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, which is responsible for all prosecutions in Scotland.

Role

The Solicitor General is a minister of the Scottish Government and supports the Lord Advocate in providing legal advice to the government and in exercising prosecutorial functions independently of political influence. Under the Scotland Act 1998, the Lord Advocate and Solicitor General are appointed by the monarch on the recommendation of the First Minister, with the agreement of the Scottish Parliament. Either Law Officer may deputise for the other across the full range of duties.

History

The office has existed since the sixteenth century, when the Solicitor acted as assistant to the Lord Advocate in the King's (later Queen's and Lord) Advocate's department. Following devolution in 1999, the Law Officers became members of the Scottish Executive, and their role in criminal prosecution was preserved in statute as independent of ministerial direction.

Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service

The Solicitor General shares responsibility for the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS), Scotland's sole public prosecuting authority, which investigates and prosecutes crime and investigates sudden and suspicious deaths. Advocates depute and procurators fiscal carry out prosecutions in the High Court of Justiciary, sheriff courts and justice of the peace courts in the name of the Lord Advocate.
