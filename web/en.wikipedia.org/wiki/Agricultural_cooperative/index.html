Agricultural cooperative

An agricultural cooperative, also known as a farmers' co-op, is a cooperative in which farmers pool their resources in certain areas of activity, typically marketing their produce, purchasing supplies, or sharing services and equipment. Like other cooperatives, they are owned and democratically controlled by their members on a one-member, one-vote basis.

Types

Marketing cooperatives collectively process, pack, and sell members' output — dairy cooperatives such as India's Amul and New Zealand's Fonterra are among the world's largest food businesses. Supply cooperatives buy inputs like seed, fertilizer, fuel, and equipment at scale. Service cooperatives provide credit, insurance, storage, transport, and electricity, and bargaining cooperatives negotiate prices with buyers on members' behalf.

History and scale

Modern agricultural cooperation traces to nineteenth-century Europe, including the Raiffeisen credit cooperatives in Germany and Danish dairy cooperatives. Agricultural cooperatives remain economically central in many countries: they handle large shares of milk, grain, and wine production in the European Union, Japan, and North America, and were instrumental in India's "White Revolution" milk expansion.
