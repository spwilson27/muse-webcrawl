Gustave Eiffel

Alexandre Gustave Eiffel
Born: 15 December 1832, Dijon, France
Died: 27 December 1923, Paris
Occupation: Civil engineer, entrepreneur
Known for: Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty framework

Alexandre Gustave Eiffel (1832–1923) was a French civil engineer best known for the Eiffel Tower in Paris, built for the 1889 World's Fair, and for designing the internal metal framework of the Statue of Liberty.

Early career

Graduating from École Centrale Paris in 1855, Eiffel specialised in iron bridges and viaducts, including the Maria Pia Bridge in Porto and the Garabit Viaduct. His company became renowned for prefabricated iron structures shipped worldwide.

Eiffel Tower

Designed by Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier in Eiffel's company, the 330-metre tower was the world's tallest structure until 1930. Initially criticised by artists, it became a global icon of France and a landmark of iron architecture.

Later work and Panama scandal

Eiffel was implicated in the Panama Canal financial scandal and convicted (later cleared on appeal of fraud charges effectively overturned), after which he devoted himself to aerodynamics and meteorology, conducting wind-tunnel experiments from the tower.

Legacy

His name is inscribed among the 72 names on the Eiffel Tower of French scientists and engineers.
