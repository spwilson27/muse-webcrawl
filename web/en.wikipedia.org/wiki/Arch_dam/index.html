An arch dam is a concrete dam that curves upstream, so that the force of the reservoir water presses against the arch and is transferred into the valley walls (abutments) rather than being resisted by the dam's weight alone. This allows a much thinner structure than a gravity dam, provided the canyon is narrow and the rock sound.

Design

Arch dams may be single- or double-curvature (curved vertically as well); arch-gravity dams such as Hoover Dam combine arch action with mass. Engineering analysis uses trial-load and finite-element methods to distribute stresses between arch and cantilever action.

History and failures

Roman and medieval precursors existed, but modern arch dams began in the 19th century. The 1959 Malpasset Dam failure in France, caused by abutment rock failure, remains a defining case study in dam engineering.
