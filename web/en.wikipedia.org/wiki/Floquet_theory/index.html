Floquet theory

Floquet theory is a branch of the theory of ordinary differential equations relating to the class of solutions to periodic linear differential equations of the form ẋ = A(t) x, where A(t) is a piecewise continuous periodic function. The theory gives the structure of the fundamental matrix solution and determines the stability of solutions.

Floquet's theorem

The main theorem, due to Gaston Floquet (1883), states that the fundamental matrix can be decomposed as Φ(t) = P(t) eBt, where P(t) is periodic with the same period as A(t) and B is a constant matrix. The eigenvalues of the monodromy matrix Φ(T) are called Floquet multipliers; those of B are Floquet exponents. If all multipliers lie inside the unit circle, solutions are stable.

Applications

Floquet theory underlies the analysis of Hill's equation and the Mathieu equation, the stability of periodic orbits, electronic band structure in crystals (where it appears as Bloch's theorem), and parametric resonance phenomena such as Faraday waves and Paul traps.
