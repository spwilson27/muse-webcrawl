Lilian Mary Baylis CH (9 May 1874 – 25 November 1937) was an English theatrical producer and manager. She managed the Old Vic theatre in London and rebuilt Sadler's Wells, establishing permanent companies for drama, opera, and ballet that are the direct ancestors of three of Britain's national performing institutions.

Early life

Baylis was born in Marylebone, London, into a musical family: her mother was a singer and her godmother was the temperance campaigner Emma Cons, who ran the Royal Victoria Hall — the "Old Vic" — as a cheap entertainment venue for working people. Baylis was a child violinist, toured with her family in South Africa, and ran a music school there before returning to London in 1898 to help her aunt at the Vic.

Career

After Emma Cons died in 1912, Baylis took sole control of the Old Vic. From 1914 she staged Shakespeare's plays there at low prices, and between 1913 and 1923 the company performed the entire First Folio — a feat no theatre had achieved. Actors who worked for her included Sybil Thorndike, John Gielgud, Ralph Richardson, and Laurence Olivier.

In 1931 she reopened the derelict Sadler's Wells Theatre in Islington, and with Ninette de Valois established the Vic-Wells Ballet there; with de Valois and the conductor Constant Lambert she built the company that became The Royal Ballet. Her opera company at Sadler's Wells evolved into the English National Opera, and her drama company forms part of the lineage of the Royal National Theatre. She was appointed a Member of the Order of the Companions of Honour in 1929.

Legacy

Baylis worked on famously small budgets and is remembered for her piety, thrift, and blunt wit, as well as for democratizing access to classic theatre, opera, and ballet. Lilian Baylis Avenue in Lambeth and a studio theatre at Sadler's Wells are named after her.
