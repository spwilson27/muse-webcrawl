International civil service

The international civil service consists of officials employed by intergovernmental organizations — above all the United Nations and its specialized agencies — who serve the international community rather than any single national government. International civil servants owe loyalty exclusively to their organization and must neither seek nor accept instructions from national authorities.

Principles

Article 100 of the UN Charter enshrines the independence of the Secretary-General and staff. Core values include integrity, impartiality, neutrality and professionalism, with recruitment on the basis of merit and equitable geographical distribution.

History and oversight

The concept developed in the League of Nations secretariat under Sir Eric Drummond. The International Civil Service Commission (ICSC), established by the UN General Assembly in 1974, regulates and coordinates conditions of service across the UN common system, including salaries based on the Noblemaire principle of comparison with the highest-paid national civil service.

Privileges and accountability

International civil servants typically enjoy functional privileges and immunities, tax arrangements specific to the system, and recourse to administrative tribunals such as the UN Appeals Tribunal and the ILO Administrative Tribunal.
