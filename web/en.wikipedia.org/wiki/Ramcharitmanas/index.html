Ramcharitmanas (Devanagari: रामचरितमानस, Rāmacaritamānasa, literally "The Lake of the Deeds of Rama") is an epic poem in the Awadhi language, composed by the 16th-century Indian bhakti poet Tulsidas (c. 1511–1623). It retells the Sanskrit Ramayana of Valmiki, the story of Rama, an incarnation of the Hindu god Vishnu. Tulsidas began composing the work in Ayodhya in 1574 CE and completed it about two years and seven months later.

Structure

The poem is divided into seven books or kandas: Bala Kanda, Ayodhya Kanda, Aranya Kanda, Kishkindha Kanda, Sundara Kanda, Lanka Kanda, and Uttara Kanda. It comprises 10,902 verses composed in metres such as chaupai, doha, chhand, and sortha. Framed as a series of dialogues — between Shiva and Parvati, the sage Yajnavalkya and Bharadwaja, and the crow Kakabhushundi and Garuda — the work presents Rama not merely as a hero but as God incarnate, the supreme reality accessible through devotion.

Significance

By rendering the Ramayana in a vernacular language rather than Sanskrit, Tulsidas made the story accessible to ordinary people, and the Ramcharitmanas became one of the most beloved and influential texts of Hindu devotional literature in northern India. It is central to the Ramanandi tradition, recited in homes and temples, enacted in Ramlila performances, and cited by Mahatma Gandhi as a profound work of devotion. The text also helped establish Awadhi and the wider Hindi literary tradition.
