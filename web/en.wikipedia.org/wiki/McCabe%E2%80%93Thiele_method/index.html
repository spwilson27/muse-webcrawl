McCabe–Thiele method

McCabe–Thiele method
TypeGraphical design method
FieldChemical engineering, distillation
Published1925, by Warren L. McCabe and Ernest Thiele

The McCabe–Thiele method is a graphical technique used in chemical engineering to calculate the number of theoretical plates, or equilibrium stages, needed to separate a binary mixture in a fractional distillation column.

The diagram

The method plots the mole fraction of the more volatile component in the liquid (x) against that in the vapour (y), together with the vapour–liquid equilibrium curve. A diagonal line y = x serves as a reference. From composition balances, a rectifying operating line is drawn from the distillate composition and a stripping operating line from the bottoms composition, joined at the feed (q-line) determined by the feed's thermal condition. Stepping off "stages" between the operating lines and the equilibrium curve counts the theoretical plates.

Assumptions

The derivation assumes constant molar overflow — equal molar heats of vaporization for the two components — so that liquid and vapour molar flows are constant within each section of the column. This makes the operating lines straight.

Use

Introduced by Warren L. McCabe and Ernest W. Thiele in 1925, the method remains a standard teaching tool and a rapid means of estimating stage requirements and the minimum reflux ratio, though rigorous tray-by-tray and computer simulation methods supersede it in industrial design.
