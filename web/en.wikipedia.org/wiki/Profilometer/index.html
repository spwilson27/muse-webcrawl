Profilometer

A profilometer is a measuring instrument used to measure the profile of a surface, in order to quantify its roughness and topography. It produces a two-dimensional profile or a three-dimensional map of surface height as a function of position, with vertical resolution that can reach the nanometre scale.

Types

Contact profilometers drag a diamond stylus across the surface under a light, controlled force; vertical movement of the stylus is converted into an electrical signal. They are robust and standardised but can scratch soft surfaces and are limited by the stylus radius. Optical (non-contact) profilometers use light — white-light interferometry, confocal microscopy, or focus variation — to build a height map without touching the sample, and are preferred for delicate or very smooth surfaces such as semiconductor wafers.

Applications

Profilometers are used in manufacturing quality control, tribology, semiconductor fabrication, optics and materials science to verify surface finish parameters such as Ra (average roughness) and to inspect steps, films and etched structures.
