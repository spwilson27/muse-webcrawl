Kafkaesque

Kafkaesque is an adjective describing concepts and situations reminiscent of the work of the Czech-born writer Franz Kafka (1883–1924). It is typically used for nightmarish, disorienting, and illogically complex circumstances — especially impersonal bureaucracies — in which an individual is powerless, oppressed by forces that cannot be understood or identified.

Meaning

The term derives from Kafka's novels The Trial and The Castle, and stories such as The Metamorphosis, in which protagonists are arrested, judged, or obstructed by faceless authorities operating by rules no one can explain. Dictionaries generally define "Kafkaesque" as having a nightmarishly complex, bizarre, or illogical quality.

Usage

Beyond literature, the word is used in journalism, law, and everyday speech for administrative mazes, opaque algorithms, and surreal encounters with institutions. Kafka scholars have sometimes objected that the popular sense flattens the humour and subtlety of Kafka's writing, but the term has become firmly established in English.
