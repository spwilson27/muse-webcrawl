Linguistic imperialism

Linguistic imperialism is a concept in linguistics and post-colonial studies describing the transfer of a dominant language to other peoples, and the power asymmetries that result. The term was popularized by Robert Phillipson's 1992 book Linguistic Imperialism, which analyzed the global spread of English.

Concept

Phillipson argued that the dominance of English is asserted and maintained by structural and cultural inequalities between English and other languages — in education, media, and international institutions — echoing earlier traditions of imperial language policy in the British and French empires. The concept has been applied to other languages as well, including French, Spanish, Russian, and Mandarin.

Criticism

Critics contend that the framework underestimates the agency of learners, who may adopt a global language for pragmatic reasons, and that English is often appropriated and reshaped locally (as in World Englishes) rather than simply imposed.
