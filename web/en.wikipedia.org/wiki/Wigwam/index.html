Wigwam

A wigwam, also called a wickiup in the western United States, is a domed or conical dwelling used by many Indigenous peoples of North America, particularly the Algonquian-speaking peoples of the Northeastern Woodlands. Unlike the portable tipi of the Plains peoples, wigwams were generally semi-permanent structures of settled or seasonally migrating communities.

A reconstructed birch-bark wigwam

Construction

A wigwam is built from a frame of bent saplings lashed together in a dome, cone, or elongated arch, covered with panels of bark (often birchbark in the north), woven rush mats, hides, or thatch, leaving a smoke hole at the top for the central fire. Sizes ranged from small single-family shelters to large multi-family longhouse-like forms among peoples such as the Wampanoag (wetu), Ojibwe (wiigiwaam), and Abenaki. Frames could remain in place while coverings were carried between seasonal camps.

Terminology

The word comes from Eastern Abenaki wigwom, "house". The southwestern term wickiup is applied to the brush-and-pole domed dwellings of peoples such as the Apache. Wigwams are still constructed today for ceremonial and educational purposes.
