Suez (Arabic: السويس, as-Suways) is a seaport city in north-eastern Egypt, on the northern shore of the Gulf of Suez at the southern entrance of the Suez Canal. The city forms its own governorate and has a population of roughly three-quarters of a million in its metropolitan area. Together with Port Said at the canal's northern end and Ismailia at its midpoint, it is one of the three Canal Zone cities.

History

A port settlement existed in the area in antiquity, near the Red Sea terminus of canals dug by the pharaohs and later rulers linking the Nile to the Red Sea; the classical town of Clysma stood nearby. Suez grew in importance as a trading and pilgrimage port under Islamic rule. Its modern significance dates from the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869, which made it a major coaling and transshipment station on the route between Europe and Asia.

The city was heavily damaged during the wars between Egypt and Israel. Israeli forces reached its outskirts in 1967, and Suez was besieged and largely evacuated during the War of Attrition and the 1973 October War, when the Battle of Suez was fought there in the war's final days. Reconstruction followed the reopening of the canal in 1975.

Economy

Suez is a centre for petroleum refining, petrochemicals, fertilisers and steel, and hosts facilities of the Suez Canal economic zone. Its ports, including Adabiya and Ain Sokhna to the south, handle a significant share of Egypt's Red Sea trade. A railway and highway connect the city with Cairo, about 130 km to the north-west, and the Ahmed Hamdi Tunnel passes under the canal nearby, linking the African mainland with the Sinai Peninsula.
