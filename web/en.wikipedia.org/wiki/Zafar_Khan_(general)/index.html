Zafar Khan was the Delhi Sultanate's foremost general against the Mongols.

Zafar Khan (died 1299), whose real name is not recorded in the surviving histories, was a general of the Delhi Sultanate under Sultan Alauddin Khalji. A convert to Islam, he rose to become one of Alauddin's most trusted commanders and the leading figure in the sultanate's defence against repeated Mongol invasions from the Chagatai Khanate. He held the title of Zafar Khan ("Victorious Khan") and governed frontier provinces including Samana and Multan.

Campaigns

Zafar Khan distinguished himself in the campaigns that followed Alauddin's accession in 1296. He defeated Mongol forces in the Punjab, notably crushing an invasion in 1297–98 near Jalandhar, where the Mongols were routed and their captured leaders sent to Delhi. His victories made his name, according to the chronicler Ziauddin Barani, a byword for terror among the Mongols, who were said to check whether a stray horse belonged to Zafar Khan before drinking from a stream.

Battle of Kili and death

In 1299 a large Chagatai army under Qutlugh Khwaja advanced on Delhi itself. At the Battle of Kili, Zafar Khan led the vanguard and charged impetuously into the Mongol host. Unsupported — the sultan's other commanders held back, whether from orders, caution or jealousy — he pursued the feigning Mongol retreat too far, was surrounded, and was killed fighting with a small band of followers. Barani's account suggests Alauddin, who regarded the popular general as a potential rival, was privately relieved at his death. The Mongols withdrew soon afterwards, but Zafar Khan's self-sacrificing charge entered Indo-Persian historical memory as an archetype of martial heroism, and his tomb near Delhi was long venerated.
