Duet

A duet is a musical composition for two performers, in which the performers have equal importance to the piece. The term is most often used for vocal works, and for piano music written for two players at one or two instruments (a piano duet is specifically for two players at one piano). Ensembles or pieces for other numbers of performers have their own names: trio (three), quartet (four), and so on.

Forms

Vocal duets are central to opera, where they often carry the emotional climax — famous examples include the Flower Duet from Delibes' Lakmé and the love duets in Verdi and Puccini. In popular music, duets pair two singers, often from different genres, and have produced many hit recordings. Instrumental duets include the violin duets of Bartók, string duos such as Beethoven's duets for viola and cello, and the extensive repertoire for two guitars and for flute and guitar.

In the Baroque and Classical eras, "duet" could also describe works for two voices with basso continuo (notably by Monteverdi and Handel), and the chamber duet became a recognised genre. The word derives from the Italian duetto, diminutive of duo, from Latin duo, "two".
