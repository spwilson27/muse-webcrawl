A bestseller is a book or other media noted for its top selling status, with bestseller lists published by newspapers, magazines, and book store chains. Some lists are broken down into classifications and specialties such as fiction, nonfiction, and children's books. The term is also used for films, recordings, and other products that achieve exceptional commercial success.

Bestseller lists

The New York Times Best Seller list, first published in 1931 and compiled from reports by selected bookstores, is the most influential in the United States; placement on it — even for a single week — is a major marketing asset and is printed on book covers. Because lists are based on samples of retail sales and bulk orders have at times been used to game rankings, compilers apply rules to discount suspicious bulk purchases. "Bestselling author" claims may rest on a list appearance in any category, region, or week, so the label's prestige varies widely.

Best-selling books of all time

Religious and political texts such as the Bible, the Quran, and Quotations from Chairman Mao have the largest estimated distributions but incomplete sales records. Among works of fiction, Charles Dickens's A Tale of Two Cities is commonly cited as the best-selling single-volume novel, with estimates above 200 million copies; J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings and Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's The Little Prince also exceed 150 million. The Harry Potter series by J. K. Rowling is the best-selling book series overall, with over 600 million copies.

Commercial dynamics

Bestseller status is self-reinforcing: prominent display, media coverage, and word of mouth drive further sales, a phenomenon sometimes called the "bestseller effect". Blockbuster strategies — large advances and marketing budgets concentrated on a few titles — dominate trade publishing economics.
