Quintet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Quintet
Musicians:Five
Common types:String quintet, piano quintet, wind quintet, brass quintet

A quintet is a group of five musicians performing together, or a piece of music written for such a group. The term applies most commonly in classical music and jazz. Quintets sit between the quartet and the sextet in the standard family of chamber music ensembles.

Classical quintets

Common classical quintet formations include:

  • String quintet — a string quartet plus an extra viola (as in Mozart's string quintets) or an extra cello (as in Schubert's String Quintet in C major, D. 956).
  • Piano quintet — piano plus string quartet, a combination used by Schumann, Brahms, Franck, and Shostakovich.
  • Wind quintet — flute, oboe, clarinet, horn, and bassoon.
  • Brass quintet — two trumpets, horn, trombone, and tuba.
  • Clarinet quintet — clarinet plus string quartet, exemplified by the quintets of Mozart and Brahms.

Notable works include Mozart's Clarinet Quintet in A major, Schubert's "Trout" Quintet (piano, violin, viola, cello, and double bass), and the piano quintets of Schumann and Brahms.

Jazz quintets

In jazz, the archetypal quintet consists of a rhythm section (piano, bass, drums) and two horns, frequently trumpet and saxophone. Famous examples include the two great quintets led by Miles Davis — the first featuring John Coltrane, and the second featuring Wayne Shorter and Herbie Hancock — as well as the bands of Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie and Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers in several configurations.

Other uses

The term is also applied to groups of five in other fields, including vocal ensembles, dance companies, and (informally) sporting line-ups such as a basketball team's starting five.
