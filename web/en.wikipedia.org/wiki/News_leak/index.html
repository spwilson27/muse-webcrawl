News leak

Related conceptsWhistleblowing, anonymous sourcing, trial balloon, embargo
Common sourcesGovernment officials, corporate insiders, dissidents

A news leak is the unauthorized or informal disclosure of confidential, embargoed, or otherwise non-public information to the news media. Leaks are a central mechanism of modern journalism and politics: officials may leak deliberately to float policy ideas ("trial balloons"), embarrass rivals, shape coverage, or expose wrongdoing, while journalists cultivate inside sources who pass information on condition of anonymity.

Types and motives

Political scientists distinguish several kinds of leaks: ego leaks, intended to enhance the source's standing; goodwill leaks, building credit with reporters; policy leaks, testing or advancing a proposal; animus leaks, damaging an opponent; and whistleblower leaks, exposing misconduct. Governments frequently practise "authorized" leaking through background briefings, unattributable statements by "senior officials," and selective declassification, while simultaneously prosecuting unauthorized disclosures. Corporate leaks include earnings figures, product plans, and merger talks, which securities laws may make unlawful to trade upon.

Famous cases

Prominent examples include the Pentagon Papers (1971), leaked by Daniel Ellsberg; the Watergate reporting guided by the source "Deep Throat" (FBI associate director Mark Felt); the 2010 disclosures published by WikiLeaks from Chelsea Manning; Edward Snowden's 2013 revelations of NSA surveillance programs; and the Panama Papers (2016), a leak of 11.5 million law-firm documents to the Süddeutsche Zeitung. Each case renewed debate over the balance between press freedom, source protection, and state secrecy; shield laws and secure submission systems such as SecureDrop are journalistic responses to increasingly aggressive leak investigations.
