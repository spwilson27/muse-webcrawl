Hunan (Chinese: 湖南; pinyin: Húnán) is a landlocked province of the People's Republic of China, part of the South Central China region. Its name means "south of the lake," referring to Dongting Lake on its northern border. Hunan borders Hubei to the north, Jiangxi to the east, Guangdong and Guangxi to the south, Guizhou to the west, and Chongqing to the northwest. Its capital and largest city is Changsha.

Geography

Hunan is ringed by mountains—the Wuling in the northwest, the Xuefeng in the west, the Nanling in the south, and the Luoxiao in the east—and opens northward to the Dongting Lake plain. The Xiang River, from which the province takes its abbreviation 湘, flows north through Changsha into Dongting Lake and thence the Yangtze. Landmarks include the Wulingyuan/Zhangjiajie sandstone pillars—an inspiration for the floating mountains in the film Avatar—Mount Heng (one of China's Five Great Mountains), and the ancient town of Fenghuang.

History

Hunan was part of the ancient state of Chu and was settled extensively by Han Chinese during the Ming and Qing dynasties ("Huguang fills with migrants"). In the 19th century it produced the Xiang Army of Zeng Guofan, decisive in suppressing the Taiping Rebellion, and reformers such as Tan Sitong. In the 20th century Hunan became famous as the home province of Mao Zedong (born in Shaoshan), Liu Shaoqi, and Peng Dehuai, and as an early center of the Chinese Communist movement. The Hunanese are jokingly said to be "afraid of nothing—except things not being spicy."

Culture and economy

Hunan cuisine (湘菜), built on chili peppers, smoked and cured meats, is counted among China's eight great cuisines, exemplified by Mao's braised pork belly (hong shao rou). The province is a major rice-producing area ("land of fish and rice"), the country's leading producer of some agricultural products, and a media hub through Hunan Television. Historic exports include Xiang embroidery, chrysanthemum stone carvings, and the kilns of Liling. It has also long been a source of migrant labor.
