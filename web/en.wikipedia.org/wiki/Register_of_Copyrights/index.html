Register of Copyrights

The Register of Copyrights is the director of the United States Copyright Office, within the Library of Congress, as provided by 17 U.S.C. § 701. The register is appointed by the Librarian of Congress and is responsible for administering the national copyright system, including registration, recordation, and the formulation of copyright policy advice to Congress.

Duties

The Register of Copyrights advises Congress on national and international issues relating to copyright and related matters, provides information and assistance to federal departments and agencies and the judiciary, participates in meetings of international intergovernmental organisations, and conducts studies and programs regarding copyright. The register also supervises the Copyright Office's day-to-day administration and can determine the acceptability of deposits for registration.

History

The position was created by an act of Congress in 1897. The first Register of Copyrights was Thorvald Solberg, who served from 1897 to 1930. Subsequent registers have included William F. Patry, Marybeth Peters, who served from 1994 to 2010, Maria Pallante, Karyn Temple (acting, then permanent), and Shira Perlmutter, appointed in 2020.
