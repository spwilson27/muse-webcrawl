Pokhran-II was a series of five nuclear weapon tests conducted by India in May 1998 at the Indian Army's Pokhran Test Range in Rajasthan, codenamed Operation Shakti. The tests were announced on 11 May 1998, when three devices — a thermonuclear device, a fission device, and a sub-kiloton device — were detonated simultaneously, followed by two further sub-kiloton devices on 13 May. The tests made India a full-fledged nuclear state and came 24 years after India's first nuclear test, Pokhran-I ("Smiling Buddha") in 1974.

Background

India's nuclear programme dated to the 1940s under Homi J. Bhabha. After the 1974 test, international pressure and technology-denial regimes slowed weaponisation. By the 1990s, the government of Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, whose party had long favoured an overt nuclear capability, authorised preparations soon after taking office in March 1998. The test preparations at Pokhran were carefully timed and camouflaged to avoid detection by American reconnaissance satellites; scientists travelled in secrecy and work was done at night.

The tests

On 11 May 1998, at approximately 15:45 IST, three devices were detonated: a claimed two-stage thermonuclear (hydrogen bomb) device, a fission device derived from the 1974 design, and a 0.2 kt sub-kiloton device. Two further sub-kiloton devices followed on 13 May. India announced a total yield consistent with its design claims, though outside seismic estimates of the combined yield were lower, leading to a long-running debate about whether the thermonuclear device achieved its full design yield — a dispute that became public in 2009 when test participant K. Santhanam questioned the thermonuclear result, while other scientists, including A. P. J. Abdul Kalam and R. Chidambaram, defended the official account.

Reactions

Prime Minister Vajpayee declared that India was now a nuclear weapons state. Pakistan responded with its own tests, Chagai-I, on 28 May 1998. The United States, Japan and other countries imposed economic sanctions on India, most of which were lifted within a few years. The tests were broadly popular domestically; 11 May is commemorated in India as National Technology Day.
