Vairocana (from Sanskrit: Vairochana, "the Illuminator" or "the Radiant One") is a cosmic buddha in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. He is regarded as the embodiment of the dharmakāya — the truth body of the Buddha, the universal principle of enlightenment — and as the personification of the concept of emptiness (śūnyatā). Vairocana occupies the center of the Five Tathāgatas (the Five Wisdom Buddhas) in many mandalas.

Scriptural basis

Vairocana is the central figure of the Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Flower Garland Sutra), in which the universe is portrayed as his body and field of activity, and of the Mahāvairocana Tantra, the foundational text of East Asian esoteric Buddhism, where he preaches the Dharma through his all-pervading radiance. In the esoteric schools of Shingon and Tendai in Japan, Dainichi Nyorai is the supreme object of devotion, identified with the Dharma body that pervades all phenomena.

Iconography

Vairocana is typically shown in royal attire, seated in meditation, displaying the wisdom-fist mudra (the left index finger grasped by the right hand) in esoteric contexts, or the dharmachakra (teaching) mudra. His name's association with light links him to the sun: the Japanese name "Dainichi" means "Great Sun." The 15-meter Great Buddha (Daibutsu) of Tōdai-ji in Nara (dedicated 752 CE) and the Spring Temple Buddha in Henan — finished in 2008 at 128 meters of statue height — both depict him.
