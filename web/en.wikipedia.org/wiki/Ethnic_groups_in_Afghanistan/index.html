Ethnic groups in Afghanistan

Afghanistan is a multiethnic and multilingual country, situated at the crossroads of Central, South, and West Asia. Its population comprises numerous ethnic groups, the largest of which are the Pashtuns, Tajiks, Hazaras, and Uzbeks, alongside Turkmens, Baloch, Nuristanis, Aimaqs, Kyrgyz, Pashais, Sikhs, Hindus, and many smaller communities. Reliable demographic data are scarce: Afghanistan has never completed a modern nationwide census, so all figures for ethnic proportions are estimates that are politically sensitive and contested.

Major groups

Pashtuns

The Pashtuns are generally considered the largest ethnic group, commonly estimated at roughly 40 percent or more of the population. Pashto-speaking and predominantly Sunni Muslim, they are organised into tribal confederations such as the Durrani and Ghilzai and predominate in the south and east, with large communities across the country and in neighbouring Pakistan. Pashtuns have historically held political power in Afghanistan since the founding of the Durrani Empire in 1747.

Tajiks

Tajiks, Persian (Dari) speakers concentrated in the northeast and west and in the cities, are usually estimated at around a quarter to a third of the population. They have traditionally been prominent in government administration, commerce, and the military leadership of the Northern Alliance.

Hazaras

The Hazaras, a predominantly Shia Muslim, Persian-speaking people of the central highlands (Hazarajat), are commonly estimated at around 9–15 percent of the population. Of partly Mongol ancestry, they suffered severe persecution in the late nineteenth century under Amir Abdur Rahman Khan and again under the Taliban, and have been prominent in migration to Iran, Pakistan, and the West.

Uzbeks and Turkmens

Uzbeks, Turkic speakers of the northern plains, are usually estimated at roughly 6–10 percent, and Turkmens at around 3 percent. Northern Afghanistan's Uzbek population was politically mobilised under Abdul Rashid Dostum from the 1990s onward.

Languages

The constitution recognises Pashto and Dari as official languages, with additional official status for other languages in regions where they predominate. Dozens of languages from the Indo-Iranian, Turkic, and other families are spoken.
