Daniel Thomas O'Bannon (September 30, 1946 – December 17, 2009) was an American screenwriter, film director and visual effects artist, best known for writing the science fiction horror classic Alien (1979) and for writing and directing The Return of the Living Dead (1985).

Career

While studying film at the University of Southern California, O'Bannon co-wrote and starred in John Carpenter's low-budget debut Dark Star (1974), also creating its effects. He worked on the visual effects of Star Wars (1977) and on Alejandro Jodorowsky's aborted Dune, where he met artist H. R. Giger. With Ronald Shusett he wrote Alien, directed by Ridley Scott; its hero, Ellen Ripley, became one of cinema's defining characters. O'Bannon and Shusett also wrote the story for Total Recall (1990), adapted from Philip K. Dick.

As a director, O'Bannon made The Return of the Living Dead, the influential zombie comedy that popularised fast-moving, brain-eating zombies, and the H. P. Lovecraft adaptation The Resurrected (1991). Other credits include Dead & Buried, Lifeforce and Alien vs. Predator (story credit). He suffered for decades from Crohn's disease, which informed the visceral "chestburster" sequence of Alien, and died in 2009.
