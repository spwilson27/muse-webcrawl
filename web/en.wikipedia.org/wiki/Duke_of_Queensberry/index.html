Duke of Queensberry

Duke of Queensberry is a title in the Peerage of Scotland created in 1684 for William Douglas, 1st Marquess of Queensberry. The Douglas family of Drumlanrig rose to prominence in the Scottish Borders, and the marquessate of Queensberry had been created in 1682. The dukedom's subsidiary titles include Marquess of Queensberry, Earl of Queensberry, Earl of Drumlanrig and Viscount Drumlanrig.

History

The 2nd Duke, James Douglas, was a leading architect of the Union of 1707 between Scotland and England, serving as Secretary of State for Scotland and receiving an English dukedom, Duke of Dover, which became extinct on his son's death. The 3rd Duke's younger son Charles succeeded, while the famous 4th Duke, William Douglas—"Old Q"—was a celebrated rake and patron of horse racing. On his death in 1810 without issue, the dukedom and the marquessate separated: the marquessate passed to a cousin, while the dukedom passed under a special remainder to Henry Scott, 3rd Duke of Buccleuch, and has since been held by the Dukes of Buccleuch and Queensberry.

The title is also famous for John Sholto Douglas, 9th Marquess of Queensberry, who lent his name to the Queensberry Rules of boxing (1867) and whose accusation against Oscar Wilde precipitated Wilde's trials in 1895.
