French Riviera

The French Riviera, known in French as the Côte d'Azur, is the Mediterranean coastline of the southeast corner of France. There is no official boundary, but it is usually considered to extend from Toulon, Le Lavandou or Saint-Tropez in the west to Menton — on the France–Italy border — in the east. The coast is entirely within the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region of France, and the Principality of Monaco is a semi-enclave within it.

History

The area became a fashionable health and holiday resort for British upper-class visitors from the late 18th century, and later attracted artists such as Picasso and Matisse, and writers including Edith Wharton and Somerset Maugham. After the Second World War it became a major tourist destination associated with glamour, film stars — boosted by the Cannes Film Festival — and yachting. Notable resorts include Nice, Cannes, Antibes, Saint-Tropez, Menton and Monaco.
