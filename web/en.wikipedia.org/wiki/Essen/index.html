Essen (German pronunciation: [ˈɛsn̩]) is the central and, after Dortmund, second-largest city of the Ruhr, the largest urban area in Germany. With roughly 580,000 inhabitants, it is the fourth-largest city in North Rhine-Westphalia and the ninth-largest city in Germany. It lies on the river Ruhr in the west of the country.

History

Essen grew around a women's abbey (Stift Essen) founded around 845, whose abbesses ruled the small ecclesiastical principality for centuries; the abbey church is now Essen Minster, home of the Golden Madonna, one of the oldest surviving sculptures of the Virgin Mary in Western art. In the 19th century the city became a heartland of the industrial revolution through coal mining and the steel and armaments works of the Krupp family, whose Villa Hügel still overlooks the Ruhr valley.

Economy and culture

With the decline of heavy industry, Essen reinvented itself as a service and corporate centre, hosting the headquarters of companies including RWE and E.ON. The Zollverein Coal Mine, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a symbol of the Ruhr's industrial heritage. Essen was a European Capital of Culture in 2010 (as part of Ruhr.2010) and was named European Green Capital for 2017. The Folkwang Museum holds a major collection of modern art, and the city hosts the annual Spiel board-game fair, the largest in the world.
