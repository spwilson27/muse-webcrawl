The American Jewish Congress (AJCongress) is an association of American Jews organised to defend Jewish interests in the United States and abroad through public policy advocacy, legislation, and the courts. Founded in 1918 as a democratic, representative body of American Jewry, it became known especially for its work on civil rights and the separation of church and state.

History

The organisation grew out of efforts during the First World War to create an elected congress that would represent American Jews at the postwar peace conference, particularly in securing minority rights for Jews in Europe. Rabbi Stephen Samuel Wise was among its principal founders and long-time leaders. Initially intended as a temporary body, it was made permanent and developed into a membership organisation combining Zionist advocacy with domestic liberal causes.

Activities

AJCongress became prominent for its legal work, filing amicus briefs and litigating cases concerning religious liberty, public-school prayer, civil rights, and discrimination, often alongside other civil-liberties groups. Its Commission on Law and Social Action, established in 1945, helped shape the strategy of using litigation to combat discrimination against Jews and other minorities. The organisation also supported the State of Israel and campaigned against antisemitism internationally. In 2010 it suspended most operations after financial losses, including investments lost in the Madoff Ponzi scheme, but was later revived under new leadership.
