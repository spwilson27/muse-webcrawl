MS Herald of Free Enterprise

MS Herald of Free Enterprise was a roll-on/roll-off ferry operated by Townsend Thoresen on routes across the English Channel. On 6 March 1987 she capsized just outside the Belgian port of Zeebrugge shortly after departure, killing 193 passengers and crew — the worst peacetime British maritime disaster since the sinking of the Iolaire in 1919.

The ship

The Herald was built in 1980 by Schichau Unterweser in Bremerhaven as one of three "Spirit" class sisters for the Dover–Calais service, later transferring to the Dover–Zeebrugge route. She could carry some 1,300 passengers and 350 cars, loading vehicles through bow and stern doors.

The disaster

The ship sailed from Zeebrugge with her bow doors still open — the assistant boatswain responsible for closing them was asleep, and no one verified their state. As she accelerated, water flooded the open car deck, and the free-surface effect destroyed her stability; she rolled onto her port side within minutes, coming to rest half-submerged on a sandbank, which prevented a complete sinking and saved many lives.

Inquiry and aftermath

The formal investigation under Mr Justice Sheen blamed the crew members directly involved but famously condemned the company "from top to bottom" with a "disease of sloppiness". A subsequent inquest returned verdicts of unlawful killing, though a prosecution of the company for corporate manslaughter failed in 1990. The disaster led to new safety rules: indicators for watertight doors on the bridge, CCTV monitoring, and stricter ro-ro design standards under SOLAS. The ship was salvaged, renamed Flushing Range, and scrapped in Taiwan in 1988.
