Genres: Hard bop, soul jazz

Horace Ward Martin Tavares Silver (September 2, 1928 – June 18, 2014) was an American jazz pianist, composer, and arranger, particularly in the hard bop style that he helped pioneer in the 1950s. His playing was influenced by gospel music, African music, and Latin American music, and his compositions became standards of the jazz repertoire.

Early life and career

Silver was born in Norwalk, Connecticut, to a father from Cape Verde and a mother of Irish-African descent; Cape Verdean folk music influenced his later writing, notably "Cape Verdean Blues". After playing with Stan Getz, he moved to New York, working with Lester Young, Coleman Hawkins, and others. In 1952 he began recording for Blue Note Records, a relationship that lasted 28 years.

Hard bop and the Jazz Messengers

With drummer Art Blakey, Silver co-founded the Jazz Messengers, whose 1954 recordings helped define hard bop — a return to blues and gospel roots in reaction to cool jazz. Silver then formed his own quintet, recording classic albums such as Horace Silver and the Jazz Messengers, Six Pieces of Silver (1956), Finger Poppin' (1959), Blowin' the Blues Away (1959), and Song for My Father (1964). The title track of the last, inspired by his father's Cape Verdean heritage, became his best-known composition.

Later years

In 1970 Silver founded his own label, Silveto, and in later years his music and writing took on philosophical and spiritual themes. He published his autobiography, Let's Get to the Nitty Gritty, in 2006. Silver died in New Rochelle, New York, in 2014, aged 85.
