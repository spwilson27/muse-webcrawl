Sizewell C nuclear power station

Sizewell C nuclear power station is a proposed nuclear power station at Sizewell in Suffolk, England, adjacent to the existing Sizewell B reactor and the decommissioned Sizewell A station. The project is led by EDF Energy and is designed to use two EPR (European Pressurised Reactor) units, the same design as the reactors under construction at Hinkley Point C in Somerset.

The station would have a generating capacity of about 3.2 gigawatts, enough to supply electricity to approximately six million homes, and would meet roughly 7% of the United Kingdom's electricity demand.

Background

Sizewell has hosted nuclear power stations since the 1960s. Sizewell A, a Magnox station, operated from 1966 until 2006. Sizewell B, a pressurised water reactor (PWR), began generating in 1995 and is the UK's only PWR. The government identified the Sizewell site as suitable for a new nuclear station in its 2011 National Policy Statement on nuclear power generation.

Development and consent

EDF carried out several stages of public consultation between 2012 and 2019 before submitting a Development Consent Order (DCO) application in May 2020. The application was accepted for examination by the Planning Inspectorate, and following a lengthy examination the Secretary of State granted development consent in July 2022. In November 2022 the UK government announced an investment of £679 million to take a stake in the project, signalling its intention to reach a final investment decision during that parliament. The government subsequently increased its involvement as EDF sought new investors to fund construction.

Design

Sizewell C is planned as a near-replica of Hinkley Point C, using two UK EPR units of about 1,630 MW each. Proponents argue that replicating the Hinkley design will reduce cost and risk through lessons learned and an established supply chain. The design includes a permanent sea defence, cooling water infrastructure drawing on the North Sea, and associated developments including road and rail improvements and temporary construction worker accommodation.

Opposition and debate

The project has been opposed by local groups such as Stop Sizewell C and Together Against Sizewell C, as well as national organisations including the RSPB, whose Minsmere nature reserve lies nearby. Concerns include the impact on the Suffolk Coast and Heaths Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, effects on coastal erosion and water supply, the cost to consumers, and whether the project represents value for money compared with renewable alternatives. Supporters, including trade unions and some local councils, point to low-carbon baseload generation, jobs, and long-term energy security.
