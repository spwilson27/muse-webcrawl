Michael Haneke (born 23 March 1942) is an Austrian film director and screenwriter. His work often examines social issues, violence and its depiction in media, and the alienation of modern life, in a cool, formally rigorous style. He has won the Palme d'Or at Cannes twice, a feat achieved by only a handful of directors.

Career

After work in Austrian and German television, Haneke's feature debut The Seventh Continent (1989) began his "glaciation trilogy" with Benny's Video (1992) and 71 Fragments of a Chronology of Chance (1994). Funny Games (1997), which he remade shot-for-shot in English in 2007, and Caché (2005) brought international fame.

Major honours

The White Ribbon (2009) and Amour (2012) both won the Palme d'Or; Amour also won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. His later films include Happy End (2017).
