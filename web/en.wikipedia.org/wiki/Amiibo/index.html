Amiibo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amiibo figurines communicate with Nintendo consoles via near-field communication.

Amiibo (stylized amiibo) is a toys-to-life platform developed by Nintendo, launched in North America in November 2014 alongside Super Smash Bros. for Wii U. The figurines, cards, and plush toys contain NFC chips that communicate wirelessly with the Wii U GamePad, Nintendo 3DS, and Nintendo Switch to unlock characters, items, costumes, and game data.

Functionality

In Super Smash Bros., scanning an amiibo creates a computer-controlled "Figure Player" that can be trained and leveled up. In other games amiibo unlock outfits (such as in Mario Kart 8), weapons and challenges (in Splatoon), or in-game helpers and items (in The Legend of Zelda series). Some amiibo store data for a specific game and may need to be reset to save data for another.

Releases and popularity

Hundreds of amiibo have been produced, spanning the Super Mario, Zelda, Pokémon, Fire Emblem, Animal Crossing, and other franchises, plus amiibo cards for Animal Crossing and Mario Sports Superstars. Early shortages of certain figures made them collectors' items, and by the early 2020s cumulative sales exceeded 70 million units. Nintendo has also produced yarn plush amiibo for Yoshi's Woolly World and cereal-box and other promotional variants.
