Pamir Mountains

The Pamir Mountains are a mountain range in Central Asia, centred on the Gorno-Badakhshan region of eastern Tajikistan, with extensions into Afghanistan, China and Kyrgyzstan. They form a knot where the Himalayas, Karakoram, Hindu Kush, Kunlun and Tian Shan converge, and are known in Persian tradition as the "Roof of the World."

Geography

The Pamirs consist of high, arid plateaus cut by deep valleys; much of the region lies above 3,500 metres. The highest summit in the Tajik Pamirs is Ismoil Somoni Peak (7,495 m), formerly Communism Peak, while Kongur Tagh in the Chinese Pamirs reaches 7,649 m. The range contains the long Fedchenko Glacier, among the longest glaciers outside the polar regions, and gives rise to the Panj and Pamir rivers forming part of the Amu Darya system.

People and history

The eastern Pamirs are home to Kyrgyz pastoralists, the western valleys to Pamiri peoples speaking Eastern Iranian languages and largely following Ismaili Islam. The Silk Road passed along the range's margins, Marco Polo described its wild sheep (the Marco Polo sheep), and in the 19th century the Pamirs were a theatre of the "Great Game" between Britain and Russia. The Pamir Highway (M41), one of the world's highest international roads, crosses the range from Dushanbe toward Osh.
