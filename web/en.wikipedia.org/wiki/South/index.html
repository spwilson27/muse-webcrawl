South

South is by convention the downward direction on most maps.

South is one of the cardinal directions or compass points. It is the opposite of north and is perpendicular to east and west. South is conventionally the down side of a map: maps are usually drawn with north at the top, south at the bottom, east on the right, and west on the left. The word derives from Old English sūþ, related to the sun, denoting the direction of the midday sun in the Northern Hemisphere.

Navigation and orientation

True south is the direction towards the geographic South Pole, along a meridian of longitude. On a compass, south lies at a bearing of 180 degrees (or 200 mils). Magnetic south points toward the Earth's magnetic pole in the Southern Hemisphere, which is currently offset from the geographic pole and gradually drifting. Navigators have long located south at solar noon, when the Sun culminates in the southern sky for observers at mid-northern latitudes; in the Southern Hemisphere the Sun is at its highest in the northern sky.

The South Pole

The South Pole, at 90°S, is the southernmost point on Earth and the point through which the planet's axis of rotation passes at its southern end. Every direction from the South Pole is north. The Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station, continuously occupied since 1957, sits near the pole on the Antarctic ice sheet at an elevation of about 2,835 metres.

Culture and usage

"The South" is used descriptively for many regions: the Southern United States, the Global South (a socioeconomic and political term grouping countries in Africa, Latin America, Asia, and Oceania), and South as the southern part of an individual country. In card games, bridge players sitting opposite North are designated South. In many languages' traditional orientation systems, such as early Chinese compasses, the needle pointed south, so south was the primary direction.
