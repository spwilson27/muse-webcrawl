Conditional probability distribution

In probability theory and statistics, given two jointly distributed random variables X and Y, the conditional probability distribution of Y given X is the probability distribution of Y when X is known to be a particular value. More generally, conditioning on an event or on the value of another random variable updates a distribution to reflect the information gained.

Discrete and continuous cases

For discrete random variables, the conditional probability mass function is defined by P(Y = y | X = x) = P(X = x, Y = y) / P(X = x), provided the denominator is positive. For continuous random variables with joint density fX,Y, the conditional density is fY|X(y|x) = fX,Y(x, y) / fX(x). Conditional distributions are themselves probability distributions: they integrate (or sum) to one, and expectations computed under them are conditional expectations.

Properties

The conditional distribution is tied to the joint and marginal distributions by the chain rule of probability and to Bayes' theorem, which expresses the posterior distribution of one variable given another. If X and Y are independent, the conditional distribution of Y given X equals the marginal distribution of Y. In measure-theoretic treatments, conditional distributions are formalised as regular conditional probabilities (Markov kernels), which handle conditioning on events of probability zero and underlie Bayesian statistics, conditional expectation, and the theory of Markov chains — where a transition kernel is precisely a family of conditional distributions.

Examples

Rolling two dice, the distribution of the total given that the first die shows 4 is a conditional distribution. In a bivariate normal distribution, the conditional distribution of one variable given the other is again normal, with mean shifted according to the correlation — a fact exploited in regression analysis.
