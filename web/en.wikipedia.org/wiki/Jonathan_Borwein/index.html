Jonathan Michael Borwein (20 May 1951 – 2 August 2016) was a Scottish-Canadian mathematician noted for his prolific work in analysis, optimisation and experimental mathematics, and for the Borwein integrals and the Bailey–Borwein–Plouffe formula for the digits of π.

Life and career

Borwein was born in St Andrews, Scotland, into an academic family; his father David Borwein was also a mathematician, and his brother Peter Borwein became his frequent collaborator. He was a Rhodes Scholar at Jesus College, Oxford, where he completed his doctorate in 1974. He held posts at Dalhousie University and then at Simon Fraser University, where with his brother he helped establish the Centre for Experimental and Constructive Mathematics. From 2009 he was Laureate Professor at the University of Newcastle, Australia.

Contributions

With his brother Peter, Borwein developed a quartically convergent algorithm for computing π and studied the Borwein integrals, whose deceptively simple pattern famously breaks down unexpectedly. He was a co-author of the Bailey–Borwein–Plouffe formula (1995), which allows the calculation of individual hexadecimal digits of π without computing the preceding ones. A champion of experimental mathematics — using computation to discover and test conjectures — he co-founded the journal Experimental Mathematics and co-authored the standard works on π and on convex analysis.

Honours

Borwein was a fellow of the Royal Society of Canada and of the Australian Academy of Science, and received the Chauvenet Prize of the Mathematical Association of America. He died in 2016 while still active in research.
