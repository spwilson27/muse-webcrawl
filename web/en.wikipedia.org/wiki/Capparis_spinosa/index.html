Capparis spinosa

Scientific classification
KingdomPlantae
OrderBrassicales

GenusCapparis
SpeciesC. spinosa L.

Capparis spinosa, the caper bush, also called Flinders rose, is a perennial plant that bears rounded, fleshy leaves and large white to pinkish-white flowers. The plant is best known for the edible flower buds (capers), used as a seasoning or garnish, and the fruit (caper berries), both of which are usually consumed pickled. Other species of Capparis are also picked along with C. spinosa for their buds and fruits.

Description

The caper bush is a scrambling, sprawling shrub typically 0.7–1 m tall with numerous branches bearing paired hooked spines (stipular thorns) — the source of the specific epithet spinosa. Leaves are alternate, simple, fleshy, and rounded. The showy flowers, on long pedicels rising from the leaf axils, have four sepals, four white to pinkish petals, and a spray of long violet stamens. The fruit is an oblong berry containing many seeds.

Distribution and habitat

The species is native to the Mediterranean region and parts of Asia and Australia, growing wild on rocky slopes, coastal cliffs, walls, and waste ground in hot, dry climates. It is cultivated commercially in Spain, Italy (notably the islands of Pantelleria and Salina), Morocco, Turkey, and other Mediterranean countries.

Culinary use

Unopened flower buds are harvested by hand and pickled in brine, vinegar, or salt. Graded by size, the smallest buds (nonpareils) are the most prized. Capers are a characteristic ingredient of Mediterranean cuisine — in dishes such as chicken piccata, puttanesca sauce, and remoulade — prized for their piquant, mustard-like flavour, which develops when glucocapparin in the buds reacts with enzymes on crushing or pickling. The larger caper berries and the salty cured buds of related species are eaten similarly.
