Uzbek (Oʻzbek tili) is a Turkic language spoken by the Uzbeks. It is the official and national language of Uzbekistan and the most widely spoken Turkic language of Central Asia, with roughly 27 million native speakers in Uzbekistan and millions more across neighboring countries and in a large migrant diaspora.

History

Uzbek belongs to the Karluk (southeastern) branch of Turkic and descends from Chagatai, the literary language of Central Asia from the 15th century onward, in which poets such as Ali-Shir Nava'i wrote. Because its speakers settled early in the oasis cities of Transoxiana, Uzbek absorbed an unusually heavy Persian (Tajik) influence. Modern standard Uzbek is based on the Fergana–Tashkent dialects; notably, the standard language has largely lost the vowel harmony characteristic of other Turkic languages, reflecting that Persian influence.

Writing systems

Uzbek was written in the Arabic script until 1929, then in Latin under Soviet latinization campaigns, and from 1940 in Cyrillic. After independence, Uzbekistan legislated a return to a Latin alphabet in 1993 (implemented in revised form from 1995), and Latin is now used in education and government, though Cyrillic remains common in print and among older generations. Uzbeks in northern Afghanistan continue to use the Arabic script.

Grammar

Uzbek is agglutinative and suffixing, with subject–object–verb order, six cases, no grammatical gender, and a distinction between formal and informal second-person pronouns. Its vocabulary includes extensive loans from Persian and Arabic, plus Russian from the Soviet period and increasing English borrowings.
