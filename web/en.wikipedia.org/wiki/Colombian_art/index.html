Colombian art

Colombian art encompasses visual arts produced in the territory of present-day Colombia from pre-Columbian times to the present. It includes the goldwork of the Muisca and Quimbaya cultures, colonial religious painting, and the internationally renowned modern art of Fernando Botero and Doris Salcedo.

Pre-Columbian art

Indigenous cultures such as the San Agustín, Tierradentro, Tolima, Quimbaya and Muisca produced monumental stone sculpture, funerary monuments and sophisticated goldwork; the Muisca raft, depicting the El Dorado ceremony, is among the most famous pieces, preserved in Bogotá's Museo del Oro.

Colonial and 19th century

Colonial art was dominated by religious painting and sculpture from workshops in Bogotá, Tunja and Popayán, blending European Baroque with local materials. In the 19th century, costumbrista watercolours and landscape painting flourished.

Modern and contemporary

Fernando Botero (1932–2023) became Colombia's most famous artist with his signature volumetric style, "Boterismo". Other major figures include Alejandro Obregón, Enrique Grau, Édgar Negret, Eduardo Ramírez Villamizar, and contemporary artist Doris Salcedo, known for installations addressing political violence.
