Watervliet Shaker Village

Watervliet Shaker Village, in Colonie, Albany County, New York, was the first Shaker settlement in the United States, founded in 1776 by Mother Ann Lee and a small band of followers who had emigrated from England. The site, originally in the area known as Niskayuna, became the seed community from which the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing spread across the northeastern and midwestern United States.

History

Ann Lee and her companions arrived in New York in 1774 and settled on leased land near Watervliet in 1776. The community, organised from 1787, developed into four "families" — Church, North, West and South — with communal property, celibacy, and distinctive worship involving marching and dance, which gave the Shakers their name. Watervliet became known for agricultural innovation, including the packaging and sale of garden seeds, and for fine craftsmanship.

Decline and preservation

Membership declined through the late nineteenth century. The South Family closed in 1925, when Albany County bought much of the land; the last Watervliet Shakers left in 1938. Ann Lee is buried in the Shaker cemetery on the site. Today the surviving Church Family buildings, including the 1848 Meeting House, are preserved as a museum operated by the Shaker Heritage Society, and the area is listed as a historic district.
