Canary Islands chiffchaff

Scientific namePhylloscopus canariensis
FamilyPhylloscopidae (leaf warblers)
RangeCanary Islands
StatusLeast Concern

The Canary Islands chiffchaff (Phylloscopus canariensis) is a small leaf warbler endemic to the Canary Islands, an archipelago off the northwest coast of Africa. It was formerly treated as a subspecies of the common chiffchaff (Phylloscopus collybita) but is now recognised as a separate species based on its distinct song, morphology and genetics.

Description

The bird is about 11 centimetres long, with olive-brown upperparts, pale underparts with a yellowish wash, a distinct pale supercilium and a fine pointed bill. Compared with the common chiffchaff it has a longer bill and relatively shorter, more rounded wings — an adaptation to a sedentary, non-migratory life.

Distribution and habitat

Two subspecies are recognised: P. c. canariensis on the western islands (Gran Canaria, Tenerife, La Gomera, El Hierro and La Palma) and P. c. exsul on Lanzarote and possibly Fuerteventura, the latter now thought likely extinct. The species inhabits gardens, scrub, cultivated land and the laurel forests of the islands, where it feeds on insects gleaned from foliage.

Song

Its song resembles the "chiff-chaff" repetition of its mainland relative but is faster and more explosive, and is heard throughout much of the year.
