Magtymguly Pyragy

Magtymguly Pyragy (c. 1724 – c. 1807) was a Turkmen spiritual leader, philosophical poet, and Sufi widely considered the greatest figure in Turkmen literature and the founder of the Turkmen literary language. He is commonly known simply as Magtymguly and revered as a national poet of Turkmenistan.

Life

Magtymguly was born in the village of Haji Govshan near Gonbad-e Kavus, in the region of the Kopet Dag mountains on the present-day Turkmen–Iranian frontier. He was the son of Döwletmämmet Azady, himself a poet and scholar, from whom he received his early education. He studied at madrasas in Khiva and Bukhara and travelled widely in Persia, Afghanistan, and India. His pen name Pyragy (Fragi) means "the separated one" — separated from his beloved Mengli, to whom many of his love poems are addressed, and in Sufi terms separated from God.

Works

More than 800 poems are attributed to Magtymguly, covering love, mourning, moral instruction, and social criticism of corrupt beys and mullahs. Writing in a Turkmen close to the spoken language and using the syllabic metres of oral poetry, he made literature accessible beyond the Persian- and Chagatai-reading elite. His enduring dream, expressed in poems such as "Türkmeniň", was the political unification of the Turkmen tribes, who in his lifetime were divided and raided by neighbouring powers.

Legacy

Magtymguly is a central symbol of Turkmen national identity: his portrait appears on banknotes, his verses are memorized in schools, and the day of his commemoration is a national occasion. His works have been translated into many languages.
