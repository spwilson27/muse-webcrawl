Paul Houlton Terry (19 February 1887 – 25 October 1971) was an American cartoonist, animator and film producer. He founded the Terrytoons studio, which produced theatrical cartoon series for 20th Century Fox including Mighty Mouse, Heckle and Jeckle, Gandy Goose and Deputy Dawg. One of the most prolific producers in animation history, Terry made more than 1,300 cartoons over a career spanning the silent era to television.

Early career

Terry was born in San Mateo, California, and raised in San Francisco, where he worked as a newspaper photographer and cartoonist. Moving to New York, he sold a comic strip, Al Falfa's Adventures, and in 1915 produced one of the earliest animated films made in the United States featuring his character Farmer Al Falfa. After service as a cameraman in the U.S. Army Signal Corps during the First World War, he worked for Bray Studios and then produced the Aesop's Film Fables series through the 1920s, among the most popular cartoons of the silent era.

Terrytoons

In 1929 Terry founded his own studio in New Rochelle, New York, and was among the first to adopt synchronised sound and, later, Technicolor and CinemaScope. Famous for an economy-driven production philosophy — he boasted of making cartoons cheaper and faster than Disney, remarking that "Disney is the Tiffany's of the business; I am the Woolworth's" — Terry nevertheless produced enduring characters. Mighty Mouse (debuting 1942 as Super Mouse) became a television staple, and the magpies Heckle and Jeckle (1946) exemplified the studio's wisecracking style. In 1955 Terry sold Terrytoons to CBS, reportedly for several million dollars, in one of the first major animation-studio sales of the television age; the studio's library anchored CBS's Saturday-morning programming for years.

Legacy

Terry's studio trained many animators who later shaped American television animation, and his shrewd retention of rights to his films made him one of the few early animation pioneers to profit handsomely from the television era. He died in New York in 1971.
