Convertible

A convertible or cabriolet is a passenger car that can be driven with or without a roof in place. The methods of retracting and storing the roof vary across manufacturers and models.

A convertible car's design allows an open-air driving experience, with the ability to provide a roof when required. A potential drawback of convertibles is their reduced structural rigidity (requiring significant engineering and modification to counteract the side effects of almost completely removing a car's roof).

The majority of convertible roofs are of a folding construction framework with the actual top made from cloth or other fabric. Other types of convertible roofs include retractable hardtops (often constructed from metal or plastic) and detachable hardtops (where a metal or plastic roof is manually removed and often stored in the trunk).

History

Early automobiles were open by default; fully enclosed bodies became common only in the 1910s and 1920s. The folding-top convertible became a distinct body style in the interwar period, and postwar models such as the Volkswagen Beetle Cabriolet and various American landau convertibles popularized the type. Modern retractable hardtops appeared in production form in the 1990s and 2000s.

Roof designs

Soft tops are typically made of vinyl or canvas over an articulated folding frame. Retractable hardtops fold into the trunk in powered sections. Some convertibles, such as many sports cars, have manually operated tops to save weight.

Safety

Modern convertibles incorporate reinforced A-pillars and, in many cases, automatically deploying rollover bars behind the seats to protect occupants in a rollover crash.
