Selimiye Mosque, Edirne

Selimiye Mosque
Location: Edirne, Turkey
Architect: Mimar Sinan
Completed: 1574/75
Style: Ottoman
Dome diameter: 31.28 m
Minarets: 4, height 84 m

The Selimiye Mosque (Turkish: Selimiye Camii) is an Ottoman imperial mosque in Edirne, Turkey, commissioned by Sultan Selim II and built by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan between 1568 and 1575. Sinan considered it his masterpiece, and it is widely regarded as the crowning achievement of Ottoman architecture. The mosque and its social complex (külliye) were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2011.

Architecture

The mosque is centred on a vast dome 31.28 metres in internal diameter — slightly larger than that of Hagia Sophia — carried on eight massive piers and ringed by semi-domes and a system of buttresses that leave the prayer hall unusually open and luminous. Four exceptionally slender minarets, each 84 metres tall with three balconies, frame the dome. The interior is decorated with Iznik tiles, carved marble and calligraphy; the sultan's lodge and the muezzin's platform rest beneath the dome's centre.

Complex

The külliye includes two madrasas, a primary school, a covered bazaar (arasta), a library, and an outer courtyard. The mosque stands on high ground and dominates the skyline of the former Ottoman capital.

History

Edirne was besieged several times in the 19th and 20th centuries; during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 some of the mosque's tiles were looted by Russian troops. Restoration work has continued into the 21st century.
