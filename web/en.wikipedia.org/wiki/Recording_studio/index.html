Recording studio

A recording studio is a specialized facility for recording and mixing of instrumental or vocal musical performances, spoken words, and other sounds. They range in size from a small in-home project studio large enough to record a single singer-guitarist to a large building with an orchestra of hundreds of musicians.

Design and rooms

Studios typically consist of several acoustically isolated spaces: a live room where performers play, a control room where the engineer and producer monitor and mix the recording through a mixing console or digital audio workstation, and smaller isolation booths for vocals or amplifiers. Rooms are treated with absorption and diffusion to control reflections, and soundproofed to prevent noise intrusion.

Equipment

Equipment typically includes a mixing console, multitrack recorders or a digital audio workstation, monitor speakers, microphones, headphones, and outboard signal processors such as compressors and reverbs. Historically studios relied on analog tape machines; by the 1990s and 2000s, computer-based recording became dominant, enabling affordable home studios.

Roles

Sessions are run by a recording engineer, who operates the equipment, often working with a record producer who guides the artistic result. Famous facilities include Abbey Road Studios in London, Capitol Studios in Los Angeles, and Sun Studio in Memphis.
