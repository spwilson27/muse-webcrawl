The Polish People's Republic (Polish: Polska Rzeczpospolita Ludowa, PRL) was the official name of Poland from 1952 to 1989, denoting the Soviet-aligned socialist state that existed from the end of the Second World War until the fall of communism. Ruled by the Polish United Workers' Party under the leadership of the Soviet bloc, it was a founding member of the Warsaw Pact and Comecon.

Establishment

As Soviet forces liberated Poland from Nazi occupation in 1944–45, a communist-led provisional government displaced the London-based government-in-exile. Rigged elections in 1947 consolidated power; the 1952 constitution proclaimed the People's Republic. Poland's borders shifted westward — losing eastern territories to the USSR and gaining former German lands — and millions of people were resettled.

Crises and change

The regime faced repeated crises: the Poznań protests of 1956 opened the "Polish October" thaw under Gomułka; student and anti-Semitic purges convulsed 1968; food-price riots in 1970 and 1976 forced concessions. In 1980 strikes at the Gdańsk Shipyard, led by Lech Wałęsa, created Solidarity, the bloc's first independent trade union, with ten million members. General Jaruzelski imposed martial law in December 1981, but the movement survived underground.

Fall

Economic failure and renewed strikes brought the 1989 Round Table talks and semi-free elections in June 1989, which Solidarity swept. Tadeusz Mazowiecki became the first non-communist prime minister in the bloc, and on 31 December 1989 the state's name reverted to the Republic of Poland, closing the PRL era.
