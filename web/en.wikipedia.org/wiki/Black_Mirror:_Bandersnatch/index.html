Black Mirror: Bandersnatch is a 2018 interactive film in the British science-fiction anthology series Black Mirror. Developed and released by Netflix, it was written by series creator Charlie Brooker and directed by David Slade. Viewers make decisions for the protagonist at numerous branching points, producing different story paths and multiple endings from roughly five hours of filmed interactive footage.

Plot

In 1984, young programmer Stefan Butler (Fionn Whitehead) is adapting a choose-your-own-adventure novel, Bandersnatch, by the troubled writer Jerome F. Davies, into a video game for the company Tuckersoft. As development proceeds, Stefan's grip on reality loosens: he begins to suspect he is being controlled — by the viewer, among other possibilities — and the narrative mirrors the branching choices imposed on him, with guidance from star programmer Colin Ritman (Will Poulter). Depending on the viewer's choices, Stefan may finish the game, suffer a breakdown, commit violence, or become aware that he is a character in a Netflix production.

Interactivity and production

Bandersnatch was Netflix's first interactive work aimed at adults, following earlier interactive children's programmes. Brooker initially resisted the format, then built the story with Twine and bespoke Netflix branching technology; the film contains over a trillion permutations of its segments, with a typical viewing lasting about 90 minutes. It was kept secret until shortly before release and was released between series four and five of Black Mirror.

Reception

The film won two Primetime Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Television Movie, and was widely discussed for its metafictional treatment of free will and its hidden paths and Easter eggs. The interactive film was removed from Netflix's catalogue in May 2025 as the company retired its interactive titles.
