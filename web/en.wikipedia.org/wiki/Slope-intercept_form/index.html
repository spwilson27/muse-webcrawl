Slope-intercept form

The slope-intercept form is a way of writing the equation of a straight line in the Cartesian plane. It takes the form

y = mx + b

where m is the slope of the line and b is its y-intercept — the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis, i.e. the point (0, b).

Parameters

The slope m measures the line's steepness and direction: it is the change in y per unit change in x ("rise over run"), equal to tan θ where θ is the angle between the line and the positive x-axis. A positive slope rises to the right, a negative slope falls, m = 0 gives a horizontal line, and a vertical line has undefined slope and cannot be written in this form.

Use

Because both parameters are read directly from the equation, slope-intercept form is the most common presentation in introductory algebra: a line can be sketched immediately by marking the intercept b on the y-axis and then using the slope to find a second point. The form is a special case of the point-slope form, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), obtained by taking the known point to be the intercept (0, b). In statistics, the fitted line of linear regression is often reported in slope-intercept form, with m the regression coefficient and b the model's constant.
