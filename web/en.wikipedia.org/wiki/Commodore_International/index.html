Commodore International

Commodore International Corporation was an American home computer and electronics manufacturer founded in 1954 by Jack Tramiel. It produced the PET, VIC-20, the best-selling Commodore 64, and the Amiga line before declaring bankruptcy on 29 April 1994.

History

Tramiel, a Polish immigrant and Auschwitz survivor, began with a typewriter repair business in Toronto, moving into calculators and then computers with the acquisition of MOS Technology in 1976, which gave Commodore the 6502 microprocessor family. The PET (1977) was one of the first all-in-one personal computers.

The 64 and Amiga era

The Commodore 64 (1982) became the highest-selling single computer model ever, with estimates of 12.5–17 million units. After Tramiel's departure in 1984, Commodore acquired Amiga Corporation and launched the Amiga 1000 in 1985; the Amiga 500 (1987) was a major success in Europe.

Decline

Despite strong products, Commodore suffered from marketing failures, management turmoil, and the rise of IBM PC compatibles. It filed for bankruptcy in April 1994, one of the most notable collapses of the early personal computer industry.
