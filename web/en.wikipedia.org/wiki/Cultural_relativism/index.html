Cultural relativism
Field: Anthropology
Key figure: Franz Boas
Opposed to: Ethnocentrism

Cultural relativism is the principle that a person's beliefs and practices should be understood in terms of their own culture, rather than judged by the criteria of another. It became a central methodological tenet of anthropology through Franz Boas and his students, including Ruth Benedict and Margaret Mead.

Forms

Descriptive relativism notes that cultures genuinely differ in moral codes; methodological relativism requires suspending judgement to understand a culture on its own terms; moral relativism holds that no culture's norms can be judged superior — a stronger claim many anthropologists do not endorse.

Debates

Critics argue relativism can excuse human-rights abuses, while defenders reply it counters ethnocentrism and colonial arrogance. The American Anthropological Association's 1947 statement questioned universal human-rights declarations, whereas its later statements affirm universal rights — an ongoing tension with universalism.
