Saint-Gotthard Massif

The Saint-Gotthard Massif (German: Gotthardmassiv or Sankt-Gotthard-Massiv, Italian: Massiccio del San Gottardo) is a mountain range in the Alps in Switzerland. It lies at the heart of the Swiss Alps, on the border between the cantons of Valais, Ticino, Uri and Graubünden. Its highest summit is the Pizzo Rotondo (3,192 m). The massif takes its name from the Gotthard Pass, one of the most important north–south crossings of the Alps.

Geography and hydrology

The massif is one of the great watershed centres of Europe. Four major rivers rise in or near it: the Rhine (from the Vorderrhein and Hinterrhein sources in the surrounding region), the Rhône, which begins at the Rhône Glacier to the west, the Reuss, which drains northward through the Schöllenen Gorge, and the Ticino, flowing south to Lake Maggiore. This gave rise to the description of the Gotthard as the "water castle of Europe."

Geology

The massif is one of the external crystalline massifs of the Alps, formed of old Variscan basement rocks — chiefly granites and gneisses — that were uplifted and exposed during the Alpine orogeny. The Gotthard granite was quarried and studied extensively during the construction of the great tunnels.

Transport

Three famous crossings pierce or surmount the massif: the Gotthard Pass road (opened to mule traffic in the medieval period and famous for the Devil's Bridge in the Schöllenen Gorge), the Gotthard railway tunnel of 1882, long the world's longest tunnel, and the Gotthard road tunnel of 1980. In 2016 the Gotthard Base Tunnel, at 57 km the longest railway tunnel in the world, opened beneath the massif.
