Kowloon Walled City Park

Garden pavilions in Kowloon Walled City Park, Hong Kong

The Kowloon Walled City Park (Chinese: 九龍寨城公園) is a historical park in Kowloon City, Hong Kong. Opened in December 1995, it occupies the site of the former Kowloon Walled City, the famously dense and largely ungoverned enclave that was demolished in 1993–94. The park is designed in the Jiangnan garden style of the early Qing dynasty and covers about 31,000 square metres.

History of the site

The Walled City began as a Chinese military outpost and fort in the nineteenth century. After Britain leased the New Territories in 1898, the enclave's ambiguous legal status allowed it to grow without building regulation into a block of some 300 interconnected high-rise buildings housing an estimated 33,000–50,000 residents—one of the most densely populated places on Earth. The governments of Britain, China and Hong Kong agreed to demolish it in 1987; evictions and demolition followed, and the site was redeveloped as a park, preserving relics including the former yamen building and remnants of the old city wall and South Gate.

Layout

The park is divided into eight landscape features, including the restored yamen (the only Qing dynasty building retained), the Garden of Four Seasons, the Mountain View Pavilion, chess gardens and a Garden of Zodiac. Two cannons, stone inscriptions and the foundation remains of the original wall are displayed as exhibits.
