Single-sex education

Single-sex education
Other namesSingle-gender education, same-sex schooling
Practised byPublic and private schools worldwide

Single-sex education, also known as single-gender education or single-sex schooling, is the practice of conducting education with male and female students attending separate classes, schools, or both. The practice was common before the 20th century, particularly in secondary and higher education. Single-sex education in many cultures is advocated on the basis of tradition as well as religion, while proponents in more recent times have argued that it improves student outcomes.

History

Before the 19th century, single-sex education was the norm in most countries, with formal schooling for girls often limited or absent. As mass public education spread in the 19th and 20th centuries, mixed-sex (coeducational) schooling became increasingly common in Europe and North America. By the late 20th century, most public schools in Western countries were coeducational, although single-sex schools remained widespread in many Muslim-majority countries and in private education.

Arguments in favour

Supporters argue that single-sex classrooms can reduce distractions, allow teaching methods tailored to perceived gender differences in learning, and increase girls' participation in mathematics and science and boys' participation in languages and the arts. Some studies have reported that girls in single-sex schools take more advanced mathematics and science courses.

Arguments against

Critics argue that the evidence for academic benefits is weak once socioeconomic factors are controlled for, and that separating students by gender may reinforce stereotypes and reduce opportunities for social interaction between boys and girls. A 2014 meta-analysis published in Psychological Bulletin found little evidence of advantages for single-sex schooling over coeducation in mathematics performance.

By country

In the United States, single-sex classes in public schools expanded after 2006 regulatory changes but remain a small minority. In the United Kingdom, most state schools are mixed, while many independent schools are single-sex. Single-sex schooling remains the dominant model in much of the Middle East and in many Catholic and Muslim school networks worldwide.
