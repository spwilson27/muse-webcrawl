Jacques Marie Émile Lacan (13 April 1901 – 9 September 1981) was a French psychoanalyst and psychiatrist. Calling for a "return to Freud", he argued that the unconscious is structured like a language, and developed concepts — the mirror stage, the registers of the Imaginary, the Symbolic and the Real, the Name-of-the-Father, and desire as the desire of the Other — that made him a central, if controversial, figure in twentieth-century philosophy, literary theory and psychoanalysis.

Life

Lacan trained in psychiatry in Paris, writing his 1932 doctoral thesis on paranoia and its relation to personality. He joined the Paris psychoanalytic society and presented his paper on the mirror stage at the 1936 Marienbad congress. After disputes over his variable-length sessions he broke with the International Psychoanalytical Association and founded his own school, the École Freudienne de Paris, in 1964, dissolving it in 1980, a year before his death.

The seminars

From 1953 until 1980 Lacan delivered an annual public seminar in Paris, which formed the vehicle for his evolving teaching. Elaborating on Freud through Saussurean linguistics, structural anthropology and, later, topology and mathematical formalisation, the seminars redefined clinical practice for a generation of analysts. His writings were collected in the Écrits (1966), a famously difficult bestseller.

Key concepts

In the mirror stage, the infant identifies with its mirror image, founding the ego in an imaginary misrecognition. The Symbolic order of language and law structures the subject, whose desire is shaped by the desire of the Other. The Real names what resists symbolisation. Later work introduced the Borromean knot linking the three registers and the formulas of sexuation.

Influence

Lacanian theory strongly influenced French intellectual life, film theory, feminism and literary studies, and Lacanian schools remain active particularly in France and Latin America. Critics have charged his style with obscurity, while defenders see the difficulty as integral to his subject matter.
