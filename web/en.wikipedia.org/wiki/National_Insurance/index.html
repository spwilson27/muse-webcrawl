National Insurance

National Insurance (NI) is the system of social security contributions in the United Kingdom. Introduced by the National Insurance Act 1911 and greatly expanded after the Second World War following the Beveridge Report, it funds contributory state benefits including the State Pension, as well as contributing to the National Health Service. Workers and employers pay National Insurance contributions (NICs) as a percentage of earnings above certain thresholds, and individuals are identified by a National Insurance number.

History

The National Insurance Act 1911, introduced by Chancellor of the Exchequer David Lloyd George, created a scheme of compulsory health and unemployment insurance for workers, financed by contributions from employees, employers and the state — famously "ninepence for fourpence". The system was overhauled after the 1942 Beveridge Report: the National Insurance Act 1946 established a comprehensive scheme covering unemployment, sickness, maternity, widowhood and old age, which took effect on the "appointed day", 5 July 1948, alongside the National Health Service. Although originally conceived as an insurance fund, in practice contributions are not segregated in individual accounts and are largely used on a pay-as-you-go basis.

Contributions

Contributions are divided into classes. Employees pay Class 1 contributions deducted from wages by their employer (primary contributions), while employers pay secondary contributions; the self-employed pay Class 2 and Class 4 contributions, and voluntary Class 3 contributions can be made to fill gaps in a contribution record. Entitlement to the full State Pension generally requires 35 qualifying years of contributions or credits. Rates and thresholds are among the most politically sensitive elements of the UK tax system, since NICs function economically much like an income tax, though they are legally distinct and do not apply to most investment income or, traditionally, to earnings above state pension age for employees.

National Insurance number

Every person in the system has a National Insurance number, in the format of two letters, six digits and a final letter (for example, QQ 12 34 56 C). Numbers are allocated automatically to children whose parents claim Child Benefit, and are used by HMRC and the Department for Work and Pensions to track contributions and benefit claims throughout a person's life.
