Jaswant Singh Rawat

Rifleman Jaswant Singh Rawat, MVC
Born: 19 August 1941, Uttarakhand
Died: 17 November 1962, Arunachal Pradesh
Unit: 4 Garhwal Rifles
Award: Maha Vir Chakra (posthumous)

Jaswant Singh Rawat (19 August 1941 – 17 November 1962) was an Indian Army soldier in the 4th battalion of the Garhwal Rifles. He was posthumously awarded the Maha Vir Chakra, India's second-highest wartime gallantry award, for his actions during the Sino-Indian War of 1962.

Battle of Nuranang

During the Battle of Nuranang in what is now Arunachal Pradesh, Rawat's battalion was ordered to withdraw. According to the celebrated account, Rawat remained behind and, with the help of two local Monpa women, set up weapons at several points to give the impression of a larger force, holding off Chinese troops for about 72 hours before he was killed on 17 November 1962.

Legacy

A shrine, Jaswant Garh, is maintained at the site by the Indian Army, where Rawat is honoured almost as a guardian spirit of the pass; he is formally treated as still on duty and was promoted posthumously over the years in army tradition. His story was dramatised in the 2019 film 72 Hours: Martyr Who Never Died.
