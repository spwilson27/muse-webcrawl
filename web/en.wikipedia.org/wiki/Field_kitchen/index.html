Field kitchen

A field kitchen is a mobile kitchen used by military units to prepare and serve food to troops in the field. Field kitchens range from trailer-mounted cooking units towed behind vehicles to containerised kitchen systems, and they played a famous role in both World Wars — the German Gulaschkanone ("goulash cannon"), a horse-drawn or trailer-mounted stove on wheels, being an iconic example.

Development

Organised military cooking developed in the 19th century as armies grew and logistics professionalised. Alexis Soyer's portable stove, designed during the Crimean War, was used by the British Army for over a century. During the First and Second World Wars, field kitchens accompanied units close behind the front line, with cooks preparing hot meals that were carried forward in insulated containers. The Soviet FK series and similar systems remained in service for decades.

Modern use

Modern field kitchens are often containerised, capable of feeding several hundred soldiers, and supplemented by ration packs. Similar mobile kitchens are also used by disaster-relief and humanitarian organisations to feed displaced populations.
