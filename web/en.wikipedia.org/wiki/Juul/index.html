Juul Labs, Inc. is an American electronic cigarette company whose flagship product, the JUUL, is a compact USB-shaped e-cigarette using pre-filled nicotine salt pods. Introduced in 2015, Juul came to dominate the US e-cigarette market — and became the focus of a national controversy over youth vaping.

History

Juul was developed by James Monsees and Adam Bowen, Stanford design graduates who had founded PAX Labs, maker of the Pax vaporiser. The Juul device used nicotine salts derived from tobacco leaf, delivering nicotine more like a conventional cigarette than earlier freebase-nicotine e-liquids. In July 2017 Juul Labs was spun out as an independent company.

Sales surged in 2017–2018, helped by sleek design and social-media-driven popularity among teenagers and young adults. In December 2018, tobacco giant Altria (owner of Marlboro) bought a 35% stake for $12.8 billion, valuing Juul at $38 billion.

Youth vaping controversy and regulation

US health officials, including Surgeon General Jerome Adams, declared youth e-cigarette use an "epidemic" in 2018; federal surveys found vaping by high-school students had spiked sharply. Accused of marketing to minors, Juul withdrew most flavoured pods (such as mango and crème) from retail sale in 2018–2019, suspended US advertising, and replaced its CEO with Altria executive K.C. Crosthwaite in 2019.

The FDA issued a marketing denial order in June 2022 that would have removed Juul products from the US market; the order was stayed in court and later rescinded for further review, and in 2025 the FDA authorised the sale of certain Juul products. Juul settled a series of lawsuits with US states and private plaintiffs over its marketing practices for more than $1 billion in total, without admitting wrongdoing in some agreements, and Altria wrote down its investment to a fraction of its value before exchanging its stake in 2023.
