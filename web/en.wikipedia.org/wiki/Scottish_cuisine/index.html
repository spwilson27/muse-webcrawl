Scottish cuisine

Scottish cuisine (Scots: Scots cuisine; Scottish Gaelic: biadh na h-Alba) encompasses the cooking styles, traditions and recipes associated with Scotland. It has distinctive attributes and dishes of its own while sharing much with wider British and European food traditions, reflecting Scotland's climate, its produce of land and sea — oats, barley, dairy produce, game, salmon and shellfish — and influences from France (through the Auld Alliance), the Norse world and later global cuisines.

Traditional dishes

The best-known dish is haggis, a savoury pudding of sheep's offal minced with onion, oatmeal and suet, traditionally encased in the animal's stomach and served with "neeps and tatties" (mashed turnip/swede and potatoes), celebrated at Burns suppers on 25 January. Other staples include:

  • Cullen skink — smoked haddock, potato and onion soup from Moray
  • Arbroath smokies and Finnian haddie — traditional smoked fish
  • Scotch broth, cock-a-leekie and partan bree (crab soup)
  • Porridge and oatcakes; stovies; forfar bridies and Scotch pies
  • Cranachan — raspberries, cream, honey, whisky and toasted oats
  • Tablet, shortbread and Dundee cake; clootie dumpling
  • Black pudding (notably Stornoway) and white pudding

Produce and drink

Scotland is noted for Aberdeen Angus beef, Scotch lamb, venison, grouse and other game, Atlantic salmon, and soft fruit. Scotch whisky is its most famous export, alongside Scottish ales; Irn-Bru is an iconic soft drink. Scotland's whisky distilleries, from Speyside to Islay, underpin a major food and drink industry.

Modern developments

Modern Scottish cooking, exemplified by Michelin-starred restaurants, reinterprets the larder with contemporary techniques. At the same time, Scotland's diet has been criticised for deep-fried foods and high sugar consumption, the "deep-fried Mars bar" of Stonehaven chip shops having become an (unendorsed) symbol. Recent Scottish governments have promoted provenance and protected food names, with several products holding protected geographical status.
