Orthonormal basis

In mathematics, particularly linear algebra, an orthonormal basis for an inner product space is a basis whose vectors are mutually orthogonal and all of unit length. Orthonormal bases simplify many computations: coordinates of a vector are given simply by inner products with the basis vectors, and lengths and angles are preserved in the same form as for Cartesian coordinates.

The standard basis of the Euclidean plane is orthonormal

Definition

A set of vectors {vi} in an inner product space is orthonormal if ⟨vi, vj⟩ = 0 for i ≠ j and ⟨vi, vi⟩ = 1 for all i — compactly, ⟨vi, vj⟩ = δij, the Kronecker delta. If, in addition, the set is a basis of the space, it is an orthonormal basis. For any vector v, v = Σ ⟨v, vi⟩ vi, and Parseval's identity gives ||v||² = Σ |⟨v, vi⟩|².

Existence and construction

Every finite-dimensional inner product space has an orthonormal basis. The Gram–Schmidt process converts any basis into an orthonormal one by successive projection and normalisation. In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, a maximal orthonormal set forms an orthonormal basis in the sense that every vector is the limit of convergent series of basis combinations; examples include the Fourier basis of trigonometric functions in L² spaces.

Applications

Orthonormal bases underlie Fourier analysis, the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators, orthonormal wavelet bases in signal processing, and the representation of quantum states. Linear maps preserving orthonormal bases are represented by orthogonal (or unitary) matrices.
