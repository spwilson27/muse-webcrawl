Patsy Mink

Patsy Matsu Mink (née Takemoto; December 6, 1927 – September 28, 2002) was an American attorney and politician from Hawaii who served in the United States House of Representatives. She was the first woman of color and the first Asian-American woman elected to Congress, and she is best known as a principal author of Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, which prohibits sex discrimination in federally funded education programs and transformed women's athletics in the United States.

Mink represented Hawaii in Congress for a total of 24 years.

Early life and career

Born in Paia, Maui, to Japanese-American parents, Mink graduated as valedictorian of Maui High School in 1944. After being rejected by medical schools — an experience she later attributed to sex discrimination — she earned a law degree from the University of Chicago in 1951, where she met her husband, John Mink. In Hawaii she entered territorial politics, helping found the Young Democrats, and served in the territorial and state legislatures.

Congress

When Hawaii's seats in Congress expanded, Mink was elected in 1964 and served six terms. A liberal Democrat, she opposed the Vietnam War, supported the Great Society, and co-founded the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus later in her career. She ran a protest campaign for the 1972 Democratic presidential nomination as an anti-war candidate. After leaving the House in 1977 she served in the Carter administration at the State Department, on the Honolulu City Council in the 1980s, and returned to Congress in 1990, serving until her death in 2002.

Title IX

Mink's signature achievement was shepherding Title IX through Congress in 1972, drawing on her own experience of discrimination. When Title IX's athletics regulations were challenged in 2002, the law's implementing rules survived review; after her death, Congress renamed the statute the Patsy T. Mink Equal Opportunity in Education Act. She also authored the Women's Educational Equity Act of 1974. In 2014 she was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
