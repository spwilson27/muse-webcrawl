Operation Faustschlag

Date: 18 February – 3 March 1918

Location: Ukraine, Belarus, and the Baltic region

Part of: Eastern Front, First World War

Belligerents: German Empire and Austria-Hungary vs. Soviet Russia

Result: Central Powers victory; Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

Operation Faustschlag ("Fist Punch", also known as the Eleven Days' War) was a Central Powers offensive in the final stages of the First World War's Eastern Front, conducted from 18 February to 3 March 1918. After negotiations at Brest-Litovsk broke down — with Soviet negotiator Leon Trotsky adopting a policy of "no war, no peace" and walking out on 10 February — Germany and Austria-Hungary resumed hostilities against Soviet Russia.

The offensive

Central Powers forces, some 53 divisions, advanced along the entire front against minimal resistance, using railways to move rapidly deep into Russian territory. German troops occupied Minsk, Kyiv, and much of Ukraine, advanced through the Baltic provinces toward Estonia, and approached Petrograd closely enough to prompt the Soviet government to move the capital to Moscow. The advance was so rapid and unopposed that it was sometimes called the "Railway War."

Outcome

Faced with the collapse of resistance, Vladimir Lenin persuaded the Soviet leadership to accept harsh German terms. The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, signed on 3 March 1918, ended Russia's participation in the war; Russia ceded control of the Baltic states, Belarus, Ukraine, and its Polish territories, losing a third of its population and a large share of its industry and farmland. A separate treaty recognized the independence of the Ukrainian People's Republic, with which the Central Powers had signed a "Bread Peace" on 9 February.

The treaty was annulled after Germany's defeat on the Western Front in November 1918, but the offensive permitted Germany to transfer large forces west for the Spring Offensive of 1918.
