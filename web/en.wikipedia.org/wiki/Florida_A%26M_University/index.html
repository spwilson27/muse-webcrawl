Florida A&M University

Florida Agricultural and Mechanical University (Florida A&M or FAMU) is a public historically black land-grant university in Tallahassee, Florida, founded in 1887 as the State Normal College for Colored Students.

History

FAMU began with 15 students and two instructors and grew under presidents including Nathan B. Young and John Robert Edward Lee.

It is the largest historically black university by enrolment in the United States in many recent years and is a member of the State University System of Florida.

Academics and culture

The university offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees, including a law school in Orlando and a notable pharmacy programme.

Its Marching 101 band is internationally famous, and its alumni include filmmakers, politicians, and athletes.
