Mathis der Maler ("Mathis the Painter") is an opera by the German composer Paul Hindemith, who also wrote the libretto. Completed in 1935, it was premiered on 28 May 1938 at the Stadttheater in Zurich, Switzerland, after the Nazi regime had made its performance in Germany impossible. The opera concerns the 16th-century painter Matthias Grünewald, creator of the Isenheim Altarpiece, and his struggle between artistic duty and social engagement during the German Peasants' War.

Background

Hindemith began the opera in 1933 as political pressure on artists in Germany was mounting. The work's theme — whether an artist should abandon his craft to join a popular revolt — mirrored Hindemith's own predicament. Wilhelm Furtwängler's public defence of the composer in 1934, the so-called "Hindemith affair", failed to lift the informal ban, and the composer eventually emigrated.

Symphony

Before the opera was staged, Hindemith drew three movements from its music as the symphony Mathis der Maler, premiered by Furtwängler and the Berlin Philharmonic in March 1934. The symphony, whose movements evoke panels of the Isenheim Altarpiece — the Concert of Angels, the Entombment and the Temptation of Saint Anthony — remains one of Hindemith's most frequently performed works.

Synopsis

The painter Mathis, in the service of the Cardinal Albrecht of Mainz, is torn between his art and sympathy for the rebellious peasants. He joins their cause, witnesses their defeat and the burning of books, and returns to his workshop, where in a visionary scene modelled on Saint Anthony's temptations he resolves to serve through his painting.
