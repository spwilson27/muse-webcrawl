Dakota Access Pipeline protests

The Dakota Access Pipeline protests, also known by the hashtag #NoDAPL, were a series of protests in 2016–2017 against construction of the Dakota Access Pipeline in North Dakota, centred on the Standing Rock Indian Reservation.

Members of the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe argued the pipeline's crossing under Lake Oahe, near their water supply, threatened drinking water and sacred sites, and that proper consultation had not occurred. Protest camps, including Oceti Sakowin, drew thousands of Native American and environmental supporters, and clashes with law enforcement drew international attention.

In December 2016 the Army Corps of Engineers denied an easement pending an environmental review, but in early 2017 the incoming Trump administration reversed course; the pipeline entered service in June 2017. Litigation over its permits continued for years afterwards.
