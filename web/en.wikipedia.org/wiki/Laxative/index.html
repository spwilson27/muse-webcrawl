Laxative

Laxatives are substances that loosen stools and increase bowel movements, used to treat and prevent constipation. They are classified by mechanism: bulk-forming agents (such as psyllium) absorb water; osmotic laxatives (such as polyethylene glycol and lactulose) draw water into the bowel; stimulant laxatives (such as senna and bisacodyl) increase intestinal motility; and stool softeners (such as docusate) ease passage.

Laxatives are available over the counter and by prescription, and are also used to clear the bowel before colonoscopy and surgery. Overuse can cause dehydration, electrolyte imbalance and dependence of normal bowel function.

Laxative abuse occurs in some eating disorders and can have serious medical consequences.
