Georges Lemaître

Georges Henri Joseph Édouard Lemaître (17 July 1894 – 20 June 1966) was a Belgian Catholic priest, astronomer and physicist who first proposed the theory of the expansion of the universe and what became known as the Big Bang.

Georges Lemaître
Born: 17 July 1894, Charleroi, Belgium
Died: 20 June 1966, Leuven
Known for: Expansion of the universe, Big Bang theory, Hubble–Lemaître law

Work

In 1927 Lemaître published a paper deriving a linear relationship between galactic distances and recession velocities — later called the Hubble–Lemaître law — two years before Edwin Hubble's observational paper. In 1931 he proposed that the universe began from a single quantum, the "primeval atom" or "Cosmic Egg".

Career

Educated at the Catholic University of Leuven, Cambridge and MIT, Lemaître served as an artillery officer in World War I, was ordained in 1923, and later became president of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences. He also contributed to cosmic ray research and computation.

Legacy

Initially met with scepticism — Albert Einstein reportedly called his physics "abominable" before later endorsing expansion — Lemaître's ideas were vindicated by the discovery of the cosmic microwave background in 1965, shortly before his death. See also Globular cluster.
