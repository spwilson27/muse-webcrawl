Derealization

Derealization is an alteration in the perception of the external world, causing those with the condition to perceive it as unreal, distant, distorted, or falsified. Other symptoms include feeling as if one's environment lacks spontaneity, emotional colouring, and depth. Derealization is a dissociative symptom that may appear in moments of severe stress, and it is a core feature of depersonalization-derealization disorder, in which it occurs alongside depersonalization, the feeling of detachment from oneself.

Symptoms

People experiencing derealization describe the world as seeming flat, lifeless, foggy, or visually distorted, as though separated from them by a pane of glass or a veil. Objects may appear at the wrong size or distance, sounds may seem muted, and time may feel altered. Crucially, reality testing remains intact: the person knows the world is real even though it does not feel real, which distinguishes derealization from psychosis.

Causes

Transient derealization is common, reported by a large proportion of the general population in response to fatigue, stress, sleep deprivation, or sensory deprivation. It can also be triggered by anxiety and panic attacks, depression, trauma, migraine, epilepsy, and the use of or withdrawal from substances such as cannabis, hallucinogens, and ketamine. Persistent derealization may form part of depersonalization-derealization disorder, which is often associated with childhood trauma or severe stress.

Treatment

There is no medication approved specifically for derealization. Treatment generally focuses on underlying conditions and includes psychotherapy, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy techniques aimed at reducing catastrophic interpretation of the symptoms, which can fuel anxiety and perpetuate the cycle.
