ITU Region

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU), in its Radio Regulations, divides the world into three ITU Regions for the purposes of managing the global radio spectrum, because frequency allocations often differ between regions. The boundaries follow lines of longitude and geographical features defined in the Regulations.

The three regions

  • Region 1: Europe, Africa, the Middle East west of the Persian Gulf including Iraq, the territory of the former USSR, and Mongolia.
  • Region 2: The Americas (North and South America), Greenland, and some eastern Pacific islands.
  • Region 3: Asia east of Iran (including most of Iran), Oceania, and the rest of the Pacific.

Boundaries

Region 1 is bounded on the east by "Line B" (running from the North Pole down the 40°E meridian and around through the Black Sea region) and on the west by "Line A" (roughly the 10°W meridian through the Atlantic). Lines B and C (near 130°E) bound Region 3. Because of these divisions, a band such as the amateur radio or broadcast allocations may be licensed differently in, for instance, the United States (Region 2) versus Europe (Region 1) — a notable example being the 40-metre amateur band and FM broadcast and mobile allocations.
