Feria de Abril

The Feria de Abril (April Fair), officially the Feria de Abril de Sevilla, is a week-long festival held in Seville, Andalusia, Spain, beginning two weeks after Holy Week (Semana Santa). It is one of Spain's most famous fairs, drawing over a million visitors.

History

The fair began in 1847 as a livestock fair, proposed by two city councillors, and gradually evolved into a general celebration. Since 1973 it has been held on a dedicated fairground, the Real de la Feria, in the Los Remedios district, crossed by streets named for famous bullfighters.

Celebrations

The fairground fills with casetas — tented pavilions belonging to families, clubs, and associations — where attendees dance sevillanas, drink sherry and rebujito, and socialize late into the night. Women traditionally wear flamenco dresses, horse-drawn carriages parade daily, and bullfights are held each afternoon at the Maestranza bullring. The fair opens with the lighting of the main gate (alumbrao) and ends with fireworks.
