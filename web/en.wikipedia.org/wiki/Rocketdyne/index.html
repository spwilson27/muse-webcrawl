Rocketdyne

Rocketdyne was an American rocket engine design and manufacturing company headquartered in Canoga Park, in the western San Fernando Valley of Los Angeles, California. It produced many of the most important engines of the early space age, including the F-1 that powered the Saturn V Moon rocket, the J-2 used on its upper stages, and the RS-25 (Space Shuttle Main Engine).

History

Rocketdyne began as a division of North American Aviation in the years after the Second World War, developing engines for missiles such as Navaho, Redstone, Jupiter and Atlas. Its engines powered nearly every major American launch vehicle of the 1950s and 1960s. The division passed through Rockwell International and Boeing ownership; in 2005 Boeing sold it to Pratt & Whitney, forming Pratt & Whitney Rocketdyne. GenCorp (later Aerojet Rocketdyne) acquired the business in 2013, and L3Harris Technologies acquired Aerojet Rocketdyne in 2023, retiring much of the historic branding.

Notable engines

Engines designed or built by Rocketdyne include the F-1, J-2, RS-25, RS-27, RS-56, RS-68, H-1 and the RL10 (developed by Pratt & Whitney but later produced under the combined company).
