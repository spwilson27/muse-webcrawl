Foundation is a science fiction novel by American writer Isaac Asimov. It is the first published novel of the Foundation series, consisting of three novellas originally published in Astounding Science Fiction magazine between 1942 and 1944. The book was first published in 1951 by Gnome Press.

The novel introduces the concept of psychohistory, a fictional science developed by the mathematician Hari Seldon that combines history, sociology, and mathematical statistics to make general predictions about the future behaviour of very large groups of people. Using psychohistory, Seldon foresees the fall of the Galactic Empire and a following dark age lasting 30,000 years. To shorten this interregnum to a mere thousand years, he establishes two Foundations at opposite ends of the galaxy: the First Foundation, dedicated to preserving and advancing scientific knowledge, and the secretive Second Foundation, devoted to developing the science of the mind.

Plot summary

The novel consists of three parts. In "The Psychohistorians", Hari Seldon stands trial on Trantor, the capital of the Galactic Empire, for treason after predicting the Empire's fall. Instead of executing him, the authorities exile Seldon and his followers to Terminus, a remote planet at the edge of the galaxy, where they are ostensibly to compile the Encyclopedia Galactica.

In "The Encyclopedists", set fifty years later, the Foundation faces its first "Seldon Crisis" as the neighbouring kingdom of Anacreon threatens Terminus. Mayor Salvor Hardin realises that the Encyclopedia is a fraud designed to keep the colonists occupied, and that the Foundation's true power lies in its monopoly of advanced technology, which it dispenses to neighbouring worlds as a quasi-religion.

In "The Mayors", Hardin consolidates power by using the Foundation's priesthood of technicians to bring the rebellious Anacreon to heel, cementing the doctrine later summarised in his epigram: "Violence is the last refuge of the incompetent."

Reception and legacy

The Foundation trilogy won a special Hugo Award in 1966 for "Best All-Time Series". The novel is widely regarded as a landmark of science fiction, notable for its ideas-driven plotting and its portrayal of politics, religion, and economics as engines of historical change across centuries. A television adaptation premiered on Apple TV+ in 2021.
