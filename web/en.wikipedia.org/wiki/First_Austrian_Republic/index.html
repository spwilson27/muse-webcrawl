First Austrian Republic

The First Austrian Republic (German: Erste Österreichische Republik) was created after the signing of the Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye on 10 September 1919 — the settlement after the end of World War I which ended the Habsburg Empire — and ended with the establishment of the Austrofascist Federal State of Austria based upon a new constitution on 1 May 1934, which replaced the republic with a one-party state.

The republic's first years were marked by hyperinflation, political polarization between the Social Democratic Workers' Party and the Christian Social Party, and the formation of rival paramilitary organizations, the Republican Schutzbund and the Heimwehr. In 1927 the July Revolt saw the Palace of Justice in Vienna burned. Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss suspended parliament in March 1933, ruled by decree, and after the brief Austrian Civil War of February 1934 banned the Social Democrats and proclaimed the authoritarian May Constitution.

Foundation

On 12 November 1918, the Provisional National Assembly proclaimed the Republic of German-Austria; the 1919 treaty prohibited union with Germany (Anschluss) and fixed Austria's borders, losing South Tyrol, and German-speaking areas of Bohemia and Moravia to the new Czechoslovakia. The constitution of 1 October 1920, largely drafted by Hans Kelsen, made Austria a federal parliamentary republic.

Economy

The new state inherited an industrial base separated from its former food and coal supplies. Hyperinflation peaked in 1922 and was stabilized with a League of Nations loan and the introduction of the schilling in 1925. The Great Depression after 1929 devastated the economy, and the collapse of the Creditanstalt bank in May 1931 triggered a Central European financial crisis.

End

After Dollfuss's assassination by Nazi agents in the failed July Putsch of 1934, Kurt Schuschnigg continued the authoritarian Federal State until the Anschluss with Nazi Germany on 12–13 March 1938.
