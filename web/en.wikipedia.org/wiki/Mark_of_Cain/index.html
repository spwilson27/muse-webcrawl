Mark of Cain

The mark of Cain is a term from the Book of Genesis. After Cain murdered his brother Abel, God condemned Cain to a life of wandering exile, but placed a mark upon him to warn others that killing Cain would provoke vengeance sevenfold.

Biblical account

In Genesis 4, Cain complains that his punishment is greater than he can bear and that anyone who finds him may kill him. God replies that whoever kills Cain will suffer vengeance seven times over, and sets a mark (ot in Hebrew) upon Cain "lest any finding him should kill him". Cain then departs to the land of Nod, east of Eden.

Interpretations

The nature of the mark is unspecified in the text, and interpretations vary widely: a physical sign such as a scar or trembling, a sign given to Cain as assurance, or a symbol of divine protection amid punishment. In later tradition the phrase came to denote any visible stigma of disgrace or guilt. Historically, some interpreters wrongly associated the mark with skin colour, a reading used to justify racism and slavery and now widely repudiated by scholars.
