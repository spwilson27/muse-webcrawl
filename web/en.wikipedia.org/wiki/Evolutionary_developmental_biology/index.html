Evolutionary developmental biology

Evolutionary developmental biology, informally evo-devo, is a field of biological research that compares the developmental processes of different organisms to infer how developmental processes evolved and to understand how modifications to development produce organismal diversity.

History

Early ideas include von Baer's laws and Haeckel's recapitulation theory. The field crystallized in the 1980s with discovery of homeobox (Hox) genes — a conserved genetic toolkit controlling body plans across animals — by researchers including Edward B. Lewis, Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard, and Eric Wieschaus in Drosophila.

Key concepts

  • Deep homology: ancient genetic circuits reused in different structures
  • Modularity and heterochrony (changes in developmental timing)
  • Phenotypic plasticity and developmental bias
  • Regulatory evolution: changes in gene regulation rather than protein sequence driving morphological change

Major figures include Sean B. Carroll, author of Endless Forms Most Beautiful. See also Developmental biology and Evolution.
