I3C (bus)

I3C (officially the MIPI I3C, Improved Inter-Integrated Circuit) is a serial communication bus specification developed by the MIPI Alliance as the successor to I²C. Announced in 2016 and first published in 2017, it targets sensors and low-power peripherals in mobile and embedded systems.

Features

I3C retains the two-wire interface (clock and data) and multi-device topology of I²C while adding much higher speeds — up to 12.5 MHz in standard SDR mode and around 25–33 MHz in high-data-rate modes — using push-pull drivers rather than open-drain. It adds dynamic address assignment, in-band interrupts (so devices need no separate interrupt pins), common command codes, multi-controller support and hot-join of devices onto an active bus. Most I²C devices can coexist on an I3C bus, easing migration.

Versions

MIPI I3C Basic, a royalty-free subset, was published so the standard can be adopted broadly, and I3C has been incorporated into specifications such as DDR5 memory modules, where it is used for the serial presence detect and hub communication alongside the sideband bus.
