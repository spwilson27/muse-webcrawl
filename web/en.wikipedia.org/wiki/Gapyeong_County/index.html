Gapyeong County

Gapyeong County (Korean: 가평군, Gapyeong-gun) is a county in Gyeonggi Province, South Korea. It was the scene of the Battle of Kapyong, a major battle of the Korean War in April 1951. The county is known for its mountain scenery and the Bukhan River, and is a popular leisure and tourism destination close to Seoul.

History

The Battle of Kapyong (22–25 April 1951) saw Commonwealth forces — including Australian, Canadian, British, and New Zealand troops — block a Chinese advance toward Seoul in the Kapyong valley. The battle is commemorated by memorials in the county, and Gapyeong maintains sister-city-style ties with communities in Australia and Canada as a result.

Tourism

Gapyeong is best known as the location of Nami Island (Namiseom), a half-moon-shaped island in the Bukhan River popular for its tree-lined lanes, and the nearby Garden of Morning Calm, a large arboretum. Jara Island hosts an international jazz festival each autumn. The area's lakes and mountains support water sports, camping, and pensions catering to visitors from the Seoul metropolitan area.

Administrative divisions

The county consists of one town (Gapyeong-eup) and five townships (myeon): Sang, Ha, Buk, Seorak, Cheongpyeong, and Sangcheon. Its population is roughly 60,000.

Transportation

Gapyeong is served by the Gyeongchun Line of the Seoul Metropolitan Subway and ITX-Cheongchun trains, making it accessible in about an hour from central Seoul.
