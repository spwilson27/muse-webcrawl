Lošinj (Italian: Lussino) is a Croatian island in the northern Adriatic Sea, in the Kvarner Gulf south of the island of Cres, from which it is separated by a narrow artificial canal at Osor. With about 75 square kilometres it is one of the larger Croatian islands, and its main settlement, Mali Lošinj ("Little Lošinj"), is in fact considerably larger than Veli Lošinj ("Great Lošinj") — a reversal explained by Mali Lošinj's superior harbour and later growth.

History and tourism

Settled since prehistory and later Greek, Roman, Venetian and Austro-Hungarian, Lošinj became famous in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries for its shipowners and sea captains, whose fleets sailed worldwide and whose wealth built the captains' houses and churches of Veli and Mali Lošinj. Under Austria the island was developed as a fashionable climatic health resort — Archdukes and Viennese physicians praised its mild winters and pine forests — and tourism remains its economy today, centred on Čikat bay's hotels and campsites. The surrounding waters are a protected reserve for a resident population of bottlenose dolphins, studied by the Blue World Institute at Veli Lošinj. In 1999 a Greek fisherman's net recovered the Apoxyomenos, a remarkably preserved Hellenistic bronze statue of an athlete, from the sea near Lošinj; after restoration it is displayed in a dedicated museum in Mali Lošinj. The island is reached by ferry and by a small airport, and is linked by bridge to Cres at Osor.
