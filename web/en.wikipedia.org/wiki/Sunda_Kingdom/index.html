Sunda Kingdom

The Sunda Kingdom (Sundanese: Karajaan Sunda) was a Sundanese Hindu kingdom located in the western part of the island of Java, in present-day Indonesia, existing from 669 to around 1579. It was the successor of Tarumanagara and, for much of its history, shared the island with the great Javanese kingdoms to the east.

CapitalKawali; later Pakuan Pajajaran
Common languageSundanese, Sanskrit
ReligionHinduism (Sunda Wiwitan tradition)
Established669
Disestablishedc. 1579

History

According to the manuscript Pustaka Rajyarajya i Bhumi Nusantara, the kingdom was founded by Tarusbawa in 669 after the fall of Tarumanagara. Its best-known era is that of King Linggabuana, who died at the Battle of Bubat in 1357, and of Sri Baduga Maharaja (Prabu Siliwangi), who ruled from Pakuan Pajajaran in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries — a period of prosperity remembered fondly in Sundanese tradition.

Fall

In 1527 the kingdom made a treaty with the Portuguese, but its ports were progressively taken by the rising Banten Sultanate; the capital fell around 1579, ending the last Hindu kingdom of West Java.
