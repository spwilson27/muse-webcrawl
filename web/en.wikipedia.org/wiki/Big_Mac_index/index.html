Big Mac index

Big Mac index
Published byThe Economist
First publishedSeptember 1986
PurposeInformal measure of purchasing power parity

The Big Mac index is a price index published since 1986 by The Economist as an informal way of measuring the purchasing power parity (PPP) between two currencies, testing how far market exchange rates depart from the rate that would make the price of a Big Mac hamburger equal everywhere.

Method

Purchasing power parity theory holds that exchange rates should equalise the price of an identical basket of goods across countries. Using the Big Mac as the basket, the index computes an implied PPP exchange rate; comparing it with the actual rate suggests whether a currency is under- or over-valued against the US dollar.

Criticism and use

Economists note limits: Big Mac prices reflect local rents, wages, taxes and competition, and the burger is not traded internationally. Adjusted versions control for GDP per capita. Despite its lightness, the index is widely cited in textbooks and the press as an accessible introduction to exchange-rate economics.
