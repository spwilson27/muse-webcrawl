The Millennium Seed Bank is an international conservation project run by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, housed at Wakehurst in West Sussex. Opened in 2000, it is the world's largest ex situ plant conservation programme: seeds of wild plant species are collected, dried and frozen in underground vaults as an insurance policy against extinction, available for research, restoration and reintroduction.

Collections and partners

Working with partner institutions in close to 100 countries, the bank reached its initial goal in 2010 of storing seed from 10 per cent of the world's wild plant species, and by the 2020s held over 2.4 billion seeds representing roughly 40,000 species — about a sixth of known plant species with bankable seed. Collections emphasise plants of drylands, useful species, and those most threatened by climate change and habitat loss. A duplicate of UK collections is also held; the bank complements Norway's Svalbard Global Seed Vault, which focuses on crops.

How it works

Seeds are cleaned, counted (often by X-ray), dried to about 5 per cent moisture in controlled humidity rooms, sealed in glass containers and stored at −20 °C, conditions under which many species remain viable for centuries. Samples are periodically germination-tested, and the bank's scientists research seed longevity, dormancy and propagation of difficult species for habitat restoration.
