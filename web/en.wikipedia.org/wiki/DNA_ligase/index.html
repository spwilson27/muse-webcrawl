DNA ligase

DNA ligase
EC number6.5.1.1 (ATP-dependent); 6.5.1.2 (NAD+-dependent)
FunctionJoins DNA strands via phosphodiester bonds
RolesDNA replication, repair, recombination

DNA ligase is a type of enzyme that facilitates the joining of DNA strands together by catalyzing the formation of a phosphodiester bond. It plays a role in repairing single-strand breaks in duplex DNA in living organisms, and in sealing the nicks between Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand during DNA replication.

Mechanism

DNA ligases catalyze the formation of a phosphodiester bond between the 3′-hydroxyl group at the end of one DNA strand and the 5′-phosphate group at the end of another. The reaction requires energy: eukaryotic and viral ligases use ATP, while many bacterial ligases use NAD+ as the energy source. The enzyme first adenylylates itself, transfers the AMP group to the 5′-phosphate at the nick, and then the 3′-OH attacks to form the bond, releasing AMP.

Types

In mammals, four ligases (I, III, IV, and the splice variant IIIβ) have distinct roles: DNA ligase I joins Okazaki fragments and participates in repair; ligase III is involved in base-excision repair and mitochondrial DNA maintenance; ligase IV performs the final step of non-homologous end joining of double-strand breaks. In E. coli, the NAD+-dependent LigA is the main ligase.

Applications

Purified DNA ligase — especially T4 DNA ligase from bacteriophage T4 — is a standard tool in molecular biology and genetic engineering, used in molecular cloning to join DNA fragments, in Gibson assembly workflows, and in ligase chain reaction assays. Defects in human ligases cause diseases, e.g., LIG4 syndrome, an immunodeficiency disorder from mutations in DNA ligase IV.
