Brendan Foster

[Image placeholder: Brendan Foster]

Sir Brendan Foster CBE (born 12 January 1948) is a British former long-distance runner, television commentator and businessman. One of Britain's leading distance runners of the 1970s, he won the bronze medal in the 10,000 metres at the 1976 Montreal Olympics, set a world record for 3,000 metres in 1974, and won gold at the 1974 European Championships (5,000 m) and the 1978 Commonwealth Games (10,000 m).

Later career

After retiring from competition in 1980, Foster became a BBC athletics commentator for many years. In 1981 he founded the Great North Run, the half-marathon between Newcastle and South Shields that grew into one of the world's largest mass-participation running events, and built the Great Run series of events. He was knighted in 2020 for services to international and national sport and culture in the North East.
