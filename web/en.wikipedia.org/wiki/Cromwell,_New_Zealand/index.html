Cromwell is a town in Central Otago in the Otago region of New Zealand, situated at the confluence of the Kawarau River and the Clutha River / Mata-Au, near the southern end of Lake Dunstan. It serves as a centre for the surrounding stone-fruit orchards and vineyards and is known for the giant fruit sculpture at its entrance.

History

Cromwell was established during the Otago gold rush of the 1860s, when gold was discovered in the area and the settlement grew at the junction of the two rivers. The construction of the Clyde Dam on the Clutha River, completed in the early 1990s, created Lake Dunstan and flooded part of the old town, including its original main street; a number of historic buildings were moved or reconstructed in the Cromwell Heritage Precinct before the waters rose.

Economy

The town's economy is based on horticulture — particularly apricots, cherries, peaches and nectarines — viticulture (Central Otago is internationally known for pinot noir), and tourism connected with the lakes and cycle trails of the region.
