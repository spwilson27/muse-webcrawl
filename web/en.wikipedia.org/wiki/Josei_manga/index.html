Josei manga (女性漫画, lit. "women's comics", pronounced [dʑoseː]) are Japanese comics aimed at adult women. They are one of the four main demographic categories of manga (alongside shōnen, shōjo and seinen), emerging as readers of girls' (shōjo) manga grew up in the 1980s and publishers launched magazines—such as Ladies Comic and You—addressing mature themes.

Characteristics

Josei works typically deal with realistic romance, careers, family, sexuality and adult relationships, in contrast to the school settings and idealised romance of shōjo manga. Art styles range from the ornate to the minimal, and stories frequently feature working women protagonists navigating workplaces and relationships. Notable genres include office romance, slice of life, and historical drama; depictions of sex are often more explicit than in shōjo but integral to character-focused storytelling.

Notable works and authors

Well-known josei series include Nana by Ai Yazawa (serialised in the shōjo magazine Cookie but widely associated with adult female readership), Paradise Kiss, Honey and Clover by Chica Umino, Chihayafuru by Yuki Suetsugu, Nodame Cantabile by Tomoko Ninomiya, Princess Jellyfish by Akiko Higashimura and Usagi Drop by Yumi Unita. Many josei manga receive live-action film and television adaptations in Japan.

History

The category grew out of the "ladies' comics" (redīsu komikku) boom of the 1980s, which initially emphasised sensational and erotic content; from the 1990s the field diversified toward literary storytelling, and the term josei came to be preferred as the more neutral demographic label used by publishers and booksellers.
