Pocket square

A folded pocket square in a suit jacket breast pocket

A pocket square is a small square of fabric worn in the breast pocket of a suit jacket or blazer as a decorative accessory. Unlike a handkerchief, which is carried for utilitarian purposes, a pocket square is intended purely for display and is generally not used for blowing one's nose or wiping hands.

History

The practice of carrying fine cloth squares dates back centuries, but the pocket square as a purely ornamental item became standard in men's fashion in the early twentieth century. During the 1920s through the 1960s it was a near-universal accompaniment to a suit, and it has enjoyed periodic revivals since, becoming a staple of modern tailored menswear.

Materials and folds

Pocket squares are commonly made of linen, cotton, silk, or wool, often with hand-rolled edges. Classic colours include plain white, though patterned and coloured squares are widely worn. Well-known folds include the flat or presidential fold, the one-point and two-point folds, the puff fold, and the more elaborate crown or cagney folds. Style guides generally advise that the square should complement, rather than exactly match, the wearer's tie.
