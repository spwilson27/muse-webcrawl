Vehicular cycling

Vehicular cycling (also known as "bicycle driving") is the practice of riding bicycles on roads in a manner that is in accordance with the principles for driving in traffic, and in a way that places responsibility for cyclists and motorists to respect each other's rights on the road. It is often associated with John Forester, whose 1976 book Effective Cycling popularised the maxim: "Cyclists fare best when they act and are treated as drivers of vehicles."

Principles

Vehicular cycling technique centres on riding predictably and visibly within the traffic lane: obeying traffic signals and signs, using lane positioning to control the lane where it is too narrow to share, signalling turns, negotiating lane changes, and using the correct lane at intersections. Advocates argue that riding in the traffic stream, rather than hugging the edge of the road, makes cyclists more visible and predictable to motorists, and reduces the most common collision types, which occur at intersections and driveways.

History and influence

The approach became influential in the United States and United Kingdom in the 1970s, when transport cycling organisations developed cycling proficiency training based on it; the League of American Bicyclists' education programmes and British Bikeability training both draw on its techniques. Forester and other vehicular cyclists have been prominent critics of separated cycling facilities such as sidepaths and some cycle tracks, arguing they can increase conflicts at junctions and undermine cyclists' status as road users.

Criticism

Critics counter that vehicular cycling suits confident, fit adult riders but deters children, older people and other potential cyclists, and that high-quality protected infrastructure attracts far more cycling overall — pointing to the Netherlands and Denmark, where mass cycling rests on separated networks. The debate between vehicular cycling and facility-based approaches has shaped cycling advocacy for decades; many advocates now support combining traffic skills education with protected infrastructure.
