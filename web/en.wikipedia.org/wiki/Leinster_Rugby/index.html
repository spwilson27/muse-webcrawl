Leinster Rugby

Leinster Rugby (Irish: Rugbaí Laighean) is one of the four professional provincial club rugby union teams from the island of Ireland, representing the province of Leinster. They compete in the United Rugby Championship and the European Rugby Champions Cup. The team represents the Leinster Branch, one of four branches of the Irish Rugby Football Union (IRFU), and plays most home games at the RDS Arena in Dublin, with larger fixtures held at the Aviva Stadium and Croke Park. Leinster are one of the most successful clubs in European rugby history, having won the European Cup four times and the domestic league a record number of times.

History

The Leinster Branch was founded in 1879 and the province played representative fixtures throughout the amateur era, touring and hosting international sides and contesting the Irish Interprovincial Championship. With the advent of professionalism in 1995, Leinster became a professional team, competing in the new Heineken Cup and, from 2001, the Celtic League, which they won in its first season (2001–02).

European success

Under coach Michael Cheika, Leinster won their first European Cup in 2009, defeating Leicester Tigers 19–16 in the final at Murrayfield, after a famous semi-final win over arch-rivals Munster at Croke Park before a then world-record club crowd of 82,208. Under Joe Schmidt they won back-to-back titles in 2011 — coming from 22–6 down at half-time to beat Northampton Saints 33–22 in Cardiff — and 2012, beating Ulster 42–14 in an all-Irish final at Twickenham. A fourth star followed in 2018 under Leo Cullen and Stuart Lancaster, defeating Racing 92 15–12 in Bilbao, in the same season they completed an unprecedented European Cup and Pro14 double. Leinster also won the Challenge Cup in 2013. In the early 2020s they suffered a series of final defeats to La Rochelle and Toulouse but remained the dominant force in the URC before winning the championship again in 2025 by beating the Bulls in the final at Croke Park.

Rivalries and players

Leinster's fiercest rivalry is with Munster, whom they meet in the annual interprovincial derbies; matches between the sides are among the best-attended club games in world rugby. The province's academy system is renowned for producing Irish internationals, including Brian O'Driscoll — widely regarded as one of the greatest centres in the sport's history — Johnny Sexton, Tadhg Furlong, Garry Ringrose and Caelan Doris.
