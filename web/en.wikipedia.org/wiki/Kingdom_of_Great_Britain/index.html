The Kingdom of Great Britain was a sovereign state in Western Europe from 1707 to the end of 1800. It was created by the Treaty of Union, agreed in 1706, and the subsequent Acts of Union passed by the Parliaments of England and Scotland, which united the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Scotland into a single kingdom encompassing the whole island of Great Britain.

History

England and Scotland had shared a monarch since the Union of the Crowns in 1603, when James VI of Scotland inherited the English throne, but remained separate states with separate parliaments. After negotiations in 1706, both parliaments passed Acts of Union, and the new Parliament of Great Britain met for the first time in October 1707.

The 18th century saw the Jacobite risings of 1715 and 1745, which attempted to restore the exiled Stuart line, and the development of the office of prime minister under Robert Walpole. Britain emerged victorious from the Seven Years' War (1756–1763), gaining Canada and dominance in India, but lost its Thirteen Colonies in the American War of Independence (1775–1783). The kingdom was a central member of the coalitions that fought Revolutionary France in the 1790s.

On 1 January 1801, under the Acts of Union 1800, the Kingdom of Great Britain united with the Kingdom of Ireland to form the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland.

Politics

The kingdom was a constitutional monarchy in which power increasingly rested with Parliament. The two main political groupings were the Whigs and the Tories. The monarchy passed from Queen Anne, the last Stuart, to the House of Hanover under the Act of Settlement 1701; George I, George II, and George III reigned over Great Britain for the remainder of its existence.
