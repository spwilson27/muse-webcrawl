United States Secretary of the Army

The Secretary of the Army (SA or SECARMY) is a senior civilian official within the United States Department of Defense, with statutory responsibility for all matters relating to the United States Army: manpower, personnel, reserve affairs, installations, environmental issues, weapons systems and equipment acquisition, communications, and financial management.

History

The position was created in 1947 by the National Security Act, which replaced the Secretary of War with the Secretary of the Army as part of the reorganisation that created the National Military Establishment (later the Department of Defense). Kenneth Claiborne Royall was the last Secretary of War and, briefly, the first Secretary of the Army in 1947. Since 1949 the Secretary of the Army has been subordinate to the Secretary of Defense.

Responsibilities

The Secretary is the head of the Department of the Army and conducts all affairs of the department subject to the authority, direction, and control of the Secretary of Defense. The Secretary works closely with the Chief of Staff of the Army, the senior military officer in the department, who acts as the Secretary's principal military adviser and as executive agent for implementing approved plans.

The Secretary is appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate. By statute the Secretary must be a civilian, appointed from civilian life, and generally may not have been an active-duty commissioned officer within the preceding five years (a period sometimes waived by Congress).

Office

The Office of the Secretary of the Army is composed of the Under Secretary of the Army, several assistant secretaries, a general counsel, an inspector general, and other officials established by law. The Secretary's offices are located in the Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia.
