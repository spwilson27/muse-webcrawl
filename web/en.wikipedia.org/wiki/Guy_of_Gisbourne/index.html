Sir Guy of Gisbourne (also spelled Gisborne, Gisburne, Gysborne and Guy of Gisburn) is a character from the Robin Hood legends of English folklore. He first appears in the early ballad "Robin Hood and Guy of Gisbourne" (Child Ballad 118), in which he is a hired assassin sent to kill Robin Hood. In later tradition he became the principal henchman of the Sheriff of Nottingham and a rival for the hand of Maid Marian, and he is now one of the best-known villains of the legend.

In the early ballads

In the ballad "Robin Hood and Guy of Gisbourne", which survives in a seventeenth-century manuscript but is generally thought to preserve a much older story, Guy is a yeoman and bounty hunter hired by the Sheriff of Nottingham to capture or kill Robin Hood. He is memorably dressed in a suit made of horsehide, complete with the horse's head as a cap — "a cap of horse's hide" — and carries a sword and dagger as well as a bow.

Guy meets Robin in the greenwood without either recognising the other. They compete in archery and then fight with swords; Robin is wounded but, after praying to the Virgin Mary, kills Guy, beheads him, and sticks his head on the end of his bow. Robin then disguises himself in Guy's horsehide outfit and bluffs his way into the Sheriff's company, rescuing Little John, who has been captured. The brutality of the story — Robin mutilating Guy's face so he cannot be recognised — is characteristic of the darker, earlier stratum of the Robin Hood tradition.

Later development of the character

In the early ballads Guy has no connection with Maid Marian and is killed in his single appearance. His transformation into a recurring villain, a would-be suitor of Marian and the Sheriff's right-hand man, belongs chiefly to later literature, theatre and film. The nineteenth-century revival of the legend, and especially the May Games and romantic novels, helped fix Guy as a Norman knight in opposition to the Saxon-born Robin.

In twentieth-century retellings Guy's role was greatly expanded. In the film The Adventures of Robin Hood (1938) he was played by Basil Rathbone as the suave, treacherous principal antagonist, plotting to usurp the throne for Prince John. Later versions have made him variously a rival claimant to Robin's own estates (as in the television series Robin of Sherwood) or a conflicted figure with a claim on the Loxley inheritance. In the BBC series Robin Hood (2006–2009), played by Richard Armitage, Guy is the Sheriff's second-in-command, in love with Marian, and ultimately responsible for her death.

Name and origins

The place name Gisbourne has been connected with Gisburn in Lancashire (now the Ribble Valley) or with Guisborough in Yorkshire. No historical original for the character has been convincingly identified, and scholars generally treat him as a literary creation of the ballad tradition, possibly influenced by stock figures of the hired killer in medieval romance.
