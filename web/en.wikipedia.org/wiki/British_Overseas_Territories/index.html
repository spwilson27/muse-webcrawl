British Overseas Territories

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Number: 14 territories
Sovereign: United Kingdom
Combined population: ~270,000

The British Overseas Territories (BOTs) are fourteen territories, all with a constitutional link to the United Kingdom, but not forming part of the UK itself. They are the remnants of the British Empire that have not been granted independence or have voted to remain British territories. The permanently inhabited territories exercise a degree of self-government, although the UK retains responsibility for defence and international relations.

History and government

The territories were known as British colonies, then British dependent territories, before the British Overseas Territories Act 2002 renamed them and restored full British citizenship to most territorial citizens. Each territory has its own constitution and legislature; a governor or commissioner appointed by the Crown represents the monarch and the UK government. Several territories—notably the Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands and Bermuda—are major offshore financial centres, while sovereignty over Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands and others is disputed by Spain and Argentina respectively.
