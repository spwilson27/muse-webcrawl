Medical privacy

Medical privacy is the practice of maintaining the confidentiality of a patient's health information. It forms part of medical ethics — reflected in the Hippocratic Oath — and is protected by law in many jurisdictions.

Legal frameworks

In the United States, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996 sets national standards for protected health information held by covered entities. In the European Union, health data is a special category under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), attracting heightened protection. Many countries impose professional secrecy duties on clinicians.

Issues

Debates concern electronic health records, data breaches, sharing for research and public health, genetic information, and patients' rights of access and correction. Breaches of medical privacy can lead to stigma, discrimination and loss of trust in healthcare.
