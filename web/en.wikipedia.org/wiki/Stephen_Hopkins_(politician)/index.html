Stephen Hopkins (politician)

Stephen Hopkins (7 March 1707 – 13 July 1785) was an American Founding Father, governor of the Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations, and a signer of the United States Declaration of Independence. A self-educated merchant, surveyor and scholar from Providence, he served as Rhode Island's governor nine times between 1755 and 1768, alternating in office with his rival Samuel Ward.

Revolutionary career

Hopkins represented Rhode Island in the Continental Congress from 1774 to 1776. Despite a palsy that made his hand tremble, he signed the Declaration of Independence with the famous remark, "My hand trembles, but my heart does not." He championed colonial rights early, writing the pamphlet The Rights of Colonies Examined (1764), and opposed slavery, freeing his own slaves and supporting Rhode Island's 1774 law restricting the slave trade.

Other pursuits

Hopkins helped found the college that became Brown University and served as its first chancellor. He was also involved in iron manufacturing and privateering ventures.
