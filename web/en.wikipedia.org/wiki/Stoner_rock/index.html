Stoner rock

Stoner rock, also known as stoner metal or desert rock, is a rock music genre that combines elements of doom metal, psychedelic rock, blues rock, and acid rock. Typically featuring slow-to-mid tempo grooves, heavily distorted bass-heavy guitar riffs, melodic vocals, and "retro" production, it emerged in the early 1990s in California's Palm Desert scene.

Characteristics

Stoner rock emphasises riff and groove over speed or technicality. Guitarists often use vintage amplifiers, down-tuning, and fuzz pedals, producing a thick, warm tone indebted to Black Sabbath and Blue Cheer. Songs tend to be long, hypnotic, and built on repetition, with lyrical themes drawn from science fiction, fantasy, and, frequently, cannabis culture.

History

The genre's pioneers were the Palm Desert bands Kyuss and Sleep, whose early-1990s albums — Kyuss's Blues for the Red Sun (1992) and Sleep's Holy Mountain (1992) — defined the sound. Queens of the Stone Age, formed by Kyuss alumni, brought a leaner variant to mainstream success. Related acts include Fu Manchu, Monster Magnet, Electric Wizard, High on Fire, Clutch, and Mastodon, while Sleep's 63-minute single-track album Dopesmoker (released 2003) became a genre landmark. The overlapping styles are sometimes grouped as stoner metal (heavier, doom-leaning) and desert rock (the Palm Desert scene specifically).

Legacy

Though never a dominant commercial force, stoner rock has proved durably influential, feeding into sludge metal and modern doom, and sustaining a strong festival and independent-label culture in Europe and North America.
