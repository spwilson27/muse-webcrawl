Cheddi Jagan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cheddi Berret Jagan (22 March 1918 – 6 March 1997) was a Guyanese politician and dentist who served as President of Guyana from 1992 until his death in 1997. He previously served as Chief Minister (1953) and Premier (1961–1964) of British Guiana. With Forbes Burnham, he founded the People's Progressive Party (PPP) in 1950, and he is regarded as a central figure — often called the "Father of the Nation" — in Guyana's independence movement.

Early life

Jagan was born at Port Mourant, British Guiana, the son of indentured labourers brought from India to work on the sugar plantations. He studied in Georgetown and then in the United States, qualifying as a dentist at Northwestern University in Chicago, where he met his wife, Janet Rosenberg.

Political career

Returning home, Jagan was elected to the Legislative Council in 1947. The PPP won the 1953 election under universal suffrage, but the colonial government suspended the constitution after 133 days, fearing his Marxist views during the Cold War. Restored to office as Premier in 1961, he was defeated in 1964 under a new proportional-representation system and spent 28 years in opposition.

Presidency and death

Jagan won the free elections of October 1992 and governed as a more pragmatic democrat, pursuing market reforms and debt relief. He died in office in Washington, D.C., in 1997 and was succeeded by his wife Janet Jagan. The Cheddi Jagan International Airport is named in his honour.
