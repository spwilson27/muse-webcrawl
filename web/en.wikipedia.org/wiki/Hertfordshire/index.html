Hertfordshire (abbreviated Herts) is a ceremonial county in the East of England, immediately north of London. It borders Bedfordshire and Cambridgeshire to the north, Essex to the east, Greater London to the south, and Buckinghamshire to the west. The county town is Hertford, though the largest settlements are Watford, Hemel Hempstead, Stevenage and St Albans. A largely prosperous commuter county, Hertfordshire contains several of England's planned settlements: Letchworth and Welwyn Garden City, the original garden cities, and post-war new towns at Stevenage, Hemel Hempstead and Hatfield.

History

St Albans stands on the Roman city of Verulamium, one of the largest towns of Roman Britain and the site of the martyrdom of St Alban, Britain's first Christian martyr; its cathedral, built from 1077 partly with Roman brick, has a nave among the longest in England. The county name derives from Hertford ("hart ford"), recorded as a burh founded under Edward the Elder in the 10th century. In the 20th century Ebenezer Howard's garden-city movement was realised at Letchworth (1903) and Welwyn Garden City (1920), strongly influencing town planning worldwide.

Geography

The north of the county is crossed by the chalk hills of the Chilterns, reaching the county top at 244 m near Tring. Rivers include the Lea, the Colne, the Stort and the upper Cam tributaries. Much of the south lies within London's metropolitan green belt, and the county's proximity to the capital shapes its economy and transport: the M1, A1(M) and M25 motorways and several main railway lines to London cross it.

Economy and culture

High-technology and pharmaceutical industries cluster around Stevenage, Welwyn Garden City and Hemel Hempstead; the University of Hertfordshire is based at Hatfield, on the former de Havilland aircraft site. Leavesden Studios near Watford — home of the Warner Bros. Studio Tour and the Harry Potter films — is a major film-production centre. Famous residents have included George Bernard Shaw at Ayot St Lawrence.
