Lucie Manette

Lucie Manette is a fictional character in Charles Dickens's 1859 novel A Tale of Two Cities. The daughter of Dr Alexandre Manette, she is the emotional centre of the novel — the "golden thread" that binds its characters together.

Role in the novel

Lucie was raised in England believing herself an orphan, her mother having died and her father, a former Bastille prisoner, presumed dead. At seventeen she learns from the banker Jarvis Lorry that her father is alive in Paris, and travels there to bring him home, nursing him back to mental health.

She marries Charles Darnay, a French émigré who has renounced his aristocratic family, the Evrémondes — the very family responsible for her father's imprisonment. During the French Revolution Darnay is arrested in Paris and condemned; Lucie's devotion, standing daily where he may see her from prison, sustains him. She is also loved by Sydney Carton, whose sacrifice closes the novel.

Characterisation

Lucie embodies Dickensian ideals of compassion, patience and domestic virtue. Critics have sometimes found her idealised, but her steadfastness motivates the actions of nearly every major character, from her father's recovery to Carton's redemption.
