League of German Girls

The League of German Girls or League of German Maidens (German: Bund Deutscher Mädel, abbreviated BDM) was the girls' wing of the Nazi Party's youth movement, the Hitler Youth. It was the only legal female youth organization in Nazi Germany, intended for girls aged 14 to 18; younger girls (10–14) belonged to the Young Girls' League (Jungmädelbund).

Organization and activities

Founded in 1930 and folded into the Hitler Youth structure after 1933, the BDM became effectively compulsory following the 1936 Hitler Youth law. Its program emphasized physical fitness, hiking and sport, household skills, and ideological indoctrination aimed at preparing girls for their prescribed roles as wives and mothers in the Nazi state. From 1937, the voluntary "Faith and Beauty" (Glaube und Schönheit) section served young women aged 17–21.

World War II

During the war, BDM members were assigned to harvest work, nursing assistance, clerical duties, and air-defence support, including operating searchlights and, late in the war, serving with anti-aircraft batteries. The organization was dissolved by the Allied Control Council in October 1945, along with other Nazi organizations.
