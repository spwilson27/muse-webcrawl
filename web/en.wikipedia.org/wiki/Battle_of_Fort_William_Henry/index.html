The Battle of Fort William Henry, more precisely the Siege of Fort William Henry, took place in August 1757 during the French and Indian War. French forces under the Marquis de Montcalm besieged the British fort at the southern end of Lake George and forced its surrender.

The siege

Lieutenant-Colonel George Monro held the fort with about 2,300 men against roughly 8,000 French regulars, Canadian militia and Native allies. After days of bombardment, and with no relief coming from Fort Edward, Monro surrendered on 9 August on terms allowing the garrison to march out with the honours of war.

The massacre

As the British column departed, some of France's Native allies, angered at receiving little plunder, attacked the column and the sick and wounded left behind. Estimates of those killed vary widely, from under 70 to several hundred. The event became notorious in British propaganda and features in James Fenimore Cooper's novel The Last of the Mohicans.

Aftermath

The French destroyed the fort and withdrew to Fort Ticonderoga. Montcalm's reputation in Europe suffered despite his efforts to stop the killings.
