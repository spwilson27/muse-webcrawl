Indian peafowl

Scientific name: Pavo cristatus

Family: Phasianidae

Range: South Asia; introduced worldwide

Status: National bird of India

The Indian peafowl (Pavo cristatus), also known as the common peafowl or blue peafowl, is a large pheasant native to South Asia. The male, or peacock, is famed for its iridescent blue plumage and spectacular train of elongated upper-tail coverts decorated with eye-spots, which it fans out in courtship display; the female, or peahen, is a mottled brown. The species is the national bird of India.

Description

Peacocks measure 195–225 cm in length including the train and weigh 4–6 kg. The train is not the true tail but a fan of coverts supported by stiff tail feathers; it is grown for the breeding season and moulted annually. Peafowl are omnivorous ground feeders, eating plants, insects, and small reptiles, and they roost in trees at night. Their loud, far-carrying calls are a familiar sound in Indian forests and villages.

Behaviour and breeding

Males display at traditional sites (leks), rattling their trains and calling to attract several females. The female alone incubates a clutch of 4–8 eggs laid in a scrape on the ground. The elaborate train is a classic case study in sexual selection: Charles Darwin wrote that the sight of a peacock's feather made him "sick", so hard was it to explain by natural selection alone.

Distribution and culture

Native to India and Sri Lanka, the Indian peafowl has been introduced to parks and estates around the world. It holds a prominent place in Hindu mythology as the mount of the god Kartikeya and is associated with Krishna, and it has been protected in India since being declared the national bird in 1963. Despite local declines from hunting and habitat change, it remains widespread and is classed as Least Concern.
