Barrio Logan

Barrio Logan is a neighbourhood in San Diego, California, south-east of downtown, recognised as a historic centre of the city's Mexican-American and Chicano community. It is best known for Chicano Park, beneath the San Diego–Coronado Bridge, whose concrete pylons carry one of the largest collections of outdoor Chicano murals in the United States.

History

The community grew in the early 20th century with Mexican families, many displaced by the Mexican Revolution, working in nearby canneries, rail yards, and the fishing industry. Mid-century highway construction and the Coronado Bridge demolished many homes; in 1970 residents occupied land promised for a park, a protest that led to the creation of Chicano Park and became a landmark of the Chicano movement.

Culture

Muralists began painting the bridge pylons in 1973, depicting Aztec and Mexican history, labour struggles, and community heroes. Chicano Park Day is celebrated each April. The neighbourhood also hosts galleries, lowrider culture, and long-standing family businesses along Logan Avenue.

Issues

Barrio Logan has long contended with industrial zoning, shipyard pollution, and gentrification pressures, with community plans seeking to separate homes from heavy industry.
