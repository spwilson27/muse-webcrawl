Bhojpuri (भोजपुरी) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken in the Bhojpur–Purvanchal region of northeastern India and the Terai region of Nepal. It is spoken across western Bihar, eastern Uttar Pradesh, northwestern Jharkhand, and adjoining parts of Nepal, and by large diaspora communities in Mauritius, Fiji, Suriname, Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, and South Africa, descended from indentured labourers who emigrated in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Classification and status

Bhojpuri belongs to the Bihari group of Eastern Indo-Aryan languages, alongside Maithili and Magahi. George Grierson's Linguistic Survey of India treated it as a distinct language, though in modern Indian census practice Bhojpuri is often enumerated under Hindi, which obscures its true number of speakers. There is an ongoing movement demanding its inclusion in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, which would give it official recognition. The language takes its name from the town of Bhojpur in Bihar.

Literature and media

Bhojpuri has a rich folk tradition of songs and ballads, and modern writers include Bhikhari Thakur, often called the "Shakespeare of Bhojpuri", whose play Bidesiya dramatised the plight of migrant labourers. The first Bhojpuri film, Ganga Maiyya Tohe Piyari Chadhaibo, was released in 1963, and Bhojpuri cinema — centred in Patna and eastern Uttar Pradesh — is now a substantial industry, alongside a popular music scene whose songs circulate widely on digital platforms.
