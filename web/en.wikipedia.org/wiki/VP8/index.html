VP8

VP8 is an open and royalty-free video compression format released by On2 Technologies and later developed by Google. It was first released in 2008 as the successor to VP7, and is best known as the video codec paired with the Vorbis or Opus audio codecs in the WebM container format for web video.

History

VP8 originated in On2's TrueMotion line of codecs and was marketed as offering quality comparable to H.264 Baseline Profile at lower complexity. After Google acquired On2 in February 2010, it released VP8 under an irrevocable royalty-free patent licence and open-sourced the code (the libvpx library) at the Google I/O conference in May 2010, launching the WebM project.

Technology

VP8 is a block-based hybrid codec: frames are divided into 16×16 macroblocks, intra-predicted or motion-compensated from reference frames (including a "golden frame" and alternate reference frame), transformed with a 4×4 DCT or Walsh–Hadamard transform, and entropy-coded with an arithmetic coder. It supports resolutions up to 16384×16384 and is designed for low decoding complexity.

Use and patent questions

VP8 became a mandatory codec in the WebRTC standard for browser-based real-time communication and was widely used for HTML5 video. Questions about third-party patent claims were largely settled in 2013, when Google reached an agreement with MPEG LA licensing VP8 and VP9 to Google and its sublicensees. VP8 was succeeded by VP9 (2013), which offers roughly 50% better compression.
