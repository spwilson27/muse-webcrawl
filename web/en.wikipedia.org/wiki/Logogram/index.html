Logogram

A logogram is a single written character that represents a complete grammatical word or morpheme. Logograms are the basic unit of a logographic writing system such as Chinese characters. Unlike letters in an alphabet, which represent sounds, a logogram stands directly for a unit of meaning, though many logograms also contain phonetic components.

Logographic systems

Full logographic systems include Chinese characters (hanzi, also adapted as Japanese kanji and Korean hanja), ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs, Sumerian and Akkadian cuneiform, and Maya script — though all of these also make extensive use of phonetic signs. In Chinese, most characters are phono-semantic compounds combining a meaning (radical) element with a sound element.

Everyday logograms

Alphabetic languages also use logograms: numerals (1, 2, 3), the ampersand (&, from Latin et), the percent sign (%), the @ sign, mathematical symbols, and currency signs ($, £, €) are read as whole words rather than sequences of sounds.

Advantages and disadvantages

Because logograms are tied to meaning rather than pronunciation, written Chinese can be read across mutually unintelligible spoken varieties. The cost is a large inventory: literacy requires learning thousands of characters, and lookup and typesetting historically posed challenges addressed by radical-stroke ordering and phonetic input methods.
