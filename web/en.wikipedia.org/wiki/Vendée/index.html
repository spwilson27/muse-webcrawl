The Vendée (French pronunciation: [vɑ̃de]) is a department in the Pays de la Loire region in western France, on the Atlantic Ocean. It is named after the Vendée River, which runs through its southeastern part. Its prefecture is La Roche-sur-Yon, and its coastline includes the resorts of Les Sables-d'Olonne and Saint-Jean-de-Monts, facing the islands of Noirmoutier and Yeu.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution in 1790 from the western part of the former province of Poitou. It is chiefly remembered for the War in the Vendée (1793–1796), a large royalist and Catholic counter-revolutionary uprising against the revolutionary government, provoked by conscription and religious persecution. The rebellion, led by figures such as Jacques Cathelineau, François de Charette, and Henri de La Rochejaquelein, was suppressed with great brutality; the campaign and the reprisals of the "infernal columns" caused very heavy loss of life and remain a subject of historical debate in France.

Geography

The Vendée comprises the wooded, hedged farmland of the Bocage in the east, the reclaimed marshlands of the Marais Poitevin in the south and the Marais Breton in the north, and a long sandy coast dotted with pine forests. The ports of Les Sables-d'Olonne host the start and finish of the Vendée Globe, the solo non-stop round-the-world yacht race held every four years since 1989.

Economy

Tourism, agriculture, food processing, and boatbuilding (notably the Bénéteau group) anchor the economy. The Puy du Fou historical theme park is one of France's most visited attractions.
