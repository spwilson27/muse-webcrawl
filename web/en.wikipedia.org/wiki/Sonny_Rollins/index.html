Sonny Rollins

Walter Theodore "Sonny" Rollins (born September 7, 1930) is an American retired jazz tenor saxophonist widely recognised as one of the most important and prolific jazz musicians of the post-bebop era. In a career spanning more than seven decades, Rollins recorded over sixty albums as a leader and collaborated with Miles Davis, John Coltrane, Thelonious Monk, Max Roach and Clifford Brown. His albums Saxophone Colossus (1956), Freedom Suite (1958) and The Bridge (1962) are considered classics, and his compositions "St. Thomas", "Oleo" and "Airegin" have become jazz standards.

Early career

Rollins grew up in Harlem, New York, and took up the saxophone as a teenager, idolising Coleman Hawkins and Lester Young. By 1949 he was recording with bebop pioneers including Bud Powell and Fats Navarro. He played with the Miles Davis quintet and the Clifford Brown–Max Roach quintet, and recorded with Thelonious Monk and John Coltrane, including the famous 1956 meeting on "Tenor Madness".

Classic period and sabbaticals

Rollins's mid-1950s trio and quartet records established his reputation for long, thematically developed improvisations. Famous for periodic withdrawals from public performance, he took a sabbatical from 1959 to 1961, practising on the Williamsburg Bridge in New York — an experience immortalised by his comeback album The Bridge (1962). He took a second sabbatical around 1969–1971 to study yoga and meditation in India and Japan.

Later years and honours

Rollins continued touring and recording into the 21st century, winning Grammy Awards including a Lifetime Achievement Award in 2004. He received the National Medal of Arts and Kennedy Center Honors in 2011. Health problems forced him to stop performing in 2014.
