Mount Jiuhua (Chinese: 九华山; pinyin: Jiǔhuá shān, "Nine Glorious Mountains") is a mountain range in Qingyang County, Anhui Province, China. It is one of the Four Sacred Buddhist Mountains of China, dedicated to Ksitigarbha (Chinese: Dizang), the bodhisattva of the underworld who vowed to save all beings in the hell realms.

History

The mountain's Buddhist importance dates to the Tang dynasty, when the Korean monk Kim Gyo-gak (Jin Qiaojue, 696–794) came from Silla to practise at Jiuhua. His austerity and longevity convinced devotees that he was an emanation of Ksitigarbha, and after his death his body was said to have remained uncorrupted, gilded and enshrined as a "flesh-body" icon. Pilgrimage grew through the Ming and Qing dynasties; the mountain came to hold some 300 temples, of which about 99 survive, including Huacheng Temple, the oldest on the mountain, and the Flesh-Body Hall (Roushen Dian) on Shendi Peak.

Geography

The range comprises some 99 peaks of weathered granite, the best known being Tiantai (Heavenly Terrace), Lotus Peak (1,342 m) and Shiwang Peak. Pine forests, waterfalls and a "Buddha's light" (glory) optical phenomenon seen above the clouds are celebrated features of the landscape.

Culture

Mount Jiuhua hosts one of China's largest Buddhist pilgrim gatherings each year on Ksitigarbha's birthday, the 30th day of the seventh lunar month. The poet Li Bai is said to have given the mountain its present name, inspired by its nine peaks resembling lotus blossoms.
