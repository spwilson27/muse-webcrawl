Scope creep

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scope creep (also called requirement creep, feature creep or kitchen-sink syndrome) is the uncontrolled growth of a project's scope — the set of features, deliverables or requirements it is expected to fulfil — after work has begun. In project management, scope creep refers specifically to changes that accumulate continuously and informally, without corresponding adjustments to schedule, budget or resources and without passing through a change-control process. It is one of the most commonly cited causes of project delay, cost overrun and failure, particularly in software development, construction and defence procurement.

Causes

  • Vague initial requirements: an ill-defined scope invites stakeholders to keep discovering what they wanted only after work starts.
  • Weak change control: accepting every request — "while you're at it…" — without assessing impact or obtaining approval.
  • Stakeholder and user involvement patterns: late-arriving stakeholders, or users excluded early, demand additions late in the project.
  • Gold plating: team members adding unrequested polish or features beyond the agreed specification.
  • External change: shifting markets, regulation or technology mid-project.

Effects

Each small addition seems harmless, but cumulatively they stretch schedules, inflate budgets, dilute quality and demoralise teams. Because estimates were made against the original scope, creep can turn an apparently healthy project into a failing one without any single dramatic decision; in fixed-price contracts it can destroy margins and end in disputes over what was included.

Management

Standard countermeasures include writing a clear scope statement and requirements baseline; using a formal change-control board or process so every addition is costed, scheduled and approved (or traded against other scope); involving users from the start; prototyping to surface hidden requirements early; and tracking scope changes in project reporting. Agile methods handle the same pressure differently: the overall vision may evolve, but work is timeboxed into iterations with a prioritised backlog, so new requests displace lower-priority items rather than simply extending the project.

Related concepts

Scope creep is distinguished from scope growth approved through change control, and from feature creep in product design, where successive versions accumulate functions (as in software "bloat"). The military jargon term "mission creep" describes the analogous expansion of a military operation's objectives.
