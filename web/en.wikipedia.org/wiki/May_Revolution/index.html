The May Revolution (Spanish: Revolución de Mayo) was a week-long sequence of events in Buenos Aires from 18 to 25 May 1810 that removed the Spanish viceroy Baltasar Hidalgo de Cisneros and established the Primera Junta, the first local government of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata. It is regarded as the starting point of the Argentine War of Independence and the birth of modern Argentina; 25 May is Argentina's national day.

Background

Napoleon's invasion of Spain and the capture of King Ferdinand VII had thrown the legitimacy of colonial government into crisis. News reached Buenos Aires in May 1810 that the Supreme Central Junta in Spain had fallen, leaving no recognised sovereign authority. Local creoles, influenced by Enlightenment ideas and the earlier British invasions of the Río de la Plata (1806–1807), which had demonstrated local military capacity, pressed for an open cabildo to decide the government's future.

Events of May Week

An open town meeting on 22 May voted to depose the viceroy. After a failed attempt to install a junta headed by Cisneros, popular demonstrations in the Plaza de la Victoria on 25 May forced the cabildo to accept a new government, the Primera Junta, presided over by Cornelio Saavedra, with Mariano Moreno and Juan José Paso as secretaries and Manuel Belgrano among its members. The junta swore loyalty to Ferdinand VII while in practice beginning self-government, and within weeks it sent expeditions to secure the interior, opening the independence struggles across South America.
