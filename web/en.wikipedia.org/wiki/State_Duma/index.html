State Duma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The State Duma is the lower house of the Federal Assembly of Russia, the country's legislature under the 1993 Constitution (the name revives the pre-revolutionary Duma of 1906–17). Its 450 deputies are elected for five years by a mixed system — half by proportional representation with a 5 per cent threshold, half in single-member districts (the mixed formula restored in 2016 after a fully proportional decade).

Constitutional role

The Duma passes federal laws (subject to Federation Council review and presidential veto, overridable by two-thirds of both chambers), must consent to the President's nominee for Prime Minister (three rejections can trigger the Duma's dissolution), controls certain appointments and the budget process, and may bring charges for presidential impeachment (a procedure with thresholds never successfully used). In practice, the "power vertical" built since the 2000s, the dominance of the pro-presidential party and managed electoral competition have made the Duma a largely compliant legislature whose floor debates matter less than negotiations within the executive — a contrast with the fractious, genuinely powerful Duma of the 1990s that impeached and defied a president.
