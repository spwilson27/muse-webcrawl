Rhondda Cynon Taf
Country: Wales
Status: County borough (principal area)
Administrative HQ: Pontypridd

Rhondda Cynon Taf (Welsh pronunciation roughly "Rhon-tha Kun-on Tav") is a county borough and principal area in South Wales, named for its three river valleys: the Rhondda, the Cynon and the Taff/Ely area. It is the third most populous local authority area in Wales.

Geography and towns

The borough stretches from the Brecon Beacons foothills in the north to the outskirts of Cardiff in the south. Its main towns are Aberdare, Pontypridd, Mountain Ash, Tonypandy, Treorchy, Porth, Ferndale and Llantrisant. The Rhondda and Cynon valleys were at the heart of the South Wales coalfield.

History

Rhondda Cynon Taf was formed on 1 April 1996 by the merger of the former Mid Glamorgan districts of Rhondda, Cynon Valley and Taff-Ely (excluding some communities that went to other authorities). The area's nineteenth- and early twentieth-century prosperity rested on steam coal, exported worldwide through Cardiff and Barry; the collapse of deep mining in the 1980s brought severe economic hardship from which the valleys have been gradually diversifying.

Government

Rhondda Cynon Taf County Borough Council administers the area, headquartered in Pontypridd. The borough elects Members of the Senedd and Members of Parliament for constituencies including Rhondda, Cynon Valley and Pontypridd.
