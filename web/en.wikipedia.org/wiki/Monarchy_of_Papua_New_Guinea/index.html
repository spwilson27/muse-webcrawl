Monarchy of Papua New Guinea

The monarchy of Papua New Guinea is a system of government in which a hereditary monarch is the sovereign and head of state of Papua New Guinea. The current monarch and head of state, since 8 September 2022, is King Charles III. Papua New Guinea is one of the fifteen Commonwealth realms that share the same monarch, but its monarchy is legally distinct: the King reigns in right of Papua New Guinea, advised only by Papua New Guinean ministers on matters of the country.

Since independence from Australia in 1975, the monarch's constitutional roles have been mostly delegated to the Governor-General of Papua New Guinea, the viceregal representative appointed by the monarch on the advice of the National Executive Council. Under the Constitution, the head of state is formally styled "the King" (the National Parliament chose to retain the Crown at independence), and the monarch's duties include appointing the Governor-General, assenting — through the Speaker and the Governor-General — to legislation, and serving as commander-in-chief symbolically. Succession is governed by the rules applicable in the Commonwealth realms.
