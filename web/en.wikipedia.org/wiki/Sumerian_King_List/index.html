Sumerian King List

The Sumerian King List is an ancient literary composition in the Sumerian language that lists the kings of Sumer (ancient southern Iraq), their supposed cities, and the lengths of their reigns. Surviving in several versions on clay tablets and prisms — most famously the Weld–Blundell Prism in the Ashmolean Museum — it was compiled in the late third and early second millennia BC, with the most complete copies dating to the Isin period.

Structure and ideology

The list is organised around the idea that kingship (nam-lugal) was "lowered from heaven" and passed from city to city: at any given time only one city held legitimate kingship over the land. When a city was "smitten with weapons", kingship moved to the next in sequence. The scheme is ideological rather than strictly historical: dynasties that in reality overlapped are presented as strictly successive.

Antediluvian kings

The opening section lists eight kings who reigned before a great Flood "swept over" the land. Their reigns are fantastically long, totalling 241,200 years:

These antediluvian rulers have often been compared with the patriarchs of Genesis and with the ten antediluvian kings in the history of Berossus. Reign lengths shrink progressively after the Flood, passing from the legendary (Etana of Kish, who "ascended to heaven"; Gilgamesh of Uruk, credited with 126 years) towards plausible historical figures.

Historical value

From the dynasty of Kish onward the list can be checked against royal inscriptions, and many of its kings — such as Mesilim, Eannatum, Lugalzagesi, and the rulers of Akkad and Ur — are independently attested. The final sections cover the Third Dynasty of Ur and the kings of Isin, from whose scribal milieu the surviving compilation probably comes. The list is therefore a key primary source for the chronology and political ideology of early Mesopotamia, even though its neat scheme of one kingship at a time is a literary construction.
