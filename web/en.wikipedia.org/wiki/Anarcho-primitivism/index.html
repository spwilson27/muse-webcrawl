Anarcho-primitivism

Anarcho-primitivism, also known as anarchist primitivism, is a political ideology that advocates a return to non-"civilized" ways of life through deindustrialization, abolition of the division of labour or specialization, abandonment or abolition of large-scale organization, and abandonment of high technology. Anarcho-primitivists critique the origins and progress of the Industrial Revolution and industrial society, arguing that the transition from hunter-gatherer to agricultural subsistence, which began around 10,000 years ago, gave rise to social stratification, coercion, and alienation.

Origins and influences

The philosophy draws on the work of anthropologists such as Marshall Sahlins, whose essay "The Original Affluent Society" argued that hunter-gatherers enjoyed abundant leisure and egalitarian social relations. Key later theorists include John Zerzan, whose works Future Primitive (1994) and Running on Emptiness (2002) indict language, agriculture, and symbolic culture itself; Fredy Perlman, author of Against His-story, Against Leviathan! (1983); and Derrick Jensen, who popularised primitivist themes in works such as A Language Older Than Words. The journal Green Anarchy became a central forum for the tendency in North America.

Critique of civilization

Primitivists argue that before agriculture, humans lived in small, egalitarian bands characterised by gift economies, relatively abundant leisure, and an absence of formal hierarchy. Agriculture, in this view, created surplus, and with it property, class, patriarchy, war, and eventually the state and industrial capitalism. Many primitivists therefore advocate the abolition of technology and a rewilding of human society, though they differ on whether this is a literal political programme or a critical perspective.

Criticism

Anarcho-primitivism has been criticised by other anarchists, notably Murray Bookchin, who denounced it as "lifestyle anarchism" and argued it abandons the social and ecological potential of technology and confederal democracy. Critics also question the feasibility of supporting the current human population without agriculture, and the romanticisation of prehistoric life spans and violence.
