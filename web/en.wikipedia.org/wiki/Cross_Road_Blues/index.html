"Cross Road Blues" (often listed as "Crossroads") is a blues song written and recorded by American blues artist Robert Johnson in 1936. Johnson performed it solo with guitar and vocals in a Delta blues style, recording two takes in San Antonio, Texas, in November 1936; the song was released by Vocalion Records in 1937. It is one of the most famous songs in the blues canon and is closely associated — in popular legend, more than in its actual lyrics — with the myth that Johnson sold his soul to the Devil at a crossroads in exchange for musical talent; the lyrics themselves describe a man stranded at a rural crossroads at sundown, failing to flag a ride and fearing the dark.

Adaptations

The song was transformed for rock audiences by Cream, whose live recording "Crossroads", from the 1968 album Wheels of Fire, featured Eric Clapton's celebrated guitar work and became a rock standard; Clapton has called Johnson his most important influence. Cream's version was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and Johnson's original was added to the National Recording Registry. Numerous other artists — including Elmore James (as "Standing at the Crossroads"), Lynyrd Skynyrd, Rush, and John Mayer — have recorded versions.
