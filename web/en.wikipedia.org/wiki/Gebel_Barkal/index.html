Gebel Barkal (also Jebel Barkal, Arabic for "Holy Mountain") is a small but prominent sandstone mountain in the Northern State of Sudan, about 400 km north of Khartoum, on a large bend of the Nile near the ancient city of Napata.

History

From about 1450 BC the Egyptians regarded the mountain as the southern home of the god Amun. Under the New Kingdom a temple of Amun was built at its foot, and when the Kushite kings of Napata conquered Egypt as the Twenty-fifth Dynasty, they greatly expanded the complex, regarding Gebel Barkal as the source of their kingship. Its distinctive pinnacle was interpreted as a rearing uraeus, symbol of royalty.

The site includes at least 13 temples and 3 palaces, and fields of small royal pyramids dating from the third century BC onwards. The great temple of Amun was excavated by George Reisner in 1916–1920 for the Boston Museum of Fine Arts.

World Heritage

In 2003 Gebel Barkal and four other Napatan sites (El-Kurru, Nuri, Sanam and Zuma) were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as "Gebel Barkal and the Sites of the Napatan Region".
