Christian Jürgensen Thomsen (29 December 1788 – 21 May 1865) was a Danish historian and museum curator who formulated the three-age system — the division of prehistory into the Stone Age, Bronze Age, and Iron Age — the foundational chronological framework of archaeology.

Work

Appointed in 1816 to organise Denmark's collection of northern antiquities, Thomsen arranged the objects not by material or style alone but by his hypothesis of successive technological ages. He published the system in his Guide to Northern Antiquities (1836; English translation 1848). Excavations in stratified sites soon confirmed the sequence, and the framework was refined by his successor J.J.A. Worsaae's fieldwork.

Legacy

Thomsen effectively created the National Museum of Denmark's prehistoric collections and demonstrated that museum arrangement could itself be a scientific instrument. His numismatic work and his mentorship of a generation of Scandinavian archaeologists reinforced Copenhagen's central place in nineteenth-century prehistory.
