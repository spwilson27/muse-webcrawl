Georges de Scudéry

Georges de Scudéry (22 August 1601 – 14 May 1667), the elder brother of Madeleine de Scudéry, was a French novelist, dramatist and poet of the Baroque period. A flamboyant soldier turned man of letters, he was a prominent, if controversial, figure in the literary quarrels of seventeenth-century Paris.

Life

Born at Le Havre into a family claiming Provençal origins, Scudéry served in the army in his youth before turning to literature in Paris around 1630. He attached himself to Cardinal Richelieu and became governor of the fortress of Notre-Dame de la Garde at Marseille — a post he treated with mock-heroic grandeur. He was elected to the Académie française in 1650. During the Fronde he followed the Prince de Condé and was exiled to Normandy, where he spent much of his later life.

Works

Scudéry wrote some sixteen plays, including the tragedy La Mort de César (1636) and the comedy L'Amour tyrannique (1638). He is best known for his criticism: his attack on Pierre Corneille's Le Cid in the famous quarrel of 1637 provoked Richelieu's intervention and the Academy's judgment on the play. Many of the huge heroic romances published under his name — Ibrahim, ou l'Illustre Bassa (1641) and Almahide (1660–63) — were in fact largely written by his sister Madeleine, though published under his name as a mark of propriety.
