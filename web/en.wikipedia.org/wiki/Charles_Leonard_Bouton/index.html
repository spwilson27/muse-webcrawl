Charles Leonard Bouton

Charles Leonard Bouton (April 25, 1869 – February 20, 1922) was an American mathematician. He is remembered for introducing the game of Nim to the mathematical literature and giving its complete theory in a 1901 paper, one of the founding results of combinatorial game theory.

Life and career

Bouton was born in St. Louis and studied at Washington University in St. Louis before earning his doctorate at the University of Leipzig in 1898, under Sophus Lie, with a dissertation on invariant theory. He joined the faculty of Harvard University, where he taught for the rest of his career, and was an editor of the Annals of Mathematics.

Nim

In "Nim, a game with a complete mathematical theory", published in the Annals of Mathematics in 1901, Bouton analysed the impartial game in which players remove objects from piles. He showed that the winning strategy depends on writing the pile sizes in binary and computing their exclusive-or (now called the nim-sum): a position is losing exactly when the nim-sum is zero. The paper is a landmark in the theory of combinatorial games.
