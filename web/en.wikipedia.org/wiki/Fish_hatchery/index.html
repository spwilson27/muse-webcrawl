A fish hatchery is a place for the artificial breeding, egg incubation, and rearing of aquatic animals through their early life stages, especially finfish and shellfish. Hatcheries produce larvae and juvenile fish (fingerlings or fry) that supply aquaculture farms, restock wild populations for conservation and recreational fishing, and support research.

Operation

Broodstock are held under controlled conditions and induced to spawn by manipulating temperature, photoperiod, or hormones. Eggs are fertilised, disinfected, and incubated in trays, jars, or upwelling systems with oxygenated water. Newly hatched larvae are fed live feeds such as rotifers and Artemia (brine shrimp), then weaned onto formulated diets before grading and transfer to ponds, raceways, cages, or release sites. Water quality — temperature, oxygen, ammonia — is monitored continuously because early life stages are highly sensitive.

History

Artificial fertilisation of fish eggs was demonstrated in the 18th century, and practical hatcheries spread from the 1850s: Joseph Remy and Antoine Géhin revived trout propagation in France, while in the United States Spencer Fullerton Baird's Fish Commission (founded 1871) built an extensive federal hatchery system to replenish depleted food fishes. The U.S. National Fish Hatchery System still operates dozens of facilities.

Purposes

  • Aquaculture production: reliable seed supply for farmed salmon, tilapia, catfish, and shrimp.
  • Stock enhancement: releasing juveniles to rebuild wild fisheries and support angling.
  • Conservation: captive breeding of endangered species, such as Pacific salmon populations listed under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.
  • Research and education.

Criticisms

Conservation geneticists warn that hatchery fish interbreeding with wild populations can reduce genetic diversity and fitness ("domestication selection"), and that large releases may spread disease or mask habitat degradation that is the true cause of decline. Modern reform programmes emphasise locally adapted broodstock, marking released fish, and integrating hatcheries with habitat restoration.
