Battle of Annaberg

The Battle of Annaberg (German: Schlacht am Annaberg; Polish: Bitwa o Górę Świętej Anny) was fought on 21–23 May 1921 during the Third Silesian Uprising. It was the largest battle of the uprisings, in which German Freikorps and Selbstschutz forces under Generalleutnant Karl Höfer retook the Annaberg (Góra Świętej Anny), a strategically and symbolically important hill in Upper Silesia, from Polish insurgents.

Background

After the First World War, the fate of industrial Upper Silesia was to be decided by plebiscite. The vote of 20 March 1921 favored Germany overall, but disputes over the partition led Wojciech Korfanty to launch the Third Silesian Uprising in May 1921. Polish insurgents seized much of the region, including the Annaberg, a limestone hill with a Franciscan monastery and pilgrimage church that commanded the Oder valley and the roads around it.

Battle

German forces, including the Freikorps Oberland and Württemberg volunteer units under the overall command of Karl Höfer, counterattacked on 21 May. After hard fighting at villages around the hill, German troops stormed the Annaberg on 23 May, driving the Polish insurgents from the position. It was the largest and bloodiest engagement of the uprisings.

Aftermath

Although the Germans retook the hill and held it against Polish counterattacks, the fighting did not determine the final settlement: the League of Nations partitioned Upper Silesia in October 1921, awarding the industrial eastern part to Poland and the west, including the Annaberg, to Germany. The hill became a major German memorial site between the wars; after 1945 the area became part of Poland.
