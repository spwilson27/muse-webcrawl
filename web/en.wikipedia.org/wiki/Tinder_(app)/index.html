Tinder (app)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tinder is an online dating and social networking application launched in 2012. Users "swipe right" to like or "swipe left" to pass on profiles shown to them, based largely on photographs and a short biography; if two users like each other they form a "match" and can exchange messages within the app. Tinder is owned by Match Group.

History

Tinder was founded within the IAC Hatch Labs incubator and launched at the University of Southern California in September 2012. It grew rapidly, popularising the swipe gesture as a cultural shorthand for quick approval or rejection. By 2014 it was registering about one billion swipes per day. The app later added paid tiers such as Tinder Plus (2015) and Tinder Gold (2017), offering features like unlimited likes, "rewind", passport location changes and seeing who has liked you.

Features and operation

Profiles are built from photos and optional biographical text, and candidates are filtered by geographic proximity, age and gender preferences. Additional features have included Super Likes, Boosts, video profiles, photo verification and safety tools such as a panic button in some markets. Its matching algorithm and the gamified swipe mechanic have been widely imitated across the dating industry.

Reception

Tinder has been credited with mainstreaming mobile dating, and criticised for encouraging superficial judgements, for harassment and safety concerns, and for opaque ranking algorithms. It remains one of the most downloaded dating apps worldwide, with tens of millions of users.
