Somali clan


Somali clans are patrilineal kin groups central to Somali social and political organisation.

Somali clans are family groups based on patrilineal descent that form the fundamental units of Somali society. Clan membership shapes identity, politics, and customary law across Somalia, Djibouti, Ethiopia's Somali Region, and northeastern Kenya.

Structure

The major clan families are traditionally given as the Darod, Hawiye, Isaaq, Dir, and Rahanweyn (Digil and Mirifle). Each divides into clans, sub-clans, and lineage groups (including the diya-paying group responsible for collective compensation). Genealogies trace descent from eponymous ancestors, often linking Somali lineages to Arabian forebears in tradition.

Society and politics

Clan elders negotiate under customary law known as xeer. Clan rivalry was a major factor in the Somali civil war after 1991, while clan-based power-sharing formulas, such as the "4.5 formula", have structured subsequent Somali governments. Clan identity also remains a strong social bond in everyday life and in the diaspora.
