League of Resident Theatres

The League of Resident Theatres (LORT) is a service organization of professional nonprofit theatres in the United States. Founded in 1966, LORT serves as the collective bargaining entity for a network of resident theatres, negotiating agreements with the actors', directors', and designers' unions on behalf of its member companies.

History

The League of Resident Theatres was established during the rapid growth of the regional theatre movement in the 1960s, when permanent resident companies outside New York City began to multiply. LORT was created to give these theatres a unified voice in negotiations with Actors' Equity Association, the union representing stage actors and stage managers. Over the following decades, its membership grew to include many of the leading regional theatres in the country.

Role and agreements

LORT negotiates and administers a master agreement with Actors' Equity Association that sets terms for employment of professional actors at member theatres. The agreement covers minimum salaries, working hours, housing, and other conditions. Member theatres are classified into categories, commonly designated by letters, based largely on the size of the theatre's box office receipts, which determines the corresponding salary minimums. LORT also bargains with other theatrical unions, including the Stage Directors and Choreographers Society and United Scenic Artists.

Membership

Membership is open to nonprofit professional theatres that maintain a resident or regularly employed company and meet the league's standards. Member theatres are located across the United States and include many of the most prominent regional stages in American theatre.
