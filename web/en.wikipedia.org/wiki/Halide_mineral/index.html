Halide minerals
Class: Strunz class 3
Examples: Halite, fluorite, sylvite, cryolite

Halide minerals are minerals in which a halogen — fluorine, chlorine, bromine or iodine — is the dominant anion, combined with metallic or semi-metallic cations. They form one of the classes of the Strunz and Dana mineral classification systems.

Examples

The best-known halide minerals are halite (NaCl, rock salt), fluorite (CaF2), sylvite (KCl), chlorargyrite (AgCl), and cryolite (Na3AlF6), historically essential in aluminium smelting. Many halides are soft, soluble, and form in evaporite deposits.

Occurrence

Halide minerals typically occur in marine evaporite sequences, in arid-region lake deposits, and as sublimates around volcanic vents. Because many are water-soluble, they are preserved mainly in dry climates or deep burial.
