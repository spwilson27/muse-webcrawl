Horton Hatches the Egg is a children's book written and illustrated by Theodor Seuss Geisel under the pen name Dr. Seuss and published in 1940 by Random House. It introduced Horton the Elephant, who later returned in Horton Hears a Who! (1954).

Plot

Mayzie, a lazy bird, persuades the kind-hearted elephant Horton to sit on her egg while she takes a short holiday — from which she never returns. Through storms, winter cold, hunters who capture him, and a sea voyage, Horton is sold to a travelling circus, yet he never leaves the egg, repeating: "I meant what I said, and I said what I meant. An elephant's faithful, one hundred per cent!"

When the egg finally hatches before an astonished circus crowd, it reveals a flying "elephant-bird" — the creature has taken on characteristics of its devoted sitter. Mayzie, who happens to be present, demands the baby back, but the crowd sides with Horton, who is rewarded for his faithfulness and takes the elephant-bird home.

Adaptations and legacy

The story was adapted as a Merrie Melodies cartoon, Horton Hatches the Egg (1942), directed by Bob Clampett. Horton and the elephant-bird also appear in the Broadway musical Seussical (2000), which combines the Horton stories. The book's refrain about Horton's faithfulness has become one of the best-known lines in Seuss's work.
