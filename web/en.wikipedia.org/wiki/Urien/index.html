Urien (Welsh: Urien Rheged) was a late 6th-century king of Rheged, a Brittonic kingdom of the "Old North" (northern England and southern Scotland), and one of the most celebrated heroes of early Welsh tradition. His court poet was Taliesin, whose surviving praise poems to Urien are among the oldest Welsh literature.

Historical record

The Historia Brittonum records that Urien, together with his sons, fought against the expanding Anglian kingdom of Bernicia under kings including Theodric and Hussa. Urien besieged the Angles on the island of Lindisfarne (Metcaud) for three days and nights and was on the point of victory when he was assassinated at the instigation of a jealous fellow British king, Morgant (Morcant). Taliesin's poems celebrate Urien's victories at battles including Gwen Ystrad and Ardderyd-era fighting, portraying him as the shield of his people and a generous lord.

Legend and literature

In later Welsh tradition Urien became an ancestor figure, father of Owain mab Urien (the Yvain of Arthurian romance) and of Morfudd, and was drawn into the Arthurian orbit: in romance, as King Urien of Gore, he is the husband of Morgan le Fay and father of Yvain. Geoffrey of Monmouth and later writers made him a king in Arthur's Britain, sometimes rebelling against Arthur before being reconciled.
