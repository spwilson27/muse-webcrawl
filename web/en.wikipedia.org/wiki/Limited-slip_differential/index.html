Limited-slip differential

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A limited-slip differential (LSD) is a type of differential gear arrangement that allows its two output shafts to rotate at different speeds but limits the maximum difference between them. In an ordinary open differential, if one driven wheel loses grip, nearly all torque is delivered to that slipping wheel and the vehicle may be unable to move. A limited-slip differential biases torque toward the wheel with more grip, improving traction, acceleration and cornering behaviour.

Types

Clutch type

The clutch-type LSD uses one or more sets of friction clutch packs, pressed together by spring preload and by ramped gears that squeeze the packs harder as input torque rises. It is the most common type in performance cars and is tunable via its preload, ramp angles (one-way, 1.5-way or two-way action) and friction discs.

Viscous

A viscous coupling uses interleaved plates in a sealed housing filled with a high-viscosity silicone fluid. When the wheels turn at different speeds the fluid shears and heats, thickening and transferring torque across the unit. Viscous LSDs are smooth and maintenance-free but react slowly and cannot transfer as much torque.

Geared (helical / Torsen)

Geared LSDs such as the Torsen use worm or helical gear meshes that resist speed differences through friction within the gear train. They respond instantly and need no fluid or clutches, but if one wheel has essentially no grip they behave like an open differential unless combined with brake-based traction control.

Applications

Limited-slip differentials are fitted to sports cars, off-road vehicles (sometimes as fully locking differentials) and racing cars. Electronic LSDs, which use the wheel-speed sensors and brake actuators, are increasingly common as software-controlled alternatives.
