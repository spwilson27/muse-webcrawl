The Miracle Worker

Playwright: William Gibson
Broadway premiere: 1959
Film: 1962, directed by Arthur Penn
Subject: Helen Keller and Anne Sullivan

The Miracle Worker is a three-act play by William Gibson, based on the story of Helen Keller and her teacher Anne Sullivan. It began as a 1957 teleplay for Playhouse 90, opened on Broadway in 1959, and was adapted into the acclaimed 1962 film directed by Arthur Penn.

Story

In 1880s Alabama, the Keller family hires the young, half-blind Anne Sullivan to teach their daughter Helen, left deaf and blind by illness in infancy and grown wild for want of communication. Through weeks of fierce discipline and fingerspelled words, Sullivan reaches the breakthrough at the water pump, when Helen grasps that the letters spelled into her hand name the water flowing over it — the moment that unlocks language.

Productions and awards

The Broadway production won the Tony Award for Best Play. Anne Bancroft (Sullivan) and Patty Duke (Keller) reprised their stage roles on film, both winning Academy Awards; Duke, at sixteen, was then the youngest competitive Oscar winner. The story has been remade for television several times, and Gibson's play remains a staple of American theatre.
