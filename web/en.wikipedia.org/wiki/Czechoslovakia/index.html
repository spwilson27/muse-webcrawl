Czechoslovakia (Czech and Slovak: Československo) was a sovereign state in Central Europe, created in 1918 from lands of the former Austria-Hungary, that existed in several forms until its peaceful dissolution on 1 January 1993 into the Czech Republic and Slovakia. It united the Czech lands of Bohemia, Moravia and Czech Silesia with Slovakia and, until 1939/1945, Subcarpathian Ruthenia.

First Republic (1918–1938)

Founded under President Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, the First Republic was a parliamentary democracy and one of the most industrialised states in Europe. Its large ethnic German minority became a focus of Nazi German agitation; the Munich Agreement of September 1938 ceded the Sudetenland to Germany, and in March 1939 Slovakia declared independence while the Czech lands became the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia. A government-in-exile under Edvard Beneš operated from London during the Second World War.

Communist era (1948–1989)

After a brief postwar coalition, the Communist Party seized power in February 1948 and Czechoslovakia became a one-party socialist state aligned with the Soviet Union. The reform movement of the Prague Spring in 1968, led by Alexander Dubček under the slogan "socialism with a human face", was crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion in August 1968, followed by two decades of "normalization". Dissenters organised around Charter 77, whose signatories included the playwright Václav Havel.

Velvet Revolution and dissolution

The Velvet Revolution of November–December 1989 ended communist rule peacefully; Havel became president and the country became the Czech and Slovak Federative Republic in 1990. Disagreements between Czech and Slovak leaders led to a negotiated dissolution — the "Velvet Divorce" — effective 1 January 1993.
