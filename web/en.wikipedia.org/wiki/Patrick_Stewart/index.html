Sir Patrick Stewart (born 13 July 1940) is an English actor. With a career spanning over seven decades of stage and screen, he has received numerous accolades, including two Laurence Olivier Awards and a Grammy Award, as well as nominations for a Tony Award, three Golden Globe Awards, four Primetime Emmy Awards and three Screen Actors Guild Awards. He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 1996 and was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II for services to drama in 2010.

Stewart gained international stardom for his leading role as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987–1994), its subsequent films, and Star Trek: Picard (2020–2023). He starred as Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men film series (2000–2017) and reprised the role in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. He is also known for his prolific stage work with the Royal Shakespeare Company.

Early life

Stewart was born in Mirfield in the West Riding of Yorkshire, the son of Gladys, a weaver and textile worker, and Alfred Stewart, a regimental sergeant major in the British Army. His childhood was marked by poverty and by domestic violence from his father, an experience that later informed his advocacy work against domestic violence. He discovered acting through a school drama teacher and left school at 15 to work as a junior reporter before committing to the theatre; he won a grant to study at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School.

Career

Stewart joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966 and remained associated with it for decades, performing in numerous productions and winning Olivier Awards for Antony and Cleopatra and Hamlet. On screen he appeared in the BBC series I, Claudius (1976) as Sejanus and in films such as Excalibur (1981) and Dune (1984) before being cast, initially reluctantly, as Picard in 1987.

The Next Generation ran for seven seasons and made Stewart a household name. He subsequently played Picard in four feature films and returned to the role more than two decades later for Star Trek: Picard. As Charles Xavier in the X-Men franchise he shared the role's later timeline with James McAvoy; his final performance as Xavier, in Logan (2017), was widely praised, and he appeared again in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022). His voice work includes the animated series American Dad!, in which he voices Deputy Director Avery Bullock, and narration for numerous documentaries.

Personal life and advocacy

Stewart is a patron of Refuge, a UK charity for women and children experiencing domestic violence, and has spoken publicly about his father's violence and its effects. He is a supporter of the Labour Party, a lifelong fan of Huddersfield Town football club, and served as chancellor of the University of Huddersfield from 2004 to 2015. He married the American singer Sunny Ozell in 2013.
