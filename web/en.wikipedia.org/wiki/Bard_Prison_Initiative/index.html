The Bard Prison Initiative (BPI) is a program of Bard College in Annandale-on-Hudson, New York, that provides a liberal arts college education to incarcerated individuals in New York State prisons. Founded in 1999 by Max Kenner, BPI enrolls incarcerated students in full-time study leading to associate and bachelor's degrees from Bard College.

History

BPI was founded in the aftermath of the 1994 United States federal crime bill, which ended Pell Grant eligibility for incarcerated students and caused most college-in-prison programs in the country to close. Max Kenner, then a Bard undergraduate, proposed that Bard create a privately funded program inside prisons. The first classes began in 2001 at Eastern New York Correctional Facility.

Program

Admission to BPI is competitive, involving written essays and interviews. Students take the same courses, taught by the same standards, as on Bard's main campus, across the humanities, social sciences, mathematics, and laboratory science. The program has operated in multiple New York State prisons, including maximum- and medium-security facilities for men and women, and has awarded hundreds of degrees.

Debate team and public attention

The BPI debate team gained national attention in 2015 when it defeated the Harvard College debate team in an exhibition debate at Eastern New York Correctional Facility. The program was the subject of the 2019 PBS documentary series College Behind Bars, directed by Lynn Novick and produced by Ken Burns.

Impact and expansion

Studies of college-in-prison programs associate them with sharply reduced recidivism, and BPI reports recidivism rates among its alumni far below national averages. BPI has advised and helped to establish similar programs at other colleges and universities through a national consortium for liberal arts in prison.
