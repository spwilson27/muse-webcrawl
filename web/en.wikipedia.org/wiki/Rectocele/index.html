Rectocele

A rectocele (also known as a posterior vaginal wall prolapse) is a bulging of the front wall of the rectum into the back wall of the vagina, occurring when the rectovaginal septum — the tissue between the rectum and vagina — becomes weak. It is a form of pelvic organ prolapse.

Causes and symptoms

Rectoceles commonly result from childbirth-related injury, chronic straining, heavy lifting, and age-related weakening of the pelvic floor. Symptoms may include a vaginal bulge, difficulty emptying the bowel, constipation, and pelvic pressure; some women press on the perineum or vaginal wall ("splinting") to complete a bowel movement. Many small rectoceles cause no symptoms.

Treatment

Management includes pelvic floor exercises, dietary fibre, and vaginal pessaries. Surgical repair (posterior colporrhaphy or site-specific repair) is considered for symptomatic cases that do not respond to conservative measures.
