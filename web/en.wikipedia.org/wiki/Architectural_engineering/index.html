Architectural engineering

Architectural engineering, also known as building engineering, is the application of engineering principles and technology to building design and construction. An architectural engineer applies the skills of many engineering disciplines to the design, construction, operation, maintenance, and renovation of buildings, working alongside architects to make buildings structurally sound, comfortable, energy-efficient, and safe.

Disciplines

The field embraces several sub-disciplines. Structural engineering ensures the frame resists gravity, wind, and seismic loads. Mechanical engineering covers heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) and plumbing. Electrical engineering addresses power distribution, lighting, and communications. Fire protection engineering, acoustics, vertical transportation, and building envelope performance are also common specializations, increasingly coordinated through building information modeling.

Education and licensure

In the United States, architectural engineering is an accredited undergraduate discipline distinct from both architecture and civil engineering, with degree programs emphasizing building systems integration; graduates may seek licensure as professional engineers. In the United Kingdom the corresponding profession developed through the Chartered Institution of Building Services Engineers (CIBSE), founded in 1897.

Scope of work

Architectural engineers analyse sites and loads, size and select systems, prepare construction documents and calculations, and verify performance during construction and operation. As buildings account for a large share of global energy use, the discipline has become central to sustainable design: demand for high-performance envelopes, heat-recovery ventilation, daylighting, and low-carbon materials has expanded the architectural engineer's role from technical support to co-authorship of building performance.
