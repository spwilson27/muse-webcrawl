Overview

Chilam Balam is the name given to a group of handwritten books compiled in Yucatec Maya, written in the Latin alphabet, in towns of Yucatán, Mexico, during the colonial period. 'Chilam' was the title of a class of oracular priests, and 'Balam' — a common Maya surname meaning jaguar — is traditionally understood as the name of a legendary prophet.

Contents

The books blend pre-Hispanic and European material: creation myths, calendars and katun prophecies, ritual and astrology, traditional medicine, chronicles of the Spanish conquest, and local town histories. The best-known, the Book of Chilam Balam of Chumayel, contains the famous prophecy passages and the 'Ritual of the Angels'.

Scholarship

Preserved anonymously by Maya scribes in at least nine towns, the Chilam Balam books are among the most important sources for colonial Maya religion and history. Modern annotated translations, notably Ralph L. Roys's edition of the Chumayel (1933), made them central to Maya studies.
