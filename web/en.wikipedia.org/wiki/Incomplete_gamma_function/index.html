Incomplete gamma function

In mathematics, the incomplete gamma functions are types of special functions that arise as solutions to definite integrals of the gamma function. The (complete) gamma function is defined by an integral over the whole positive real line; the incomplete gamma functions restrict that integral to a finite range, producing functions of two variables.

The lower (red) and upper (blue) incomplete gamma functions sum to Γ(s).

Definition

The upper incomplete gamma function is defined as

Γ(s, x) = ∫x∞ ts−1 e−t dt,

while the lower incomplete gamma function is

γ(s, x) = ∫0x ts−1 e−t dt.

Because the two integrals together cover the full range, they satisfy γ(s, x) + Γ(s, x) = Γ(s), the ordinary gamma function. The regularized gamma functions divide by Γ(s): P(s, x) = γ(s, x)/Γ(s) and Q(s, x) = Γ(s, x)/Γ(s), with P + Q = 1.

Properties and computation

The incomplete gamma functions satisfy recurrence relations in the parameter s, admit series expansions useful for small x and continued-fraction expansions useful for large x, and can be continued analytically to complex values of both arguments. Special cases connect them to the error function and to the exponential integral.

Applications

The regularized functions are exactly the cumulative distribution functions of the gamma and chi-squared distributions, so they are used constantly in statistics to compute p-values and confidence intervals. They also appear in physics and engineering in problems involving diffusion, radiation, and waiting times.
