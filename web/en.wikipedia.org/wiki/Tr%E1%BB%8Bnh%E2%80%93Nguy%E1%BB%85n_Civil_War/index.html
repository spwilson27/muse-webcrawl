Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War

Date1627–1672 (main phase); sporadic conflict to 1777
LocationCentral Vietnam (modern Quảng Bình and Hà Tĩnh provinces)
ResultStalemate; de facto partition of Vietnam along the Gianh River
CombatantsTrịnh lords (Đàng Ngoài, "Tonkin") vs. Nguyễn lords (Đàng Trong, "Cochinchina")

The Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War (Vietnamese: Chiến tranh Trịnh–Nguyễn) was a long war between the two ruling families of Vietnam, the Trịnh lords of the north and the Nguyễn lords of the south, from 1627 to 1672. The war effectively divided Vietnam into two states along the Gianh River for more than a century, although both sides nominally acknowledged the authority of the Lê emperors.

Background

In the early 16th century, the Lê dynasty had weakened and Vietnam fell under the control of the Mạc dynasty. The Trịnh and Nguyễn families, allied under Trịnh Kiểm, fought to restore the Lê emperors. After the restoration in 1592, Trịnh Tùng dominated the court in Thăng Long (Hanoi), while Nguyễn Hoàng governed the southern frontier from Quảng Nam. By the 1620s the two families had become rivals.

The war

Between 1627 and 1672 the Trịnh launched seven major campaigns to conquer the south. The Nguyễn, advised by Đào Duy Từ, built formidable defensive lines known as the Lũy Thầy (the Walls of Đổng Hới) across the narrow coastal plain near the Gianh River, and repelled every invasion. The Trịnh, in turn, defeated Nguyễn counterattacks. Neither side could achieve a decisive victory, and a truce in 1672 divided the country at the Linh (Gianh) River.

Aftermath

The partition lasted until the Tây Sơn rebellion of the 1770s, which overthrew both regimes and briefly united Vietnam before Nguyễn Ánh founded the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802.
