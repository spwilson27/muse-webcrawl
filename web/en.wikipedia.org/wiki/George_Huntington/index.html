George Sumner Huntington (April 9, 1850 – March 3, 1916) was an American physician who described the hereditary disorder now known as Huntington's disease in a single celebrated paper published when he was only 21 years old.

Life and the 1872 paper

Huntington came from a family of physicians in East Hampton, Long Island — his father and grandfather had both practised there and had observed the same family lines of hereditary chorea. After graduating from the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Columbia University in 1871, he returned to East Hampton and wrote "On Chorea", published in the Medical and Surgical Reporter of Philadelphia on 13 April 1872. In a short postscript-like passage, he described a form of chorea confined to certain families, characterised by its hereditary nature, its tendency to insanity and suicide, and its onset in adult life — a description so accurate that William Osler praised it as a model of clinical observation.

Later life

Huntington spent most of his career as a general practitioner in small communities, never seeking academic fame, though the disorder was named for him by later writers. He died in 1916 in Cairo, New York. The mutant gene responsible for Huntington's disease (HTT, encoding huntingtin) was identified in 1993.
