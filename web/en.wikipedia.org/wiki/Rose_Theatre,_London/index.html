Rose Theatre, London

Location: Bankside, Southwark, London

Built: 1587

Owner: Philip Henslowe

Status: Demolished by 1606; remains excavated 1989

The Rose Theatre was an Elizabethan playhouse built in 1587 by theatre entrepreneur Philip Henslowe on Bankside in Southwark, London — the first successful purpose-built playhouse to stage the plays of William Shakespeare and Christopher Marlowe on a major scale. It stood just south of the River Thames, in the Liberty of the Clink, outside the jurisdiction of the City of London.

History

Henslowe built the Rose on a plot leased in an area of gardens (the "rose gardens" that gave it its name), for a cost of several hundred pounds. The polygonal timber playhouse had a thatched roof over the galleries and an open yard around a raised stage. In 1592 it was enlarged, the stage moved back and roofed. The Admiral's Men, led by the great tragedian Edward Alleyn, Henslowe's son-in-law, were the resident company from 1594; Henslowe's surviving diary records payments for plays by Marlowe, Ben Jonson, Thomas Dekker, and many others, and the Rose hosted premieres including Marlowe's Doctor Faustus and The Jew of Malta and the anonymous A Knack to Know a Knave.

After the company moved to the new Fortune Theatre in 1600, the Rose declined; the lease expired and it was demolished by about 1606.

Excavation

The site was rediscovered in 1989 during redevelopment, when archaeologists from the Museum of London excavated roughly two-thirds of the ground plan — an irregular fourteen-sided polygon — along with the foundations of two building phases, hazelnut-shell yard surfacing, and artefacts including a skull possibly used as a prop. A major public campaign, supported by actors including Peggy Ashcroft and Ian McKellen, saved the remains from destruction; they were preserved beneath a protective cover under the new office building. The preserved site, maintained by the Rose Theatre Trust, is occasionally opened to the public and used for performances and exhibitions.
