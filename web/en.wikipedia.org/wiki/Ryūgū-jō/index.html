Ryūgū-jō (Japanese: 竜宮城, "Dragon Palace Castle") is, in Japanese folklore, the undersea palace of Ryūjin (also called Ryūō), the Dragon King who rules the sea. Built of red and white coral, the palace stands on the floor of the ocean, in some tellings near the Ryukyu Islands. Its legendary description holds that each of the palace's four sides faces a different season, and that a single day within the palace equals a century in the world above.

The legend of Urashima Tarō

Ryūgū-jō is best known from the tale of Urashima Tarō, a kind fisherman who rescues a sea turtle — in some versions a disguised daughter of the Dragon King — and is carried down to the palace as a reward. There he is feasted and entertained by the princess Otohime for what seems only a few days. Longing for home, he returns to the surface carrying a forbidden casket, the tamatebako, and discovers that centuries have passed: everyone he knew is long dead. When he opens the casket, the smoke that escapes ages him instantly to an old man. The story is one of Japan's most famous folktales and a classic treatment of the theme of time passing differently in otherworldly realms.

Description and symbolism

Descriptions of the palace vary by version, but recurring elements include walls of coral and crystal, roofs of fish scales, gardens of jewelled trees, and halls attended by sea creatures. The palace embodies the wealth and mystery of the sea and the otherness of the world beneath it. In some traditions Ryūjin controls the tides from the palace with magical tide jewels, the kanju and manju.

In culture

The name Ryūgū-jō is widely used in Japan: railway stations, hotels, aquariums and public buildings have borne it, including Katase-Enoshima Station in Fujisawa, whose design evokes the palace. It also appears frequently in manga, anime, games and literature, and the asteroid probe Hayabusa2's target asteroid was named Ryugu in reference to the palace.
