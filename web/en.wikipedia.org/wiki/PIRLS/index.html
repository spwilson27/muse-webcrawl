PIRLS

The Progress in International Reading Literacy Study (PIRLS) is an international assessment of reading comprehension that has been conducted every five years since 2001 by the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA). PIRLS assesses the reading literacy of fourth-grade students (around age 10) in participating countries and provides comparative data on national education policies and achievement trends.

Framework and design

PIRLS evaluates two overarching purposes for reading: reading for literary experience, and reading to acquire and use information. Students read passages and answer comprehension questions covering processes such as retrieving explicitly stated information, making straightforward inferences, interpreting and integrating ideas, and evaluating content and textual elements. Background questionnaires collect information from students, parents, teachers, and school principals about factors associated with reading achievement.

Cycles and participation

The first PIRLS assessment was administered in 2001 in 35 countries. Subsequent cycles were held in 2006, 2011, 2016, and 2021, with participation growing to around 60 education systems. In 2016, an easier assessment called PIRLS Literacy was introduced for developing countries, and an extension to digital reading, ePIRLS, assessed online reading comprehension.

Countries that have consistently performed near the top of the PIRLS rankings include Singapore, Hong Kong, Ireland, and the Russian Federation. Results are reported on a scale with a centerpoint of 500 and a standard deviation of 100.

Relationship to other studies

PIRLS is one of the IEA's core studies, together with TIMSS, which assesses mathematics and science achievement at the fourth and eighth grades. The OECD's PISA study assesses 15-year-olds and is a separate programme.
