Detroit techno

Detroit techno is a type of techno music that generally includes the first techno productions by Detroit-based artists during the 1980s and early 1990s. Prominent Detroit techno artists include Juan Atkins, Eddie Fowlkes, Derrick May, Jeff Mills, Kevin Saunderson, Blake Baxter, Drexciya, Mike Banks, James Pennington and Carl Craig. At the time, the local scene was small and focused around a handful of clubs and record labels, but its sound — synthesising Chicago house, European synth-pop and electro, funk and futuristic imagery — became one of the foundational styles of electronic dance music worldwide.

Origins

The Belleville Three — Atkins, May and Saunderson, friends at Belleville High School in a middle-class Detroit suburb — absorbed the eclectic broadcasts of Detroit radio DJ The Electrifying Mojo, who mixed Kraftwerk, Prince, Parliament-Funkadelic and new wave. Atkins began releasing electro records with Cybotron (with Rik Davis), notably "Clear" and "Techno City" (1984), then founded Metroplex Records in 1985 and recorded as Model 500. May's Transmat and Saunderson's KMS labels followed, and May's Rhythim Is Rhythim track "Strings of Life" (1987) became an anthem of the British acid house and rave explosion.

Sound and themes

Detroit techno typically combines machine-like drum machine rhythms (especially the Roland TR-909), synthesiser basslines and melancholic or cosmic melodic textures. Its imagery drew on Detroit's industrial landscape, Afrofuturism, and science fiction; Atkins took inspiration from Alvin Toffler's The Third Wave, whose "techno rebels" gave the genre its name in common usage, popularised by the 1988 UK compilation Techno! The New Dance Sound of Detroit.

Second wave and legacy

A second wave in the early 1990s — including Jeff Mills, Robert Hood (who pioneered minimal techno), Underground Resistance, Carl Craig and Drexciya — hardened and diversified the sound. Detroit techno found an especially devoted audience in Europe, influencing rave, IDM, and later global techno, while Detroit itself celebrates the music annually at the Movement festival (formerly the Detroit Electronic Music Festival).
