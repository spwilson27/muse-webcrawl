The Helsinki Stock Exchange (Finnish: Helsingin Pörssi, Swedish: Helsingforsbörsen), now known as Nasdaq Helsinki, is a stock exchange located in Helsinki, Finland. Since 2003 it has been part of Nasdaq Nordic, and since 2008 part of the Nasdaq group.

History

The exchange held its first transaction on 7 October 1912. It operated as a free-form association for decades before becoming a limited company in 1995 and merging with the derivatives exchange in 1997. In 2003 it merged with the Swedish OM group, forming OMX, which was acquired by Nasdaq in 2008. The exchange building, designed by Lars Sonck, stands on Fabianinkatu in central Helsinki.

Trading

Trading takes place in equities, bonds, warrants and derivatives. The main benchmark index is the OMX Helsinki 25, comprising the 25 most traded stocks; major listed companies historically include Nokia, which long dominated the exchange's market capitalisation.
