Memorial ACTe

Memorial ACTe (Centre caribéen d'expressions et de mémoire de la traite et de l'esclavage) is a museum and memorial in Pointe-à-Pitre, Guadeloupe, dedicated to the history of the slave trade and slavery. Inaugurated on 10 May 2015 by French president François Hollande, it is described as the largest museum in the world devoted to the memory of slavery.

Architecture

The museum stands on the site of the former Darboussier sugar factory. Designed by Guadeloupean architects of the Berthelot/Mocka-Célestine (BMC) agency, the building is notable for its long black-granite volume wrapped in a silver-coloured metallic lattice representing the roots of a courbaril ("shoe-black") tree, intended to symbolise memory rooted in Caribbean soil. A memorial garden and a footbridge linking the building to the city form part of the complex.

Exhibitions

The permanent exhibition traces the history of slavery from antiquity through the transatlantic slave trade, the plantation system in the Caribbean, abolition, and the legacies of slavery in contemporary societies, combining artefacts, documents, audiovisual installations and works of contemporary art.
