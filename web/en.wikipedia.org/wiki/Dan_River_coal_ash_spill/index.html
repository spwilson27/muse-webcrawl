CauseRuptured stormwater pipe beneath a coal ash pond

The Dan River at Eden, North Carolina, site of the 2014 spill.

The Dan River coal ash spill occurred on February 2, 2014, when a stormwater pipe ruptured beneath a coal ash storage pond at Duke Energy's retired Dan River Steam Station in Eden, North Carolina. The break released tens of thousands of tons of coal ash and millions of gallons of contaminated pond water into the Dan River, coating the river bottom with ash deposits for many miles downstream.

The spill

A corrugated metal stormwater pipe running under the 27-acre primary ash pond failed, allowing ash and water to flow into the river. Duke Energy estimated that up to 39,000 tons of ash and 27 million gallons of ash basin water escaped. The spill was discovered by a security guard; public notification followed the next day.

Environmental impact

Coal ash contains toxic heavy metals including arsenic, lead, mercury and selenium. Downstream drinking-water intakes, including those in Danville, Virginia, were monitored; officials reported that treatment kept tap water within drinking-water standards. A thick layer of ash accumulated on the riverbed and along the banks, and cleanup dredging was undertaken at several locations.

Aftermath

The spill intensified scrutiny of coal ash storage across North Carolina and the United States. In 2015, Duke Energy subsidiaries pleaded guilty to criminal violations of the Clean Water Act related to the spill and other ash sites, agreeing to pay about $102 million in fines and restitution. North Carolina's Coal Ash Management Act of 2014 required the closure and excavation of ash ponds at the Dan River site and other priority locations.
