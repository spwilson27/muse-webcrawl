United States Central Command

CENTCOM
Founded: 1 January 1983
Headquarters: MacDill Air Force Base, Florida
Role: Geographic combatant command

The United States Central Command (USCENTCOM or CENTCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands of the U.S. Department of Defense. It was established in 1983, taking over the responsibilities of the Rapid Deployment Joint Task Force.

CENTCOM's area of responsibility includes the Middle East, Central Asia, and parts of South Asia, covering 21 countries including Egypt, Iraq, Afghanistan, Iran, and the states of the Arabian Peninsula. Its headquarters is at MacDill Air Force Base in Tampa, Florida, with a forward headquarters in Qatar.

History

The command grew out of the Carter Doctrine of 1980, which declared that the United States would use military force if necessary to defend its interests in the Persian Gulf. CENTCOM directed Operation Earnest Will during the Iran–Iraq War and, in 1990–91, conducted Operation Desert Shield and Operation Desert Storm to liberate Kuwait from Iraqi occupation.

Operations

Following the September 11 attacks, CENTCOM led the invasion of Afghanistan in 2001 (Operation Enduring Freedom) and the invasion of Iraq in 2003 (Operation Iraqi Freedom). From 2014 it directed Operation Inherent Resolve against the Islamic State in Iraq and Syria. Commanders have included Generals Norman Schwarzkopf Jr., Tommy Franks, David Petraeus, and Lloyd Austin.

Components

Service components include U.S. Army Central (ARCENT), U.S. Naval Forces Central Command (NAVCENT), U.S. Air Forces Central (AFCENT), U.S. Marine Corps Forces Central Command (MARCENT), and U.S. Space Forces Central.
