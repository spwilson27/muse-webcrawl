Émile Édouard Charles Antoine Zola (2 April 1840 – 29 September 1902) was a French novelist, journalist and playwright, the most prominent representative of the literary school of naturalism and an important figure in the political liberalisation of France.

Life and career

Zola was born in Paris to an Italian-born engineer father and a French mother, and grew up in Aix-en-Provence, where he befriended the painter Paul Cézanne. After early work in journalism and publishing, he achieved success with Thérèse Raquin (1867) and then embarked on his monumental cycle Les Rougon-Macquart: twenty novels tracing two branches of a family through the Second French Empire, applying his theory that character is shaped by heredity and environment. Volumes such as L'Assommoir, Nana and Germinal (1885), a harrowing depiction of a coal miners' strike, made him France's best-known writer.

The Dreyfus affair

In January 1898 Zola published J'Accuse...!, an open letter to the President of France accusing the military of wrongfully convicting the Jewish officer Alfred Dreyfus of treason. Convicted of libel, Zola fled briefly to England; his intervention turned the Dreyfus affair into a national crisis and helped secure Dreyfus's eventual exoneration.

Death

Zola died in 1902 of carbon monoxide poisoning from a blocked chimney, in circumstances that have long fuelled speculation. In 1908 his remains were transferred to the Panthéon.
