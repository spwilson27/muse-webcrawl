Sirius Satellite Radio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sirius Satellite Radio Founded: 1990 (as Satellite CD Radio)
Service launched: 1 July 2002
Headquarters: New York City, United States
Coverage: North America
Fate: Merged with XM in 2008 to form Sirius XM

Sirius Satellite Radio was an American satellite radio service that broadcast subscription-based digital audio channels across North America from its own constellation of satellites. Founded in 1990 as Satellite CD Radio and renamed Sirius after the brightest star in the sky (and the "dog star", reflected in its dog logo), the company launched its service on 1 July 2002, several months after its rival XM Satellite Radio.

Service and technology

Sirius originally operated three satellites in highly inclined elliptical geosynchronous orbits (a Tundra-type configuration), arranged so that at least one satellite was always high in the sky over North America, reducing dropouts from buildings and terrain; terrestrial repeaters filled gaps in cities. Subscribers paid a monthly fee for more than 120 channels of commercial-free music, sports, news and talk programming received on dedicated car and home receivers. Sirius secured exclusive rights to the NFL, NBA and NASCAR and, most famously, signed shock jock Howard Stern to a headline five-year, $500 million contract beginning in 2006, a deal credited with driving a surge in subscriptions.

Competition and merger

Sirius and XM competed expensively for car-manufacturer deals, sports rights and on-air talent, and both accumulated heavy losses. In February 2007 they announced a merger valued at about $13 billion; after a lengthy regulatory review the FCC approved the combination in July 2008, and the merged company became Sirius XM Radio (now Sirius XM Holdings). The two channel line-ups were progressively unified, although legacy Sirius and XM receivers continued to operate on their separate satellite systems for many years.

Legacy

The Sirius name survived as one of the merged company's two flagship brands ("SiriusXM"), and its original strategy — exclusive content, factory-installed car radios and subscription revenue — became the foundation of the largest audio entertainment company in North America.
