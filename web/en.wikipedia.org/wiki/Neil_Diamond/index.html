Neil Diamond

Neil Leslie Diamond (born January 24, 1941) is an American singer-songwriter. He has sold more than 56.5 million records in the United States alone and over 130 million worldwide, making him one of the best-selling musicians of all time. Diamond has had ten No. 1 singles on the Billboard Hot 100 and Adult Contemporary charts, and 38 songs in the Top 10 of the Adult Contemporary chart.

Early life and career

Diamond was born in Brooklyn, New York, to a Jewish family descended from Russian and Polish immigrants. He began writing songs as a teenager and worked as a staff songwriter in the Brill Building tradition before signing to Bang Records in 1966. His early hits included "Solitary Man," "Cherry, Cherry," and "Kentucky Woman." He also wrote "I'm a Believer," a major hit for the Monkees, and "Red Red Wine," later a No. 1 for UB40.

Success

Moving to Uni Records, Diamond reached stardom with songs such as "Sweet Caroline" (1969), "Holly Holy," "Cracklin' Rosie" (his first Billboard Hot 100 No. 1, in 1970), "Song Sung Blue," and "I Am... I Said." "Sweet Caroline" became a cultural fixture, sung at sporting events worldwide. His soundtrack for the 1980 film The Jazz Singer, in which he also starred, produced the hits "Love on the Rocks," "Hello Again," and "America."

Later career

Diamond continued recording and touring for decades, and in the 2000s worked with producer Rick Rubin on the albums 12 Songs (2005) and Home Before Dark (2008), the latter his first album to debut at No. 1. In January 2018 he announced his retirement from touring after a diagnosis of Parkinson's disease. A jukebox musical based on his life and songs, A Beautiful Noise, opened on Broadway in 2022.

Honours

Diamond was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1984 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2011. He received a Kennedy Center Honor in 2011 and the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2018.
