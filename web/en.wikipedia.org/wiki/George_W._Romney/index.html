George Wilcken Romney (July 8, 1907 – July 26, 1995) was an American businessman and politician. He was chairman and president of American Motors Corporation from 1954 to 1962, the 43rd governor of Michigan from 1963 to 1969, and the United States secretary of housing and urban development from 1969 to 1973.

Early life and business career

Romney was born in a Mormon colony in Chihuahua, Mexico, to American parents; the family returned to the United States during the Mexican Revolution. After missionary service and work as a lobbyist for the aluminium and automobile industries, he joined Nash-Kelvinator and became president of the merged American Motors Corporation in 1954, championing compact cars such as the Rambler and becoming a nationally known business figure.

Governor and national office

Elected governor of Michigan in 1962, Romney introduced a state income tax, supported civil rights legislation, and was a leading moderate Republican. He sought the 1968 Republican presidential nomination, but his campaign collapsed after he remarked in 1967 that he had been subjected to "brainwashing" by generals and diplomats on a visit to Vietnam. Under President Richard Nixon he served as Secretary of Housing and Urban Development, promoting fair housing and subsidised housing programmes before leaving in 1973. His son Mitt Romney later became governor of Massachusetts, the 2012 Republican presidential nominee, and a U.S. senator from Utah.
