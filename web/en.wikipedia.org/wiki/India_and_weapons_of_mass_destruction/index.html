India and weapons of mass destruction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

India possesses nuclear weapons and previously developed chemical weapons. Although India has not released any official statements about the size of its nuclear arsenal, recent estimates suggest that India has a stockpile of around 160–180 warheads. It has conducted six nuclear tests: one in 1974 (code-named Smiling Buddha) and five more in 1998 (Operation Shakti). India is not a signatory to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) or the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty, but it is a member of the International Atomic Energy Agency.

History

India's nuclear programme traces its origins to the work of Homi J. Bhabha, who founded the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Atomic Energy Commission of India in the 1940s. The Sino-Indian War of 1962 and China's first nuclear test in 1964 intensified debate within India about developing its own nuclear capability. Under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, India conducted its first nuclear test at Pokhran on 18 May 1974, describing it as a "peaceful nuclear explosion".

Operation Shakti

On 11 and 13 May 1998, India conducted five underground nuclear tests at the Pokhran Test Range in Rajasthan, collectively known as Operation Shakti. The tests included a thermonuclear device. The tests provoked international condemnation and sanctions, and Pakistan responded with its own series of tests later that month. In 1999 India released a draft nuclear doctrine committing to a credible minimum deterrent and a policy of no first use.

Delivery systems

India maintains a nuclear triad. Land-based ballistic missiles include the Prithvi and Agni series, with the Agni-V providing intercontinental range. Air-delivered weapons can be carried by aircraft of the Indian Air Force. The sea-based leg is centred on the Arihant-class ballistic missile submarines, the first of which, INS Arihant, was commissioned in 2016 and completed its first deterrence patrol in 2018.

Chemical and biological weapons

India ratified the Chemical Weapons Convention in 1996 and declared a stockpile of chemical weapons, which it destroyed by 2009, becoming one of the first signatories to eliminate its declared stockpile. India has been a party to the Biological Weapons Convention since 1974 and maintains that its biotechnology activities are exclusively peaceful.

International relations

In 2008 the Nuclear Suppliers Group granted India a waiver allowing civil nuclear trade despite its non-signatory status to the NPT, following the India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement. India has since concluded civil nuclear cooperation agreements with several countries and joined the Missile Technology Control Regime (2016), the Wassenaar Arrangement (2017) and the Australia Group (2018).
