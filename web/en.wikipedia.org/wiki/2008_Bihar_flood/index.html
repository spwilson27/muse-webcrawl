2008 Bihar flood

DateAugust 2008
LocationBihar, India; Nepal
CauseBreach of Kosi River embankment
Affected~2.7 million people in Bihar

The 2008 Bihar flood was one of the most disastrous floods in the history of Bihar, India. On 18 August 2008, the Kosi River breached its eastern embankment near Kusaha in Nepal and shifted course, inundating districts of northern Bihar that had not flooded in decades.

Impact

The river carved a new channel across densely populated farmland. Roughly 2.7 million people in districts including Supaul, Saharsa, Madhepura, Araria, and Purnia were affected; hundreds died, hundreds of thousands of homes were destroyed, and over a million people were evacuated to relief camps.

Response

The Indian government declared the flood a national calamity and mounted a huge relief and rescue operation using the army, navy, and air force. The breach was eventually repaired in early 2009, and debate continued over embankment-based flood control on the Kosi, long nicknamed the "Sorrow of Bihar".
