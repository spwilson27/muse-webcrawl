International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service

AbbreviationIERS
Formation1987
TypeScientific service
PurposeEarth rotation monitoring, reference systems and reference frames
Central BureauFrankfurt am Main, Germany

The International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS) is an international service responsible for maintaining global time and reference frame standards relevant to Earth measurement and positional astronomy. It provides data and standards relating to the Earth's rotation, the International Celestial Reference System (ICRS) and its realization, the International Celestial Reference Frame (ICRF), and geodetic products used in navigation, satellite positioning and Earth science.

The IERS was established in 1987 by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) and the International Union of Geodesy and Geophysics (IUGG), replacing the earlier International Polar Motion Service and the Earth rotation section of the Bureau International de l'Heure.

Functions

The IERS determines and publishes Earth orientation parameters, including UT1, polar motion coordinates, celestial pole offsets, and the difference between atomic time and Universal Time that underlies the insertion of leap seconds. When the difference between UT1 and UTC approaches 0.9 seconds, the IERS announces in its Bulletin C whether a leap second will be inserted.

The service also maintains the International Terrestrial Reference Frame (ITRF), a precise realization of the terrestrial reference system used by GPS and other satellite navigation systems, and publishes the IERS Conventions describing models and procedures for geodetic and astronomical data analysis.

Organization

The IERS consists of a Directing Board, a Central Bureau (hosted by the Federal Agency for Cartography and Geodesy in Frankfurt am Main, Germany), and a network of technique centres, product centres, combination centres and analysis coordinators drawing on techniques such as very-long-baseline interferometry, satellite laser ranging, GNSS and DORIS.
