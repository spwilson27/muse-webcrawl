Footloose

Directed by: Herbert Ross
Written by: Dean Pitchford
Starring: Kevin Bacon, Lori Singer, John Lithgow
Release date: 17 February 1984
Box office: $80 million

Footloose is a 1984 American musical drama film directed by Herbert Ross and written by Dean Pitchford. It stars Kevin Bacon as Ren McCormack, a teenager from Chicago who moves to a small town where the local minister has banned dancing and rock music.

Plot

Ren McCormack moves with his mother to the rural town of Bomont, where Reverend Shaw Moore has persuaded the town council to outlaw dancing following the death of several local teenagers, including the Reverend's son, in a car accident after a night of partying. Ren befriends Willard and falls for the Reverend's rebellious daughter Ariel. Determined to hold a senior prom, Ren challenges the town's ordinances and eventually persuades the council — and the Reverend — that dancing is not a crime. The film closes with the students celebrating at a prom just outside the town limits.

Production

The story was loosely inspired by Elmore City, Oklahoma, where dancing had been banned for nearly a century before high school students challenged the ban in 1980. Kevin Bacon's breakthrough role made him a star; the warehouse dance scene used doubles for some gymnastic sequences.

Music

The soundtrack was a major commercial success, reaching number one on the Billboard 200. It includes Kenny Loggins's title song "Footloose" and "Let's Hear It for the Boy" by Deniece Williams, both of which topped the Billboard Hot 100 and were nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Legacy

The film has been frequently referenced in popular culture, and Bacon's dance scenes remain iconic. A stage musical adaptation opened on Broadway in 1998, and a remake film was released in 2011.
