Blazer

A classic navy blazer with metal buttons

A blazer is a type of jacket resembling a suit jacket, but cut more casually. A blazer is generally distinguished from a sport coat as a more formal garment and tailored from solid colour fabrics, often navy blue, with metal buttons. Blazers are worn by both men and women as part of school uniforms, club dress, business-casual attire, and smart-casual outfits, typically paired with trousers of a contrasting colour such as grey flannel, khaki chinos, or white ducks.

Origins

Two institutions claim the blazer's origin. In 1825 the Lady Margaret Boat Club of St John's College, Cambridge, adopted bright scarlet ("blazing red") jackets, giving rise to the verb "to blaze" for the garment's vivid colour. Alternatively, the crew of HMS Blazer in 1845 were issued navy jackets with brass buttons by their captain to smarten their appearance before an official visit, and the style entered naval and civilian dress. Both claims are repeated in histories of menswear; the rowing-club tradition is widely accepted as the source of the name.

Styles

Classic blazers are single- or double-breasted with metal (often brass or gilt) buttons bearing a club, school, or regimental crest; patch pockets and a breast-pocket badge are common in club and school blazers. Rowing, cricket, and tennis clubs developed distinctive striped and piped blazers, worn at regattas such as Henley. The reefer blazer, a double-breasted navy jacket, derives from naval officer dress. Women's blazers, popularized in the 20th century and a staple of 1980s power dressing, follow similar tailoring in a wide range of fabrics and colours.

Modern use

The blazer is a fixture of school uniforms in Britain, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, India, and Japan, usually in school colours with a crest. Airlines and yacht clubs also prescribe blazers. In business dress it occupies a middle ground between the suit and the sport coat, standard in "smart casual" dress codes.
