Ben Jonson

Benjamin Jonson (c. 11 June 1572 – 18 August 1637) was an English playwright and poet, a major figure of Jacobean theatre second in reputation in his age only to William Shakespeare.

Ben Jonson
Born: c. 1572, London
Died: 18 August 1637, London
Occupation: Playwright, poet, actor
Notable works: Volpone, The Alchemist, Bartholomew Fair

Life

Educated at Westminster School, Jonson worked briefly as a bricklayer and soldier before turning to theatre. He killed fellow actor Gabriel Spencer in a duel in 1598 and narrowly escaped hanging. He became a favourite at the court of James I, writing many masques, often with designer Inigo Jones.

Works

His comedies of humours include Every Man in His Humour (1598), Volpone (1606), The Alchemist (1610), and Bartholomew Fair (1614). His 1616 folio Works helped establish the drama as literature. Younger poets calling themselves the Tribe of Ben gathered around him.

Legacy

Jonson is buried in Westminster Abbey under the inscription "O rare Ben Jonson".
