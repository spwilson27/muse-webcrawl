The Pragmatic Programmer is a book about computer programming and software engineering written by Andrew Hunt and David Thomas, first published in 1999. It is one of the most widely recommended books on the craft of programming, structured as a collection of short sections, each offering practical advice illustrated with anecdotes and examples.

Contents

The book's themes include taking responsibility for one's work and career ("care about your craft"), the DRY principle ("Don't Repeat Yourself"), orthogonality, tracer bullets and prototyping, the "broken windows" theory of software entropy, estimating, refactoring, testing, and automation. Many of its aphorisms — such as "Don't live with broken windows" and "Use a single editor well" — have passed into common programming parlance. The book makes frequent use of metaphors drawn from other crafts, including woodworking and stone soup.

Legacy

The success of the book led the authors to found The Pragmatic Bookshelf publishing company, and the "Pragmatic" brand was extended to a series of books by other authors. A fully revised 20th Anniversary Edition was published in 2019, updating the examples and advice for contemporary development practice while retaining the book's core philosophy of pragmatic, journeyman-to-master professional development.
