Group algebra

In mathematics, the group algebra of a given group is a certain algebra constructed from the group and a ring, in such a way that the representation theory of the group becomes the module theory of the algebra. For a group G and a commutative ring R, the group algebra RG consists of all formal finite sums of elements of G with coefficients in R, with multiplication extending the group operation.

Definition

As a set, RG is the free R-module with basis G. Addition is componentwise, and multiplication is defined by the convolution-like rule: the product of basis elements is their product in the group, extended bilinearly. When G is finite and R is a field whose characteristic does not divide the order of G, Maschke's theorem says the group algebra is semisimple.

Relation to representation theory

There is a one-to-one correspondence between representations of G over R and modules over the group algebra RG. This perspective is foundational in representation theory: questions about group representations translate into questions about algebras, where tools such as idempotents and projective modules can be applied.

Examples

The group algebra of the integers is the ring of Laurent polynomials. The complex group algebra of a finite abelian group is isomorphic to a direct product of copies of the complex numbers, one for each irreducible representation. For locally compact groups there are related constructions, such as the group C*-algebra and the group von Neumann algebra, which are central in harmonic analysis and operator algebras.
