Kubu Island

Kubu Island (Ga'nnyo) is an island of dry granite rock located in the Makgadikgadi Pan area of Botswana. The island is located a few kilometres away from the villages of Orapa and Letlhakane, and can be accessed via Mmatshumo in the Boteti district. The entire island, roughly a kilometre long, is a national monument of Botswana and is managed and protected by the local community trust of Mmatshumo; guides are available and there is a rudimentary campsite.

Description

Kubu — the word means "hippopotamus" in Tswana — was once an island in the great prehistoric Lake Makgadikgadi, which covered much of the Kalahari in wetter times. As the lake dried thousands of years ago, it left the island stranded in a sea of salt pan. Its shoreline boulders still show the dark waterlines of the ancient lake, and fossilised guano and beach pebbles mark old water levels.

The island is crowned by numerous ancient baobab trees and the ruins of dry-stone walls, including a circular enclosure thought to be a ritual or initiation site associated with the Great Zimbabwe culture; archaeological finds include stone tools, pottery and ostrich-eggshell beads. The stark contrast of gnarled baobabs, white granite and the empty white pan makes Kubu one of Botswana's most photographed and spiritually evocative landscapes. Access is by four-wheel-drive track across the pan, which becomes impassable in the wet season.
