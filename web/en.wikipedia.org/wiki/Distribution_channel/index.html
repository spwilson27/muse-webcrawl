Distribution channel

A distribution channel (also called a marketing channel) is the chain of businesses or intermediaries through which a good or service passes from the producer until it reaches the final buyer or end consumer. Distribution channels can include wholesalers, retailers, distributors, and the Internet. A distribution channel can also refer to the path that payments take in the opposite direction, from the end consumer to the original vendor.

Types of channels

A direct channel lets a producer sell straight to consumers, for example through its own stores or website. Indirect channels use one or more intermediaries: a one-level channel adds a retailer, a two-level channel adds a wholesaler, and longer chains may add distributors and agents.

Channel functions

Intermediaries perform functions including breaking bulk, holding inventory, providing assortment, financing, market information, promotion, and after-sales service. Designing a channel involves decisions about channel length, the intensity of distribution (intensive, selective, or exclusive), and the management of conflict between channel members.
