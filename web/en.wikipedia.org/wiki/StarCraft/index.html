StarCraft

Developer: Blizzard Entertainment
Publisher: Blizzard Entertainment
Release: 31 March 1998 (Windows)
Genre: Real-time strategy
Modes: Single-player, multiplayer
Series: StarCraft

StarCraft is a military science fiction real-time strategy video game developed and published by Blizzard Entertainment, released for Microsoft Windows on 31 March 1998. Set in the Koprulu sector in the 26th century, the game revolves around a galactic struggle between three species: the adaptable Terrans, exiled human descendants; the insectoid Zerg, driven by a hivemind to assimilate other species; and the technologically advanced, psionic Protoss. It was followed later in 1998 by the expansion pack StarCraft: Brood War, and by the sequel StarCraft II: Wings of Liberty in 2010.

Gameplay

StarCraft's defining innovation was the radical asymmetry of its three playable races: each has entirely different units, buildings and mechanics — the Terrans build mobile structures and versatile infantry, the Zerg produce cheap swarming units from a creep-spreading hive, and the Protoss field expensive, powerful units with regenerating shields. Players gather minerals and vespene gas, construct bases and armies, and destroy the enemy. Despite the asymmetry, the game was celebrated for its balance, which underpinned its competitive longevity.

Story

The single-player campaign is divided into three episodes, one per race, telling an interlocking story of the fall of the Terran Confederacy, the rise of Arcturus Mengsk's Dominion, the Zerg Overmind's invasion of the Protoss homeworld Aiur, and the emergence of Sarah Kerrigan, a Terran ghost agent infested and transformed into the Zerg Queen of Blades.

Reception and legacy

StarCraft sold more than 11 million copies and is regarded as one of the greatest and most influential video games ever made, credited with popularising the real-time strategy genre and pioneering esports: in South Korea, professional StarCraft — especially Brood War — became a mass spectator phenomenon with televised leagues and celebrity players. A high-definition remaster, StarCraft: Remastered, was released in 2017. See also StarCraft in esports.
