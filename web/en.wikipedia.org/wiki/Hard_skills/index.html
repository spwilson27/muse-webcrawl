Hard skills, also known as technical skills, are teachable abilities or skill sets that are easy to quantify. Typically, hard skills are learned in the classroom, through books or other training materials, or on the job. They are specific, observable abilities such as proficiency in a foreign language, typing speed, machine operation, or computer programming.

Characteristics

Hard skills are conventionally contrasted with soft skills: they are rule-based, job- and industry-specific, and can be objectively measured and certified through examinations, degrees, and licences. Employers commonly screen candidates for hard skills through résumés, portfolios, and technical tests.

Examples

  • Proficiency in a programming language or software application
  • Accounting, data analysis, and mathematical modelling
  • Operating specialised machinery or laboratory equipment
  • Speaking or writing a foreign language
  • Professional certifications and licences (e.g., a commercial driving licence)

Criticism and skills frameworks

Human-resources literature notes that the hard/soft distinction can be misleading, since many measurable "hard" skills depend on judgement and context, while some "soft" skills can themselves be trained and assessed. Skills taxonomies such as ESCO and O*NET instead classify skills by function and proficiency level.
