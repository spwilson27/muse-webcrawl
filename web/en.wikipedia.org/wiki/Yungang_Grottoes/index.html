Yungang Grottoes

Yungang Grottoes 云冈石窟
LocationDatong, Shanxi, China
Period5th–6th century CE, Northern Wei dynasty
Caves254 grottoes; about 51,000 statues
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 2001)

The Yungang Grottoes, near Datong in Shanxi province, are a celebrated complex of ancient Chinese Buddhist cave temples carved into a sandstone cliff beginning in the 5th century under the patronage of the Northern Wei dynasty. Extending for about a kilometre, the site preserves some 254 caves and niches containing roughly 51,000 statues, ranging from colossal Buddhas over 16 metres tall to miniatures only a few centimetres high.

History

Work began around 460 CE under the monk Tanyao, after the Northern Wei emperors — themselves converts to Buddhism — restored imperial support for the religion following an earlier persecution. The earliest five caves (16–20, the "Tanyao Caves") contain monumental Buddha images associated with the first five Northern Wei emperors. Carving continued in phases for decades, blending Indian and Central Asian artistic influences arriving along the Silk Road with emerging Chinese styles.

Significance

Yungang represents the peak of early Chinese Buddhist rock-cut art and a key moment in the transmission of Buddhist sculpture from India into East Asia. Together with the later Mogao Caves at Dunhuang and the Longmen Grottoes near Luoyang, it forms one of the three great grotto complexes of China. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001.
