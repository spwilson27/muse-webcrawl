Anicca

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Anicca (Pali: अनिच्च; Sanskrit: anitya) is a Buddhist term meaning impermanence. It is one of the three marks of existence (tilakkhaṇa), together with suffering (dukkha) and no-self (anattā). The doctrine asserts that all conditioned existence, without exception, is transient, evanescent and inconstant.

Doctrine

In the Buddha's teaching, everything in the world of experience arises and passes away: material form, feelings, perceptions, mental formations and consciousness are all in flux. The Buddha summarised the insight in the formula "whatever has the nature to arise has the nature to cease". Clinging to things as if they were permanent is identified as a fundamental cause of suffering, because what one grasps will inevitably change or be lost.

Relation to the other marks

Anicca is presented as the ground of the other two marks: because things are impermanent, they are unsatisfactory (dukkha) when clung to, and because they change independently of one's wishes, they cannot be an enduring self (anattā). The traditional formula states: "all conditioned things are impermanent, all conditioned things are suffering, all things are not-self."

Practice and later thought

Insight into impermanence (anicca-saññā) is cultivated in vipassanā meditation, in which practitioners observe the arising and passing of sensations and thoughts moment by moment. Later Buddhist philosophy, including the momentariness (kṣaṇikavāda) theories of the Abhidharma schools and Dharmakīrti, analysed impermanence as the instantaneous perishing of entities. The concept has parallels in the emphasis on transience in Japanese aesthetics, such as mono no aware.
