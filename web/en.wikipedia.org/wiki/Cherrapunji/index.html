Cherrapunji

Cherrapunji (also spelled Cherrapunjee; locally known as Sohra) is a subdivision and town in the East Khasi Hills district of the Indian state of Meghalaya. It is widely credited as one of the wettest places on Earth, famous for its extraordinary annual rainfall, living root bridges, and deep gorges cut into the southern edge of the Shillong Plateau.

Geography and climate

Cherrapunji sits on a plateau at the edge of a cliff overlooking the plains of Bangladesh. Moist air from the Bay of Bengal is funnelled up the steep Khasi Hills and condenses abruptly, producing some of the heaviest orographic rainfall recorded anywhere. The town held world records for rainfall in a calendar month (9,300 mm in July 1861) and in a year (26,461 mm between August 1860 and July 1861). Its average annual rainfall of roughly 11,777 mm rivals that of nearby Mawsynram, which is often cited as the wettest inhabited place.

Despite the deluge, the thin soils and deforested plateau mean the area can face water shortages in the dry winter months, an apparent paradox that has drawn scientific attention.

Living root bridges

The Khasi people around Cherrapunji have for centuries trained the aerial roots of rubber fig trees (Ficus elastica) across streams to form living root bridges. The most famous is the double-decker root bridge at Nongriat village. These bridges strengthen with age and can support dozens of people at once.

Tourism

Attractions include Nohkalikai Falls — among India's tallest plunge waterfalls at about 340 metres — Mawsmai Cave, the Eco Park, and viewpoints over the Bangladesh plains. The town is about 53 km from Shillong, the state capital.
