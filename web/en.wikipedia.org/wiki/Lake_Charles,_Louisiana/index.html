Lake Charles is a city in Calcasieu Parish, Louisiana, United States, and serves as the parish seat. Located on Lake Charles, Prien Lake, and the Calcasieu River in southwestern Louisiana, it is the fifth-largest city in the state and the principal city of its metropolitan area, with a population of roughly 85,000.

History

The city was founded in 1861 as Charleston, reportedly named for an early settler, Charles Sallier; it was renamed Lake Charles when incorporated in 1867 to avoid confusion with Charleston, South Carolina. The lumber industry and the Calcasieu River made it a port town, and the construction of the ship channel to the Gulf of Mexico in the twentieth century turned it into a major petrochemical and refining centre. The Port of Lake Charles is among the busiest in the United States by tonnage, and the region hosts major liquefied natural gas (LNG) export terminals.

Economy and culture

Petrochemicals, refining, gaming (riverboat and resort casinos), and shipping dominate the economy. McNeese State University is located in the city. Lake Charles is known for its festivals, including the Louisiana Pirate Festival — a nod to legends of Jean Lafitte's treasure in the area — and Contraband Days.

Hurricanes

The city has been struck by several devastating hurricanes, most notably Audrey (1957), Rita (2005), and Laura (2020), a Category 4 storm that caused catastrophic damage, followed only weeks later by Hurricane Delta.
