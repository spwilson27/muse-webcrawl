Kamakura shogunate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Great Buddha (Daibutsu) of Kamakura, a bronze Amida from 1252]

The Kamakura shogunate (1185–1333) was the first military government (bakufu) of Japan, founded by Minamoto no Yoritomo after his clan's victory in the Genpei War, and ruled from the coastal town of Kamakura. It marks the beginning of nearly seven centuries of samurai government: the emperor and court remained in Kyoto, but real power lay with the shogun, his Hōjō regents (who soon dominated the office), and the network of vassals (gokenin), stewards (jitō) and military governors (shugo) who administered the land. The shogunate repelled the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281 and fell in 1333 to imperial loyalist and rival-warrior coalitions.

Warrior government

Yoritomo, exiled as a boy after his father's failed Heiji Rebellion, raised the east in 1180; his half-brother Yoshitsune's brilliant generalship destroyed the Taira at Dan-no-ura (1185) — and Yoshitsune himself was soon hunted down at Yoritomo's order, becoming Japan's archetypal tragic hero. Granted the title sei-i taishōgun in 1192, Yoritomo built a parallel state of land-stewards and constables; after his death (1199) his widow Masako ('the nun shogun') and her Hōjō family held power as regents (shikken) for puppet shoguns, crushing the court's restoration attempt in the Jōkyū War (1221).

Mongols and fall

The Mongol demands of 1268, refused, brought invasions in 1274 and 1281; storms (the divine winds, kamikaze) destroyed both fleets, but the defence bankrupted the shogunate, which could not reward its vassals from conquered land. Discontent fed the campaigns of Ashikaga Takauji and Nitta Yoshisada, who destroyed the Hōjō at Kamakura in 1333 for the Emperor Go-Daigo's Kenmu Restoration — which itself collapsed within years into the Ashikaga shogunate and the wars of the Northern and Southern Courts.
