Rainbow trout

Rainbow trout, showing the characteristic pink-red lateral band.

The rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) is a species of trout native to cold-water tributaries of the Pacific Ocean in North America and Asia. It is named for the broad pink to red stripe along its flank and the black spots covering its body and tail. Sea-run individuals, called steelhead, migrate to the ocean and return to freshwater to spawn.

Habitat and life cycle

Rainbow trout inhabit well-oxygenated streams, rivers, and lakes, feeding on insects, crustaceans, and small fish. Spawning occurs in gravel nests (redds) in spring. Resident fish typically weigh 0.5–2 kg, while steelhead may exceed 9 kg.

Aquaculture and introduction

The species is one of the most widely farmed and introduced fish in the world, raised commercially for food and stocked for sport fishing on every continent except Antarctica. Escaped or introduced rainbow trout have become invasive in some regions, competing and hybridising with native trout, and the species is listed among the world's 100 worst invasive alien species.
