Tropicália

Tropicália, also known as Tropicalismo, was a Brazilian artistic and musical movement that emerged in the late 1960s, during the country's military dictatorship. It blended Brazilian traditions such as samba and bossa nova with foreign influences including rock and roll, psychedelia, and avant-garde art, deliberately mixing high and low culture.

Key figures

The movement's central musicians included Caetano Veloso, Gilberto Gil, Gal Costa, Tom Zé, and the band Os Mutantes; the 1968 collaborative album Tropicália: ou Panis et Circencis served as its manifesto. The name derived from an installation by artist Hélio Oiticica.

Repression and legacy

The military regime viewed Tropicália's provocations with suspicion; Veloso and Gil were arrested in late 1968 and later exiled to London. Despite its brief original run, the movement profoundly shaped Brazilian popular music and has been cited as an influence by musicians worldwide.
