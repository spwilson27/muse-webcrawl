Ten Tragic Days

The Ten Tragic Days (Spanish: La Decena Trágica) were a series of events during the Mexican Revolution that took place in Mexico City from 9 to 19 February 1913, culminating in the overthrow and murder of President Francisco I. Madero.

Events

Rebel forces under General Félix Díaz and Bernardo Reyes rose against Madero. Reyes was killed on the first day, but Díaz's troops occupied the Ciudadela fortress and exchanged artillery fire with loyalist forces under Victoriano Huerta, causing heavy civilian casualties. As the crisis deepened, Huerta secretly negotiated with Díaz — an arrangement brokered at the U.S. embassy in the so-called Pact of the Embassy. Huerta then arrested Madero and Vice President José María Pino Suárez, forced their resignations, and briefly took the presidency. Madero and Pino Suárez were murdered on 22 February 1913.

Aftermath

Huerta's coup provoked renewed revolutionary warfare led by Venustiano Carranza's Constitutionalist Army, and deepened the Revolution's violence.
