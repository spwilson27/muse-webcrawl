Hash browns, also spelled hashed browns, are a popular American breakfast food consisting of finely julienned or shredded potatoes that have been fried until golden brown and crisp, usually in oil on a griddle or in a pan.

History

Hash browns began appearing on breakfast menus in New York City in the 1890s, arising from the broader tradition of "hashed" potatoes — chopped leftover potatoes fried with onions. They became a staple of American diners and hotel breakfasts in the early 20th century, and after the Second World War frozen hash browns, sold as pre-formed patties or loose shreds, spread the dish to home kitchens and fast-food restaurants.

Preparation

Potatoes are peeled and shredded, diced, or riced, often rinsed and squeezed dry to remove starch and moisture, then seasoned and fried in a thin layer so that the exterior crisps while the interior stays soft. Variations include patties deep-fried like those served at fast-food outlets, home fries made from cubed potatoes, and versions mixed with onion, peppers, or cheese.

Related dishes

Similar potato preparations include the Swiss rösti, the British hash brown served in cooked breakfasts, latkes in Jewish cuisine, and the Spanish tortilla. In the American South, "scattered" hash browns topped with various ingredients are a signature item of the Waffle House chain.
