Reverse-transcriptase inhibitor

Reverse-transcriptase inhibitors (RTIs) are a class of antiretroviral drugs used to treat HIV infection or AIDS, and in some cases hepatitis B. RTIs inhibit activity of reverse transcriptase, a viral DNA polymerase that is required for replication of HIV and other retroviruses.

Classes

Nucleoside and nucleotide reverse-transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs and NtRTIs) are competitive substrate inhibitors: they mimic normal nucleosides and, once incorporated into the viral DNA chain, prevent the addition of further nucleotides, causing chain termination. Zidovudine (AZT) was the first approved antiretroviral drug, in 1987. Non-nucleoside reverse-transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) instead bind directly to an allosteric pocket of the enzyme and block its activity.

Use

RTIs form the backbone of combination antiretroviral therapy (highly active antiretroviral therapy, HAART), in which two NRTIs are commonly combined with a drug from another class.
