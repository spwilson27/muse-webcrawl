Aintree is a village and civil parish in the Metropolitan Borough of Sefton, Merseyside, England. It lies on the A59 road about six miles (ten kilometres) north-east of Liverpool city centre, historically in Lancashire. Aintree is known worldwide as the home of Aintree Racecourse, the venue of the Grand National steeplechase.

History

The name Aintree is recorded from the thirteenth century and is usually explained as Old English or Norse for "one tree" or "tree standing alone". The village was a small agricultural settlement until the nineteenth century, when transport improvements — the Leeds and Liverpool Canal passes nearby — and suburban growth from Liverpool transformed it.

Sport

Aintree Racecourse opened in 1829 and has hosted the Grand National since the race's official beginning in 1839; the meeting draws a global television audience of hundreds of millions. Famous fences include Becher's Brook, the Canal Turn, and The Chair. Aintree also hosted motor racing: the Aintree Circuit, built within the racecourse grounds, staged the British Grand Prix five times between 1955 and 1962, with winners including Stirling Moss and Jim Clark.

Transport

Aintree railway station, on the Northern Line of Merseyrail, serves the village and the racecourse, and the A59 and A580 roads connect it to Liverpool and the motorway network.
