Water dog (breed type)

Water dogs are a type of gundog bred to flush and retrieve game from water. The term covers several distinct breeds developed in maritime and wetland regions of Europe and North America, typically characterised by dense, curly or wavy weather-resistant coats, strong swimming ability, and an eagerness to work in water.

History

Water dogs were developed over centuries by fishermen and waterfowl hunters. The Portuguese Water Dog worked aboard fishing boats, herding fish into nets and retrieving lost tackle, while the Irish Water Spaniel and the now-extinct English Water Spaniel served wildfowlers in marshes. The barbet of France is among the oldest water dog types and contributed to the development of the poodle, which was itself originally a water retriever (its name derives from the German pudeln, "to splash").

Breeds

Breeds commonly classed as water dogs include the Portuguese Water Dog, Spanish Water Dog, Irish Water Spaniel, American Water Spaniel, Boykin Spaniel, Lagotto Romagnolo, Barbet, Wetterhoun, and the standard poodle in its original working role. Many retrievers, such as the Labrador Retriever and Curly-coated Retriever, share water-working traits but are classified as retrievers rather than water spaniels or water dogs proper.

Characteristics

Water dogs are generally medium-sized, intelligent, and trainable, with waterproof double or single curly coats that reduce drag and protect against cold water. They are known for strong retrieving instincts and, in modern times, are kept largely as companions and sporting dogs.
