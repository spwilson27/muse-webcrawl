The Ghan is an Australian passenger train service operated by Journey Beyond between Adelaide, Alice Springs and Darwin. The 2,979-kilometre journey through the centre of the continent takes about 54 hours, including extended stops for off-train excursions. The name is shortened from "The Afghan Express", honouring the Afghan cameleers who pioneered transport routes through the outback in the 19th century.

History

The original Ghan began running in 1929 over the narrow-gauge Central Australia Railway from Port Augusta to Alice Springs, a line notorious for delays caused by washouts and sand drifts; the entire return journey could take weeks. A new standard-gauge line built to the west, via Tarcoola, opened in 1980, retiring the old route. The final link north from Alice Springs to Darwin was completed in 2004, fulfilling a century-old ambition for a transcontinental railway from south to north.

Service

The train typically consists of stainless-steel carriages built by Comeng, hauled by Pacific National locomotives, and includes Platinum and Gold class sleeper cabins, lounge cars and the Queen Adelaide Restaurant car. Seasonal "Ghan Expedition" services run for four days with additional stops such as Coober Pedy and Katherine Gorge. The Ghan is considered one of the world's great rail journeys and a sister service to the Indian Pacific.
