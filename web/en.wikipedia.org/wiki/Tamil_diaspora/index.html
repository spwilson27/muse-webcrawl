Tamil diaspora

The Tamil diaspora refers to the descendants of Tamil emigrants from southern India and northern Sri Lanka who settled in other parts of the world. Significant Tamil-speaking populations now live in Malaysia, Singapore, South Africa, Mauritius, Réunion, Fiji, Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, and, following later waves of migration, in Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Australia and the United States.

Colonial-era migration

The largest early movements came under British and French colonial labour systems in the 19th and early 20th centuries, when Tamil workers were recruited — often as indentured labourers — for sugar, tea, coffee and rubber plantations in Malaya, Ceylon, Natal, Mauritius, Fiji and the Caribbean. These communities retained Tamil language, Hindu temple traditions and festivals such as Thaipusam, which remains a major public celebration in Malaysia and Singapore.

Sri Lankan Tamil diaspora

A distinct wave followed the Sri Lankan civil war (1983–2009), when hundreds of thousands of Sri Lankan Tamils sought refuge abroad. Large communities formed in Toronto — home to one of the largest Tamil populations outside South Asia — as well as in London, Paris and other European and Australian cities. This diaspora has been politically active on human rights issues relating to Sri Lanka.

Culture

Diaspora communities maintain Tamil schools, temples, media and film culture, and observe the Tamil calendar festivals such as Pongal and Puthandu. In several countries, January is celebrated as Tamil Heritage Month.
