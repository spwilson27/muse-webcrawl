Genetic genealogy is the use of genealogical DNA tests — DNA-based genetic testing — combined with traditional genealogical methods to infer genetic relationships between individuals, find undiscovered relatives, and test hypotheses about family history and origins.

Types of test

Three kinds of DNA are used. Autosomal DNA tests survey hundreds of thousands of single-nucleotide polymorphisms across the chromosomes inherited from both parents, revealing relatives within roughly the last five to seven generations and giving broad ethnic-composition estimates. Y-DNA tests follow the paternal line (fathers pass the Y chromosome to sons nearly unchanged) and are used in surname projects to trace direct male-line ancestry over many centuries. Mitochondrial DNA tests trace the direct maternal line, since mtDNA is passed from mother to children.

Methods and applications

Genetic genealogists compare shared DNA measured in centimorgans, triangulate segments among matches, and build out matches' family trees to locate most recent common ancestors. The tools have been used to break through adoption and unknown-parentage cases, to test surname and clan traditions, and by law enforcement — most famously in the 2018 identification of the Golden State Killer through relatives' profiles uploaded to GEDmatch, a technique termed forensic genetic genealogy.

Privacy and ethics

The field raises significant privacy questions: testing one person reveals information about many relatives, unexpected parentage discoveries are common, and databases can be accessed by police or breached by hackers. Major testing companies differ in their law-enforcement access and opt-in policies.
