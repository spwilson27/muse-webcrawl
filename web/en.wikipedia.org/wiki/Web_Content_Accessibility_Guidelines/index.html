Web Content Accessibility Guidelines

AbbreviationWCAG
PublisherWorld Wide Web Consortium (W3C), Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI)
VersionsWCAG 1.0 (1999); 2.0 (2008); 2.1 (2018); 2.2 (2023)
PrinciplesPerceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust (POUR)
Conformance levelsA, AA, AAA

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are part of a series of web accessibility guidelines published by the Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI) of the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), the main international standards organisation for the Internet. They are a set of recommendations for making web content more accessible, primarily for people with disabilities — including blindness and low vision, deafness, limited movement, speech disabilities and cognitive limitations — but also for a wider range of devices and situations, such as mobile phones and slow connections.

Versions

WCAG 1.0 became a W3C Recommendation in May 1999, organised around 14 guidelines with checkpoints at three priority levels. WCAG 2.0, published in December 2008, restructured the guidelines around four principles — content must be perceivable, interface components operable, information understandable, and content robust enough for assistive technologies — with testable success criteria at levels A, AA and AAA. WCAG 2.0 was also adopted as ISO/IEC 40500. WCAG 2.1 (June 2018) added 17 criteria addressing mobile accessibility, low vision and cognitive disabilities. WCAG 2.2, published in October 2023, added nine further criteria, including focus visibility and appearance, accessible authentication and dragging alternatives. A future WCAG 3.0 is under development with a different conformance model.

Legal status

WCAG is incorporated by reference into accessibility law and regulation in many jurisdictions: the refreshed U.S. Section 508 standards require WCAG 2.0 A/AA; the European standard EN 301 549 and the European Accessibility Act reference WCAG 2.1 AA; and courts in the United States have treated the guidelines as the benchmark in Americans with Disabilities Act web-accessibility litigation. Level AA is the most commonly required conformance target.
