Saint Timothy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Timothy]
Timothy, bishop of Ephesus

Timothy (Greek: Τιμόθεος, Timótheos, meaning "honouring God" or "honoured by God"; c. 17 – c. 97) was an early Christian evangelist and the first bishop of Ephesus, best known as the companion and co-worker of the Apostle Paul and the recipient of the two Epistles to Timothy in the New Testament. He is venerated as a saint and apostle in the Eastern Orthodox, Roman Catholic, and Lutheran traditions. His feast day is 26 January (Roman Catholic) and 22 January (Orthodox, with Barnabas and Titus).

Life

Timothy was born in Lystra in Lycaonia, the son of a Jewish mother, Eunice, and a Greek father; his grandmother Lois is also named in scripture for her faith. Paul met him during his second missionary journey (c. 50–52 AD) and took him as a companion, circumcising him to avoid offence among the Jews of the region. Timothy travelled with Paul through Macedonia and Greece, co-sent several of Paul's letters (including 2 Corinthians, Philippians, Colossians, and both Thessalonian letters), and was entrusted with missions to Thessalonica, Corinth, and Ephesus.

Epistles and later tradition

The First and Second Epistles to Timothy, along with Titus, form the "Pastoral Epistles," addressing church order and ministry; tradition ascribes them to Paul, though many modern scholars regard them as written by a later follower. According to later tradition, Timothy became the first bishop of Ephesus and was martyred around 97 AD, stoned to death by pagans while attempting to stop a procession in honour of Artemis. His relics were translated to Constantinople in the 4th century.
