Headliner (performances)

A headliner is the main attraction or most prominent performer at a concert, music festival, comedy show or other live performance event. The headliner typically performs last, receives top billing in advertising, and is the act expected to draw the largest audience.

Role

At music festivals, headliners close each day's programme on the main stage and are printed largest on posters and line-up announcements. Supporting acts performing earlier are often called openers or support acts. Headliners command the highest fees and may influence ticket sales for the whole event.

Billing disputes

The order and prominence of billing can be contentious; co-headlining arrangements, in which two acts share top billing and may alternate closing, are common on major tours. In stand-up comedy, the headliner performs the longest final set, following the opener and the feature act.

History

The term derives from newspaper headlines and vaudeville posters, where the star act's name appeared in the largest type at the top of the bill.
