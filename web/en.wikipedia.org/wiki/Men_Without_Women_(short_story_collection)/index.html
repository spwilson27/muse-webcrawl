Men Without Women is the second collection of short stories by American author Ernest Hemingway, published by Charles Scribner's Sons on 11 October 1927. The volume contains fourteen stories, ten of which had previously appeared in magazines. As the title suggests, the stories deal predominantly with male worlds — bullfighting, boxing, soldiering, fishing — and with stoicism, injury and loss, in the terse "iceberg" prose style Hemingway was developing.

Contents

  1. "The Undefeated" — an ageing bullfighter, Manuel Garcia, insists on one more fight.
  2. "In Another Country" — a wounded American officer undergoes rehabilitation in Milan during the First World War.
  3. "Hills Like White Elephants" — a couple at a Spanish railway station discuss an unnamed operation, widely read as abortion.
  4. "The Killers" — two hitmen wait in a diner for a former boxer who does not flee.
  5. "Che Ti Dice La Patria?" — a journey through Fascist Italy.
  6. "Fifty Grand" — a boxer bets against himself.
  7. "A Simple Enquiry"
  8. "Ten Indians" — a Nick Adams story.
  9. "The Light of the World"
  10. "God Rest You Merry, Gentlemen"
  11. "A Pursuit Race"
  12. "Today Is Friday" — Roman soldiers after the Crucifixion.
  13. "Banal Story"
  14. "Now I Lay Me" — a Nick Adams story of insomnia and war wounds.

Themes and reception

The collection is noted for stories such as "Hills Like White Elephants" and "The Killers", now among the most anthologised in American literature, which advance almost entirely through dialogue and understatement. Contemporary reviews were mixed but generally recognised Hemingway's growing mastery; the collection consolidated his reputation following the success of The Sun Also Rises (1926). Several stories feature his semi-autobiographical protagonist Nick Adams.
