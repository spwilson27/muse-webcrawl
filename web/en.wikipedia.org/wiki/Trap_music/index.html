Trap is a subgenre of hip hop music that originated in the Southern United States, particularly Atlanta, in the 1990s and 2000s. The term "trap" originally referred to places where drug deals took place, and the genre's lyrics frequently deal with street life, drug culture and survival in impoverished neighbourhoods.

Characteristics

Trap production is defined by its use of the Roland TR-808 drum machine: booming, pitched sub-bass kick drums, crisp snares on the backbeat, and rapid hi-hat patterns full of rolls, triplets and subdivisions played with varied pitch and velocity. Melodies tend to be sparse, dark and minor-key, built from synthesiser strings, bells, pianos or orchestral samples. Tempos are typically around 130–150 beats per minute in double-time feel (effectively 65–75 bpm for the drum pattern).

History

Early trap can be heard in the work of Memphis and Houston artists in the 1990s, but the style crystallised in Atlanta in the early 2000s with albums such as T.I.'s Trap Muzik (2003) and releases by Young Jeezy and Gucci Mane. Producers including Shawty Redd, Drumma Boy, Zaytoven and later Lex Luger codified the sound. Luger's dramatic, orchestral production on Waka Flocka Flame's Flockaveli (2010) proved hugely influential, and by the mid-2010s trap had become the dominant sound of mainstream hip hop and pop, carried by artists such as Future, Migos, 21 Savage, Travis Scott and Cardi B.

Subgenres and influence

Trap has spawned numerous offshoots: EDM trap adapted its drums to festival electronic music; drill (from Chicago, later UK and Brooklyn variants) darkened its beats and lyricism; Latin trap carried it into Spanish-language markets; and phonk revived Memphis samples over trap drums. Its hi-hat programming and 808 patterns are now ubiquitous across pop, R&B and electronic music.
