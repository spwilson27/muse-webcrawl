Laplace operator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Laplace operator or Laplacian is a differential operator given by the divergence of the gradient of a scalar function on Euclidean space. It is usually denoted by the symbols ∇·∇, ∇², or Δ. In the Cartesian coordinate system the Laplacian of a function f is the sum of the unmixed second partial derivatives of f:

Δf = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² + ∂²f/∂z²

The operator is named after the French mathematician Pierre-Simon Laplace, who developed its use in celestial mechanics and potential theory in the 1780s.

Definition and properties

The Laplacian Δf(p) at a point p measures the extent to which the average value of f over small spheres (or circles) around p deviates from f(p): it is, up to a constant factor, the rate at which that average difference vanishes as the radius shrinks. Functions with Δf = 0 everywhere are called harmonic functions. The Laplacian is linear, invariant under rotations and translations of Euclidean space, and self-adjoint on suitable function spaces; it is a special case of a second-order elliptic operator.

Applications

The Laplacian appears throughout mathematical physics. Laplace's equation Δf = 0 governs gravitational and electrostatic potentials in free space; Poisson's equation Δf = g generalises it in the presence of sources. It is the spatial part of the wave equation, the heat equation (∂u/∂t = αΔu), and the Schrödinger equation of quantum mechanics. In fluid mechanics it appears in the Navier–Stokes equations, and in image processing discrete Laplacians are used for edge detection and sharpening.

Coordinate expressions and generalisations

Closed-form expressions exist in polar, cylindrical, and spherical coordinates, where the spherical form includes angular parts related to the spherical harmonics. On Riemannian manifolds the generalisation is the Laplace–Beltrami operator; for vector fields the vector Laplacian is defined componentwise in Cartesian coordinates. Discrete analogues on graphs — the graph Laplacian — underpin spectral graph theory, clustering, and modern work on large-scale computation and data analysis.
