Inlays and onlays

Inlays and onlays are indirect dental restorations fabricated outside the mouth — typically in a dental laboratory or by chairside CAD/CAM milling — and then cemented onto a prepared tooth. An inlay fits within the cusps of the tooth, inside the prepared cavity, while an onlay extends over one or more cusps, covering part of the biting surface. They offer a middle option between a direct filling and a full crown.

Materials and procedure

Inlays and onlays are made from porcelain or other ceramics, composite resin, or gold alloys. Gold is exceptionally durable but conspicuous; ceramic versions match tooth colour and are widely preferred for visible teeth. Traditionally the procedure required two visits — one for preparation and impressions, another for cementation — but same-day CAD/CAM systems such as CEREC allow design and milling in a single appointment.

Indications

These restorations are used when a tooth is too damaged for a simple filling but retains enough healthy structure that a crown, which removes more tooth, is unnecessary. Because they are made outside the mouth, they can achieve better contours and marginal fit than large direct fillings, and they strengthen the remaining tooth structure. Costs are typically higher than fillings and comparable to crowns.
