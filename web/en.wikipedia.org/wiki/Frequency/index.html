Frequency

Frequency
Common symbolsf, ν
SI unithertz (Hz)
Derivationsf = 1 / T

Frequency (symbol f) is the number of occurrences of a repeating event per unit of time. It is also occasionally referred to as temporal frequency for clarity and to distinguish it from spatial frequency. Ordinary frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), the number of cycles per second. The corresponding period is the time duration of one cycle in a repeating event, so the period is the reciprocal of the frequency: T = 1/f.

For example, if a heart beats at a frequency of 120 times a minute (2 hertz), the period T — the interval at which the beats repeat — is half a second (60 seconds divided by 120 beats).

Definitions

For periodic waves in nondispersive media, frequency has an inverse relationship to the wavelength λ. When waves travel from one medium to another, their frequency remains the same — only their wavelength and speed change. The relation is f = v / λ, where v is the phase velocity of the wave.

Measurement

Frequency can be measured by counting the number of occurrences of an event within a fixed time interval and dividing. For higher frequencies, devices such as the stroboscope and the frequency counter are used. The frequency of a rotating or oscillating object is often expressed in revolutions per minute (rpm) for rotational frequency.

Examples

The frequency of the A above middle C used as a tuning reference is conventionally 440 Hz. Human hearing ranges from roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Alternating current electrical systems operate at 50 or 60 Hz depending on the country. Radio waves span frequencies from about 30 Hz to 300 GHz.
