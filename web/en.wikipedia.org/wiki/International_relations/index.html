International relations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

International relations (IR) is the study of the political, economic and social interaction among sovereign states, international organisations, multinational corporations and other transnational actors. As an academic discipline it emerged after the First World War — the first chair was founded at Aberystwyth in 1919 — and draws on political science, history, economics, law and sociology.

Theories

The field is organised by competing paradigms. Realism (Morgenthau, Waltz) centres power, anarchy and self-help; liberalism stresses institutions, interdependence, trade and democracy's pacific tendencies; constructivism (Wendt: "anarchy is what states make of it") foregrounds identities and norms; and Marxist, feminist, postcolonial and green theories interrogate the field's assumptions and whose security and knowledge count. Foreign policy analysis, international political economy, security studies and global governance are major subfields; methods range from archival case studies to game theory, statistics and experiments.

Subject matter

IR examines war and peace, alliances, international law and organisations, trade and finance regimes, migration, climate governance and the shifting distribution of power — notably debates about US primacy, China's rise and multipolarity in the 21st century.
