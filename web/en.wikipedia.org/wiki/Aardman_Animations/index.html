Aardman Animations Limited is a British animation studio based in Bristol, England, best known for films and series made in stop motion with Plasticine clay models. Its best-known creations are Wallace and Gromit, Shaun the Sheep, and Morph, and its features include Chicken Run (2000), the highest-grossing stop-motion film of its era.

History

School friends Peter Lord and David Sproxton founded Aardman in 1972, taking the name — so the story goes — from a character in a cartoon they sold to the BBC, choosing it partly so it would appear early in alphabetical listings. In the 1970s they created Morph, a small clay character appearing in the children's programme Take Hart. Animator Nick Park joined in 1985; his student film A Grand Day Out, begun at the National Film and Television School and completed at Aardman, introduced Wallace and Gromit, while his Creature Comforts (1989) won the studio its first Academy Award.

Films and series

Aardman's Wallace & Gromit shorts, including The Wrong Trousers (1993) and A Close Shave (1995), won Academy Awards, as did the feature The Curse of the Were-Rabbit (2005). Other works include Chicken Run and its 2023 sequel, Shaun the Sheep (TV series and films), Flushed Away and Arthur Christmas (CGI), The Pirates! In an Adventure with Scientists!, and Early Man. In 2018 Lord and Sproxton transferred majority ownership to the studio's employees.
