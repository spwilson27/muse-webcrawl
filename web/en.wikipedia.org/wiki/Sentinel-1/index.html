Sentinel-1

Sentinel-1 is a pair of Earth observation satellites in the European Space Agency's Copernicus Programme, carrying a C-band synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) instrument that images the Earth's surface in all weather, day and night. The first satellite, Sentinel-1A, was launched on 3 April 2014, and Sentinel-1B followed on 25 April 2016, both on Soyuz rockets from Kourou, French Guiana.

Mission

The satellites fly in the same orbital plane 180° apart, together giving a six-day repeat cycle at the equator. Radar interferometry (InSAR) with Sentinel-1 data allows millimetre-to-centimetre-scale measurement of ground deformation from earthquakes, volcanoes, landslides, and subsidence. Other applications include monitoring sea ice and the Arctic environment, maritime surveillance and oil-spill detection, mapping of floods and other natural disasters in support of emergency response, forest and agriculture monitoring, and tracking land-use change.

Satellites

Each spacecraft carries a 12-metre SAR antenna and an Optical Communication Payload for laser relay of data via the European Data Relay System. Sentinel-1B suffered a power-system anomaly in December 2021 and its mission was officially ended in August 2022; a replacement, Sentinel-1C, was launched on 5 December 2024 on a Vega-C rocket, and Sentinel-1D is planned to complete the replenished constellation. Data from the mission are free and open under the Copernicus data policy.
