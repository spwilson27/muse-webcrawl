Momotarō (桃太郎, "Peach Boy") is a popular hero of Japanese folklore. In the best-known version of the tale, Momotarō was born from a giant peach that an elderly, childless couple found floating down a river. The old woman had been washing clothes when she spotted the peach; when the couple cut it open, a healthy baby boy emerged, sent by the heavens in answer to their prayers.

The tale

Momotarō grew up strong and kind. Hearing that a band of oni (ogres or demons) living on the island of Onigashima were plundering nearby villages, he set out to defeat them. His foster mother gave him kibi dango (millet dumplings) for the journey. Along the way, Momotarō shared the dumplings with a dog, a monkey, and a pheasant, who each joined his expedition.

Reaching Onigashima, the companions fought the oni chief and his band; with the animals' help Momotarō prevailed. The oni surrendered, promised to do no more harm, and gave up their treasure, which Momotarō carried home on a cart. He and his parents lived happily on the riches, and the hero became a model of filial piety and courage.

Origins and variants

The tale circulated widely in the Edo period and was standardized during the Meiji era, when it appeared in national textbooks. Regional variants differ in detail: in some, Momotarō is not born from the peach but is given to the couple by eating it, restoring their youth. The story is strongly associated with Okayama, where Kibitsuhiko Shrine and local peaches feature in the local telling.

Cultural impact

Momotarō became a symbol of Japan itself, used in wartime propaganda — most famously in the animated film Momotaro: Sacred Sailors (1945). He remains ubiquitous in Japanese popular culture, advertising, railways, and children's media, and is the mascot figure of Okayama Prefecture.
