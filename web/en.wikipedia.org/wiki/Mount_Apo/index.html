Mount Apo is the highest mountain in the Philippines, standing 2,954 metres (9,692 ft) above sea level. A potentially active stratovolcano, it straddles the boundary between Davao del Sur and Cotabato provinces on the island of Mindanao, overlooking Davao City.

The mountain is the centrepiece of Mount Apo Natural Park, a protected area of about 54,974 hectares. Its forests are among the most biologically rich in the Philippines, home to the critically endangered Philippine eagle, as well as hundreds of other bird species, endemic mammals, and the waling-waling orchid.

Geology and activity

Apo is a basaltic-to-andesitic stratovolcano with three peaks and a broad summit crater containing a small lake. Its volcanic history is poorly documented and no eruptions are recorded in historical times, but hot springs, solfataras, and fumaroles on its flanks indicate an active hydrothermal system. Geothermal wells on the north-western flank supply power to the Mindanao grid.

Climbing

Apo is one of the Philippines' most popular mountaineering destinations. Several trails reach the summit, the best known starting from Kidapawan City and passing Lake Venado. The mountain is sacred to the indigenous Lumad peoples, including the Bagobo and Manobo, and climbers are asked to observe local customs and permit rules set by the Protected Area Management Board.
