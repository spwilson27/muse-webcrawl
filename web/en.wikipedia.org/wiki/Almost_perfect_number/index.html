In mathematics, an almost perfect number (sometimes also called a slightly defective number) is a natural number n such that the sum of all divisors of n (the sum-of-divisors function σ(n)) is equal to 2n − 1. Almost perfect numbers are therefore a slight variant of perfect numbers, for which σ(n) = 2n.

Known examples

The only known almost perfect numbers are the powers of 2, that is, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and so on. This can be verified directly: for n = 2k, the sum of the divisors is 1 + 2 + 4 + … + 2k = 2k+1 − 1 = 2n − 1.

Open problems

It has not been proved that all almost perfect numbers are powers of 2. However, substantial restrictions on any counterexample are known: an almost perfect number that is not a power of 2 must be a square number greater than 1035 and must have at least three distinct prime factors. Whether any such number exists remains an open question in number theory.

Almost perfect numbers are a special case of quasiperfect numbers, natural numbers n for which σ(n) = 2n + 1; no quasiperfect number is known at all.
