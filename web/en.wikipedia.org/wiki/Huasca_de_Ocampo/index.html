Huasca de Ocampo

The Basaltic Prisms of Santa María Regla, near Huasca.

Huasca de Ocampo is a town and municipality in the Mexican state of Hidalgo, located about 34 kilometres from the state capital, Pachuca. The town was the first in Mexico to be designated a Pueblo Mágico ("Magical Town") under the federal tourism programme, in 2001. Its name derives from Nahuatl Huascazaloya, "place of joy or rejoicing"; the suffix honours the liberal politician Melchor Ocampo.

History

The area's prosperity grew from silver mining in the surrounding Comarca Minera. In the 18th century the Conde de Regla, Pedro Romero de Terreros — one of the richest men in New Spain — built great smelting haciendas here, including Santa María Regla and San Miguel Regla, to process ore from Real del Monte. Legends of duendes (goblins or elves) are a strong local tradition, and the town has a museum devoted to them.

Attractions

Huasca is best known for the Basaltic Prisms (Prismas Basálticos) at Santa María Regla: tall geometric columns of basalt formed by the slow cooling of lava, flanking a ravine and waterfall — a formation admired by the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, who sketched it in 1803. Other attractions include the former mining haciendas, the Peña del Aire viewpoint over a deep canyon, trout farms, and forests used for ecotourism.
