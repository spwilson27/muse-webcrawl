War in Amhara

The War in Amhara is an armed conflict in Ethiopia's Amhara Region between the Ethiopian federal government and the Fano militia, an ethno-nationalist armed movement drawn largely from Amhara regional forces and local militias. The fighting began in 2023, after the federal government moved to disband regional special forces following the end of the Tigray War, and escalated into a full insurgency. The Ethiopian government declared a state of emergency in the region in August 2023.

Background

During the Tigray War (2020–2022), Amhara regional forces and Fano militias fought alongside the Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF) against the Tigray People's Liberation Front, and Amhara forces took control of disputed territories including Welkait and Raya. After the Pretoria Agreement ended the Tigray War in November 2022, relations between the federal government of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed and its former Amhara allies deteriorated, particularly over the disputed territories and the April 2023 federal decision to integrate regional special forces into the federal army and police. Amhara units resisted disarmament, and clashes erupted across the region in mid-2023.

Course of the war

In late July and August 2023, Fano fighters seized control of several towns, including Lalibela and parts of Gondar and Debre Tabor, and fighting reached the regional capital, Bahir Dar. The federal government declared a six-month state of emergency on 4 August 2023 and deployed the ENDF, which retook the major towns, though Fano retained control of much of the countryside and continued guerrilla operations. The conflict has featured drone strikes by federal forces, repeated offensives, and heavy fighting around cities such as Gondar and Bahir Dar in 2024 and 2025.

Humanitarian impact

The war has caused large numbers of civilian casualties and displaced hundreds of thousands of people. Human rights organisations have documented extrajudicial killings, drone strikes hitting civilian gatherings, mass arrests under the state of emergency, and prolonged shutdowns of internet and telecommunications in the region. Aid delivery and schooling have been severely disrupted across Amhara.
