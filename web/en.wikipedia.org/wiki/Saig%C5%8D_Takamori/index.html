Saigō Takamori

Saigō Takamori (西郷 隆盛)
Born: 23 January 1828, Kagoshima, Satsuma Domain
Died: 24 September 1877, Kagoshima
Allegiance: Satsuma Domain; Empire of Japan
Battles: Boshin War; Satsuma Rebellion

Saigō Takamori (西郷 隆盛, January 23, 1828 – September 24, 1877) was a Japanese samurai and statesman, one of the most influential figures of the Meiji Restoration. He commanded imperial forces in the Boshin War and became a senior leader of the new Meiji government, but resigned in 1873 after losing the debate over invading Korea (Seikanron).

Satsuma Rebellion

In 1877, disgruntled samurai of Satsuma rose against the government in the Satsuma Rebellion, persuading the reluctant Saigō to lead them. Despite early successes, the rebels were defeated by the numerically superior conscript Imperial Army. Saigō was mortally wounded at the Battle of Shiroyama and died there; legend holds he was carried away and died with honour rather than be captured.

Legacy

Saigō was pardoned posthumously in 1891 and is remembered as the archetypal loyal samurai — often called "the last true samurai". A famous bronze statue of him stands in Ueno Park, Tokyo.
