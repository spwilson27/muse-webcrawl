Northern Expedition

The Northern Expedition (Chinese: 北伐; pinyin: Běifá) was a military campaign launched by the National Revolutionary Army (NRA) of the Kuomintang (KMT) against the Beiyang government and regional warlords in northern China between 1926 and 1928. Its goal was the reunification of China, which had fragmented into warlord cliques following the death of Yuan Shikai in 1916.

Background

After the death of Sun Yat-sen in 1925, Chiang Kai-shek emerged as commander of the NRA, trained at the Whampoa Military Academy with Soviet assistance. The campaign was conducted during the First United Front between the KMT and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), with Soviet advisers such as Vasily Blyukher (Galen) contributing to planning.

First phase (1926–1927)

The expedition began in July 1926 from Guangdong province. The NRA rapidly defeated the forces of Wu Peifu in Hunan and Hubei, capturing Wuhan, and then turned east against Sun Chuanfang, taking Nanchang, Shanghai and Nanjing by the spring of 1927. In April 1927, Chiang purged communists and labour activists in the Shanghai Massacre, splitting the United Front and establishing a rival national government in Nanjing to the leftist KMT government in Wuhan, which itself expelled the communists months later.

Second phase and conclusion (1928)

After consolidating his position and defeating internal KMT rivals, Chiang resumed the offensive in April 1928 against the Fengtian clique of Zhang Zuolin. Following Zhang's assassination by Japanese officers in the Huanggutun incident, his son Zhang Xueliang pledged allegiance to the Nanjing government in December 1928 — the Northeast Flag Replacement — formally completing the reunification of China under the Nationalist government and ending the Warlord Era. Nominal unification nevertheless left powerful regional militarists semi-autonomous, setting the stage for the Central Plains War of 1930.
