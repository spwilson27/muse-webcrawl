James Francis Cauty (born 19 December 1956) is an English musician and artist, best known as one half, with Bill Drummond, of the electronic music group the KLF. Notorious for their situationist pranks as much as their early-1990s stadium house hits, Cauty and Drummond deleted the KLF's catalogue at the peak of its success and, as the K Foundation, burned one million pounds in cash on the island of Jura in 1994.

Early career

Cauty grew up on the Wirral and in Devon, where as a teenager he drew the celebrated 1970s poster of Gandalf and Middle-earth characters for the retailer Athena. He played guitar in the band Brilliant in the early 1980s before working with Drummond, first as the Justified Ancients of Mu Mu and from 1988 as the KLF.

The KLF and the K Foundation

The KLF scored international hits with "What Time Is Love?", "3 a.m. Eternal" and "Justified & Ancient" and were briefly among the world's biggest-selling singles acts, before quitting the music business in 1992 after a deliberately chaotic performance at the BRIT Awards. As the K Foundation they awarded £40,000 to the "worst artist" of the Turner Prize shortlist and then burned £1 million — the bulk of their remaining earnings — a filmed act that remains one of the most notorious gestures in British art. Cauty was also a founding member of ambient house pioneers the Orb.

Later work

Cauty has since worked as a visual artist, producing provocative works including miniature riot scenes and the dystopian model village Aftermath Dislocation Principle, and as a member of the band Blacksmoke. In 2021 the KLF's music returned to streaming platforms and shops.
