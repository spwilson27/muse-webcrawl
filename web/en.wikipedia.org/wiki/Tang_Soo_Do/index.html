Tang Soo Do

Tang Soo Do (Hangul: 당수도) is a Korean martial art incorporating fighting principles from subak and shotokan karate. Its name, "the way of the Tang hand", reflects its claimed roots in Tang-dynasty China by way of Okinawan and Japanese karate.

History

The art was systematised by Hwang Kee, who founded the Moo Duk Kwan in Seoul in 1945 after studying martial arts in Manchuria and Korea. When the Korean government promoted unification of the kwans into taekwondo in the 1950s–60s, Hwang Kee resisted absorption, and Tang Soo Do survived as a distinct tradition, spreading internationally — notably to the United States, where Chuck Norris was among its famous practitioners and popularisers.

Practice

Training combines forms (hyung), one-step sparring, free sparring, breaking, and self-defence techniques, with characteristic high kicks and hand strikes derived from karate. Practitioners wear a dobok, often trimmed in midnight blue or green, and progress through gup grades to dan ranks.
