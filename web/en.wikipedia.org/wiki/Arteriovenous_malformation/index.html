Arteriovenous malformation

SpecialtyNeurosurgery, interventional radiology
SymptomsSeizures, headache, haemorrhage, neurological deficit
TreatmentSurgery, embolisation, radiosurgery, observation

An arteriovenous malformation (AVM) is an abnormal tangle of blood vessels (a nidus) in which arteries connect directly to veins without an intervening capillary bed. AVMs are usually congenital and can occur anywhere in the body, but cerebral AVMs are of greatest clinical concern because of the risk of intracranial haemorrhage.

Signs and symptoms

Many AVMs are asymptomatic and found incidentally. Symptomatic cerebral AVMs may present with seizures, headaches, focal neurological deficits, or sudden haemorrhage, which can be life-threatening. The annual rupture risk of an unruptured AVM is often estimated at roughly 2–4 percent, higher after a previous bleed.

Diagnosis and grading

Diagnosis uses CT, MRI and catheter angiography, the gold standard. Brain AVMs are commonly graded with the Spetzler–Martin scale, which considers size, eloquence of adjacent brain and venous drainage, guiding treatment decisions.

Treatment

Options include microsurgical resection, endovascular embolisation, stereotactic radiosurgery (such as Gamma Knife), or conservative monitoring, often in combination. The ARUBA trial suggested that medical management alone may be preferable for some unruptured AVMs, a conclusion that remains debated.
