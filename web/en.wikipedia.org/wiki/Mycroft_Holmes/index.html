Mycroft Holmes is a fictional character appearing in stories written by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. He is the elder brother (by seven years) of the celebrated detective Sherlock Holmes. Possessing deductive powers exceeding even those of his brother, Mycroft is nonetheless incapable of performing detective work similar to Sherlock's due to his laziness and lack of interest in physical effort.

Character

Mycroft holds a vague but highly influential position in the British government. Sherlock describes him by saying that "occasionally he is the British government" — Mycroft serves as a kind of human database and clearing-house for interdepartmental intelligence, memorising the details of every policy question so that ministers can consult him.

A founding member of the Diogenes Club, a gentlemen's club where members are forbidden to speak, Mycroft appears or is mentioned in four stories: "The Greek Interpreter", "The Final Problem", "The Adventure of the Empty House", and "The Adventure of the Bruce-Partington Plans", in which he plays an unusually active role.

In adaptations

Mycroft has been portrayed on screen by numerous actors, including Christopher Lee, Richard E. Grant, Stephen Fry in Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows (2011), and Mark Gatiss in the BBC series Sherlock, in which his role in the intelligence services is greatly expanded.
