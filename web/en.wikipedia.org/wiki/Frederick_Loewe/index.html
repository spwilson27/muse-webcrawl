Frederick Loewe (born Fritz Löwe; June 10, 1901 – February 14, 1988) was an Austrian-American composer. He collaborated with lyricist Alan Jay Lerner on a series of Broadway musicals, including Brigadoon, Paint Your Wagon, My Fair Lady and Camelot, and the film Gigi, all noted for their lush, operetta-inflected melodies.

Early life

Loewe was born in Berlin to Viennese parents; his father Edmund Löwe was a celebrated operetta tenor who starred in The Merry Widow. A child prodigy, Fritz studied piano at the Stern Conservatory, composed songs as a boy, and at thirteen was the youngest piano soloist ever to appear with the Berlin Philharmonic. He emigrated to the United States in 1924 and worked at odd jobs — cowboy, boxer, gold prospector and silent-film accompanist — before finding his way to Broadway.

Partnership with Lerner

Loewe met Alan Jay Lerner at the Lambs Club in 1942. Their breakthrough was Brigadoon (1947), followed by Paint Your Wagon (1951). My Fair Lady (1956), adapted from Shaw's Pygmalion, was a phenomenon — running 2,717 performances on Broadway and winning multiple Tony Awards; songs such as "I Could Have Danced All Night" and "On the Street Where You Live" became standards. The film Gigi (1958) won nine Academy Awards, and Camelot (1960) became indelibly associated with the Kennedy era. The partnership strained and effectively ended after Camelot, reuniting only for the stage version of Gigi (1973) and the film The Little Prince (1974).

Later life

Loewe retired wealthy to Palm Springs, California, where he died in 1988. He was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1972, and the Frederick Loewe Foundation continues his philanthropy.
