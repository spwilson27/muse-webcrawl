McGregor, the UFC's first simultaneous two-division champion

Conor Anthony McGregor (born 14 July 1988) is an Irish professional mixed martial artist. He is a former Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) Featherweight and Lightweight Champion, becoming the first fighter to hold UFC championships in two weight classes simultaneously. He is also a former Cage Warriors featherweight and lightweight champion. McGregor's drawing power has made him the biggest pay-per-view star in MMA history; his fights headline most of the best-selling UFC events of all time.

Career

McGregor began his professional MMA career in 2008 in Dublin, training under John Kavanagh at Straight Blast Gym Ireland. After winning Cage Warriors titles in two divisions, he signed with the UFC in 2013. In December 2015 he knocked out longtime champion José Aldo in 13 seconds at UFC 194 — the fastest title-fight finish in UFC history — to unify the featherweight championship. After two famous bouts with Nate Diaz in 2016, he defeated Eddie Alvarez at UFC 205 in November 2016 to win the lightweight title and become the UFC's first simultaneous two-division champion.

In August 2017 McGregor crossed over to professional boxing, losing by TKO in the tenth round to Floyd Mayweather Jr. in one of the highest-grossing fights in history. He returned to the UFC in October 2018, losing a lightweight title fight to Khabib Nurmagomedov at UFC 229, the best-selling MMA event of all time. A comeback win over Donald Cerrone in January 2020 was followed by two losses to Dustin Poirier in 2021, the second ending with a broken leg. Repeatedly announced returns have since been delayed by injury.

Business and personal life

Outside fighting, McGregor founded the Proper No. Twelve Irish whiskey brand, selling a majority stake in a deal valuing it at up to $600 million, and has topped Forbes' list of the world's highest-paid athletes (2021). His career has also been marked by controversies and legal cases, including criminal charges in the United States and Ireland and a 2024 civil jury verdict in Dublin that found him liable for assaulting a woman in 2018, awarding damages. In 2025 he announced, then suspended, a campaign for the presidency of Ireland.
