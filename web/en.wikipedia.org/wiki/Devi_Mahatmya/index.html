Devi Mahatmya

Durga's victory over Mahishasura is the text's central episode

The Devi Mahatmya (Sanskrit: देवीमाहात्म्यम्, "Glory of the Goddess"), also known as the Durga Saptashati or Chandi Path, is a Hindu religious text of 700 verses describing the goddess Durga's victories over demonic forces. It forms part of the Markandeya Purana and is the foundational scripture of goddess worship (Shaktism), usually dated to the 5th–6th century CE.

Contents

The text is framed as a dialogue in which the sage Markandeya relates three episodes (caritas): the goddess as Mahamaya, who withdraws the cosmic sleep from Vishnu so he may slay the demons Madhu and Kaitabha; the birth of Durga from the combined energies of the gods and her destruction of the buffalo demon Mahishasura; and the victories of the goddess, as Kaushiki and through her fierce emanations the Matrikas and Kali, over the demons Shumbha and Nishumbha and their generals including Raktabija, whose every drop of spilled blood produced another demon. Hymns interspersed in the narrative praise the goddess as the power inherent in all beings.

Ritual use

The Devi Mahatmya is ritually recited, especially during the autumn festival of Navratri and Durga Puja, when its 700 verses are chanted over one or more days. Its theology — the goddess as supreme, all-pervading power (shakti) — profoundly influenced later Shakta texts including the Devi-Bhagavata Purana.
