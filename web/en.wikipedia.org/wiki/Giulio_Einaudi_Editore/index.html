Giulio Einaudi Editore, commonly known as Einaudi, is one of Italy's most prestigious publishing houses. It was founded in Turin in 1933 by Giulio Einaudi and became a central institution of Italian intellectual and literary culture.

History

Einaudi grew out of the circle around the journal La Cultura and the antifascist intellectuals of Turin, including Leone Ginzburg and Cesare Pavese, who served as an editor and translator. The house published translations of major international authors and works of history, philosophy, and social science, often under the constraints of fascist censorship.

Publishing programme

After the Second World War, Einaudi published authors such as Italo Calvino (who also worked as an editor there), Primo Levi, and Natalia Ginzburg, alongside landmark scholarly series. Financial difficulties led to its acquisition by the Mondadori group in the 1990s, under which it continues as a distinct imprint.
