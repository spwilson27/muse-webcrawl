Citlaltépetl

Elevation5,636 m (18,491 ft)
LocationPuebla–Veracruz border, Mexico
TypeStratovolcano

Citlaltépetl, also known as Pico de Orizaba, is an active volcano, the highest mountain in Mexico and the third highest in North America, after Denali in the United States and Mount Logan in Canada. It rises 5,636 metres (18,491 ft) above sea level in the eastern end of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt, on the border between the states of Veracruz and Puebla.

Name

The name Citlaltépetl comes from Nahuatl citlalli ("star") and tepētl ("hill"), hence "Star Hill". In Spanish it is most often called Pico de Orizaba, after the nearby city of Orizaba.

Geology and climbing

The stratovolcano is currently dormant but not extinct; its last eruption was in the 19th century. Its glacier-covered summit is a popular objective for mountaineers, and the mountain lies within Pico de Orizaba National Park, established in 1937.
