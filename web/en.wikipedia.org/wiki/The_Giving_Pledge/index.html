The Giving Pledge is a charitable campaign founded in August 2010 by Bill Gates, Melinda French Gates and Warren Buffett. Signatories make a public, morally binding (but not legally enforceable) commitment to give the majority of their wealth to philanthropy, either during their lifetimes or in their wills.

History

The campaign grew out of dinners hosted by the Gateses and Buffett in 2009–2010 to discuss philanthropy with other billionaires. It was announced in August 2010 with 40 signatories from the United States. By the following decade the pledge had attracted more than 230 signatories from over 28 countries, including figures such as Michael Bloomberg, MacKenzie Scott, Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, and Richard Branson. Each signatory publishes a letter explaining their reasons for giving.

Criticism

Critics have noted that the pledge is voluntary and unaudited, that donations may flow to donor-controlled foundations rather than directly to operating charities, and that large-scale philanthropy gives the very wealthy outsized influence over public priorities. Supporters respond that the pledge has normalised large-scale giving and shifted cultural expectations of billionaire responsibility.
