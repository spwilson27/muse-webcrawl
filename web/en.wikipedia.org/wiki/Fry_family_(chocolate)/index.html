Fry family (chocolate)

The Fry family was a prominent Quaker family in Bristol, England, whose business, J. S. Fry & Sons, became one of the largest chocolate manufacturers in the world during the 19th century. Founded by Joseph Fry, the firm is credited with producing the first solid chocolate bar for eating, launched in 1847, and the first filled chocolate cream bar, Fry's Chocolate Cream, in 1866.

Origins

Joseph Fry (1728–1787), a Quaker apothecary, began making chocolate in Bristol around 1761. The business passed through the family, and under Joseph Storrs Fry it greatly expanded, absorbing competitors and moving to a large factory in central Bristol. Like other Quaker firms (Cadbury and Rowntree), Fry's combined industrial success with philanthropy and model working conditions relatively advanced for the era.

Products and merger

Fry's products included Fry's Chocolate Cream, the Turkish Delight bar, and Fry's Cocoa. In 1919 J. S. Fry & Sons merged with Cadbury, though the Fry name and Bristol factory continued for decades; production at Somerdale, Keynsham, ended in 2011. Family members were also notable in public life and science, including Elizabeth Fry, the famous prison reformer, who married into the family.
