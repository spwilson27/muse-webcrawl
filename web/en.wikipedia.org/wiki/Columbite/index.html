Columbite

Columbite
Formula: (Fe,Mn)Nb2O6
Colour: Black to brownish black
Hardness: 6 (Mohs)
Importance: Principal ore of niobium

Columbite, also called niobite, is a black oxide mineral with the general formula (Fe,Mn)Nb2O6. It forms a solid-solution series with tantalite, the tantalum-rich end member; together the minerals are widely known in mining as coltan when both are present. Columbite is the most important ore of the metal niobium.

Properties

Columbite is orthorhombic, submetallic in lustre, and unusually dense, with specific gravity rising with niobium and tantalum content. It is typically found as short, thick crystals or massive grains in granitic pegmatites, often associated with cassiterite, spodumene, and tantalite.

Name and history

The mineral was described from specimens sent from Connecticut to the British Museum in the early 1800s; analysis of the material led Charles Hatchett in 1801 to announce a new element he named columbium, after Columbia, a poetic name for America. The element was later shown to be the same as niobium, though the name columbium persisted in American metallurgy for many years.

Occurrence and use

Significant production comes from Brazil, Canada, Australia, and central Africa. Niobium extracted from columbite is used mainly as an alloying element that strengthens steels and in superalloys and superconducting magnets.
