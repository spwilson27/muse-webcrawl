Aretha Franklin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Aretha Franklin at the piano in the late 1960s, the Atlantic years: the Queen of Soul mid-song, eyes closed]

Aretha Louise Franklin (25 March 1942 – 16 August 2018) was an American singer, songwriter and pianist, the 'Queen of Soul'. Beginning as a gospel prodigy in her father's Detroit church, she crossed to secular music at Columbia Records with modest success, then at Atlantic (from 1967) detonated: 'I Never Loved a Man (the Way I Love You)', 'Respect' — her reworking of Otis Redding's song into a feminist and civil-rights anthem — 'Chain of Fools', '(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman' and 'Think' made her the voice of an era.

Franklin won 18 Grammy Awards, was the first woman inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1987), sang at the funerals and inaugurations of American history — for Martin Luther King Jr. in 1968, and 'My Country, 'Tis of Thee' at Barack Obama's first inauguration in 2009, in the famous bow-tied hat now held by the Smithsonian.

Gospel roots and Atlantic breakthrough

Born in Memphis and raised in Detroit, where her father C. L. Franklin's New Bethel Baptist Church drew gospel's royalty (Mahalia Jackson was a family friend), Aretha recorded her first gospel album at 14. Jerry Wexler at Atlantic took her to Muscle Shoals and then New York with the Swampers and her sisters Carolyn and Erma on backing vocals; the run of 1967–68 singles remains soul's high-water mark.

Later career

The live gospel album Amazing Grace (1972) is her best-selling record and, in Sydney Pollack's long-shelved film of it (released 2018), one of music's great documents. Later hits ('Freeway of Love', the duet 'I Knew You Were Waiting (for Me)' with George Michael, 1987) and marathon public singings (the 1998 Grammy substitution for Pavarotti, aria and all) sustained a reign of five decades. She died of pancreatic cancer in Detroit in 2018; her funeral ran eight hours and the whole music world came.
