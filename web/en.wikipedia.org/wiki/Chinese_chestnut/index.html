Chinese chestnut

Castanea mollissima foliage and burs

The Chinese chestnut (Castanea mollissima), also known as the Chinese hairy chestnut, is a species of chestnut native to China, Taiwan, and Korea, in the family Fagaceae. It is a deciduous tree growing to about 20 metres tall with a wide crown. The leaves are simple, toothed, and densely hairy beneath, giving the species its epithet mollissima ("very soft").

Cultivation and uses

The Chinese chestnut has been cultivated in East Asia for thousands of years for its sweet, edible nuts, which are eaten roasted, boiled, or ground into flour. It was introduced to North America in the early 20th century. Crucially, the species coevolved with the chestnut blight fungus Cryphonectria parasitica and has substantial natural resistance to it, unlike the American chestnut, which the blight nearly exterminated. For this reason, Chinese chestnuts and Chinese × American hybrids are central to efforts to restore chestnut forests in North America.

Description

The tree produces spiny burs containing two or three glossy brown nuts. It is hardy, drought-tolerant once established, and prefers acidic, well-drained soils in full sun. Numerous cultivars have been selected for nut size, yield, and blight resistance.
