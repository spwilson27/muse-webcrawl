Government-designated city

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Yokohama, the most populous government-designated city in Japan.

A government-designated city, formally a "city designated by government ordinance" (Japanese: 政令指定都市, seirei shitei toshi), is a Japanese city with a population greater than 500,000 that has been designated by order of the Cabinet of Japan under Article 252 of the Local Autonomy Law. Designated cities are delegated many of the administrative functions normally performed by prefectural governments, including aspects of education, welfare, sanitation, city planning, and business licensing.

Structure

Designated cities are subdivided into wards (ku), each with its own ward office providing local services. Unlike the special wards of Tokyo, these wards are purely administrative subdivisions without elected assemblies or independent corporate status.

History and list

The first five cities designated in 1956 were Osaka, Nagoya, Kyoto, Yokohama, and Kobe, followed by Kitakyushu in 1963. Successive waves of designation, often following municipal mergers that pushed cities over the population threshold, brought the total to 20 with the addition of Kumamoto in 2012. The designated cities include Sapporo, Sendai, Saitama, Chiba, Kawasaki, Niigata, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu, Sakai, Okayama, Hiroshima, Fukuoka, and others.

Comparison with other tiers

Designated cities sit at the top of a hierarchy of specially empowered municipalities, above core cities (chūkakushi, population over 200,000) and special cities. The system is intended to give Japan's largest urban centres administrative capacity comparable to that of prefectures.
