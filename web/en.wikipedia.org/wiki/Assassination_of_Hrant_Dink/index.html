The assassination of Hrant Dink occurred on 19 January 2007 in Istanbul, when the Turkish-Armenian journalist Hrant Dink, founder and editor of the bilingual newspaper Agos, was shot dead outside his office by a 17-year-old Turkish nationalist, Ogün Samast.

Background

Dink was a prominent voice for Turkish-Armenian reconciliation and for democratisation in Turkey. He had been prosecuted and convicted under Article 301 of the Turkish Penal Code for "denigrating Turkishness" in connection with his writing on the Armenian genocide, and had received nationalist death threats, which he had reported to the police.

The murder and reaction

Samast shot Dink three times in the head and neck in broad daylight and fled; he was captured the next day in Samsun after his father alerted police from CCTV images. The murder provoked an enormous public reaction: more than 100,000 people marched at Dink's funeral in Istanbul, many carrying placards reading "We are all Hrant Dink" and "We are all Armenians" — a landmark moment in Turkish public life.

Trials

Samast was sentenced in 2011 to nearly 23 years in prison. A wider trial of alleged instigators and of officials accused of negligence dragged on for years; the European Court of Human Rights had already ruled in 2010 that Turkey had failed to protect Dink's life and freedom of expression. Further trials of public officials concluded in 2021 with convictions of several former police and gendarmerie officers. Samast was released on parole in November 2023, prompting renewed public debate.
