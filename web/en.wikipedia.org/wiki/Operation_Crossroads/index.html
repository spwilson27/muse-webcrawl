Operation Crossroads

Test siteBikini Atoll, Marshall Islands
PeriodJuly 1946

Operation Crossroads was a pair of nuclear weapon tests conducted by the United States at Bikini Atoll in mid-1946. Its purpose was to test the effect of nuclear weapons on warships. The Crossroads tests were the first of many nuclear tests held in the Marshall Islands and the first to be publicly announced beforehand and observed by an invited audience, including a large press corps.

The tests

A fleet of 95 target vessels was assembled in Bikini Lagoon, including battleships, aircraft carriers, cruisers, destroyers, and submarines, among them captured Japanese and German ships. Two tests were conducted: Able, an airburst on 1 July 1946, and Baker, an underwater detonation on 25 July 1946. Both bombs were 23-kiloton plutonium implosion devices of the Fat Man type.

The Baker shot produced severe radioactive contamination of the target fleet, far beyond expectations, because the underwater explosion vaporised seawater and sprayed radioactive material over the ships. A planned third test, Charlie, was cancelled. The contaminated ships could not be decontaminated and most were eventually sunk or scuttled.

Aftermath

The Bikini islanders, who had been relocated to Rongerik Atoll, suffered hardship from food shortages; the population was moved several times and Bikini remained uninhabitable for decades because of residual radioactivity. Operation Crossroads remains one of the most photographed nuclear test series.
