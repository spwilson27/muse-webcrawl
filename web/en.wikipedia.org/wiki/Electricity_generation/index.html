Electricity generation

Electricity generation is the process of generating electric power from sources of primary energy. For utilities in the electric power industry, it is the stage prior to its delivery (transmission, distribution, etc.) to end users or its storage.

Usable electricity is not freely available in nature in large amounts, so it must be "produced" by transforming other energy sources. Most electricity is generated by electromechanical generators driven by heat engines, hydroelectric turbines, wind turbines, or other prime movers.

Methods

Thermal power

Coal, natural gas, and nuclear power stations burn fuel or use nuclear fission to heat water into steam, which drives steam turbines connected to generators. Gas turbines and combined-cycle plants offer higher efficiency. Fossil fuels remain the largest source worldwide, though their share is declining.

Renewables

Hydroelectricity is the largest renewable source, followed by wind power and solar photovoltaics, which convert sunlight directly into electricity without moving parts. Geothermal, biomass, and tidal power contribute smaller shares.

Global production

World electricity generation exceeds 29,000 terawatt-hours per year. China is the largest producer, followed by the United States and India. Coal provides roughly a third of global electricity, natural gas about a fifth, and hydro, nuclear, wind, and solar most of the remainder, with low-carbon sources together now supplying around 40% of the total.

Organisation

Generation has historically been organised through centralised power stations feeding transmission grids. Liberalised markets in many countries separate generation from transmission and retail, while the growth of rooftop solar and storage has increased distributed generation.
