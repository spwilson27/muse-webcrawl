The Korean Broadcasting System (KBS) is the national public broadcaster of South Korea. It operates television channels KBS1 and KBS2, several radio networks, and the international service KBS World.

History

KBS traces its origin to radio broadcasting begun in 1927 under Japanese rule. Television broadcasting started in 1961, and KBS was reorganised as a public corporation in 1973. It is funded principally by a television licence fee supplemented by advertising on some services.

Programming

KBS is known for news, historical dramas (sageuk), variety programmes and coverage of national events. Its dramas have been exported widely as part of the Korean Wave. KBS World broadcasts Korean programming with subtitles to audiences abroad.

Structure

The broadcaster is governed by a board of directors appointed by the President of South Korea, a system periodically debated in discussions of media independence.
