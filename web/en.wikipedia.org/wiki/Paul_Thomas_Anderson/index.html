Paul Thomas Anderson (born June 26, 1970) is an American filmmaker. He is widely regarded as one of the most preeminent filmmakers of his generation, known for films distinguished by long takes, complex narratives, and explorations of flawed characters, ambition, and family.

Early life

Anderson was born in Studio City, Los Angeles, the son of actor Ernie Anderson, the voice of ABC and the host of the late-night horror show Ghoulardi in Cleveland. Anderson developed an early obsession with cinema, making films with a Betamovie camera as a teenager. He briefly attended New York University's film school before dropping out to pursue filmmaking directly.

Career

After the short film Cigarettes & Coffee (1993) attracted attention, Anderson made his feature debut with Hard Eight (1996). His breakthrough came with Boogie Nights (1997), an ensemble drama about the 1970s pornography industry, followed by the critically acclaimed Magnolia (1999) and the romantic drama Punch-Drunk Love (2002).

There Will Be Blood (2007), a loose adaptation of Upton Sinclair's novel Oil!, is frequently cited among the greatest films of the 21st century; Daniel Day-Lewis won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of oilman Daniel Plainview. Anderson's subsequent films include The Master (2012), Inherent Vice (2014), Phantom Thread (2017), and Licorice Pizza (2021).

Style and themes

Anderson's films are noted for bold visual style, extended tracking shots, recurring collaborations with actors such as Philip Seymour Hoffman and Daniel Day-Lewis, and soundtracks by Jonny Greenwood of Radiohead. Recurring themes include dysfunctional families, surrogate father figures, alienation, and California landscapes.

Personal life

Anderson has been in a relationship with actress and comedian Maya Rudolph since 2001; they have four children and live in the San Fernando Valley.
