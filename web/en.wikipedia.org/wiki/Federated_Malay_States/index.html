Federated Malay States

Existed1895–1946 (effectively to 1942)
CapitalKuala Lumpur
Member statesPerak, Selangor, Negeri Sembilan, Pahang
StatusBritish protectorate federation

The Federated Malay States (FMS) was a federation of four protected Malay states — Perak, Selangor, Negeri Sembilan, and Pahang — established by the British in 1895 on the Malay Peninsula. It formed the economic heartland of British Malaya, producing much of the world's tin and rubber.

Formation

Beginning with the Pangkor Treaty of 1874, Britain installed Residents whose "advice" Malay rulers were bound to accept in all matters except religion and custom. In 1895 the four states signed a treaty of federation, taking effect in 1896, with Kuala Lumpur as the federal capital and Frank Swettenham as the first Resident-General. Centralized federal departments ran railways, public works, police, and finance, increasingly overshadowing the state governments and the individual Residents.

Economy and society

Federation revenues rested on tin mining in the Kinta Valley and Selangor and, after 1900, booming rubber plantations. Large numbers of Chinese and Indian labourers immigrated to work the mines and estates, reshaping the peninsula's demography. The remaining Malay states — Kedah, Perlis, Kelantan, Terengganu, and Johor — stayed outside the federation as the Unfederated Malay States.

End

The federation collapsed with the Japanese occupation of 1942. In 1946 its states joined the short-lived Malayan Union, and in 1948 they became part of the Federation of Malaya, the forerunner of modern Malaysia.
