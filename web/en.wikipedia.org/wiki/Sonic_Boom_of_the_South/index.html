Sonic Boom of the South

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sonic Boom of the South is the marching band of Jackson State University in Jackson, Mississippi. Founded in the 1940s, it is one of the most celebrated bands in the tradition of historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs), renowned for its high-stepping style, powerful brass sound, elaborate halftime shows, and the dance line known as the Prancing J-Settes. The band's motto is "The Baddest Band in the Land."

History

The Jackson State band programme grew from a small ensemble in the 1940s into a major unit under director William W. Davis in the 1960s. In 1971 Dowell Taylor, a former member, became director and gave the band its name and signature identity; the nickname "Sonic Boom of the South" was adopted from the JB's popular "Soul Power" era slang for explosive sound. Taylor expanded the band's size, introduced the famous "Tiger Run" entrance and the "Get Ready" theme based on the Temptations' song, and set the standard of precision drill and showmanship that successors have maintained.

Style and reputation

The Sonic Boom combines military-style precision with show-style entertainment: high knee lifts, rocking and dancing in the stands, a large tuba section, and arrangements spanning funk, soul, gospel, and current popular music. Its halftime "fifth quarter" battles against rival bands — above all Southern University's Human Jukebox — are major attractions of Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC) football. The band has performed at presidential inaugurations, NFL games, and Motown and television specials, and in 2021 was featured in the documentary tradition of HBCU band culture that also inspired the film Drumline. Jackson State's rise in national profile under football coach Deion Sanders (2020–2022) brought the band still wider audiences.
