Koebner phenomenon

The Koebner phenomenon (also called the isomorphic response) is the appearance of new skin lesions of a pre-existing dermatosis at sites of skin injury, such as scratches, burns, surgical scars or friction. It is named after the German dermatologist Heinrich Koebner, who described it in psoriasis patients in the 1870s.

Conditions

The phenomenon is classically seen in psoriasis, lichen planus and vitiligo. Lesions typically appear 10–20 days after trauma and take the shape of the injury — for example, a line of psoriasis along a scratch. A related "reverse Koebner" describes lesions clearing at injured sites.

Significance

Awareness of Koebnerisation influences management: patients are advised to minimise skin trauma, and elective surgery or tattooing may be reconsidered during active disease.
