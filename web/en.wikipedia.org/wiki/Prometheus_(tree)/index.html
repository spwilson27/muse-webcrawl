Prometheus (tree)

Prometheus (record designation WPN-114) was a Great Basin bristlecone pine (Pinus longaeva) growing near the tree line on Wheeler Peak in what is now Great Basin National Park, Nevada, United States. When it was cut down in 1964 for dendrochronological research, it proved to be at least 4,862 years old — and very likely close to 5,000 — making it the oldest known non-clonal organism on Earth at the time of its death. The tree was named after Prometheus, the Titan of Greek mythology who stole fire from the gods and gave it to humanity.

A Great Basin bristlecone pine near the Prometheus site

Discovery and study

The grove on Wheeler Peak had been noted by naturalists in the 1950s. In the summer of 1964, Donald R. Currey, a graduate student studying Little Ice Age glacial movements, obtained permission from the U.S. Forest Service to take core samples from the trees. When his increment borer broke after he had extracted only partial cores, the tree WPN-114 — by then nicknamed Prometheus — was felled so that complete cross-sections could be obtained. Counting the rings on the sections revealed an age of 4,862 years, far beyond what Currey had expected; because the innermost rings were missing, the tree's true age was probably around 5,000 years.

Aftermath

The felling of Prometheus caused lasting controversy. It became a cause célèbre for the conservation movement and a cautionary tale in environmental ethics: the oldest known living thing had been killed in the course of studying it. The incident contributed to stronger protections for bristlecone pines and helped build support for the creation of Great Basin National Park in 1986. Sections of the trunk are preserved at the park's visitor center and other institutions. A remnant of the stump still stands at the site, which is not marked on public maps.

Prometheus remained the oldest verified individual tree until 2012, when an unnamed bristlecone pine in the White Mountains of California was reported with an age of 5,062 years.
