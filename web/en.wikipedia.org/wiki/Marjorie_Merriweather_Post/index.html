Marjorie Merriweather Post (15 March 1887 – 12 September 1973) was an American businesswoman, socialite and philanthropist. Heir to the Post Cereal Company founded by her father C. W. Post, she became owner of the enlarged General Foods Corporation and for a time was the wealthiest woman in the United States.

Business career

Post joined the company's board as a young woman and, on her father's death in 1914, took control, expanding the business through acquisitions such as Clarence Birdseye's frozen-food patents, which became the foundation of General Foods. She was a director of the company for decades.

Estates and collections

Post built three celebrated estates: Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida (1924–1927), which she willed to the United States government and which later passed to Donald Trump; Hillwood in Washington, D.C., now a museum of her outstanding collection of Russian imperial art, including Fabergé eggs; and Camp Topridge in the Adirondacks. She also owned the yacht Sea Cloud. During her third husband Joseph E. Davies' ambassadorship to the Soviet Union (1936–38) she acquired many Russian treasures then being sold by the Soviet state.
