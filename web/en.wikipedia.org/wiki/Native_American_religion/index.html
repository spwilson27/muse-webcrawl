Native American religion

Native American religions are the Indigenous spiritual practices of the peoples of the Americas. They vary greatly between nations and tribes, but commonly emphasise a close relationship between human beings, the natural world and the spirit world, and are often transmitted orally through story, ceremony and ritual rather than written scripture.

Characteristics

Many traditions feature ceremonies such as the Sun Dance of the Plains peoples, the sweat lodge, vision quests, and seasonal dances and feasts tied to agricultural and hunting cycles. Sacred places in the landscape, ancestral beings and animal powers figure prominently in many belief systems.

History

From the colonial era onward, Native religious practices were suppressed by missionary activity and government policies in the United States and Canada, including the banning of ceremonies in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The American Indian Religious Freedom Act of 1978 affirmed the right of Native peoples to practise their traditional religions, and many ceremonies have since undergone revival.
