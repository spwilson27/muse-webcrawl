Raghunath Temple, Devprayag

The Raghunath Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to Rama (worshipped here as Raghunath) in Devprayag, in the Tehri Garhwal district of Uttarakhand, India. It stands above the sangam (confluence) of the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers, where they merge to form the Ganges, and is one of the most important shrines of the town, itself one of the five sacred confluences of the Pancha Prayag.

Legend and history

Tradition holds that Rama performed penance at Devprayag to expiate the sin of killing Ravana, a Brahmin, and the temple marks the spot. The shrine is considered very ancient; the present structure, built of large stones in the North Indian style with a pyramidal shikhara, has been rebuilt and renovated over the centuries, including after earthquake damage in the 19th century. The deity is flanked by images of Sita and Lakshmana, and the temple complex includes smaller shrines and inscriptions associated with pilgrims and patrons, among them rulers of Garhwal.

Worship

The temple is a major stop for pilgrims travelling to the Char Dham shrines of Badrinath and Kedarnath, who traditionally bathe at the confluence before visiting Raghunath. Rama Navami is celebrated here with particular devotion.
