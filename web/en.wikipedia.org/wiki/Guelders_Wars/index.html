Guelders Wars

The Guelders Wars were a series of conflicts in the Low Countries between the Duchy of Guelders and the Habsburg Netherlands, fought intermittently in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries.

Background

Charles of Egmond, Duke of Guelders from 1492, resisted Habsburg attempts to absorb his duchy into their Burgundian inheritance, receiving support from France. Under his famous field commander Maarten van Rossum, Guelders forces raided deep into Holland and Brabant, sacking The Hague in 1528.

Outcome

Charles died in 1538 and was succeeded by William, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, whose claims collapsed after his defeat in 1543. By the Treaty of Venlo (1543), Guelders was incorporated into the Habsburg Netherlands, ending its independence.
