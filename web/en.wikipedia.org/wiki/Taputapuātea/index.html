Taputapuātea

Taputapuātea is a commune of French Polynesia, an overseas collectivity of France in the Pacific Ocean, on the island of Raiatea. It takes its name from the great marae complex of Taputapuātea, once the religious and political centre of eastern Polynesia.

Marae Taputapuātea

The marae at Te Pō on Cape Matahira-i-te-rā was dedicated to the god 'Oro and served as the hub of the 'Oro cult and of a voyaging alliance (Te Aoa Uri and Te Aoa Tea) linking chiefs and priests across the Society, Cook, Austral and Tuamotu islands. Its great stone ahu platform measures about 43 by 13 metres, surrounded by other marae, ceremonial platforms, archery grounds and sacred sites across a landscape stretching from the lagoon to the inland valley of 'Ōpoa. From Taputapuātea, voyaging canoes are traditionally said to have set out to settle Hawai'i, Rapa Nui (Easter Island) and Aotearoa (New Zealand).

Recognition

The Taputapuātea landscape was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2017, the first cultural site in French Polynesia so recognised, honouring a thousand years of Mā'ohi civilisation. Since 1994 the site has hosted gatherings of Polynesian voyaging societies, including the arrival of traditional double-hulled canoes retracing ancestral routes.
