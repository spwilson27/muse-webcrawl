Aberdeen Proving Ground (APG) is a United States Army facility located adjacent to Aberdeen, Harford County, Maryland, near the head of Chesapeake Bay. Established in 1917 as the Army's first ordnance proving ground, it is one of the world's oldest and largest weapons-testing installations, and a major centre for military research, development, test and evaluation — as well as a census-designated place.

History

Congress authorised the proving ground in 1917 because the Sandy Hook Proving Ground in New Jersey could no longer handle larger artillery. The first shot was fired in January 1918. The Edgewood Arsenal area, acquired the same year, became the centre of American chemical weapons development and production, filling artillery shells with agents such as mustard gas. During the Second World War the Ballistic Research Laboratory at APG built and operated ENIAC, one of the first electronic general-purpose computers, to compute firing tables.

Activities

Today APG hosts the Aberdeen Test Center, which tests vehicles, weapons and equipment in environments from arctic to desert simulation; the DEVCOM Chemical Biological Center at Edgewood; the Army Public Health Center; CCDC research directorates; and medical research on chemical defence. The post is Harford County's largest employer, with more than 20,000 civilian, military and contractor personnel. The 2005 Base Realignment and Closure round moved additional command-and-control and communications-electronics missions to APG from Fort Monmouth.

Environmental legacy

Decades of chemical weapons work left significant contamination; portions of the Edgewood and Aberdeen areas are on the Superfund National Priorities List, and unexploded ordnance and buried chemical materiel continue to be remediated under long-running cleanup programmes.
