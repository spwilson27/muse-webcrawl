South Eastern and Chatham Railway

SuccessorSouthern Railway
Formation1899 (joint managing committee)
RegionKent and south-east England

The South Eastern and Chatham Railway (SECR) was the working union formed in 1899 between two formerly fiercely rival British railway companies, the South Eastern Railway (SER) and the London, Chatham and Dover Railway (LC&DR). The two companies had competed wastefully for traffic between London and the Kent coast and Channel ports, and the union ended decades of rivalry while keeping both companies legally distinct until the 1923 Grouping, when they became part of the Southern Railway.

Background

The SER and LC&DR built duplicate routes to Dover, Canterbury, and other Kent towns, competing with famously extravagant facilities. Financial pressure, especially on the LC&DR, led to the 1899 agreement creating a joint managing committee; trains thereafter ran under the SECR name and livery.

Operations

The SECR operated commuter services in south-east London, boat trains to Dover and Folkestone for continental connections, and services across Kent. Under chief mechanical engineer Harry Wainwright, and later Richard Maunsell, it built well-regarded locomotives at Ashford Works.
