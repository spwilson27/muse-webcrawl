River Stour

River Stour
CountryEngland
Principal riversGreat Stour (Kent); Stours of Dorset, Suffolk, Warwickshire and Worcestershire

River Stour is the name of five rivers in England. The name, pronounced variously (rhyming with "tour", "tower" or "store" depending on the river), derives from an Old English or Brittonic word for a strong or large stream. The rivers are in Kent, Dorset, Suffolk (on the Essex border), Warwickshire and Worcestershire.

The rivers

Great Stour, Kent

The River Great Stour rises near Lenham and flows about 93 km (58 mi) through Ashford and Canterbury to the English Channel at Pegwell Bay near Sandwich. It is the second-longest river in Kent; the Little Stour joins it at Plucks Gutter, and the combined stream passes through the former port of Sandwich, once a Cinque Port before its harbour silted up. Through Canterbury the Stour flows in several channels around and through the historic city.

River Stour, Dorset

The Dorset Stour rises at Stourhead in Wiltshire and flows about 97 km (60 mi) south-east through Dorset, past Gillingham, Sturminster Newton, Blandford Forum, Wimborne Minster and Bournemouth, to the sea at Christchurch Harbour. It drains much of the Blackmore Vale and is known for its clear chalkstream-influenced lower reaches and for floods managed by a washland system.

River Stour, Suffolk

The Suffolk Stour, treated in its own article, forms most of the Essex–Suffolk border and flows through the Dedham Vale of "Constable Country". See River Stour, Suffolk.

River Stour, Worcestershire

The Worcestershire Stour rises in the Clent Hills and flows through Halesowen, Stourbridge and Kidderminster to join the River Severn at Stourport-on-Severn, a town created at the junction of the river with the Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canal.

River Stour, Warwickshire

The Warwickshire Stour is a tributary of the River Avon, rising near Swalcliffe in Oxfordshire and flowing through Shipston-on-Stour to join the Avon below Stratford-upon-Avon.
