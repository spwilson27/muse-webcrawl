Marie-Esprit-Léon Walras (16 December 1834 – 5 January 1910) was a French mathematical economist and Georgist. He formulated the marginal theory of value and pioneered the development of general equilibrium theory. Joseph Schumpeter called him "the greatest of all economists".

Life and work

Walras studied at the École des Mines in Paris and worked in banking, journalism and railway administration before being appointed, in 1870, to the new chair of political economy at the University of Lausanne in Switzerland, where he founded the Lausanne School of economics. His major work, Éléments d'économie politique pure ("Elements of Pure Economics", 1874–77), presented the first comprehensive mathematical treatment of general equilibrium: a system of simultaneous equations describing how supply and demand in all markets interact to determine prices.

Key ideas

Walras developed the concept of marginal utility (which he called rareté) as the basis of value, independently of Jevons and Menger. He described price adjustment through a hypothetical process of "groping" (tâtonnement), in which an auctioneer calls out prices until all markets clear. Walras's law states that the total value of excess demand across all markets must be zero, so that if all markets but one are in equilibrium, the last must be as well. His framework laid the foundations of modern neoclassical economics.
