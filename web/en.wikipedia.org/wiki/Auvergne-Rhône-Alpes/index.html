Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes (French pronunciation: [ovɛʁɲ ʁon alp]) is a region of east-central France created by the territorial reform of 2014–2016 through the merger of the former regions of Auvergne and Rhône-Alpes. Its prefecture and largest city is Lyon.

Geography

The region extends from the Massif Central in the west — including the volcanic Chaîne des Puys around Clermont-Ferrand — to the French Alps in the east, where Mont Blanc (4,806 m), the highest summit in the European Union, stands on the Italian border. The Rhône and Loire both rise within the region. Major cities besides Lyon include Grenoble, Saint-Étienne, Annecy, Chambéry and Clermont-Ferrand.

Economy

Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes is France's second-largest regional economy, with strengths in chemicals and pharmaceuticals (Lyon), microelectronics (Grenoble), rubber (Michelin, headquartered in Clermont-Ferrand), winter-sports tourism (Chamonix, Courchevel, Val d'Isère) and hydroelectric power from Alpine dams. It is also a major transport crossroads between Paris, the Mediterranean, Italy and Switzerland.
