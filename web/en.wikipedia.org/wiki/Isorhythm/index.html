Isorhythm

Isorhythm (from Greek isos, equal, and rhythmos, rhythm) is a compositional technique of late medieval music in which a repeating rhythmic pattern, the talea, is combined with a repeating melodic pattern, the color, typically in the tenor voice. Because the two patterns are usually of different lengths, they fall out of phase with one another, producing a complex, shifting structure.

An isorhythmic motet by Guillaume de Machaut.

History

The technique flourished in the 14th and early 15th centuries in the works of composers of the Ars nova and Ars subtilior, notably Philippe de Vitry and Guillaume de Machaut, whose motets are classic examples. Isorhythm continued in masses by Guillaume Du Fay and others. The term itself was coined by the musicologist Friedrich Ludwig around 1900. Some 20th-century composers, including Olivier Messiaen, revived isorhythmic ideas.
