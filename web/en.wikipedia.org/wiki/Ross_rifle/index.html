Ross rifle

The Ross rifle is a straight-pull bolt-action rifle chambered in .303 British, designed by Sir Charles Ross and produced in Canada. It was the standard service rifle of Canadian forces at the start of the First World War.

History

Canada adopted the Ross after Britain refused to supply Lee–Enfield rifles during the Second Boer War era disputes. Successive marks (Mk I, II and III) entered service from 1903. The Mk III of 1910 was highly accurate and prized by marksmen and snipers.

First World War service

In the trenches the rifle proved ill-suited to mud and rapid fire: its tight tolerances caused jamming with British-manufactured ammunition of variable quality, and the bolt could be reassembled incorrectly so that it failed to lock, endangering the user. After complaints from the Canadian Expeditionary Force, the Ross was replaced by the Lee–Enfield in 1916, a political scandal contributing to the fall of Minister of Militia Sam Hughes. The Ross remained in use as a sniper rifle, where its accuracy excelled, and saw second-line service in the Second World War.
