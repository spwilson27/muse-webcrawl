History of the Philippines (1898–1946)

The history of the Philippines from 1898 to 1946 covers the period of American rule, beginning with the Spanish–American War and the cession of the archipelago by Spain under the Treaty of Paris (1898), through the Philippine–American War, the Insular Government, the Commonwealth era, the Japanese occupation of the Second World War, and ending with the recognition of Philippine independence on 4 July 1946.

Manila was the seat of both the American colonial government and the Commonwealth.

Revolution and war with the United States

Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence on 12 June 1898 and the First Philippine Republic at Malolos in January 1899. The United States, having bought the islands from Spain for $20 million, refused to recognise it; fighting began in February 1899. The Philippine–American War's conventional phase ended with Aguinaldo's capture in 1901, though resistance — and brutal counterinsurgency, including the Balangiga massacre's aftermath on Samar — continued for years. Filipino deaths are estimated in the hundreds of thousands.

American colonial rule

Civil government under Governor-General William Howard Taft (from 1901) introduced public schools (staffed by the "Thomasites"), public health, and infrastructure. The Philippine Assembly (1907) and the Jones Law (1916) expanded Filipino participation and promised eventual independence. The Tydings–McDuffie Act (1934) created the Commonwealth of the Philippines (1935) under President Manuel L. Quezon, with Manuel Roxas and Sergio Osmeña among its leaders, as a ten-year transition to independence.

Japanese occupation and liberation

Japan invaded in December 1941; MacArthur's forces withdrew to Bataan and Corregidor, which fell in spring 1942, followed by the Bataan Death March. A collaborationist republic under José P. Laurel was proclaimed in 1943 while guerrilla resistance continued. American forces returned at Leyte in October 1944; the Battle of Manila (February 1945) destroyed much of the capital and killed some 100,000 civilians.

Independence

Manuel Roxas won the April 1946 presidential election. On 4 July 1946 the United States recognised the Republic of the Philippines, ending 48 years of American sovereignty, though the Bell Trade Act and the 1947 Military Bases Agreement preserved deep American economic and military ties.
