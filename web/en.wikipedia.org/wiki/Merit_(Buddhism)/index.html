Merit (Buddhism)

Offering alms to monks is a classic way of making merit.

Merit (Sanskrit: puṇya; Pali: puñña) is a concept in Buddhism and other Indian religions referring to the beneficial, protective force accumulated by good deeds, acts and thoughts. Merit-making is fundamental to Buddhist practice, especially for laypeople: it is believed to contribute to a person's growth toward enlightenment, to determine the quality of future rebirths within saṃsāra, and to bring happiness and good fortune in this life.

Merit-making

Traditional texts describe three bases of meritorious action: generosity (dāna), virtue or moral conduct (sīla), and mental development (bhāvanā, including meditation). Later lists expand these to ten bases, adding reverence, service, listening to and teaching the Dharma, rejoicing in others' merit, and holding right views. The most widespread merit-making practice across Buddhist Asia is giving — above all, offering food, robes and support to the monastic sangha, held to be a uniquely fruitful "field of merit". Building temples, commissioning scriptures, releasing captive animals and sponsoring rituals are also common.

Transfer and dedication of merit

Merit can be transferred to others — especially deceased relatives, to relieve their suffering as hungry ghosts — a practice prominent in festivals such as Ullambana (the Ghost Festival) and in Theravāda water-pouring ceremonies. In Mahāyāna Buddhism, bodhisattvas dedicate all their merit to the enlightenment of all beings, and merit is treated less as a personal stockpile than as something to be shared without limit.

Interpretations

Scholars have debated how merit relates to the doctrines of karma and no-self: merit is sometimes described as a quasi-substance that accrues to a person, and sometimes, as in many modernist readings, as simply the wholesome mental states and resulting karmic tendencies produced by good action. However understood, merit-making structures the reciprocal economy between laity and monastics throughout the Buddhist world, and remains a central motivation for donation, pilgrimage and festival observance from Sri Lanka to Japan.
