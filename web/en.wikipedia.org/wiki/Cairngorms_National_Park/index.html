Cairngorms National Park

Cairngorms National Park
Established: 2003 (extended 2010)
Area: 4,528 km²
Authority: Cairngorms National Park Authority
Headquarters: Grantown-on-Spey

Cairngorms National Park (Scottish Gaelic: Pàirc Nàiseanta a' Mhonaidh Ruaidh) is a national park in northeast Scotland, established in 2003. It is the largest national park in the British Isles, covering much of the Cairngorms mountain range and surrounding straths.

Geography

The park extends over parts of five local authority areas — Highland, Aberdeenshire, Moray, Angus and Perth and Kinross — and includes the Cairngorm massif, the upper reaches of Strathspey, Glen Livet, Deeside (the setting of Balmoral Castle), the Angus Glens and the Drumochter and Monadhliath hills.

Conservation

About half the park is designated for its wildlife value: it contains a quarter of Britain's threatened species, extensive Caledonian pinewood, arctic-alpine plateau, and the headwaters of the Spey, Dee and Don rivers. Rewilding projects include the Cairngorms Connect partnership restoring habitats across a large contiguous area.

Communities and tourism

Around 18,000 people live within the park, in towns such as Aviemore, Ballater, Braemar, Grantown-on-Spey, Kingussie, Newtonmore and Tomintoul. Tourism, skiing at Cairngorm Mountain and the Lecht, whisky distilleries and field sports are major parts of the local economy.
