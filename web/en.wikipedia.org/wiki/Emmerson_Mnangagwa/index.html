Emmerson Mnangagwa

Emmerson Dambudzo Mnangagwa (born 15 September 1942) is a Zimbabwean politician who has served as the third president of Zimbabwe since 24 November 2017. A member of ZANU–PF and a longtime ally of former president Robert Mugabe, he held a series of cabinet portfolios and was Vice-President from 2014 until his dismissal in November 2017, which precipitated the military intervention that ended Mugabe's rule. He is nicknamed "Ngwena" (Shona for "The Crocodile").

Early life and liberation struggle

Mnangagwa was born in Shabani (now Zvishavane) in Southern Rhodesia and spent part of his youth in Northern Rhodesia (Zambia), where he studied law. He joined the liberation movement, received military training in Tanzania, Egypt, and China, and led a sabotage unit known as the "Crocodile Gang" in 1964–65, attacking infrastructure including a train. Captured by Rhodesian forces, he was sentenced to death but spared because of his age (his stated birth year made him a minor), and instead served ten years in prison, where he studied law by correspondence. Released and deported to Zambia in 1975, he later joined Robert Mugabe in Mozambique as a special assistant during the Rhodesian Bush War.

Government career under Mugabe

After independence in 1980, Mnangagwa was Zimbabwe's first Minister of State Security, overseeing the Central Intelligence Organisation during the Gukurahundi period (1983–1987), in which thousands of Ndebele civilians were killed — a role for which critics hold him partly responsible, an accusation he has deflected. He later served as Minister of Justice, Finance (acting), Speaker of Parliament (2000–2005), Minister of Rural Housing, Minister of Defence (2009–2013), and Minister of Justice again, before becoming Vice-President and Mugabe's presumed successor in December 2014. His rivalry with the "G40" faction around First Lady Grace Mugabe defined ZANU–PF politics in 2015–17.

Presidency

Mugabe dismissed Mnangagwa on 6 November 2017. Mnangagwa fled to South Africa, claiming an assassination attempt (he had earlier fallen ill at a rally amid poisoning allegations). The military then intervened on 14–15 November 2017; Mugabe resigned on 21 November, and Mnangagwa was sworn in on 24 November, promising a "new democracy," economic revival, and re-engagement with the West ("Zimbabwe is open for business"). He won the July 2018 general election, whose results were disputed by the opposition MDC Alliance; post-election protests were suppressed by soldiers with loss of life. He was re-elected in August 2023 in another disputed vote criticized by regional and international observers. His presidency has faced persistent economic crisis, inflation, corruption allegations, and sanctions disputes.
