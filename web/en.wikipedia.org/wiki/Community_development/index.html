Community development

Community development is a process where community members come together to take collective action and generate solutions to common problems. It ranges from small initiatives within a neighbourhood to broader interventions in housing, health, education, employment and the environment, and is a central concern of social work, public health and international development.

Principles

Common principles include participation and empowerment of local people, building on community assets and capacities (asset-based community development), social justice, and sustainability. Practitioners act as facilitators rather than directors, helping communities identify priorities and organise.

History

Roots lie in 19th-century settlement houses, colonial-era welfare programmes, and post-war reconstruction. The United Nations promoted community development in the 1950s as a method for improving conditions in developing countries. Figures such as Saul Alinsky shaped community organising in the United States, while Paulo Freire's pedagogy influenced participatory approaches worldwide.

Approaches

  • Community economic development and social enterprise
  • Participatory planning and participatory rural appraisal
  • Capacity building and leadership development
  • Community-based participatory research
