Pickup truck

A pickup truck or pickup is a light-duty truck with an enclosed passenger cab and an open cargo bed at the rear. In Australia and New Zealand, vehicles of this type derived from passenger cars are called "utes". Pickups are widely used for hauling, towing, agriculture, construction, and personal transport, especially in North America.

History

The first factory-built pickup was produced by Ford in 1925, based on the Model T. After the Second World War, manufacturers such as Chevrolet, GMC, Dodge, and International Harvester expanded the market; by the late 20th century full-size pickups from Ford (F-Series), General Motors, and Ram became among the best-selling vehicles in the United States.

Types

Pickups are classified as compact, mid-size, or full-size, with cab styles including regular cab, extended cab, and crew cab, and bed lengths typically from about 1.5 to 2.4 metres. Most use body-on-frame construction with rear-wheel or four-wheel drive, though unibody models such as the Honda Ridgeline also exist. Electric pickups emerged in the 2020s, including the Rivian R1T and Tesla Cybertruck.
