Egri Bikavér

TypeRed wine blend
OriginEger, Hungary
Key grapesKékfrankos, Kadarka, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, and others

Egri Bikavér, known in English as Bull's Blood of Eger, is a red wine blend produced in the Eger wine region of northern Hungary. It is Hungary's best-known red wine and a protected designation of origin.

Name and legend

The name Bikavér means "bull's blood". Legend associates it with the 1552 Siege of Eger, when Hungarian defenders led by István Dobó repelled a much larger Ottoman army; stories claim the defenders' beards were stained red by wine (or, in embellished versions, that the wine was mixed with bull's blood to give them strength), terrifying the attackers.

Production rules

Modern regulations require Egri Bikavér to be a blend of at least three grape varieties, with no single variety dominating beyond set limits. Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch) typically forms the backbone, alongside varieties such as Kadarka, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Merlot, Pinot Noir, and Syrah. The wine is aged in oak barrels, and higher categories — Superior and Grand Superior — have stricter yield and ageing requirements.

Character

The wine is characteristically deep ruby in colour, medium- to full-bodied, with flavours of dark berries, plum, spice, and earthy notes, reflecting the volcanic soils of the Eger region.
