Music streaming

Music streaming is the delivery of recorded music to listeners over the internet on demand, without requiring the listener to own or download a permanent copy. Streaming services such as Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Amazon Music, and Tidal licence catalogues from record labels and distributors and provide access through subscriptions, advertising-supported free tiers, or both.

History

Early experiments in streaming audio date to the 1990s, with services such as RealAudio and early internet radio. The file-sharing service Napster (1999) demonstrated mass demand for digital music but was shut down over copyright infringement. Legal on-demand streaming took off after Spotify's launch in 2008, and by the mid-2010s streaming had overtaken downloads and then physical media as the music industry's largest source of revenue. According to the IFPI, streaming now accounts for the majority of global recorded-music income.

Business model

Services pay royalties to rights holders under either a pro-rata model, in which each track receives a share of the subscription pool proportional to its stream count, or a user-centric model, in which a subscriber's fee is divided only among the artists that subscriber heard. Per-stream payouts are small — commonly estimated at a few tenths of a cent — which has drawn persistent criticism from musicians and prompted debate over reform, including proposals such as SoundCloud's fan-powered royalties.

Technology

Streaming platforms deliver compressed audio (AAC, Ogg Vorbis, Opus, or FLAC for lossless tiers) over adaptive bitrate protocols, using content delivery networks to cache popular tracks near listeners. Recommendation systems based on collaborative filtering and audio analysis drive personalised playlists such as Spotify's Discover Weekly.

Impact

Streaming shifted listening from albums to playlists and singles, globalised exposure for regional genres such as K-pop and reggaeton, and made chart success depend heavily on first-week stream counts. It is also credited with reducing music piracy by offering a convenient legal alternative.
