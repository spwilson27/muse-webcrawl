SoFi Stadium is a sports and entertainment stadium in Inglewood, California, United States. It serves as the home of the Los Angeles Rams and the Los Angeles Chargers of the National Football League. The stadium is the centerpiece of Hollywood Park, a large mixed-use development built on the site of the former Hollywood Park Racetrack, developed by Rams owner Stan Kroenke.

Design

Designed by HKS, the stadium is covered by a massive single-piece translucent canopy of ETFE panels that also shelters an adjacent plaza and a 6,000-seat performance venue (YouTube Theater). The seating bowl is sunk about 100 feet (30 m) below ground level, in part because of height restrictions related to the flight path of nearby Los Angeles International Airport. Its signature feature is the Infinity Screen by Samsung, a huge oval, double-sided 4K videoboard suspended over the field — the largest of its kind. The playing surface is artificial turf. The stadium cost roughly $5 billion including the surrounding development, making it among the most expensive stadiums ever built.

History and events

Construction began in November 2016 and the stadium formally opened on September 8, 2020; because of the COVID-19 pandemic, its first NFL season was played without spectators. SoFi Stadium hosted Super Bowl LVI in February 2022, in which the Rams defeated the Cincinnati Bengals on their home field. It hosted the College Football Playoff National Championship in January 2023 and WrestleMania 39 in 2023. The stadium is scheduled to host matches during the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and ceremonies and swimming events for the 2028 Summer Olympics. Naming rights are held by the financial technology company SoFi under a 20-year agreement.
