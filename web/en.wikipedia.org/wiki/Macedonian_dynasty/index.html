Macedonian dynasty

The Macedonian dynasty ruled the Byzantine Empire from 867 to 1056. Founded by Basil I, a peasant from the theme of Macedonia (in Thrace) who usurped the throne from Michael III, the dynasty presided over a period of territorial expansion, military success, and cultural flourishing often called the Macedonian Renaissance.

History

Under emperors such as Leo VI the Wise, Constantine VII Porphyrogennetos, Romanos I Lekapenos (a co-emperor by marriage), Nikephoros II Phokas, John I Tzimiskes, and Basil II, the empire reconquered Crete, Cyprus, and much of Syria, including Antioch, and pushed into Armenia and Mesopotamia. Basil II destroyed the First Bulgarian Empire, earning the epithet "Bulgar-slayer", and by his death in 1025 the empire stood at its greatest extent since the seventh century.

Culture and decline

The era produced encyclopaedic scholarship sponsored by Constantine VII, illuminated manuscripts such as the Paris Psalter, and a revival of classical learning. After Basil II's death, a succession of shorter-lived emperors and empresses, ending with Theodora in 1056, saw military aristocratic factions struggle for power, setting the stage for the crises of the later eleventh century.
