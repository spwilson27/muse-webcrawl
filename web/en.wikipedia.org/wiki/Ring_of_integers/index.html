Ring of integers

In mathematics, the ring of integers of an algebraic number field K is the ring of all algebraic integers contained in K. An algebraic integer is a root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients.

Definition and properties

Denoted OK, the ring of integers is the integral closure of the integers Z in K. It is a Dedekind domain of dimension one, finitely generated as a Z-module, with a basis of deg(K) elements called an integral basis. For K = Q, OK = Z; for K = Q(i), it is the Gaussian integers; for Q(√d), its form depends on d modulo 4.

Role in number theory

Rings of integers generalise the integers to number fields: prime factorisation of ideals always exists uniquely even when factorisation of elements fails — the classic example being 6 = 2·3 = (1+√−5)(1−√−5) in Z[√−5]. Class groups measuring this failure, units (by Dirichlet's unit theorem), and discriminants are central objects of algebraic number theory.
