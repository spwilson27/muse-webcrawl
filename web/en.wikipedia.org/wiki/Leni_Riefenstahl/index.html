Helene Bertha Amalie "Leni" Riefenstahl (German: [ˈleːniː ˈʁiːfn̩ʃtaːl]; August 22, 1902 – September 8, 2003) was a German film director, dancer, actress, and photographer. She is best known for her key role in producing Nazi propaganda films, including Triumph of the Will (1935) and Olympia (1938), works celebrated for their technical innovation and condemned for their glorification of Adolf Hitler's regime — a combination that has made her one of the most controversial figures in cinema history.

Career

Riefenstahl began as a dancer and became famous in the "mountain films" of Arnold Fanck, learning film craft as an actress. She directed and starred in The Blue Light (1932), which brought her to Hitler's attention. Personally commissioned by Hitler, she filmed the 1934 Nuremberg rally as Triumph of the Will (1935), a landmark of propaganda cinema. Her two-part Olympia (1938), covering the 1936 Berlin Olympics, pioneered techniques including tracking shots, underwater photography, and telephoto framing, and won first prize at the Venice Film Festival.

Post-war life

After the war Riefenstahl was arrested and held for several years; a denazification tribunal classified her as a "fellow traveler" rather than an active Nazi, but the association ruined her film career. She reinvented herself as a photographer, publishing acclaimed books on the Nuba people of Sudan in the 1970s and on underwater life, and in her nineties released the diving film Impressionen unter Wasser (2002). She died in 2003, aged 101. Her memoir appeared in 1987, and she was the subject of the documentary The Wonderful, Horrible Life of Leni Riefenstahl (1993).
