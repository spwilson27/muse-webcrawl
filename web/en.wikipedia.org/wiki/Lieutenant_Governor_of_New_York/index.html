Lieutenant Governor of New York

Lieutenant Governor of New York
Style: The Honorable
Term length: Four years, renewable
Formation: 1777
First holder: Pierre Van Cortlandt
Succession: First in line to the governorship

The Lieutenant Governor of New York is a constitutional office in the executive branch of the Government of New York State. The lieutenant governor is elected on a joint ticket with the governor to a four-year term, serves as president of the New York State Senate, and is first in the line of succession to the governorship.

Duties

Under the New York Constitution, the lieutenant governor presides over the State Senate and may cast a tie-breaking vote. The officeholder also serves on various boards and commissions, including the Court for the Trial of Impeachments, and may be assigned additional duties by the governor. If the office of governor becomes vacant, the lieutenant governor becomes governor for the remainder of the term.

Election

Candidates for governor and lieutenant governor are nominated separately in primary elections but run together as a single ticket in the general election. This arrangement has occasionally paired a governor and lieutenant governor from rival factions of the same party.

History

The office dates to the first New York Constitution of 1777; Pierre Van Cortlandt was the first holder. Several lieutenant governors have succeeded to the governorship, including Theodore Roosevelt's predecessor chain and, in modern times, David Paterson, who became governor in 2008 upon the resignation of Eliot Spitzer. A notable episode occurred in 2009, when Governor Paterson appointed Richard Ravitch as lieutenant governor during a leadership crisis in the State Senate, an appointment upheld by the New York Court of Appeals.
