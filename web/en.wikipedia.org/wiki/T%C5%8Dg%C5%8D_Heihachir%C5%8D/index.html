Marshal-Admiral Marquis Tōgō Heihachirō (1848–1934) was a Japanese admiral and one of Japan's greatest naval heroes. As commander-in-chief of the Combined Fleet during the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–05, he decisively defeated the Russian Baltic Fleet at the Battle of Tsushima.

Career

Born in Kagoshima to a samurai family of Satsuma Domain, Tōgō fought as a young sailor in the Boshin War and later studied naval science in Britain from 1871 to 1878, training aboard British ships. During the First Sino-Japanese War he commanded the cruiser Naniwa. In the Russo-Japanese War his flagship was the battleship Mikasa; his victory at Tsushima Bay in May 1905, in which the Russian fleet was virtually annihilated, established Japan as a great power. He was later chief of the Naval General Staff and was given the unprecedented honour of a state funeral in 1934.

Legacy

Tōgō was honoured abroad as well as at home — Western journalists compared him to Nelson, and he became known in Japan as the "God of War". His flagship Mikasa is preserved as a museum ship at Yokosuka, and shrines (Tōgō shrines) were erected in Tokyo and elsewhere.
