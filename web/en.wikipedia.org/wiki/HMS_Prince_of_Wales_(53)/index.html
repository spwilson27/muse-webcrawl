HMS Prince of Wales was a King George V-class battleship of the Royal Navy, built at Cammell Laird's Birkenhead shipyard. Although still incomplete and with civilian technicians aboard, she saw action within months of commissioning, fighting the German battleship Bismarck in May 1941, and was sunk by Japanese land-based aircraft in December of the same year — an event that demonstrated the vulnerability of capital ships without air cover and effectively ended the era of the battleship.

Battle of the Denmark Strait

On 24 May 1941, Prince of Wales and the battlecruiser HMS Hood intercepted Bismarck and the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen in the Denmark Strait. Hood was destroyed with almost all hands; Prince of Wales, suffering gun malfunctions and several hits, landed hits on Bismarck that caused a fuel leak — a wound that contributed to the German ship's later interception and sinking. In August 1941, Prince of Wales carried Prime Minister Winston Churchill across the Atlantic to the meeting with President Franklin D. Roosevelt that produced the Atlantic Charter.

Force Z and sinking

Sent to Singapore as the core, with the battlecruiser HMS Repulse, of Force Z to deter Japanese expansion, Prince of Wales sailed on 8 December 1941 to intercept Japanese invasion convoys. On 10 December, off Kuantan, Malaya, both ships were attacked by waves of Japanese bombers and torpedo aircraft. Prince of Wales was crippled early by a torpedo hit that damaged a propeller shaft and flooded the ship, and sank shortly after Repulse, with the loss of 327 of her crew, including Admiral Sir Tom Phillips and her captain, John Leach. The wreck lies upside down in about 68 metres of water in the South China Sea.
