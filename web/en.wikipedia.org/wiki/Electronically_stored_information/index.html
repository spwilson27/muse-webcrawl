Electronically stored information

Electronically stored information (ESI), for the purposes of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, is information created, manipulated, communicated, stored, and best utilised in digital form, requiring the use of computer hardware and software. ESI discovery — e-discovery — became an express part of U.S. civil discovery with the December 1, 2006 amendments to the Rules.

Scope under the Rules

Rule 26 requires early discussion of ESI, preservation and form of production; Rule 34 covers requests for ESI; Rule 37(e), revised in 2015, governs sanctions for lost ESI that should have been preserved — requiring prejudice for curative measures and intent to deprive for the harshest sanctions such as adverse-inference instructions or dismissal. Sources that are not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost may be protected unless good cause is shown.

Practice

E-discovery involves identification, preservation (litigation holds), collection, processing, review (often with technology-assisted review / predictive coding), and production. Landmark guidance includes the Zubulake decisions and The Sedona Principles. Failure to preserve can lead to spoliation disputes.
