The Orange River (Afrikaans/Dutch: Oranjerivier), known in Lesotho as the Senqu River, is the longest river in South Africa and one of the longest rivers in Africa. It rises in the Drakensberg mountains of Lesotho, flows westward through South Africa — forming the southern border of the Free State, the border between South Africa and Namibia, and passing through the Northern Cape — and empties into the Atlantic Ocean at Alexander Bay.

Course

From its source near Mount aux Sources at over 3,000 metres elevation, the river descends through the Lesotho highlands, where it is dammed at Katse and Mohale as part of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project, which transfers water to the Vaal system supplying Gauteng. In South Africa it is impounded by the Gariep Dam (the country's largest reservoir) and the Vanderkloof Dam. Below the confluence with its largest tributary, the Vaal, the river crosses arid country, passes the Augrabies Falls, and forms the international boundary with Namibia through the Richtersveld before reaching the Atlantic.

Name and history

The river's Khoi name was Gariep, meaning simply "river". Dutch explorer Colonel Robert Jacob Gordon named it the Orange River around 1779 in honour of the Dutch House of Orange. The name Gariep survives in the Gariep Dam and the proposed renaming debates in South Africa. Diamonds were discovered along the lower river in the 1860s, sparking the South African diamond rush, and alluvial diamonds are still mined near the mouth.

Economy and ecology

The Orange-Senqu basin supports irrigation agriculture (notably wine grapes, citrus and lucerne), hydroelectric power and urban water supply across four countries: Lesotho, South Africa, Namibia and Botswana. Water management is coordinated under the Orange-Senqu River Commission (ORASECOM, established 2000). Flow regulation, alien vegetation and water quality are the main environmental concerns.
