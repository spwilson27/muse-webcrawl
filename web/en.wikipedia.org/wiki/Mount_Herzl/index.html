Mount Herzl (Hebrew: הר הרצל, Har Herzl) is a hill in western Jerusalem that serves as Israel's national cemetery and principal site of official commemoration. It is named after Theodor Herzl, founder of modern political Zionism, whose remains were reinterred there in 1949.

Burial grounds

The northern slope holds the main military cemetery of the Israel Defense Forces, where fallen soldiers, including recipients of Israel's highest decorations, are buried in terraces of uniform graves. On the summit lies Herzl's black granite tomb, beside the graves of Zionist leaders and a "Great Leaders of the Nation" section containing prime ministers Yitzhak Rabin (with his wife Leah), Golda Meir and Levi Eshkol, and presidents including Chaim Weizmann and Zalman Shazar. A separate section commemorates Jewish victims of terrorism, and a memorial marks the missing in action.

Memorials and ceremonies

Mount Herzl Plaza hosts the state ceremony that opens Independence Day, with the lighting of twelve torches. The hill is directly connected to the Yad Vashem Holocaust memorial by a memorial path. Sites at the complex include the Herzl Museum, Mount Herzl Educational Campus, the National Memorial Hall for Israel's Fallen (opened 2017), and memorials to the victims of the 1948 convoy attacks, Ethiopian Jews who died en route to Israel, and others. The Jerusalem Light Rail's Mount Herzl station is the line's western terminus.
