Sensei

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sensei (先生), also written sensai in some romanisations, is a Japanese honorific term literally meaning "person born before another" or "one who comes before". In general usage it is used, with proper formality, after a person's name to mean "teacher" or "master", and is applied to schoolteachers, doctors, lawyers, politicians, clergy, and other authority figures.

Usage

The two characters that make up the term are 先 (sen, "before") and 生 (sei, "life/birth"), together suggesting one who has gone before on the path of life. The word is prefaced by the person's name or used in direct address. It is used both in Japan and, through the spread of Japanese martial arts, in dōjō worldwide, where the sensei is the instructor of the school or style.

In Japanese, sensei is also a title automatically given to certain professions regardless of personal acquaintance: teachers, physicians, dentists, veterinarians, attorneys, and members of the Diet are all addressed as sensei. It can also be used as a standalone noun meaning simply "teacher".

Related terms

A student or junior is a kōhai, while a senior is a senpai. In karate, the sensei's most senior assistant may be called sensei in their own right in some schools, while head instructors may bear titles such as shihan ("master instructor"). The term should not be confused with sensei written with different characters in other contexts, or with the Chinese cognate xiānshēng ("Mr.").
