Flag state

The flag state of a merchant vessel is the jurisdiction under whose laws the vessel is registered or licensed, and is deemed the nationality of the vessel. A merchant vessel must be registered in a state, which then has the right and duty to enforce regulations on vessels registered under its flag, including inspection, certification, and safety and pollution-prevention standards. The concept is codified in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), which provides that ships have the nationality of the state whose flag they are entitled to fly and that there must be a "genuine link" between the ship and that state.

Jurisdiction

On the high seas, a ship is generally subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of its flag state. The flag state is responsible for ensuring compliance with international conventions such as SOLAS and MARPOL, often delegating surveys to classification societies. Port states may also inspect foreign vessels under port state control regimes.

Flags of convenience

Some states operate open registries allowing foreign owners to register vessels for tax, regulatory, or cost reasons — so-called flags of convenience. Panama, Liberia, and the Marshall Islands host the world's largest registries by tonnage. Critics associate open registries with substandard safety and labour conditions, while supporters point to their enforcement records and economic efficiency.
