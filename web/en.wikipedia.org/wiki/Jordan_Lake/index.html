Jordan Lake

Location: Chatham County, North Carolina, U.S.
Type: Reservoir
Surface area: 13,940 acres (56.4 km²)
Created: 1982 (B. Everett Jordan Dam)
Purposes: Flood control, water supply, recreation

B. Everett Jordan Lake—often simply Jordan Lake—is a reservoir in New Hope Valley, west of Cary and south of Durham in Chatham County, North Carolina, United States. Formed by the B. Everett Jordan Dam on the Haw River, the 13,940-acre lake was created by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for flood control following devastating flooding from Hurricane Hazel in 1954, and now also supplies drinking water to municipalities in the Research Triangle region.

History

The New Hope Lake project was authorised by Congress in 1963. Construction of the dam ran from 1967 to 1982, inundating the New Hope valley. The lake was renamed in 1974 for former U.S. senator B. Everett Jordan. Much of the surrounding undeveloped shoreline is managed as the Jordan Lake State Recreation Area and as game lands by the North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission.

Wildlife and recreation

Jordan Lake is noted for its large population of bald eagles—one of the largest summer concentrations in the eastern United States—and for osprey, herons, and migrating waterfowl. The state recreation area offers campgrounds, swim beaches, boat ramps, and hiking trails spread across several access areas along the lake's roughly 180 miles of shoreline. The lake is popular for fishing for largemouth bass, crappie, and catfish.
