European Environment Agency

European Environment Agency (EEA)
Formed: Regulation adopted 1990; operational 1994
Headquarters: Copenhagen, Denmark
Members: EU member states plus other European countries (38 member and cooperating countries)
Task: Independent environmental information and assessments

The European Environment Agency (EEA) is an agency of the European Union whose task is to provide sound, independent information on the environment. Based in Copenhagen, Denmark, it supports sustainable development and helps achieve significant and measurable improvement in Europe's environment through the provision of timely, targeted, relevant and reliable information to policy-makers and the public.

History and membership

The EEA was established by EEC Regulation 1210/1990 and became operational in 1994. Membership is open not only to EU member states but also to other European countries; Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland and Turkey are members, and the six Western Balkan countries cooperate with the agency, bringing the network to 38 countries.

Work

The agency coordinates the European Environment Information and Observation Network (Eionet) and publishes regular assessments, most comprehensively the five-yearly European environment — state and outlook report (SOER). It maintains datasets and indicators on climate change, air and water quality, biodiversity, waste and land use, and serves as a reference centre for European environmental data.
