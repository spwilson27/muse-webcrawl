Ketef Hinnom

Ketef Hinnom, southwest of Jerusalem's Old City

Ketef Hinnom (Hebrew: כתף הינום, "Shoulder of Hinnom") is an archaeological site overlooking the Hinnom Valley, on the southwest shoulder of ancient Jerusalem, near St Andrew's Church and the Jerusalem Cinematheque. The site consists of a series of rock-hewn burial caves dating to the First Temple period.

The silver scrolls

During excavations led by Gabriel Barkay in 1979, two tiny silver scrolls were found in a repository of one of the burial caves. Dated to the late 7th or early 6th century BC — the late First Temple period, just before the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem — the scrolls were laboriously unrolled and found to bear inscriptions in paleo-Hebrew script closely matching the Priestly Blessing from the Book of Numbers ("The LORD bless you and keep you...").

The Ketef Hinnom scrolls are the oldest surviving texts currently known from the Hebrew Bible, and among the earliest extra-biblical attestations of the divine name YHWH. They also contain a phrase echoing Deuteronomy about keeping the commandments.

Burial caves

The caves follow the typical Iron Age Judahite plan: a chamber with rock-cut benches on which bodies were laid, and a repository beneath or behind where bones and grave goods were gathered after decomposition. Despite ancient looting, the repository yielded over a thousand objects, including pottery, jewellery, arrowheads, and the two scrolls.
