"Roll Over Beethoven" is a 1956 hit single written and recorded by Chuck Berry, originally released by Chess Records. One of the most covered songs in rock music, it is a celebration of rock and roll itself: the narrator asks the disc jockey to play rhythm and blues instead of classical music — to "tell Tchaikovsky the news."

Composition and recording

Berry said the song was partly autobiographical, recalling his sister Lucy hogging the family piano to play classical music while he wanted to play boogie-woogie and blues. Berry's recording features his signature guitar introduction, Johnnie Johnson on piano, Willie Dixon on bass, and Fred Below on drums. It reached the US Top 30 and number 2 on the R&B chart.

Legacy and cover versions

The song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and is included in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry. Notable versions include:

  • The Beatles (1963, on With the Beatles, sung by George Harrison)
  • Electric Light Orchestra (1973, an extended version interpolating Beethoven's Fifth Symphony)
  • Carl Perkins, Jerry Lee Lewis, Status Quo, and many others

Its opening lines — "I'm gonna write a little letter, gonna mail it to my local DJ" — and its title's playful challenge to the classical canon made it an anthem of the rock and roll era.
