Csárdás

The csárdás is a traditional Hungarian folk dance, and by extension a style of music, characterised by a dramatic change of tempo: a slow, stately introduction (lassú) gives way to a fast, whirling section (friss). The name derives from csárda, a roadside tavern where the dance was performed.

Origins

The csárdás developed in the nineteenth century from the verbunkos, the recruiting dance used by hussars. By the era of Hungarian nationalism it had become the national dance, danced by couples in folk costume, with improvised figures, heel-clicking and spinning.

In classical music

Composers drew heavily on the form: Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsodies, Brahms's Hungarian Dances, and the famous Csárdás (1904) for violin by Vittorio Monti, originally for mandolin, all use the lassú–friss structure. Csárdás numbers also feature in operettas such as Die Csárdásfürstin by Emmerich Kálmán.
