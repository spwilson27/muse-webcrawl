The Arena of Nîmes (French: Arènes de Nîmes) is a Roman amphitheatre in the city of Nîmes in southern France. Built around 70 AD, shortly after the Colosseum in Rome, it is one of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres in the world and remains in use today for concerts, festivals, and bull-related events.

Architecture

The elliptical arena measures about 133 metres long and 101 metres wide, with an arena floor of roughly 68 by 38 metres. Its two-storey façade, 21 metres high, is pierced by 60 arches on each level and topped by an attic. In antiquity a velarium (awning) could be rigged over the seating to shade spectators. The cavea was divided into sections by social rank, with seating for approximately 24,000 spectators.

History

After the fall of the Roman Empire, the amphitheatre was fortified and filled with houses, becoming a small walled town with chapels and a castle. These dwellings were cleared in the 19th century, when the building was restored for public spectacles. Since 1853 it has hosted bullfights during the city's ferias, and in the modern era it serves as a major concert venue.
