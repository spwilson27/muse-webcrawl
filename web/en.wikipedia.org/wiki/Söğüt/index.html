Söğüt

CountryTurkey
ProvinceBilecik
Historical roleFirst capital of the Ottoman beylik

Söğüt (Ottoman Turkish: سكود) is a town and district of Bilecik Province in the Marmara region of Turkey. It is famed as the birthplace of the Ottoman Empire: tradition holds that Ertuğrul, leader of the Kayı clan of the Oghuz Turks, was granted the area as a frontier fief by the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, and his son Osman I made it the first capital of the nascent Ottoman state around 1299.

History

The town preserves the tomb attributed to Ertuğrul Gazi, honoured each year by commemorative ceremonies (the Ertuğrul Gazi Commemoration and Söğüt Festival) attended by Turkish state officials. Other monuments include the tomb of Sheikh Edebali nearby at Bilecik, the Hamidiye Mosque and the Ertuğrul Gazi museum, which displays ethnographic material of the local Yörük population.

Geography and economy

Söğüt lies in a hilly valley in northwestern Anatolia about 50 km from Eskişehir. Its modern economy rests on agriculture, ceramics and marble; the town's population is roughly 13,000, with some 19,000 in the district.
