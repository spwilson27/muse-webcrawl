Six connexin proteins assemble into a connexon (hemichannel)

Connexins (Cx) are a family of large, structurally related transmembrane proteins that assemble to form vertebrate gap junctions. An intercellular channel is formed when two plasma membranes come into close proximity, allowing direct passage of ions and small molecules between adjacent cells. Each gap junction channel is composed of two connexons (or hemichannels), and each connexon is composed of six connexin subunits. Gap junctions are essential for many physiological processes, including the coordinated depolarisation of cardiac muscle, neuronal synchronisation in some circuits, metabolic coupling, and the regulation of cell growth and differentiation.

Structure and nomenclature

Connexins span the membrane four times, with two extracellular loops containing conserved cysteine residues that participate in docking between connexons. Connexins are named by their predicted molecular mass in kilodaltons — for example, connexin 43 (Cx43), the most widely expressed, is a 43-kDa protein encoded by the GJA1 gene. Humans have 21 connexin genes, classified into alpha (GJA), beta (GJB), gamma (GJC), delta (GJD) and epsilon (GJE) subfamilies. Connexons may be homomeric or heteromeric, and channels homotypic or heterotypic, giving gap junctions diverse conductance and permeability properties. Unpaired hemichannels can also open to the extracellular space, releasing signalling molecules such as ATP.

Disease

Mutations in connexin genes cause a range of human disorders:

  • Cx26 (GJB2) — the commonest cause of hereditary non-syndromic sensorineural deafness, and syndromic deafness with skin disease in other mutations.
  • Cx32 (GJB1) — X-linked Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease.
  • Cx43 (GJA1) — oculodentodigital dysplasia.
  • Cx47 (GJC2) — Pelizaeus–Merzbacher-like disease and hereditary spastic paraplegia.
  • Cx40 and Cx43 variants are associated with atrial fibrillation.

The unrelated innexin family forms gap junctions in invertebrates; vertebrate pannexins are structurally similar to innexins and form large single-membrane channels rather than intercellular junctions.
