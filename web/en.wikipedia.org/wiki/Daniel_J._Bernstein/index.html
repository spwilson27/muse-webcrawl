Daniel J. Bernstein

Daniel Julius Bernstein (born October 29, 1971), known as djb, is an American mathematician, cryptologist, and computer scientist. He is a professor at the University of Illinois Chicago and a visiting professor at Ruhr University Bochum, Germany. Bernstein is known for widely used secure software — including qmail and djbdns — and for influential cryptographic designs such as the Salsa20 and ChaCha stream ciphers, the Curve25519 elliptic curve, and the Ed25519 signature scheme.

Software

In the 1990s Bernstein wrote qmail, a mail transfer agent designed with security as its primary goal, and later the djbdns suite of DNS tools. He offered cash rewards for security holes in his software and famously challenged the U.S. government's export controls on cryptography: his lawsuit, Bernstein v. United States, helped establish that source code is speech protected by the First Amendment, contributing to the liberalisation of encryption export rules.

Cryptography

Bernstein's cryptographic work emphasises speed, simplicity, and resistance to side-channel attacks. His ChaCha cipher (with Peter Schwabe and others' Poly1305 authenticator forming ChaCha20-Poly1305) is deployed in TLS as an alternative to AES-GCM; Curve25519 underlies the widely used X25519 key exchange in protocols such as Signal, WireGuard, and TLS 1.3. He has also contributed to post-quantum cryptography, co-designing the Classic McEliece code-based cryptosystem, and to factoring research, including designs for sieving hardware that stirred debate about the cost of attacking RSA keys.
