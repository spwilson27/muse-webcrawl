The Germanisches Nationalmuseum (GNM) is a museum in Nuremberg devoted to German culture and art from prehistoric times to the present day. Founded in 1852 by the Franconian baron Hans von und zu Aufseß, it is the largest museum of German cultural history, housing a collection of some 1.3 million objects, of which about 25,000 are on display.

Collections

Holdings include the earliest surviving terrestrial globe, Martin Behaim's Erdapfel of 1492, major works by Albrecht Dürer, the Codex Aureus of Echternach, medieval sculpture, historic musical instruments, weapons and armour, scientific instruments, toys, and an extensive archive and library of German art and literature. The museum incorporates the buildings of a former Carthusian monastery, including its cloister and church.

Research

As a member of the Leibniz Association, the GNM is also a research institution, conducting projects in art history, conservation and material culture.
