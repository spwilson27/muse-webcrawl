GS1 is a not-for-profit, international organisation developing and maintaining standards including barcodes. GS1 standards, services and solutions are designed to improve the efficiency, safety and visibility of supply chains across physical and digital channels in 25 sectors. The best-known standard is the barcode — over 10 billion GS1 barcodes are scanned every day worldwide. GS1's identifier keys include the GTIN (Global Trade Item Number, found in UPC and EAN barcodes), GLN (locations), SSCC (logistics units) and GDSN data-synchronisation services.

History

In 1973 the U.S. grocery industry adopted the Universal Product Code (UPC); the first retail scan took place on 26 June 1974 at a Marsh supermarket in Troy, Ohio, when a pack of Wrigley's gum was scanned. The Uniform Code Council administered the UPC in the U.S., while Europe developed the compatible EAN system from 1977. The two organisations merged their governance over time and in 2005 relaunched globally under the single name GS1, with the former UCC becoming GS1 US.

Standards and work

Beyond barcodes (EAN/UPC, GS1-128, GS1 DataMatrix, GS1 QR codes and RFID EPC tags), GS1 maintains the Global Data Synchronisation Network (GDSN), the GS1 Global Registry for verified company and product data, and sector standards used in healthcare (patient safety and traceability), food, retail and transport. Recent initiatives include the migration to two-dimensional "Sunrise 2027" QR codes at point of sale and Digital Product Passports for sustainability regulation.
