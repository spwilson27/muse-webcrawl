Kingdom of Saba
CapitalMarib, later Sirwah
RegionSouth Arabia (modern Yemen)
Periodc. 1200 BCE – 275 CE (debated)

The Kingdom of Saba (Sabaʾ, biblical Sheba) was an ancient South Arabian kingdom centred at Marib in modern Yemen, prosperous from the incense trade.

History and trade

Sabaean caravans carried frankincense and myrrh north to Mesopotamia and the Mediterranean along the Incense Route. The great Marib Dam irrigated extensive agriculture. Sabaean inscriptions in the South Arabian script record kings titled mukarrib and later malik. Saba expanded across South Arabia, planted colonies in Ethiopia, and vied with Qataban, Hadhramaut and Himyar, which absorbed it around 275 CE.

Queen of Sheba

The Bible and Qur'an tell of the Queen of Sheba (Bilqis) visiting King Solomon; Ethiopian tradition made her ancestress of its emperors. No contemporary inscription names her, and historians treat the visit as legend built on real trade contacts.
