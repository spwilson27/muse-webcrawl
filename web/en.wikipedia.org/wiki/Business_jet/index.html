Business jet

A business jet, private jet, or bizjet is a jet aircraft designed for transporting small groups of people, typically business executives and high-net-worth individuals. Business jets are generally designed for faster air travel and more personal comfort than commercial aircraft, and may be adapted for other roles, such as casualty evacuation or express parcel deliveries, and some are used by public bodies, government officials, VIPs or the armed forces.

History

The first purpose-built business jet was the Lockheed JetStar, which first flew in 1957 and entered service in 1961. The Learjet 23, introduced in 1964, popularised the category as a fast, relatively affordable executive transport. Subsequent decades saw the growth of manufacturers including Cessna (Citation series), Dassault (Falcon), Bombardier (Learjet, Challenger, Global), Gulfstream, and Embraer.

Classes

Business jets are commonly grouped by size and range: very light jets (VLJs) seating 4–8 passengers; light jets; mid-size and super mid-size jets; large-cabin and ultra-long-range jets such as the Gulfstream G650 and Bombardier Global 7500, capable of intercontinental flights exceeding 13,000 km; and bizliners converted from airliners, such as the Boeing Business Jet and Airbus Corporate Jet.

Ownership and operation

Business jets are owned outright, shared through fractional ownership programmes such as NetJets, or chartered on demand. Operating costs are far higher per seat than commercial aviation, but proponents cite time savings, schedule flexibility, privacy, and access to thousands of smaller airfields not served by airlines.

Criticism

Private jets attract criticism for their disproportionate carbon emissions per passenger and for tax and regulatory treatment. Their use by celebrities and executives has become a focus of climate activism and media tracking.
