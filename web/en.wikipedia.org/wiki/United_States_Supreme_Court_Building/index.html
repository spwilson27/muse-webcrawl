The Supreme Court Building houses the Supreme Court of the United States. It is located at 1 First Street in Northeast Washington, D.C., in the block immediately east of the United States Capitol. Completed in 1935, it was the first permanent home of the Court, which for its first 146 years had met in chambers within the Capitol.

History

Chief Justice and former President William Howard Taft campaigned for a separate building to assert the Court's independence as a coequal branch of government. Congress authorized the project in 1928, and Taft selected architect Cass Gilbert, designer of the Minnesota State Capitol and the Woolworth Building. Taft and Gilbert both died before its completion. President Herbert Hoover laid the cornerstone on October 13, 1932, and the Court held its first session in the building on October 7, 1935.

Architecture

The building is clad in Vermont marble and rises four stories above ground. The main entrance, up a broad flight of steps on the west facade, features a portico of sixteen Corinthian columns supporting the pediment inscribed with the motto "Equal Justice Under Law." Flanking the steps are James Earle Fraser's marble statues, the Contemplation of Justice and the Authority of Law. Inside, the Great Hall leads to the Courtroom, with its mahogany bench, 24 columns of Italian marble, and friezes by Adolph Weinman depicting lawgivers including Hammurabi, Moses, Solon, Confucius, Muhammad and Napoleon.

Above the Courtroom sit the justices' chambers, conference rooms and the Supreme Court Library. The building also contains a gym on the top floor — sometimes called the "highest court in the land" — with a basketball court.
