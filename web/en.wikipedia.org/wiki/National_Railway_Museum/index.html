The National Railway Museum (NRM) is a museum in York forming part of the British Science Museum Group. It tells the story of rail transport in Britain and its impact on society, and is the home of national collections of historically significant railway vehicles. Admission has been free since 2001.

Collections

The museum houses around 280 locomotives and items of rolling stock, some 6,000 other large objects, and millions of documents, drawings and photographs. Star exhibits include Mallard, the world's fastest steam locomotive; a replica of Stephenson's Rocket; the only surviving Shinkansen bullet train vehicle outside Japan; and a Eurostar power car. Royal trains and the luxurious carriages used by British monarchs from Queen Victoria onwards form a distinctive part of the collection.

History

The museum opened in 1975 on the site of the former York North locomotive depot, bringing together earlier collections from the British Transport Museum at Clapham and the York Railway Museum. Its Great Hall occupies the 1877 locomotive roundhouse. The museum also operates Locomotion at Shildon, County Durham, opened in 2004, and the Search Engine archive and library.
