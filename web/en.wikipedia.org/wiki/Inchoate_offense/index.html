Inchoate offense

An inchoate offense (also inchoate offence or inchoate crime) is a crime of preparing for or seeking to commit another crime. The principal inchoate offences in common-law systems are attempt, conspiracy and solicitation (incitement). Inchoate liability allows law enforcement to intervene before the target offence is completed, and punishes conduct that demonstrates a firm criminal purpose.

Principal forms

Attempt requires an intention to commit the substantive offence plus an act that goes beyond mere preparation — under the American Model Penal Code, a "substantial step" strongly corroborative of criminal purpose. Conspiracy is an agreement between two or more persons to commit an unlawful act, often with an overt act in furtherance. Solicitation consists of commanding, counselling or encouraging another to commit a crime.

Merger and defences

Under the merger doctrine, a defendant generally cannot be convicted of both an inchoate offence and the completed offence arising from the same conduct. Impossibility may be a defence where the intended act is not actually criminal (legal impossibility), while factual impossibility — such as shooting a corpse believed to be alive — is generally not a defence under modern codes.
