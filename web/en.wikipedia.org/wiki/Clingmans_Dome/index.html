Clingmans Dome

Clingmans Dome — known since 2024 by its Cherokee name Kuwohi in official usage restored that year — is a mountain in the Great Smoky Mountains of Tennessee and North Carolina, United States. At 6,643 feet (2,025 m) it is the highest point in Tennessee, in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, and on the Appalachian Trail.

Description

A paved road from Newfound Gap leads to a parking area, from which a steep half-mile trail climbs to a concrete observation tower built in 1959, offering 360-degree views that can exceed 100 miles on clear days — though the summit is cloud-covered much of the year and receives about 85 inches of rain annually. Its spruce–fir forest has been heavily damaged by the balsam woolly adelgid and air pollution.

Name

The Cherokee name Kuwohi ('mulberry place') was restored by the U.S. Board on Geographic Names in September 2024 at the request of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians; the name Clingmans Dome had honoured Confederate general and U.S. senator Thomas Lanier Clingman.
