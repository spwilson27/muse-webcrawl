Pratt & Whitney JT8D

TypeLow-bypass turbofan
ManufacturerPratt & Whitney
First run1960

A JT8D installed on a Boeing 727

The Pratt & Whitney JT8D is a low-bypass turbofan engine introduced in the 1960s. It powered the Boeing 727, Boeing 737-100/-200 and McDonnell Douglas DC-9 and MD-80 series, becoming one of the best-selling jet engines of the twentieth century, with more than 14,000 built.

Design and variants

The JT8D is a two-spool axial-flow turbofan derived in part from the J52 turbojet. Successive variants raised thrust from about 12,250 lbf to over 21,000 lbf in the JT8D-200 series used on the MD-80. Re-engined Super 27 conversions and hush kits kept many JT8D-powered aircraft in service as noise rules tightened.
