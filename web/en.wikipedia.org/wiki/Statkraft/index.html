Statkraft

Statkraft AS is a hydropower company, fully owned by the Norwegian state. The Statkraft Group is a generator of renewable energy, as well as Norway's largest and the Nordic region's third-largest energy producer. Statkraft develops and produces hydropower, wind power, gas power, district heating and solar power, and is also a player in the international energy markets. The company has about 7,000 employees in more than 20 countries.

History

The Norwegian state acquired its first rights to a waterfall in 1895, when it bought the Paulenfoss waterfall in southern Norway in order to provide electricity for the state railways. Over the following decades the state built up a substantial portfolio of waterfalls and power stations, administered through the Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate (NVE). In 1986 the state's power business was separated from the NVE and organized as Statkraft, initially an administrative entity, and in 1992 it was established as a state enterprise (statsforetak) under the name Statkraft SF. In 2004 Statkraft was converted into a limited company, Statkraft AS, wholly owned by the state through the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries.

Operations

Statkraft is Europe's largest generator of renewable energy. Hydropower accounts for the bulk of its production: the company operates several hundred hydropower plants, most of them in Norway, but also in Sweden, Germany, the United Kingdom, and outside Europe in countries such as Peru, Chile, Brazil, India, Nepal and the Philippines. It also has significant investments in onshore wind power, offshore wind (including a share of the Dogger Bank projects), solar power, and gas-fired power in Germany. In Norway, Statkraft also operates district heating plants in several cities.

Statkraft is a major participant in energy trading and origination, with trading offices in Oslo, London, Düsseldorf and Amsterdam, and it supplies power purchase agreements to industrial customers.
