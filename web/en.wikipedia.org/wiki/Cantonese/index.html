Cantonese

Cantonese is a language of the Yue branch of the Sinitic (Chinese) languages, traditionally spoken in Guangdong and Guangxi in southern China, and the dominant language of Hong Kong and Macau.

Features

Standard Cantonese is based on the Guangzhou (Canton) dialect. It preserves final consonants -p, -t, -k, -m lost in Mandarin, and has six tones. It is written with traditional Chinese characters, supplemented by Cantonese-specific characters in informal writing. Unlike Mandarin, it is the medium of instruction and government in Hong Kong, and of a major cinema and Cantopop music industry.

Distribution

Beyond its home region, Cantonese is spoken in overseas Chinatowns historically settled from Guangdong, and in Vietnam, Malaysia and elsewhere. Debates continue over whether Yue varieties are "dialects" of Chinese or separate languages, as they are not mutually intelligible with Mandarin in speech.
