Louis-Hector Berlioz (11 December 1803 – 8 March 1869) was a French Romantic composer and conductor. Largely self-taught in orchestration, he transformed the expressive resources of the orchestra and produced works of unprecedented scale and colour, most famously the Symphonie fantastique (1830). Underappreciated in France during his lifetime, he supported himself as a critic and found his greatest successes conducting in Germany, England and Russia; posterity ranks him among the most original composers of the nineteenth century.

Life

Berlioz was born in La Côte-Saint-André in south-eastern France, the son of a physician who sent him to Paris to study medicine. Abandoning medicine for music, he entered the Paris Conservatoire, studied with Jean-François Le Sueur, and won the Prix de Rome in 1830 after several attempts. That same year he composed the Symphonie fantastique, a programmatic symphony depicting an artist's opium-haunted obsession with an actress, Harriet Smithson, whom he later married. The marriage was unhappy; his later partner was the singer Marie Recio.

Career and works

Berlioz's works include the monumental Grande messe des morts (Requiem, 1837) for vast forces, the dramatic symphony Roméo et Juliette (1839), the opera Benvenuto Cellini (1838), the dramatic legend La damnation de Faust (1846), the Te Deum, and his masterpiece the opera Les Troyens (composed 1856–1858), which was not staged complete in his lifetime. His Treatise on Instrumentation (1844) remains a landmark of orchestrational thought, studied by generations of composers. He also wrote brilliant, combative music criticism and a celebrated Mémoires.

Reception and legacy

Parisian institutions long kept Berlioz at arm's length, and he never obtained the conducting or teaching posts he sought; his income came largely from journalism and foreign tours, where Liszt, and audiences in Weimar, London and St Petersburg, championed him. From the mid-twentieth century, conductors such as Charles Munch and Colin Davis led a revival that restored his operas and sacred works to the repertoire.
