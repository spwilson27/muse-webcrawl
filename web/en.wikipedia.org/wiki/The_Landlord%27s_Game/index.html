The Landlord's Game

The Landlord's Game is a board game patented in 1904 by Elizabeth Magie. Designed to demonstrate the economic principles of Georgism and the harms of land monopolies, it is a direct ancestor of Monopoly.

DesignerElizabeth Magie
Patented1904 (US748626)
GenreEconomics board game

Design

Players travel around a square board buying properties, paying rent and being sent to jail. Magie supplied two rule sets: a monopolist version, in which one player bankrupts the rest, and an anti-monopolist "Prosperity" version in which wealth creation rewards all players — a contrast intended to teach the virtues of a land value tax.

History

The game spread hand-made through communities, including Atlantic City Quakers who localised the street names. Charles Darrow later commercialised a version as Monopoly, sold to Parker Brothers in 1935; Magie's role was largely erased until rediscovered in the 1970s during litigation over the game's origins.
