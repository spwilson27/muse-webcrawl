List of domesticated plants

Domestication of plants began in the Neolithic period, roughly 12,000 years ago, in several independent centres of origin around the world — the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, New Guinea, and parts of Africa and North America among them. Through artificial selection, farmers transformed wild species into the crops on which human civilisation depends. This article lists major domesticated plants grouped by region of domestication and use.

Fertile Crescent (Southwest Asia)

  • Cereals: emmer and einkorn wheat, barley, rye, oats (secondary)
  • Pulses: lentil, pea, chickpea, bitter vetch, faba bean
  • Others: flax, olive, grape, fig, date palm, almond, pistachio

East and South Asia

  • Rice (Asian rice, Oryza sativa), foxtail and broomcorn millet, soybean, adzuki bean, buckwheat
  • Tea, peach, apricot, citrus (mandarin, orange), lychee, mango (South Asia), banana and plantain (New Guinea/Southeast Asia), coconut, taro, sugarcane (New Guinea), hemp, cucumber, eggplant (brinjal)

The Americas

  • Maize (corn), common bean, squash and pumpkins (the "Three Sisters" of Mesoamerica)
  • Potato and oca (Andes), sweet potato, cassava (manioc), peanut, quinoa, tomato, peppers (Capsicum), cacao, tobacco, cotton, sunflower (North America), pineapple, papaya, avocado, vanilla

Africa

  • Sorghum, pearl millet, finger millet, African rice (Oryza glaberrima), yam, cowpea, oil palm, coffee (Ethiopia), teff, okra, watermelon

Later and industrial-era domestications

  • Sugar beet (18th–19th century), rubber (Hevea), strawberry (hybrid, 18th century), kiwifruit (20th century), blueberry and cranberry (20th century), macadamia, triticale (a human-made wheat–rye hybrid), canola (bred from rapeseed in the 1970s)

Just a handful of crops — rice, wheat, and maize above all — now supply the majority of humanity's calories, a concentration that makes the world's food supply both efficient and vulnerable.
