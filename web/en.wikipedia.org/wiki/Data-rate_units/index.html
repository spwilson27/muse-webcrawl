Data-rate units

In telecommunications and computing, bit rate is the number of bits conveyed or processed per unit of time. Data rates are commonly expressed in multiples of the bit per second (bit/s), using decimal SI prefixes in telecommunications and, in some computing contexts, binary prefixes.

Typical data rates span from kilobits per second for legacy modems to terabits per second for backbone links.

Bytes and binary prefixes

Data rates may also be expressed in bytes per second (B/s), where one byte equals eight bits. File-transfer rates are often shown this way, which can cause confusion when compared with link speeds quoted in bits per second. Binary prefixes (kibi-, mebi-, gibi-) denote multiples of 1,024 and are standardized by the IEC; they are used mainly for quantities of data rather than rates.

Gross and net rates

The gross bit rate is the total rate including protocol overhead, error-correction codes and framing, while the net bit rate (throughput) counts only useful payload data. Related measures include baud rate (symbols per second) and channel capacity as bounded by the Shannon–Hartley theorem.
