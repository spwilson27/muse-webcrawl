MilkyWay@home models the structure of the Milky Way, including stellar streams torn from dwarf galaxies.

MilkyWay@home is a volunteer computing project based at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) that runs on the BOINC (Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing) platform. Volunteers donate idle computing time on their personal computers and graphics cards to help create a highly accurate three-dimensional model of the Milky Way galaxy.

Scientific goals

The project uses data from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey to study stellar streams — elongated trails of stars stripped from dwarf galaxies and globular clusters as they are tidally disrupted by the Milky Way. By fitting models of these streams, particularly the Sagittarius stream, MilkyWay@home's volunteers help measure the distribution of mass, including dark matter, in the galactic halo, and constrain the galaxy's gravitational potential and formation history.

The project has two main applications: one optimises models of stellar streams against survey data using evolutionary and particle-swarm algorithms, and another runs N-body simulations of dwarf galaxies colliding with the Milky Way to reproduce the observed streams.

Computing

MilkyWay@home was among the first BOINC projects to make heavy use of GPU computing, with applications for NVIDIA and AMD graphics processors delivering large speedups over CPU-only computation. At its peak, the combined computing power donated to the project rivalled that of contemporary supercomputers. Results produced with the project's data have been published in astrophysics and computer science venues.
