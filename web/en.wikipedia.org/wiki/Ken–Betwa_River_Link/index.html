Ken–Betwa River Link

The Ken and Betwa rivers in Bundelkhand

The Ken–Betwa River Link is a river-interlinking project in India that aims to transfer surplus water from the Ken River to the Betwa River, both tributaries of the Yamuna, to provide irrigation and drinking water to the drought-prone Bundelkhand region spanning Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. It is the first project to be implemented under India's National Perspective Plan for interlinking of rivers.

Project description

The project's centrepiece is the Daudhan dam on the Ken River in Madhya Pradesh, together with a link canal of about 221 kilometres carrying water to the Betwa basin. The project is intended to irrigate roughly 9 lakh hectares (about 900,000 hectares) of land, supply drinking water to around 6.2 million people, and generate hydropower. The Union Cabinet approved the project in December 2021 at an estimated cost of about ₹44,605 crore, with a target completion within about eight years.

Criticism and environmental concerns

The project has drawn sustained criticism from environmentalists because a substantial part of the Panna Tiger Reserve would be submerged by the Daudhan reservoir, threatening tiger habitat as well as the habitat of gharials in the Ken Gharial Sanctuary and vulture nesting sites. Critics have also questioned the hydrological assumption that the Ken basin has surplus water, and raised concerns about downstream effects and the displacement of villages.

History

The proposal dates to the 1980s, when the National Water Development Agency was established to study inter-basin transfers. A memorandum of understanding between Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and the central government was signed in 2005, and detailed project reports were prepared over the following decade. The foundation stone for construction was laid in December 2024.
