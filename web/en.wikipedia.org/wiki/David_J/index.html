David J (born David John Haskins, 24 April 1957) is an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as the bassist of the gothic rock pioneers Bauhaus and later of Love and Rockets. His melodic, dub-influenced bass playing and dry, literate songwriting were central to both bands, and he has maintained a parallel solo career spanning folk, cabaret and experimental music.

Bauhaus

Haskins adopted the stage name David J — to avoid confusion with his younger brother Kevin Haskins, the band's drummer — when Bauhaus formed in Northampton in 1978. Their debut single "Bela Lugosi's Dead" (1979) became a founding document of gothic rock. David J wrote or co-wrote songs including "Kick in the Eye" and, on his own, much of the band's later material; the band split in 1983 after the album Burning from the Inside, and has reunited for tours and recordings several times since, notably in 1998, 2005–2008 and 2019–2022.

Love and Rockets and solo work

With Daniel Ash and Kevin Haskins he formed Love and Rockets, whose self-titled 1989 album produced the US hit "So Alive". His solo albums include Etiquette of Violence (1983) and Songs from Another Season (1990), the latter containing "I'll Be Your Chauffeur", and he has collaborated extensively with the writer Alan Moore — the two grew up in Northampton — in the Moon and Serpent Grand Egyptian Theatre of Marvels. He has also written for the stage and published a memoir, Who Killed Mister Moonlight? (2014), about his Bauhaus years.
