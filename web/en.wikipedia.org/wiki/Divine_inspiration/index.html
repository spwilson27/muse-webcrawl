Divine inspiration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Divine inspiration is the concept of a supernatural force, typically a deity, causing a person or people to experience a creative desire or insight. It has been a commonly reported aspect of many religions for thousands of years. Divine inspiration is often closely tied to the concept of revelation: information or knowledge being imparted to a person by the divine.

In religion

In the Abrahamic religions, inspiration is central to the authority of scripture. Biblical inspiration is the doctrine in Christian theology that the human writers of the Bible were guided by the Holy Spirit so that their writings may be designated the word of God; views range from verbal plenary inspiration, holding every word to be inspired, to more limited models in which the ideas but not the wording are inspired. In Judaism, the Torah is traditionally held to have been dictated by God to Moses, and the prophets to have spoken under divine influence. In Islam, the Qur'an is held to have been revealed verbatim by God to Muhammad through the angel Gabriel, a process known as wāhy.

In art and literature

In ancient Greek culture, inspiration came from the Muses, goddesses invoked by poets at the opening of works such as the Iliad and Odyssey. Plato described poetic inspiration as a kind of divine madness in which the poet is a passive vessel. The literal etymology of "inspiration" – a breathing-in, from the Latin inspirare – reflects the idea of a spirit entering the person. The concept persisted in ideas of artistic genius and remains a live topic in philosophy of religion and creativity research, where sudden insight is also studied in psychological terms such as incubation and illumination.
