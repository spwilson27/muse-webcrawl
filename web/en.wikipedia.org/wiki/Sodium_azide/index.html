Sodium azide


Formula: NaN3
Molar mass: 65.01 g/mol
Appearance: White crystalline solid
Density: 1.846 g/cm3
Hazards: Highly toxic, explosive under certain conditions

Sodium azide is an inorganic compound with the formula NaN3. This colorless salt is the gas-forming component in many car airbag systems. It is used for the preparation of other azide compounds and is a valuable reagent in chemical synthesis and a preservative in laboratories. It is highly toxic.

Structure and preparation

Sodium azide is an ionic solid consisting of sodium cations and linear azide anions (N3−). It is prepared industrially by the Wislicenus process, reacting sodium amide with nitrous oxide at elevated temperature; alternatively it can be made from sodium nitrite and hydrazine.

Applications

In airbag systems, an electrical signal ignites sodium azide, which rapidly decomposes to sodium metal and nitrogen gas that inflates the bag; a subsequent reaction with potassium nitrate and silicon dioxide converts the reactive sodium into inert silicate glass. In laboratories, sodium azide is used as a preservative in reagents and as a reagent for introducing azide groups, which can be reduced to amines or undergo click chemistry.

Safety

Sodium azide is acutely toxic, comparable to cyanides; it inhibits cytochrome c oxidase and can be absorbed through the skin. With acids it releases highly toxic hydrazoic acid gas, and with heavy metals it forms explosive azides, a hazard in plumbing systems.
