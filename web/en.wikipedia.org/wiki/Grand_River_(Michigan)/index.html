The Grand River is a 252-mile-long (406 km) river in the Lower Peninsula of the U.S. state of Michigan, the longest river entirely within the state. It rises in Jackson County in southern Michigan, flows generally northwest through Lansing and Grand Rapids, and empties into Lake Michigan at Grand Haven. Its watershed, the largest in Michigan, drains some 5,572 square miles.

Course

The Grand River begins near the boundary of Jackson and Hillsdale counties and winds through the cities of Jackson, Eaton Rapids, Lansing — where it is joined by the Red Cedar River — Ionia, and Grand Rapids, Michigan's second-largest city, which grew up at the river's rapids. Below Grand Rapids it passes through the Coopersville area and the Grand River estuary before reaching Lake Michigan at Grand Haven.

History

Known to the Odawa and Ojibwe peoples as O-wash-ta-nong ("far-away water"), the river was a major fur-trade route in the French and British colonial periods. In the nineteenth century it carried logs, gypsum and manufactured goods, and its rapids powered mills at Grand Rapids. Severe flooding in 1904 and again in 2013 caused extensive damage in the Grand Rapids area.

Ecology and recreation

The river supports popular sport fisheries, including salmon and steelhead runs that surmount the Fish Ladder in downtown Grand Rapids. Major tributaries include the Red Cedar, Looking Glass, Maple, Flat and Thornapple rivers. Efforts continue to restore the namesake rapids in Grand Rapids for habitat and whitewater recreation.
