Aare

The Aare is the longest river that both rises and ends entirely within Switzerland. Its total length is about 295 kilometres (183 mi), and it drains most of the Swiss Plateau. The Aare rises in the Aare glaciers of the Bernese Alps, flows through Lake Brienz and Lake Thun, passes the Swiss capital Bern — whose old town occupies a peninsula in a loop of the river — and continues through Aarau and Brugg before joining the Rhine at Koblenz, opposite Waldshut in Germany.

Course

From the Unteraar and Oberaar glaciers above the Grimsel Pass, the Aare descends through the Hasli valley to Meiringen — near the Aare Gorge (Aareschlucht) — and flows into Lake Brienz, then through Interlaken into Lake Thun. Below Thun it turns northwest to Bern, then northeast past Solothurn, where it receives the Emme, and on to Aarau. Near Brugg it is joined by the Reuss and Limmat in the so-called "Wasserschloss" (water castle) of Switzerland before flowing north to the Rhine.

Use

The river is used for hydroelectric power (including run-of-river plants and storage schemes in its headwaters), navigation is limited to lakes and short stretches, and in Bern swimming in the fast-flowing Aare is a celebrated local summer tradition. The river has also seen major floods, notably in 2005.
