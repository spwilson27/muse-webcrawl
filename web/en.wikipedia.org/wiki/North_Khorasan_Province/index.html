North Khorasan Province (Persian: استان خراسان شمالی) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the northeast of the country. Its capital is the city of Bojnord. The province was created in 2004 when the former Khorasan Province was divided into three provinces: North Khorasan, Razavi Khorasan, and South Khorasan.

Geography

The province borders Turkmenistan to the north and the Iranian provinces of Golestan, Semnan, and Razavi Khorasan. It is crossed by the Kopet Dag and Aladagh mountain ranges, and the Atrek River flows along part of the northern frontier. Parts of the province are semi-arid steppe.

People

North Khorasan is ethnically diverse. Its population includes Persians, Khorasani Turks, Kurds (many deported here in the Safavid era to defend the frontier against Uzbek raids), Turkmen, and Baloch. Persian is the lingua franca, while North Khorasan Kurdish (Kurmanji) and Khorasani Turkish are widely spoken in rural districts. The province is sometimes called a "treasure of cultures" for this diversity.

Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay: wheat, barley, grapes, apples, and — notably — barberry and saffron in the south. The province is also known for Turkmen horse breeding and the Khorasani Kurdish sport of wrestling with chukheh (traditional costume wrestling), performed at Nowruz gatherings.
