Cholesteric liquid crystal

A cholesteric liquid crystal, also known as a chiral nematic liquid crystal, is a type of liquid crystal with a helical structure that is therefore chiral. Cholesteric liquid crystals are composed of nematic mesogenic molecules containing a chiral center which produces intermolecular forces that favor alignment between molecules at a slight angle to one another.

Structure

The structure of cholesteric liquid crystals is characterized by a twist of the director perpendicular to an axis, with the molecular orientation rotating in a helix. The distance over which the director rotates by a full 360 degrees is called the pitch. Because the periodic helical structure has a period comparable to the wavelength of visible light, cholesteric materials selectively reflect light, producing vivid structural colors.

Applications

Because the pitch depends on temperature, cholesteric liquid crystals change color with temperature and are used in thermometers and mood rings. They are also used in reflective displays, smart windows, and optical sensors.

History

The cholesteric phase was first observed in derivatives of cholesterol, from which the name derives, although the materials used today are usually synthetic chiral nematics not chemically related to cholesterol.
