Code of Points (gymnastics)

Artistic gymnastics is scored under the FIG Code of Points

Governing body: International Gymnastics Federation (FIG)

Applies to: Artistic gymnastics (separate codes exist for rhythmic, trampoline, acrobatic and aerobic gymnastics)

Current cycle: 2025–2028

The Code of Points is the rule book that governs scoring in artistic gymnastics, published by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG). It defines the difficulty values of every recognised element, the composition requirements for routines on each apparatus, the deductions for errors in execution, and the procedures judges follow. Separate codes exist for men's and women's artistic gymnastics, and the code is revised for each Olympic cycle.

The modern scoring system

Until 2005, routines were scored against the famous "perfect 10.0" maximum. After controversies at the 2004 Athens Olympics, the FIG adopted an open-ended system, effective from 2006, in which the final score is the sum of two components:

Difficulty (D) score: The sum of the values of the gymnast's hardest elements, plus connection value and composition requirements. Element difficulty is graded by letter (A = 0.1 up through J or beyond). There is no theoretical maximum.

Execution (E) score: Starting from 10.0, judges deduct for errors of form, technique and artistry — falls cost 1.00, large steps 0.3, bent knees 0.1–0.5, and so on. Neutral deductions may be taken for boundary violations, overtime or attire.

Named elements

A gymnast who is the first to perform a new element successfully at a major competition may have it named after them in the Code (for example, the Biles on vault and floor). Named elements generally receive high difficulty values, and the eponymous rules have become a notable part of the sport's culture.

Criticism

The open-ended system has been criticised for rewarding difficulty at the expense of clean execution, for making scores harder for casual audiences to interpret than the old 10.0 system, and for encouraging gymnasts to attempt risky skills. Successive revisions of the Code have sought to rebalance difficulty and execution and to strengthen artistry requirements, particularly in the women's code.
