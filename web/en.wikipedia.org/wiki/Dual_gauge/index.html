Dual gauge

Dual gauge (also called mixed gauge) is a railway track configuration that allows trains of two different track gauges to use the same track. The most common arrangement uses three rails: one common rail and one additional rail for each gauge, so that, for example, both standard gauge (1,435 mm) and a broad or narrow gauge can operate over the same formation.

Applications

Dual gauge track is used where lines of different gauges meet or share a corridor, such as at border stations between countries with different national gauges, on shared approach tracks to ports and terminals, and in regions — notably parts of Australia, India, and Spain — where gauge standardisation is incomplete. Four-rail layouts also exist, typically where the two gauges are too close in width for a three-rail arrangement, or on bridges and in tunnels where the two tracks are centred on the same axis.

Operational issues

Dual gauge complicates turnouts (points), which must guide wheels of both gauges, and track maintenance, since wear patterns differ. Speed restrictions may apply. Alternatives include bogie exchange, variable-gauge axles, and transshipment of freight. Gauge conversion projects, such as Australia's progressive standardisation and India's Project Unigauge, have removed many dual-gauge sections, but the arrangement remains common where conversion is impractical.
