Dichroic filter

Image: Dichroic filters

A dichroic filter, thin-film filter, or interference filter is a colour filter used to selectively pass light of a small range of colours while reflecting other colours. By contrast, ordinary coloured filters are absorptive: they absorb unwanted wavelengths and convert them to heat, which can damage the filter and limits the sharpness of the transition between passed and blocked bands.

Principle

Dichroic filters are built from stacks of optical thin-film layers with differing refractive indices deposited on a glass substrate. The layers produce constructive and destructive interference for specific wavelengths, so the wavelength bands transmitted and reflected depend on the layer thicknesses and the angle of incidence. Because unwanted light is reflected rather than absorbed, dichroic filters can handle high light intensities and have precise, steep spectral edges.

Applications

Dichroic filters and mirrors are used in fluorescence microscopy (as excitation filters, emission filters, and dichroic beamsplitters), in LCD and DLP projectors to split and recombine colour channels, in stage and architectural lighting, in laser systems, and in optical instruments. A dichroic prism performs a similar separating function in cameras.
