John Henry Constantine Whitehead FRS (11 November 1904 – 8 May 1960), known as Henry Whitehead, was a British mathematician and one of the founders of homotopy theory. He was born in India, the son of the Bishop of Madras; his uncle was the philosopher Alfred North Whitehead.

Life and work

Whitehead was educated at Eton and Balliol College, Oxford, where he first worked in the Foreign Office before returning to mathematics. He did his doctorate at Princeton University under Oswald Veblen, writing on differential geometry. With Veblen he co-authored The Foundations of Differential Geometry (1932).

In the 1930s Whitehead turned to topology. He introduced the notion of the CW complex, which became a standard setting for homotopy theory, proved Whitehead's theorem — that a weak homotopy equivalence between CW complexes is a homotopy equivalence — and defined the Whitehead product and Whitehead torsion. The Whitehead manifold, an exotic contractible 3-manifold not homeomorphic to Euclidean space, is also named for him.

During the Second World War he worked on operations research for the Admiralty and at Bletchley Park. In 1947 he became Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics at Oxford, where he built a leading school of topology. His collected works were published in four volumes as The Mathematical Works of J. H. C. Whitehead. He died suddenly in 1960 while visiting Princeton.
