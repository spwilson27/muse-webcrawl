Polynomial hierarchy

In computational complexity theory, the polynomial hierarchy (sometimes abbreviated PH) is a hierarchy of complexity classes generalising the classes P, NP, and co-NP. Each level of the hierarchy consists of problems solvable in polynomial time with a bounded number of alternations of existential and universal quantification, equivalently by oracle Turing machines with access to the level below.

The levels of the polynomial hierarchy, generalising NP and co-NP.

Definition

The zeroth level is P. The first level consists of NP (Σ1p) and co-NP (Π1p); higher levels Σk+1p are defined as NP with an oracle for Σkp, with Π and Δ classes defined symmetrically. The union of all levels is PH. Complete problems for the second level include quantified Boolean formula variants with one alternation and problems such as certain succinct versions of NP-complete problems.

Properties

PH is contained in PSPACE; by Toda's theorem, PH is contained in P#P. It is widely believed that the hierarchy does not collapse: if any level equalled the one below it, all higher levels would equal it as well, and if P = NP the whole hierarchy collapses to P. The hierarchy is the polynomial-time analogue of the arithmetical hierarchy of computability theory.
