Sinitic languages

The Sinitic languages, often synonymous with the Chinese languages, are a branch of the Sino-Tibetan language family spoken natively by the Han Chinese majority and many ethnic minorities in China and the Chinese diaspora.

Classification

Sinitic is usually divided into seven to ten major groups, including Mandarin, Wu, Yue (Cantonese), Min, Hakka, Gan and Xiang. These groups are often mutually unintelligible in spoken form, though they share the Chinese written tradition. There is ongoing debate over whether the varieties are "dialects" of a single Chinese language or separate languages; linguistically they span a diversity comparable to the Romance languages.

History

The family descends from Old Chinese, documented from the second millennium BCE, through Middle Chinese to the modern varieties. Standard Chinese, based on the Beijing dialect of Mandarin, is the official language of China and Taiwan and one of the official languages of Singapore.
