Hyperbolic trajectory

In astrodynamics or celestial mechanics, a hyperbolic trajectory or hyperbolic orbit is the trajectory of any object around a central body with more than enough velocity to escape the central body's gravitational pull. The name derives from Newton announcing in the Principia that such trajectories are shaped like a hyperbola, following from Kepler's laws applied to conic sections.

A hyperbolic trajectory has an orbital eccentricity greater than 1 and a negative semi-major axis. A body on such a trajectory approaches the central body, is deflected around it, and departs with a residual excess velocity — the hyperbolic excess velocity, often denoted v∞ — that it keeps at infinite distance.

Parameters

Like an elliptical orbit, a hyperbolic orbit is defined by its semi-major axis (negative for hyperbolas), eccentricity, inclination, longitude of the ascending node, argument of periapsis and true anomaly. The shape of the hyperbola is governed by the impact parameter and the deflection angle, which depends on the body's approach speed and the central body's gravitational parameter.

Applications

Hyperbolic trajectories describe planetary gravity assist flybys: a spacecraft entering a planet's sphere of influence follows a hyperbola relative to that planet, gaining or losing heliocentric energy in the process. They also describe interstellar objects such as ʻOumuamua and 2I/Borisov passing through the Solar System, and any body that has been gravitationally scattered onto an escape path.

Radial hyperbolic trajectory

A radial hyperbolic trajectory is the degenerate case in which the orbit is a straight line: the object falls directly toward, or is ejected directly away from, the central body.
