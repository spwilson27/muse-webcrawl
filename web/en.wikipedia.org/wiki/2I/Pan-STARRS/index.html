Pan-STARRS

The Panoramic Survey Telescope and Rapid Response System (Pan-STARRS) is an astronomical survey conducted by the University of Hawai'i Institute for Astronomy using telescopes on Haleakalā, Maui. Its primary mission is to detect near-Earth objects that threaten impact with Earth, but it also maps large fractions of the sky repeatedly.

Telescopes

The first telescope, PS1, began full-time science operations in 2010 with a 1.4-gigapixel camera, at the time the largest digital camera in the world. A second telescope, PS2, followed. The wide field of view allows Pan-STARRS to image three-quarters of the visible sky multiple times per month.

Discoveries

Pan-STARRS has discovered large numbers of asteroids, comets, and supernovae. Its most famous find is 1I/'Oumuamua, discovered by Robert Weryk in October 2017 — the first interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System. Pan-STARRS data also contributed to the cataloguing of many Kuiper belt objects and distant supernovae used in cosmology.
