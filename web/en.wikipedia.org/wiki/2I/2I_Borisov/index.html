2I/Borisov

2I/Borisov, originally designated C/2019 Q4 (Borisov), is the first observed interstellar comet and the second observed interstellar object overall, after 1I/ʻOumuamua. It was discovered on August 30, 2019, by the Crimean amateur astronomer and telescope maker Gennadiy Borisov, using a 0.65-metre telescope he built himself.

Trajectory

The comet follows a strongly hyperbolic orbit with an eccentricity of about 3.36, far above 1, proving it is not gravitationally bound to the Sun. It reached perihelion at about 2.006 AU on December 8, 2019, and passed closest to Earth at about 1.94 AU later that month, traveling at roughly 32 km/s relative to the Sun at perihelion.

Composition

Unlike the enigmatic ʻOumuamua, Borisov behaved like an ordinary solar-system comet, with a coma and tail of dust and gas. Spectroscopy showed it was unusually rich in carbon monoxide, and later analysis suggested it may be an exceptionally pristine object — possibly never having passed close to any star before. In March 2020 astronomers observed the comet shedding a large fragment.
