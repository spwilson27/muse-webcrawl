Haleakalā National Park is an American national park on the island of Maui in Hawaii. It preserves the summit and flanks of the Haleakalā volcano, from the stark volcanic landscape of the summit depression down through cloud forest to the lush Kīpahulu coast with its waterfalls and freshwater pools.

Districts

The park has two main districts, not connected by road: the Summit District, famous for sunrise viewing above a sea of clouds, and the coastal Kīpahulu District near Hana. Together they protect more endangered species than any other United States national park, including the silversword plant and the nēnē (Hawaiian goose).

Designations

Haleakalā was first protected as part of Hawaii National Park in 1916 and became a separate national park in 1961. It is also an International Biosphere Reserve and was designated an International Dark Sky Park in recognition of its exceptionally clear night skies.
