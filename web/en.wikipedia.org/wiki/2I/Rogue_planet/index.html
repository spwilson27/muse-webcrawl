Rogue planet

A rogue planet, also called a free-floating planet, interstellar planet, or orphan planet, is a planetary-mass object that orbits a galactic centre directly rather than a star. Such worlds are thought to have been ejected from the planetary systems in which they formed, or to have formed in isolation by the collapse of small gas clouds, similar to sub-brown dwarfs.

Detection

Lacking starlight, rogue planets are extremely difficult to see. Most candidates have been found by gravitational microlensing surveys such as OGLE and MOA, which catch the brief brightening of a background star as a foreground planet's gravity bends its light. A few young, hot candidates in nearby star-forming regions have been directly imaged. Analysis of microlensing statistics suggests free-floating planets may be very numerous — potentially comparable in number to stars in the Milky Way.

Properties

A sufficiently massive rogue planet with a thick hydrogen atmosphere could in principle retain internal heat and maintain liquid water beneath an ice crust, leading to speculation about subsurface habitability. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, planned for launch in the late 2020s, is expected to conduct a dedicated microlensing census of these objects.
