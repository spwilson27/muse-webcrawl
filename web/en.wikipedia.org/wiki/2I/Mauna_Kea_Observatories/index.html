Mauna Kea Observatories

LocationMauna Kea, Hawai'i, United States
Altitude4,205 m (13,796 ft)
Established1967

The Mauna Kea Observatories (MKO) are a group of independent astronomical research facilities located on the summit of Mauna Kea on the island of Hawai'i. The summit is one of the world's premier sites for ground-based astronomy because of its high altitude, dry air, stable atmosphere, and isolation from light pollution.

Facilities

The site hosts more than a dozen telescopes operated by institutions from many countries, including the twin 10-metre Keck telescopes, the 8.2-metre Subaru Telescope, the Canada–France–Hawaii Telescope, the Gemini North telescope, and the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope for submillimetre astronomy.

Observing conditions

Mauna Kea sits above much of the atmosphere's water vapour, giving exceptionally clear skies in both optical and infrared wavelengths. The planned Thirty Meter Telescope has been controversial because the summit is sacred in Native Hawaiian culture, prompting sustained protests and legal challenges.

Discoveries

Observatories on Mauna Kea contributed to observations of the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov and numerous exoplanet and cosmology studies.
