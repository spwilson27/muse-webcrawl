The Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST) is a solar telescope on the summit of Haleakalā on the Hawaiian island of Maui. With its 4-metre primary mirror it is the largest solar telescope in the world, and it can resolve features on the Sun as small as about 20 kilometres across.

Science goals

DKIST studies the Sun's magnetic field, which drives sunspots, flares and coronal mass ejections. Its instruments measure magnetic fields in the corona directly — something no earlier telescope could do routinely — improving forecasts of space weather that can affect satellites, power grids and communications on Earth.

Name

The telescope is named for Daniel K. Inouye, the long-serving United States Senator from Hawaii. It released its first images, showing boiling plasma cells on the solar surface, in early 2020.
