Hyperbolic asteroid

A hyperbolic asteroid is a small Solar System body on a hyperbolic trajectory — an orbit with an eccentricity greater than 1 that is not bound to the Sun. Such an object either originates outside the Solar System (an interstellar object) or is a Solar System body that has been gravitationally perturbed onto an escape path, for example by a close encounter with Jupiter.

Examples

The best-known hyperbolic asteroid is 1I/ʻOumuamua (2017), the first detected interstellar object, whose eccentricity of about 1.2 and high inbound velocity showed it came from beyond the Solar System. A handful of native objects are also temporarily hyperbolic: the asteroid A/2018 V3, for example, is a Solar System body on an ejection trajectory. Distinguishing true interstellar visitors requires checking whether the excess velocity could instead be explained by planetary perturbations or non-gravitational forces such as outgassing or the Yarkovsky effect.

Designation

Interstellar objects receive a permanent "I" number (1I, 2I, 3I, ...). Bodies merely on hyperbolic escape paths retain conventional minor-planet designations, sometimes with the "A/" prefix used for objects with asteroid-like orbits but comet-like characteristics.
