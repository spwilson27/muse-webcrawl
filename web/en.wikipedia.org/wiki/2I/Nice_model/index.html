Nice model

The Nice model is a scenario for the dynamical evolution of the Solar System, named after the city of Nice in France, where it was developed in 2005. It proposes that the giant planets formed in a compact configuration and later migrated to their current orbits after the dispersal of the gas disk.

Description

In the model, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune originally orbited much closer together, surrounded by a massive disk of planetesimals. Slow migration driven by scattering of those planetesimals eventually triggered an instability: the ice giants were thrown outward, ploughing through the disk and scattering comets and asteroids throughout the system.

Implications

The instability is invoked to explain the Late Heavy Bombardment of the inner planets, the capture of Jupiter's Trojan asteroids, the structure of the Kuiper belt, and the capture of irregular moons. The scattering phase would also have ejected enormous numbers of comets into interstellar space, seeding other systems with visitors analogous to 2I/Borisov.

Later developments

Subsequent work, including the "jumping Jupiter" variant and the Nice 2 model, refined the scenario to match the terrestrial planets' survival and the asteroid belt's composition. Some versions invoke a fifth giant planet that was ejected entirely.
