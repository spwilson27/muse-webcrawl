2I/Borisov is the first identified interstellar comet, and the second observed interstellar object after 1I/ʻOumuamua. It was discovered on 30 August 2019 by the amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov at the MARGO observatory in Crimea, using a telescope he built himself.

Trajectory

Borisov followed a strongly hyperbolic orbit with an eccentricity of about 3.36, proving that it is not gravitationally bound to the Sun and originated around another star. It passed perihelion at just over 2 AU from the Sun on 8 December 2019 and made its closest approach to Earth later that month.

Composition

Unlike ʻOumuamua, Borisov showed a conventional coma and tail of dust and gas, closely resembling comets native to the Solar System. Spectroscopic observations found it unusually rich in carbon monoxide, offering clues about the cold outer regions of the protoplanetary disk in which it formed.
