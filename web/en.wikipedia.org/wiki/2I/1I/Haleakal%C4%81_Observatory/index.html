Haleakalā Observatory

LocationSummit of Haleakalā, Maui, Hawaii, US
Altitude3,050 m (10,000 ft)
Established1961
Notable instrumentsPan-STARRS, Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope, AEOS

Haleakalā Observatory, also known as the Haleakalā High Altitude Observatory Site, is Hawaii's first astronomical research observatory. It stands near the summit of Haleakalā, the dormant volcano that forms most of Maui, at an altitude of about 3,050 metres, where the air is exceptionally clear, dry, and dark — conditions that place the site among the finest in the world for both night-time and solar astronomy.

History

The site was developed by the University of Hawaii beginning in 1961 with a solar observatory built to study the Sun's corona. Its strategic value also attracted the US Department of Defense, which uses Haleakalā for satellite tracking and laser ranging; the Air Force's 3.67 m Advanced Electro Optical System (AEOS) telescope is among the largest at the site.

Telescopes

The observatory hosts the Pan-STARRS system of wide-field survey telescopes, used to search for asteroids, comets, and variable stars; the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST), the world's largest solar telescope with a 4-metre mirror, completed in 2020; the Faulkes Telescope North, part of the Las Cumbres Observatory network; and numerous space-surveillance instruments.

Discoveries

Haleakalā's Pan-STARRS 1 telescope discovered 1I/ʻOumuamua in October 2017, the first known interstellar object, along with hundreds of near-Earth asteroids and numerous comets. The site lies within a landscape sacred to Native Hawaiians, and construction of new facilities, including DKIST, has been the subject of protest and cultural consultation.
