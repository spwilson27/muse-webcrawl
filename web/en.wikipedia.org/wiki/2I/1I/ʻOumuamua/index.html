ʻOumuamua is the first known interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System. Formally designated 1I/2017 U1, it was discovered by Robert Weryk using the Pan-STARRS telescope at Haleakalā Observatory, Hawaii, on 19 October 2017, approximately 40 days after it passed its closest point to the Sun. When first sighted it was about 33 million kilometres from Earth and already moving away.

Interstellar origin

Based on its trajectory, ʻOumuamua entered the Solar System on a strongly hyperbolic orbit with an eccentricity of 1.201 — far too high to have been produced by perturbations from the planets — approaching from roughly the direction of the star Vega in the constellation Lyra at about 26 km/s relative to the Sun. The "1I" designation created a new class for interstellar objects; the name, chosen by the Pan-STARRS team and approved by the International Astronomical Union, is Hawaiian for a scout or messenger sent ahead to reach out.

Physical characteristics

ʻOumuamua showed no visible coma or tail, yet its trajectory displayed a small non-gravitational acceleration consistent with outgassing. Its light curve varied dramatically, implying tumbling motion and an extremely elongated or flattened shape — early estimates suggested a cigar-like body roughly 230 metres long, though later analyses favoured a flattened disc-like shape. Its surface was dark and reddened, similar to organic-rich outer Solar System bodies.

Interpretations

Proposed explanations for its properties include a fragment of a nitrogen-ice exo-Pluto, a hydrogen-ice iceberg, and a cometary body producing an invisible hydrogen-rich coma. A controversial hypothesis by Harvard astronomers Shmuel Bialy and Avi Loeb suggested it might be a thin artificial light sail, a claim widely discussed but considered implausible by most researchers. In 2023, a study argued that ordinary water-ice outgassing of molecular hydrogen trapped in amorphous ice could account for the acceleration, resolving much of the puzzle without exotic assumptions. ʻOumuamua is now beyond reach in the constellation Pegasus, heading out of the Solar System permanently.
