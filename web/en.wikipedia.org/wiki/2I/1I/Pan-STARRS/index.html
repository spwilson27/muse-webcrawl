Pan-STARRS

The Panoramic Survey Telescope and Rapid Response System (Pan-STARRS) is an astronomical survey facility developed and operated by the Institute for Astronomy at the University of Hawaiʻi. Located at Haleakalā Observatory in Hawaii, it uses two 1.8-metre Ritchey–Chrétien telescopes (PS1 and PS2) equipped with gigapixel cameras — PS1's camera, at 1.4 gigapixels, was the largest digital camera in astronomy when commissioned.

Survey and discoveries

Pan-STARRS surveys three-quarters of the sky repeatedly, hunting for moving and transient objects. The survey has discovered large numbers of near-Earth objects, comets, and supernovae. Its most famous find came on October 19, 2017, when Robert Weryk spotted a fast-moving object that proved to be 1I/ʻOumuamua, the first known interstellar object. It also discovered comet C/2011 L4 (PANSTARRS), a naked-eye comet of 2013.

Data releases

The project's public data releases, hosted by the Space Telescope Science Institute, include photometry for billions of stars and galaxies and underpin studies of the Milky Way's structure, dark energy, and the solar system's small-body populations.
