Interstellar object

An interstellar object is an astronomical body — such as a comet, asteroid, or rogue planet — located in interstellar space and not gravitationally bound to any star. Such objects originate in other planetary systems and are ejected, most often by gravitational scattering from giant planets or passing stars.

Confirmed examples

The first interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System was 1I/ʻOumuamua, found in October 2017 by the Pan-STARRS survey; its highly elongated shape and non-gravitational acceleration provoked wide debate. The second, 2I/Borisov (2019), was an active comet with a conventional coma. A third, 3I/ATLAS, was discovered in July 2025. Confirmed interstellar objects are given designations with the letter "I" (for interstellar) in place of the usual comet/asteroid prefixes.

Detection

Interstellar visitors are identified by hyperbolic trajectories with orbital eccentricity greater than 1. Simulations suggest such objects pass through the inner Solar System regularly, but most are too faint to detect; wide-field surveys such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST are expected to find many more.
