In astrodynamics, a hyperbolic trajectory is the path of an object moving with more than enough speed to escape the gravitational pull of a central body. The trajectory is an open hyperbola with eccentricity greater than one, in contrast to the closed ellipse of a bound orbit.

Characteristics

An object on a hyperbolic trajectory approaches the central body along one asymptote, reaches periapsis, and departs along another, never to return. Its excess speed at infinite distance, known as hyperbolic excess velocity, is a key parameter in planning interplanetary gravity-assist manoeuvres.

Natural examples

The interstellar visitors 1I/ʻOumuamua and 2I/Borisov travelled through the Solar System on hyperbolic trajectories, identifying them as objects from other planetary systems.
