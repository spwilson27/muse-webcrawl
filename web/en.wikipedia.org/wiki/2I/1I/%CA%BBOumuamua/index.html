ʻOumuamua

Designation1I/2017 U1 (ʻOumuamua)
Discovered19 October 2017, Pan-STARRS, Haleakalā
TypeInterstellar object

ʻOumuamua (formally 1I/2017 U1) is the first interstellar object confirmed to have passed through the Solar System. This page sits at a nested path formed from links discussing it together with the later interstellar comet 2I/Borisov and the discovery telescope programme 1I designations. The object itself — a reddish, highly elongated or flattened body a few hundred metres across — approached from the direction of Vega, passed inside Mercury's orbit in September 2017, and is now receding into interstellar space, never to return.

Key facts

Astronomers were struck by ʻOumuamua's lack of a coma, its tumbling rotation, and a slight non-gravitational acceleration. Hypotheses have included a hydrogen-ice or nitrogen-ice fragment of an exo-Pluto, an unusually porous dust aggregate, and other exotic compositions. Follow-up observations with the Spitzer Space Telescope set limits on its size and reflectivity, and no radio signals were detected by Breakthrough Listen.

See also

2I/Borisov, the second detected interstellar object (2019), behaved like a typical comet, sharpening the puzzle of ʻOumuamua's properties. The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's surveys are expected to find many more interstellar visitors.
