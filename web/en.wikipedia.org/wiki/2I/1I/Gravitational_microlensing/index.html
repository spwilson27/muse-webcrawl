Gravitational microlensing

Gravitational microlensing
EffectBrightening of a background star by a foreground mass
Based onGeneral relativity
UsesExoplanet detection; dark matter searches

Gravitational microlensing is an astronomical phenomenon in which the gravity of a foreground object bends the light of a background star, temporarily magnifying it. When the two stars align closely as seen from Earth, the background star brightens smoothly over days or weeks before fading again.

Exoplanet detection

If the foreground lens star hosts a planet, the planet's own gravity produces a brief additional spike in the light curve. Microlensing is especially sensitive to cold planets orbiting far from their stars, and to free-floating rogue planets that emit no light of their own.

Surveys

Dedicated surveys such as OGLE and MOA monitor millions of stars toward the galactic bulge, recording thousands of microlensing events. The forthcoming Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is expected to conduct a major space-based microlensing survey.
