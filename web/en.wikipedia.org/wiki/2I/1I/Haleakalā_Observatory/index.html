The Haleakalā Observatory, also known as the Haleakalā High Altitude Observatory Site, is Hawaii's first astronomical research observatory. It is located on the island of Maui and sits atop the summit of Haleakalā, a dormant volcano more than 3,000 metres (10,000 feet) above sea level. The observatory is owned by the Institute for Astronomy of the University of Hawaiʻi, which operates some of the facilities, while others are operated by agencies including the United States Air Force and the Las Cumbres Observatory Global Telescope Network.

History

Because of its high elevation, extremely dry air, and distance from city light pollution, the summit of Haleakalā was identified in the mid-1950s as an outstanding site for astronomical observation. The observatory was established in 1961, and its location above much of the Earth's water vapour, combined with some of the clearest skies in the world, has made it valuable for both optical and infrared astronomy and for tracking satellites and space debris.

Telescopes and instruments

The site hosts numerous instruments. The Pan-STARRS system, with its two 1.8-metre Ritchey–Chrétien telescopes, surveys the sky for near-Earth objects and transient phenomena; Pan-STARRS 1 discovered the first known interstellar object, ʻOumuamua, in 2017. The Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST), a 4-metre solar telescope completed around 2020, is the largest solar telescope in the world and studies the Sun's magnetic field in unprecedented detail. Other facilities include the 3.67-metre Advanced Electro Optical System (AEOS) telescope operated by the Air Force, the 2-metre Faulkes Telescope North of the Las Cumbres Observatory, and the ATLAS-HKO asteroid warning telescope.

Significance

Haleakalā, whose name means "house of the sun" in Hawaiian, is sacred in Native Hawaiian tradition, and the construction of telescopes on the summit has at times been the subject of protest and legal challenge. The observatory remains a major site for solar astronomy, planetary defence and space surveillance.
