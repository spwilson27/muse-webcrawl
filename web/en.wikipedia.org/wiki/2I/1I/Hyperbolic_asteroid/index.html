Hyperbolic asteroid

A hyperbolic asteroid is a small Solar System body whose orbit is a hyperbolic trajectory, meaning it has an orbital eccentricity greater than 1 and is not gravitationally bound to the Sun. Such objects pass through the Solar System once and then leave forever, unless their orbits are altered by a gravitational encounter with a planet.

Most asteroids and comets follow elliptical orbits with eccentricity below 1. A hyperbolic orbit can arise in two ways: the object may be an interstellar visitor arriving from outside the Solar System, or a native Solar System body may be perturbed — usually by a close encounter with Jupiter — onto an escape trajectory.

Interstellar objects

The first object ever identified as interstellar, ʻOumuamua, discovered in 2017, was initially catalogued as a hyperbolic asteroid under the designation A/2017 U1 before the new interstellar designation "1I" was created for it. Its eccentricity of about 1.20 far exceeded that of any known native object. The second interstellar visitor, 2I/Borisov, discovered in 2019, was clearly a comet with an eccentricity of about 3.36.

Native hyperbolic asteroids

A small number of native Solar System asteroids have been observed on slightly hyperbolic trajectories, typically with eccentricities only marginally above 1. These are generally interpreted as objects that have been gravitationally scattered by the giant planets, or whose orbit solutions are affected by non-gravitational forces such as the Yarkovsky effect. Because orbital determinations carry uncertainty, some apparently hyperbolic solutions later revert to elliptical orbits as more observations accumulate.

Notation

Before 2017, hyperbolic small bodies were generally catalogued as comets. The discovery of ʻOumuamua led the International Astronomical Union to introduce the "I" numbering series for interstellar objects, so that unambiguously interstellar asteroids and comets could be distinguished from gravitationally scattered native bodies.
