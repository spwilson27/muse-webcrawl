TypeShield volcano

Haleakalā ("house of the sun" in Hawaiian) is a massive shield volcano that forms most of the eastern part of the island of Maui in Hawaii. Its summit, Puʻu ʻUlaʻula, rises 3,055 metres above sea level and overlooks a vast erosion valley commonly called the Haleakalā Crater, studded with colourful cinder cones.

Observatory

The clear, dry and stable air at the summit makes Haleakalā Observatory one of the world's important astronomical sites. It hosts the Pan-STARRS telescopes, which discovered the interstellar object ʻOumuamua in 2017, and the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope, the largest solar telescope on Earth.

Culture

In Hawaiian tradition, the demigod Māui stood on Haleakalā's summit and lassoed the sun to slow its journey across the sky and lengthen the day. Sunrise at the summit is a celebrated visitor experience, and the mountain lies within Haleakalā National Park.
