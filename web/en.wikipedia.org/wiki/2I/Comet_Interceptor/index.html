Comet Interceptor

Comet Interceptor

Type: Space probe (ESA F-class mission)

Operator: ESA, with JAXA contribution

Planned launch: 2029 (with the ARIEL exoplanet telescope)

Parking orbit: Sun–Earth L2 halo orbit

Target: A yet-undiscovered pristine comet or interstellar object

Comet Interceptor is a robotic spacecraft mission being developed by the European Space Agency (ESA), with a contribution from the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA). Selected in 2019 as ESA's first "Fast" (F-class) mission, it is planned for launch in 2029 alongside the ARIEL exoplanet observatory. Uniquely, it will be launched before its target is known.

Mission concept

After launch, Comet Interceptor will travel to the Sun–Earth L2 point and wait in a halo orbit, possibly for several years. When astronomers discover a suitable long-period comet entering the inner Solar System for the first time — a "dynamically new" comet whose surface has never been altered by the Sun's heat — the spacecraft will depart to intercept it. The mission could alternatively target an interstellar object like ʻOumuamua or 2I/Borisov if one is discovered on a reachable trajectory in time; the Vera Rubin Observatory's surveys are expected to greatly increase the rate of such discoveries.

Spacecraft

The mission consists of a main spacecraft and two small probes, B1 (ESA) and B2 (JAXA), which will be released near the comet so that the three craft sample the coma from different distances simultaneously, giving a three-dimensional picture of how the comet interacts with the solar wind. The main craft carries instruments including imagers and mass and plasma analysers, and acts as a communications relay.
