An exoplanet, or extrasolar planet, is a planet outside the Solar System. The first confirmed detections came in 1992, when Aleksander Wolszczan and Dale Frail found planets orbiting the pulsar PSR B1257+12; in 1995 Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz announced 51 Pegasi b, the first exoplanet found around a Sun-like star, work recognised by the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Detection methods

Most exoplanets are found indirectly: the transit method measures the tiny dip in starlight when a planet crosses its star, while the radial-velocity method detects the star's wobble under the planet's gravity. Gravitational microlensing can find cold and rogue planets, and a small number of planets have been imaged directly.

Diversity

Known exoplanets include hot Jupiters skimming their stars, super-Earths and mini-Neptunes with no Solar System counterpart, and rocky worlds in the habitable zones of their stars. Planetesimals ejected from such systems become the interstellar comets and asteroids occasionally seen passing through our own neighbourhood.
