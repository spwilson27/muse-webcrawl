Interstellar travel

Interstellar travel is the hypothetical travel of spacecraft between star systems. Because the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, is about 4.24 light-years away, interstellar travel is vastly more demanding than travel within the Solar System: at the speed of the fastest current spacecraft, the journey would take tens of thousands of years.

Challenges

The fundamental obstacle is propulsion. Chemical rockets cannot carry enough propellant to reach a significant fraction of the speed of light. Proposed solutions include nuclear fusion rockets, antimatter propulsion, solar sails pushed by giant lasers, and concepts such as the Bussard ramjet that collect fuel en route. Energy requirements are enormous, and shielding against interstellar dust at relativistic speeds is another serious problem.

Generation ships and probes

For crewed missions, concepts include generation ships, in which multiple generations live and die aboard, and sleeper ships using suspended animation. Uncrewed probes are more plausible in the near term: the Breakthrough Starshot initiative aims to accelerate gram-scale light sails to about 20 percent of the speed of light, reaching Alpha Centauri in roughly two decades.

Interstellar objects

Nature performs interstellar travel routinely: objects such as 'Oumuamua and the comet 2I/Borisov have passed through the Solar System from other stars, demonstrating that material routinely travels between star systems over geological timescales.
