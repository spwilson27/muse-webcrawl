2I/Borisov, originally designated C/2019 Q4 (Borisov), is the first observed interstellar comet and the second observed interstellar object overall, after 1I/ʻOumuamua. It was discovered by the amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov on 30 August 2019 using a telescope he built himself at the MARGO observatory in Crimea.

Interstellar origin

Borisov's orbit is strongly hyperbolic, with an eccentricity of about 3.36 — far beyond anything gravitationally bound to the Sun, and not explainable by planetary perturbations. This confirmed that the comet formed around another star and is merely passing through the Solar System. It reached perihelion at about 2 AU from the Sun on 8 December 2019 and passed about 1.9 AU from Earth.

Composition

Unlike the enigmatic ʻOumuamua, Borisov behaved like a fairly ordinary comet, developing a coma and tail of dust and gas as it warmed. Spectroscopy showed water, carbon monoxide and cyanogen among its outgassing species; notably, its carbon monoxide abundance was unusually high compared with Solar System comets, suggesting it formed in a cold region around a cool red dwarf star. Its nucleus was estimated at roughly 1 kilometre across, and observations in 2020 suggested the nucleus may have split into at least two pieces.

Significance

Borisov demonstrated that interstellar comets can resemble familiar Solar System comets, supporting the idea that planet formation around other stars produces similar icy bodies. It remains one of only two confirmed interstellar objects observed passing through the Solar System.
