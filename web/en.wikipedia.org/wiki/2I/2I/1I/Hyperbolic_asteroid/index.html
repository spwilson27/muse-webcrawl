Hyperbolic asteroid
TypeSmall Solar System body on a hyperbolic trajectory
Orbital eccentricityGreater than 1
Example1I/ʻOumuamua

A hyperbolic asteroid is a small rocky body travelling on a hyperbolic trajectory through the Solar System, with an orbital eccentricity greater than one. Such a trajectory means the object is not gravitationally bound to the Sun: it approaches once, swings around the Sun, and leaves forever.

Origins

Solar System asteroids can be nudged onto weakly hyperbolic paths by close encounters with planets, but a strongly hyperbolic trajectory indicates an interstellar origin — the object formed around another star and wandered through interstellar space before passing near the Sun.

Known examples

The first confirmed hyperbolic asteroid was 1I/ʻOumuamua, discovered in 2017. Its elongated shape, tumbling rotation and slight non-gravitational acceleration made it one of the most studied small bodies in history.
