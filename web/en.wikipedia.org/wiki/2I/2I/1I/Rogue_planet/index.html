Rogue planet

Rogue planet
DefinitionPlanetary-mass object not bound to a star
DetectionGravitational microlensing; direct imaging (young objects)
Estimated abundancePossibly billions in the Milky Way

A rogue planet, also called a free-floating planet, is a planetary-mass object that orbits the galaxy directly rather than orbiting a star. Such worlds were either ejected from the planetary systems in which they formed or, like the smallest brown dwarfs, collapsed directly from gas clouds.

Detection

Rogue planets emit almost no light, so they are usually found by gravitational microlensing: for a brief period their gravity magnifies the light of a background star. Young, still-warm rogue planets have also been photographed directly in nearby star-forming regions.

Habitability speculation

Although a rogue planet's surface would be frozen in perpetual darkness, geothermal heat — or a thick hydrogen atmosphere trapping internal warmth — might sustain liquid-water oceans beneath an icy crust, raising speculation about subsurface life far from any star.
