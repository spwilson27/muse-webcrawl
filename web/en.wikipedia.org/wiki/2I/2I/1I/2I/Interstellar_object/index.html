Interstellar object
DefinitionA body in interstellar space not bound to a star
First detected1I/ʻOumuamua (2017)
Second detected2I/Borisov (2019)

An interstellar object is an astronomical body — such as an asteroid or comet — that resides in interstellar space and is not gravitationally bound to any star. When such an object passes through the Solar System it can be recognised by its hyperbolic trajectory, which proves it came from outside the Sun's gravitational domain.

Detection

The first interstellar object identified passing through the Solar System was 1I/ʻOumuamua, found by the Pan-STARRS survey in October 2017. The comet 2I/Borisov followed in 2019. Astronomers expect that wide-field surveys such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory will discover many more.

Formation

Interstellar objects are thought to be planetesimals ejected from their home planetary systems during formation, when gravitational encounters with giant planets or passing stars flung them into the galaxy. They may carry material formed around other stars, making them uniquely valuable probes of exoplanetary systems.
