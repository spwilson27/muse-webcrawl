2I/Borisov, originally designated C/2019 Q4 (Borisov), is the first observed interstellar comet and the second observed interstellar object after ʻOumuamua. It was discovered by the Crimean amateur astronomer and telescope maker Gennadiy Borisov at his MARGO observatory in Nauchnij, Crimea, on 30 August 2019, using a 0.65-metre telescope of his own design.

Unlike ʻOumuamua, Borisov was discovered before it reached perihelion, giving astronomers months to study it inbound. Its orbit has an eccentricity of about 3.36 — far above that of any Solar System comet — proving conclusively that it originated around another star. The comet reached perihelion at just over 2 AU from the Sun on 8 December 2019 and made its closest approach to Earth on 28 December 2019.

Physical characteristics

Observations by the Hubble Space Telescope and ground-based observatories showed that Borisov closely resembles ordinary Solar System comets in its dust production and chemical composition, though its spectrum indicated an unusually high abundance of carbon monoxide relative to water — suggesting it formed in a cold region around a cool red dwarf star. Its solid nucleus was measured to be only about 0.4 to 0.5 kilometres (0.25 to 0.3 miles) across, much smaller than early estimates based on its coma.

In March 2020, observations suggested the nucleus had begun to fragment, ejecting a bright fragment that subsequently faded, consistent with the comet shedding material after perihelion.

Origin

Tracing back its velocity, astronomers proposed that Borisov may have passed relatively near the binary red dwarf system Kruger 60 about a million years ago, which has been suggested — though not proven — as a possible home system. The comet's velocity relative to the local standard of rest was about 32 km/s, too fast for the comet to have formed in the immediate stellar neighbourhood of the Sun.

Significance

2I/Borisov demonstrated that other planetary systems produce comets much like our own, and that such objects are common enough to be detected with modest instruments. Its discovery established the interstellar "I" designation series and motivated searches for further interstellar visitors, as well as mission concepts such as the European Space Agency's planned Comet Interceptor.
