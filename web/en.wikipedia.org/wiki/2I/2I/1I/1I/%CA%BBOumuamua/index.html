1I/ʻOumuamua is the first known interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System. Discovered by Robert Weryk using the Pan-STARRS telescope at Haleakalā Observatory in October 2017, it was already heading away from the Sun on a strongly hyperbolic trajectory, proving it originated in another planetary system. Its name is Hawaiian for "scout" or "messenger from afar arriving first".

Observations

ʻOumuamua showed dramatic brightness variations suggesting an extremely elongated or flattened shape and a tumbling rotation. It displayed no visible coma, yet careful astrometry revealed a small non-gravitational acceleration, usually attributed to comet-like outgassing of volatile ices.

Origin

The object's incoming direction, not far from the star Vega, and its velocity relative to nearby stars suggest it has wandered the galaxy for a long time; its exact home system remains unknown.
