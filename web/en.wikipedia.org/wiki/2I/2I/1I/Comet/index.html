CompositionIce, dust and rocky material
FeaturesNucleus, coma, dust tail and ion tail
Famous exampleHalley's Comet

A comet is an icy small Solar System body that, when passing close to the Sun, warms and begins to release gases — a process called outgassing. This produces a glowing atmosphere, the coma, and sometimes two tails of dust and ionised gas extending millions of kilometres into space.

Orbits

Short-period comets originate in the Kuiper belt and return in less than 200 years; long-period comets come from the spherical Oort cloud far beyond the planets and may take millions of years to orbit the Sun. The interstellar comet 2I/Borisov, discovered in 2019, was the first known comet from another star system.

Structure

The solid nucleus, typically a few kilometres across, is a loose aggregate of ice and dust sometimes called a "dirty snowball". Spacecraft have visited several nuclei, beginning with the Giotto mission to Halley's Comet in 1986.
