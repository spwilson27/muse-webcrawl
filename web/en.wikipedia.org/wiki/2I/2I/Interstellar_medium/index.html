Interstellar medium
CompositionGas (~99%) and dust (~1%)
Main componentHydrogen
RoleReservoir for star formation

The interstellar medium (ISM) is the matter and radiation that exists in the space between stars within a galaxy. It consists overwhelmingly of gas — mainly hydrogen with helium and traces of heavier elements — mixed with microscopic dust grains, cosmic rays and magnetic fields.

Phases

The ISM exists in several phases in rough pressure equilibrium, from cold, dense molecular clouds only tens of degrees above absolute zero, to hot, tenuous ionised gas at millions of degrees created by supernova explosions.

Star formation

Stars form when the densest parts of molecular clouds collapse under gravity. In turn, stellar winds, radiation and supernovae return enriched material to the ISM, driving the cycle of galactic chemical evolution. Interstellar objects such as comets ejected from planetary systems also drift through this medium for billions of years.
