Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest in the Solar System. A gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, Jupiter has a mass more than two and a half times that of all the other planets combined, yet only about one-thousandth that of the Sun.

Atmosphere

Jupiter's atmosphere is banded by powerful zonal jets and dotted with long-lived storms, most famously the Great Red Spot, an anticyclone larger than Earth that has been observed for centuries. The visible atmosphere consists mainly of hydrogen and helium with traces of methane, ammonia, and water vapour.

Interior and magnetosphere

Beneath the clouds, hydrogen is compressed into a liquid metallic state that generates the strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System. Jupiter likely has a diffuse rocky core. Its magnetosphere traps intense radiation belts and interacts strongly with the solar wind.

Moons

The four Galilean moons — Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — were discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610. Europa and Ganymede are believed to harbour subsurface oceans, making them targets in the search for habitable environments. Jupiter also plays a key gravitational role in shaping the asteroid belt and scattering comets, including interstellar visitors, onto new trajectories.
