The Consolidated B-24 Liberator is an American heavy bomber, designed by Consolidated Aircraft of San Diego, California. It was known within the company as the Model 32, and some initial production aircraft were laid down as export models designated as various LB-30s, in the Land Bomber design-category.

Design and development

The B-24 was designed around the high-efficiency "Davis wing," a long, thin airfoil that gave the aircraft greater range and lifting capacity than the B-17 Flying Fortress, though at the cost of more demanding handling. Its distinctive twin tail and tricycle landing gear set it apart from contemporaries. The aircraft had a spacious box-like fuselage with a roll-up bomb bay door arrangement.

Production

With 18,188 units built, the B-24 remains the most-produced American military aircraft in history, as well as the most-produced heavy bomber and multi-engine aircraft. Liberators were assembled by Consolidated, Ford Motor Company (at the vast Willow Run plant in Michigan), Douglas Aircraft, and North American Aviation under the pooled production system.

Operational history

The Liberator served in every branch of the American armed forces during World War II, in all theaters. It flew strategic bombing missions over Europe — including the costly low-level raid on the Ploiești oil fields in Romania in August 1943 — long-range anti-submarine patrols that helped close the mid-Atlantic "air gap," and transport and maritime missions in the Pacific. A transport derivative, the C-87 Liberator Express, and the single-tailed PB4Y-2 Privateer naval patrol bomber were developed from the design.

Specifications (B-24J)

Crew: 11. Length: 67 ft 8 in (20.6 m). Wingspan: 110 ft (33.5 m). Maximum speed: 290 mph (467 km/h). Range: 2,100 mi (3,400 km) with typical load. Armament: ten .50-caliber machine guns; bomb load up to 8,000 lb (3,600 kg) on short-range missions.
