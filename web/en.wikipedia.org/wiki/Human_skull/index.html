Human skull

The human skull is the bony structure that forms the head in the human skeleton. It supports the structures of the face and forms a protective cavity for the brain. The skull consists of two parts, of different embryological origin — the neurocranium and the facial skeleton (or viscerocranium).

Structure

An adult human skull consists of 22 bones: 8 cranial bones forming the cranium and 14 facial bones. Except for the mandible (jawbone), all the bones are joined by sutures — rigid fibrous joints that allow almost no movement. In infants, the cranial bones are separated by fontanelles, which close during early childhood.

Functions

The skull protects the brain from injury, fixes the distance between the eyes to allow stereoscopic vision, and anchors the teeth and muscles of mastication. Its foramina allow the passage of cranial nerves and blood vessels.
