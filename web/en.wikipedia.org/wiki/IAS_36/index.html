IAS 36

IAS 36, Impairment of Assets, is an International Accounting Standard issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) under IFRS. Its objective is to ensure that an entity's assets are carried at no more than their recoverable amount, and to define how the recoverable amount is measured and when an impairment loss is recognised or reversed.

Key requirements

At each reporting date an entity assesses whether there is any indication of impairment; goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets must be tested annually regardless. Recoverable amount is the higher of an asset's fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use — the present value of the future cash flows expected from the asset. Where an individual asset's recoverable amount cannot be estimated, the test is performed at the level of the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs. An impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss when carrying amount exceeds recoverable amount; losses on goodwill can never be reversed, while reversals for other assets are permitted if estimates change.
