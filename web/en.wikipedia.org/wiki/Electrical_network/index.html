Electrical network

An electrical network is an interconnection of electrical components — such as batteries, resistors, inductors, capacitors, switches and transistors — or a model of such an interconnection, consisting of electrical elements connected by ideal conductors. A network that contains active electronic components is known as an electronic circuit.

Analysis

Networks are analysed using Kirchhoff's circuit laws: currents at a node sum to zero, and voltages around a loop sum to zero. Combined with component laws such as Ohm's law, these yield equations solvable by nodal or mesh analysis. Powerful simplifications include Thévenin's and Norton's theorems, which reduce any linear network to a single source and impedance.

Classification

Networks may be linear or nonlinear, lumped or distributed, passive or active, and are studied in time or frequency domains. Two-port network parameters (Z, Y, h and S-parameters) describe complex systems by their terminal behaviour. Large interconnected power systems are sometimes also called electrical networks.

Design

Network design spans printed circuit boards, integrated circuits, filters, matching networks for antennas, and power distribution systems, and is now almost always done with computer simulation (SPICE and related tools).
