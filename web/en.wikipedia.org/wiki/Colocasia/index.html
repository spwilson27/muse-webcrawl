Colocasia

Colocasia is a genus of flowering plants in the family Araceae, native to southeastern Asia and the Indian subcontinent. Some species are widely cultivated and naturalised in tropical and subtropical regions around the world. The best-known species, Colocasia esculenta, is taro, one of the world's oldest food crops.

Description

Colocasia species are perennial herbs with large, heart- or arrow-shaped leaves borne on long stalks, giving rise to the common name "elephant ears" (shared with related genera such as Alocasia and Xanthosoma). They grow from corms or rhizomes and produce the characteristic aroid inflorescence of a spadix surrounded by a spathe.

Cultivation and uses

Taro is grown for its starchy corms and its leaves, which are eaten as a vegetable; both must be cooked to destroy irritating calcium oxalate crystals (raphides) present in the raw tissues. The plant is a staple in Pacific Island, African, South Asian, and Caribbean cuisines, and in Hawaii it is pounded into poi. Other Colocasia species and their many ornamental cultivars are grown as foliage plants for gardens and ponds in warm climates.

Species

The genus includes species such as C. esculenta, C. gigantea (giant taro), and C. fallax; classifications vary as molecular studies revise relationships within the arum family.
