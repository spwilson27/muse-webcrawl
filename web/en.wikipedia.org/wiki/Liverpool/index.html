Liverpool is a port city in Merseyside, North West England, on the eastern side of the Mersey Estuary. Founded as a borough in 1207, it grew into one of the world's great ports through the Atlantic trade — including, notoriously, the transatlantic slave trade — and later through transatlantic passenger and cargo shipping. It is internationally known as the birthplace of the Beatles and for its two Premier League football clubs, Liverpool F.C. and Everton F.C.

History

King John granted Liverpool its first charter in 1207. The building of the Old Dock in 1715, the world's first commercial wet dock, launched the city's rise; by the early 19th century, 40 per cent of the world's trade passed through Liverpool, and it rivalled London in wealth. Liverpool ships and merchants were deeply involved in the slave trade until its abolition in 1807. The city was the principal European port of emigration to North America and Australia, and home to the first intercity railway (to Manchester, 1830). Heavy bombing in the Second World War (the Liverpool Blitz) and the containerisation of shipping drove decline from the 1960s; the waterfront was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004 but was controversially removed from the list in 2021.

Culture

Liverpool's cultural legacy centres on music: beyond the Beatles, the Merseybeat scene and later acts from Echo & the Bunnymen to Frankie Goes to Hollywood made the city a pop capital; it hosted the Eurovision Song Contest in 2023. The Walker Art Gallery, Tate Liverpool and the Museum of Liverpool anchor a museum sector second in England only to London's. Residents, known as Scousers or Liverpudlians, speak a distinctive accent, Scouse. The "Three Graces" — the Royal Liver Building (1911), Cunard Building and Port of Liverpool Building — dominate the Pier Head waterfront.

Sport

Liverpool F.C., based at Anfield, is one of England's most successful clubs, with numerous league titles and European Cups; Everton F.C., founded in 1878, moved to the new Hill Dickinson Stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock in 2025. The Grand National steeplechase is run at Aintree Racecourse.
