Eidi (gift)

An elder giving Eidi to a child

Eidi (Urdu: عیدی; Arabic: عيدية, romanized: ʿīdiyya) is a gift, traditionally of money, given by elders to children and younger relatives during the Islamic festivals of Eid al-Fitr (Chaand Raat and the days following) and Eid al-Adha. The custom is widespread in South Asia, the Middle East and Muslim diaspora communities.

Custom

On Eid morning, after the Eid prayer and festive meals, children typically greet elders — often touching their feet or embracing them and saying "Eid Mubarak" — and receive crisp new banknotes, sometimes presented in decorative envelopes. The amount is symbolic rather than fixed, and receiving Eidi is considered a highlight of childhood Eid celebrations. In some regions the practice extends to gifts for daughters-in-law, employees, and domestic staff, and to sweets and new clothes in place of cash. Banks in countries such as Pakistan see heavy demand for fresh currency notes before Eid al-Fitr specifically to meet Eidi giving.

Modern practice

Digital payments and mobile money have added e-Eidi transfers, especially for relatives abroad; several banks and wallet apps in South Asia and the Gulf run seasonal Eidi features. The tradition is comparable to money-gift customs at other festivals, such as hongbao at Lunar New Year.
