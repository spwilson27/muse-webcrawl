Galantine

A galantine is a French dish of de-boned, stuffed meat, most commonly poultry or fish, that is poached and served cold, coated with aspic. Galantines are often stuffed with forcemeat and pressed into a cylindrical shape. Since deboning poultry is thought of as difficult and time-consuming, this is a rather elaborate recipe, one often seen in haute cuisine and mentioned frequently in classical French cookery from the medieval period onward.

Preparation

The bird is skinned and boned whole, keeping the skin intact as far as possible. The meat is combined with a seasoned forcemeat — ground meat and fat enriched with eggs or cream, pistachios, truffles, tongue or ham — spread over the skin, rolled and tied into a neat cylinder, then gently poached in stock. Once chilled and pressed, the galantine is sliced to reveal a mosaic cross-section and glazed with aspic or a chaud-froid sauce.

Related dishes

The galantine belongs to the charcuterie family of cold, moulded preparations alongside the ballotine (similar but usually roasted and served warm), terrines and pâtés en croûte. In medieval English cookery, "galantine" also named a spiced sauce of galangal, ginger and breadcrumbs served with fish and meat, one of several senses recorded in early cookery manuscripts.
