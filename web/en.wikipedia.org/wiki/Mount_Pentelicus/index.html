Mount Pentelicus or Pentelikon (Greek: Πεντελικόν) is a mountain in Attica, Greece, northeast of Athens, much of it now within the city's northern suburbs. It is celebrated for its marble, quarried since antiquity and used in many of the most famous buildings of classical Athens.

Pentelic marble

The mountain's marble is a fine-grained, pure white stone with a faint golden tint, prized for its slight translucency. Quarries on the southwest slope supplied the Parthenon, the Erechtheion, the Propylaea and the Temple of Olympian Zeus, as well as Roman and later monuments; a paved slipway, the "way of the marble", carried blocks down toward Athens. The ancient quarries — notably the large open quarry cave known as the Spilia — can still be visited, and extraction continues today under strict limits, with Pentelic marble used in the restoration of the Acropolis monuments.

The mountain today

Pentelicus is also known for the Byzantine monastery of Penteli (founded 1578), the Davelis cave with its cave chapel, and the marble-clad suburbs of Pendeli, Melissia and Kifisia on its slopes. Much of its forest was destroyed by wildfires in the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries, after which extensive reforestation has been attempted.
