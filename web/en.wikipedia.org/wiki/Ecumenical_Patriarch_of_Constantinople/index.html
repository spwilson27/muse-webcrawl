The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople is the senior bishop of the Eastern Orthodox Church, regarded as primus inter pares ("first among equals") among the heads of the autocephalous Orthodox churches, and as the spiritual leader of the worldwide Orthodox Christian community. His official title is Archbishop of Constantinople, New Rome, and Ecumenical Patriarch.

The patriarchate traces its founding to the Apostle Andrew, held to be the first bishop of Byzantium. The see rose in importance after Constantine made the city his capital ("New Rome") in 330, and the Council of Constantinople (381) and Council of Chalcedon (451) confirmed its rank second only to Rome — a ranking disputed by the papacy and a contributing factor in the East–West Schism of 1054.

History

Under the Byzantine Empire the patriarch was a central religious and political figure, crowning emperors and presiding over the church of the empire. After the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople in 1453, Sultan Mehmed II recognised Patriarch Gennadios II and made the patriarchate responsible for the Orthodox millet, giving the patriarch civil as well as religious authority over Orthodox Christians in the empire. The patriarchate survived the dissolution of the Ottoman Empire and remains in Istanbul, though its flock in Turkey has dwindled to a few thousand.

Role and authority

The Ecumenical Patriarch convenes pan-Orthodox councils, coordinates relations among the autocephalous churches, and is the church's chief representative in ecumenical dialogue, including with the Roman Catholic Church. He exercises direct jurisdiction over several dioceses outside the territory of other churches, including much of the Orthodox diaspora. The extent of his authority over other churches is contested, notably by the Moscow Patriarchate; the 2018 grant of autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine led Moscow to break communion with Constantinople.

Bartholomew I

The current patriarch, Bartholomew I (born Dimitrios Arhondonis, 1940), was enthroned in 1991 and is the longest-serving patriarch in centuries. He is noted for his environmental advocacy — earning the epithet "the Green Patriarch" — and for meetings with Popes John Paul II, Benedict XVI and Francis, including the historic 2014 prayer meeting in Jerusalem commemorating the 1964 meeting of Paul VI and Athenagoras.
