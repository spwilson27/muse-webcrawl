National Historic Sites of Canada
French: Lieux historiques nationaux du Canada
Number: Over 970 sites
First designated: 1917 onwards
Administered in part by: Parks Canada

National Historic Sites of Canada (French: lieux historiques nationaux du Canada) are places designated by the Government of Canada as being of national historic significance. More than 970 sites have been designated across every province and territory, including buildings, battlefields, archaeological sites, cultural landscapes and districts.

Designation

Sites are designated by the federal Minister responsible for Parks Canada, on the advice of the Historic Sites and Monuments Board of Canada, an advisory body created in 1919. Designations are usually marked with a federal plaque and are recorded in the Canadian Register of Historic Places. Canada also designates National Historic Events and National Historic Persons.

Administration

Parks Canada directly administers around 170 of the sites as part of the national historic sites system; the rest are owned by other governments, institutions, communities or private owners. Well-known sites include L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland, the Fortress of Louisbourg in Nova Scotia, and the Rideau Canal in Ontario, the latter two also recognized internationally.
