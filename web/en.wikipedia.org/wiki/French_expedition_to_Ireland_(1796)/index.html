French expedition to Ireland (1796)

The French fleet reached Bantry Bay but could not land.

The French expedition to Ireland of 1796, known in Ireland as the Expédition d'Irlande, was an attempted landing in Ireland by a French Revolutionary force intended to support the Society of United Irishmen and destabilise the United Kingdom during the French Revolutionary Wars.

The expedition

In December 1796 a fleet of 43 ships carrying some 15,000 troops under General Lazare Hoche sailed from Brest, evading the Royal Navy. The United Irishman leader Theobald Wolfe Tone accompanied the expedition as an adjutant. Storms scattered the fleet; part of it, including several thousand troops, reached Bantry Bay in County Cork, but gales prevented a landing, and the frigate carrying Hoche had been separated from the fleet. The flagship Fraternité never arrived in time, and after days of worsening weather the surviving ships withdrew to France, losing several vessels, including the ship of the line Droits de l'Homme, wrecked on the return voyage.

Consequences

The failure convinced the United Irishmen to rise without waiting for a full French landing, contributing to the Irish Rebellion of 1798, when a much smaller French force under General Humbert landed at Killala but was defeated. The near-miss of 1796 also spurred British coastal defences, including the construction of Martello towers around Ireland.
