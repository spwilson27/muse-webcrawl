International humanitarian law (IHL), also known as the law of armed conflict or the law of war, is the body of law that seeks to limit the effects of armed conflict for humanitarian reasons. It protects persons who are not, or no longer, participating in hostilities, and restricts the means and methods of warfare.

Its core instruments are the four Geneva Conventions of 1949 and their Additional Protocols, plus treaties on specific weapons (chemical, biological, landmines, cluster munitions) and the Hague Conventions on conduct of hostilities. Key principles: distinction (between combatants and civilians), proportionality, military necessity and humanity. Serious violations are war crimes, prosecuted by national courts and the International Criminal Court; the ICRC is IHL's recognised guardian.
