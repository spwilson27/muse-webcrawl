Footloose (1984 film)

Footloose
Directed by: Herbert Ross
Written by: Dean Pitchford
Starring: Kevin Bacon, Lori Singer, John Lithgow, Dianne Wiest, Chris Penn, Sarah Jessica Parker
Released: 17 February 1984
Running time: 107 minutes

Footloose is a 1984 American musical drama film directed by Herbert Ross and written by Dean Pitchford. It stars Kevin Bacon as Ren McCormack, a teenager from Chicago who moves to a small town where dancing and rock music have been banned by the local council under the influence of a conservative minister, played by John Lithgow.

Plot

Ren McCormack moves with his mother to the small town of Bomont, where the Reverend Shaw Moore has persuaded the council to outlaw dancing after a fatal car accident involving local teenagers. Ren befriends Willard and falls for the reverend's rebellious daughter Ariel. Determined to hold a senior prom, Ren challenges the ban, arguing before the council that dance is a joyful and scripturally supported expression. The ban is eventually lifted and the students celebrate with a dance.

Music

The soundtrack was a major commercial success. Kenny Loggins performed the title song "Footloose", which reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100, and Deniece Williams's "Let's Hear It for the Boy" also topped the charts. Other contributors included Bonnie Tyler ("Holding Out for a Hero") and Mike Reno and Ann Wilson ("Almost Paradise").

Legacy

The film was loosely inspired by events in Elmore City, Oklahoma, where dancing had been banned for decades. A stage musical adaptation premiered in 1998, and a remake film was released in 2011.
