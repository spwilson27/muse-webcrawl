The Lowry is a theatre and gallery complex at Salford Quays in Salford, Greater Manchester, England. Named after the artist L. S. Lowry (1887–1976), famous for his paintings of industrial life in North West England, it opened on 28 April 2000 and was officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 12 October 2000.

Architecture

Designed by architect Michael Wilford, the building is clad in stainless steel and glass and stands on a triangular site at the end of Pier 8, surrounded on three sides by water. Its ship-like form reflects the maritime heritage of the Manchester Ship Canal. Inside, the building contains two theatres — the large Lyric Theatre and the smaller, flexible Quays Theatre — together with gallery spaces, bars and a restaurant.

Collections and programme

The Lowry houses the world's largest public collection of paintings and drawings by L. S. Lowry, including his 1949 painting The Pond and the famous Coming from the Mill (1930). In addition to the permanent Lowry exhibition, the galleries host touring exhibitions of contemporary and historical art and photography.

The Lyric Theatre is one of the largest touring stages in the UK and presents drama, opera, ballet, dance and musicals; it is a regular home to Opera North productions in the region. The venue attracts around 850,000 visitors annually and is a centrepiece of the regenerated Salford Quays, alongside MediaCityUK and the Imperial War Museum North across the water.
