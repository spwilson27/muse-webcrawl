Tommy Caldwell (born August 11, 1978) is an American rock climber who has completed some of the most difficult big-wall free climbs in the world, most famously the first free ascent of the Dawn Wall on El Capitan in Yosemite National Park, accomplished with Kevin Jorgeson in January 2015 after seven years of effort.

Life and career

Caldwell grew up in Colorado, where his mountain-guide father introduced him to climbing. In 2000, while on a climbing trip to Kyrgyzstan, Caldwell and three partners (including Beth Rodden, whom he later married) were taken hostage by Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan militants; they escaped after six days when Caldwell pushed a captor off a cliff. In 2001 Caldwell lost most of his left index finger in a table-saw accident and, after doctors reattached it, chose to have it amputated to preserve his climbing career; he went on to become one of the world's leading big-wall climbers.

Major ascents

Caldwell freed numerous routes on El Capitan, including the Salathé Wall, the Nose (in under two hours with Alex Honnold in 2018), and the Dawn Wall, widely considered among the hardest big-wall free climbs in the world. The Dawn Wall ascent was the subject of the 2017 documentary The Dawn Wall, and Caldwell told his story in the memoir The Push (2017).
