North American Numbering Plan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The North American Numbering Plan (NANP) is a telephone numbering plan for twenty-five regions in North America, the Caribbean, and certain Atlantic and Pacific territories. It was developed shortly after World War II by AT&T in conjunction with the Bell System's independent telephone operators, and came into force in 1951.

Structure

Telephone numbers in the NANP use the format NPA-NXX-XXXX: a three-digit area code (Numbering Plan Area), a three-digit central office code, and a four-digit station number. The country code for the entire plan is +1. Digits were originally restricted so that the middle digit of an area code was 0 or 1 and central office codes could not have 0 or 1 as their second digit, allowing equipment to distinguish the two — restrictions relaxed with interchangeable NPA codes in 1995.

Administration

The plan is administered by the North American Numbering Plan Administrator (NANPA). Member countries include the United States, Canada, Bermuda, and many Caribbean nations such as Jamaica, the Bahamas, Barbados, and the Dominican Republic; each nation regulates numbering within its jurisdiction, mostly through national regulators.

History and expansion

The original 1947 assignment created 86 area codes; the iconic codes 212 (New York City), 213 (Los Angeles), and 312 (Chicago) reflected rotary-dial convenience for large cities. As demand grew with population growth, fax machines, and mobile phones, area codes were multiplied by geographic splits and, from the 1990s, overlays. Special codes include 800 and other 8XX toll-free codes, 900 premium-rate numbers, and 911 for emergencies, which are reserved across the plan.
