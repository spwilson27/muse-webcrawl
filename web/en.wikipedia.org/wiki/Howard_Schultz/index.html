Howard D. Schultz (born July 19, 1953) is an American businessman and author who served as chief executive officer (CEO) of the Starbucks Coffee Company from 1986 to 2000, from 2008 to 2017, and as interim CEO from 2022 to 2023. Schultz also owned the Seattle SuperSonics basketball team from 2001 to 2006.

Early life and education

Schultz was born in Brooklyn, New York, to a Jewish family. His father, Fred Schultz, was a former United States Army soldier and later a truck driver; his mother, Elaine, was a homemaker. He grew up in the Canarsie Bayview Houses, a public housing project in Brooklyn. Schultz attended Canarsie High School, graduating in 1971. He earned an athletic scholarship to Northern Michigan University, where he received a bachelor's degree in communications in 1975 — becoming the first person in his family to graduate from college.

Career

After college, Schultz worked at Xerox Corporation as a sales representative and later at Hammarplast, a Swedish housewares company, where he rose to vice president. While visiting a client in Seattle — the then small coffee bean retailer Starbucks — in 1981, he was struck by the company's knowledge of coffee. Schultz joined Starbucks as director of operations and marketing in 1982.

After a trip to Milan, Italy, in 1983, Schultz became convinced that Starbucks should sell espresso beverages in the Italian café style, not just beans. The company's founders were reluctant, so Schultz left in 1985 to found his own coffee bar chain, Il Giornale. In 1987, with the backing of investors, he purchased Starbucks and merged it with Il Giornale, becoming CEO of the combined company.

Expansion of Starbucks

Under Schultz, Starbucks grew from a handful of stores into the world's largest coffeehouse chain, with tens of thousands of locations worldwide. Schultz stepped down as CEO in 2000 to become chairman, returned as CEO in January 2008 during the financial crisis, and retired again in 2017, succeeded by Kevin Johnson. Following Johnson's departure, Schultz returned as interim CEO in April 2022 and served until March 2023, when Laxman Narasimhan took over.

Seattle SuperSonics

In 2001, Schultz led an ownership group that purchased the Seattle SuperSonics of the NBA and the Seattle Storm of the WNBA. The SuperSonics were sold in 2006 to an Oklahoma City group led by Clay Bennett, and the team relocated to Oklahoma City in 2008, becoming the Thunder — a move that remained controversial in Seattle.

Political considerations

In January 2019, Schultz announced he was seriously considering an independent run for President of the United States in 2020. He faced criticism from Democrats concerned he would split the anti-Trump vote, and in September 2019 he announced he would not run, citing health concerns following back surgeries.

Personal life

Schultz is married to Sheri Kersch Schultz, with whom he has two children. He is the author of several books, including Pour Your Heart Into It (1997), Onward (2011), and From the Ground Up (2019). He has been recognized with numerous awards for his business leadership and philanthropy through the Schultz Family Foundation.
