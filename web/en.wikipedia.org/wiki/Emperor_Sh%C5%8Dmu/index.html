Emperor Shōmu
Reign: 724–749
Predecessor: Empress Genshō
Successor: Empress Kōken
Era: Nara period

Emperor Shōmu (701 – 4 June 756) was the 45th emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession, reigning from 724 until his abdication in 749. A devout Buddhist, he is best known for commissioning the Tōdai-ji temple in Nara and its colossal Great Buddha (Daibutsu) statue.

Reign

Shōmu promoted Buddhism as a protector of the state, ordering the construction of provincial temples (kokubunji) in every province and issuing the edict for the Great Buddha in 743. The bronze statue, over 15 metres tall, was dedicated in 752. His reign also saw the compilation of poetry and the flourishing of Nara culture, though it was troubled by smallpox epidemics and rebellions.

Legacy

The Shōsōin repository at Tōdai-ji preserves thousands of objects from Shōmu's court, including items from as far away as Persia, testimony to Silk Road exchange.
