Ballachulish (Scottish Gaelic: Baile a' Chaolais, "village of the narrows") is a village in Lochaber, Highland, Scotland, at the entrance to Glencoe on the shores of Loch Linnhe and Loch Leven. The name refers to the narrow strait between the two lochs, crossed until 1975 by a ferry and now by the Ballachulish Bridge.

History

The village grew with the slate industry: the Ballachulish slate quarries, worked from 1693, supplied roofing slate across Scotland and beyond, and the distinctive settlement of workers' cottages developed beside them. The quarries closed in 1955. In 1752 the nearby area was the scene of the Appin Murder—Colin Campbell of Glenure was shot near Ballachulish, and James Stewart of the Glens was hanged for the crime at Cnap a' Chaolais above the ferry, an event fictionalised in Robert Louis Stevenson's Kidnapped. A monument marks the spot.

Geography and economy

Ballachulish lies on the A82 road between Fort William and Glasgow, overlooked by Beinn a' Bheithir. The former quarry has been redeveloped with walks and interpretation. Tourism, serving visitors to Glencoe and walkers on routes such as the West Highland Way corridor, now dominates the local economy, alongside the visitor centre and hotel trade. The village has a shinty tradition; Ballachulish Camanachd Club was among the early powers of the sport.
