Sunlight (cleaning product)

Sunlight is a brand of laundry soap, dishwashing liquid, and other household cleaning products originally introduced by the British company Lever Brothers in 1885. It was one of the world's first branded, packaged laundry soaps, replacing the unbranded cut soap sold by grocers, and it became a flagship product of Unilever, which Lever Brothers co-founded in 1929. The brand remains widely sold in Africa, South Asia, and parts of Europe, where the traditional yellow bar soap is a household staple.

History

William Hesketh Lever and his brother James founded Lever Brothers and began producing Sunlight soap in Warrington, England, using glycerin and vegetable oils such as palm oil and pine kernel oil rather than the tallow traditionally used. The soap was milled, stamped with its name, and wrapped — an innovation in branding and marketing for the era. In 1888 the company began building the model village of Port Sunlight in Cheshire to house its workers, and production expanded to factories around the world.

Products

The Sunlight name has been applied to laundry bars, powders, and liquids, and to dishwashing liquids in many markets; in several countries Sunlight dishwashing liquid is a market leader. In North America the brand was long associated with dish detergent and laundry products, though Unilever divested the North American Sunlight laundry and dish business to Sun Products in 2008.

Cultural significance

Sunlight's early advertising campaigns, using the slogan asking why women allowed "Monday washday" drudgery, are studied as landmarks of Victorian marketing. The brand's longevity and its centrality in the history of Lever Brothers make it an emblem of the rise of branded consumer goods.
