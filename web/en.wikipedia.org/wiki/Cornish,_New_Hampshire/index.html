Cornish, New Hampshire

Cornish is a town in Sullivan County, New Hampshire, United States. The population was about 1,600 at recent censuses. Cornish is best known as the home of the Cornish Colony of artists and writers, centred on the sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens, and as the longtime residence of the reclusive author J. D. Salinger.

History

The town was chartered in 1763 and named after Sir Samuel Cornish, a British naval officer. In 1885 Saint-Gaudens bought property in Cornish, attracting a colony of artists, sculptors and writers that flourished into the early twentieth century. His home and studio are preserved as the Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park. From 1953 until his death in 2010, J. D. Salinger lived in Cornish in near-total seclusion.

Geography and landmarks

Cornish lies along the Connecticut River, across from Windsor, Vermont, to which it is connected by the Cornish–Windsor Covered Bridge, one of the longest wooden covered bridges in the United States. The town contains several other historic covered bridges.
