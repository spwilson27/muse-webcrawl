Hereford (Welsh: Henffordd) is a cathedral city and the county town of Herefordshire, England. It lies on the River Wye, approximately 16 miles (26 km) east of the border with Wales and 24 miles (39 km) southwest of Worcester. With a population of about 53,000, it is the largest settlement in the county.

History

Hereford originated as a Saxon military settlement guarding a ford on the Wye — its name means "army ford". A bishopric was established by the late 7th century. The city suffered in the Danish and Welsh wars, was granted a charter by Richard I in 1189, and saw action in the Wars of the Roses and the English Civil War, when it changed hands several times. The medieval castle was demolished after the Restoration.

Landmarks

Hereford Cathedral, founded in the 7th century and rebuilt in Norman times, houses the famous Mappa Mundi, a medieval map of the world created around 1300, and one of only four surviving copies of the 1217 Magna Carta, as well as a chained library. Other landmarks include the Old House (a 17th-century timber-framed building, now a museum), the Cider Museum, and the medieval Wye Bridge. The city gives its name to the Hereford breed of cattle, recognised worldwide.

Economy and culture

The surrounding county is famed for cider production (notably Bulmers, founded in Hereford in 1887), hops, and fruit. Hereford is home to the Hereford Museum and Art Gallery, the Courtyard theatre, and Hereford FC, successor to Hereford United, famous for its 1972 FA Cup giant-killing of Newcastle United.
