Wolframite

Wolframite is an iron manganese tungstate mineral with the chemical formula (Fe,Mn)WO4. It is the principal ore of tungsten and forms a solid solution series between ferberite (iron-rich) and hübnerite (manganese-rich).

Properties and occurrence

Wolframite is dark brown to black, with a submetallic lustre, hardness of about 4–4.5 and a high specific gravity (7–7.5). It occurs in quartz veins and pegmatites associated with granitic rocks, commonly with cassiterite. Major deposits have been worked in China, Portugal (Panasqueira), Bolivia and historically in Cornwall and the Erzgebirge.

Name

The name derives from German Wolfram, the older name of tungsten, whose chemical symbol W comes from it.
