Kate Warne

Kate Warne
Born: 1833, Erin, New York
Died: 28 January 1868, Chicago, Illinois
Occupation: detective
Known for: first female detective in the United States; foiling the Baltimore Plot

Kate Warne (1833 – 28 January 1868) was an American detective, best known as the first woman employed as a detective in the United States, hired by Allan Pinkerton in 1856. She worked for the Pinkerton National Detective Agency, took part in uncovering the Baltimore Plot to assassinate President-elect Abraham Lincoln in 1861, and later headed the agency's Female Detective Bureau.

Career

Warne, a young widow, persuaded Pinkerton to hire her by arguing that women could gain confidences and access places closed to male operatives. She quickly proved successful at undercover work, including solving an express-company theft by befriending the suspect's wife. During the Civil War she gathered intelligence for the Union, often posing as a Southern sympathiser.

Baltimore Plot

In February 1861 Warne and other Pinkerton agents uncovered a plan to assassinate Lincoln as he passed through Baltimore. Warne helped arrange the secret night journey by train that carried Lincoln safely to Washington, reportedly disguising herself and posing as an attendant for an "invalid" traveller. The agency's motto "We Never Sleep" and its open-eye logo are associated with this episode.

Death and legacy

Warne died of pneumonia in Chicago in 1868 and was buried in the Pinkerton family plot at Graceland Cemetery. She is remembered as a pioneering figure in American law enforcement.
