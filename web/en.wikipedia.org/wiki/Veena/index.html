Veena

ClassificationString instrument (chordophone), lute family
RegionIndia
Related instrumentsSitar, rudra veena, vichitra veena

The veena is a family of plucked string instruments from India, among the oldest continuously played instruments in Indian classical music. The best-known form today is the Saraswati veena of Carnatic music, a long-necked lute with a large resonating body, a second resonator gourd, 24 frets, and seven strings.

Construction and playing

The Saraswati veena is traditionally carved from a single block of jackfruit wood. Four main playing strings pass over high brass frets set in wax, and three drone strings run along the side; the player plucks with the right hand (sometimes with wire plectra) while the left hand stops and bends notes on the frets. Other veena types include the rudra veena (bin) of Hindustani music, the fretless vichitra veena, and the chitra veena (gottuvadhyam), played with a slide.

Cultural significance

The veena is the attribute of Saraswati, the Hindu goddess of learning and music, and of the sage Narada. The term veena in early Sanskrit texts refers generally to stringed instruments, and the instrument symbolises classical Indian music itself. Noted modern exponents include Emani Sankara Sastry, Chitti Babu, and S. Balachander.
