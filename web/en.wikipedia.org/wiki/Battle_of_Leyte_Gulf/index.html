The largest naval battle of the Second World War, October 1944.

The Battle of Leyte Gulf, fought from 23 to 26 October 1944, is generally considered the largest naval battle of the Second World War and possibly the largest naval battle in history. It comprised four main engagements — the Battle of the Sibuyan Sea, the Battle of Surigao Strait, the Battle off Cape Engaño and the Battle off Samar — between the Imperial Japanese Navy and the U.S. Third and Seventh Fleets supporting the American landings on Leyte in the Philippines.

Background

Japan's Shō-Gō 1 plan sought to destroy the American invasion fleet: Ozawa's Northern Force of carriers (nearly devoid of aircraft) would decoy Halsey's Third Fleet north, while Kurita's powerful Center Force and the Southern Force of Nishimura and Shima closed on the Leyte anchorages from the west.

Battle

American submarines and carrier aircraft mauled Kurita's force in the Sibuyan Sea, sinking the super-battleship Musashi, but Kurita pressed on. In Surigao Strait, in history's last battleship-versus-battleship action, Rear Admiral Oldendorf's force annihilated the Southern Force. Halsey, meanwhile, raced north after Ozawa's decoy carriers, leaving San Bernardino Strait unguarded. Kurita's battleships fell upon the escort carriers of "Taffy 3" off Samar on 25 October; the ferocious resistance of the outgunned Americans, including destroyers charging battleships, convinced Kurita to withdraw. Halsey sank Ozawa's four carriers at Cape Engaño but turned back too late to intercept Kurita. The battle also saw the first organized kamikaze attacks.

Aftermath

Japan lost 28 warships, including four carriers and three battleships, and its navy never again mounted a major operation. Allied losses were far lighter though keenly felt off Samar. The victory secured the Leyte landings and the liberation of the Philippines.
