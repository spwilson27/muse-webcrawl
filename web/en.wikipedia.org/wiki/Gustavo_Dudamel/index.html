Gustavo Adolfo Dudamel Ramírez (born 26 January 1981) is a Venezuelan conductor and violinist. He is the music director of the Simón Bolívar Symphony Orchestra and was music and artistic director of the Los Angeles Philharmonic from 2009, becoming one of the most celebrated conductors of his generation. In 2023 it was announced that he would become music and artistic director of the New York Philharmonic beginning in the 2026–27 season.

Early life and El Sistema

Dudamel was born in Barquisimeto, Venezuela, into a musical family; his father was a trombonist and his mother a voice teacher. He began violin studies at the age of ten through El Sistema, Venezuela's famous programme of free classical music education for children, founded by José Antonio Abreu. He studied conducting with Rodolfo Saglimbeni and Abreu and became music director of the Simón Bolívar Youth Orchestra in 1999, aged 18.

Career

His international breakthrough came in 2004, when he won the inaugural Bamberger Symphoniker Gustav Mahler Conducting Prize. Debuts with leading orchestras followed, and in 2007 he became music director of the Gothenburg Symphony. His tenure in Los Angeles, beginning in 2009, was marked by energetic programming, the Youth Orchestra Los Angeles (YOLA) education initiative modelled on El Sistema, and high-profile performances including the complete Mahler symphonies. He has conducted the Berlin and Vienna Philharmonics and led productions at La Scala and other opera houses.

Dudamel has recorded extensively for Deutsche Grammophon, winning multiple Grammy Awards, and has received honours including the Order of Francisco de Miranda in Venezuela and honorary doctorates. He is widely credited with popularising classical music to new audiences, though his position toward the Venezuelan government — which funded El Sistema — drew criticism during the protests of 2017, after which he spoke out against the government.
