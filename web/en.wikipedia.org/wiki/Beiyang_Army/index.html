Beiyang Army

Troops of the Beiyang Army, China's first modernised army.

The Beiyang Army (Chinese: 北洋軍; pinyin: Běiyáng-jūn, "Northern Ocean Army") was a powerful, Western-style imperial Chinese army established by the Qing dynasty in the 1890s and built up by Yuan Shikai. It was the first properly modernised army in China, trained by foreign instructors and equipped with modern weapons.

History

The army originated in the Newly Created Army formed after China's defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War; Yuan Shikai took command at Xiaozhan, near Tianjin, in 1895 and expanded it into the core of the Beiyang force, organised in divisions on the Japanese and German model, with its own military schools and arsenals. Its loyalty was to Yuan personally rather than to the throne, giving him decisive leverage in the revolution of 1911.

Fragmentation

After Yuan's death in 1916 the army split into competing cliques — principally the Zhili, Anhui and Fengtian factions — whose struggles for control of the Beijing government defined the Warlord Era. The Beiyang government remained the internationally recognised government of China until its defeat by the Nationalists' Northern Expedition in 1928.
