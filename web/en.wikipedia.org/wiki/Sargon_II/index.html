Sargon II

Sargon II (Neo-Assyrian Akkadian: Šarru-kīn, "the king is legitimate"; died 705 BC) was king of the Neo-Assyrian Empire from 722 BC until his death in battle in 705 BC. He seized the throne from his brother Shalmaneser V and proved one of Assyria's most energetic conquerors, subduing Samaria, defeating Urartu, reconquering Babylon and campaigning from Cyprus to Iran.

Reign

Sargon completed the conquest of the northern kingdom of Israel: Samaria fell in 722–720 BC and some 27,000 of its inhabitants were deported, a defining episode in the history of the "Ten Lost Tribes." In 714 BC he conducted a devastating campaign against Urartu, famously reported in his "Letter to the god Ashur," sacking the temple of Haldi at Musasir. He defeated the Babylonian ruler Marduk-apla-iddina II (Merodach-Baladan) and took Babylon in 710 BC, being crowned its king. His troops reached Cyprus, where a stele of his was erected at Kition.

Dur-Sharrukin

From about 717 BC Sargon built a new royal capital, Dur-Sharrukin ("Fort Sargon," modern Khorsabad), with a vast palace decorated with carved orthostat reliefs and colossal lamassu statues. He moved the court there in 706 BC but died the following year while campaigning in Anatolia; his body was never recovered, a catastrophe regarded as a divine judgement that led his son Sennacherib to abandon the city.

Legacy

Sargon's reign marks the height of Assyrian power. His annals and palace inscriptions are key sources for the period, and his dynasty — the Sargonid dynasty — ruled Assyria until its fall in 609 BC.
