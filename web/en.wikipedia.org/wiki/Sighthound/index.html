Sighthound

Sighthounds (also called gazehounds) are a type of hound dog that hunts primarily by sight and speed, rather than tracking by scent like scent hounds. They are among the oldest recognisable types of dog, depicted in art and artefacts spanning thousands of years, from ancient Egyptian tomb paintings to Celtic and Roman works.

Sighthounds are characterised by a distinctive build: a deep chest housing large lungs and heart, a long narrow head (often dolichocephalic), a tucked-up waist, long legs, and a flexible spine that allows an extended double-suspension gallop. Their vision is exceptionally acute, with a wide field of view — in some breeds approaching 270 degrees — and a high proportion of visual streak ganglion cells adapted to detecting movement across open terrain.

History

Sighthound-type dogs may descend from early Celtic hounds and from dogs kept in the Fertile Crescent. Ancient Egyptian depictions show slim, long-legged dogs with erect or semi-erect ears hunting gazelle. The Saluki, often called the Royal Dog of Egypt, is among the oldest known breeds, with remains suggesting antiquity of several thousand years. Sighthounds spread along trade routes across the Middle East, Central Asia, and Europe, giving rise to regional varieties adapted to local quarry and terrain.

Breeds

Recognised sighthound breeds include the Greyhound, Whippet, Saluki, Afghan Hound, Borzoi, Irish Wolfhound, Scottish Deerhound, the Italian Greyhound, the Spanish Galgo, the Hungarian Magyar agár, the Polish Chart Polski, and the Azawakh and Sloughi of Africa. Related primitive types, such as the Pharaoh Hound and Ibizan Hound, combine sight and scent hunting.

Hunting and sport

Traditionally, sighthounds coursed game such as hare, deer, gazelle, and wolf across open ground. Modern pursuits include lure coursing, amateur racing, and professional Greyhound racing, the latter now banned or declining in many jurisdictions on animal welfare grounds. In some regions, sighthound crosses known as lurchers are bred for working ability.

Temperament

Despite their athleticism, most sighthounds are calm, quiet, and undemanding indoors, often described as "couch potatoes" between bursts of exercise. They tend to be independent, gentle, and sensitive, with a strong prey drive that makes off-lead exercise in unsecured areas risky.
