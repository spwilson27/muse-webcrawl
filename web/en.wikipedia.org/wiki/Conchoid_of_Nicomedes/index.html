Conchoid of Nicomedes

The conchoid of Nicomedes, also known simply as a conchoid, is a plane curve invented by the ancient Greek mathematician Nicomedes (c. 280–210 BC). It is a special case of the general conchoid construction: given a fixed point (the pole), a fixed line (the directrix), and a fixed distance, the curve consists of points located that distance from the intersection of the directrix with lines drawn through the pole.

Construction and equation

In polar coordinates, with the pole at the origin and the directrix the line x = a, the conchoid has equation r = a sec θ ± d, where d is the fixed distance. The curve has two branches: an outer branch and an inner branch, whose shape depends on the ratio d/a — the inner branch may form a loop, a cusp, or a smooth wave. In Cartesian coordinates the equation is (x − a)2(x2 + y2) = d2x2, a quartic curve.

Applications

Nicomedes devised the curve to solve the classical problems of trisecting an angle and doubling the cube, both impossible with straightedge and compass alone. He reportedly built a mechanical device for drawing the conchoid.
