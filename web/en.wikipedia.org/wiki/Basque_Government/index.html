The Basque Government (Basque: Eusko Jaurlaritza; Spanish: Gobierno Vasco) is the governing body of the Basque Autonomous Community of Spain. It exercises executive power within the framework of the Statute of Autonomy of the Basque Country, approved in 1979, and is headed by the president, known in Basque as the lehendakari.

History

The first Basque Government was formed in October 1936 during the Spanish Civil War, led by José Antonio Aguirre of the Basque Nationalist Party. After the fall of Bilbao in 1937, the government continued in exile for decades. Following the restoration of democracy in Spain, the Statute of Gernika (1979) re-established Basque autonomy, and elections in 1980 returned a fully functioning Basque Government seated in Vitoria-Gasteiz.

Competences

The Basque Government holds broad competences including education, health (through Osakidetza), public safety (the Ertzaintza police force), culture and language policy promoting the Basque language, and economic development. Under the Economic Agreement (Concierto Económico), the Basque Country collects its own taxes and remits a quota to the Spanish state — a distinctive feature of its autonomy.

Structure

The government is organised into departments, each led by a councillor appointed by the lehendakari. It is accountable to the Basque Parliament, which elects the lehendakari following regional elections held every four years.
