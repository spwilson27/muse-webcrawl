Aztec Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aztec Empire
Period: 1428–1521
Capital: Tenochtitlan (site of Mexico City)
Language: Nahuatl

The Aztec Empire was a Mesoamerican state centred in the Valley of Mexico from 1428 until its conquest by Spain in 1521. It was ruled from Tenochtitlan, the island capital of the Mexica people — a city of canals and causeways on Lake Texcoco, with perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, larger than almost any city in contemporary Europe.

The empire

The Triple Alliance of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan exacted tribute — food, feathers, jade, warriors' costumes and sacrificial victims — from hundreds of subject city-states, without directly administering them. Aztec society was highly stratified and literate in a pictographic script; its calendar, mathematics, medicine and market system (Tlatelolco's market drew tens of thousands daily) impressed the conquistadors who destroyed it. Human sacrifice, practised on a scale historians debate but which was certainly substantial, was central to its cosmology of feeding the sun to keep the world alive.

Conquest

Hernán Cortés landed in 1519, allied with the Aztecs' enemies (above all Tlaxcala), seized the emperor Moctezuma II, survived expulsion in the Noche Triste (1520), and returned to besiege Tenochtitlan in 1521 with brigantines on the lake and smallpox ravaging the city. The last emperor, Cuauhtémoc, surrendered on 13 August 1521. Mexico City rose on the ruins; Nahuatl survives in millions of speakers and in words the world uses daily — chocolate, tomato, avocado, coyote. The empire's story is the Americas' counterpart to the Inca fall a decade later.
