Violence against women

Violence against women (VAW), also known as gender-based violence, is violence directed against a woman because she is a woman, or violence that affects women disproportionately. The United Nations' 1993 Declaration on the Elimination of Violence Against Women defines it as "any act of gender-based violence that results in, or is likely to result in, physical, sexual, or mental harm or suffering to women, including threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life."

Forms

It encompasses intimate partner violence and domestic violence, sexual violence including rape and sexual harassment, harmful practices such as female genital mutilation, child and forced marriage, dowry-related violence, so-called honour crimes, femicide, human trafficking, and online harassment. The World Health Organization has estimated that about one in three women worldwide has experienced physical or sexual violence in her lifetime, most often by an intimate partner.

Causes and effects

Analyses commonly use an ecological framework spanning individual, relationship, community, and societal factors, including gender inequality and norms condoning violence. Consequences include injury, depression, anxiety, sexually transmitted infections, unwanted pregnancy, and death, as well as large economic costs.

International response

Key instruments include CEDAW (1979), the UN Declaration (1993), the Beijing Platform for Action (1995), and the Council of Europe's Istanbul Convention (2011). November 25 is observed as the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women.
