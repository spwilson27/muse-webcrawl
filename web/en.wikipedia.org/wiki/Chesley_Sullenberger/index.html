Chesley Burnett "Sully" Sullenberger III (born 23 January 1951) is an American retired fighter pilot, diplomat, and airline pilot. He is best known for his role as pilot in command of US Airways Flight 1549, which he ditched in the Hudson River off Manhattan on 15 January 2009, after both engines were disabled by a bird strike; all 155 people aboard survived. The event became known as the "Miracle on the Hudson".

Early life and military career

Sullenberger grew up in Denison, Texas, and learned to fly as a teenager. He attended the United States Air Force Academy, graduating in 1973, and flew the F-4 Phantom II, becoming a flight leader and training officer and participating in accident investigations. He later earned a master's degree in industrial psychology from Purdue University and one in public administration from the University of Northern Colorado.

Flight 1549

On 15 January 2009, the Airbus A320 Sullenberger was captaining struck a flock of Canada geese shortly after takeoff from LaGuardia Airport, losing all engine power. Judging that he could reach neither LaGuardia nor Teterboro Airport, Sullenberger, with first officer Jeffrey Skiles, glided the aircraft to a water landing on the Hudson River. All passengers and crew evacuated and were rescued by nearby ferries and boats. Sullenberger was widely praised for his calm decision-making; he later said his training and experience in gliders and accident investigation had prepared him for the emergency.

Later career

Sullenberger retired from US Airways in 2010 and became an advocate for aviation safety, testifying before Congress and co-chairing the EAA Young Eagles youth aviation programme. His memoir Highest Duty (2009) was adapted into the 2016 Clint Eastwood film Sully, starring Tom Hanks. In 2021–2022 he served as the United States representative to the International Civil Aviation Organization.
