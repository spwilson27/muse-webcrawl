Johnson & Johnson (J&J) is an American multinational pharmaceutical and medical technologies corporation founded in 1886 and headquartered in New Brunswick, New Jersey. It is one of the world's largest healthcare companies.

History

Brothers Robert Wood Johnson I, James Wood Johnson and Edward Mead Johnson founded the company in 1886 to manufacture sterile surgical dressings, inspired by Joseph Lister's antiseptic methods. The company popularised first-aid kits and created the Band-Aid brand in 1920. Robert Wood Johnson II, who led the firm from 1932, wrote the company's Credo in 1943, a statement of responsibilities to customers, employees, communities and shareholders that still frames its corporate philosophy.

Business

Following the 2023 spin-off of its consumer health division as Kenvue — the maker of Band-Aid, Tylenol and Listerine brands under licence arrangements — Johnson & Johnson focuses on two segments: Innovative Medicine (the former Janssen pharmaceuticals business, with major products in oncology, immunology and neuroscience) and MedTech (surgical, orthopaedic and cardiovascular devices). The company is a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average and has one of the longest records of consecutive dividend increases among US companies.

Notable events

The company's handling of the 1982 Chicago Tylenol murders, in which cyanide-laced capsules killed seven people, is widely taught as a model of crisis management: J&J recalled 31 million bottles nationwide and introduced tamper-evident packaging. In later decades the company faced extensive litigation over talc-based baby powder, opioids and surgical mesh products.
