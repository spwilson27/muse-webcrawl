Colonial Parkway

The Colonial Parkway is a 23-mile (37 km) scenic parkway in the U.S. state of Virginia. It links the three communities of Virginia's Historic Triangle — Jamestown, Williamsburg, and Yorktown — and runs through Colonial National Historical Park. Designed to evoke an early American roadway, the parkway is a unit of the National Park Service and is notable for its decorative brick bridges, river views, and absence of commercial traffic.

Design

Conceived in the 1930s as part of the creation of Colonial National Monument, the parkway was engineered as a carefully landscaped "museum road." Its river-washed gravel aggregate surface gives it a distinctive pale, cobbled appearance, and overpasses are faced with hand-made brick in colonial Flemish bond. Commercial vehicles are prohibited, traffic signals are absent, and the speed limit is low; the road passes beneath Williamsburg through tunnels and follows the shores of the James and York rivers.

History

The first section, between Yorktown and Williamsburg, opened in the 1930s; construction continued after the Second World War, and the final link to Jamestown Island was completed in time for the 1957 Jamestown Festival marking the 350th anniversary of the first permanent English settlement. In 1989 the parkway was the scene of a series of unsolved murders of couples parked along it, known as the Colonial Parkway murders, which remain a cold case.

Route

From Jamestown the parkway crosses Powhatan Creek and the James River's tributaries, passes Colonial Williamsburg, then runs northeast along the York River past the Naval Weapons Station to the Yorktown Battlefield and the Yorktown Victory Monument.
