Umma (modern Tell Jokha) was an ancient Sumerian city-state in southern Mesopotamia, in what is now Dhi Qar Governorate, Iraq. It is best known for its long-running border conflict with the neighbouring city-state of Lagash and as the home of Lugalzagesi, the last Sumerian king before the rise of Sargon of Akkad.

Tell Jokha, identified with ancient Umma.

History

Umma's patron god was Shara, and its rulers bore the title ensi. The city's rivalry with Lagash over the fertile border district of Gu-Edinna is documented for some 150 years in royal inscriptions, beginning around 2450 BC: the boundary was repeatedly fixed by arbitration (going back to Mesilim of Kish), violated, and fought over, a struggle recorded most famously on Lagash's Stele of the Vultures and the cones of Entemena.

Under Lugalzagesi (c. 2350 BC), Umma defeated Lagash, and Lugalzagesi went on to conquer most of Sumer, claiming rule "from the Lower Sea to the Upper Sea", before being defeated and captured by Sargon of Akkad, founder of the Akkadian Empire. In the Ur III period Umma was a major provincial capital; tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets from its provincial archive survive, recording the administration of agriculture, livestock, and labour. This abundance is partly due to extensive illicit digging at the site in the early 20th century and again after 2003.

Archaeology

Tell Jokha was first noted by early travellers and was looted heavily in the early 1900s; scientific excavation has been limited, and much of the site's documentation comes from the enormous corpus of Umma tablets in museum collections worldwide.
