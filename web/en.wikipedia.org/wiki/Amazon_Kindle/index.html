Amazon Kindle

Amazon Kindle is a series of e-readers designed and marketed by Amazon. First released on 19 November 2007, Kindle devices use E Ink electronic paper displays that mimic the appearance of printed paper, are readable in bright sunlight, and consume very little power. Kindles allow users to browse, buy, download, and read e-books, newspapers, magazines, and other digital media via wireless networking from the Kindle Store.

History

The original Kindle, developed by Amazon's Lab126 subsidiary, sold out within hours of launch at $399. Successive generations added touchscreens, built-in front lighting (Paperwhite, 2012), higher-resolution waterproof models, and the premium Oasis line. The Kindle name derives from "kindling" a fire — evoking the lighting of a passion for reading. Amazon's Kindle Direct Publishing, launched alongside the device, let authors self-publish e-books, reshaping the publishing industry.

Formats and ecosystem

Kindles historically used Amazon's proprietary AZW format, later KF8/AZW3 and KFX, alongside support for PDF and (via conversion and later directly) EPUB. In 2022 Amazon phased out its MOBI support for Send to Kindle in favour of EPUB. Kindle apps for phones, tablets, and computers extend the ecosystem, and "Whispernet" wireless delivery over Wi-Fi (and formerly 3G) syncs libraries and reading positions between devices.
