Robert Reamer


Born: 1873, Oberlin, Ohio, U.S.
Died: 1938, Seattle, Washington, U.S.
Occupation: Architect
Known for: Old Faithful Inn

Robert Chambers Reamer (1873–1938) was an American architect, most noted as the designer of the Old Faithful Inn in Yellowstone National Park, one of the most celebrated works of rustic "parkitecture" in the United States.

Career

Reamer began his career as a draftsman in Detroit and later worked in San Diego before being engaged by the Yellowstone Park Association. For the Old Faithful Inn (built 1903–1904), he used local lodgepole pine logs and rhyolite stone to create a building that echoed the surrounding forest, with a soaring seven-storey lobby framed in gnarled log work. Its success made it the model for grand lodges across the American national parks.

He went on to design many other Yellowstone structures, including the Canyon Hotel (1911, demolished 1959), the Lake Yellowstone Hotel redesign, and the Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel, as well as hotels, theatres and residences in Seattle, where he later practised — among them the 5th Avenue Theatre interiors and the Edmond Meany Hotel.
