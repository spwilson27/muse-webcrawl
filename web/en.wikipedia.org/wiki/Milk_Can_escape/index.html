Milk Can escape

The Milk Can escape is a famous escapology act introduced by Harry Houdini in 1908. Houdini was handcuffed and sealed inside an oversized galvanised steel milk can filled with water, which was then locked inside a wooden cabinet or behind a curtain, and emerged free after holding his breath while the audience waited.

The secret

The can's neck and lid were constructed so that the lid, secured externally with hasps and padlocks, could be rotated and lifted from inside: the riveted collar was not attached to the body of the can, allowing Houdini to push up the inner lid assembly, climb out, and reseat it so the locks appeared undisturbed. Because the can was filled to the brim, escaping also required breath control while submerged.

Legacy

The escape became one of Houdini's signature effects and was widely imitated. An original Milk Can is held by magic-history collections and museums. Later variations substituted other liquids, including beer.
