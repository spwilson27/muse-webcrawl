The Cisalpine Republic (Italian: Repubblica Cisalpina) was a sister republic of Revolutionary France in northern Italy, created by Napoleon Bonaparte during his first Italian campaign. It united the former Transpadane and Cispadane republics into a single state with its capital at Milan, encompassing much of Lombardy, Emilia, and Romagna.

Formation

After his victories over Austria in 1796–97, Napoleon merged the Cispadane Republic (Modena, Reggio, Ferrara, Bologna, and Romagna) with the Transpadane Republic (Lombardy) on 29 June 1797, forming the Cisalpine Republic. Its constitution, modelled on the French Constitution of 1795, provided for a five-member Directory and a bicameral legislature. The state adopted a green-white-red tricolour — the direct ancestor of the modern Italian flag — first raised at Reggio Emilia in January 1797 by the Cispadane Republic. The Treaty of Campo-Formio (October 1797) secured Austrian recognition of the republic, though Venice and its mainland territories were handed to Austria.

Second Coalition and restoration

In 1799 Austro-Russian forces overran Italy during the War of the Second Coalition and the republic collapsed. Napoleon's victory at Marengo (14 June 1800) restored French control, and the Cisalpine Republic was re-established, its territory enlarged by the Treaty of Lunéville (1801) to include the Novarese and, briefly, Venetian lands west of the Adige.

Becoming the Italian Republic

In January 1802 an assembly at Lyon, attended by Italian deputies, adopted a new constitution renaming the state the Italian Republic, with Napoleon as its president and Francesco Melzi d'Eril as vice-president. In 1805, after Napoleon crowned himself Emperor of the French, the republic was transformed into the Kingdom of Italy.

Legacy

Despite being a French client state subject to heavy requisitions and political interference, the Cisalpine Republic introduced modern legal codes, abolished feudal privileges, and gave Italians their first experience of a large unified state in the peninsula — a precedent for the later Risorgimento. Its tricolour endures as Italy's national flag.
