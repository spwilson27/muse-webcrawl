Alfred Deakin

Alfred Deakin (3 August 1856 – 7 October 1919) was an Australian politician who served as the second prime minister of Australia, holding office three times (1903–04, 1905–08, 1909–10). A leader of the federation movement, he was a dominant figure in the new Commonwealth's first decade.

Early life

Born in Fitzroy, Melbourne, Deakin worked as a teacher and journalist, studied law, and entered the Victorian parliament in 1879. He became a minister, promoted irrigation after visiting California, and was a leading Victorian delegate to the federation conventions of the 1890s.

Prime minister

As attorney-general under Edmund Barton and then prime minister, Deakin's governments built the foundations of the Australian state: protective tariffs, the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration, old-age pensions, the beginnings of an Australian navy, and the White Australia policy framework. In 1909 he merged his Protectionists with Joseph Cook's Anti-Socialists in the "Fusion" to counter the rising Labor Party.

Later life

Deakin retired from parliament in 1913 as his memory failed, likely from early-onset Alzheimer's disease. A prolific and anonymous political commentator, his writings remain a major source on federation.
