Polish–Teutonic War (1519–1521)
Date: 1519 – 1521
Location: Prussia
Result: Truce of Toruń; later Treaty of Kraków (1525)

The Polish–Teutonic War of 1519–1521 was a conflict between the Kingdom of Poland and the Teutonic Order, fought over the Grand Master Albert of Hohenzollern's refusal to pay homage to King Sigismund I the Old for the Order's Prussian lands.

Course of the war

Fighting began at the end of 1519. Teutonic forces raided Royal Prussia, while Polish forces besieged Marienburg and campaigned in Ducal Prussia. Neither side could force a decisive result, and an armistice mediated by Emperor Charles V and King Louis II of Hungary was agreed at Toruń in April 1521.

Outcome

The four-year truce expired in 1525, by which time Albert had embraced Lutheranism and negotiated the Treaty of Kraków, secularising the Order's Prussian territory as the Duchy of Prussia, held as a Polish fief.
