Jules Régis Debray (born 2 September 1940) is a French philosopher, journalist, former government official, and academic. He is known for his involvement with the Marxist revolutionary Che Guevara in Bolivia in 1967, for his book Révolution dans la révolution? (Revolution in the Revolution?), and for founding the discipline of mediology — the study of how ideas are transmitted through material and institutional means.

Bolivia

A graduate of the École Normale Supérieure, where he studied under Louis Althusser, Debray travelled to Cuba and then Bolivia as a journalist and intermediary for Guevara's guerrilla campaign. His 1967 book codified the "foco" theory of guerrilla warfare. Debray was captured by the Bolivian Army in April 1967, tried by a military court, and sentenced to 30 years' imprisonment. An international campaign for his release — supported by figures such as Jean-Paul Sartre and Charles de Gaulle — secured his freedom in 1970 after just over three years.

Later career

After the election of François Mitterrand in 1981, Debray became an adviser on foreign affairs at the Élysée Palace. He later served on the Conseil d'État and held academic posts, developing mediology in works such as Vie et mort de l'image (1992) and Transmitting Culture. He wrote extensively on religion, national identity, and the French Republic, including the dialogues published as Le siècle et la règle, and served on the Stasi Commission on secularism in 2003.

Debray's prolific output spans philosophy, political memoir, and travel writing; he has remained a prominent and sometimes controversial public intellectual in France.
