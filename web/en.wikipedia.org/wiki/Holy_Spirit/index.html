Holy Spirit

This article is about the Christian concept. For the term in Judaism, see Holy Spirit in Judaism.


The Holy Spirit is traditionally depicted as a dove, from the baptism of Jesus.

In Christianity, the Holy Spirit (or Holy Ghost) is the third person of the Trinity, consubstantial with God the Father and God the Son. Christians believe the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father (and, in Western theology, from the Son — the Filioque), and plays an active role in creation, revelation, the life of Jesus, and the sanctification of believers.

In Scripture

The Hebrew Bible speaks of the ruach (wind, breath, spirit) of God hovering over the waters in Genesis, inspiring prophets and craftsmen. In the New Testament the Spirit descends on Jesus at his baptism in the form of a dove, is promised by Jesus as the Paraclete ("advocate" or "comforter") in John's Gospel, and descends on the apostles at Pentecost with wind and tongues of fire, enabling them to speak in many languages. Paul's letters describe the Spirit's fruits (love, joy, peace, and others) and gifts, including prophecy, healing and speaking in tongues.

Doctrine

The Nicene Creed (325, expanded at Constantinople in 381) confesses the Spirit as "the Lord, the giver of life, who proceeds from the Father, who with the Father and the Son is adored and glorified". The Western addition of the Filioque clause ("and the Son") became a major cause of the East–West Schism of 1054. Most mainstream Christian churches — Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox and most Protestant — are Trinitarian and affirm the Spirit's full divinity; some groups, such as Unitarians and Oneness Pentecostals, hold differing views.

Pentecostalism and charismatic movements

Pentecostal and charismatic Christianity emphasise a "baptism in the Holy Spirit", evidenced by glossolalia (speaking in tongues) and other charismatic gifts. Since the early twentieth century these movements have grown into one of the largest strands of world Christianity, particularly in Africa, Latin America and Asia.

Symbols and feasts

The Spirit is symbolised by a dove, fire, wind, water, oil and light. The feast of Pentecost, fifty days after Easter, celebrates the Spirit's descent on the apostles.
