Studbook

A studbook records the pedigrees of breeding animals of a recognised breed

A studbook is an official list of animals within a specific breed whose parents are known; individual animals are recorded by breeders with a breed registry or studbook authority, which may be a breed association, club or government body. Studbooks are maintained for horses, dogs, cattle, cats and other domesticated species, and, by extension, for captive populations of endangered wild animals in zoos.

Types

A closed studbook admits only offspring of already-registered parents, so all members trace to a foundation stock recorded when the book was opened — as with the General Stud Book for Thoroughbred horses, founded in 1791. An open studbook allows animals to be registered after inspection against the breed standard even if their parents were unregistered. Appendix registries form a middle path, recording part-bred animals that may "grade up" over generations.

Zoo studbooks

For endangered species, regional and international studbooks track every individual in captivity — births, deaths, transfers and parentage — so coordinators can plan pairings that preserve genetic diversity and avoid inbreeding. Notable examples include the studbooks for the golden lion tamarin and the Arabian oryx, both central to successful reintroduction programmes.
