Glivenko–Cantelli theorem

In the theory of probability, the Glivenko–Cantelli theorem states that the empirical distribution function of a random sample converges uniformly to the true cumulative distribution function almost surely. It is sometimes called the fundamental theorem of statistics.

Statement

Given independent, identically distributed random variables with distribution function F, and letting Fn denote the empirical distribution function based on n observations, the theorem asserts that supx |Fn(x) − F(x)| → 0 almost surely as n → ∞.

History and significance

The result was proved by Valery Glivenko and Francesco Cantelli in 1933. It strengthens the law of large numbers from pointwise to uniform convergence and motivates extensions to general classes of functions, where classes satisfying such uniform convergence are called Glivenko–Cantelli classes — a central concept in empirical process theory and statistical learning.
