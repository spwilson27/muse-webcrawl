Stephen Timoshenko

Stephen Prokofievich Timoshenko (22 December 1878 – 29 May 1972) was a Ukrainian-born American engineer and academician, regarded as the father of modern engineering mechanics. He pioneered work in elasticity, strength of materials and structural mechanics, including the Timoshenko beam theory, which accounts for shear deformation in beams.

Career

Educated in Kyiv and Saint Petersburg, Timoshenko taught in Ukraine and at Göttingen before emigrating to the United States in 1922 amid the Russian Civil War. He worked at Westinghouse, then held professorships at the University of Michigan and Stanford University, where his textbooks — including Strength of Materials, Theory of Elasticity (with J. N. Goodier) and History of Strength of Materials — trained generations of engineers.

Legacy

The Timoshenko Medal of the American Society of Mechanical Engineers, established in 1957, was first awarded to Timoshenko himself. His application of mathematical rigor to practical engineering problems transformed the discipline worldwide.
