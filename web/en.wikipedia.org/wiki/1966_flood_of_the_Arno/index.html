1966 flood of the Arno

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1966 flood of the Arno (Italian: Alluvione di Firenze) struck Florence, Italy, on 4–5 November 1966 after torrential rain swelled the Arno River. It was the worst flood in the city's history since 1557, killing at least 101 people and damaging or destroying millions of masterpieces of art and rare books.

The flood

After weeks of rain, the Arno burst through the city in the early hours of 4 November — a public holiday commemorating Italy's First World War victory, which left many institutions unprepared. Water rose as high as 6.7 metres in the Santa Croce quarter; heating oil from ruptured tanks stained the mud, worsening the damage to frescoes, paintings, and library collections.

Damage to art and rescue

Among the losses and casualties among artworks: Cimabue's great Crucifix in Santa Croce was gravely damaged, the doors of the Baptistery were torn free (and recovered), and the National Central Library saw over a million volumes inundated in its riverside storeys. Thousands of young volunteers from Italy and abroad — the angeli del fango, the "mud angels" — flocked to Florence to rescue books and artworks, and the disaster spurred the modern discipline of art conservation and flood-response planning for cultural heritage. Restoration of some works continued for decades.
