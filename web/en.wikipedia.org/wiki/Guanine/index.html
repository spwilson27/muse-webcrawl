Guanine

Guanine
Formula: C5H5N5O
One of the four main nucleobases in DNA and RNA
Symbol: G

Guanine (symbol G) is one of the four main nucleobases found in the nucleic acids DNA and RNA, the others being adenine, cytosine, and thymine (uracil in RNA). It is a purine derivative: a fused pyrimidine-imidazole ring system with conjugated double bonds. In DNA, guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds, forming the G≡C base pair.

History

Guanine was first isolated in 1844 by Julius Bodo Unger from guano—the excrement of seabirds—from which it takes its name. It was among the first nucleobases identified; Albrecht Kossel and others established its structure and its role in nucleic acids in the late 19th century.

Other roles

Beyond nucleic acids, guanine derivatives are central to cell biology: GTP is an energy carrier and signalling molecule used by G proteins, and cyclic GMP acts as a second messenger. Guanine crystals are also responsible for the silvery iridescence of many fish scales and are used as a pearlescent pigment in cosmetics.
