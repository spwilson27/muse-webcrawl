The United States national laboratories are a system of federally funded research and development centers sponsored chiefly by the Department of Energy (DOE), which maintains 17 national laboratories. Most operate under the government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO) model, in which universities or private consortia manage the facilities for the government. The system grew out of the Manhattan Project and now employs tens of thousands of scientists and engineers working on energy, national security, basic science, and environmental management.

Department of Energy laboratories

The DOE laboratories include Los Alamos and Lawrence Livermore (nuclear weapons design), Sandia (weapons engineering), Oak Ridge, Argonne, Brookhaven, Lawrence Berkeley, Fermi (Fermilab), SLAC, Pacific Northwest, Idaho, the National Renewable Energy Laboratory, Princeton Plasma Physics, Ames, Jefferson Lab, Savannah River, and the National Energy Technology Laboratory.

Other national laboratories

Other agencies run laboratory systems often grouped under the same heading: the National Institute of Standards and Technology, the National Institutes of Health intramural program, NASA centers, and defense laboratories such as the Naval Research Laboratory and the Air Force Research Laboratory.

User facilities

Many laboratories host scientific user facilities — synchrotrons, neutron sources, supercomputers, and accelerators — open to academic and industrial researchers through peer-reviewed proposals.
