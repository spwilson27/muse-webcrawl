Phosphatidylinositol

Phosphatidylinositol (abbreviated PtdIns or PI) is a class of phospholipids forming a minor but functionally crucial component of eukaryotic cell membranes. Its structure consists of a glycerol backbone bearing two fatty-acid chains and a phosphate group linked to the sugar alcohol inositol.

Phosphoinositides

Phosphorylation of the inositol ring at the 3, 4, and 5 positions by specific kinases (and removal by phosphatases) generates seven phosphoinositides, including phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) and phosphatidylinositol 3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3). These lipids act as membrane identity markers and docking sites that recruit proteins containing PH, PX, FYVE, and other lipid-binding domains to specific organelles.

Signalling roles

PIP2 is hydrolysed by phospholipase C into two second messengers: inositol trisphosphate (IP3), which releases calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum, and diacylglycerol (DAG), which activates protein kinase C. PIP3, produced by phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), drives the Akt/mTOR growth and survival pathway; its antagonist, the lipid phosphatase PTEN, is a major tumour suppressor. Defects in phosphoinositide metabolism are implicated in cancer, diabetes, and neurological disorders.
