Rill

In hillslope geomorphology, a rill is a shallow channel cut into soil by the erosive action of flowing water. Rills are narrow, typically no more than a few centimetres deep, and can be removed by normal tillage — a feature distinguishing them from larger, permanent gullies.

Formation

Rills form when surface runoff concentrates into small flows with enough shear stress to detach and transport soil particles, a process called rill erosion. It commonly occurs on bare, sloping agricultural land after heavy rain, often alongside sheet erosion between the channels.

Impact and control

Rill erosion is a major contributor to soil loss worldwide. It is controlled by contour ploughing, cover crops, mulching, terracing, and maintaining vegetation buffers that slow runoff and increase infiltration. On planetary surfaces, features described as rills — lunar rilles are a separate, volcanic or tectonic usage of the related word.
