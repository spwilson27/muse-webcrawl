Scalar field

In mathematics and physics, a scalar field is a function assigning a single number (a scalar) to every point in a space. The scalar may represent a physical quantity, such as temperature or pressure, or an abstract mathematical value. Scalar fields are contrasted with vector fields, which assign a vector to each point, and with tensor fields of higher rank.

A scalar field visualised by colour: each point of the plane carries one numerical value.

Examples

Everyday examples include the temperature distribution in a room, the pressure field in a fluid, and elevation on a topographic map. In physics, the electric potential is a scalar field whose gradient gives the electric field; the Higgs field is a fundamental scalar field in the Standard Model of particle physics; and hypothetical fields such as the inflaton are invoked in cosmology. Weather maps of temperature or barometric pressure are classical visualisations of scalar fields over the Earth's surface.

Mathematical treatment

A scalar field on a region of space is a differentiable function φ(x, y, z) (or, in space-time, φ(x)). Its key derived quantities are the gradient ∇φ, a vector field pointing in the direction of steepest increase, and the Laplacian ∇²φ, which governs diffusion and wave equations such as the heat equation and the Klein–Gordon equation for relativistic scalar particles. In quantum field theory, scalar fields are quantised into spin-0 particles, of which the Higgs boson is the confirmed example.
