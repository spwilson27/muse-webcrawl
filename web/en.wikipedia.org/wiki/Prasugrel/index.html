Prasugrel

Prasugrel, sold under the brand names Effient and Efient, is a medication used to prevent formation of blood clots in people with acute coronary syndrome undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (stent placement). It belongs to the thienopyridine class of P2Y12 platelet inhibitors, alongside clopidogrel and the related drug ticagrelor (a different chemical class). It was developed by Daiichi Sankyo and Eli Lilly and approved for medical use in Europe and the United States in 2009.

Medical uses

Prasugrel is used, in combination with low-dose aspirin, to reduce the rate of thrombotic cardiovascular events (including stent thrombosis) in patients with acute coronary syndrome — unstable angina, NSTEMI, or STEMI — who are managed with angioplasty and stenting. It is not generally used for medically managed patients.

Pharmacology

Like clopidogrel, prasugrel is a prodrug, but its activation is more efficient and less dependent on variable CYP enzymes: intestinal and plasma esterases first hydrolyse it, after which a single CYP-mediated step produces the active metabolite, which irreversibly blocks the platelet P2Y12 ADP receptor for the life of the platelet. The result is a faster onset and more consistent, more potent platelet inhibition than clopidogrel.

Adverse effects and trials

The pivotal TRITON-TIMI 38 trial found prasugrel reduced ischaemic events compared with clopidogrel but increased major and life-threatening bleeding. It is contraindicated in patients with prior stroke or transient ischaemic attack, and caution or dose reduction applies in patients over 75 years of age or under 60 kg. The excess bleeding risk led regulators to restrict its use to patients proceeding to PCI.
