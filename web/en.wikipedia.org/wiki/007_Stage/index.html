The 007 Stage, officially the Albert R. Broccoli 007 Stage, is one of the largest sound stages in the world, located at Pinewood Studios in Buckinghamshire, England. It was built in 1976 especially for the James Bond film The Spy Who Loved Me (1977) and is named after the 007 codename of the fictional secret agent.

History

For The Spy Who Loved Me, production designer Ken Adam needed to build the interior of a supertanker — the Liparus set, capable of holding multiple full-size submarine pens — which no existing stage could accommodate. The 007 Stage was constructed at a cost of about $1.8 million. The set required so much light that cinematographer Claude Renoir was nearly unable to see to work; Stanley Kubrick secretly visited to advise on lighting the vast interior.

On 27 June 1984, shortly before filming of A View to a Kill began, the stage burned down after a gas canister explosion during production of Ridley Scott's Legend. It was rebuilt and reopened in January 1985, renamed in honour of Bond producer Albert R. "Cubby" Broccoli. The stage caught fire again in 2006 after production wrapped on Casino Royale, and was rebuilt once more, reopening in 2007.

Dimensions and use

The stage measures roughly 102 m × 42 m (336 ft × 135 ft) with a height of about 15 m (50 ft), giving an area near 59,000 square feet (5,500 m²). It includes a permanent water tank for underwater and flooding scenes. Although created for the Bond series, the stage has hosted many other major productions, including Superman films, Aliens, Batman, Lara Croft: Tomb Raider, Charlie and the Chocolate Factory and Mamma Mia!
