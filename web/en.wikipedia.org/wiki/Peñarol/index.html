Club Atlético Peñarol is a Uruguayan sports club based in Montevideo, best known for its professional football team. Founded on 28 September 1891 as the Central Uruguay Railway Cricket Club (CURCC) by employees of the British-owned railway company, it took its present name in 1913–14; the club's name derives from the Peñarol district of Montevideo, itself named after the Italian town of Pinerolo. Peñarol is one of the most successful clubs in South American football.

History

Peñarol and its great rival Nacional dominate Uruguayan football; their derby, the Clásico del fútbol uruguayo, is one of the oldest rivalries in world football outside Britain. The club has won the Uruguayan Primera División championship more than fifty times (including titles won as CURCC, whose counting is disputed by Nacional).

International titles

Peñarol won the Copa Libertadores five times (1960, 1961, 1966, 1982 and 1987) and the Intercontinental Cup three times (1961, 1966 and 1982), becoming the first club to win the Intercontinental Cup three times. It won the inaugural Copa Libertadores in 1960. In 2009 the International Federation of Football History & Statistics (IFFHS) named Peñarol the South American Club of the 20th Century.

Stadium

For most of its history Peñarol played home matches at the national Estadio Centenario. In 2016 it opened its own stadium, the Estadio Campeón del Siglo ("Champion of the Century Stadium"), with a capacity of about 40,000. The club also fields teams in basketball and other sports, and its yellow-and-black striped shirts are said to be inspired by the colours of the Rocket locomotive.
