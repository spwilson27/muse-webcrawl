Gamebook


A gamebook is a work of printed fiction that lets the reader make choices that affect the story.

A gamebook is a work of printed fiction that allows the reader to participate in the story by making choices. The narrative branches along various paths, typically through numbered paragraphs or pages: at the end of each section the reader chooses between options, each directing them to another numbered section.

History

Branching fiction has precursors in mid-20th-century experimental literature, but the gamebook format reached mass popularity from the late 1970s. The American Choose Your Own Adventure series (from 1979) sold hundreds of millions of copies aimed at children, using second-person narration without dice or rules. The British Fighting Fantasy series (from 1982, created by Steve Jackson and Ian Livingstone, beginning with The Warlock of Firetop Mountain) added role-playing elements — skill, stamina, and luck scores, dice-based combat, and inventories — and inspired series such as Lone Wolf by Joe Dever, notable for continuity across books, and Sorcery!.

Format and legacy

Gamebooks combine narrative with varying degrees of game mechanics: pure choice-based branching, dice and character statistics, or (rarely) maps and puzzles. The genre declined in the 1990s as video games grew, but experienced revivals through reprints, mobile app adaptations, and digital interactive fiction; its branching-choice structure strongly influenced adventure games and narrative video games.
