10 Gigabit Ethernet

10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GE, 10GbE or 10 GigE) is a group of Ethernet standards carrying traffic at 10 gigabits per second. The first standard, IEEE 802.3ae, was published in 2002 and initially defined fibre-optic links only; later amendments added copper cabling, including 10GBASE-T over twisted pair. Unlike earlier Ethernet, 10 Gigabit Ethernet operates only in full duplex over switched links.

Speed10 Gbit/s
First standardIEEE 802.3ae-2002
OperationFull duplex only
MediaSingle/multi-mode fibre, twinax, Cat 6a UTP

Standards and media

The 802.3ae standard defined LAN (10GBASE-SR, -LR, -ER) and WAN physical layers over fibre, with ranges from tens of metres on multimode fibre to 40 km on single-mode fibre. 10GBASE-T (802.3an, 2006) brought 10 Gbit/s to twisted-pair cabling — up to 100 m on Category 6a — at the cost, initially, of higher power consumption and latency. For short in-rack connections, 10GBASE-CR direct-attach twinax cables with SFP+ connectors became popular in data centres.

Applications

10 Gigabit Ethernet was adopted first for backbone links, metropolitan networks and storage networking, then as a server connection in data centres, where it enabled consolidation of LAN and storage traffic and supported virtualisation at scale. Modular form factors — XENPAK, X2, XFP and the compact SFP+ — succeeded one another as the technology matured and prices fell. It was in turn succeeded in high-end roles by 25, 40 and 100 Gigabit Ethernet.
