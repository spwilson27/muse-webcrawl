Tlatelolco massacre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tlatelolco massacre (Spanish: La masacre de Tlatelolco) was the killing of an estimated 300–400 students and civilians by Mexican government forces on 2 October 1968, in the Plaza de las Tres Culturas in the Tlatelolco section of Mexico City, ten days before the opening of the Summer Olympics in the city.

Background

Throughout the summer of 1968, a student movement had grown in Mexico City, demanding democratic reforms, university autonomy, the release of political prisoners and the dismissal of the chief of police. The government of President Gustavo Díaz Ordaz, determined to present a stable image for the Olympic Games, responded with escalating repression, including the occupation of the National Autonomous University (UNAM) campus by troops.

2 October 1968

On the afternoon of 2 October, several thousand students and supporters gathered peacefully in the Plaza de las Tres Culturas for a rally of the National Strike Council. As the rally ended, army troops and plainclothes agents of the paramilitary "Batallón Olimpia", identifiable by a white glove or handkerchief, surrounded the plaza and opened fire on the crowd. Snipers on surrounding rooftops returned fire, and soldiers fired into the square for hours. The government reported only a few dozen dead; later investigations and declassified documents support estimates in the hundreds, and the precise death toll remains disputed.

Aftermath

The massacre ended the 1968 student movement and became a symbol of state violence in Mexico, seared into public memory by Elena Poniatowska's 1971 oral history La noche de Tlatelolco. In the 2000s, a special prosecutor investigated former officials, and a memorial and museum now mark the plaza. The event remains a central episode in Mexico's democratic history.
