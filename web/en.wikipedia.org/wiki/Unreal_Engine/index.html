Unreal Engine (UE) is a 3D computer graphics game engine developed by Epic Games, first showcased in the 1998 first-person shooter Unreal. Initially built for PC shooters, it has grown into one of the most widely used engines in the video game industry and is also used extensively in film and television production, architecture, automotive visualization, and simulation. Its visual scripting system, Blueprint, and its marketplace ecosystem made it a standard tool for independent developers as well as major studios.

History

Unreal Engine 1–3

The first-generation engine powered Unreal (1998) and Unreal Tournament (1999), written largely by Epic founder Tim Sweeney. Unreal Engine 2 powered the Xbox-era Unreal Tournament 2003/2004 and licensed titles such as BioShock. Unreal Engine 3 (2006), debuting with Gears of War, became the dominant engine of the PlayStation 3/Xbox 360 generation, licensing to hundreds of studios.

Unreal Engine 4

Unreal Engine 4 (2014) introduced the Blueprint visual scripting system and a subscription model; in 2015 Epic made it free with a 5% royalty on gross revenue above a threshold, and released its source code to registered developers. UE4 powered games including Fortnite Battle Royale, PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds, and Final Fantasy VII Remake.

Unreal Engine 5

Announced in 2020 and released in April 2022, Unreal Engine 5 introduced Nanite (virtualized micropolygon geometry), Lumen (fully dynamic global illumination), and World Partition for large open worlds. It was first demonstrated with the "Lumen in the Land of Nanite" PlayStation 5 demo and Epic's The Matrix Awakens. UE5 has been adopted for major titles and for virtual production on series such as The Mandalorian, which combined earlier Unreal technology with LED volume stages.

Licensing and use

Since 2015 the engine has been free to download, with royalties applying after a product earns a set lifetime revenue threshold (raised to $1 million in 2020). Beyond games, Unreal is used in broadcast graphics, concert visuals, architectural walkthroughs, and automotive configurators; Epic also develops the MetaHuman framework for photorealistic digital characters.
