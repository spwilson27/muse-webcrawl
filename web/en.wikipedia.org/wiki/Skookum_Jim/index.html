Skookum Jim

Skookum Jim
Born: Keish, c. 1855, near Bennett Lake, Yukon
Died: 11 July 1916, Whitehorse, Yukon
Known for: Co-discovery of gold that started the Klondike Gold Rush

Keish (c. 1855 – July 11, 1916), better known as Skookum Jim Mason, was a member of the Tagish First Nation in what is now the Yukon Territory of Canada. He is credited as one of the co-discoverers of the gold that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush. "Skookum" is a Chinook Jargon word meaning strong or reliable — a nickname earned by his feats of strength, including carrying 156-pound (71 kg) loads over the Chilkoot Pass.

In August 1896 Keish, his sister Kate (Shaaw Tláa) and her husband George Carmack, and Keish's nephew Dawson Charlie (K̲áa Goox̱), were travelling near the mouth of the Klondike River. Guided by Keish's knowledge of the country and, in Tagish tradition, by a vision involving his Frog spirit helper and a storm, the party found rich gold on Rabbit Creek — soon renamed Bonanza Creek — on August 16, 1896. The discovery claim was registered in Carmack's name, and credit in most early accounts went to Carmack, though Tagish oral history and later scholarship recognize Keish's central role.

Keish became wealthy from his claims — he is often described as one of the richest Indigenous men of his time — and used his fortune to help his community, funding supplies during hard winters. He died in Whitehorse in 1916. In 2020 he was named a National Historic Person by the Government of Canada, recognising the Tagish contribution to the gold rush.
