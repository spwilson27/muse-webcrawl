Hauts-de-Seine (French pronunciation: [o d(ə) sɛn], "Upper Seine") is a department in the Île-de-France region of France, forming the western inner suburbs of Paris. It takes its name from the River Seine, which loops through it, and — despite the name suggesting heights — it is a small, densely urbanised department. It is distinct from the northern region of Hauts-de-France.

Geography and economy

Hauts-de-Seine covers 36 communes arranged in a crescent west of Paris, from Gennevilliers in the north to Antony in the south. Its best-known feature is La Défense, Europe's largest purpose-built business district, spanning Puteaux, Courbevoie, and Nanterre and crowned by the Grande Arche. The department also contains wealthy residential suburbs such as Neuilly-sur-Seine, Boulogne-Billancourt (long a centre of the French film and aviation industries), Saint-Cloud, and Sèvres, home of the international standards bureau and the national ceramics museum. Hauts-de-Seine is consistently among the richest departments in France by household income.

History

The department was created on 1 January 1968 under the law re-organising the Paris region, from parts of the former departments of Seine and Seine-et-Oise. It quickly became a stronghold of the Gaullist and later mainstream right; Nicolas Sarkozy served as mayor of Neuilly-sur-Seine and then president of the departmental council before becoming President of France.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Parc de Saint-Cloud, the Albert Kahn Museum and gardens in Boulogne-Billancourt, the Château de Malmaison (Josephine Bonaparte's residence, in Rueil-Malmaison), and the La Défense Arena in Nanterre.
