The Haunting is a 1963 British supernatural horror film directed by Robert Wise and adapted by Nelson Gidding from Shirley Jackson's 1959 novel The Haunting of Hill House. It stars Julie Harris as Eleanor Lance, a shy, psychologically fragile woman invited by parapsychologist Dr. John Markway (Richard Johnson) to spend time in Hill House, a New England mansion with a history of violent death, alongside the clairvoyant Theodora (Claire Bloom) and the sceptical heir Luke (Russ Tamblyn).

Style

The film is celebrated for generating terror almost entirely through suggestion: distorted wide-angle cinematography, unsettling sound design, a famously bending door, and Eleanor's interior monologue blur the line between haunting and mental breakdown. Exterior shots used Ettington Park in Warwickshire, its stone façade given a sinister aspect by infrared photography.

Legacy

Initially a modest success, The Haunting is now considered one of the greatest horror films ever made; Martin Scorsese has named it the scariest film he has seen. A poorly received remake, also titled The Haunting, appeared in 1999. Jackson's novel has since been adapted again, including the 2018 television series The Haunting of Hill House.
