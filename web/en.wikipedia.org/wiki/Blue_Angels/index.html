The Blue Angels is the United States Navy's flight demonstration squadron, formed in 1946. It is the second oldest formal aerobatic team in the world. Flying blue-and-gold F/A-18 Super Hornets in formations as close as 18 inches apart, the team performs at air shows across the United States before millions of spectators each year.

History

The team was established by Chief of Naval Operations Admiral Chester Nimitz to raise public interest in naval aviation after the Second World War, and flew its first show in June 1946 in Grumman F6F Hellcats. It took its name from the Blue Angel nightclub in New York. Over the decades it has flown the F8F Bearcat, F9F Panther and Cougar, F11F Tiger, F-4 Phantom II, A-4 Skyhawk, and, from 1986 to 2020, the F/A-18 Hornet, before transitioning to the Super Hornet.

The show

A demonstration combines the six-jet "Diamond" formation manoeuvres with opposing solos performing high-speed passes, and closes with the delta formation. The support C-130, nicknamed "Fat Albert", traditionally opens the show. Pilots serve two to three years, are selected from fleet squadrons, and fly aircraft stripped of paint tolerances that could still be returned to combat duty within days.
