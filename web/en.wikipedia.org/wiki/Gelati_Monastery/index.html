Gelati Monastery

Gelati Monastery — Kutaisi, Imereti, Georgia. Founded 1106 by David IV of Georgia. UNESCO World Heritage Site (listed 1994).

Gelati is a medieval monastic complex near Kutaisi in the Imereti region of western Georgia. One of the largest medieval Orthodox monasteries, it was also a centre of science and education, and the Gelati Academy was one of the most important centres of culture in ancient Georgia.

History

The monastery was founded in 1106 by King David IV of Georgia ("David the Builder"), who is buried there. For a long period the monastery was one of the main cultural and intellectual centers in Georgia; its academy employed celebrated Georgian philosophers such as Ioane Petritsi and Arsen Ikaltoeli. Local tradition held Gelati to be a "second Jerusalem" and a "new Athens".

The main church, dedicated to the Virgin Mary, is famed for its mosaics, including a large apse mosaic of the Virgin and Child flanked by archangels, dated to the 1120s–1130s. Other churches in the complex are dedicated to St George and St Nicholas, and there are extensive medieval frescoes and enamel and metalwork treasures, many now kept in the Georgian National Museum.

World Heritage status

Gelati Monastery was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1994, jointly with the nearby Bagrati Cathedral; in 2017 the site listing was revised to Gelati Monastery alone after reconstruction work on Bagrati Cathedral led to its removal from the property and transfer to the List of World Heritage in Danger discussion (Bagrati was removed from the World Heritage List in 2017, while Gelati retained the status).

Burials

The monastery is the burial place of several Georgian kings, including David IV, Demetrius I, and George III.
