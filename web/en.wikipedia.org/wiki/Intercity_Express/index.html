Intercity Express

Intercity Express (ICE)
Operator: Deutsche Bahn
Manufacturer: Siemens and partners
Entered service: 1991
Maximum speed: 300 km/h (ICE 3)

The Intercity Express (ICE) is a system of high-speed trains predominantly running in Germany, operated by Deutsche Bahn. ICE trains are the highest category of rail service in Germany and connect major cities at speeds of up to 300 km/h.

History

Development began in the 1980s under the name InterCityExperimental, which set a world rail speed record of 406.9 km/h in 1988. Regular ICE service started in 1991 with the ICE 1. Successive generations — ICE 2, ICE 3, ICE 4 and the ICE T tilting trains — expanded the network, including international services to Austria, Switzerland, France, Belgium, the Netherlands and Denmark.

Accidents

The worst accident in the system's history was the Eschede derailment of 1998, in which 101 people died after a wheel tyre failed on an ICE 1 at high speed, prompting design and inspection changes across the fleet.
