Kanhaiyalal Maneklal Munshi (30 December 1887 – 8 February 1971), popularly known as K. M. Munshi or by his pen name Ghanshyam Vyas, was an Indian independence activist, politician, writer, and educationist from Gujarat. He founded the Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, an educational trust, in 1938.

Career

Trained as a lawyer, Munshi joined the Indian independence movement and the Indian National Congress, serving as Home Minister of Bombay Presidency in 1937. After independence he was a member of the Constituent Assembly of India, India's Agent-General in Hyderabad during its accession, Minister for Food and Agriculture, and Governor of Uttar Pradesh (1952–1957). He later helped found the Swatantra Party.

Literary work

Munshi was a prolific author in Gujarati and English, celebrated for historical novels such as the Patan trilogy (Patan ni Prabhuta, Gujarat no Nath, Rajadhiraj) and the Krishnavatara series. He played a leading role in the reconstruction of the Somnath temple and championed the Van Mahotsav tree-planting festival.
