Full stop

The full stop (British English), also known as a period (American English), is the punctuation mark ( . ) placed at the end of a declarative sentence to indicate a full stop, or after abbreviations. In English it is conventionally followed by a single space before the next sentence begins (two spaces were common in typewritten text).

History

The system of dots marking pauses is credited to Aristophanes of Byzantium, head of the Library of Alexandria in the 3rd century BC, whose low, middle and high points distinguished short, medium and long pauses. The marks were regularised in medieval Latin scribal practice and standardised by early printers; the low point settled into its modern role ending sentences, and the English name "period" derives from Latin periodus, a complete sentence.

Other uses

Beyond ending sentences, the mark serves in abbreviations (e.g., "Dr.", "etc."), in decimal numbers in most English-speaking countries (3.14), in computing as a separator in file names and internet domain names (the "dot" in "dot-com"), and as a delimiter in version numbers and IP addresses. In texting and instant messaging, including or omitting a final full stop has acquired pragmatic meanings, with the mark sometimes read as abrupt or insincere in informal chat.
