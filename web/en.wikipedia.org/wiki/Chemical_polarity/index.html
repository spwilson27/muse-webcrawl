Chemical polarity

In chemistry, polarity is a separation of electric charge leading to a molecule or its chemical groups having an electric dipole moment, with a negatively charged end and a positively charged end. Polar molecules must contain one or more polar bonds due to a difference in electronegativity between the bonded atoms. Molecules containing polar bonds have no molecular polarity if the bond dipoles cancel each other out by symmetry, as in carbon dioxide.

Bond polarity

When two atoms of different electronegativity form a covalent bond, the shared electrons are drawn toward the more electronegative atom, which acquires a partial negative charge (δ−) while the other acquires a partial positive charge (δ+). The larger the difference in electronegativity, the more polar the bond; bonds between identical atoms, such as in H2 or Cl2, are non-polar. Bond polarity is commonly classified along a continuum from non-polar covalent through polar covalent to ionic.

Molecular polarity

Whether a whole molecule is polar depends both on its polar bonds and on its molecular geometry. Water (H2O) is polar: its two O–H bonds are polar and its bent shape means their dipoles do not cancel, giving water a dipole moment of about 1.85 D. By contrast, carbon dioxide (CO2) has two polar C=O bonds arranged linearly, so the dipoles cancel and the molecule is non-polar. Similarly, boron trifluoride (BF3) is non-polar by symmetry, whereas ammonia (NH3) is polar because of its trigonal pyramidal shape and lone pair.

Consequences

Polarity governs many physical properties. Polar molecules interact through dipole–dipole forces and hydrogen bonding, giving them higher boiling points and greater solubility in polar solvents such as water; the rule of thumb "like dissolves like" summarises the miscibility of substances of similar polarity. Polarity explains why oil (non-polar) and water (polar) do not mix, why detergents — whose molecules have a polar head and a non-polar tail — can emulsify greases, and how cell membranes form from amphiphilic phospholipids. Molecular polarity also underlies the dielectric properties of solvents and is central to chromatography, spectroscopy and the behaviour of biomolecules.
