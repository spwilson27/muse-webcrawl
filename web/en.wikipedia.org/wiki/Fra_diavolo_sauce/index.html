Fra diavolo sauce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fra diavolo sauce (from Italian fra diavolo, "brother devil") is a spicy tomato sauce seasoned with crushed red pepper flakes and garlic, most often served with seafood and pasta. It is a staple of Italian-American cuisine, where it is typically paired with shrimp, lobster, clams, mussels, or a mixture of seafood over linguine or another long pasta.

Origin of the name

The name refers to Fra Diavolo ("Brother Devil"), the nickname of Michele Pezza (1771–1806), a Neapolitan guerrilla leader and brigand who resisted the French occupation of Naples. The fiery sauce is said to have been named in his honour — or after his notoriously hot temper. A similar spicy tomato preparation exists in southern Italian cooking under names such as all'arrabbiata ("angry style"), though the seafood version is generally regarded as an Italian-American creation, popularised by restaurants in New York and other American cities in the early 20th century.

Preparation

The sauce is made by sautéing garlic (and often onion) in olive oil, adding crushed red pepper, tomatoes — fresh or canned San Marzano-style — white wine, and herbs such as oregano, basil, and parsley. The seafood is cooked directly in the simmering sauce so that its juices flavour the tomatoes, and the whole dish is tossed with pasta and finished with additional pepper flakes to taste. Versions vary widely in heat, from a gentle warmth to the aggressively spicy preparations the "devil" name implies.
