Time shifting

Time shifting is the process of recording and storing media for later consumption. It was popularised by the video cassette recorder (VCR), which let viewers record television broadcasts and watch them at a time of their choosing, and later by digital video recorders and streaming catch-up services.

Legal history

In the United States, the Supreme Court's 1984 decision in Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios (the "Betamax case") held that home time shifting was fair use. Similar questions later arose for remote-storage DVRs, addressed in Cartoon Network v. CSC Holdings (2008).

Impact

Time shifting changed television economics and scheduling, enabled commercial skipping, and paved the way for on-demand streaming, in which the distinction between broadcast time and viewing time largely disappears.
