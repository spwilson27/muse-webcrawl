Altan Khan

Born: 1507
Died: 1582
Title: Khan of the Tümed Mongols
Reign: 1543–1582

Altan Khan (1507–1582) was a leader of the Tümed Mongols and the de facto ruler of the Right Wing, or western tribes, of the Mongols during the mid-sixteenth century. Although Altan never claimed the title of Great Khan for himself, he was the most powerful Mongol ruler after Dayan Khan and is remembered for restoring Mongol influence, founding the city of Hohhot, and re-establishing relations between Mongolia and Tibet, thereby playing a central role in the revival of Tibetan Buddhism among the Mongols.

Early life and rise

Altan was a grandson of Dayan Khan (1464–1517/1543), who had reunified the Mongols, and a younger brother of Gün Bilig. After Dayan Khan's death the Mongol empire was divided among his descendants; Altan received the Tümed tribe and lands around modern Hohhot in Inner Mongolia. From the 1540s onward he led repeated raids into Ming China, reaching the outskirts of Beijing in 1550 during the so-called Gengxu Incident, when his forces plundered the suburbs of the capital before withdrawing.

Relations with Ming China

Altan's raids were aimed less at conquest than at forcing the Ming court to grant trade rights and border markets. After decades of conflict, the Longqing Emperor concluded a peace settlement in 1570–1571: Altan received the title Shunyi Wang ("Prince Who Follows Righteousness"), border markets were opened, and the frontier stabilized. This treaty largely ended major Mongol raiding for a generation.

Buddhism and Tibet

In 1578 Altan met the Tibetan lama Sonam Gyatso at Lake Qinghai (Kokonor). Altan bestowed on him the title "Dalai Lama" — dalai being the Mongolian translation of gyatso, "ocean" — a title applied retroactively to Sonam Gyatso's two predecessors, making him the third Dalai Lama. Their alliance bound the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism to Mongol patronage and accelerated the conversion of the Mongols to Buddhism.

Legacy

Altan founded Kökeqota, "the Blue City" — modern Hohhot, capital of Inner Mongolia — as a trading and religious centre. He died in 1582, leaving a legacy as the most energetic Mongol leader of the sixteenth century and as co-founder, with Sonam Gyatso, of the Dalai Lama institution.
