Industrial robot

An industrial robot is a robot system used for manufacturing. Industrial robots are automated, programmable and capable of movement on two or more axes. Typical applications include welding, painting, assembly, pick and place, packaging and palletising, product inspection and testing. They are widely used to achieve high productivity and consistent quality, and to perform tasks that are dangerous or repetitive for human workers.

Types and configurations

The most common configuration is the articulated robot, a jointed arm resembling a human arm with rotary joints. Other configurations include SCARA robots for fast pick-and-place, Cartesian (gantry) robots moving along orthogonal axes, delta robots for high-speed picking, and cylindrical and polar robots. Collaborative robots ("cobots") are designed to work alongside people without safety fencing, using force and proximity sensing.

History

The first industrial robot, the Unimate, was installed at a General Motors plant in 1961 to handle hot die-cast parts; it was based on the patents of George Devol and was commercialised by Joseph Engelberger's company Unimation. Robots spread through the automotive industry in the 1970s, especially in Japan, and later into electronics, logistics and food production.

Programming and control

Robots are programmed by teach pendants, by physically guiding the arm ("lead-through teaching"), or offline using simulation software. Modern controllers coordinate motion with machine vision, force sensors and factory networks.

Market

Annual installations number in the hundreds of thousands worldwide, with the largest markets in China, Japan, the United States, South Korea and Germany — a grouping often called the "big five" of robot adoption.
