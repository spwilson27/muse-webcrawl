Russian Futurism was a movement of Russian poets and artists who adopted the principles of Filippo Tommaso Marinetti's Italian Futurist Manifesto (1909) while developing a distinctly Russian avant-garde. Although the umbrella term covers several rival groupings, the dominant tendency was Cubo-Futurism, practised by the Hylaea group around David Burliuk, Velimir Khlebnikov, Aleksei Kruchenykh and Vladimir Mayakovsky.

History

The Hylaea group formed around 1910 and issued its defining manifesto, A Slap in the Face of Public Taste, in December 1912, demanding that Pushkin, Dostoevsky and Tolstoy be "thrown overboard from the steamship of modernity." The Futurists cultivated scandal: poets performed in painted faces and outlandish dress, lectured to hostile audiences, and published deliberately provocative, crudely printed books. Their poetry experimented with zaum — a "transrational" language of invented words, exemplified by Kruchenykh's poem "Dyr bul shchyl" (1913) — and with neologism, broken syntax and folk and archaic sources. In painting, Cubo-Futurism fused Cubist fragmentation with Futurist dynamism; Natalia Goncharova, Mikhail Larionov and Kazimir Malevich all passed through the movement, Malevich going on to found Suprematism with his Black Square (1915). The 1913 Futurist opera Victory over the Sun, with text by Kruchenykh, music by Mikhail Matyushin and designs by Malevich, is a landmark of the avant-garde.

A rival camp, the Ego-Futurists of Igor Severyanin, emphasised self-display and lyricism, while the Centrifuge group included Boris Pasternak. After the 1917 Revolution many Futurists placed their work at the service of the new regime — Mayakovsky produced propaganda posters and the journal LEF — but by the late 1920s the movement was being displaced by official Soviet aesthetics, and it effectively ended with Mayakovsky's suicide in 1930.

Characteristics

  • Rejection of literary classics and museum culture; celebration of speed, machinery, the city and youth.
  • Provocation and scandal as artistic strategy; art taken into the street.
  • Word-creation and zaum; deformation of syntax, spelling and book design.
  • In painting, a synthesis of Cubism, Futurism and Russian folk and icon traditions (Neo-primitivism).

Legacy

Russian Futurism shaped the Russian avant-garde that fed into Constructivism and influenced modernist poetry throughout Europe. Khlebnikov's experiments and Mayakovsky's verse remain central to Russian literature of the twentieth century.
