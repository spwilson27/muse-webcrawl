Sir Basil Urwin Spence (13 November 1907 – 19 November 1976) was a Scottish architect, one of the most celebrated British architects of the post-war period. He is best known for designing the new Coventry Cathedral, consecrated in 1962, which rose beside the ruins of the old cathedral destroyed in the Blitz.

Early career

Born in Bombay and educated at George Watson's College in Edinburgh, Spence studied architecture at the Edinburgh College of Art and worked in London with Edwin Lutyens's office before establishing his own practice. During the Second World War he served in the Royal Engineers and took part in the Normandy landings.

Major works

Spence won the competition for Coventry Cathedral in 1950; the building, with sculpture by Jacob Epstein, engraved glass by John Piper and a tapestry by Graham Sutherland, became an icon of post-war reconstruction. Other notable works include the Beehive executive wing of the New Zealand Parliament in Wellington, the British Embassy in Rome, university buildings at Sussex, Exeter and Southampton, and the Household Cavalry barracks at Hyde Park, London.

Honours

He was knighted in 1960 and served as president of the Royal Institute of British Architects from 1958 to 1960.
