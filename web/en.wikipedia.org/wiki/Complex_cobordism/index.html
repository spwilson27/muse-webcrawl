Complex cobordism

In mathematics, complex cobordism is an extraordinary cohomology theory related to cobordism classes of complex-oriented manifolds. Its spectrum, denoted MU, is one of the central objects of algebraic topology: it is the universal complex-oriented cohomology theory, and its coefficient ring is closely connected to the theory of formal group laws. Complex cobordism was introduced in the early 1960s in work of Atiyah, Milnor and Novikov, building on Thom's work on cobordism.

The cobordism ring

Two stably complex manifolds are cobordant if their disjoint union is the boundary of a stably complex manifold of one higher dimension. The set of cobordism classes forms a graded ring ΩU* under disjoint union and Cartesian product. Novikov and Milnor showed that this ring is a polynomial algebra over the integers, generated by classes in each even degree; rationally, the generators can be taken to be complex projective spaces and certain Milnor hypersurfaces. The Chern numbers give complete invariants of complex cobordism classes.

Formal group laws

Quillen's theorem (1969) states that the coefficient ring MU* is isomorphic to Lazard's universal ring classifying formal group laws, so that complex cobordism is the universal complex-oriented cohomology theory. This bridge between topology and algebra underlies much of modern stable homotopy theory, including the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence, which computes stable homotopy groups of spheres starting from complex cobordism, and the later development of chromatic homotopy theory and elliptic cohomology.
