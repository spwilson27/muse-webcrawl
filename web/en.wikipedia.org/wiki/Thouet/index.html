Thouet

The Thouet is a river in western France, a left-bank tributary of the Loire. It is about 142 kilometres (88 miles) long and flows through the departments of Deux-Sèvres and Maine-et-Loire in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine and Pays de la Loire regions.

Course

The Thouet rises near Le Beugnon in the Gâtine hills of Deux-Sèvres. It flows generally north and north-east past Parthenay, Thouars and Saumur, passing through a valley known for its Romanesque churches and vineyards, before joining the Loire at Saumur.

Tributaries and settlements

Its principal tributaries include the Dive, the Argenton and the Thouaret. The towns along its course include Parthenay, Saint-Loup-Lamairé, Airvault, Thouars, Montreuil-Bellay and Saumur. At Thouars the river is overlooked by the great seventeenth-century château of the La Trémoille family, and at Saumur it passes beneath the historic bridge district near the château.

Heritage

The Thouet valley (Val du Thouet) is promoted as a greenway for cycling and canoeing, and the river's banks host a rich Romanesque heritage, including the church of Saint-Pierre at Parthenay and the abbey of Saint-Jouin-de-Marnes nearby.
