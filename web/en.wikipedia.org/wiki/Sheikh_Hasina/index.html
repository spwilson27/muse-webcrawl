Sheikh Hasina Wazed (born 28 September 1947) is a Bangladeshi politician who served as prime minister of Bangladesh from 1996 to 2001 and again from 2009 until her resignation and flight from the country in August 2024 during mass protests. The daughter of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, the founding leader of Bangladesh, she was the country's longest-serving prime minister and one of its two dominant political figures — with Khaleda Zia — for more than three decades.

Early life

Hasina was born in Tungipara in Gopalganj District, the eldest of five children of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and Sheikh Fazilatunnesa Mujib. She studied Bengali literature at the University of Dhaka and was active in student politics. On 15 August 1975 her father, mother, three brothers and other family members were assassinated in a military coup; Hasina and her sister Sheikh Rehana survived because they were visiting West Germany. She lived in exile in India until returning to Bangladesh in 1981, having been elected president of the Awami League in her absence.

Political career

Through the 1980s Hasina led the Awami League in opposition to the military regime of Hussain Muhammad Ershad, enduring periods of detention, and played a central role in the 1990 pro-democracy uprising. She became prime minister after the 1996 election, signing the Chittagong Hill Tracts Peace Accord and the Ganges water-sharing treaty with India. Defeated in 2001, she survived a 2004 grenade attack on a rally that killed 24 people, and was imprisoned on corruption charges during the 2007–08 military-backed caretaker government.

Second premiership (2009–2024)

Hasina returned to power with a landslide in the December 2008 election. Her governments oversaw rapid economic growth driven by the garment industry, large infrastructure projects (including the Padma Bridge and Dhaka Metro Rail), war-crimes trials of 1971 collaborators, and the abolition of the caretaker-government system. Re-elections in 2014, 2018 and 2024 were boycotted or disputed by the opposition, and her rule drew growing criticism for authoritarianism, suppression of dissent and enforced disappearances.

Fall from power

In July 2024, student-led protests against public-sector job quotas escalated into a nationwide movement after a violent crackdown in which hundreds were killed. On 5 August 2024 Hasina resigned and fled to India; an interim government under Muhammad Yunus took power. She was subsequently tried in absentia by Bangladesh's International Crimes Tribunal in connection with the protest killings, and her Awami League was banned from political activity.
