Campus radio (also known as college radio, university radio or student radio) is a type of radio station run by the students of a college, university or other educational institution. Programming produced specifically by students is, for many stations, a hallmark of campus radio. Stations may be operated for the purpose of training professional radio personnel, or to provide an alternative to commercial broadcasting.

Campus stations are generally licensed and regulated by national broadcasting authorities, but many in North America operate under low-power or carrier-current arrangements, or broadcast exclusively online. Because they are usually non-commercial and staffed by volunteers, campus stations are known for eclectic programming, including independent and local music unlikely to be heard on commercial outlets.

History

Some of the earliest radio stations in the United States and Canada began as university experiments in wireless telegraphy and broadcasting in the 1910s and 1920s. Stations such as WHA at the University of Wisconsin and CKUA at the University of Alberta grew from educational broadcasting initiatives. In the United Kingdom, student radio developed later, with University Radio York (URY), founded in 1968, among the pioneers. Australia's 2MCE and Canada's network of campus stations expanded through the 1970s alongside public and community broadcasting reforms.

Operation

Campus stations are typically staffed by student volunteers, with day-to-day management by an elected or appointed station manager and a small number of paid staff. Funding comes from student levies, university grants, sponsorships, and fundraising. Many stations hold memberships in national associations such as the National Campus and Community Radio Association (NCRA) in Canada, the Student Radio Association (SRA) in the United Kingdom, and the Intercollegiate Broadcasting System (IBS) in the United States.

Programming

Programming commonly includes genre-based music shows (indie rock, hip hop, electronic, jazz, metal), campus and local news, sports coverage of university teams, spoken-word and comedy shows, and programming in languages spoken by the student body. The freeform format, in which DJs choose their own music, is a longstanding tradition at many stations.
