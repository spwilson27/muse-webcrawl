Victoria and Albert Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The V&A's Grand Entrance on Cromwell Road, Aston Webb's Portland stone facade crowned by the central tower]

The Victoria and Albert Museum (abbreviated V&A) in London is the world's largest museum of applied arts, decorative arts and design, with a permanent collection of over 2.27 million objects. Founded in 1852 after the Great Exhibition and named (in 1899) for Queen Victoria and Prince Albert, it occupies the South Kensington 'Albertopolis' alongside the Natural History and Science Museums.

The V&A's remit is encyclopaedic and material: ceramics, glass, textiles, costume, silver, ironwork, jewellery, furniture, medieval objects, sculpture, prints, drawings and photographs from Europe, North America, Asia and North Africa across 5,000 years.

Origins and mission

Henry Cole, the museum's first director, founded it as an instrument of design education — the 'South Kensington system' — buying the best of the 1851 Exhibition and cast courts (full-scale plaster reproductions, including Trajan's Column in two halves) to teach British manufacturers taste. The mission persists in the museum's self-description as a place for makers; its slogan of the 1980s — 'an ace caff with quite a nice museum attached' (in the famous advertising line) — announced its popular reinvention.

The modern V&A

Free admission (since 2001), blockbuster exhibitions (David Bowie Is, 2013; Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty, 2015, among the most-visited in its history), the Exhibition Road Quarter (2017, Amanda Levete's porcelain courtyard), V&A Dundee (2018) and V&A East in Stratford (opening in phases in the mid-2020s) have made the V&A a family of museums and a byword for the populist blockbuster done well.
