Trinity (nuclear test)

Trinity was the code name of the first detonation of a nuclear weapon, conducted by the United States Army on 16 July 1945 in the Jornada del Muerto desert, New Mexico, as part of the Manhattan Project.

The test

The plutonium implosion device, nicknamed the Gadget, was detonated atop a 100-foot tower. Scientific director J. Robert Oppenheimer named the test Trinity, possibly after a John Donne poem. Observers including Kenneth Bainbridge witnessed a flash brighter than the sun; Oppenheimer later recalled the Bhagavad Gita line Now I am become Death.

Aftermath

The success led to the use of nuclear weapons on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. The site is now Trinity Site National Historic Landmark. See Manhattan Project.
