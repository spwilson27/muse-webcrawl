Comrades Marathon

The Comrades Marathon is an ultramarathon of approximately 88 kilometres (55 mi) which is run annually in the KwaZulu-Natal province of South Africa between the cities of Durban and Pietermaritzburg. It is the world's largest and oldest ultramarathon race.

History

The race was founded by Vic Clapham, a First World War veteran, as a memorial to South African soldiers killed in the war. It was first run on 24 May 1921 (Empire Day) with 34 runners, and has been held every year since except during the Second World War and in 2020–2021, when it was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The direction of the race alternates each year between the "up" run from Durban to Pietermaritzburg and the "down" run in the reverse direction.

The race

The field is capped at around 20,000–25,000 entrants. Runners have 12 hours to complete the course, with a series of cut-off points along the way. Medals are awarded according to finishing time and position, including gold medals for the top finishers, and special awards for completing ten races (the green number). Famous features of the route include the "Big Five" hills: Cowies Hill, Fields Hill, Botha's Hill, Inchanga, and Polly Shortts.

Records and notable runners

Legendary competitors include Wally Hayward, Bruce Fordyce, who won the race nine times, and Alan Robb. The event attracts elite international ultrarunners as well as thousands of amateurs, and holds a central place in South African sporting culture.
