Mincha

Mincha (Hebrew: מִנְחָה, pronounced min-KHAH; sometimes spelled Minha) is the afternoon prayer service in Judaism. Its name derives from the Hebrew word for a meal offering, and according to tradition it corresponds to the afternoon daily offering (tamid) that was sacrificed in the Temple in Jerusalem.

Origin and timing

Tradition attributes the institution of Mincha to the patriarch Isaac, based on Genesis 24:63, "Isaac went out to meditate in the field toward evening." Mincha may be recited from half an hour after halachic noon (mincha gedola) until sunset; many authorities permit recitation from mincha ketana, two and a half halachic hours before sunset. On Fridays, Mincha is often prayed earlier so that the Sabbath evening service can follow without interruption.

Structure

The service consists principally of Ashrei (Psalm 145), the Amidah — the central standing prayer of nineteen blessings on weekdays — and, on most days, Tachanun (supplications). It is followed by Aleinu. On fast days, a Torah reading is added, and during the Ten Days of Repentance the Avinu Malkeinu prayer is recited. Unlike the morning service, Mincha is usually brief, and in many workplaces and schools short Mincha gatherings are held during the afternoon break.

Related services

Mincha is the second of the three daily obligatory prayers, preceded by Shacharit in the morning and followed by Maariv in the evening. On Shabbat, Mincha includes a reading from the following week's Torah portion, and a shortened Amidah.
