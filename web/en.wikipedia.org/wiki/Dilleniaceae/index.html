Dilleniaceae

KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms; Eudicots
OrderDilleniales
FamilyDilleniaceae

Dilleniaceae is a family of flowering plants with 11 genera and about 430 known species. Such a family has been universally recognized by taxonomists. It is known to gardeners for the genus Hibbertia, which contains many commercially valuable garden species. The family is distributed through the tropics and subtropics plus Australia, with a few species extending into temperate regions.

Description

Members of the family are mostly trees, shrubs, and woody vines (lianas), though some Hibbertia species are herbaceous. The leaves are generally simple, often large, with prominent parallel lateral veins ending in teeth at the margin — a characteristic feature of the family. The flowers are usually showy, with five persistent sepals, five (sometimes fewer) petals, and numerous stamens. The ovary is superior and consists of one to many carpels. Fruits are follicles, berries, or capsules, and the seeds often bear an aril.

Taxonomy

The family is named after the German botanist Johann Jacob Dillenius (1684–1747). In the APG IV system it is placed alone in the order Dilleniales, whose position among the eudicots — near the superasterids — was long uncertain. Earlier systems, such as Cronquist's, grouped Dilleniaceae in the subclass Dilleniidae with families now known to be unrelated. Genera include Dillenia, Hibbertia (the largest, with around 150 species, centred in Australia), Tetracera, Curatella, Davilla, Doliocarpus, and Acrotrema.

Uses

Several Hibbertia species are grown as ornamentals for their bright yellow flowers. Dillenia indica, the elephant apple, produces large edible fruits used in curries and jellies in South and Southeast Asia, and its timber is used locally. Species of Tetracera have rough leaves traditionally used as a natural sandpaper, and various members of the family feature in traditional medicine.
