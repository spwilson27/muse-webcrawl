Guppy

Scientific name: Poecilia reticulata

Family: Poeciliidae

Native range: Northern South America and Caribbean islands

The guppy (Poecilia reticulata), also called the millionfish or rainbow fish, is a small tropical freshwater fish native to north-eastern South America and nearby islands, and one of the world's most popular aquarium fish. It is named after Robert John Lechmere Guppy, who collected specimens in Trinidad.

Biology

Guppies are livebearers: females give birth to free-swimming young. Males are smaller (about 1.5–3.5 cm) and much more brightly coloured than the drabber, larger females — a classic example of sexual dimorphism shaped by sexual selection and predation. Wild guppies feed on algae and insect larvae, including mosquito larvae, and have been introduced worldwide for mosquito control.

In science and aquaria

Easy to breed and fast-generating, guppies are model organisms in evolutionary biology and genetics, and hundreds of ornamental strains have been bred for colour and fin shape.
