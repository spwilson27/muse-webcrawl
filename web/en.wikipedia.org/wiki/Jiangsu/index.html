Jiangsu

Jiangsu is a coastal province of the People's Republic of China, located along the country's eastern seaboard on the Yellow Sea. Its capital is Nanjing, a former national capital, and its largest city is Suzhou. With over 80 million inhabitants packed into roughly 107,000 square kilometres, Jiangsu is the most densely populated of China's provinces. The name combines the first characters of two former prefectures, Jiangning (Nanjing) and Suzhou.

Geography

The province is a low-lying alluvial plain built up by the Yangtze and Huai rivers; the Yangtze crosses its south, where the river's delta forms one of China's most urbanised and prosperous regions. Jiangsu contains China's largest freshwater lakes in the south, Taihu, and the fourth-largest, Hongze Lake, in the north. The Grand Canal runs the length of the province, linking the historic canal cities of Suzhou, Wuxi, Changzhou, Yangzhou and Huai'an. Historically, northern Jiangsu (Subei) has been poorer than the wealthy south (Sunan), a gap provincial policy has long sought to close.

Economy and culture

Jiangsu has one of the largest provincial economies in China — vying with Guangdong for first place — built on electronics, machinery, textiles, chemicals and shipbuilding, together with a dense network of township-and-village and private enterprises around Suzhou and Wuxi. The province is famous for the Classical Gardens of Suzhou (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Kunqu opera, Suzhou embroidery, and Huaiyang cuisine, one of the Four Great Traditions of Chinese cooking. Educationally it is among China's strongest provinces, though its university entrance examination has a reputation for difficulty.
