The Cheltenham Festival is a horse racing-based meeting in the National Hunt racing calendar in the United Kingdom, with race prize money second only to the Grand National. The four-day festival takes place annually in March at Cheltenham Racecourse in Gloucestershire. It usually coincides with Saint Patrick's Day and is particularly popular with Irish visitors.

Overview

The festival features several Grade 1 races, including the Cheltenham Gold Cup, the Champion Hurdle, the Queen Mother Champion Chase and the Stayers' Hurdle. Large amounts of money are gambled during the festival week, and it is often considered the Olympics of jump racing — the meeting at which the season's championships are effectively decided and where British- and Irish-trained horses meet in force, a rivalry celebrated as the Prestbury Cup.

History

Racing at Cheltenham dates to the early nineteenth century, and the National Hunt Meeting was first held there in 1860, though it alternated venues before settling permanently at Prestbury Park in the early twentieth century. The Gold Cup was inaugurated in 1924 and the Champion Hurdle in 1927. The festival expanded from three days to four in 2005.

Notable races

The week opens on Champion Day and builds through Ladies' Day and St Patrick's Thursday to Gold Cup Day. Famous races include the Supreme Novices' Hurdle, whose start is greeted by the traditional "Cheltenham roar", the Arkle Challenge Trophy, the Ryanair Chase, and the Triumph Hurdle. Legendary horses such as Golden Miller, Arkle, Best Mate and Kauto Star built their reputations at the meeting.
