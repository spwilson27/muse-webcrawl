Plain English

TypeWriting style / movement
PrinciplesClear structure, everyday words, short sentences
RelatedPlain language, legal drafting reform

Plain English (or layman's terms) is a mode of English communication that emphasizes clarity, brevity, and the avoidance of technical language, particularly in legal and official documents. The plain English movement aims to make writing efficient and understandable for its intended audience.

Principles

Guides recommend planning before writing, using short sentences, preferring active voice and everyday vocabulary, designing documents with informative headings and lists, and testing drafts on readers. The style avoids jargon, nominalizations, and archaic legalisms such as "hereby" and "aforesaid."

History and campaigns

The Plain English Campaign, founded in Britain in 1979 by Chrissie Maher, awards the Crystal Mark to clear documents and lampoons gobbledegook with the Golden Bull award. In the United States, the Plain Writing Act of 2010 requires federal agencies to use clear communication the public can understand.

Criticism

Some critics argue that plain language can oversimplify, while supporters respond that precision and clarity are compatible goals.
