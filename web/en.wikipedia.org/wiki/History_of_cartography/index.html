History of cartography

DisciplineCartography
Key figuresPtolemy, Mercator, Ortelius
MilestonesPtolemy's Geography; Mercator projection 1569; modern GIS

The history of cartography spans humanity's attempts to depict the Earth and heavens, from Babylonian clay tablets to satellite imagery and digital maps.

Ancient and medieval maps

The Babylonian Imago Mundi (c. 600 BCE) is among the earliest world maps. Greek scholars — Eratosthenes, who measured Earth's circumference, and Ptolemy, whose Geography (2nd century CE) laid out coordinates — founded scientific cartography. Medieval Europe produced symbolic T and O maps and detailed portolan charts of coastlines, while Islamic geographers preserved and extended Greek knowledge.

Age of Discovery

Oceanic voyages filled in the Americas and Pacific. Mercator's 1569 projection served navigators, and Ortelius's Theatrum Orbis Terrarum (1570) was the first modern atlas. National surveys, triangulation and the Cassini map of France brought accuracy to land mapping.

Modern cartography

Aerial photography, remote sensing, GPS and geographic information systems (GIS) transformed map-making in the 20th century; online mapping and crowdsourced projects such as OpenStreetMap made maps interactive and continuously updated.
