Ten-Day War
Date27 June – 7 July 1991
LocationSlovenia
ResultSlovenian victory; Brioni Agreement

The Ten-Day War (Slovene: desetdnevna vojna), sometimes called the Slovenian War of Independence (slovenska osamosvojitvena vojna), was a brief conflict between the Slovenian Territorial Defence and the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) following Slovenia's declaration of independence on 25 June 1991. It lasted from 27 June to 7 July 1991 and was the opening conflict of the Yugoslav Wars.

Slovenian forces during the conflict, 1991

Background

After a plebiscite in December 1990 in which nearly 89 per cent of the electorate voted for independence, Slovenia prepared carefully for armed resistance, secretly reorganising its Territorial Defence and importing weapons. On 25 June 1991 Slovenia and Croatia declared independence from the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

The war

JNA units moved to seize border crossings and Ljubljana's airport on 27 June. Slovenian forces set up roadblocks, blockaded JNA barracks, cut their water and electricity, and shot down two JNA helicopters over Ljubljana. Fighting was limited but sharp at several locations. Casualties were relatively light: dozens were killed on both sides, including 39 JNA soldiers and 18 Slovenian defenders by common estimates, along with several foreign truck drivers caught at blockades.

Outcome

Under European Community mediation the Brioni Agreement, signed on 7 July 1991, ended the war: Slovenia and Croatia agreed to a three-month moratorium on implementing independence, while the JNA withdrew to its barracks and subsequently left Slovenia entirely by October 1991. Slovenia's independence was internationally recognised in the following months and it joined the United Nations in May 1992.
