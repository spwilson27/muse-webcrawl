James Doolittle

James Harold Doolittle (December 14, 1896 – September 27, 1993) was an American military general and aviation pioneer who received the Medal of Honor for his leadership of the Doolittle Raid, a bold long-range retaliatory air raid on Japanese targets during World War II.

Aviation pioneer

Doolittle earned his wings during World War I and became famous in the 1920s for record-breaking flights and air racing, winning the Schneider, Bendix and Thompson trophies. He earned a doctorate in aeronautical engineering from MIT — among the first awarded in the United States — and in 1929 made the first successful flight entirely by instruments, taking off, flying and landing using cockpit instruments alone. In the 1930s he worked for Shell Oil, promoting the development of high-octane aviation fuel.

Doolittle Raid

On April 18, 1942, then-Lieutenant Colonel Doolittle led sixteen B-25B Mitchell bombers launched from the aircraft carrier USS Hornet on a one-way mission to bomb Tokyo and other targets in Japan. Though the material damage was modest, the raid greatly boosted American morale and caused Japan to divert resources to homeland defense. Doolittle was promoted to brigadier general and awarded the Medal of Honor.

Later command

Doolittle subsequently commanded the Twelfth Air Force in North Africa, the Fifteenth Air Force in the Mediterranean, and the Eighth Air Force in Europe and the Pacific, finishing the war as a lieutenant general. After the war he returned to Shell and served on numerous government boards. In 1985 he was promoted to four-star general on the retired list. He died in 1993 at the age of 96.
