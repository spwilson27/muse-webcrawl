CD8

CD8 (cluster of differentiation 8) is a transmembrane glycoprotein that serves as a co-receptor for the T-cell receptor (TCR). Like the TCR, CD8 binds to a major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecule, but is specific for the MHC class I protein. CD8 is a defining surface marker of cytotoxic T lymphocytes, commonly called CD8+ T cells.

Structure and function

CD8 consists of two chains, α and β, each containing an immunoglobulin variable-like domain connected to the membrane by a stalk. The most common form is the αβ heterodimer. CD8 binds the invariant α3 domain of MHC class I while the TCR engages the peptide presented, and its cytoplasmic tail recruits the kinase Lck, strengthening signalling and lowering the threshold for T-cell activation.

Role in immunity

CD8+ cytotoxic T cells kill virus-infected and malignant cells that present foreign peptides on MHC class I. CD8 counts in blood are clinically important: the CD4/CD8 ratio is monitored in HIV infection, in which CD4+ helper cells are depleted. CD8+ T cells are also the principal effector cells targeted by many cancer vaccines and adoptive cell therapies.
