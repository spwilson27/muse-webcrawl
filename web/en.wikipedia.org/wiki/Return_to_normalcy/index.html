Return to normalcy

"Return to normalcy" was the campaign slogan of Warren G. Harding in the 1920 United States presidential election. Speaking in Boston on 14 May 1920, Harding declared: "America's present need is not heroics, but healing; not nostrums, but normalcy; not revolution, but restoration..." The phrase captured public weariness after the First World War, the influenza pandemic, labour unrest and the reforming exertions of the Progressive Era, and promised a calm, business-friendly government.

The word "normalcy"

The slogan was mocked by opponents who insisted the correct noun was "normality", and President Woodrow Wilson reportedly sniped at Harding's usage. In fact "normalcy" had existed in English since the 1850s, chiefly in mathematics, and Harding's use popularised it in general American English, where it remains common.

Meaning and legacy

Harding's appeal helped him to a landslide victory over Democrat James M. Cox. In office, "normalcy" meant a retreat from wartime and Progressive-era activism: lower taxes, high tariffs, reduced regulation, immigration restriction and a foreign policy of limited engagement (the Senate had rejected the League of Nations). The phrase has been revived in later eras to describe a longing, after crisis, for ordinary life — notably in commentary following other wars and during the COVID-19 pandemic.
