The Dresden Elbe Valley is a former UNESCO World Heritage Site in Dresden, Germany: an 18-kilometre stretch of cultural landscape along the Elbe, encompassing the baroque old town skyline, palaces such as Pillnitz, vineyards, meadows and nineteenth-century suburbs. Inscribed in 2004, it was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2006 over plans for the Waldschlösschen Bridge, and on 25 June 2009 the World Heritage Committee voted to remove it from the World Heritage List — the first such delisting in Europe and only the second worldwide, after Oman's Arabian Oryx Sanctuary in 2007.

The landscape

The valley combines Dresden's historic centre — the Zwinger, Semperoper, Frauenkirche, Brühl's Terrace and the Residenzschloss — with a largely unspoilt river landscape of flood meadows, the palaces of Pillnitz, Albrechtsberg and Lingner on the slopes, and the "Blue Wonder" steel bridge of 1893. UNESCO praised it as an outstanding cultural landscape integrating a baroque capital with its riverine setting.

The bridge controversy

Plans for a new Elbe crossing at the Waldschlösschen meadow predated inscription; in a 2005 referendum Dresden's citizens voted in favour of the bridge. UNESCO warned that a four-lane steel arch bridge across the meadows would irreversibly damage the valley's outstanding universal value, proposed alternatives including a tunnel, and placed the site on the danger list. Legally bound by the referendum and unwilling to stop construction, the city and the state of Saxony proceeded; construction began in 2007. In June 2009 the committee, meeting in Seville, voted 14–4 to delist. The Waldschlösschen Bridge opened in August 2013.

Significance

The case is a landmark in heritage governance: it demonstrated that UNESCO's ultimate sanction is real, crystallised debates about democratic local preferences versus international obligations, and is routinely cited alongside the later delisting of Liverpool in discussions of the credibility of the system.
