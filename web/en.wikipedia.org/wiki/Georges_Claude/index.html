Georges Claude

Georges Claude (24 September 1870 – 23 May 1960) was a French engineer and inventor, creator of the industrial liquefaction of air and of neon lighting.

Born: Paris, France
Died: Saint-Cloud, France
Known for: Neon lamp; Air Liquide; Claude process

Georges Claude was a French engineer, chemist, and inventor. He is best known as the founder of Air Liquide, the industrial gases company, and as the inventor of the neon tube, which made him famous as the "Edison of France".

Air liquefaction

Claude studied at the École supérieure de physique et de chimie industrielles in Paris. In 1902 he patented a method of liquefying air in which compressed air performed work in an expansion engine, cooling itself far more efficiently than in the rival Linde process. The same year he co-founded the company L'Air Liquide to exploit the liquefaction of air and the separation of its components — oxygen, nitrogen, and the rare gases — for industry.

Neon lighting

Applying high voltage to the recovered neon gas in sealed tubes, Claude produced a brilliant orange-red light, first demonstrated publicly in Paris in 1910. His company Claude Neon sold signs — famously the early Cinzano and Paris Opéra advertisements — and by the 1920s neon signs had spread to the United States and transformed urban advertising. Claude also experimented with exploiting ocean thermal energy, building test plants in Cuba (1930) and off Brazil.

Later life and disgrace

Claude dabbled in politics with right-wing and monarchist movements. During the Second World War he supported the Vichy regime and collaboration with Nazi Germany; after the liberation he was tried in 1945, expelled from the Academy of Sciences, and imprisoned until 1949. He died in Saint-Cloud in 1960.
