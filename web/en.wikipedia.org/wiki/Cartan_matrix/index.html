In mathematics, the term Cartan matrix has several related meanings, most prominently a square integer matrix associated with a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra (or, more generally, a root system) that encodes the angles and relative lengths between simple roots. It is named after French mathematician Élie Cartan.

Definition

Given simple roots α1, …, αr, the Cartan matrix A has entries Aij = 2(αi, αj) / (αj, αj). For finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebras the diagonal entries are 2 and the off-diagonal entries are 0, −1, −2 or −3, with Aij = 0 exactly when Aji = 0. The matrix determines the Lie algebra up to isomorphism and is equivalent to the data of its Dynkin diagram: vertices joined by edges according to the off-diagonal entries, with arrows pointing toward the shorter root.

Classification

Positive-definite Cartan matrices correspond to the four classical families of Lie algebras (An, Bn, Cn, Dn) and the five exceptional types (E6, E7, E8, F4, G2). Relaxing positive-definiteness to positive-semidefiniteness gives the affine Cartan matrices of affine Lie algebras.

Other uses

The name "Cartan matrix" is also used in algebraic geometry for the intersection matrix of curves on a surface, and in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras for a matrix built from the multiplicities of simple modules in projective covers.
