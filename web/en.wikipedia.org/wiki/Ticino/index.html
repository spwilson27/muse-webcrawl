Ticino, officially the Republic and Canton of Ticino (Italian: Repubblica e Cantone Ticino), is the southernmost canton of Switzerland. It is the only canton where Italian is the sole official language, and it takes its name from the Ticino river, which flows from the St. Gotthard area through the canton into Lake Maggiore and on into Italy.

Geography

Ticino lies south of the Alps, bordering the Italian regions of Piedmont and Lombardy. The canton encompasses the northern shore of Lake Maggiore and the western shore of Lake Lugano. Its terrain ranges from the high Alps of the north (including the Gotthard massif and the Lepontine Alps) to the mild, Mediterranean-influenced lake districts of the south around Lugano, Locarno, and Ascona, which enjoy a notably sunny climate and palm-lined promenades. Major centers include the capital Bellinzona—famed for its three medieval castles, a UNESCO World Heritage Site—and Lugano, the canton's largest city and a major financial center.

History

The area was conquered from the Duchy of Milan by the Swiss Confederates in the fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries and governed as subject territories (bailiwicks). With Napoleon's reorganization, Ticino became a full canton of the Swiss Confederation under the Act of Mediation of 1803. The canton saw liberal-radical conflicts in the nineteenth century and remained relatively poor, with heavy emigration, until tourism, banking, and industry transformed its economy in the twentieth century. The Gotthard railway tunnel (1882), the Gotthard road tunnel (1980), and the Gotthard Base Tunnel (2016)—the world's longest railway tunnel—pass under the canton, making it a key north–south transit corridor through the Alps.

Culture and economy

Ticino is culturally Italian-speaking Switzerland, with its own Italian-language media, the Università della Svizzera italiana in Lugano, and festivals such as the Locarno Film Festival, one of the world's oldest. The economy is based on services—especially banking and finance centered on Lugano—tourism, and some manufacturing and viticulture (notably merlot wine).
