Fallout (franchise)

Fallout is a media franchise of post-apocalyptic role-playing video games created by Tim Cain and Leonard Boyarsky at Interplay Entertainment. The series is set in an alternate-history United States that diverged after the Second World War along the lines of 1950s pulp science fiction: after a global nuclear war in 2077 between the U.S. and China, survivors dwell in underground Vaults or scrape by in the irradiated wasteland. Its visual identity — the Vault Boy mascot, Pip-Boy wrist computers, Nuka-Cola, and power armour — blends retro-futurism with dark satire of American culture.

Games

The original Fallout (1997) and Fallout 2 (1998), developed by Black Isle Studios, were isometric, turn-based RPGs celebrated for open-ended quests and moral choice. After Interplay's decline, Bethesda Game Studios acquired the franchise and reinvented it as a first-person action RPG with Fallout 3 (2008), set in Washington, D.C. Obsidian Entertainment's Fallout: New Vegas (2010) became a fan favourite for its writing; Fallout 4 (2015) was set in Boston; and the online Fallout 76 (2018) launched poorly but was extensively improved. Spin-offs include Fallout Tactics, Fallout Shelter, and the strategy of the SPECIAL character system running throughout.

Other media

The franchise expanded with the acclaimed 2024 Amazon Prime Video television series, board games, novels and merchandise, and the series remains one of the best-selling RPG franchises of all time.
