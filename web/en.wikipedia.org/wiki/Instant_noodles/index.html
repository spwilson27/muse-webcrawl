Instant noodles

Instant noodles are a type of noodle dish sold in a precooked and dried noodle block with flavouring powder and/or seasoning oil, usually in a separate packet. They are designed to be prepared in a few minutes by soaking in hot water. Modern instant noodles were invented in Japan in 1958 by Momofuku Ando of Nissin Foods, and have become a staple convenience food worldwide, with roughly 120 billion servings consumed annually.

History

Ando developed "flash frying" — briefly frying steamed, seasoned noodles in oil to dehydrate them — in a shed behind his house in Ikeda, Osaka, and launched Chicken Ramen in 1958. In 1971 Nissin introduced Cup Noodles, sold in a waterproof foam cup into which hot water is poured directly; the idea came to Ando after watching American buyers break ramen blocks into cups and eat with forks, which also inspired including small fork-friendly noodle lengths and dried toppings. Instant noodles spread rapidly through Asia and then the world, and in 2005 a vacuum-packed variety ("Space Ram") was eaten aboard the Space Shuttle.

Production and preparation

Wheat dough with alkaline salts (kansui) is rolled, cut, steamed, and then dehydrated by frying or hot-air drying before packaging with flavour sachets. Consumers add boiling water and wait about three minutes. Instant noodles are eaten plain as a snack in some countries, or cooked with eggs, vegetables, and meats, or stir-fried, as in Indonesia's mie goreng.

Market and culture

China (including Hong Kong) is the largest market by servings, followed by Indonesia, Vietnam, India, and Japan. Brands such as Indomie, Maggi, Shin Ramyun, Maruchan, and Top Ramen have strong regional identities, and instant noodles hold an outsized cultural place — Japan voted them its most important twentieth-century invention in a 2000 poll. The CupNoodles Museum in Yokohama commemorates Ando and his invention.

Health

Instant noodles are high in sodium and refined carbohydrate and low in fibre, protein, and micronutrients; frequent consumption has been associated in studies with poorer diet quality and metabolic syndrome risk. Manufacturers have responded with reduced-sodium, air-dried, and fortified versions.
