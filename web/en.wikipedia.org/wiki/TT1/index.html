TT1

Location: Theban Necropolis, Egypt

Occupant: Sennofri (Senneferi)

Period: New Kingdom, 18th Dynasty

Reign: Thutmose III

TT1 (Theban Tomb 1) is the designation of an ancient Egyptian tomb located at Deir el-Medina, part of the Theban Necropolis on the west bank of the Nile opposite Luxor. It was the burial place of the ancient Egyptian official Sennofri (also rendered Senneferi) and members of his family.

The tomb owner

Sennofri lived during the reign of Pharaoh Thutmose III of the 18th Dynasty (c. 1479–1425 BC). He held titles associated with the administration of gold and the treasury, sometimes rendered "Overseer of the Gold Land of Amun". His wife was named Meryt. Their names and titles appear in the inscriptions and decoration of the tomb chapel.

Description

Like other tombs of the Deir el-Medina workmen's village, TT1 consists of a small decorated chapel and a burial chamber reached by a shaft or descending corridor. The wall paintings and funerary goods reflect the beliefs of the New Kingdom about the afterlife, with scenes of offering bearers, funerary rituals, and the deceased before the gods. Funerary equipment from the tomb, including stelae, has entered museum collections.

Theban tombs

The Theban tombs are numbered in the order of their modern discovery or recording; TT1 begins the standard list compiled in the topographical bibliography of Porter and Moss. The tombs of Deir el-Medina are of particular interest because they belonged to the artisans who built the royal tombs in the nearby Valley of the Kings.
