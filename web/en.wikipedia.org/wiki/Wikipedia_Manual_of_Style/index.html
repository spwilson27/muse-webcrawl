Wikipedia: Manual of Style

Type: Editorial guideline

Applies to: English Wikipedia articles

The Wikipedia Manual of Style (MoS or MOS) is the English Wikipedia's style guide for articles. It establishes standards for article structure, writing, formatting, and usage, with the aim of making articles clearer, more consistent, and easier to read. Editors should follow it closely, though it is a guideline rather than an inflexible rule; common sense and occasional exceptions may apply.

Contents

The main page of the Manual of Style covers article titles, the lead section, layout, writing principles such as clarity and precision, national varieties of English, punctuation, capitalisation, dates and numbers, and conventions for lists, images, and links. Detailed subpages address subject-specific topics including biographies, mathematics, music, film, television, and legal articles.

Key principles

Among its best-known recommendations are that articles begin with a concise lead section summarising the topic; that the first mention of the article's subject be in bold type; that dates and units follow consistent formats; that editors avoid peacock terms and unexplained jargon; and that articles retain the variety of English in which they were first substantially written when they have a strong national tie.

Development

The Manual of Style grew organically from 2001 as editors codified existing practice, and it has been revised continuously through community discussion. Other language editions of Wikipedia maintain their own style guides, which may differ in detail.
