Fayette County is a county in the U.S. state of West Virginia. Its county seat is Fayetteville, and its largest city is Oak Hill. The county was formed in 1831 from parts of Greenbrier, Kanawha, Nicholas, and Logan counties and named for the Marquis de Lafayette.

Geography and history

The county lies on the Allegheny Plateau and is bisected by the deep gorge of the New River. Rich bituminous coal seams made it a major coal-producing county from the late nineteenth century, with railroads built along the river to haul coal from company towns such as Kay Moor and Nuttallburg. The completion of the New River Gorge Bridge in 1977 — one of the longest steel arch spans in the world — transformed travel and became the county's emblem.

Recreation

The New River Gorge is famed for whitewater rafting, rock climbing, and the annual Bridge Day festival. In December 2020, the gorge was designated New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, America's 63rd national park, much of it lying within Fayette County — a shift toward tourism in a region long dependent on coal.
