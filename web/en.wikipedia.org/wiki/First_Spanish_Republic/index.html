First Spanish Republic

The Spanish Republic (Spanish: República española), historiographically referred to as the First Spanish Republic, was the political regime that existed in Spain from 11 February 1873 to 29 December 1874. It was proclaimed after the abdication of King Amadeo I amid the turmoil of the Sexenio Democrático, and it collapsed in barely twenty-two months under the strain of three simultaneous wars and internal fragmentation, ending with the restoration of the Bourbon monarchy.


Allegorical depiction of the Spanish Republic proclaimed in 1873.

Proclamation and governments

Amadeo of Savoy, elected king in 1870 after the revolution that deposed Isabella II, abdicated in February 1873, exhausted by political instability and the ongoing Ten Years' War in Cuba. The same night, the Cortes proclaimed a republic almost by default; many of its deputies were monarchists with no alternative candidate. The Republic cycled through four presidents in eleven months: Estanislao Figueras, Francesc Pi i Margall (the theorist of federalism, author of Las nacionalidades), Nicolás Salmerón, and Emilio Castelar, whose authoritarian turn to restore order alienated the federalists.

Cantonal rebellion and wars

The federal republicans, impatient with the slow Constituent Cortes, launched the Cantonal rebellion in July 1873: cities such as Cartagena, Valencia, Seville, and Cádiz declared themselves independent cantons. Cartagena, fortified and holding much of the navy, held out until January 1874. Simultaneously the government fought the Third Carlist War in the north (the pretender Carlos VII fielding a real army in the Basque Country, Navarre, and Catalonia) and the Ten Years' War in Cuba. Pi i Margall resigned when he refused to crush the cantonalists by force; Salmerón resigned rather than sign death sentences; Castelar suspended the Cortes and ruled by decree.

Collapse

When the Cortes reconvened in January 1874 and moved against Castelar, General Manuel Pavía dissolved the assembly by coup (3 January 1874), installing General Serrano as head of a conservative republican government. On 29 December 1874 General Arsenio Martínez Campos pronounced at Sagunto for Alfonso, son of Isabella II, and the Bourbon Restoration began with minimal resistance. The First Republic is remembered as a cautionary tale of idealism without state power; its federal project was revived nostalgically by later republicans, and the Second Spanish Republic of 1931 consciously defined itself against its predecessor's chaos.
