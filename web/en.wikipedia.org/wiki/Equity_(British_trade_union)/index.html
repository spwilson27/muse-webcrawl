Equity (British trade union)

Founded: 1930 (as British Actors' Equity Association)
Members: ~47,000
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Affiliation: TUC

Equity is the trade union for performers and creative practitioners in the United Kingdom, representing actors, singers, dancers, choreographers, stage managers, theatre directors and designers, variety and circus artists, and presenters. Founded in 1930 as the British Actors' Equity Association, it was modelled on the American Actors' Equity Association.

History

Equity was established following organising work by actors including Godfrey Tearle, and was registered as a trade union in 1930. Like its American counterpart, it has historically operated a policy that members must take unique professional names, since no two members may work under the same name — the origin of many actors' stage names. Its badge and collective agreements with producers' bodies such as UK Theatre and the PACT producers' association set minimum pay and conditions across theatre, film and television.

Activities

The union negotiates industry-wide agreements, campaigns on issues including funding for the arts, diversity, bullying and harassment (including through its Safe Space campaign), and performers' rights and royalties. It publishes the Spotlight-linked casting directories historically, and administers pension and welfare funds for members. Famous members to have served as president include Vanessa Redgrave and Penelope Keith; Malcolm Sinclair served as president in recent years.
