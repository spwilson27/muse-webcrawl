EU Battlegroup

An EU Battlegroup (EU BG) is a rapid-reaction military unit forming part of the European Union's Common Security and Defence Policy. Each battlegroup is a battalion-sized force of about 1,500 troops, reinforced with combat support and service support elements, provided by one or more member states on a rotating standby roster. The concept reached full operational capability in January 2007, with two battlegroups on standby at any time, deployable within roughly 10 days for missions lasting up to 120 days.

Origins

The battlegroup concept built on experience with the EU-led Operation Artemis in the Democratic Republic of Congo (2003) and on earlier Franco-British proposals. Battlegroups are intended for autonomous crisis-management tasks under the Petersberg tasks, such as conflict prevention, peacekeeping, and evacuation operations, ideally with a United Nations mandate.

Notable record

Despite being on continuous standby since 2007, no EU Battlegroup has ever been deployed as such, a fact frequently cited in debates about European strategic autonomy; political disagreement among member states and funding rules are commonly blamed. The EU's 2022 Strategic Compass proposed transforming the concept into an EU Rapid Deployment Capacity of up to 5,000 troops.
