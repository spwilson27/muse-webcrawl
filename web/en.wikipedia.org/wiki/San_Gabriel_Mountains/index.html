The San Gabriel Mountains (Spanish: Sierra de San Gabriel) are a mountain range located in Los Angeles and San Bernardino counties, California. The range forms the northern boundary of the Los Angeles Basin and separates the Greater Los Angeles Area from the Mojave Desert to the north. The range is part of the Transverse Ranges, named for their east–west orientation, anomalous in the predominantly north–south trending ranges of California.

The San Gabriel Mountains form the backdrop to Los Angeles

Geography

The range extends roughly 68 miles (109 km) from the Cajon Pass in the east to the Newhall Pass in the west. Its highest summit is Mount San Antonio, commonly known as Mount Baldy, at 10,064 feet (3,068 m). Other notable peaks include Mount Wilson (5,710 ft), home to the historic Mount Wilson Observatory, and Mount Baden-Powell (9,399 ft). The range is bounded by the San Andreas Fault along its northern flank; the mountains are rising rapidly in geological terms, lifted by tectonic compression along the fault system.

National monument and recreation

Much of the range lies within the Angeles National Forest, established in 1908. In 2014, President Barack Obama designated a large portion of the range as the San Gabriel Mountains National Monument, recognizing its ecological, geological, and recreational significance; the monument was expanded in 2024. The mountains are a major recreation area for the Los Angeles metropolitan population, offering hiking, camping, skiing at Mount Baldy and Mountain High, and the scenic Angeles Crest Highway, which traverses the range.

Ecology

The range supports chaparral and oak woodland at lower elevations and coniferous forest higher up. Wildlife includes bighorn sheep, mountain lions, black bears, and the endangered San Gabriel Mountains population of Nelson's bighorn sheep. The range is also prone to wildfires, including the 2020 Bobcat Fire, one of the largest in Los Angeles County history.
