The Premier of New South Wales is the head of government of the Australian state of New South Wales. The premier is the leader of the political party or coalition that commands a majority in the Legislative Assembly of the Parliament of New South Wales, and is appointed by the Governor of New South Wales. The role is equivalent to that of a prime minister at state level; the corresponding federal office is the Prime Minister of Australia.

History

The office was established in 1856 with the granting of responsible government to the colony of New South Wales. Stuart Donaldson became the first premier (then styled Colonial Secretary of the ministry he headed) on 6 June 1856, though his ministry lasted only about two months amid the turbulent parliament of the era. After Federation in 1901, New South Wales became a state of the Commonwealth of Australia and its premier continued as head of the state government. Historically the office was frequently held for short periods; the longest-serving premier is Robert Askin (1965–1975) for the Liberal Party, while Neville Wran (1976–1986) is the longest-serving Labor premier.

Role and powers

The premier chairs the state Cabinet, advises the Governor on the appointment of ministers, sets the legislative agenda, and represents New South Wales in intergovernmental forums such as National Cabinet alongside other state premiers and the Prime Minister. State governments in Australia are responsible for schools, hospitals, police, transport, and utilities, so the premiership carries wide executive responsibilities. Since 1995, New South Wales parliaments have had fixed four-year terms, with elections held on the fourth Saturday in March.
