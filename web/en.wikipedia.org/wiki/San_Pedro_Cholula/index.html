San Pedro Cholula is a municipality and city in the Mexican state of Puebla, forming the western half of the ancient city of Cholula, one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the Americas. Together with the neighbouring municipality of San Andrés Cholula, it forms the Cholula metropolitan area adjacent to the state capital, Puebla.

Nuestra Señora de los Remedios atop the Great Pyramid of Cholula

History

Cholula was a major religious and commercial centre of Mesoamerica, sacred to the god Quetzalcoatl, and home to the Great Pyramid of Cholula (Tlachihualtepetl), the largest pyramid by volume known in the world. In October 1519, during the Spanish conquest, Hernán Cortés's forces massacred thousands of Cholultecas in the Cholula massacre. In the colonial period the settlement was divided, and the Spanish founded numerous churches; local tradition holds that Cholula has a church for every day of the year.

Landmarks

The Great Pyramid, much of it excavated with tunnels in the 20th century, is crowned by the colonial church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios. Other landmarks in San Pedro Cholula include the Franciscan convent of San Gabriel, with its Capilla Real and its many domes, the zócalo (main square), and the Portal Guerrero, an arcade of 46 arches considered the longest in Mexico. The town has been designated a Pueblo Mágico by the Mexican government.

Culture

San Pedro Cholula retains strong pre-Hispanic and Catholic traditions, including the Quetzalcoatl festival at the spring equinox and elaborate Holy Week observances. It is also a university and nightlife centre for the Puebla metropolitan area.
