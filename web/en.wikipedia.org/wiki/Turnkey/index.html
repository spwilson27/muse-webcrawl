Turnkey

Turnkey is an adjective describing a product, project, or service that is complete and ready for immediate use by the buyer — supposedly requiring nothing more than "turning the key" to start operating. A turnkey contract or turnkey project is an arrangement in which a single contractor takes full responsibility for designing, building, and commissioning a facility and hands it over to the client in working order, for an agreed (often fixed) price and by an agreed date.

In construction and industry

Turnkey contracting is common for industrial plants, power stations, refineries, factories, and infrastructure, especially in international projects where the client may lack local expertise. It is closely related to the EPC (engineering, procurement, and construction) contract model. The contractor bears most of the integration and performance risk, while the client gives up detailed control over design decisions; advocates argue this gives price certainty and a single point of responsibility, while critics note the premium charged for that risk transfer and the potential for disputes over performance guarantees.

Other uses

In real estate, a "turnkey property" is a renovated or newly built home or rental property sold fully ready for occupancy or for immediate rental by an investor. In computing and business, "turnkey systems" or "turnkey solutions" are complete packages of hardware and software, or fully equipped business formats (similar to a franchise), sold ready to run. A "turnkey" website or product is pre-built and configured so the buyer only has to brand and operate it. In corrections slang, a "turnkey" is also an old term for a jailer — the officer who literally turned the key of the cells.
