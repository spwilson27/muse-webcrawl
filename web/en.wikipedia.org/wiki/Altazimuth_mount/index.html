An altazimuth mount or alt-azimuth mount (alt-az) is a simple two-axis mount for supporting and rotating an instrument about two perpendicular axes — one vertical (azimuth) and the other horizontal (altitude, or elevation). It is named for the horizontal coordinate system (altitude and azimuth) in which the instrument's pointing is expressed. The instrument is pointed in the desired direction by rotating it about both axes.

Applications

Altazimuth mountings are the simplest and cheapest type of telescope mount and are widely used for amateur telescopes, spotting scopes, cameras, and binoculars. The popular Dobsonian telescope is a form of altazimuth mount. For tracking celestial objects, however, an alt-az mount must drive both axes at continuously varying rates, and the field of view rotates with respect to the instrument ("field rotation"), so equatorial mounts were historically preferred for professional astronomy and long-exposure photography.

With the advent of computer control, these difficulties became manageable: nearly all modern large professional telescopes — including the Keck telescopes, the Very Large Telescope, the Gran Telescopio Canarias, and the planned Extremely Large Telescope — use altazimuth mounts, because they are more compact, more rigid, and much cheaper to build at great apertures than equatorial mounts. A field de-rotator corrects image rotation at the focal plane. Radio antennas and satellite dishes likewise typically elevate and rotate in alt-az fashion.
