National Ambient Air Quality Standards

The National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS, pronounced "nacks") are limits set by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) on the concentration of certain air pollutants in outdoor air, under the authority of the Clean Air Act. The standards are intended to protect public health and the environment, and they apply throughout the United States.

Criteria pollutants

The Clean Air Act requires the EPA to set NAAQS for pollutants considered harmful to public health and the environment. Six "criteria pollutants" are currently regulated:

  • Particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10)
  • Ground-level ozone (O3)
  • Carbon monoxide (CO)
  • Lead (Pb)
  • Sulfur dioxide (SO2)
  • Nitrogen dioxide (NO2)

Primary and secondary standards

Primary standards provide public health protection, including protecting the health of sensitive populations such as asthmatics, children, and the elderly. Secondary standards provide public welfare protection, including protection against decreased visibility and damage to animals, crops, vegetation, and buildings.

Implementation

Areas that do not meet a standard are designated "nonattainment areas," and states must develop State Implementation Plans (SIPs) showing how they will bring those areas into compliance. The EPA is required to review each standard every five years and revise them as appropriate based on the latest scientific evidence.
