Aerial Experiment Association

The Aerial Experiment Association (AEA) was a Canadian-American aeronautical research group founded in 1907 by Alexander Graham Bell at Beinn Bhreagh, Nova Scotia, and Hammondsport, New York. Its members included Frederick W. "Casey" Baldwin, J. A. D. McCurdy, Glenn Curtiss, and Thomas Selfridge.

Work

The association's goal was to build a practical flying machine. Its experiments progressed from the tetrahedral kite Cygnet to a series of powered biplanes: the Red Wing (March 1908), the White Wing, the June Bug — which won the Scientific American trophy on 4 July 1908 for the first public flight of over one kilometre in North America — and the Silver Dart, which in February 1909 made the first powered flight in Canada and the British Empire. The AEA pioneered the use of ailerons for lateral control.

Dissolution

The association disbanded on 31 March 1909, its funding exhausted. Its members went on to major roles in early aviation: Curtiss founded his own aeroplane company, and McCurdy and Baldwin established Canadian Aerodrome. Thomas Selfridge became the first person to die in a powered aeroplane crash, in September 1908, while flying as a passenger with Orville Wright.
