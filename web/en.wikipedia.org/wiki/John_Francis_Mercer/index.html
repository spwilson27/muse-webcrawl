John Francis Mercer

John Francis Mercer (17 May 1759 – 30 August 1821) was an American lawyer, planter, and politician from Virginia and Maryland. He fought in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War, served as a delegate for Maryland in the Constitutional Convention of 1787 (which he ultimately refused to sign), and later served as the 10th Governor of Maryland.

Early life and military service

Mercer was born in Stafford County, Virginia, the son of John Mercer. He studied law at the College of William & Mary and served as an officer in the 3rd Virginia Regiment during the Revolutionary War, fighting at Brandywine and other engagements. He was badly wounded at the Battle of Cowpens's aftermath campaigns and rose to the rank of lieutenant colonel.

Constitutional Convention

Mercer represented Maryland at the Philadelphia Convention of 1787. Although he participated in the debates, he grew disillusioned with the strong central government proposed, left the convention before it concluded, and campaigned against ratification in Maryland.

Later career

Mercer served in the U.S. House of Representatives from Maryland in the 1790s and as Governor of Maryland from 1801 to 1803. He died at his estate in Anne Arundel County in 1821.
