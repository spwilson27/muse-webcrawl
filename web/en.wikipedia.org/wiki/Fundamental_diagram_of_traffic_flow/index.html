Fundamental diagram of traffic flow

The fundamental diagram of traffic flow is a diagram in traffic flow theory that gives a relation between traffic flow (vehicles per hour) and traffic density (vehicles per kilometre). It is a central concept in the macroscopic description of road traffic.

The diagram typically plots flow q against density k, with q = k × v, where v is mean speed. Flow rises with density up to a maximum (capacity) at a critical density, then falls as congestion sets in.

Variants

Related diagrams plot speed against density and flow against speed. Empirical observations date to the work of Bruce Greenshields in the 1930s, whose linear speed–density model produces a parabolic flow–density curve.

The fundamental diagram underlies models such as the Lighthill–Whitham–Richards kinematic wave model and is used in ramp metering, capacity analysis and traffic simulation.
