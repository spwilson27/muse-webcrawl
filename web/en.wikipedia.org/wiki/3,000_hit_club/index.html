3,000 hit club

Ty Cobb reached 3,000 hits in 1921

The 3,000 hit club comprises the Major League Baseball players who have accumulated 3,000 or more hits during the regular season. Membership is one of the most prestigious achievements in baseball, reflecting both excellence and longevity.

Overview

The club's first member was Cap Anson in the 19th century. Ty Cobb, the dominant hitter of the dead-ball era, accumulated 4,189 hits (by modern accounting), a record that stood for more than half a century until Pete Rose broke it in 1985 and finished with 4,256. Hank Aaron, Stan Musial, Tris Speaker, Derek Jeter, Honus Wagner, Albert Pujols and others fill out the upper ranks of the list.

Hall of Fame connection

Traditionally, reaching 3,000 hits has virtually guaranteed election to the Baseball Hall of Fame. Exceptions involve players excluded over gambling (Pete Rose) or those whose candidacies have been damaged by performance-enhancing drug associations (Rafael Palmeiro, Alex Rodriguez, and Manny Ramirez, who fell short of the milestone). Ichiro Suzuki, elected in 2025, amassed more combined professional hits (4,367) than any player in history when his Japanese league totals are included.

Recent members

Recent inductees into the club include Adrián Beltré (2017), Albert Pujols (2018) and Miguel Cabrera (2022), the first Venezuelan to achieve the feat and, with his 500th home run the previous season, a member of both exclusive clubs.
