Anger management

Anger management is a psycho-therapeutic program for anger prevention and control. It has been described as deploying anger successfully: anger is frequently a result of frustration, or of feeling blocked or thwarted from something important. Anger management programs teach participants to recognize signs of anger, calm down, and deal with triggers in a productive way; the goal is not to suppress anger but to express it appropriately.

History

Techniques for controlling anger have roots in ancient philosophy — Seneca's essay De Ira ("On Anger") advised delaying reactions — and in modern psychology, with structured programs emerging in the 1970s. Psychologist Raymond Novaco developed a stress-inoculation approach to anger in 1975, combining relaxation training, cognitive restructuring and skills practice, which became the model for many later programs.

Techniques and programs

Anger management interventions commonly include:

  • Relaxation training — deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation and visualization to lower physiological arousal.
  • Cognitive restructuring — identifying irrational or hostile thoughts and replacing them with more balanced appraisals.
  • Problem solving and communication skills — learning assertive (rather than aggressive) expression, listening and negotiating.
  • Exposure and stress inoculation — rehearsing responses to provocative situations in graduated steps.

Programs are delivered individually, in groups, in workplaces and schools, and are frequently court-ordered for offenders in domestic violence or road rage cases. Programs typically last from a few sessions to several months.

Effectiveness

Meta-analyses of anger treatment, including cognitive behavioral approaches, generally find moderate positive effects in reducing anger and aggression. Anger management is distinguished from treatment of intermittent explosive disorder and other conditions in which anger is a symptom, which may require specific clinical care or medication.
