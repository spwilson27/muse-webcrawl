Andrew Stephen Grove (born Gróf András István; 2 September 1936 – 21 March 2016) was a Hungarian-born American businessman, engineer, and author. As a Holocaust survivor who fled Hungary after the 1956 revolution, he became the third employee and longtime chief executive of Intel, helping to build it into the world's largest semiconductor company.

Intel

Grove earned a doctorate in chemical engineering from UC Berkeley in 1963 and worked at Fairchild Semiconductor before joining Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore on the day Intel was founded in 1968. As president (1979) and CEO (1987–1998), he drove Intel's decisive pivot from memory chips to microprocessors, supplying the brains of the personal-computer revolution. His management philosophy, set out in High Output Management (1983) and Only the Paranoid Survive (1996), introduced ideas such as "strategic inflection points" and popularized Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) in Silicon Valley.

Later life

Time named Grove its Man of the Year for 1997. After stepping down as CEO he remained chairman and a senior adviser, taught at Stanford, and wrote and campaigned on healthcare, energy, and American manufacturing competitiveness. He died in 2016 at the age of 79.
