Hiatellidae

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumMollusca
ClassBivalvia
OrderAdapedonta
FamilyHiatellidae

Hiatellidae is a family of marine bivalve molluscs. Members of the family are typically burrowing clams that bore into or nestle within soft rock, clay, peat or firm sediment, and some attach themselves in crevices with a byssus. Their shells are often irregular in shape, reflecting the contours of the cavities they inhabit.

Description

Hiatellids have elongated, inequilateral shells that gape at the posterior end, where the long fused siphons emerge. The shell surface is often rough, with irregular concentric sculpture, and the hinge is weak, with at most small cardinal teeth. Because individuals mould their growth to the shape of their burrows, shells vary considerably within species, which historically made the taxonomy of the family difficult.

Genera

Well-known genera include Hiatella (for example the Arctic hiatella, Hiatella arctica, one of the most widespread boreal bivalves), Panopea (the geoducks, among the largest burrowing clams in the world), Saxicavella and Cyrtodaria, the latter including the extinct-in-some-regions northern propeller clam.

Distribution

The family is cosmopolitan, occurring in cold and temperate seas worldwide, from the intertidal zone to considerable depths. Geoducks of the genus Panopea are commercially harvested in the Pacific Northwest and New Zealand.
