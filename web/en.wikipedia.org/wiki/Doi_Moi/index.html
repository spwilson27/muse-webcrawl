Đổi Mới introduced market reforms in Vietnam

Đổi Mới

Đổi Mới (Vietnamese: [ɗo᷉i mə̌ːi]; transl. "renovation" or "innovation") is the name given to the economic reforms initiated in Vietnam in 1986 with the goal of creating a "socialist-oriented market economy". The term đổi mới itself is a general term with wide use in the Vietnamese language meaning "innovate" or "renovate". However, the Đổi Mới Policy refers specifically to these reforms.

Background

Following reunification in 1976, Vietnam's centrally planned economy performed poorly, with inflation reaching several hundred percent by the mid-1980s and the country dependent on Soviet aid. The Sixth National Congress of the Communist Party of Vietnam in December 1986 formally adopted the reform programme.

Reforms

The reforms dismantled agricultural collectives (recognising the household as the basic production unit), abolished the system of subsidies and price controls, opened the economy to foreign investment, allowed private enterprise, and reformed the banking and foreign trade systems. Vietnam normalised relations with the United States in 1995, joined ASEAN the same year, and became a member of the WTO in 2007.

Results

Đổi Mới transformed Vietnam from one of the world's poorest countries into a lower-middle-income economy with sustained high growth; the poverty rate fell from around 58 percent in 1993 to single digits in the 2010s, and Vietnam became a major exporter of rice, coffee, electronics and garments.
