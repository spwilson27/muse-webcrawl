Emily Post (née Price; c. October 27, 1872 – September 25, 1960) was an American author and socialite famous for writing about etiquette. Her name became synonymous with proper manners in American culture.

Life and career

Born in Baltimore to architect Bruce Price and Josephine Lee Price, she married banker Edwin Main Post in 1892; the marriage ended in divorce in 1905, after which she supported herself by writing — first light novels such as Flight of a Moth (1904), then her landmark work. Her 1922 book Etiquette in Society, in Business, in Politics, and at Home, published by Funk & Wagnalls, was an immediate bestseller and has been revised and reissued continuously ever since, most recently by later generations of her family.

Influence

Post wrote a syndicated newspaper column, hosted a radio program, and in 1946 founded the Emily Post Institute, which continues to publish etiquette guidance. Her philosophy emphasized that manners rest on consideration, respect, and honesty rather than rigid formality. The book's fictional households — the Worldlys, the Richanvulgars, and the Kindharts — illustrated good and bad behavior for generations of readers.
