Bowling (cricket)

In the sport of cricket, bowling is the action of propelling the ball toward the wicket defended by the batsman. A player skilled at bowling is called a bowler; bowling the ball is distinguished from throwing by a strict rule: once the bowler's arm passes the shoulder in the delivery swing, the elbow must not straighten, on pain of the delivery being judged an illegal no-ball (a practice known as "throwing" or "chucking").

Types of bowling

Bowlers are conventionally divided into fast bowlers (or pace bowlers) and spin bowlers. Fast bowlers rely on speed — the quickest exceed 90 mph (145 km/h) — and on movement of the ball through the air (swing) or off the seam. Medium-pacers trade pace for accuracy and swing. Spin bowlers bowl much more slowly, imparting revolutions to make the ball turn sharply after bouncing: right-arm leg break, left-arm orthodox and off break are the main varieties. An over consists of six legal deliveries by one bowler from one end of the pitch.

Strategy

Captains rotate bowlers to rest them and to vary the attack, set fields to support particular plans, and select bowlers according to pitch and weather conditions. Bowling performance is measured principally by wickets taken and runs conceded, summarised in the bowling average, economy rate and strike rate. Famous bowlers include Muttiah Muralitharan and Shane Warne (spin) and Glenn McGrath and James Anderson (pace).
