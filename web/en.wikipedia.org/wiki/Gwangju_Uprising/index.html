Gwangju Uprising

The Gwangju Uprising, also known in South Korea as the May 18 Democratic Uprising, was a popular uprising in the city of Gwangju, South Korea, from 18 to 27 May 1980. Citizens took up arms against the military government of Chun Doo-hwan, controlling the city for several days before the uprising was crushed by the South Korean Army with significant loss of life.

Citizen militia members during the uprising

Events

Following the assassination of President Park Chung Hee in October 1979 and Chun Doo-hwan's military coup, martial law was extended nationwide on 17 May 1980 and universities were closed. In Gwangju, paratroopers brutally beat student protesters from Chonnam National University, provoking mass demonstrations. As violence escalated, citizens raided armouries and formed militias; the army briefly withdrew, and for several days residents organised a self-governing city with citizens' committees maintaining order and negotiating with authorities.

On 27 May, army units retook the city in a pre-dawn assault centred on the Provincial Office. Estimates of the death toll vary: official figures record around 200 dead, while other estimates run considerably higher. The event was suppressed in domestic media for years, but became a foundational symbol of South Korea's democratisation movement. Today 18 May is a national day of commemoration, archives of the uprising are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register, and former president Chun was convicted in 1996 partly for his role in the suppression.
