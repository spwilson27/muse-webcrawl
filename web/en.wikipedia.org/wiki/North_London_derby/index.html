The North London derby is the name of the local derby between the English association football clubs Arsenal and Tottenham Hotspur, both based in North London. It is one of the most fiercely contested rivalries in English football.

Although the clubs first met in 1887, the rivalry began in earnest in 1913, when Arsenal — then based at Woolwich in south-east London — moved to Highbury, just a few miles from Tottenham's White Hart Lane ground. Tottenham's supporters viewed the move as an encroachment on their territory, and tensions deepened in 1919 when Arsenal were controversially elected to the expanded First Division at Tottenham's expense.

History

The first Football League meeting took place on 4 December 1909 at the Manor Ground, Plumstead, Arsenal winning 1–0. The fixture has since been played in the top flight in most seasons, with famous episodes including Arsenal winning the league title at White Hart Lane in 1971 (completing the Double) and again in 2004, when the "Invincibles" secured the championship on their rivals' ground. Tottenham's notable derby triumphs include the 1991 FA Cup semi-final at Wembley and several high-scoring encounters in the Premier League era.

Culture

The rivalry extends beyond the pitch: Tottenham fans celebrate "St Totteringham's Day" when Arsenal can no longer finish above them, while Arsenal supporters long marked the reverse during their long run of finishing above Spurs. Players who cross the divide — most famously Sol Campbell, who moved from Tottenham to Arsenal in 2001 — are met with lasting hostility from the fans they left behind.
