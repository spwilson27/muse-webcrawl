New World Pictures (also known as New World Entertainment and New World Communications) was an American independent production, development, and distribution company founded in 1970 by B-movie producer Roger Corman, who had left American International Pictures that year. It became famous for low-budget genre films, imported art-house pictures, and later for an expansive television and home-video empire in the 1980s.

History

Corman founded New World in 1970 after selling his interest in American International Pictures. The company's formula was lean production: cheaply made exploitation films — car-chase pictures, nurse films, women-in-prison movies — shot quickly and marketed aggressively. Early successes included The Student Nurses (1970), Death Race 2000 (1975), Rock 'n' Roll High School (1979), and Piranha (1978). New World also became an important American distributor of foreign art films by directors such as Ingmar Bergman, Federico Fellini, Akira Kurosawa, and Andrzej Wajda, winning Academy Awards for titles like Amarcord and The Tin Drum.

The company served as a training ground for later-famous filmmakers and actors, including Jonathan Demme, Joe Dante, Ron Howard, John Sayles, Martin Scorsese (whose Boxcar Bertha was made for Corman), James Cameron, and Gale Anne Hurd. Corman sold New World in 1983 to an investment group, after which the company expanded into television production, home video, and broadcasting. In 1989 it was acquired by investor Ronald Perelman, and its television arm produced series such as The Wonder Years. Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation acquired New World Communications in 1996–97, principally for its television stations, which became core Fox affiliates; the film library passed through several hands thereafter.
