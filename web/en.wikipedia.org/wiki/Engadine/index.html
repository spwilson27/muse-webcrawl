Engadin

The Engadin or Engadine (Romansh: Engiadina; German: Engadin; Italian: Engadina) is a long high Alpine valley region in the eastern Swiss Alps in the canton of Graubünden, Switzerland. It follows the route of the Inn river from its source at the Maloja Pass, from the southwest running roughly northeast, until the Inn flows into Austria, one hundred kilometres downstream. The Engadin is protected by high mountains on all sides and is famous for its sunny climate, beautiful landscapes, and outdoor activities.

Geography

The valley is divided into the Upper Engadin, a broad, high valley with a chain of lakes (including Lake Sils, Lake Silvaplana and Lake St. Moritz) surrounded by high peaks, and the Lower Engadin, a narrower, more rugged valley where the Inn flows through deep gorges. The resort town of St. Moritz, host of the Winter Olympics in 1928 and 1948, is the most famous settlement in the Upper Engadin; Scuol is the principal town of the Lower Engadin.

Culture and language

The Engadin is one of the principal strongholds of the Romansh language, spoken in its Vallader and Puter idioms, alongside German and Italian. The region is known for its distinctive sgraffito-decorated houses, in which geometric patterns and inscriptions are scratched into wet plaster facades.

Access

The valley is served by the Rhaetian Railway, whose Albula and Bernina lines are part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and by road passes including the Julier, Albula, Flüela and Bernina passes.
