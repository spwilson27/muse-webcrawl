Shot put

The shot put is a track and field event in which an athlete "puts" (pushes, rather than throws) a heavy spherical ball — the shot — as far as possible from within a circle 2.135 m in diameter. The men's shot weighs 7.26 kg (16 lb) and the women's 4 kg (8.8 lb). The event has been part of the modern Olympic Games for men since 1896 and for women since 1948.

Technique

Two techniques dominate: the glide, in which the athlete powers across the circle in a low crouch before delivering the shot, and the spin (rotational technique), adapted from the discus throw by Aleksandr Baryshnikov in the 1970s and popularised by American throwers, which builds greater momentum. The shot must be held close to the neck and released above shoulder height with a pushing motion; the athlete must not leave the circle before the shot lands.

Records

The men's world record is 23.56 m, set by Ryan Crouser of the United States in 2023; Crouser also holds the Olympic record. The women's world record of 22.63 m was set by Natalya Lisovskaya of the Soviet Union in 1987. Randy Barnes and Crouser are among the few men to have exceeded 23 metres.
