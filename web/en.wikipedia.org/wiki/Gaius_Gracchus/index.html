Gaius Gracchus

Gaius Sempronius Gracchus (c. 154 – 121 BC) was a Roman politician and reformer who served as tribune of the plebs in 123 and 122 BC. With his elder brother Tiberius, he is remembered as one of the Gracchi, whose land and citizenship reforms shook the late Roman Republic.


Office: Tribune of the plebs (123–122 BC)
Died: 121 BC, Rome
Brother: Tiberius Gracchus

Reforms

Gaius revived land redistribution, introduced subsidised grain for Rome's citizens, reformed jury courts to include equestrians, extended rights in the provinces, and proposed citizenship for Rome's Italian allies — a proposal that cost him support.

Downfall

Opposition led by the Senate and consul Opimius culminated in the first senatus consultum ultimum; Gaius fled and ordered his enslaved follower to kill him on the Aventine in 121 BC, and thousands of his supporters were executed. See Gaius Marius and Galatia.
