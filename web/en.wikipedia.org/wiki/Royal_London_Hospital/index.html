Royal London Hospital

The Royal London Hospital
Location: Whitechapel, London, England
Founded: 1740 (as the London Infirmary)
Type: Teaching hospital
Network: Barts Health NHS Trust

The Royal London Hospital is a large teaching hospital in Whitechapel, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets. It is part of Barts Health NHS Trust and is associated with Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry at Queen Mary University of London.

History

The hospital was founded in 1740 as the London Infirmary, initially in Moorfields, before moving to Whitechapel in 1757. It became the London Hospital and grew into one of the largest voluntary hospitals in Britain, serving the poor of the East End. In 1990, on its 250th anniversary, "Royal" was added to its name.

The hospital is famously connected with Joseph Merrick, known as the "Elephant Man", who lived in the hospital from 1886 until his death in 1890 under the care of surgeon Frederick Treves. It also has historic links with Edith Cavell, who trained as a nurse there, and with nurse Eva Luckes, who reformed nursing education at the hospital in the late nineteenth century.

Modern development

A major redevelopment under a Private Finance Initiative produced a new hospital building with distinctive blue cladding, opened in stages from 2012. The new facility brought together services including one of London's four major trauma centres, an emergency department, and the base of London's Air Ambulance, which operates from a helipad on the roof — at the time of construction the highest hospital helipad in Europe.

The old hospital site houses the Royal London Hospital Museum, which displays artefacts including material relating to Merrick and the Jack the Ripper case, some victims of which were treated or examined at the hospital.
