Kazuyo Sejima (Japanese: 妹島 和世, born October 29, 1956) is a Japanese architect known for designs built from clean, white, minimalist elements and luminous, transparent spaces. With her partner Ryue Nishizawa, she co-founded the Tokyo-based firm SANAA in 1995, and in 2010 they jointly received the Pritzker Architecture Prize, architecture's highest honor; Sejima was only the second woman to win it.

Career

After graduating from Japan Women's University, Sejima apprenticed with architect Toyo Ito before founding her own practice in 1987. Early works such as the Saishunkan Seiyaku Women's Dormitory (1991) established her reputation for diagrammatic, lightness-driven design. Through SANAA she and Nishizawa produced internationally acclaimed buildings including the 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa (2004), the Glass Pavilion at the Toledo Museum of Art (2006), the New Museum in New York (2007), and the Rolex Learning Center at EPFL in Lausanne (2010), famous for its single undulating floor slab.

Style and recognition

Sejima's architecture favors thin planes, glass, polished metal, and white surfaces, dissolving boundaries between inside and outside and between program elements. In 2010 she became the first woman to direct the Venice Biennale of Architecture. In 2022 France appointed her an Officer of the Order of Arts and Letters, and she has taught at universities including Princeton and the Polytechnic University of Milan.
