Condition monitoring

Condition monitoring (CM) is the process of monitoring a parameter of condition in machinery (such as vibration, temperature, or oil debris) in order to identify a significant change which is indicative of a developing fault. It is a major component of predictive maintenance: instead of servicing equipment on a fixed schedule or after breakdown, maintenance is performed when measurements show that failure is approaching.

Techniques

The most widely used technique is vibration analysis, since rotating machinery exhibits characteristic vibration signatures for imbalance, misalignment, looseness, bearing wear, and gear defects. Other methods include thermography (infrared imaging of hot spots), oil analysis and wear-debris analysis, ultrasound for leak and bearing detection, motor-current signature analysis, and acoustic emission monitoring.

Implementation

Monitoring may be periodic, using hand-held instruments on a route, or continuous, using permanently installed sensors feeding alarms and trend plots. Modern systems integrate with the industrial Internet of things and use machine-learning models to detect anomalies. Standards such as the ISO 17359 guideline and the ISO 13372 vocabulary govern terminology and practice, and bodies such as the Vibration Institute certify analysts.

Benefits and limits

Properly applied, condition monitoring reduces unplanned downtime, secondary damage, and spare-parts inventory. Its effectiveness depends on selecting measurable failure precursors, setting sensible alarm levels, and acting on findings; some failure modes develop too quickly or leave no signature suitable for monitoring.
