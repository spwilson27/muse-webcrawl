Toy

A toy is an object used for play. Toys are generally associated with children and pets, but it is not unusual for adults and some non-domesticated animals to play with toys as well. Many items are designed to be used as toys, but goods produced for other purposes can also be used; a child may pick up a household object, such as a stick or a cardboard box, and "play" with it in an imaginative way. Some toys are produced primarily as collectors' items and are intended for display only.

Traditional toys: a rocking horse, wooden blocks, and a doll.

History

Toys are among the oldest human artefacts. Archaeologists have found dolls, animal figures, wheeled carts, and marbles at sites dating back thousands of years: clay animals on wheels from the Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 2500 BCE), rattles and yo-yos in ancient Egypt and Greece, and kites in ancient China. In the ancient world, toys were typically made from materials found in nature, such as rock, wood, and clay.

During the Enlightenment, attitudes toward childhood changed and toys became more widespread as children came to be seen as people in themselves rather than extensions of their parents. The Industrial Revolution and mass production transformed toys from handcrafted objects into inexpensive manufactured goods, and the 20th century added plastics, electronics, and licensed characters from film and television.

Child development

Playing with toys is regarded as an important means by which children grow and learn about the world. Different toys serve different developmental purposes: construction toys such as blocks and Lego develop motor skills and spatial reasoning; dolls and action figures support social and imaginative play; puzzles exercise problem-solving; and board games teach rule-following and turn-taking. Developmental psychologists distinguish types of play — practice play, symbolic play, constructive play, and games with rules — each supported by different kinds of toys.

Types

  • Dolls and action figures — representations of people, animals, and fictional characters.
  • Construction sets — interlocking bricks, blocks, and model kits.
  • Vehicles — toy cars, trains, and aircraft, from wooden pull-alongs to die-cast models.
  • Puzzles and games — jigsaw puzzles, board games, and card games.
  • Educational toys — designed to teach concepts such as numbers, letters, or science.
  • Electronic and digital toys — video games, interactive robots, and learning tablets.
  • Outdoor toys — balls, kites, bicycles, swings, and water toys.

Gender and marketing

Toys have historically been marketed along gender lines, with dolls and domestic playsets aimed at girls and vehicles, weapons, and construction sets at boys. Since the late 20th century, researchers and retailers have debated the effects of this division on children's development and career interests, and some manufacturers have moved toward gender-neutral packaging.

Safety and regulation

Because toys are used by children, they are subject to safety regulations in many countries covering choking hazards, sharp edges, toxic paints, and flammability. Standards include the European Union's Toy Safety Directive and the United States' ASTM F963 standard. Product recalls are issued when toys are found to pose risks, such as small magnets or lead-contaminated paint.
