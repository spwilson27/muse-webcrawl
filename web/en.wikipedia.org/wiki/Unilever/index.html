Unilever plc is a British multinational consumer goods company headquartered in London, England. It is one of the world's largest consumer goods companies, with products sold in around 190 countries. Its portfolio spans food and refreshments, home care, and beauty and personal care.

History

Unilever was formed on 2 September 1929 by the merger of the Dutch margarine producer Margarine Unie and the British soapmaker Lever Brothers, founded by William Hesketh Lever. Both companies depended on palm oil imports, and the merger allowed them to purchase raw materials more cheaply in bulk. For most of the 20th century the group operated a dual structure, with Unilever NV in Rotterdam and Unilever plc in London sharing directors and operating as a single business.

Through the twentieth century Unilever expanded by acquisition, buying brands such as Pepsodent, Pond's, Brooke Bond (PG Tips), Ben & Jerry's (2000) and Alberto-Culver (2010), while also building a major presence in India through Hindustan Unilever. In 2020 the company unified its legal structure under a single parent, Unilever plc, ending the Anglo-Dutch dual structure after ninety years.

Operations and brands

Unilever organises its business into divisions covering Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care, Home Care, Nutrition and Ice Cream. Well-known brands include Dove, Knorr, Hellmann's, Magnum, Wall's, Lipton, Surf, Omo, Rexona (Sure), Axe (Lynx), Sunsilk, Vaseline and Domestos. In 2024 the company announced plans to separate its ice cream business.

Sustainability

The company launched the Unilever Sustainable Living Plan in 2010, setting targets on health, environmental footprint and livelihoods. Unilever has been both praised for leadership in corporate sustainability and criticised over plastic packaging waste and palm oil sourcing; it was a founding member of the Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil.
