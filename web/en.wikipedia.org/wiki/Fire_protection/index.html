Fire protection

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Active and passive systems protect buildings and occupants from fire.

Fire protection is the study and practice of mitigating the unwanted effects of potentially destructive fires. It involves the study of the behaviour, compartmentalisation, suppression, and investigation of fire and its related emergencies, as well as the research and development, production, testing, and application of mitigating systems.

Goals

The principal goals of fire protection are protecting life, property, and the environment, and ensuring continuity of operations. These are pursued through building codes, fire safety engineering, and emergency response planning.

Active and passive protection

Active fire protection systems act to detect and suppress fire: smoke detectors, fire alarms, automatic sprinklers, and portable fire extinguishers. Passive fire protection is built into the structure: fire-rated walls and doors, compartmentation, and protected escape routes that contain fire and give occupants time to evacuate. Fire protection engineers design and assess these systems, and fire departments provide suppression and rescue services.
