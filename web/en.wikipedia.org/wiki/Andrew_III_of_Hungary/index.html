Andrew III the Venetian (Hungarian: III. András; c. 1265 – 14 January 1301) was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1290 to 1301. He was the last male member of the House of Árpád, the dynasty that had ruled Hungary since its foundation.

Background

Andrew was born in Venice, the son of Stephen the Posthumous, a disputed younger son of Andrew II of Hungary, and Tomasina Morosini. Raised in Venice, his claim to the Árpád inheritance was long contested, and he was briefly imprisoned in Vienna after an early attempt to assert his rights.

Reign

After the assassination of Ladislaus IV in 1290, Andrew was crowned king at Székesfehérvár. His reign was marked by struggles against powerful oligarchs such as Matthew Csák and the Kőszegi family, and by rival claims backed by the papacy and the Angevins of Naples. He issued decrees strengthening royal authority and held diets with the prelates, barons, and nobility.

Death and succession

Andrew died suddenly at Buda in January 1301 without a male heir. His death ended the Árpád dynasty and opened a period of interregnum and competing claims to the Hungarian throne.
