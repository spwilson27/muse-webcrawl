Job interview

Job interview
PurposeAssessment of a job applicant by a prospective employer
Common formatsOne-on-one, panel, group, telephone, video, case interviews
RelatedRecruitment, job vacancy, curriculum vitae

A job interview is a structured or semi-structured conversation between a job applicant and a representative of an employer, conducted to assess whether the applicant should be hired. Interviews are one of the most widely used devices for employee selection, and typically follow screening of written applications against a job vacancy.

Types

  • Structured interviews follow a fixed set of questions scored against predetermined criteria; research in industrial-organizational psychology finds them among the most valid predictors of job performance.
  • Unstructured interviews are conversational and flexible, letting the interviewer follow up on points of interest, but are more susceptible to bias.
  • Behavioral interviews ask candidates to describe past behaviour ("Tell me about a time when…"), based on the premise that past performance predicts future performance.
  • Situational interviews present hypothetical work scenarios.
  • Panel and group interviews involve multiple interviewers, or multiple candidates together.
  • Remote interviews by telephone or video have become standard, especially for early screening rounds.

Process

An interview process commonly includes screening calls, one or more rounds of interviews, work samples or skills tests, and reference checks. Both parties evaluate fit: the employer assesses qualifications and interpersonal skills, while the applicant evaluates the role and organization.

Validity and bias

Meta-analyses (notably Schmidt and Hunter, 1998) rank structured interviews among the strongest single predictors of job performance, especially combined with general mental ability tests. Unstructured interviews are vulnerable to first-impression effects, similarity bias, and halo effects; employment law in many jurisdictions also constrains permissible questions (e.g., about age, family status, or disability).
