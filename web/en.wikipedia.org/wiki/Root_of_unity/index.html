Root of unity

In mathematics, a root of unity is any complex number that yields 1 when raised to some positive integer power n. That is, a number z is a root of unity if zn = 1 for some n ≥ 1. The nth roots of unity are given by e2πik/n for k = 0, 1, …, n − 1, and form the vertices of a regular n-gon on the unit circle in the complex plane.

Primitive roots

An nth root of unity is called primitive if n is the smallest positive integer for which its power equals 1. There are φ(n) primitive nth roots of unity, where φ is Euler's totient function. They are the roots of the nth cyclotomic polynomial Φn(x), which plays a central role in algebraic number theory through the cyclotomic fields it generates.

Properties and applications

The nth roots of unity form a cyclic group under multiplication, generated by any primitive root. Roots of unity underlie the discrete Fourier transform and its fast algorithms, the theory of constructible polygons (Gauss showed the regular 17-gon is constructible because 17 is a Fermat prime), and many results in number theory and group representation theory. A special case, e2πi/n, is fundamental in signal processing and crystallography.
