Skanderbeg

Gjergj Kastrioti (c. 1405 – 17 January 1468), commonly known as Skanderbeg, was an Albanian feudal lord and military commander who led a rebellion against the Ottoman Empire in what is today Albania, North Macedonia, Greece, Kosovo, Montenegro and Serbia. He is regarded as the national hero of Albania.

Born: c. 1405, Principality of Kastrioti
Died: 17 January 1468, Lezhë
Allegiance: League of Lezhë
Known for: Resistance to the Ottoman Empire

Early life

A member of the Kastrioti family, Gjergj was taken hostage by the Ottoman court as a youth, educated in the sultan's service and given the name Iskender (Alexander). He rose in the Ottoman military, earning the title bey — hence "Skanderbeg", from Iskender Bey.

Rebellion

In 1443, during the Battle of Niš, he deserted the Ottomans and returned to Albania, capturing the fortress of Krujë and raising the double-headed eagle banner of the Kastrioti. In 1444 he united Albanian princes in the League of Lezhë. For 25 years his comparatively small forces resisted repeated Ottoman campaigns, winning most engagements, including the sieges of Krujë.

Death and legacy

Skanderbeg died of malaria in 1468. Albanian resistance continued for about another decade. His military skill was celebrated across Europe; popes praised him as a defender of Christendom, and his helmet and sword are preserved in Vienna.
