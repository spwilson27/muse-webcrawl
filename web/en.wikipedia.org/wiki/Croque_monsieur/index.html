A croque monsieur (French pronunciation: [kʁɔk məsjø]) is a hot sandwich made with ham and cheese, a staple of French cafés and bistros. The name comes from the French words croque ("crunchy bite") and monsieur ("gentleman"). The sandwich traditionally consists of baked or boiled ham and sliced cheese — usually Gruyère or Emmental — between slices of brioche-like bread (pain de mie), topped with grated cheese and béchamel or Mornay sauce, then grilled or baked until the cheese melts and browns.

History

The croque monsieur first appeared on Parisian café menus around 1910. Its origin is sometimes credited to Michel Lunarca, proprietor of the Bel Âge café on the Boulevard des Capucines. The sandwich achieved literary fame through a mention in Marcel Proust's In Search of Lost Time (1919).

Variations

A croque monsieur served with a poached or fried egg on top is a croque madame — the egg said to resemble a woman's hat. Other variants include the croque provençal (with tomato), croque auvergnat (with blue cheese), croque norvégien (with smoked salmon replacing the ham) and the Hawaiian croque (with pineapple). Similar sandwiches exist elsewhere, such as the Portuguese francesinha and the Dutch tosti.
