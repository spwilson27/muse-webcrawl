RIAA certification

RIAA certification is a system, administered by the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA), for certifying the shipment and consumption figures of sound recordings in the United States. The best-known awards are Gold and Platinum records, presented as framed gold- or platinum-plated discs.

Thresholds

The Gold & Platinum program was launched in 1958, initially for singles. Current thresholds for albums are: Gold at 500,000 units, Platinum at 1,000,000 units, Multi-Platinum in increments of 1,000,000, and Diamond at 10,000,000 units (the Diamond award was introduced in 1999). Singles use the same thresholds for digital releases; physical singles and shortform releases have lower thresholds. "Units" today combine traditional album sales, track-equivalent albums, and on-demand streams according to RIAA formulas.

History and criteria

Criteria have changed repeatedly to reflect the market: originally based on dollar sales or units shipped, the program moved to audited unit counts net of returns, and in the 2010s incorporated digital downloads and streaming. In 2013, on-demand audio and video streams were included in singles certification; in 2016, album certifications incorporated track equivalents and streaming equivalents. The RIAA also runs Los Premios De Oro y Platino for Latin recordings, with lower thresholds.

Notable records

The Eagles' Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975) is among the highest-certified albums in the United States, certified 38× Platinum, and Michael Jackson's Thriller is certified 34× Platinum. Certifications are audited by independent accountants based on label-submitted data.
