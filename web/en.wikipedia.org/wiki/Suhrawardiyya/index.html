Suhrawardiyya

FounderShihab al-Din Umar al-Suhrawardi
Origin12th–13th century, Baghdad
TypeSufi order (tariqa)
RegionPersia, Iraq, South Asia

The Suhrawardiyya (Arabic: السهروردية) is a Sufi order (tariqa) founded by Shihab al-Din Umar al-Suhrawardi (c. 1145–1234), a Persian Sufi master based in Baghdad. Drawing on the teachings of his uncle Abu al-Najib al-Suhrawardi (d. 1168), author of the influential Sufi manual Adab al-Muridin ("Manners of the Disciples"), the order became one of the major organised Sufi brotherhoods of the medieval Islamic world. It is not to be confused with the Illuminationist (Ishraqi) philosophy of the unrelated Shihab al-Din Yahya al-Suhrawardi (d. 1191).

Teachings

Shihab al-Din Umar's masterpiece, the 'Awarif al-Ma'arif ("Gifts of Gnosis"), became one of the most widely read Sufi manuals, studied from Anatolia to India. The Suhrawardiyya is noted for combining strict observance of the sharia with Sufi devotion, and for accepting — unusually among early orders — close relations with political rulers: Shihab al-Din served as an envoy and adviser to the Abbasid caliph al-Nasir, whose court promoted the futuwwa (chivalric) movement with which the order was associated.

Organisation and spread

Disciples were trained in communal lodges (khanqahs) with regulated rules for retreat (khalwa), remembrance (dhikr) and service. From Baghdad the order spread into Persia, Syria and Anatolia. Its greatest expansion was in South Asia, where Baha al-Din Zakariya of Multan (d. 1262) established the order in the Punjab and Sindh, building a wealthy khanqah and maintaining ties with the Delhi Sultanate. Other notable figures include Jalal al-Din Tabrizi in Bengal and the shrine culture that grew around Suhrawardi saints in Uch and Multan.

Legacy

The Suhrawardiyya was a formative influence on Sufi organisation in India, interacting and sometimes competing with the Chishtiyya, which it preceded there. Branches and offshoots — among them the Firdausiyya of Bihar — carried its teachings across the subcontinent, and the 'Awarif al-Ma'arif remains a standard text in Sufi curricula.
