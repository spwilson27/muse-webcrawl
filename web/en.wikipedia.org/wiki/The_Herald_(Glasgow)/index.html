The Herald is a Scottish broadsheet newspaper founded in 1783 as the Glasgow Advertiser. Published in Glasgow by Newsquest, it is one of the oldest continuously published English-language newspapers in the world, and Scotland's national broadsheet of record.

History

The paper was founded by John Mennons in January 1783. It became the Glasgow Herald in 1805 and for most of its history was identified with Glasgow's mercantile and industrial establishment. In 1992 it dropped "Glasgow" from its title to become simply The Herald, reflecting ambitions as a paper for all of Scotland. A Sunday edition, The Herald on Sunday, was launched in 2018 after the closure of the Sunday Herald.

Content and stance

The Herald covers Scottish, UK and international news, politics, business, sport and culture. It supported the Union during the 2014 Scottish independence referendum. Its sister title The National, launched in 2014, takes the opposite, pro-independence stance.

Circulation

Like most British newspapers, its print circulation has declined steeply since the late twentieth century, offset in part by digital subscriptions through its website, heraldscotland.com.
