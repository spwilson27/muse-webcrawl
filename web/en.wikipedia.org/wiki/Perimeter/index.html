Perimeter

A perimeter is the length of the closed path that surrounds a two-dimensional shape. The word comes from the Greek peri (around) and metron (measure). For a polygon, the perimeter is the sum of the lengths of all its sides. The perimeter of a circle is called its circumference.

Formulas

  • Triangle: a + b + c, the sum of the three sides.
  • Rectangle: 2(l + w), where l is length and w is width.
  • Square: 4s, where s is the side length.
  • Regular polygon: n × s, where n is the number of sides and s the side length.
  • Circle: 2πr, where r is the radius and π ≈ 3.14159.

For irregular shapes and curves, the perimeter is the total length of the boundary, which may be computed by integration along the curve.

Properties

For a given area, the circle has the smallest possible perimeter of any shape — the isoperimetric inequality. Conversely, among rectangles with a given perimeter, the square encloses the largest area. Perimeter has practical importance in fields such as construction (fencing, skirting), agriculture, and geography, where the length of a boundary must be measured or purchased.

The concept is related but distinct from area, which measures the surface enclosed. Confusing the two is a common error in elementary geometry education.
