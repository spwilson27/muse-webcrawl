WWVB

WWVB is a time signal radio station near Fort Collins, Colorado, and is operated by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Most radio-controlled clocks in North America use WWVB's transmissions to set their time automatically. The continuously broadcast signal at 60 kHz carries a time code that encodes the current UTC time and date, along with information about daylight saving time and leap seconds.

Signal

WWVB radiates at 60 kHz with an effective radiated power of about 70 kW. The signal can be received across most of North America, especially at night. The time code is transmitted at one bit per second: the original format uses amplitude modulation (reducing carrier power for a fraction of each second), and a phase-modulation layer was added in 2012 to improve reception and add additional information. A complete time frame is sent each minute.

History

Experimental low-frequency broadcasts on the site began in the 1950s; WWVB began broadcasting at 60 kHz in 1963, and the time code format followed in 1965. Power increases and antenna upgrades over the decades improved coverage. In 2018, the station's future was briefly in question when a federal budget proposal suggested closing the NIST time stations, but funding was maintained.
