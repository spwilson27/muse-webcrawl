Sketch comedy

Sketch comedy comprises a series of short, amusing scenes or vignettes, usually between one and ten minutes long, performed by a group of comic actors or comedians. A single scene is called a "sketch." The form developed from vaudeville and variety shows in the United States and music hall in Britain, and flourished on radio, then television.

Landmark sketch shows include Your Show of Shows, Monty Python's Flying Circus (1969–1974), Saturday Night Live (from 1975), SCTV, In Living Color, The Kids in the Hall, Mr. Show, Chappelle's Show, and Key & Peele. In Britain, series such as The Two Ronnies, The Fast Show, and Little Britain defined the genre for their generations.

Structure

Unlike a sitcom, a sketch show strings together unrelated scenes, often with recurring characters and catchphrases. Sketches typically follow a simple rule: establish a comic premise, escalate it ("heightening"), and end on a punchline or "blackout." Live sketch troupes such as The Groundlings, The Second City, and the Upright Citizens Brigade train performers in both writing and improvisation, feeding talent into television.

Online era

Since the 2000s, sketch comedy has thrived online, where short-form videos on YouTube and social media replicate the format for a new audience.
