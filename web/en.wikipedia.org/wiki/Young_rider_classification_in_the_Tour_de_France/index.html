Young rider classification in the Tour de France

The young rider classification is a secondary classification in the Tour de France, established in 1975, that recognises the highest-placed rider under a specified age limit in the general classification. Its leader wears the white jersey (maillot blanc).

Rules

The classification is calculated in the same way as the general classification — by cumulative riding time — but is restricted to eligible young riders. Eligibility has changed over the years: it has variously been limited to riders under 23, under 25, or under 26, and for a period to neo-professionals and riders in their first Tours. Under current rules, riders who are 25 or younger on 1 January of the year of the race are eligible.

History

From 1968 to 1975 the white jersey was awarded to the leader of the combination classification. When that classification was restructured, the white jersey was reassigned in 1975 to the best young rider, with Francesco Moser its first winner. Between 1989 and 1999 the classification continued to be calculated but the white jersey was not awarded, the competition being recognised only informally; the jersey returned in 2000.

Notable winners

Several white jersey winners have gone on to win the Tour itself, including Laurent Fignon, Greg LeMond, Jan Ullrich, Andy Schleck, Egan Bernal, and Tadej Pogačar, who won the classification four consecutive times (2020–2023) while also winning the yellow jersey twice in that span. Marco Pantani and Andy Schleck are among those who won the young rider classification in successive years.
