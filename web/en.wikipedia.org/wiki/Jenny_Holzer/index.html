Jenny Holzer (born July 29, 1950) is an American neo-conceptual artist, based in Hoosick, New York. The main focus of her work is the delivery of words and ideas in public spaces, most famously through large-scale projections, electronic LED signs, carved stone benches and posters.

Career

Holzer emerged in the late 1970s with the Truisms (1977–79), a series of one-line aphorisms — "Abuse of power comes as no surprise," "Protect me from what I want" — printed on posters wheatpasted around Manhattan and later inscribed on T-shirts, plaques and electronic signs. Her contradictory, deadpan statements borrow the language of advertising and official pronouncements to provoke reflection on power, violence, sex and consumption.

Later series include the Inflammatory Essays (1979–82), Laments (1989), the Mother and Child texts, and works using declassified United States government documents concerning the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, often rendered as silkscreened paintings of redacted pages. In 1990 Holzer became the first woman to represent the United States at the Venice Biennale, where she won the Golden Lion.

Style and themes

Holzer's practice demonstrates how language operates in public life: authoritative typography and impersonal display media lend her texts a false officialdom that their unsettling content undermines. Her work is installed in museums, airports, stadiums and on buildings worldwide, and she has projected texts onto landmarks including the River Arno in Florence and the facade of the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao.
