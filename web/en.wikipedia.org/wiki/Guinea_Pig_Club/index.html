Guinea Pig Club

The Guinea Pig Club was a social club and mutual support network for Allied aircrew who underwent experimental reconstructive plastic surgery at the Queen Victoria Hospital, East Grinstead, Sussex, during the Second World War. It was founded in 1941 by patients of the surgeon Archibald McIndoe, who treated burns and other severe injuries, pioneering techniques in plastic surgery.

Members had typically suffered severe burns, often to the face and hands, in aircraft fires. The club took its name from the experimental nature of the new surgical techniques — patients joked that they were McIndoe's "guinea pigs". By the end of the war the club had 649 members, drawn from Britain, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and other Allied nations.

The club continued as a charitable organisation supporting members and their families for the rest of their lives. The last annual dinner was held in 2007 as membership dwindled, and the club was formally wound up in 2016. Its members were commemorated by a monument in East Grinstead, unveiled in 2016.
