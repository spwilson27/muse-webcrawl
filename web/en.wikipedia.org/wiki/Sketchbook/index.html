Sketchbook

A sketchbook is a book or pad with blank pages for sketching, and is frequently used by artists for drawing or painting as part of their creative process. It can be used to record observations, develop compositions, practice technique, and experiment with media. Sketchbooks may be bound hardcover volumes or spiral-bound pads, and their paper varies in weight and tooth depending on the intended medium.

Types and formats

Sketchbooks come in many sizes, commonly measured by standard paper formats (A6 through A3 and beyond) and in rectangular or square proportions. Paper types include smooth bristol for ink and marker, textured watercolor paper, toned paper, and mixed-media paper. Bindings range from stitched and case-bound books, which lie flat poorly but protect pages, to wire-bound pads that fold back fully.

Use in art practice

Artists historically filled sketchbooks with studies — Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks, the sketchbooks of Delacroix, Van Gogh, and Turner are famous examples that document working methods, preparatory drawings, and daily observation. Art schools commonly require students to maintain sketchbooks as evidence of visual research and development. Urban sketching, travel journals, and nature journals are popular contemporary sketchbook practices, and some sketchbook pages are finished works in their own right while others remain private exercises.

Digital sketchbooks

With the advent of graphics tablets and tablet computers, digital sketchbooks — apps that simulate pages and allow unlimited undo, layers, and media simulation — have become common in concept art, animation, and illustration workflows. Community projects such as The Sketchbook Project, which archived thousands of physical sketchbooks in a public library in Brooklyn, reflect the sketchbook's cultural role as an intimate, portable studio.
