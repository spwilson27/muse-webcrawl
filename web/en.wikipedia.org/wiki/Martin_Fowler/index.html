Martin Fowler (born 18 December 1963) is a British-American software developer, author and international speaker on software development, specialising in object-oriented analysis and design, UML, patterns, and agile software development methodologies, including extreme programming. He has been Chief Scientist of Thoughtworks, a global software consultancy, since 2000.

Career and writings

Fowler was born and raised in Walsall, England, and graduated from University College London in 1986. He worked in software consulting in London before moving to the United States. His book Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code (1999; second edition 2018) popularised the practice of refactoring and catalogued dozens of code transformations. Other influential books include UML Distilled (1997), Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture (2002), and Domain-Specific Languages (2010).

Fowler was one of the seventeen signatories of the Manifesto for Agile Software Development in 2001. Through his long-running website and "bliki" — a blend of blog and wiki — he has introduced or popularised widely used concepts such as continuous integration, dependency injection, the strangler fig approach to legacy replacement, and (with Sam Newman) microservices. He also coined the term "agile fluency" discussions and maintains influential online guides to topics such as microservices and event-driven architecture.

Personal life

Fowler lives in Melrose, Massachusetts. He is a Fellow of the British Computer Society's Distinguished Fellow award holder and an elected member of various professional bodies.
