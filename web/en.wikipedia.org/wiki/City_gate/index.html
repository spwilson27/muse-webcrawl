City gate

A city gate is a gate constructed within a city wall to control entry and exit, collect tolls and provide defence. City gates were a standard feature of walled towns and cities from ancient times until the early modern period, when walls lost their military function.

Function and design

Gates were usually the most strongly fortified points of a wall circuit, often flanked by towers and fitted with heavy doors, portcullises and guardrooms. They served as customs posts where goods were taxed, as ceremonial entrances for processions, and as places where notices were posted and justice displayed. Many gates incorporated chapels, prisons or council rooms.

Surviving examples

Famous surviving gates include the Brandenburg Gate in Berlin, the gates of Jerusalem's Old City walls such as Jaffa Gate and Damascus Gate, the gatehouses of York and Canterbury in England, and the monumental gates of Chinese cities such as Xi'an. From the 18th century onward, many gates were demolished to ease traffic, while others, like triumphal arches, survived as monuments detached from their walls.
