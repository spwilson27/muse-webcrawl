Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc. is an American video game holding company based in New York City, founded by Ryan Brant in September 1993. It is one of the largest game publishers in the world, owning the publishing labels Rockstar Games and 2K, and the mobile developer Zynga. Its best-known franchises include Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead, NBA 2K, BioShock, Borderlands, Civilization and Mafia.

History

Take-Two grew through acquisitions in the late 1990s and founded Rockstar Games in 1998, which became its flagship label after the success of Grand Theft Auto III (2001). The company founded the 2K label in 2005 after acquiring Visual Concepts and the rights to ESPN-branded sports games, and survived an accounting scandal and management upheaval in the mid-2000s; investor Strauss Zelnick's group took control in 2007, and Zelnick has led the company since. In 2008 Take-Two rejected a hostile takeover bid from Electronic Arts. Its acquisition of mobile publisher Zynga for about $12.7 billion, completed in May 2022, was among the largest deals in industry history. Grand Theft Auto V (2013) became one of the best-selling games of all time, and anticipation for Grand Theft Auto VI has repeatedly moved the company's share price.

Structure

Take-Two operates largely decentralised labels: Rockstar Games (with studios including Rockstar North), 2K (Firaxis, Gearbox, Visual Concepts, Hangar 13 and others), Zynga for mobile and social games, Private Division for independent titles, and Ghost Story Games, the studio led by Ken Levine. The company is listed on NASDAQ and is a component of the S&P 500.
