The Tennessee Titans are a professional American football team based in Nashville, Tennessee. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) South division. The franchise was founded in 1960 by Bud Adams as the Houston Oilers, a charter member of the American Football League (AFL), and won the first two AFL championships in 1960 and 1961.

History

After decades in Houston — including the "Luv Ya Blue" era under coach Bum Phillips and running back Earl Campbell, and the prolific run-and-shoot teams led by quarterback Warren Moon — Adams moved the team to Tennessee in 1997 after failing to secure a new stadium in Houston. The club played one season in Memphis and one at Vanderbilt Stadium in Nashville as the Tennessee Oilers, then became the Titans in 1999 when it moved into its new Nashville stadium.

The first season as the Titans produced the franchise's most famous moments: the "Music City Miracle", a last-second lateral-play kickoff return that beat the Buffalo Bills in the 1999 playoffs, and a run to Super Bowl XXXIV, where the Titans lost 23–16 to the St. Louis Rams, with receiver Kevin Dyson tackled one yard short of the goal line on the final play — an ending known simply as "The Tackle" or "One Yard Short".

Later years

Under head coach Jeff Fisher and quarterback Steve McNair — co-MVP of the NFL in 2003 — the Titans were regular contenders in the early 2000s. Later eras featured running back Chris Johnson, one of only a handful of players to rush for 2,000 yards in a season (2009), and the Mike Vrabel-coached teams of 2018–2023 built around Derrick Henry, which reached the AFC Championship Game in the 2019 season and earned the AFC's top seed in 2021. The team retired the numbers of McNair and running back Eddie George, central figures of the 1999 Super Bowl run. The Titans' principal rivals include the Houston Texans, the successor franchise in their original city.
