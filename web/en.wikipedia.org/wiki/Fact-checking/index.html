Fact-checking

Fact-checking is the process of verifying the factual accuracy of claims in reporting, speeches, and other texts. It exists in two traditions: internal checking of manuscripts before publication, long practised by magazine research departments (famously at Time and The New Yorker), and external, post-publication checking of public claims by dedicated organisations.

Political fact-checking

Independent fact-checking organisations such as FactCheck.org (founded 2003), PolitiFact (2007, Pulitzer Prize winner), Snopes (specialising in urban legends and viral claims), Full Fact (UK), and agency desks at AFP, Reuters, and the Associated Press rate politicians' statements, often with verdict scales such as PolitiFact's Truth-O-Meter. The International Fact-Checking Network (IFCN), founded in 2015 at the Poynter Institute, maintains a code of principles and verifies signatories.

Effectiveness and criticism

Research generally finds corrections reduce misperceptions, though effects vary and early fears of a universal "backfire effect" have largely not been replicated. Fact-checkers face criticism over selection bias in which claims are chosen, and platforms' partnerships with fact-checkers for labelling false social-media content have been politically contested; Meta ended its third-party fact-checking programme in the United States in 2025 in favour of community notes.
