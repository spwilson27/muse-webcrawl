January Events

The January Events (Lithuanian: Sausio įvykiai) were a series of violent confrontations between January 11 and 13, 1991, in which Soviet military forces attempted to suppress Lithuania's restoration of independence. Fourteen Lithuanian civilians were killed and hundreds injured, most of them unarmed demonstrators defending the Vilnius TV Tower and the Lithuanian Radio and Television headquarters.

Background

On March 11, 1990, Lithuania became the first Soviet republic to declare the restoration of its independence. Moscow responded with an economic blockade and mounting pressure. In January 1991 Soviet authorities attempted to stage a "national salvation committee" takeover, and paratroopers and KGB units seized buildings in Vilnius.

The attacks

On the night of January 12–13, Soviet tanks and troops stormed the TV tower and the radio and television building. Crowds of civilians surrounded the sites; fourteen people were killed, including one woman crushed by a tank. The parliament building, defended by volunteers and barricades, was not attacked. Similar seizures occurred in other Lithuanian towns.

Aftermath

International condemnation and the failure to topple the Lithuanian government strengthened the independence movement; Iceland soon recognised Lithuania, and after the failed August coup in Moscow the Soviet Union itself recognised Lithuanian independence in September 1991. January 13 is commemorated in Lithuania as the Day of the Defenders of Freedom.
