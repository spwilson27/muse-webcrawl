Digital revolution

Image: Devices of the digital age

The digital revolution, also known as the third industrial revolution, is the shift from mechanical and analogue electronic technology to digital electronics which began in the second half of the 20th century. It has been driven by the mass production and widespread adoption of digital logic circuits, and its derived technologies including the computer, digital cellular phone, and the Internet.

History

Key foundations were laid by the invention of the transistor (1947) and the integrated circuit (1958–59), followed by metal-oxide-semiconductor (MOS) technology and the microprocessor (1971). Personal computers spread in the late 1970s and 1980s, the World Wide Web was opened to the public in the early 1990s, and mobile phones and broadband access became mass-market in the 2000s. The smartphone era, beginning around 2007, put networked computing in billions of pockets.

Effects

The revolution transformed work, commerce, media, and social life: information became cheap to copy and transmit, industries from music to retail were restructured, and new firms and business models emerged around software and platforms. It also raised concerns about privacy, surveillance, misinformation, job displacement through automation, and the digital divide between connected and unconnected populations. The digital revolution underlies the "information age" and is frequently compared in scale to the earlier agricultural and industrial revolutions.
