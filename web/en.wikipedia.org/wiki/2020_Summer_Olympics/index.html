The 2020 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXXII Olympiad and branded as Tokyo 2020, were an international multi-sport event held from 23 July to 8 August 2021 in Tokyo, Japan. Originally scheduled for the summer of 2020, the Games were postponed by a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first postponement in Olympic history — but retained the "Tokyo 2020" name. Most events were held without spectators.

New sports

Five sports were added or returned: skateboarding, sport climbing, surfing and karate made their Olympic debuts, while baseball and softball returned for the first time since 2008. New mixed-gender events appeared in several sports.

Highlights

The United States topped the medal table with 39 golds and 113 total medals, narrowly ahead of China. Hosts Japan finished third with a record 27 gold medals. Notable performances included gymnast Simone Biles, who withdrew from several events to protect her mental health, prompting wide discussion; Italian sprinter Marcell Jacobs's surprise 100 metres gold; and world records in the men's and women's 400 metres hurdles by Karsten Warholm and Sydney McLaughlin.

Legacy

The Games cost roughly US$15 billion by official accounting, with no spectator ticket revenue. The medals were made from metals recycled from discarded consumer electronics, and the podiums from recycled plastic, reflecting the organisers' sustainability theme.
