Barangaroo

Barangaroo (died 1791) was a Cammeraygal woman of the Eora nation in the Sydney region of Australia, known for her strong-willed interactions with the British colonists of the First Fleet era. She was the second wife of Bennelong, a prominent Wangal man who acted as an intermediary between Aboriginal people and the settlers.

Life

Colonial accounts, particularly those of Watkin Tench and David Collins, describe Barangaroo as determined to maintain Aboriginal customs: she refused European clothing, objected to the flogging of a convict she was made to witness, and resisted Bennelong's accommodation with Governor Arthur Phillip. She gave birth to a daughter, Dilboong, and died in 1791, shortly after childbirth, at Sydney Cove.

Legacy

The Sydney waterfront precinct of Barangaroo, on the western edge of the central business district, is named in her honour, recognising her significance in the early contact history of the colony.
