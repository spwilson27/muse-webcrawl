Royal Military Canal

LocationKent and East Sussex, England
Length28 miles (45 km)
Built1804–1809
PurposeDefence against Napoleonic invasion

The Royal Military Canal is a canal running for 28 miles (45 km) between Seabrook near Folkestone and Cliff End near Hastings in south-east England. It was built between 1804 and 1809 as a defensive work against a feared invasion by Napoleon.

Construction

The canal was designed so that its towpath side formed a parapet and each reach bent in a zigzag, allowing muskets to enfilade attacking troops. It formed the third line of defence behind the sea and the Martello towers then being built along the coast. Garrison troops guarded it at regular intervals.

Later use

The invasion never came, and the canal had a limited commercial life carrying goods between Rye and Hythe. Today it is a scheduled monument and a haven for wildlife, bordered by the Royal Military Canal Path long-distance footpath, with boating, fishing and cycling along much of its length. It passes through the flat grazing marshes of Romney Marsh.
