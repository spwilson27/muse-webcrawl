French Grand Prix

The French Grand Prix at Circuit Paul Ricard.

The French Grand Prix (French: Grand Prix de France) is an auto race held as part of the Formula One World Championship. It is one of the oldest motor races in the world: the first Grand Prix, organised by the Automobile Club de France, was held in 1906 on public roads near Le Mans and won by Ferenc Szisz in a Renault.

History

The race was a fixture of the inaugural Formula One championship in 1950 and was held at circuits including Reims-Gueux, Rouen-Les-Essarts, Clermont-Ferrand, Dijon-Prenois, and Magny-Cours, which hosted the event from 1991 to 2008. After a ten-year absence, the Grand Prix returned in 2018 at the Circuit Paul Ricard near Le Castellet, where it was held until 2022. Michael Schumacher holds the record for most French Grand Prix victories, with eight; French driver Alain Prost won six.

Significance

The French race gave the world the very term "Grand Prix" (grand prize), and France is credited with pioneering organised motor racing in the 1890s and 1900s.
