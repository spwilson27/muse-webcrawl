Photochromic lens

A photochromic lens is an optical lens that darkens on exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation and returns to a clear state when the UV source is removed. Photochromic lenses are used in eyeglasses that adapt automatically to changing light conditions, acting as clear lenses indoors and as sunglasses outdoors. The best-known brand is Transitions, though many manufacturers offer photochromic products.

How it works

Photochromism is a reversible change of colour induced by light. In classical glass photochromic lenses, developed at Corning in the 1960s, silver halide crystals embedded in the glass darken as UV light reduces silver ions to metallic silver, and fade again in the absence of UV. Most modern plastic lenses instead use organic photochromic dyes — naphthopyrans and oxazines — applied in a coating or embedded in the lens material ("in-mass" technology); UV exposure changes the molecules' structure so that they absorb visible light.

Characteristics

Lenses typically take from seconds to about a minute to darken and several minutes to fade fully, and their behaviour depends on temperature — they darken more in cold weather and fade faster when warm. Because car windscreens block most UV, standard photochromic lenses darken little inside a vehicle; special products respond partly to visible light to address this. Photochromic lenses also provide UV protection for the eyes.
