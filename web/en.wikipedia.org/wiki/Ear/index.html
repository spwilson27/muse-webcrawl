Ear

The ear is the organ that enables hearing and, in mammals, balance. It is divided into the outer ear (pinna and ear canal collecting sound), the middle ear (tympanic membrane and the three ossicles — malleus, incus, stapes — that amplify vibrations), and the inner ear (the spiral cochlea converting sound to nerve signals, and the vestibular system of semicircular canals sensing head motion).

Ears are vulnerable to infection (otitis media/externa), damaging noise, age-related hearing loss and tinnitus. Animals' ears vary hugely: insects hear with legs or antennae, fish sense particle motion and use lateral lines, and owls' asymmetric ears locate prey in total darkness. The outer ear's shape also helps judge sound direction (elevation cues).
