Alydar (March 23, 1975 – November 15, 1990) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse and sire, best known for finishing second to Affirmed in all three legs of the 1978 American Triple Crown. Their rivalry is considered one of the greatest in racing history: the margins in the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes and Belmont Stakes were 1½ lengths, a neck, and a head respectively.

Racing career

Bred and owned by the legendary Calumet Farm and trained by John Veitch, Alydar was ridden in most of his major races by Jorge Velásquez. As a two-year-old in 1977 he beat Affirmed in the Great American Stakes and the Champagne Stakes, though Affirmed won their other meetings and the divisional championship. In the 1978 Belmont Stakes, Alydar and Affirmed raced side by side for the final seven furlongs in a duel often described as among the finest in the sport; Affirmed prevailed by a head.

Alydar went on to win the Travers Stakes (via the disqualification of Affirmed), the Whitney Handicap and the Arlington Classic. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1989.

Stud career and death

At stud at Calumet Farm, Alydar became a highly successful and valuable sire, fathering Alysheba, Easy Goer, Strike the Gold and Turkoman, and becoming a leading broodmare sire. In November 1990 he fractured a leg in his stall — the same leg he had injured earlier that year — and was euthanized the following day. The circumstances of his death, and a large insurance payout amid Calumet Farm's financial collapse, were later the subject of an FBI investigation and the book Broken Trail and related reporting; his death remains a famous controversy in the horse-racing world.
