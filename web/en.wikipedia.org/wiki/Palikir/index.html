Palikir is the capital of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM), an island nation in the western Pacific Ocean. It is located on the northwestern side of Pohnpei Island, within Sokehs Municipality, a few kilometres southwest of the island's main town and former colonial capital, Kolonia. With only several thousand inhabitants, Palikir is one of the smallest national capitals in the world.

History

Pohnpei passed through Spanish, German, and Japanese colonial rule and then came under United States administration as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands after the Second World War. When the Federated States of Micronesia entered into the Compact of Free Association with the United States and became self-governing in 1986, the federal government was initially based in Kolonia. A purpose-built capital complex at Palikir, financed largely with American assistance, was completed and occupied in 1989, housing the FSM Congress, the president's office, the supreme court, and federal departments in low-rise buildings set in landscaped grounds below the Sokehs Ridge.

Geography and climate

Palikir sits at the foot of forested volcanic hills near the island's north coast. Pohnpei is one of the wettest places on Earth, with annual rainfall in the island's interior measured in several metres; Palikir has a tropical rainforest climate with high temperatures and humidity year-round. The nearby Sokehs Ridge, a prominent mountain spine, was the scene of the 1910–11 Sokehs Rebellion against German rule. Offshore, the ancient artificial-islet city of Nan Madol lies on Pohnpei's eastern coast.

Government and life

As the seat of the FSM's federal institutions, Palikir hosts the national government, foreign embassies (including those of the United States, Australia, China, and Japan), and the main campus of the College of Micronesia-FSM. Most commerce and services remain in Kolonia, which also serves the island's airport and seaport; Palikir itself is primarily an administrative town of government offices and residential areas for officials.
