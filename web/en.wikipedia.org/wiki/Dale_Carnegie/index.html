Dale Carnegie

Born: Dale Carnagey, November 24, 1888, Maryville, Missouri, U.S.
Died: November 1, 1955 (aged 66), Queens, New York City
Occupation: Writer, lecturer
Notable work: How to Win Friends and Influence People

Dale Carnegie (born Dale Carnagey; November 24, 1888 – November 1, 1955) was an American writer and lecturer, and the developer of courses in self-improvement, salesmanship, corporate training, public speaking, and interpersonal skills. He is best known as the author of How to Win Friends and Influence People (1936), one of the best-selling self-help books of all time.

Early life

Born into poverty on a farm in Maryville, Missouri, Carnegie attended State Teachers College in Warrensburg and worked as a travelling salesman for Armour and Company before trying his hand at acting in New York. In 1912 he began teaching public speaking classes for the YMCA, which grew into his signature training courses.

Works

His other books include How to Stop Worrying and Start Living (1948), Lincoln the Unknown (1932) and The Quick and Easy Way to Effective Speaking. How to Win Friends and Influence People has sold tens of millions of copies and set out principles such as "don't criticize, condemn, or complain", "give honest and sincere appreciation", and "talk in terms of the other person's interests".

Legacy

The Dale Carnegie Training organization he founded continues to offer courses in more than 90 countries. Carnegie died of Hodgkin's disease in Forest Hills, New York, in 1955.
