Ditchling Beacon

Ditchling Beacon is the highest point on the South Downs in East Sussex, England, at 248 metres (814 feet) above sea level. It is a prominent landmark north of Brighton, within the South Downs National Park, and is topped by a trig point and the remains of an Iron Age hillfort.

History

The hill is crowned by a small Iron Age hillfort enclosing about 5.5 hectares. The site takes its modern name from its use as a beacon hill: warning beacons were lit on prominent hills along the south coast, notably at the time of the Spanish Armada in 1588 and during the Napoleonic Wars.

Access and recreation

The South Downs Way long-distance path passes over the beacon, and the Clayton to Offham Escarpment below it is a Site of Special Scientific Interest noted for chalk grassland wildlife. A small car park near the summit serves walkers, paragliders, and cyclists tackling the steep Ditchling Beacon road climb from the village of Ditchling below.
