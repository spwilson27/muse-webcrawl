Chief of mission

FieldDiplomacy
Typical holdersAmbassadors; chargés d'affaires
Legal basis (US)Foreign Service Act of 1980

In diplomacy, a chief of mission is the head of a diplomatic mission — usually an ambassador accredited to a host country — who is responsible for all activities of the sending state's government agencies in that country.

United States usage

Under the U.S. Foreign Service Act of 1980, the chief of mission has full responsibility for the direction, coordination, and supervision of all U.S. Government executive branch employees in the country, except those under a geographic combatant commander. A letter of instruction from the President formalizes this authority.

Deputy

The chief of mission is assisted by a deputy chief of mission (DCM), who acts as chargé d'affaires in the ambassador's absence.
