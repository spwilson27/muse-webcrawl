General assembly

[Image placeholder: Assembly hall]

A general assembly or general meeting is a meeting of all the members of an organisation or company, or of a deliberative body whose membership meets as a whole. The term is used by international organisations, legislatures, churches, and corporations to describe their principal plenary gathering or governing body.

Uses of the term

The best-known example is the United Nations General Assembly, the main deliberative and policymaking organ of the UN, comprising all 193 member states, each with one vote. Several U.S. states call their legislatures the General Assembly (for example, the Virginia General Assembly — the oldest continuously operating legislative body in the Americas, founded in 1619 — and those of Pennsylvania, Maryland, and Illinois). In the Presbyterian tradition, the General Assembly is the highest church court, and many denominations use the same term for their supreme governing conferences.

In corporate law, a company's general meeting — the annual general meeting (AGM) or an extraordinary general meeting (EGM) — is the occasion on which shareholders vote on directors, accounts, dividends, and major transactions. Quorum and proxy rules for such meetings are set by company law and the organisation's bylaws.
