El Lissitzky

Geometric abstraction in the spirit of Lissitzky's Proun compositions

Lazar Markovich Lissitzky (Russian: Лазарь Маркович Лисицкий; 23 November 1890 – 30 December 1941), better known as El Lissitzky, was a Russian artist, designer, photographer, typographer and architect. He was a leading figure of the Russian avant-garde and, with Kazimir Malevich, a developer of Suprematism; his work profoundly influenced the Bauhaus, De Stijl and modern graphic design.

Life and work

Born in Pochinok, Smolensk Governorate, Lissitzky studied engineering in Darmstadt before returning to Russia during the First World War. After the 1917 Revolution he taught with Marc Chagall in Vitebsk, where he fell under Malevich's influence. His propaganda poster Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge (1919–20) became an icon of revolutionary art. He called his mature paintings Proun ("Project for the Affirmation of the New"), describing them as "the interchange station from painting to architecture".

As a cultural ambassador in Germany in the 1920s he collaborated with Kurt Schwitters, László Moholy-Nagy and Theo van Doesburg, helped organise the 1922 Düsseldorf Congress of International Progressive Artists, and designed landmark exhibitions such as the Pressa pavilion in Cologne (1928), pioneering photomontage and immersive display design. His utopian architectural project the Wolkenbügel ("cloud iron") was never built. Returning to the USSR, he produced state propaganda, books and magazines until his death in Moscow in 1941.
