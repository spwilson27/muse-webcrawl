Periodontal disease, also known as gum disease, is a set of inflammatory conditions affecting the tissues surrounding the teeth (the periodontium: gingiva, periodontal ligament, cementum and alveolar bone). In its early stage, called gingivitis, the gums become swollen and red and may bleed; it is reversible with good oral hygiene. In its more serious form, called periodontitis, the gums can pull away from the tooth, supporting bone can be lost, and the teeth may loosen or fall out. Bad breath may also occur. Periodontitis is the leading cause of tooth loss in adults worldwide.

Causes and risk factors

Periodontal disease is generally due to bacteria in the mouth infecting the tissue around the teeth. Dental plaque — a biofilm that accumulates on teeth — triggers the inflammatory response; mineralised plaque (calculus/tartar) sustains it. Factors that increase the risk of disease include smoking (the strongest modifiable risk factor), diabetes mellitus, poor oral hygiene, heredity, stress, and certain medications that reduce saliva flow. Specific bacteria such as Porphyromonas gingivalis, Tannerella forsythia and Treponema denticola are strongly associated with destructive disease.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis involves inspecting the soft gum tissues and measuring the depth of the pockets around teeth with a periodontal probe, together with dental X-rays to assess alveolar bone loss, which is used for staging and grading in the 2017 classification of periodontal diseases. Treatment of gingivitis and early periodontitis centres on professional cleaning — scaling and root planing — and meticulous home plaque control. Advanced cases may require periodontal surgery, regenerative procedures or antibiotics. Severe periodontitis affects roughly 10–15% of adults globally, and associations have been reported with cardiovascular disease, adverse pregnancy outcomes and poor glycaemic control.

Prevention

  • Twice-daily tooth brushing and interdental cleaning
  • Regular professional cleaning and check-ups
  • Smoking cessation and good diabetes control
