Alpha-adrenergic receptor

The alpha-adrenergic receptors (or adrenoceptors) are a class of G protein-coupled receptors that are targets of the catecholamines, especially norepinephrine (noradrenaline) and epinephrine (adrenaline). They are divided into two main groups, α1 and α2, each with three subtypes, and play central roles in the regulation of blood pressure, smooth muscle contraction, and neurotransmitter release in the sympathetic nervous system.


Schematic of a G protein-coupled receptor

Classification

Raymond Ahlquist's 1948 classification split adrenergic responses into alpha and beta types. Molecular cloning later identified the subtypes:

  • α1 receptors (α1A, α1B, α1D) couple to Gq proteins, activating phospholipase C and raising intracellular calcium. They are located mainly on vascular smooth muscle, where they cause vasoconstriction, and on the radial muscle of the iris, the bladder neck and the prostate.
  • α2 receptors (α2A, α2B, α2C) couple to Gi proteins, inhibiting adenylyl cyclase. Presynaptic α2 receptors provide negative feedback that reduces norepinephrine release; they are also found in the brainstem, pancreas and platelets.

Physiological roles

Stimulation of α1 receptors constricts blood vessels and raises blood pressure, dilates the pupil (mydriasis), contracts the pilomotor muscles producing "goose bumps", and promotes glycogenolysis. α2 stimulation in the central nervous system reduces sympathetic outflow, producing sedation and lowering blood pressure, while peripheral α2 activation inhibits insulin secretion and promotes platelet aggregation.

Pharmacology

Agonists. Phenylephrine (α1) is used as a decongestant and pressor agent; oxymetazoline and xylometazoline are topical α agonists used in nasal sprays. Clonidine and dexmedetomidine are centrally acting α2 agonists used in hypertension, anaesthesia and sedation.

Antagonists (alpha blockers). Prazosin, doxazosin and terazosin block α1 receptors and are used in hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia; tamsulosin is relatively selective for the α1A subtype in the prostate. Non-selective blockers such as phentolamine and phenoxybenzamine are used in the management of phaeochromocytoma. Yohimbine is a prototypical α2 antagonist.
