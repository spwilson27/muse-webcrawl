Henri du Vergier, comte de La Rochejaquelein
Born: 30 August 1772, Mauléon
Died: 28 January 1794, Noyant (aged 21)
Rank: Generalissimo of the Catholic and Royal Army

Henri du Vergier, comte de La Rochejaquelein (30 August 1772 – 28 January 1794) was the youngest general of the royalist Catholic and Royal Army during the War in the Vendée, the counter-revolutionary uprising against the French Republic in western France. Elected commander at 20, he became famous for his personal bravery and for his reputed words to his men: "Mes amis, si j'avance, suivez-moi; si je recule, tuez-moi; si je meurs, vengez-moi" ("My friends, if I advance, follow me; if I retreat, kill me; if I die, avenge me").

Life and campaigns

Born at the Château de la Durbelière near Mauléon (Deux-Sèvres), La Rochejaquelein served briefly in the Constitutional Guard of Louis XVI and defended the Tuileries Palace on 10 August 1792. When the Vendée rose in March 1793, local peasants called on him to lead them; after an initial victory at Aubiers, he joined the Catholic and Royal Army, fighting alongside Bonchamps, d'Elbée, Cathelineau and Stofflet in the capture of Bressuire, Thouars, Fontenay-le-Comte and Saumur, and in the attacks on Nantes and Luçon.

Generalissimo and death

After the death of d'Elbée's effective leadership and the disaster at Cholet (October 1793), La Rochejaquelein, aged 21, was elected generalissimo for the Virée de Galerne, the march across the Loire that won at Laval and Entrammes but failed before Granville and was destroyed at Le Mans and Savenay. Returning to the Vendée to continue guerrilla resistance, he was shot dead by a Republican soldier near Noyant on 28 January 1794 while parleying or hunting, accounts differing. His daring generalship made him the emblematic hero of the Vendéen cause; his widow, Victoire de Donnissan, wrote celebrated memoirs of the war.
