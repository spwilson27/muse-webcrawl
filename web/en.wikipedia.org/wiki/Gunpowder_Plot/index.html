The Gunpowder Plot of 1605 was a failed attempt by a group of English Catholics, led by Robert Catesby, to assassinate King James I by blowing up the House of Lords during the State Opening of Parliament on 5 November 1605. The conspirators hoped to end the persecution of Catholics and install James's daughter Elizabeth as a Catholic monarch.

The plot

Thirteen conspirators, including Guy Fawkes—a soldier with experience in the Spanish Netherlands—rented an undercroft beneath the House of Lords and stored 36 barrels of gunpowder there. Fawkes was to light the fuse and escape across the Thames. An anonymous letter to Lord Monteagle on 26 October warned him to stay away; the letter reached the king, and searches of the cellars in the early hours of 5 November found Fawkes guarding the barrels.

Aftermath

Fawkes was tortured on the rack and revealed the plot. The surviving conspirators fled to the Midlands; Catesby and three others were killed at Holbeche House on 8 November. The remaining eight were tried in January 1606 and executed by hanging, drawing, and quartering. The King James Version translation project, begun the previous year, proceeded amid heightened anti-Catholic sentiment.

Legacy

Parliament passed the Thanksgiving Act, mandating annual commemoration on 5 November. Bonfire Night (Guy Fawkes Night) is still celebrated in the United Kingdom with fireworks and effigies, accompanied by the rhyme "Remember, remember, the fifth of November." Before each State Opening, the Yeomen of the Guard ceremonially search the cellars of Parliament—a ritual that continues today. Debates of the era over authority and resistance echoed later in the clashes between Crown and Parliament that animated the Levellers and the Putney Debates of the 1640s.
