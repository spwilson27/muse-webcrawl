Microcytic anemia

Microcytic anaemia (American spelling: microcytic anemia) is a class of anaemia in which red blood cells are abnormally small, as measured by the mean corpuscular volume (MCV), typically below 80 femtolitres. The microcytes are often also hypochromic, containing less haemoglobin than normal and appearing pale on a blood smear.

Causes

Microcytosis results from defective haemoglobin synthesis. The principal causes, summarized by the mnemonic TAILS, are:

  • Thalassaemia — inherited defects in globin chain production.
  • Anaemia of chronic disease (in some cases).
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia — the most common cause worldwide, from blood loss, poor intake, or malabsorption.
  • Lead poisoning — lead inhibits enzymes of haem synthesis.
  • Sideroblastic anaemia — defective haem synthesis with ringed sideroblasts in the marrow.

Diagnosis

Evaluation begins with a complete blood count showing a low MCV and low haemoglobin. Iron studies — serum ferritin, serum iron, total iron-binding capacity, and transferrin saturation — distinguish iron deficiency (low ferritin) from thalassaemia trait (often a disproportionately high red cell count with mild anaemia) and from anaemia of chronic disease. Haemoglobin electrophoresis is used to diagnose thalassaemias and other haemoglobinopathies.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: iron replacement and investigation of blood loss for iron deficiency; chelation for lead poisoning; and supportive care, transfusions, or disease-specific therapy for thalassaemia and sideroblastic anaemia.
