Warrnambool

Warrnambool is a city on the south-western coast of Victoria, Australia, at the western end of the Great Ocean Road. It is the largest city in the region and a significant regional centre with a population of roughly 35,000.

History

The area is the traditional country of the Gunditjmara people, whose aquaculture system at Budj Bim is World Heritage listed. European settlement began in the 1840s around Lady Bay, and Warrnambool grew as a port and agricultural service town for the surrounding dairy country. The name is believed to derive from an Aboriginal word for a local volcanic cone.

Attractions

Flagstaff Hill Maritime Museum preserves the town's seafaring history, including the wreck-strewn "Shipwreck Coast" offshore. Southern right whales calve at Logan's Beach each winter, and nearby Tower Hill is a wildlife reserve inside an extinct volcano. The city is also known for the Grand Annual Sprintcar Classic and its May Racing Carnival steeplechase meeting.
