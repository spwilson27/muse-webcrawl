Administration (law)

In the law of several Commonwealth jurisdictions, including England and Wales, Australia and New Zealand, administration is a procedure under insolvency law by which a company that is, or is likely to become, unable to pay its debts is placed under the control of an insolvency practitioner (the administrator), who manages its affairs, business and property with the aim of rescuing the company, achieving a better result for creditors than an immediate liquidation, or realising property to distribute to secured or preferential creditors.

United Kingdom

Under Schedule B1 to the Insolvency Act 1986 (inserted by the Enterprise Act 2002), an administrator may be appointed by the court, by the holder of a qualifying floating charge, or out of court by the company or its directors. On appointment a statutory moratorium prevents creditors from enforcing security or starting legal proceedings without permission. The administrator must perform their functions "in the interests of the company's creditors as a whole" and as quickly and efficiently as reasonably practicable. Administration ordinarily lasts one year, extendable by consent or court order, and may end in rescue, a company voluntary arrangement, or liquidation.

Other jurisdictions

Australian "voluntary administration" under Part 5.3A of the Corporations Act 2001 is a related but distinct process, designed to maximise the chance of a company surviving or to produce a better return for creditors through a deed of company arrangement. Similar rescue-oriented procedures exist in other Commonwealth countries, influenced by the UK model.
