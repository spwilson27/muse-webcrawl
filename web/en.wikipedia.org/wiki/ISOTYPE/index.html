Isotype (picture language)

Isotype (International System of Typographic Picture Education) is a method of showing social, technological, biological, and historical connections in pictorial form. It was developed in Vienna in the 1920s and 1930s by the Austrian sociologist and philosopher Otto Neurath, working with Marie Neurath and the artist Gerd Arntz, first as the "Vienna Method of Pictorial Statistics".

Principles

In an Isotype chart, quantities are represented not by varying the size of a symbol but by repeating a standard symbol a corresponding number of times—"a larger number by a larger row, never by a larger picture". Gerd Arntz designed roughly 4,000 pictograms in a clean, modular style. Marie Neurath acted as "transformer", mediating between data specialists and graphic designers—a role now recognised as an early model of information design practice.

Influence

After the rise of Nazism the team relocated to The Hague and then to England, where the Isotype Institute produced books and wartime information materials. Isotype profoundly influenced modern pictograms, infographics, signage systems (such as Olympic and airport symbols), and the pictorial-unit charts still used in data journalism.
