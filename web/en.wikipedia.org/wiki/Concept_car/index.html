Concept car

A concept car (also known as a concept vehicle, show vehicle, or prototype) is a car made to showcase new styling and/or new technology. They are often shown at motor shows to gauge customer reaction to new and radical designs which may or may not be mass-produced.

History

General Motors designer Harley Earl is credited with inventing the concept car, through his Buick Y-Job project of 1938, the first purpose-built show car. Manufacturers subsequently used concept cars to explore styling themes, preview future production models, and generate publicity. Famous examples include the Lincoln Futura (later adapted as television's Batmobile), the Chevrolet Volt concept that led directly to a production electric-drive vehicle, and numerous futuristic designs by European coachbuilders.

Design

Concept cars often exaggerate: they may feature unconventional doors, exotic materials, advanced powertrains, and interiors unconstrained by cost or safety regulations. Many are static mock-ups or use modified production underpinnings. Designs are frequently toned down before reaching production because of manufacturing, regulatory, and cost constraints.

Purpose

Beyond public display, concept cars serve as brand statements, design-direction signals to suppliers and investors, and testbeds for technologies such as autonomous driving, electric propulsion, and new human–machine interfaces.
