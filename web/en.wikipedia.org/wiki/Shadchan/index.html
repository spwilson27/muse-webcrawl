Shadchan

A shadchan (Hebrew: שַׁדְּכָן, pl. shadchanim; feminine shadchanit) is a Jewish matchmaker who assists single people in finding a spouse. The proposed match arranged by a shadchan is called a shidduch (pl. shidduchim). The institution is rooted in rabbinic tradition: the Talmud describes God himself as occupied with making matches, and matchmaking came to be viewed as a sacred occupation, akin to the work of Eliezer, Abraham's servant, who found a wife for Isaac.

Role in Orthodox communities

Shadchanim play a particularly important role in Orthodox and especially Haredi communities, where social contact between unmarried men and women is limited and dating is undertaken primarily with marriage in view. A shadchan typically gathers detailed information about candidates — family background (yichus), religious observance, education, health, character and personal goals — often on a standardized résumé-like form. When a potential match looks promising, the shadchan proposes the idea to both families, who may make inquiries (redd shidduchim investigations) before the couple meets.

If the parties agree, the couple meets — traditionally first in a public or semi-formal setting — for a series of dates whose purpose is to determine compatibility. If they decide to marry, a celebration (l'chaim or vort) announces the engagement. It is customary for the shadchan to receive a fee (shadchanus) from both families upon a successful engagement, the amount varying widely by community.

History

Professional matchmakers were a fixture of Jewish life in Eastern Europe for centuries. The shadchan appears frequently in Yiddish literature and theatre, sometimes sympathetically and sometimes as a comic figure exaggerating the virtues of his clients. The character of Yente in Fiddler on the Roof is the best-known fictional shadchan. In the modern era the role has expanded to include dating services, singles events, and websites and apps, though traditional shadchanim remain central in insular communities.
