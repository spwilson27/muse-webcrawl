Basil Fawlty

Basil Fawlty is a fictional character portrayed by John Cleese in the British television sitcom Fawlty Towers (1975–1979). He is the snobbish, irascible and put-upon owner of a hotel in Torquay, Devon, and is routinely voted among the greatest British television characters of all time.

Character

Basil is a socially ambitious but perpetually frustrated man whose attempts to elevate his hotel's class are sabotaged by difficult guests, his own temper, and his sharp-tongued wife Sybil, whose ring of "Basil!" punctuates his schemes. His abuse falls especially heavily on Manuel, the hapless Spanish waiter "from Barcelona," whose poor English triggers Basil's catchphrase apology "He's from Barcelona."

Cleese based the character on Donald Sinclair, a real Torquay hotelier whose behaviour had astonished the Monty Python team when they stayed at his hotel in 1970. Famous episodes featuring Basil include "The Germans," in which a concussed Basil repeatedly violates his own instruction "Don't mention the war," culminating in his goose-stepping impersonation of Hitler.

Legacy

Despite appearing in only twelve episodes, Basil Fawlty became one of British comedy's most enduring figures, and the "Fawlty" manner remains shorthand for catastrophically rude customer service.
