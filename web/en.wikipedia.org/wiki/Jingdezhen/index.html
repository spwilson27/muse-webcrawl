Jingdezhen has produced porcelain for more than a thousand years.

Jingdezhen (Chinese: 景德镇; pinyin: Jǐngdézhèn) is a prefecture-level city in northeastern Jiangxi province, China, with a total population of roughly 1.6 million. It is known as the "Porcelain Capital" because it has been the centre of Chinese ceramic production for more than 1,000 years, and its wares — especially blue-and-white porcelain — were exported around the world.

History

Pottery was made in the area as early as the Han dynasty. The town received its present name in 1004, from the reign title Jingde of the Emperor Zhenzong of Song, whose court ordered porcelain marked with the era name. Under the Yuan dynasty the imperial kilns were established at Zhushan, and in the Ming and Qing dynasties Jingdezhen supplied the imperial court while also mass-producing wares for domestic and foreign markets. Blue-and-white porcelain from Jingdezhen reached Europe aboard Portuguese, Dutch, and British East India Company ships and strongly influenced European ceramics, including Delftware.

The industry depended on local deposits of porcelain stone and nearby kaolin from Gaoling village — the source of the word "kaolin" itself. Production was organised through an elaborate division of labour among throwers, painters, glazers, and kiln workers.

Modern city

Today Jingdezhen combines large-scale industrial ceramics with a thriving community of studio potters and artists. Its imperial kiln sites and museums, including the Jingdezhen China Ceramics Museum, attract visitors and researchers, and former factory districts such as Taoxichuan have been redeveloped as creative quarters.
