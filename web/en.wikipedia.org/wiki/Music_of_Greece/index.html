Music of Greece

The music of Greece is highly diverse, reflecting the country's position between Europe and Asia Minor and its long history. Greek musical traditions include ancient and Byzantine chant, regional folk styles, and the urban popular genres of the 20th century, notably rebetiko and laïko.

History

Music was central to ancient Greek culture: the aulos and lyre accompanied poetry, drama and ritual, and Greek theorists developed systems of modes (harmoniai) and mathematics of pitch that influenced all later Western theory. Byzantine chant, the monophonic liturgical music of the Greek Orthodox Church, preserved and developed these traditions through the medieval period.

Folk music

Regional folk styles (dhimotiká) vary widely: mainland dances such as the syrtos and kalamatianos in asymmetric and 7/8 rhythms, Epirot polyphonic singing (inscribed by UNESCO), Cretan music centred on the lyra and laouto, and island styles such as the violin-led music of the Aegean and Ionian kantádhes serenades.

Rebetiko and popular music

Rebetiko, the "Greek urban blues", emerged in the hashish dens and prisons of Piraeus and Thessaloniki in the 1920s–30s among refugees from Asia Minor, centred on the bouzouki and singers such as Markos Vamvakaris and Vassilis Tsitsanis. After the war it fed into mainstream laïko song, while composers Mikis Theodorakis and Manos Hatzidakis created éntekhno ("art song"), setting poetry by Greek Nobel laureates to music. Nana Mouskouri, Demis Roussos and Vangelis achieved international fame, and Greece won the Eurovision Song Contest in 2005 with Helena Paparizou's "My Number One".
