Picture superiority effect

The picture superiority effect is a phenomenon in cognitive psychology in which concepts presented as pictures are more likely to be remembered than the same concepts presented as words. Studies since the 1960s and 1970s, notably by Roger Shepard and Lionel Standing, showed remarkably high recognition rates for large sets of previously seen pictures.

Explanations

The most influential explanation is Allan Paivio's dual-coding theory: pictures are encoded both as an image code and, through labelling, as a verbal code, giving two retrieval routes, whereas words are usually encoded only verbally. Pictures are also often more distinctive and are processed more deeply at a perceptual level.

Applications

The effect has practical implications for education, advertising, and user-interface design, where combining images with text generally improves recall of the material. The advantage persists, though reduced, in older adults and is studied in relation to memory disorders.
