Gateway Arch

Height630 ft (192 m)

The Gateway Arch is a 630-foot (192 m) stainless-steel monument in St. Louis, Missouri, United States, the centrepiece of Gateway Arch National Park. Designed by Finnish-American architect Eero Saarinen, it is the world's tallest arch and the tallest man-made monument in the United States. Built as a memorial to the westward expansion of the United States, it symbolises St. Louis's role as the "Gateway to the West".

Design

Saarinen won the 1947 design competition for the Jefferson National Expansion Memorial with a soaring catenary arch. The curve is a weighted catenary — the shape a chain takes under its own weight, adjusted so that the legs carry greater load — with an equilateral-triangle cross-section tapering from 54 feet wide at the base to 17 feet at the top. The structure is built of steel and concrete sections clad in stainless steel panels. Tragically, Saarinen died in 1961, before construction began.

Construction

Construction ran from February 1963 to October 1965; the two legs, built simultaneously with creeper derricks, were joined by the final keystone section on 28 October 1965. The monument opened to the public in July 1967 after completion of its unique tram system, in which small five-seat capsules carry visitors up the hollow legs to an observation deck at the top.

The park

The surrounding memorial, designated a national park in 2018 and renamed Gateway Arch National Park, includes the Old Courthouse — where the Dred Scott case was first tried — and an underground museum beneath the Arch, extensively renovated in 2018, telling the story of westward expansion and Native American history.
