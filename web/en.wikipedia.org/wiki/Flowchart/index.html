Flowchart

A flowchart is a type of diagram that represents a workflow or process, showing steps as boxes of various kinds connected by arrows, commonly used in analysing, designing, documenting, or managing a process or programme.

History

Flow process charts were introduced in the 1920s by industrial engineers Frank and Lillian Gilbreth, and flowcharts became standard for computer programming in the mid-20th century.

Standard symbols were formalised by ANSI and ISO, including the terminator, process, decision (diamond), input/output (parallelogram), and document shapes.

Use

Decision diamonds pose yes/no questions, arrows indicate flow of control, and the diagram ends at a terminator symbol.

Though high-level programming languages reduced their role in coding, flowcharts remain common in business process modelling and education.
