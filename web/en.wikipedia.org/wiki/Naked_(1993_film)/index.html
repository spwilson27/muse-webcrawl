Naked (1993 film)

Naked is a 1993 British black comedy-drama film written and directed by Mike Leigh. Starring David Thewlis in a career-defining performance, the film follows Johnny, a homeless, unemployed drifter who flees Manchester for London after committing a sexual assault, and spends several nights wandering the city engaging strangers in intense, often philosophical conversations.

Plot

Johnny (David Thewlis), a disaffected young man from Manchester, flees to London and turns up at the flat of his ex-girlfriend Louise (Lesley Sharp). While Louise is at work he seduces her flatmate Sophie (Katrin Cartlidge), then leaves to roam the streets. Over a series of nocturnal encounters — with an evangelical poster-biller, a night security guard named Brian, a homeless Scottish couple, and a waitress — Johnny delivers articulate, nihilistic monologues on religion, the apocalypse, and the futility of modern life. Meanwhile, the flat is visited by the women's landlord, a brutal yuppie credited as "Jeremy G. Smart" (also known as Sebastian), whose predatory behaviour parallels Johnny's. Beaten and robbed, Johnny returns to the flat; the film ends ambiguously, with Johnny hobbling away into the street despite the women's pleas for him to seek help.

Reception

The film premiered at the 1993 Cannes Film Festival, where Leigh won the Best Director award and Thewlis won Best Actor. Critics praised Thewlis's performance as one of the finest of the decade, though the film's bleakness and its treatment of female characters divided opinion. Naked is frequently listed among the greatest British films of all time and was selected for preservation in critical polls of 1990s cinema.

Themes

The film is noted for its improvised development process — Leigh and his cast built characters and dialogue through months of rehearsal — and for Johnny's erudite, conspiratorial monologues, which touch on the Book of Revelation, chaos theory, and the approaching millennium.
