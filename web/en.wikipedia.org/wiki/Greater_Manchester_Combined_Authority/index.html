The Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA) is the strategic local government body for Greater Manchester, England, established on 1 April 2011 as the first combined authority in England. It coordinates the ten metropolitan borough councils of Greater Manchester (Bolton, Bury, Manchester, Oldham, Rochdale, Salford, Stockport, Tameside, Trafford, and Wigan) on economic development, regeneration, and transport, and since the 2014 "Devo Manc" agreement has accumulated further devolved powers over skills, housing, health and social care integration, policing and fire (via the mayor), and franchised bus services.

Structure and the mayor

The GMCA is chaired by the directly elected Mayor of Greater Manchester, first elected in May 2017, when Andy Burnham won the inaugural contest; he was re-elected in 2021 and 2024. Its voting members are the mayor and the ten borough leaders. Transport for Greater Manchester (TfGM) is its transport executive, responsible for the Metrolink tram network and, from 2023–25, the phased introduction of bus franchising under the "Bee Network" brand — the first franchised bus system outside London under the Bus Services Act 2017.

Devolution deals

Successive devolution deals with the UK government (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 and the 2023 "trailblazer" deal) gave Greater Manchester control over skills budgets, an investment fund, strategic planning (the "Places for Everyone" plan), and greater fiscal flexibilities, and made it the national test-bed for English devolution. The GMCA also hosts the Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service accountability (through the mayor as police and crime commissioner equivalent functions) and the region's health and social care partnership.
