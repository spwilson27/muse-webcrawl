Dombey and Son is a novel by Charles Dickens, published in monthly parts between October 1846 and April 1848. Its full title is Dealings with the Firm of Dombey and Son: Wholesale, Retail and for Exportation. The novel is a study of pride and its consequences, structured around the themes of business, family and the new railway age.

Plot

Paul Dombey, a wealthy shipping magnate, longs for a son to inherit his firm; his wife dies giving birth to a frail boy, also Paul, while his devoted daughter Florence is neglected as worthless to the business. Little Paul dies in childhood, devastating his father. Dombey's second marriage, to the proud Edith Granger, collapses in hatred; his villainous manager Carker ruins the firm and elopes with Edith. Bankrupted and broken, Dombey is finally reconciled with Florence, whose steadfast love redeems him.

Themes and reception

The novel is noted for its early and powerful depiction of the railways as agents of change and death, and for characters such as Captain Cuttle and the irrepressible Major Bagstock. Dickens planned the novel more carefully than his earlier works, and it is regarded as a bridge to the mature novels of his middle period.
