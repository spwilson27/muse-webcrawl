Byron Bancroft "Ban" Johnson (January 5, 1874 – March 28, 1931) was an American executive in professional baseball who founded the American League and served as its first president from 1900 to 1927. Johnson transformed a minor league, the Western League, into a major league that challenged the established National League and helped create the modern structure of Major League Baseball, including the World Series.

Early life

Johnson was born in Norwalk, Ohio, and worked as a sportswriter for the Cincinnati Commercial-Gazette, where he befriended Reds manager Charles Comiskey. In 1893 he became president of the Western League, a struggling minor league, and set about cleaning up the game's rowdy image by supporting umpires and fining abusive players.

American League

In 1900 Johnson renamed his circuit the American League and in 1901 declared it a major league, placing teams in large eastern cities and raiding National League rosters with higher salaries. The resulting "baseball war" ended with the National Agreement of 1903, which recognised the two leagues as equals and established the World Series. Johnson dominated the American League for two decades, but his authority eroded after the appointment of Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis following the Black Sox Scandal of 1919. He retired in 1927.

Legacy

Johnson was elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame by the Veterans Committee in 1937, among its earliest inductees.
