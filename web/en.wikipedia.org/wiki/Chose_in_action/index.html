Chose in action

A chose in action, also known as a thing in action, is a term in English common law for a type of intangible personal property: a right to sue for or recover something — such as a debt, damages, or money — that can only be obtained through legal action, as opposed to a chose in possession, which can be physically held.

Meaning

The phrase derives from French chose, meaning "thing". Examples include debts owed, shares in a company, intellectual property rights, insurance policy benefits, and rights under a contract. At early common law choses in action were generally not assignable, but equity and later statute (notably the Judicature Act 1873 in England) permitted assignment, subject to formalities.

Modern law

Today choses in action are central to commercial law: receivables can be sold or used as security, and statutory assignment allows creditors to transfer debts. They are divided into legal choses (enforceable at common law) and equitable choses (enforceable in equity).
