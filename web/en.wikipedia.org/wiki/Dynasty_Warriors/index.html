Dynasty Warriors

Genre: Hack and slash
Developer: Omega Force
Publisher: Koei (now Koei Tecmo)
First release: 1997

Dynasty Warriors (真・三國無双, Shin Sangokumusō in Japan) is a series of Japanese hack and slash action video games created by Koei and developed by Omega Force. The series is a spin-off of Koei's turn-based strategy Romance of the Three Kingdoms series, itself loosely based on the Chinese historical text Records of the Three Kingdoms and the 14th-century novel of the same name.

The first game in the series, titled Dynasty Warriors in English and Sangokumusō in Japanese, is a fighting game, a genre different from the rest of the series. All English titles are a numeral ahead of their Japanese counterparts due to the English localisation counting the fighting game as the first in the series. The series formula was established by Dynasty Warriors 2 (2000), in which a single hero battles through enormous battlefields against hundreds of enemy soldiers — the "one warrior versus a thousand" (ichiki tōsen) gameplay that became the template for the wider "Warriors" (Musō) franchise, including Samurai Warriors, Warriors Orochi, and licensed titles such as Hyrule Warriors and Fire Emblem Warriors.

Gameplay

Players take control of an officer from the Three Kingdoms period — serving the kingdoms of Wei, Wu, Shu, or Jin, or unaffiliated "Other" forces — and fight through stages retelling battles such as the Yellow Turban Rebellion, Hu Lao Gate, Chibi, and Wuzhang Plains. Combat centres on chaining normal and charge attacks, building a gauge to unleash a devastating Musou attack, and managing the flow of battle by defeating enemy officers and completing objectives. Characters gain experience, weapons, and items across battles.

Main series

Main numbered entries run from the original fighting game (1997) through Dynasty Warriors 2 (2000), 3 (2001), 4 (2003), 5 (2005), 6 (2008), 7 (2011), 8 (2013), and Dynasty Warriors 9 (2018), which introduced an open-world structure to mixed reception. Dynasty Warriors: Origins (2025) rebooted the formula with a single nameless protagonist and was widely praised. Most entries received expanded Xtreme Legends and Empires spin-off editions, the latter adding strategy elements.

Reception and legacy

The series has sold over 21 million copies worldwide and defined the large-scale crowd-combat genre. Reviewers have praised the power fantasy of cutting through armies and the series' dramatized retelling of Three Kingdoms history, while criticising repetition and technical shortcomings in some entries. Its character designs and rock soundtrack are signature elements.
