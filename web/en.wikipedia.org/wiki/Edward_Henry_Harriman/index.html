Edward Henry Harriman (February 20, 1848 – September 9, 1909) was an American financier and railroad executive. Beginning as a Wall Street broker, he rebuilt the bankrupt Union Pacific Railroad after 1897 into one of the most profitable transportation systems in the world.

Railroad empire

Harriman gained control of the Southern Pacific Railroad and engaged in a famous 1901 battle with James J. Hill for control of the Northern Pacific, which triggered a stock market panic and led to the Northern Securities holding company, later dissolved by the Supreme Court as violating the Sherman Antitrust Act.

Other activities

In 1899 he funded the Harriman Alaska Expedition, a major scientific voyage. His widow Mary Averell Harriman used the family fortune for philanthropy, including funding the development of Harriman State Park in New York.
