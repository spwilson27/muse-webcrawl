Council of Clermont

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Council of Clermont was a synod of bishops and lay lords held in November 1095 at Clermont, in the Kingdom of France, under Pope Urban II. It is famous for the speech Urban delivered on 27 November 1095, in response to an appeal for military aid from the Byzantine emperor Alexios I Komnenos, calling on Western Christians to take up arms and liberate the Holy Land from Muslim rule — the appeal that launched the First Crusade.

Proceedings

The council also enacted reforming decrees concerning simony, clerical marriage, and the Peace and Truce of God. Urban's sermon, delivered in the open air because of the size of the crowd, is reported in several chronicle versions; according to tradition the audience answered with the cry "Deus vult!" ("God wills it!"). Those who took the vow sewed crosses of cloth to their garments, becoming known as crusaders.

Consequences

The response far exceeded expectations: waves of knights and commoners set out in 1096, culminating in the capture of Jerusalem in July 1099.
