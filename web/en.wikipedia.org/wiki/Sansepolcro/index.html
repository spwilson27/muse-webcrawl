Sansepolcro

Country: Italy
Region: Tuscany
Province: Arezzo (AR)
Population: about 16,000

Sansepolcro, formerly Borgo Santo Sepolcro, is a town and comune founded in the 11th century, located in the Italian Province of Arezzo in the eastern part of the region of Tuscany. Situated on the upper course of the Tiber river, the town is known as the birthplace of the painters Piero della Francesca, Raffaellino del Colle, and the mathematician Luca Pacioli.

History

According to tradition, the town was founded around the year 1000 by two pilgrims, Arcanus and Giles, who on their return from the Holy Land built a chapel dedicated to the Holy Sepulchre (Santo Sepolcro), around which the town grew. The town passed under various rulers, including the Malatesta and Florence, becoming part of the Grand Duchy of Tuscany.

Main sights

The Museo Civico di Sansepolcro preserves major works by Piero della Francesca, including the Resurrection — famously described by Aldous Huxley as "the greatest picture in the world" — and the Polyptych of the Misericordia. The cathedral of San Giovanni Evangelista, the fortress designed with input from Giuliano da Sangallo, and numerous medieval and Renaissance palaces also stand in the historic centre.

Culture

Sansepolcro is known for its lace-making tradition and for the Palio della Balestra, a crossbow competition with Gubbio maintained since the 15th century. The town was the birthplace in 1932 of the Buitoni pasta company founder's dynasty, and a Buitoni pasta factory remains an important local employer.
