Kanhoji Angre (Marathi: कान्होजी आंग्रे; August 1669 – 4 July 1729) was an admiral of the Maratha Navy who commanded the naval forces of the Maratha Empire in the early eighteenth century. From his fortified bases along the Konkan coast — above all the sea-fort of Vijaydurg — he dominated the waters of the western Indian coast and resisted, for three decades, the combined efforts of the British East India Company, the Portuguese, and the Siddis of Janjira.

Career

Angre rose under the patronage of the Maratha court and was appointed Sarkhel (admiral) in the 1690s. He fortified a chain of coastal strongholds between Mumbai and Goa, levied the chauth toll on shipping that used his waters, and captured or contested European merchant vessels that refused his passes. Repeated joint expeditions against him — including the English-Portuguese assault on Vijaydurg in 1721–1722 — failed, and he remained undefeated at sea until his death in 1729.

Legacy

To European records he was long portrayed as a pirate, but in India he is remembered as a founder of the Maratha navy and defender of the coast. The Indian Navy's Western Naval Command base in Mumbai, INS Angre, is named after him, as is the naval station at Alibag near his ancestral village.
