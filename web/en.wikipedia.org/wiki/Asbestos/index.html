Asbestos is a group of naturally occurring fibrous silicate minerals that were widely used in industry and construction for their resistance to heat, fire, and chemical attack, and for their tensile strength and insulating properties. Prolonged inhalation of asbestos fibres is now known to cause serious and often fatal diseases, including asbestosis, lung cancer, and mesothelioma, leading to comprehensive bans in many countries.

Types and uses

The principal commercial form is chrysotile (white asbestos), a serpentine mineral that historically accounted for the great majority of world consumption. Amphibole forms — crocidolite (blue asbestos), amosite (brown asbestos), tremolite, actinolite, and anthophyllite — are straighter, more needle-like, and generally considered more hazardous. Asbestos was used in thousands of products: roofing and insulation, asbestos cement, brake linings, gaskets, fireproof textiles, and pipe lagging.

Health effects

Microscopic fibres released when asbestos materials are cut, drilled, or deteriorate can lodge in the lungs and pleura. Diseases typically appear after latency periods of decades. Asbestosis is a fibrotic scarring of the lungs; mesothelioma is a cancer of the lining of the chest and abdomen almost exclusively associated with asbestos exposure; asbestos also increases the risk of lung cancer, an effect greatly multiplied by smoking. The World Health Organization and the International Agency for Research on Cancer classify all forms of asbestos as carcinogenic to humans, and occupational exposure is estimated to cause well over 100,000 deaths globally each year.

Regulation and legacy

Health concerns were documented in the early twentieth century, but regulation came mainly in the 1970s and 1980s, followed by bans — the European Union banned all uses by 2005; Australia banned it in 2003; Canada, long a major producer, banned it in 2018. The United States enacted a ban on chrysotile in 2024. Asbestos mining and use continue in some countries, including Russia, Kazakhstan, and China, and large quantities remain in place in older buildings, making safe removal (abatement) a major industry. Litigation over asbestos disease has been among the largest mass torts in history, bankrupting major manufacturers such as Johns Manville.
