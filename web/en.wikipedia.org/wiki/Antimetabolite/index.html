Antimetabolite

An antimetabolite is a chemical compound that inhibits the use of a metabolite—one of the normal chemicals involved in metabolism—by resembling it structurally. Antimetabolites are widely used in medicine as chemotherapy agents against cancer, as immunosuppressants, and as antimicrobial drugs, because they interfere with DNA synthesis and cell division, processes that are particularly active in rapidly proliferating cells.

Antimetabolites mimic natural metabolites and block essential biosynthetic pathways

Mechanism of action

Antimetabolites typically act during the S phase of the cell cycle, when DNA is replicated. They may be incorporated into DNA or RNA in place of the normal building blocks, producing defective nucleic acids, or they may block the enzymes that synthesize nucleotides, starving the cell of the materials needed for replication. Because cancer cells divide more rapidly than most normal cells, they are preferentially harmed, though fast-growing normal tissues—such as bone marrow, hair follicles, and the gastrointestinal lining—are also affected, accounting for many side effects.

Classes

Antifolates

Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, blocking the production of tetrahydrofolate needed for purine and thymidylate synthesis. It is used in cancer chemotherapy and, at lower doses, as an immunosuppressant in autoimmune disease. Pemetrexed is another antifolate used in lung cancer and mesothelioma.

Purine analogues

These agents, including 6-mercaptopurine, fludarabine, and cladribine, mimic purine bases and disrupt DNA synthesis; they are used chiefly in leukemias and lymphomas. Azathioprine, a prodrug of mercaptopurine, is used as an immunosuppressant.

Pyrimidine analogues

5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) and its oral prodrug capecitabine are central to the treatment of colorectal and other gastrointestinal cancers. Cytarabine is important in acute leukemias, and gemcitabine is used in pancreatic and other cancers.

Other uses

Sulfonamide antibiotics act as antimetabolites of para-aminobenzoic acid in bacteria, and trimethoprim blocks bacterial folate synthesis. Some antiviral drugs are nucleoside analogues that function similarly against viral replication.
