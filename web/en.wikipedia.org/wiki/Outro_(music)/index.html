Outro (music)

An outro (also called an out-chorus or coda in some contexts) is the concluding section of a piece of music. The term is a portmanteau formed as the opposite of "intro", and is widely used in popular music to describe the passage that brings a song to its close, whether by fade-out, a final repeated chorus, a breakdown, or a decisive final cadence.

Forms

Common outro techniques include:

  • Fade-out — the volume gradually decreases to silence over a repeated figure, standard in mid-20th-century pop recordings.
  • Cold ending — the music stops abruptly on a final hit or sustained chord.
  • Breakdown outro — instruments drop out one by one until only a single element remains.
  • Tag or vamp — a short figure repeated with improvisation, common in jazz, gospel and R&B.
  • Skit or spoken outro — common in hip-hop albums, where a closing track ("outro") may feature speech or a final statement rather than a full song.

In hip-hop and albums

On many albums, especially in hip-hop and R&B, the final track is explicitly titled "Outro" and serves as a closing statement, complementing an "Intro" track at the start. Such outros often reprise themes or production motifs from the album.
