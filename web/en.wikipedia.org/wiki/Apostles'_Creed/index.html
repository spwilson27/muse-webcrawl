Apostles' Creed

Medieval tradition assigned each clause of the Creed to one of the twelve apostles.

The Apostles' Creed (Latin: Symbolum Apostolorum or Symbolum Apostolicum), sometimes titled the Apostolic Creed or the Symbol of the Apostles, is a Christian creed or "symbol of faith". It developed from the Old Roman Creed used at baptism in Rome and reached its present form by about the 8th century. It is used by many Christian churches for both liturgical and catechetical purposes, most visibly by liturgical churches of Western Christian tradition, including the Latin Church, Lutheranism, Anglicanism, and Western Rite Orthodoxy.

Text

I believe in God, the Father almighty,
Creator of heaven and earth,
and in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord,
who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,
born of the Virgin Mary,
suffered under Pontius Pilate,
was crucified, died and was buried;
he descended into hell;
on the third day he rose again from the dead;
he ascended into heaven,
and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty;
from there he will come to judge the living and the dead.

I believe in the Holy Spirit,
the holy catholic Church,
the communion of saints,
the forgiveness of sins,
the resurrection of the body,
and life everlasting. Amen.

History

The wording derives from the Old Roman Symbol, a baptismal profession attested in Rome by the early 3rd century in the interrogatory creed associated with Hippolytus. A later legend claimed that the Twelve Apostles composed the creed together on the day of Pentecost, each contributing one article; the claim was already doubted by Renaissance scholars and is rejected by modern scholarship.

The received form of the text first appears in full in the 8th century in the writings of Saint Pirminius and in Frankish sacramentaries, and was spread throughout the Carolingian world. Notable differences from the Old Roman Symbol include the phrases "Creator of heaven and earth" and "he descended into hell" (descendit ad inferos) — the descent into hell — and "the communion of saints".

Use

The Creed is said at baptism and in the daily offices of the Western Church; in the Roman Rite it may be used at Sunday Mass, especially in Lent and Eastertide, in place of the Nicene Creed. Because it is shorter and older, it is also the creed most shared across Western denominations and is recognised by many ecumenical agreements.
