Megastructure

A megastructure is a very large artificial object, although the limits of precisely how large vary considerably. The term is typically applied in discussions of hypothetical structures in astroengineering, in architectural theory, and in science fiction. Examples of megastructures in fiction and speculative engineering include the Dyson sphere, the Ringworld, orbital habitats such as the O'Neill cylinder, and city-sized arcologies.

In architecture

In architectural usage, "megastructure" describes a concept popularised in the 1960s, notably by Reyner Banham's 1976 book Megastructure: Urban Futures of the Recent Past. Megastructuralist architects such as Archigram, Cedric Price, and the Japanese Metabolists envisioned vast frameworks into which smaller, replaceable units could be plugged, allowing cities to grow and adapt organically.

In astroengineering

Proposed stellar-scale megastructures include Dyson spheres and swarms for capturing a star's energy, Shkadov thrusters for moving stars, Alderson disks, and Bishop rings. The possible observational signatures of such structures — such as anomalous infrared excess or unusual transit dimming, as discussed for the star KIC 8462852 (Tabby's Star) — are a subject of SETI research.
