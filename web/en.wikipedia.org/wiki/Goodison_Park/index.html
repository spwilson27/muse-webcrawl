Goodison Park

Goodison Park is a football stadium in the Walton area of Liverpool, England, located two miles north of the city centre. It was the home ground of Premier League club Everton from its opening in 1892 until 2025 — among the longest tenancies of any major English club at a single ground — after which the men's team moved to the new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock and Goodison became the home of Everton Women. Its capacity at closure for men's football was 39,414.

History

Everton originally played at Anfield, but a rent dispute with landlord John Houlding led the club to leave in 1892; Houlding went on to found Liverpool F.C. Everton bought land at Mere Green Field, renamed after the adjacent Goodison Road, and built one of the first purpose-built football grounds in England. The first match was played on 24 August 1892, and the stadium was officially opened two days later. It was the first English league ground to have a three-tiered stand and undertook pioneering features, including undersoil heating (1958) and a church, St Luke the Evangelist, famously wedged into one corner of the ground.

Goodison hosted matches at the 1966 FIFA World Cup, including the semi-final between West Germany and the Soviet Union, and an FA Cup final in 1894. Its record attendance was 78,299 for a Merseyside derby against Liverpool in 1948.

Stands

The four stands are the Main Stand, the Gwladys Street End — home of the most vocal support — the Bullens Road Stand, and the Park End Stand, the most recent, built in 1994 when the stadium became all-seated following the Taylor Report.

Final season and future

Everton played their final men's first-team match at Goodison in May 2025, ending 133 years at the ground. Rather than being demolished, the stadium was retained as the home of Everton Women, making it the largest dedicated women's football stadium in England.
