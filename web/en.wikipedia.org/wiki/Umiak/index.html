Umiak

The umiak, umialak, umiaq, umiac, oomiac, or oomiack is a type of open skin boat, used by Yupik and Inuit peoples of the Arctic for the transport of people and freight, as well as for whaling. Like the kayak, it is traditionally propelled by paddles, though it has also been sailed and, in modern times, fitted with outboard motors.

Construction

Umiaks are built around a frame of driftwood or whalebone lashed together, over which a covering of walrus or bearded seal skin is stretched. The skins are sewn together, sealed with oil to prevent leaking, and lashed to the frame. Unlike the kayak, which is fully decked, the umiak is open-topped, exposing passengers and cargo to the elements.

Size and use

Umiaks vary greatly in size, from small family boats to large whaling vessels nine metres (30 feet) or more in length, capable of carrying twenty or more people. Historically they were often paddled by women—one origin sometimes given for the name is "women's boat"—in contrast to the kayak, typically used by men for hunting. In whaling communities such as those of northern Alaska, large umiaks crewed by an umialik (boat owner and captain) pursued bowhead whales during the spring migration, a tradition that continues today.
