Italian fashion

Milan Fashion Week, one of the "Big Four" fashion weeks of the world.

Italian fashion is one of the most prominent and influential fashion industries in the world. Together with France, the United States, Britain, and Japan, Italy is counted among the leading countries in fashion design, manufacturing, and export. Milan is regarded as a global fashion capital, hosting one of the "Big Four" fashion weeks alongside Paris, London, and New York.

History

Italy was a centre of luxury craft in the Renaissance, when Florence, Venice, and Genoa supplied Europe with silk, velvet, and jewellery. The modern industry took shape after the Second World War: in 1951 Giovanni Battista Giorgini organised fashion shows in Florence that introduced Italian designers to American buyers, shifting attention from Paris. By the 1970s and 1980s, Milan had become the capital of prêt-à-porter, powered by houses such as Armani, Versace, Prada, Gucci, Valentino, Dolce & Gabbana, Ferragamo, and Bulgari.

Industry

Italian fashion is distinguished by its strong manufacturing base — family-run industrial districts specialising in textiles (Como silk, Biella wool), leather goods (Tuscany), and footwear (the Marche). The sector, branded "Made in Italy," is a major export earner and is marketed worldwide as a byword for quality materials, tailoring, and craftsmanship.
