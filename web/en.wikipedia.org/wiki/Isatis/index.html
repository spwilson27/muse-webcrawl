Isatis

Family: Brassicaceae

Notable species: Isatis tinctoria (woad)

Distribution: Central and Southwest Asia, Europe

Isatis is a genus of flowering plants in the family Brassicaceae, native to Central and Southwest Asia and parts of Europe. The genus includes around 50 species of annual, biennial, and perennial herbs, many of which have become naturalized elsewhere through cultivation and trade.

Woad

The best-known species is Isatis tinctoria, or woad, historically one of Europe's principal sources of blue dye. Woad was cultivated from antiquity until it was largely displaced by imported indigo and, later, synthetic dyes. The plant also has a long history in traditional medicine; its root (banlangen) is used in Chinese herbal medicine.

Description

Isatis species bear small yellow flowers in branched inflorescences and flattened, winged, one-seeded fruits (silicles) that hang downward. The genus's taxonomy has shifted with molecular studies, with some species moved to related genera.
