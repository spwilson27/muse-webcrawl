Third ventricle


The ventricular system; the third ventricle lies in the midline

The third ventricle is one of the four connected fluid-filled cavities of the ventricular system within the human brain. It is a midline slit-like cavity in the diencephalon, situated between the two thalami, and is filled with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

Structure

The third ventricle is bounded laterally by the thalamus and hypothalamus on each side. Its floor is formed by structures of the hypothalamus, including the optic chiasm, the infundibulum of the pituitary gland, the tuber cinereum, and the mammillary bodies. Its roof contains the choroid plexus, which produces cerebrospinal fluid.

Connections

  • Anteriorly, it communicates with each lateral ventricle through the interventricular foramina (foramina of Monro).
  • Posteriorly, it connects to the fourth ventricle via the cerebral aqueduct (aqueduct of Sylvius).

Cerebrospinal fluid flows from the lateral ventricles through the foramina of Monro into the third ventricle, then via the cerebral aqueduct to the fourth ventricle and onward into the subarachnoid space.

Development and clinical significance

The third ventricle develops from the cavity of the embryonic forebrain (prosencephalon). Obstruction of CSF flow at the cerebral aqueduct can cause the ventricle to dilate in obstructive hydrocephalus. Masses arising within or near the third ventricle, such as colloid cysts at the foramen of Monro, can produce intermittent obstruction and headaches. Enlargement of the third ventricle is also observed in some neurodegenerative and psychiatric conditions.
