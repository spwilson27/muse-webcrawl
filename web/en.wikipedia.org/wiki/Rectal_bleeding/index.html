Rectal bleeding

Rectal bleeding is the passage of blood through the anus. Bright red blood (haematochezia) usually originates in the lower gastrointestinal tract, such as the colon and rectum, whereas dark, tarry stool (melena) typically indicates bleeding higher in the digestive tract. Common causes include haemorrhoids, anal fissures, diverticulosis, inflammatory bowel disease, polyps and colorectal cancer.

Evaluation depends on the amount and duration of bleeding and the patient's age and associated symptoms, and may involve examination, blood tests, endoscopy or imaging. Significant or persistent rectal bleeding requires medical assessment, and unstable patients with major bleeding need emergency treatment.
