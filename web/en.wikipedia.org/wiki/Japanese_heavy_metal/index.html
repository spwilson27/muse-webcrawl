Japanese heavy metal

Japanese heavy metal is the tradition of heavy metal music that developed in Japan from the late 1970s onward, paralleling the New Wave of British Heavy Metal while developing distinctive national features — close ties to the visual kei movement, a strong market for imported Western bands, and homegrown acts ranging from the pioneering Bow Wow and Loudness to the internationally successful X Japan and, in the 2010s, the "kawaii metal" phenomenon Babymetal.

Origins

Hard rock acts such as Flower Travellin' Band (whose 1971 album Satori anticipated metal styles) and Bow Wow (formed 1975, later Vow Wow) laid the groundwork. The explosion came in the early 1980s: Loudness, formed in Osaka in 1981 by guitarist Akira Takasaki and drummer Munetaka Higuchi, became the first Japanese metal band to sign a U.S. deal and tour America and Europe, with albums such as Thunder in the East (1985). Earthshaker, Anthem, Seikima-II (with their demon makeup and mock mythology) and the all-female Show-Ya built a domestic scene supported by dedicated magazines such as Burrn! and the Meguro Rock-May-Kan live house in Tokyo.

Visual kei and X Japan

In the mid-1980s bands including X Japan (founded as X in 1982 in Chiba), Buck-Tick, D'erlanger and Dead End combined metal musicianship with flamboyant makeup, hair and costume — the movement labelled visual kei. X Japan, led by drummer-pianist Yoshiki and singer Toshi, with virtuoso guitarist hide, rose from the indie underground (releasing Vanishing Vision on Yoshiki's own Extasy Records in 1988) to stadium status, selling tens of millions of records and helping define Japanese rock's commercial mainstream before their 1997 breakup.

Underground and extreme metal

Japan also sustained respected extreme-metal undergrounds: thrash bands such as United and Outrage; black metal pioneers Sigh, who mixed avant-garde and traditional instruments; doom and noise acts Boris and Corrupted, influential in Western drone and sludge circles; and grindcore and death-metal scenes centred on independent labels. Japanese audiences' appetite for Western metal made Japan a uniquely important touring market, with the loud-voice magazine culture and festival circuit (later including Loud Park, founded 2006) supporting it.

2010s: Babymetal and beyond

In 2010 the agency Amuse formed Babymetal, fusing idol-pop choreography and vocals with full-scale metal backing; songs like "Gimme Chocolate!!" went viral worldwide, and the group played Wembley Arena and headlined international festivals, sparking debate about authenticity while introducing global audiences to Japanese metal. Veteran acts such as Loudness and Anthem continued recording and touring, and visual-kei-descended bands — Dir En Grey, The Gazette, Maximum the Hormone — carried heavy Japanese music to worldwide festival audiences, confirming Japan as one of metal's most durable national scenes.
