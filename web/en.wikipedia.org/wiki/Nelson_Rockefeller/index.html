Nelson Rockefeller

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nelson Aldrich Rockefeller (July 8, 1908 – January 26, 1979), sometimes referred to by his nickname Rocky, was an American businessman and politician who served as the 41st vice president of the United States from 1974 to 1977 under President Gerald Ford. A member of the Republican Party and the Rockefeller family, he previously served as the 49th governor of New York between 1959 and 1973. He also served as assistant secretary of State for American Republic Affairs for Presidents Franklin D. Roosevelt and Harry S. Truman (1944–1945), and as Undersecretary of Health, Education and Welfare under Dwight D. Eisenhower (1953–1954).

Rockefeller served as assistant secretary of state for American Republic Affairs for presidents Roosevelt and Truman as well as Undersecretary of Health, Education and Welfare under Eisenhower. A son of John D. Rockefeller Jr. and Abby Aldrich Rockefeller, and a grandson of John D. Rockefeller, he was president of the Museum of Modern Art, which his mother co-founded, and developed Rockefeller Center.

Governor of New York

Elected in 1958, Rockefeller served four terms as governor, presiding over an activist, modernising administration: he expanded the State University of New York into one of the largest public university systems in the country, built vast amounts of public housing and infrastructure, created the state Office of Parks and Recreation, enacted pioneering environmental and anti-discrimination measures, and drove the construction of the Empire State Plaza in Albany. His tenure also included the harsh Rockefeller drug laws of 1973 and the violent suppression of the 1971 Attica Prison uprising, in which 43 people died — events that damaged his liberal reputation. He represented the liberal, internationalist "Eastern Establishment" wing of the Republican Party — the "Rockefeller Republicans" — and sought the Republican presidential nomination in 1960, 1964 and 1968, losing each time to the party's conservative movement, which coalesced around Barry Goldwater and Richard Nixon.

Vice presidency

After Nixon resigned in August 1974, President Gerald Ford nominated Rockefeller to fill the vice-presidential vacancy under the Twenty-fifth Amendment; he was confirmed after lengthy hearings and served from December 19, 1974 to January 20, 1977. Sidelined by Ford's staff and resented by conservatives, he was dropped from the 1976 ticket in favour of Bob Dole.

Personal life and death

Rockefeller was a major art collector, a founder of the Museum of Primitive Art, and president of the Museum of Modern Art. His first marriage, to Mary Todhunter Clark, ended in divorce in 1962; he married Margaretta "Happy" Murphy in 1963. He died of a heart attack on January 26, 1979, in New York City, aged 70.
