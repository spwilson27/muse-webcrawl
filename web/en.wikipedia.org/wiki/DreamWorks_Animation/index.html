DreamWorks Animation

DreamWorks Animation LLC (abbreviated as DWA, also known as DreamWorks Animation Studios or simply DreamWorks) is an American animation studio that produces animated feature films and television programs and is a subsidiary of Universal Pictures, a division of NBCUniversal, which is itself a division of Comcast. The studio has released a total of 51 feature films, including several of the highest-grossing animated films of all time, with Shrek 2 (2004) having been the highest-grossing of all time until Toy Story 3 (2010).

History

DreamWorks Animation originated as the animation division of DreamWorks Pictures, founded in 1994 by Steven Spielberg, Jeffrey Katzenberg and David Geffen. Its first film, Antz, was released in 1998, followed by the traditionally animated The Prince of Egypt the same year. The studio achieved enormous success with Shrek (2001), which won the first Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

In 2004 the animation division was spun off into a separate publicly traded company. In August 2016, NBCUniversal acquired DreamWorks Animation for approximately $3.8 billion, making it a division of the Universal Filmed Entertainment Group.
