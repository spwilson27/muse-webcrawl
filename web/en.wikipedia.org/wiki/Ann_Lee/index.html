Ann Lee

Born29 February 1736, Manchester, England
Died8 September 1784, Watervliet, New York
Known forLeader of the Shakers
TitleMother Ann

Ann Lee (29 February 1736 – 8 September 1784), commonly known as Mother Ann Lee, was the founding leader of the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing, better known as the Shakers. Persecuted in England for her religious preaching, she emigrated to America in 1774 with a small band of followers and established the first Shaker community at Watervliet, New York.

Early life

Lee was born in Manchester to a blacksmith's family and worked in a cotton factory and as a cook. She joined the Wardley Society, a Quaker offshoot whose worship involved trembling and shaking — the origin of the name "Shaking Quakers" or "Shakers". After a painful marriage and the deaths of her four children in infancy, Lee concluded that celibacy was essential to holiness, a doctrine that became central to Shaker belief.

Ministry

Following a period of imprisonment in 1770, during which she reported receiving visions, Lee proclaimed herself the female counterpart of Christ — "the mother in Christ" — in accordance with the Shaker belief in a dual male-female nature of God. In 1774 she sailed for America, and from 1781 to 1783 she undertook a missionary tour of New England that won many converts but also violent opposition. She died in 1784, not long after being beaten by a mob during her travels.

Legacy

Under successors such as Joseph Meacham and Lucy Wright, the Shakers grew into a network of communal villages celebrated for their craftsmanship, music and egalitarian organisation. Lee is remembered by believers as the female manifestation of Christ's second appearing.
