Eastern world

The Eastern world, also known as the East or historically the Orient, is an umbrella term for various cultures and regions, primarily relative to Europe. In its broadest usage it comprises Asia, including East Asia, Southeast Asia, South Asia and sometimes the Middle East, and stands in contrast to the Western world. The concept is Eurocentric in origin and its boundaries have shifted across history.

History of the concept

The East–West distinction dates to classical antiquity's contrast between Europe and Asia, structured by the Greco-Persian Wars, and was later reshaped by the Roman Empire's division, the spread of Christianity and Islam, and European colonial expansion. During the Cold War, "the East" acquired a political meaning, denoting the communist Eastern Bloc, while "Eastern world" in cultural usage continued to refer mainly to Asia.

Usage and criticism

Scholars such as Edward Said, in his 1978 book Orientalism, criticised Western representations of the East as essentialising and serving colonial power. Modern usage varies: in geopolitics the term may group the Asian economies; in culture it may contrast value systems, religions (such as Buddhism, Hinduism, Islam, Confucianism and Taoism) and philosophical traditions with those of the West. Because of its vagueness and historical baggage, terms like "Asia-Pacific" are often preferred in formal contexts.
