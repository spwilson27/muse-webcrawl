Zygmunt Janiszewski

Zygmunt Janiszewski (12 June 1888 – 3 January 1920) was a Polish mathematician who worked primarily in topology and set theory. He was a driving force behind the creation of the Polish School of Mathematics.

Life and work

Janiszewski studied in Zürich, Munich, Göttingen, and Paris, where he completed his doctorate under Henri Lebesgue in 1911 with a thesis on topology. He taught at the University of Warsaw and served in the Polish Legions during the First World War. His topological results include Janiszewski's theorem on planar curves.

Legacy

Believing Polish mathematics needed a distinct identity built around set theory, topology, and logic, Janiszewski proposed concentrating research in these areas and founded — though he did not live to see — the journal Fundamenta Mathematicae, the first journal devoted to a single mathematical field. He died at 31 in the 1920 influenza pandemic, having donated his estate to mathematics education and scholarships.
