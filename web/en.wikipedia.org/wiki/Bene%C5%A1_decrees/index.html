Beneš decrees
Issuer: Edvard Beneš
Period: 1940-1945

The Beneš decrees are a series of decrees issued by the Czechoslovak government-in-exile and post-war government under President Edvard Beneš during and after the Second World War.

Contents

The decrees covered the restoration of Czechoslovakia, punishment of collaborators, and the confiscation of property and loss of citizenship for many ethnic Germans and Hungarians, forming the legal basis for their expulsion from Czechoslovakia.

Aftermath

Around three million Sudeten Germans were expelled in 1945-46. The decrees remain politically sensitive in relations between the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Germany, Austria and Hungary.

Legal status

Most decrees are spent, but their symbolic and property-related effects continue to be debated in Czech politics and European courts.
