The Northern Cheyenne Indian Reservation is the home of the federally recognised Northern Cheyenne Tribe in southeastern Montana. It lies in Big Horn and Rosebud counties, west of the Tongue River and adjacent to the Crow Reservation. The reservation is the homeland secured by the Northern Cheyenne after their forced removal to Indian Territory (Oklahoma) and their celebrated flight home in 1878–79, known as the Northern Cheyenne Exodus.

History

The Northern Cheyenne, close allies of the Lakota, fought in the Great Sioux War of 1876, including the Battle of the Little Bighorn. After surrendering, most were deported to Oklahoma, where disease and starvation decimated them. In September 1878 chiefs Dull Knife and Little Wolf led about 350 people north; Little Wolf's band reached Montana and was eventually allowed to remain near Fort Keogh. The reservation was established by executive order in 1884 and enlarged in 1900.

Government and community

The tribe is governed under a constitution adopted under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934, with an elected tribal council and president headquartered at Lame Deer, the main community. Other communities include Busby, Ashland, Birney and Muddy Cluster. Chief Dull Knife College in Lame Deer provides higher education. The Cheyenne language (Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse) is taught in immersion programmes, though few fluent speakers remain.

Economy

Ranching, forestry, tribal government and education are major employers. The reservation holds large coal deposits beneath its land; the tribe has long debated development and in the 2010s asserted control over its mineral future. The reservation borders the Custer National Forest, and the annual Northern Cheyenne Fourth of July powwow is a major cultural gathering.
