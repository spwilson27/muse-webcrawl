Magnetic moment

In electromagnetism, the magnetic moment or magnetic dipole moment is a vector quantity describing the strength and orientation of a magnetic source such as a loop of electric current or a permanent magnet. The magnetic moment determines the torque a magnet experiences in an external magnetic field, and the magnetic field the source itself produces at a distance.

Definition

For a planar loop of current I enclosing area A, the magnitude of the magnetic moment is μ = IA, and its direction is along the loop's axis, given by the right-hand rule. For a general current distribution, the moment is defined by an integral over the currents. A magnet in a uniform magnetic field B experiences a torque τ = μ × B, and its potential energy is U = −μ · B.

Magnetic moments of particles

Elementary particles possess intrinsic magnetic moments associated with their spin. The electron's magnetic moment, dominated by its spin and its negative charge, is close to one Bohr magneton (μB ≈ 9.274×10−24 J/T), with a small anomalous correction predicted by quantum electrodynamics to extraordinary precision. The proton and neutron also have magnetic moments expressed in nuclear magnetons; the neutron's nonzero moment was historically evidence of its internal structure of quarks.

Atomic and molecular moments

In atoms, magnetic moments arise from electron orbital motion and spin, combined according to the rules of angular momentum coupling. The net atomic moment underlies paramagnetism, ferromagnetism, and related phenomena; in ferromagnetic materials, exchange interactions align atomic moments within domains, producing strong permanent magnets.
