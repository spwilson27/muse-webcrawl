Hvítá River

Hvítá (Icelandic for "white river") is one of the most common river names in Iceland, borne by several glacial rivers whose milky, sediment-laden waters give them their name. The best known is the Hvítá in the south of the country, which flows from the glacier lake Hvítárvatn at the edge of the Langjökull ice cap.

The southern Hvítá

The southern Hvítá runs for about 140 kilometres before joining the Sog to form the Ölfusá, Iceland's largest river by discharge. Along its course it passes through the Hreppar countryside and plunges over Gullfoss, the famous two-tiered waterfall that is one of Iceland's most visited natural attractions and a stop on the Golden Circle route. Downstream of Gullfoss the river cuts through the Hvítá canyon, popular for white-water rafting. The river also carries runs of Atlantic salmon and is fished in summer.

Other rivers of the name

Another Hvítá flows through the Borgarfjörður district in western Iceland, fed by tributaries from Langjökull and known for salmon fishing, while the Hvítá in the east flows from Vatnajökull towards the Arctic Ocean near the Jökulsá á Fjöllum region. All share the characteristic pale colour of glacial meltwater rich in suspended rock flour.
