Pantograph (transport)

A pantograph is an apparatus mounted on the roof of an electric train, tram, or electric bus to collect power through contact with an overhead catenary wire. Its jointed, spring- or air-loaded frame keeps a carbon contact strip pressed against the wire at varying heights.

Design

Modern pantographs are usually single-arm ("Z"-shaped) designs, replacing older diamond frames. The contact strip is sacrificial and made of carbon to reduce wear on the overhead wire. Pantographs are used with both DC and AC electrification systems worldwide.

Operation

Drivers raise and lower the pantograph pneumatically; automatic dropping devices lower it if the strip is damaged, preventing it from tearing down the overhead line equipment.
