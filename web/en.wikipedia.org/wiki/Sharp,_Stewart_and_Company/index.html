Sharp, Stewart and Company was a British locomotive manufacturer based first in Manchester and later in Glasgow. The firm built steam locomotives for railways in Britain and around the world during the 19th century.

History

The business grew out of the engineering firm Sharp, Roberts and Company, which built its first locomotive in 1833. After Richard Roberts left, the firm traded as Sharp Brothers and then, from 1852, as Sharp, Stewart and Company at the Atlas Works in Manchester. In 1888 the company moved to the Atlas Works in Springburn, Glasgow.

Merger

In 1903 Sharp, Stewart merged with Neilson and Company and Dübs and Company to form the North British Locomotive Company, then the largest locomotive builder in Britain. Sharp, Stewart locomotives survive in preservation in several countries, including examples exported to India, Japan and South America.
