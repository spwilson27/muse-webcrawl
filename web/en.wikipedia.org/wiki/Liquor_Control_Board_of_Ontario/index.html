Liquor Control Board of Ontario (LCBO)

Founded: 1927

Type: Crown agency of Ontario

Headquarters: Toronto, Ontario, Canada

Role: Retail and wholesale of beverage alcohol in Ontario

The Liquor Control Board of Ontario (LCBO) is a Crown corporation that retails and distributes alcoholic beverages throughout the Canadian province of Ontario. It was established in 1927, following the end of prohibition in Ontario, under the government of Premier Howard Ferguson. The LCBO is one of the world's largest buyers and retailers of alcoholic beverages, operating hundreds of stores across the province and generating substantial revenue for the provincial government.

History

Prohibition in Ontario lasted from 1916 to 1927. After a referendum showed public support for controlled government sale rather than a return to open sale, the province created the LCBO to sell liquor under strict rules, initially requiring purchasers to hold permits and record their purchases. Over the following decades the stores modernized, moving from counter-service to self-service formats.

Operations

The LCBO sells spirits, wine, and beer through its store network, online sales, and wholesale channels supplying bars, restaurants, and other retailers. Its specialty wine service, Vintages, offers fine wines and premium spirits. The LCBO also has responsibility for aspects of alcohol policy, including social responsibility programs concerning responsible consumption.
