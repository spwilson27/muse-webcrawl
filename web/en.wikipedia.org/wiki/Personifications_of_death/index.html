Personifications of death

Personifications of death are representations of death as a sentient figure in the mythology, religion, and folklore of many cultures. Such figures may act as psychopomps — guides who escort the souls of the dead to the afterlife — or as deities who actively cause death, collect souls, or rule over the realm of the dead.

Examples by culture

In European tradition the most familiar figure is the Grim Reaper, a skeletal being in a black hooded cloak carrying a scythe, an image consolidated in the late medieval period. Classical Greek mythology had Thanatos, the gentle personification of non-violent death, and the Keres, spirits of violent death. Norse tradition had Hel, ruler of the dead realm of the same name; Egyptian mythology had Anubis and Osiris; Aztec religion had Mictlantecuhtli; and in Japan the shinigami and the figure of Enma appear in folk belief. In Abrahamic traditions the Angel of Death appears in various forms, such as Azrael in Islam. In Mexico, the folk saint Santa Muerte is venerated as a personification of death.

In popular culture

Death personified is a recurring character in literature and media, from the figure in The Seventh Seal to Terry Pratchett's DEATH in the Discworld novels and Death in Neil Gaiman's The Sandman.
