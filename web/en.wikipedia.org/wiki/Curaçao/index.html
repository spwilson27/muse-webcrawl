Curaçao

Handelskade, Willemstad — the historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Curaçao is a Lesser Antilles island in the southern Caribbean Sea, about 65 km north of the Venezuelan coast, and a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Together with Aruba and Bonaire it forms the Leeward Antilles "ABC islands". Its capital is Willemstad, whose historic centre, with its colourful Dutch colonial waterfront buildings, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The island covers about 444 km² and has a population of roughly 150,000.

History

The island was originally inhabited by Arawak peoples. Spanish explorers arrived in 1499; Alonso de Ojeda's expedition is traditionally credited with the first European landing. The Dutch West India Company seized Curaçao in 1634, valuing its natural harbour and its position for trade with the Spanish Main. Willemstad grew into a major entrepôt, including in the Atlantic slave trade; slavery was abolished by the Netherlands in 1863. In the 20th century the discovery of oil in Venezuela's Maracaibo Basin led Shell to build a large refinery on the island, long its economic mainstay. Curaçao was part of the Netherlands Antilles until that country's dissolution on 10 October 2010, when Curaçao became a separate constituent country within the Kingdom.

Culture and language

Most Curaçaoans speak Papiamentu, a Portuguese- and Spanish-based creole, alongside Dutch (the language of government and education), English and Spanish. The island's culture blends African, European and Latin American influences; carnival, the music styles of tumba and tambú, and dishes such as keshi yena are characteristic. The blue liqueur known as Curaçao takes its name from the island and the laraha citrus fruit grown there.

Economy

Tourism, the oil refinery and transshipment, offshore financial services and the port of Willemstad are the main economic activities. Curaçao's dry climate, beaches and coral reefs attract divers and visitors year-round, and the island lies south of the main hurricane belt.
