José María Teclo Morelos Pérez y Pavón (1765–1815) was a Mexican Roman Catholic priest and revolutionary rebel leader who led the Mexican War of Independence movement after the execution of its founder, Miguel Hidalgo, in 1811.

Insurgency

Morelos proved a brilliant military strategist, capturing much of southern Mexico and besieging Acapulco. He convened the Congress of Chilpancingo, which issued the Solemn Act of the Declaration of Independence of Northern America in 1813, and authored the Sentimientos de la Nación, a foundational document calling for equality, abolition of slavery, and popular sovereignty. Captured by royalist forces in 1815, he was tried and executed by firing squad.

Legacy

The city of Morelia and the state of Morelos are named in his honour, and he is regarded as a national hero of Mexico.
