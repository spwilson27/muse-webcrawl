Dillenia indica, commonly known as the elephant apple, is an evergreen large shrub or small to medium-sized tree in the family Dilleniaceae, growing to about 15 metres tall. It is native to southeastern Asia, from India and Sri Lanka eastward through southern China to Indonesia, and is widely cultivated as an ornamental and fruit tree.

Description

The leaves are large, 15–36 cm long, with a prominently corrugated surface and serrated margins. The flowers are large and showy, up to 20 cm in diameter, with five white petals and numerous yellow stamens; they open at night and last a single day. The fruit is a large, round, greenish-yellow aggregate fruit 5–12 cm in diameter, composed of enlarged fleshy sepals enclosing many seeds embedded in a glutinous pulp. The unripe fruit is sour and astringent.

Uses

The fleshy sepals are used in South and Southeast Asian cooking: in Assam and Bengal the sour fruit (ou tenga, chalta) is cooked in fish curries and dals, and elsewhere it is made into jams, jellies, and sherbets. The juice is used as a cough mixture and the mucilage applied to the hair. Elephants feed on the fruit, hence the common name, and monkeys and other animals help disperse the seeds.

Cultivation

The tree prefers moist, fertile soils in tropical and subtropical climates and is commonly planted along roadsides and in gardens for its shade and ornamental flowers.
