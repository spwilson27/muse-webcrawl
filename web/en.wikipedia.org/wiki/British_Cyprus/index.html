British Cyprus was the island of Cyprus under British administration from 1878 to 1960. Britain took over the administration of the island from the Ottoman Empire under the Cyprus Convention of 1878, annexed it outright at the outbreak of the First World War in 1914, and governed it as a Crown colony from 1925 until independence in 1960.

Acquisition and early rule

Under the secret Cyprus Convention, concluded at the time of the Congress of Berlin, Britain occupied and administered Cyprus while it formally remained Ottoman territory, in return for a British guarantee of Ottoman possessions in Asia against Russia. The island was valued chiefly as a strategic position in the eastern Mediterranean; the harbour of Famagusta was developed and the port of Limassol used for trade. The annual "tribute" paid to the Ottoman treasury remained controversial throughout the period.

Enosis and interwar unrest

Many Greek Cypriots sought enosis — union with Greece — a demand pressed on Britain from the beginning. Frustration erupted in the October 1931 riots (the Oktovriana), in which Government House in Nicosia was burned; Britain responded by suspending the constitution and ruling autocratically through the 1930s. Turkish Cypriots, about 18 per cent of the population, generally opposed enosis and increasingly favoured partition (taksim) or continued British rule.

The EOKA emergency and independence

After a 1950 church-organised plebiscite in which Greek Cypriots overwhelmingly endorsed enosis, the armed campaign of EOKA, led by Georgios Grivas, began on 1 April 1955. A state of emergency followed; Archbishop Makarios III was exiled to the Seychelles in 1956. Exhausted by the insurgency and intercommunal violence, Britain negotiated the Zurich and London Agreements of 1959 with Greece and Turkey. Cyprus became an independent republic on 16 August 1960, with Britain retaining the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia.

Legacy

British rule left Cyprus with English-language legal and administrative institutions, left-hand traffic, and continuing membership of the Commonwealth; the two Sovereign Base Areas remain British territory and a live issue in Cypriot politics.
