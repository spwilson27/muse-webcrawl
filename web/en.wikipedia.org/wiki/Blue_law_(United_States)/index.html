Blue laws in the United States

[Image placeholder: Blue laws in the United States]

Blue laws, also known as Sunday laws, are laws in the United States that restrict or ban some or all Sunday activities, particularly retail sales, for religious or secular reasons. They were historically promoted to observe a day of worship or rest, especially by Christian denominations.

History

Sunday-closing laws date to the colonial era; the name "blue law" is often traced to an apocryphal 1781 account of strict New Haven ordinances supposedly printed on blue paper. In McGowan v. Maryland (1961), the U.S. Supreme Court upheld Maryland's Sunday closing laws, reasoning that they had acquired a secular purpose of providing a uniform day of rest.

Modern status

Most states have repealed general Sunday sales bans, but restrictions persist in places: Bergen County, New Jersey, famously retains broad Sunday retail closures, and many states still restrict Sunday sales of alcohol or automobiles. Some limits have also been relaxed through local-option referendums.
