Mongkut (Thai: มงกุฎ; 18 October 1804 – 1 October 1868), regnal name Phra Bat Somdet Phra Chom Klao Chao Yu Hua, also known as Rama IV, was the fourth king of Siam (Thailand) under the Chakri dynasty, reigning from 1851 to 1868. A former Buddhist monk for 27 years, he was a scholar of languages, science and astronomy who opened Siam to Western trade and ideas while preserving its independence, and he is remembered in Thailand as the "Father of Science and Technology". Outside Thailand he is best known — in a heavily fictionalised form — as the king in Anna and the King of Siam and the musical The King and I.

Life as a monk

Born at the Thonburi Palace, Mongkut was the son of Rama II and the expected heir, but on his father's death in 1824 the throne passed to his half-brother Nangklao (Rama III). Mongkut entered the monkhood, where he spent 27 years. Dissatisfied with the lax discipline he found, he founded the Thammayut Nikaya, a reformist order of Theravada Buddhism emphasising strict adherence to the Pali canon and rational interpretation, which remains one of Thailand's two monastic orders. As a monk he travelled widely in the countryside, learned Latin from a French bishop and English from American missionaries, and studied Western astronomy and science.

Reign

On Rama III's death in 1851 Mongkut ascended the throne at 46, with his brother Pinklao as Second King. Facing pressure from Britain and France, then expanding in Burma, Malaya and Indochina, he chose engagement over isolation: in 1855 he signed the Bowring Treaty with Britain, opening trade, fixing low tariffs and granting extraterritoriality, followed by similar treaties with the United States (1856) and other powers. He modernised the administration and military, promoted Western education and printing, reformed laws (including measures on the status of women and the abolition of prostration before the king by courtiers in Western dress contexts), and founded the Royal Thai Mint. His reforms laid the groundwork for the fuller modernisation carried out by his son Chulalongkorn.

Astronomy and death

Mongkut's most famous scientific feat was his precise calculation of the total solar eclipse of 18 August 1868, whose path of totality over southern Siam he predicted — by traditional accounts, two years in advance and more accurately than contemporary French astronomers. He organised an observation expedition to Wa Ko, near Hua Hin, attended by the French and British officials. During the trip he contracted malaria and died in Bangkok on 1 October 1868. Thailand marks 18 August as National Science Day in his honour.

Family and portrayal

Mongkut had numerous consorts in the Inner Court and 82 children, among them Chulalongkorn (Rama V) and the reform-minded prince Devawongse. His court employed the English governess Anna Leonowens (1862–1867) to teach his children; her embellished memoirs inspired Margaret Landon's novel, the Rodgers and Hammerstein musical The King and I (1951) and several films, which are banned in Thailand for their perceived disrespect toward the king.
