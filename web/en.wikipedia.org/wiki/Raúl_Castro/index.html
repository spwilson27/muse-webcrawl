Raúl Modesto Castro Ruz (born 3 June 1931) is a Cuban retired politician and general who served as president of Cuba from 2008 to 2018 and as First Secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba from 2011 to 2021. The younger brother of Fidel Castro, he was a commander of the Cuban Revolution, then Minister of the Revolutionary Armed Forces for nearly five decades, before succeeding his brother and overseeing a cautious programme of economic reform and the 2014–2016 rapprochement with the United States.

Revolutionary career

Born at the family estate in Birán, Raúl joined his brother's movement against the Batista dictatorship, took part in the failed Moncada Barracks attack of 26 July 1953 and was imprisoned on the Isle of Pines. Exiled to Mexico, he met Che Guevara and returned to Cuba aboard the yacht Granma in 1956. In February 1958 he opened the Frank País Eastern Front in the Sierra Cristal, displaying the organisational discipline that made him Fidel's most trusted deputy.

Defence minister

From 1959 to 2008 Raúl Castro headed the Revolutionary Armed Forces, building them into one of Latin America's most effective militaries and directing Cuba's interventions in Angola and Ethiopia. After the collapse of the Soviet Union he pushed the military into tourism and business management during the "Special Period".

Presidency

He became acting president on 31 July 2006 when Fidel fell ill, and was formally elected president by the National Assembly on 24 February 2008. His government legalised private small businesses and farmers' markets, distributed idle land, abolished exit permits, limited presidential terms, and in December 2014 — in a simultaneous announcement with Barack Obama — restored diplomatic relations with the United States, leading to the reopening of embassies in 2015. He stepped down as president in April 2018 in favour of Miguel Díaz-Canel, and retired as party leader in April 2021, ending more than six decades of Castro family rule.
