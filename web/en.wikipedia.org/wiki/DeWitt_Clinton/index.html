DeWitt Clinton

Born2 March 1769, Little Britain, New York
Died11 February 1828, Albany, New York
OfficesUS Senator; Mayor of New York City; Governor of New York (1817–1822, 1825–1828)

DeWitt Clinton (2 March 1769 – 11 February 1828) was an American politician and naturalist who served as a United States senator, mayor of New York City and sixth governor of New York. He is best remembered as the driving force behind the construction of the Erie Canal, which transformed New York into the young nation's commercial centre.

Clinton's Erie Canal linked the Great Lakes to the Hudson River and the Atlantic.

Early life and career

Clinton was born in Little Britain, New York, the nephew of long-serving governor George Clinton. Educated at Kingston Academy and Columbia College, he entered politics as his uncle's secretary. He served in the state legislature and the US Senate before becoming mayor of New York City, an office he held for most of 1803–1815. As mayor he promoted public education, planning and sanitation, and helped found institutions including the New-York Historical Society. In 1812 he ran for president as a fusion candidate backed by dissident Republicans and Federalists, losing to James Madison.

The Erie Canal

As governor from 1817, Clinton championed the canal connecting Lake Erie to the Hudson at Albany. Derided as "Clinton's Ditch", the 363-mile waterway was completed in 1825, an engineering triumph that slashed freight costs, spurred settlement of the Old Northwest and made New York City the nation's leading port. Removed from the canal commission by political rivals in 1824, he was triumphantly re-elected governor and presided over the canal's opening, pouring Lake Erie water into New York Harbor in the "Wedding of the Waters" ceremony of November 1825. He died in office in 1828.
