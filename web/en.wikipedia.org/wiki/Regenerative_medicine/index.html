Regenerative medicine

Regenerative medicine aims to repair or replace damaged tissues and organs — through stem cells, tissue engineering, gene therapy and biomaterials — rather than merely treating symptoms.

Approaches

Established successes include bone-marrow (haematopoietic stem cell) transplantation for blood cancers, skin grafts grown for burns, and corneal stem cell therapy. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs, Yamanaka 2006–07) can be made from a patient's own cells and turned towards many cell types.

Engineers combine cells with scaffold materials and growth factors: lab-grown bladders and tracheas have been implanted; organoids (mini-organs from stem cells) serve research and drug testing.

Challenges

Major hurdles remain: guiding cells to integrate and function, avoiding tumours and immune rejection, and scaling manufacture. Many marketed ‘stem cell’ treatments are unproven, and regulators warn against clinics selling them. Genuine trials for Parkinson's disease, heart failure and Diabetes are under way.
