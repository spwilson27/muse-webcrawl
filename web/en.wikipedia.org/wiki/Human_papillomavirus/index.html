Human papillomavirus


Electron micrograph of human papillomavirus (HPV).

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a group of related viruses. Some types can cause health problems, including genital warts and cancers. HPV is the most common sexually transmitted infection (STI) worldwide. There are more than 200 related viruses, of which more than 40 can be spread through direct sexual contact; about a dozen types are classified as high-risk because they can cause cancer.

Disease

Most HPV infections cause no symptoms and resolve spontaneously. Persistent infection with high-risk types, especially HPV 16 and 18, can lead to cervical cancer, as well as cancers of the anus, oropharynx, penis, vagina, and vulva. Low-risk types such as HPV 6 and 11 cause most genital warts and recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.

Prevention

Vaccines such as Gardasil 9 protect against the HPV types that cause most HPV cancers and genital warts, and are recommended for adolescents and young adults. Cervical cancer screening (Pap tests and HPV tests) is recommended for early detection of precancerous changes.
