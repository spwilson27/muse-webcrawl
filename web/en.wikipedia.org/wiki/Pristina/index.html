Pristina (Albanian Prishtina) is the capital and largest city of Kosovo. It is the administrative center of the eponymous municipality and district. Pristina is Kosovo's primary economic, financial, political, and trade center, and the seat of its government and parliament.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Neolithic period; the Roman city of Ulpiana stood nearby. In the medieval period Pristina was a royal estate of Serbian kings, and after the Battle of Kosovo in 1389 it gradually came under Ottoman rule, under which it became an important trading town on the routes between the Adriatic and the central Balkans. Following the Balkan Wars it was incorporated into Serbia, and after the Second World War it became the capital of the Autonomous Province of Kosovo within Yugoslavia.

Pristina was heavily affected by the Kosovo War of 1998–1999. After Kosovo's declaration of independence in 2008, the city became the capital of the new state, hosting government institutions, foreign embassies, and the University of Pristina. Notable landmarks include the National Library of Kosovo, famous for its distinctive domed design, the Newborn monument commemorating independence, and the Cathedral of Saint Mother Teresa.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly ethnic Albanian. The municipality had roughly 200,000 inhabitants at the 2011 census, with subsequent estimates placing the figure higher owing to internal migration to the capital.

Transport

Pristina International Airport, southwest of the city, is Kosovo's only international airport. The city is a hub of the national road network, with motorways connecting it to Albania and North Macedonia.
