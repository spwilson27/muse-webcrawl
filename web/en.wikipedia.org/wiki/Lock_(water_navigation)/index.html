Lock (water navigation)

[Image: A canal lock filling to raise a narrowboat to the higher level]

Overview

A lock is a device used for raising and lowering boats and ships between stretches of water of different levels on river and canal waterways. The defining feature is a fixed chamber whose water level can be varied: the vessel enters, gates close, water is admitted or released through paddles/slucues until the level equalises with the other side, and the far gates open.

History and types

Chamber (pound) locks were developed in China (984 AD, by Qiao Weiyue) and in medieval Europe (the Netherlands and Italy, 14th–15th centuries; Leonardo designed mitre gates). Locks range from cottage-garden canal locks to the colossal ship locks of the Panama Canal (whose 2016 Neopanamax locks are 427 m long) and the Three Gorges ship lift/locks. Flight staircases (e.g. Caen Hill's 29 locks) climb hills. Flash locks and boat lifts are alternative technologies.
