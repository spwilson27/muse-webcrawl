Narmada Bachao Andolan

The Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA; Hindi: नर्मदा बचाओ आंदोलन, "Save the Narmada Movement") is an Indian social movement led by tribal people, Adivasis, farmers, environmentalists and human rights activists against a large number of dam projects across the Narmada River, which flows through the states of Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.

The movement is best known for its decades-long campaign against the Sardar Sarovar Dam, one of the largest dams proposed on the river. Its most prominent leader is Medha Patkar, and the writer Arundhati Roy became a well-known supporter.

History

The movement began in the mid-1980s as protests by communities facing submergence by the Sardar Sarovar and other Narmada valley projects coalesced into an organised struggle over rehabilitation, environmental impact, and the costs and benefits of large dams. The NBA used marches, hunger strikes (janandolan), sit-ins in rising reservoir waters (jal satyagraha), and litigation to press its case.

Impact

The movement's campaign led the World Bank to commission an independent review (the Morse Commission) of the Sardar Sarovar project, and the Bank withdrew from financing it in 1993. In 2000 the Supreme Court of India allowed construction of the dam to proceed subject to rehabilitation conditions — a ruling the NBA criticised. The Sardar Sarovar Dam was completed and dedicated in 2017, while the NBA continued to campaign over the rehabilitation of displaced families. The movement is widely regarded as a landmark in Indian environmental and civil-society activism and influenced global debates about large dams, including the World Commission on Dams.
