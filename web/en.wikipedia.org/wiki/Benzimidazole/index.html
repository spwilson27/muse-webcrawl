Benzimidazole is an organic compound with the formula C₇H₆N₂. It is a bicyclic aromatic heterocycle consisting of a benzene ring fused to an imidazole ring. Benzimidazole itself is a white solid, but far more important is the benzimidazole ring system, which occurs in many pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals and natural products.

Structure and properties

The molecule is planar and aromatic. The imidazole portion carries one pyrrole-like N–H nitrogen and one pyridine-like nitrogen, making the compound both weakly basic (the conjugate acid has a pKa of about 5.5) and weakly acidic at the N–H (pKa about 12.8). The two nitrogens can exchange the proton by tautomerism, so 5- and 6-substituted benzimidazoles are equivalent in the unsubstituted N–H form. Benzimidazoles readily form metal complexes through the pyridine-like nitrogen.

Synthesis

Benzimidazole is classically prepared by condensing o-phenylenediamine with formic acid. Substituted benzimidazoles are obtained analogously from o-phenylenediamine and carboxylic acids or aldehydes under oxidative conditions — a general route that makes the scaffold a favourite starting point in medicinal chemistry.

Occurrence and applications

The benzimidazole nucleus appears in nature in vitamin B12, whose lower axial ligand is 5,6-dimethylbenzimidazole. Synthetic benzimidazoles are an important drug class:

  • Proton pump inhibitors such as omeprazole and pantoprazole, among the most widely prescribed drugs in the world.
  • Anthelmintics such as albendazole, mebendazole and thiabendazole, which bind parasite tubulin.
  • Antihypertensives of the "sartan" family, e.g. candesartan and telmisartan, contain benzimidazole cores.

Beyond medicine, benzimidazole derivatives are used as fungicides (e.g. benomyl, carbendazim), corrosion inhibitors, and as monomers for high-performance polymers such as polybenzimidazole (PBI), a heat-resistant fibre and membrane material used in fuel cells.
