Mass ratio

In aerospace engineering, the mass ratio is a measure of the efficiency of a rocket: the ratio of the rocket's wet mass (structure, propellant, and payload) to its dry mass (structure and payload without propellant).

Definition

Mass ratio = (dry mass + propellant) / dry mass. Through the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation, the achievable change in velocity Δv = exhaust velocity × ln(mass ratio). Higher mass ratios give greater Δv for a given engine.

Typical values

Single-stage rockets typically achieve mass ratios around 10–20 with modern structures; staging effectively multiplies performance by discarding empty tanks. In chemistry, "mass ratio" can also refer to reaction mass efficiency measures.
