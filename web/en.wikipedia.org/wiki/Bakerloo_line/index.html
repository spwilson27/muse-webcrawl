The Bakerloo line is a London Underground line that runs from Harrow & Wealdstone in suburban north-west London to Elephant & Castle in south London, via the West End. Its name is a portmanteau of two of its original stations, Baker Street and Waterloo. Printed in brown on the Tube map, the line serves 25 stations over about 23 kilometres (14.5 miles) and carries well over 100 million passengers a year.

History

The line opened on 10 March 1906 as the Baker Street & Waterloo Railway, promoted by the financier Whitaker Wright, whose fraud and suicide in 1904 nearly wrecked the project before Charles Yerkes's Underground group took control. Extensions followed to Paddington, Queen's Park and, over surface tracks shared with what became London Overground, to Watford Junction. In 1939 a new branch to Finchley Road and Wembley Park opened (later transferred to the Jubilee line in 1979), and the section north of Queen's Park was progressively cut back to Harrow & Wealdstone. Stonebridge Park and other stations retain notable Leslie Green and later architecture.

Services and future

Trains are formed of 1972 Stock, among the oldest still in service on the Underground. Most services run between Queen's Park and Elephant & Castle, with fewer continuing to Harrow & Wealdstone. A long-planned extension from Elephant & Castle via Old Kent Road to Lewisham has been safeguarded, and replacement of the ageing fleet is proposed as part of the Underground's upgrade programme.
