ADMET

ADMET is an acronym used in pharmacology and drug discovery for absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity — the disposition of a pharmaceutical compound within an organism. The related term ADME omits the explicit toxicity component.

Components

Absorption describes how a drug enters the bloodstream; distribution how it spreads through tissues; metabolism how enzymes, especially hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes, chemically modify it; excretion how it and its metabolites leave the body, mainly via the kidneys and liver; and toxicity the adverse effects it may cause.

In drug development

Poor ADMET properties are a leading cause of late-stage drug failures, so pharmaceutical research uses in vitro assays and in silico predictive models early in discovery to screen out unsuitable candidates. See also Pharmacokinetics.
