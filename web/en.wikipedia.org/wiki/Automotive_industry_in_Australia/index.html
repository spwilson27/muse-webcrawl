Automotive industry in Australia

The automotive industry in Australia was a significant sector of the nation's manufacturing economy for most of the twentieth century and into the twenty-first. At its peak the industry supported complete design, engineering and volume production of passenger cars unique to the Australian market, most famously by Holden, Ford Australia, Toyota Australia and Mitsubishi Motors Australia.

History

Local coachbuilding gave way to integrated manufacturing after the Second World War, when the federal government encouraged domestic production; the Holden 48-215 of 1948 became the first mass-produced Australian-designed car. Tariff protection sustained the industry for decades, but the Button car plan of the 1980s began a long liberalization that consolidated models and plants. Successive reductions in assistance, a strong Australian dollar and small domestic market scale made local production increasingly unviable.

End of mass production

Mitsubishi closed its Adelaide plant in 2008. In 2013–2014 Ford, General Motors Holden and Toyota each announced they would end Australian manufacturing; the last Australian-built cars rolled off the lines in 2017, when Toyota's Altona plant and Holden's Elizabeth plant closed within weeks of each other. Australia now imports all new passenger vehicles, principally from Japan, Thailand, South Korea and China, although a local engineering, design and aftermarket sector persists, and firms such as Ford's Australian product development centre continue to design vehicles for global markets.
