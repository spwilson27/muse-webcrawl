HD 93129A

ConstellationCarina
Distanceabout 7,500 light-years
SystemTrumpler 14, Carina Nebula
ComponentsAa (O2 If*), Ab (O3.5 V), B (O3.5 V)

HD 93129A is a massive binary star system in the Carina Nebula, about 7,500 light-years from Earth. It lies within the young open cluster Trumpler 14 and consists of two of the most luminous and massive stars known in the Milky Way.

Components

The primary component, HD 93129Aa, is a spectral type O2 If* supergiant, one of the earliest-type O stars known, with a surface temperature of roughly 42,500 K and a luminosity well over a million times that of the Sun. Its close companion, HD 93129Ab, is an O3.5 main-sequence star. A third component, HD 93129B, also an O3.5 dwarf, lies a few arcseconds away.

Properties

Both components of HD 93129A are among the intrinsically brightest stars in the Galaxy, each radiating several million solar luminosities when bolometric corrections are included. The system drives powerful stellar winds, and the collision of the winds of Aa and Ab produces thermal X-ray emission. Because of its extreme properties, HD 93129A is a key test case for theories of massive-star evolution and mass loss.
