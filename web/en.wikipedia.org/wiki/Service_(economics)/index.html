Service (economics)

A service is an act or use for which a consumer, company, or government is willing to pay. In economics, services are intangible activities that are produced and consumed simultaneously, do not result in the ownership of anything physical, and cannot be stored for later use. Examples include work done by barbers, doctors, lawyers, mechanics, banks, insurance companies and other service providers.

Characteristics

Services are traditionally distinguished from goods by four characteristics, often abbreviated IHIP: intangibility (they cannot be touched or seen before purchase), heterogeneity (quality varies with provider and occasion), inseparability (production and consumption occur together), and perishability (unused capacity, such as an empty hotel room, cannot be inventoried).

Service sector

The service sector, or tertiary sector, is the largest part of most developed economies, commonly accounting for more than 70% of GDP and employment in advanced countries. It encompasses retail, transport, hospitality, finance, health care, education, professional services, information technology and public administration. The long-run growth of the service share of output is a standard feature of economic development, described in the three-sector model of primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing) and tertiary activities.
