Mineraloid

A mineraloid is a naturally occurring, mineral-like substance that does not exhibit crystallinity — that is, it lacks the ordered atomic structure required by the strict definition of a mineral. Mineraloids possess chemical compositions that may vary beyond fixed ranges and have amorphous structures.

Examples

Common mineraloids include obsidian (a volcanic glass), opal (hydrated silica gel), amber (fossilised tree resin), pearl, jet (a type of lignite), lechatelierite (fused silica glass) and shungite. Some, like opal, were historically classified as minerals; others, such as amber, are organic in origin.

Properties

Because they are amorphous, mineraloids show no cleavage planes based on crystal structure and may display conchoidal fracture. They are widely used as gemstones and ornamental materials.
