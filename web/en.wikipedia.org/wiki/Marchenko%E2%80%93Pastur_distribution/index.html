Marchenko–Pastur distribution

The Marchenko–Pastur distribution, also called the Marchenko–Pastur law, is the limiting distribution of singular values of large rectangular random matrices. It was derived by Volodymyr Marchenko and Leonid Pastur in 1967.

Statement

For an N × T matrix with independent entries of mean zero and variance σ², as both dimensions grow with fixed ratio, the empirical distribution of eigenvalues of the sample covariance matrix converges to a density supported on an interval [λ−, λ+] determined by σ² and the aspect ratio, with a point mass at zero when the ratio exceeds one. The law is fundamental in random matrix theory, free probability, multivariate statistics, and wireless communications.
