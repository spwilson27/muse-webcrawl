Ex cathedra

Ex cathedra is a Latin phrase meaning "from the chair", used in Roman Catholic theology to describe a definitive teaching by the Pope when he speaks in his capacity as pastor and teacher of all Christians, defining a doctrine concerning faith or morals to be held by the whole Church. Such a pronouncement is considered, under the doctrine of papal infallibility, to be preserved from error.

Ex cathedra
Context: Roman Catholic theology
Related: Papal infallibility, First Vatican Council

Definition

The phrase refers to the cathedra, the chair of a bishop as symbol of teaching authority, ultimately that of Saint Peter. The First Vatican Council's constitution Pastor aeternus (1870) defined the conditions: the Pope must speak as supreme pastor, define a doctrine of faith or morals, and intend it to bind the universal Church.

Instances

Only two declarations are widely agreed to meet the definition unambiguously: Immaculate Conception by Pius IX in Ineffabilis Deus (1854), and the Assumption of Mary by Pius XII in Munificentissimus Deus (1950). Earlier papal teachings are debated by theologians and historians.
