Huntington's disease (HD), also known as Huntington's chorea, is a neurodegenerative disorder that is mostly inherited. It typically begins between the ages of 30 and 50 and progressively affects movement, cognition and behaviour. The condition is named after the American physician George Huntington, who described it in 1872.

Signs and symptoms

Early symptoms are often subtle changes in mood, cognition and coordination. As the disease advances, uncoordinated, jerky body movements known as chorea become more apparent, along with declining mental abilities, dementia, psychiatric problems such as depression, and difficulties with speech and swallowing. Physical abilities gradually worsen until coordinated movement becomes very difficult.

Cause

HD is caused by an expanded CAG trinucleotide repeat in the huntingtin gene (HTT) on chromosome 4. A longer repeat generally leads to earlier onset, and the repeat can expand when passed from parent to child, producing the phenomenon of anticipation. Each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting the mutation.

Diagnosis and treatment

Genetic testing can confirm the diagnosis at any stage, including before symptoms appear. There is currently no cure; treatments such as tetrabenazine can reduce chorea, and multidisciplinary care addresses symptoms and quality of life. Life expectancy is typically 15 to 20 years from the onset of visible symptoms.
