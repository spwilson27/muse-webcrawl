1840s in Western fashion

Fashion in the 1840s in Western Europe and North America is characterised by a retreat from the exuberance of the 1830s toward a narrower, more sober silhouette. Narrow sleeves, lowered and sloping shoulders, and pointed bonnets defined women's dress, while men's clothing grew darker and more restrained.

Women's fashion

The wide, gigot sleeves of the previous decade deflated into close-fitting sleeves set into dropped shoulder seams. Skirts widened gradually through layered petticoats, foreshadowing the crinoline. Necklines were modest; evening dresses often had a bertha collar. Hair was parted in the centre and drawn smoothly over the ears.

Men's fashion

Men wore frock coats with nipped waists, increasingly in dark colours, with trousers replacing breeches for day wear. The top hat became standard, and the cravat gave way to smaller neckties. Waistcoats remained a permitted outlet for colour and pattern.

Technology and society

The spread of the sewing machine patent, ready-made clothing, and fashion magazines such as Godey's Lady's Book widened access to fashionable dress beyond the elite.
