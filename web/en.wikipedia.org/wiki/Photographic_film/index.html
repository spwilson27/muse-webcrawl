Photographic film

Photographic film is a strip or sheet of transparent plastic film base coated with a light-sensitive emulsion containing microscopic silver halide crystals. When exposed to light and chemically developed, the emulsion forms a visible image.

Structure and chemistry

The emulsion's sensitivity (film speed, expressed as an ISO number) depends on crystal size and composition. Exposure creates an invisible latent image, which development amplifies into metallic silver (black-and-white) or dye clouds (colour film). Colour films contain three emulsion layers sensitive to blue, green and red light.

Formats

Common formats include 35 mm (135) roll film, 120 medium-format film, large-format sheet film, and motion-picture stocks. Instant film incorporates its own processing chemistry.

History and decline

Flexible roll film, commercialised by George Eastman's Kodak in the late 1880s, made photography widely accessible. Digital photography displaced film in the 2000s, but film survives in artistic, cinematic and archival niches, with a modest revival among enthusiasts.
