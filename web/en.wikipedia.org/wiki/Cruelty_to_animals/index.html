Cruelty to animals

Cruelty to animals, also called animal abuse, animal neglect or animal cruelty, is the infliction of suffering or harm upon non-human animals by humans, whether by omission (neglect) or by commission.

Forms

Forms include neglect, abandonment, organised fighting such as dogfighting and cockfighting, cruelty in intensive farming, fur production, and animal testing. Laws and the concept of animal welfare vary widely by country and by species.

History and law

Early laws include Ireland's 1635 act and Britain's Cruel Treatment of Cattle Act 1822 and Cruelty to Animals Act 1835, followed by the founding of the RSPCA (1824). Modern statutes, such as the UK Animal Welfare Act 2006 and the US Animal Welfare Act and PACT Act (2019), set duties of care and penalties.

Link debates

Research discusses a statistical association between animal cruelty and interpersonal violence, though causation and interpretation are debated.
