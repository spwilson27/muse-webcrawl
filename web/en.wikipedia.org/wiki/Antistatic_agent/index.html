Antistatic agent

An antistatic agent is a compound used for the treatment of materials or their surfaces in order to reduce or eliminate the buildup of static electricity. Static charge can damage sensitive electronic components, attract dust, cause materials to cling, and in flammable environments produce sparks capable of igniting vapors or dusts.

Mechanism and types

Most antistatic agents work by making a surface slightly conductive or hygroscopic: they attract moisture from the air, forming a thin water layer that dissipates charge. External agents are applied to finished goods as sprays, dips, or wipes, and include quaternary ammonium salts, ethoxylated amines, and glycerol-based compounds — as in fabric softeners and hair conditioners. Internal antistatic agents are compounded directly into polymers during manufacture; they migrate gradually to the surface ("blooming") and provide longer-lasting protection.

Applications

Antistatic treatments are used on plastics, textiles, packaging films for electronics, flooring, and fuel and solvent handling equipment. In electronics manufacturing, antistatic bags, mats, and sprays form part of electrostatic discharge (ESD) control programs. Effectiveness is typically characterized by surface resistivity or static decay time, and performance depends strongly on ambient humidity.
