Howth (Irish: Beann Éadair, meaning "Éadar's peak") is an affluent peninsular village and outer suburb of Dublin, Ireland, situated on the Howth Head peninsula at the northern side of Dublin Bay. Originally a small fishing village, it has developed into a busy coastal suburb with a working harbour, yacht marina, restaurants and popular cliff walks. The settlement occupies the greater part of the peninsula, which is connected to the mainland by the narrow isthmus at Sutton.

History

The name Howth is thought to derive from the Old Norse hǫfuð, "head", reflecting Viking settlement from around 819 AD. The area appears in Irish legend — including a tale of Éadar — and was the site of a Norse trading port. After the Anglo-Norman invasion, the St Lawrence family became lords of Howth, holding the lands from 1177 for more than 800 years; their seat, Howth Castle, stands near the village. In July 1914, the yacht Asgard landed a cargo of rifles at Howth for the Irish Volunteers, an event commemorated on the harbour's east pier.

Features

Howth Harbour, built in the early 19th century, was for a time the packet station for the mail boats between Ireland and Holyhead before the service moved to Dún Laoghaire. Today it hosts a fishing fleet, Ireland's national fishery training centre nearby, a large marina, and popular seafood restaurants. Landmarks include the Martello tower on the harbour, the Baily Lighthouse on the south-eastern point of the head, Howth Castle and its rhododendron gardens, and the ruins of St Mary's Abbey. Ireland's Eye, a small island bird sanctuary, lies just offshore. The village is served by a DART railway station and is the terminus of the Dublin–Howth line.
