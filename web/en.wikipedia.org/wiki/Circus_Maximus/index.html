Circus Maximus

The Circus Maximus (Latin for "greatest circus") is an ancient Roman chariot-racing stadium and mass entertainment venue in Rome, Italy, situated in the valley between the Aventine and Palatine hills. It was the largest stadium in ancient Rome and the Roman world.

Circus Maximus
Location: Rome, Italy
Length: c. 621 m; Width: c. 118 m
Capacity: c. 150,000
Built: traditionally 6th century BC; rebuilt under Trajan

History

Tradition dates its first construction to the kings of Rome. Julius Caesar enlarged it, and after fire damage Trajan rebuilt it in stone. It hosted chariot races — notably the four factions: Reds, Blues, Greens and Whites — as well as gladiatorial shows, wild-beast hunts, public feasts and triumphal processions. The central barrier (spina) carried Egyptian obelisks, two of which now stand in Piazza del Popolo and by the Lateran.

Later use

The last races were held in the 6th century AD. The site is now a public park, used for concerts and celebrations, retaining its elongated outline.
