Baily Lighthouse

[Image: The Baily Lighthouse on Howth Head, County Dublin]

The Baily Lighthouse is a lighthouse on the southeastern part of Howth Head in County Dublin, Ireland. It marks the northern entrance to Dublin Bay and is operated by the Commissioners of Irish Lights. The lighthouse takes its name from the Baily (or Bailey) area of Howth.

History

A coal-fired light and later a primitive lighthouse existed on the headland from the 17th century; the first substantial tower was lit in 1790 but stood too high and was often obscured by fog. The present lower tower, designed under George Halpin, came into service in 1814. A new optic installed in 1865 made the Baily a pioneering station for gas illumination experiments in the 1860s–70s. The station also operated a famous experimental fog bell and later fog signals.

Automation

The Baily was the last Irish lighthouse to be automated and to lose its resident keepers, in March 1997. The former keepers' houses are now used as accommodation for visiting artists and writers.
