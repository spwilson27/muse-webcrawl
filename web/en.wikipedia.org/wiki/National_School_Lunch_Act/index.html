National School Lunch Act

The National School Lunch Act (NSLA), officially the Richard B. Russell National School Lunch Act, is a United States federal law signed by President Harry S. Truman in 1946 that created the National School Lunch Program. The program provides nutritionally balanced, low-cost or free lunches to children in public and nonprofit private schools and residential child care institutions.

Background

School feeding efforts in the United States dated to the 19th century and expanded during the Great Depression, when federal surplus commodities were distributed to schools. Concerns about malnutrition among military draftees rejected during the Second World War helped build support for a permanent program, framed both as a child-health measure and as support for American agriculture. Senator Richard Russell of Georgia was a leading sponsor.

The program

Under the act, the United States Department of Agriculture reimburses participating schools for meals served, at higher rates for children from low-income families who qualify for free or reduced-price meals. Schools must serve lunches meeting federal nutrition standards. The program has grown to serve roughly 30 million children daily and is the second-largest U.S. food and nutrition assistance program after the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. The act has been amended many times, notably by the Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010, which updated nutrition standards.
