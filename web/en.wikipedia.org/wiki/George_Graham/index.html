George Graham (7 July 1673 – 16 November 1751) was a British clockmaker, watchmaker and maker of scientific instruments, widely regarded as one of the most important horologists of the eighteenth century. He was the inventor of the deadbeat escapement and the mercury-compensated pendulum, and he produced astronomical instruments used throughout Europe.

Early life and career

Graham was born in Kirklinton, Cumberland, and moved to London, where he was apprenticed to the watchmaker Henry Aske in 1687. He later entered the workshop of the celebrated horologist Thomas Tompion, became his trusted assistant and business partner, and married Tompion's niece, Elizabeth Tompion. On Tompion's death in 1713 Graham inherited the workshop at the corner of Water Lane and Fleet Street in London.

Inventions and work

Around 1715 Graham devised the deadbeat escapement, which greatly reduced the recoil and friction of earlier anchor escapements in pendulum clocks and so improved long-term timekeeping. In 1721 he invented the mercury pendulum, in which a jar of mercury expands and contracts with temperature to compensate for the thermal expansion of the pendulum rod — a design that remained standard in precision regulators for more than a century. He also developed the cylinder escapement for watches and improved the construction of the orrery, the mechanical model of the solar system that takes its name from the Earl of Orrery, for whom such a device was made using Graham's design.

Graham made astronomical instruments for the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, including the great mural quadrant used by Edmond Halley, and supplied instruments to observatories across Europe. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1721 and published several papers in its Philosophical Transactions.

Legacy

Graham died in London in 1751 and was buried in the nave of Westminster Abbey in the same tomb as Tompion. The modern Swiss watch brand Graham is named in his honour. His precision regulators, signed "Geo. Graham London", remain highly prized by collectors.
