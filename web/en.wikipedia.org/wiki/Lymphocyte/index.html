Lymphocyte

A lymphocyte is a type of White blood cell that carries out adaptive immunity — the specific, remembering arm of the Immune system. The three main types are B cells, T cells and natural killer (NK) cells.

Types and functions

B lymphocytes mature in bone marrow and produce antibodies; on meeting their antigen they become plasma cells and memory cells. T lymphocytes mature in the thymus: helper T cells (CD4) coordinate responses, cytotoxic T cells (CD8) kill infected and cancerous cells. NK cells are innate lymphocytes that kill abnormal cells without prior sensitisation. Together lymphocytes make up 20–40% of white blood cells and most of the cells in the lymph nodes and spleen.

Clinical significance

HIV destroys CD4 helper T cells, causing AIDS. Cancers of lymphocytes are the lymphomas and lymphocytic leukaemias; engineered T cells (CAR-T therapy) are a breakthrough cancer treatment, and lymphocyte counts feature in routine blood tests.
