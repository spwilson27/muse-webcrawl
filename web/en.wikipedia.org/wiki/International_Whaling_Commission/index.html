The International Whaling Commission (IWC) is the international body established by the International Convention for the Regulation of Whaling, signed in Washington, D.C., on 2 December 1946, to provide for the proper conservation of whale stocks and the orderly development of the whaling industry. Its headquarters are in Impington, near Cambridge, England.

Role and decisions

The Commission sets (or prohibits) catch limits, designates whale sanctuaries, coordinates research and adopts regulations by a three-quarters majority. Its most consequential decision was the 1982 adoption of a moratorium on commercial whaling, which took effect in the 1985–86 season with catch limits set to zero. The IWC also established the Indian Ocean Sanctuary (1979) and the Southern Ocean Sanctuary (1994); a proposed South Atlantic Sanctuary has repeatedly been debated.

Controversies

Members are divided between conservationist governments and whaling states such as Norway, Iceland and (until its withdrawal in 2019) Japan, which resumed commercial whaling within its own waters after leaving the IWC. "Scientific permit" whaling under Article VIII of the Convention was long contested; in 2014 the International Court of Justice ruled that Japan's Antarctic JARPA II programme was not for purposes of scientific research. Aboriginal subsistence whaling quotas are separately authorised for indigenous communities in Alaska, Chukotka, Greenland and elsewhere.
