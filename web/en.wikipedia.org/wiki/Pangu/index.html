Pangu

Pangu (Chinese: 盤古) is a primordial being and creation figure in Chinese mythology and Taoism. According to the myth, Pangu emerged from a cosmic egg and separated heaven and earth, which had been joined in formless chaos; in many versions his body then became the features of the world after his death.

The myth

In the earliest written accounts, from the third century CE — notably Xu Zheng's Sanwu Liji — the universe began as an egg-like chaos in which Pangu slept for 18,000 years. Waking, he broke the egg (in later retellings, with an axe), and the light, pure parts rose to become the sky (yang) while the heavy, turbid parts sank to become the earth (yin). Each day Pangu grew taller, pushing heaven and earth further apart, until after another 18,000 years they were fixed in place.

Transformation of the body

When Pangu died, his body transformed into the world: his breath became the wind and clouds, his voice the thunder, his eyes the sun and moon, his limbs the mountains, his blood the rivers, his flesh the soil, his hair the stars and plants, and the parasites on his body, impregnated by the wind, became human beings. The myth is also preserved among peoples of southern China, including in the traditions of the Yao and Miao.
