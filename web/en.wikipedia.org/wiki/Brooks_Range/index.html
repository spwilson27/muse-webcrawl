The Brooks Range is a mountain range in far northern Alaska stretching about 1,100 km (700 miles) from west to east across the state's Arctic region, with extensions into Canada's Yukon. It forms the drainage divide between rivers flowing north to the Arctic Ocean and those flowing south to the Yukon River.

Geography

The range was named in 1925 for geologist Alfred Hulse Brooks. Its highest summit is Mount Isto (2,736 m / 8,976 ft), identified by modern surveys as slightly higher than the long-assumed highest peak, Mount Chamberlin. The range includes the remote Gates of the Arctic National Park and the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, home to caribou, grizzly bears, wolves, and Dall sheep.

Crossings

The Dalton Highway crosses the range at Atigun Pass (1,415 m), the only road crossing, built to support the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, which carries oil from Prudhoe Bay on the Arctic coast south to Valdez.
