Trauma center

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A trauma center, or trauma centre, is a hospital equipped and staffed to provide comprehensive emergency medical services to patients suffering traumatic injuries. It differs from a general hospital emergency department in maintaining in-house, around-the-clock coverage by trauma surgeons, anaesthesiologists, and specialists such as neurosurgeons and orthopaedic surgeons, together with immediately available operating rooms, intensive care, imaging, and blood transfusion services.

Levels

In the United States, the American College of Surgeons verifies trauma centers against criteria published in its resources document, and states designate them within regional trauma systems:

  • Level I — provides total care for every aspect of injury, typically at a large teaching hospital, with research and education obligations and minimum annual volumes of severely injured patients.
  • Level II — provides definitive care comparable in most respects to Level I but without the same research and residency requirements.
  • Level III — provides resuscitation, emergency surgery, and stabilisation, with transfer agreements for patients needing higher-level care.
  • Level IV and V — rural and smaller facilities that evaluate, stabilise, and transfer patients, providing advanced life support before transport.

Separate criteria exist for paediatric trauma centers. Other countries use analogous models — for example, England's major trauma centres within regional trauma networks established from 2012, and major trauma services in Australia and Germany.

History

The concept grew out of military experience in Korea and Vietnam, where rapid evacuation and forward surgery cut mortality, and from early civilian pioneers such as the trauma unit at Cook County Hospital in Chicago (1966) and R Adams Cowley's shock trauma centre in Baltimore. Studies of preventable deaths in the 1960s and 1970s spurred organised trauma systems, in which emergency medical services triage patients in the field so the seriously injured bypass nearer hospitals for designated centres. Research consistently finds that treatment at a Level I centre reduces mortality for major trauma by roughly 15–25 per cent compared with non-trauma hospitals.

Operation

A "trauma activation" assembles the team before or as the patient arrives. Care follows ATLS principles, with massive-transfusion protocols, trauma intensive care, interventional radiology, and rehabilitation integrated into the same system. Trauma centres also run performance-improvement programmes, registries, and injury-prevention outreach.
