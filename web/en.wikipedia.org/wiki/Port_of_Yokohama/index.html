Port of Yokohama

The Port of Yokohama is a seaport in Yokohama, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, operated by the Yokohama Port and Harbor Bureau of the city government. Located on Tokyo Bay, it is one of Japan's principal international trading ports.

History

The port opened to foreign trade in 1859 under the Treaty of Amity and Commerce between Japan and the United States, making Yokohama one of the first Japanese ports opened after the end of sakoku (the policy of isolation). It rapidly became Japan's main gateway for trade, especially the export of raw silk, and a foreign settlement and Chinatown grew up around the port. Much of the port was rebuilt after the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and again after the Second World War, during which it served as a major supply base for occupation forces.

Facilities

The port includes the Honmoku, Minami Honmoku, Daikoku, and Ōsanbashi piers; Minami Honmoku has deep-water container berths able to serve the largest container ships. Ōsanbashi serves as the international passenger terminal, and the waterfront forms part of the Minato Mirai 21 redevelopment district.
