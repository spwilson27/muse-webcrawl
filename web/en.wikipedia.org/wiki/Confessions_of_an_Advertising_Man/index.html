Confessions of an Advertising Man

Confessions of an Advertising Man is a 1963 book by David Ogilvy, founder of the advertising agency Ogilvy & Mather. It is one of the best-known and best-selling books on advertising, setting out Ogilvy's philosophy of research-driven, consumer-respecting advertising and his rules for running an agency and creating campaigns.

Content

Drawing on his experience building campaigns for brands such as Hathaway shirts (the famous "man in the Hathaway shirt" with an eye patch), Rolls-Royce (whose advertisement led with the line "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock"), Dove, and Schweppes, Ogilvy argued that "the consumer isn't a moron; she is your wife," and that advertising should sell on facts, long informative copy, and respect for the reader's intelligence. Chapters cover how to get clients, how to build an agency, how to write headlines and copy, and how to advertise food, travel, and pharmaceuticals.

Reception and influence

The book has sold millions of copies, has been translated into many languages, and remains a standard text in the advertising industry. An updated edition appeared in 1988. Ogilvy's precepts — the primacy of the headline, the value of research, and brand image — helped define the "creative revolution" era of American advertising and the doctrine of brand-building Ogilvy championed.
