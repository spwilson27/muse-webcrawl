Ip Man (葉問, 1893–1972), also spelled Yip Man, was a Chinese martial artist and a grandmaster of the martial art Wing Chun when he was 20. He had several students who later became martial arts masters, most notably Bruce Lee.

Life

Ip was born in Foshan, Guangdong, to a wealthy family and received a traditional Chinese education. He began learning Wing Chun from Chan Wah-shun at about the age of nine; after Chan's retirement he continued under Chan's senior student Ng Chung-sok and, according to tradition, under Leung Bik, the son of Leung Jan. He worked for a time as a police officer in Foshan. During the Second Sino-Japanese War and the subsequent civil war, his family lost its wealth; in 1949, after the Communist victory in China, Ip moved to Hong Kong.

Teaching in Hong Kong

In Hong Kong Ip began teaching Wing Chun publicly, initially to restaurant workers, and over two decades built the art's modern following. His students included Wong Shun-leung and the young Bruce Lee, who studied with him in the late 1950s. Ip's sons Ip Chun and Ip Ching later became grandmasters themselves. Shortly before his death from throat cancer in 1972, Ip recorded his forms on film so that his system would be preserved.

Legacy

Ip Man's life has been dramatised in a successful film series starring Donnie Yen, beginning with Ip Man (2008), as well as in The Grandmaster (2013) by Wong Kar-wai. The films greatly increased worldwide interest in Wing Chun. A museum dedicated to him stands in Foshan.
