Matroska

Filename extensions.mkv, .mka, .mks, .mk3d
Initial releaseDecember 2002
TypeMultimedia container format
LicenseOpen standard, royalty-free

The Matroska Multimedia Container is a free and open-source container format that can hold an unlimited number of video, audio, picture, and subtitle tracks in a single file. Its name derives from the Russian matryoshka nesting dolls, reflecting its structure of nested elements; the format is built on EBML, a binary analogue of XML.

Features

Matroska files for video use the .mkv extension, audio-only files .mka, subtitles .mks, and stereoscopic video .mk3d. Features include fast seeking, chaptering, menus, rich metadata (tags), multiple selectable audio and subtitle streams, and error resilience for streaming and damaged files. Unlike codec formats such as H.264 or AAC, Matroska specifies only the container; virtually any codec can be stored inside it.

Adoption and standardisation

Announced in December 2002 as a fork of the Multimedia Container Format project, Matroska became widely used for high-definition video distribution and is supported by most media players, including VLC, and by many televisions and streaming tools. The WebM format is a restricted subset of Matroska. In 2015 the format's maintainers began standardisation through the IETF's CELLAR working group, publishing the EBML and Matroska specifications as RFCs.
