Dali, Yunnan

Dali City (Chinese: 大理市; pinyin: Dàlǐ Shì) is a county-level city in northwestern Yunnan, China, and the seat of the Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture. It lies on the western shore of Erhai Lake, at the foot of the Cangshan mountains, roughly 300 kilometres (190 mi) northwest of the provincial capital Kunming. Dali is one of China’s most popular tourist destinations, celebrated for its well-preserved old town, Bai ethnic culture, and scenery of "wind, flowers, snow, and moon".

History

The Dali region was the heartland of two successive kingdoms of the Bai and Yi peoples: Nanzhao (738–902), which at its height dominated much of southwest China and upper Southeast Asia, and its successor the Dali Kingdom (937–1253), from which the city takes its name. The Mongols under Kublai Khan conquered the kingdom in 1253, incorporating Yunnan into China proper. The old town’s surviving city wall and gates date largely to the Ming dynasty, when the prefectural seat was rebuilt. In the 19th century Dali was briefly the capital of the Panthay Rebellion’s sultanate (1856–1873).

Sights and culture

The city’s best-known monument is the Three Pagodas of Chongsheng Temple, the tallest of which was built in the 9th century during the Nanzhao period. Other attractions include Dali Old Town, the ancient city of Shaxi on the Tea Horse Road, the lakeside town of Shuanglang, and butterfly spring. The local Bai people, who form a large share of the population, are known for tie-dyed cloth, marble quarrying — the Chinese word for marble, dàlǐshí, literally "Dali stone" — and festivals such as the Third Month Fair and the Torch Festival.
