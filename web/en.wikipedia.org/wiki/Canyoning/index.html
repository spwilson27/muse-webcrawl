Canyoning

Canyoning (in the United States often called canyoneering) is an outdoor sport that involves travelling in canyons using a variety of techniques that may include walking, scrambling, climbing, jumping, abseiling (rappelling), and swimming. Although canyoning is often undertaken for the enjoyment of the remote and rugged environment, it requires navigating a variety of water and escape features.

Techniques and equipment

Canyoning routes typically descend a watercourse, following the line of least resistance through gorges, waterfalls, and plunge pools. Participants use equipment borrowed from caving, mountaineering, and whitewater sports, including harnesses, descenders, static ropes, helmets, and wetsuits. Technical canyons require anchor building, rope rigging, and sometimes the use of bolts or natural anchors such as trees and boulders.

Regions

Well-known canyoning areas include the Colorado Plateau in the southwestern United States, home to narrow "slot canyons" such as those in Zion National Park; the Pyrenees of Spain and France; the Blue Mountains of Australia; and the gorges of Slovenia, Switzerland, and New Zealand. The sport is graded by technical difficulty, water volume, and commitment level.

Hazards

The principal dangers of canyoning are flash floods, hypothermia, drowning in hydraulic features, and falls. Because many canyons have no escape route once entered, canyoners monitor weather forecasts carefully and avoid descending when rain is possible anywhere in the drainage.
