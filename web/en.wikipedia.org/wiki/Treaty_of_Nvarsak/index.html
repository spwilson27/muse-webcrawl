Treaty of Nvarsak

The Treaty of Nvarsak was an agreement signed in 484 between the Armenian rebels led by Vahan Mamikonian and the Sasanian Persian Empire, represented by the Persian commander Shapur Mihran. It ended a period of armed Armenian resistance against Persian attempts to impose Zoroastrianism on Christian Armenia.

Under the treaty, the Persians conceded freedom of worship to the Armenians, guaranteeing that Christianity could be practised openly, that Zoroastrian fire temples in Armenia would be removed, and that no Armenian would be forced to convert. The Armenian nobility in turn recognised Sasanian suzerainty, and Vahan Mamikonian was later appointed marzban (governor) of Persian Armenia in 485.

Background

Armenian resistance to Persian religious policy had erupted in the revolt of 450–451 led by Vardan Mamikonian, which ended in defeat at the Battle of Avarayr but hardened Armenian resolve. A renewed rebellion began in 481 under Vahan Mamikonian, Vardan's nephew. After several years of guerrilla warfare the Sasanian king Balash, facing internal difficulties and war with the Hephthalites, chose negotiation over continued campaigning.

Legacy

The treaty is remembered in Armenian historiography as a vindication of the struggle begun at Avarayr: although the Armenians remained within the Persian Empire, they secured the right to practise their faith, which became a cornerstone of Armenian national identity.
