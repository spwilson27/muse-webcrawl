Robert James Lee Hawke (9 December 1929 – 16 May 2019) was an Australian politician and trade unionist who served as the 23rd prime minister of Australia from 1983 to 1991, holding office as leader of the Australian Labor Party (ALP). He is the third-longest-serving prime minister and the longest-serving Labor prime minister.

Early life and union career

Hawke was educated at the University of Western Australia and, as a Rhodes Scholar, at Oxford, where he famously set a world record for drinking a yard of ale. After working as a research officer for the Australian Council of Trade Unions (ACTU), he became its president in 1969 and became a nationally known advocate and industrial negotiator.

Prime Ministership

Entering parliament for Wills in 1980, Hawke became Labor leader only weeks before the 1983 election, which he won in a landslide. The Hawke government floated the dollar, deregulated finance, cut tariffs, introduced Medicare, brokered the Prices and Incomes Accord with the unions, and pursued environmental and land-rights reforms. Hawke won four elections (1983, 1984, 1987, 1990). Facing recession and declining support, he lost the leadership to his treasurer, Paul Keating, in December 1991.

Later life

Hawke remained a revered elder statesman of the Labor Party and an enduringly popular public figure. He died in Sydney in May 2019, days before the 2019 federal election.
