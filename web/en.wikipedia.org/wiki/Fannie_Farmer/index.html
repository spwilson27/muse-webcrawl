Fannie Farmer

Fannie Merritt Farmer (23 March 1857 – 15 January 1915) was an American culinary expert, author, and educator whose Boston Cooking-School Cook Book (1896) became a landmark in American cookery. She is credited with popularizing standardized level measurements in recipes.

Fannie Farmer
Born: 23 Mar 1857, Boston
Died: 15 Jan 1915, Boston
Known for: Boston Cooking-School Cook Book (1896)

Life

After a stroke in her teens left her partially paralyzed, Farmer turned to cooking, graduated from the Boston Cooking School in 1889, and became its principal in 1894. She later founded Miss Farmer's School of Cookery in 1902, lectured at Harvard Medical School on diet for the sick, and wrote on convalescent cookery.

Legacy

The Fannie Farmer cookbook continued in many editions through the 20th century, notably revised by Marion Cunningham, and helped establish precise, reproducible home recipes in the United States.
