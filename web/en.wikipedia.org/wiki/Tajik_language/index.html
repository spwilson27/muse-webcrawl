Tajik (Тоҷикӣ, Tojikī), historically also called Tajiki Persian, is the variety of Persian spoken in Tajikistan and parts of Uzbekistan. It is mutually intelligible with the Persian of Iran and the Dari of Afghanistan, from which it differs mainly in vocabulary (including many Russian loanwords), pronunciation, and its use of the Cyrillic alphabet. Tajik is the official language of Tajikistan and is spoken by the Tajik people of Central Asia, with historic urban centres in Bukhara and Samarkand (now in Uzbekistan).

History

New Persian spread into Central Asia from the 9th–10th centuries under the Samanids, whose court at Bukhara patronised poets such as Rudaki, traditionally regarded as the father of Persian poetry. The Persian of Transoxiana remained the region's literary and administrative language under successive Turkic dynasties. In the Soviet period, the establishment of the Tajik Soviet Socialist Republic (1929) institutionalised "Tajik" as a separate standard language, and its script was changed—first from the Arabic alphabet to Latin in 1928–30, and then to a modified Cyrillic in 1939–40.

Features

Tajik retains some archaic features of Persian, such as the continuants of Middle Persian vocabulary and distinctions lost in Tehrani speech, while its colloquial grammar includes characteristic use of the present-continuous with istoda. Russian loanwords (e.g. for modern technology and administration) distinguish it from Iranian Persian, though since independence in 1991 there has been a policy of replacing some with Persian or Arabic-derived terms. Literary Tajik remains close to the classical Persian of Rudaki, Ferdowsi and Saadi, whose works are taught as national heritage.

Script question

Tajikistan retains Cyrillic officially, though the Arabic script has been promoted symbolically and taught in schools since a 1989 language law affirming ties to Perso-Arabic culture. Periodic debates about script reform continue; neighbouring Iran and Afghanistan write essentially the same language in the Perso-Arabic alphabet.
