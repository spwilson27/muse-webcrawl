Northern Virginia

Arlington and the Potomac River

Northern Virginia, often called NoVA, is a region of the Commonwealth of Virginia comprising the counties and independent cities closest to Washington, D.C., including Arlington, Fairfax, Loudoun and Prince William counties and the cities of Alexandria, Fairfax, Falls Church, Manassas and Manassas Park, with definitions sometimes extending to outer counties such as Fauquier, Stafford and Spotsylvania.

Economy

The region forms the Virginia portion of the Washington metropolitan area and has a population of more than three million, making it the most populous region of the state. Its economy is driven by the federal government and its contractors: the Pentagon in Arlington is among the world's largest office buildings, and the Central Intelligence Agency is headquartered at Langley. Northern Virginia is also a major hub of the internet — a large share of the world's web traffic passes through data centres in Loudoun County's "Data Center Alley" — and Amazon chose Arlington for its second headquarters, HQ2.

Character

Northern Virginia is among the most affluent and highly educated regions in the United States; Fairfax and Loudoun counties regularly rank among the richest counties in the nation. Demographically and politically it differs markedly from the rest of Virginia, having become a Democratic stronghold that has moved statewide elections. The region nevertheless preserves historic sites including Mount Vernon, Arlington National Cemetery and the Manassas (Bull Run) Civil War battlefields.

Transport

The Washington Metro's Silver, Orange, Blue and Yellow lines serve the inner suburbs, and the region is crossed by Interstates 66, 95 and 495 (the Capital Beltway). Washington Dulles International Airport lies in Loudoun and Fairfax counties, and Ronald Reagan National Airport in Arlington.
