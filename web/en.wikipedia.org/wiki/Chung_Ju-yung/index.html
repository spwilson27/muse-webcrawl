Chung Ju-yung (1915–2001)

Chung Ju-yung (Korean: 정주영; November 25, 1915 – March 21, 2001) was a South Korean industrialist and the founder of the Hyundai Group, one of South Korea's largest conglomerates (chaebol). Born the eldest son of a poor farming family in what is now North Korea, he rose to become one of the most influential businessmen in Korean history.

Early life and founding of Hyundai

Chung left home as a teenager, working as a labourer, delivery boy, and bookkeeper before opening a rice shop in Seoul. In 1947 he founded Hyundai as a construction company. Hyundai won major contracts during and after the Korean War, rebuilding infrastructure and later building expressways, shipyards, and plants across Korea and the Middle East.

Expansion

Under Chung, Hyundai expanded into shipbuilding (Hyundai Heavy Industries), automobiles (Hyundai Motor Company, founded in 1967), electronics, and finance. His management philosophy, captured in his motto "Do it — let's try", emphasised boldness, speed, and perseverance. He personally led ambitious projects such as the construction of the world's largest shipyard at Ulsan.

Later life

Chung played a leading role in bringing the 1988 Summer Olympics to Seoul. In 1992 he ran for President of South Korea, finishing third. In his later years he championed engagement with North Korea, famously leading herds of cattle across the DMZ as gifts in 1998, and promoted the Mount Kumgang tourism project. He died in Seoul in 2001.
