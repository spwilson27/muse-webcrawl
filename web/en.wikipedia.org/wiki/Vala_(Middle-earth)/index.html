Vala (Middle-earth)

The Valar (singular Vala) are characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. They are "angelic powers" who, after being created by Eru Ilúvatar, descend into Arda (the world) to prepare it for the coming of Elves and Men. They dwell principally in Valinor, in Aman, across the sea from Middle-earth.

The fourteen Valar

There are fourteen chief Valar, seven male and seven female, not counting Melkor, who fell into evil and is no longer counted among them. The mightiest are Manwë, the Elder King and ruler of Arda; Ulmo, lord of waters; Aulë the smith; Oromë the huntsman; Mandos, judge of the dead; Lórien, master of dreams; and Tulkas the strong. The Queens (Valier) include Varda, kindler of the stars and wife of Manwë; Yavanna, giver of fruits; Nienna, lady of grief and pity; Estë the healer; Vairë the weaver; Vána the ever-young; and Nessa the dancer.

Role

The Valar shaped the lands and raised the Two Trees of Valinor, whose light was captured in the Silmarils. After the destruction of the Trees and the rebellion of the Noldor, they largely withdrew from direct intervention in Middle-earth, acting at last in the War of Wrath at the end of the First Age. The Maiar, lesser spirits of the same order, include the Istari (Wizards) and Sauron.
