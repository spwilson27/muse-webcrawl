Professional wrestling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Professional wrestling combines athletic performance with scripted storytelling.

Professional wrestling, often promoted under the qualifier "sports entertainment", is a form of performing art centred around staged, athletic simulated combat, with the goal of entertaining audiences. Although combat is staged, professional wrestling matches involve a combination of premade sequences and improvisation, where wrestlers respond to unforeseen situations. The drama and spectacle involved in having to perform those high-risk maneuvers are the sources of professional wrestling's inherent danger, despite the predetermined nature of professional wrestling matches.

Kayfabe and structure

For most of its history, the industry guarded the fiction that matches were genuine contests—a convention known as kayfabe. Outcomes are predetermined, usually by a "booker", to advance storylines built around characters ("gimmicks") portrayed as heroes ("faces" or "babyfaces") and villains ("heels"). Matches are typically won by pinfall, submission, count-out, or disqualification. Modern promotions, above all WWE, openly present the product as entertainment while performers still require exceptional athleticism and take real physical risks.

History

Professional wrestling grew out of 19th-century carnival catch wrestling and strongman exhibitions in Europe and North America. In the 20th century it developed distinct national styles: the American territory system, Mexican lucha libre with its masked wrestlers, and Japanese puroresu, which emphasises hard-hitting realism. Television made the business: the 1980s "Rock 'n' Wrestling" boom and the 1990s "Attitude Era" and Monday Night Wars between WWE (then WWF) and WCW took it to peak popularity in the United States.

Health and safety

Despite predetermined results, professional wrestling carries serious risks, including musculoskeletal injury, concussion, and long-term brain damage; wrestlers historically had elevated rates of early mortality, prompting major promotions to introduce wellness policies, concussion protocols, and restrictions on unprotected chair shots and other high-risk manoeuvres.
