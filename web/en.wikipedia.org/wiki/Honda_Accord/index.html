Honda Accord


A Honda Accord sedan

The Honda Accord is a series of automobiles manufactured by Honda since 1976, best known for its four-door sedan variant, which has been one of the best-selling cars in the United States since 1989. The Accord nameplate has been applied to a variety of vehicles worldwide, including coupes, station wagons, hatchbacks, and crossovers.

History

Honda launched the Accord in 1976 as a compact hatchback, larger and more refined than the Civic. A four-door sedan followed in 1979. By its second generation (1982), the Accord became the first car from a Japanese manufacturer to be produced in the United States, at Honda's plant in Marysville, Ohio. In 1989 the Accord was the first imported nameplate to become the best-selling car in the United States.

Generations

The Accord has passed through eleven generations. The fourth generation (1990–1993) moved the car firmly into the mid-size class in North America. Later generations introduced hybrid variants, first in 2005 with a V6 hybrid, and again from 2014 with a plug-in and two-motor hybrid system. The eleventh generation, introduced for the 2023 model year, is offered only as a sedan, with hybrid powertrains standard on most trims and a turbocharged 1.5-litre four-cylinder engine on base models.

Reception

The Accord has been widely praised for its reliability, fuel economy, resale value, and balanced handling, and it has appeared on Car and Driver's 10Best list a record number of times. Cumulative global sales have exceeded 18 million units, making it one of the most successful automobile nameplates in history.
