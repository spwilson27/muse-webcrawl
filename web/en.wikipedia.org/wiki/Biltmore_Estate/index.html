Biltmore Estate

Biltmore Estate is a historic house museum and tourist attraction in Asheville, North Carolina. Biltmore House, the main residence, is a Châteauesque-style mansion built for George Washington Vanderbilt II between 1889 and 1895 and is the largest privately owned house in the United States, at 178,926 square feet (16,622.8 m²) of floor space and 135,280 square feet of living area.

Biltmore Estate
Location: Asheville, North Carolina, US
Built: 1889–1895
Architect: Richard Morris Hunt
Landscape: Frederick Law Olmsted
Owner: The Biltmore Company (Vanderbilt descendants)

History

George Vanderbilt began acquiring land near Asheville in 1888, drawn by the Blue Ridge Mountain scenery. Architect Richard Morris Hunt modelled the house on French Loire Valley châteaux; Frederick Law Olmsted designed the grounds, including approach road, gardens and managed forests that became a birthplace of American forestry under Gifford Pinchot and Carl Schenck.

The house

The house has 250 rooms, including 35 bedrooms, 43 bathrooms, 65 fireplaces, a banquet hall, library of some 23,000 volumes, indoor pool and bowling alley. It opened to guests on Christmas Eve 1895.

Today

Still owned by Vanderbilt descendants through The Biltmore Company, the 8,000-acre estate includes gardens, a winery, Antler Hill Village, hotels and trails, and receives over a million visitors a year.
