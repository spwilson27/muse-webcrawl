Catholic Church in Germany


Members: about 20 million (roughly a quarter of the population, declining)
Dioceses: 27 (7 archdioceses)
Governing body: German Bishops' Conference

The Catholic Church in Germany (German: Katholische Kirche in Deutschland) is part of the worldwide Catholic Church in communion with the Pope. With around 20 million members it is one of the two large churches in Germany alongside the Protestant Church (EKD). Catholics are concentrated in the south and west — Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg, the Rhineland, and the Münsterland — a pattern rooted in the confessional geography established by the Peace of Augsburg (1555) and the Peace of Westphalia (1648).

History

Christianity reached the region in Roman times; the Frankish church was reorganised by St Boniface in the eighth century. Germany was the birthplace of the Protestant Reformation under Martin Luther (1517), which split the church in the empire. In the nineteenth century the Kulturkampf of Bismarck's empire persecuted Catholic institutions, answered politically by the Centre Party. Under National Socialism the 1933 Reichskonkordat failed to protect the church, though figures such as Bishop Clemens August Graf von Galen publicly protested against the euthanasia programme. In East Germany the church lived under an atheist state; reunification in 1990 joined the highly secularised east with the more churched west.

Organisation and public status

The church in Germany has 27 dioceses, including the archdioceses of Cologne, Munich and Freising, Freiburg, Hamburg, Berlin, Bamberg, and Paderborn. As a public-law corporation it benefits from the church tax (Kirchensteuer), levied by the state on members' income — a system that makes the German church among the wealthiest in the world, funding extensive charitable work through Caritas, one of the country's largest employers.

Recent developments

Membership has declined steeply in the twenty-first century, with record numbers of formal departures following abuse scandals investigated by the 2018 MHG study. The German bishops' "Synodal Way" (2019–2023), a reform process addressing power, sexual morality, priestly life, and the role of women, drew both domestic support and concern from the Vatican over its doctrinal implications. In 2013 Germany produced a pope emeritus, Benedict XVI (Joseph Ratzinger), the first German pope since the eleventh century.
