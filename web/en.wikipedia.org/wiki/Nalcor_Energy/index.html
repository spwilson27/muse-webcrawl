Nalcor Energy

Nalcor Energy was a Canadian energy corporation headquartered in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador. Established in 2007 by the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador as the parent company of the province's energy assets, Nalcor held interests in electricity generation and transmission, oil and gas, and energy marketing. Its subsidiaries included Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro, the Churchill Falls (Labrador) Corporation, and Nalcor Energy Oil and Gas.

Operations

Nalcor managed the province's share in major energy projects including the Churchill Falls hydroelectric generating station in Labrador (in partnership with Hydro-Québec), offshore oil fields such as Hibernia, Terra Nova, White Rose and Hebron, and the Bay d'Espoir hydroelectric system.

Muskrat Falls

Nalcor was the proponent of the Lower Churchill Project, whose first phase, the 824 MW Muskrat Falls generating station and associated transmission links (the Labrador–Island Link and the Maritime Link), became notorious for cost overruns: the project's estimated cost roughly doubled from about $7.4 billion at sanction in 2012 to more than $13 billion. A provincial public inquiry (2018–2020) examined the project and was highly critical of its planning and oversight.

Dissolution

In 2021 the provincial government announced that Nalcor Energy would be wound down and folded into Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro, in part in response to the findings of the Muskrat Falls inquiry and reviews of the province's fiscal situation.
