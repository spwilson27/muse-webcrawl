Nicola Pisano

Bornc. 1220, Apulia, Italy
Diedc. 1284, Pisa, Italy
Known forSculpture; baptistery pulpits of Pisa and Siena

Nicola Pisano (c. 1220 – c. 1284) was an Italian sculptor whose work, strongly influenced by classical Roman sculpture, is often considered a beginning of modern sculpture and a precursor of the Renaissance. He is famous for the marble pulpits he carved for the baptisteries of Pisa (1260) and Siena Cathedral (1265–1268).

Style

Probably trained in the imperial workshops of Frederick II in Apulia, Nicola revived antique forms: his biblical figures have the weight, drapery and composure of Roman sarcophagus reliefs. The Pisa pulpit, supported by lions and classical columns, presents scenes from the life of Christ in crowded, sculptural relief.

Works and legacy

Other works include the Arca di San Domenico in Bologna (with his workshop) and contributions at Lucca and Pistoia. His son Giovanni Pisano carried his innovations toward a more expressive Gothic style.
