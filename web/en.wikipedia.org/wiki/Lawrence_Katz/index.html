Lawrence Francis Katz (born December 23, 1956) is an American economist and the Elisabeth Allison Professor of Economics at Harvard University. His research focuses on labor economics, including wage inequality, the impact of technological change and globalization on the labor market, immigration, and the economics of education.

Early life and education

Katz received his A.B. from the University of California, Berkeley in 1979 and his Ph.D. in economics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1985. He joined the faculty of Harvard University, where he has spent most of his academic career.

Career

Katz served as the chief economist of the U.S. Department of Labor from 1993 to 1994 during the Clinton administration. He is a research associate of the National Bureau of Economic Research and has served as an editor of the Quarterly Journal of Economics.

With Claudia Goldin, Katz co-authored The Race between Education and Technology (2008), a widely cited account of how the interaction between educational attainment and skill-biased technological change shaped American income inequality over the twentieth century. His other work includes influential studies of minimum wages (with Alan Krueger), the effects of immigration on native workers, and randomized evaluations of social programs such as the Moving to Opportunity housing experiment, on which he collaborated with Raj Chetty and Nathaniel Hendren.

Honors

Katz is a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and of the Econometric Society. He has received awards recognizing his contributions to labor economics and to the mentoring of graduate students.
