Swirral Edge

ElevationApprox. 900 m (2,950 ft) along the ridge
LocationLake District, Cumbria, England
Parent peakHelvellyn
TypeArête

Swirral Edge is a sharp arête in the English Lake District that rises from the col below Catstye Cam to the summit plateau of Helvellyn, England's third-highest mountain. Together with its more famous neighbour Striding Edge, it forms the classic horseshoe route around Red Tarn.

Description

The ridge descends north-east from Helvellyn's summit to a col and then rises again to the conical peak of Catstye Cam. On its northern side the ground falls steeply toward Brown Cove, while to the south the ridge overlooks Red Tarn. The crest is rocky and narrow in places, requiring care — particularly the short, steep descent at the point where the edge leaves the Helvellyn plateau, sometimes called the "exit crack" or the awkward step, which can be treacherous in ice or high wind.

Walking

Swirral Edge is usually combined with Striding Edge in a circular walk from Glenridding or Patterdale via Red Tarn. Although generally regarded as less exposed than Striding Edge, it is a serious mountain route in winter conditions, and accidents have occurred in poor weather. In snow it becomes an alpine-style climb, and the cornice that can form along its crest is a recognised hazard.
