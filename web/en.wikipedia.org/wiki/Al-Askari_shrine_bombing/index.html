The al-Askari shrine bombing was a terrorist attack on one of Shia Islam's holiest sites, the al-Askari shrine (Golden Mosque) in Samarra, Iraq, on 22 February 2006. Gunmen entered the shrine before dawn, overpowered its guards and planted explosives that destroyed the shrine's great golden dome. No one was killed in the blast itself, but the desecration triggered an eruption of sectarian violence between Iraq's Shia and Sunni communities that killed well over a thousand people within days and is widely regarded as the event that tipped Iraq into full-scale sectarian civil war.

The attack

In the early hours of 22 February 2006, men dressed in military uniforms entered the shrine complex, tied up the guards at gunpoint and set off two bombs beneath the dome, bringing it down. The shrine houses the tombs of the tenth and eleventh Shia Imams, Ali al-Hadi and Hasan al-Askari, and is associated with the twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi. Iraqi and US officials attributed the attack to al-Qaeda in Iraq, led by Abu Musab al-Zarqawi, whose strategy was explicitly to provoke a sectarian war.

Aftermath

Retaliatory attacks on Sunni mosques began within hours. Over the following days scores of mosques were attacked, hundreds of bodies were found in Baghdad and elsewhere, and the death toll exceeded 1,000; thousands of families fled mixed neighbourhoods, hardening Iraq's sectarian geography. Grand Ayatollah Ali al-Sistani and other leaders called for restraint, but the violence marked the start of the worst phase of the Iraqi civil war in 2006–2007.

Second attack and rebuilding

On 13 June 2007 a second bombing destroyed the shrine's two remaining minarets, again blamed on al-Qaeda in Iraq. The Iraqi government, with UNESCO's involvement, undertook reconstruction; the golden dome was rebuilt and the shrine reopened to pilgrims in 2009, with restoration of the minarets and ornamentation continuing afterwards.
