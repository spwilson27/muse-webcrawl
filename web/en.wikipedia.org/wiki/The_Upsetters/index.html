The Upsetters is the name given to the house band of Jamaican record producer Lee "Scratch" Perry. Named after Perry's 1968 hit "I Am the Upsetter" and his Upsetter Records label and record shop, the band backed Perry's productions from the late 1960s through the classic years of his Black Ark studio in the 1970s.

History

The original Upsetters drew on members of other leading session bands, including the Gladiators' Albert Griffiths and the Hippy Boys, whose line-up included the future Wailers rhythm section Aston "Family Man" Barrett (bass) and Carlton Barrett (drums). In this early period the Upsetters scored an international hit of their own with "Return of Django" (1969), a UK top-five single. They also backed Bob Marley and the Wailers on Perry's celebrated early-1970s sessions.

At the Black Ark, Perry's own studio (1973–79), a new generation of Upsetters — including Boris Gardiner, Sly Dunbar, Robbie Shakespeare, Winston Wright, and others at various times — played on landmark recordings by Junior Murvin ("Police and Thieves"), Max Romeo (War Ina Babylon), the Congos (Heart of the Congos), and Perry's own dub albums such as Super Ape (1976). The Black Ark sound, built from a modest four-track recorder and Perry's eccentric production techniques, proved enormously influential on dub, post-punk, and hip hop. Perry later revived the Upsetters name for touring and recording line-ups in subsequent decades.
