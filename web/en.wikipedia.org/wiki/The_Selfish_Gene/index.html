The Selfish Gene

Author: Richard Dawkins
Published: 1976, Oxford University Press

The Selfish Gene is a 1976 popular science book by British evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins. It presents a gene-centred view of evolution: natural selection acts most fundamentally at the level of genes (replicators), with organisms serving as "survival machines" — vehicles genes build to copy themselves into future generations.

Ideas

Building on the work of George C. Williams, W. D. Hamilton, John Maynard Smith, and Robert Trivers, Dawkins explains altruism through kin selection and reciprocal altruism, introduces evolutionarily stable strategies through game theory, and argues against naive group selection. In the final chapter he coined the term meme for units of cultural transmission analogous to genes, founding the field later called memetics.

"Selfish" terminology

Dawkins later said he might equally have called the book The Immortal Gene; "selfish" refers to the gene's effects on survival, not to conscious motives. A second edition (1989) added chapters on the evolution of cooperation and on the "extended phenotype," the subject of his 1982 book.

Influence

The book popularized gene-centred evolution, influenced sociobiology and evolutionary psychology, and has sold over a million copies. In a 2017 Royal Society poll it was voted the most inspiring science book of all time.
