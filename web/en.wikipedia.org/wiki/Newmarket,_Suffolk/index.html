Newmarket is a market town and civil parish in the West Suffolk district of Suffolk, England. It is widely considered the birthplace and global centre of Thoroughbred horse racing, with two racecourses, an extensive complex of training grounds and stables, the headquarters of the Jockey Club's successor bodies, the National Stud, and the National Horse Racing Museum.

Horse racing

Racing at Newmarket dates to the early 17th century, when James I built a palace there and Charles II — the first monarch to be a racehorse owner in the modern sense — made the town a royal sporting retreat, instituting the Town Plate, still run today. The Jockey Club, founded around 1750, governed British racing from Newmarket for over two centuries. The town's two courses, the Rowley Mile and the July Course, host the first two British Classics of the season, the 2,000 Guineas and 1,000 Guineas, in spring. Around 3,000 racehorses are in training in and around the town, with morning strings crossing the High Street and exercising on the Heath, a vast expanse of chalk grassland maintained for gallops since the Stuart era. The bloodstock auctions of Tattersalls, headquartered there, are among the world's most important.

The town

Newmarket grew up on the Roman Icknield Way; its name records a "new market" granted a charter in the early 13th century. Unusually, the town was historically split between Suffolk and Cambridgeshire — the High Street formed the boundary — until local government changes placed it wholly in Suffolk. King Charles II's Palace House survives in part and now houses the National Horse Racing Museum. The town also gives its name to the Newmarket sausage, a seasoned pork sausage with protected geographical status.
