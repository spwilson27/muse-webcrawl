Directivity

Directivity
DomainAntenna theory, acoustics
DefinitionRatio of maximum radiation intensity to average radiation intensity
UnitsDimensionless, often expressed in dBi
RelatedAntenna gain, radiation pattern

Directivity is a fundamental parameter of an antenna which measures the degree to which the radiation emitted is concentrated in a single direction. It is the ratio of the radiation intensity in a given direction from the antenna to the radiation intensity averaged over all directions. The average radiation intensity is equal to the total power radiated by the antenna divided by 4π. If the direction is not specified, the direction of maximum radiation intensity is implied.

Definition

Directivity D is dimensionless and is commonly expressed in decibels relative to an isotropic radiator (dBi). An isotropic antenna, radiating equally in all directions, has a directivity of exactly 1 (0 dBi); a half-wave dipole has a directivity of about 1.64 (2.15 dBi). Directivity is related to antenna gain G through the antenna's radiation efficiency: G = η · D.

Applications

High-directivity antennas — such as parabolic reflectors and phased arrays — concentrate energy into narrow beams, increasing range and reducing interference; they are essential for radar, satellite communications, and radio astronomy. The same concept applies in acoustics to loudspeakers and microphones, describing how sensitivity varies with direction.
