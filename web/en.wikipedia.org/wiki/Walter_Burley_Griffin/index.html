Walter Burley Griffin

Walter Burley Griffin (November 24, 1876 – February 11, 1937) was an American architect and landscape architect. He is known for designing Canberra, Australia's capital city, along with his wife Marion Mahony Griffin. He has also been credited with the development of the L-shaped floor plan, the carport, and an innovative system of cast concrete construction.

Career

Griffin worked in Frank Lloyd Wright's Oak Park studio in the Prairie School style before winning the 1911 international competition to design Australia's new federal capital. He moved to Australia in 1914, designed the Sydney suburb of Castlecrag, and later designed university buildings and the city of Leura. In 1935 he moved to India, where he died in Lucknow two years later.

Legacy

Lake Burley Griffin in Canberra is named in his honour. His plans for Canberra, though heavily modified, still determine the city's geometric layout.
