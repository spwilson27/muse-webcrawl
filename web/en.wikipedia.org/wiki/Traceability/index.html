Traceability

Traceability is the capability to trace the history, application, or location of an object through recorded identification. In supply chains it means following a product or its components from raw materials through production, distribution, and sale, so that its origin and handling can be verified at any point.

Applications

Food safety: regulations such as the EU General Food Law require "one step back, one step forward" traceability so contaminated products can be recalled quickly. Manufacturing: lot and serial numbers let producers link defects to specific batches and processes. Metrology: measurement traceability is the unbroken chain of calibrations linking an instrument's reading to national or international standards (the SI). Software engineering: requirements traceability links requirements to design elements, code, and tests.

Methods

Traceability is implemented through identifiers such as barcodes, QR codes, and RFID tags, recorded in chain-of-custody documentation or digital systems. Standards including GS1 identifiers and ISO 9001 quality-management requirements underpin most industrial practice, and distributed-ledger ("blockchain") systems have been marketed for high-value or high-risk supply chains.
