Sacks spiral

The Sacks spiral is a variant of the Ulam spiral devised by Robert Sacks in 1994. It plots the natural numbers along an Archimedean spiral rather than a square spiral, placing each perfect square along a single ray to the right, and reveals striking curved alignments of prime numbers.

Construction

In polar coordinates, a number n is placed at an angle determined by the fractional part of the square root of n and at a radius proportional to that square root. Equivalently, each square n² lies on the positive horizontal axis, and the numbers between consecutive squares wrap once around the spiral. Because the underlying curve is smooth rather than angular, patterns hidden in the Ulam spiral become arcs: primes cluster along certain curves corresponding to polynomial families related to Euler's prime-generating polynomial n² − n + 41.

Related spirals

The Sacks spiral belongs to a family of number visualisations that includes the Ulam spiral (1963), the Klauber triangle, and the prime-number helix. None of these patterns has yielded a proof about the distribution of primes, but they vividly display the tendency of primes to concentrate along quadratic polynomials.
