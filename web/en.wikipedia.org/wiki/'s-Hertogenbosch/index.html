's-Hertogenbosch (Den Bosch)
Country: Netherlands
Province: North Brabant
Population: about 160,000
Founded: 1185

's-Hertogenbosch, commonly known as Den Bosch, is a city and municipality in the southern Netherlands, the capital of the province of North Brabant. Its name means "the duke's forest", referring to Henry I, Duke of Brabant, who granted it city rights in 1185. Unusually for Dutch, the official name begins with a lowercase-letter apostrophe form, 's-, a contraction of des ("of the").

History

Founded as a fortified trading town at the confluence of the Aa and Dommel rivers, Den Bosch became one of the largest cities of the medieval Netherlands. It was famously besieged and captured by Frederick Henry of Orange in 1629. The painter Hieronymus Bosch was born and worked here, and the Jheronimus Bosch Art Center commemorates him.

Sights and culture

The Gothic St. John's Cathedral (Sint-Janskathedraal) is one of the finest churches in the Netherlands. The Binnendieze, a network of canals beneath the old town, is a popular boat tour. The local pastry, the Bossche bol — a large choux bun filled with whipped cream and coated in chocolate — is a civic symbol.
