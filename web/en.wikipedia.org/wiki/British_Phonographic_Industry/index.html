The British Phonographic Industry (BPI) is the British recorded music industry's trade association. Founded in 1973, it represents hundreds of member companies, from the UK arms of the three major record groups (Universal, Sony and Warner) to independent labels, and is estimated to speak for around 85 per cent of the recorded music consumed legally in the United Kingdom.

Functions

The BPI lobbies government on copyright and music policy, compiles industry statistics and market research, and conducts anti-piracy enforcement on behalf of its members, including site-blocking actions against infringing services. It is a member of the international federation of the recording industry, the IFPI, and administers the ISRC system for UK recordings through its subsidiary work with Phonographic Performance Limited (PPL) on neighbouring rights licensing.

Certified awards

Since 1973 the BPI has awarded Silver, Gold and Platinum discs for sales of singles and albums in the UK. Thresholds were recast over time as formats changed: albums currently qualify at 60,000 (Silver), 100,000 (Gold) and 300,000 (Platinum) units, while singles thresholds rose with the download and streaming eras; streaming data were incorporated into singles certifications in 2013 and into album certifications in 2015, with multi-Platinum awards for the biggest sellers.

BRIT Awards and the Mercury Prize

The BPI organises the BRIT Awards, the UK's flagship popular music awards ceremony, first held in 1977 and staged annually since 1982, raising money for The BRIT Trust, the industry's charity supporting music education, including the BRIT School in Croydon. The BPI also founded the Mercury Prize in 1992, awarded each year for the best album from the United Kingdom and Ireland as judged by an independent panel.
