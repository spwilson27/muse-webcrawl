Sandman

The Sandman is a mythical character in Western and Northern European folklore who puts people to sleep and encourages dreams by sprinkling magical sand onto their eyes.

Folklore

The Sandman is a traditional character in many children's stories and books. In Scandinavian folklore, he is said to sprinkle sand or dust on or into the eyes of children at night to bring on sleep and dreams. The grit or "sleep" (rheum) in one's eyes upon waking is supposed to be the result of the Sandman's work the previous night.

In literature

Hans Christian Andersen's 1841 folk tale "Ole Lukøje" (Ole Shut-eye) introduced the Sandman, named Ole Lukøje, who visits a boy named Hjalmar every night for a whole year and tells him stories. A more sinister portrayal appears in E. T. A. Hoffmann's short story "Der Sandmann" (1816), in which the Sandman is a malevolent figure who throws sand in the eyes of children who will not sleep, causing their eyes to fall out and bleed, and then feeds them to his children on the moon. This story is a key text of the uncanny in psychoanalysis, discussed by Sigmund Freud in his 1919 essay "The Uncanny".

In popular culture

The character has appeared in many forms, including the song "Mr. Sandman" (1954) by the Chordettes, Metallica's "Enter Sandman" (1991), Neil Gaiman's comic book series The Sandman (1989–1996) featuring Morpheus, the lord of dreams, and its subsequent television adaptation. The Sandman also appears as a figure in numerous films and children's television programs.
