Telesurgery

Telesurgery, also called remote surgery, is the ability for a doctor to perform surgery on a patient even though they are not physically in the same location. It combines robotic surgery with high-speed telecommunications, allowing specialist expertise to be delivered at a distance.

History

The landmark demonstration was the Operation Lindbergh of 7 September 2001, in which surgeons in New York used the ZEUS robotic system to remove the gallbladder of a patient in Strasbourg, France, over a dedicated transatlantic fibre-optic link with a round-trip delay of about 155 milliseconds. Earlier, Jacques Marescaux led the team; the operation proved transcontinental telesurgery feasible.

Requirements and limits

Telesurgery requires reliable low-latency connections, robotic instruments with adequate dexterity, and an on-site team able to intervene. Latency above roughly 300 ms noticeably degrades performance. Cost, licensing across jurisdictions, liability and network reliability remain barriers to routine use.
