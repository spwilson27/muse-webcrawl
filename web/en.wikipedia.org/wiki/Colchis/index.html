Colchis (Georgian: კოლხეთი, Kolkheti) was an ancient region and kingdom on the eastern coast of the Black Sea, corresponding roughly to the western part of modern Georgia. In Greek mythology it was the home of Medea and the destination of Jason and the Argonauts, who sailed there in quest of the Golden Fleece.

History

The Colchian culture, noted for its bronze and gold metalwork, flourished from the second millennium BC. Greek colonies such as Phasis and Dioscurias were founded on its coast from the 6th century BC, trading for gold, timber, linen, and slaves. The kingdom came under the sway of Mithridates VI of Pontus, then of Rome; in Roman and Byzantine times its western part was known as Lazica. It is regarded, together with Iberia in the east, as one of the cradles of the Georgian people.

The Golden Fleece

Ancient authors connected the legend of the Golden Fleece with a real Colchian practice: Strabo reports that locals collected alluvial gold from mountain streams using fleecy sheepskins, which trapped the gold particles — a technique long practised in Georgia's Svaneti region.
