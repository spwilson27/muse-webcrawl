James Springer White (4 August 1821 – 6 August 1881), also known as Elder White, was an American religious leader who co-founded the Seventh-day Adventist Church with his wife Ellen G. White and Joseph Bates. He served several terms as president of the church's General Conference and was instrumental in establishing its publishing work and institutions.

Early life and Millerism

White was born in Palmyra, Maine, and as a young man taught school. Baptised into the Christian Connexion, he became a preacher in the Millerite movement, which expected the imminent return of Christ in 1843–44. After the Great Disappointment of October 1844 he joined the small group of Adventists who came to accept the seventh-day Sabbath.

Marriage and publishing

In 1846 he married Ellen Harmon, whose visions the Sabbatarian Adventists came to accept as a prophetic gift. The Whites travelled and preached among scattered Adventist groups in New England. In 1849 James began publishing The Present Truth, the first Sabbatarian Adventist periodical, followed in 1850 by the Advent Review and Sabbath Herald, the church's principal paper. The publishing work moved to Rochester, New York, and then in 1855 to Battle Creek, Michigan, which became the movement's headquarters.

Church organisation

White was a leading advocate of formal church organisation, which many Adventists initially resisted as "Babylon". His efforts led to the adoption of the name Seventh-day Adventist in 1860 and the formation of the General Conference in 1863, of which he served as president in 1865–67, 1869–71 and 1874–80. He also promoted the church's health and education work, helped found Battle Creek College, signed the articles of incorporation of the Western Health Reform Institute, and in 1865 took over the publishing association. Plagued by recurrent strokes from overwork, he died in Battle Creek in 1881.
