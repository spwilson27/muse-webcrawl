A stone ship, or ship setting, is a type of funerary monument found in Scandinavia and around the Baltic Sea, in which upright stones are arranged in the outline of a ship. The settings range from small examples a few metres long to monumental arrangements; Ale's Stones in southern Sweden is about 67 metres long and comprises 59 large boulders.

Function

Stone ships are associated with burials and cremation cemeteries. The ship held deep symbolic importance in Norse culture as the vessel that carried the dead to the afterlife, and stone ships are interpreted as symbolic ships for those who could not be buried in a real vessel, or as permanent markers over graves. Some settings contain no burial and may have served as cenotaphs or cult sites.

Distribution and dating

Examples are found in Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Germany's Baltic coast, and the Baltic states, with a particularly rich concentration on the Swedish island of Gotland. The earliest date to the Nordic Bronze Age, and the tradition continued through the Iron Age into the Viking Age.
