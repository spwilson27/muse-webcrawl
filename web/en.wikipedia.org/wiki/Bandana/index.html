Bandana

A bandana (from Sanskrit bandhana, "a bond, tie") or bandanna is a type of large, usually colourful kerchief, originating from the Indian subcontinent, often worn on the head or around the neck of a person. It is considered to be a hat in some contexts. Bandanas are frequently printed in a paisley pattern and are most often used to hold hair back, either as an attractive head covering or as a practical one to keep sweat off the face and eyes during physical activity.

History

Bandanas originate from India as brightly coloured handkerchiefs of silk and cotton with spots in white on coloured grounds, chiefly red and blue. The silk styles were made of the finest quality yarns and were popular. Cotton bandanas in the distinctive paisley-derived print became mass-produced in Europe and America in the 18th and 19th centuries and were associated with sailors, cowboys, farmers, miners and other manual workers, who used them against dust and sweat.

Uses

Beyond practical work wear, bandanas have served as fashion items, as gang and subcultural identifiers (including the coloured bandanas associated with Los Angeles street gangs), as campaign and advertising cloths, and as improvised face coverings. The "bandana code" or handkerchief code has been used within parts of the gay community to signal preferences. Folded bandanas are also worn by motorcyclists and outdoors enthusiasts, sometimes soaked in water for cooling.
