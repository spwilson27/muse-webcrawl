Round Midnight is a 1986 French-American musical drama film directed by Bertrand Tavernier and starring jazz saxophonist Dexter Gordon in a fictionalised role based on the lives of pianist Bud Powell and saxophonist Lester Young. The title comes from Thelonious Monk's famous composition "'Round Midnight". The film tells the story of Dale Turner, an ageing, alcoholic American tenor saxophonist living in Paris in the late 1950s, and his friendship with Francis Borler, a young French graphic designer and devoted fan who tries to save him from self-destruction.

Plot

Francis, struggling after a divorce, idolises Turner and begins waiting outside the Paris club where he plays, the Blue Note. The two form a deep friendship; Francis takes Turner into his home, keeps him away from alcohol and helps him record again. Eventually Turner returns to New York, where, despite Francis's hopes, he dies — the film closing on the understanding that his music, preserved on record, outlives him.

Music

The score was arranged by Herbie Hancock, who also appears in the film, and features performances by musicians including Wayne Shorter, Bobby Hutcherson, Ron Carter and Tony Williams. The music was recorded live on the set to preserve the spontaneity of real jazz performance. Hancock won the Academy Award for Best Original Score.

Reception

Dexter Gordon's performance was widely acclaimed and earned him an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor — a rare honour for a jazz musician in a leading dramatic role. The film is regarded as one of the finest movies about jazz, praised for its unsentimental portrayal of musicianship and addiction. See also jazz and Dexter Gordon.
