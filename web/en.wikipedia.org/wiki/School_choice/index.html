School choice

School choice programs let families select schools beyond their assigned neighbourhood school

School choice is a term for pre-college public education options that allow parents and students to select alternatives to the public school to which they would normally be assigned. The idea received a classic formulation in Milton Friedman's 1955 essay "The Role of Government in Education", which proposed vouchers to finance schooling at the school of the family's choice.

Forms

  • Charter schools — publicly funded, independently operated schools freed from some regulations in exchange for accountability
  • Vouchers — public funds directed to private school tuition; upheld for religious schools by the U.S. Supreme Court in Zelman v. Simmons-Harris (2002)
  • Education savings accounts (ESAs) and tax-credit scholarships
  • Magnet schools — public schools with specialised curricula drawing students across zones
  • Open enrolment — choice among public schools within or across districts
  • Homeschooling — regulated parent-directed education

The debate

Supporters argue that choice introduces competition that raises quality, empowers parents — particularly low-income families otherwise confined to underperforming schools — and matches children to schools that fit their needs. Critics argue that choice programs divert funds from public schools, can increase segregation, lack accountability, and may "cream-skim" the most engaged families, leaving the hardest-to-educate students in weakened schools.

Evidence

Research findings are mixed and context-dependent. Lottery-based studies of some urban charter networks show sizable achievement gains, while evaluations of statewide voucher programs in Louisiana and Indiana found initial test-score declines that moderated over time. Studies also examine effects on non-choosers, graduation, and college attendance. Internationally, systems such as those of Sweden (friskolor), Chile, and the Netherlands incorporate extensive publicly funded choice with varying regulatory frameworks.
