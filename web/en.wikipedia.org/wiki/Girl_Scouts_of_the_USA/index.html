Girl Scouts of the USA

Girl Scouts of the United States of America (GSUSA), commonly referred to as the Girl Scouts, is a youth organization for girls in the United States and American girls living abroad. It was founded by Juliette Gordon Low in 1912, a year after she had met Robert Baden-Powell, the founder of Scouting (formerly Boy Scouts), in England. Low held the organization's first meeting in her home in Savannah, Georgia, on March 12, 1912.

Founded: 12 March 1912
Founder: Juliette Gordon Low
Headquarters: New York City
Membership: over 1.7 million girls and adults

Program

Girls are organized by age level — Daisies, Brownies, Juniors, Cadettes, Seniors, and Ambassadors — and progress through activities in outdoor skills, STEM, entrepreneurship, and civic engagement, earning badges and the Bronze, Silver, and Gold Awards, the Gold Award being the highest achievement. The organization is famous for its annual Girl Scout Cookies sale, begun in 1917, which funds troop activities and teaches business skills; it is the largest girl-run business in the world.

Structure and membership

GSUSA is a member of the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts. Membership is open to all girls in grades K–12, and the organization has long had policies of non-discrimination. More than 50 million American women are Girl Scout alumnae, including a majority of women elected to the U.S. Congress in recent decades.
