Marmots are large ground squirrels in the genus Marmota, with 15 species living in mountainous areas of Europe, Asia, and North America. These herbivores are active during the summer, often hibernate in underground burrows, and use high-pitched whistles to warn their colonies of danger — hence nicknames such as "whistle pig" for the North American groundhog.

Marmots typically live in burrows and hibernate there through the winter. Most marmots are highly social and live in extended family colonies. They are the heaviest true hibernators: adults of the largest species, such as the alpine marmot, weigh from 3 to 8 kilograms, and the animals may live 15 years or more in the wild.

Distribution and habitat

Marmots are found across the Northern Hemisphere: the alpine marmot in the Alps, Pyrenees, and Carpathians; steppe and bobak marmots across Central Asia; and in North America the groundhog (woodchuck) at low elevations in the east, the yellow-bellied marmot in western mountains, the hoary marmot in the northwest, and the Vancouver Island marmot, one of the world's rarest mammals, on Vancouver Island in Canada.

Biology

Marmots have stocky bodies, short powerful legs with strong claws for digging, and dense fur. Their diet consists mainly of grasses, herbs, flowers, and sometimes insects. Breeding occurs shortly after emergence from hibernation in spring; litters of about two to five pups are born after roughly a month's gestation. Hibernation may last seven to nine months in cold climates, during which body temperature drops close to ambient and the heart rate falls to a few beats per minute.

Relation to humans

The groundhog is famous in North American folklore through Groundhog Day. Marmots have been hunted for food, fur, and fat in parts of Asia, where they can also carry plague fleas; the tarbagan marmot of Mongolia is historically associated with pneumonic plague outbreaks. Several species are threatened by habitat loss and hunting, while introduced alpine marmots have established populations outside their native range in the Pyrenees.
