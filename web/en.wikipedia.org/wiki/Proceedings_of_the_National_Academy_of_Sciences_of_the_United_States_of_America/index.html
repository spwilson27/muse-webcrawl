Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (often abbreviated PNAS or Proc Natl Acad Sci USA) is a peer-reviewed multidisciplinary scientific journal. It is the official journal of the National Academy of Sciences, published since 1915, and publishes original research, scientific reviews, commentaries, and academy proceedings. The journal covers the biological, physical, and social sciences and, as of 2022, had a journal impact factor in the region of 11–12.

PNAS is the second most cited scientific journal, with more than 1.9 million citations from 2008 to 2018. Its articles have included landmark papers across the sciences, including contributions by members of the Academy and, historically, papers "communicated" by Academy members on behalf of non-members.

History

PNAS was established by the National Academy of Sciences (NAS) in 1914 and began publication in 1915. The NAS was founded in 1863 by Act of Congress signed by President Abraham Lincoln to serve as an official adviser to the federal government in all matters of science. The journal was founded under the leadership of biologist Edwin Bidwell Wilson. Since 2019 the journal has been published on a continuous online basis with weekly issues.

Peer review

Articles in PNAS are contributed by Academy members or submitted directly. Direct submissions undergo standard peer review; contributed papers are reviewed by referees chosen by the contributing member. A third track, introduced in 2010 for elected members, allows members to contribute papers reviewed under their own arrangement, subject to editorial oversight. The acceptance rate for direct submissions is low, around 15–20%.

PNAS Nexus

In 2022 the Academy launched PNAS Nexus, a fully open-access companion journal publishing research across the biological, physical, social and health sciences and engineering.
