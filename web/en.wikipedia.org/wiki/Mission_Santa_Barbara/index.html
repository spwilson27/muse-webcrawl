Mission Santa Barbara (Spanish: Misión de Santa Bárbara) is a Spanish mission in Santa Barbara, California, founded by the Franciscan order on 4 December 1786. The tenth of the California missions, it is often called the "Queen of the Missions" for its graceful twin-towered church, and it remains an active parish church administered by Franciscans.

History

The mission was established by Father Fermín de Lasuén on the feast day of Saint Barbara, near the Chumash village of Syukhtun. The present stone church, built of sandstone by Chumash labourers, was completed in 1820 after an earlier church was destroyed by the great earthquake of 1812; its façade was modelled on a Roman temple design from Vitruvius. An aqueduct and dam system supplied water from Mission Creek. After secularisation in the 1830s the mission declined, but uniquely among the California missions it has been in continuous Franciscan hands, and it served as an apostolic college and seminary site. Saint Junípero Serra's successor Lasuén and several early friars are buried in its crypt.

Today

The mission is a National Historic Landmark and a major tourist attraction; its grounds include a museum, gardens, a historic cemetery, and the Santa Barbara Mission Archive-Library, an important repository for California colonial history.
