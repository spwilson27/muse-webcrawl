Geocaching

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Geocaching is an outdoor recreational activity in which participants use a Global Positioning System (GPS) receiver or mobile device and other navigational techniques to hide and seek containers, called geocaches or caches, at specific locations marked by coordinates all over the world. A typical cache is a small waterproof container containing a logbook and sometimes small items for trading. Millions of geocaches are hidden worldwide, and millions of registered users participate through websites and apps such as geocaching.com, operated by Groundspeak.

History

Geocaching began on May 3, 2000, one day after the United States government switched off selective availability, dramatically improving civilian GPS accuracy. Computer consultant Dave Ulmer of Beavercreek, Oregon, placed the first documented GPS-located stash — a bucket of trinkets in the woods near Portland — and posted its coordinates to a Usenet newsgroup. Within days it had been found twice and logged. The term "geocaching," coined to replace the less respectable-sounding "GPS stash hunt," combined geo (Earth) with cache (a hidden store). The original stash site is commemorated by the Original Stash Tribute Plaque.

Cache types

Beyond the traditional cache, variants include multi-caches (with clues leading through intermediate stages), mystery or puzzle caches (requiring a puzzle to be solved for the final coordinates), EarthCaches (teaching a geological lesson at a natural feature), letterbox hybrids, event caches, and virtual and webcam caches, most of which were grandfathered after 2005. Trackable items such as Travel Bugs and geocoins carry unique codes and travel from cache to cache toward goals set by their owners.

Rules and etiquette

Finders sign the logbook and log their visit online; if they take a trade item they are expected to leave something of equal or greater value. People unaware of the game are called "muggles." Cache owners must obtain permission where required, and caches may not be buried or placed in restricted locations.
