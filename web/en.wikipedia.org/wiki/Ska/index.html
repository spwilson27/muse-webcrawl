Ska

Ska is a music genre that originated in Jamaica in the late 1950s and was the precursor to rocksteady and reggae. It is commonly thought to have developed from mento and calypso combined with American jazz and rhythm and blues. Ska is characterized by a walking bass line accented with rhythms on the offbeat (the "skank"), prominent horn sections, and a fast tempo.

Stylistic originsMento, calypso, jazz, rhythm and blues
Cultural originsLate 1950s, Jamaica
Derivative formsRocksteady, reggae, dancehall
Subgenres2 Tone, ska punk, ska-jazz, third wave ska

History

Original Jamaican ska (1960s)

Ska emerged in Kingston studios in the early 1960s, around Jamaican independence in 1962. Producers such as Duke Reid, Coxsone Dodd, and Prince Buster recorded house bands that defined the sound; the Skatalites, formed in 1964, became the genre's definitive ensemble. Artists like Desmond Dekker, Jimmy Cliff, and a young Bob Marley with the Wailers recorded ska hits. As tempos slowed in the heat of 1966, ska gave way to rocksteady and then reggae.

2 Tone (late 1970s–1980s)

Ska was revived in England around 1979 by the 2 Tone movement, which fused ska with punk energy. Bands such as The Specials, Madness, The Selecter, The Beat (English Beat), and Bad Manners scored major UK hits. The 2 Tone label's black-and-white checkerboard imagery and multiracial line-ups echoed the music's integrated ethos.

Third wave and ska punk

A third wave flourished in the 1990s, particularly in the United States, where ska punk bands mixed ska rhythms with punk and hardcore: The Mighty Mighty Bosstones, No Doubt, Sublime, Reel Big Fish, Less Than Jake, and Rancid brought the sound to mainstream rock audiences. Ska remains active in regional scenes worldwide, including in Japan, Latin America, and Europe.

Characteristics

Ska typically features guitar and piano emphasizing the offbeat, a walking bass line, drums stressing beats two and four or using the "one drop" pattern in later styles, and brass instruments — trumpet, trombone, saxophone — playing melodic riffs. The dance associated with the music is known as skanking.
