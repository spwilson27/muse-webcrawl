Anti-villain

An anti-villain is a character in fiction who functions as an antagonist — opposing the hero — but who possesses heroic qualities, sympathetic motives, or noble goals pursued by villainous means. The anti-villain is the mirror image of the anti-hero: where an anti-hero does good through flawed or ignoble traits, an anti-villain does wrong, or serves a wrongful cause, while displaying traits the audience can admire or understand.

Characteristics

Anti-villains may be motivated by ideology, loyalty, love, or a distorted sense of justice. They often adhere to a personal code of honor, show mercy or reluctance, and may even ally with the protagonist when circumstances change. Because their goals can be understandable — sometimes even admirable — anti-villains generate moral ambiguity and sympathy unusual for antagonists.

Examples

Frequently cited examples include Magneto in the X-Men stories, whose campaign for mutant survival stems from his experience of the Holocaust; Roy Batty in Blade Runner, who seeks only a longer life; and Ozymandias in Watchmen, who engineers mass death to avert nuclear war. Literary antecedents include Shakespeare's sympathetic antagonists and Milton's charismatic Satan in Paradise Lost, a reading popularized by Romantic critics.
