Lorraine (French: Lorraine; German: Lothringen) is a historical and cultural region in north-eastern France, corresponding to the former administrative region of Lorraine (1982–2015), which comprised the departments of Meurthe-et-Moselle, Meuse, Moselle and Vosges. Since 2016 it has formed part of the larger region of Grand Est. Its name derives from Lothair II, whose kingdom of Lotharingia was established by the Treaty of Prüm in 855.

History

For a thousand years Lorraine was a contested borderland between France and the German-speaking Holy Roman Empire. The Duchy of Lorraine was formally incorporated into France in 1766 on the death of Stanisław Leszczyński, the former king of Poland and last duke, whose legacy includes Nancy's magnificent Place Stanislas. After the Franco-Prussian War, the Treaty of Frankfurt (1871) annexed Moselle (with Alsace) to the German Empire as part of Alsace-Lorraine; it returned to France after the First World War, was annexed again by Nazi Germany in 1940, and restored in 1945. The region's rich coal and iron deposits made it a heartland of French steel, an industry that declined sharply from the 1970s.

Culture

Lorraine's symbols include the double-barred Cross of Lorraine — adopted by the Free French during the Second World War — and the mirabelle plum and quiche lorraine in its cuisine. Joan of Arc was born at Domrémy-la-Pucelle in the Vosges. Germanic Lorraine Franconian dialects are still spoken in parts of Moselle.
