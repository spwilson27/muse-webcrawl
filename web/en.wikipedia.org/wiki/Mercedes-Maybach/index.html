Mercedes-Maybach

The Mercedes-Maybach S-Class is the flagship of the Mercedes-Benz range.

Mercedes-Maybach is a luxury sub-brand of the German carmaker Mercedes-Benz Group, offering ultra-luxury versions of Mercedes-Benz vehicles. The name honours Maybach, the historic German luxury marque founded by Wilhelm Maybach and his son Karl in 1909, which built opulent cars in the 1920s and 1930s and later produced engines for German armoured vehicles.

Maybach as a standalone brand

DaimlerChrysler revived Maybach as an ultra-luxury standalone marque in 2002, launching the Maybach 57 and 62 saloons — named for their lengths in decimetres — built largely by hand in Sindelfingen. Intended to compete with BMW's Rolls-Royce and Volkswagen's Bentley, the cars featured extraordinarily lavish interiors but sold far below projections: only a few thousand were built over a decade, compared with tens of thousands of rivals. Special models included the Landaulet and the Exelero concept car. Daimler discontinued the marque in 2013 after sustained losses.

The Mercedes-Maybach sub-brand

In November 2014 Mercedes-Benz relaunched the name as Mercedes-Maybach, a sub-brand analogous to Mercedes-AMG but focused on luxury rather than performance. The first model was the Mercedes-Maybach S-Class (X222), an extended, more sumptuously trimmed version of the S-Class aimed especially at chauffeur-driven buyers in China, which became the brand's largest market. The line-up later included the Maybach GLS SUV, the S-Class of the W223 generation with optional two-tone paint and reclining executive rear seats, and the all-electric EQS SUV. Limited editions, such as those created with designer Virgil Abloh, further positioned the brand at the summit of the Mercedes range.
