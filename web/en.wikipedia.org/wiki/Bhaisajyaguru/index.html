Bhaisajyaguru (Sanskrit: भैषज्यगुरु, "Medicine Master and King of Lapis Lazuli Light"), formally Bhaisajyaguruvaiduryaprabharaja, is the Buddha of healing and medicine in Mahayana Buddhism. Commonly called the Medicine Buddha, he is described as a doctor who cures suffering using the medicine of his teachings.

Origin

Bhaisajyaguru is described in the Bhaisajyaguruvaiduryaprabharaja Sutra (Medicine Buddha Sutra), composed in the early centuries CE. In the sutra, the Buddha explains that Bhaisajyaguru made twelve great vows upon attaining enlightenment, vowing among other things to heal the sick, the blind and the disabled, to relieve hunger and poverty, and to guide beings to liberation.

Iconography

Bhaisajyaguru is traditionally depicted with skin of lapis lazuli blue, seated, wearing monastic robes. His left hand rests in his lap in the meditation mudra holding a bowl of healing nectar (amrita); his right hand is in the gift-giving mudra holding a branch of the myrobalan plant, a famed medicinal herb. In East Asia he is often flanked by the bodhisattvas Suryaprabha and Candraprabha.

Worship

Devotion to the Medicine Buddha spread from India through China, Korea, Japan and Tibet. In Japan, as Yakushi Nyorai, he was among the first Buddhist deities venerated, and temples such as Yakushi-ji in Nara were dedicated to him. In Tibetan Buddhism he is central to traditions of Buddhist medicine (Sowa Rigpa). His mantra, given in the sutra, is recited for healing and the relief of suffering.
