World Expo 88, also known as Expo 88, was a specialised exposition held in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, from 30 April to 30 October 1988. The fair was held under the theme "Leisure in the Age of Technology" and coincided with the bicentennial celebrations of European settlement in Australia. It was staged on a 40-hectare site on the south bank of the Brisbane River, across from the city centre.

The exposition

The site featured pavilions from more than fifty nations and many corporations, linked by a monorail and shaded by distinctive white sunsails designed to shelter visitors from the subtropical sun. Landmarks included the 88-metre Sky Needle, the Nepalese Peace Pagoda — one of the few pavilions retained after the fair — and a replica of HMS Endeavour. Entertainment included daily parades, concerts and fireworks, and the fair's platypus mascot, Expo Oz, became widely recognised. Attendance reached approximately 18.5 million visits, far exceeding forecasts.

Legacy

After Expo closed, the site was redeveloped as the South Bank Parklands, which opened in 1992 and remain one of Brisbane's principal cultural and recreational precincts. The event is widely credited with transforming Brisbane's self-image and international profile, changing perceptions of the city from a large country town into a modern subtropical capital, and accelerating development and tourism in Queensland. Commemorations were held for the 20th, 25th and 30th anniversaries of the fair.
