Papal encyclical

A papal encyclical is a letter sent by the pope, usually addressed to the bishops of the Catholic Church or to the whole Church, dealing with matters of doctrine, morals, or discipline. The term derives from the Greek egkyklios, "circular" — a letter meant for circulation. Encyclicals are among the most authoritative instruments of the pope's ordinary teaching office (magisterium), though they rank below dogmatic definitions and apostolic constitutions. They are traditionally cited by their opening words in Latin.

History

In the modern sense the genre is associated with Benedict XIV, whose Ubi primum (1740) is often regarded as the first encyclical. Pius IX and Leo XIII greatly expanded their use: Leo XIII issued some 75 encyclicals, including Rerum novarum (1891) on labour and capital, which founded modern Catholic social teaching. Successive popes used encyclicals to address theology, politics, and social questions throughout the 20th and 21st centuries.

Notable encyclicals

  • Rerum novarum (1891, Leo XIII) — rights and duties of capital and labour
  • Quadragesimo anno (1931, Pius XI) — social order, forty years after Rerum novarum
  • Mit brennender Sorge (1937, Pius XI) — condemnation of Nazi ideology, issued in German
  • Humani generis (1950, Pius XII) — theology and evolution
  • Pacem in terris (1963, John XXIII) — peace and human rights, addressed to "all men of good will"
  • Humanae vitae (1968, Paul VI) — reaffirmation of the ban on artificial contraception
  • Redemptor hominis (1979) and Laborem exercens (1981, John Paul II)
  • Deus caritas est (2005, Benedict XVI) — Christian love
  • Laudato si' (2015, Francis) — care for the environment
  • Fratelli tutti (2020, Francis) — fraternity and social friendship

Authority

Encyclicals normally express the ordinary magisterium and, per Catholic teaching in Lumen gentium, call for religious submission of mind and will from the faithful. Statements within them are rarely held to be infallible unless reiterating previously defined doctrine.
