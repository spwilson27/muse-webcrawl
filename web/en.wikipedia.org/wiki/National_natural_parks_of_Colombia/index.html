National natural parks of Colombia

The national natural parks of Colombia (Spanish: Parques Nacionales Naturales de Colombia) are protected areas administered by the National Natural Parks System, a unit of Colombia's Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development. The system protects areas of outstanding ecological, geological and cultural value across the country's highly diverse regions, from Caribbean coastlines to Amazon rainforest and Andean páramos.

Overview

Colombia is one of the world's most biodiverse countries, and its park system covers a substantial share of the national territory. Well known parks include Tayrona National Natural Park on the Caribbean coast, Los Nevados National Natural Park in the Central Cordillera, Amacayacu National Park in the Amazon, and the Serranía de la Macarena, famous for the Caño Cristales river. The island reserves of Malpelo and Gorgona protect marine ecosystems in the Pacific.

Administration

The parks are managed by Parques Nacionales Naturales de Colombia, which oversees conservation, research and regulated ecotourism. Several protected areas overlap with territories of Indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities, whose participation in management is recognised in Colombian law.
