Matthew Paul Le Tissier (born 14 October 1968) is a Guernsey-born former English professional footballer who played as an attacking midfielder. He spent his entire professional club career with Southampton, where he scored 161 goals in 443 league appearances, and earned eight caps for the England national team.

Le Tissier was renowned for his technical skills, creativity, and spectacular goals, as well as his exceptional penalty-taking record: he converted 47 of the 48 penalties he took during his career. Southampton fans nicknamed him "Le God" for his loyalty and brilliance. Despite interest from larger clubs, he remained with Southampton for his whole career, helping them repeatedly avoid relegation from the top flight.

Career

Born in Guernsey, Le Tissier signed for Southampton in 1985 and made his debut in 1986. He was named PFA Young Player of the Year in 1990. His finest season was arguably 1993–94, when he scored 25 league goals. In 2001–02 he made one of the final appearances at The Dell and was present for the opening of St Mary's Stadium.

Internationally, Le Tissier was often overlooked despite scoring a hat-trick for England B against Russia in 1998; he was not selected for the 1998 FIFA World Cup squad. He earned eight senior caps between 1994 and 1997.

Post-playing career

After retiring in 2002, Le Tissier worked as a pundit, most notably on Sky Sports' Soccer Saturday, a role he held until 2020. He also served as club ambassador and honorary president of Southampton. In later years he has attracted attention for expressing controversial views on social media, including scepticism about COVID-19 measures.
