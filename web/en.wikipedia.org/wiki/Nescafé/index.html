Nescafé

Nescafé is a brand of instant coffee made by the Swiss company Nestlé. The name is a portmanteau of "Nestlé" and "café". First sold in Switzerland in 1938, it grew into the world's largest coffee brand, sold in hundreds of countries in many varieties, from the original Nescafé Original to premium freeze-dried lines such as Nescafé Gold and the Dolce Gusto capsule system.

History

In the 1930s, Brazil asked Nestlé to find a use for its surplus coffee beans. Food scientist Max Morgenthaler led a seven-year effort to produce a soluble coffee powder that retained flavour when mixed with hot water. Nescafé launched in Switzerland on 1 April 1938. During the Second World War it became a staple of US servicemen's rations — the entire output of the US plant was reserved for the military — which spread its popularity worldwide after the war. Improvements included the introduction of freeze-dried Nescafé Gold Blend in 1965, which better preserved aroma.

Products and marketing

Nescafé is marketed in many regional variants and strengths, including Nescafé 3-in-1 sachets (coffee, sugar, and creamer), ready-to-drink canned coffees popular in Asia, and the Dolce Gusto machine system launched in 2006. The brand is famous for long-running campaigns such as the "Gold Blend couple" television advertisements in the UK. Nestlé has promoted sustainability programmes, including the Nescafé Plan (2010) for responsible coffee sourcing and farmer support.

Market position

Nescafé remains one of the world's most valuable food brands and the global leader in instant coffee, a segment that dominates coffee consumption in many markets, including much of Asia and Eastern Europe, even as fresh and capsule coffee grows in Western markets.
