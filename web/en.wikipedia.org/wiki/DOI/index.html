Digital object identifier

A digital object identifier (DOI) is a persistent identifier or handle used to uniquely identify various objects, standardized by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO 26324). DOIs are a type of Handle System handle and fit within the URI system. They are widely used to identify academic, professional, and government information, such as journal articles, research reports, data sets, and official publications.

Structure

A DOI name consists of a prefix beginning with "10.", assigned to a registrant by a registration agency, followed by a suffix chosen by the registrant — for example, 10.1000/xyz123. Unlike a URL, a DOI identifies the object itself rather than its location: the metadata record for the DOI is updated if the object moves, so links using the DOI continue to resolve, typically through the doi.org proxy.

History and use

The DOI system was launched in 2000 by the International DOI Foundation, drawing on the Corporation for National Research Initiatives' Handle System and the publishing industry's need for persistent links for online journals. Registration agencies include Crossref, whose DOIs for scholarly articles are now standard practice in academic citation, and DataCite, which issues DOIs for research data. Well over 300 million DOI names have been assigned. DOIs are also used in citation styles and reference managers, and they underpin services for citation tracking and open-access discovery.
