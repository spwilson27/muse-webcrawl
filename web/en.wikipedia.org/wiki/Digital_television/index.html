Digital television

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Digital television (DTV) is the transmission of television signals using digital encoding, in contrast to the earlier analogue television technology which used analogue signals. Digital transmission makes far more efficient use of radio spectrum: several programme channels (a multiplex) can occupy the bandwidth of a single analogue channel, with better picture and sound quality, electronic programme guides, and interactive services. During the 2000s and 2010s most countries completed a "digital switchover", switching off analogue terrestrial broadcasts.

Standards

Several incompatible terrestrial standards emerged:

  • DVB-T/T2 (Digital Video Broadcasting – Terrestrial), used in Europe, much of Asia, Africa, and Oceania. Companion standards DVB-S/S2 serve satellite and DVB-C cable.
  • ATSC, used in North America and South Korea; ATSC 3.0 ("NextGen TV") adds 4K, HDR, and IP-based delivery.
  • ISDB-T, developed in Japan and adopted widely in Latin America, with 1seg mobile reception.
  • DTMB, used in China.

Video is usually compressed with MPEG-2, H.264/MPEG-4 AVC, or HEVC, within an MPEG transport stream. Reception requires a TV with an integrated tuner or a set-top box.

Transition

The first regular digital terrestrial services launched in the United Kingdom and the United States in 1998. Berlin completed the first full analogue switch-off in 2003; the Netherlands followed in 2006, the United States ended full-power analogue broadcasts on 12 June 2009, and the UK completed switchover in 2012. Freed spectrum (the "digital dividend") was reallocated largely to mobile broadband. Digital switchover was a major logistical exercise involving public information campaigns and subsidised converter boxes for vulnerable households.

Advantages and issues

Digital signals resist noise but fail abruptly at the reception threshold (the "cliff effect"), whereas analogue signals degrade gradually. Benefits include more channels, high-definition and later ultra-high-definition pictures, surround sound, subtitles and audio description, and data services. Satellite, cable, and IPTV systems made parallel digital transitions, and streaming over the open Internet now competes with all of them.
