Armenian genocide denial

Armenian genocide denial is the denial that the Ottoman Empire and its ruling Committee of Union and Progress systematically massacred and deported Armenians during the First World War, an event — the Armenian genocide — in which an estimated 600,000 to 1.5 million Armenians died. Denial is the official position of the Republic of Turkey and has been a central element of Turkish foreign policy toward countries that formally recognise the genocide.

Turkish state position

Successive Turkish governments have argued that the deaths resulted from wartime conditions, famine, disease, and intercommunal violence in which Muslims also died; that deportations were a security measure directed against Armenian collaboration with Russia; and that the death toll is exaggerated. Turkey disputes the classification of the events as genocide, maintaining that there was no central intent to destroy the Armenians as a group. Article 301 of the Turkish penal code ("insulting Turkishness") was used to prosecute writers who affirmed the genocide, including novelist Orhan Pamuk (charges dropped) and journalist Hrant Dink, who was assassinated in 2007 after his prosecution.

Scholarly consensus

The overwhelming majority of historians and genocide scholars — including the International Association of Genocide Scholars — affirm that the events constitute genocide, based on Ottoman records, diplomatic reports from Germany, Austria-Hungary, and the United States, missionary accounts, and survivor testimony. Scholars of denial literature characterise it as a paradigmatic case of state-sponsored historical negationism, comparing its methods to those of Holocaust denial, though it is conducted from within a state apparatus rather than by fringe groups.

International recognition

More than thirty countries, as well as the European Parliament, the Council of Europe, and (in 2021) the President of the United States, have formally recognised the genocide. Turkey has responded to recognition resolutions with diplomatic protests and, at times, recalls of ambassadors. Azerbaijan also officially denies the genocide, aligning with Turkey.
