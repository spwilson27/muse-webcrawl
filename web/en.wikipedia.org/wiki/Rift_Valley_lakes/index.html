The Rift Valley lakes are a series of lakes in the East African Rift, the continental rift zone running through eastern Africa from Ethiopia to Mozambique. Formed in tectonic grabens as the African Plate splits apart, they include some of the oldest, deepest and most biologically diverse lakes on Earth.

Major lakes

The western (Albertine) branch holds Lake Tanganyika — the world's second-deepest and second-oldest freshwater lake — and lakes Kivu, Edward and Albert. The eastern (Gregory) branch contains a chain of shallower lakes in Ethiopia and Kenya, many of them alkaline "soda lakes" such as Nakuru, Bogoria and Magadi, famous for vast flocks of lesser flamingos feeding on cyanobacteria. Lake Turkana, the world's largest permanent desert lake, lies at the Kenyan–Ethiopian border, and Lake Malawi (Nyasa) in the south-east is renowned, like Tanganyika, for its extraordinary radiation of endemic cichlid fish — hundreds of species found nowhere else. Lake Victoria, Africa's largest lake by area, sits on the uplifted plateau between the two rift branches rather than in a rift basin proper.

Ecology and human significance

The lakes support major fisheries and the livelihoods of millions, but face pressures from overfishing, introduced species (notably the Nile perch in Lake Victoria), pollution and climate change. The rift's shores and sediments — including the Turkana Basin and Olduvai Gorge region — have yielded pivotal fossils in human evolution.
