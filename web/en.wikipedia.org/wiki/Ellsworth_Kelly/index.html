Ellsworth Kelly

Ellsworth Kelly (May 31, 1923 – December 27, 2015) was an American painter, sculptor, and printmaker associated with hard-edge painting, Color Field painting, and minimalism. His works are characterised by precise shapes, flat planes of saturated colour, and forms abstracted from observations of the world around him.

Life and career

Kelly was born in Newburgh, New York. He studied at the Pratt Institute in Brooklyn before serving in the US Army during the Second World War, where he worked in camouflage design. After the war he studied at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, and then lived in Paris from 1948 to 1954, where he developed his mature abstract style, influenced by artists such as Jean Arp and Constantin Brâncuși, and by the windows, shadows, and plants he observed around him.

Returning to New York in 1954, Kelly became known for shaped canvases, multi-panel colour paintings such as Colors for a Large Wall (1951), and later for monumental sculptures and reliefs in painted aluminium and steel. His late work includes the Austin, a stone building conceived as a work of art, completed posthumously in 2018 at the Blanton Museum of Art. Kelly died in Spencertown, New York, in 2015, aged 92.

Style

Kelly insisted his abstractions derived from visual experience — a shadow on a wall, the curve of a leaf, the shape of a window — rather than from geometric theory, and he sought to suppress gesture and personal expression in favour of clarity of form and colour.
