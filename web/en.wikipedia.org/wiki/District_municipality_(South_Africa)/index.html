District municipality (South Africa)

In South Africa, a district municipality is a municipality which executes some of the functions of local government for a district that includes several local municipalities. It is one of the three categories of municipality defined in the Constitution, alongside metropolitan and local municipalities.

Structure

South Africa's nine provinces (outside the eight metropolitan municipalities) are divided into district municipalities, each subdivided into local municipalities; together the two tiers share service-delivery functions such as water, sanitation, electricity reticulation in some areas, municipal health, and regional planning. There are 44 district municipalities. The country is entirely covered by municipalities — a "wall-to-wall" system introduced with the 2000 local-government demarcation.

Councils

District councils consist of councillors elected by proportional representation together with representatives delegated from the local municipalities in the district, roughly a 60:40 split. District mayors are elected by the council. Boundaries are set by the Municipal Demarcation Board.
