Amidah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A worshipper reciting the Amidah prayer

The Amidah (Hebrew: תפילת העמידה, "the standing prayer"), also called the Shemoneh Esreh ("the Eighteen Benedictions"), is the central prayer of Jewish liturgy. Observant Jews recite it three times daily — morning (Shacharit), afternoon (Mincha) and evening (Maariv) — standing, facing Jerusalem, and traditionally in a voice audible only to oneself.

Structure

The weekday Amidah consists of nineteen blessings: three opening blessings of praise (including Avot, "ancestors", and Gevurot, "mighty deeds"), thirteen middle blessings of petition — for wisdom, repentance, forgiveness, healing, prosperity, ingathering of exiles, justice and more — and three closing blessings of thanksgiving, including the priestly blessing and a prayer for peace. On Shabbat and festivals the middle petitions are replaced by a single blessing sanctifying the day, giving seven blessings; the Musaf service adds an additional Amidah.

Origins

According to rabbinic tradition, the prayer was composed by the Men of the Great Assembly and was formally arranged after the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE) under Rabban Gamaliel II at Yavneh. It is recited aloud in a repetition by the prayer leader in congregational services, during which the Kedushah is added.
