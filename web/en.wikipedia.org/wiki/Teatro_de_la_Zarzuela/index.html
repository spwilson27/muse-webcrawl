The Teatro de la Zarzuela is a theatre in Madrid, Spain, dedicated principally to zarzuela, the traditional Spanish lyric-dramatic genre combining spoken dialogue with opera-like song. Located on Calle Jovellanos near the Cortes, it was inaugurated on 10 October 1856, the birthday of Queen Isabella II, with a performance attended by the monarch.

History

The theatre was built by the Sociedad Lírico Española to give the revived zarzuela a permanent home; composers such as Francisco Asenjo Barbieri, a driving force behind the project, premiered major works there during the genre's golden age. A fire destroyed the building in 1909; it was rebuilt and reopened in 1913, retaining the original facade lines. Later remodelled in the 1950s under the Ministry of Culture, it has hosted seasons of zarzuela, opera, dance and concerts.

Present day

Today the theatre is managed by the Instituto Nacional de las Artes Escénicas y de la Música (INAEM) and remains the principal house for zarzuela in Spain, staging classic titles such as La verbena de la Paloma and Doña Francisquita alongside new commissions. A proposed merger with the Teatro Real in 2018 was abandoned after protests from staff and artists.
