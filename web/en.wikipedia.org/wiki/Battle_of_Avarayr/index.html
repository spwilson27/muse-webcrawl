The Battle of Avarayr was fought on 26 May 451 between a Christian Armenian army under Vardan Mamikonian and the forces of the Persian Sasanian Empire on the Avarayr plain in Vaspurakan. Although the Armenians were defeated and Vardan killed, the battle is regarded in Armenian tradition as a moral victory: the resistance eventually compelled Persia to abandon its attempt to impose Zoroastrianism on Armenia.

Background

Armenia, the first nation to adopt Christianity as a state religion (traditionally in 301 AD), had been partitioned between the Roman and Persian empires in 387, with the larger eastern portion under Persian suzerainty. In 449, Shah Yazdegerd II issued an edict requiring the Armenians to adopt Zoroastrianism. The Armenian nobility and clergy, meeting in council, refused, and an armed revolt broke out under Vardan Mamikonian, the hereditary sparapet (commander-in-chief).

The battle

According to the historian Elishe, whose History of Vardan and the Armenian War is the principal source, the Armenian host of some 60,000 faced a much larger Persian army, which included war elephants and the elite Immortals regiment. The Armenians, fighting with religious fervour, initially drove back the Persian right wing, but Persian reinforcements and the elephants broke their line. Vardan and many of the leading nobles fell on the field.

Aftermath

Persian victory proved pyrrhic: guerrilla resistance continued for decades, and the cost of holding Armenia down outweighed any benefit. In 484 the Treaty of Nvarsak granted the Armenians freedom of worship and removed Zoroastrian altars. Vardan Mamikonian was canonised by the Armenian Apostolic Church, and the battle remains a central episode of Armenian national identity, commemorated on the feast of Vardanants.
