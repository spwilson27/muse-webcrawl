Volcanic glass

Volcanic glass is the amorphous (uncrystallized) product of rapidly cooling magma. Like all types of glass, it is a state of matter intermediate between the closely packed, highly ordered array of a crystal and the highly disordered array of a gas. Rapid cooling prevents atoms from arranging into a crystal lattice.

Types

The best-known volcanic glass is obsidian, a usually black, silica-rich glass formed from felsic lava. Other forms include pumice (a frothy, vesicular glass), tachylite (basaltic glass), sideromelane, and Pele's hair — thin glass filaments drawn from molten lava fountains. Perlite is a hydrated volcanic glass that expands when heated.

Properties and uses

Volcanic glass is brittle and breaks with conchoidal fracture, producing very sharp edges; obsidian was prized for stone tools and blades from prehistoric times and is still used for surgical scalpels. Over geological time glass devitrifies and hydrates, and the degree of hydration of obsidian artifacts is used in obsidian hydration dating.
