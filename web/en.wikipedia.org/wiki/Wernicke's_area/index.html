Wernicke's area

Part ofCerebral cortex (temporal lobe)
LocationPosterior superior temporal gyrus (Brodmann area 22), dominant hemisphere
FunctionLanguage comprehension
Named afterCarl Wernicke

Wernicke's area is one of the two parts of the cerebral cortex linked to speech production and comprehension, the other being Broca's area. It is located in the posterior section of the superior temporal gyrus (Brodmann area 22) in the dominant — usually left — hemisphere, and is traditionally considered the seat of language comprehension.

History

The region is named after German physician Carl Wernicke, who in 1874 described patients whose speech remained fluent but incomprehensible, and who could not understand spoken language, following damage to this posterior temporal region — a pattern now called Wernicke's aphasia (receptive or fluent aphasia). In the classic Wernicke–Geschwind model, Wernicke's area connects to Broca's area via the arcuate fasciculus.

Function

Wernicke's area is involved in the comprehension of written and spoken language and in selecting words by meaning. Damage produces speech that is fluent and grammatically varied but empty of content, with neologisms and severe comprehension deficits. Modern neuroimaging suggests language processing is distributed across a wider network than the classical model assumed, with this region contributing especially to the sound-based (phonological) representation of words.
