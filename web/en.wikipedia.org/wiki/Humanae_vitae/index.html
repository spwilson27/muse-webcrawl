Humanae vitae

Humanae vitae
Type: Papal encyclical
Author: Pope Paul VI
Date: 25 July 1968
Subject: Marriage, sexuality, contraception
Latin title meaning: "Of human life"

Humanae vitae (Latin, "of human life") is an encyclical written by Pope Paul VI and dated 25 July 1968. Subtitled "On the Regulation of Birth," it re-affirmed the Catholic Church's traditional teaching that every marital act must remain open to procreation, thereby rejecting artificial contraception, while permitting natural family planning.

Background and content

A pontifical commission convened by John XXIII and expanded by Paul VI had reportedly favoured a change in teaching; Paul VI instead upheld the prohibition, arguing for the inseparable connection between the unitive and procreative meanings of the marital act and warning of consequences including infidelity and a general lowering of morality.

Reception

The encyclical proved highly controversial, prompting public dissent from many theologians and widespread non-compliance among lay Catholics, while supporters — including later popes John Paul II, whose Theology of the Body developed its arguments — have defended it as prophetic.
