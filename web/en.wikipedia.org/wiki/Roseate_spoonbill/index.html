Roseate spoonbill

The roseate spoonbill (Platalea ajaja) is a gregarious wading bird of the ibis and spoonbill family, Threskiornithidae. It is a resident breeder in both North and South America, and is unmistakable for its vivid pink plumage and long, flat, spoon-shaped bill.

Description

The roseate spoonbill is 71–86 cm (28–34 in) long with a wingspan of 120–133 cm (47–52 in). Adults have a bare greenish head, white neck, back and breast, and rich pink wings and underparts, with crimson highlights on the shoulders and rump; the pink colour derives from carotenoid pigments in its food, as in flamingos. The bill is grey and flattened into a broad spoon at the tip. Young birds are much paler, gaining full colour by about three years of age.

Behaviour

Spoonbills feed in shallow fresh and coastal waters, sweeping the partly open bill from side to side; the bill snaps shut on contact with small aquatic prey — crustaceans, insects and small fish. They nest in colonies in mangroves and shrubs over water, building bulky stick nests and laying two to five white, brown-spotted eggs.

Distribution and conservation

The species breeds along the Gulf Coast of the United States (notably Florida, Louisiana and Texas), through the Caribbean and Central America, and widely in South America east of the Andes as far south as Argentina. Plume hunting for the feather trade devastated United States populations in the 19th century, but the species has recovered under protection, though loss of feeding wetlands remains a concern.
