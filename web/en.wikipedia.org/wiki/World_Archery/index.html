The World Archery Federation (WA, formerly FITA from the French Fédération Internationale de Tir à l'Arc) is the international governing body for the sport of archery. Founded in 1931 in Lwów, Poland (now Lviv, Ukraine), it is headquartered in Lausanne, Switzerland, and is recognised by the International Olympic Committee.

History

FITA was founded on 4 September 1931 by representatives of France, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Poland, the United States, Hungary and Italy, initially to standardise rules for international target archery competition. The first World Archery Championships were held in Lwów in 1931. Archery returned to the Olympic Games at Munich 1972 after a long absence, with FITA as the governing federation. In 2011 the organisation adopted the name World Archery to modernise its brand.

Disciplines and events

World Archery governs target archery (recurve and compound), field archery, 3D archery, para-archery and other disciplines. Its flagship events include the World Archery Championships (held since 1931, generally every two years), the Archery World Cup circuit (introduced in 2006), and the archery competitions at the Olympic and Paralympic Games. Recurve archery is the Olympic discipline, contested at 70 metres.

Membership

The federation comprises more than 150 member national associations organised into five continental associations: World Archery Europe, World Archery Asia, World Archery Americas, World Archery Africa and World Archery Oceania.
