The Potala Palace is a monumental palace-fortress in Lhasa, the capital of the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. It was the winter palace of the Dalai Lamas from 1649 until 1959, and is named after Mount Potalaka, the mythical abode of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It is now a museum and UNESCO World Heritage Site, and one of the most recognisable landmarks of Tibetan Buddhism.

History

A palace was first built on Marpo Ri (Red Hill) by the Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo in the 7th century. The present structure was begun in 1645 by the Fifth Dalai Lama, Lozang Gyatso, whose advisers chose the site of the earlier fort. The White Palace was completed in 1649; the Red Palace, containing reliquary stupas and chapels, was added between 1690 and 1694. The palace served as the seat of Tibet's government and the Dalai Lama's residence until the 14th Dalai Lama fled to India in 1959.

Architecture

Built at an altitude of about 3,700 metres, the palace rises 13 storeys and contains more than 1,000 rooms, 10,000 shrines, and some 200,000 statues. Its massive inward-sloping walls, up to five metres thick at the base, are constructed of stone, earth, and timber, with copper poured into the foundations for earthquake resistance. The White Palace housed living quarters, offices, and monastic schools; the Red Palace is devoted to religious study, prayer, and the gilded burial stupas of past Dalai Lamas, the largest of which, for the Fifth Dalai Lama, is over 14 metres high and covered in gold and jewels.

Collections and pilgrimage

The palace preserves vast collections of murals, thangkas, scriptures, porcelain, jade, and gold objects. It remains a major pilgrimage site; devotees circumambulate the palace along the Lingkhor path and climb its stairways to the chapels. Since 1994 the palace, together with the Jokhang Temple and Norbulingka, has been listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Conservation and tourism

The Chinese government has funded major restoration programmes, and visitor numbers are now capped and ticketed to protect the structure. The palace appears on the Chinese 50-yuan banknote.
