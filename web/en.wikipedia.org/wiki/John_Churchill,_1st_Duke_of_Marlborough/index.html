John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough (26 May 1650 – 16 June 1722) was an English soldier and statesman whose career spanned five reigns, from Charles II to George I. As commander of British and Allied forces in the War of the Spanish Succession, his victories at Blenheim (1704), Ramillies (1706), Oudenarde (1708), and Malplaquet (1709) established him as one of the greatest military commanders in British history and secured Britain's emergence as a great power.

Early career

Of impoverished Royalist gentry stock, Churchill entered the court as a page and gained military experience at Tangier and in French service under Turenne. He rose under James II — his wife Sarah's intimacy with Princess Anne aided the couple — distinguished himself at Sedgemoor (1685), but deserted James for William of Orange in the Glorious Revolution of 1688, a decision rewarded with the earldom of Marlborough. Suspected of Jacobite contacts, he fell in and out of favour during the 1690s, fighting in Ireland and Flanders.

War of the Spanish Succession

Appointed captain-general and ambassador to the Dutch Republic in 1701, Marlborough held together the Grand Alliance against Louis XIV's France. In 1704 he marched his army from the Low Countries to the Danube — a feat of logistics and deception — and, with Prince Eugene of Savoy, destroyed the Franco-Bavarian army at Blenheim, saving Vienna and shattering the myth of French invincibility. Ramillies (1706) won the Spanish Netherlands; Oudenarde (1708) and the costly Malplaquet (1709) kept France on the defensive. He never lost a battle or a siege he undertook.

Fall and last years

Entangled in party politics — the Churchills backed the Tories' rivals as Sarah quarrelled with Queen Anne — Marlborough was dismissed in 1711 amid charges of embezzlement and went into exile. Restored under George I, he suffered strokes from 1716 and died in 1722. His reward for Blenheim, the royal manor of Woodstock, became Blenheim Palace, designed by Vanbrugh. His descendant Winston Churchill wrote a celebrated four-volume biography rehabilitating his reputation.
