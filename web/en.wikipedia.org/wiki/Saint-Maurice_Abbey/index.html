The Abbey of Saint-Maurice d'Agaune (French: Abbaye de Saint-Maurice) is a Swiss monastery of Canons Regular of St Augustine, in Saint-Maurice, Valais. Founded in 515 by the Burgundian king Sigismund, it is one of the oldest continuously occupied monasteries in the Western world, and is famous for its perpetual psalmody and its rich treasury of early medieval goldsmith work.

History

The abbey stands at Agaunum, the site where, according to tradition, the Theban Legion under Saint Maurice was martyred around the end of the 3rd century. King Sigismund founded the monastery in 515 and instituted the laus perennis, the continuous chanting of the psalms by relays of monks, which was maintained for centuries. The abbey became a major pilgrimage centre on the route over the Great St Bernard Pass and a territorial abbey whose abbot is bishop of the Diocese of Bethlehem (a titular arrangement) and head of a territorial abbacy.

The present abbey church, built in the 17th century and enlarged since, stands against a cliff; a rockfall in 1942 destroyed part of the complex. The treasury contains outstanding Merovingian and Carolingian pieces, including the ewer of Saint Maurice and reliquaries associated with the Theban martyrs.
