Pink slime

"Pink slime" is the media nickname for lean finely textured beef.

Pink slime is a pejorative term for a meat by-product known in the industry as lean finely textured beef (LFTB), and similar products such as finely textured meat. It is made from fatty beef trimmings that are heated and spun in a centrifuge to separate the lean meat, then treated with ammonium hydroxide gas (or, in another process, citric acid) to reduce bacterial contamination, and frozen into blocks for blending into ground beef and other processed meats.

2012 controversy

The product had been approved by the US Department of Agriculture and used for years, mostly without consumers' knowledge, since it was not separately labelled. In March 2012 an ABC News series popularised the name "pink slime" — a phrase reportedly coined by a USDA microbiologist, Gerald Zirnstein, in a 2002 internal email. The ensuing public outcry led major supermarket chains and fast-food companies to drop the ingredient, school districts to opt out, and the largest producer, Beef Products Inc., to close three of its four plants and lay off hundreds of workers.

Aftermath

Beef Products Inc. sued ABC News for defamation in 2012, seeking billions of dollars; the case was settled in 2017 for at least $177 million. Production partially recovered in later years. In 2018–2019 the USDA ruled that LFTB could be labelled simply as "ground beef." Regulators in the EU and Canada have not permitted the ammonium-hydroxide process for human food in the same way, though related products made with citric acid are treated differently.

Not to be confused with mechanically separated meat or with the unrelated use of "pink slime" for low-quality pseudo-news websites.
