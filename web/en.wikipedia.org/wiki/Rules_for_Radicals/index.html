Rules for Radicals

Rules for Radicals: A Pragmatic Primer for Realistic Radicals is a 1971 book by American community organizer and writer Saul Alinsky. It is Alinsky's best-known work and a foundational text in the field of community organizing, setting out tactics for groups seeking political and social change.

Background

Alinsky drew on decades of organizing experience in Chicago and elsewhere, including his work with the Industrial Areas Foundation, which he founded in 1940. The book was published the year before his death in 1972 and was aimed at a younger generation of activists shaped by the social movements of the 1960s.

The thirteen rules

The book's most famous chapter lists thirteen tactical rules for organizers confronting established power, including: "Power is not only what you have but what the enemy thinks you have"; "Never go outside the expertise of your people"; "Whenever possible go outside the expertise of the enemy"; "Make the enemy live up to its own book of rules"; "Ridicule is man's most potent weapon"; and "Pick the target, freeze it, personalize it, and polarize it."

Influence

The book has influenced organizers across the political spectrum, and its tactics are frequently cited in discussions of grassroots campaigning. Alinsky dedicated a playful acknowledgment in the book to Lucifer, whom he called the first radical.
