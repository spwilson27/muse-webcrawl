Athlete's foot

Medical name: Tinea pedis
Cause: Dermatophyte fungi, esp. Trichophyton rubrum
Symptoms: Itching, scaling, redness between the toes
Treatment: Topical antifungals; oral antifungals if severe

Interdigital tinea pedis, the most common presentation.

Athlete's foot, medically known as tinea pedis, is a common fungal infection of the skin of the feet caused by dermatophyte fungi, most often Trichophyton rubrum, Trichophyton interdigitale and Epidermophyton floccosum. The name arose because the condition is frequently acquired in communal showers, locker rooms and swimming pools used by athletes. It is estimated to affect 15–25 per cent of the population at any time.

Signs and symptoms

The classic interdigital form causes itching, scaling, redness and softened, white, macerated skin between the toes, especially between the fourth and fifth toes. Other patterns include the "moccasin" type, with dry scaling over the sole and heel; a vesicular (blistering) form on the instep; and ulcerative forms, usually complicated by bacterial infection. The infection may spread to the toenails (onychomycosis) or to the hands and groin.

Cause and transmission

Dermatophytes thrive in warm, moist environments and feed on keratin. Infection spreads by contact with contaminated surfaces — floors, towels, footwear — and is favoured by occlusive shoes, sweaty feet and walking barefoot in communal areas.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical and can be confirmed by microscopy of skin scrapings in potassium hydroxide (KOH) or by fungal culture. Most cases respond to over-the-counter topical antifungal creams such as terbinafine or clotrimazole applied for one to four weeks. Widespread, moccasin-type or nail-involved cases may need oral terbinafine or itraconazole. Keeping the feet dry, changing socks, and not sharing towels help prevent recurrence.
