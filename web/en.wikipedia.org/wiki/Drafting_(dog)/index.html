Drafting (dog)

Dog drafting
Also calledCarting
Typical breedsBernese Mountain Dog, Greater Swiss Mountain Dog, Newfoundland, Saint Bernard, Leonberger
UsesHistorically haulage; today sport, parades, therapy and farm work

Drafting, also known as carting, is the use of dogs to pull carts, wagons, sledges or other loads. Large, strong breeds were once working draught animals across Europe and North America, and drafting survives today as a competitive dog sport, a heritage activity and a form of exercise and engagement for working breeds.

History

Dog carts were once a common sight in much of Europe: Belgian and Dutch milk and bread carts, market deliveries, and the carts of pedlars were routinely dog-drawn, and in Switzerland the Bernese Mountain Dog hauled dairy produce from alpine farms. In Britain, concern over cruelty and road safety led to the banning of dog carts in London in 1839 and across the UK in 1854, one of the earliest pieces of animal-welfare legislation. Elsewhere dog haulage persisted into the 20th century, and dog teams also pulled sledges and travois in North America and Siberia.

Equipment and training

A drafting dog wears a padded draft harness that distributes the load across the chest and shoulders, attached by traces to a balanced two- or four-wheeled cart. Training begins with the dog walking beside an empty cart to become accustomed to its movement and noise, progressing to light loads; draught work is reserved for physically mature dogs, and loads must be balanced so the dog pulls rather than carries weight.

Modern sport

Breed clubs for the Bernese, Greater Swiss, Newfoundland and other draught breeds run drafting tests and competitions judging obedience, manoeuvrability through courses, and freight hauls, with titles awarded for progressively difficult work. Draft dogs also appear in parades, historical re-enactments and Christmas tree and farm haulage.
