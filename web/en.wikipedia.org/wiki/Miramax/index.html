Miramax

Miramax, LLC, formerly Miramax Films, is an American film and television production and distribution company founded in 1979 by brothers Bob and Harvey Weinstein in Buffalo, New York. Named after their parents Miriam and Max, the company became the leading producer and distributor of independent films in the 1990s, championing directors such as Steven Soderbergh, Quentin Tarantino, Robert Rodriguez, and Kevin Smith, and winning numerous Academy Awards.

History

Miramax began by distributing art-house and foreign films, achieving a breakthrough with Sex, Lies, and Videotape (1989) and The Crying Game (1992). In 1993 the Walt Disney Company acquired Miramax for a reported US$60–80 million, with the Weinsteins continuing to run it. Under Disney, Miramax released Pulp Fiction (1994), The English Patient (1996), Shakespeare in Love (1998), and many other commercial and critical successes, while its Dimension Films label handled genre and horror titles such as Scream.

The Weinsteins left in 2005 to form The Weinstein Company. Disney sold Miramax in 2010 to an investor group (Filmyard Holdings) for about US$660 million; it was subsequently sold to beIN Media Group in 2016. In 2020, Paramount Pictures acquired a 49% stake, and Miramax became a joint venture producing new films and exploiting its library of roughly 700 titles. Miramax's legacy was later overshadowed by the 2017 revelations of Harvey Weinstein's sexual abuse, which brought down his later company and catalysed the #MeToo movement.
