The Lower Galilee (Hebrew: הגליל התחתון, HaGalil HaTahton) is a sub-region of the Galilee in northern Israel. It stretches from the Jezreel Valley in the south to the Beit HaKerem Valley in the north, and from the Jordan Rift Valley and Sea of Galilee in the east to the coastal plain near Haifa in the west. Unlike the higher, more rugged Upper Galilee, the Lower Galilee consists of low east–west limestone ridges separated by broad fertile valleys, rarely exceeding 500 metres in elevation.

Geography

The region's ridges — including the Nazareth range and the Tur'an and Yodfat hills — are divided by valleys such as Beit Netofa, which holds the National Water Carrier's reservoir. The climate is Mediterranean, warmer and drier than the Upper Galilee, supporting olive groves, carob, and oak scrub. Its highest summit is Mount Tabor (575 m), rising abruptly above the Jezreel Valley and traditionally identified as the site of the Transfiguration of Jesus.

History

In the Second Temple period, the Lower Galilee was the most densely settled Jewish region of the country; Josephus, who governed Galilee during the First Jewish–Roman War, described its many towns and villages. After the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136 CE), the centre of Jewish life shifted here from Judaea: the Sanhedrin sat successively at Usha, Shefa-Amr, Beit Shearim, Sepphoris, and Tiberias, and the Mishnah was compiled in the region. Today the population is mixed, with Jewish towns such as Nazareth Illit (Nof HaGalil) and large Arab localities including Nazareth, Sakhnin, and Kafr Kanna.
