Ketogenesis

The HMG-CoA pathway of ketogenesis

Ketogenesis is the biochemical process through which organisms produce ketone bodies by breaking down fatty acids and ketogenic amino acids. It occurs primarily in the mitochondria of liver cells and supplies energy to extrahepatic tissues, including the brain, when glucose is in short supply, such as during fasting, carbohydrate restriction, or prolonged exercise.

Pathway

Acetyl-CoA generated by fatty acid oxidation is converted in hepatic mitochondria through the HMG-CoA pathway: two acetyl-CoA molecules condense to acetoacetyl-CoA, a third is added by HMG-CoA synthase 2 (the rate-limiting enzyme) to form HMG-CoA, and HMG-CoA lyase then releases acetoacetate. Acetoacetate can be reduced to beta-hydroxybutyrate or spontaneously decarboxylate to acetone. The liver exports acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate to other tissues, where they are reconverted to acetyl-CoA for the citric acid cycle (ketolysis); the liver itself cannot use ketone bodies because it lacks the enzyme thiophorase.

Regulation

Ketogenesis is stimulated by low insulin and high glucagon, which promote lipolysis and divert acetyl-CoA away from the citric acid cycle as oxaloacetate is drawn off for gluconeogenesis.

Clinical significance

Normal ketogenesis underlies the ketogenic diet and nutritional ketosis. Uncontrolled ketogenesis in insulin deficiency leads to diabetic ketoacidosis, a dangerous accumulation of ketone bodies that acidifies the blood.
