The Catholic Church, also known as the Roman Catholic Church, is the largest Christian church, with roughly 1.4 billion baptised members worldwide (approximate, often cited). Led by the pope (Bishop of Rome) in communion with bishops around the world, it traces its origin to Jesus Christ and the apostles, particularly Saint Peter, held to be the first pope.

Catholic doctrine is expressed in the Nicene Creed, seven sacraments, and the Catechism; the Church runs vast networks of parishes, schools, universities, hospitals and charities. Centred in Vatican City within Rome, it shaped European history through monasticism, scholasticism, missions and art — and was divided from Eastern Orthodoxy in 1054 (Great Schism) and from Protestant churches in the 16th-century Reformation. The Second Vatican Council (1962–65) modernised liturgy (vernacular Mass) and ecumenical relations.
