Retail Price Index

The Retail Price Index (RPI) is a measure of inflation published monthly by the Office for National Statistics in the United Kingdom. It measures the change in the cost of a representative basket of retail goods and services and was first calculated in 1947. Unlike the newer Consumer Prices Index (CPI), the RPI includes housing costs such as mortgage interest payments and council tax.

History and status

The RPI was for decades the UK's principal inflation measure and was used for indexation of pensions, benefits, tax allowances and index-linked gilts. It differs from CPI in population coverage, in the items included, and in its use of the Carli formula for elementary aggregation, which gives the RPI a systematic upward tendency known as the "formula effect". In 2013 the RPI lost its designation as a national statistic. In 2020 the government and the UK Statistics Authority confirmed that from 2030 the RPI would be aligned with the methods of CPIH (CPI including owner-occupiers' housing costs), a change long anticipated but expected to reduce RPI values.

Uses

Despite criticism, the RPI remains embedded in many long-term contracts, including index-linked government bonds, regulated rail fares, student loan interest and some occupational pensions. Variants include RPIX (excluding mortgage interest) and RPIY.
