Mysteries (novel)

Author: Knut Hamsun
Original title: Mysterier
Language: Norwegian
Published: 1892
Genre: Psychological novel

Mysteries (Norwegian: Mysterier, 1892) is the second novel by Norwegian author Knut Hamsun. It is regarded as a pioneering work of modernist and psychological fiction, anticipating techniques later associated with stream of consciousness writing.

Plot

The novel follows Johan Nilsen Nagel, an eccentric stranger who arrives in a small Norwegian coastal town in the summer of 1891. Nagel, who carries a vial of prussic acid in his waistcoat pocket, behaves erratically — alternately generous and provocative — and becomes entangled with the local community, including the parson's daughter Dagny Kielland and the tormented cripple known as Minutten. His stay ends in obsession and his eventual disappearance into the sea.

Reception

Together with Hunger (1890) and Pan (1894), Mysteries established Hamsun's reputation as a writer of the inner life. Isaac Bashevis Singer praised Hamsun as the father of the modern school of literature, and the novel has influenced generations of writers with its portrait of an irrational, divided protagonist.
