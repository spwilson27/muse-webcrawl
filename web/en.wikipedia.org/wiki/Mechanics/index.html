Mechanics

Mechanics (from Ancient Greek μηχανική, mēkhanikḗ, lit. "of machines") is the area of physics concerned with the relationships between force, matter, and motion among physical objects. Forces applied to objects may result in displacements, which are changes of an object's position relative to its environment.

Branches

Theoretical expositions of this branch of physics have their origins in Ancient Greece, for example in the writings of Aristotle and Archimedes. The foundations of modern mechanics were laid by Galileo Galilei and Isaac Newton; classical mechanics, formulated in Newton's laws of motion, describes macroscopic objects, while quantum mechanics governs atomic and subatomic scales, and relativistic mechanics applies at speeds near that of light. Within classical mechanics, subfields include statics, dynamics, kinematics, solid mechanics, fluid mechanics, and continuum mechanics. Analytical reformulations — Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics — underpin much of modern theoretical physics.

Applications

Mechanics is fundamental to engineering disciplines including mechanical, civil, aerospace, and biomedical engineering, as well as to astronomy (celestial mechanics), materials science, and biomechanics.
