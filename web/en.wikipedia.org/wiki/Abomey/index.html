Abomey

Abomey is a city in the Zou Department of Benin, the former capital of the Kingdom of Dahomey. Located about 110 km northwest of Cotonou, it is best known for the Royal Palaces of Abomey, a group of earthen palaces built by the Fon kings of Dahomey between the mid-17th and late 19th centuries, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.

History

According to tradition, Abomey was founded around 1625–1645 by Houegbadja, regarded as the founder of the Dahomey kingdom, whose motto — that the kingdom should always grow larger — shaped its expansionist character. Each king built a new palace within a common walled precinct, decorating its bas-reliefs with symbols recounting his reign, deeds and proverbs. Twelve palaces were constructed in succession, of which the best preserved are those of Kings Ghezo and Glele, now housing the Historical Museum of Abomey (founded 1943).

Abomey was a centre of the Atlantic slave trade, with captives from Dahomey's wars sold through the port of Ouidah; it was also the base of the celebrated women soldiers known as the Dahomey Amazons (Agojie). King Behanzin's resistance to French invasion ended with the burning of parts of Abomey in 1892 and his exile; the kingdom was absorbed into French Dahomey by 1900.

Heritage

The royal palaces site covers about 40 hectares and was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger from 1985 to 2007 due to fire and storm damage. In 2021 France returned 26 royal treasures looted from Abomey in 1892, including thrones and statues of the kings, which were exhibited in Cotonou; the works are associated with a planned museum in Abomey.
