Ventromedial prefrontal cortex

The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) is the lower, medial portion of the prefrontal cortex in the mammalian brain, roughly corresponding to Brodmann areas 10, 11, 12, 14, 25 and parts of 32. It is heavily connected with the amygdala, hippocampus, insula and ventral striatum, and plays a central role in emotion regulation, valuation and decision-making.

Functions

The vmPFC helps assign value to choices, consolidate extinction of conditioned fear, regulate negative emotion, and integrate emotional information into social and moral judgement. It is a core node of the default mode network and is active during self-referential thought, mentalising and simulating future events.

Damage and disorders

Lesions of the vmPFC — classically illustrated by the case of Phineas Gage, though his lesion was broader — produce disinhibition, poor decision-making under uncertainty, blunted affect and utilitarian shifts in moral dilemmas, while leaving intellect largely intact (the pattern studied by António Damásio under the somatic marker hypothesis). Dysfunction of the region is implicated in post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, addiction and anxiety disorders.
