Bengali, also known endonymically as Bangla (বাংলা), is an Indo-Aryan language native to the Bengal region of South Asia. It is the national and official language of Bangladesh and the second most widely spoken language in India, where it is official in West Bengal, Tripura and parts of Assam. With roughly 240 million native speakers it is among the five or six most spoken native languages in the world.

History

Bengali developed from the eastern Middle Indo-Aryan dialects (Magadhi Prakrit and its Apabhraṃśa stage) around the turn of the second millennium CE. The earliest surviving Bengali literature, the Buddhist Charyapada songs, dates to roughly the 8th–12th centuries. Under the Bengal Sultanate and Mughal rule the language absorbed extensive Persian and Arabic vocabulary; in the nineteenth century, prose was standardised by writers associated with Fort William College and by figures such as Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, and the modern literary language flowered in the Bengal Renaissance with Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, Michael Madhusudan Dutt and Rabindranath Tagore, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913.

Language movement

The demand that Bengali be recognised as a state language of Pakistan produced the Language Movement in East Bengal; on 21 February 1952 students and activists were shot by police in Dhaka, a date now commemorated worldwide as UNESCO's International Mother Language Day. Bengali nationalism fed into the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971 and independence.

Features

Bengali is written in the Bengali–Assamese script, an abugida with an inherent vowel, characterised by a horizontal headstroke linking characters. The language has a rich system of conjunct consonants, no grammatical gender, a subject–object–verb word order, and elaborate honorific distinctions in pronouns and verbs. Two standard registers historically coexisted — sadhu bhasha (Sanskritised) and chalit bhasha (colloquial) — with the latter now dominant. Dialects vary considerably across Bengal, from the standard based on the Nadia–Kolkata variety to eastern dialects such as Sylheti and Chittagonian, sometimes classified as separate languages.
