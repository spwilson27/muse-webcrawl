Maquiladora

A maquiladora (Spanish: [makilaˈðoɾa]), also known as a maquila, is a factory in Mexico run by a foreign company that imports materials and equipment duty-free for assembly or manufacturing and then exports the assembled product back to the originating country. Most are located in northern border cities such as Tijuana, Ciudad Juárez, Reynosa and Mexicali, and are owned by United States, Japanese, Korean and European corporations.

History

The system originated with Mexico's Border Industrialization Program of 1965, launched after the end of the US bracero guest-worker programme to create employment along the border. The industry grew rapidly, and expanded further after the North American Free Trade Agreement took effect in 1994; by the 2000s the maquila sector employed well over a million workers, producing electronics, auto parts, textiles, medical devices and aerospace components.

Criticism and conditions

Maquiladoras have been praised as a source of jobs and foreign exchange, and criticised for low wages, long hours, weak unions, occupational hazards, and environmental damage in border cities. In Ciudad Juárez, the employment of large numbers of young migrant women in the factories formed part of the social background of the city's notorious wave of femicides in the 1990s and 2000s.
