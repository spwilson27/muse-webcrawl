Underwater sports

Underwater sports is a group of competitive sports involving the use of one or a combination of the following underwater diving techniques—breath-hold, snorkelling, or scuba diving, including the use of equipment such as diving masks and fins. These sports are conducted in the natural environment, and in swim pools. The international governing body is the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS).

Disciplines

Recognised disciplines include finswimming (swimming with fins on the surface or underwater), freediving disciplines such as dynamic and constant-weight apnea, underwater hockey (octopush), underwater rugby, underwater target shooting, underwater orienteering, spearfishing, sport diving events, aquathlon (underwater wrestling), and underwater photography and videography competitions. Underwater hockey, invented in England in 1954, and underwater rugby, developed in Germany in the 1960s, are team sports played at the bottom of swimming pools.

Organisation

National federations affiliated with CMAS organise most world championships in these disciplines. Some sports, notably underwater hockey and rugby, also have their own international competitions and governing arrangements, and freediving is additionally governed by AIDA International.
