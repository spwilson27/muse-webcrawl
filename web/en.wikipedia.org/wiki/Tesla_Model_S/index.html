The Tesla Model S is a battery-electric, five-door luxury liftback produced by Tesla, Inc. since 2012. It was the first vehicle designed from scratch by Tesla (following the Roadster) and is widely credited with demonstrating that electric cars could outperform comparable petrol luxury sedans, helping to catalyse the wider industry shift to electrification.

History

Unveiled as a prototype in 2009 and designed under Franz von Holzhausen, the Model S entered production at the former NUMMI plant in Fremont, California, with first deliveries in June 2012. It won Motor Trend's Car of the Year for 2013. Tesla progressively increased battery capacity and performance, introduced dual-motor all-wheel drive and "Ludicrous" acceleration in 2014–15, and gave the car a facelift in 2016. A major 2021 refresh added a minimalist interior with a yoke steering control and the tri-motor Plaid version, among the quickest production cars ever sold, with over 1,000 horsepower and a claimed 0–60 mph time under two seconds.

Features

The Model S pioneered over-the-air software updates, a large central touchscreen controlling most functions, a flat underfloor battery pack giving a low centre of gravity, and access to Tesla's Supercharger network. Its driver-assistance suite, marketed as Autopilot (with optional "Full Self-Driving" capability), has been both influential and controversial. Early long-range versions achieved EPA ratings approaching 400 miles per charge, long the benchmark for production EVs.

Reception

The Model S received outstanding safety ratings and strong reviews for performance and technology, alongside criticism of build consistency and repair networks. Combined Model S and Model X production passed several hundred thousand units, and the car remains Tesla's flagship sedan.
