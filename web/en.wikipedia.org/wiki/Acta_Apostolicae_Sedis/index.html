Acta Apostolicae Sedis (Latin for "Acts of the Apostolic See"), abbreviated AAS, is the official gazette of the Holy See, published monthly in Vatican City. It contains the principal public documents of the Pope and the Roman Curia: encyclicals, apostolic constitutions, decrees, appointments of bishops and other official acts.

History

The journal was established by Pope Pius X with the apostolic constitution Promulgandi Pontificias Constitutiones of 29 September 1908 and began publication in January 1909. It replaced the earlier Acta Sanctae Sedis (1865–1908), a privately published collection that had nonetheless been treated as authoritative.

Legal significance

Under canon law, universal ecclesiastical laws are promulgated by publication in the Acta Apostolicae Sedis and ordinarily take effect three months after the date of the issue in which they appear, unless the law itself provides otherwise. The gazette is therefore the authoritative reference for the text of Vatican legislation. Back issues are published on the Holy See's website.
