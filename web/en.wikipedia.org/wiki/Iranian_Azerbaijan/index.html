Iranian Azerbaijan

Iranian Azerbaijan (Persian: آذربایجان ایران), also known as Persian Azerbaijan, is a historical and geographical region in northwestern Iran bordering Turkey, Iraq, Armenia, and the Republic of Azerbaijan. It principally comprises the modern provinces of East Azerbaijan, West Azerbaijan, and Ardabil, together with parts of Zanjan. Its largest city is Tabriz, long one of Iran's most important commercial and political centres.

History

The name Azerbaijan derives from Atropates, the satrap who governed the region — ancient Media Atropatene — after the death of Alexander the Great. The region was a crossroads of Persian, Turkic, Kurdish, Armenian, and Arab peoples. Following the Russo-Persian wars of the nineteenth century and the Treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828), historical Azerbaijan was split along the Aras River: the north went to the Russian Empire (today's Republic of Azerbaijan) and the south remained in Iran.

Tabriz was a centre of the Persian Constitutional Revolution (1905–1911). In 1945–46, during the post-Second World War Iran crisis, the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government under Jafar Pishevari briefly governed the region before Iranian forces re-established control in December 1946.

People and language

The population is predominantly ethnic Azerbaijanis, who speak Azerbaijani (a Turkic language) and form Iran's largest ethnic minority; most also speak Persian. Kurdish, Armenian, Talysh, and Tati-speaking minorities live in parts of the region. Iranian Azerbaijanis are predominantly Shia Muslims, like the majority of Iran.
