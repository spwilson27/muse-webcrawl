Clave (rhythm)

A clave (Spanish: key) is a rhythmic pattern of accented beats used as a tool for temporal organization in Afro-Cuban music. It is the structural core of genres including Cuban son, salsa, rumba, and Latin jazz: all parts of the arrangement — melodies, chord changes, and other percussion — are aligned to the clave's direction.

The five-stroke son clave pattern, the foundation of salsa and Cuban popular music.

Son and rumba clave

The most common pattern is the son clave, five strokes in a two-measure cycle grouped 3+2 or 2+3: in 4/4, the "3-side" plays on beat 1, the "and of 2", and beat 4, while the "2-side" plays on beats 2 and 3. The rumba clave delays the third stroke of the 3-side by an eighth note (to the "and of 4"), giving it a more driving feel. Whether a song is "in 3-2" or "2-3 clave" is determined by its chord progressions and melody; playing "out of clave" (cruzado) is considered a serious error by Latin musicians.

Related patterns

Clave patterns derive from West and Central African bell patterns (such as the standard pattern of Afro-Cuban 6/8 music) brought to Cuba through the slave trade. The related bossa nova clave in Brazilian music and the "Bo Diddley beat" in American rhythm and blues both descend from the same rhythmic cell.
