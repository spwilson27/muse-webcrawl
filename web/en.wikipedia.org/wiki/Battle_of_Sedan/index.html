Battle of Sedan

The Battle of Sedan was fought from 1 to 2 September 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War. The French Army of Châlons, accompanied by Emperor Napoleon III, was encircled and forced to surrender at the fortress town of Sedan — a catastrophe that ended the Second French Empire and effectively decided the war in Prussia's favour. A separate, famous Battle of Sedan also occurred in 1940.

Background

MacMahon's army marched to relieve Bazaine, besieged at Metz, but was intercepted and driven into the bend of the Meuse at Sedan after fighting at Beaumont. German armies under Moltke seized the surrounding heights.

Battle

German artillery pounded the packed French in the Sedan pocket. MacMahon was wounded early; command passed confusedly between Ducrot and Wimpffen. Desperate cavalry charges under General Margueritte and Gallifet at Floing — admired even by Wilhelm I — failed. With no escape, Napoleon III ordered the white flag raised on 2 September.

Aftermath

France lost an entire field army and its emperor. In Paris the Second Empire collapsed and the Third Republic was proclaimed on 4 September; the war continued with the siege of Paris, but German unification was proclaimed at Versailles in January 1871. Sedan Day became a national holiday in Imperial Germany.
