Henry B. Steagall

Henry Bascom Steagall (May 19, 1875 – November 22, 1943) was a United States representative from Alabama and chairman of the House Banking and Currency Committee. He is best remembered as the House sponsor of the Glass–Steagall Act of 1933, a landmark of New Deal financial reform.

Career

Born in Clopton, Dale County, Alabama, Steagall studied law, practised in Ozark, and served in the Alabama state legislature before election to Congress in 1914. He represented Alabama's 3rd congressional district for nearly thirty years, until his death in office in Washington, D.C.

Legislation

With Senator Carter Glass, Steagall sponsored the Banking Act of 1933 (Glass–Steagall), which separated commercial from investment banking and created federal deposit insurance through the FDIC. He also co-sponsored the Wagner–Steagall Housing Act of 1937, which created the United States Housing Authority and established the federal public-housing programme. As Banking Committee chairman he was a central figure in New Deal finance legislation.
