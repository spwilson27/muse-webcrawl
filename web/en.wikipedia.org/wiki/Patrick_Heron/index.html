Patrick Heron

Patrick Heron
Born: 30 January 1920, Leeds, England
Died: 20 March 1999, Zennor, Cornwall
Known for: Painting, art criticism, textile design

Patrick Heron (1920–1999) was a British abstract painter, critic, and designer, and one of the leading figures of the St Ives school of modern art in Cornwall. His work is characterized by luminous colour and an exploration of the spatial effects of juxtaposed colour areas.

Life and work

Heron was educated at St George's School, Harpenden, and studied part-time at the Slade School of Fine Art. During the Second World War he worked as an agricultural labourer as a conscientious objector and later for Bernard Leach's pottery in St Ives. In the 1940s and 1950s he was also an influential art critic, writing for the New Statesman and other journals, and designed silk scarves for his father's firm, Cresta Silks. Influenced first by Matisse and Braque, Heron moved to full abstraction in 1956, producing works of interlocking "garden" forms and later the celebrated colour-field paintings of discs, wiggly lines, and soft-edged shapes.

Recognition

Heron exhibited internationally, represented Britain at the São Paulo Bienal in 1953 (winning a prize), and had major retrospectives including at the Tate Gallery in 1985 and Tate St Ives in 2018. From 1956 he lived at Eagles Nest, Zennor, overlooking the Cornish coast, whose light and landscape deeply informed the sensuous colour of his mature paintings.
