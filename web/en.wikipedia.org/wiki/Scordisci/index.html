Scordisci

RegionConfluence of the Danube, Sava and Drava (Pannonia / Moesia)
Erac. 3rd century BC – 1st century AD
LanguageCeltic (probably)
Main centresSingidunum, Taurunum

The Scordisci were a Celtic tribe (or federation of tribes) that settled in the region of the confluence of the rivers Danube, Sava and Drava — in present-day Serbia, Croatia and neighbouring areas — after the Gallic invasion of the Balkans in 279 BC. Ancient authors such as Strabo and Livy describe them as powerful and warlike neighbours of the Illyrians and Thracians.

History

The Scordisci are generally held to have formed from remnants of the Celtic invaders who attacked Macedonia and Greece in 279 BC, settling among the indigenous Illyrian and Thracian populations of the middle Danube. Their centres included Singidunum (modern Belgrade) and Taurunum (modern Zemun). They repeatedly raided Macedonia and fought the Romans during the 2nd and 1st centuries BC, defeating or killing Roman commanders on several occasions before being subdued in a series of campaigns; they suffered a major defeat by the Dacian king Burebista and were further weakened by Roman operations culminating under Tiberius, after which they were incorporated into the Roman provinces of Pannonia and Moesia.

Culture

Archaeology in the region shows a La Tène Celtic material culture mixed with local traditions. Greek and Roman writers attributed fierce customs to the Scordisci — some reports, probably exaggerated, describe human sacrifice and drinking cups made from enemies' skulls. Strabo notes their neighbours the Autariatae and records that the Scordisci had once been powerful enough to reach the Adriatic.
