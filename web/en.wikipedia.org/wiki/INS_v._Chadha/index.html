INS v. Chadha

INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decided: 23 June 1983
Chief Justice: Warren Burger (majority)

Immigration and Naturalization Service v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983), was a landmark United States Supreme Court case that invalidated the one-house legislative veto, holding it unconstitutional under the Presentment Clause and the bicameralism requirements of the Constitution.

Facts

Jagdish Rai Chadha, born in Kenya to Indian parents and holding a British passport, overstayed a student visa. An immigration judge suspended his deportation under the Immigration and Nationality Act, but the House of Representatives, without a hearing or recorded reasons, passed a resolution vetoing the suspension for Chadha and five others.

Decision

Chief Justice Burger's 7–2 majority held that the House's action was legislative in character — it altered legal rights and duties — and therefore had to satisfy bicameral passage and presentment to the President. Because it did not, the one-house veto provision was unconstitutional, and was severable, so Chadha's suspension stood. Justice White dissented, calling the legislative veto an indispensable modern tool used in nearly 200 statutes; Justice Rehnquist dissented on severability grounds.

Impact

The ruling technically cast doubt on legislative-veto provisions across the federal code, though Congress has continued to use informal veto-like arrangements, and it remains a cornerstone case on separation of powers, bicameralism and presentment. See Separation of powers.
