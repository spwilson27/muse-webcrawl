The Wendish Crusade (German: Wendenkreuzzug) was a military campaign in 1147, one of the Northern Crusades and a part of the Second Crusade, directed against the Polabian Slavs (or "Wends") east of the Elbe in what is now northern Germany and Poland.

Background

When the Second Crusade was preached in 1147, many Saxon nobles preferred to fight the pagan Slavs on their own frontier rather than travel to the Holy Land. Bernard of Clairvaux endorsed the diversion, promising the same spiritual rewards, and Pope Eugenius III sanctioned the campaign.

Campaign

Saxon, Danish and Polish forces moved against Slav strongholds such as Dobin and Demmin. The campaign achieved little lasting conversion: the Slav leader Niklot negotiated terms, besieged fortresses held out, and the crusaders withdrew, although the campaign foreshadowed the later German eastward expansion and the eventual Christianisation and assimilation of the region.
