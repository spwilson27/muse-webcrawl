Battle of Greece

The Battle of Greece (also known as Operation Marita, German: Unternehmen Marita) was the invasion of Greece by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy in April 1941, during the Second World War. After the Greek army had repelled Italy's invasion of October 1940, Germany intervened through Bulgaria and Yugoslavia; mainland Greece was overrun within weeks, and an airborne assault seized Crete in May–June 1941.

Background

Italy's attack from Albania on 28 October 1940 was thrown back by the Greek army, which advanced into Albania. Fearing British air bases in Greece, Hitler ordered Operation Marita. Britain sent W Force — some 58,000 Commonwealth troops under General Henry Maitland Wilson — to support Greece, while Germany's Twelfth Army assembled in Bulgaria and the invasion of Yugoslavia (Operation 25) cleared the flank.

The campaign

Germany attacked on 6 April 1941. German armour broke through the Metaxas Line's flanks via Yugoslav Macedonia, taking Thessaloniki on 9 April and cutting off the Greek army facing the Italians in Albania, which surrendered on 23 April. Commonwealth forces conducted a fighting withdrawal to Thermopylae and were evacuated to Crete and Egypt; Athens fell on 27 April. The Battle of Crete (20 May – 1 June) completed the conquest at heavy cost to German paratroops.

Aftermath

Greece was partitioned into German, Italian and Bulgarian occupation zones and suffered a devastating famine and resistance struggle. Historians debate whether the Balkan campaign delayed Operation Barbarossa — the invasion of the Soviet Union — with serious consequences for Germany.
