Overview

Angina, also called angina pectoris, is chest pain or pressure caused by insufficient oxygen-rich blood flow to the heart muscle (myocardial ischaemia), most often due to coronary artery disease. It is typically felt as squeezing, heaviness or tightness in the centre or left of the chest, and may radiate to the neck, jaw, shoulder or arm.

Types and management

Stable angina is triggered predictably by exertion or stress and relieved by rest or nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate). Unstable angina — occurring at rest, worsening, or new and severe — is a medical emergency, as it may herald a heart attack. Variant (Prinzmetal) angina arises from coronary spasm, and microvascular angina from small-vessel dysfunction. Diagnosis uses history, ECG, stress testing and coronary imaging; treatment addresses risk factors, symptoms (beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, nitrates) and, where indicated, revascularisation.
