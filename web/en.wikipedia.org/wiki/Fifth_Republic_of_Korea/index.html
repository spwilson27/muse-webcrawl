The Fifth Republic of Korea was the government of South Korea from 1981 to 1988, established under a new constitution promulgated after the coup d'état of December 1979 and the subsequent events of 1980. It was led throughout by President Chun Doo-hwan.

Establishment

After the assassination of President Park Chung-hee in October 1979, Major General Chun Doo-hwan seized power in the coup of 12 December 1979. Following the suppression of the Gwangju Uprising in May 1980, Chun became president under the Yushin system and then under a new constitution approved by referendum in October 1980, inaugurating the Fifth Republic in March 1981. The constitution provided for a single, non-renewable seven-year presidential term and indirect election by an electoral college.

Politics and society

The regime pursued economic stabilisation and growth, and South Korea experienced strong export-led growth during the period, alongside preparations to host the 1988 Summer Olympics in Seoul. Political repression continued, but opposition movements — students, labour, and pro-democracy groups — grew steadily. In June 1987, mass pro-democracy protests (the June Democratic Struggle) forced the regime to accept constitutional reform introducing direct presidential elections.

End

Roh Tae-woo, Chun's designated successor, won the direct election of December 1987, and the Sixth Republic was inaugurated in February 1988 under a new constitution that remains in force, with amendments, today.
