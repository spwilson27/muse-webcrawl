Edna Ferber (15 August 1885 – 16 April 1968) was an American novelist, short story writer and playwright. Her best-known novels include So Big (1924), which won the Pulitzer Prize, Show Boat (1926), which became the celebrated Jerome Kern and Oscar Hammerstein II musical, Cimarron (1930), adapted into an Academy Award–winning film, and Giant (1952), filmed in 1956 with Elizabeth Taylor, Rock Hudson and James Dean.

Early life and journalism

Ferber was born in Kalamazoo, Michigan, to a Hungarian-born Jewish storekeeper and his Chicago-born wife, and grew up in small Midwestern towns, including Appleton, Wisconsin, where she experienced antisemitism at school. After high school she became a reporter for the Appleton Daily Crescent and then the Milwaukee Journal — an apprenticeship that sharpened the ear for dialogue and the eye for American regional life that mark her fiction.

Fiction

Her early Emma McChesney stories, about a travelling saleswoman, were popular successes. So Big (1924), the story of Chicago truck-farmer and widow Selina DeJong, won the Pulitzer Prize for the Novel in 1925. Ferber specialised in panoramic novels of American places rendered with meticulous research: the Mississippi riverboat world of Show Boat, the Oklahoma land rush of Cimarron, the Texas oil and cattle empire of Giant, and Alaska in Ice Palace (1958). Many of her heroines are strong, independent working women, and her books frequently confronted ethnic prejudice and discrimination.

Theatre

In collaboration with George S. Kaufman she wrote successful Broadway plays including The Royal Family (1927), a satire of the Barrymore acting dynasty, Dinner at Eight (1932) and Stage Door (1936). She was a member of the Algonquin Round Table circle of New York wits, though she kept her distance from its idleness. Ferber never married and published two volumes of autobiography, A Peculiar Treasure (1939) and A Kind of Magic (1963). She died in New York in 1968.
