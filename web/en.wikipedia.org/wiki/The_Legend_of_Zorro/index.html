The Legend of Zorro is a 2005 American swashbuckler film directed by Martin Campbell, and the sequel to The Mask of Zorro (1998). Antonio Banderas and Catherine Zeta-Jones reprise their roles as Alejandro de la Vega (Zorro) and Elena de la Vega. Set in 1850, as California stands on the verge of statehood, the film follows Alejandro as he is drawn back into his double life despite promises to his wife, while a secret society plots to destroy the young United States using a powerful new explosive.

Plot

Ten years after the events of the first film, Alejandro and Elena have a son, Joaquin. Elena, frustrated by Alejandro's continued life as Zorro and by his absences, divorces him. Alejandro's investigations uncover the Knights of Aragon, a clandestine order led by Count Armand (Rufus Sewell), Elena's childhood friend, who is smuggling nitroglycerin disguised as bars of soap to Confederate sympathisers. In the climax, Zorro and Elena foil the plot to blow up a train carrying the document of California's admission to the Union, and the family is reconciled — with Joaquin now in on the secret.

Reception

The film received mixed reviews from critics, who praised its action set-pieces and the leads' chemistry but found the plot convoluted and the tone more family-oriented than its predecessor. It grossed about $142 million worldwide against a $75 million budget.
