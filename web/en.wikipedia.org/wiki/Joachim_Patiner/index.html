Joachim Patinir

Joachim Patinir, also called Patenier (c. 1480 – 5 October 1524), was a Flemish Renaissance painter of history and landscape subjects. He is considered the first specialist landscape painter in Northern Renaissance art and a pioneer of the Weltlandschaft ("world landscape") style, with vast panoramic vistas seen from a high viewpoint, small figures, and atmospheric blue-green distance.

Life

Patinir was born near Dinant in Wallonia. He became a master in the Guild of Saint Luke in Antwerp in 1515. Albrecht Dürer, who met him in 1520–21 and drew his portrait, called him "the good landscape painter". He collaborated with other artists, including Quentin Matsys, who painted figures in his landscapes.

Work

Only a small number of signed works survive, including Landscape with Charon Crossing the Styx (Prado), The Temptation of Saint Anthony, and Rest on the Flight into Egypt. Religious narratives are set within expansive imaginary landscapes of rocky crags, rivers, and distant towns, divided into brown foreground, green middle ground, and blue distance.

Legacy

Patinir's world landscapes influenced Pieter Bruegel the Elder and the development of landscape as an independent genre in European painting.
