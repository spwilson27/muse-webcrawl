Cedar Key, Florida

Cedar Key is a city in Levy County, Florida, United States. The population was 687 at the 2020 census. One of the oldest ports in the state, Cedar Key is a small coastal community on a cluster of islands — known as keys — in the Gulf of Mexico, connected to the mainland by State Road 24. It is noted for its clam aquaculture, historic district, and arts festivals.

History

The Cedar Keys were inhabited by Indigenous peoples for thousands of years; shell middens on the islands date back millennia. In the 19th century, Cedar Key became a major port: it was the western terminus of Florida's first cross-state railroad, the Florida Railroad, completed in 1861, and a centre for the pencil-cedar timber trade, supplying mills such as the Eberhard Faber mill. During the Civil War the port was blockaded and raided. A devastating hurricane in 1896 destroyed much of the original town on Atsena Otie Key, and the community rebuilt on nearby Way Key, the site of the present city.

Geography and economy

Cedar Key lies about 50 miles (80 km) southwest of Gainesville. The surrounding waters are part of the Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge and the Big Bend Seagrasses Aquatic Preserve. After the 1994 statewide ban on commercial net fishing, the town reinvented itself through clam farming, and Cedar Key became one of the largest producers of farm-raised hard clams in the United States. Tourism, fishing, and kayaking also support the local economy.

Hurricanes

The low-lying town has been repeatedly struck by hurricanes, including the 1896 Cedar Keys hurricane, Hurricane Elena in 1985, Hurricane Idalia in 2023, and Hurricane Helene in 2024, which caused severe flooding in the downtown area.
