Taliesin (pronounced "tally-ESS-in") is the estate, home, studio and school of the American architect Frank Lloyd Wright, located near Spring Green in Sauk County, Wisconsin. Wright began building it in 1911 on land belonging to his mother's family, naming it after Taliesin, the Welsh bard of legend — Wright was of Welsh descent. It became the laboratory of his "organic architecture" and the summer counterpart to Taliesin West in Arizona.

History

The estate has a tragic history. On 15 August 1914, while Wright was away in Chicago, a servant, Julian Carlton, set fire to the living quarters and murdered seven people with an axe as they tried to escape, among them Wright's partner Martha "Mamah" Borthwick Cheney and her two children. Wright rebuilt the house (Taliesin II), only for an electrical fire to destroy the living quarters again in 1925; he rebuilt once more (Taliesin III). In 1932 he founded the Taliesin Fellowship on the estate, an apprenticeship programme through which young architects lived and worked with him; the estate's Hillside Home School buildings were adapted as their drafting studio and theatre.

Architecture

The complex includes the main house and studio, the Hillside buildings, a theatre, and farm buildings, all in Wright's horizontal Prairie manner, built of local limestone and wood and integrated into the brow of a hill overlooking the Wisconsin River valley — Wright insisted the house be built "of the hill, not on the hill." In 2019 Taliesin was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright". The estate is preserved by Taliesin Preservation, Inc., and is open for tours.
