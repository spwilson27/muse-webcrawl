Sonny Bono

Salvatore Phillip "Sonny" Bono
Born: 16 February 1935, Detroit, Michigan, US
Died: 5 January 1998, South Lake Tahoe, California
Occupations: Singer, songwriter, actor, politician
Offices: Mayor of Palm Springs (1988–1992); US Representative from California (1995–1998)

Salvatore Phillip Bono (16 February 1935 – 5 January 1998), known as Sonny Bono, was an American singer, songwriter, actor and politician. He achieved fame as one half of the duo Sonny & Cher with his then-wife Cher, and later served in the United States House of Representatives.

Entertainment career

Bono began as a songwriter for Specialty Records and worked as a promotion man and apprentice to producer Phil Spector. With Cher he recorded hits including "I Got You Babe" (1965) and wrote songs such as "Needles and Pins". In the 1970s the pair starred in the variety series The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour.

Political career

Frustrated by bureaucracy while opening a restaurant, Bono entered politics, becoming mayor of Palm Springs, California (1988–1992). In 1994 he was elected to Congress as a Republican. His most lasting legislative legacy is the Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998, nicknamed the "Sonny Bono Act", which extended US copyright terms by twenty years.

Death

Bono died on 5 January 1998 after striking a tree while skiing at the Heavenly Mountain Resort near South Lake Tahoe. His widow, Mary Bono, succeeded him in Congress.
