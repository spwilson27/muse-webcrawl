Impossibility

Impossibility is the state or property of something that cannot be, cannot occur, or cannot be done. In philosophy and logic the concept is analysed in terms of modality: a proposition is impossible if it is false in every possible world, in contrast to what is merely contingently false or necessarily true.

Kinds of impossibility

Philosophers commonly distinguish several kinds. Logical impossibility involves a contradiction, such as a square circle or a married bachelor. Metaphysical impossibility concerns what could not obtain given the nature of things, even without formal contradiction. Physical (nomological) impossibility is what is ruled out by the laws of nature, such as travelling faster than light. Practical impossibility refers to what is beyond available means, technology or resources, though not impossible in principle.

In law and other fields

In contract law, impossibility is a doctrine under which a party may be excused from performance when an unforeseen event makes performance objectively impossible. In mathematics and computer science, impossibility results — such as Gödel's incompleteness theorems or the halting problem — prove that certain tasks or proofs cannot exist at all.
