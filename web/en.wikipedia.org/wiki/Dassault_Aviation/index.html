Dassault Aviation SA is a French aerospace corporation that builds military aircraft, business jets and space systems. It is the last major aircraft manufacturer in the world still controlled by its founding family and bearing its founder's name. The company forms part of Groupe Dassault.

History

Marcel Bloch founded the company in 1929; its aircraft served France in the Second World War, after which Bloch — a survivor of Buchenwald who changed his surname to Dassault, his brother's Resistance codename — rebuilt the firm. Its postwar jets included the Ouragan, the Mystère series and the delta-winged Mirage III, exported worldwide. Later military programmes include the Mirage 2000, the carrier-capable Rafale omnirole fighter and the nEUROn stealth unmanned combat demonstrator.

Products

Dassault's civil line, the Falcon family of business jets (Falcon 50, 900, 2000, 7X/8X and the ultra-long-range 10X), accounts for roughly half of turnover; the company is also a partner in the European Future Combat Air System. Since 2019 it has owned Dassault Systèmes' sibling software lineage only through the wider Dassault group; its own digital design tools pioneered computer-aided aircraft design with CATIA.
