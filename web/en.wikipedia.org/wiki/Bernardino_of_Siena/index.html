Bernardino of Siena (8 September 1380 – 20 May 1444), also known as Bernardine of Siena, was an Italian Catholic priest and Franciscan missionary preacher. He was a systematizer of Scholastic economics, and his preaching, in which he relentlessly denounced the sins of his age — usury, sodomy, witchcraft, and political factionalism — made him one of the most famous preachers of the fifteenth century.

Life

Bernardino was born in Massa Marittima, in the Republic of Siena, into the noble Albizzeschi family. Orphaned at an early age, he was raised by an aunt in Siena, where he studied civil and canon law. During the plague of 1400 he worked in the hospital of Santa Maria della Scala, nursing the sick. In 1402 he joined the Franciscan Order, and in 1404, on his twenty-fourth birthday, he began his preaching career.

For more than thirty years Bernardino travelled throughout Italy preaching in town squares to enormous crowds, often numbering in the tens of thousands. He promoted devotion to the Holy Name of Jesus, displaying the IHS Christogram on a tablet surrounded by rays of the sun, which became his emblem. His sermons were frequently followed by "bonfires of the vanities," in which gambling equipment, cosmetics, and other objects of luxury were publicly burned.

Economics

Bernardino was the first theologian to write a systematic treatise on economics from a Scholastic standpoint, in his work On Contracts and Usury. He condemned usury but recognised the legitimacy of some forms of profit, discussing entrepreneurship, the justification of private property, and the ethics of trade. He is also known for distinguishing between the just price and market manipulation.

Death and canonization

Bernardino died at L'Aquila in 1444 while on a preaching tour. He was canonized by Pope Nicholas V only six years later, in 1450. His feast day is 20 May. He is regarded as the patron saint of advertising and communications because of his skill as a popular preacher and his use of a memorable visual symbol.
