Ireland and World War I

During the First World War (1914–1918), Ireland was part of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. Around 200,000 Irishmen volunteered to serve in the British Army, and some 30,000–49,000 died, at Gallipoli, the Somme, Passchendaele, and elsewhere. Conscription was never extended to Ireland.

Home Rule suspended

The Home Rule Act received royal assent in September 1914 but was suspended for the war's duration. Nationalist leader John Redmond urged Irish Volunteers to enlist in support of Britain, splitting the movement; a rump under Eoin MacNeill refused. Ulster unionists joined the 36th (Ulster) Division, while nationalist recruits formed much of the 16th (Irish) Division.

Easter Rising

In April 1916, Irish republicans staged the Easter Rising in Dublin seeking independence. Its suppression and the execution of its leaders transformed Irish opinion, boosted Sinn Féin, and set in motion the Irish War of Independence (1919–1921) and partition, changing Ireland's relationship to the war's memory for generations.
