Sunraysia

Sunraysia is an informal region of north-western Victoria and south-western New South Wales, Australia, centred on the city of Mildura. The name — a coined blend of "sun" and "raisin" — was promoted in the 1920s by entrepreneur Jack De Garis to market the district's dried vine fruits. Irrigated by the Murray River, Sunraysia is one of Australia's most important horticultural regions, producing the great majority of the nation's dried grapes as well as table grapes, citrus, almonds, olives and wine grapes.

State: Victoria / New South Wales
Centre: Mildura
Known for: Dried vine fruit, citrus, table grapes

History

The region's irrigation settlement began in 1887 when Canadian brothers George and William Chaffey established the Mildura Irrigation Colony under agreement with the Victorian government. Despite early financial collapse, soldier-settler schemes after both World Wars expanded irrigation to settlements including Red Cliffs, Irymple, Merbein and, across the river in New South Wales, Buronga and Gol Gol.

Economy

Sunraysia produces around 95 per cent of Australia's dried vine fruit (sultanas, raisins and currants) and a large share of its table grapes and citrus exports. The name is used by the local daily newspaper, the Sunraysia Daily (founded 1920), and by regional institutions such as the Sunraysia Institute of TAFE. Water availability from the Murray–Darling Basin and competition from imports are the industry's recurring challenges.
