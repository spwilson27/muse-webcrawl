Deutz AG

Deutz AG is a German internal combustion engine manufacturer based in Cologne, in the district of Deutz. Founded in 1864 by Nikolaus Otto and Eugen Langen, it is regarded as the oldest engine factory in the world and the cradle of the four-stroke engine.

History

The company began as N. A. Otto & Cie, producing atmospheric gas engines. In 1876 Otto developed the compressed-charge four-stroke engine — the "Otto cycle" — a landmark in engineering history. Gottlieb Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach worked at Deutz before founding their own firm. The company became Klöckner-Humboldt-Deutz (KHD) in 1938, building Magirus-Deutz trucks and Deutz-Fahr tractors, before refocusing on engines and reverting to the Deutz AG name in 1997.

Products

Deutz produces air- and liquid-cooled diesel engines from roughly 25 to 620 kW for agricultural machinery, construction equipment, commercial vehicles, rail vehicles, marine applications and stationary power generation. It has also developed hydrogen and electric drive concepts for off-highway use.

Organisation

Deutz AG is listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (SDAX) and employs several thousand people, with production in Germany, Spain, China and the United States.
