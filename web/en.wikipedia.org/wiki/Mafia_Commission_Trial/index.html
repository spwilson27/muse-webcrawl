The Mafia Commission Trial (in full, United States v. Anthony Salerno, et al.) was a criminal trial in New York City, United States. Using evidence obtained by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, eleven organized crime figures, including the heads of New York City's so-called "Five Families", were indicted by United States Attorney Rudolph Giuliani under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO) on February 25, 1985. Eight of the defendants were convicted under RICO on November 19, 1986, except for Paul Castellano, who was murdered in December 1985, and nine were sentenced on January 13, 1987.

Background

In the early 1980s, the FBI mounted a major offensive against the American Mafia, using wiretaps and informants to gather evidence against the leadership of New York's Five Families — the Gambino, Lucchese, Colombo, Bonanno, and Genovese families. Federal investigators had long suspected that a ruling body known as "The Commission" coordinated the activities of the families, settled disputes, and approved murders. The trial marked the first time prosecutors sought to convict the Mafia's entire governing structure in a single proceeding.

The defendants

The principal defendants included Anthony "Fat Tony" Salerno, reputed boss of the Genovese family; Carmine "The Snake" Persico, boss of the Colombo family; and Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo, boss of the Lucchese family. Gambino boss Paul Castellano was indicted as well, but he was assassinated on December 16, 1985, outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan, in a hit orchestrated by John Gotti. Persico, an experienced litigant, chose to represent himself at trial.

The trial

The prosecution argued that the Commission oversaw a vast racketeering enterprise, including a concrete "club" that rigged bids on construction contracts in New York, extorting percentages on jobs worth millions of dollars. Evidence included surveillance photographs and secretly recorded conversations, among them recordings made inside the Palma Boys Social Club in East Harlem. The defense argued that the Commission was a myth invented by prosecutors.

Verdict and sentences

On November 19, 1986, the jury convicted eight defendants on all counts. On January 13, 1987, Salerno, Persico, and Corallo were each sentenced to 100 years in prison; other defendants received sentences of 40 to 100 years. Salerno died in prison in 1992, Corallo in 2000, and Persico in 2019.

Aftermath

The trial is widely regarded as a landmark in the federal campaign against organized crime, demonstrating that the RICO statute could be used to dismantle the Mafia's leadership in a single case. It paved the way for later prosecutions, including the trial of John Gotti in 1992.
