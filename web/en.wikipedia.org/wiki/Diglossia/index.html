Diglossia

Diglossia
Field: Sociolinguistics
Defined by: Charles A. Ferguson (1959)
Examples: Arabic, Greek, Swiss German, Haitian Creole
Related: Demotic Greek, Register

Diglossia is a situation in which two distinct varieties of a language — a prestigious 'high' (H) variety and a vernacular 'low' (L) variety — are used in complementary social contexts within one speech community.

Ferguson's model

Charles Ferguson's 1959 definition cited Arabic (Classical/Modern Standard vs colloquial), Greek (Katharevousa vs Demotic), Swiss German (Standard German vs Swiss dialects) and Haitian French/Creole. H is used for sermons, literature, news and education; L for home and everyday conversation.

Characteristics

H is usually learned at school, has a literary tradition and complex grammar, and is no one's mother tongue, while L is acquired natively. Joshua Fishman later extended the term to cases involving different languages.

Outcomes

Diglossic situations may persist for centuries, or resolve when the low variety gains official status, as Demotic did in Greece in 1976.
