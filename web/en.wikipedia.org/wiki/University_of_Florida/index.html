University of Florida

The University of Florida (Florida or UF) is a public land-grant research university in Gainesville, Florida. It is the flagship university of the State University System of Florida and one of the largest universities in the United States, with more than 60,000 students. UF is a member of the Association of American Universities and is consistently ranked among the top public universities in the country.

History

The university traces its origins to 1853 and was consolidated in Gainesville in 1905 under the Buckman Act, which merged several state institutions. It admitted women beginning in 1947 and was racially integrated in 1958. The university's health center, UF Health Shands, and its Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences (IFAS) are major components.

Research and notable facts

UF is classified among universities with very high research activity, with annual research expenditures exceeding $1 billion. The sports drink Gatorade was invented at UF in 1965 by a team led by Robert Cade to help the football team combat dehydration; the drink's name derives from the Florida Gators.

Athletics

The Florida Gators compete in the Southeastern Conference and have won national championships in football, men's and women's basketball, and many other sports. Ben Hill Griffin Stadium, nicknamed "The Swamp," seats nearly 90,000.
