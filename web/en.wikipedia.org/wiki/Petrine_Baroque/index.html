Periodc. 1697–1730s
RegionRussia, esp. Saint Petersburg

Petrine Baroque is a style of Baroque architecture and decoration favoured by Peter the Great and widely used in the construction of his new capital, Saint Petersburg, in the first decades of the 18th century. It is distinct from the contemporary Naryshkin Baroque of Moscow for its clearer, more restrained, and more rationally ordered Western European character.

Characteristics

The style drew on influences Peter encountered during his travels in the Netherlands, Germany, and Sweden: Dutch classicism, North German brick architecture, and elements of French and Italian Baroque, blended with local building practices. Favourite features included symmetrical plans, flat pilasters, restrained rustication, tall windows with white frames contrasting against coloured (often red or yellow) walls, and tented or spired bell towers borrowed from Russian tradition.

Major works

Landmarks built under Domenico Trezzini and his circle include the Peter and Paul Cathedral (1712–1733) with its slender gilded spire, the Twelve Collegia building, the Summer Palace of Peter the Great, and the Kunstkamera; other examples include the Menshikov Palace, the Cabin of Peter the Great's contemporaries at Peterhof, and the Alexander Nevsky Lavra. Under Peter's successors the style gave way first to the more exuberant Elizabethan Baroque of Bartolomeo Rastrelli.
