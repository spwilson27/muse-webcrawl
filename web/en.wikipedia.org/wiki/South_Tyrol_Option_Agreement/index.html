South Tyrol Option Agreement
Signed: 23 June 1939
Parties: Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy
Subject: Resettlement of German speakers from South Tyrol

The South Tyrol Option Agreement (German: Option in Südtirol) was a 1939 agreement between Adolf Hitler and Benito Mussolini under which the German-speaking population of South Tyrol — an Austrian territory annexed by Italy after the First World War — was forced to choose between emigrating to Nazi Germany or remaining in Italy and accepting forced Italianisation.

Background

After 1919, Fascist Italy pursued harsh Italianisation of South Tyrol, banning German in schools and public life. Within the Axis alliance, the Tyrol question was an irritant; Hitler prioritised the alliance with Mussolini over the South Tyroleans and agreed to resolve the issue by population transfer.

The option and its aftermath

In the 1939 vote, roughly 86 percent of those eligible opted to emigrate ("Optanten"), while those who chose to stay ("Dableiber") were often stigmatised. Before the Second World War halted the programme, about 75,000 people actually left. After 1945, many emigrants returned, and the question was eventually addressed by autonomy arrangements under the 1946 Gruber–De Gasperi Agreement and later autonomy statutes.
