Próspera is a privately governed charter city and special economic zone on the island of Roatán in Honduras. It was established under Honduras's ZEDE law (Zones for Employment and Economic Development), which permitted semi-autonomous zones with their own legal, regulatory and tax systems.

Overview

Launched in 2020 by Honduras Próspera Inc., the project sits on Roatán's north shore near Crawfish Rock. It offers residents and businesses a choice of regulatory regimes, low taxes, a common-law-inspired legal code, and an online residency and business-registration system ("ePróspera"). Bitcoin was adopted as a unit of account and legal tender within the zone in 2022. Backers describe it as an experiment in competitive governance aimed at attracting investment and jobs.

Controversy and legal status

The ZEDE framework has been controversial in Honduras: critics, including local communities on Roatán, have raised concerns about sovereignty, land rights and consultation. In 2022 the Honduran Congress voted to repeal the ZEDE law, and in 2024 the Supreme Court declared the ZEDE regime unconstitutional, while recognising acquired rights for existing zones for a transitional period. Próspera's operator has pursued investor-state arbitration claims against Honduras seeking billions of dollars in damages, arguing that repeal violates investment treaties and legal-stability agreements.
