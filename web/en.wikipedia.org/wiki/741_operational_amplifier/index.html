741 operational amplifier

The 741 is a general-purpose operational amplifier integrated circuit first produced by Fairchild Semiconductor in 1968. Designed by Dave Fullagar, building on the earlier 709 design of Bob Widlar, the 741 became the archetypal op-amp and remains in production from many manufacturers more than five decades later.

Characteristics

The 741 features internal frequency compensation, which makes it stable in closed-loop circuits without external components, together with short-circuit protection of the output. It is normally supplied from dual rails of up to ±22 V (typically ±15 V) and packaged in an 8-pin DIP or similar package, with pins for inverting and non-inverting inputs, output, supply rails, and an offset-null adjustment.

Performance

Typical figures include an open-loop gain of about 200,000, input impedance around 2 MΩ, a slew rate of roughly 0.5 V/µs, and a gain-bandwidth product near 1 MHz. Modest by modern standards, these specifications are more than adequate for audio, instrumentation and educational use.

Legacy

The 741's simplicity, low cost and ubiquity made it the standard teaching op-amp and a default choice in countless designs. Its family includes improved successors such as the TL071/081 series and dual and quad variants, but the original part endures as an industry benchmark.
