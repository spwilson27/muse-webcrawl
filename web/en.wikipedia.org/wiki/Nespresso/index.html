Nespresso is an operating unit of the Nestlé Group, based in Lausanne, Switzerland. Nespresso machines brew espresso and coffee from coffee capsules (or pods in bar machines), a type of pre-apportioned single-use container, or reusable capsules for home use, of ground coffee beans. Machines are sold by Nestlé and by licensee manufacturers such as Breville, De'Longhi, and Krups.

History

The system was invented by Nestlé engineer Eric Favre, inspired by the queues outside a popular Rome espresso bar; the first patents were filed in 1976 and Nespresso was launched commercially in 1986, initially for offices. The consumer market took off in the 1990s and 2000s, aided by the Nespresso Club direct-sales model, boutiques, and an advertising campaign featuring George Clooney from 2006. In 2014 Nespresso launched the VertuoLine system in North America, using larger, barcoded capsules read by the machine.

Business and criticism

Nespresso's key patents began expiring around 2012, after litigation against makers of compatible capsules. The brand markets itself as premium and emphasizes a capsule recycling program, though the environmental impact of single-use aluminium capsules has drawn criticism. In 2022 Nespresso obtained B Corp certification.
