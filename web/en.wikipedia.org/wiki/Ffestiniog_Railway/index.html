Ffestiniog Railway
Welsh: Rheilffordd Ffestiniog
Route: Porthmadog to Blaenau Ffestiniog
Length: 13.5 miles (21.7 km)
Gauge: 1 ft 11½ in (597 mm)
Opened: 1836

The Ffestiniog Railway (Welsh: Rheilffordd Ffestiniog) is a 1 ft 11½ in (597 mm) narrow-gauge heritage railway in Gwynedd, Wales, running 13.5 miles (21.7 km) from the harbour at Porthmadog to the slate-mining town of Blaenau Ffestiniog through the Vale of Ffestiniog in Snowdonia. Incorporated by Act of Parliament in 1832 and opened in 1836, it is the oldest surviving independent railway company in the world.

History

The line was built to carry slate from the quarries around Blaenau Ffestiniog down to the sea at Porthmadog. Originally worked by gravity — loaded slate wagons rolled downhill under control of brakesmen, with horses hauling the empty wagons back up — it introduced steam locomotives in 1863, becoming one of the first narrow-gauge railways to do so, and carried passengers from 1865. It pioneered the Fairlie articulated locomotive, whose double-ended design suited the line's tight curves and steep gradients.

Decline and revival

The slate industry's collapse led the railway to close in 1946. A preservation society took over in the 1950s and reopened the line in stages from 1955 — one of the earliest volunteer-led railway restorations in Britain. Reopening to Blaenau Ffestiniog in 1982 required a deviation around the Llyn Ystradau reservoir, which had flooded the original route, including a spiral (the "Deviation") built largely by volunteers. Together with the Welsh Highland Railway, with which it shares Porthmadog Harbour station, it forms a major tourist attraction, and its Boston Lodge works remains one of the oldest railway workshops in continuous use.
