Diacetyl

Diacetyl (systematically butane-2,3-dione) is an organic compound with the formula (CH3CO)2. It is a yellow-green liquid with an intensely buttery flavour and is a natural by-product of fermentation.

Occurrence and flavour

Diacetyl arises in alcoholic fermentation and is responsible for buttery or butterscotch notes in beer and wine; brewers allow a "diacetyl rest" so yeast can reabsorb it. It is used as a flavouring in margarine, snacks and microwave popcorn.

Health concerns

Inhalation of diacetyl vapour in flavouring factories has been linked to bronchiolitis obliterans, a serious lung disease nicknamed "popcorn worker's lung". This led to reforms in occupational exposure and reformulation of many products.

Chemistry

As the smallest vicinal diketone, diacetyl is used in analytical chemistry and as a reagent; it gives the Voges–Proskauer test used in microbiology.
