HMS Ajax was a Royal Navy warship bearing pennant number 22. The name was most famously carried by the Leander-class light cruiser launched in 1934, which fought at the Battle of the River Plate in December 1939 against the German pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee.

The River Plate cruiser

The 1934 Ajax, a Leander-class light cruiser of 7,270 tons armed with eight 6-inch guns, served with Commodore Henry Harwood's South Atlantic Division. On 13 December 1939, with HMS Exeter and HMNZS Achilles, she engaged the Graf Spee off the River Plate estuary; damage inflicted in the battle drove the German ship into Montevideo, where she was scuttled. Ajax later served in the Mediterranean, including the Battle of Cape Matapan and the evacuation of Crete, and supported the Normandy landings in 1944. She was scrapped in 1949.

Later ships

The name was subsequently borne by a Leopard-class frigate launched in 1962, and was assigned to the ninth Astute-class submarine announced in the 2020s. The town of Ajax, Ontario, Canada, was named in honour of the River Plate cruiser.
