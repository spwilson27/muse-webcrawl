Antimicrobial stewardship

Antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) is the systematic effort to educate and persuade prescribers of antimicrobials to follow evidence-based prescribing, in order to reduce antimicrobial resistance and improve patient outcomes. Antimicrobial stewardship programmes in hospitals and the community aim to ensure that patients receive the right antimicrobial, at the right dose, by the right route, for the right duration — and only when one is needed.

Rationale

Antimicrobial use, both appropriate and inappropriate, selects for resistant organisms. Rising resistance, combined with a sparse pipeline of new antibiotics, has made conserving existing drugs a global priority. Inappropriate prescribing — such as antibiotics for viral infections, unnecessarily broad-spectrum agents, or excessive durations — also exposes patients to adverse effects including Clostridioides difficile infection.

Strategies

Core strategies include prospective audit and feedback, preauthorisation of restricted agents, guidelines and clinical pathways, intravenous-to-oral switch, dose optimisation, de-escalation based on microbiology results, and education. Stewardship teams are typically led by infectious-disease physicians and pharmacists working with microbiology and infection prevention. National and international action plans, such as the WHO Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance (2015), promote stewardship in human health, agriculture and veterinary practice.
