Handloading

Handloading, or reloading, is the practice of making firearm cartridges by assembling the individual components (case, primer, propellant, and projectile), rather than purchasing factory-loaded ammunition. "Reloading" strictly refers to reusing spent cartridge cases, while "handloading" covers assembling ammunition from new components, though the terms are often used interchangeably.

Process

The basic steps include cleaning and inspecting cases, resizing and depriming them, seating a new primer, measuring and dispensing a powder charge, seating and crimping a bullet, and checking overall cartridge length. Equipment ranges from simple hand tools to single-stage, turret, and progressive presses.

Reasons

Handloaders cite economy (especially from reusing cases), improved accuracy through tailoring loads to a particular firearm, access to obsolete or wildcat calibres, and the hobby itself. Safe practice depends on following published load data from powder and bullet manufacturers, since overpressure loads are dangerous.
