Wenceslaus I (Czech: Václav; c. 907 – 28 September 935), also known as Saint Wenceslaus, was Duke of Bohemia from about 921 until his assassination in 935. Raised in the Christian faith by his grandmother, Saint Ludmila, he became known for his piety, generosity to the poor, and promotion of Christianity in Bohemia.

Reign

Wenceslaus succeeded his father Vratislaus as a minor, with his mother Drahomíra acting as regent until he assumed power around 924. He founded a rotunda dedicated to St Vitus at Prague Castle — the precursor of today's St Vitus Cathedral — and sought peace with East Francia, submitting to Henry the Fowler after a campaign in 929.

Assassination and veneration

On 28 September 935, Wenceslaus was murdered at Stará Boleslav by followers of his younger brother Boleslaus, who succeeded him and who may have been implicated in the plot. Almost immediately venerated as a martyr and saint, Wenceslaus became the patron saint of Bohemia and of the Czech state; his feast day, 28 September, is a Czech national holiday. He is the subject of the Christmas carol "Good King Wenceslas", based on legends of his winter charity, and his crown and relics are among the Bohemian crown jewels.
