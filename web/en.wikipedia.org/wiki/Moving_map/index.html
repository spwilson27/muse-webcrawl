Moving map

A moving map is a navigation display that shows the current position of a vehicle — and often its route, track, and surroundings — on a map that scrolls or redraws continuously as the vehicle moves, so that the vehicle symbol stays near the centre of the display.

History

Early moving maps were electromechanical: RAF bombers of the Second World War used chart displays driven by the navigation system, and later airliners such as the de Havilland Comet era fleets experimented with film-strip map projectors. The concept became widespread with electronic flight displays ("glass cockpits") in the 1980s, and consumer satellite navigation in the 1990s and 2000s put moving maps into cars, boats, and eventually smartphones.

Applications

In aviation, moving maps appear on pilots' multifunction displays and electronic flight bags, and on passenger in-flight entertainment systems, where they show the aircraft's position, altitude, speed, and time to destination. In cars and phones, moving map displays underpin turn-by-turn navigation, integrating real-time traffic data. Maritime electronic chart systems (ECDIS) provide an equivalent moving display for ships.
