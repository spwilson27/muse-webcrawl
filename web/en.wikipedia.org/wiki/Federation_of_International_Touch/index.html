Federation of International Touch

The Federation of International Touch (FIT) is the international governing body for the sport of touch (also called touch football or touch rugby), a minimal-contact form of rugby football in which players tackle opponents by touching them rather than by force. FIT organises the Touch World Cup and sets the playing rules used by its member national associations.

History

Touch developed in Australia in the 1960s as a training and social game derived from rugby league, with organised competitions in Sydney and Brisbane by the early 1970s. The Federation of International Touch was founded in 1985, during the sport's international spread, and the first Touch World Cup was held on the Gold Coast, Australia, in 1988. Australia and New Zealand have remained the dominant nations, though the sport is now played in dozens of countries across Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas, with World Cups staged roughly every four years (recent editions including Malaysia 2019 and England 2024).

The sport

Touch is played by teams of six on a field about 70 by 50 metres. Attackers advance the ball by running and passing (backwards, as in rugby); defenders stop them with a one-handed touch, after which play restarts with a "rollball". After six touches possession changes. The game emphasises speed, agility and evasive skill, and is played in men's, women's and mixed divisions and in age-group categories.
