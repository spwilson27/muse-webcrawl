The Woman in Black (play)

Written byStephen Mallatratt (adaptation)
Based onNovel by Susan Hill (1983)
Premiere1987, Scarborough

The Woman in Black is a stage play adapted by Stephen Mallatratt from the 1983 ghost novel by Susan Hill. It is famous for achieving its terrifying effects with only two speaking actors and almost no scenery, relying on lighting, sound and the audience's imagination.

Plot and staging

Arthur Kipps, haunted for decades by his encounter as a young solicitor with the spectral Woman in Black at remote Eel Marsh House, hires an actor to help him tell his story to his family. The play becomes a play-within-a-play: the Actor plays young Kipps while Kipps takes all the other parts, and as the rehearsal darkens into reality the Woman herself — played by a third, uncredited performer glimpsed rather than announced — appears. The novel's sting, in which the Woman's curse claims Kipps's wife and child, is preserved.

Production history

Premiered at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, in 1987, the play transferred to London's Fortune Theatre in 1989 and ran there until 2023 — one of the longest runs in West End history, second among plays only to The Mousetrap. It toured constantly and became a staple of school theatre syllabuses in Britain. The source novel was also filmed in 2012 with Daniel Radcliffe, but the stage version is a distinct adaptation with its own framing device.
