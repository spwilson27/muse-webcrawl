Triforium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Triforium
LocationAbove the nave arcade, below the clerestory
Typical stylesRomanesque, Gothic
FormShallow arched gallery or blind arcade

A triforium is an interior gallery or shallow passageway set into the thickness of the wall above the arches of the nave, choir, or transept of a church and below the clerestory windows. It typically opens onto the main space through a series of small arches, often grouped in pairs or threes within each bay. In the classic three-storey elevation of Gothic church design — arcade, triforium, clerestory — the triforium forms the middle band.

History and function

The triforium originated in Romanesque architecture, where a passageway sometimes ran within the wall above the aisle vaults. Unlike a full tribune gallery, which is deep enough to stand in and overlooks the nave through wide openings, the triforium is usually a shallow, dark passage or even a purely decorative blind arcade with no passage behind it. Its functions were partly practical — lightening the wall mass, providing access for maintenance, and articulating the elevation — and partly aesthetic, creating a horizontal band of shadow and rhythm that mediates between the large arches below and the windows above.

In early Gothic cathedrals such as Laon and Notre-Dame de Paris, the triforium is a genuine passageway with elegant paired openings. As Gothic architects sought ever larger clerestory windows in the Rayonnant style, the triforium shrank, became glazed in some French churches (as at Amiens and in the Sainte-Chapelle-influenced choirs), or was merged visually with the clerestory. In English Gothic, triforia are often richly decorated blind arcades, as in Salisbury and Lincoln cathedrals.

Terminology

The origin of the word is uncertain; it has been linked to Latin tres ("three") and foris ("door, opening"), supposedly describing the triple openings typical of each bay, but this etymology is disputed. A blind triforium has no opening into a passage, consisting only of an applied arcade against a solid wall.
