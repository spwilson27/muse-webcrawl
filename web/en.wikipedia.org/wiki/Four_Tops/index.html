Four Tops

Members (classic line-up)Levi Stubbs, Abdul "Duke" Fakir, Renaldo "Obie" Benson, Lawrence Payton

The Four Tops are an American vocal quartet from Detroit who helped define the Motown sound of the 1960s. The group—lead singer Levi Stubbs with Abdul "Duke" Fakir, Renaldo "Obie" Benson, and Lawrence Payton—were remarkable for keeping the same line-up for over four decades, from 1953 until Payton's death in 1997.

Career

Originally called the Four Aims, the group signed to Motown Records in 1963 and was paired with the songwriting team Holland–Dozier–Holland. Their hits included "Baby I Need Your Loving" (1964), "I Can't Help Myself (Sugar Pie Honey Bunch)" (1965), which topped the Billboard Hot 100, and "Reach Out I'll Be There" (1966), widely regarded as one of the greatest Motown recordings. Other major songs include "Standing in the Shadows of Love", "Bernadette", "It's the Same Old Song", and "Ain't No Woman (Like the One I've Got)" (1973) after their move to ABC-Dunhill.

Legacy

The Four Tops were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2009. Rolling Stone ranked them among the greatest artists of all time, and "Reach Out I'll Be There" was selected for preservation in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
