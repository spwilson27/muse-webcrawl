FOXP2

Forkhead box protein P2
Gene: FOXP2
Location: chromosome 7q31.1
Type: transcription factor
Associated condition: speech and language disorder (SPCH1)

Forkhead box protein P2 (FOXP2) is a protein encoded by the FOXP2 gene on human chromosome 7. It is a transcription factor — a protein that regulates the expression of other genes — and is expressed in the brain, lungs, heart and gut during development. FOXP2 became famous as the first gene found to be involved in speech and language, and it is often popularly (if misleadingly) called "the language gene".

Discovery

The gene was identified in 2001 through study of the KE family, a large British family in which about half the members have a severe inherited speech and language disorder affecting articulation and grammar. Affected members carry a mutation in FOXP2. The disorder, designated SPCH1, involves developmental verbal dyspraxia — difficulty sequencing the mouth movements needed for speech — alongside wider language impairments.

Evolution

FOXP2 is highly conserved among vertebrates, differing between humans and chimpanzees by only two amino acid changes, which occurred after the human lineage split from other apes. Songbirds, bats and mice use their versions of the gene in vocal learning and motor control, and experiments in mice and zebra finches show that disrupting FOXP2 impairs learned vocalisations and motor learning. The human version of the protein, when introduced into mice, alters their vocalisations and increases neural plasticity.

Function

FOXP2 regulates hundreds of target genes involved in neural development, including genes affecting the growth of neurites and synapse formation. It is especially active in circuits involving the basal ganglia and cortex, which underpin learning of motor sequences.
