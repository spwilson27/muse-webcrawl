Billet

Billet
MeaningLodging for soldiers; also a semi-finished metal bar
OriginFrom Anglo-Norman billette, a note or order

A billet is a living-quarters assignment for personnel, particularly military personnel, in which a soldier is lodged in a private home or other non-military building. The term derives from the written order, or billet, directing the householder to provide lodging.

Military usage

Billeting was a standard means of housing armies for centuries, since standing barracks were rarely sufficient. Laws such as the English Petition of Right of 1628 and the Third Amendment to the United States Constitution restricted the compulsory quartering of soldiers in private homes. Modern usage extends the word to any assigned post, duty position, or accommodation for military members.

Metallurgy

In metalworking, a billet is a semi-finished length of metal — usually with a square or round cross-section smaller than that of a bloom — produced by casting or extrusion and intended for further processing into bars, rods, or machined parts. "Billet" components in engineering are often parts machined from a solid block of such material rather than cast or forged to shape.

Other uses

In heraldry, a billet is a rectangular charge. In wood processing, a billet is a short chunk of firewood or a piece of timber prepared for turning.
