Olave Baden-Powell

Olave St Clair Baden-Powell, Baroness Baden-Powell (née Soames; 22 February 1889 – 25 June 1977) was a leading figure in the Girl Guide and Girl Scout movements. She was the wife of Robert Baden-Powell, founder of Scouting, and served as Chief Guide of the worldwide Guiding movement for decades, helping it grow to millions of members across the globe. She and her husband shared the same birthday, 22 February, which is now celebrated by Guides and Scouts as World Thinking Day.

Early life and marriage

Olave Soames was born in Chesterfield, Derbyshire, the daughter of artist and brewery owner Harold Soames. In January 1912 she met Robert Baden-Powell aboard the ocean liner Arcadian on a voyage to New York; despite an age difference of 32 years, they became engaged and married on 30 October 1912. They had three children: Peter, Heather, and Betty.

Guiding work

During the First World War Olave became directly involved in Guiding, which had been founded in 1910 for girls. In 1918 she was appointed Chief Guide. She travelled tirelessly — enduring hardship, wartime conditions, and later ill health — promoting Guiding in dozens of countries, and she played a central role in establishing the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS) in 1928 and the world centre Our Chalet in Switzerland. In 1932 she was created a Dame Grand Cross of the Order of the British Empire (GBE) for her services to Guiding.

Later life

After her husband's death in Kenya in 1941, Olave returned to England, living at Hampton Court Palace in a grace-and-favour apartment. She continued travelling and writing on behalf of Guiding well into old age, recording her memoirs in Window on My Heart (1973). She died in 1977 at Birtley House, Bramley, Surrey, and her ashes were taken to Kenya to be interred beside her husband at Nyeri.
