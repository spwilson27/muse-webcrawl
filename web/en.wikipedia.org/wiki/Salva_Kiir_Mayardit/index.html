Salva Kiir Mayardit (born 13 September 1951), commonly known as Salva Kiir, is a South Sudanese politician who has served as President of South Sudan since its independence on 9 July 2011. He is a founding member of the Sudan People's Liberation Movement (SPLM) and was a senior commander of its armed wing, the SPLA, during the Second Sudanese Civil War. He is recognisable by his trademark black cowboy hat.

Early life and liberation struggle

Kiir was born into a Dinka family in Akon, in what is now Warrap State. He joined the Anyanya rebel movement in the late 1960s during the First Sudanese Civil War, later trained as an officer in the Sudanese army after the 1972 Addis Ababa Agreement, and in 1983 joined John Garang in founding the SPLM/SPLA. Rising to become Garang's deputy and head of military intelligence, he was Garang's designated successor; after Garang's death in a helicopter crash in July 2005, Kiir became First Vice President of Sudan and President of the Government of Southern Sudan under the Comprehensive Peace Agreement.

Presidency

Kiir won the 2010 southern presidential election and led South Sudan to independence following the 2011 referendum. His presidency was soon overshadowed by conflict: in December 2013, a political dispute with his dismissed vice president Riek Machar erupted into civil war, whose fighting killed an estimated several hundred thousand people and displaced millions. A revitalised peace agreement signed in 2018 brought Machar back as first vice president in a unity government from 2020, though implementation has repeatedly stalled and elections have been postponed several times. Kiir's government has also faced international criticism over corruption and restrictions on dissent, alongside severe economic and humanitarian crises driven by floods, oil dependence and regional war.
