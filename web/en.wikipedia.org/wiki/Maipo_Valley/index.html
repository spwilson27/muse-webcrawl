Maipo Valley

The Maipo Valley is a wine region of Chile centred on the valley of the Maipo River, south of the capital Santiago, in the Central Valley. It is the country's oldest and most famous wine region, sometimes called the "Bordeaux of South America", and is particularly renowned for its red wines, above all Cabernet Sauvignon.

Geography and climate

The valley runs from the foothills of the Andes east of Santiago down toward the Coastal Range and the Pacific. Vineyards at higher elevations in the east (Alto Maipo) benefit from large day–night temperature differences and alluvial, rocky soils, producing structured, age-worthy reds, while areas closer to the coast are cooler and suited to other varieties. The climate is Mediterranean, with warm dry summers and most rain falling in winter; irrigation commonly uses water from the Maipo River.

Wine production

Viticulture dates to the Spanish colonial era, and many of Chile's historic wineries were founded near Santiago in the nineteenth century, including Concha y Toro, Santa Rita and Cousiño Macul. Cabernet Sauvignon dominates plantings, alongside Merlot, Carmenère, Syrah and Chardonnay. The region is divided into sub-zones such as Alto Maipo, and areas around Puente Alto and Pirque are noted for some of Chile's most acclaimed wines, including Don Melchor and Almaviva.
