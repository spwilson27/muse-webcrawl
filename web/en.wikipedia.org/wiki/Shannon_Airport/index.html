Shannon Airport

Shannon Airport (Irish: Aerfort na Sionainne) (IATA: SNN, ICAO: EINN) is an international airport located in County Clare in the Republic of Ireland. It is adjacent to the Shannon Estuary and lies midway between Ennis and Limerick. The airport is a major gateway for transatlantic travel and has a long history as a refuelling stop on routes between Europe and North America.

History

Shannon's origins lie in the 1930s, when Foynes, on the estuary, served as the European terminus for transatlantic flying boats. A land airport was developed at Rineanna during the Second World War, and in 1945 Shannon received the first scheduled transatlantic landplane service. Because early post-war aircraft lacked the range to cross the Atlantic non-stop from more easterly European cities, Shannon became a vital technical stop.

In 1947 the world's first duty-free shop was established at Shannon, an innovation devised by Brendan O'Regan that was later copied by airports worldwide. The surrounding Shannon Free Zone, created in 1959, was the world's first modern free trade zone and attracted multinational investment to the Irish mid-west.

Shannon was also notable as the only Irish airport regularly used by Soviet carrier Aeroflot during the Cold War, serving as a western refuelling point for flights to Cuba and South America. Since 2017 the airport has hosted United States border preclearance facilities, allowing passengers to complete US immigration and customs inspection before departure.

Facilities

The airport has a single runway, 06/24, at 3,199 metres one of the longest in Ireland, capable of handling the largest commercial aircraft. It was once designated an emergency landing site for the NASA Space Shuttle. The single passenger terminal handles scheduled services to the United Kingdom, continental Europe and North America, and the airport serves as an important diversion field for transatlantic flights.
