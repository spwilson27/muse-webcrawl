Cheating in video games

Cheating in video games involves a video game player using various methods to gain advantages beyond normal gameplay, usually to make the game easier. Cheats may be activated from within the game itself (a cheat code implemented by the original game developers), or created by third-party software (a game trainer or debugger) or hardware (a cheat cartridge).

Cheat codes and third-party tools can alter gameplay

History

Cheating in video games has existed for almost the entire history of video games. Early cheat codes were implemented by developers for testing purposes, allowing testers to skip levels or gain invincibility. The most famous early example is the Konami Code, first used in the 1986 release of Gradius and popularised by Contra. Hardware devices such as the Game Genie and GameShark later allowed players to modify game data directly.

Methods

Cheating methods include cheat codes, built-in developer consoles, saved-game editing, trainers that alter memory values while a game runs, and exploitation of glitches or bugs. In online multiplayer games, cheats such as aimbots, wallhacks and speed hacks are implemented through third-party software and are typically prohibited by the game's terms of service.

Anti-cheat measures

Publishers deploy anti-cheat software such as Valve Anti-Cheat (VAC), Easy Anti-Cheat and BattlEye to detect and ban cheaters. Methods include signature scanning, behavioural analysis, server-side validation and hardware bans. The use of cheats in esports competitions has led to lifetime bans and forfeited prize money for professional players caught cheating.
