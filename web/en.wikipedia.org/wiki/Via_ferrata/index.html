Via ferrata

A via ferrata (Italian for "iron path"; plural vie ferrate) is a protected climbing route found in the Alps and other mountain ranges, equipped with fixed steel cables, iron rungs, pegs, ladders and bridges that allow climbers to move along exposed rock faces with limited technical climbing skill.

Equipment and technique

Climbers wear a harness with a purpose-built via ferrata lanyard set — two carabiners attached to an energy-absorbing tear-webbing system — clipping onto the cable as they progress so that a fall is arrested. Helmets and gloves are standard. Routes are graded for difficulty (commonly A–E in the German system), from paths with occasional cable protection to steep, strenuous climbs over overhangs.

History

Simple protected paths existed in the nineteenth-century Alps, but the form developed during the First World War, when Italian and Austro-Hungarian troops installed fixed ropes and ladders to move men and supplies through the Dolomites. Many wartime routes were later restored as recreational vie ferrate, and the activity has spread to France, Spain, Switzerland, North America and beyond.
