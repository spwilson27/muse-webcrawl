Mount Lemmon Survey

The Mount Lemmon Survey (MLS) is an astronomical survey conducted from the Mount Lemmon Observatory in the Santa Catalina Mountains near Tucson, Arizona, as part of the Catalina Sky Survey. Using a 1.52-metre (60-inch) Cassegrain telescope, it searches for near-Earth objects (NEOs), including potentially hazardous asteroids and comets.

Operations

The survey began in 2004 with upgraded detectors and has consistently been among the most productive NEO discovery programmes in the world, finding hundreds of near-Earth asteroids per year along with main-belt asteroids and comets. Its high-altitude site and sensitive wide-field camera allow it to detect faint, fast-moving objects.

Notable discoveries

Mount Lemmon Survey discoveries include numerous comets bearing the names of its observers and asteroids of scientific note, such as objects later visited by spacecraft or identified as unusually close Earth approachers. Observations from the survey feed into the Minor Planet Center's global effort to catalogue solar system small bodies and assess impact risk.
