Marcia Llyneth Griffiths (born 23 November 1949) is a Jamaican singer widely regarded as the "Queen of Reggae". One of the most successful female reggae artists of all time, she has had a career spanning six decades, from the ska and rocksteady era of the 1960s through to the present day.

Career

Griffiths began her career in 1964, performing with Byron Lee and the Dragonaires before joining Clement "Coxsone" Dodd's Studio One label, where she recorded a series of duets with Bob Andy as Bob and Marcia. Their version of Nina Simone's "Young, Gifted and Black" was a major international hit in 1970, reaching the top five in the United Kingdom. She also recorded duets with Tony Gregory and Jeff Dixon, and solo material at Studio One.

From 1974 to 1981 Griffiths was a member of the I Threes, the backing vocal trio (with Rita Marley and Judy Mowatt) that supported Bob Marley and the Wailers on record and on tour around the world.

Solo success

As a solo artist, Griffiths scored hits including "Feel Like Jumping", "Truly", "Stepping Out of Babylon" and "Dreamland". Her 1989 recording of "Electric Boogie" (written by Bunny Wailer), originally recorded in 1982, became a worldwide hit and popularized the "Electric Slide" line dance, reaching the top 40 of the Billboard Hot 100 in the United States. She continued to record and tour extensively, releasing albums such as Indomitable (1995), Land of Love (1997), Marcia Griffiths and Friends (2012), and Timeless (2019), an album of Studio One covers.

Honours

In 2014 Griffiths received the Order of Distinction (Commander class) from the Jamaican government for her contribution to music. She remains one of the most enduring and revered figures in reggae.
