The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK; "Dravidian Progressive Federation") is an Indian political party based in the state of Tamil Nadu. Founded in 1949 by C. N. Annadurai after a split from Periyar E. V. Ramasamy's Dravidar Kazhagam, it has been one of the two dominant parties of Tamil Nadu politics for over half a century, alternating in power with its offshoot and rival, the AIADMK.

History

The DMK grew out of the Self-Respect Movement and the Justice Party tradition, championing Tamil identity, opposition to caste hierarchy and Brahmin dominance, rationalism, and resistance to the imposition of Hindi. It first won power in Madras State in 1967 under Annadurai, ending Congress rule in the state permanently. After Annadurai's death in 1969, M. Karunanidhi led the party for nearly five decades, serving five terms as Chief Minister. In 1972 M. G. Ramachandran split away to form the AIADMK, beginning the two-party Dravidian duopoly that has governed Tamil Nadu ever since.

Policies and governance

DMK governments have been associated with extensive welfare schemes — including subsidised rice, free electricity for farmers, and widespread reservation (affirmative action) in education and employment — as well as with renaming Madras State as Tamil Nadu, legalising self-respect marriages, and asserting state autonomy within the Indian federation. The party has been part of national coalition governments in New Delhi, including the UPA, and since 2019 has led the INDIA bloc's Tamil Nadu alliance. After Karunanidhi's death in 2018, his son M. K. Stalin became party president and led the DMK back to power in the 2021 assembly election, becoming Chief Minister.

Organisation and media

The party is noted for its deep cadre organisation, its use of Tamil cinema and literature as political vehicles — Annadurai and Karunanidhi were both major screenwriters — and its party organs and affiliated media, including the newspaper Murasoli and Sun TV network links through the Karunanidhi family.
