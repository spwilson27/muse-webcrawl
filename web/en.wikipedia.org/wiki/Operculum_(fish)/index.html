Operculum (fish)

Operculum
SystemRespiratory / skeletal
Found inBony fish (Osteichthyes)
FunctionCovers and protects the gills; pumps water over them

The operculum is a series of bones found in bony fishes and chimaeras that serves as a facial support structure and a protective covering for the gills. It is a hard, plate-like flap on each side of the head. In most species it also plays an active role in respiration: the opercular pump helps move water over the gills.

Anatomy

The operculum is composed of four fused bones: the opercle, preopercle, interopercle and subopercle. The preopercle sits beneath and in front of the opercle; in some fishes its rear edge bears spines or serrations and may have defensive functions. The hyomandibular bone braces the opercular series against the skull and connects it to the jaw apparatus.

Function

During breathing, the operculum opens and closes in coordination with the floor of the mouth (buccal pumping), drawing oxygenated water in through the mouth and expelling it past the gill filaments beneath the operculum. This two-pump system lets many bony fishes ventilate their gills while stationary. The operculum also protects the delicate gills from injury and, in some species, contributes to feeding and sound production.

Development and use

The operculum first appears early in larval development; in zebrafish its bone formation begins within days of hatching, making it a model system for studying bone development. Growth rings in the opercular bones of some species have been used to estimate fish age, though scales and otoliths are more commonly used.
