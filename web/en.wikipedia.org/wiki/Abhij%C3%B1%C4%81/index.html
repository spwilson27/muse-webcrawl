Abhijñā (Sanskrit: अभिज्ञा; Pali: abhiññā) is a Buddhist term generally translated as "direct knowledge", "higher knowledge", or "supernormal knowledge". In Buddhism, the term refers to special powers and knowledge attained through virtuous living and meditation.

The six abhijñās

Canonical texts list six kinds of higher knowledge: (1) various supernatural powers (iddhi), such as walking on water or passing through walls; (2) the divine ear, or clairaudience; (3) knowledge of the minds of others; (4) recollection of one's former lives; (5) the divine eye, seeing beings dying and being reborn according to their karma; and (6) knowledge of the destruction of the defilements, which marks the attainment of liberation.

Interpretation

The first five abhijñās are considered attainable by non-Buddhist contemplatives as well, while the sixth is unique to the Buddhist path of awakening. Buddhist commentators note that the Buddha both praised the knowledge leading to liberation and discouraged the display of miraculous powers for mere show.
