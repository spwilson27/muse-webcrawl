Sergeant

The three-chevron insignia of a sergeant in many Commonwealth armies.

Sergeant (abbreviated Sgt) is a rank of non-commissioned officer (NCO) in the armed forces of many countries, as well as a rank in numerous police and other uniformed services. It is typically senior to a corporal and junior to senior NCO grades such as staff sergeant or sergeant first class. The word derives, through Old French sergent, from the Latin serviens, "one who serves."

History

In medieval usage a sergeant was a servant or attendant, then a fighting man below the rank of knight, and later a class of legal and household officers (as in serjeants-at-law and the Serjeant at Arms). The military rank crystallized in the early modern period: by the eighteenth century, sergeants in European armies were the experienced professional core of infantry companies — responsible for drill, discipline, and administration, closing the ranks in battle and carrying espontoon or halberd, and later standardizing musket drill. The sergeants' mess and the saying that NCOs are "the backbone of the army" reflect this enduring role.

Use by country

United Kingdom

In the British Army and Royal Marines, a sergeant (three chevrons) is a senior NCO, typically a platoon or troop sergeant, senior to a corporal and junior to a staff sergeant (or colour sergeant in the infantry and Royal Marines). The spelling "serjeant" survives in some regiments, such as The Rifles. In British police forces, sergeant is the first supervisory rank, above constable and below inspector.

United States

The United States Army has several sergeant grades (pay grades E-5 to E-9): sergeant, staff sergeant, sergeant first class, master sergeant, first sergeant, sergeant major, and command sergeant major. A sergeant (E-5) commonly leads a fire team or squad. The Marine Corps uses a similar ladder; the Air Force's sergeant-equivalent grades include staff sergeant and technical sergeant. In American policing, sergeant is likewise a first-line supervisory rank.

Other countries

Most Commonwealth and many NATO and other forces use sergeant ranks roughly equivalent to NATO grade OR-6, though duties and seniority vary. In some countries the police rank of sergeant has been replaced (as in parts of Australia), while in others it remains the standard designation for shift supervisors and station officers.

Duties

Sergeants translate officers' orders into execution: they train soldiers, maintain discipline and standards, supervise daily work, manage equipment, and look after their subordinates' welfare. In combat arms units a platoon sergeant is the platoon commander's principal assistant and the senior enlisted mentor of the platoon. Drill sergeants and their equivalents are responsible for initial training of recruits.
