Construction in Hong Kong

Construction in Hong Kong refers to the building and civil engineering industry of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. The sector is characterised by high-rise residential and commercial development on steep, densely populated terrain, extensive land reclamation, and large-scale infrastructure such as the MTR railway network, bridges, and tunnels. Construction accounts for a significant share of employment and economic activity in the territory.

RegionHong Kong
Key featuresHigh-rise housing, reclamation, transport infrastructure
RegulatorBuildings Department; Construction Industry Council

Characteristics

Because most of Hong Kong's terrain is steep and hilly, development concentrates on reclaimed land and limited flat ground, producing some of the world's tallest and densest residential buildings. The use of bamboo scaffolding remains a distinctive and widely used technique on building sites. Public housing built by the Hong Kong Housing Authority accommodates a large share of the population.

Major projects

Landmark projects include the Hong Kong International Airport at Chek Lap Kok (part of the Airport Core Programme of the 1990s), the Tsing Ma Bridge, the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, and continuing extensions of the MTR. Government ten-year infrastructure programmes have sustained demand, though the industry faces labour shortages, an ageing workforce, and high construction costs.

Regulation

The Buildings Department oversees building safety and standards, while the Construction Industry Council, established in 2007, coordinates industry training and registration of construction workers. Contractors are registered and graded under government lists for public works.
