Chicago Bears

The Chicago Bears are a professional American football team based in Chicago. The Bears compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) North division. The franchise was founded in Decatur, Illinois, on September 20, 1919, became professional by 1920, and moved to Chicago in 1921. It is one of only two remaining franchises from the NFL's founding in 1920, along with the Arizona Cardinals.

History

The club was founded by the A. E. Staley food starch company as the Decatur Staleys and coached by George Halas, who bought the team and moved it to Chicago in 1921, renaming it the Bears in 1922 in reference to its landlords, the Chicago Cubs baseball team. Halas coached the team in four separate stints over forty years and is remembered as "Papa Bear".

The Bears dominated the early NFL, winning championships in the 1920s, 1930s and 1940s; the 1940 championship game, a 73–0 defeat of Washington using the T formation, remains the largest margin of victory in NFL history. The team won its eighth NFL title in 1963.

The 1985 Bears

The 1985 Bears, coached by Mike Ditka with defensive coordinator Buddy Ryan, are widely regarded as one of the greatest teams in NFL history. Led on defense by Mike Singletary, Richard Dent and Dan Hampton, and on offense by quarterback Jim McMahon and running back Walter Payton, the team went 15–1 in the regular season, shut out both opponents in the NFC playoffs, and defeated the New England Patriots 46–10 in Super Bowl XX. The team's "Super Bowl Shuffle" rap video became a cultural phenomenon.

Rivalries and records

The Bears' chief rivals are the Green Bay Packers, in the NFL's longest-running rivalry, dating to 1921. The franchise has retired more uniform numbers (14) and has more members in the Pro Football Hall of Fame than any other NFL team. The Bears have played at Soldier Field since 1971 (with a temporary move to the University of Illinois during the stadium's 2002–03 renovation).
