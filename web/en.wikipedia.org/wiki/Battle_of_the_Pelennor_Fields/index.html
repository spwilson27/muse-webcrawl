Battle of the Pelennor Fields

The Battle of the Pelennor Fields is a fictional battle in J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings, fought on 15 March 3019 of the Third Age on the Pelennor Fields outside Minas Tirith. It is the largest battle of the War of the Ring, in which the forces of Gondor and Rohan defeat the armies of Sauron besieging the city.

Course of the battle

Mordor's forces, having crossed the Anduin at Osgiliath and broken the Rammas Echor, besieged Minas Tirith; the great ram Grond breached the city gate as the Witch-king of Angmar entered. At dawn the horns of Rohan sounded: King Théoden had led some 6,000 Rohirrim past the enemy's outposts and charged the besiegers from the north, overrunning the siege works and driving the Haradrim cavalry before him.

Théoden was killed when his horse Snowmane fell, crushed by the Witch-king's fell beast. His niece Éowyn, disguised as the rider "Dernhelm" and accompanied by the hobbit Meriadoc Brandybuck, confronted the Witch-king; Merry's blow from behind the knee broke the spell of his protection, and Éowyn slew him, fulfilling the prophecy that he would not fall "by the hand of man". In the afternoon Aragorn arrived by ship up the Anduin with reinforcements from southern Gondor — having seized the Corsairs' fleet at Pelargir — and the allied forces closed on the enemy from two sides. By nightfall Sauron's army was destroyed or routed, though the victory cost Gondor and Rohan dearly, including Théoden, Denethor (who died by his own hand during the siege), and many captains.

Aftermath

The victory relieved Minas Tirith but could not win the war, since Sauron's main strength remained; the Captains of the West therefore resolved to march on the Black Gate as a diversion while the Ring-bearer completed his quest. The battle is a centrepiece of The Return of the King and of Peter Jackson's 2003 film adaptation.
