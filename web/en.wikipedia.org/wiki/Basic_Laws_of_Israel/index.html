Basic Laws of Israel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Basic Laws of Israel are the constitutional laws of the State of Israel. Because the Constituent Assembly of 1949 could not agree on a full constitution (over religion-and-state, rights and the executive's form), the Knesset adopted the Harari Resolution of 13 June 1950, providing that the constitution would be built “chapter by chapter” as separate Basic Laws. Fourteen Basic Laws have been enacted (the count varies slightly with how amendments are tallied) — on the Knesset (1958), the President, the Government, the Judiciary, the State Comptroller and others — culminating for rights purposes in Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty and Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation (1992, revised 1994).

In the United Mizrahi Bank case (1995) the Supreme Court, per President Aharon Barak, held that the Basic Laws are constitutional norms empowering judicial review of ordinary legislation — the “constitutional revolution”. The system's open questions (whether the Knesset sitting as constituent assembly can entrench laws against its own future majorities; who, if anyone, can review a Basic Law itself — the Court in January 2024 struck down an amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary for the first time, in the reasonableness-standard case) became the centre of Israel's 2023–24 constitutional crisis over the judicial overhaul. Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People (2018) added a contested identity chapter to the structure.
