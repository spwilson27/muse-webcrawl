Chamberlin, Powell and Bon

Chamberlin, Powell and Bon (CPB) was a British firm of architects, formed in 1952 by Peter Chamberlin, Geoffry Powell and Christoph Bon. The practice is best known for the Barbican Estate in the City of London, one of the largest and most ambitious examples of post-war Brutalist housing in Britain.

Formation

Powell and Bon had won the competition for the Golden Lane Estate in 1951 while still students and lecturers at Kingston School of Art; Chamberlin, who had been invited to join them, completed the partnership in 1952. Golden Lane, built for the City of London on a bomb-damaged site, established their reputation for high-density housing with generous landscaping and communal facilities.

Barbican Estate

The firm's masterwork, the Barbican, was built between 1965 and 1976 on a 35-acre site devastated by the Blitz. It combines over 2,000 flats in terraces and three 43-storey towers — among the tallest residential buildings in London at the time — with the Barbican Centre arts complex, the Barbican Library, the Museum of London (since relocated), schools, a lake and gardens. Its raised podium walkways ("highwalks") separate pedestrians from traffic. The estate was Grade II listed in 2001.

Other work

CPB designed much of the post-war expansion of the University of Leeds, housing at Vanbrugh Park and elsewhere in London, and buildings for the University of Birmingham and New Hall, Cambridge (now Murray Edwards College).
