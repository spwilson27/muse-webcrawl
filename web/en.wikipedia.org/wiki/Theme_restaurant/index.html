Theme restaurant

A theme restaurant is a restaurant in which the décor, atmosphere, staff costumes, and often the food and menu names are unified around a particular theme—such as a historical era, a place, a film or fictional world, or an activity—so that the dining experience itself becomes a form of entertainment. Theme restaurants differ from ordinary restaurants in that the concept, rather than cuisine alone, is the primary attraction for customers.

History

Precursors include the elaborate supper clubs and tiki bars of the mid-twentieth century, such as Trader Vic's and Don the Beachcomber, which immersed diners in a Polynesian fantasy. The genre expanded in the 1980s and 1990s with large chain concepts including Planet Hollywood, the Hard Rock Cafe, Rainforest Cafe, and Medieval Times, in which memorabilia, animatronics, costumed performers, and themed retail areas were integral to the visit. The Hard Rock Cafe, founded in London in 1971, is often credited with pioneering the modern theme restaurant on a global scale.

Characteristics

Common features include extensive set-like interiors, staff in character or costume, themed menus and merchandise, live performance or interactive elements, and locations concentrated in tourist districts. Because the concept investment is high, theme restaurants are especially sensitive to changing fashions; many 1990s concepts contracted sharply once novelty faded, while experience-led concepts—dinner theatres, character dining, and modern Japanese concepts such as maid cafés and ninja restaurants—have persisted or revived.
