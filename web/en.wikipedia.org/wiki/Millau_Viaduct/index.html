Millau Viaduct

Millau Viaduct
Location: Millau, France
Length: 2,460 m
Height: 336.4 m (structural)
Opened: December 2004
Designer: Michel Virlogeux; architect Norman Foster

The Millau Viaduct (French: Viaduc de Millau) is a multispan cable-stayed road bridge that carries the A75 autoroute across the gorge valley of the river Tarn near Millau in southern France. It is the tallest bridge in the world, with one mast rising 336.4 metres above the structure's base.

Design and construction

Engineered by Michel Virlogeux with architecture by Foster + Partners, the bridge has seven slender piers and eight steel spans totalling 2,460 metres. Construction began in 2001; the deck was launched from both ends and joined in 2004, and the bridge opened to traffic that December.

Purpose

Before the bridge, traffic between Paris and the Mediterranean had to descend into the Tarn valley and pass through Millau, causing notorious summer congestion. The viaduct removed that bottleneck.
