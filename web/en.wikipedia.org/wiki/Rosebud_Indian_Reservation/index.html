Rosebud Indian Reservation

The Rosebud Indian Reservation is a Native American reservation in south-central South Dakota, United States, home to the federally recognized Rosebud Sioux Tribe — the Sicangu Lakota Oyate ("Burnt Thigh Nation"). Established in 1889 from the division of the Great Sioux Reservation, the reservation today encompasses Todd County entirely, along with portions of neighbouring counties and off-reservation trust lands.

History

The Sicangu Lakota followed the leadership of chiefs such as Spotted Tail, who negotiated with the United States after the 1868 Fort Laramie Treaty created the Great Sioux Reservation. The Rosebud Agency was founded in 1878; allotment under the Dawes Act and the 1889 breakup of the Great Sioux Reservation reduced tribal land and opened much of it to non-Indian settlement. The Ghost Dance movement spread through Rosebud in 1890 in the months before the Wounded Knee Massacre on the neighbouring Pine Ridge Reservation.

The reservation today

The tribe is governed under a 1935 Indian Reorganization Act constitution by an elected council and president. Tribal institutions include Sinte Gleska University, founded in 1970 and named for Spotted Tail, one of the first tribal colleges. The reservation faces persistent challenges of poverty and infrastructure, alongside ongoing Lakota language and cultural revitalization efforts.
