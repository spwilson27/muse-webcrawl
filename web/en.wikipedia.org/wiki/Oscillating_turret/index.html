Oscillating turret

An oscillating turret is a form of tank turret in which the gun is fixed rigidly in the upper turret section, and the whole upper assembly pivots up and down to elevate the gun, rather than the gun elevating within the turret on trunnions. The design makes it much easier to fit an autoloader, because the loader mechanism moves with the gun, and it allows a smaller, lighter turret for a given gun calibre.

History and examples

Oscillating turrets were most famously used on French vehicles of the 1950s, including the AMX-13 light tank, the AMX-50 heavy tank prototypes and the Panhard EBR armoured car, and on the Austrian SK-105 Kürassier light tank, which used a turret derived from the AMX-13's. American experiments included the T69 and T54E1 prototypes.

Drawbacks

The vulnerable flexible seal between the upper and lower turret sections could not easily be made proof against nuclear, biological and chemical contamination, complicating overpressure protection, and sealing and armour coverage at the joint were weak points. For these reasons oscillating turrets fell out of favour, and autoloaders were later integrated into conventional turrets.
