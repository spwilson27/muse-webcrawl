Malév Hungarian Airlines

Malév Hungarian Airlines (Hungarian: Malév Magyar Légiközlekedési Vállalat) was the national flag carrier of Hungary. Founded in 1946 as Maszovlet, a Hungarian–Soviet joint venture, it became wholly state-owned in 1954 and was based at Budapest Liszt Ferenc International Airport (formerly Ferihegy). The airline ceased operations on 3 February 2012 after being declared insolvent, ending 66 years of continuous service.

History

The company began as Maszovlet on 29 March 1946, flying Lisunov Li-2 aircraft (the Soviet-built DC-3). When the Soviet Union sold its stake to Hungary in 1954, the airline was renamed Malév. In the jet age it introduced Ilyushin Il-18 turboprops and, in 1968, the Tupolev Tu-134, followed by the long-haul Tu-154 in 1973.

After the fall of communism, Malév westernised its fleet with Boeing 737s, Fokker 70s and Bombardier Dash 8 Q400s, and in 2007 it joined the Oneworld alliance. Privatisation attempts in the 1990s and 2000s — involving Alitalia and later the Russian AirBridge group — failed to produce a stable owner, and the airline returned to state hands.

Collapse

In January 2012 the European Commission ruled that state aid granted to Malév between 2007 and 2010 was illegal and ordered its repayment. Unable to raise the funds, the airline grounded all flights at 06:00 on 3 February 2012. Its routes were largely taken over by low-cost carriers, principally Wizz Air and Ryanair, and Hungary has had no national flag carrier since.

Destinations and fleet

At its peak Malév served around 50 destinations in 34 countries, focused on Europe and the eastern Mediterranean, with earlier long-haul services to New York, Toronto and Bangkok. Its final fleet consisted of Boeing 737-600, -700 and -800 aircraft, Fokker 70s and Dash 8 Q400 turboprops, painted in a white livery with a stylised blue tail.
