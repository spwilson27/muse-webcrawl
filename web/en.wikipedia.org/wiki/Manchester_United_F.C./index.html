Manchester United Football Club, commonly referred to as Man United, is a professional football club based in Old Trafford, Greater Manchester, England. It competes in the Premier League, the top flight of English football, and is one of the most widely supported clubs in the world.

History

Founded as Newton Heath in 1878 and renamed Manchester United in 1902, the club rose under Matt Busby after the Second World War. Busby's young "Busby Babes" won two league titles before eight players died in the Munich air disaster of 6 February 1958. Busby rebuilt, and in 1968 United became the first English club to win the European Cup. Under Sir Alex Ferguson (1986–2013) United dominated the Premier League era, winning 13 league titles and, in 1999, an unprecedented Treble of league, FA Cup and Champions League — secured by two injury-time goals against Bayern Munich. Since Ferguson's retirement the club has struggled to recapture that dominance despite further cup successes. The Glazer family, owners since 2005, sold a minority stake to Sir Jim Ratcliffe's INEOS group in 2024.
