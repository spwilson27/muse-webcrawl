Patter song

A patter song is a song characterised by a fast tempo, a rapid succession of syllables and usually a tongue-twisting, comic text. Patter songs are a hallmark of comic opera, operetta and musical theatre, and serve as showpieces of diction and breath control for the performer.

History

The style has roots in Italian opera buffa, in the quick-fire arias of Rossini and Donizetti. Its most famous exponents are W. S. Gilbert and Arthur Sullivan: examples include "I am the very model of a modern Major-General" from The Pirates of Penzance, the Lord Chancellor's nightmare song from Iolanthe, and "My name is John Wellington Wells" from The Sorcerer. The patter song often builds in speed, with the final verse delivered fastest.

Later examples

The tradition continued in musical theatre and popular song, in works by Noël Coward ("Mad Dogs and Englishmen"), Stephen Sondheim, and in comic songs such as Danny Kaye's performances and Tom Lehrer's "The Elements", which lists the chemical elements to a Sullivan tune. Rapid-fire list songs in modern musicals and rap-influenced numbers are often compared to patter songs.
