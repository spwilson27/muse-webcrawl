Point-of-view shot

A point-of-view shot (also known as a POV shot, first-person shot or a subjective camera) is a film technique in which the camera shows what a character is looking at, as if the audience were seeing through the character's eyes. The shot is usually edited in sequence: a character is shown looking at something, the POV shot reveals what they see, and a reaction shot then shows their response — a structure analysed in the context of the Kuleshov effect.

Usage

POV shots create identification between the audience and the character and are used across genres: in horror films, to show events from the perspective of the monster or the victim; in thrillers, to generate suspense; and in action films, to immerse the viewer in stunts or chases. Directors may impose physical cues on the image — blinking, blurred vision, or the frame edges of glasses or a mask — to strengthen the illusion.

Extended subjective camera

Some films sustain the technique at length. Lady in the Lake (1947) was shot almost entirely from the protagonist's viewpoint, and Hardcore Henry (2015) used body-mounted cameras to produce a feature-length first-person action film modelled on video-game aesthetics. Video games, especially first-person shooters, are conventionally rendered from a continuous POV.

Relation to other shots

The POV shot is a specific type of eyeline match within continuity editing. A shot in which the camera occupies a character's position but the character also appears is not a true POV shot; similarly, an over-the-shoulder shot approximates but does not equal the character's exact view.
