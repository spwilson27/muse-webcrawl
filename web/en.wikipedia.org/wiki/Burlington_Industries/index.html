Burlington Industries was a diversified American manufacturer of textiles, including fabric for clothing, home furnishings, and industrial uses. Founded in 1923 in Burlington, North Carolina by J. Spencer Love, it grew to become, for a time, the largest textile manufacturer in the world.

History

Love began with a small rayon weaving plant financed largely with borrowed money. The company expanded rapidly through acquisitions and new plants across the American South, diversifying into hosiery, carpets, furniture fabrics, and glass fibre. By the 1960s Burlington was a Fortune 500 company and a dominant name in American textiles.

Decline and bankruptcy

From the 1980s onward the company faced rising import competition, heavy debt from a leveraged defence against a takeover attempt, and shrinking margins. Burlington Industries filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2001. Its businesses were subsequently sold; investor Wilbur Ross acquired much of the company and combined it with other textile assets to form International Textile Group.

Legacy

The Burlington name survives in various licensed brands and successor operations. The company's former headquarters city, Greensboro, North Carolina, long served as a centre of the American textile industry.
