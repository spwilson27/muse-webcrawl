Solicitor General of the United States

The Solicitor General of the United States (USSG) is the federal official who represents the federal government of the United States before the Supreme Court. The office is within the Department of Justice.

Solicitor General
Established1870
AppointerPresident, with Senate confirmation
DepartmentU.S. Department of Justice

Duties

The Solicitor General decides which cases the government will appeal, approves amicus curiae briefs by federal agencies, and personally argues many of the government's Supreme Court cases. The office is sometimes called the 'tenth justice' because of its close, trusted relationship with the Court, which frequently invites the SG's views (CVSG) in cases where the government is not a party.

History

The office was created by Congress in 1870 alongside the Department of Justice. Notable holders include Thurgood Marshall, later a Supreme Court justice; Robert Bork; Elena Kagan, also later a justice; and Neal Katyal as acting SG.

Office structure

The SG is assisted by deputies and assistants, career lawyers who prepare briefs in the government's appellate litigation nationwide.
