Culture of Iran

The culture of Iran (Persian culture) is one of the oldest in the world, with roots stretching back thousands of years. Iran has long been a centre of art, literature, philosophy, and science, and its cultural influence extends across Central Asia, the Caucasus, and South Asia — a region sometimes called the Greater Iran or the Persianate world.

Literature

Persian literature is renowned for its poetry. The Shahnameh ("Book of Kings"), Ferdowsi's epic of some 50,000 couplets, retells the mythical and historical past of Persia and is central to Iranian identity. Other celebrated poets include Rumi, Hafez, Saadi, and Omar Khayyam, whose works have been translated worldwide. Poetry remains a living part of everyday life; Iranians commonly quote classical verses in conversation.

Art and architecture

Persian art is famous for miniature painting, calligraphy, carpet weaving, and tilework. Persian gardens, laid out in the formal chahar bagh pattern, influenced garden design from India to Spain. Islamic-era architecture produced soaring minarets, turquoise domes, and elaborate muqarnas vaulting, seen in cities such as Isfahan, Shiraz, and Yazd.

Festivals and customs

Nowruz, the Persian New Year at the spring equinox, is the most important festival, celebrated with the haft-sin table of seven symbolic items. Other observances include Yalda Night at the winter solstice and, in the Islamic calendar, Ramadan and Muharram commemorations. The elaborate etiquette system of ta'arof governs polite social interaction.

Cuisine

Iranian cuisine features rice dishes such as chelow kebab, herb-rich stews like ghormeh sabzi, saffron, dried fruits, and flatbreads baked in stone ovens. Tea, served strong in glasses, is the social drink of choice.
