Mirabai

Bornc. 1498, Kurki, Rajasthan, India
Diedc. 1546/1547, Dwarka
OccupationPoet, saint
MovementBhakti movement

Mirabai (c. 1498 – c. 1546/1547), often rendered Meera, was a 16th-century Hindu mystic poet and devotee of Krishna. She is a celebrated saint of the Bhakti movement, known for her devotional songs (bhajans) expressing her love for Krishna, whom she regarded as her divine husband.

Life

According to tradition, Mirabai was born into a Rajput royal family in Kurki, in present-day Rajasthan, and was married into the royal family of Mewar. After her husband's death she devoted herself entirely to Krishna, defying social and family conventions of widowhood. Legends tell of attempts on her life by her in-laws, from which she was miraculously saved, and of her final years spent in Vrindavan and Dwarka.

Poetry

Mirabai composed hundreds of devotional songs in Rajasthani, Braj and Gujarati, which remain immensely popular in India. Her poems express longing, ecstasy and surrender to Krishna, and frequently challenge caste and gender norms. Her compositions are sung as bhajans across the subcontinent and have inspired numerous films, plays and musical renditions.
