Zacatecas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zacatecas (Spanish pronunciation: [sakaˈtekas]) is a state in north-central Mexico, bordered by Durango, Coahuila, Nuevo León, San Luis Potosí, Guanajuato, Jalisco, Aguascalientes, and Nayarit. Its capital and largest city is Zacatecas City. The state is historically associated with silver mining — it was one of the richest mining centers of colonial New Spain.

History

The area was inhabited by peoples including the Zacatecos, from whom the name derives. After the Spanish discovery of rich silver deposits in 1546, Zacatecas City was founded in 1548 and became a key stop on the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro, the silver route now recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the historic center of the city was likewise inscribed in 1993 for its Baroque colonial architecture built of pink stone (cantera).

During the Mexican Revolution, the Battle of Zacatecas (June 1914), in which Pancho Villa's División del Norte captured the city from federal forces, was one of the bloodiest and most decisive battles of the conflict. Today the state's economy combines mining, agriculture (notably beans and chiles), and tourism; the Cerro de la Bufa overlooks the capital and is served by a cable car (teleférico).
