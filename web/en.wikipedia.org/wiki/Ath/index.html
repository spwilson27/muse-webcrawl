Ath (French pronunciation: [at]; Dutch: Aat, Picard: Ât) is a city and municipality of Wallonia located in the province of Hainaut, Belgium. On 1 January 2023, Ath had a total population of around 29,630. The total area is 126.95 km² which gives a population density of about 233 inhabitants per km².

The municipality consists of the following districts: Arbre, Ath, Bouvignies, Ghislenghien, Gibecq, Houtaing, Irchonwelz, Isières, Lanquesaint, Ligne, Maffle, Mainvault, Meslin-l'Évêque, Moulbaix, Ormeignies, Ostiches, Rebaix, Villers-Notre-Dame, and Villers-Saint-Amand.

History

Ath grew around a fortress built by Baldwin IV, Count of Hainaut, in about 1160. Its medieval tower, the Tour Burbant, still dominates the old town. The town later passed through the hands of the Spanish Netherlands and France before becoming Belgian in 1830. In July 2004, a gas pipeline explosion at the Ghislenghien industrial zone in the municipality killed 24 people in one of Belgium's worst industrial disasters.

Ducasse d'Ath

Ath is known for the Ducasse d'Ath, a popular festival held annually on the fourth weekend of August. Originating in the 15th century, the procession features giant figures (géants) such as Goliath, his wife, and the horse Bayard, each carried by porters through the streets. The festival is recognised by UNESCO as one of the Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, as part of the processional giants and dragons of Belgium and France.
