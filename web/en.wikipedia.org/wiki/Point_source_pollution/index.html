Point source pollution

A point source of pollution is a single identifiable source of air, water, thermal, noise or light pollution. A point source has negligible extent, distinguishing it from other pollution source geometries (such as nonpoint source or area source). The sources are called point sources because in mathematical modeling, they can be approximated as a mathematical point to simplify analysis.

Examples and regulation

Examples include factory smokestacks, wastewater treatment plant discharge pipes, oil spills from a single tanker, and individual chimneys. In the United States, the Clean Water Act defines a point source broadly and requires discharge permits under the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES), making point sources generally easier to regulate than diffuse nonpoint sources such as agricultural runoff.
