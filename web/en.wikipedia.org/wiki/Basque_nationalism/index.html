Basque nationalism

Basque nationalism (Basque: eusko abertzaletasuna) is a form of nationalism that asserts that Basques, an ethnic group indigenous to the western Pyrenees, are a nation and promotes their political unity and, for some currents, independence. It is centred on the Basque Country spanning parts of northern Spain and southwestern France.

Origins

The movement arose in the late 19th century, catalysed by the abolition of the Basque fueros (traditional charters) after the Carlist Wars and by rapid industrialisation around Bilbao. Sabino Arana founded the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV/EAJ) in 1895, coined the name Euzkadi for the homeland and designed the ikurrina flag.

Franco era and ETA

After the Spanish Civil War, in which the Basque government of José Antonio Aguirre allied with the Republic, Franco's dictatorship repressed Basque language and institutions. The armed group ETA, founded in 1959, pursued independence through violence for five decades, killing more than 800 people before declaring a permanent ceasefire in 2011 and dissolving in 2018.

Autonomy

Since 1979 the Basque Autonomous Community in Spain has enjoyed wide self-government under the Statute of Gernika, with mainstream nationalist parties (PNV and EH Bildu) leading its politics, while cultural nationalism promotes the Basque language, Euskara.
