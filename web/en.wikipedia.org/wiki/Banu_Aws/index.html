Banu Aws

The Banu Aws (Arabic: بنو أوس) was one of the two main Arab tribes of Medina (then Yathrib) at the time of the Prophet Muhammad, the other being the Banu Khazraj. Together the Aws and Khazraj were known after the Hijra as the Ansar ("the Helpers"), the Medinan supporters of Muhammad and the Meccan emigrants (Muhajirun). The Aws were originally of South Arabian (Yemeni/Qahtani) origin and had settled in Yathrib centuries before Islam.

History

The Aws and Khazraj fought a long series of wars for control of Yathrib, culminating in the Battle of Bu'ath (c. 617), which left both tribes exhausted. Representatives of the Aws were among the Medinans who met Muhammad at Aqaba and pledged allegiance (620–622), inviting him to arbitrate their feuds; after the Hijra of 622 the Aws largely accepted Islam and formed part of the new community.

Prominent members included Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, the tribe's chief, whose judgment after the Battle of the Trench determined the fate of the Banu Qurayza; Usayd ibn Hudayr; and Muhammad ibn Maslama. The rivalry between Aws and Khazraj continued in attenuated form in early Islamic politics, including the meeting at the Saqifa after Muhammad's death.
