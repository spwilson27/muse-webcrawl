Lao cuisine

Lao cuisine or Laotian cuisine is the national cuisine of Laos. The staple food of the Lao is sticky rice (Lao: khao niao), which is eaten by hand and accompanies nearly every meal. Galangal, lemongrass, and padaek (fermented fish sauce) are important ingredients.

The most famous Lao dish is larb (also spelled laap or larp), a spicy mixture of marinated meat or fish, sometimes raw, mixed with lime juice, chilli, mint, ground toasted rice, and herbs. Another signature dish is tam maak hoong, a spicy green papaya salad. Lao cuisine differs from neighbouring Thai cuisine in its greater use of bitter flavours, sticky rice as the staple rather than jasmine rice, and generous use of fresh herbs.

Ingredients

Common ingredients include sticky rice, padaek, fish sauce, lime, chillies, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, dill, mint, coriander, and Makrut lime leaves. Meals feature soups, grilled meats and fish, stews such as or lam, and a variety of jaew (chilli dips) eaten with raw or steamed vegetables.

Eating customs

Sticky rice is rolled into small balls with the fingers and used to scoop up other dishes. Traditionally, diners sit on a reed mat around a raised rattan tray called a pha khao, sharing dishes communally. Forks and spoons are now common, but sticky rice is still eaten by hand.
