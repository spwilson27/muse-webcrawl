Ludwig Mies van der Rohe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Barcelona Pavilion: marble, glass and steel planes floating over the reflecting pool, the most influential room never lived in]

Ludwig Mies van der Rohe (born Maria Ludwig Michael Mies; 27 March 1886 – 17 August 1969) was a German-American architect, furniture designer and the last director of the Bauhaus, regarded — with Le Corbusier, Walter Gropius and Frank Lloyd Wright — as a pioneer of modern architecture. His mature work pursues an architecture of 'almost nothing' (beinahe nichts): skin-and-bones construction, universal open space, and the maxims 'less is more' and 'God is in the details'.

His key works are the German Pavilion in Barcelona (1929, rebuilt 1986), the Tugendhat House in Brno (1930), the Farnsworth House in Illinois (1951), the Seagram Building in New York (1958, with Philip Johnson) and the Neue Nationalgalerie in Berlin (1968). Born in Aachen, a stonemason's son without formal architectural training, Mies worked for Peter Behrens alongside Gropius and Le Corbusier, ran the Bauhaus from 1930 until its closure under Nazi pressure in 1933, and emigrated to America in 1938 to direct architecture at what became the Illinois Institute of Technology.

The universal space

Mies's American career codified the steel-and-glass office tower (860–880 Lake Shore Drive; the Seagram's bronze-and-amber perfection, its plaza a gift to the street that zoning law soon imitated worldwide) and the campus of IIT itself. The Farnsworth House — a glass box for a doctor who then sued him — became modernism's most argued-over jewel, its transparency a philosophy and a provocation.

Legacy

Mies's aphorisms are the profession's scripture and its target: postmodernism defined itself against his restraint, minimalism descends from it, and the corporate glass city of the late twentieth century is his unintended monument — he sought timeless order, the market took the curtain wall. His furniture (the Barcelona chair, 1929) remains royalty-bearing design royalty.
