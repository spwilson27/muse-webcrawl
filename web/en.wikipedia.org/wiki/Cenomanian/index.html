Cenomanian

The Cenomanian is, in the International Commission on Stratigraphy's geological timescale, the oldest or earliest age of the Late Cretaceous Epoch or the lowest stage of the Upper Cretaceous Series. An age is a unit of geochronology; it is equivalent to a stage in stratigraphy. The Cenomanian spanned the time between 100.5 and 93.9 million years ago. It is preceded by the Albian and is followed by the Turonian. The Upper Cenomanian is sometimes informally subdivided into the Lower, Middle and Upper subages.

Stratigraphic definitions

The Cenomanian was introduced in scientific literature by French palaeontologist Alcide d'Orbigny in 1847. Its name comes from the New Latin name of the French city of Le Mans (Cenomanus), in the department of Sarthe. The base of the Cenomanian stage (also the base of the Upper Cretaceous series) is placed at the first appearance of the planktonic foraminiferan species Rotalipora globotruncanoides in the stratigraphic record. The reference profile (GSSP) for the base is located in an outcrop at the western flank of Mont Risou, near Rosans in the French Alps (Hautes-Alpes department). The top of the Cenomanian (the base of the Turonian) is at the first appearance of the ammonite species Watinoceras devonense.

Fauna and events

The Cenomanian was a time of high global sea level and warm greenhouse climate. Large predatory dinosaurs such as Carcharodontosaurus and Spinosaurus lived in North Africa, while the Western Interior Seaway covered much of North America. Near the end of the stage, the Cenomanian–Turonian boundary event (anoxic event 2) caused a significant extinction of marine species, including many planktonic foraminifera and ammonites, associated with a major positive carbon-isotope excursion and widespread deposition of black shales.
