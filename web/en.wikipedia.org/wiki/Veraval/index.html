Veraval

Veraval is a city and municipality in the Gir Somnath district of Gujarat, India, situated on the coast of the Arabian Sea. It is one of India's major fishing ports and serves as the gateway to the nearby temple town of Somnath, a few kilometres to the east, site of the first of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva.

History

Veraval was established as a port town in the 13th or 14th century and became an important departure point for pilgrims travelling to Mecca under the Nawabs of Junagadh, whose principality administered it. The old port, Junagadh Gate, and the Nawab's summer palace are among its historic remains. After Indian independence, Veraval was part of Junagadh district until the creation of Gir Somnath district in 2013.

Economy

Fishing is the backbone of Veraval's economy: it is among the largest fish landing centres in India, with a substantial export trade in processed seafood and many fish-meal and fish-processing plants. The city also hosts industries in cement, rayon (Grasim Industries began its Indian Rayon plant here), and groundnut processing, and has large boat-building yards producing traditional wooden fishing vessels. Tourism associated with Somnath and the nearby Gir National Park further supports the local economy.

Transport

Veraval is connected by rail to Rajkot and Ahmedabad and by road along the Gujarat coastal highway. The nearest airport is at Diu and Rajkot.
