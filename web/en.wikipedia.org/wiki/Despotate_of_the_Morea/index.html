Despotate of the Morea

The Despotate of the Morea (Greek: Δεσποτᾶτον τοῦ Μορέως) was a province of the Byzantine Empire that existed between the mid-14th century and the mid-15th century. Its territory varied in size over its existence but eventually embraced most of the Peloponnese (then known as the Morea) in southern Greece. It was ruled by emperors' sons, who were awarded the title of despotes.

Its capital was Mystras, near ancient Sparta, which became a centre of the Palaiologan Renaissance and home to scholars such as Gemistus Pletho. Under the Kantakouzenos and Palaiologos despots, the Despotate expanded at the expense of the Latin Principality of Achaea, and by 1430 nearly the whole peninsula was back in Byzantine hands.

Following the fall of Constantinople in 1453, the Despotate was one of the last remnants of the empire; it was conquered by the Ottoman Empire under Mehmed II in 1460.
