Luk thung

Luk thung (Thai: ลูกทุ่ง, "children of the field") is the most popular form of traditional country music in Thailand. Emerging in the first half of the twentieth century, it tells of rural life, poverty, hard work in the rice fields and unrequited love, and it is distinguished from the urban pop of Bangkok and from mor lam, the folk music of the Lao-speaking northeast.

History

The genre grew out of phleng Thai sakon and rural folk song, crystallising in the 1960s with singers such as Suraphon Sombatcharoen, known as the "King of Luk Thung", whose song "Sib Hok Pi Haeng Kwam Lang" ("Sixteen Years of Our Past") became a standard. In the 1970s and 1980s stars like Waiphot Phetsuphan and Sayan Sanya dominated, and Pumpuang Duangjan, the "Queen of Luk Thung", modernised the genre with electronic dance arrangements (electronic luk thung) before her early death in 1992, after which large country-music stage troupes with elaborate dancers (hang khreuang) flourished.

Characteristics

Luk thung lyrics use the language of the countryside, slow vibrato-rich singing, and themes of migration to Bangkok, buffaloes, rice farming and the lives of truck drivers and factory workers. Songs are performed with big touring stage shows featuring costumed dance troupes. Its audience is principally the rural population and urban migrants, and its stars have often become national celebrities.
