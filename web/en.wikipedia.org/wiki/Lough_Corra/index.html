Lough Corra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lough Corra is a small lake (lough) in County Galway, Ireland, situated in the Connemara region in the valley between the Twelve Bens (Benna Beola) mountain range to the north and the Maumturks to the south. It lies along the course of the Bealnabrack River system that connects the larger lakes of the area, in a chain of small loughs between Lough Inagh and the Derryclare and Ballynahinch waters.

Geography

The lake sits in a glaciated valley of Precambrian quartzite and schist typical of Connemara, surrounded by blanket bog and rough grazing. Like other Connemara loughs, its waters are acidic and nutrient-poor (oligotrophic), stained brown by peat. The surrounding landscape is within the Twelve Bens/Garraun Complex Special Area of Conservation, designated for its upland and wetland habitats.

Ecology and fishing

Lough Corra supports populations of brown trout and is visited by migratory Atlantic salmon and sea trout passing through the connected river systems toward Ballynahinch and the sea. The valley's lakes are valued by anglers, and the lough's margins provide habitat for breeding waders and for otters. The name "Corra" derives from the Irish corra, relating to a weir or a heron, both features of Irish lake lore.
