Frequency response

In signal processing and electronics, the frequency response of a system is the quantitative measure of the magnitude and phase of the output as a function of input frequency. It characterises how a system responds to sinusoidal inputs of different frequencies and is widely used in the design and analysis of filters, amplifiers, control systems and audio equipment.

Definition

For a linear time-invariant system, the frequency response is the ratio of the output spectrum to the input spectrum, equivalently the Fourier transform of the system's impulse response. It is a complex-valued function H(jω) whose magnitude gives the gain and whose argument gives the phase shift at each frequency ω.

Measurement and representation

Frequency response is commonly displayed as a Bode plot: two graphs, of magnitude (usually in decibels) and phase (in degrees), plotted against frequency on a logarithmic scale. In audio, the frequency response of loudspeakers, microphones and headphones is often quoted as a range, such as 20 Hz to 20 kHz, with a tolerance in decibels indicating flatness.

Measurement may be made by sweeping a sine wave through the frequency range of interest, by analysing the response to an impulse, or with broadband noise and a spectrum analyser. Departures from a flat response colour the sound of audio equipment, while in control systems the frequency response determines stability margins such as gain margin and phase margin.
