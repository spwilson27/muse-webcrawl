The Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) extinction event, formerly the K–T extinction, was a sudden mass extinction of some three-quarters of the plant and animal species on Earth approximately 66 million years ago. It ended the reign of the non-avian dinosaurs and marked the boundary between the Mesozoic and Cenozoic eras.

Victims and survivors

Extinct groups include all non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, mosasaurs, plesiosaurs and ammonites, along with many birds, mammals and flowering plants. Survivors included avian dinosaurs (birds), crocodilians, turtles, frogs and the small feathered and furred lineages from which modern fauna descended; mammals and birds then radiated rapidly into emptied ecological niches.

Cause

In 1980 Luis and Walter Alvarez and colleagues reported a worldwide layer of iridium-rich clay at the K–Pg boundary and proposed impact by an asteroid about 10 km wide. The buried Chicxulub crater on Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, about 180 km across, was identified in the early 1990s as the impact site. The impact would have caused megatsunamis, wildfires, acid rain and an "impact winter" of sulphate aerosols that shut down photosynthesis. Massive Deccan Traps volcanism in India may have added climate stress; the relative weights of impact and volcanism are still studied, but the impact is regarded as the primary trigger.

Evidence

Key evidence includes the global iridium anomaly, shocked quartz, tektites and tsunami deposits around the Gulf of Mexico, and the precise dating of the boundary layer to the crater's age.
