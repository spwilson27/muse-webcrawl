Transcription (linguistics)

Phonetic transcription using the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA).

Transcription in the linguistic sense is the systematic representation of spoken language in written form. The source can either be utterances (speech or sign language) or preexisting text in another writing system.

Transcription should not be confused with translation, which means representing the meaning of text, or with transliteration, which represents the spelling of a text in another script. In linguistics, transcription is one of the central tasks of phonetics and discourse analysis.

Phonetic transcription

Phonetic transcription represents the sounds of speech. A distinction is drawn between phonemic (broad) transcription, written between slashes / /, which records only the sounds that distinguish meaning in a given language, and phonetic (narrow) transcription, written between square brackets [ ], which records finer detail such as aspiration or vowel quality. The standard system is the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), maintained since 1888.

Discourse and conversation transcription

In conversation analysis and linguistic anthropology, transcription systems such as that developed by Gail Jefferson record not only words but pauses, overlaps, intonation, laughter and other features of interaction. Such transcripts are analytical representations, and choices about what to include shape the resulting analysis.

Applications

Transcription underpins dictionary pronunciation guides, language documentation and revitalisation, speech-language pathology, forensic phonetics, and the training of speech recognition systems.
