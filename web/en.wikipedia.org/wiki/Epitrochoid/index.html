Epitrochoid

An epitrochoid (from Greek epi, "upon", and trochos, "wheel") is a roulette traced by a point attached to a circle of radius r rolling around the outside of a fixed circle of radius R, where the point is at a distance d from the centre of the rolling circle. It belongs to the family of curves that includes the epicycloid (the special case d = r, where the point lies on the rim) and is the analogue outside the circle of the hypotrochoid traced inside one.

Equations

With the fixed circle centred at the origin, the curve is given parametrically by

x(θ) = (R + r) cos θ − d cos(((R + r)/r) θ)
y(θ) = (R + r) sin θ − d sin(((R + r)/r) θ)

If R/r is a rational number n, the curve closes after n revolutions and has n lobes. When d < r the curve is smooth and wavy; when d > r it forms loops.

Special cases and applications

Special cases include the epicycloid (d = r), the limaçon (R = r), and — when R = 2r and d = r — a shape related to the ellipse. The epitrochoid is famous in engineering as the profile of the housing of the Wankel rotary engine: the engine's approximately triangular rotor moves within an epitrochoid-shaped chamber (strictly, an epitrochoid offset by the rotor's sealing geometry) so that its apex seals remain in constant contact with the wall while the three working chambers vary in volume. The Spirograph toy produces epitrochoids and hypotrochoids by rolling toothed wheels with a pen inserted at an offset hole.
