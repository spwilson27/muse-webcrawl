The township of Thursday Island, the administrative centre of the Torres Strait.

Thursday Island, known to its traditional owners as Waiben, is an island in the Torres Strait, about 39 kilometres north of Cape York Peninsula in far north Queensland, Australia. Although small — about 3.5 square kilometres — it is the administrative, commercial and transport centre of the Torres Strait Islands, with a hospital, courts, government offices, a port and regular air and ferry connections to the mainland and the other islands.

History

The island lies within the traditional lands of the Kaurareg people. In 1877 the Queensland government moved the administrative seat of the Torres Strait from Somerset to Thursday Island, attracted by its sheltered deep-water harbour on the main shipping route through the strait. From the 1880s the island boomed with the pearling industry: fleets of luggers worked the pearl shell and bêche-de-mer grounds, crewed by divers and workers from Japan, the Philippines, Malaya, the Pacific islands and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities. The resulting multicultural society — and the cemeteries where generations of Japanese pearl divers are buried — remain defining features of the island.

Defence and the world wars

Alarmed by fears of Russian attack, Queensland built Green Hill Fort on the island in 1891–1893, its guns commanding the strait; the fort is now a museum. During the Second World War Thursday Island became a major Australian and American military base, and its Japanese residents were interned. Remarkably, the island itself escaped bombing, although nearby Horn Island airfield was attacked repeatedly.

The island today

Today Thursday Island's population of roughly two and a half thousand is predominantly Torres Strait Islander, with a rich mix of other heritages from the pearling era. The island hosts the Gab Titui Cultural Centre, showcasing Torres Strait art and history, and serves as the service hub for the outer islands. Its economy depends on public administration, fishing (including crayfish and prawns), tourism and its port.
