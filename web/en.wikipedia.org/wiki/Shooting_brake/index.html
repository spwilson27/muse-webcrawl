Shooting brake

A shooting brake (also spelled shooting-brake) is a car body style that combines elements of a coupé and an estate car (station wagon), typically with a sleek, sloping rear roofline and a hatchback tailgate. The term originated in 19th-century Britain for a horse-drawn wagon used to carry shooting parties, their equipment, and game.

History

Early shooting brakes were custom-built on luxury chassis by coachbuilders such as Mulliner, serving country estates. In the motor age the layout was revived by manufacturers including Reliant (Scimitar GTE), Jensen, and Volvo (P1800 ES), and in the 21st century by premium brands such as Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, and Ferrari, whose GTC4Lusso is a modern example.

Characteristics

Unlike a conventional estate, a shooting brake usually has two doors (or a coupé-like four-door profile) and prioritises style over maximum cargo volume, with a steeply raked rear window and an elongated roof.
