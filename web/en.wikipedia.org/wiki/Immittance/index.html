Immittance

Immittance unifies the concepts of impedance and admittance

Immittance is a term used in electrical engineering and acoustics to refer to either electrical impedance or electrical admittance, regarded together. The reciprocal relationship between impedance Z and admittance Y, i.e. Y = 1/Z, motivated the invention of the term by Hendrik Bode, who found it convenient to have a single term that could refer to either function as context required.

Use in network analysis

In network analysis, immittance functions describe the driving-point behaviour of a one-port network: the ratio of voltage to current (impedance) or current to voltage (admittance) as a function of complex frequency. Properties such as positive-realness apply equally to both formulations, so theorems can be stated once for immittances in general.

Acoustic immittance

In audiology, acoustic immittance measurement assesses the function of the middle ear by measuring how much sound energy is admitted into the ear canal. Tympanometry is the best-known immittance test; it helps diagnose middle-ear disorders such as otitis media with effusion and ossicular discontinuity.
