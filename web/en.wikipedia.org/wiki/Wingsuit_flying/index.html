Wingsuit flying
Type: Air sport
Equipment: Wingsuit, parachute, helmet
Typical glide ratio: 2.5:1 to 3.5:1
Ancestor: Birdman suits of the 1930s

Wingsuit flying (or wingsuiting) is the sport of gliding through the air using a jumpsuit with fabric wings between the arms and body and between the legs, which increases the wearer's surface area and generates lift. A wingsuit flyer typically jumps from an aircraft or a high fixed object (BASE), glides forward at ratios around 3:1 — three metres forward for every metre descended — and deploys a parachute to land. Modern suits allow skilled pilots to steer, dive and flare, and to perform proximity flying close to terrain, one of the sport's most dangerous disciplines.

History

Early pioneers such as Clem Sohn and Leo Valentin experimented with rigid and canvas wings in the 1930s and 1950s, often fatally; of the early "birdmen", most died. Safe wingsuiting became practical in the mid-1990s with Patrick de Gayardon's ram-air fabric wing design, and in 1999 Jari Kuosma founded BirdMan, the first company to sell wingsuits commercially. Subsequent manufacturers such as Squirrel and Phoenix-Fly advanced designs, and wingsuit performance competitions and formation records followed.

Training and safety

Governing bodies generally require a minimum of about 200 skydives before a first wingsuit jump, followed by coaching. Flights end with parachute deployment; in BASE wingsuiting the margin for error near terrain is extremely small, and the discipline has a high fatality rate — experienced flyers such as Dean Potter and Alexander Polli were killed in wingsuit BASE accidents. In 2012 Gary Connery completed the first wingsuit landing without a parachute, landing on a runway of stacked cardboard boxes.
