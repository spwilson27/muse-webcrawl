List of North American tornadoes and tornado outbreaks

This is a list of notable tornadoes and tornado outbreaks affecting North America, which experiences more tornadoes than any other continent, with the great majority occurring in the United States — on average about 1,200 per year. Events are rated primarily on the Fujita scale (before 2007) and the Enhanced Fujita scale (from 2007).

Deadliest and most notable events (selected)

  • Tri-State Tornado (18 March 1925) — the deadliest tornado in US history, killing 695 people across Missouri, Illinois, and Indiana, with the longest recorded track (219 miles).
  • Natchez tornado (7 May 1840) — killed 317, the second-deadliest US tornado.
  • St. Louis tornado (27 May 1896) — killed 255.
  • Tupelo–Gainesville outbreak (April 1936) — Tupelo, Mississippi (216 deaths) and Gainesville, Georgia (203 deaths).
  • Woodward, Oklahoma (9 April 1947) — 181 deaths.
  • Flint–Worcester outbreak (June 1953) — Flint, Michigan (116 deaths) and Worcester, Massachusetts (94 deaths).
  • Super Outbreak (3–4 April 1974) — 148 tornadoes across 13 states, including multiple F5s; 319 deaths.
  • Andover, Kansas outbreak (26 April 1991) — famous F5 tornado filmed at McConnell Air Force Base.
  • Oklahoma tornado outbreak (3 May 1999) — the Bridge Creek–Moore F5, with the highest wind speeds ever measured on Earth (about 318 mph by Doppler radar).
  • 2011 Super Outbreak (25–28 April 2011) — the largest tornado outbreak on record: 360 tornadoes, including four EF5s, and 324 deaths; the Hackleburg and Tuscaloosa–Birmingham EF4s were especially destructive.
  • Joplin tornado (22 May 2011) — the costliest single US tornado, an EF5 that killed 158 in Joplin, Missouri.
  • El Reno tornado (31 May 2013) — the widest tornado ever recorded, 2.6 miles across; killed eight, including three storm researchers.
  • December 2021 Quad-State outbreak — a long-track EF4 devastated Mayfield, Kentucky.

Canadian and Mexican tornadoes

Canada records roughly 60–100 tornadoes per year, including the Edmonton tornado of 31 July 1987 ("Black Friday", 27 deaths) and the Elie, Manitoba F5 of 2007, Canada's first officially rated F5. Mexico's deadliest tornado struck Piedras Negras in 2007.

The complete list is organised chronologically by year and links to detailed articles on each outbreak.
