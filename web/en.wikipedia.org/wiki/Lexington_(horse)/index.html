Lexington (horse)

Lexington
Species: Thoroughbred racehorse
Foaled: 1850, Kentucky
Sire: Boston
Record: 7 starts, 6 wins

Lexington (1850–1875) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse who became the most successful sire in United States history, leading the sire list a record sixteen times. Bred in Kentucky and originally named Darley, he was by the great racer Boston out of Alice Carneal.

Racing career

Lexington won six of his seven starts, including famous match races against Lecomte at the Metairie Course in New Orleans, where he set a world record for four miles. His career was cut short by failing eyesight, and he was retired to stud in 1855.

Stud career and legacy

Standing first at Nantura Stock Farm and later at Woodburn Stud, Lexington sired numerous champions, including Kentucky, Asteroid, and the ancestors of much of the American Thoroughbred. He led the North American sire list sixteen times, fourteen of them consecutively. His skeleton is displayed at the Smithsonian Institution, and he was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1955.
