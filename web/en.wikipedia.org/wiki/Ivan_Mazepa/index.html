Ivan Mazepa

Ivan Mazepa
Born: 20 March 1639, Mazepintsi, Kiev Voivodeship
Died: 2 October 1709, Bendery, Principality of Moldavia
Hetman of Zaporizhian Host: 1687–1708

Ivan Stepanovych Mazepa (20 March 1639 – 2 October 1709) was a Ukrainian military, political, and civic leader who served as the Hetman of the Zaporozhian Host and the Left-bank Ukraine from 1687 to 1708. He is known for his role during the Great Northern War, when he sided with Charles XII of Sweden against Peter I of Russia.

Early life

Born into a noble family near Bila Tserkva, Mazepa was educated at the Kyiv-Mohyla Academy and in Warsaw, and served at the court of King John II Casimir of Poland before entering Cossack service under Hetmans Petro Doroshenko and Ivan Samoilovych.

Hetmanate

As hetman, Mazepa consolidated his authority, patronized education and the church, and funded construction in the Ukrainian Baroque style, including work on Kyiv's St. Sophia Cathedral and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. He initially enjoyed the confidence of Peter I, receiving the Order of St. Andrew.

Alliance with Sweden and downfall

In 1708, as Swedish forces advanced toward Ukraine, Mazepa concluded an alliance with Charles XII. After the Swedish defeat at the Battle of Poltava in 1709, Mazepa fled with Charles to Bendery in Ottoman-controlled Moldavia, where he died. In Russia he was anathematized by the Orthodox Church and long portrayed as a traitor, while in Ukraine he is regarded as a national hero; his portrait appears on the 10-hryvnia banknote.

Legacy

Mazepa's dramatic biography inspired works by Lord Byron, Alexander Pushkin, Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (the opera Mazeppa), and Franz Liszt.
