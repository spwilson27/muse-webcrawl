Maurice d'Elbée

Maurice Joseph Louis Gigost d'Elbée (21 March 1752 – 6 January 1794) was a French Royalist military leader and one of the principal commanders of the Catholic and Royal Army during the War in the Vendée, the counter-revolutionary uprising against the French Republic that began in 1793.

Life and role in the Vendée

D'Elbée was born in Dresden, where his father served in the Saxon army, and was naturalized French in 1777. He served as an infantry officer before retiring to his estates near Beaupréau in Anjou. When the Vendée rose in March 1793, local peasants persuaded him to lead them. Pious and charismatic, he became second-in-command of the Catholic and Royal Army under Jacques Cathelineau, and succeeded Cathelineau as generalissimo after the latter's death in July 1793 following the assault on Nantes.

Defeat and death

D'Elbée led the Vendéens in victories such as the Battle of Coron but the army's fortunes declined; he was seriously wounded at the Second Battle of Cholet in October 1793 and replaced by Henri de La Rochejaquelein. Carried with the retreating army during the Virée de Galerne, he was captured after the fall of Noirmoutier and executed by firing squad, seated in an armchair because of his wounds, on 6 January 1794. He is remembered for restraining his troops from massacring Republican prisoners after the Battle of Chemillé, an episode known through the "Pater de d'Elbée" anecdote.
