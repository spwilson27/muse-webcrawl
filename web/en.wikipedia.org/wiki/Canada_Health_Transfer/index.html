Canada Health Transfer

The Canada Health Transfer (CHT) is the largest major federal transfer to Canada's provinces and territories, providing long-term funding in support of provincial and territorial health care systems. It was created in 2004 when the former Canada Health and Social Transfer was split into the Canada Health Transfer and the Canada Social Transfer.

Operation

The transfer consists of cash payments and tax points, and is allocated on an equal per-capita basis. To receive their full share, provinces must comply with the criteria of the Canada Health Act, which requires public administration, comprehensiveness, universality, portability and accessibility of insured hospital and physician services, and prohibits extra-billing and user charges for those services.

Escalation and debate

The value of the transfer grows over time according to an escalator formula; under agreements reached in the 2010s, the cash transfer grows with nominal GDP, subject to a floor of three per cent. Provincial governments have frequently argued that the federal share of health spending is too low, while Ottawa has supplemented the CHT with targeted funding for priorities such as home care, mental health and reducing surgical backlogs. In 2023 the federal government reached new funding agreements with the provinces worth roughly $46 billion in additional funding over ten years.
