Jerome Robbins (born Jerome Wilson Rabinowitz; 11 October 1918 – 29 July 1998) was an American choreographer, dancer, director, and theater producer who worked in classical ballet, on Broadway, and in film.

Career

Robbins's first major success was the ballet Fancy Free (1944) with Leonard Bernstein, expanded the same year into the musical On the Town. He choreographed and often directed a string of Broadway landmarks: The King and I (1951), Peter Pan (1954), West Side Story (1957, with Bernstein), Gypsy (1959), and Fiddler on the Roof (1964). He co-directed the film of West Side Story (1961), winning the Academy Award for Best Director.

Later years

From 1969 he was a ballet master with the New York City Ballet, creating works such as Dances at a Gathering (1969) and The Goldberg Variations (1971). His career was shadowed by his 1953 testimony naming Communist associates before the House Un-American Activities Committee.
