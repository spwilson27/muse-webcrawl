Zagreb mummy

The Zagreb mummy is an ancient Egyptian mummy held by the Archaeological Museum in Zagreb, Croatia. It is famous because its linen wrappings carry the Liber Linteus Zagrabiensis, the longest known text in the Etruscan language — the only surviving example of an ancient book written on linen.

History

The mummy was bought in Alexandria in 1848 by the Croatian nobleman and collector Mihailo Barić, who kept it in his home in Vienna. After his death it passed to the museum in Zagreb, where in the 1890s the German Egyptologist Heinrich Brugsch, examining the unwrapped linen, recognised that the script was not Egyptian hieratic. In 1892 the Austrian scholar Jakob Krall identified the language as Etruscan. X-ray and other examinations suggest the mummy is that of a woman, probably middle-aged when she died in Ptolemaic Egypt.

The linen book

The wrappings, when reassembled, form a linen sheet of about 3.4 square metres originally written in twelve columns of text in black and red ink — the Liber Linteus, dated to roughly the third or second century BC. Only about 1,200 words are legible, but comparison with shorter Etruscan inscriptions shows it to be a ritual calendar prescribing offerings and ceremonies to various gods on specific dates. The linen had apparently been carried to Egypt, cut into strips and reused as mummy bandages by embalmers — an accidental preservation of a text that would otherwise certainly have perished.

Significance

Because Etruscan remains only partly understood, the Zagreb text is one of the most important documents for the study of the language and of Etruscan religion. The mummy and its wrappings are the centrepiece of the Egyptian collection of the Archaeological Museum in Zagreb.
