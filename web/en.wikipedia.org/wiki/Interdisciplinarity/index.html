Interdisciplinarity

Interdisciplinarity or interdisciplinary studies involves the combination of multiple academic disciplines into one activity, such as a research project. It draws knowledge from several fields, including sociology, anthropology, psychology, economics and others, to address questions that cross traditional disciplinary boundaries.

History

Although the term was coined in the twentieth century, interdisciplinary work has ancient roots: Greek philosophy, for example, drew no sharp lines between what are now separate subjects. In the modern university, calls for integration grew after the Second World War with the rise of area studies, environmental research and general systems theory.

Concepts

Scholars distinguish interdisciplinarity from multidisciplinarity, where specialists work in parallel, and from transdisciplinarity, which seeks a unity of knowledge beyond disciplinary perspectives. Interdisciplinary research is often driven by complex real-world problems — climate change, public health, sustainability — that no single field can solve alone.

In education

Interdisciplinary programmes have become common in higher education, from liberal arts curricula to dedicated institutes and journals. Critics note that evaluation, funding and hiring remain organised largely by discipline, which can disadvantage interdisciplinary scholars.
