NetJets

NetJets Inc. is an American company that sells fractional ownership shares in private business jets together with aircraft management and on-demand charter services. Founded in 1964 as Executive Jet Aviation, it is the largest private jet operator in the world and a subsidiary of Berkshire Hathaway since 1998.

History

Executive Jet Aviation was founded in 1964 and was an early corporate air charter service, at one time chaired by retired Air Force general Curtis LeMay. In 1984 the company was acquired by a group including Goldman Sachs executive Richard Santulli, who in 1986 launched the NetJets fractional ownership programme in the United States — the first of its kind, allowing customers to buy a share (as small as one-sixteenth) of a specific aircraft type in exchange for a guaranteed number of flight hours.

Warren Buffett, himself a NetJets customer, acquired the company for Berkshire Hathaway in 1998 for about $725 million. The business expanded through NetJets Europe and later into the Middle East and Asia, and through services such as the Marquis Jet card, which sells flight time in 25-hour blocks without ownership.

Operations

NetJets operates a fleet of more than 750 aircraft across types from light jets such as the Embraer Phenom 300 to ultra-long-range jets such as the Bombardier Global 7500 and Gulfstream G700. Owners pay an acquisition cost for their share, monthly management fees, and occupied hourly rates. Sister company Executive Jet Management manages wholly owned aircraft for clients.

Safety and labour relations

The company markets rigorous pilot training and maintenance standards. Its pilots are represented by the NetJets Association of Shared Aircraft Pilots, and contract negotiations have periodically been contentious, including a major agreement reached in 2024 covering pay and work rules.
