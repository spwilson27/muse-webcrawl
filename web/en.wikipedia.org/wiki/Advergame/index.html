Advergame

An advergame is a video game that advertises a product, service, organization, or viewpoint. The term is a portmanteau of "advertising" and "game". Advergames are a form of advertainment and are typically commissioned by brands to promote brand awareness and engagement, often distributed free of charge through company websites, social media, or mobile app stores.

History

Early advergames appeared in the 1980s, when companies distributed promotional games on floppy disks; notable examples include Pepsi Invaders (1983), a modified version of Space Invaders given to Coca-Cola executives, and Cool Spot (1993), featuring the 7 Up mascot. The genre expanded rapidly with the rise of Flash games on brand websites in the late 1990s and 2000s, and again with mobile gaming.

Types

Advergames are commonly grouped into three types: ATL (above-the-line) advergames built as standalone promotional experiences; BTL (below-the-line) games that integrate products into existing gameplay; and TTL (through-the-line) games using hyperlinks and cross-promotion. A related practice, in-game advertising, places brand messages inside independently developed games.

Effectiveness and criticism

Marketers value advergames for their long engagement times compared with conventional advertisements. Critics have raised concerns about advertising directed at children, who may not recognize the persuasive intent of a game built around a snack food or toy brand.
