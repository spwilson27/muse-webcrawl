South African Airways

IATA / ICAO: SA / SAA

Founded: 1 February 1934

Hub: O. R. Tambo International Airport, Johannesburg

Alliance: Star Alliance (joined 2006)

Frequent-flyer programme: Voyager

South African Airways (SAA) is the flag carrier airline of South Africa. Founded in 1934 when the government acquired Union Airways, it is headquartered at Kempton Park, Ekurhuleni, Gauteng, and operates from its hub at O. R. Tambo International Airport in Johannesburg to destinations across Africa and to selected intercontinental points.

History

SAA began with services inherited from Union Airways and expanded after the Second World War, introducing the Douglas DC-4 and later jet services to Europe with the Boeing 707 in 1960. During the apartheid era many African states denied SAA overflight rights, forcing long detours around the "bulge" of Africa on European routes; the airline responded with long-range aircraft such as the Boeing 747SP. Notable events include the loss of Flight 295 (the Helderberg) in the Indian Ocean in 1987, killing all 159 aboard.

After South Africa's democratic transition in 1994, SAA re-entered African airspace, rebranded with a new livery based on the national flag, and in 2006 became the first African carrier to join the Star Alliance.

Financial crisis and relaunch

The airline recorded persistent losses in the 2010s amid allegations of mismanagement and state capture. In December 2019 SAA entered business rescue; operations were suspended in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic. A restructured, much smaller SAA resumed flights in September 2021 and has since rebuilt a regional and limited intercontinental network.
