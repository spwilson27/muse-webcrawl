Maktab al-Khidamat (Arabic: مكتب الخدمات, "Services Bureau"), also known as the Afghan Services Bureau, was an organisation founded in Peshawar, Pakistan, in 1984 by the Palestinian scholar Abdullah Azzam and his former student Osama bin Laden. Its stated purpose was to raise funds and recruit foreign (mainly Arab) Muslim volunteers — the so-called "Afghan Arabs" — to fight alongside the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviet Union in the Soviet–Afghan War.

Activities

The bureau ran guesthouses in Peshawar, arranged travel and training for volunteers, channelled donations from the Gulf and elsewhere, and maintained offices or representatives in dozens of countries, including the Al Kifah Refugee Center in Brooklyn, New York. Azzam's preaching and publications, notably the magazine Al-Jihad, were central to its recruitment efforts.

After the war

After the Soviet withdrawal in 1989, Azzam was assassinated in Peshawar in November of that year. Bin Laden, meanwhile, had founded al-Qaeda in 1988. Historians and intelligence analysts debate the degree of organisational continuity between Maktab al-Khidamat and al-Qaeda; it is generally regarded as an important precursor and source of personnel and networks for the later organisation, though Azzam had envisaged a defensive jihad limited to Muslim lands rather than bin Laden's global campaign.
