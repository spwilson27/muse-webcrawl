Kortrijk (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈkɔrtrɛik]; French: Courtrai) is a Belgian city and municipality in the Flemish province of West Flanders. It is the capital and largest city of the arrondissement of Kortrijk, which is both a judicial and an administrative arrondissement. The wider urban area, known as the Lille–Kortrijk–Tournai Eurometropolis together with the French city of Lille, had over two million inhabitants in the early 21st century.

The city stands on the river Leie (French: Lys), about 25 kilometres southwest of Ghent and close to the French border. Its medieval centre preserves the famous Broel Towers on the river, a Béguinage founded in 1238, and the Church of Our Lady, where the spurs taken at the Battle of the Golden Spurs were once hung.

History

Kortrijk originated as the Roman settlement of Cortoriacum, a crossroads on the roads between Tongeren and Cassel and between Bavay and the coast. In the Middle Ages it grew into one of the richest cloth towns of Flanders, its prosperity built on the flax of the Leie, whose waters were ideal for retting and gave the river its nickname, the "Golden River".

On 11 July 1302, Flemish urban militias defeated a French royal army at the Battle of the Golden Spurs (Dutch: Guldensporenslag) on the Groeninge fields just outside the city walls. The victory, in which hundreds of golden spurs were collected from fallen French knights, remains a defining event in Flemish history, and 11 July is the official holiday of the Flemish Community.

The town suffered heavily in later centuries — besieged during the wars of Louis XIV and bombed in both World Wars — but was rebuilt each time. Textiles, especially flax and later furniture and machinery, remained the economic backbone into the modern era.

Sights

  • The Broel Towers, two near-identical medieval towers on the Leie, remnants of the old city fortifications.
  • The Béguinage of Saint Elizabeth, founded in 1238 by Joan of Constantinople, a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  • The Church of Our Lady (Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk), containing Anthony van Dyck's Raising of the Cross.
  • The Groeninge Monument and Groeninge Gate, commemorating the 1302 battle.
  • The Gothic town hall and the belfry on the Grote Markt.

Economy and transport

Kortrijk hosts the Kortrijk Xpo exhibition centre and textile and machine-building firms; the bus manufacturer Van Hool originated here. The city is served by Kortrijk railway station on the line between Ghent and Lille, by the A17/E403 and A14/E17 motorways, and by Kortrijk–Wevelgem International Airport.
