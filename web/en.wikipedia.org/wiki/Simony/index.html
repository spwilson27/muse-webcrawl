Simony

Simony is the act of selling church offices and roles or sacred things. It is named after Simon Magus, who in the Acts of the Apostles offered money to the apostles Peter and John to obtain the power of conferring the Holy Spirit.

History

Simony was a recurring problem in the medieval Church, as wealthy families and rulers bought bishoprics and abbacies, treating them as sources of revenue and power. It became a central target of the eleventh-century Gregorian Reform under Pope Gregory VII, and figures such as Peter Damian denounced it fiercely. The Church's canon law condemned simony as a grave offence, and Dante placed simoniac popes head-downward in holes in the rock in the Inferno.

Forms

Simony could involve direct payment, gifts, services or political favours in exchange for ordination, benefices, indulgences or sacraments. The abuse was among the grievances that fed into the Protestant Reformation, and the term is still used for the buying and selling of spiritual goods.
