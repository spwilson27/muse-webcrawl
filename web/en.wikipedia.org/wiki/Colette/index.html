Sidonie-Gabrielle Colette (28 January 1873 – 3 August 1954), known mononymously as Colette, was a French author and woman of letters. She was also a mime, actress, and journalist. She is best known in the English-speaking world for her 1944 novella Gigi, the basis of the Lerner and Loewe musical and the 1958 Hollywood film of the same name. Nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1948, she was the first woman admitted to the Académie Goncourt and its first female president.

Life

Born in the village of Saint-Sauveur-en-Puisaye in Burgundy, Colette married the writer and publisher Henry Gauthier-Villars ("Willy") in 1893. Under his direction — and initially under his name — she wrote the four Claudine novels (1900–1903), semi-autobiographical accounts of a spirited schoolgirl that became a publishing sensation in France. After separating from Willy, she performed in music halls across France, an unconventional life she recounted in The Vagabond (1910) and her memoirs.

Major works

  • Chéri (1920) — the tragic love affair between an ageing courtesan, Léa, and her much younger lover.
  • The Ripening Seed (1923) — a coming-of-age novel of adolescent love on the Breton coast.
  • Sido (1930) — a lyrical portrait of her beloved mother.
  • Gigi (1944) — the story of a young Parisian girl groomed for the life of a courtesan who instead marries her suitor.

Later life and honours

During the Second World War Colette remained in Paris, writing in her apartment at the Palais-Royal and sheltering her Jewish husband, Maurice Goudeket, who had been arrested by the Gestapo in 1941 and later released. On her death in 1954 she was given a state funeral — the first French woman of letters so honoured — though the Catholic Church denied her religious rites because of her divorces. She is buried at Père Lachaise Cemetery.

Legacy

Colette is celebrated for her sensual, precise prose, her exploration of female desire and independence, and depictions of nature drawn from her Burgundy childhood. Her works remain widely read in France, where she is regarded as one of the country's greatest 20th-century writers.
