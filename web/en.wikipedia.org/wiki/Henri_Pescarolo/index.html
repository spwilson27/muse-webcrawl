Henri Jacques William Pescarolo (born 25 September 1942) is a French former racing driver and team owner, celebrated above all for his record at the 24 Hours of Le Mans, which he contested 33 times and won four times. Instantly recognisable by his green helmet and beard, he is one of the most enduring figures in endurance racing.

Career

Pescarolo rose through French junior formulae with Matra, winning the French Formula Three championship in 1967 and finishing runner-up in European Formula Two in 1968. He raced in Formula One between 1968 and 1976 for Matra, March, Williams and BRM, scoring his best result of third at the 1970 Monaco Grand Prix, without the competitive machinery to fulfil his single-seater promise.

His greatest successes came in sports cars with Matra. Partnering Graham Hill in 1972 and Gérard Larrousse in 1973 and 1974, he won Le Mans three consecutive times in the wailing V12 Matra-Simca prototypes. A fourth victory came in 1984 with Klaus Ludwig in a Joest Racing Porsche 956. He also competed in the Dakar Rally and set records as a pilot, having taken up flying.

Team owner

After retiring from driving in 1999 — his final Le Mans as a driver came at the age of 56 — he founded Pescarolo Sport, which ran Courage and later Pescarolo-badged prototypes at Le Mans and in the Le Mans Series with notable success against factory teams, winning the Le Mans Series LMP1 title in 2005 and 2006. Financial difficulties led to the team's sale and eventual closure around 2010.
