An airline alliance is an agreement between airlines to cooperate at a substantial level, sharing revenue or costs on routes, coordinating schedules, recognising each other's frequent-flyer programmes, and offering seamless connections and through-check-in across their networks.

Tails of aircraft from several allied airlines

The three global alliances

Star Alliance, founded on 14 May 1997 by Air Canada, Lufthansa, Scandinavian Airlines, Thai Airways and United Airlines, is the largest, with more than two dozen member airlines. Oneworld followed in 1999 (founded by American Airlines, British Airways, Cathay Pacific and Qantas), and SkyTeam in 2000 (Aeroméxico, Air France, Delta and Korean Air).

Benefits and criticism

Passengers gain global networks, lounge access and reciprocal mileage earning; airlines gain feed traffic and economies of scale without mergers — important where foreign-ownership rules bar cross-border consolidation. Critics argue alliances can reduce competition on overlapping routes, leading regulators to require antitrust review and, in some cases, slot remedies.
