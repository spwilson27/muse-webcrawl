Mendoza wine

Mendoza wine refers to wine produced in the Argentine province of Mendoza, the centre of the Argentine wine industry. Mendoza accounts for about two-thirds of Argentina's wine production and is internationally famous as the adopted home of the Malbec grape, which produces deep-coloured, plush red wines that achieved worldwide acclaim from the 1990s onward.

Terroir

Vineyards lie at high altitude — typically 600 to 1,100 metres in Luján de Cuyo and Maipú, and up to 1,500 metres in the Uco Valley — giving intense sunlight, large day–night temperature variation, and low disease pressure in the dry desert air. Irrigation is supplied by meltwater from the Andes via a network of canals dating in part to pre-Columbian and colonial acequias.

History and grapes

Viticulture arrived with Spanish colonists in the 16th century. French varieties arrived in the 19th century, including Malbec, brought by Michel Aimé Pouget in 1853. After decades of high-volume domestic production, the industry reoriented toward quality exports in the 1990s under investors and winemakers such as Nicolás Catena. Besides Malbec, Mendoza produces Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Torrontés (mainly further north), Syrah, Bonarda and Tempranillo.
