International Volunteer Day (IVD), officially the International Volunteer Day for Economic and Social Development, is an international observance mandated by the United Nations General Assembly in 1985 and celebrated annually on 5 December. It offers an opportunity for volunteer-involving organisations and individual volunteers to promote volunteerism, encourage governments to support volunteer efforts, and recognise volunteers' contributions to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals at local, national and international levels.

History

The Day was adopted on 17 December 1985 through General Assembly resolution A/RES/40/212. Since then, governments, the UN system, civil society organisations and volunteers have marked the day with rallies, campaigns, clean-ups, award ceremonies and other events. The United Nations Volunteers programme, which administers the observance, announces an annual theme; recent campaigns have emphasised solidarity through volunteering and the role of volunteers in climate action and crisis response.

Observance

Events on and around 5 December include national volunteer awards, the publication of research on the state of volunteering — including UNV's periodic State of the World's Volunteerism Report — and online campaigns using the hashtag #IVD. The day is distinct from the broader International Year of Volunteers proclaimed for 2001 and again for 2026.
