Infantry of the British Army

The infantry of the British Army comprises the regiments of foot soldiers who fight primarily on foot, forming the largest branch of the Army. British infantry units include the Foot Guards, line infantry, the Rifles, airborne and special-forces support units, and regiments recruited from Scotland, Wales, Ireland and the Gurkhas of Nepal.

Organisation

Infantry battalions are grouped into regiments, many of which trace their lineage to the regiments raised in the 17th and 18th centuries and bear battle honours from conflicts worldwide. Successive rounds of amalgamation — notably the 2004–2006 restructuring — consolidated single-battalion regiments into larger multi-battalion regiments such as the Royal Regiment of Scotland, the Princess of Wales's Royal Regiment and the Rifles. Roles include armoured, mechanised, light and air assault infantry.

Recruitment and training

Infantry recruits train at the Infantry Training Centre at Catterick Garrison, completing the Combat Infantryman's Course; Guards and parachute training are conducted at additional locations. The infantry has borne the brunt of casualties in Britain's wars and remains central to operations from the World Wars to Iraq and Afghanistan.
