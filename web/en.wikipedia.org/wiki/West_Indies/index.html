Overview

The West Indies is a subregion of North America, surrounded by the North Atlantic Ocean and the Caribbean Sea, that comprises some 7,000 islands, islets, reefs and cays across three archipelagos: the Greater Antilles, the Lesser Antilles and the Lucayan Archipelago (Bahamas and Turks and Caicos). The name is a survival of Columbus's error in believing he had reached Asia's "Indies"; its peoples were long called "Indians" in European languages for the same reason.

Culture and sport

The region spans Spanish, French, English, Dutch and Creole cultures. "West Indies" survives most prominently in cricket: the West Indies cricket team unites the English-speaking Caribbean nations and won the first two Cricket World Cups (1975, 1979). The University of the West Indies (founded 1948) similarly serves the region from campuses in Jamaica, Trinidad, Barbados and beyond.
