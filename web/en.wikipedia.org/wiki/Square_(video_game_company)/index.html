Square Co., Ltd. (株式会社スクウェア, Kabushiki-gaisha Sukuwea) was a Japanese video game development and publishing company. It was founded in September 1986 by Masafumi Miyamoto in Tokyo, originally as a computer game software division of the electronics company Den-Yu-Sha.

History

Square's first releases were modest, and by 1987 the company faced bankruptcy. Designer Hironobu Sakaguchi created what he expected to be his final game before leaving the industry; released in December 1987, Final Fantasy became a major hit and one of the most influential role-playing game franchises of all time. Subsequent successes included Chrono Trigger (1995), Final Fantasy VII (1997) for the PlayStation, Xenogears, and Parasite Eve.

Square expanded into film with the computer-animated feature Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within (2001), whose commercial failure caused heavy losses and hastened a merger with rival publisher Enix. On April 1, 2003, Square merged with Enix to form Square Enix, with Square shareholders receiving a minority stake in the combined company.

Legacy

Square is remembered for popularising the Japanese role-playing game worldwide and for franchises including Final Fantasy, Dragon Quest's rival series SaGa and Mana, and collaborations such as Kingdom Hearts (developed at the end of the company's independent life). Its music, composed notably by Nobuo Uematsu, remains widely performed.
