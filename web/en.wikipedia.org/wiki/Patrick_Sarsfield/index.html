Patrick Sarsfield

Patrick Sarsfield, 1st Earl of Lucan (c. 1655 – 21 August 1693) was an Irish soldier and leading Jacobite in the Williamite War in Ireland. Celebrated for his cavalry leadership and his defence of Limerick, he became one of the most enduring heroes of Irish nationalist memory, and his name was later given to the rallying cry "Remember Limerick" and to units of Irish soldiers in foreign service.

Early career

Sarsfield was born into an Old English Catholic landowning family at Lucan, County Dublin. After military service in English regiments in France during the 1670s, he returned to England, survived involvement in the politics of the Exclusion Crisis era, and rose in the army of James II, whom he followed to Ireland after the Glorious Revolution of 1688.

Williamite War

Sarsfield fought at the Battle of the Boyne (1690) and distinguished himself in the defence of Limerick later that year, leading a daring night raid that destroyed William of Orange's siege train at Ballyneety — an exploit that forced William to raise the first Siege of Limerick. After the Jacobite defeat at Aughrim (1691), Sarsfield negotiated the Treaty of Limerick, whose military articles allowed some 14,000 Irish soldiers to sail for France — the "Flight of the Wild Geese" — while its civil articles, soon broken by the English parliament, promised protections for Irish Catholics.

Death and legacy

Commissioned in French service, Sarsfield was mortally wounded fighting the Dutch at the Battle of Landen in 1693; legend holds that, seeing his own blood, he exclaimed, "Oh, if this were only shed for Ireland." James II had created him Earl of Lucan in 1691. Brigades of the Irish diaspora and later Irish nationalist movements honoured his memory, and Count Sarsfield remains a byword for gallant, romantic resistance.
