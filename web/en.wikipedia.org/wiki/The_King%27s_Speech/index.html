The King's Speech is a 2010 British historical drama film directed by Tom Hooper and written by David Seidler. Colin Firth plays Prince Albert, Duke of York (later King George VI), who copes with a stammer; Geoffrey Rush plays Lionel Logue, an Australian speech and language therapist who helps him overcome it.

Plot

Prince Albert, the second son of King George V, suffers from a debilitating speech impediment and dreads public speaking. After many failed treatments, his wife Elizabeth persuades him to see Lionel Logue, an unconventional Australian therapist. Through a mixture of psychological and physical techniques, and a growing friendship, the Duke gradually gains confidence. When his elder brother Edward VIII abdicates in 1936, Albert becomes king and must deliver radio broadcasts to the nation, culminating in his first wartime speech following Britain's declaration of war on Germany in 1939.

Reception and awards

The film was a major commercial and critical success. At the 83rd Academy Awards it won four Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for Firth, and Best Original Screenplay for Seidler, from twelve nominations. It also won seven BAFTA Awards, including Best Film.

Historical background

Seidler, who himself had a stutter as a child, researched Logue's methods and obtained permission from Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother to depict her husband's story, on the condition that the film not be made during her lifetime. Mark Logue, Lionel's grandson, published the therapists' notebooks as The King's Speech: How One Man Saved the British Monarchy.
