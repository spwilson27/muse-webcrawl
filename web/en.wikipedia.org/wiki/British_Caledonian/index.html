British Caledonian

British Caledonian (BCAL) was a British independent airline that operated from 1970 until 1988. Created by the merger of Caledonian Airways and British United Airways, it was for much of its life the principal private-sector rival to the state-owned British Airways, styling itself "Britain's second force" airline.

A BCAL DC-10-30; the tail carried the Scottish lion rampant.

History

The airline was formed on 20 November 1970 following the Edwards Committee report, which recommended the creation of a strong independent "second force" in British aviation. Based at London Gatwick Airport, British Caledonian built a network of scheduled services to Africa, South America, the United States and the Far East, and became known for its Scottish identity, tartan-clad cabin crew and high service standards.

Financial difficulties following the early-1980s recession, the Falklands War (which cost it lucrative South American routes) and US deregulation weakened the airline. In July 1987 British Airways announced a takeover bid, which was cleared despite a counter-bid from SAS; British Caledonian was absorbed into British Airways on 14 April 1988, ending its 17 years of operation.

Fleet

The fleet included the Boeing 707, BAC One-Eleven, McDonnell Douglas DC-10-30 and, in its final years, the Boeing 747. The airline was the UK launch customer for the Airbus A310 order that never entered service before the takeover.
