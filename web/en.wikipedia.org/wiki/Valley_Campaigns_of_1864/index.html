Valley campaigns of 1864

The Valley campaigns of 1864 were a series of operations in the Shenandoah Valley of Virginia during the American Civil War, forming part of Union general-in-chief Ulysses S. Grant's coordinated strategy to destroy Confederate armies and the resources sustaining them. The Valley — the "breadbasket of the Confederacy" — and its role as an invasion corridor toward Washington made it a repeated battleground.

Early's operations, May–August 1864

In May 1864 Union Major General Franz Sigel advanced up the Valley and was defeated by Confederate Major General John C. Breckinridge at the Battle of New Market (15 May, famous for the charge of the Virginia Military Institute cadets). Lieutenant General Jubal Early then took command of the Confederate Army of the Valley, marched down the Valley, crossed the Potomac, won the Battle of Monocacy (9 July) — a tactical victory that delayed him enough to save Washington — and reached the outskirts of the capital, skirmishing at Fort Stevens before withdrawing. Early's men also raided into Pennsylvania, where Chambersburg was burned after refusing a ransom.

Sheridan's campaign, August–October 1864

Grant placed Major General Philip Sheridan in command of the Army of the Shenandoah with orders to destroy Early's army and render the Valley unable to support the Confederacy. Sheridan defeated Early at the Third Battle of Winchester (19 September) and at Fisher's Hill (22 September), then systematically devastated crops, barns and mills in an operation remembered locally as "The Burning". Early counterattacked at dawn at Cedar Creek (19 October); Sheridan, returning from Winchester, famously rallied his retreating army and won a decisive victory that effectively ended major Confederate resistance in the Valley.

Outcome

The campaigns eliminated the Shenandoah as a Confederate granary and invasion route, boosted Northern morale in time for Abraham Lincoln's re-election, and cemented Sheridan's reputation. Remnants of Early's force were defeated at Waynesboro in March 1865 and the Valley remained in Union hands until the war's end.
