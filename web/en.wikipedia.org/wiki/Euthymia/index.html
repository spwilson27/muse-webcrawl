Euthymia

Euthymia is a stable, calm, and even mood state. In psychiatry, the term describes the baseline mood of a person with a mood disorder, such as bipolar disorder, during periods when they are neither manic nor depressed. A euthymic patient shows a reasonably cheerful and tranquil disposition without the extreme highs of mania or hypomania or the lows of depression.

Use in mood disorders

Assessment of euthymia is central to the long-term management of bipolar disorder, since treatment aims to maximise the time patients spend in a euthymic state and to prevent relapse into mood episodes. Residual subsyndromal symptoms during apparent euthymia are associated with a higher risk of recurrence, and researchers distinguish simple absence of symptoms from "euthymia with functionality", in which the person also regains normal social and occupational functioning and a subjective sense of wellbeing.

Terminology

The word comes from the Greek eu (well) and thymos (soul, emotion). It contrasts with dysthymia (chronically low mood) and cyclothymia. The philosopher Democritus used "euthymia" for a state of contentment and cheerfulness, a meaning reflected in Seneca's essay De tranquillitate animi.
