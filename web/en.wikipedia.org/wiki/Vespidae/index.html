OrderHymenoptera
SuperfamilyVespoidea
FamilyVespidae
SpeciesAbout 5,000 described

The Vespidae are a large, diverse, cosmopolitan family of wasps, including nearly all the known eusocial wasps (such as hornets, yellowjackets and paper wasps) and many solitary wasps (such as potter wasps and mason wasps). The family contains roughly 5,000 species in six subfamilies.

Biology

Social vespids build nests of a paper-like material made from chewed wood fibres, either hanging exposed combs (paper wasps, Polistes) or enclosing the combs in a papery envelope (yellowjackets and hornets). Colonies are founded in spring by a single fertilised queen and can grow to thousands of workers by late summer; workers are sterile females that forage for insects to feed the larvae and collect nectar and other carbohydrates for energy. Solitary vespids, such as potter wasps, build small mud nests and provision them with paralysed prey.

Subfamilies

The six subfamilies are Eumeninae (potter and mason wasps, the largest subfamily), Euparagiinae, Masarinae (pollen wasps), Polistinae (paper wasps), Stenogastrinae (hover wasps) and Vespinae (hornets and yellowjackets). Vespids are recognised by their wings folding longitudinally at rest and by their pronotum extending back to the tegulae.
