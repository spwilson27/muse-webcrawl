Laguerre polynomials

In mathematics, the Laguerre polynomials, named after Edmond Laguerre (1834–1886), are solutions of Laguerre's equation, a second-order linear differential equation. They form an orthogonal polynomial sequence on the interval [0, ∞) with respect to the weight function e−x.

Definition

Laguerre's equation is x y′′ + (1 − x) y′ + n y = 0, where n is a non-negative integer. The polynomial solutions are denoted Ln(x) and can be defined by the Rodrigues formula: Ln(x) = (ex/n!) dn/dxn (e−x xn). The first few are L0(x) = 1, L1(x) = 1 − x, and L2(x) = (x² − 4x + 2)/2.

Recurrence relations

The Laguerre polynomials satisfy the three-term recurrence (n+1) Ln+1(x) = (2n + 1 − x) Ln(x) − n Ln−1(x), together with derivative identities such as x Ln′(x) = n Ln(x) − n Ln−1(x).

Generalized Laguerre polynomials

The generalized (or associated) Laguerre polynomials Ln(α)(x) arise for general α > −1 and satisfy a corresponding generalization of Laguerre's equation. They appear in quantum mechanics, notably in the radial part of the wavefunction of the hydrogen atom.

Applications

Laguerre polynomials are used in numerical analysis (Gauss–Laguerre quadrature), probability (the gamma distribution), and physics, including the solution of the Schrödinger equation for hydrogen-like atoms.
