The Arcades Project

The Arcades Project (German: Das Passagen-Werk) is an unfinished work by German philosopher and cultural critic Walter Benjamin, written between 1927 and his death in 1940. Much of Benjamin's later life was devoted to it. The work takes as its object the Paris arcades — the covered shopping passages built in the early 19th century — and through them the whole material culture of 19th-century Paris, which Benjamin called the "capital of the nineteenth century."

Contents and method

Rather than a conventional monograph, the Passagen-Werk is a vast montage of quotations and commentary organized into 36 files ("convolutes") lettered A to Z and beyond, covering topics such as fashion, boredom, the flâneur, iron construction, prostitution, Baudelaire, Haussmann's renovation of Paris, and the Paris Commune. Benjamin intended the work as a materialist history of modernity in which the quotations themselves would bear the argument — a method he described as assembling "the refuse" and "rags" of history into a dialectical image.

Publication and influence

Benjamin left the manuscript behind when he fled Paris in 1940; it was preserved through Georges Bataille's hiding of materials in the Bibliothèque nationale. The papers were edited and published in German in 1982 and in English translation in 1999. Though unfinished, the work has become one of the most influential texts of 20th-century cultural studies, shaping scholarship on modernity, urban experience, and commodity culture.
