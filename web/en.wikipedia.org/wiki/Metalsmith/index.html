A metalsmith or simply smith is a craftsperson fashioning useful items (for example, tools, kitchenware, tableware, jewellery, armour, and weapons) out of various metals. Smithing is one of the oldest metalworking occupations. Shaping metal with a hammer (forging) is the archetypical component of the craft, but metalsmiths also cast, solder, chase, and finish metal.

Types

Specialised smiths are named for their material or product: a blacksmith works iron and steel; a goldsmith and silversmith work precious metals, much modern work being jewellery; a whitesmith finishes tin and light metals; a brownsmith works copper and brass; and a farrier shoes horses. Other historic specialists include armourers, bladesmiths, locksmiths, and pewtersmiths.

History

Metalworking began with hammered copper and gold in the Neolithic period and transformed societies with the Bronze Age and Iron Age. Smiths held high status in many cultures and feature prominently in mythology — Hephaestus and Vulcan, Thor, and Wayland the Smith among them. Medieval guilds regulated training through apprenticeship, journeyman, and master stages. Industrialisation displaced many village smiths, but smithing persists as a trade in farriery, industrial forging, and as a vibrant studio craft and art form.

Tools and techniques

The forge heats metal to a malleable state; the smith then works it on an anvil with hammers and tongs through processes including drawing out, upsetting, bending, punching, and forge welding. Finishing techniques include filing, grinding, hardening and tempering of steel, and patination of non-ferrous metals.
