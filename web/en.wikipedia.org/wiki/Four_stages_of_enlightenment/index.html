Four stages of enlightenment

The four stages of enlightenment in Theravada Buddhism are four progressive stages culminating in full enlightenment as an arhat. Practitioners who attain these stages are collectively known as the ariya-puggala, the "noble persons", and progress is marked by the abandonment of the ten fetters (saṃyojana) that bind beings to saṃsāra.

The four stages

Stream-enterer (sotāpanna): one who has "entered the stream" flowing toward nirvana. The stream-enterer has abandoned the first three fetters—identity view (belief in a self), sceptical doubt, and attachment to rites and rituals—and will be reborn no more than seven times, never in a lower realm.

Once-returner (sakadāgāmin): one who will return to the human world only once more. The once-returner has additionally weakened sensual desire and ill will.

Non-returner (anāgāmin): one who has fully abandoned the first five fetters, including sensual desire and ill will, and will not return to the human world, attaining enlightenment in a Pure Abode after death.

Arhat (arahant): one who has abandoned all ten fetters, including conceit, restlessness, and ignorance, and is fully liberated, with no further rebirth.

Path and fruit

Each stage is traditionally divided into the moment of "path" (magga) and the moment of "fruit" (phala), yielding eight types of noble persons. The schema of progressive purification is elaborated in commentaries such as Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga.
