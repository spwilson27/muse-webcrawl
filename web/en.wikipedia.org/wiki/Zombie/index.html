A zombie is a mythological undead corporeal revenant created through the reanimation of a corpse. Zombies are most commonly found in works of horror and fantasy. The term comes from Haitian folklore, in which a zombie is a dead body reanimated through various methods, most commonly magical practices within the religion of Vodou.

Origins in Haiti

In Haitian Vodou, a zonbi is a corpse raised by a sorcerer (bokor) and kept in a mindless, enslaved state — a fate reflecting the historical horror of slavery itself. Ethnobotanist Wade Davis controversially proposed that such "zombies" were living people drugged with preparations containing tetrodotoxin and later "raised" in a suggestible trance state.

Modern popular culture

Zombies entered Western entertainment through travel writing and early films such as White Zombie (1932). George A. Romero's Night of the Living Dead (1968) and Dawn of the Dead (1978) reinvented them as flesh-eating ghouls arising in an apocalypse — the template for thousands of films, television series such as The Walking Dead, video games including Resident Evil, and "zombie walks" by fans. Later works introduced fast-moving or infection-based zombies, and the figure has become a versatile metaphor for pandemics, consumerism, and loss of agency.
