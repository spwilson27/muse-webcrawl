Olympic Games opening ceremony

The opening ceremony of the Olympic Games is the official ceremony by which the Games are inaugurated. The Olympic Charter mandates various elements: an artistic programme showcasing the host nation's culture, the parade of nations, the raising of the Olympic flag, the taking of the Olympic oath, and the lighting of the Olympic cauldron by the final torchbearer of the relay.

Traditions

During the parade of nations, athletes enter the stadium grouped by delegation. By tradition established in 1928, Greece enters first, in honor of the ancient Games, and the host nation enters last; other delegations follow in alphabetical order of the host language. The head of state of the host nation formally declares the Games open, and doves or their symbolic equivalent are released as a gesture of peace. Since 1920 the Olympic oath has been taken by an athlete, a judge, and (since 2012) a coach.

Notable ceremonies

Opening ceremonies have grown into lavish spectacles. The Beijing 2008 ceremony, directed by Zhang Yimou, was acclaimed for its scale; London 2012's "Isles of Wonder," directed by Danny Boyle, celebrated British culture and included the Queen's filmed appearance with James Bond; Paris 2024 broke with stadium tradition by staging the ceremony along the Seine. The lighting of the cauldron — by archer's arrow at Barcelona 1992 or by Muhammad Ali at Atlanta 1996 — is often the ceremony's centerpiece. The Games are also held at venues such as the Olympic Stadium in London.
