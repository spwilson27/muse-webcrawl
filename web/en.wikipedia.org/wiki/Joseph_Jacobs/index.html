Joseph Jacobs

Joseph Jacobs (29 August 1854 – 30 January 1916) was an Australian-born English folklorist, literary critic, historian, and Jewish scholar. He popularised English fairy tales for generations of children through collections such as English Fairy Tales (1890) and More English Fairy Tales (1894) — the books that gave wide currency to stories including "Jack and the Beanstalk", "Goldilocks and the Three Bears", and "The Three Little Pigs" — and he served as revising editor of the Jewish Encyclopedia in New York.

Life and career

Jacobs was born in Sydney, Australia, and educated at Sydney Grammar School and St John's College, Cambridge. In London he became secretary of the Society of Hebrew Literature and a prominent member of the Anglo-Jewish community, writing on Jewish history, statistics, and the persecution of Jews in Europe. An exhibition of Anglo-Jewish historical documents in 1887, which he helped organise, stimulated the founding of the Jewish Historical Society of England, of which he was president.

In 1900 Jacobs moved to New York to serve as revising editor of the Jewish Encyclopedia (1901–1906), to which he contributed hundreds of articles. He later taught at the Jewish Theological Seminary of America.

Folklore work

Jacobs applied the comparative methods of the new science of folklore to English tales, editing collections of English, Celtic, and Indian fairy tales and the fables of Aesop and Bidpai, and contributing to Andrew Lang's circle of folklorists. His children's collections aimed to do for English children what the Grimms had done for Germany: give them a national store of tales, retold in a lively style. He also wrote an influential early study, The Fables of Aesop (1889), and used statistical comparison of tale variants — his "diffusion" studies were early steps toward the historical-geographic method of folktale research.
