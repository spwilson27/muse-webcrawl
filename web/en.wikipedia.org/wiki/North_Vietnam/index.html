North Vietnam, officially the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV), was a socialist state in Southeast Asia that existed from 1945 to 1976. Proclaimed by Ho Chi Minh in Hanoi on 2 September 1945, it fought France in the First Indochina War and, after the 1954 Geneva Accords partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel, governed the northern half while the US-backed Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam) governed the south. During the Vietnam War the DRV, supported by the Soviet Union and China, directed and supplied the insurgency in the south and withstood years of US bombing. Following the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, North and South were formally reunified on 2 July 1976 as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam.

Founding and the First Indochina War

The DRV was proclaimed after the August Revolution of 1945, in the vacuum left by Japan's defeat. France, determined to restore its Indochinese federation, returned by negotiation and then force; full-scale war began in December 1946. After eight years of guerrilla and then conventional warfare, the Viet Minh's victory at Dien Bien Phu in May 1954 forced the Geneva settlement. Elections to unify the country were scheduled for 1956 but never held, as the southern government of Ngo Dinh Diem, backed by the United States, refused to participate.

Socialist construction in the North

In the North, the Workers' Party (later Communist Party) under Ho Chi Minh and party secretary Lê Duẩn carried out land reform in 1953–1956, redistributing land to poor peasants; its excesses — thousands executed or imprisoned — forced a public "rectification of errors" and the removal of party secretary Trường Chinh. Agriculture was collectivised by 1960 and heavy industry built with Soviet and Chinese aid, though the economy remained poor and war-dominated. The 1960 party congress formally committed the DRV to supporting "revolution" in the South.

The Vietnam War

From 1959 the DRV infiltrated cadres and supplies southward along the Ho Chi Minh trail through Laos, and sponsored the National Liberation Front (the Viet Cong, founded 1960). After the Gulf of Tonkin incident in 1964, the United States escalated: Operation Rolling Thunder (1965–1968) dropped hundreds of thousands of tons of bombs on the North, targeting industry, transport and the trail network, yet failed to break Hanoi's will or stop infiltration. Mass mobilisation, dispersal of industry, vast air defences (including Soviet-supplied SAMs) and some 320,000 Chinese support troops in rear areas sustained the war effort. The 1972 Easter Offensive and the Linebacker II "Christmas bombing" preceded the Paris Peace Accords of January 1973, under which US forces withdrew while North Vietnamese troops remained in the South.

Reunification

With US aid to Saigon cut and American airpower gone, the DRV launched its final offensive in spring 1975; Saigon fell on 30 April. A provisional revolutionary government administered the South for a year before formal reunification on 2 July 1976 created the Socialist Republic of Vietnam, with Hanoi as capital and Tôn Đức Thắng as president. The DRV's flag, anthem and party structures passed to the unified state.
