Sedimentation

Placeholder image: sediment settling to the bottom of a liquid.

Sedimentation is the process by which particles suspended in a fluid settle out of the fluid and come to rest under the influence of gravity (or, in a centrifuge, centrifugal force). The settling velocity of a small spherical particle in a viscous fluid is described by Stokes' law: larger and denser particles settle faster, while very fine particles may remain suspended indefinitely due to Brownian motion and electrostatic repulsion.

Applications

Sedimentation is a basic step in water and wastewater treatment, where clarifiers and settling tanks remove suspended solids before filtration and disinfection. In geology, sedimentation is the deposition stage of the formation of sedimentary rocks: particles eroded and transported by water, wind, or ice accumulate in layers that are later buried and lithified. The erythrocyte sedimentation rate is a common laboratory test in medicine that measures how quickly red blood cells settle in a tube of blood, as an indicator of inflammation.
