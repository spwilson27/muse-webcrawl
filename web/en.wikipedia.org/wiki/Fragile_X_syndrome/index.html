Fragile X syndrome

Fragile X syndrome (FXS) is a genetic disorder characterised by mild-to-moderate intellectual disability. It is caused by an expansion of the CGG trinucleotide repeat in the FMR1 gene on the X chromosome, which silences the gene and reduces production of FMRP, a protein important for neural development. FXS is the most common inherited cause of intellectual disability and a leading known genetic cause of autism spectrum disorder.

Signs and symptoms

Features may include developmental delays, learning disability, anxiety, hyperactivity, autistic behaviours, a long and narrow face, large ears, and (in post-pubertal males) enlarged testicles (macroorchidism). Females with the full mutation are usually less severely affected because of X-inactivation.

Cause and related conditions

People with 55–200 CGG repeats have a premutation, which can expand to a full mutation when passed by a mother. Premutation carriers are at risk of fragile X-associated tremor/ataxia syndrome (FXTAS) and primary ovarian insufficiency (FXPOI). There is no cure; management includes special education, behavioural therapy and medication for associated symptoms.
