The Two Micron All-Sky Survey, or 2MASS, was a major astronomical survey of the entire sky in near-infrared light, conducted between 1997 and 2001. It produced a catalogue of roughly 471 million point sources and over 1.6 million extended sources, and remains one of the most widely used astronomical datasets.

Observations

2MASS used two dedicated 1.3-metre telescopes, one at the Fred Lawrence Whipple Observatory on Mount Hopkins, Arizona, for the northern sky, and one at the Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory in Chile for the southern sky. Each telescope observed simultaneously in three near-infrared bands: J (1.25 µm), H (1.65 µm) and Ks (2.17 µm). The project was a collaboration between the University of Massachusetts Amherst and NASA's Infrared Processing and Analysis Center (IPAC) at Caltech.

Scientific results

Because it observed in the infrared, 2MASS could see through much of the dust that obscures visible light, mapping the structure of the Milky Way and nearby galaxies. Its catalogue enabled the discovery of numerous brown dwarfs, including many of the coolest known, and is routinely used as a reference catalogue for calibrating other observations and for identifying targets for follow-up.
