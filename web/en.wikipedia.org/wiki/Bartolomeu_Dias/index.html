Overview

Bartolomeu Dias (c. 1450–1500) was a Portuguese mariner and explorer who in 1488 became the first European known to sail around the southern tip of Africa, opening the sea route from Europe to Asia. Sent by King John II to find the realm of Prester John and the source of Indian spices, Dias sailed beyond the furthest point of Diogo Cão, was carried by a storm around the Cape (which he named the Cape of Storms, later renamed the Cape of Good Hope), and reached the Great Fish River before his crew forced the return.

Later life

Dias supervised construction of Vasco da Gama's ships for the 1497–99 India voyage and sailed with Pedro Álvares Cabral's fleet in 1500, taking part in the sighting of Brazil; he died when his ship was lost in a storm near the Cape in May 1500. His padrão (stone pillar) at Kwaaihoek was rediscovered in fragments in the 20th century.
