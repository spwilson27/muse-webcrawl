The Canterbury Tales
Author: Geoffrey Chaucer
Written: c. 1387–1400, Middle English
Form: Frame narrative, verse and prose
Tales: 24 (of a planned ~120)

The Canterbury Tales is a collection of 24 stories by Geoffrey Chaucer, written in Middle English at the end of the 14th century. A group of pilgrims travelling from London to Thomas Becket's shrine at Canterbury Cathedral agree to a storytelling contest hosted by Harry Bailly.

The tales

The pilgrims — Knight, Miller, Wife of Bath, Pardoner, Nun's Priest and others — span medieval society; their tales range from the Knight's courtly romance to bawdy fabliaux and moral exempla, with satire of the Church.

Significance

Unfinished at Chaucer's death, it is a foundational work of English literature, popularising English as a literary language.
