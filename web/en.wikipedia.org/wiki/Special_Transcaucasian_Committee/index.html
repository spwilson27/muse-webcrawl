Special Transcaucasian Committee

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Special Transcaucasian Committee (known by its Russian acronym OZAKOM, from Osobyi Zakavkazskii Komitet) was an administrative body established by the Russian Provisional Government in 1917 to govern Transcaucasia — the modern territories of Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan — following the February Revolution and the collapse of imperial authority at the end of the First World War.

History

Formed in March 1917 and seated in Tiflis (Tbilisi), the committee was chaired by Vasily Kharlamov, a Kadet deputy of the State Duma, and included Georgian, Armenian and Azerbaijani representatives. It replaced the administration of the abolished Caucasus Viceroyalty but commanded limited authority amid revolutionary upheaval, ethnic tension and the ongoing Caucasus campaign against the Ottoman Empire.

Dissolution

After the October Revolution, the committee gave way in November 1917 to the Transcaucasian Commissariat, and the region's affairs passed to the Transcaucasian Seim and, in 1918, to independent republics.
