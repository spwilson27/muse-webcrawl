Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument

Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument
LocationNorthwestern Hawaiian Islands, Pacific Ocean
AreaAbout 583,000 sq mi (1.51 million km²) after the 2016 expansion
EstablishedJune 15, 2006, by President George W. Bush; expanded August 26, 2016, by President Barack Obama
RecognitionUNESCO World Heritage Site (2010), mixed cultural and natural

Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument is a vast protected area encompassing the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands and surrounding waters. At roughly 583,000 square miles, it is one of the largest marine conservation areas in the world, safeguarding coral reefs, atolls, deep-water habitats and cultural sites of profound importance to Native Hawaiians.

The name joins Papahānaumoku, the earth-mother figure, and Wākea, the sky father, in Hawaiian cosmology, reflecting the region's role as a place of ancestral origin and return. The monument contains Midway Atoll, Kure Atoll, French Frigate Shoals, Laysan and Lisianski islands, and the sacred island of Nihoa and Mokumanamana (Necker) with their archaeological shrines.

Wildlife and ecosystems

The monument shelters more than 7,000 marine species, about a quarter of them endemic, including threatened Hawaiian monk seals, green sea turtles, Laysan and black-footed albatrosses, and apex predators such as sharks and jacks that are rare elsewhere. Its isolation has preserved reef ecosystems of exceptional richness.

Management and history

The area is co-managed by NOAA, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the State of Hawaiʻi and the Office of Hawaiian Affairs. President Theodore Roosevelt first protected the islands' birdlife in 1909; the region later became a coral reef ecosystem reserve before George W. Bush designated the monument in 2006, and President Obama's 2016 proclamation roughly quadrupled its size. In 2010 UNESCO listed it as a mixed World Heritage Site, recognized for both nature and Native Hawaiian culture. Commercial fishing is prohibited, and access is tightly permitted for research, conservation and Native Hawaiian practice.
