Chongqing (Chinese: 重庆; pinyin: Chóngqìng) is a major city in southwestern China and one of the country's four direct-administered municipalities, alongside Beijing, Shanghai, and Tianjin. Set in the hills at the confluence of the Yangtze and Jialing rivers, it is a principal economic, manufacturing, and transport hub of western China. The municipality covers an area the size of a small province, with a population of roughly 32 million, while the dense urban core is famous for its steep, multi-level streetscapes built into the mountainsides.

History

The region was the heartland of the ancient Ba kingdom. The city received the name Chongqing ("redoubled celebration") in the Southern Song dynasty. It opened to foreign trade in the late 19th century as a treaty port. During the Second Sino-Japanese War, Chongqing served as the wartime capital of the Republic of China (1937–1946), enduring heavy Japanese bombing raids. After 1949 it was administered as part of Sichuan province until 1997, when it was separated to become a direct-administered municipality, in part to manage resettlement and development connected with the Three Gorges Dam project.

Geography and climate

Chongqing's urban area occupies a hilly peninsula between the Yangtze and Jialing rivers. Its subtropical monsoon climate brings hot, humid summers — the city is traditionally counted among China's "furnaces" — and mild, foggy winters; it is nicknamed the "fog capital." The surrounding municipality includes farmland, mountains, and the western stretch of the Three Gorges.

Economy and transport

Historically a centre of steel, chemicals, and machinery, Chongqing has become one of the world's largest producers of laptop computers and a major base for automobiles and motorcycles. It is a leading inland port on the Yangtze, a terminus of China–Europe freight railways, and home to one of China's busiest airports. Its urban rail transit system is noted for monorail lines threaded through buildings and over steep terrain.

Culture

Chongqing is the home of Sichuan-style hot pot, celebrated for its numbing-spicy broth of Sichuan pepper and chillies. Landmarks include the Hongya Cave complex, the Dazu Rock Carvings (a UNESCO World Heritage Site at the municipality's edge), the Yangtze River Cableway, and the Liberation Monument (Jiefangbei) in the central business district.
