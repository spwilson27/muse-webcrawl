Cannon Air Force Base

Cannon Air Force Base is a United States Air Force base located approximately seven miles southwest of Clovis, New Mexico. It is home to the 27th Special Operations Wing of Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC), whose aircrews fly the AC-130J Ghostrider gunship, MC-130J Commando II, CV-22 Osprey, and other special operations aircraft.

History

The airfield began in 1942 as a wartime bomber training base known as Clovis Army Airfield. After periods of inactivation and reactivation, it was named in 1957 for General John K. Cannon, a former commander of U.S. air forces in Europe. For most of the Cold War and after, Cannon hosted fighter wings, culminating in the 27th Fighter Wing's operation of the F-111, including the EF-111A Raven, and later the F-16 Fighting Falcon.

The 2005 Base Realignment and Closure round initially recommended closing Cannon, but the base was reprieved and reassigned to Air Force Special Operations Command. On October 1, 2007, the 27th Special Operations Wing was activated at Cannon, and the base grew substantially as AFSOC squadrons moved in.

Units and aircraft

The 27th Special Operations Wing operates the AC-130J Ghostrider, MC-130J Commando II, CV-22B Osprey, U-28A Draco, and remotely piloted aircraft including the MQ-9 Reaper flown from associated facilities. The Melrose Air Force Range, about 25 miles southwest, provides training airspace and bombing ranges administered by the base.
