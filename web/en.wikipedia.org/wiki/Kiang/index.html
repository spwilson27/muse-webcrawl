Kiang

The kiang (Equus kiang) is the largest of the wild asses. Native to the Tibetan Plateau and surrounding high country of Ladakh, Nepal and Sikkim, it inhabits alpine grasslands and steppe at elevations of roughly 2,700 to 5,400 metres.

Description

Kiangs stand about 140 cm at the shoulder and have a rich reddish-brown coat in summer, darkening in winter, with white underparts and a dark dorsal stripe. They are hardy grazers, feeding on grasses and sedges, and can travel long distances between water and grazing grounds. Social groups include harems led by a stallion, bachelor groups and larger seasonal herds.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern overall, with the largest populations in China, though numbers in India are much smaller. Threats include competition with livestock, fencing of rangelands and poaching in some areas. The kiang is closely related to the Asiatic wild ass (onager/kulan group) and to the African wild ass, ancestor of the donkey.
