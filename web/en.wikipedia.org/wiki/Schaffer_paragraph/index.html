Schaffer paragraph

Structure of a Schaffer paragraph

The Schaffer paragraph is a method of structured paragraph writing developed by Jane Schaffer, a high-school English teacher from San Diego, California. It is widely taught in American middle and high schools as a scaffold for analytical and expository writing.

Structure

A basic Schaffer body paragraph consists of:

  • Topic sentence (TS) — states the main idea of the paragraph.
  • Concrete detail (CD) — evidence: a fact, quotation, or example supporting the topic sentence.
  • Commentary (CM) — analysis explaining the significance of the concrete detail (usually two sentences).
  • Concrete detail (CD) — a second piece of evidence.
  • Commentary (CM) — further analysis (usually two sentences).
  • Concluding sentence (CS) — wraps up the paragraph's argument.

The method prescribes a ratio of commentary to concrete detail (commonly 2:1) to encourage students to analyse rather than merely summarise.

Use and criticism

Supporters value the model for giving novice writers a clear, repeatable structure. Critics argue that its formula can become restrictive if treated as an end in itself rather than as a temporary scaffold toward more flexible writing.
