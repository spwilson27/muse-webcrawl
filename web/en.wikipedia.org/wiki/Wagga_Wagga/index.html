Wagga Wagga (informally called Wagga) is a major regional city in the Riverina region of New South Wales, Australia. Straddling the Murrumbidgee River, with part of the city on the south bank and the suburbs of North Wagga and East Wagga on the north, it is the state's largest inland city and an important agricultural, military and transport hub for the surrounding region.

The name comes from the Wiradjuri language of the region's traditional owners and is generally taken to mean "the place of many crows", wagga being a word for crow and the repetition indicating plurality. The city was proclaimed a municipality in 1870 and a city in 1946.

History and economy

European exploration of the area followed Charles Sturt's expedition down the Murrumbidgee in 1829–30, and pastoral settlement began in the 1830s. The city grew as a crossing point on the stock routes and, from 1878, as a railhead on the Main Southern railway.

Wagga is home to a campus of Charles Sturt University, the Kapooka Army Recruit Training Centre, and RAAF Base Wagga. The surrounding district produces wheat, canola, livestock and wine grapes, and the city markets itself as the "City of Good Sports", having produced many prominent Australian sportspeople.
