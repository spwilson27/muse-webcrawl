Mingun

Mingun is a town in Sagaing Region, northwestern Myanmar (Burma), located on the western bank of the Irrawaddy River, about 11 km upriver from Mandalay. It is famous for several monumental, unfinished projects of King Bodawpaya (reigned 1782–1819) of the Konbaung dynasty.

Sights

The Mingun Pahtodawgyi is an enormous incomplete stupa begun in 1790. Had it been finished to its intended height of about 150 metres, it would have been among the largest stupas in the world; work stopped at roughly one third of that height, and the great brick mass was cracked by an earthquake in 1839. Beside it, the Mingun Bell, cast in 1808–1810, weighs about 90 tonnes and was the world's largest functioning bell until surpassed in 2000. The white Hsinbyume Pagoda (1816), built by Prince Bagyidaw in memory of his wife, models the mythical Mount Meru with seven wavy terraces. The completed Settawya Pagoda houses a marble footprint of the Buddha. Mingun is today a popular excursion from Mandalay, usually reached by boat up the Irrawaddy.
