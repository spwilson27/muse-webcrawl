Epiclesis

The epiclesis (also spelled epiklesis; from Greek ἐπίκλησις, "invocation") is the part of the Eucharistic prayer (anaphora) in which the priest invokes the Holy Spirit upon the bread and wine, asking that they be changed into the Body and Blood of Christ. It is a central element of Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Eastern Catholic liturgies, and appears in adapted form in many Western liturgical traditions.

In Eastern liturgies

In the Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom, after the institution narrative the priest prays: "Again we offer You this spiritual and unbloody worship, and we ask, pray, and beseech You: send down Your Holy Spirit upon us and upon these gifts here offered... And make this bread the precious Body of Your Christ... and that which is in this cup, the precious Blood of Your Christ... changing them by Your Holy Spirit." The people respond with a triple "Amen".

Theological significance

Eastern theology generally locates the consecration in the whole anaphora, with the epiclesis as its climax, whereas medieval Western theology emphasised the words of institution. This difference was a notable point of East–West controversy, though modern ecumenical dialogue has softened the contrast. The Roman Missal's newer Eucharistic prayers (II–IV, composed after the Second Vatican Council) include an epiclesis before the institution narrative, invoking the Spirit upon the gifts, and a second invocation for the unity of the communicants.
