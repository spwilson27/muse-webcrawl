London Season

The London Season was, historically, the period of the year when the British social elite resided in London and participated in a round of debutante balls, dinners, dances and major social and sporting events. At its height in the 19th and early 20th centuries, the Season ran roughly from after Easter until mid-August ("the Glorious Twelfth", when grouse shooting began and Parliament adjourned), when families withdrew to their country estates.

Origins and rituals

The Season coalesced around the parliamentary calendar: landowners in London for the sitting of Parliament brought their families, and the Season became a marriage market in which young women — debutantes — were formally "presented" at court, wearing prescribed white dresses and plumes, and then launched into society at balls and parties. Presentation at court was abolished by Queen Elizabeth II in 1958, a turning point after which the traditional Season declined.

The modern Season

Today "the Season" denotes the calendar of major annual social and sporting events from spring to late summer attended by the British social and sporting calendar's followers: the Chelsea Flower Show, Royal Ascot, Wimbledon, Henley Royal Regatta, the Epsom Derby, Cowes Week, the Royal Academy Summer Exhibition, Glyndebourne, the Proms and the Henley Festival. Queen Charlotte's Ball, first held in 1780, survives as the centrepiece of the modern debutante season.
