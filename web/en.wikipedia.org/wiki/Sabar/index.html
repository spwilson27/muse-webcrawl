Sabar

Sabar
OriginSenegal (Wolof people)
ClassificationMembranophone (goblet/hand drum)
RelatedMbalax music; tama (talking drum)

The sabar is a traditional drum of the Wolof people of Senegal, and by extension the family of rhythms and dances performed with it. A set of sabar drums of graduated sizes is played with one hand and a thin stick, producing the explosive rhythms that underpin Senegalese mbalax popular music.

Sabar drummers performing at a celebration in Senegal.

Instruments and performance

A sabar ensemble typically includes several drum types — including the nder (lead drum), the gorong talmbat, the mbëng-mbëng and the tungune — each carved from a single piece of wood with a goatskin head. Performances accompany dances at wrestling matches, weddings, baptisms and the women's dance gatherings called tanibers. Specific rhythms and dance steps are associated with particular families, events and even individuals.

Cultural role

Sabar rhythms were carried into modern popular music by musicians such as Youssou N'Dour, whose mbalax style fuses sabar drumming with Western instruments. The sabar tradition is transmitted through griot families and remains a living part of Senegalese social life.
