Music of Brazil

The music of Brazil encompasses various regional musical styles influenced by European, African and Amerindian forms. Brazil is renowned for genres such as samba, bossa nova, choro, forró, frevo, maracatu, MPB (música popular brasileira) and sertanejo.

Origins

Brazilian music developed from the encounter of Portuguese colonial culture, the music of enslaved Africans, and Indigenous traditions. Religious festivals, work songs, and dance forms such as the lundu and maxixe laid the groundwork for the urban popular music of Rio de Janeiro in the late nineteenth century.

Samba

Samba emerged in Afro-Brazilian communities in Rio de Janeiro in the early twentieth century. "Pelo Telefone" (1917), credited to Donga, is usually regarded as the first recorded samba. Samba schools, founded from the late 1920s, organise the great carnival parades of the Sambadrome, and samba de enredo, pagode, and partido-alto are among its many subgenres.

Bossa nova and MPB

Bossa nova, developed in the late 1950s by João Gilberto, Antônio Carlos Jobim and Vinícius de Moraes, fused samba rhythms with jazz harmony; "The Girl from Ipanema" became a worldwide standard. In the later 1960s the festival movement and Tropicália — led by Caetano Veloso and Gilberto Gil — reshaped Brazilian popular music, which came to be marketed broadly as MPB.

Regional and contemporary styles

The Northeast is home to forró, baião (popularised by Luiz Gonzaga), frevo and maracatu; the South and interior to sertanejo; Bahia to axé and samba-reggae. Later waves include Brazilian rock, funk carioca from Rio's favelas, and globally successful pop artists such as Anitta.
