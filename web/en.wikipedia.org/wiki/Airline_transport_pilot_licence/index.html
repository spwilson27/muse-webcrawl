Airline transport pilot licence

The airline transport pilot licence (ATPL), known in the United States as the airline transport pilot (ATP) certificate, is the highest level of aircraft pilot licence. It permits the holder to act as pilot in command of aircraft operated for commercial air transport — in practice, to captain airliners.

Requirements

Under ICAO standards an ATPL applicant must be at least 21 years old, hold a commercial pilot licence and an instrument rating, and have accumulated at least 1,500 hours of flight time, including specified amounts of cross-country, night, and instrument experience. Applicants must pass demanding written examinations in air law, aircraft systems, meteorology, navigation, and human performance, plus a flight test.

Variants

In EASA countries a licence remains "frozen" until the flight-experience requirements are met, allowing graduates of integrated training courses to work as first officers while accumulating hours. In the United States, since 2013, first officers at Part 121 airlines must also hold an ATP or restricted ATP certificate, a rule introduced after the Colgan Air Flight 3407 accident.
