Krav Maga (Hebrew: קרב מגע, "contact combat") is an Israeli martial art developed for the Israel Defense Forces (IDF). It is derived from a combination of techniques used in aikido, boxing, judo, karate, and wrestling. It is known for its focus on real-world situations, direct movements, and efficient counter-attacks.

History

Krav Maga was developed by Imi Lichtenfeld, a Hungarian-born Israeli martial artist who used his training as a boxer and wrestler to defend Jewish neighbourhoods against fascist groups in Bratislava during the 1930s. After immigrating to Mandatory Palestine, he taught hand-to-hand combat to the Haganah paramilitary organisation. Following the establishment of Israel in 1948, Lichtenfeld became chief instructor of physical fitness and Krav Maga at the IDF School of Combat Fitness. After his retirement from the military, he adapted the system for civilian use, and it later spread worldwide through schools and federations.

Basic principles

Krav Maga has a philosophy emphasising aggression and simultaneous defensive and offensive manoeuvres. Its core principles include: threat neutralisation; an emphasis on pre-emptive and retaliatory strikes; striking to vulnerable areas of the body such as the eyes, throat, and groin; maintaining awareness of surroundings; and using nearby objects as weapons or shields. Unlike many competitive martial arts, Krav Maga has no sporting rules, no weight classes, and no set uniforms; training commonly simulates realistic scenarios such as multiple attackers, confined spaces, and armed assailants.

Techniques

Techniques are drawn from several disciplines: punches, kicks, knees, and elbows from boxing and karate; throws and takedowns from judo and wrestling; ground fighting; and defences against chokes, bear hugs, and attacks with sticks, knives, and firearms. Curricula are commonly organised by belt or "patch" levels running from Practitioner levels P1–P5 through Graduate (G1–G5) to Expert (E1–E5).

Use by security forces

Krav Maga remains the official self-defence system of the IDF and is taught to military and law-enforcement units in many countries, as well as in civilian schools on every continent.
