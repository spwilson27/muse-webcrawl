Tendring District

County: Essex, England
Admin HQ: Clacton-on-Sea
Created: 1 April 1974
Population: approx. 145,000

Tendring is a local government district in north-east Essex, England. It extends from the River Stour in the north, across the peninsula formed by the coast, to the River Colne in the south, and includes the seaside resorts of Clacton-on-Sea (the largest town and administrative centre), Frinton-on-Sea, Walton-on-the-Naze, Brightlingsea and Dovercourt, the port town of Harwich, and the town of Manningtree, which claims to be England's smallest town.

History

The district was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, merging the borough of Harwich with several urban and rural districts. It takes its name from the historic Tendring Hundred and the village of Tendring at its centre. The area was formerly written as "Tendering" in some sources; the name is often loosely associated with a Saxon settler called Tendra or a similar form.

The Tendring peninsula is bounded by the estuaries of the Stour and Colne and by the North Sea. Harwich, at the mouth of the Stour, has a long maritime history as a packet-boat and ferry port — it was the home port of the Mayflower's captain Christopher Jones — and today handles ferry services to the Hook of Holland.

Governance and economy

Tendring District Council provides district-level services and elects 48 councillors; Essex County Council provides county services. The economy is strongly influenced by tourism at the traditional resorts, by the ports of Harwich and Brightlingsea, and by a high proportion of retired residents — the Jaywick area near Clacton has repeatedly been ranked among the most deprived neighbourhoods in England. The district's coastline includes the Naze, a headland of geological importance noted for fossils and for the 1720 Hanoverian Naze Tower.
