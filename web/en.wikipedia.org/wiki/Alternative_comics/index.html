Alternative comics

Alternative comics
Origin: United States, 1970s–1980s
Roots: Underground comix
Publishers: Fantagraphics, Drawn & Quarterly, Top Shelf
Genres: Autobiography, literary fiction, avant-garde

Alternative comics or independent comics are a branch of the comics medium that grew out of the underground comix movement of the 1960s and 1970s, covering a wide range of genres, styles and subject matter outside the superhero-dominated mainstream of publishers such as Marvel and DC.

History

As underground comix waned in the mid-1970s, creators sought new outlets. Raw (1980–1991), edited by Art Spiegelman and Françoise Mouly, published avant-garde and international work and serialised Spiegelman's Maus. Dave Sim's self-published Cerebus (from 1977) proved long-form independent series viable. Publishers Fantagraphics Books (Love and Rockets by the Hernandez brothers, Eightball by Daniel Clowes) and later Drawn & Quarterly, Top Shelf and Pantheon built the alternative sector, distributed through the direct market of comics shops and increasingly through bookstores.

Characteristics

Alternative comics favour creator ownership, autobiography and literary or journalistic subjects, diverse art styles, and formats from minicomics and anthology magazines to the graphic novel. Notable creators include Chris Ware (Jimmy Corrigan), Joe Sacco (comics journalism), Marjane Satrapi (Persepolis), Alison Bechdel (Fun Home) and Adrian Tomine.

Distinction from indie and underground

"Alternative" generally denotes the post-underground, bookstore-oriented wave, distinct from both the countercultural underground comix and from genre "independent" comics that imitate mainstream styles.
