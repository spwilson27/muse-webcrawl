Debye model

In thermodynamics and solid-state physics, the Debye model, proposed by Peter Debye in 1912, is a method for calculating the heat capacity of a solid from its lattice vibrations (phonons).

Unlike the earlier Einstein model, which treated atoms as independent oscillators, the Debye model treats vibrations as collective modes with a maximum frequency, the Debye frequency. It correctly predicts the cubic (T³) dependence of heat capacity at low temperatures and approaches the Dulong–Petit law at high temperatures.

Debye temperature

Each material is characterised by a Debye temperature θD; lead, for example, has a low Debye temperature of about 105 K, while diamond's exceeds 2,000 K.
