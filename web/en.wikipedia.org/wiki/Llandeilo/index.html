Llandeilo
Country: Wales
Principal area: Carmarthenshire
River: Tywi
Population: about 1,800

Llandeilo is a market town and community in Carmarthenshire, Wales, situated on the River Tywi at the western edge of the Brecon Beacons National Park. It lies on the A483 road between Swansea and Llandovery and is served by a station on the Heart of Wales line.

History

The town takes its name from Saint Teilo, a 6th-century Welsh saint, to whom the parish church is dedicated. Nearby stood Dinefwr Castle, a stronghold of the princes of Deheubarth, including Rhys ap Gruffydd (the Lord Rhys). Llandeilo developed as a market town and coaching stop, and its prosperity in the Georgian period is reflected in the elegant terraces along its main streets. The single-arched stone bridge over the Tywi was completed in 1848, replacing earlier bridges swept away by floods.

Geology

Llandeilo gives its name to the Llandeilo Group and the Llandeilan age of the Ordovician period, the local flagstones having been studied in the pioneering geology of Roderick Murchison.

Local attractions

Nearby sights include the National Botanic Garden of Wales at Llanarthne, the ruined Carreg Cennen Castle, Aberglasney Gardens and Newton House at Dinefwr Park. The town is known for its independent shops, antique dealers and cafés.
