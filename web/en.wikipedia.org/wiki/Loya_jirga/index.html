A loya jirga (Pashto: لویه جرګه, "grand assembly") is a special type of jirga, or assembly, that has traditionally been held in Afghanistan to choose a new head of state, approve a new constitution, or decide other matters of overriding national importance. Participants are typically tribal elders, religious leaders, and representatives of communities from across the country, and decisions are reached by consensus rather than by vote.

Etymology

The word jirga is of Pashto origin and denotes a council or assembly; loya means "great" or "grand". The institution has roots in the consultative traditions of Pashtun tribal society, where the jirga served as the principal mechanism of collective decision-making and dispute resolution.

History

Loya jirgas have been convened at pivotal moments in Afghan history. In 1747, a loya jirga at Kandahar selected Ahmad Shah Durrani as ruler, an event often regarded as the founding of the modern Afghan state. A loya jirga in 1919 endorsed Amanullah Khan's claim to full independence from British influence over foreign affairs. In 1964, a loya jirga approved a new constitution under King Zahir Shah that introduced significant political reforms.

Following the fall of the Taliban in 2001, an emergency loya jirga was held in Kabul in June 2002, electing Hamid Karzai as head of the transitional administration. A constitutional loya jirga in late 2003 and early 2004 debated and ratified the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan. Consultative peace jirgas were held in 2019 and 2020, the latter voting on the release of Taliban prisoners as part of the peace process.

Procedure

Delegates are typically selected through local and regional processes, with seats allocated among provinces, tribes, and social groups, sometimes including reserved representation for women, refugees, and nomadic communities. Sessions may last days or weeks. The assembly deliberates until a consensus emerges; formal voting is comparatively rare, and the legitimacy of the outcome rests on the perception of broad agreement.
