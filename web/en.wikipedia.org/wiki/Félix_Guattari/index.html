Félix Guattari

Pierre-Félix Guattari (30 March 1930 – 29 August 1992) was a French philosopher, political activist and psychoanalyst. He is best known for his long collaboration with Gilles Deleuze, with whom he wrote Anti-Oedipus (1972) and A Thousand Plateaus (1980), the two volumes of Capitalism and Schizophrenia. Guattari spent much of his working life at the experimental La Borde clinic, where he developed practices of institutional psychotherapy and the approach he called schizoanalysis.

Life and career

Guattari joined the La Borde clinic at Cour-Cheverny in 1955, shortly after its founding by Jean Oury, and remained associated with it for the rest of his life. Trained in the circle of Jacques Lacan, he later broke with Lacanian orthodoxy. He was active in far-left politics, opposing French colonial wars and supporting movements ranging from the Italian autonomists to anti-psychiatry networks. His meetings with Deleuze beginning in 1969 produced one of the most famous intellectual partnerships of postwar French thought.

Work

In Anti-Oedipus, Deleuze and Guattari attacked orthodox psychoanalysis, arguing that desire is productive rather than grounded in lack, and that capitalism both decodes social flows and reterritorializes them. Guattari's independent works include Molecular Revolution (1977), The Three Ecologies (1989), which extended ecology to social relations and human subjectivity, and Chaosmosis (1992). His concepts of ecosophy, transversality and the refrain have been influential in philosophy, art theory and political activism.

Guattari died of a heart attack at the La Borde clinic in 1992.
