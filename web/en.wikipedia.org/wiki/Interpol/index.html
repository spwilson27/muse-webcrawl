Interpol

International Criminal Police Organization
Common name: Interpol
Formed: 1923
Headquarters: Lyon, France
Member countries: 196
Motto: "Connecting police for a safer world"

The International Criminal Police Organization, commonly known as Interpol, is an intergovernmental organisation that facilitates worldwide police cooperation and crime control. Founded in 1923 as the International Criminal Police Commission, it is headquartered in Lyon, France, and counts 196 member countries, making it one of the world's largest international organisations.

Role and functions

Interpol is not a supranational police force: it has no agents with powers of arrest. Instead it provides a secure global communications network (I-24/7), databases on stolen documents, fingerprints, DNA profiles, stolen vehicles and works of art, and a system of colour-coded notices. The best known is the Red Notice, a request to law enforcement worldwide to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition. Member states coordinate work through National Central Bureaus staffed by their own police officers.

History

The organisation grew out of the First International Criminal Police Congress held in Monaco in 1914 and was formally established in Vienna in 1923. After the Second World War it was reconstituted, adopted the name Interpol (its telegraphic address) in 1956, and moved its headquarters to France. Its constitution forbids it from undertaking interventions of a political, military, religious or racial character.

Criticism

Interpol has faced criticism over alleged abuse of the Red Notice system by some governments to pursue political opponents, journalists and businesspeople, leading to reforms in its review processes and the creation of a strengthened Commission for the Control of Interpol's Files.
