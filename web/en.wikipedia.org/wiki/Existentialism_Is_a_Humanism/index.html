Existentialism Is a Humanism (French: L'existentialisme est un humanisme) is a 1946 work by the philosopher Jean-Paul Sartre, based on a lecture by the same name he gave at Club Maintenant in Paris on 29 October 1945. In early editions, the lecture was published in English as Existentialism; the title was later changed when a new translation appeared in 2007.

Content

In the lecture, Sartre set out to defend existentialism against criticisms from Communists and Christians, and to explain its central doctrines in accessible terms. He famously declared that "existence precedes essence": human beings have no predetermined nature, but instead define themselves through their choices and actions. He argued that man is "condemned to be free", that each person bears complete responsibility for what they make of themselves, and that in choosing for oneself one chooses an image of man for all humanity.

Sartre distinguished his atheistic existentialism from religious varieties associated with thinkers such as Karl Jaspers and Gabriel Marcel, and addressed concepts including anguish, abandonment, despair, and bad faith.

Reception

The lecture and its published version became the most widely read introduction to existentialism and greatly increased Sartre's public profile. However, Sartre later expressed reservations about the work, regarding it as an oversimplification of the philosophy set out in his 1943 treatise Being and Nothingness. Martin Heidegger responded critically to Sartre's use of the term "humanism" in his Letter on Humanism (1947).
