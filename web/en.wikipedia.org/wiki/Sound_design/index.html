Sound design

Sound design is the art and practice of creating soundtracks for a variety of needs. It involves specifying, acquiring or creating auditory elements using audio production techniques and tools. It is employed in a variety of disciplines including filmmaking, television production, video game development, theatre, live performance, sound art, post-production, radio, new media and musical instrument development.

History

The use of sound to evoke emotion and reflect mood has a long history in theatre and radio drama, but the modern discipline of sound design emerged in cinema in the 1970s. Walter Murch, who coined the term in connection with his work on films such as Apocalypse Now (1979), treated the soundtrack as a compositional whole rather than a collection of effects. Ben Burtt's created sounds for Star Wars (1977) — such as the lightsaber's hum and R2-D2's voice — demonstrated how invented sounds could define characters and worlds.

Practice

A sound designer typically works with a director and supervising sound editor to build a film's or production's sonic palette: dialogue, ambience, hard effects, Foley (performed everyday sounds), and designed effects created from recorded material and synthesis. In video games, sound designers create interactive audio that responds dynamically to gameplay. Sound designers commonly work with digital audio workstations, field recording equipment, synthesisers and extensive sound effects libraries.

Recognition

Sound design in film is recognised by the Academy Award for Best Sound (and formerly Best Sound Editing), the BAFTA Award for Best Sound, and Tony Awards for sound design in theatre, introduced in 2008.
