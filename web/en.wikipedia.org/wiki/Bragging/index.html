Boasting

[Image: A speaker exaggeratedly praising his own achievements]

Boasting or bragging is speaking with excessive pride and self-satisfaction about one's achievements, possessions, or abilities. Boasting occurs when someone feels a sense of satisfaction or when someone feels that whatever occurred proves their superiority and is recounting accomplishments so that others will feel admiration or envy.

Individuals construct an image of themselves, a personal identity, and present themselves in a manner that is consistent with that image. Research on self-presentation distinguishes self-promotion — presenting one's abilities favourably — from more exaggerated forms of boasting that audiences tend to discount. Words such as hubris, vainglory, and braggadocio denote related concepts, and the stock braggart soldier (the miles gloriosus) has been a comic figure since Roman theatre.

Cultural and social aspects

Norms governing boasting vary by culture and context. Many societies sanction it through proverbs against self-praise and through religious teachings that treat pride as a vice; the Book of Proverbs and the Epistle of James, among other texts, warn against boastfulness. At the same time, some settings — competitive sport, sales, political campaigning, and job interviews — reward confident self-promotion, and psychologists note a persistent tension for individuals between gaining status through displayed competence and maintaining likeability through modesty.

Humblebragging

The term humblebrag, coined by comedian and writer Harris Wittels in 2010, describes a boast disguised as a self-deprecating or complaining remark, particularly on social media. Studies suggest observers often judge humblebragging as less sincere and less likeable than straightforward boasting.
