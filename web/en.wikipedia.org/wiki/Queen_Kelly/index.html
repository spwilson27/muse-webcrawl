Queen Kelly is a 1929 American silent drama film directed by Erich von Stroheim and starring Gloria Swanson. Financed in part by Joseph P. Kennedy and produced through Swanson's own company, it is one of the most famous unfinished films in cinema history: production was shut down before the concluding reels were shot, after the story's planned second half—set largely in German East Africa—proved too controversial and expensive.

Plot

In the fictional Central European kingdom of Cobourg-Nassau, the dissolute Crown Prince Wolfram is betrothed to the mad Queen Regina V, though he dreads the marriage. Riding with his regiment, Wolfram encounters a procession of convent girls; one of them, the Irish orphan Kitty Kelly, catches his eye when her undergarments slip in view of the soldiers. Wolfram engineers a fire at the convent to meet her again, and the two fall in love. When the Queen discovers them together, she drives Kelly from the palace with a whip. Despondent, Kelly attempts to drown herself but is rescued and sent to relatives in Dar es Salaam, where the story was to continue with her inheriting a brothel—a conclusion never filmed as planned.

Production

Swanson, then one of the silent era's biggest stars, hired von Stroheim after the success of their earlier collaboration The Wedding March (1928). Von Stroheim's famously extravagant methods—lavish sets, huge quantities of footage, and an insistence on period detail—pushed costs upward. As the talkie revolution arrived, producer Joseph P. Kennedy lost confidence in a silent production whose African brothel sequences raised censorship concerns, and filming was halted in early 1929.

Release and versions

The footage shot in Europe was edited into a shortened version released in Europe and South America in 1932, with an ending hastily assembled from the existing material in which Kelly dies. Swanson retained rights to the film and refused for decades to allow screenings in the United States. In the 1980s a restoration by Kino International, incorporating stills and a reconstruction of the intended African sequences, brought the film back into circulation, and it is now regarded as a major, if fragmentary, work of late silent cinema. Footage from Queen Kelly also appears in Billy Wilder's Sunset Boulevard (1950), in which Swanson plays the faded silent star Norma Desmond watching her own youthful triumph.

Legacy

Though incomplete, Queen Kelly is frequently cited in histories of silent film as the project that ended von Stroheim's directorial career in Hollywood and as a cautionary tale about auteur excess and studio finances at the transition to sound.
