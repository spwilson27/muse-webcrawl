1221 Amor, the namesake of the Amor asteroid group

1221 Amor is a stony near-Earth asteroid and the prototype of the Amor group of asteroids. It was discovered on 12 March 1932 by Belgian astronomer Eugène Delporte at the Royal Observatory of Belgium in Uccle. The asteroid measures approximately 1.5 kilometres in diameter and gave its name to an entire dynamical class of Earth-approaching asteroids.

Discovery and naming

Amor was discovered during a photographic survey at Uccle in 1932. It was named after Amor, also known as Cupid, the Roman god of love. The name was proposed in keeping with the tradition of naming near-Earth asteroids after mythological figures.

Orbit and classification

Amor orbits the Sun at a distance of roughly 1.09–2.75 AU once every 2 years and 8 months. Its perihelion lies just outside Earth's orbit, which defines the Amor group: asteroids whose orbits approach Earth from outside, with perihelia between 1.017 and 1.3 AU, but which do not cross Earth's orbit. During its discovery apparition, Amor made a notably close approach to Earth, drawing attention to the population of Earth-approaching bodies.

Physical characteristics

Amor is classified as an S-type (stony) asteroid. Radar and photometric observations indicate an elongated shape with a rotation period of roughly 92 hours, making it a slow rotator. Its albedo is consistent with a silicate-rich surface.
