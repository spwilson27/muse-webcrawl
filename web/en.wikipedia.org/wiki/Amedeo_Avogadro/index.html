Amedeo Avogadro

Born: 9 August 1776, Turin

Lorenzo Romano Amedeo Carlo Avogadro, Count of Quaregna and Cerreto (1776–1856), was an Italian scientist best known for Avogadro's hypothesis (1811): equal volumes of gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules.

The hypothesis resolved the confusion between atoms and molecules and let chemists fix atomic weights consistently — but it was ignored for nearly fifty years until Stanislao Cannizzaro championed it at the Karlsruhe Congress of 1860. The number of entities in a mole, the Avogadro constant, was named in his honour by Jean Perrin in 1909.

[Image: Portrait of Amedeo Avogadro]

Life

Trained in law, Avogadro turned to science and held the chair of mathematical physics at Turin. He published little-known papers in French journals, which contributed to his work being overlooked during his lifetime.

The constant

The Avogadro constant, 6.02214076×10²³ mol⁻¹, has been an exactly defined SI constant since 2019; Perrin's Brownian-motion experiments helped establish its value and the reality of molecules.
