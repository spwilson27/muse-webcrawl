LGBT rights at the United Nations

Discussions of LGBT rights at the United Nations have included resolutions and joint statements in the General Assembly and the UN Human Rights Council, attention by UN treaty bodies, and work by the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights and other agencies. Progress has been contested: a bloc of supportive states, largely from Europe and the Americas, has advanced statements and resolutions against violence and discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity, while many states in Africa, Asia and the Middle East, plus the Holy See and others, have opposed them on religious, cultural or sovereignty grounds.

Milestones

The UN Human Rights Committee's 1994 decision in Toonen v. Australia held that laws criminalizing homosexual conduct violated the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. NGOs gained a foothold when ILGA, after expulsion in the 1990s, eventually secured ECOSOC consultative status (2011), alongside other LGBT organizations. In 2008, a statement on human rights, sexual orientation and gender identity was read in the General Assembly on behalf of 66 states; an opposing statement was backed by 57. In June 2011, the Human Rights Council adopted resolution 17/19 — the first UN resolution specifically on sexual orientation and gender identity — requesting a study of discriminatory laws and violence worldwide, published later that year. A stronger resolution followed in 2014.

Independent Expert and campaigns

In 2016 the Human Rights Council created the mandate of Independent Expert on protection against violence and discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity, first held by Vitit Muntarbhorn and from 2018 by Victor Madrigal-Borloz; attempts in the General Assembly to block the mandate failed. The OHCHR runs the UN Free & Equal campaign for LGBT equality, and Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon and his successor António Guterres have publicly supported LGBT rights, extending staff benefits to same-sex partners of UN employees. Resolutions renewing the Independent Expert mandate passed again in 2019 and 2022, with gradually shifting voting patterns.
