Geometric topology

Knots and 3-manifolds are central objects

In mathematics, geometric topology is the study of manifolds and the maps between them, particularly embeddings of one manifold into another. It focuses on low-dimensional manifolds — surfaces, 3-manifolds and 4-manifolds — where geometric and combinatorial methods are especially powerful.

Topics

Central topics include knot theory, the classification of 3-manifolds, handle decompositions, Heegaard splittings, and the topology of 4-manifolds, where exotic smooth structures make dimension four exceptional. Geometric topology differs from algebraic topology in its emphasis on direct geometric constructions, though the two fields interact closely.

Geometrisation

The field was transformed by William Thurston's geometrisation conjecture (1982), which asserts that every closed 3-manifold decomposes into pieces each carrying one of eight model geometries. Grigori Perelman's proof (2002–2003), using Richard Hamilton's Ricci flow, also resolved the century-old Poincaré conjecture as a special case.

Low versus high dimensions

In dimensions five and above, surgery theory and the h-cobordism theorem make manifold classification comparatively tractable, whereas dimensions three and four — the dimensions of physical space and spacetime — display the richest and most difficult phenomena, including exotic ℝ⁴s.
