Quality planning

Quality planning is the process of identifying which quality standards are relevant to a project, product or service and determining how to satisfy them. It is one of the foundational processes of quality management: in Joseph Juran's quality trilogy, quality planning stands alongside quality control and quality improvement as one of the three universal managerial processes for managing quality.

Juran's quality planning road map links customer needs to process design.

Juran's road map

Juran described quality planning as a universal sequence of steps: identify the customers; determine their needs; translate those needs into the organisation's language; develop product features that respond to the needs; develop processes able to produce those features; and transfer the plans to the operating forces. Failure to plan adequately — building products around internal assumptions rather than customer needs — was, in Juran's analysis, the source of most chronic quality problems.

In project and product management

In project management frameworks such as the PMBOK Guide, "Plan Quality Management" is the process of defining quality requirements and documenting how the project will demonstrate compliance. In automotive and manufacturing supply chains, Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) is a structured method of defining and establishing the steps necessary to ensure a product satisfies the customer, providing a framework for design reviews, failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA), control plans and production part approval.

Outputs

Typical outputs of quality planning include a quality management plan, quality metrics, checklists, process improvement plans and updated requirements documentation. The quality plan may draw on cost–benefit analysis, benchmarking, design of experiments and quality function deployment (the "house of quality").
