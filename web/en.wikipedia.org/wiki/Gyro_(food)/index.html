Gyro (food)

Gyro
TypeMeat dish, sandwich
Place of originGreece
Main ingredientsMeat (pork, chicken, lamb), pita, tomato, onion, tzatziki

A gyro (Greek: γύρος, lit. 'turn') is a Greek dish of meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie, sliced, and served in a pita wrap with tomato, onion, fries and tzatziki sauce. In Greece the meat is most often pork or chicken, while lamb or beef mixtures are common in the United States.

History

Grilling a vertical stack of meat slices and cutting it off as it cooks was developed in Bursa in the 19th-century Ottoman Empire as döner kebab. Greek refugees brought the method to Greece after 1922, where it evolved into gyros, with local seasonings and accompaniments.

Preparation

Seasoned meat is stacked on a tall vertical spit rotating in front of a heat source. As the outer layer crisps, it is shaved off in thin slices. The meat is wrapped in an oiled, lightly grilled pita.

Related dishes

See souvlaki, döner kebab, shawarma and tacos al pastor, all descended from vertical-spit roasting.
