Varanasi (also known as Benares or Banaras, and in ancient times as Kashi) is a city on the Ganges river in northern India that is regarded as the spiritual capital of India and one of the holiest cities in Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with a history of settlement extending back some 3,000 years.

Religious significance

Hindus believe that dying in Varanasi, or being cremated on its riverfront steps (ghats), grants moksha—liberation from the cycle of rebirth. The city is sacred to Shiva, who is said to have founded it, and its most famous temple is the Kashi Vishwanath Temple, one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines. Along the western bank of the Ganges stretch nearly a hundred ghats, including Dashashwamedh Ghat, site of the nightly Ganga Aarti fire ceremony, and Manikarnika Ghat, the principal cremation ground. Varanasi is also where the Buddha gave his first sermon, at nearby Sarnath, and it is a birthplace of two Jain Tirthankaras.

History and culture

Varanasi was a centre of learning, silk weaving, music and philosophy for centuries; the Banaras Hindu University (founded 1916) remains one of India's leading universities. The city is famed for Banarasi silk saris, its school of Hindustani classical music (the Benares gharana), and poets and writers such as Kabir and Tulsidas, who composed the Ramcharitmanas there. Mark Twain wrote that Benares "is older than history, older than tradition, older even than legend, and looks twice as old as all of them put together."

Economy and transport

Tourism and pilgrimage dominate the economy, alongside silk and brocade weaving, brassware and the manufacture of Banarasi paan and sweets. The city is served by Lal Bahadur Shastri International Airport and is a major rail junction in northern India.
