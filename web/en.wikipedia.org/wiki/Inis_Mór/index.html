Inis Mór

Inis Mór (Irish for "big island"; anglicised Inishmore) is the largest of the Aran Islands in Galway Bay, County Galway, Ireland. About 14 km long and 3.8 km wide, it has a population of roughly 800 and is the most visited of the islands, reached by ferry from Rossaveal and Doolin and by air from Inverin. The main settlement is Cill Rónáin (Kilronan).

Geography and landscape

The island is an extension of the limestone karst of the Burren, with bare pavements, thin soils, and a dense network of dry-stone walls enclosing small fields laboriously created with sand and seaweed. Irish is the everyday language of the island, which is part of the Gaeltacht.

Sights

The island's most famous monument is Dún Aonghasa, a semicircular prehistoric stone fort perched on a 100-metre cliff on the southern coast. Other sites include the Worm Hole (Poll na bPéist), a natural rectangular rock pool; the early Christian churches of Teampall Bheanáin, one of Ireland's smallest churches; and Dún Dúchathair, the Black Fort. The island featured in the 1934 film Man of Aran by Robert Flaherty and is associated with the playwright J. M. Synge, who learned Irish there.

Economy

Tourism is the main industry, together with fishing and the production of traditional Aran knitwear. Red Bull Cliff Diving events at the Worm Hole drew international attention.
