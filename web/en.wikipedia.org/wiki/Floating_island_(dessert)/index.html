Floating island (dessert)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A floating island (French: île flottante) is a dessert of French origin consisting of meringue floating on crème anglaise (a vanilla custard sauce). The meringue is prepared from whipped egg whites, sugar and vanilla extract and baked or poached in a bain-marie, then laid on a bed of the custard. The dish is sometimes topped with caramel sauce or praline, and variations are found throughout Europe under names such as œufs à la neige ("eggs in snow") and, in Central Europe, "bird's milk".

Preparation

The custard is made from egg yolks, milk, cream, sugar and vanilla, gently heated until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon. The meringue may be formed in a mould and baked, or spooned into quenelles and poached in milk or simmering water. The finished dessert is served chilled, with the meringue "island" resting on the sea of custard.

Related desserts

The Hungarian madártej ("bird's milk"), the Romanian lapte de pasăre, the Slovak vtáčie mlieko and the Polish zupa "nic" are closely related preparations in which dumplings of beaten egg white float in vanilla custard. The dish is distinct from the French pastry baba au rhum traditions but shares the classic French repertoire of egg-based desserts.
