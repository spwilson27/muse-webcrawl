E-Z notation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The E and Z configurations of a substituted alkene.

E–Z notation is the system recommended by IUPAC for describing the stereochemistry of double bonds in organic chemistry, most commonly the carbon–carbon double bonds of alkenes. It forms part of the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules and supersedes the older cis/trans terminology, which is unambiguous only when each carbon of the double bond bears one hydrogen or two identical reference groups.

Assignment

To assign a descriptor, the two substituents on each carbon of the double bond are ranked by the CIP sequence rules, which are based primarily on atomic number. If the two higher-priority groups lie on the same side of the double bond, the configuration is designated Z, from the German zusammen ("together"). If the two higher-priority groups lie on opposite sides, the configuration is E, from entgegen ("opposite").

The descriptor is written in italics, in parentheses, as a prefix to the name, as in (E)-but-2-ene. For molecules containing several double bonds, each is assigned separately, with locants where needed, e.g. (2E,4Z)-hexa-2,4-dienoic acid.

Relationship to cis/trans

The E/Z system does not always coincide with cis/trans: a double bond described as trans with respect to two specified groups may be Z if priority ordering places those groups differently. For tri- and tetrasubstituted alkenes, cis and trans have no clear meaning at all, whereas E and Z remain fully defined. The notation also applies to C=N bonds in imines and oximes, and to N=N bonds, using lone pairs as the lowest-priority "substituent".

Examples

Fumaric acid is the (E) isomer of butenedioic acid, while maleic acid is the (Z) isomer. In the drug tamoxifen, only the (Z) isomer has the desired anti-oestrogenic activity, illustrating the practical importance of the distinction.
