Television studio

A television studio is an installation designed for television production, in which video productions take place for recording or live broadcast. It is a purpose-built space — usually a large, acoustically treated, windowless floor with a high ceiling carrying a lighting grid — together with associated control and support rooms. Television studios are used for programmes such as news bulletins, talk shows, game shows, sitcoms recorded before a studio audience, and election coverage.

Layout and facilities

The main components of a television studio complex include:

  • The studio floor, where sets are built and performances take place, with multiple studio cameras on pedestals or handheld
  • The production control room (gallery), where the director, producer, and vision mixer select between camera feeds and graphics
  • The sound control room, where audio is mixed
  • Lighting and engineering areas, including the apparatus room housing video servers and technical infrastructure
  • Support spaces: green rooms, dressing rooms, makeup and wardrobe, scenery workshops, and storage

Studios are soundproofed and air-conditioned quietly to avoid audible background noise, and floors are flat and smooth so cameras can glide silently. Green screens and virtual sets allow computer-generated backgrounds to be composited in real time.

Major studio centres

Famous studio complexes include Television Centre and Broadcasting House in London, the Hollywood studios of Los Angeles, and national broadcasting centres such as NHK's in Tokyo and the CRTN studios in Beijing. Large entertainment formats may hire stages at film studio lots, while local stations operate smaller studios for news. In the 21st century, many studios have converted to high-definition and IP-based production, and virtual production using LED volume stages has begun to merge television and film techniques.
