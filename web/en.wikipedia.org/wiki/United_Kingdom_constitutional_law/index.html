UK constitutional law

United Kingdom constitutional law comprises the laws and principles governing the exercise of political power in the United Kingdom. Unlike most countries, the UK has no single codified constitutional document; instead, its constitution is uncodified, drawn from Acts of Parliament, court judgments, constitutional conventions, and authoritative works.

Central principles include the sovereignty of Parliament, the rule of law, and a partial separation of powers. Key statutes include Magna Carta (1215), the Bill of Rights 1689, the Acts of Union, the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949, the Human Rights Act 1998, and the devolution statutes for Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.

Institutions

The constitution structures the relationships between the monarch, Parliament (the House of Commons and House of Lords), the executive government, and the judiciary. Since 2009, the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom has been the highest court. Constitutional conventions — unwritten political rules, such as the monarch acting on ministerial advice — play a major role alongside legal rules.
