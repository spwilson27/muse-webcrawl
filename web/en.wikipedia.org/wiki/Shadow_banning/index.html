Shadow banning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shadow banning, also called stealth banning, hell banning, ghost banning or comment ghosting, is the practice of blocking or partially blocking a user or reducing the visibility of their content on an online platform, in such a way that the user does not immediately realise the sanction is in place. Unlike an outright ban, the affected user can continue to post and interact, but their audience sees little or none of their activity.

History

Variants of the technique date to early online forums. The Something Awful forums used a "hell ban" in which disruptive users could browse and post normally but were invisible to everyone else, the intention being that, receiving no response, they would eventually leave. Similar functions were built into forum software such as vBulletin's "Tachy Goes to Coventry" feature. The term "shadow ban" spread widely in the 2010s as large social media platforms adopted algorithmic ranking and content moderation at scale.

Platform practices

Major platforms have generally denied shadow banning in the covert sense while acknowledging that they down-rank or limit distribution of some content. Twitter stated that it did not "shadow ban" but did rank tweets and could reduce the visibility of accounts judged to exhibit suspicious or disruptive behaviour; internal documents released in 2022 (the "Twitter Files") showed extensive use of "visibility filtering". Instagram similarly maintained that accounts could be made "not recommendable" without notice, and later introduced an "account status" page to show users when their content had been restricted.

Controversy

Shadow banning is controversial because of its opacity: critics argue it allows platforms to suppress lawful speech, including political speech, without accountability, while platforms contend that discreet down-ranking is an effective, less confrontational tool against spam and abuse. The practice has featured in legislative proposals and litigation concerning social media moderation in the United States and the European Union.
