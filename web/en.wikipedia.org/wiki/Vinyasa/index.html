Vinyasa

Vinyāsa (Sanskrit: विन्यास) is a Sanskrit term used in yoga. In its broad sense it means a stepwise sequencing of movements in which postures (asanas) are linked together in a smooth, flowing manner coordinated with the breath. In modern studio usage, "vinyasa" often refers to a dynamic style of yoga class in which this flowing method is central.

Meaning and origins

The word combines vi ("in a special way") and nyasa ("to place"), giving the sense of "placing in a special way." In the tradition of T. Krishnamacharya and his students, vinyasa describes the deliberate, breath-synchronized transitions between held postures. In Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, as taught by K. Pattabhi Jois, fixed sequences of poses are joined by a repeated movement sequence.

The vinyasa sequence

In Sun Salutations and in "power yoga" classes, the word often denotes a specific transitional sequence: from plank pose, lowering through chaturanga dandasana (a low push-up position), rising into upward-facing dog, and returning to downward-facing dog. Teachers may cue students to "take a vinyasa" meaning to perform this sequence between sides or posture groups. Vinyasa flow classes vary widely in pace and intensity and are among the most popular forms of modern postural yoga in the West.
