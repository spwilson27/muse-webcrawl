Bulldog

The Bulldog is a British breed of dog of mastiff type. It may also be known as the English Bulldog or British Bulldog. It is a medium-sized, muscular dog of around 40–55 lb (18–25 kg). It has large, thick folds of skin on its brow, a pushed-in face, and distinctive pushed-back nose. The breed is widely recognised as a symbol of England and of British determination.

History

The Bulldog descends from dogs used in the blood sport of bull-baiting, which was outlawed in England by the Cruelty to Animals Act 1835. Enthusiasts subsequently bred the dogs away from aggression toward a companionable temperament, and the breed was recognised as a show breed in the late nineteenth century.

Characteristics

Bulldogs have short, smooth coats in colours including red, fawn, white and brindle, a massive head with an undershot jaw, and a characteristic rolling gait. They are known for a calm, courageous and friendly disposition.

Health

The breed's extremely short muzzle makes it brachycephalic, predisposing it to breathing difficulties (brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome), overheating, skin fold infections and other health problems; selective breeding for exaggerated features has been a subject of veterinary and welfare concern.
