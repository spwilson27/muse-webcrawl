Abimelech

Abimelech (Hebrew: אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, "my father is king") is the name of several figures in the Hebrew Bible, most prominently a king of Gerar who encounters both Abraham and Isaac, and Abimelech son of Gideon, who briefly ruled Shechem in the Book of Judges.

Abimelech of Gerar

In Genesis 20, Abraham, fearing for his life, presents his wife Sarah as his sister in Gerar; King Abimelech takes her into his household but, warned by God in a dream, restores her unharmed and reproaches Abraham, who explains that Sarah is in fact his half-sister. A parallel episode in Genesis 26 has Isaac make the same claim about Rebekah, and Abimelech — possibly the same king or a successor bearing the dynastic name — discovering the truth when he sees Isaac sporting with her. The two conclude covenants at Beersheba over disputed wells. Many scholars treat "Abimelech" as a Philistine royal title and the paired stories as variants of a single tradition.

Abimelech son of Gideon

Judges 9 tells of Abimelech, Gideon's son by a Shechemite concubine, who persuaded the lords of Shechem to make him king and murdered his seventy brothers, only Jotham escaping. After three years, conflict with Shechem led to civil war; Abimelech destroyed the city and sowed it with salt, but at Thebez a woman dropped a millstone from a tower onto his head, and he ordered his armour-bearer to kill him so it would not be said a woman had slain him. The story includes Jotham's fable of the bramble, one of the oldest fables in the Bible, and presents Abimelech's kingship as a curse.
