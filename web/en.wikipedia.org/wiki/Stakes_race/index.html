Stakes race

A stakes race is a Thoroughbred horse race in which the owners of the horses engaged contribute to the purse money from which the prizes are paid, through nomination, entry, and starting fees ("stakes"). The track or sponsoring organization commonly adds further money to the purse. Because owners must pay to enter, stakes races attract a higher class of horse than claiming or allowance races, and the most prestigious races in the sport — such as the Kentucky Derby, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, and the Melbourne Cup — are stakes races.

Graded and listed stakes

In many jurisdictions the best stakes races are classified as graded (North America: Grade I, II, and III) or Group races (Europe, Australia, and elsewhere: Group 1, 2, and 3), with "listed" races ranked just below. The grading, reassessed annually by committees using criteria such as purse, recent fields, and the quality of placed horses, strongly influences a horse's value for breeding. Black-type status — the bold-face type in sales catalogues for winners and placed horses in stakes races — is a key marker in pedigrees.

Types of stakes races

Stakes races may be run as handicaps, in which weights are assigned to equalize the field, or at weight-for-age or set-weight conditions. Two-year-old and three-year-old classics, sprint and route (distance) championships, and major turf races are all typically stakes events. A "sweepstakes" is an older synonym, preserved in names such as the Belmont Stakes — the word "Stakes" itself appears in the titles of many famous races.
