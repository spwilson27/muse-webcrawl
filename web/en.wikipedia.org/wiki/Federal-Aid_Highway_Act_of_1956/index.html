The Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956, long title "An Act to amend and supplement the Federal Aid Road Act approved July 11, 1916", popularly known as the National Interstate and Defense Highways Act, was enacted on 29 June 1956. The act authorised the construction of 41,000 miles (66,000 km) of the Interstate Highway System, the largest public works project in American history at that time, with the federal government paying 90% of the cost.

Provisions

The legislation created the Highway Trust Fund, financed by an increased federal gasoline tax, to pay for construction on a pay-as-you-go basis. Uniform design standards were set for the new freeway network, including controlled access, divided carriageways, and design speeds suitable for high-speed travel and military mobilisation.

Impact

The Interstate System transformed American transportation, commerce, and suburban development over the following decades. It also displaced many urban neighbourhoods and remains a subject of debate over its social and environmental effects.
