Prime quadruplet

A prime quadruplet (sometimes called a prime quadruple) is a set of four prime numbers of the form {p, p + 2, p + 6, p + 8}. This represents the closest possible grouping of four primes larger than 3, and is the only length-4 prime constellation.

Properties

For a prime quadruplet, p must be a prime such that p, p + 2 (twin prime pair), p + 6, and p + 8 (a second twin prime pair) are all prime. The first prime quadruplet is {5, 7, 11, 13}. Apart from this first quadruplet, the common difference pattern forces p to end in the digit 1 in decimal, so all subsequent quadruplets are of the form {30n + 11, 30n + 13, 30n + 17, 30n + 19} for some integer n; the next examples are {101, 103, 107, 109} and {191, 193, 197, 199}. The number in the middle of the quadruplet, p + 4 or equivalently the average, is divisible by 5 in the first case and always composite in general.

Distribution

It is not known whether there are infinitely many prime quadruplets; this is a special case of the prime k-tuple conjecture. Brun's theorem implies that the sum of the reciprocals of the primes in prime quadruplets converges; the corresponding constant, known as Brun's constant for prime quadruplets, is approximately 0.87058 83800.
