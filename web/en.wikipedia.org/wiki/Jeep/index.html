Jeep is an American automobile brand, now owned by multinational corporation Stellantis. Jeep has been part of Chrysler since 1987, when Chrysler acquired the Jeep brand, along with other assets, from its previous owner American Motors Corporation (AMC).

Jeep's current product range consists solely of sport utility vehicles — both crossovers and fully off-road worthy SUVs and models, including one pickup truck. Previously, Jeep's range included other pickups, as well as small vans, and a few roadsters. Some of Jeep's vehicles — such as the Grand Cherokee — reach into the luxury SUV segment, a market segment the 1963 Wagoneer is considered to have started. Jeep sold 1.4 million SUVs globally in 2016, up from 500,000 in 2008, two-thirds of which in North America, and was Fiat-Chrysler's best selling brand in the US during the first half of 2017.

The term "jeep" became generic for light military 4×4 vehicles; the definitive origin of the name remains debated, with popular explanations including a slurring of "GP" ("general purpose") and the influence of "Eugene the Jeep," a character in the Popeye comic strip.

History

The Jeep story begins in 1940, when the U.S. Army issued a specification for a light reconnaissance vehicle. American Bantam Car Company produced the first prototype, but production contracts for the standardized Willys MB and Ford GPW went to Willys-Overland and Ford because Bantam lacked capacity. More than 640,000 military jeeps were built during the Second World War, serving in every theater and widely exported under Lend-Lease.

After the war, Willys marketed civilian versions, beginning with the CJ-2A in 1945, and registered "Jeep" as a trademark. The brand passed through Kaiser Jeep (1953–1970), American Motors Corporation (1970–1987), Chrysler (1987–1998), DaimlerChrysler, Chrysler Group/FCA, and finally Stellantis in 2021. The Toledo, Ohio plant complex has built Jeeps continuously since 1941 and is closely identified with the brand.

The wartime jeep

The Willys MB was a quarter-ton, four-wheel-drive utility vehicle powered by the 60-hp Willys "Go Devil" L-head four-cylinder engine. It weighed about 1,040 kg (2,293 lb), carried four, and became one of the most iconic vehicles of the war — described by General Dwight D. Eisenhower as one of the tools that won the war for the Allies. Ford built the nearly identical GPW under license. Variants included the amphibious Ford GPA "Seep" and radio, ambulance, and weapons-carrier conversions.

Civilian models

Civilian Jeep (CJ) models ran from 1945 to 1986, evolving through CJ-2A, CJ-3A, CJ-5, CJ-6, CJ-7, and CJ-8 Scrambler. The CJ line was succeeded by the Wrangler (YJ, TJ, JK, and JL generations), which retains the classic open-top, solid-axle design. Other landmark models include the Jeepster, the Willys Jeep Station Wagon (1946, often called the first all-steel station wagon and a forerunner of the SUV), the Wagoneer/Grand Wagoneer luxury wagons, the Cherokee (XJ), which popularized the unibody SUV, and the Grand Cherokee (introduced 1992).

Current models

As of the mid-2020s, the Jeep range includes the Wrangler, Gladiator pickup, Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L, Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer, Compass, Renegade (outside North America), Cherokee (revived), the electric Recon and Wagoneer S, and market-specific models such as the Brazilian-built Commander. Jeep has announced electrified "4xe" plug-in hybrid versions of its core models and plans for expanded EV offerings under Stellantis's Dare Forward strategy.

The word "jeep" remains protected as a trademark in most markets, while in many countries it is also used generically — sometimes controversially — for off-road vehicles generally.
