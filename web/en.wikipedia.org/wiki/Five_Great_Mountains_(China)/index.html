Five Great Mountains (China)

Chinese五岳 (Wǔyuè)
CountryChina

The Five Great Mountains (Chinese: 五岳; pinyin: Wǔyuè) are five sacred mountains in China associated with Taoism and with the imperial cult of the Chinese emperors. Tradition held that the emperor, as Son of Heaven, should make pilgrimages to them; according to legend the practice reaches back to the mythical Emperor Shun.

The five mountains

  • Mount Tai (Tài Shān), Shandong — the Eastern Mountain, the most revered of the five, associated with sunrise, birth and renewal.
  • Mount Hua (Huà Shān), Shaanxi — the Western Mountain, famed for its precipitous plank paths.
  • Mount Heng (Héng Shān), Hunan — the Southern Mountain.
  • Mount Heng (Héng Shān, different characters), Shanxi — the Northern Mountain.
  • Mount Song (Sōng Shān), Henan — the Central Mountain, near the Shaolin Temple.

The mountains are major tourist destinations and UNESCO-recognized landscapes, dotted with temples, stelae and inscriptions accumulated over two millennia of pilgrimage. They should not be confused with the Four Sacred Mountains of Buddhism in China.
