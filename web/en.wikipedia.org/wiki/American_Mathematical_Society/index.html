American Mathematical Society

Founded: 1888 (as New York Mathematical Society)
Headquarters: Providence, Rhode Island
Membership: ~30,000
President: elected biennially

The American Mathematical Society (AMS) is an association of professional mathematicians dedicated to the interests of mathematical research and scholarship, serving the mathematical community through publications, meetings, advocacy and outreach. Founded in 1888 as the New York Mathematical Society, it adopted its present name in 1894 and is headquartered in Providence, Rhode Island.

Activities

The AMS publishes leading research journals, including the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, Transactions, Proceedings and the Bulletin, and maintains Mathematical Reviews and its online database MathSciNet, the principal reviewing service for mathematics. It organises the annual Joint Mathematics Meetings (with the Mathematical Association of America) and numerous sectional and specialist conferences.

Prizes and role

The Society awards major prizes including the Cole Prize, the Steele Prizes and the Veblen Prize, and elects Fellows of the AMS. It also sets standards for mathematical typesetting — its TeX-related tools, such as AMS-LaTeX and the amsmath package, are used by mathematicians worldwide — and it advocates for mathematics funding and education in Washington. With around 30,000 members, it is one of the largest mathematical societies in the world.
