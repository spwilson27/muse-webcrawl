The General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon is an American single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft originally developed by General Dynamics for the United States Air Force (USAF). Designed as an air superiority day fighter, it evolved into a successful all-weather multirole aircraft. Over 4,600 aircraft have been built since production began in 1976; although no longer purchased by the USAF, improved versions are built for export. In US service it is officially the Fighting Falcon but is commonly known by its nickname "Viper".

Design

The F-16 was among the first aircraft designed with relaxed static stability and a fly-by-wire flight control system, a frameless bubble canopy for visibility, a side-mounted control stick, and a seat reclined 30 degrees to reduce g-effects on the pilot. It is armed with an internal 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon and has 11 hardpoints for missiles, bombs and pods.

Operational history

F-16s have served in numerous conflicts, including the Gulf War, the Balkan wars, the War in Afghanistan, and with Israel — which used F-16s in the 1981 raid on Iraq's Osirak reactor — the Netherlands, Denmark, Ukraine (from 2024) and many others. It remains the most numerous Western fighter in service worldwide.
