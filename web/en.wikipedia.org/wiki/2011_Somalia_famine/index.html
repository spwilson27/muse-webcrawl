2011 Somalia famine

The 2011 Somalia famine was a famine that occurred in several regions of the Horn of Africa as a result of the 2011 East Africa drought and severely affected the Bakool and Lower Shabelle regions of southern Somalia. On 20 July 2011, the United Nations officially declared a famine in parts of southern Somalia, the first time a famine had been declared by the UN in nearly thirty years. Tens of thousands of people are thought to have died before the famine was declared, and estimates of total deaths commonly cited range between 50,000 and 260,000.

2011 Somalia famine

Country: Somalia
Location: Southern Somalia (Bakool, Lower Shabelle and other regions)
Period: July 2011 – February 2012
Total deaths: Estimated 50,000–260,000
Causes: Drought, conflict, high food prices

Background

The drought of 2011 was the worst in the Horn of Africa in 60 years. Two consecutive rainy seasons failed across Somalia, Kenya and Ethiopia, leading to widespread crop failure, livestock deaths and soaring food prices. In southern Somalia, the Islamist militant group al-Shabaab, which controlled much of the affected territory, had expelled several humanitarian agencies in 2009 and 2010 and restricted the movement of people seeking aid, worsening the crisis.

Declaration and response

The UN declared famine in Bakool and Lower Shabelle on 20 July 2011 after survey data showed acute malnutrition rates above 30 percent and death rates exceeding two people per 10,000 per day. Famine conditions were later declared in additional regions, including parts of Mogadishu. Hundreds of thousands of Somalis fled to refugee camps in Kenya and Ethiopia, notably the Dadaab complex in Kenya, which became the largest refugee camp in the world. International donors pledged hundreds of millions of dollars, though aid agencies reported that funding and access constraints hampered the response.

End of the famine

In February 2012 the United Nations announced that famine conditions in Somalia had ended, following an exceptional harvest and sustained humanitarian assistance. However, millions of people remained in need of food assistance, and the UN warned that the situation remained fragile.
