Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD), formerly Theater High Altitude Area Defense, is an American anti-ballistic missile defense system designed to shoot down short-, medium-, and intermediate-range ballistic missiles in their terminal phase (descent or reentry). The interceptor uses hit-to-kill technology, destroying targets by kinetic energy rather than with a warhead of its own.

System

A THAAD battery consists of truck-mounted launchers (each carrying eight interceptors), the AN/TPY-2 X-band radar, a fire control and communications unit, and support equipment. The system can intercept targets inside and just outside the atmosphere at ranges of up to about 200 km. THAAD forms the middle layer of the US missile defense architecture, between the shorter-range Patriot and the exoatmospheric Ground-Based Midcourse Defense and Aegis systems.

Deployment and controversy

THAAD batteries have been deployed to Guam, Israel, the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia and South Korea. The 2016–17 deployment to Seongju, South Korea, intended to counter North Korean missiles, provoked strong opposition from China, which objected to the system's powerful radar, and triggered unofficial Chinese economic retaliation against South Korean businesses. The first combat use of THAAD occurred in January 2022, when a UAE-operated battery intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile during an attack on Abu Dhabi.
