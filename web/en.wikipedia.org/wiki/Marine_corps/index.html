Marine corps

A marine corps is a military force specialised in expeditionary and amphibious warfare: conducting operations launched from the sea, securing beachheads and ports, and serving aboard naval vessels. Marines are typically part of, or closely associated with, a country's navy, though some corps operate as a separate service branch.

Roles

Marine forces combine infantry with supporting arms — artillery, armour, engineers, and increasingly organic aviation — organised to deploy rapidly from amphibious shipping. Historically marines also guarded warships, enforced discipline aboard, and manned ships' guns.

Notable corps

The largest and most famous is the United States Marine Corps, founded in 1775. Others include the British Royal Marines (1664), the Spanish Naval Infantry (1537, often cited as the oldest marine corps), the Netherlands Marine Corps, the Republic of Korea Marine Corps, and the Royal Thai Marine Corps.
