Maulana Karenga

Maulana Ndabezitha Karenga
Born: July 14, 1941, Parsonsburg, Maryland
Known for: Creator of Kwanzaa; founder of the US Organization; Kawaida philosophy

Maulana Ndabezitha Karenga (born Ronald McKinley Everett, July 14, 1941) is an American professor and activist. He is best known as the creator of the African-American and pan-African holiday Kwanzaa, and as the founder of the black nationalist US Organization in 1965. He is a professor of Africana studies at California State University, Long Beach.

Early life and education

Karenga was born in Maryland, the fourteenth child in a family of thirteen siblings. He studied at Los Angeles City College, UCLA, and later earned doctorates in political science and social ethics.

US Organization and Kawaida

In 1965, in the aftermath of the Watts Rebellion, Karenga founded the US Organization. He developed the philosophy of Kawaida, emphasizing African cultural values, self-determination and community building, summarized in the Nguzo Saba (the Seven Principles), which also form the basis of Kwanzaa, first celebrated in 1966.

Controversy and imprisonment

In 1971 Karenga was convicted of felony assault and false imprisonment in connection with the treatment of two female members of his organization, and served time in prison until 1975. After his release he resumed academic work and activism.

Later career

Karenga has authored numerous books on African studies, ethics and Maatian thought, and remains a prominent public intellectual. Kwanzaa is now observed by millions of people annually from December 26 to January 1.
