BRCT domain

The BRCT domain (BRCA1 C Terminus domain) is a protein domain found in proteins involved in DNA damage response, cell cycle checkpoint control and DNA repair. It takes its name from its discovery in the C-terminal region of BRCA1, the breast and ovarian cancer susceptibility protein.

Structure and function

A single BRCT domain is roughly 90–100 amino acids long and folds into a compact structure of β-sheets flanked by α-helices. BRCT domains frequently occur in tandem pairs, which together form a binding pocket that recognises phosphorylated serine motifs in partner proteins. Through these phospho-dependent protein–protein interactions, BRCT-containing proteins act as scaffolds that assemble signalling and repair complexes at sites of DNA damage.

Proteins containing BRCT domains

Examples include BRCA1, 53BP1, MDC1, TOPBP1, DNA polymerase lambda and the XRCC1 and DNA ligase proteins. Mutations that disrupt the tandem BRCT domains of BRCA1 — including several known cancer-predisposing variants — abolish its phosphopeptide binding and tumour suppressor activity, linking the domain directly to hereditary breast and ovarian cancer.
