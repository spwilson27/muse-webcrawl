Syrian Coastal Mountain Range

LocationWestern Syria
GeographyParallel to the Mediterranean coast
RegionLatakia, Tartus, Hama and Homs governorates

The Syrian Coastal Mountain Range (Arabic: سلسلة جبال الساحل السوري) is a mountain range in western Syria. It runs roughly north to south, parallel to the Mediterranean coastline, separating the narrow coastal plain from the interior plateau and the valley of the Orontes River.

Geography

The range extends for roughly 130 kilometres (81 mi) along the Syrian coast, from the Turkish border region in the north to the gap near Homs in the south. The mountains are mostly limestone, with heavily dissected slopes, deep river gorges and extensive pine and oak forest. The highest summits rise to well over 1,500 metres, and the range receives some of the highest rainfall in Syria because of Mediterranean moisture carried inland.

Significance

The mountains overlook the ports of Latakia and Tartus and form the historic heartland of several religious communities, including Alawites, whose presence in the range dates back centuries. Castles and medieval fortresses, such as Salah al-Din Castle (Qal'at Sahyun), dot the ridges, reflecting the range's strategic value during the Crusades. South of the range, the Homs Gap provides the principal passage between the coast and the Syrian interior.
