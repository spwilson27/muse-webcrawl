Christian Goldbach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christian Goldbach (18 March 1690 – 20 November 1764) was a Prussian mathematician connected with the court of the tsars in Russia. He is best remembered for Goldbach's conjecture, one of the oldest and best-known unsolved problems in number theory.

Life

Born in Königsberg, the son of a pastor, Goldbach studied law and mathematics and traveled widely in Europe, meeting many leading scholars including Leibniz and the Bernoulli family. In 1725 he moved to Saint Petersburg, becoming professor of mathematics and historian of the newly founded Imperial Academy of Sciences. He later served as a tutor to Tsarevich Peter and as an official in the Russian foreign ministry, also working on cryptography.

Mathematics

In a letter to Leonhard Euler of 7 June 1742, Goldbach proposed the conjecture that every even integer greater than two is the sum of two prime numbers — a statement verified for enormous ranges but still unproven. He also worked on infinite series, the theory of curves, and corresponded extensively with Euler, in whose letters many of Euler's own results on number theory first appeared.
