Iron deficiency

Iron deficiency, or sideropenia, is the state in which a body lacks enough iron to supply its needs. Iron is present in all cells and has several vital functions, including carrying oxygen as part of haemoglobin and acting as a cofactor for enzymes. Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency worldwide and the leading cause of anaemia; when severe enough to reduce haemoglobin production it produces iron-deficiency anaemia.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms may include fatigue, weakness, pallor, shortness of breath, dizziness, brittle nails and koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails), sore tongue, and pica — cravings for non-food substances such as ice (pagophagia) or clay. Hair loss and restless legs syndrome are also associated. Symptoms often develop gradually and may be absent until anaemia is present.

Causes

Causes include blood loss (menstruation, gastrointestinal bleeding, frequent blood donation), inadequate dietary intake, malabsorption (as in coeliac disease or after gastric surgery), and increased requirements during pregnancy and growth. In regions where they are endemic, hookworm infestation is an important cause.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by blood tests, particularly serum ferritin, which reflects iron stores, along with transferrin saturation and a full blood count showing microcytic, hypochromic red cells. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and replenishes iron, orally with ferrous salts such as ferrous sulfate, or intravenously when oral iron is not tolerated or absorbed. Dietary sources include red meat, legumes, leafy green vegetables and iron-fortified foods; vitamin C enhances absorption.
