Vaporwave

Vaporwave is a microgenre of electronic music and an Internet aesthetic that emerged in the early 2010s. It is often characterized by a nostalgic or surrealist engagement with 1980s and 1990s popular entertainment, technology and advertising, through chopped and slowed samples of smooth jazz, elevator music, R&B and lounge music.

Characteristics

Visually, vaporwave uses early Internet imagery, late-1990s web design, glitch art, Roman busts, tropical landscapes, pastel pinks and aquas, and Japanese or Chinese text used decoratively. The cover of Floral Shoppe (2011) by Macintosh Plus — with a marble bust and pink background — became iconic.

History and interpretation

Early works included Eccojams by Chuck Person (Daniel Lopatin) and releases by James Ferraro. Critics have read the genre variously as a critique of consumer capitalism and Internet culture, and as a nostalgic celebration of it. Subgenres and successors include future funk, hardvapour, mallsoft and the broader synthwave-adjacent "aesthetic" culture online.

Influence

Vaporwave aesthetics spread into fashion, graphic design, memes and mainstream advertising in the mid-2010s, and remain a recognisable visual language for retro-futurist nostalgia.
