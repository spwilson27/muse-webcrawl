David Van Cortlandt Crosby (14 August 1941 – 18 January 2023) was an American singer, songwriter and guitarist. He first found fame as a founding member of the Byrds, whose folk-rock hits of 1965 included "Mr. Tambourine Man", and then co-founded the supergroup Crosby, Stills & Nash (later also with Neil Young), celebrated for its intricate vocal harmonies and Crosby's rhythmically adventurous songs such as "Guinnevere" and "Long Time Gone". He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice, once with each band — one of very few artists so honoured.

Career

Born into a show-business family in Los Angeles (his father was the cinematographer Floyd Crosby), Crosby drifted from drama studies into the folk scene and then to the Byrds in 1964. Fired from the Byrds in 1967 amid internal friction, he formed CSN with Stephen Stills and Graham Nash in 1968; their debut album and appearance at Woodstock made them central figures of the era. His solo debut, If I Could Only Remember My Name (1971), long a cult favourite, was later critically revalued as a masterpiece of the Laurel Canyon sound.

Personal struggles and later years

Crosby's career was repeatedly derailed by drug addiction; he served a prison sentence in Texas in the mid-1980s and received a liver transplant in 1994. Clean thereafter, he enjoyed a late creative renaissance, releasing a string of well-reviewed solo albums from Croz (2014) onward and touring almost until his death in January 2023 at the age of 81.
