Imamate of Futa Jallon

The Imamate of Futa Jallon (also spelled Fouta Djallon) was a Fulani Islamic theocratic state in the Futa Jallon highlands of present-day Guinea, West Africa. It was established following a jihad launched in 1725 by Fulani Muslim clerics against the indigenous non-Muslim peoples and nominally Muslim Fulani rulers of the region, and it endured until French colonial conquest in the late nineteenth century.

History

Under the leadership of Karamokho Alfa (Ibrahim Musa Sambegou) and the military commander Ibrahim Sori, the Fulani forces defeated the local Jalunke (Dialonke) people, notably at the Battle of Talansan around 1727. Karamokho Alfa became the first Almami (imam-ruler) of the new state, with its capital at Timbo.

Government

The imamate was a federation of nine provinces (diwe) governed under Islamic law. Leadership alternated, often contentiously, between the Alfaya faction (descendants of Karamokho Alfa) and the Soriya faction (descendants of Ibrahim Sori), an arrangement that produced recurrent civil conflict.

Economy and society

The state participated in trade with the coast, exchanging goods including slaves, and its economy rested heavily on slave labour in agriculture. The imamate fell under French control following the agreements and campaigns of the 1890s, ending with the French victory at Porédaka in 1896.
