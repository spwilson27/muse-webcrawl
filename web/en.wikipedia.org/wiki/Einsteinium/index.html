Einsteinium

Einsteinium is a synthetic chemical element with the symbol Es and atomic number 99. It is a member of the actinide series and is named after Albert Einstein. Einsteinium is a soft, silvery, paramagnetic metal that does not occur in nature and must be produced in nuclear reactors or particle accelerators.

Discovery

Einsteinium was first identified in December 1952 by a team led by Albert Ghiorso at the University of California, Berkeley, in the debris of the Ivy Mike hydrogen bomb test at Enewetak Atoll. The intense neutron flux of the explosion had transformed uranium atoms by multiple neutron captures into very heavy nuclei. The discovery was kept secret for military reasons until 1955, when the element was announced and named in honour of Einstein. The neighbouring element fermium was found in the same debris.

Production and properties

Einsteinium is produced in small quantities by neutron irradiation of plutonium or californium in high-flux reactors such as the High Flux Isotope Reactor at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. It has 19 known isotopes; the longest-lived, einsteinium-252, has a half-life of about 472 days, while the most commonly produced, einsteinium-253, decays with a half-life of about 20.5 days. Because it is intensely radioactive and available only in microgram quantities, einsteinium has no commercial uses and is produced mainly for basic research, including the synthesis of still heavier elements — it was used in 1955 to create the first atoms of mendelevium.
