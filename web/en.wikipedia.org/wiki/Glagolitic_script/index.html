The Glagolitic script is the oldest known Slavic alphabet. It was invented in the 9th century by the Byzantine monks Saints Cyril and Methodius to translate the Bible and other texts into Old Church Slavonic for their mission to Great Moravia (863 AD). The name derives from the Old Church Slavonic word glagolъ, "word" or "utterance". The script was probably modelled in part on Greek cursive, with additional invented characters for Slavic sounds absent from Greek.

History and use

After the death of Methodius (885), his disciples were expelled from Moravia and carried Glagolitic to Bulgaria, where the Preslav and Ohrid literary schools flourished; there a new script, later called Cyrillic, gradually displaced Glagolitic in the east. Glagolitic survived longest in Croatia, where an angular form of the script was used in liturgical books and inscriptions for nearly a thousand years — uniquely, Croatian Glagolitic priests were permitted to celebrate the Roman rite Mass in Slavonic. Important monuments include the Codex Zographensis, the Kiev Missal and the Baška tablet (c. 1100), a stone inscription recording a donation by the Croatian king Zvonimir.

Forms and legacy

Two main styles are distinguished: the earlier rounded (Bulgarian) form and the later angular (Croatian) form. Glagolitic has been encoded in Unicode since version 4.1 (2005). The script remains a symbol of Croatian cultural identity, appearing on coins, monuments and in public art. See also Old Church Slavonic and Saints Cyril and Methodius.
