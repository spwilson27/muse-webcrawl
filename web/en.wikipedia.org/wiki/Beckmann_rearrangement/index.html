Beckmann rearrangement

The Beckmann rearrangement, named after German chemist Ernst Otto Beckmann (1853–1923), is an organic reaction in which an oxime is converted into a substituted amide under acidic conditions. Beckmann first reported the transformation in 1886 while developing methods to distinguish ketoximes from aldoximes.

Mechanism

In the standard mechanism, the hydroxyl group of the oxime is first converted into a better leaving group by protonation or by reaction with reagents such as phosphorus pentachloride, thionyl chloride, or tosyl chloride. The group positioned anti (trans) to the leaving group then migrates from carbon to nitrogen as the leaving group departs, producing a nitrilium ion. Hydrolysis of this intermediate yields the amide. Because the migrating group moves with retention of configuration at the migrating carbon, and because migration is stereospecific with respect to the oxime geometry, the reaction can be used to determine oxime configuration.

Industrial application

The most important application is the rearrangement of cyclohexanone oxime to ε-caprolactam, the monomer for nylon-6, produced on a scale of millions of tonnes per year. The traditional process uses fuming sulfuric acid (oleum) and generates large quantities of ammonium sulfate by-product, which has motivated the development of catalytic vapour-phase processes over zeolites and other alternative routes. Under strongly acidic, dehydrating conditions, aldoximes may instead dehydrate to nitriles, and certain oximes undergo the abnormal Beckmann fragmentation, cleaving to a nitrile and a carbocation-derived product.
