Delaunay triangulation

In geometry, a Delaunay triangulation for a given set P of discrete points in a general position is a triangulation such that no point in P is inside the circumcircle of any triangle in the triangulation. The concept is named after Boris Delaunay, who described it in 1934. Delaunay triangulations maximise the minimum of all the angles of the triangles in the triangulation, so they tend to avoid sliver triangles, and they are widely used in computational geometry, mesh generation and computer graphics.

Properties and relation to Voronoi diagrams

The Delaunay triangulation is the dual graph of the Voronoi diagram: two points are connected by a Delaunay edge exactly when their Voronoi cells share an edge, and the circumcentres of Delaunay triangles are the vertices of the Voronoi diagram. For points in general position (no four cocircular) the triangulation is unique. It contains the Euclidean minimum spanning tree, the nearest-neighbour graph and the Gabriel graph as subgraphs, and the shortest path between two points within the triangulation approximates the Euclidean distance (it is a geometric spanner).

Algorithms

Standard algorithms include the incremental Bowyer–Watson algorithm, edge-flipping algorithms that replace pairs of triangles until the local Delaunay condition is satisfied, and divide-and-conquer and sweep-line methods running in O(n log n) time. The definition extends to higher dimensions, where triangles are replaced by simplices and circumcircles by circumscribed spheres; in three dimensions these are Delaunay tetrahedra. Delaunay refinement algorithms, such as Chew's and Ruppert's, add points to guarantee minimum angle or radius–edge quality for finite element meshes.

Applications

Applications include terrain modelling and surface reconstruction from scattered data, finite element analysis, terrain and scientific visualisation, path planning, and reconstructing surfaces from point clouds in computer vision.
