Service quality

Service quality (SQ) is a measure of how well the service delivered by an organisation matches customer expectations. Assessing service quality is a central concern of services marketing, because delivering quality service is considered an essential strategy for success and survival in competitive environments.

The gap model and SERVQUAL

The most influential framework was developed by A. Parasuraman, Valarie Zeithaml, and Leonard Berry, whose "gap model" (1985) holds that service quality is the discrepancy between customer expectations and perceptions of the service actually received. Their SERVQUAL instrument measures this gap across five dimensions:

  • Reliability — ability to perform the promised service dependably and accurately.
  • Assurance — knowledge and courtesy of employees and their ability to inspire trust.
  • Tangibles — physical facilities, equipment, and appearance of personnel.
  • Empathy — caring, individualised attention provided to customers.
  • Responsiveness — willingness to help customers and provide prompt service.

Measurement and criticism

SERVQUAL has been widely applied in industries including retailing, banking, healthcare, hospitality, and telecommunications. It has also been criticised on conceptual and methodological grounds, prompting alternatives such as SERVPERF, which measures performance perceptions alone. Other approaches include mystery shopping, customer satisfaction surveys, complaint analysis, and metrics such as the Net Promoter Score.

Importance

High service quality is associated with customer satisfaction, loyalty, positive word of mouth, and profitability. Because services are intangible, heterogeneous, and produced and consumed simultaneously, managing their quality requires attention not only to outcomes but also to the processes by which services are delivered and to employee training and empowerment.
