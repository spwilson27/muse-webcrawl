Moldovan language

Moldovan (Romanian: limba moldovenească) is one of the two local names for the Romanian language in Moldova. The other, and now official, name is Romanian. Linguists generally regard the spoken and written language of Moldova as identical to standard Romanian, which belongs to the Eastern Romance branch of the Romance languages.

History

During the Soviet period, the Moldavian SSR used a Cyrillic alphabet for "Moldavian," and Soviet linguistics promoted the idea of a language distinct from Romanian. In 1989, near the end of Soviet rule, Moldova restored the Latin script. The 1994 Constitution of the independent Republic of Moldova named the state language "Moldovan," but the Constitutional Court ruled in 2013 that the Declaration of Independence — which names it Romanian — takes precedence. In March 2023, Moldova's parliament adopted legislation replacing "Moldovan language" with "Romanian language" throughout the country's laws.

Usage

The name "Moldovan" remains in use in the breakaway region of Transnistria, where a Cyrillic script is still used, and in some official contexts such as the ISO 639 language codes, which historically assigned Moldovan the codes mo and mol (retired in 2008 in favour of Romanian codes). Everyday speech in Moldova includes regional vocabulary and Russian loanwords, but these do not constitute a separate standard language.
