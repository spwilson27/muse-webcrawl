Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature

FieldNumerical analysis
TypeNumerical integration rule

Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature is a method of numerical integration based on expanding the integrand in terms of Chebyshev polynomials and integrating the resulting series exactly. It was published by Charles W. Clenshaw and A. R. Curtis in 1960.

Method

The quadrature nodes are the extrema of Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind (Chebyshev–Gauss–Lobatto points) on the interval [−1, 1]. Because these points are nested when the number of points doubles, function evaluations can be reused in adaptive schemes, a property it shares with Fejér quadrature.

Accuracy

Although long considered less accurate than Gauss–Legendre quadrature for a fixed number of points, Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature converges at a comparable rate for smooth integrands and is widely used in practice, notably in Chebfun.
