Nemean lion

The Nemean lion was a monster in Greek mythology that inhabited the region of Nemea in the Peloponnese. Its hide was impenetrable to weapons, and its slaying was the first of the twelve labours imposed on the hero Heracles.

The first labour

Sent by King Eurystheus, Heracles found that his arrows, sword and club were useless against the lion's invulnerable pelt. He therefore wrestled the beast in its cave and strangled it with his bare hands, afterwards using the lion's own claw — the only thing sharp enough to cut its skin — to flay the pelt. Heracles wore the lion skin as armour for the rest of his life, making it his most recognisable attribute in art. When he returned with the skin, the terrified Eurystheus hid in a large bronze jar and ordered that future labours be reported from outside the city walls.

Origins and constellation

The lion was said to be the offspring of Typhon and Echidna, or in some accounts of Selene, the moon goddess. According to one tradition Zeus placed the lion among the stars as the constellation Leo.
