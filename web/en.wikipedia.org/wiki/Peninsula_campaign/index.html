Peninsula campaign

DateMarch – July 1862
TheatreAmerican Civil War, Virginia
Union commanderGeorge B. McClellan
Confederate commandersJoseph E. Johnston; Robert E. Lee
ResultConfederate strategic victory

The Peninsula campaign (also known as the Peninsular campaign) was a major Union operation launched in March 1862 during the American Civil War. Major General George B. McClellan landed the Army of the Potomac at Fort Monroe and advanced up the Virginia Peninsula toward the Confederate capital, Richmond.

Course of the campaign

McClellan's huge army besieged Yorktown for a month, allowing the Confederates to strengthen Richmond's defences. The armies fought at Williamsburg and Seven Pines, where Confederate commander Joseph E. Johnston was wounded and replaced by Robert E. Lee. In late June Lee seized the initiative in the Seven Days Battles, a series of heavy engagements that, while costly to both sides, forced McClellan to retreat to Harrison's Landing on the James River.

Outcome

Although the Union army was not destroyed, the campaign ended in strategic failure: Richmond was not taken, and the Army of the Potomac was withdrawn in August 1862. The campaign established Lee's reputation and shifted the war in Virginia back toward northern Virginia.
