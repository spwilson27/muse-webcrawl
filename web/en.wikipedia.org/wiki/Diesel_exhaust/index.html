Diesel exhaust

Image: Diesel exhaust plume

Diesel exhaust is the gaseous and particulate emission from a diesel engine, consisting of the products of combustion of diesel fuel together with excess air. Its composition includes carbon dioxide, water vapour, nitrogen oxides (NOx), carbon monoxide, unburned hydrocarbons, sulfur compounds, and diesel particulate matter (soot).

Composition

Because diesel engines run lean (with excess air), carbon monoxide and hydrocarbon emissions are relatively low, but the high combustion temperatures and pressures favour formation of nitrogen oxides, and locally fuel-rich zones produce carbonaceous soot particles coated with organic compounds, including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. Diesel exhaust fluid (a urea solution) is used in selective catalytic reduction systems to convert NOx to nitrogen and water.

Health effects

In 2012 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified diesel engine exhaust as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), based on evidence linking occupational exposure to lung cancer. Diesel particulates also contribute to urban air pollution and respiratory disease. Regulatory limits in the United States, Europe, and elsewhere have progressively tightened, driving adoption of diesel particulate filters and aftertreatment systems.
