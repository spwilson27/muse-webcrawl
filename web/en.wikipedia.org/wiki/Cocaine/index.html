Cocaine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Source: Coca plant (Erythroxylum coca) leaves
Type: Stimulant, local anesthetic
Legal status: Controlled nearly worldwide; limited medical use
Dependence liability: High

Cocaine is a tropane alkaloid and powerful central nervous system stimulant obtained from the leaves of the coca plant (Erythroxylum coca), native to western South America. It is used recreationally as a snorted, injected, or smoked (freebase/"crack") drug, and medically as a local anesthetic and vasoconstrictor, particularly in nasal surgery. Most countries prohibit its production, distribution, and possession except for licensed medical and scientific purposes.

Pharmacology

Cocaine blocks the reuptake of dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine at nerve terminals, raising their concentrations in synapses. The dopamine transporter blockade underlies its euphoric and reinforcing effects. It also blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, which produces local anesthesia but also contributes to cardiac toxicity. Effects begin within seconds to minutes depending on route and typically last 15–60 minutes, producing euphoria, alertness, decreased appetite, and increased heart rate and blood pressure.

History

Andean peoples have chewed coca leaves for millennia for stamina and altitude tolerance. The alkaloid was isolated in 1859–60 by Albert Niemann in Germany. In the 1880s cocaine was promoted as a wonder drug: Sigmund Freud advocated its use, and Karl Koller demonstrated its value as an eye anesthetic. Cocaine was an ingredient in the original Coca-Cola formula (removed by the early 20th century) and countless patent medicines. Mounting recognition of addiction led to regulation— notably the US Harrison Narcotics Tax Act of 1914—and eventually to global prohibition through the UN Single Convention of 1961.

Health effects and addiction

Cocaine use carries risks of myocardial infarction, stroke, arrhythmia, seizures, hyperthermia, and psychosis; chronic use is associated with nasal septum damage (when snorted) and severe psychological dependence. "Crack" cocaine, a smokable freebase form that spread in the 1980s, produces an especially brief, intense high and high addiction liability. Treatment of cocaine use disorder is primarily behavioural; no approved pharmacotherapy exists, though contingency management shows the best evidence.

Production and trade

Coca is cultivated principally in Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia. The illicit trade in cocaine, controlled by cartels and trafficking networks, has driven violence and corruption in producer and transit countries and remains a major focus of international drug policy.
