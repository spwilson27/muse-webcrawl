Lorraine campaign

Dates: 1 September – 18 December 1944

Location: Lorraine, France

Result: Allied victory

Allied commander: George S. Patton (Third Army)

The Lorraine campaign was the operations of the U.S. Third Army in Lorraine during World War II from September 1 through December 18, 1944. Official U.S. Army campaign names for this period and location are Northern France and Rhineland.

Background

Following the breakout from Normandy and the rapid pursuit across France in August 1944, General George S. Patton's Third Army raced eastward toward the German border. By early September, however, the advance ground to a halt: supply lines stretched back to the Normandy beaches were inadequate, fuel was prioritised to other armies, and autumn rains turned the country into mud.

The campaign

The campaign in Lorraine was characterised by grinding battles against prepared German positions, most famously around the fortified city of Metz, whose ring of forts resisted for weeks. The Third Army also fought to cross the Moselle River and drive through the Maginot and Siegfried Line fortifications. Battles included the fighting around Nancy, Arracourt — one of the largest tank battles involving U.S. forces in the war — and the eventual capture of Metz on 22 November 1944.

Aftermath

By mid-December the Third Army had reached the Saar River and breached parts of the Siegfried Line, but the campaign had cost time, men and matériel. When the German Ardennes offensive (the Battle of the Bulge) opened on 16 December 1944, Patton famously pivoted his army north to relieve Bastogne, ending the Lorraine campaign.
