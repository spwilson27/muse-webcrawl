John Gilbert (born John Cecil Pringle; July 10, 1897 – January 9, 1936) was an American actor, screenwriter and director. He rose to fame during the silent film era and became one of the most popular leading men of the 1920s, rivalling Rudolph Valentino as a romantic screen idol.

Silent film stardom

The son of stock-company actors, Gilbert worked as an extra and bit player before signing with Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. His breakthrough came with The Merry Widow and the anti-war epic The Big Parade (both 1925), the latter one of the most successful silent films ever made. His on-screen romances with Greta Garbo in films such as Flesh and the Devil (1926) and Love (1927) made the pair one of Hollywood's celebrated couples, on and off screen.

Decline in the sound era

Gilbert's career faltered with the arrival of talking pictures, a decline variously attributed to his strained relationship with studio head Louis B. Mayer, changing tastes and alcoholism; the popular tale that his voice was comically unsuited to sound is now largely discounted by historians. Garbo insisted on him as her co-star in Queen Christina (1933), but his career did not recover, and he died of a heart attack in 1936 at the age of 38.
