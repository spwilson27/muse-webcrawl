Mount Meron (Hebrew: הַר מֵירוֹן, Har Meron; Arabic: جبل الجرمق, Jabal al-Jarmaq) is a mountain in the Upper Galilee region of Israel. At 1,208 metres (3,963 ft) above sea level, it is the highest peak in Israel within the Green Line (the higher Mount Hermon lies in the Golan Heights). The mountain is a major nature reserve, noted for its Mediterranean woodland, and it is a site of major religious significance in Judaism.

Mount Meron, the highest peak in the Galilee.

Religious significance

At the foot of the mountain, in the village of Meron, is the tomb of the 2nd-century sage Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, traditionally credited as the author of the Zohar, the foundational work of Kabbalah. Every year on the festival of Lag BaOmer, hundreds of thousands of pilgrims gather at the tomb for celebrations (hilula) involving bonfires, music, and dancing — one of the largest annual gatherings in Israel. On 30 April 2021, a crowd crush at the event killed 45 people, the deadliest civilian disaster in Israel's history, prompting major reforms to the site's management and safety arrangements.

Nature reserve

The Mount Meron Nature Reserve, declared in 1965, covers about 84,000 dunams, making it one of the largest nature reserves in northern Israel. Its dense woodland of Palestine oak, terebinth, and other Mediterranean species shelters wild boar, gazelles, and diverse birdlife. The Israel National Trail passes over the summit, which offers views across the Galilee to the Mediterranean and Mount Hermon.

Facilities

The summit hosts an Israel Defense Forces air traffic control and surveillance installation, recognizable by its radar domes. A road reaches the top, and hiking trails circle the peak.
