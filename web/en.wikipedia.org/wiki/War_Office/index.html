War Office

The War Office was a department of the British Government responsible for the administration of the British Army between 1857 and 1964, when its functions were transferred to the newly unified Ministry of Defence. The name is also associated with its former headquarters, the Old War Office Building on Whitehall in London.

History

The office developed from the post of Secretary at War, established in the 17th century. In the aftermath of the Crimean War, which exposed severe administrative failings, the War Office was reorganised; the office of Secretary of State for War was created in 1854 and the department was progressively consolidated. Under Edward Cardwell's reforms (1868–1874) the Commander-in-Chief's headquarters moved into the War Office and the army's administration was centralised. H. O. Arnold-Forster and Richard Haldane later carried out further reforms, including the creation of the Army Council in 1904 and of the general staff.

Building and dissolution

From 1906 the department was housed in the grand Baroque-style War Office Building in Whitehall, designed by William Young and completed in 1906. In 1964 the War Office, Admiralty and Air Ministry were merged into the Ministry of Defence, ending the department's separate existence. The historic building was subsequently sold and redeveloped as a luxury hotel and residences, opening as The OWO in 2023.
