Steeplechase

Steeplechase is the name of several sports involving racing over obstacles. In athletics it is a long-distance track event with barriers and a water jump; in horse racing it is a form of jump racing over fences; the term also covers similar races in other sports.

Athletics

The standard Olympic steeplechase is run over 3,000 metres, with 28 fixed barriers and seven water jumps. The barriers are 91.4 cm high for men and 76.2 cm for women, and the water jump consists of a barrier followed by a pit of water. The event evolved from cross-country races in Britain and Ireland and has been an Olympic event for men since 1920 and for women since 2008. Kenyan runners have long dominated the men's event.

Horse racing

In horse racing, a steeplechase is a race over large fixed fences, a specialisation of National Hunt racing. Famous examples include the Grand National at Aintree and the Cheltenham Gold Cup. The name derives from eighteenth-century Irish races run cross-country between church steeples.
