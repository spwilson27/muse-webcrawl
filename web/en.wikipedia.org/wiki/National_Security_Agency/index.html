National Security Agency

The National Security Agency (NSA) is a national-level intelligence agency of the United States Department of Defense, responsible for global monitoring, collection and processing of information for foreign intelligence and counterintelligence purposes (signals intelligence, or SIGINT), and for protecting U.S. government communications and information systems (information assurance). Headquartered at Fort George G. Meade in Maryland, the NSA is one of the largest organisations in the U.S. Intelligence Community; its director also commands U.S. Cyber Command and heads the Central Security Service.

History

The NSA was created by a secret memorandum of President Harry S. Truman on 24 October 1952 (effective 4 November 1952), consolidating military cryptologic work after the failures of the Armed Forces Security Agency. Its existence was not publicly acknowledged for years — staff joked that NSA stood for "No Such Agency". The agency's work was exposed to public scrutiny by the Church Committee in the 1970s, which revealed programmes such as SHAMROCK (bulk collection of international telegrams) and MINARET (watch-listing of American dissidents), leading to the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act of 1978.

Mission

The NSA's foreign intelligence mission covers signals intelligence — intercepting and analysing foreign communications and electronic signals — and since the early 2000s large-scale collection from internet backbone traffic and undersea cables, often in partnership with the other "Five Eyes" agencies (the UK's GCHQ, Canada's CSE, Australia's ASD and New Zealand's GCSB). Its defensive mission, exercised through the Cybersecurity Directorate established in 2019, develops cryptographic standards and protects classified U.S. networks. The NSA is believed to be the largest employer of mathematicians in the United States.

Snowden disclosures

In June 2013, contractor Edward Snowden disclosed a vast trove of classified documents describing NSA programmes including PRISM (collection from major internet companies under FISA court orders), bulk telephony metadata collection under Section 215 of the USA PATRIOT Act, and the tapping of allied leaders' communications. The disclosures triggered a worldwide debate on surveillance and privacy, led to reforms under the USA FREEDOM Act of 2015 ending the bulk telephony programme, and made the NSA a household name. The agency has also suffered major security breaches, including the 2016–17 leak of its hacking tools by the Shadow Brokers group, some of which powered the WannaCry and NotPetya attacks.
