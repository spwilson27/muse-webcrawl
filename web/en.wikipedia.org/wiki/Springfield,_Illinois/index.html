Springfield is the capital city of the U.S. state of Illinois and the county seat of Sangamon County. Its population was 114,394 at the 2020 census, making it the state's seventh-most populous city. Springfield is best known as the home of Abraham Lincoln for more than two decades before his presidency, and many of its principal attractions — his home, law offices, tomb, and presidential library and museum — commemorate his life.

History

The first settlers arrived in 1819, and the settlement (initially called Calhoun) became the Sangamon county seat in 1821 and was renamed Springfield. In 1837 the state capital was moved from Vandalia to Springfield, largely through the lobbying of a group of local legislators headed by the young Abraham Lincoln, known as the "Long Nine". Lincoln moved to Springfield the same year, practised law there, served in the state legislature and the U.S. House of Representatives, married Mary Todd in 1842, and delivered his famous "House Divided" speech in the Old State Capitol in 1858. He departed for Washington in February 1861 with a farewell address at the Great Western depot, and returned only in death: his body lay in state in the capitol before burial at Oak Ridge Cemetery, now the Lincoln Tomb State Historic Site.

In 1908 the city was the scene of a race riot that shocked the nation and led directly to the founding of the NAACP the following year. During the twentieth century Springfield's economy centred on state government, insurance, and health care, alongside its position on historic U.S. Route 66.

Geography and climate

Springfield lies in the central Illinois prairie near the Sangamon River, with Lake Springfield, a man-made reservoir built in the 1930s, on its southern edge. It has a humid continental climate with hot summers and cold winters.

Attractions

Major sites include the Lincoln Home National Historic Site, the Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum (opened 2005), the Lincoln Tomb, the Illinois State Capitol — whose dome is taller than that of the U.S. Capitol — the Old State Capitol, the Dana–Thomas House designed by Frank Lloyd Wright, and the Illinois State Museum. Springfield is also the claimed home of the horseshoe sandwich, an open-faced sandwich covered in cheese sauce, and hosts the Illinois State Fair each August.
