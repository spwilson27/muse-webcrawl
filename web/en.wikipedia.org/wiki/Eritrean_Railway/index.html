The Eritrean Railway is the only railway system in Eritrea. Built under Italian colonial rule between 1887 and 1932, it climbs from the Red Sea port of Massawa at sea level to the capital Asmara at 2,394 m, one of the most dramatic mountain railways in Africa, with dozens of tunnels and viaducts.

History

Construction began from Massawa in 1887, reaching Asmara in 1911 and Agordat in 1932; a planned extension to Biscia was completed in the 1930s. The line declined during the Eritrean War of Independence, when track was dismantled and operations ceased in 1978.

Reconstruction

After independence in 1993, Eritrea rebuilt the Massawa–Asmara section largely with local labour and salvaged materials; heritage steam services resumed in stages by 2003. Vintage Italian Mallet steam locomotives and Littorina railcars now serve mainly tourist and charter traffic alongside limited freight.
