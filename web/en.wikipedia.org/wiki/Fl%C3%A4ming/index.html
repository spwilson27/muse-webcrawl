Fläming

The Fläming is a range of low hills and a historic region in the German states of Brandenburg and Saxony-Anhalt, southwest of Berlin. Formed during the Ice Age, it reaches heights of about 200 metres at the Hagelberg, one of the highest points in Brandenburg.

History

The name derives from Flemish settlers (from Flanders) who colonised the region in the 12th century during the German eastward expansion, bringing drainage and farming techniques. The Fläming is known for its medieval fieldstone churches, small villages, and castles such as Rabenstein and Eisenhardt in Bad Belzig, the region's principal town.

Nature and tourism

Much of the area is protected as the High Fläming Nature Park (Naturpark Hoher Fläming), with forests, heathland, and the distinctive Rummel glacial meltwater channels. It is popular for cycling and hiking, including a well-known network of skating and cycling paths.
