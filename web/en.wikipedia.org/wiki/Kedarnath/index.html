Kedarnath is a town in Rudraprayag district of the Indian state of Uttarakhand, situated in the Garhwal Himalayas near the head of the Mandakini River at about 3,583 metres above sea level. It is renowned for the Kedarnath Temple, dedicated to the god Shiva, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four sites of the Char Dham pilgrimage.

The temple

Tradition attributes the temple's founding to the Pandavas of the Mahabharata and its revival to the philosopher Adi Shankaracharya in the 8th century CE. The stone temple opens only for about six months a year (roughly April/May to October/November) because of extreme winter cold; the deity is then carried down to Ukhimath. Pilgrims traditionally reach the town by a trek of about 16–22 km from Gaurikund.

2013 floods

In June 2013, catastrophic flash floods and a glacial-lake outburst devastated Kedarnath, killing thousands in the region. The temple itself survived, shielded in part by a large boulder that came to rest behind it. The town has since been rebuilt with new infrastructure and a wider approach path.
