Atiyah–Singer index theorem

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem is a theorem in differential geometry, proven by Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer in 1963, which states that for an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold, the analytical index (related to the dimension of the space of solutions) equals the topological index (defined in terms of characteristic classes).

Statement

In its classical form, the theorem equates the difference of the dimensions of the kernel and cokernel of an elliptic operator with an integral over the manifold of combinations of Chern and Todd classes. It unified and generalised earlier results including the Riemann–Roch theorem and the Hirzebruch signature theorem.

Impact

The theorem became a cornerstone linking analysis, geometry and topology, and influenced mathematical physics through its applications to gauge theory and anomalies in quantum field theory. Atiyah and Singer received the Abel Prize in 2004 partly for this work.
