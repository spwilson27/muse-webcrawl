Trim tab

A trim tab is a small, adjustable hinged surface set into the trailing edge of a primary flight control surface — an elevator, rudder or aileron — on an aircraft. By deflecting the tab, the pilot generates an aerodynamic force that holds the larger control surface in the desired position, trimming the aircraft to fly hands-off at a chosen speed or attitude without constant control pressure.

Operation

A trim tab works in the opposite direction to the surface it trims: to hold an elevator nose-up, the tab deflects downward, producing an upward force on the trailing edge that keeps the elevator raised. Tabs are adjusted from the cockpit by a trim wheel, crank or electric switch. Some aircraft instead use an adjustable stabiliser or anti-servo tabs, which move with the surface to increase control feel. On boats, similar trim tabs mounted on the transom are used to adjust the vessel's running attitude and to correct listing.

Servo tabs

In a servo tab system, the pilot's controls move only the small tab, and the tab's aerodynamic force then moves the main control surface — an arrangement used on large aircraft before fully powered controls became standard.
