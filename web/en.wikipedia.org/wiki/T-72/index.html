T-72

The T-72 is a family of Soviet main battle tanks that entered production in 1973. It was a design that leaned heavily on the experience gained with the T-64 while simplifying it for mass production. The T-72 was widely exported and licensed for production in several countries, and it has been used in numerous conflicts; it remains in service in upgraded forms in many armies.

Design

The T-72 has a crew of three, using a carousel autoloader for its 125 mm smoothbore gun. It is powered by developments of the V-2 diesel engine family. Early models had relatively simple fire control, but later variants added improved sights, explosive reactive armour, and the ability to fire gun-launched anti-tank guided missiles.

The tank's design lineage continues in the Russian T-90, which is essentially a thoroughly modernised T-72 variant.

Service history

T-72s have fought in the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, the Yugoslav Wars, the Chechen Wars, the Russo-Georgian War, the Syrian Civil War and the Russo-Ukrainian War, among many others. The tank became notorious in some conflicts for catastrophic ammunition explosions caused by its carousel autoloader arrangement, in which a penetrating hit could detonate the stored rounds and blow the turret off — a phenomenon widely documented in footage from Ukraine.

Operators

More than 25,000 T-72s were built. Operators have included Russia, Ukraine, India, Poland, Iraq, Syria and dozens of other states.
