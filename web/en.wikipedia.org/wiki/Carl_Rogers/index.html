Carl Ransom Rogers (8 January 1902 – 4 February 1987) was an American psychologist, widely regarded as one of the founders of humanistic psychology and of psychotherapy research. He developed person-centered therapy (originally "client-centered therapy"), one of the most influential schools of counselling and clinical practice of the 20th century.

Theory and practice

Rogers held that people have an inherent "actualising tendency" toward growth, and that psychological distress arises from incongruence between the self-concept and experience. He proposed that three therapist-provided conditions are necessary and sufficient for therapeutic change: congruence (genuineness), unconditional positive regard (non-judgemental acceptance), and empathic understanding of the client's internal frame of reference. The approach is non-directive: the therapist does not diagnose or interpret but helps clients find their own solutions.

Rogers was a pioneer of empirical psychotherapy research, recording and analysing therapy sessions and testing his hypotheses quantitatively. His ideas spread far beyond the clinic into education ("student-centered learning", set out in Freedom to Learn), management, and conflict resolution; in his later years he facilitated encounter groups between conflicting parties in Northern Ireland, South Africa and elsewhere, and was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 1987, the year of his death.
