Grise Fiord (Inuktitut: ᐊᐅᔪᐃᑦᑐᖅ, Aujuittuq, "place that never thaws") is an Inuit hamlet on the southern tip of Ellesmere Island in the Qikiqtani Region of Nunavut, Canada. It is Canada's northernmost civilian community.

History

The community was created in 1953 by the Government of Canada as part of the High Arctic relocation, in which Inuit families from Inukjuak (Port Harrison), Quebec, and from Pond Inlet were moved to Grise Fiord and Resolute to establish settlements that would assert Canadian sovereignty in the High Arctic during the Cold War. The relocatees endured severe hardship in an unfamiliar and harsher environment than promised; the federal government formally apologized for the relocation in 2010.

The name Grise Fiord, meaning "pig fjord" in Norwegian, was given by explorer Otto Sverdrup in reference to the grunting of walruses he encountered there.

Geography and life

The hamlet sits on the shore of Grise Fiord, a waterway opening into Jones Sound, surrounded by steep mountains. It is served by Grise Fiord Airport and by annual sealift. Hunting and trapping remain central to the local economy alongside public-sector employment. The community experiences weeks of continuous darkness in winter and continuous daylight in summer.
