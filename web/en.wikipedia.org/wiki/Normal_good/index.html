Normal good


Labelled placeholder: normal good Engel curve

In economics, a normal good is a type of good with a positive income elasticity of demand: demand for the good increases as consumer income rises, and decreases when income falls. This means that the income effect reinforces the substitution effect for normal goods. Most consumer goods are normal goods. Examples include clothing, organic foods, entertainment, and higher-quality versions of staple products.

Normal goods contrast with inferior goods, for which demand falls as income rises (for example, instant noodles or bus travel when consumers can afford alternatives). Some goods may behave as normal for a certain income range and inferior for another.

Analysis

Within normal goods, economists sometimes distinguish necessity goods, whose income elasticity is positive but less than one (demand grows more slowly than income), from luxury goods, whose income elasticity is greater than one (demand grows faster than income). The relationship between income and quantity demanded is summarised by the Engel curve and governed by Engel's law.

Examples

  • Organic and fresh foods
  • Clothing and footwear of standard quality
  • Entertainment and restaurant meals
  • Household appliances and furniture
  • Healthcare services
