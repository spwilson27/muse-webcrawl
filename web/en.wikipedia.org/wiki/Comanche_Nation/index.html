Comanche Nation

Comanche Nation
Self-name: Nʉmʉnʉʉ ("the People")
Headquarters: Lawton, Oklahoma
Language: Comanche (Numic, Uto-Aztecan)
Status: Federally recognised tribe

The Comanche Nation, known in their own language as the Nʉmʉnʉʉ, is a federally recognised Native American tribe headquartered near Lawton, Oklahoma. The Comanche historically dominated a vast territory called Comanchería, spanning parts of present-day Texas, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Kansas, and Colorado, where they were renowned as horsemen and hunters of the southern Plains.

History

Originally part of the Shoshone peoples of the Great Basin and Wyoming, the Comanche moved south in the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries after acquiring horses from Spanish settlements, becoming the pre-eminent equestrian power of the southern Plains. They traded and warred with Spain, Mexico, Texas, and the United States for more than a century. Leaders such as Quanah Parker guided the tribe through its final resistance; in 1875 the last free bands surrendered and settled on a reservation in Indian Territory under the Medicine Lodge Treaty arrangements.

Government and culture

Today the Comanche Nation is governed by an elected tribal council and chairman under its constitution, with enrolled membership numbering in the tens of thousands. The tribe operates cultural programmes, a language revitalisation effort for the severely endangered Comanche language, casinos and other enterprises, and the Comanche National Museum and Cultural Center. Comanche Code Talkers served with distinction in the Second World War.

Historical bands

The historic Comanche were organised into autonomous bands, including the Penateka, Nokoni, Quahadi, Yamparika, and Kotsoteka, united by language and kinship rather than a central government.
