Polyacrylonitrile

Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) is a synthetic, semicrystalline organic polymer resin, with the linear formula (CH2CHCN)n. Though it is thermoplastic, it does not melt under normal conditions; it degrades before melting. Nearly all PAN produced is used as a copolymer with other monomers. PAN is the precursor for high-quality carbon fiber, and it is an important constituent of acrylic fibers used in clothing and home furnishings.

History

Polyacrylonitrile was first synthesised in 1930 by Hans Fikentscher and Claus Heuck at IG Farben. Because the polymer did not melt and resisted common solvents, commercial development stalled until new solvents were found in the 1940s, enabling the production of acrylic fibres such as DuPont's Orlon.

Uses

Homopolymers of PAN have been used as fibres in hot gas filtration systems, outdoor awnings, sails for yachts, and fibre-reinforced concrete. Copolymers containing PAN are used as fibres to make knitted clothing like socks and sweaters, as well as outdoor products such as tents. PAN is the standard precursor for carbon fibre: by market share, about 90% of carbon fibre production uses PAN precursor, which is oxidised and carbonised to give the final product.
