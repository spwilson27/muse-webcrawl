Purépecha Empire


The yácatas of Tzintzuntzan, capital of the Purépecha state.

The Purépecha Empire, also known as the Tarascan Empire, was a Mesoamerican state centred in the Lake Pátzcuaro basin of present-day Michoacán, Mexico. Founded in the early 14th century, it grew to be the second-largest state in Mesoamerica after the Aztec Empire, covering roughly 75,000 km² and ruling perhaps a million people. It was the only Mesoamerican state never conquered by the Aztecs, and it fell to Spanish forces in the 1520s–1530s.

History

According to the Relación de Michoacán (c. 1540), the Purépecha state was founded by Tariácuri, who united the lake basin from his centres at Pátzcuaro, Ihuatzio, and Tzintzuntzan. His successors expanded the realm by conquest and built a centralized, hereditary monarchy ruled by the cazonci—unusually centralized for Mesoamerica, where city-state confederacies were more typical. The empire repeatedly defeated Aztec invasions, most decisively in 1478–79, and maintained a heavily fortified eastern frontier.

Society and economy

The capital Tzintzuntzan, with its distinctive keyhole-shaped yácata pyramids, housed perhaps 25,000–35,000 people. The Purépecha were famed metalworkers: they produced copper and bronze tools, bells, and ornaments, and worked gold and turquoise mosaic. The empire's language, Purépecha, is a language isolate unrelated to its neighbours, and today is still spoken by over 100,000 people in Michoacán.

Spanish conquest

After the fall of the Aztecs, the cazonci Tangáxoan II submitted to the Spanish and was baptized, but in 1530 the conquistador Nuño de Guzmán had him tortured and executed, an act that provoked revolts and later contributed to Guzmán's disgrace. Franciscan missionaries such as Vasco de Quiroga subsequently reshaped the region, founding craft villages around the lake.
