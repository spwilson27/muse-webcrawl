Hentai

Hentai (変態) is a Japanese word meaning "pervert" or "abnormality". In English and other languages outside Japan, the term has come to refer specifically to pornographic anime and manga. Within Japan itself, the word is not the standard industry term for such material; adult works are instead marketed as ero (from "erotic"), "18-kin" (restricted to adults), or with genre-specific labels.

Etymology and usage

The word combines the characters for "change/strange" and "state/attitude", and in Meiji-era Japan it appeared in psychological literature rendering concepts such as sexual perversion. In modern Japanese slang, hentai commonly denotes a perverted person. The Western use of the word for pornographic Japanese animation and comics became established in the 1990s as anime fandom grew abroad.

History

Erotic imagery has a long history in Japanese art, from Edo-period shunga woodblock prints to early manga. In the post-war period, erotic gekiga and magazines such as Manga Erotopia (1973) developed the genre. The 1980s brought adult original video animations (OVAs) — Lolita Anime (1984) is often cited as the first erotic anime OVA — and the rise of adult computer games (eroge). In the 1990s, works such as Urotsukidōji became notorious in the West and shaped foreign perceptions of the genre. The amateur dōjinshi market, centred on events such as Comiket, remains a major channel for adult comics in Japan.

Genres and terms

Western fandom has adopted a vocabulary of subgenres and terms, many of them Japanese loanwords, covering themes such as yaoi and yuri (male and female same-sex romance), and the "tentacle" motif popularly linked to censorship rules that prohibited depiction of genitalia under Article 175 of the Japanese Penal Code, leading creators to substitute other imagery.

Regulation

In Japan, Article 175 of the Penal Code criminalises distribution of "obscene" material, which historically led to mosaic pixelation in commercial adult media. In Western countries, animated material is regulated under general obscenity and child-protection law; depictions of minors in sexual contexts are illegal in many jurisdictions regardless of the fictional, drawn nature of the content, and enforcement has been a recurring legal and political controversy.
