Frontbencher

A frontbencher is a member of parliament (MP) who sits on the front benches of the chamber because they hold a senior position within their party's parliamentary leadership. In government, frontbenchers are ministers of the Crown; in opposition, they are members of the shadow cabinet or junior shadow ministers and official spokespersons.

Role and obligations

Frontbenchers speak for their party on their assigned portfolio, lead debates, answer questions in the chamber and represent the party in the media. In return for this prominence, they are bound by collective responsibility and party discipline: a frontbencher who cannot support the party line is expected to resign their position, as happens, for example, when ministers resign over a policy disagreement. Appointment to and removal from the front bench is generally in the gift of the party leader.

The term is used in the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and other parliaments derived from Westminster. Its opposite is the backbencher, an MP without ministerial or spokesperson duties, who is traditionally freer to voice personal or constituency concerns.
