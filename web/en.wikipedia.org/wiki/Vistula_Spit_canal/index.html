The Vistula Spit canal (officially the Shipping Channel through the Vistula Spit, Polish: Kanał żeglugowy przez Mierzeję Wiślaną) is a canal connecting the Vistula Lagoon with the Gulf of Gdańsk in the Baltic Sea. It cuts across the Polish part of the Vistula Spit near the village of Nowy Świat, allowing ships to reach the Polish port of Elbląg without passing through the Russian-controlled Strait of Baltiysk.

Construction

The idea of digging a channel through the spit had been proposed repeatedly since the 16th century and was revived after 1989, but successive governments shelved it on cost and environmental grounds. The Law and Justice government approved the project in 2016, and construction by a Polish-Belgian consortium began in October 2019. The canal was officially opened on 17 September 2022, the anniversary of the Soviet invasion of Poland in 1939.

Description

The waterway is about 1.3 kilometres long, including an approximately 1-kilometre cut through the spit itself, and has a depth of 5 metres and a width allowing vessels of up to 100 metres in length and 20 metres beam. A lock (named after Captain Leszek Wiktorowicz) manages the differing water levels, and two swing bridges carry the coastal road over the channel. An artificial island in the lagoon, built from dredged material, serves as a bird habitat. A second phase dredged the fairway across the lagoon and the Elbląg river to bring larger vessels into Elbląg port.

Reactions

The government presented the canal as a matter of national sovereignty, ending dependence on Russian permission for access to Elbląg. Russia criticised the project, and environmental groups objected to damage to the spit's forests and to the lagoon's ecology. Critics also questioned its commercial significance, noting Elbląg's small cargo volumes.
