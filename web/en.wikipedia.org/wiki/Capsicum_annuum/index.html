Capsicum annuum

FamilySolanaceae
GenusCapsicum

OriginAmericas (Mexico region)

Capsicum annuum is a species of the plant genus Capsicum native to southern North America, the Caribbean, and northern South America. It is the most widely cultivated and economically important of the five domesticated capsicum species, encompassing an enormous range of pepper varieties, from the sweet bell pepper with no heat to hot varieties such as the jalapeño and cayenne.

Varieties

Cultivars of C. annuum include bell peppers, jalapeños, cayenne, New Mexico chiles, poblano/ancho, serrano, peperoncino, pimiento, and the ornamental "Christmas peppers". Heat is measured in Scoville Heat Units and comes from capsaicin concentrated in the placental tissue of the fruit; bell peppers rate 0 SHU, jalapeños roughly 2,500–8,000, and cayenne 30,000–50,000.

Cultivation and uses

The species was domesticated in the Americas and spread worldwide after the Columbian exchange, transforming cuisines from Hungary (paprika) to Thailand and India. Plants are grown as short-lived perennials in frost-free climates and as annuals elsewhere. Beyond fresh and dried fruit, the species provides paprika and chilli powder, oleoresin extracts, and is used in folk remedies and as a source of natural colourants.
