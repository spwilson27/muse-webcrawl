Bombardier Inc. is a Canadian manufacturer of business jets headquartered in Montreal, Quebec. Founded by Joseph-Armand Bombardier to market his snowmobiles, the company grew into a diversified conglomerate spanning recreational products, rail transportation and commercial aircraft. After a period of financial difficulty in the 2010s, Bombardier divested its rail division to Alstom (completed 2021) and its commercial aircraft programmes, leaving business aviation as its sole business.

History

Snowmobile origins

Joseph-Armand Bombardier, a mechanic from Valcourt, Quebec, patented a tracked vehicle for travelling over snow and in 1937 began selling the B7 snowmobile. His company later developed the hugely successful Ski-Doo recreational snowmobile, launched in 1959. The recreational products business was spun off in 2003 as BRP Inc.

Diversification into rail and aerospace

In 1974 Bombardier acquired MLW-Worthington and won the contract to supply rolling stock for the Montreal Metro, beginning its rail business. Aerospace expansion followed through acquisitions: Canadair (1986), which brought the Challenger business jet and the CL-215 water bomber; Short Brothers of Belfast (1989); Learjet (1990); and de Havilland Canada (1992), maker of the Dash 8 turboprop.

Commercial aircraft and the CSeries crisis

The CRJ regional jet family made Bombardier the world's third-largest aircraft manufacturer. Its ambitious CSeries narrow-body airliner, launched in 2008, suffered delays and cost overruns that brought the company close to bankruptcy in 2015, requiring a bailout from the Quebec government. After a trade dispute with Boeing, Airbus acquired a majority stake in the programme in 2018 and rebranded it the Airbus A220. Bombardier sold the CRJ programme to Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (2020) and its remaining regional aircraft operations progressively.

Refocus on business aviation

Since 2021 Bombardier has concentrated on its Global and Challenger business jet families, defence conversions, and aftermarket services, while reducing the large debt accumulated during the CSeries era.
