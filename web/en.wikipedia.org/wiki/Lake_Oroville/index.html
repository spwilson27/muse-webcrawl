Lake Oroville is a reservoir formed by the Oroville Dam impounding the Feather River in the Sierra Nevada foothills of Butte County, California. It is the keystone reservoir of the California State Water Project, storing winter and spring runoff for release into the Feather and Sacramento rivers for water supply, flood control, hydroelectric generation and recreation. At 770 feet (235 m) high, Oroville Dam is the tallest earthfill dam in the United States.

History

Construction of the dam began in 1961 and it was dedicated in 1968. The reservoir submerged portions of the Western Pacific Railroad's Feather River Route, which was relocated to higher ground.

2017 spillway crisis

In February 2017, following heavy winter storms, the dam's concrete main spillway fractured at high releases, and water overtopped the never-before-used emergency spillway, eroding its hillside. Fear of a collapse of the emergency spillway weir prompted the evacuation of nearly 190,000 people downstream in Butte, Sutter and Yuba counties. The main spillway was rebuilt at a cost of about $1.1 billion and returned to use in April 2019.

Drought and recovery

The reservoir fell to record-low levels during the 2020–22 drought — in September 2021 it reached about 22% of capacity, and the Hyatt Powerplant shut down for the first time for lack of water. Heavy storms in the winter of 2022–23 refilled the lake almost to full pool.

Recreation

The Lake Oroville State Recreation Area offers boating, houseboating, fishing for bass and planted salmon, camping and trails. The Bidwell Canyon and Lime Saddle areas are major marinas, and the lake is ringed by car-top and hike-in campsites.
