Political warfare

Political warfare is the use of political means to compel an opponent to do one's will, including propaganda, psychological operations, disinformation, diplomatic pressure, economic coercion and the support of friendly political forces abroad — short of, or in preparation for, open armed conflict. The term was central to early Cold War strategy.

InstrumentsPropaganda, covert action, diplomacy, economic statecraft
Key theoristGeorge F. Kennan
Key institutionsPolicy Planning Staff, Political Warfare Executive, OPC

Definition

In a famous memorandum of 4 May 1948, the American diplomat George F. Kennan, director of the State Department's Policy Planning Staff, defined political warfare as "the logical application of Clausewitz's doctrine in time of peace": the employment of all the means at a nation's command, short of war, to achieve its national objectives. Such operations, he wrote, could be both overt and covert.

History

During the Second World War, Britain's Political Warfare Executive (PWE) ran propaganda, including "black" radio stations purporting to be German, against Nazi Germany. In the Cold War, the United States combined overt instruments (the Marshall Plan, Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, the United States Information Agency) with covert action run first by the Office of Policy Coordination (1948) and then the CIA — supporting anti-communist parties, unions, journals and front organisations.

The Soviet Union pursued its own political warfare through "active measures" (aktivnye meropriyatiya): disinformation, forgeries and agents of influence designed to weaken adversaries. Concepts akin to political warfare also feature in Chinese strategic thought under the "three warfares" (public opinion, psychological and legal warfare).

Contemporary usage

Analysts increasingly apply the term to twenty-first-century statecraft below the threshold of war — sometimes called hybrid or grey-zone warfare — including election interference, social-media influence operations, economic coercion and lawfare.
