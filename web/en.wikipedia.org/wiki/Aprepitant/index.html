Aprepitant

Trade name: Emend

Class: NK1 receptor antagonist

Routes: By mouth, intravenous

ATC code: A04AD12

Aprepitant, sold under the brand name Emend among others, is a medication used to prevent chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and to prevent postoperative nausea and vomiting. It was the first neurokinin-1 (NK1) receptor antagonist to reach the market, approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2003.

Mechanism of action

Aprepitant is a selective, high-affinity antagonist of human substance P / neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptors. Substance P is a neuropeptide involved in the vomiting reflex, and blocking its receptors in the central nervous system inhibits both the acute and delayed phases of chemotherapy-induced emesis. It has little or no affinity for serotonin (5-HT3), dopamine, or corticosteroid receptors, and is therefore combined with drugs acting on those targets.

Medical use

For chemotherapy patients, aprepitant is typically given as a three-day oral regimen (125 mg on day 1, then 80 mg on days 2 and 3) together with a 5-HT3 antagonist such as ondansetron and the corticosteroid dexamethasone. Its intravenous prodrug, fosaprepitant, can be given as a single infusion on day 1.

Side effects and interactions

Common side effects include fatigue, hiccups, constipation, and headache. Aprepitant is a substrate and moderate inhibitor of the liver enzyme CYP3A4, so it interacts with many other drugs; it increases exposure to dexamethasone (whose dose is reduced in combination regimens) and can reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives.
