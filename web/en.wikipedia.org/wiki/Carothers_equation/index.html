Carothers equation

Carothers equation
FieldPolymer chemistry
Named afterWallace Carothers
Applies toStep-growth polymerisation
Key relationX̄n = 1 / (1 − p)

In polymer chemistry, the Carothers equation, derived by Wallace Carothers in the 1930s, relates the number-average degree of polymerisation (X̄n) of a step-growth polymer to the extent of reaction (p) of the functional groups.

The equation

For a polymerisation with stoichiometrically balanced functional groups, the equation is:

X̄n = 1 / (1 − p)

where p is the fraction of functional groups that have reacted. The relation shows why step-growth polymerisation requires extremely high conversions to achieve long chains: at p = 0.90 the average chain is only 10 units long, while p = 0.99 gives 100 units and p = 0.999 gives 1,000. For systems with a stoichiometric imbalance ratio r (≤ 1), the modified equation X̄n = (1 + r) / (1 + r − 2rp) applies, showing that excess of one monomer limits molecular weight — a principle used industrially to control chain length.

Significance

Carothers developed the equation while creating the first synthetic fibres at DuPont, including nylon. It remains a foundational result of polymer science, distinguishing step-growth kinetics — where molecular weight builds slowly and monomer is consumed early — from chain-growth polymerisation.
