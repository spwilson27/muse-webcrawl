Tougaloo College

Motto: Where History Meets the Future
Established: 1869
Type: Private liberal arts HBCU
Location: Tougaloo, Mississippi, United States
President: Donzell Lee (interim leadership following recent transitions)
Students: approx. 650

Tougaloo College is a private, historically Black, liberal arts college in Tougaloo, Mississippi, just north of Jackson. It is affiliated with the United Church of Christ and was founded in 1869 by the American Missionary Association, shortly after the American Civil War ended, on the site of a former slave plantation.

History

In 1869 the American Missionary Association of New York purchased 500 acres of land from John Boddie, a former slave owner, to establish a school for the education of freed slaves and their children. The institution was chartered as Tougaloo University by the State of Mississippi in 1871 and later became Tougaloo College.

During the Civil Rights Movement, Tougaloo students and faculty played a prominent role. The college served as a safe haven and meeting place for activists, and its students — including the group known as the Tougaloo Nine — staged a sit-in at the Jackson Municipal Library in 1961, one of the first civil rights demonstrations in Mississippi. The historic Woodworth Chapel on campus, built in 1901, hosted figures such as Martin Luther King Jr.

Academics

Tougaloo offers bachelor's degrees in the humanities, natural sciences, and social sciences, and is known for producing a disproportionately high number of graduates who go on to earn doctoral degrees, particularly in the sciences. It is a member of the United Negro College Fund.
