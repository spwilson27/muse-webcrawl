Northwest Territories

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Northwest Territories
Capital: Yellowknife
Population: about 45,000
Area: about 1.35 million km²
Indigenous peoples: Over half the population — Dene, Inuvialuit, Métis

The Northwest Territories is a territory of Canada in the country's far north, bordered by the Arctic Ocean and encompassing the western half of the Canadian Arctic mainland and Victoria and Banks islands. Created in 1870 from Rupert's Land and the North-Western Territory, it once covered most of Canada; the creation of Yukon (1898), Alberta and Saskatchewan (1905) and Nunavut (1999) reduced it to its present — still enormous — extent.

More than half its roughly 45,000 residents are Indigenous: Dene nations of the Mackenzie Valley, Inuvialuit of the Beaufort coast, and Métis. Land-claim and self-government agreements (Inuvialuit 1984, Tłı̨chǫ 2003 among them) make the territory a leader in Indigenous governance; devolution of land and resource control from Ottawa followed in 2014. Yellowknife, on Great Slave Lake, is the capital and largest centre.

The economy runs on diamonds (from 1998, Canada became a top producer), oil and gas on hold, tourism under the aurora, and government. Climate change — permafrost thaw slumping roads and runways, the Mackenzie running low, wildfire evacuations of the capital itself in 2023 — is the territory's defining pressure, in the vastness east of Alaska and west of Nunavut.
