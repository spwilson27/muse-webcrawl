The Bluebell Railway is an 11-mile (17.7 km) heritage line in Sussex, England, almost entirely within East Sussex with a short section in West Sussex. Steam trains are operated between Sheffield Park and East Grinstead, with intermediate stations at Horsted Keynes and Kingscote. It was the first standard-gauge preserved passenger railway in the United Kingdom, reopening part of the Lewes to East Grinstead line in 1960.

History

The line was opened in 1882 by the Lewes and East Grinstead Railway, later absorbed by the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway. British Railways closed the line in 1958. The Bluebell Railway Preservation Society was formed in 1959 and began running trains between Sheffield Park and Horsted Keynes in August 1960. Extensions to Kingscote opened in 1994, and to East Grinstead in March 2013, completing the line's connection with the national network.

Rolling stock

The railway holds one of the largest collections of preserved steam locomotives and carriages in Britain, with a strong emphasis on the Southern Railway and its predecessors. Notable locomotives include the Adams radial tank No. 488, SECR P-class tanks, and Bulleid Pacifics. The carriage fleet includes rare vintage stock, much of it restored at Horsted Keynes.

Stations

Sheffield Park houses the locomotive sheds and the SteamWorks! exhibition. Horsted Keynes is restored to a 1920s Southern Railway appearance and has frequently been used as a film and television location, notably as "Downton" station. Kingscote is a small country station restored to 1950s condition. East Grinstead provides interchange with Southern services to London.
