Chinese noodles

Chinese noodles (Chinese: 麵; pinyin: miàn) are a staple of Chinese cuisine, varying widely according to region of production, ingredients, shape, width, and manner of preparation. Noodles are an essential ingredient and staple in Chinese cuisine, and they have been part of Chinese food culture for over 4,000 years.

History

The earliest written record of noodles is found in a Chinese dictionary dated to the Eastern Han period (25–220 AD). In 2005, a team of Chinese archaeologists reported finding an earthenware bowl at the Lajia archaeological site containing noodles approximately 4,000 years old, made from millet — the oldest noodles ever discovered.

Styles and varieties

Wheat noodles (mian) predominate in northern China, while rice noodles (fen) are more common in the south. Well-known techniques include hand-pulled lamian, knife-cut daoxiao mian, and bamboo-pressed jook-sing noodles of Guangzhou. Cellophane noodles (fensi) are made from mung bean starch, and cu mian are thick wheat noodles used in Shanghai-style fried dishes.

Preparation

Noodles may be boiled, stir-fried (chao mian), served in soup (tang mian), or dressed with sauce after draining (gan mian, as in Shaanxi's biangbiang noodles). Famous dishes include Lanzhou beef noodle soup, dan dan noodles, zhajiangmian, and wonton noodles.

Symbolism

Because of their length, noodles symbolize longevity and are traditionally served at birthdays and Chinese New Year.
