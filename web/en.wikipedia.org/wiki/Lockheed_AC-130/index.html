Lockheed AC-130

The Lockheed AC-130 gunship is a heavily armed, long-endurance, ground-attack variant of the C-130 Hercules transport, fixed-wing aircraft. It carries a wide array of ground-attack weapons that are integrated with sophisticated sensors, navigation, and fire-control systems. Unlike other modern military fixed-wing aircraft, the AC-130 relies on visual targeting: because its common operational profile employs orbital attack patterns at night or in inclement weather, the AC-130 has large eye windows and long loiter time over the target area.

Developed from: Lockheed C-130 Hercules

Design and development

The AC-130 project began during the Vietnam War as "Project Gunship II" to replace the Douglas AC-47 Spooky, providing longer loiter, greater firepower, and truck-interdiction capability along the Ho Chi Minh trail. All weapons are mounted on the port side of the aircraft, which fires while flying in a left-hand pylon turn around the target.

Armament

Earlier variants carried combinations of 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannons, 40 mm Bofors autocannon, and a 105 mm M102 howitzer — the largest gun mounted on a production aircraft. The current AC-130J Ghostrider, based on the C-130J Super Hercules, is armed with a 30 mm GAU-23/A cannon and 105 mm howitzer, plus precision-guided munitions such as GBU-39 small-diameter bombs, AGM-176 Griffin missiles, and AGM-114 Hellfire missiles on wing pylons.

Variants and service

Variants have included the AC-130A Spectre, AC-130E/H Spectre, AC-130U Spooky II, AC-130W Stinger II, and AC-130J Ghostrider. Gunships have served in Vietnam, Grenada, Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, Afghanistan, Iraq, Libya, and Syria, operated by Air Force Special Operations Command, principally the 4th Special Operations Squadron at Hurlburt Field, Florida.
