The Brocken, also called the Blocksberg, is the highest peak in the Harz mountain range and in northern Germany, rising to 1,141 metres above sea level in the state of Saxony-Anhalt, within the Harz National Park. Famous for its harsh climate, its folklore of witches, and the optical phenomenon named after it, the Brocken is one of Germany's most visited summits.

Climate and nature

The treeless, granite summit has a subarctic microclimate comparable to mountains 600–700 metres higher or to Iceland: it is shrouded in fog on some 300 days a year, with gale-force winds and snow cover for much of the year. This produces the Brocken spectre, a magnified shadow of an observer cast onto mist, often ringed by a rainbow-like glory, and the rare Brocken bow. The mountain's alpine garden, founded in 1890, cultivates plants from high mountains worldwide.

Folklore and literature

Since the early modern period the Brocken has been the legendary meeting place of witches on Walpurgis Night (30 April), immortalised in Goethe's Faust, in which Mephistopheles leads Faust to the witches' sabbath on the mountain. Heinrich Heine's Die Harzreise also describes an ascent. Walpurgis celebrations remain a major annual tourist event in the Harz.

History

A weather station has operated on the summit since 1895, and the peak is served since 1899 by the narrow-gauge Brocken Railway steam trains of the Harz Narrow Gauge Railways. During the Cold War the Brocken, close to the inner German border, was a closed military area used by East German and Soviet forces for surveillance and signals intelligence; its towers and transmitter remain landmarks. The summit reopened to the public in December 1989, shortly after the fall of the Berlin Wall.
