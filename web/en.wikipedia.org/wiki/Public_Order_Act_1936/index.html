Public Order Act 1936

Citation: 1 Edw. 8 & 1 Geo. 6 c. 6
Royal assent: 18 December 1936
Commencement: 1 January 1937
Status: Partially in force (as amended)

The Public Order Act 1936 (1 Edw. 8 & 1 Geo. 6 c. 6) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom passed to control extremist political movements in the 1930s, most notably the British Union of Fascists led by Oswald Mosley. It was introduced following widespread disorder, including the Battle of Cable Street in October 1936, in which anti-fascist demonstrators clashed with police protecting a fascist march through London's East End.

Provisions

  • Section 1 prohibited the wearing of political uniforms in public places or at public meetings, subject to exceptions such as for ceremonial occasions. This struck directly at the paramilitary appearance of the fascist "Blackshirts" and other uniformed movements.
  • Section 2 prohibited quasi-military organisations — bodies organised or trained so as to be capable of being employed for the use or display of physical force in promoting a political object.
  • Section 3 gave chief police officers, and in London the Commissioner, powers to impose conditions on public processions and, where serious disorder was feared, to seek bans on processions for up to three months.
  • Section 5, as enacted, made it an offence to use threatening, abusive or insulting words or behaviour in a public place or at a public meeting with intent to provoke a breach of the peace, or whereby a breach of the peace was likely to be occasioned.

Later history

Most of the Act's disorder offences were reworked by the Public Order Act 1986, which repealed section 5 and replaced it with new offences of fear or provocation of violence and intentional harassment, alarm or distress. Sections 1 and 2, however, remained in force: the ban on quasi-military organisations has occasionally been invoked against paramilitary-style groups, and the prohibition on political uniforms survives as the reason modern political movements in Britain do not march in uniform.
