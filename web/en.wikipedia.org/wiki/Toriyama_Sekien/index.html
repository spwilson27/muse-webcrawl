Toriyama Sekien (鳥山 石燕, 1712–1788) was an 18th-century Japanese scholar, poet, and artist of the ukiyo-e school, best known for his illustrated catalogues of yōkai — the ghosts, demons, and spirits of Japanese folklore.

Life

Sekien was a retainer of the Tokugawa shogunate who studied painting under Kanō-school masters and taught many pupils, including Kitagawa Utamaro, who would become one of the most celebrated ukiyo-e artists. His pen name "Sekien" means "stone swallow."

The yōkai catalogues

Beginning with Gazu Hyakki Yagyō ("The Illustrated Night Parade of a Hundred Demons", 1776), Sekien published four books that systematically depicted and described yōkai. Drawing on folklore, literature, and his own invention, he effectively created canonical visual forms for many creatures — some he may have invented outright — and sparked a lasting popular fascination with the supernatural in Edo-period Japan.

Influence

Sekien's encyclopaedic approach influenced later folklore studies and modern manga artists such as Shigeru Mizuki.
