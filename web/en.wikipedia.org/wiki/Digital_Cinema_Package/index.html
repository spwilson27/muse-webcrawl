Digital Cinema Package

Image: DCP distribution hard drive

A Digital Cinema Package (DCP) is a collection of digital files used to store and convey digital cinema audio, image, and data streams to cinemas. The term was popularised by Digital Cinema Initiatives, whose specification defines how feature films are distributed to and played back by DCI-compliant projection systems.

Contents

A DCP contains one or more compositions, each described by a Composition Playlist (CPL) referencing track files: an image track (JPEG 2000 codestreams, 2K or 4K, in X'Y'Z' colour space), one or more audio tracks (24-bit uncompressed PCM, up to 16 channels), and optional subtitle or closed-caption tracks. Track files are wrapped as MXF files, and asset maps and packing lists (in XML) describe the package contents. Feature-length DCPs are typically tens to a few hundred gigabytes in size.

Security and playback

Commercial DCPs are usually encrypted with AES, and each cinema server is issued a time-limited Key Delivery Message (KDM) that unlocks the content for specific playback windows. DCPs are delivered to theatres on ruggedised hard drives or by satellite and network transfer, and are ingested into the theatre management system before screening.
