Program evaluation and review technique

The program evaluation and review technique (PERT) is a statistical tool used in project management, designed to analyze and represent the tasks involved in completing a project, and in particular the time needed to complete each task and the minimum time needed to complete the whole project. It incorporates uncertainty by allowing activity durations to be estimated probabilistically.

History

PERT was developed in 1957–1958 by the United States Navy, with the consulting firm Booz Allen Hamilton, to manage the Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile program, coordinating thousands of contractors. A similar deterministic method, the critical path method (CPM), was developed independently at DuPont in the same period. PERT is credited with helping bring the Polaris program to completion ahead of schedule.

Method

A PERT chart represents a project as a network of events (nodes) connected by activities (arrows) with precedence relationships. For each activity, three time estimates are made: an optimistic time (O), a most likely time (M), and a pessimistic time (P). The expected duration is calculated as a weighted average, TE = (O + 4M + P) / 6, based on an assumed beta distribution. Analysis of the network identifies the critical path — the longest sequence of dependent activities — which determines the minimum project duration, and the slack (float) available on non-critical paths.

Applications and criticism

PERT is used for large, complex, non-repetitive projects such as construction, aerospace development, and major events. Criticisms include its reliance on subjective estimates, the assumption of independence between activities, and the tendency of the critical-path calculation to underestimate project duration when near-critical paths are ignored.
