Giambattista Basile (15 February 1566 – 23 February 1632) was an Italian poet, courtier, soldier and collector of fairy tales. His posthumously published collection Lo cunto de li cunti overo lo trattenemiento de peccerille ("The Tale of Tales, or Entertainment for Little Ones", 1634–1636), commonly called the Pentamerone, is the first great European collection of fairy tales and contains the earliest known versions of such famous stories as Cinderella, Sleeping Beauty, Rapunzel, Puss in Boots and Hansel and Gretel.

Life

Born near Naples, Basile spent much of his youth as a soldier of fortune, serving the Venetian Republic in Crete and elsewhere. Returning to Naples, he entered the service of several great patrons, including Don Marino II Caracciolo, Prince of Avellino, and later the Duke of Mantua, Ferdinando Gonzaga. His sister Adriana Basile was one of the most celebrated singers of the age. Basile published pastoral poetry, madrigals and courtly works in Italian, but his masterpiece, written in his native Neapolitan dialect, appeared only after his death, issued by his sister under the anagrammatic pseudonym Gian Alesio Abbattutis.

The Pentamerone

The Pentamerone is structured, like Boccaccio's Decameron and the frame tales of the East, as fifty tales told over five days by ten women, framed by the story of the princess Zoza. Written in a dense, exuberantly baroque Neapolitan, the tales draw on oral folklore and combine wonder with earthy humour and proverb-laden moral endings. Versions of Basile's stories influenced Charles Perrault and the Brothers Grimm; Basile's Cenerentola is the first recorded Cinderella with the fairy godmother and the lost slipper test, and his Sole, Luna e Talia is the darkest early Sleeping Beauty. Jacob Grimm praised the collection, and it was translated into German in the nineteenth century and into English by John Edward Taylor (1848) and later by Sir Richard Burton and Nancy Canepa.
