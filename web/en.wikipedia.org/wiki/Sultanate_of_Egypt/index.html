Sultanate of Egypt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Period: 1914–1922
Status: British protectorate
Capital: Cairo
Sultans: Hussein Kamel (1914–1917); Fuad I (1917–1922)

The Sultanate of Egypt was the short-lived protectorate that the United Kingdom imposed over Egypt between 1914 and 1922. It was established when Britain deposed the pro-Ottoman Khedive Abbas II upon the outbreak of the First World War and declared Egypt a British protectorate, severing the country's centuries-old (by then largely nominal) link with the Ottoman Empire.

History

Hussein Kamel, uncle of the deposed khedive, was installed as Sultan of Egypt in December 1914, the first ruler of Egypt to bear the title of sultan since the Ottoman conquest in 1517. Egyptian wartime grievances — requisitioning, inflation, and the conscription of labour for the Egyptian Labour Corps — fuelled nationalist resentment. After Hussein Kamel's death in 1917 he was succeeded by his brother Fuad I. The arrest and exile of the nationalist leader Saad Zaghloul in March 1919 triggered the Egyptian Revolution of 1919, a nationwide uprising that pushed Britain toward concessions.

End of the protectorate

Following the Milner Mission and continued unrest, Britain issued the Unilateral Declaration of Egyptian Independence on 28 February 1922, ending the protectorate while reserving several matters — imperial communications, defence, foreign interests, and the Sudan — to British discretion. Fuad I then assumed the title of King of Egypt, and the sultanate was replaced by the Kingdom of Egypt.
