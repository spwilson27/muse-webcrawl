The University of Georgia (UGA) is a public land-grant research university in Athens, Georgia. Chartered by the state of Georgia in 1785, it is the oldest public university in the United States and the flagship institution of the University System of Georgia. Classes began in 1801, and the first class graduated in 1804.

UGA comprises 18 schools and colleges, including the Franklin College of Arts and Sciences, the Terry College of Business, the School of Law, the College of Veterinary Medicine, and the Grady College of Journalism and Mass Communication, whose Peabody Awards program is internationally known. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity."

History

Abraham Baldwin, a signer of the U.S. Constitution, drafted the university's charter and served as its first president. The campus's oldest building, Old College (1806), still stands on North Campus, entered through the cast-iron Arch. The university closed during the Civil War and reopened in 1866. It admitted its first women in 1918 and was desegregated in 1961 when Charlayne Hunter and Hamilton Holmes enrolled.

Athletics

The Georgia Bulldogs compete in the Southeastern Conference. The football team, playing at Sanford Stadium, won national championships in 1942, 1980, and back-to-back College Football Playoff titles in the 2021 and 2022 seasons. The live bulldog mascot, Uga, is one of the most famous in college sports.
