Fréchet distribution

Named afterMaurice Fréchet
Supportx > 0
TypeExtreme value distribution (Type II)
ParametersShape α > 0; scale s > 0; location m

The Fréchet distribution, also known as the inverse Weibull distribution, is a continuous probability distribution obtained as the limiting distribution of the maxima of a sequence of independent random variables with heavy (power-law) tails. Named after Maurice Fréchet, it is the Type II member of the generalized extreme value family, alongside the Gumbel and (reversed) Weibull distributions.

Definition

Its cumulative distribution function is P(X ≤ x) = exp(−(x/s)−α) for x > 0, where α > 0 is a shape parameter and s > 0 a scale parameter. Moments exist only when the order of the moment is less than α, reflecting the distribution's fat tail.

Applications

The Fréchet distribution is used in extreme value analysis for phenomena with heavy-tailed maxima, including maximum rainfall and river discharges in hydrology, extreme wind speeds, financial loss modeling, and failure analysis through its inverse-Weibull form.
