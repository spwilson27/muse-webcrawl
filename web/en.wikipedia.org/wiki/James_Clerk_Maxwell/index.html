James Clerk Maxwell

James Clerk Maxwell (13 June 1831 – 5 November 1879) was a Scottish physicist whose most notable achievement was formulating the classical theory of electromagnetic radiation — Maxwell's equations — bringing together electricity, magnetism and light for the first time. Einstein later called this 'the most profound and the most fruitful that physics has experienced since the time of Newton'.

Maxwell also created the statistical theory of gases (Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution), produced the first durable colour photograph (1861), analysed Saturn's rings as swarms of particles, and worked on control theory (governors) and dimensional analysis. He founded and directed Cambridge's Cavendish Laboratory. He died of cancer at 48. His equations implied electromagnetic waves at light speed, predicting radio waves confirmed by Hertz a decade after his death.
