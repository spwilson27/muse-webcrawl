Cyperaceae

A sedge, showing the family's characteristic triangular stem.

The Cyperaceae are a family of graminoid (grass-like), monocotyledonous flowering plants known as sedges. The family is large, with some 5,500 known species in about 90 genera, the largest being the genus Carex with over 2,000 species. Sedges are distributed worldwide, with particular diversity in wetlands and in poor soils of temperate and cold regions.

Description

Sedges superficially resemble grasses and rushes but can often be distinguished by the mnemonic "sedges have edges": many have stems with triangular cross-sections. Their flowers are small and wind-pollinated, arranged in spikelets, and their leaves are typically arranged in three ranks. Many species spread by rhizomes and form dense stands in marshes, fens, and wet meadows.

Ecology and uses

Cyperaceae are ecologically dominant in many wetland ecosystems and provide food and cover for waterfowl and other wildlife. Economically important members include papyrus (Cyperus papyrus), used by the ancient Egyptians to make a writing material; water chestnut (Eleocharis dulcis); tiger nut (Cyperus esculentus); and various species used for thatching, weaving, and erosion control. A few, such as purple nutsedge (Cyperus rotundus), are among the world's worst agricultural weeds.
