Housing First

Housing First is a policy and program model for addressing homelessness that provides people with immediate, permanent housing without preconditions such as sobriety or participation in treatment, combined with voluntary supportive services. It contrasts with the older "staircase" or treatment-first model, in which people were expected to demonstrate "housing readiness" before receiving a permanent home.

Origins and principles

The model was developed in New York City in the early 1990s by psychologist Sam Tsemberis and the organization Pathways to Housing, initially for people with serious mental illness and substance use problems who were living on the streets. Its core principles include consumer choice, separation of housing from services, harm reduction, and the view of housing as a basic human right and a platform from which recovery can proceed.

Evidence and adoption

Randomized trials in the United States and Canada — notably Canada's five-city At Home/Chez Soi study — found that Housing First participants retained housing at much higher rates than those receiving treatment as usual, and that the model can offset costs through reduced use of emergency rooms, shelters and jails. Finland adopted Housing First as national policy and is widely cited for substantially reducing long-term homelessness. The approach has also been adopted in many US federal, state and city programs, including veteran homelessness initiatives.
