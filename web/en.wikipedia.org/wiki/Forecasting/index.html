Forecasting

Forecasting is the process of making predictions based on past and present data. Later these can be compared (resolved) against what happens. For example, a company might estimate its revenue in the next year, then compare it against the actual results creating a variance actual analysis. Prediction is a similar but more general term. Forecasting might refer to specific formal statistical methods employing time series, cross-sectional or longitudinal data, or alternatively to less formal judgmental methods or the process of prediction and assessment in general.

Applications

Forecasting is used in many fields: in business for demand planning, inventory and capacity decisions, and financial budgeting; in economics for predicting GDP growth, inflation, and unemployment; in meteorology as weather forecasting; in energy for load forecasting; in public health for anticipating disease outbreaks; and in technology and futurology for anticipating trends. Forecast horizons range from minutes (nowcasting) to decades (long-range scenarios).

Methods

Quantitative methods include time series models — such as moving averages, exponential smoothing, and ARIMA — and causal (econometric) models that relate the forecast variable to explanatory variables through regression. Qualitative methods, used when data are scarce or unreliable, include the Delphi method, market research, scenario building, and structured expert judgment. Modern practice increasingly uses machine learning methods and forecast combination, which typically improves accuracy by averaging several models.

Accuracy

Forecast accuracy is measured with error statistics such as mean absolute error (MAE), mean squared error, and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), ideally on out-of-sample data. Prediction intervals quantify forecast uncertainty. Large-scale evaluations, such as the Makridakis (M) competitions, have shaped best practice, showing that relatively simple statistical methods are often hard to beat and that combining forecasts improves robustness.
