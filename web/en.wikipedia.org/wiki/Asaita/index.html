Asaita (also spelled Asayta or Assayta) is a town in northeastern Ethiopia, in the Afar Region. Situated on the Awash River near the border with Djibouti, it was historically the capital of the Sultanate of Aussa, the principal Afar sultanate, and served as the first capital of the Afar Region before the regional capital was moved to Semera.

Geography

Asaita lies in the lower Awash valley at the edge of the Danakil Depression, one of the hottest inhabited regions on earth. The Awash River, which terminates in the nearby Lake Abhe on the Djibouti border, supports irrigated agriculture — historically cotton and sugar plantations — in an otherwise arid landscape. The town is connected by road to the port of Djibouti and to the regional capital Semera.

History

Asaita was the seat of the Mudaito dynasty of Aussa, whose sultans ruled the lower Awash and traded with the coast at Tadjoura and Zeila. In the imperial period the area was developed for commercial cotton growing. Under the Derg and after Ethiopian federalization in 1991, Asaita became the capital of the new Afar Region; because of its remoteness and limited infrastructure, the purpose-built town of Semera on the Awash–Assab highway was later chosen as the regional capital instead. Asaita remains an important market and administrative town for the surrounding Afar pastoralist communities.
