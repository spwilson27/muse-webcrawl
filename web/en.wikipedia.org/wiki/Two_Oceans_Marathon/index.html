The Two Oceans Marathon is a 56 km (35 mi) ultramarathon and 21 km half marathon held annually in Cape Town, South Africa, on the Saturday of the Easter weekend. Billed as "the world's most beautiful marathon", its route circles the Cape Peninsula, passing the shores historically associated with both the Atlantic and Indian Oceans that give the race its name.

Route

The ultra starts in Newlands and runs south through Muizenberg and Fish Hoek, along the coast to Noordhoek, then up over the spectacular Chapman's Peak Drive to Hout Bay. From there runners climb Constantia Nek before descending through the Southern Suburbs to the finish at the University of Cape Town in Rondebosch. An alternative route via Ou Kaapse Weg is used when Chapman's Peak is closed by rockfalls or fire. The half marathon follows a shorter course through the Southern Suburbs.

History

The race was first run in 1970, when it attracted only 26 runners as a training event for the Comrades Marathon. It has grown into one of South Africa's premier road races, with combined fields in the tens of thousands, and forms with Comrades the twin pillars of the country's ultrarunning calendar. The event was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Records and traditions

The men's course record of 3:03:44 was set by Thompson Magawana in 1988, the year he also won Comrades; the women's record of 3:29:29 was set by Frith van der Merwe in 1989, and her margin of victory remains legendary in South African running. Finishers within the seven-hour limit receive medals graded by finishing position and time, and runners who complete ten ultras earn the coveted blue number for life.
