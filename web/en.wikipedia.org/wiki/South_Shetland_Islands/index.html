South Shetland Islands

The South Shetland Islands are a group of Antarctic islands lying about 120 kilometres north of the Antarctic Peninsula, at roughly 62° south latitude. They extend some 540 kilometres from Smith Island and Low Island in the west to Elephant Island and Clarence Island in the east.

Geography and climate

The archipelago includes eleven main islands, among them King George Island, Livingston Island, Deception Island — the flooded caldera of an active volcano — and Elephant Island, famous as the refuge of Shackleton's Endurance crew in 1916. The climate is maritime Antarctic: cold, windy and humid, with extensive ice cover in winter.

History

The islands were sighted by William Smith in 1819 and were quickly exploited by sealers, whose activity nearly exterminated the fur seal populations. In the 20th century they hosted whaling stations, notably at Deception Island.

Research and claims

Today the islands host numerous scientific research stations, the largest concentration being on King George Island, whose Fildes Peninsula holds stations of many nations plus an airfield. The islands are claimed by Britain, Chile and Argentina, but under the Antarctic Treaty all territorial claims are held in abeyance and the islands are used only for peaceful research.
