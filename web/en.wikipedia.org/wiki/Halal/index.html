Halal

Halal (Arabic: حلال, ḥalāl) is an Arabic word that translates to "permissible" or "lawful" in English. In Islamic jurisprudence it refers to that which is permitted under Sharia, in contrast to haram, "forbidden". The term most commonly arises in connection with food and drink, and with the slaughter of animals for meat, known as dhabihah.

Food

For meat to be halal, the animal must be healthy at the time of slaughter, the name of God must be invoked, the throat must be cut with a sharp knife severing the windpipe, oesophagus and major blood vessels, and the blood must be drained. Certain foods are categorically forbidden (haram), including pork and its by-products, blood, carrion, animals slaughtered in the name of anything other than God, and intoxicants such as alcohol. Seafood is generally considered permissible. Opinions differ among schools of law and certifying bodies on details such as stunning before slaughter and machine slaughter, so halal certification standards vary by country and organisation.

Beyond food

The halal/haram distinction applies to many areas of life, including finance (prohibiting interest, riba), cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, clothing, and behaviour. A large global industry has developed around halal certification and halal tourism, serving the world's roughly two billion Muslims.
