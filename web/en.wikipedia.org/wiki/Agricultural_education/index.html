Agricultural education

Agricultural education is the teaching of agriculture, natural resources, and land management, delivered through formal schooling, vocational training, and extension services. It aims to prepare students for careers in farming, agribusiness, food production, environmental management, and related sciences, and to improve the productivity and sustainability of agricultural practice.

History

Systematic agricultural instruction grew in the nineteenth century with agricultural colleges and experiment stations. In the United States, the Morrill Land-Grant Acts of 1862 and 1890 created land-grant universities charged with teaching agriculture and the mechanical arts; the Smith-Hughes Act of 1917 funded vocational agriculture in public schools, and the Cooperative Extension Service carried research findings directly to farmers. Youth organisations such as 4-H and the FFA (Future Farmers of America) became integral parts of the system.

Content and delivery

Curricula span crop and animal science, soil and water management, agricultural economics, mechanics, food science, and environmental conservation. Instruction commonly combines classroom study, supervised practical experience on farms or in laboratories, and leadership development. At the tertiary level, agricultural universities worldwide conduct teaching and research supporting national food security and rural development.
