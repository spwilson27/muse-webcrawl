Choke (firearms)

A choke is a tapered constriction of the bore of a shotgun barrel at the muzzle end. Chokes are used to shape the spread of shot in flight: a tighter choke keeps the shot column together longer, producing a denser pattern at longer range, while a more open choke spreads the shot sooner for closer targets. Chokes may be fixed (machined into the barrel) or interchangeable screw-in tubes.

Function

As the shot charge leaves the muzzle, aerodynamic forces begin to disperse the pellets. The choke's gradual constriction — the difference between bore diameter and muzzle diameter, typically only a few hundredths of an inch — compresses the shot column slightly before release, reducing the rate of spread. Pattern performance is usually expressed as the percentage of pellets striking within a 30-inch circle at 40 yards (or 25 yards for open chokes).

Common constrictions

For a 12-gauge barrel, the standard designations, from most open to tightest, are approximately:

  • Cylinder — no constriction; about 40% of pellets in the circle at 40 yards.
  • Skeet — about 0.005 in constriction, for short-range clay targets.
  • Improved cylinder — about 0.010 in; roughly 50% pattern.
  • Modified — about 0.020 in; roughly 60% pattern, a common all-round hunting choke.
  • Improved modified — about 0.025–0.030 in.
  • Full — about 0.035–0.040 in; roughly 70% pattern, for long-range shots such as trap shooting and turkey hunting.
  • Extra full / turkey — still tighter, for maximum density at extended range.

History and variations

Muzzle constriction was experimented with in the 18th and 19th centuries; demand rose with wildfowling and, later, clay target sports. In the 20th century, adjustable "poly-chokes" and then interchangeable choke tubes (popularised from the 1960s–1980s) let a single barrel serve multiple purposes. Specialised designs include rifled choke tubes, which impart slight spin to stabilise slugs and spread buckshot for defence, and "spread" chokes for very close work. Using hard non-toxic shot such as steel in tight lead-only chokes can damage a barrel, so choke tubes are rated by material.
