ATSC

ATSC may refer to the Advanced Television Systems Committee, an international nonprofit organization that develops standards for digital television, or to the ATSC standards themselves — the digital terrestrial television broadcasting system used primarily in North America and South Korea, succeeding the analog NTSC system.

The standards

The original ATSC standard (ATSC 1.0), adopted in the United States in 1996, uses 8VSB modulation in a 6 MHz channel and MPEG-2 video, carrying one HDTV program or several standard-definition subchannels along with Dolby Digital (AC-3) audio. Full-power US stations completed the transition from analog in June 2009. The next-generation system, ATSC 3.0 ("NextGen TV"), is based on OFDM and IP transport and has been deployed voluntarily in the US and adopted in South Korea since 2017.

The organization

Founded in 1982 by industry groups to coordinate US advanced television work, the committee's members include broadcasters, equipment manufacturers, and technology companies. It maintains standards through specialized technology groups and publishes recommended practices for transmission and receivers.
