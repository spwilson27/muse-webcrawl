Pyrimidine

IUPAC namePyrimidine
FormulaC4H4N2
Molar mass80.09 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid / white solid
Melting point20–22 °C
Boiling point123–124 °C

Pyrimidine is an aromatic heterocyclic organic compound similar to pyridine. It is one of the three diazines — six-membered rings with two nitrogen atoms — with the nitrogen atoms at positions 1 and 3 of the ring. The other diazines are pyrazine (nitrogens at 1 and 4) and pyridazine (nitrogens at 1 and 2).

Pyrimidine bases

Pyrimidine derivatives are of fundamental biological importance. Three pyrimidine bases occur in nucleic acids: cytosine, found in both DNA and RNA; thymine, found in DNA; and uracil, found in RNA. In standard base pairing, these pyrimidines form hydrogen bonds with complementary purines: cytosine pairs with guanine, while thymine and uracil pair with adenine. Other important pyrimidines include the vitamins thiamine (vitamin B1) and riboflavin analogues, barbiturates, and many pharmaceutical agents such as the anticancer drug fluorouracil.

Properties and synthesis

Pyrimidine itself is a planar, aromatic molecule that is less basic than pyridine because the second ring nitrogen withdraws electron density. It can be prepared by the catalytic dechlorination of 2,4-dichloropyrimidine, or industrially by condensation reactions of 1,3-dicarbonyl compounds with amidines — a general route to substituted pyrimidines.
