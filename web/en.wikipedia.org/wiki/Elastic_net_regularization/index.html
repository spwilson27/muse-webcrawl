Elastic net regularization

In statistics and machine learning, the elastic net is a regularized regression method that linearly combines the L1 and L2 penalties of the lasso and ridge regression methods. It was introduced by Hui Zou and Trevor Hastie in a 2005 paper, "Regularization and variable selection via the elastic net".

Formulation

Given data with predictors X and response y, the elastic net estimator minimises the residual sum of squares plus a penalty mixing the two norms:

minβ ‖y − Xβ‖² + λ [ α‖β‖₁ + (1 − α)‖β‖²₂ / 2 ],

where λ ≥ 0 controls overall shrinkage and α ∈ [0, 1] balances the lasso (α = 1) and ridge (α = 0) components.

Motivation

The lasso performs variable selection but behaves erratically with groups of highly correlated predictors — tending to pick one arbitrarily — and cannot select more predictors than observations. Ridge regression handles correlation smoothly but performs no selection. The elastic net inherits the lasso's sparsity while exhibiting a "grouping effect": strongly correlated variables tend to enter or leave the model together, with similar coefficients. Zou and Hastie originally proposed a two-stage "naive elastic net" with a rescaling correction; later implementations, such as the coordinate-descent algorithm in the glmnet package, solve the penalised problem directly.
