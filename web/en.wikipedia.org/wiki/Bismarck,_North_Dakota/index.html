Bismarck is the capital city of the U.S. state of North Dakota and the county seat of Burleigh County. It is the state's second-most populous city, after Fargo. The city was founded in 1872 on the east bank of the Missouri River and named in 1873 for German chancellor Otto von Bismarck, in the hope of attracting German investment in the Northern Pacific Railway.

History

The area was long inhabited by the Mandan and later the Hidatsa and Arikara peoples; Lewis and Clark passed through in 1804–1805. The settlement was first named Missouri Crossing, then Edwinton, before being renamed Bismarck. It became capital of Dakota Territory in 1883 and state capital of North Dakota at statehood in 1889. The Art Deco North Dakota State Capitol, completed in 1934 after fire destroyed the original capitol in 1930, is at 19 storeys the tallest building in the state.

Geography and climate

Bismarck lies on the eastern bank of the Missouri River, directly across from Mandan, its sister city. It has a humid continental climate with cold, snowy winters and warm summers, and is known for wide temperature extremes. The Bismarck–Mandan metropolitan area has a population of roughly 130,000.

Economy

State government is the largest employer, followed by healthcare systems such as Sanford Health and CHI St. Alexius Health. Bismarck is a regional retail, medical and transportation hub for central North Dakota, served by Interstate 94, the BNSF Railway and Bismarck Municipal Airport. Recreational attractions include the Dakota Zoo, Sertoma Park along the Missouri, and nearby Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park.
