Enoshima Shrine

Enoshima Shrine (江島神社, Enoshima-jinja) is a Shinto shrine on the island of Enoshima in Fujisawa, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan. Dedicated to the goddess Benzaiten (in her Shinto form associated with the three Munakata goddesses), it has for centuries been one of the principal centres of Benzaiten worship in Japan, drawing pilgrims who pray for fortune, prosperity, the performing arts and safe passage at sea.

Layout and history

The shrine consists of three separate shrines spread over the small island:

  • Hetsumiya, the lower shrine, enshrining Tagitsuhime-no-mikoto; its present hall was rebuilt in 1679.
  • Nakatsumiya, the middle shrine, founded in 853 and enshrining Ichikishimahime-no-mikoto; the current building dates from 1689.
  • Okutsumiya, the inner shrine, enshrining Tagirihime-no-mikoto, traditionally founded in 1206 by Minamoto no Sanetomo.

Tradition holds that the goddess was first enshrined in the caves of the island in 552, at the command of Emperor Kinmei. Under the syncretic Buddhism-Shinto practice of the medieval period, Enoshima's deity was identified with Benzaiten, frequently depicted holding a biwa lute; the shrine preserves a famed statue of Myōon Benzaiten and the eight-armed Happi Benzaiten. During the Kamakura period the island was a major pilgrimage destination, and the approach over the causeway from Katase remains lined with shops and inns today. With the Meiji government's separation of Shinto and Buddhism, the site formally became a Shinto shrine.

Festivals and sights

Enoshima Shrine is counted among the great Benzaiten shrines of Japan alongside those at Miyajima and Chikubushima. A treasure hall exhibits shrine artefacts, and coins washed in the Zeniarai ("money-washing") pond are said to multiply. The island's caves (Iwaya), associated with the goddess's legend and with a visiting dragon deity, form part of the pilgrimage circuit.
