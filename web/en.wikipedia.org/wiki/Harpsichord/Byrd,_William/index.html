William Byrd

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

William Byrd (c. 1540 – 4 July 1623) was an English composer of the Renaissance. Widely regarded as one of the greatest composers of his era, he studied under Thomas Tallis and became organist and choirmaster of Lincoln Cathedral and later a Gentleman of the Chapel Royal. His output spans sacred Latin motets and Anglican service music, consort and keyboard music, and some of the finest English madrigals.

Keyboard music and the virginalists

Byrd was a foundational figure of the English school of harpsichord and virginals music. His keyboard works — pavans, galliards, fantasias, variations and character pieces such as "The Bells" and "The Carman's Whistle" — were gathered in collections including Parthenia (1612/13), the first printed collection of English keyboard music, in which his music appeared alongside that of John Bull and Orlando Gibbons, and in the manuscript My Ladye Nevells Booke and the Fitzwilliam Virginal Book. His keyboard writing, with its dance rhythms, variation technique and cross-rhythms, shaped generations of composers.

Later life and legacy

A lifelong Roman Catholic in Protestant England, Byrd composed three Latin Masses and the two books of Gradualia for clandestine Catholic worship while also writing for the Anglican liturgy. He died in 1623 at Stondon Massey, Essex, and is remembered as the pre-eminent composer of Elizabethan and Jacobean music.
