Just Fontaine

Died1 March 2023 (aged 89), Toulouse, France
PositionStriker

Just Fontaine (18 August 1933 – 1 March 2023) was a French footballer who played as a striker. He is best remembered for scoring 13 goals in a single FIFA World Cup tournament, at the 1958 World Cup in Sweden — a record that still stands.

Club career

Born in Marrakech, Fontaine began his career with USM Casablanca before moving to Nice in 1953, where he won the French championship in 1956. He then joined Stade de Reims, winning three league titles and reaching the 1959 European Cup final, and finishing as the competition's top scorer. A recurring leg injury forced his retirement in 1962 at age 28.

International career

Fontaine scored 30 goals in just 21 appearances for France. At the 1958 World Cup he scored in every match, including four goals against West Germany in the third-place play-off, as France finished third. His 13-goal haul remains the single-tournament record, ahead of Gerd Müller's 10 in 1970.

Later life

After retiring, Fontaine briefly managed the French national team and coached clubs including Paris Saint-Germain, whom he led to promotion. In 2004 Pelé named him among the 125 greatest living footballers. He died in Toulouse on 1 March 2023.
