Laurence Tisch

Born: 1923, Brooklyn, New York
Died: 2003
Known for: Co-founder of Loews Corporation; CEO of CBS

Laurence Alan Tisch (1923–2003) was an American businessman, investor and philanthropist. With his brother Preston Robert (Bob) Tisch, he built Loews Corporation from a hotel business into a diversified conglomerate with interests in hotels, theatres, insurance (CNA), tobacco (Lorillard), energy and broadcasting.

Career

The brothers began by buying and running hotels in New Jersey and Florida after World War II, then acquired the Loew's theatre chain in 1959–60, retaining the name though shedding most theatres. Laurence Tisch became famous as a value investor and, from 1986, as the rescuer-turned-CEO of CBS: after Loews accumulated a large stake and helped repel takeover bids, Tisch became CEO and undertook deep cost cuts and asset sales, a tenure criticised for shrinking CBS News. CBS was sold to Westinghouse Electric in 1995.

Philanthropy

Tisch gave major support to New York University (whose Tisch School of the Arts bears the family name, honouring his brother Preston), the Metropolitan Museum of Art, NYU Langone's Tisch Hospital, and the Tisch Zoo at Central Park. He died in November 2003, shortly after his brother.
