Leitner system

The Leitner system is a widely used method of efficiently using flashcards that was proposed by the German science journalist Sebastian Leitner in the 1970s, described in his book So lernt man lernen ("How to learn to learn", 1972). It is a simple implementation of the principle of spaced repetition, where cards are reviewed at increasing intervals.

Method

In the Leitner system, flashcards are sorted into groups (compartments of a card box) according to how well the learner knows each one. The learner tries to recall the solution written on a flashcard. If they succeed, the card is promoted to the next group, where it will be reviewed less frequently. If they fail, the card is demoted — in the original system, sent back to the first group — so that it will be reviewed again soon.

A common arrangement uses three to five boxes:

  • Box 1 — new and failed cards; reviewed every session (daily).
  • Box 2 — cards answered correctly once; reviewed every two days.
  • Box 3 — cards answered correctly twice; reviewed every four days.
  • Further boxes double the interval, up to weekly, monthly, and finally "retired" cards that have been thoroughly learned.

Advantages and use

The system automatically focuses study time on the material the learner finds hardest, because difficult cards cycle through the first box frequently while easy cards are seen only occasionally. It requires no technology and has been applied especially to vocabulary learning in foreign languages. Modern spaced-repetition software such as Anki and SuperMemo generalises the Leitner system with algorithmic scheduling of individual items based on a model of forgetting.
