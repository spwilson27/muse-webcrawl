Tetraethyllead

Tetraethyllead
Formula: Pb(C₂H₅)₄
Use: Antiknock additive in leaded petrol
Discovered as additive: 1921, Thomas Midgley Jr.
Status: Banned for road vehicles worldwide by 2021

Tetraethyllead (TEL) is an organolead compound once widely added to petrol as an antiknock agent, sold as "leaded petrol". Despite known neurotoxicity, it was marketed from the 1920s by General Motors, DuPont and Standard Oil interests.

Health and environment

Combustion spread lead across the environment, raising blood-lead levels and harming children's cognitive development. Geochemist Clair Patterson's work exposed contamination and drove regulation.

Phase-out

The US phased it out from the 1970s (also to protect catalytic converters); Algeria, the last country for road fuel, ended it in 2021. TEL remains in some aviation gasoline (avgas).
