Site reliability engineering

Site reliability engineering (SRE) is a discipline in the field of software engineering and IT infrastructure engineering that uses software engineering approaches to build systems and create solutions to problems in operational aspects. Site reliability engineers bridge the gap between platform developers and IT operations, providing standardised solutions to software deployments and deployments monitoring.

History

The field of site reliability engineering originated at Google in the early 2000s. The concept was created by Ben Treynor Sloss, who joined Google in 2003 and founded a team of software engineers to run a production environment. The SRE team is credited with pioneering practices such as error budgets, service level objectives (SLOs), and the elimination of toil through automation. Google published the influential book Site Reliability Engineering: How Google Runs Production Systems in 2016, which helped popularise the discipline across the technology industry.

Principles

SRE applies software engineering principles to operations problems. Key practices include defining service level indicators (SLIs) and service level objectives, managing risk through error budgets, automating repetitive operational work, blameless postmortems after incidents, and treating operations as a software problem. SRE teams typically aim to spend no more than half their time on manual operational work ("toil"), devoting the remainder to engineering projects that improve reliability.

Roles and responsibilities

Site reliability engineers are responsible for the availability, latency, performance, efficiency, change management, monitoring, emergency response, and capacity planning of services. They work closely with product development teams, sharing ownership of production systems and often participating in on-call rotations.
