GarageBand


The GarageBand multitrack editing interface on macOS.

GarageBand is a digital audio workstation (DAW) developed by Apple for macOS, iPadOS, and iOS devices that allows users to create music and podcasts. It is designed to enable users with no musical training to compose original music using thousands of pre-made MIDI keyboards and loops. Announced by Steve Jobs at Macworld in January 2004 as part of the iLife suite, GarageBand grew out of technology from the German company Emagic, which Apple had acquired in 2002.

Features

GarageBand offers multitrack audio and MIDI recording, virtual software instruments, guitar amplifiers and stompbox effects, thousands of Apple Loops, and tools such as Drummer, which generates realistic drum tracks. Projects can be opened in Apple's professional DAW, Logic Pro. The iOS version adds touch instruments and Live Loops for grid-based performance.

Notable uses

Despite being aimed at amateurs, GarageBand has been used in professional releases: Nine Inch Nails released multitrack GarageBand files of singles for fans to remix, and songs recorded largely in GarageBand include Grimes' early work and Steve Lacy's productions for Kendrick Lamar and his own albums, recorded on an iPhone.

Reception

The program has been praised for making music production accessible and has been credited with helping launch the home-recording and podcasting movements of the 2000s.
