Erga omnes

Erga omnes is a Latin phrase meaning "towards all" or "in relation to everyone". In international law, obligations erga omnes are duties that a state owes to the international community as a whole, so that every state has a legal interest in their observance and may invoke responsibility for their breach, even if it is not the state specially injured. The concept marks an exception to the traditional bilateral structure of international obligations and gives legal expression to the idea that some norms protect values common to all humanity.

Origin: Barcelona Traction

The phrase entered modern jurisprudence in the International Court of Justice's 1970 judgment in the Barcelona Traction case (Belgium v. Spain). Although Belgium lost on other grounds, the Court distinguished obligations owed to another state from those owed "towards the international community as a whole", which "by their very nature... are the concern of all States". As examples it cited the outlawing of aggression and genocide and the principles and rules protecting basic human rights, including protection from slavery and racial discrimination.

Scope and consequences

Obligations erga omnes are closely related to peremptory norms (jus cogens), though the concepts are distinct: jus cogens concerns the hierarchy and non-derogability of norms, while erga omnes concerns the scope of who may invoke them. The International Law Commission's Articles on State Responsibility (2001) provide (Articles 42 and 48) that a state other than an injured state may invoke responsibility where the obligation breached is owed to the international community as a whole, and may seek cessation and, in some views, reparation in the interest of the beneficiaries. The Court has since applied the idea in cases concerning self-determination (the East Timor and Wall opinions), the Genocide Convention (whose obligations it called erga omnes partes — owed among the parties to the treaty), and the Convention against Torture (Belgium v. Senegal, 2012).

Related concepts

A treaty obligation owed to all other parties collectively is sometimes called erga omnes partes. In private law, rights effective against the whole world (such as property rights) are contrasted with rights in personam effective only against specific persons.
