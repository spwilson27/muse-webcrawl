Speech intelligibility

In speech communication, intelligibility is a measure of how comprehensible speech is in given conditions — the proportion of a message that a listener can correctly understand. It is a key quantity in acoustics, audiology, telecommunications, and human–computer interaction.

Factors

Intelligibility depends on the signal-to-noise ratio, reverberation time, distortion, the speaker's clarity and rate, the listener's hearing and familiarity with the language, and contextual cues. Background noise especially degrades intelligibility for hearing-impaired and non-native listeners.

Measurement

Subjective tests present listeners with words or sentences (such as rhyme tests or the matrix sentence test) and score the percentage correct. Objective predictors include the Speech Transmission Index (STI), the Speech Intelligibility Index (SII), and STOI, which estimate intelligibility from the acoustic signal without human listeners.
