A master's degree is a postgraduate academic degree awarded by universities or colleges upon completion of a course of study demonstrating mastery of a specific field or area of professional practice. It usually requires previous study at the bachelor's level, and may be either a standalone qualification or part of a longer programme.

Types

Common master's degrees include the Master of Arts (MA), Master of Science (MS or MSc), Master of Business Administration (MBA), Master of Engineering (MEng), Master of Laws (LLM), and Master of Public Health (MPH). Programmes may be research-based, requiring a substantial thesis, course-based, or a mixture of both; many include an internship or capstone project.

History

The master's degree originated in the medieval European universities, where the magister was licensed to teach. In the Bologna process framework adopted across Europe, the master's is the second-cycle qualification, typically requiring 90–120 ECTS credits after a bachelor's degree.

Admission and study

Admission normally requires a relevant bachelor's degree with a satisfactory grade average. Full-time programmes commonly last one to two years; part-time and professional programmes may take three years or more. Graduates may proceed to doctoral study or enter professions where the degree is a standard credential.
