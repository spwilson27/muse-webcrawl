Abilene paradox

Coined byJerry B. Harvey
Introduced1974 article, "The Abilene Paradox and Other Meditations on Management"
FieldOrganisational behaviour, group dynamics
Also called"Taking a trip to Abilene"

The Abilene paradox is a group dynamic in which a group of people collectively decides on a course of action that none of its individual members actually wants, because each member mistakenly believes their own preferences run counter to the group's and therefore does not raise objections. It is a classic illustration of the failure to manage agreement, as distinct from the better-known problem of managing conflict.

The anecdote

Management scholar Jerry B. Harvey introduced the concept in 1974 with a story from his own family. On a hot afternoon in Coleman, Texas, his father-in-law cheerfully suggested driving 53 miles to Abilene for dinner. Each family member — none of whom wanted to go, and who would rather have stayed home playing dominoes — politely assented, each supposing the others were enthusiastic. After an unpleasant four-hour trip in a sweltering, dust-storm-blown car, they returned home and one by one admitted they had never wanted to go, blaming each other for the decision nobody had made.

Explanation

Harvey attributed the paradox to "action anxiety", a negative fantasy about the consequences of speaking honestly (ridicule, exclusion, conflict), which leads members to misrepresent their preferences, causing the group to act on false information about its own collective desires, generating frustration and counterproductive outcomes. Harvey argued the phenomenon is common in organisations, where employees publicly endorse plans they privately consider unwise. The paradox is related to, but distinct from, groupthink (in which dissent is suppressed amid genuine internal consensus-building pressure) and pluralistic ignorance.

Applications

The story became a staple of management training (including a popular training film, The Abilene Paradox), invoked whenever teams embark on projects nobody believes in — "Are we on the road to Abilene?" serves as a prompt to surface true opinions.
