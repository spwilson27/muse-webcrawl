The Australian Council of Trade Unions (ACTU) is the largest peak body representing trade union organisations in Australia. Founded in 1927, it coordinates more than forty affiliated unions covering well over a million workers, advocates in national wage cases and industrial relations policy, and campaigns on workplace rights, superannuation and social policy. It is based at Melbourne's historic Trades Hall.

History

The ACTU was formed in 1927 as a national federation following earlier inter-union congresses. Under leaders such as Albert Monk and, later, Bob Hawke — president from 1969 to 1980 before becoming Prime Minister — it became a central actor in Australia's centralised wage-fixing system. In 1983 the ACTU entered the Prices and Incomes Accord with the Hawke Labor government, trading wage restraint for social-wage improvements including Medicare and award superannuation. It led the successful "Your Rights at Work" campaign against the Howard government's WorkChoices laws in 2005–07, widely credited with contributing to that government's defeat.

Structure and role

The ACTU is governed by a congress held every three years and an executive of affiliate leaders; its president and secretary are its public faces — recent presidents include Ged Kearney and Michele O'Neil, and Sally McManus became secretary in 2017. State and territory trades and labour councils are affiliated branches. The ACTU represents Australian workers internationally through the International Trade Union Confederation and campaigns on issues including wage growth, insecure work, gender equity and workplace safety. Union density in Australia has declined from around half the workforce in the 1970s to roughly one in eight employees in the 2020s, a central strategic challenge for the movement.
