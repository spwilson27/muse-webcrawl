The Battle of Alexander at Issus

The Battle of Alexander at Issus (German: Alexanderschlacht) is a 1529 oil painting by the German artist Albrecht Altdorfer, a pioneer of landscape painting. It depicts the Battle of Issus in 333 BC, in which Alexander the Great defeated Darius III of Persia.

ArtistAlbrecht Altdorfer
Year1529
MediumOil on panel
Dimensions158.4 cm × 120.3 cm
LocationAlte Pinakothek, Munich

Description

Commissioned by Duke William IV of Bavaria, the painting shows a vast panorama in which thousands of tiny soldiers clash beneath a dramatic sky of setting sun and rising moon. A floating tablet in the sky bears a Latin inscription describing Alexander's victory. The landscape stretches from the Mediterranean and Cyprus to the Nile delta and the Red Sea, compressing the known world into a single cosmic view.

Interpretation

The painting was part of a cycle of historical battle scenes for the Munich Residenz. Contemporary viewers connected Alexander's triumph over the Persians with Christian Europe's struggle against the Ottoman Turks, who besieged Vienna in the year the painting was completed.
