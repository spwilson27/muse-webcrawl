Esgaroth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Esgaroth, also known as Lake-town, is a fictional town in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. It is a town of Men built on wooden pilings in the middle of the Long Lake, south of the Lonely Mountain (Erebor) and east of Mirkwood. It features prominently in The Hobbit (1937).

Description

Esgaroth was a trading community: goods from the Woodland Realm of the Elves came down the Forest River in barrels, and trade flowed south down the River Running (Celduin) to Dorwinion and beyond. The town was built entirely on wood, standing on huge stilts driven into the lake bed, connected to the shore by a great wooden bridge. Building on the water protected the town from attack — and, the townsfolk believed, from the dragon Smaug in the mountain to the north. The town was governed by a Master, elected from among its leading merchants, assisted by councillors.

The name Esgaroth is explained in Tolkien's etymologies as a name in the language of the Northmen of the region, possibly related to a word for reeds or sedges growing at the lake's edge. The town had an earlier predecessor on the shore, destroyed when Smaug took Erebor in T.A. 2770.

Role in The Hobbit

Thorin Oakenshield's company is received with enthusiasm in Lake-town when they arrive in barrels floated down the Forest River; the Master publicly feasts them while privately doubting their quest. After the company departs for the Lonely Mountain, Bilbo Baggins provokes Smaug, who attacks Esgaroth in revenge and burns it. Bard the Bowman, a grim town guardsman descended from Girion, Lord of Dale, kills Smaug with his black arrow, striking the one bare patch in the dragon's armour.

The survivors, led by Bard, make a camp on the shore and demand recompense from Thorin, setting in motion the quarrel that leads to the Battle of the Five Armies. After the battle, Esgaroth is rebuilt with treasure from the mountain, and Bard refounds the neighbouring kingdom of Dale.
