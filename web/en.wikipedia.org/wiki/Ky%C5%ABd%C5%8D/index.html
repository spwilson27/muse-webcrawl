Kyūdō

Kyūdō is the Japanese martial art of archery, descended from the kyūjutsu ("art of archery") practised by the samurai class. It emphasises meditative form and spiritual discipline alongside accuracy.

Practice

Practitioners use the yumi, an asymmetrical longbow over two metres tall, and shoot at targets typically 28 metres away in standard practice (kinteki), or 60 metres in long-distance shooting (enteki). The shooting sequence, the hassetsu, consists of eight stages performed with prescribed form. Kyūdō is governed internationally by the International Kyudo Federation.

Philosophy

Kyūdō teaching stresses the unity of body, bow, and mind; the ideal shot is seen as an expression of correct form rather than mere hitting of the target, a view expressed in the motto "shin, zen, bi" (truth, goodness, beauty).
