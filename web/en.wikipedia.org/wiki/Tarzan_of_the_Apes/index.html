Tarzan of the Apes

Tarzan of the Apes is a 1912 novel by American writer Edgar Rice Burroughs, the first in a series about the title character Tarzan. It was first published in the pulp magazine The All-Story in October 1912 and in book form in 1914. The story follows John Clayton, Viscount Greystoke, orphaned in the African jungle and raised by great apes, who grows up to become lord of the jungle and eventually encounters other Europeans, including Jane Porter.

Plot summary

After a mutiny, Lord and Lady Greystoke are marooned on the coast of Africa. Their infant son survives them and, following the death of his mother and the killing of his father by the ape Kerchak, is adopted by the she-ape Kala. Named Tarzan ("White Skin" in the ape tongue), he teaches himself to read from the books in his parents' cabin and grows into a formidable hunter who eventually kills Kerchak and becomes king of the apes. When another party of castaways — including Jane Porter, whom he loves — arrives, Tarzan protects them, and at the novel's close follows Jane to America, renouncing his claim to her hand when he learns she is engaged to his own cousin, the current Lord Greystoke.

Legacy

Burroughs wrote some two dozen Tarzan sequels. The character became one of the most recognisable in popular culture, spawning films from the 1918 silent version and the Johnny Weissmuller series onward, as well as comics, radio and television. Modern criticism has examined the novel's imperial and racial assumptions alongside its enduring adventure appeal.
