B major is a major scale based on B, with the pitches B, C♯, D♯, E, F♯, G♯ and A♯. Its key signature has five sharps. Its relative minor is G-sharp minor and its parallel minor is B minor.

Characteristics

The key is enharmonically equivalent to C-flat major, which has seven flats and is rarely used. Because of its five sharps, B major is less common than neighbouring keys, though it appears in works where modulation from E major or F-sharp major is natural. On string instruments it can resonate brightly, and several composers have associated it with strong or radiant moods in historical key-character descriptions.

Notable compositions

Examples include the second movement of Beethoven's Symphony No. 4 (in the related context), Schubert's Piano Sonata in B major, D 575, and Chopin's Nocturne in B major, Op. 9 No. 3.
