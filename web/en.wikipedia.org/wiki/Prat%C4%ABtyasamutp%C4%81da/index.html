Pratītyasamutpāda

Pratītyasamutpāda (Sanskrit; Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda), commonly translated as dependent origination or dependent arising, is a central doctrine of Buddhism. It holds that all phenomena arise in dependence upon other phenomena: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also ceases to exist." Nothing has independent, permanent self-existence (svabhāva), a teaching closely linked to emptiness (śūnyatā) and no-self (anattā).

The twelve nidānas

The doctrine is classically presented as a chain of twelve links (nidānas) explaining the arising of suffering and rebirth: ignorance, volitional formations, consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense bases, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and ageing-and-death. Reversing the chain — removing ignorance — is presented as the path to liberation.

Interpretations

Different Buddhist schools interpret the chain differently: as a process spanning three lifetimes, as moment-to-moment psychological processes, or as a statement of radical interdependence. In Mahāyāna thought, especially in Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka, pratītyasamutpāda is equated with emptiness itself.

Significance

Dependent origination is considered one of the Buddha's most distinctive insights, underpinning the Four Noble Truths and distinguishing Buddhist metaphysics from both eternalism and nihilism.
