Georg Henrik von Wright (14 June 1916 – 16 June 2003) was a Finnish philosopher specialising in philosophical logic, philosophy of action, and the philosophy of Ludwig Wittgenstein, whose literary executor and successor at Cambridge he became.

Career

Von Wright studied at the University of Helsinki and at Cambridge, where he came under Wittgenstein's influence. In 1948 he succeeded Wittgenstein as professor of philosophy at Cambridge, returning to Helsinki in 1951. He later served as chancellor of Åbo Akademi University and as president of the Academy of Finland.

Work

His 1951 paper "Deontic Logic" founded the modern study of the logic of norms and obligations. His books include Norm and Action (1963), Explanation and Understanding (1971), which defended a distinction between causal explanation in science and understanding in the humanities, and The Varieties of Goodness (1963). He also edited several of Wittgenstein's posthumous works.
