Chemical engineering is the branch of engineering that designs and operates processes converting raw materials into useful products safely, economically and sustainably.

Scope

Chemical engineers apply chemistry, physics, mathematics and biology to industries including petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, food, semiconductors, water treatment and energy. The discipline is organised around unit operations - distillation, filtration, heat exchange, absorption - and unit processes such as reaction and polymerisation.

History

George Edward Davis systematised the field in Britain in the 1880s; MIT offered an early course, and the American Institute of Chemical Engineers was founded in 1908. The concept of unit operations (Little, 1915) and later transport phenomena unified its teaching.

Modern practice

Today the field includes process simulation, safety and risk analysis, biochemical and materials engineering, carbon capture and green chemistry.
