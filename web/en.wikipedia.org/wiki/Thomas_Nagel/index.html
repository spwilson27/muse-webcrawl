Thomas Nagel

Thomas Nagel (born July 4, 1937) is an American philosopher. His work focuses on the philosophy of mind, political philosophy, and ethics. He is University Professor of Philosophy and Law Emeritus at New York University. Nagel is best known for his 1974 essay "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?", which argued that consciousness has an irreducibly subjective character that physicalist theories cannot capture, and for his critique of reductive accounts of reason and value.

Life and career

Nagel was born in Belgrade to a Jewish family and spent his childhood in the United States. He studied at Cornell, Oxford (under J. L. Austin and others), and Harvard, where he received his PhD in 1963 under John Rawls. He taught at Princeton from 1966 to 1980 before moving to NYU, where he helped build one of the leading philosophy departments in the world.

Philosophy of mind

In "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) Nagel argued that an organism has conscious mental states if and only if there is something that it is like to be that organism — a subjective point of view that cannot be fully described by objective physical facts. The essay became one of the most widely cited challenges to physicalism in the philosophy of mind. His book The View from Nowhere (1986) explores the tension between subjective and objective standpoints across philosophy.

Ethics and politics

Nagel's The Possibility of Altruism (1970) defends a Kantian account of moral motivation; Mortal Questions (1979) collects essays on death, luck, and the absurd; and Equality and Partiality (1991) examines the conflict between personal and impersonal values in political theory. His 2012 book Mind and Cosmos, which criticised materialist neo-Darwinian accounts of consciousness and cognition, proved controversial.
