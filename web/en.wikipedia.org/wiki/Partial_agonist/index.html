Partial agonist

In pharmacology, a partial agonist is a drug that binds to and activates a given receptor, but produces only a partial physiological response compared with a full agonist, even at full receptor occupancy. Partial agonists have intermediate intrinsic efficacy.

Behaviour

Because they occupy receptors while producing submaximal activation, partial agonists can act as functional antagonists in the presence of a full agonist: they compete for binding and thereby reduce the overall response. In the absence of the full agonist they provide a moderate level of stimulation, giving them a stabilising, "buffering" character.

Examples

Examples include aripiprazole, a partial agonist at dopamine D2 receptors used as an antipsychotic; varenicline, a partial agonist at nicotinic receptors used for smoking cessation; pindolol, a beta blocker with intrinsic sympathomimetic activity; and buprenorphine, a partial agonist at mu-opioid receptors.
