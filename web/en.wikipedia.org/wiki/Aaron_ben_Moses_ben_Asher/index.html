Aaron ben Moses ben Asher

Aaron ben Moses ben Asher (Hebrew: אהרן בן משה בן אשר; died c. 960 CE) was a Jewish scribe and scholar of Tiberias who refined the system of vowel points and accent marks used to fix the pronunciation and chanting of the Hebrew Bible. He was the last and most prominent member of the ben Asher family of Masoretes, who over five generations produced authoritative manuscripts of the Bible. The text he prepared became the standard ("Masoretic") text of the Hebrew Bible used to this day.

Work

Aaron ben Asher vocalised and corrected the great Bible codex now known as the Aleppo Codex (c. 920 CE), written by the scribe Solomon ben Buya'a; its colophon credits ben Asher with adding the vowel points, accents and Masoretic notes. The codex was later kept in Jerusalem and Aleppo and was endorsed by Maimonides, who used its arrangement of open and closed sections as the model for his own Torah scroll. Ben Asher also wrote a treatise on the accents and vocalisation, Dikduke ha-Te'amim ("Grammatical rules of the accents"), and Masoretic notes that circulated widely.

The ben Asher–ben Naphtali controversy

Ben Asher's system differed in a few hundred details from that of his contemporary Moses ben David ben Naphtali; the ben Asher tradition prevailed and underlies nearly all printed Hebrew Bibles. Tradition also associates ben Asher with the Karaite movement, though scholars debate the extent of his involvement.
