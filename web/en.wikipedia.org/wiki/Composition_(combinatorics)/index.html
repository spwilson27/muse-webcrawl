Composition (combinatorics)

In mathematics, a composition of an integer n is a way of writing n as the sum of a sequence of positive integers, where the order of the parts matters. Compositions contrast with partitions, in which the order of the parts is disregarded.

Examples

The number 4 has eight compositions: 4, 3+1, 1+3, 2+2, 2+1+1, 1+2+1, 1+1+2, and 1+1+1+1. By contrast, 4 has only five partitions, since for example 3+1 and 1+3 are the same partition.

Counting

The number of compositions of n is 2n−1. A short proof imagines n units in a row: between consecutive units there are n−1 gaps, and each gap either separates two parts or not, giving 2n−1 choices. More generally, the number of compositions of n into exactly k parts is the binomial coefficient C(n−1, k−1).

Applications

Compositions appear in combinatorics, probability, and the analysis of algorithms, and correspond bijectively to subsets of an (n−1)-element set and to binary strings of length n−1.
