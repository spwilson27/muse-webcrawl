Chindwin River

The Chindwin River (Burmese: ချင်းတွင်းမြစ်) is a river flowing entirely in Myanmar (Burma), and the largest tributary of the country's main river, the Irrawaddy. It is about 1,207 kilometres (750 mi) long.

Course

The Chindwin rises in the hills of Kachin State near Shwedagon and the Hukawng Valley, where it is formed by several headstreams including the Tanai. It flows northwest, then turns south through the Naga Hills region, past Homalin, Mawlaik, Kalewa, and Monywa, before joining the Irrawaddy near Myingyan in central Myanmar.

History

During the Second World War, the Chindwin formed a major barrier in the Burma campaign; the Chindits under Orde Wingate crossed it in 1943 and 1944, and the river was a key line in the Japanese U-Go offensive toward Imphal in 1944. The river is navigable by steamers as far as Homalin during the rainy season.
