Céide Fields

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Céide Fields (Irish: Achaidh Chéide) are a Neolithic field system preserved beneath blanket bog near Ballycastle in County Mayo, Ireland. Dating to roughly 5,500 years ago, they are among the oldest known enclosed field systems in the world, consisting of stone walls dividing the land into fields for grazing and cultivation.

Discovery

In the 1930s local schoolteacher Patrick Caulfield noticed lines of stones while cutting turf for fuel and recognised them as ancient walls. Decades later his son, archaeologist Seamas Caulfield, investigated the site systematically, revealing an extensive landscape of walled fields, houses, and megalithic tombs preserved under the bog.

Significance

The fields demonstrate that Neolithic farmers in Ireland organised large-scale livestock farming and land division by around 3700–3200 BC. An award-winning visitor centre, housed in a distinctive pyramid-shaped building, interprets the site and the archaeology of the bog.
