JAK2

Janus kinase 2

Symbol: JAK2

Gene location: Chromosome 9p24.1 (human)

Family: Janus kinase family of tyrosine kinases

Key mutation: V617F

Janus kinase 2 (commonly called JAK2) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase and a member of the Janus kinase family, which also includes JAK1, JAK3, and TYK2. In humans it is encoded by the JAK2 gene on chromosome 9. JAK2 transduces signals from many cytokine and hormone receptors — including those for erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, and several interleukins — through the JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

Function

When a ligand binds its receptor, associated JAK2 molecules phosphorylate one another and the receptor's cytoplasmic tail, creating docking sites for STAT transcription factors. Phosphorylated STATs dimerize, enter the nucleus, and switch on genes controlling blood-cell production, immune function, and growth. Because the erythropoietin receptor has no intrinsic kinase activity, JAK2 is essential for red blood cell formation; mice lacking JAK2 die in embryonic development from failed erythropoiesis.

The V617F mutation

In 2005, several groups independently identified a single acquired point mutation in JAK2 — substitution of phenylalanine for valine at position 617 (V617F) — in patients with myeloproliferative neoplasms. The mutation locks the kinase in an active state, driving blood-cell overproduction without the normal hormonal signal. It is found in roughly 95% of patients with polycythemia vera and in about half of those with essential thrombocythemia and primary myelofibrosis. Its discovery transformed diagnosis of these disorders and made JAK2 testing a standard part of hematology workups.

Clinical significance

Other JAK2 mutations, particularly in exon 12, account for most V617F-negative cases of polycythemia vera. JAK2 rearrangements also occur in some leukemias and lymphomas. The JAK inhibitor ruxolitinib, approved for myelofibrosis and polycythemia vera, directly targets the pathway the mutant kinase activates.
