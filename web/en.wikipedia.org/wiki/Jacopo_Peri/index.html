Jacopo Peri

Born: 20 August 1561, Rome, Papal States

Died: 12 August 1633 (aged 71), Florence, Grand Duchy of Tuscany

Occupation: Composer, singer

Jacopo Peri (20 August 1561 – 12 August 1633), known under the pseudonym Il Zazzerino, was an Italian composer and singer of the transitional period between the Renaissance and Baroque styles, and is often called the inventor of opera. He wrote the first work to be called an opera today, Dafne (around 1597), and also the first opera to have survived to the present day, Euridice (1600).

Life

Peri was born in Rome but studied in Florence with Cristofano Malvezzi. He served the Medici court in Florence as a singer and keyboard player and became involved with the Florentine Camerata, the circle of musicians and intellectuals around Jacopo Corsi and Ottavio Rinuccini who sought to revive the sung drama of ancient Greece.

Works

Working with the poet Rinuccini, Peri composed Dafne for performance during Carnival 1597–98; its music is mostly lost. His Euridice, performed in October 1600 for the wedding of Maria de' Medici and Henri IV of France, survives and is the earliest opera whose score is extant. In these works Peri developed the stile recitativo, a style of singing midway between speech and song, accompanied by a small group of continuo instruments.

Legacy

Peri's experiments, alongside those of Giulio Caccini and Claudio Monteverdi, laid the foundations of opera as a dramatic and musical form.
