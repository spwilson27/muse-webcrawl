Pyramidal peak


The Matterhorn, a classic glacial horn

A pyramidal peak, sometimes called a glacial horn, is an angular, sharply pointed mountain peak which results from cirque erosion due to multiple glaciers diverging from a central point. Pyramidal peaks are often examples of nunataks.

Formation

Glaciers, typically forming in cirques on the flanks of a mountain, develop basins on several sides. As these glaciers grind away at the headwalls, the divides between them — arêtes — become narrower and steeper. When at least three cirques erode back into a central massif, they leave a steep-sided pyramid of rock. If glacial erosion continues, the peak may eventually be reduced or destroyed.

Examples

The most famous example is the Matterhorn in the Pennine Alps on the Swiss–Italian border. Other celebrated horns include K2 and Mount Everest in Asia (the latter also shaped by uplift and river erosion), the Grand Teton in Wyoming, Mount Assiniboine in Canada — nicknamed the "Matterhorn of the Rockies" — and Carrantuohill's neighbouring peaks in Ireland. Mont Blanc's Aiguille du Midi is a sharply pointed horn accessible by cable car.
