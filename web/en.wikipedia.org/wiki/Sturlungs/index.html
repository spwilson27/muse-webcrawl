Sturlungs

The Sturlung age of 13th-century Iceland, recorded in the Sturlunga saga

The Sturlungs (Icelandic: Sturlungar) were a powerful family clan of the Icelandic Commonwealth in the 13th century, descended from Sturla Þórðarson the elder (d. 1183) of Hvammur in western Iceland. Their name gives the term for the period of civil strife that ended the Commonwealth — the Age of the Sturlungs (c. 1220–1264).

History

Under Sturla's grandsons — the brothers Þórður, Sighvatur and the famous historian and lawspeaker Snorri Sturluson — the family accumulated chieftaincies (goðorð) and wealth, and came into violent rivalry with other great clans, the Haukdælir, Oddaverjar, Ásbirningar and Vatnsfirðingar. Snorri was murdered in 1241 by men acting for his former son-in-law Gizur Þorvaldsson, on the instructions of King Haakon IV of Norway, who progressively drew the Icelandic chieftains into vassalage. Decades of battles, burnings (such as the Flugumýri burning of 1253) and assassinations followed, until in 1262–64 the Icelanders accepted the Old Covenant (Gamli sáttmáli), submitting to the Norwegian crown and ending the Commonwealth.

Sources

The era is documented in the Sturlunga saga, a compilation of contemporary sagas, much of it written by Sturla Þórðarson the younger (1214–1284), Snorri's nephew — an unusually near-eyewitness source for medieval history.
