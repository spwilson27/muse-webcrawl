Tibetan Buddhism is a form of Buddhism practised in Tibet, the Himalayas, Mongolia and parts of Siberia and Central Asia, combining Mahayana and Vajrayana (tantric) teachings with elements of the indigenous Bön religion. It reached Tibet from India mainly in the 7th–11th centuries.

Schools and practice

Its four major schools — Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya and Gelug — differ in lineage and emphasis but share practices such as mantra recitation, mandalas, prayer wheels and flags, and monastic debate. The Gelug school, founded by Tsongkhapa, produced the line of Dalai Lamas based at Lhasa. Since 1959 an exile community led from Dharamshala, India, has carried Tibetan Buddhism worldwide.
