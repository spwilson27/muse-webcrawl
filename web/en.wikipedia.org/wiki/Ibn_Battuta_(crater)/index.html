Ibn Battuta (crater)

Ibn Battuta
PlanetMoon
CoordinatesApproximately 7°S, 50°E (Mare Fecunditatis)

EponymIbn Battuta, Moroccan explorer (1304–1369)

Ibn Battuta is a small lunar impact crater located in the Mare Fecunditatis, on the eastern part of the Moon's near side. It lies to the southwest of the larger crater Lindbergh and northeast of Goclenius, within the basaltic plains of the mare.

The bowl-shaped lunar crater Ibn Battuta in Mare Fecunditatis.

Description

The crater is a simple, bowl-shaped formation with a circular rim, an inner wall that slopes down to a small flat floor at the centre, and little sign of significant erosion or terracing. Its modest size and fresh-looking form are typical of small secondary-age craters on the lunar maria. The name, approved by the International Astronomical Union in 1976, honours the Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta, whose Rihla recounts journeys of some 117,000 km across the medieval Islamic world and beyond — among the greatest travel accounts of the pre-modern era.
