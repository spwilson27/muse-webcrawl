Wold's theorem

Wold's theorem, proved by Herman Wold in 1938, is a fundamental result in time series analysis. It states that every covariance-stationary stochastic process can be decomposed into two uncorrelated parts: a deterministic component and a stochastic component that is an infinite moving average of uncorrelated innovations.

Statement

Any weakly stationary process can be written as a sum of a purely deterministic process, predictable from its own past, and a purely non-deterministic process represented as an infinite-order moving average MA(∞) of white-noise innovations. This decomposition justifies the widespread use of ARMA models in econometrics and signal processing.

Significance

The theorem does not say the innovations are independent, only uncorrelated, and it applies to second-order stationarity rather than strict stationarity. It underpins forecasting theory and spectral analysis of stationary processes.
