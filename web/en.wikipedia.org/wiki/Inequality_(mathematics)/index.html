Inequality (mathematics)

In mathematics, an inequality is a relation which makes a non-equal comparison between two numbers or other mathematical expressions. Inequalities are used to compare quantities that are not necessarily equal, using symbols such as < (less than), > (greater than), ≤ (less than or equal to) and ≥ (greater than or equal to).

Properties

Inequalities can be manipulated in ways similar to equations: quantities may be added to or subtracted from both sides, and both sides may be multiplied or divided by a positive number without changing the truth of the statement. Multiplication or division by a negative number, however, reverses the direction of the inequality sign.

Famous inequalities

  • Triangle inequality — the sum of two sides of a triangle is at least the third side; generalised in analysis.
  • Cauchy–Schwarz inequality — bounds the inner product of two vectors.
  • AM–GM inequality — the arithmetic mean is at least the geometric mean.
  • Jensen's inequality and Chebyshev's inequality — fundamental in probability and analysis.

Applications

Inequalities underpin optimisation, including linear programming, and appear throughout analysis, number theory, geometry and statistics, where they express bounds and estimates.
