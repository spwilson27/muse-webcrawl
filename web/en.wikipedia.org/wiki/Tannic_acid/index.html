Tannic acid

Tannic acid is a specific form of tannin, a type of polyphenol. Its weak acidity (pKa around 6) is due to its numerous phenol groups. The chemical formula for commercial tannic acid is often given as C76H52O46, corresponding to decagalloyl glucose, although the commercial product is actually a mixture of polygalloyl glucoses or polygalloyl quinic acid esters extracted mainly from plant sources such as oak galls, sumac and tara pods.

Properties and sources

Tannic acid is a yellow to light brown powder that dissolves readily in water and alcohol. It occurs in nutgalls formed by insects on oak trees (the richest traditional source), in tea, coffee, wine aged in oak, and many fruits. Like other tannins it precipitates proteins, which underlies both its astringent taste and its historical use in tanning leather.

Uses

Tannic acid is used in the manufacture of inks (including iron gall ink), as a mordant in dyeing, in wood staining and rust-conversion coatings, as a clarifying agent in brewing and winemaking, and in medicine as an astringent. It was formerly used as an antidote for alkaloid poisoning because it precipitates many alkaloids and some metal ions, though this use is now discouraged.
