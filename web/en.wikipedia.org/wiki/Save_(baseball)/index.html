Save (baseball)

In baseball, a save (abbreviated SV or S) is the successful preservation of a lead by a relief pitcher who finishes a game won by his team. The save became an official Major League Baseball statistic in 1969, having been devised by sportswriter Jerome Holtzman in 1960 to recognise effective relief work that wins and losses did not capture.

Criteria

A pitcher is credited with a save when he meets all of the following: he finishes a game won by his team; he is not the winning pitcher; and he either (a) enters with a lead of no more than three runs and pitches at least one inning, (b) enters with the potential tying run on base, at bat or on deck, or (c) pitches at least three innings effectively in relief. A reliever who enters in a save situation and surrenders the lead is charged with a blown save.

History and usage

The statistic helped drive the evolution of specialised bullpens and the modern one-inning closer. Mariano Rivera is the all-time saves leader with 652, followed by Trevor Hoffman (601), the first man to 500 and 600. Single-season records are headed by Francisco Rodríguez's 62 saves in 2008. Critics argue the save rule distorts managerial behaviour, reserving the best relievers for the ninth inning rather than the highest-leverage situations.

Related statistics

The hold, an unofficial statistic, credits a middle reliever who preserves a lead in a save situation but does not finish the game. A "tough save" and inherited-runner statistics are used in advanced analysis.
