Freddy Fender

Freddy Fender (born Baldemar Garza Huerta; June 4, 1937 – October 14, 2006) was an American Tejano, country, and rock and roll musician, known for his bilingual hit singles "Before the Next Teardrop Falls" and "Wasted Days and Wasted Nights". A pioneer of Tex-Mex and Chicano rock, he later found renewed success with the Texas Tornados supergroup.

Early life and career

Born in San Benito, Texas, to Mexican migrant worker parents, Fender began performing as a child and first recorded Spanish-language rock and roll under the name El Bebop Kid. His 1959 recording of "Wasted Days and Wasted Nights" was becoming a hit when a marijuana arrest and prison term interrupted his career in 1960.

Comeback and later years

In 1974–75 Fender returned with "Before the Next Teardrop Falls", which topped both the country and pop charts, followed by a hit re-recording of "Wasted Days and Wasted Nights". He won CMA and Grammy honours, and from 1989 toured with the Texas Tornados alongside Doug Sahm, Flaco Jiménez, and Augie Meyers. He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and was posthumously inducted into the Tejano Music Hall of Fame.
