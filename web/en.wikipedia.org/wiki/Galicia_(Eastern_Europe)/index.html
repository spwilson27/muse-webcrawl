Galicia (Ukrainian and Polish: Galicja; German: Galizien) is a historical and geographic region spanning what is now southeastern Poland and western Ukraine, on the northern slopes of the Carpathians. It should not be confused with the Spanish region of Galicia in northwestern Iberia.

History

The region takes its name from the medieval Principality of Halych, centred on the town of Halych (Galich). In the 13th and 14th centuries the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia was a major East Slavic state before the lands were divided between the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, and later absorbed into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

In the First Partition of Poland in 1772, the Habsburg monarchy annexed the region and organized it as the Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria, a crown land of the Austrian Empire. Its capital, Lemberg (Polish Lwów, Ukrainian Lviv), became a major centre of Polish, Ukrainian, Jewish, German and Armenian culture. Galicia remained under Habsburg rule until the empire's collapse in 1918.

After the First World War, Galicia was contested in the Polish–Ukrainian War and incorporated into the Second Polish Republic. In 1939 it was divided between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact; after 1945 the eastern part was assigned to the Ukrainian SSR and the western part to Poland, a division that roughly persists in the modern Polish–Ukrainian border.

Population and culture

Historical Galicia was ethnically and religiously mixed, home to Poles, Ukrainians (Ruthenians), Jews — who formed a large share of many towns — Germans, Armenians and others. It is associated with a rich folk culture, Hasidic Jewish heritage, and the oil industry that developed around Boryslav in the 19th century, once among the world's largest sources of petroleum.

Modern usage

Today "Galicia" survives as an informal regional designation: in Poland it generally corresponds to the Lesser Poland and Subcarpathian voivodeships, and in Ukraine to the Lviv, Ivano-Frankivsk and Ternopil oblasts.
