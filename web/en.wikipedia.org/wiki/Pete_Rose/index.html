Pete Rose

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Peter Edward Rose Sr. (14 April 1941 – 30 September 2024), nicknamed "Charlie Hustle", was an American professional baseball player and manager. He played in Major League Baseball from 1963 to 1986, most prominently for the Cincinnati Reds, whose "Big Red Machine" teams won consecutive World Series in 1975 and 1976; he also won a third championship with the Philadelphia Phillies in 1980 and finished his career with the Montreal Expos and a return to Cincinnati as player-manager.

Career

Rose is Major League Baseball's all-time leader in hits (4,256), games played (3,562), at-bats (14,053) and singles (3,215). A switch hitter, he won three batting titles, two Gold Gloves, the 1973 National League MVP award and the 1975 World Series MVP award, and was selected to 17 All-Star teams at an unequalled five positions. He broke Ty Cobb's hit record on 11 September 1985 with his 4,192nd hit.

Ban and ineligibility

In 1989, amid an investigation (the Dowd Report) concluding that he had bet on baseball, including on his own Reds, Rose accepted placement on the permanently ineligible list, ending his managerial career and barring him from election to the Baseball Hall of Fame. He applied for reinstatement several times and later admitted in his 2004 autobiography that he had bet on baseball and on the Reds. After Rose's death in 2024, Commissioner Rob Manfred in 2025 removed deceased players, including Rose, from the permanently ineligible list, ruling that lifetime bans end at death and making him eligible for Hall of Fame consideration.

Legacy

Rose's aggressive style — headfirst slides, sprinting to first base on walks — made "Charlie Hustle" a byword for effort, and his exclusion from Cooperstown despite holding the sport's most famous record was one of baseball's longest-running controversies.
