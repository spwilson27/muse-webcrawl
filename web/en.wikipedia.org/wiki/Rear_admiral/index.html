Rear admiral

Rear admiral is a flag officer rank used by English-speaking navies. In many navies it is the lowest of the admiral ranks, above commodore or captain and below vice admiral. The rank originated in the sailing era, when squadrons were divided into van, centre and rear divisions; the rear admiral commanded the rear division of a fleet, subordinate to the admiral of the centre and the vice admiral of the van.

United States

In the United States Navy and Coast Guard, rear admiral is divided into two grades: rear admiral (lower half), a one-star rank (pay grade O-7) equivalent to a brigadier general, and rear admiral (upper half), a two-star rank (pay grade O-8) equivalent to a major general. The lower-half grade descends from the former rank of commodore, which was replaced in the late nineteenth century and re-adopted in modified form in 1985.

United Kingdom

In the Royal Navy, rear admiral is a two-star rank (OF-7), senior to commodore and junior to vice admiral. Holders typically command carrier strike groups, hold senior staff posts, or serve in senior tri-service appointments.

Other countries

Equivalent ranks include contre-amiral in France, Konteradmiral in Germany, and contrammiraglio in Italy. Many Commonwealth navies use the same title and insignia pattern as the Royal Navy.
