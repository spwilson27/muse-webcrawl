Comparative military ranks

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Comparative military ranks are tables and reference systems that align the officer and enlisted ranks of different countries' armed forces so that equivalent grades can be recognised across nations. For alliance operations, NATO standardises grades with codes (OF-1 to OF-10 for officers, OR-1 to OR-9 for other ranks) under STANAG 2116, allowing, for example, a British brigadier, a US brigadier general, and a French général de brigade to be treated as equivalent OF-6 officers.

Enlisted ranks and difficulties

Other ranks span private soldiers (OR-1/2), corporals (OR-3/4), sergeants (OR-5/6), and senior non-commissioned and warrant officers up to OR-9 (e.g., US Command Sergeant Major or British Warrant Officer Class 1). Equivalences are approximate: titles with the same name may carry different seniority (a US Navy captain is OF-5, an army captain OF-2), naval and air force titles differ again, and some grades — such as the five-star ranks — are now largely honorary or wartime-only. Rank comparison tables are nonetheless essential for protocol, command relationships, and pay grading in multinational forces.
