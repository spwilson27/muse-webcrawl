Don the Beachcomber

Founded1933 (as Don's Beachcomber), Hollywood, California
FounderErnest Raymond Beaumont Gantt ("Donn Beach")
TypeTiki bar and restaurant chain

Don the Beachcomber was a chain of tiki bars and restaurants founded by Ernest Raymond Beaumont Gantt, better known as Donn Beach. Opened in Hollywood in 1933, shortly after the repeal of Prohibition, it is credited with inventing the tiki bar genre and the elaborate rum-based "exotic drinks" that defined it.

History

Gantt, a former bootlegger and world traveller, decorated his bar with South Seas artefacts collected on his travels, serving Cantonese-influenced food alongside complex rum cocktails assembled from secret blends of syrups and spices, mixed by bartenders ("Don's mixers") who kept recipes compartmentalized. His inventions include the Zombie, the Navy Grog, and the original recipe behind the Mai Tai's rum-blend style.

The bar became a celebrity haunt, and after Gantt's wartime service the brand expanded through his ex-wife Sunny Sund under the name Don the Beachcomber, with locations including Chicago, Las Vegas, Palm Springs, and Hawaii. Gantt himself later settled in Hawaii and opened a rival operation in Waikiki, barred from using his own name there by the divorce settlement, and created the International Market Place's famous banyan-tree bar.

Legacy

Don the Beachcomber's success inspired Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron and a wave of Polynesian-themed restaurants across postwar America. The original restaurants eventually closed, but the name and style have been revived by tiki enthusiasts; Gantt is recognized, alongside Trader Vic, as a founding father of tiki culture.
