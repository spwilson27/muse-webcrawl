Grapheme

Graphemes are the smallest contrastive units of a writing system.

In linguistics, a grapheme is the smallest functional unit of a writing system — the written counterpart of the phoneme in spoken language. Any particular mark that is recognised as representing the same unit, despite differences of shape or handwriting, is an allograph of that grapheme (for example, <a> and <ɑ>, or uppercase and lowercase forms, are allographs of one grapheme in many analyses).

Concepts

Graphemes include letters, logograms, numerals, punctuation marks and other symbols. In alphabetic systems there is an approximate — often imperfect — correspondence between graphemes and phonemes. In English, single phonemes may be written with multi-letter graphemes such as <sh>, <ch> and <ough>, while the same grapheme may represent several sounds. The study of writing systems is called graphemics or graphology in its linguistic sense.

Grapheme clusters in computing

In digital text, a user-perceived "character" may consist of several Unicode code points — a base character plus combining accents, or joined emoji sequences. Unicode therefore defines the extended grapheme cluster, the unit that text editors and rendering engines treat as a single character for cursor movement, selection and deletion.
