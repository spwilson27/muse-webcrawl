J. Sterling Morton

Julius Sterling Morton

Born: April 22, 1832, Adams, New York

Died: April 27, 1902, Lake Forest, Illinois

Office: 3rd United States Secretary of Agriculture (1893–1897)

Known for: Founding Arbor Day

Julius Sterling Morton (April 22, 1832 – April 27, 1902) was a Nebraska newspaper editor and politician who served as the third United States Secretary of Agriculture under President Grover Cleveland. He is best remembered as the founder of Arbor Day, the tree-planting holiday first celebrated in Nebraska in 1872 and now observed in all fifty states and many countries.

Early life

Morton was born in Adams, Jefferson County, New York, to Julius Dewey Morton and Emeline Sterling Morton, who operated a general store. The family moved west to Michigan, where Morton attended Wesleyan Seminary in Albion and later the University of Michigan. In 1854 he married Caroline Joy French, and the couple settled in the newly organized Nebraska Territory, first at Bellevue and then at Nebraska City.

Nebraska career

In Nebraska City, Morton edited the Nebraska City News, one of the territory's earliest newspapers, and became a leading figure in territorial politics. He served in the territorial legislature and twice ran unsuccessfully for governor. A conservative Democrat, Morton advocated free trade, hard money, and agricultural development of the plains.

Morton built a 52-room estate west of Nebraska City that grew into Arbor Lodge, surrounded by orchards and plantings he developed himself. The property is now Arbor Lodge State Historical Park.

Arbor Day

Convinced that the treeless plains needed windbreaks, shade, and timber, Morton proposed a dedicated tree-planting day. On January 4, 1872, the Nebraska State Board of Agriculture adopted his resolution, and the first Arbor Day was celebrated on April 10, 1872, when an estimated one million trees were planted in Nebraska. Morton declared that "other holidays repose upon the past; Arbor Day proposes for the future." Nebraska made it a legal holiday in 1885, fixing it on Morton's birthday, April 22.

Secretary of Agriculture

President Grover Cleveland appointed Morton Secretary of Agriculture in 1893, a position he held until 1897. He streamlined the department, opposed the use of its seed distribution for political patronage, and expanded its scientific work on forestry and plant introduction.

Death and legacy

Morton died in 1902 at the home of his son in Lake Forest, Illinois. His sons included Joy Morton, founder of the Morton Salt Company and the Morton Arboretum in Lisle, Illinois. Arbor Day is now celebrated nationally on the last Friday in April, and Morton's birthday is commemorated by the Arbor Day Foundation, headquartered in Nebraska City.
