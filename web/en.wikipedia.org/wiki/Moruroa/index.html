Moruroa (often misspelled "Mururoa") is an atoll in the Tuamotu Archipelago of French Polynesia in the southern Pacific Ocean. It is best known as the principal site of French nuclear weapons testing between 1966 and 1996.

Nuclear testing

France conducted 193 nuclear tests at Moruroa and neighbouring Fangataufa between 1966 and 1996. The first tests were atmospheric, exploded from barges or balloons over the lagoon; after international protest, testing moved underground into shafts drilled into the atoll's basalt from 1975. A controversial final series of tests in 1995–96, announced by President Jacques Chirac, provoked worldwide demonstrations before France signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty.

Legacy

The atoll remains a restricted military zone monitored for radiation leakage and geological stability. Veterans, Polynesian workers, and downwind communities have campaigned for recognition and compensation for health effects attributed to the tests; France passed a compensation law in 2010 and broadened it in later years.
