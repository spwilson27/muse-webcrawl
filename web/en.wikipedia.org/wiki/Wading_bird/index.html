Wading bird

Wading birds, in the broad sense, are long-legged birds that wade in shallow water to feed. The term usually refers to herons, egrets, bitterns, storks, ibises, spoonbills, flamingos and cranes — distinct from the zoological use of "wader" for shorebirds of the order Charadriiformes.

Characteristics

Wading birds have long legs and often long necks and bills adapted for striking, probing or sweeping for fish, amphibians, crustaceans and aquatic invertebrates in marshes, flooded fields, lagoons and riverbanks. Many species nest colonially in trees or reedbeds near wetlands.

Groups

The classic wading birds are placed mainly in the order Pelecaniformes (herons, ibises, spoonbills, egrets), Ciconiiformes (storks), Gruiformes (cranes and rails) and Phoenicopteriformes (flamingos).

Ecology

They are indicators of wetland health and are threatened by wetland drainage, pollution and disturbance of breeding colonies.
