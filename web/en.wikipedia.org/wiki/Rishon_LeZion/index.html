Rishon LeZion

Rishon LeZion (Hebrew: "First to Zion") is a city in the Central District of Israel, south of Tel Aviv, and part of the Gush Dan metropolitan area. Founded in 1882, it is one of the oldest Zionist settlements in Israel and is today the country's fourth-largest city, with a population of roughly a quarter of a million.

History

Rishon LeZion was established on 31 July 1882 by ten members of the Hovevei Zion movement from Kharkiv (in present-day Ukraine), led by Zalman David Levontin, who purchased land southeast of Jaffa. The settlement struggled with poor soil, water shortages and debt, and was rescued by the support of Baron Edmond de Rothschild, under whose patronage it became a centre of wine production: the Carmel-Mizrahi winery, founded in 1886, was the first major industrial enterprise of the Yishuv. The settlement is also credited with designing the first version of the flag that became the flag of Israel, and with early revivals of spoken Hebrew — its school was the first to teach entirely in Hebrew.

The modern city

Granted city status in 1950, Rishon LeZion has grown rapidly, especially with immigration from the former Soviet Union in the 1990s. New western neighbourhoods spread toward the Mediterranean coast, and the city is now a significant commercial and residential centre with an industrial zone, a college, and the headquarters of several national companies.
