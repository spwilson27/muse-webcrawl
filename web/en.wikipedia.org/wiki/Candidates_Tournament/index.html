Candidates Tournament

The Candidates Tournament is a chess tournament organised by FIDE, the world chess federation, as the final qualification event for the World Chess Championship. The winner of the Candidates earns the right to challenge the reigning World Chess Champion in a title match.

History

The Candidates cycle began in 1950, when the first Candidates Tournament was held in Budapest and won by David Bronstein. For much of the post-war era the Candidates took the form of a large round-robin tournament, famous editions including Zürich 1953, won by Vasily Smyslov and regarded as one of the greatest tournaments ever played. Between 1965 and 1985 the format changed to a series of knockout matches, which produced challengers such as Boris Spassky, Bobby Fischer, Anatoly Karpov and Viktor Korchnoi.

During the split in the world championship (1993–2006) rival Candidates events were held. After reunification, the modern Candidates settled, from 2013 onwards, on an eight-player double round-robin format.

Modern format

Eight players qualify through routes including the FIDE World Cup, the Grand Swiss, the FIDE Circuit and rating. The tournament is a double round-robin: each player meets every other twice, once with each colour. Classical time controls are used, with tie-breaks by rapid and blitz games if necessary. Recent winners include Viswanathan Anand (2014), Sergey Karjakin (2016), Fabiano Caruana (2018), Ian Nepomniachtchi (2020–21 and 2022) and Gukesh Dommaraju, who won the 2024 Candidates in Toronto to become the youngest challenger in championship history.

A separate Women's Candidates Tournament determines the challenger for the Women's World Chess Championship.
