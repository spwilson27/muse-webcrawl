Hand dryer

A hand dryer is an electric machine which might use a heating element and an air blower to dry the hands after hand washing, or a powerful jet of unheated air. It is commonly used in public toilets as a cheaper alternative to paper towels, or alongside them.

History

The earliest patent for a hand dryer was filed in 1921. Electric hand dryers came into widespread use in public washrooms during the twentieth century. High-speed "jet" dryers, which strip water from the hands with fast-moving air rather than heat, were introduced in the 1990s and 2000s and dry hands in around ten to fifteen seconds.

Operation

Conventional dryers blow warm air over the hands, evaporating moisture, and typically take 30 seconds or more. Jet air dryers use high-velocity air, sometimes filtered with HEPA media, and may collect water in a reservoir rather than blowing it onto the floor.

Hygiene and environment

Studies comparing hand dryers and paper towels reach varying conclusions about bacterial dispersal and hand hygiene outcomes. Dryers generally have lower per-use cost and produce less solid waste, while towels avoid the energy use and noise of dryers.
