Worcester is a cathedral city in Worcestershire, England, of which it is the county town. It is 30 miles (48 km) south-west of Birmingham and 27 miles (43 km) north of Gloucester, on the River Severn. The city is best known for its medieval cathedral, the final battle of the English Civil War fought there in 1651, Royal Worcester porcelain, and as the birthplace of Lea & Perrins Worcestershire sauce and of the composer Edward Elgar.

History

Ironworking at Worcester dates to Roman times, when the settlement stood on the road between Gloucester and Wroxeter. The Anglo-Saxon town was fortified against the Danes, and its bishopric — founded in 680 — produced St Wulfstan, the last Anglo-Saxon bishop to survive the Norman Conquest. Worcester Cathedral holds the tomb of King John, buried there in 1216 by his own wish near St Wulfstan's shrine.

On 3 September 1651 the Battle of Worcester ended the Civil Wars: Oliver Cromwell's Parliamentarians decisively defeated the mainly Scottish Royalist army of Charles II, who escaped — famously hiding in an oak tree at Boscobel — and fled to France. Cromwell called it "a crowning mercy".

The cathedral

Worcester Cathedral, built between 1084 and 1504 in every English Gothic style, overlooks the Severn. It contains the tomb of King John, Prince Arthur's Chantry, a Norman crypt and one of England's finest cathedral libraries.

Economy and products

Royal Worcester porcelain was manufactured in the city from 1751 until the factory's closure in 2009; the Museum of Royal Worcester preserves its archive. Lea & Perrins created their fermented Worcestershire sauce in the 1830s at a chemist's shop on Broad Street, and it is still made in Worcester. Gloves, engineering and the University of Worcester are modern mainstays.

Culture and sport

Edward Elgar was born at Lower Broadheath outside the city and is commemorated by a statue facing the cathedral. The Three Choirs Festival rotates to Worcester. Worcester Racecourse ("Pitchcroft") and Worcestershire County Cricket Club's New Road ground — famously picturesque and famously flooded by the Severn — anchor the sporting life of the city.

Transport

The city has two stations, Foregate Street and Shrub Hill, and lies near the M5 motorway. The Worcester and Birmingham Canal links the Severn to the Midlands network at Diglis Basin.
