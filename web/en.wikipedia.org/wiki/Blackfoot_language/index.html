The Blackfoot language, also called Siksiká (ᓱᖽᐧᖺ, "black foot"), is an Algonquian language spoken by the Blackfoot (Niitsitapi) peoples of the northern Plains: the Siksika, Kainai and Piegan nations in Alberta, Canada, and the South Piegan in Montana, United States.

Classification and dialectology

Blackfoot belongs to the Plains Algonquian grouping but is notably divergent from other Algonquian languages, having undergone extensive phonological and morphological change — so much so that it is not mutually intelligible with its relatives. Its varieties are named after the confederacy's divisions: Siksiká, Kainai (Blood), North Piikani and South Piegan (Amskapi Piikani), which differ mainly in pronunciation and vocabulary.

Phonology and grammar

Blackfoot has a small consonant inventory with a three-way vowel system distinguished by length, plus distinctive pitch accent. It is highly polysynthetic: complex verbs encode subject, object, direction and obviation, and nouns are classified as animate or inanimate. Characteristic features include the "direct–inverse" system and a strong distinction between proximate and obviative third persons in discourse.

Status and revitalisation

The number of fluent speakers is estimated in the low thousands, most of them older adults, and the language is classified as endangered. Revitalisation efforts include immersion schools such as the Piegan Institute's Cuts Wood School in Montana and Alberta school programmes, orthography standardisation and dictionaries such as Frantz and Russell's Blackfoot Dictionary of Stems, Roots, and Affixes.
