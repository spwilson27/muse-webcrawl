Chūgoku region

The Chūgoku region (Japanese: 中国地方, Chūgoku-chihō), also known as the San'in-San'yō region (山陰山陽地方, San'in-San'yō-chihō), is the westernmost region of Honshū, the largest island of Japan. It consists of the prefectures of Hiroshima, Okayama, Shimane, Tottori, and Yamaguchi. In 2020, the region had a population of roughly 7.3 million people.

Geography

The Chūgoku region is divided by the Chūgoku Mountains, which run east–west through its centre, into two sub-regions with markedly different climates and characters. The San'in ("shady side of the mountains") sub-region, comprising Shimane and Tottori prefectures on the northern coast facing the Sea of Japan, experiences heavy snowfall in winter. The San'yō ("sunny side") sub-region, comprising Hiroshima, Okayama, and Yamaguchi on the southern coast facing the Seto Inland Sea, has a milder climate and receives less precipitation.

The largest city is Hiroshima, followed by Okayama. The region is connected to Shikoku by bridges across the Seto Inland Sea, including the Seto-Ōhashi Bridge between Okayama and Kagawa prefectures, and to Kyushu by the Kanmon Straits tunnels and bridges between Yamaguchi and Fukuoka prefectures.

History

The name Chūgoku means "middle country" in Japanese and dates from the era when Japan was administered under the ritsuryō system, when provinces were classified by their distance from the capital at Kyoto: near provinces (Kinai), middle provinces (Chūgoku), and far provinces (Engoku). The term was later applied to the whole western end of Honshū.

During the Sengoku period the region was dominated by the Mōri clan, whose seat at Hiroshima Castle was established by Mōri Terumoto. The region was also the base of the Chōshū Domain (present-day Yamaguchi Prefecture), which, together with Satsuma Domain, played a leading role in the Meiji Restoration of 1868.

On 6 August 1945, Hiroshima became the first city in history to be targeted by an atomic weapon, an event commemorated by the Hiroshima Peace Memorial (Genbaku Dome), a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Places of interest

  • Itsukushima Shrine and Miyajima (Hiroshima) — famous for its "floating" torii gate.
  • Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park and the Atomic Bomb Dome.
  • Izumo Taisha (Shimane) — one of Japan's oldest and most important Shinto shrines.
  • Tottori Sand Dunes — the largest sand dunes in Japan.
  • Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter (Okayama) — preserved Edo-period merchant district.
  • Kintaikyō Bridge (Iwakuni, Yamaguchi) — a five-arched wooden bridge first built in 1673.
  • Akiyoshidō (Yamaguchi) — one of Japan's largest limestone caves.

Economy

The San'yō coast is heavily industrialised, with steelworks, shipbuilding, petrochemical plants, and automobile manufacturing (Mazda is headquartered in Hiroshima Prefecture). The San'in coast's economy relies more on agriculture, fishing, and tourism.
