Business process modeling

FieldBusiness process management
PurposeRepresenting and analysing organisational processes
NotationsBPMN, flowcharts, EPC, UML activity diagrams

A process model drawn in BPMN notation.

Business process modeling (American English) or modelling (British English) is the activity of representing the processes of an enterprise so that the current ("as-is") process can be analysed, improved, automated, and used to design a future ("to-be") state. It is a core technique of business process management and business analysis.

Notations and tools

Early approaches used simple flowcharts and data-flow diagrams. Modern practice is dominated by the Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN), an OMG standard with events, activities, gateways, and pools, designed to be understandable by business users while also being executable by process engines. Other approaches include Event-driven Process Chains (EPC), UML activity diagrams, Petri nets, and value-stream mapping from lean manufacturing.

Purpose

Models provide a shared language between managers, analysts, and software developers; support compliance and documentation requirements such as ISO 9001; enable simulation of bottlenecks and costs; and form the basis for workflow automation and process mining. Effective modeling balances fidelity with readability, modelling at different levels of abstraction for different audiences.
