Parkinson's disease dementia

Parkinson's disease dementia (PDD) is a dementia that develops in the course of Parkinson's disease. A large proportion of people with Parkinson's eventually develop dementia, typically many years after the onset of motor symptoms; by common convention, dementia beginning more than a year after motor onset is classified as PDD, while earlier dementia suggests dementia with Lewy bodies.

Symptoms

The cognitive profile is dominated by executive dysfunction, impaired attention, visuospatial difficulties, and slowed thinking, with memory impairment often less prominent early than in Alzheimer's disease. Hallucinations, fluctuating cognition, depression, and apathy are frequent.

Pathophysiology and treatment

PDD is associated with Lewy body pathology spreading into limbic and cortical regions, often combined with Alzheimer-type changes and a marked cholinergic deficit. Cholinesterase inhibitors such as rivastigmine are approved for treatment and can modestly improve cognition and behaviour.
