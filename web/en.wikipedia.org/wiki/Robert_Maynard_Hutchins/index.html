Robert Maynard Hutchins


Born: January 17, 1899, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Died: May 17, 1977, Santa Barbara, California
Office: President of the University of Chicago (1929–1945)

Robert Maynard Hutchins (January 17, 1899 – May 17, 1977) was an American educational philosopher and university administrator, best known as president and then chancellor of the University of Chicago, where he reshaped undergraduate education around the Great Books and abolished varsity football.

Career

After serving in the First World War, Hutchins studied at Oberlin and Yale, becoming dean of Yale Law School at 28. In 1929, aged 30, he became president of the University of Chicago. There he introduced a liberal-arts core curriculum grounded in discussion of classic texts, championed the Great Books programme with Mortimer Adler, eliminated football in 1939, and defended academic freedom through controversial decades.

After the Second World War Hutchins chaired the Commission on Freedom of the Press, whose 1947 report set out the doctrine of social responsibility of the media. He later headed the Ford Foundation's Fund for the Republic and in 1959 founded the Center for the Study of Democratic Institutions in Santa Barbara, California.
