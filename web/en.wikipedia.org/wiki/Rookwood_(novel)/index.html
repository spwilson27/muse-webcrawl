Rookwood (novel)

Rookwood is a novel by William Harrison Ainsworth published in 1834. It is a historical and Gothic romance set largely in Yorkshire, revolving around the Rookwood family, a disputed inheritance, and the secrets of the family's past following the death of Sir Piers Rookwood.

Plot and themes

The story follows the revelation that the presumed heir, Ranulph Rookwood, is illegitimate, and the competing claim of Luke Bradley, whose identity and parentage are bound up with a gypsy mother and a mysterious hand discovered in a tomb. The novel combines Gothic elements — a family curse, a prophetic ballad, and the legend that the fall of a branch from the Rookwood tree foretells a death — with elements of the historical novel modelled on Walter Scott.

Dick Turpin

Rookwood is best remembered for its portrayal of the highwayman Dick Turpin, and in particular for Turpin's legendary overnight ride from London to York on his mare Black Bess, an episode largely of Ainsworth's invention. The novel's romanticised Turpin did much to create the enduring popular image of the dashing highwayman.
