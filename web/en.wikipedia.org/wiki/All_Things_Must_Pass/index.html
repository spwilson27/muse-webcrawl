All Things Must Pass

All Things Must Pass is the third studio album by the English musician George Harrison, released as a triple album in November 1970 on Apple Records. It was Harrison's first solo work after the break-up of the Beatles and is widely regarded as one of the finest solo albums by any former Beatle.

Background and recording

During the Beatles' later years Harrison had accumulated a large backlog of songs the band had not recorded. Co-produced with Phil Spector, whose "Wall of Sound" production shapes much of the record, the sessions in London featured an all-star cast including Eric Clapton, Ringo Starr, Billy Preston, members of Badfinger and the musicians who soon formed Derek and the Dominos.

Music

The album includes the hit singles "My Sweet Lord" and "What Is Life", alongside songs such as "Isn't It a Pity", "Beware of Darkness", "Wah-Wah" and the title track, blending rock with Harrison's Hindu spirituality. A third disc, Apple Jam, collected informal studio jams. "My Sweet Lord" later became the subject of a famous copyright suit over its resemblance to the Chiffons' "He's So Fine".

Reception

The album topped charts internationally and has repeatedly appeared on lists of the greatest albums of all time. A remixed 50th-anniversary edition was released in 2021.
