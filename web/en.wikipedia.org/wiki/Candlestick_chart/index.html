A candlestick chart (also called a Japanese candlestick chart or K-line) is a style of financial chart used to describe price movements of a security, derivative, or currency. Each "candlestick" typically shows one day of data, displaying the open, high, low, and closing prices.

Structure

Each candlestick comprises a body — the thick part representing the range between the opening and closing prices — and shadows (or wicks), thin lines showing the high and low prices of the period. If the closing price is above the opening price, the body is usually coloured white, green, or left hollow; if the close is below the open, the body is coloured black or red.

History

Candlestick charts were developed in the 18th century by the Japanese rice trader Munehisa Homma, who is said to have used them to trade the Osaka rice market. They were introduced to the Western world in the late 20th century, notably by Steve Nison's 1991 book Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques.

Patterns

Traders recognise numerous named candlestick patterns — such as the doji, hammer, engulfing pattern, and morning star — which are interpreted as signals of trend continuation or reversal. Candlestick analysis is widely used in technical analysis, though its predictive value is debated by academics.
