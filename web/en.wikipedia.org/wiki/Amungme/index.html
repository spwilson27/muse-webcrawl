Amungme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amungme highlanders in the Tembagapura area of Central Papua, Indonesia.

The Amungme are an Indigenous people of the highlands of Central Papua, Indonesia, numbering roughly 13,000–17,000. Their homeland lies in the high valleys around Tembagapura and the Sudirman (Nassau) Range near Puncak Jaya, the highest mountain in Oceania. They speak Amung (also called Uhunduni), a language of the Trans–New Guinea family.

Culture and livelihood

Traditionally the Amungme were semi-nomadic horticulturalists cultivating sweet potato, taro, and other crops in high-altitude gardens, organized in patrilineal clans and moieties. Their spiritual life centers on sacred mountains and ancestral lands; the peaks of the Sudirman Range hold deep religious significance. Most Amungme today profess Christianity, introduced by missionaries in the twentieth century, layered over older beliefs and customs of feasting, exchange, and dispute settlement.

The Grasberg mine

Amungme land hosts the Grasberg mine, one of the world's largest gold and copper mines, operated by Freeport-McMoRan. The mine's development from the 1970s brought displacement, environmental damage from riverine tailings disposal, and disputes over land rights and benefit sharing. In 2000, Amungme leaders signed an agreement with Freeport providing for trust funds and recognition of land rights, though grievances over human rights and environmental harm have continued and have drawn international attention.
