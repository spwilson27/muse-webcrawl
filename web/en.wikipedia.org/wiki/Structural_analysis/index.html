Structural analysis

Structural analysis determines the effects of loads on physical structures.

Structural analysis is the determination of the effects of loads on physical structures and their components. Structures subject to this type of analysis include all that must withstand loads, such as buildings, bridges, aircraft, machinery, and vehicles. Structural analysis employs the fields of applied mechanics, materials science, and applied mathematics to compute a structure's deformations, internal forces, stresses, support reactions, velocities, accelerations, and stability. The results of the analysis are used to verify a structure's fitness for use, often precluding physical tests.

Classification

Structures are classified as statically determinate or indeterminate depending on whether equilibrium equations alone suffice to find the reactions and internal forces. Analyses are likewise classified as linear or nonlinear (material or geometric nonlinearity), and static or dynamic (including modal, response-spectrum, and time-history analyses for earthquakes and wind).

Methods

  • Classical methods — methods of joints and sections for trusses; slope–deflection and moment-distribution methods for frames; virtual work for deflections; influence lines for moving loads on bridges.
  • Matrix methods — the stiffness (displacement) and flexibility (force) methods, organized for computer solution and forming the basis of modern structural software.
  • Finite element method (FEM) — the structure is divided into elements connected at nodes; assembly of the global stiffness matrix K and solution of K u = F gives displacements and, from them, stresses. FEM generalizes structural analysis to plates, shells, and continua.
  • Plastic analysis — for ultimate-load design, using mechanisms and yield-line theory.

Loads and design

Analysts apply dead loads, live (imposed) loads, wind, snow, seismic actions, temperature effects, and accidental loads, combined according to building codes (such as ASCE 7 or the Eurocodes) with load and resistance factors. Verification against failure modes (yielding, buckling, fatigue, overturning) and serviceability limits (deflection, vibration, cracking) completes the design loop with structural engineering.
