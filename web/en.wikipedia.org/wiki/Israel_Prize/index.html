Israel Prize

The Israel Prize (Hebrew: פרס ישראל, pras isra'el) is Israel's foremost cultural and scientific prize, awarded annually by the State of Israel to individuals or groups who have displayed excellence in an artistic or academic field, or who have made a special contribution to Israeli culture and society. It is generally considered the state's highest cultural honour.

History

The prize was instituted in 1953 at the initiative of the then Minister of Education, Ben-Zion Dinur, who himself received the prize in 1958 and 1973. It has been awarded ever since, with few exceptions, on the eve of Israel's Independence Day (Yom Ha'atzmaut) in a state ceremony in Jerusalem, attended by the President of Israel, the Prime Minister, the Speaker of the Knesset, and the President of the Supreme Court.

Categories

The prize is awarded in four broad fields, with subcategories that rotate from year to year:

  • Humanities, social sciences, and Jewish studies
  • Natural and exact sciences
  • Culture, arts, communications, and sports
  • Lifetime achievement and special contribution to society and the state (awarded intermittently since 1972)

Selection

Recipients, who must be Israeli citizens or organisations, are chosen by committees of judges appointed by the Minister of Education for each category; nominations are invited from the public. The committees' deliberations are confidential. In recent decades the prize has occasionally been the subject of public controversy, when governments have sought to intervene in committee decisions or candidates have declined the award.

Notable recipients

Well-known laureates include the writer Shmuel Yosef Agnon, the philosopher Martin Buber, the archaeologist Yigael Yadin, and the poet Yehuda Amichai. The prize carries a monetary award in addition to its prestige.
