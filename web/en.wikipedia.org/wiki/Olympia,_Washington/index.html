Olympia, Washington

Olympia is the capital city of the U.S. state of Washington and the county seat of Thurston County. Its population was about 55,605 at the 2020 census. Olympia lies at the southern end of Puget Sound, on Budd Inlet, near the mouth of the Deschutes River and the former Tumwater Falls.

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by Coast Salish peoples, including the Squaxin and Nisqually. American settlers arrived in the 1840s; the settlement was named Olympia in 1850 after the view of the Olympic Mountains to the northwest. It became the capital of Washington Territory in 1853 and retained that status at statehood in 1889, seeing off challenges from rival towns.

Landmarks and culture

The Washington State Capitol campus, with its 287-foot (87 m) legislative building dome — one of the tallest masonry domes in the world — overlooks Capitol Lake. Olympia is home to The Evergreen State College and has a noted independent music scene, influential in the development of indie rock and the riot grrrl movement, and the K Records and Kill Rock Stars labels.

Economy

State government is the largest employer. The historic Olympia Brewing Company in neighbouring Tumwater, closed in 2003, made the city's artesian wells famous under the slogan "It's the Water".
