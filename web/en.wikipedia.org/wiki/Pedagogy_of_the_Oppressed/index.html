Pedagogy of the Oppressed (Portuguese: Pedagogia do Oprimido) is a book by Brazilian educator and philosopher Paulo Freire, commonly considered one of the foundational texts of the critical pedagogy movement. First published in Portuguese in 1968 and translated into English in 1970, the book proposes a pedagogy with a new relationship between teacher, student, and society.

Freire wrote the book while in exile from Brazil, drawing on his experience teaching adult literacy in the country's impoverished northeast. It is dedicated "to the oppressed, and to those who suffer with them and fight at their side." The work has been translated into numerous languages and, according to some analyses, is among the most frequently cited works in the social sciences.

Contents

The book is organized into four chapters. The first chapter examines the justification for a pedagogy of the oppressed, the contradiction of oppressor and oppressed, and the way that reality transforms both. The second chapter contrasts the "banking model" of education—in which students passively receive deposits of knowledge from the teacher—with a problem-posing pedagogy built on dialogue and mutual inquiry. The third chapter develops the concept of dialogics, the word, and generative themes. The fourth chapter distinguishes dialogical from anti-dialogical action and applies the theory to revolutionary leadership and cultural action.

Key concepts

Central ideas include conscientização (critical consciousness), by which the oppressed perceive social, political, and economic contradictions and act against oppressive elements of reality; praxis, reflection and action directed at transforming the world; and the rejection of education as an instrument of domination. Freire argues that education is never neutral: it either functions as an instrument to integrate learners into the existing system or becomes a practice of freedom.

Reception and influence

The book became a landmark of critical pedagogy and influenced liberation theology, adult education, and literacy campaigns in Latin America, Africa, and beyond. It has also attracted criticism, including from conservative educators who object to its explicitly political aims, and from some scholars who have questioned aspects of its argument or its application in classroom practice. Freire's later works, including Pedagogy of Hope (1992), revisit and extend its themes.
