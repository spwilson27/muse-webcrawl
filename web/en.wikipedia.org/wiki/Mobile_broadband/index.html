Mobile broadband

Mobile broadband is the marketing term for wireless Internet access delivered through cellular networks to portable devices such as smartphones, tablets, laptops and mobile hotspots. Unlike fixed broadband, it allows users to connect wherever cellular coverage is available.

Technology

Mobile broadband is provided by successive generations of cellular standards:

  • 3G technologies such as UMTS/HSPA and CDMA2000/EV-DO brought data rates of a few megabits per second in the 2000s.
  • 4G LTE and LTE Advanced, deployed from around 2009–2010, raised typical speeds to tens of megabits per second and made video streaming and app-based services routine.
  • 5G NR, launched commercially from 2019, offers still higher throughput, lower latency and support for massive numbers of connected devices, using both sub-6 GHz and millimetre-wave spectrum.

Access devices

Access is built into smartphones and many tablets and laptops. Dedicated devices include USB modems ("dongles"), portable Wi-Fi hotspots (MiFi devices) and fixed-wireless home routers that connect a household over the cellular network.

Coverage and use

Mobile broadband is the primary means of Internet access for much of the world's population, particularly in regions where fixed infrastructure is limited. By the early 2020s, mobile networks covered more than 95 per cent of the world's population, and mobile connections numbered in the billions. Usage is commonly sold with monthly data allowances, though unlimited plans are widespread in some markets.
