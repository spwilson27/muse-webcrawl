David Alfaro Siqueiros

David Alfaro Siqueiros (born José de Jesús Alfaro Siqueiros; December 29, 1896 – January 6, 1974) was a Mexican social realist painter, best known for his large public murals in fresco and other media, and a leading figure, with Diego Rivera and José Clemente Orozco, of the Mexican muralism movement — the group known as "Los Tres Grandes." A committed communist, he was also a political activist, soldier, and would-be assassin of Leon Trotsky.

Life and work

Siqueiros fought in the Mexican Revolution and later in the Spanish Civil War on the Republican side. His murals, marked by dramatic foreshortening, sweeping movement, and revolutionary themes, include works at the National Preparatory School, Chapultepec Castle, and the Polyforum Cultural Siqueiros in Mexico City, whose exterior bears his enormous mural The March of Humanity (1966–1971), among the largest murals in the world. In Los Angeles in 1932 he painted América Tropical, whose anti-imperialist imagery led to its whitewashing; it was conserved and reopened to public view in 2012. Siqueiros experimented with industrial paints and the spray gun, techniques that influenced the young Jackson Pollock, who attended his 1936 New York workshop.

Politics

A Stalinist, Siqueiros led a machine-gun assault on Trotsky's house in Coyoacán in May 1940, which Trotsky survived; he was imprisoned or exiled several times, including a four-year sentence in the 1960s for "social dissolution."
