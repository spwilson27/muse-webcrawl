Six Yogas of Naropa

Tibetanནཱ་རོ་ཆོས་དྲུག, na ro chos drug
TraditionVajrayana Buddhism (esp. Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism)

The Six Yogas of Naropa (Tibetan: na ro chos drug, "the six doctrines/dharmas of Naropa") are a set of advanced Tibetan Buddhist tantric practices compiled by the Indian mahasiddha Naropa (1016–1100), received by him from his teacher Tilopa and transmitted to Tibet by Marpa the Translator. They form a core curriculum of the Kagyu school — and, in modified form, are also practised in the Gelug tradition — aiming at enlightenment within one lifetime, or at the latest in the intermediate state (bardo) after death.

The six practices

  1. Tummo (gtum mo, "inner heat") — generation of psychic heat through breath retention and visualisation at the navel chakra, the foundation practice; adepts famously demonstrate it by drying wet sheets on their bodies in freezing weather
  2. Illusory body (sgyu lus) — realising the body and all phenomena as illusory, like a reflection or dream
  3. Dream yoga (rmi lam) — maintaining awareness within dreams, transforming and multiplying dream contents
  4. Clear light ('od gsal) — recognising the luminous nature of mind, practised in waking meditation and at the moment of sleep and death
  5. Bardo (bar do) — instructions for navigating the intermediate state between death and rebirth
  6. Phowa ('pho ba) — transference of consciousness at death to a pure realm

Transmission and practice

The yogas presuppose tantric empowerment, preliminaries (ngöndro) and mastery of the generation stage of deity yoga, and were traditionally kept secret, taught one-to-one. In modern times they have been popularised through translations and teachers such as Lama Yeshe and Glenn Mullin, and tummo has attracted scientific study: Herbert Benson's research in the 1980s documented practitioners raising peripheral body temperature, and later studies have examined its effects on core temperature and cognition. A parallel set, the Six Yogas of Niguma, is preserved in the Shangpa Kagyu lineage.
