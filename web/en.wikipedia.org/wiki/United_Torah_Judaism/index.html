United Torah Judaism

United Torah Judaism (Hebrew: יהדות התורה המאוחדת, Yahadut HaTorah HaMeuhedet) is a political alliance in Israel representing Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) Ashkenazi Jews in the Knesset. It is an alliance of two parties: Agudat Yisrael, representing Hasidic communities, and Degel HaTorah, representing the Lithuanian (non-Hasidic) yeshiva world.

History

The alliance was formed in 1992, when Degel HaTorah, which had split from Agudat Yisrael in 1988, rejoined in a joint list for the Knesset elections. Since then, the two factions have generally run together, agreeing to share seats between them, though the internal balance has shifted over time toward Degel HaTorah.

Positions

United Torah Judaism focuses on issues affecting the Haredi community: funding for yeshivas and religious education, exemptions from military service for full-time Torah students, Sabbath observance in the public sphere, religious status-quo arrangements, and child allowances and welfare benefits for large families. The party has frequently joined governing coalitions, supporting both right-wing and centrist governments in exchange for concessions on these issues.

Electoral performance

The alliance typically wins between five and eight Knesset seats, reflecting the size of the Ashkenazi Haredi population.
