Learning sciences

The learning sciences is an interdisciplinary field that studies teaching and learning, drawing on cognitive psychology, educational psychology, computer science, anthropology, sociology, neuroscience, and design. Emerging as a distinct field in the late 1980s and early 1990s, it emphasizes understanding learning in real-world settings and designing innovative learning environments.

History

The field coalesced around the founding of the Journal of the Learning Sciences in 1991 and early graduate programs such as Northwestern University's program, founded in 1991. It grew from cognitive science and artificial intelligence in education, expanding to include situated cognition, sociocultural theory, and design-based research.

Research themes

Learning scientists study how people learn concepts and practices, how technology can scaffold collaboration and inquiry, and how classrooms and informal settings (museums, online communities) shape learning. Characteristic methods include design-based research, learning analytics, and video analysis of interaction. The International Society of the Learning Sciences (ISLS) sponsors the field's main conferences and journals.
