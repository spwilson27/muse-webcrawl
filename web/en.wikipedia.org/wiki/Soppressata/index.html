Soppressata

Soppressata is an Italian dry salami, produced in several regional varieties. Two principal types are made: a cured dry sausage typical of Basilicata, Apulia, and Calabria, and a very different uncured salami made in Tuscany and Liguria. It is often seasoned with hot pepper, especially in the Calabrian style.

Production

Soppressata is traditionally made from lean cuts of pork, coarsely chopped rather than finely ground, mixed with diced fat, salt, and spices. The mixture is stuffed into large casings and pressed — the name derives from soppressare, "to press" — giving the sausage a characteristic flattened oval cross-section. It is then dried and cured for several weeks.

Regional varieties

Soppressata di Calabria holds DOP (protected designation of origin) status and is characteristically spicy, flavoured with Calabrian chilli pepper. Varieties from Basilicata and Apulia are often milder. In Tuscany, soppressata toscana is an uncured product made from leftover cuts of the pig's head, set in gelatin, closer to head cheese than to salami.

Use

Soppressata is eaten sliced as an antipasto, on sandwiches, and as a pizza topping; spicy soppressata became especially popular on pizzas in North America in the 2010s.
