Elementary and Secondary Education Act

The Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) was passed as a part of United States President Lyndon B. Johnson's "War on Poverty" and has been the most far-reaching federal legislation affecting education ever passed by the United States Congress. Johnson proposed the bill on January 12, 1965, and signed it into law on April 11, 1965, only 87 days later.

Background

The act was a significant piece of the Great Society domestic agenda. It represented a major commitment by the federal government to education quality and to closing the achievement gap between children from low-income families and their peers. Title I of the act provided funding to local educational agencies serving large concentrations of children from low-income families.

Provisions

The original act contained six titles. Title I provided financial assistance to schools with high numbers of children from low-income families. Other titles funded school library resources, supplementary educational centers, state departments of education, and educational research.

Reauthorizations

The ESEA has been reauthorized many times. Major revisions include the Improving America's Schools Act of 1994, the No Child Left Behind Act of 2001, which introduced standards-based accountability and annual testing, and the Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015, which shifted significant authority back to the states while retaining annual testing requirements.

Legacy

The ESEA established the modern framework for federal involvement in primary and secondary education in the United States. Title I remains the largest federal funding program for elementary and secondary education.
