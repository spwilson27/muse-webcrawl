Quick reaction alert

Quick reaction alert (QRA) is a state of readiness and a mode of operation of air defence fighters, maintained at all hours to intercept unidentified or potentially hostile aircraft. In NATO, QRA forces police alliance airspace under the NATO Integrated Air and Missile Defence System.

Operation

Armed interceptor aircraft and crews are held at readiness — typically able to be airborne within minutes — at designated air bases. When radar or air-traffic reports identify a track of interest, the fighters are "scrambled" to intercept, identify the aircraft visually, and escort it as necessary.

United Kingdom

The Royal Air Force maintains QRA from RAF Coningsby in England and RAF Lossiemouth in Scotland, flown by Typhoon FGR4s. British QRA jets regularly intercept Russian military aircraft approaching UK airspace and respond to civilian airliners that lose radio contact. During the Cold War, the role was performed by Lightnings and Phantoms against Soviet bombers probing UK defences.

Elsewhere

Similar alert arrangements exist worldwide, including Baltic Air Policing, in which NATO allies rotate detachments to protect the airspace of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania.
