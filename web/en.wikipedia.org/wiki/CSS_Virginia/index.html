CSS Virginia

CSS Virginia was the first steam-powered ironclad warship built by the Confederate States Navy during the first year of the American Civil War. She was constructed as a casemate ironclad ram built on the raised hull of the scuttled steam frigate USS Merrimack. Virginia was one of the ships engaged in the two-day Battle of Hampton Roads in March 1862, opposing the Union's USS Monitor. The battle is chiefly significant in naval history as the first meeting in combat of ironclad warships.

Construction

When Virginia seceded in April 1861, Union forces burned the Gosport Navy Yard at Norfolk, scuttling the steam frigate Merrimack. The Confederates raised the hull and, under a design by John Mercer Brooke, John L. Porter, and William P. Williamson, cut it down and built a sloped casemate of heavy timber plated with two layers of 2-inch iron rolled at the Tredegar Iron Works in Richmond. The ship was armed with ten guns and fitted with a cast-iron ram.

Battle of Hampton Roads

On 8 March 1862, captained by Flag Officer Franklin Buchanan, Virginia steamed into Hampton Roads and destroyed the wooden Union warships USS Cumberland (rammed and sunk) and USS Congress (set afire), and ran USS Minnesota aground, before withdrawing at dusk; Buchanan was wounded. The following day, 9 March, she fought the newly arrived Union ironclad USS Monitor to a tactical stalemate in a four-hour duel at point-blank range — neither ship could seriously damage the other — a result that announced the end of the wooden warship era.

End

Virginia's deep draft confined her to the Norfolk area, and she was unable to challenge the Union blockade further. When Union forces retook Norfolk in May 1862, her crew could not lighten her enough to escape up the James River; on 11 May 1862 she was run aground off Craney Island and burned, her magazine exploding in the early morning.
