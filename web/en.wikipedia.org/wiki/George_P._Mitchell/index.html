George Phydias Mitchell (21 May 1919 – 26 July 2013) was an American businessman, real estate developer and philanthropist, widely credited with pioneering the economic extraction of shale gas through hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling.

Early life and career

Mitchell was born in Galveston, Texas, the son of Greek immigrants. He studied petroleum engineering and geology at Texas A&M University, graduating first in his class in 1940, and served as an officer in the Army Corps of Engineers during the Second World War. After the war he built an independent oil and gas company, Mitchell Energy & Development, in Texas.

Shale gas

Beginning in the 1980s, Mitchell's company spent nearly two decades experimenting in the Barnett Shale of north Texas. By combining hydraulic fracturing with horizontal drilling — and developing slickwater fracturing techniques in 1997–98 — Mitchell Energy made shale gas production commercially viable. The breakthrough launched the shale revolution that transformed the United States into the world's largest natural gas producer. Mitchell sold his company to Devon Energy in 2002 for about $3.1 billion.

The Woodlands and philanthropy

Mitchell founded The Woodlands, a large master-planned community north of Houston, in 1974, guided by principles of environmental planning. With his wife Cynthia he established the Cynthia and George Mitchell Foundation, supporting science and sustainability, and founded the Houston Advanced Research Center and the Mitchell Institute for Fundamental Physics and Astronomy at Texas A&M.
