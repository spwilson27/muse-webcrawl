Peterson Space Force Base, previously Peterson Air Force Base, is a U.S. Space Force base located in Colorado Springs, Colorado. It shares runways with Colorado Springs Municipal Airport. The base was first established in 1942 during the Second World War as Colorado Springs Army Air Base and renamed for First Lieutenant Edward J. Peterson, a Colorado pilot killed in a crash that year.

Role and tenants

Peterson is a centre of U.S. military space operations. It has served as headquarters for Air Force Space Command and, following the creation of the United States Space Force in 2019, hosts Space Operations Command. The base is also closely associated with North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) and United States Northern Command, whose headquarters functions are located at Peterson, and supports the headquarters of U.S. Space Command for periods of its history. Space Delta units based there conduct missile warning, space surveillance, satellite command and control, and positioning, navigation, and timing (GPS) operations.

History and facilities

During the Cold War the base supported air defence missions and the Cheyenne Mountain complex nearby. Peterson is home to the Peterson Air and Space Museum, housed in the original 1941 municipal airport terminal and hangars, preserving the aviation heritage of the site. In 2021 the installation was formally renamed Peterson Space Force Base.
