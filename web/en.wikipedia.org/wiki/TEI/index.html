TEI

The Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) is a text-centric community of practice in the academic field of digital humanities, operating continuously since 1987. The TEI produces and maintains international standards for the representation of texts in digital form, chiefly the TEI Guidelines, which specify an XML vocabulary for encoding prose, poetry, drama, dictionaries, manuscripts and many other kinds of text.

History

The initiative began at a planning meeting held at Vassar College in 1987, funded by the U.S. National Endowment for the Humanities, with the goal of ending the proliferation of incompatible encoding formats for electronic texts. The first full version of the Guidelines, known as P3, appeared in 1994; the current version, P5, released in 2007, is maintained as an XML-based system and updated regularly.

The TEI Guidelines

The Guidelines define several hundred elements organised into modules, covering the TEI header (metadata), core text structures, transcription of primary sources, names and dates, corpora, dictionaries, and more. Projects customise the standard through a formal customisation mechanism ("One Document Does-it-all", or ODD), producing tailored schemas from the full specification.

Use

TEI encoding is used by libraries, scholarly editions and digital archives worldwide, including projects such as the Oxford Text Archive and numerous critical editions of literary and historical texts.
