Wheatley Stable

Wheatley Stable was an American thoroughbred horse racing stable founded in 1926 by Gladys Mills Phipps and her brother Ogden L. Mills, descended from the Phipps family. Its horses raced in the distinctive colors of a yellow cap and purple sleeves with a yellow sash.

History

The stable, named for Mrs. Phipps's childhood home on Long Island, became one of the most successful racing operations in the United States. Its most famous homebred was Seabiscuit, foaled at Wheatley in 1933 and sold before becoming a champion under Charles S. Howard. Other notable Wheatley horses included Bold Ruler, winner of the 1957 Preakness Stakes and eight-time leading sire in North America, and his great offspring Misty Morn, Buckpasser was bred by Ogden Phipps separately. Bold Ruler's descendants, through Wheatley-bred mares, include Triple Crown winner Secretariat.

Legacy

Following the death of Gladys Mills Phipps in 1970 the stable was disbanded, but the Wheatley name remains influential through the pedigrees of many champion thoroughbreds.
