Hindu astrology

Hindu astrology, known in Sanskrit as Jyotiṣa ("the science of light"), is the traditional Indian system of astrology. It is one of the six Vedāṅga, the auxiliary disciplines connected with the study of the Vedas, where it originally served the practical purpose of fixing the calendar for sacrifices and rituals. It remains widely practised in India and the Hindu diaspora in personal consultation, matchmaking and the timing of ceremonies.

Basic elements

Hindu astrology uses the sidereal zodiac: twelve signs (rāśi) measured against the fixed stars rather than against the equinoxes used by Western (tropical) astrology. The difference between the two, the ayanāṁśa, is currently about 24 degrees. The ecliptic is further divided into 27 (occasionally 28) lunar mansions called nakṣatra, which are fundamental to timing and to the dashā system of planetary periods.

Nine celestial bodies, the navagraha, are used: the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn, together with Rāhu and Ketu, the ascending and descending lunar nodes, treated as shadow planets. The ascendant (lagna) — the sign rising on the eastern horizon at birth — anchors the birth chart (janma kuṇḍalī).

Dashas and prediction

The best-known predictive technique is the Viṁśottarī daśā, a 120-year cycle in which successive periods of life are ruled by the nine grahas in a fixed sequence and proportion; sub-periods are analysed in fine detail. Other branches include muhūrta (electional astrology, choosing auspicious times), horary astrology (praśna), and the analysis of omens (nimitta).

Texts

Classical authorities include the Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, attributed to the sage Parāśara, Varāhamihira's sixth-century Bṛhat Jātaka and Bṛhat Saṁhitā, and the astronomical Sūrya Siddhānta. In the twentieth century the system was popularised internationally by writers such as B. V. Raman.
