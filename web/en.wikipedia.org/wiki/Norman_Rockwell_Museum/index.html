Overview

The Norman Rockwell Museum is an art museum in Stockbridge, Massachusetts, dedicated to the art of Norman Rockwell and to the field of illustration generally. It houses the world's largest public collection of Rockwell's work, including the artist's final studio.

History

The museum was founded in 1969 with Rockwell's cooperation; the artist placed his studio and personal collection in trust with the museum. It originally occupied a building on Stockbridge's Main Street — the same street Rockwell depicted in a famous Christmas painting — before moving to its present purpose-built location in 1993.

In 2008 the museum received the National Humanities Medal, the first art museum to be so honoured.

Collection

The collection includes hundreds of paintings and drawings by Rockwell, among them works relating to his Four Freedoms series, and a complete set of his 323 covers for The Saturday Evening Post. The museum also maintains an archive of Rockwell's reference photographs and correspondence, and mounts exhibitions of other notable illustrators.
