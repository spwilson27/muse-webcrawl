The Nakagin Capsule Tower Building (中銀カプセルタワービル, Nakagin Kapuseru Tawā Biru) was a mixed-use residential and office tower in Ginza, Tokyo, Japan, designed by architect Kisho Kurokawa. Built between 1970 and 1972, it was a rare remaining example of Japanese Metabolism architecture, an avant-garde movement that fused ideas of organic biological growth with architecture.

Design

The building consisted of two interconnected concrete towers, eleven and thirteen stories high, serving as structural cores onto which 140 prefabricated steel capsules were attached with high-tension bolts. Each capsule measured 2.5 m × 4 m × 2.5 m and functioned as a small studio apartment or office for a single occupant, furnished with a built-in bed, storage, a bathroom unit, and appliances such as a reel-to-reel tape deck and a circular window resembling a porthole.

The capsules were intended to embody the Metabolism philosophy: they were designed to be individually replaced every 25 years so the building could adapt and "grow". In practice, none of the capsules were ever replaced, and the building deteriorated over time.

Preservation campaign and demolition

Residents campaigned for years either to renovate the building or replace its capsules, and preservationists worldwide listed it among endangered modern landmarks. In 2021 the owners voted to sell the site, and demolition began in April 2022. About 23 capsules were salvaged for museums and restoration projects, including collections in Japan and abroad; one capsule was acquired by the Museum of Modern Art in New York.
