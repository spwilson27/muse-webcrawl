Cyfarthfa Ironworks


Image placeholder: Cyfarthfa Ironworks

The Cyfarthfa Ironworks were a major iron and later steel works in Merthyr Tydfil, South Wales. Founded in 1765, they grew under the Crawshay family to become, for a time, the largest ironworks in the world, employing around 5,000 people at their peak in the early nineteenth century.

History

The works were established by Anthony Bacon on land leased at Cyfarthfa near the River Taff, exploiting local supplies of ironstone, coal, and limestone. Under Richard Crawshay and his descendants the works pioneered the use of the puddling process and supplied rails, cannon, and iron for bridges and ships worldwide.

Decline and remains

The works declined as the industry shifted to steel and coastal sites, and finally closed in 1919, with a brief reopening before final closure in 1928. Surviving structures include blast furnace remains, and the site is overlooked by Cyfarthfa Castle, now a museum.
