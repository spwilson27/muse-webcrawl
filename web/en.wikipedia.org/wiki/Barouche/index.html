Barouche

A barouche is a large, four-wheeled horse-drawn carriage with a foldable hood (calash top) at the rear. It has a high driver's box seat at the front and two transverse benches inside facing each other, seating four passengers. Fashionable in the 19th century, the barouche was used for leisurely drives and formal occasions in fine weather.

TypeHorse-drawn carriage
Wheels4
Capacity4 passengers plus driver
Popular19th century

Design

The name derives from the German Barutsche, itself from Italian baroccio. The folding leather hood could be raised over the rear seat for shelter, while the front seat remained open. Suspension by elliptical or perch springs gave a comfortable ride suited to park driving.

Usage

Barouches feature prominently in Regency and Victorian literature — Jane Austen's novels include the barouche-landau of Lady Catherine de Bourgh — as markers of wealth and social status. Variants include the barouche-landau, in which the whole body could be opened, and the sociable.
