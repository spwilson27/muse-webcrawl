TypeRegulatory agency

The Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) is a public organization in Canada with authority as a regulatory agency over broadcasting and telecommunications. It was created in 1976, taking over telecommunications responsibilities from the Canadian Transport Commission and broadcasting duties from the Canadian Radio and Television Commission.

The CRTC regulates Canadian television and radio stations, cable and satellite distribution, and telephone and internet service providers, with goals that include supporting Canadian content and ensuring accessible, affordable communications services.

Mandate

The Commission operates under the Broadcasting Act and the Telecommunications Act. Its broadcasting mandate includes licensing broadcasters, enforcing Canadian content requirements, and promoting Canadian programming; its telecommunications mandate covers competition, consumer protection, and wholesale access rules.

Notable decisions

The CRTC has made prominent rulings on net neutrality, wholesale internet rates, wireless codes of conduct, and the simultaneous substitution of Canadian advertisements into United States television signals during events such as the Super Bowl.

Structure

The Commission is led by a chairperson and commissioners appointed by the Governor in Council, with regional commissioners across Canada. Decisions can be appealed to the Federal Court of Appeal or reviewed by Cabinet.
