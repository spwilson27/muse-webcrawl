Hwang Dong-hyuk (Korean: 황동혁; born 26 May 1971) is a South Korean film director, producer, and screenwriter. He is best known internationally as the creator of the Netflix survival drama series Squid Game (2021–2025), which became the platform's most-watched series and made him the first Korean to win the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series.

Early life and education

Hwang was born and raised in Ssangmun-dong, Seoul. He studied at Seoul National University, graduating with a bachelor's and a master's degree in communication, and later earned an MFA in film production from the University of Southern California in Los Angeles, where his short film Miracle Mile (2004) won student awards.

Career

Hwang's feature debut was My Father (2007), about a Korean adoptee searching for his birth parents. He gained wider attention with Silenced (2011), based on the real-life abuse scandal at Gwangju Inhwa School; the film's public impact led South Korea to pass strengthened legislation against sex crimes against minors and disabled people, popularly called the "Dogani law". He followed with the hit comedy-drama Miss Granny (2014), remade in numerous countries, and the historical drama The Fortress (2017).

Squid Game

Hwang first wrote Squid Game in 2008–2009, drawing on his own financial struggles and on Korean and Japanese survival fiction, but studios rejected it for a decade as too violent and implausible. Netflix commissioned it in 2019, and the nine-episode first season premiered in September 2021, reaching number one in more than 90 countries and becoming a global cultural phenomenon. Hwang directed and wrote the entire first season himself, later returning for a second season (2024) and a third and final season (2025). The series won multiple Emmy Awards and made stars of its cast, including Lee Jung-jae and Jung Ho-yeon.
