Adjoint representation

In mathematics, the adjoint representation (or adjoint action) of a Lie group G is a way of representing the elements of the group as linear transformations of the group's Lie algebra, considered as a vector space. Intuitively, it describes how the infinitesimal symmetries of the group act on the group itself by conjugation. For a matrix Lie group, the adjoint action of an element g on a Lie algebra element X is simply Adg(X) = gXg−1.

Definition

Let G be a Lie group with Lie algebra g, identified with the tangent space at the identity. For each g in G, conjugation cg: h ↦ ghg−1 is a smooth automorphism of G fixing the identity; its derivative at the identity is a linear automorphism Adg of g. The assignment g ↦ Adg is a group homomorphism from G to GL(g), the adjoint representation. Its own derivative at the identity is the adjoint representation of the Lie algebra, ad: g → gl(g), given by adX(Y) = [X, Y], the Lie bracket.

Properties

The kernel of Ad consists of the center of the group (when G is connected), so the adjoint representation factors through the quotient G/Z(G). The adjoint representation of a semisimple group is faithful up to this center, and the invariant Killing form on g is defined by B(X, Y) = tr(adX adY). In physics, fields transforming under the adjoint representation — such as the gluon fields of QCD, which form an octet in the adjoint of SU(3) — are said to be "in the adjoint"; gauge bosons always transform in the adjoint representation of their gauge group.

Examples

For G = SO(3), the Lie algebra can be identified with R³ with the cross product as bracket, and the adjoint representation is the standard rotation action of SO(3) on R³. For the special unitary group SU(2), the adjoint action gives the familiar double cover of SO(3). For GL(n), the adjoint action is conjugation of matrices, whose decomposition into trace-free and scalar parts underlies the representation theory of the general linear group.
