Setsubun (節分) is the Japanese festival held on the day before the beginning of spring in the old Japanese lunar calendar, in modern practice usually on 3 February. The name means "seasonal division"; originally each of the four seasonal transitions had a setsubun, but the spring one — associated with the lunar New Year — became the festival.

Customs

The best-known custom is mamemaki, the throwing of roasted soybeans (fukumame, "fortune beans") at a person wearing an oni (demon) mask, or out of the door, while chanting "Oni wa soto! Fuku wa uchi!" — "Demons out! Luck in!" The beans symbolically purify the household and drive away evil spirits; participants traditionally eat one bean for each year of their life. A more recent custom, originating in the Kansai region, is eating an uncut ehōmaki sushi roll in silence while facing the year's lucky direction.
