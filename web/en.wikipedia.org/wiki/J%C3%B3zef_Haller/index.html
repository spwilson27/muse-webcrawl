Józef Haller

Józef Haller de Hallenburg (13 August 1873 – 4 June 1960) was a Polish general and political figure. He is best known for commanding the Polish Army in France during the First World War, known as the Blue Army or Haller's Army, and for the symbolic "Wedding to the Sea" ceremony at Puck in 1920, which marked Poland's regained access to the Baltic.

Early career

Haller was born in Jurczyce near Kraków, then in Austria-Hungary. He studied at the military academy in Vienna and served in the Austro-Hungarian artillery. After retiring, he became involved in Polish cooperative and scouting movements in Galicia.

First World War

At the outbreak of the First World War, Haller helped organise Polish legionary units within the Austro-Hungarian Army. In 1918 he broke with Austria and crossed to France, where he took command of the Polish Army being formed there under the Polish National Committee. The Blue Army, some 100,000 strong, was transported to Poland in 1919 and fought in the Polish–Ukrainian and Polish–Soviet wars.

Later life

Haller served as a deputy to the Sejm and as inspector of artillery. A critic of Józef Piłsudski, he emigrated after the May Coup of 1926 disputes and returned during the Second World War to serve in the Polish government-in-exile. He died in London in 1960.
