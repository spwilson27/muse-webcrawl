Computer magazine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A computer magazine is a periodical publication — historically in print, and increasingly online — devoted to computers, computing technology, and related subjects such as software, video games, and the internet. Computer magazines range from professional and scientific journals for researchers and IT professionals to consumer titles aimed at home users, hobbyists, and gamers.

History

The genre grew alongside the computer industry itself. Early examples served mainframe and minicomputer professionals, but the arrival of microcomputers in the late 1970s created a mass market: Byte (founded 1975) became famous for technical depth and its Small Systems Journal orientation, and titles such as Creative Computing, Kilobaud Microcomputing, and Dr. Dobb's Journal flourished. In the 1980s and 1990s, general-interest and platform-specific magazines proliferated — PC Magazine, PC World, Macworld, Amiga Format, and many others — often carrying type-in program listings, reviews, and cover disks or CDs. Many of the longest-running titles were published by Ziff Davis and IDG.

Decline of print and move online

From the 2000s onward, the rapid availability of news, reviews, and tutorials on the web undermined print circulation and advertising. Many landmark magazines ceased print publication or ended entirely: Byte ended its print run in 1998, and PC Magazine published its last print issue in 2009, continuing as the online PCMag. Surviving publications generally operate primarily as websites, while the enthusiast niche persists in titles and communities covering retrocomputing, Linux, and gaming.
