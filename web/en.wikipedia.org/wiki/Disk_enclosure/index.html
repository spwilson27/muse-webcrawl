Disk enclosure

A disk enclosure is a specialized enclosure designed to hold and power hard disk drives or solid-state drives while providing a mechanism to allow them to communicate to one or more separate computers. Drive enclosures provide power to the drives and convert the data sent across their native data bus into a format usable by an external connection on the computer to which it is connected.

Types

Enclosures range from simple single-drive units, often sold as external hard drives, to multi-bay units supporting RAID configurations. Larger enclosures used in data centers may hold dozens of drives in a rackmount chassis and connect to servers over high-speed storage fabrics such as Fibre Channel or iSCSI.

Advantages

Separate enclosures allow storage capacity to be expanded independently of the host computer, simplify drive replacement, and make it easy to move large amounts of data between machines. Enclosures with multiple bays can also provide redundancy through mirrored or parity-based RAID levels.

Disadvantages

An external enclosure adds cost, cabling, and a separate power supply. Performance can be limited by the external interface; for example, a fast NVMe SSD in a USB enclosure may be bottlenecked well below its native speed.
