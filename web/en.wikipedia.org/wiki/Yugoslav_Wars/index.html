Yugoslav Wars
Date31 March 1991 – 12 November 2001
LocationSlovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Kosovo, North Macedonia
ResultBreakup of Yugoslavia; independence of successor states

The Yugoslav Wars were a series of separate but related ethnic conflicts, wars of independence and insurgencies fought in the former Yugoslavia from 1991 to 2001. The wars ended Yugoslavia and were marked by ethnic cleansing, war crimes and genocide, the charges on which the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia prosecuted political and military leaders.

Background

The death of Tito in 1980, economic crisis, and the federal structure of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia allowed nationalist leaders — notably Slobodan Milošević in Serbia and Franjo Tuđman in Croatia — to mobilise ethnic constituencies. Multi-party elections in 1990 brought nationalist governments to power in most republics.

The wars

  • Ten-Day War (1991): Slovenia's brief war of independence against the Yugoslav People's Army.
  • Croatian War of Independence (1991–1995): Croat forces fought Serb rebels backed by Belgrade; ended with Operations Flash and Storm and the Erdut Agreement.
  • Bosnian War (1992–1995): the bloodiest conflict, including the siege of Sarajevo and the Srebrenica genocide of 1995; ended by the Dayton Agreement.
  • Kosovo War (1998–1999): fighting between Yugoslav forces and the Kosovo Liberation Army, ended after NATO's 78-day bombing campaign.
  • Insurgency in the Preševo Valley (1999–2001) and the 2001 insurgency in Macedonia, ended by negotiated settlements.

Human cost and aftermath

Estimates place total deaths around 130,000–140,000, with millions displaced; Bosnia and Herzegovina alone lost over 100,000 dead. The wars left a legacy of contested memory, ongoing war-crimes trials, and successor states at varying stages of European Union integration.
