1900 Buganda Agreement

The 1900 Buganda Agreement (also called the Uganda Agreement, 1900) was an agreement between the British government, represented by Sir Harry Johnston, and the Kingdom of Buganda, signed on 10 March 1900. It formalised British rule over Buganda within the Uganda Protectorate while retaining the kabaka and chiefs in subordinate roles.

Buganda Agreement
Signed: 10 March 1900
Parties: Britain, Buganda
Signatory: Harry Johnston

Terms

Buganda was recognised as a province of the protectorate, its boundaries defined, and the kabaka — then the infant Daudi Cwa II, with regents including Apolo Kaggwa — recognised if loyal. A lukiko (council) was formalised. Land was divided: about half became Crown land, the rest allocated as mailo estates to chiefs and notables, creating a landlord class. Hut and gun taxes were introduced.

Consequences

The mailo system shaped Ugandan land politics for a century. Buganda's privileged status fuelled tensions with other kingdoms and later with independent Uganda under Mutesa II.
