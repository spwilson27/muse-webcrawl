Scrabster (Scottish Gaelic: Sgrabastair) is a small settlement on Thurso Bay in Caithness on the far north coast of mainland Scotland. It is best known for Scrabster Harbour, an important fishing port and the mainland terminal for the ferry service across the Pentland Firth to Stromness in Orkney, operated by NorthLink Ferries with the ship MV Hamnavoe. Scrabster lies just west of Thurso, the most northerly town on the British mainland.

Harbour

The harbour has been developed since the nineteenth century and is managed by the Scrabster Harbour Trust, established by Act of Parliament in 1841. It is one of Scotland's significant white-fish ports, with a fish market serving boats landing catches from northern waters, and it also handles cargo, oil-related traffic serving the Pentland Firth, and visiting cruise ships. A major redevelopment in the early 2010s, assisted by European funding, deepened and extended the harbour to attract new business including renewables support vessels.

History and surroundings

Near the harbour stands Scrabster Castle, a ruined bishop's palace of the bishops of Caithness, and the area has older remains including a broch at Scrabster. The lighthouse at Holborn Head, built in 1862 by David and Thomas Stevenson, guards the western entrance to Thurso Bay nearby. A lifeboat station of the Royal National Lifeboat Institution has operated at Scrabster since the nineteenth century.
