Talk box

A talk box and its sound tube

A talk box (or talkbox) is an effects unit that allows a musician to shape the sound of an instrument — usually an electric guitar or synthesiser — using their mouth. The instrument's amplified sound is driven through a sealed compression driver and up a plastic tube into the performer's mouth; by changing the shape of the mouth and silently mouthing words, the performer filters the sound, and a microphone picks up the result, making the instrument appear to "talk" or sing.

History and notable users

Precursors included the "singing" steel guitar effects of the 1930s–40s and the Sonovox. The modern device was developed in the late 1960s and commercialised in the 1970s, notably by Heil Sound. Peter Frampton popularised it on his 1976 album Frampton Comes Alive! ("Do You Feel Like We Do" and "Show Me the Way"), Joe Walsh used it on "Rocky Mountain Way", and Roger Troutman of Zapp made it a signature of funk with hits like "More Bounce to the Ounce". It has since been used across rock, funk, and electronic music.

Distinction from vocoder and Auto-Tune

The talk box is often confused with the vocoder, which electronically analyses and resynthesises the voice, and with pitch-correction effects; unlike both, the talk box uses the performer's actual mouth as an acoustic filter and involves no processing of the voice itself.
