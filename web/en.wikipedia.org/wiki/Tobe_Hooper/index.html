Tobe Hooper

Tobe Hooper (January 25, 1943 – August 26, 2017) was an American film director, screenwriter and producer, best known for his work in the horror genre. His 1974 film The Texas Chain Saw Massacre is widely regarded as one of the most influential horror films ever made.

Career

Hooper, a former college instructor and documentary cameraman from Austin, Texas, co-wrote and directed The Texas Chain Saw Massacre on a tiny budget; it became a huge commercial success and a cultural landmark. He followed it with Eaten Alive (1976), the TV miniseries Salem's Lot (1979) and The Funhouse (1981). In 1982 he directed Poltergeist, produced by Steven Spielberg, another major hit. Later films include Lifeforce (1985), Invaders from Mars (1986) and The Texas Chainsaw Massacre 2 (1986), along with extensive television work.

Legacy

Hooper died in Sherman Oaks, Los Angeles, in 2017. His raw, documentary-style approach to horror influenced generations of filmmakers, and the original Chain Saw is preserved in the Museum of Modern Art's collection.
