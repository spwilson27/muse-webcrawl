False flag

A false flag operation is an act committed with the intent of disguising the actual source of responsibility and pinning blame on another party. The term originates in naval warfare, where a ship would fly the flag of a neutral or enemy country to deceive others; international law later came to require warships to display their true colours before engaging. In modern usage the concept extends to military operations, espionage, political dirty tricks and, in internet security, to "false flag" hacking carried out under another actor's apparent identity.

Documented historical examples

  • Mukden Incident (1931): Japanese officers staged an explosion on the South Manchuria Railway and blamed Chinese forces, providing a pretext for the invasion of Manchuria.
  • Gleiwitz incident (1939): Nazi SS operatives, as part of Operation Himmler, attacked a German radio station while dressed in Polish uniforms, manufacturing a justification for the invasion of Poland.
  • Soviet shelling of Mainila (1939): the USSR accused Finland of shelling a Soviet village, later shown to have been staged, as a pretext for the Winter War.
  • Operation Northwoods (1962): a proposal drafted by the US Joint Chiefs of Staff to stage attacks and blame Cuba; it was rejected by President Kennedy and never carried out, and was declassified decades later.
  • Lavon Affair (1954): an Israeli covert operation in Egypt, exposed when operatives planting bombs at Western targets were caught.
  • Second Italo-Ethiopian War pretexts (1934–35) and other manufactured border incidents are also frequently discussed by historians.

Use of the term

Because genuine false-flag operations are documented, the term is also widely invoked — often without evidence — in conspiracy theories about terror attacks and other events; analysts caution that the documented frequency of real false flags is low relative to the number of events so labelled in public debate. The concept is distinct from, though related to, a casus belli, a "wag the dog" diversion, and cover stories in intelligence operations.
