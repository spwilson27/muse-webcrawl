Realis mood

A realis mood (abbreviated REAL) is a grammatical mood which is used principally to indicate that something is actually the case or actually a fact. It is an epistemic mood, in the wider sense, that asserts that an event or state of affairs has occurred, is occurring, or is certain to occur. Realis moods are contrasted with irrealis moods, which are used for situations that are hypothetical, counterfactual, desired, or not yet realized.

Usage

The most common realis mood is the indicative mood, which is used for factual statements and positive beliefs — for example, "He works" or "She has left." In many languages, however, the realis/irrealis distinction is marked with dedicated verb forms rather than the European indicative/subjunctive contrast. Such systems are found in many Indigenous languages of the Americas, Papuan languages, and Austronesian languages.

Marking

In some languages realis forms are morphologically unmarked (the "zero" form), while irrealis forms carry special affixes. In others, both categories are overtly marked. The boundary between realis and irrealis clauses varies across languages: future events, for instance, are treated as realis in some languages and as irrealis in others, reflecting the uncertainty of events that have not yet happened.
