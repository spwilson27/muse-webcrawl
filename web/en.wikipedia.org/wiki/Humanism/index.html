Humanism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Painting detail of Renaissance scholars studying classical manuscripts]

Humanism is a philosophical stance that emphasises the value and agency of human beings and attaches primary importance to human rather than divine or supernatural matters. The term covers the Renaissance cultural movement centred on classical learning, and modern secular humanism, an ethical outlook based on reason and compassion without reliance on religious doctrine.

Renaissance humanism

Renaissance humanists such as Petrarch and Erasmus revived the study of Greek and Latin classics — grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry and moral philosophy, the studia humanitatis — and promoted education and civic engagement. Their scholarship helped reshape European art, science and religion.

Secular humanism

Modern humanist movements advocate rational inquiry, human rights and ethics grounded in human welfare, and in some countries humanist celebrants conduct non-religious naming ceremonies, weddings and funerals.
