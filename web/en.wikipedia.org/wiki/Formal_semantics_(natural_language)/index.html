Formal semantics (natural language)

Formal semantics is the study of the meaning of natural language expressions using the tools of logic and mathematics. Building on the work of Richard Montague in the 1970s, formal semanticists construct explicit, compositional theories that assign meanings to words and rules for combining them, so that the meaning of any sentence can be derived from the meanings of its parts and the way they are syntactically combined — the principle of compositionality.

Foundations

The field grows out of philosophical logic: Gottlob Frege's distinction between sense and reference, Alfred Tarski's theory of truth for formalised languages, and Rudolf Carnap's work on intensions. Montague's papers, including "English as a Formal Language" and the "PTQ" fragment (1973), argued that there is no important theoretical difference between natural and formal languages, and that English fragments could be given a rigorous model-theoretic interpretation via translation into intensional logic.

Core machinery

  • Model theory — sentences are evaluated against models consisting of a domain of individuals and an interpretation function; a declarative sentence is true or false relative to a model and assignment.
  • Types — expressions are typed: individuals (type e), truth values (type t), and functions between them. Noun phrases are commonly analysed as generalised quantifiers, functions from properties to truth values.
  • Lambda calculus — used to build and combine meanings, with function application and lambda abstraction as the modes of composition.
  • Possible worlds and intensions — the meaning of a sentence can be modelled as a function from possible worlds (or times and worlds) to truth values, capturing modality, tense, and propositional attitudes.

Phenomena studied

Formal semantics has produced influential analyses of quantification and scope ambiguity, anaphora and binding, tense and aspect, modality, conditionals, plurality and mass nouns, presupposition, focus, questions, and degree constructions such as comparatives. Discourse-level extensions, notably Hans Kamp's Discourse Representation Theory and Irene Heim's File Change Semantics, model how sentences update a shared information state across a conversation.

Relation to other fields

Formal semantics sits at the interface of linguistics, philosophy of language, logic, and cognitive science. Its results depend on syntactic theory (meanings compose along syntactic structure) and feed into pragmatics, which studies the aspects of meaning that go beyond literal truth conditions.
