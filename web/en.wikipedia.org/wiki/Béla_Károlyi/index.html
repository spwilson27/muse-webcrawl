Béla Károlyi (Hungarian: [ˈbeːlɒ ˈkaːroji]; September 13, 1942 – November 15, 2024) was a Romanian-American gymnastics coach of Hungarian ethnicity. With his wife, Márta Károlyi, he coached nine Olympic champions, fifteen world champions, and numerous continental champions in Romania and the United States, including Nadia Comăneci, Mary Lou Retton, and Kerri Strug. The Károlyis defected to the United States in 1981 and built a training empire in Texas, but their methods were also widely criticised as harsh and abusive, and their ranch later became central to the USA Gymnastics sexual abuse scandal.

Career in Romania

Károlyi was born in Cluj (Kolozsvár) in northern Transylvania. After studying physical education, he and Márta established a gymnastics school in Onești, where their experimental programme of selecting very young rural children produced Comăneci. At the 1976 Montreal Olympics, Comăneci scored the first perfect 10 in Olympic gymnastics history, and Károlyi became famous as her mustachioed, combative coach. Friction with the Romanian federation, which he accused of interfering and taking credit, deepened through the 1980 Moscow Olympics.

Defection and American success

During a 1981 exhibition tour of the United States, the Károlyis defected, leaving their young daughter behind in Romania (she joined them years later). Settling in Houston, Texas, they built a gym that attracted America's best young gymnasts. Károlyi coached Mary Lou Retton to the all-around gold medal at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics and the "Magnificent Seven" United States team to gold at Atlanta in 1996 — the Games at which the injured Kerri Strug performed her famous one-legged vault under his exhortation. He served as U.S. national team coordinator at the 2000 Olympics, while Márta held the post from 2001 to 2016.

Criticism and controversy

Former gymnasts and journalists criticised the Károlyis' regime of strict weight control, gruelling training through injuries, and verbal intimidation, described in Joan Ryan's 1995 book Little Girls in Pretty Boxes and in accounts by gymnasts such as Dominique Moceanu. The Karolyi Ranch in Huntsville, Texas served as the U.S. women's national team training centre until 2018, when USA Gymnastics severed ties after the conviction of team doctor Larry Nassar, who had abused hundreds of athletes, many at the ranch; the Károlyis denied any knowledge of Nassar's crimes, and Béla's legacy remains sharply contested. He died in Indianapolis in November 2024, aged 82.
