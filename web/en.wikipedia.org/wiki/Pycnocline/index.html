Pycnocline

Pycnocline
DefinitionLayer of rapid density change with depth
Typical depth (ocean)Roughly 200–1,000 m for the main pycnocline
Driven byThermocline and halocline gradients

In oceanography and limnology, a pycnocline is a layer in a body of water where the density changes most rapidly with depth. Because seawater density is controlled mainly by temperature and salinity, the pycnocline usually coincides with the thermocline (rapid temperature decrease) and/or the halocline (rapid salinity change). The pycnocline separates the well-mixed surface layer from the deep water below and acts as a partial barrier to vertical mixing.

Ocean structure

The open ocean typically has a three-layer vertical structure: a surface mixed layer (roughly the upper 100–200 m), the main pycnocline beneath it (roughly 200–1,000 m, strongest in low and middle latitudes), and the deep zone, where density increases only slowly. In tropical and temperate waters the permanent pycnocline is dominated by the thermocline; a shallower, seasonal pycnocline can develop in summer. In polar regions, where temperature contrasts are weak, salinity — through sea-ice formation and melt — controls the pycnocline, and in high latitudes the pycnocline may be weak or absent, allowing deep convection.

Significance

The density step of the pycnocline strongly stratifies the ocean: it limits the downward mixing of heat, oxygen and dissolved gases and the upward supply of nutrients, shaping biological productivity. Internal waves travel along the pycnocline, and its depth and sharpness vary with season, weather and climate — warming of surface waters tends to strengthen stratification, a trend with implications for ocean circulation and marine ecosystems. Freshwater lakes develop analogous seasonal pycnoclines during summer stratification, between the epilimnion and hypolimnion.

Related terms

A chemocline denotes a gradient in chemical composition, and a lutocline a gradient in suspended sediment concentration. The general term for any such rapid-change layer in the water column is a cline.
