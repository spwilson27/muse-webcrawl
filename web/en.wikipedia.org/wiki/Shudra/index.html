Shudra

Shudra (Sanskrit: शूद्र, romanized: Śūdra) is one of the four varnas in the traditional Hindu classification of society, positioned below the Brahmin (priests and teachers), Kshatriya (warriors and rulers), and Vaishya (farmers and merchants). Classical texts assign to Shudras the work of service, labour, and craft production. The varna is traditionally explained in the Purusha Sukta of the Rigveda, in which the Shudras are said to have emerged from the feet of the cosmic being Purusha.

In classical texts

Dharmashastra literature, including the Manusmriti, assigned Shudras duties of serving the three higher varnas, and restricted their access to Vedic study, sacrifice, and the sacred thread ceremony that marks the "twice-born" (dvija) status of the higher varnas. The interpretation, severity, and antiquity of these restrictions have long been debated by scholars, and epics such as the Mahabharata contain divergent views: figures such as Vidura, a counsellor famed for wisdom, are portrayed with Shudra parentage.

History and social reality

In practice, the varna framework overlapped with thousands of hereditary occupational communities called jatis, and the social and economic position of groups classified as Shudra varied greatly by region and period. Many farming and artisan communities counted as Shudra formed the demographic majority in much of India, and some — landholding peasant castes prominent in regional kingdoms — wielded considerable wealth and power. Under British colonial rule, caste classifications were systematized in censuses, sharpening varna identities in some areas while blurring them in others.

Modern India

The Constitution of India abolished untouchability and prohibited caste discrimination, and provides for affirmative action (reservation) in education, employment, and legislatures. Many communities historically classified as Shudra fall within the administrative category of Other Backward Classes (OBC), though the mapping of varna to modern categories is imperfect and contested. Reformers from Jyotirao Phule to B. R. Ambedkar criticized the varna hierarchy, while the 1990 implementation of the Mandal Commission recommendations, expanding reservations for OBCs, was a landmark in modern Indian politics.
