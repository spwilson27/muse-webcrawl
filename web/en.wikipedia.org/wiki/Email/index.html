Email

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: An inbox interface with a list of message subjects and senders]

Electronic mail (email, e-mail) is a method of transmitting and receiving messages using electronic devices. It was conceived in the 1960s as messages between users of time-sharing mainframes, took networked form on the ARPANET in 1971 (Ray Tomlinson choosing the '@' sign to separate user and host), and became the internet's first 'killer app'.

Email is delivered by SMTP (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol, 1982), stored on servers, and read via POP3, IMAP, or webmail interfaces. Messages are formatted per RFC 822 and its successors, extended by MIME for attachments and non-ASCII text.

Use and problems

Billions of messages are sent daily for business, personal correspondence, notifications, and marketing. The system's openness enabled spam — unsolicited bulk mail, a large fraction of all traffic at its peak — countered by filters, authentication standards (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), and legislation. Phishing via email remains a leading vector for fraud and intrusion.

Major providers include Gmail (2004, which popularised huge free storage and search), Outlook/Hotmail, Yahoo Mail, and Apple Mail clients; email remains a universal identity anchor for online accounts despite competition from messaging apps.
