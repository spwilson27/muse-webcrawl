The 1960 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XVII Olympiad, were held in Rome, Italy, from August 25 to September 11, 1960. Rome had originally been awarded the 1908 Games but declined because of the eruption of Mount Vesuvius. The 1960 Games blended ancient venues — wrestling was held in the Basilica of Maxentius and gymnastics in the Baths of Caracalla — with modern stadiums, and they were the first Summer Olympics to be widely televised.

Highlights

Ethiopian marathon runner Abebe Bikila won the marathon running barefoot, becoming the first black African Olympic champion. American boxer Cassius Clay (later Muhammad Ali) won the light-heavyweight gold medal. American sprinter Wilma Rudolph, who had overcome polio as a child, won three gold medals. Danish cyclist Knud Enemark collapsed and died during the team time trial after taking amphetamines, an event that pushed the Olympic movement toward formal anti-doping rules. Soviet gymnast Larisa Latynina led a dominant Soviet team, and the Soviet Union topped the medal table with 43 golds and 103 total medals, ahead of the United States.

Notable firsts

The Games marked the debut of a combined German team from East and West, the first appearance of several newly independent African nations, and the first time the Olympic hymn composed by Spyridon Samaras was adopted as the official anthem. The Rome Games also saw the last appearance of South Africa until 1992, as the country was banned from 1964 over apartheid.
