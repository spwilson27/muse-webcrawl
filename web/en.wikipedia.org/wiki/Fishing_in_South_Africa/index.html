Fishing in South Africa

Fishing in South Africa is a significant industry along the country's roughly 2,800 km coastline, which spans the cold Atlantic Benguela Current on the west coast and the warm Indian Ocean Agulhas Current on the east coast. The contrast between the nutrient-rich, upwelling-driven west coast and the warmer east coast gives South Africa a productive marine environment, and its commercial fisheries are among the most valuable in Africa.

Commercial fisheries

The most important commercial sectors include the deep-sea trawl fishery for Cape hake, the small pelagic fishery for sardine (pilchard) and anchovy, the rock lobster fisheries (West Coast rock lobster and South Coast rock lobster), the squid fishery based in the Eastern Cape, and the abalone fishery. Line fisheries target species such as snoek, yellowtail, and kob, and there is a substantial tuna and swordfish longline sector. Hake is the country's most valuable fishery, with a large share of the catch exported to Europe.

Management

Fisheries are managed under the Marine Living Resources Act of 1998, which established a system of total allowable catches and allocated fishing rights, with the stated aims of sustainability and of transforming an industry that was historically concentrated in a few large companies. The post-apartheid rights-allocation process — including the long-term rights allocations and later small-scale fisheries policy recognising artisanal fishing communities — has been politically contentious.

Problems

The industry faces chronic illegal fishing and poaching, particularly of abalone (perlemoen), which is poached for export to East Asia and has been linked to organised crime and the drug trade; the abalone fishery was closed in 2008 because of stock collapse and later partially reopened. Overfishing, poaching of rock lobster, declining sardine stocks, and the effects of environmental change on the Benguela ecosystem are continuing management challenges. Recreational angling and subsistence fishing are also widespread along the coast.
