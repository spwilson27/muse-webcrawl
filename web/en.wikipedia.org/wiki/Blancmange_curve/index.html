The blancmange curve is a curve in mathematics that is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere — an early example of a pathological function. It was first described by the Japanese mathematician Teiji Takagi in 1901 and is also called the Takagi curve. Its English name comes from its resemblance to the ridged, wobbly surface of a blancmange pudding.

Definition

Let s(x) be the distance from x to the nearest integer — a triangle wave of period 1. The blancmange function is the sum of scaled copies of this wave at ever finer scales:

blanc(x) = Σn=0∞ s(2n x) / 2n

Each term adds smaller ripples to the previous approximation; the series converges uniformly, so the limiting curve is continuous, but the ripples never smooth out at any scale, so no tangent exists anywhere.

Properties

The curve is self-similar under scaling, has a maximum value of 2/3 at points such as x = 1/3, and is a classic example in real analysis teaching alongside the Weierstrass function. Its graph is also related to the binary (dyadic) expansion of numbers and has been studied in connection with fractal geometry and the theory of nowhere-differentiable functions.
