Arthur Cayley (1821–1895)

Arthur Cayley (16 August 1821 – 26 January 1895) was a British mathematician who worked mostly on algebra and helped found the modern British school of pure mathematics. He was one of the most prolific mathematicians of the nineteenth century, publishing nearly a thousand papers.

Life

Cayley was educated at King's College School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he graduated as Senior Wrangler. Unable to support himself in mathematics alone, he trained as a lawyer and practised conveyancing for fourteen years, during which he published some 250 mathematical papers. In 1863 he became the first Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics at Cambridge, a post he held until his death.

Work

Cayley's contributions include the Cayley–Hamilton theorem in linear algebra, the systematic theory of matrices (his 1858 Memoir on the Theory of Matrices), the theory of invariants with James Joseph Sylvester, and the Cayley table describing finite groups. He proved that every finite group is isomorphic to a subgroup of a permutation group (Cayley's theorem), developed the octonions (Cayley numbers), and made foundational contributions to algebraic geometry and the theory of n-dimensional geometry.
