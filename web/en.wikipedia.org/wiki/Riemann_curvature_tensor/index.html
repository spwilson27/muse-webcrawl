Riemann curvature tensor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the mathematical field of differential geometry, the Riemann curvature tensor or Riemann–Christoffel tensor is the most common way to express the curvature of Riemannian manifolds. It assigns a tensor to each point of a Riemannian manifold that measures the extent to which the metric tensor is not locally isometric to that of Euclidean space. The curvature tensor can also be defined for pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, and plays a central role in the theory of general relativity.

Definition

Let (M, g) be a Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and let ∇ be the Levi-Civita connection. The Riemann curvature tensor is defined for vector fields X, Y, Z by:

R(X, Y)Z = ∇X∇YZ − ∇Y∇XZ − ∇[X,Y]Z

The tensor thus measures the noncommutativity of the covariant derivative, and hence the failure of a vector to return to itself after parallel transport around an infinitesimal loop.

Geometric meaning

Informally, parallel transport of a vector around a small closed loop on a curved space generally rotates the vector; the Riemann tensor quantifies this rotation per unit area of the loop. On a flat space the tensor vanishes identically. Equivalently, it measures how nearby geodesics deviate from one another, through the geodesic deviation equation.

Symmetries and identities

The Riemann tensor satisfies a set of algebraic symmetries: it is antisymmetric in its first two and last two arguments, symmetric under exchange of the pairs, and obeys the first (algebraic) Bianchi identity. It also satisfies the second (differential) Bianchi identity. In n dimensions it has n²(n² − 1)/12 independent components — 20 in four-dimensional spacetime.

Contractions

Contracting the Riemann tensor yields the Ricci curvature tensor and the scalar curvature. In general relativity, the Einstein field equations relate the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature to the energy–momentum content of spacetime, while the full Riemann tensor also encodes tidal (Weyl) curvature in vacuum.

History

The tensor is named for Bernhard Riemann, whose 1854 habilitation lecture founded Riemannian geometry, and Elwin Bruno Christoffel, who developed much of the tensor calculus. Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and Tullio Levi-Civita systematized the theory around 1900.
