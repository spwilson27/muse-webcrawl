The Nemean Games (Greek: Νέμεα or Νέμεια) were one of the four Panhellenic Games of Ancient Greece, held at Nemea in the Argolis every two years, in the second and fourth years of each Olympiad. Together with the Olympic, Pythian and Isthmian Games, they formed the circuit (periodos) of crown games whose victors were celebrated across the Greek world.

Mythological origins

Tradition connected the games with two myths. In one, they were funeral games for the infant Opheltes, who was killed by a serpent while his nurse Hypsipyle was showing the Seven against Thebes the way to a spring; the seer Amphiaraus renamed the child Archemorus, "beginning of doom", and the judges of the games wore dark mourning dress in his memory. In the other account, Heracles founded the games after slaying the Nemean Lion, the first of his Twelve Labours, and dedicated them to Zeus.

History

The historical games are traditionally dated from 573 BC. The sanctuary was controlled first by the small city of Cleonae and later by Argos. The Temple of Zeus at Nemea, built around 330 BC, stood within a sacred precinct that included a stadium with a vaulted entrance tunnel — among the earliest examples of its kind — whose stone still bears athletes' graffiti. The games were transferred to Argos around 271 BC and, despite attempts at revival, never returned permanently; they faded out in late antiquity. The victor's prize was a wreath of fresh wild celery.

Events

The programme resembled that of Olympia: foot races (stadion, diaulos, dolichos and the race in armour, the hoplitodromos), wrestling, boxing, pankration, the pentathlon, horse and chariot racing, and contests for boys. Nemea also included musical competitions in some periods. Victorious athletes were honoured with victory odes, most famously by Pindar, whose Nemean Odes commemorate winners of the games.

Modern revival

Excavations by the University of California, Berkeley, from 1973 onwards uncovered the stadium, baths and other facilities. Since 1996 the Society for the Revival of the Nemean Games has organised modern re-enactment games, in which participants of all ages run barefoot in the ancient stadium.
