The House of Orange-Nassau is the reigning royal house of the Netherlands. It originated when William I, Prince of Orange—"William the Silent"—a count of Nassau, inherited the Principality of Orange in 1544 and went on to lead the Dutch Revolt against Spain.

Stadtholders of the Dutch Republic

William the Silent led the revolt that created the Dutch Republic; his descendants served as stadtholders of most Dutch provinces and as leading commanders, including Maurice of Nassau and Frederick Henry. William III of Orange also became King of England, Scotland, and Ireland in 1689. The main line died out with William III in 1702; the stadtholderate passed to the Nassau-Dietz branch.

Kingdom of the Netherlands

After the Napoleonic Wars, William Frederick of the Nassau-Dietz line became King William I of the Netherlands in 1815. His successors include William II, William III, and three queens regnant: Wilhelmina, Juliana, and Beatrix. Willem-Alexander succeeded on 30 April 2013 and is the first Dutch king since 1890.
