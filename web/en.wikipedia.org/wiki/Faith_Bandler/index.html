Faith Bandler AC MBE (27 September 1918 – 13 February 2015), born Ida Lessing Faith Mussing, was an Australian civil rights activist of South Sea Islander and Scottish-Indian heritage. A campaigner for the rights of Aboriginal Australians and Torres Strait Islanders, she was best known for her leadership of the successful campaign for the 1967 referendum on Aboriginal Australians.

Early life

Bandler was born at Tumbulgum in northern New South Wales. Her father, Wacvie Mussingkon, had been taken from Biap island in the New Hebrides (now Vanuatu) as a boy in 1883 and worked as a labourer in the Queensland cane fields — part of the "blackbirding" trade. He later became a preacher and union activist. Bandler left school during the Great Depression and trained as a dressmaker in Sydney.

War service and marriage

During the Second World War she served in the Australian Women's Land Army, working on farms around Sydney. After the war she campaigned with other Land Army women for equal pay. In 1952 she married Hans Peter Bandler, an Austrian Jewish refugee and civil engineer.

Activism

Bandler co-founded the Aboriginal–Australian Fellowship in 1956 and became a central figure in the Federal Council for the Advancement of Aborigines and Torres Strait Islanders (FCAATSI), serving as its general secretary. From 1957 she led the decade-long national campaign of petitions and lobbying that culminated in the referendum of 27 May 1967, which passed with 90.77% support — the highest "Yes" vote in any Australian referendum — empowering the Commonwealth to legislate for Aboriginal people and count them in the census.

From the 1970s she also campaigned for recognition of Australia's South Sea Islander community, descendents of the blackbirded labourers. Bandler was appointed MBE in 1976 and a Companion of the Order of Australia (AC) in 2009, and received a state funeral in Sydney in 2015.
