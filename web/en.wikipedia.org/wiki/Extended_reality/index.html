Extended reality

Extended reality (XR)
EncompassesVirtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), mixed reality (MR)
Key devicesHead-mounted displays, smart glasses, smartphones
ApplicationsGaming, training, medicine, design, retail

Extended reality (XR) is an umbrella term for immersive technologies that merge the physical and virtual worlds, including virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR) and mixed reality (MR), as well as any future realities such technologies might bring. XR covers the spectrum described by the reality–virtuality continuum proposed by Paul Milgram and Fumio Kishino in 1994, ranging from the entirely real environment to a fully virtual one.

Components

  • Virtual reality replaces the user's view of the world with a fully simulated environment, typically via a head-mounted display.
  • Augmented reality overlays digital information or objects onto the real world, viewed through a phone camera or transparent glasses.
  • Mixed reality anchors virtual objects to the real environment so that they can be occluded by, and interact with, physical surroundings.

Applications

XR is used in entertainment and gaming, surgical and industrial training, remote assistance, architecture and product design, education, and therapy. Major consumer products have included Microsoft HoloLens, Meta Quest headsets, HTC Vive, and Apple Vision Pro, which Apple markets as "spatial computing".

Challenges

Open issues include display resolution and field of view, motion sickness, battery life, the cost of headsets, motion tracking accuracy, and privacy concerns arising from cameras and sensors that continuously map the user's environment.
