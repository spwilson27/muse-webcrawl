Jeffrey Wigand

Jeffrey Wigand (born December 17, 1942) is an American biochemist and former vice president of research and development at Brown & Williamson tobacco company who became a prominent whistleblower. He revealed that tobacco executives knew nicotine was addictive and that cigarettes were manipulated to enhance its effects.

BornDecember 17, 1942, New York City

Known forTobacco industry disclosures (1990s)

Whistleblowing

After being fired in 1993, Wigand cooperated with CBS's 60 Minutes and testified in litigation against the tobacco industry, despite legal threats and a smear campaign. His story was dramatized in the 1999 film The Insider, in which he was portrayed by Russell Crowe.

Later work

Wigand founded the non-profit Smoke-Free Kids, Inc., and has worked as an educator and consultant on tobacco control and whistleblower protection.
