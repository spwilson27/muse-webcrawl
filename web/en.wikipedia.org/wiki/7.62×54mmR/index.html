The 7.62×54mmR is a rimmed rifle cartridge developed by the Russian Empire and introduced as a service cartridge in 1891, chambered in the Mosin–Nagant rifle. It is one of the few rimmed, bottlenecked cartridges still in regular military service and among the longest-serving military cartridges in history.

History

The cartridge was designed alongside the Mosin–Nagant Model 1891 rifle. The "R" in the designation stands for rimmed. Originally loaded with a round-nosed bullet, it was updated in 1908 with a pointed "spitzer" projectile. It served through both world wars in rifles, carbines, and machine guns such as the Maxim and Degtyaryov designs.

Modern use

Today the cartridge remains standard for the Dragunov SVD sniper rifle, the PK/PKM general-purpose machine gun family, and the SV-98, and it is produced commercially for sporting use, including in surplus Mosin–Nagant rifles popular with civilian shooters.

Performance

With ballistics comparable to the 7.62×51mm NATO and .30-06 Springfield classes, standard ball loads drive a roughly 9.5–11.7 g bullet at around 800–860 m/s, giving an effective range well beyond 800 metres in suitable weapons.
