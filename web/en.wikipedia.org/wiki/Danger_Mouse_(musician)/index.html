Brian Joseph Burton (born 29 July 1977), known professionally as Danger Mouse, is an American musician, songwriter and record producer. He rose to fame in 2004 with The Grey Album, a mashup combining vocals from Jay-Z's The Black Album with instrumentals from the Beatles' "White Album", which brought him international attention amid a high-profile copyright dispute with EMI.

Career

In 2006 Burton formed Gnarls Barkley with singer CeeLo Green; their single "Crazy" became the first song to reach number one in the UK on download sales alone, and the album St. Elsewhere won Grammy Awards. He also formed Broken Bells with James Mercer of the Shins, releasing their self-titled debut in 2010.

As a producer Burton has worked with Gorillaz (Demon Days), Beck, The Black Keys — winning multiple Grammy Awards including Producer of the Year, Non-Classical in 2011 — Norah Jones, U2, Red Hot Chili Peppers (The Getaway), Adele (on 25), A$AP Rocky, Karen O (the collaborative album Lux Prima, 2019) and Michael Kiwanuka. His 2011 album Rome, with composer Daniele Luppi, was a homage to Ennio Morricone's spaghetti-western scores.

Style

Burton's production blends hip-hop sampling techniques with vintage soul, psychedelic rock and cinematic orchestration. He takes his stage name from the British cartoon series Danger Mouse, and early in his career performed wearing a mouse costume.
