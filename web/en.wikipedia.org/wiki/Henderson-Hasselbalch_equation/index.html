Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

In chemistry and biochemistry, the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation relates the pH of a solution of a weak acid to the acid dissociation constant (pKa) and the ratio of the concentrations of the acid and its conjugate base:

pH = pKa + log10( [A−] / [HA] )

History

Lawrence Joseph Henderson derived an early form of the equation in 1908 while studying buffers in biological systems. Karl Albert Hasselbalch re-expressed it in logarithmic terms in 1916–1917 in work on the pH of blood, giving the equation its modern form.

Applications and limitations

The equation is used to estimate the pH of buffer solutions, to design buffers for laboratory and pharmaceutical work, and in clinical medicine to analyse blood gases and acid–base balance. It is an approximation: it assumes equilibrium concentrations can be replaced by initial (analytical) concentrations, which fails for very dilute solutions, very strong acids or bases, or when the buffer is far from its pKa. When [A−] = [HA], pH equals pKa, where buffer capacity is maximal.
