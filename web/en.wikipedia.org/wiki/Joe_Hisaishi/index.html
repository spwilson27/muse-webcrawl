Joe Hisaishi (born Mamoru Fujisawa, December 6, 1950) is a Japanese composer, musical director, conductor, and pianist, known for scoring over 100 films. He is best known for his long collaboration with animator Hayao Miyazaki, having composed the music for nearly all of Miyazaki's Studio Ghibli films, from Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984) through The Boy and the Heron (2023), as well as for his work with director Takeshi Kitano.

Career

Hisaishi studied composition at the Kunitachi College of Music, where he developed an interest in minimalist music, and began his career as a typist of musical scores and arranger. He took his stage name in homage to American musician Quincy Jones (rendered phonetically in Japanese). His breakthrough came with Miyazaki's Nausicaä, followed by scores including Castle in the Sky (1986), My Neighbor Totoro (1988), Princess Mononoke (1997), and Spirited Away (2001), whose music—combining orchestral writing, piano solos, and elements of minimalism and Japanese traditional music—became internationally beloved.

With Takeshi Kitano, Hisaishi scored films such as Sonatine (1993), Hana-bi (1997), and Kikujiro (1999). He also composed for Departures (2008), which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. Beyond film, Hisaishi has released numerous solo albums, conducted major orchestras, and served as composer-in-residence and music director with orchestras in Japan and abroad, including the New Japan Philharmonic's World Dream Orchestra.
