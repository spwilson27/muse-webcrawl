Kidron Valley

The Kidron Valley (classical Hebrew: נחל קדרון, Naḥal Qidron; Arabic: وادي الجوز, Wadi el-Joz) is the valley on the eastern side of the Old City of Jerusalem, separating the Temple Mount from the Mount of Olives. It continues south-eastward through the Judaean Desert to the Dead Sea. The intermittent stream that flowed through it, the Kidron Brook, is mentioned frequently in the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament.

Biblical associations

King David fled across the Kidron when escaping from Absalom; King Hezekiah and Josiah are described as cleansing the Temple by burning idolatrous objects in the valley; and in the Gospels Jesus crossed the Kidron on the night of his arrest on the way to the Garden of Gethsemane, which lies at the foot of the Mount of Olives. Jewish, Christian and Muslim eschatological traditions associate the valley with the Last Judgment, and the prophet Joel's "Valley of Jehoshaphat" is commonly identified with it.

Tombs and monuments

The steep western slope of the valley below the Mount of Olives is covered by an extensive Jewish cemetery. Its most famous monuments are the monumental rock-cut tombs of the late Second Temple period in the lower valley: the so-called Tomb of Absalom (Yad Avshalom), the Tomb of Benei Hezir, and the Tomb of Zechariah. The Gihon Spring, Jerusalem's principal ancient water source, emerges in the valley near the City of David, and the valley also contains the Tomb of Pharaoh's Daughter and numerous burial caves of the Silwan necropolis.
