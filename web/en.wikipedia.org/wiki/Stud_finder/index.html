Stud finder

A stud finder (also stud detector or stud sensor) is a handheld device used to locate the framing studs hidden behind wall surfaces, typically drywall, so that pictures, shelves or fixtures can be fastened securely into solid timber or metal framing rather than into hollow wallboard.

Types

Magnetic stud finders use a magnet that is attracted to the nails or screws fastening the wallboard to the studs; they are simple and need no battery but detect only the fasteners. Electronic stud finders use capacitive sensors that measure changes in the dielectric constant of the wall as the device passes over a stud, and can locate stud edges or centres. More advanced models detect live electrical wiring and metal pipes, and radar-based scanners can image several kinds of objects within the wall.

Use

The user slides the device slowly across the wall; lights or beeps indicate the stud's edges, from which its centre — typically at standard spacings such as 16 or 24 inches in North American construction — is marked.
