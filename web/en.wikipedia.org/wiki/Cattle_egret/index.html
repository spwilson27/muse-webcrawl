Cattle egret

Scientific name: Bubulcus ibis
Family: Ardeidae
Conservation status: Least Concern

The cattle egret (Bubulcus ibis) is a cosmopolitan species of heron (family Ardeidae) found in the tropics, subtropics, and warm-temperate zones. It is the only member of the monotypic genus Bubulcus, although some authorities regard its two subspecies as full species.

It is a white bird adorned with buff plumes in the breeding season. It nests in colonies, usually near bodies of water and often with other wading birds. Unlike most other herons, it feeds in relatively dry grassy habitats, often accompanying cattle or other large mammals, as it catches insect and small vertebrate prey disturbed by these animals. This commensalism benefits the egret, and removal of ticks and flies from cattle is popularly, if only partly accurately, supposed to benefit the stock.

Distribution and habitat

Originally native to parts of Asia, Africa and Europe, the cattle egret has undergone a remarkable and rapid expansion of its range in the 20th century, naturally colonising the Americas, Australia and New Zealand. It is one of the few bird species to have colonised new continents without deliberate human introduction.

Feeding

The cattle egret feeds on a wide range of prey, particularly insects, especially grasshoppers, crickets, flies and moths, as well as spiders, frogs and earthworms. It typically follows grazing livestock and farm machinery, taking prey flushed from cover.
