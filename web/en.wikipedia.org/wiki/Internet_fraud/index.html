Internet fraud

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A phishing message, a common form of internet fraud

Internet fraud is a type of cybercrime fraud or deception that makes use of the Internet. It generally involves using online services and software to defraud victims or otherwise take advantage of them, for example by stealing personal information, payment card details or money.

Types

  • Phishing — fraudulent messages and websites that imitate trusted institutions to harvest credentials or financial data; variants include spear phishing, whaling and smishing/vishing by text and voice.
  • Advance-fee fraud — victims are promised a large sum in exchange for an upfront payment; the classic "Nigerian prince" or 419 scam is the best-known example.
  • Online shopping and auction fraud — goods paid for are never delivered, are counterfeit, or are grossly misrepresented.
  • Romance scams — fraudsters build fake relationships on dating sites and social media to solicit money.
  • Investment and cryptocurrency fraud — bogus trading platforms and "pig butchering" schemes promising high returns.
  • Business email compromise — spoofed or hijacked accounts used to redirect payments.
  • Identity theft, carding and account takeover — stolen personal and card data traded on underground markets.
  • Tech support scams, ransomware extortion and fake charities.

Scale and reporting

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) receives hundreds of thousands of complaints annually, with reported losses in the billions of US dollars; reported figures are believed to undercount actual victimisation significantly. In the United Kingdom, Action Fraud serves a similar reporting role. Prevention advice focuses on verifying requests through independent channels, using multi-factor authentication, and treating unsolicited offers with scepticism.
