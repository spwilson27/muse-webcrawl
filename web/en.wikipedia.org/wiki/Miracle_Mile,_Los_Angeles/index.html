Miracle Mile, Los Angeles

The Miracle Mile is a neighbourhood in the Wilshire District of Los Angeles, California, centred on a stretch of Wilshire Boulevard between Fairfax and Highland Avenues. Developed in the 1920s by A. W. Ross as an automobile-oriented shopping district to draw commerce away from downtown, it was one of America's first commercial strips designed for the car, with department stores set behind parking lots and signage scaled for drivers.

Museum Row

The eastern portion, Museum Row, hosts the Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA), the La Brea Tar Pits and Page Museum, the Petersen Automotive Museum, the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures, and the Craft Contemporary museum. The district preserves notable examples of Art Deco and Streamline Moderne architecture, including the former May Company building, now the Academy Museum.

History

Ross bought land on then-unpaved Wilshire in 1921, reasoning that the future lay westward; sceptics called the venture "Ross's folly", but within a decade the avenue's success prompted the name "Miracle Mile". The area mixes mid-rise offices, media companies, and dense residential streets to the north and south.
