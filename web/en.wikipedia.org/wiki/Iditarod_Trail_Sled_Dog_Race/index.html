Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race

The Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race, more commonly known as The Iditarod, is an annual long-distance sled dog race held in Alaska in early March. Mushers and a team of dogs travel roughly 1,000 miles (about 1,600 km) from Anchorage to Nome, typically in eight to fifteen days.

History

The race was founded in 1973 to preserve sled dog culture and the historic Iditarod Trail. It commemorates the 1925 serum run to Nome, when diphtheria antitoxin was relayed by dog sled teams — most famously Balto's team — to the stricken town.

The race

Teams generally start with twelve to fourteen dogs. Mushers must care for their dogs at checkpoints, and animal welfare rules govern rest, food and veterinary checks. Rick Swenson is the only five-time winner; the course alternates between northern and southern routes in different years.
