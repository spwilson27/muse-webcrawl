Chemiluminescence

A chemiluminescent reaction in an Erlenmeyer flask

Chemiluminescence (also chemoluminescence) is the emission of light (luminescence) as the result of a chemical reaction. Given reactants A and B, with an excited intermediate ◊, [A] + [B] → [◊] → [Products] + light. For example, if [A] is luminol and [B] is hydrogen peroxide in the presence of a suitable catalyst we have:

luminol + H2O2 → 3-APA[◊] → 3-APA + light

where 3-APA is 3-aminophthalate in its excited state, which decays to a lower energy level by emitting light.

History

The first chemiluminescent compound to be discovered was lophine (2,4,5-triphenylimidazole), reported in 1877. In the early 1900s it was noticed that luciferins and luciferases produced light in living organisms, a phenomenon classed as bioluminescence.

Applications

Chemiluminescence is used in gas analysis (for example, detection of nitrogen oxides and ozone), in forensic science — where the oxidation of luminol by iron in hemoglobin is used to detect traces of blood — and in immunoassays such as Western blotting. Everyday examples include glow sticks, in which the reaction of hydrogen peroxide with phenyl oxalate esters produces light.
