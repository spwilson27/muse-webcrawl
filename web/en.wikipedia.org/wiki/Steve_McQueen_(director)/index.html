Steve McQueen (director)

Sir Steven Rodney McQueen CBE (born 9 October 1969) is a British film director, producer, screenwriter and video artist. He received the Academy Award for Best Picture for 12 Years a Slave (2013), becoming the first Black British and first Black filmmaker to win that award as director-producer.

Steve McQueen
Born: 9 October 1969, London, England
Occupation: Film director, artist
Notable works: Hunger, Shame, 12 Years a Slave, Small Axe, Blitz
Awards: Turner Prize (1999), Academy Award

Art career

McQueen studied at Goldsmiths and made short silent films such as Bear (1993) and Deadpan (1997). He won the Turner Prize in 1999 and represented Britain at the Venice Biennale in 2009.

Films

His feature debut Hunger (2008), about Bobby Sands, won the Caméra d'Or at Cannes. Shame (2011) starred Michael Fassbender; 12 Years a Slave (2013) won three Oscars. Later works include Widows (2018), the acclaimed BBC anthology Small Axe (2020), the documentary Occupied City (2023) and Blitz (2024). He was knighted in 2020.
