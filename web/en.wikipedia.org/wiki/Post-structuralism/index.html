Post-structuralism

Post-structuralism is a philosophical movement that emerged in France in the 1960s and 1970s as a critique of structuralism. It questions the stability of meaning, binary oppositions, and the idea of a unified subject or centre of language and knowledge.

Key figures

Jacques Derrida (deconstruction, différance), Michel Foucault (power/knowledge, genealogy), Gilles Deleuze, Jean-François Lyotard, Roland Barthes (death of the author), and Julia Kristeva.

Concepts

Texts have no fixed meaning; meaning is deferred through chains of signifiers. Knowledge is entangled with power. The subject is constructed by language and discourse rather than prior to it.

Influence

Post-structuralism shaped literary theory, cultural studies, postmodernism, feminism, and postcolonial theory, and remains contested in analytic philosophy and science debates.
