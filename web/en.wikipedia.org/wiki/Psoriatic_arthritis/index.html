Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory arthritis associated with the skin disease psoriasis. It is classed among the seronegative spondyloarthropathies — "seronegative" because rheumatoid factor is typically absent. Inflammation affects the joints, the entheses (where tendons and ligaments attach to bone), the spine and the nails. Estimates suggest that up to roughly a third of people with psoriasis develop psoriatic arthritis, often about a decade after skin disease appears, although in a minority the arthritis comes first.

Signs and symptoms

Characteristic features include swollen, painful joints; dactylitis, a uniform "sausage-like" swelling of an entire finger or toe; enthesitis, often at the Achilles tendon or plantar fascia; nail pitting, onycholysis and discoloration; and inflammatory back pain when the spine and sacroiliac joints are involved. Five historical patterns are described — asymmetric oligoarthritis, symmetric polyarthritis resembling rheumatoid arthritis, distal interphalangeal predominance, spondylitis and the destructive arthritis mutilans — though patients commonly overlap patterns over time.

Causes and mechanism

The cause is a combination of genetic susceptibility (notably HLA-B27 for spinal disease and other HLA class I associations) and immune dysregulation involving the IL-23/Th17 axis and tumour necrosis factor. Obesity, smoking and streptococcal infection are among the environmental correlates. The same inflammatory pathways drive skin and joint disease, which is why many therapies are effective for both.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by imaging (ultrasound, MRI and X-ray findings such as erosions with new bone formation, the "pencil-in-cup" deformity) and laboratory tests; the CASPAR classification criteria combine current or past psoriasis, nail dystrophy, negative rheumatoid factor, dactylitis and characteristic radiographic changes.

Treatment

Management is graduated according to severity and the domains involved: NSAIDs and physiotherapy for mild disease; conventional DMARDs such as methotrexate (best for skin and peripheral joints); and biologic or targeted synthetic drugs — TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, IL-12/23 and IL-23 inhibitors, and JAK inhibitors — for more active disease. Comorbid cardiovascular and metabolic risk, which is elevated in PsA, is also addressed.
