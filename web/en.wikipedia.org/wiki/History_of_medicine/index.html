History of medicine

DisciplineMedicine
Key figuresHippocrates, Galen, Avicenna, Pasteur, Fleming

The history of medicine traces healing practices from prehistoric herbalism and ancient medical systems through the scientific revolution in medicine to today's evidence-based practice.

Ancient medicine

Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Indian (Ayurveda) and Chinese traditions developed systematic pharmacopoeias and surgery. Greek medicine, associated with Hippocrates (5th century BCE), emphasised natural causes, prognosis and clinical observation; Galen's humoral system dominated in Europe and the Islamic world, where scholars such as Avicenna systematised it in The Canon of Medicine.

Scientific transformation

Vesalius reformed anatomy (1543), Harvey described circulation (1628), and Jenner introduced vaccination (1796). In the 19th century, anaesthesia (1846), antisepsis (Lister), germ theory (Pasteur, Koch) and public-health reform revolutionised surgery and infectious disease.

Modern medicine

The 20th century brought antibiotics (penicillin, 1928/1941), imaging, organ transplantation, the polio vaccine, and the molecular revolution of DNA and genomics — alongside new challenges of chronic disease, cost, and equitable access.
