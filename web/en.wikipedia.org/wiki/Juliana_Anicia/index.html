Juliana Anicia

Anicia Juliana (c. 462 – 527/528) was a Roman imperial princess, the daughter of the western emperor Olybrius and a descendant of the Theodosian dynasty. She was one of the great aristocratic patrons of art and architecture in early sixth-century Constantinople.

Life

Born in Constantinople, Juliana married the powerful general Areobindus Dagalaifus Areobindus, who was briefly acclaimed emperor during the riots of 512. Though she hoped for imperial dignity for her family, she instead channelled her immense wealth into building and philanthropy in the capital.

Patronage

Juliana rebuilt the church of St. Polyeuktos (524–527), then the largest church in Constantinople, adorned with an epigram celebrating her imperial descent and claiming to surpass Solomon's Temple — a challenge later answered by Justinian's Hagia Sophia. She also restored the church of St. Euphemia and is traditionally associated with the Vienna Dioscurides, a luxurious illuminated manuscript of Dioscorides' herbal presented to her around 512, whose dedication portrait is among the earliest author portraits in medieval art.

Legacy

Excavations of St. Polyeuktos revealed sculpture of remarkable quality, and its innovations influenced Byzantine architecture. Juliana remains a model of female imperial patronage in late antiquity.
