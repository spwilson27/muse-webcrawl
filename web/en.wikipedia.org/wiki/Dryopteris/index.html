Dryopteris

Dryopteris, commonly called the wood ferns, male ferns (particularly Dryopteris filix-mas), or buckler ferns, is a fern genus in the family Dryopteridaceae, subfamily Dryopteridoideae, according to the Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I). It is a very large genus containing around 300–400 species, distributed widely across the northern hemisphere and in mountainous regions of the tropics.

Description

Species of Dryopteris are terrestrial ferns with stout, often erect rhizomes covered in scales. The fronds are generally bipinnate to tripinnate, forming shuttlecock-like crowns. The sori (clusters of sporangia) on the underside of fertile fronds are round and covered by a kidney-shaped (reniform) indusium — a feature that distinguishes the genus from many related ferns. The name combines the Greek drys (oak) and pteris (fern), referring to ferns found growing in oak woodlands.

Taxonomy and hybridisation

Dryopteris is notorious among botanists for extensive hybridisation and polyploidy, particularly in the D. filix-mas and D. carthusiana species complexes, which include allotetraploid species derived from crosses between diploid ancestors. Apomixis (asexual seed-like spore production) is also common in some Asian groups of the genus.

Uses

Many species and cultivars are grown as ornamental plants in shady gardens, including D. affinis (golden-scaled male fern), D. erythrosora (autumn fern, with copper-red young fronds), and D. wallichiana. The rhizome of D. filix-mas was traditionally used as an anthelmintic to expel tapeworms, though it is toxic and has been superseded by safer medicines.
