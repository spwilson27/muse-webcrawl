Lie bracket of vector fields

In the mathematical field of differential geometry, the Lie bracket of vector fields, also known as the Jacobi–Lie bracket or the commutator of vector fields, is an operator that assigns to any two vector fields X and Y on a smooth manifold M a third vector field denoted [X, Y].

Conceptually, the Lie bracket [X, Y] is the derivative of Y along the flow generated by X. This generalizes to the Lie derivative, which allows the differentiation of any tensor field along the flow of another vector field. The Lie bracket is a special case of the Lie derivative in the sense that [X, Y] = LXY.

Definition

Vector fields can be regarded as derivations acting on the ring of smooth functions on the manifold. The Lie bracket is then defined as the commutator of these derivations:

[X, Y]f = X(Yf) − Y(Xf)

for every smooth function f. In local coordinates this yields an explicit formula in terms of the partial derivatives of the components of X and Y.

Properties

The Lie bracket is bilinear over the real numbers, antisymmetric ([X, Y] = −[Y, X]), and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Together these properties make the set of all vector fields on M into an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. Unlike the wedge product of differential forms, the bracket is not a tensor: it depends on the values of the fields in a neighbourhood, not merely at a point.

Two vector fields commute — that is, [X, Y] = 0 — exactly when their flows commute. This fact underlies the Frobenius theorem on the integrability of distributions and is fundamental in control theory and the study of symmetries of differential equations.
