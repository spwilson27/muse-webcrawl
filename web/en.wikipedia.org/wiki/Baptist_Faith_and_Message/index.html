Baptist Faith and Message

The Baptist Faith and Message (BF&M) is the statement of faith of the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC), the largest Baptist denomination in the United States. It summarizes key Southern Baptist understandings of Christian doctrine in eighteen articles, covering the Scriptures, God, humanity, salvation, the church, baptism and the Lord's Supper, evangelism, education, stewardship, cooperation, and social order.

History

The original Baptist Faith and Message was adopted in 1925, drawing heavily on the 1833 New Hampshire Confession of Faith. Its adoption came partly in response to the controversy over evolution then dividing American Protestantism; the 1925 statement affirmed the authority of Scripture while avoiding explicit language on creation. A revised edition was adopted in 1963, chaired by pastor Herschel H. Hobbs, largely reaffirming the 1925 text with additions on the sanctity of life and other matters.

The 2000 revision

The most recent revision was adopted at the SBC annual meeting in Orlando, Florida, in June 2000. It included several changes that proved controversial: removing the statement that Jesus Christ is "the criterion by which the Bible is to be interpreted," affirming the ordination of only men to the pastoral office, describing the husband as head of the family in a complementarian framework, and adding language opposing abortion, homosexuality, and pornography. Critics, particularly among moderate Baptists, saw the revision as a move toward creedalism, while supporters defended it as a faithful restatement of Baptist belief.

Status

Southern Baptists historically resist creeds, and the BF&M is presented as a confession of faith rather than a binding creed. Individual churches are autonomous and not required to adopt it, though SBC entities and mission boards have required employees and missionaries to affirm it, a policy that generated significant debate after 2000.
