São Miguel das Missões is a municipality in the northwestern part of the state of Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, best known for the ruins of the Jesuit mission of São Miguel Arcanjo, one of the most important surviving monuments of the Guarani missions. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983, together with the missions of San Ignacio Miní, Santa Ana, Nuestra Señora de Loreto and Santa María la Mayor in Argentina.

History

The mission of São Miguel was founded in 1632 by the Jesuit Cristóvão de Mendoza in the Tape region of present-day Rio Grande do Sul. Raids by Portuguese bandeirantes from São Paulo forced the Guarani population westward, and the mission was re-established at its present site in 1687. Under Jesuit direction the settlement became one of the largest of the thirty Guarani reductions, its population reaching several thousand inhabitants who farmed, raised cattle and produced crafts, music and printed books.

Between 1735 and 1745 the mission church, dedicated to the Archangel Michael, was erected to designs associated with the Italian Jesuit architect Giovanni Battista Primoli (Giuseppe Bressanone). Built of red sandstone, the church was among the finest examples of the so-called Guarani baroque. The Treaty of Madrid (1750), which transferred the seven missions east of the Uruguay River to Portugal, provoked the Guarani War (1754–1756); the mission was burned and largely abandoned, and the Jesuits were expelled from Portuguese territory in 1759.

The ruins

Today the preserved remains include the façade and one tower of the church, fragments of the cloister, the cemetery and the layout of the mission town, including the central plaza. The site is administered by Brazil's national heritage institute (IPHAN). A small museum designed by Lúcio Costa in the 1940s houses Guarani religious sculpture recovered from the missions. In 2016 a tornado struck the town and damaged part of the ruin complex, prompting restoration work.

Legacy

São Miguel das Missões is the best preserved of the seven missions of the Sete Povos das Missões in Brazil and has become a symbol of Guarani and gaucho regional identity. The story of the missions was popularized internationally by the 1986 film The Mission.
