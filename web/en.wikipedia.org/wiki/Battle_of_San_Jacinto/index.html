The Battle of San Jacinto (Spanish: Batalla de San Jacinto), fought on April 21, 1836, in present-day Harris County, Texas, was the decisive battle of the Texas Revolution. Led by General Sam Houston, the Texian Army engaged and defeated General Antonio López de Santa Anna's Mexican army in a fight that lasted just 18 minutes.

After the fall of the Alamo and the Goliad massacre, Houston's army had retreated eastward in the "Runaway Scrape." Near the confluence of the San Jacinto River and Buffalo Bayou, Houston turned on his pursuers. On the afternoon of April 21, while Santa Anna's men rested during siesta, the Texians advanced across the open prairie screened by tall grass and a slight rise, shouting "Remember the Alamo!" and "Remember Goliad!" The Mexican camp was overrun; Santa Anna was captured the following day, disguised in a private soldier's uniform, and brought before Houston.

Background

In early 1836 Santa Anna personally led the Mexican Army of Operations into Texas to suppress the rebellion. The Alamo fell on March 6 after a 13-day siege, and on March 27 Colonel James Fannin's command was executed at Goliad on Santa Anna's orders. Houston's retreat drew Santa Anna deeper into East Texas and stretched his supply lines; confident the rebels were beaten, Santa Anna divided his forces and pursued with a flying column of roughly 700–900 men, reinforced on the morning of April 21 by about 540 troops under General Martín Perfecto de Cos.

The battle

Houston destroyed Vince's Bridge, the only escape route across Vince Bayou, cutting off retreat for both armies. At about 4:30 p.m. the Texian line — some 910 men with two small cannon, the "Twin Sisters" — advanced in near silence until within 200 yards of the Mexican breastworks. The attack achieved complete surprise. The brief battle became a rout and, in revenge for the Alamo and Goliad, an extended killing; Texian officers struggled to restrain their men. Roughly 630 Mexican soldiers were killed and 730 taken prisoner, against only nine Texians killed or mortally wounded — one of the most lopsided victories in American military history.

Aftermath

Santa Anna, captured on April 22, ordered his remaining forces to retreat south of the Rio Grande and signed the Treaties of Velasco on May 14, 1836, recognizing Texas independence in exchange for his life and safe passage. Although Mexico never ratified the treaties and periodically disputed the boundary, the Republic of Texas remained de facto independent until its annexation by the United States in 1845. The battleground is preserved as the San Jacinto Battleground State Historic Site, marked by the 567-foot (173 m) San Jacinto Monument, completed in 1939 and taller than the Washington Monument.
