Plan–Do–Study–Act

The Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) cycle is an iterative four-step method for testing changes and improving processes, widely used in quality improvement, especially in healthcare. It derives from the work of Walter A. Shewhart and W. Edwards Deming, and is closely related to the Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) cycle used in manufacturing and management.

The four steps

Plan: identify a problem or opportunity, form a theory of what change will improve it, and state explicit predictions. Do: carry out the change on a small scale, observing and documenting what actually happens, including unexpected effects. Study: analyse the results against the predictions and summarise what was learned. Act: decide whether to adopt the change, adapt it and test again, or abandon it — and plan the next cycle. The emphasis on small, rapid tests distinguishes PDSA from large-scale rollouts: a change is tried by one person, for one day, on one patient or unit, before resources are committed.

Use

Popularised by the Institute for Healthcare Improvement as part of its Model for Improvement, PDSA cycles are now standard practice in hospital quality work, education reform, and public services, and underpin rapid-cycle evaluation in industry. Deming, who credited Shewhart, preferred "Study" to "Check" because the step is about learning from data, not merely verifying conformance. Critics note that in practice cycles are often run superficially — without predictions or real analysis — which undermines the learning the method is designed to produce.
