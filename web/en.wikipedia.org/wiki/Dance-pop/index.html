Dance-pop

Stylistic origins: Pop, disco, synth-pop, post-disco, R&B
Cultural origins: Late 1970s–early 1980s, United States
Derivative forms: Teen pop, Eurodance (in part)

Dance-pop is a popular music subgenre that originated in the late 1970s to early 1980s. It is generally uptempo music intended for nightclubs with the intention of being danceable, but also suitable for contemporary hit radio. Combining the pop song structure with strong dance rhythms, it typically features simple, catchy melodies, prominent hooks and a verse–chorus format.

History

As disco declined, producers carried its rhythmic drive into mainstream pop through artists such as Madonna, Michael Jackson and Kylie Minogue, whose records dominated 1980s charts. The production teams of Stock Aitken Waterman in Britain and later Swedish producers such as Max Martin defined the genre's polished sound through the 1990s with acts like Britney Spears and the Spice Girls.

Characteristics and later development

Dance-pop songs generally run three to five minutes, use electronic drums and synthesisers, and emphasise rhythm over lyrical complexity. In the 2000s and 2010s the genre absorbed elements of EDM and hip hop, with artists such as Lady Gaga, Rihanna, Katy Perry and Dua Lipa scoring major hits in the style.
