Eastman Kodak Company

The Eastman Kodak Company, commonly known as Kodak, is an American technology company based in Rochester, New York, best known historically for its dominance of photographic film. Founded by George Eastman and Henry A. Strong in 1892 (with roots in the Eastman Dry Plate Company of 1880), Kodak for most of the 20th century held a dominant position in photography, popularising the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest."

History

Eastman introduced the Kodak camera in 1888 and the inexpensive Brownie camera in 1900, bringing photography to the mass market. Kodak produced roll film, Kodachrome colour film (1935), Instamatic cameras and motion-picture film stocks used across Hollywood. The company diversified into photocopiers, chemicals and pharmaceuticals.

Although a Kodak engineer, Steven Sasson, built the first digital camera in 1975, Kodak was slow to abandon its profitable film business. As digital photography swept the market in the 2000s, revenues collapsed; Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in January 2012, sold many patents and businesses, and emerged in 2013 as a smaller company focused on commercial printing, advanced materials and chemicals, while licensing the Kodak brand for consumer products.

Business today

Kodak today supplies digital and offset printing systems, plates and software to commercial printers, continues to manufacture motion-picture and industrial films, and licenses its trademark widely.
