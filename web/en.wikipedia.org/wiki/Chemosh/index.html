Chemosh

Chemosh (Moabite: 𐤊𐤌𐤔 Kamōš) was the national god of the Moabites, a people who lived east of the Dead Sea in what is now Jordan. He is known from the Hebrew Bible, where he is called "the abomination of Moab," and from Moabite inscriptions, above all the Mesha Stele erected by King Mesha in the 9th century BCE.

In the Mesha Stele

In the inscription, Mesha attributes Moab's subjugation by Israel to Chemosh's anger with his land, and credits the god with later victories, including the capture of Israelite towns and the devotion of their populations to destruction (the ḥērem) in Chemosh's honour. Mesha also records building a high place for Chemosh at Qerihoh, probably Dibon.

In the Hebrew Bible

The Bible names Chemosh as the god of Moab (Numbers 21:29; Jeremiah 48) and says Solomon built a shrine for him on the Mount of Olives, later destroyed by Josiah. Judges 11:24 has Jephthah address the Ammonites as if Chemosh were their god as well, which scholars debate. Some passages and inscriptions suggest an association with the god Ashtar under the compound name Ashtar-Chemosh.
