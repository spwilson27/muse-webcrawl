Maurice Denis

Maurice Denis (25 November 1870 – 13 November 1943) was a French painter, decorative artist and writer, a central figure of the Nabi group and an influential theorist of modern art. His 1890 dictum — "Remember that a painting — before being a warhorse, a naked woman or some story or other — is essentially a flat surface covered with colours assembled in a certain order" — became a manifesto for abstract and modernist painting.

Life and work

Denis was born in Granville, Normandy, and raised in Saint-Germain-en-Laye. Studying at the Académie Julian and the École des Beaux-Arts, he joined with Paul Sérusier, Pierre Bonnard, Édouard Vuillard and Ker-Xavier Roussel in forming the Nabis around 1888–89, inspired by Sérusier's Talisman, painted under Gauguin's guidance. Denis's paintings combined Symbolist spirituality with flat areas of pure colour; a devout Catholic, he sought a modern sacred art, producing murals, stained glass, mosaics and theatre sets. In 1919, with George Desvallières, he founded the Ateliers d'Art Sacré to renew Christian art. His house in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, Le Prieuré, is now a museum devoted to the Nabis.

Writing and influence

Denis's theoretical writings, collected in Théories (1912) and later volumes, charted a path from Impressionism through Cézanne — whose work his 1907 essay helped establish — to a classical, ordered modernism. His influence extended to the Fauvists and to later generations of abstract painters who took up his insistence on the flatness and materiality of the painted surface.
