Glycosaminoglycan

Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) or mucopolysaccharides are long, linear polysaccharides consisting of repeating disaccharide units. The repeating two-sugar unit typically consists of a uronic sugar and an amino sugar, except in the case of the sulfated glycosaminoglycan keratan, where, in place of the uronic sugar it has galactose.

Because the disaccharide units carry carboxyl and often sulfate groups, GAGs are highly negatively charged and attract water, giving cartilage its resistance to compression. With the exception of hyaluronan, GAGs are usually covalently attached to core proteins to form proteoglycans, and are synthesised in the Golgi apparatus.

The principal glycosaminoglycans are hyaluronan, chondroitin sulfate, dermatan sulfate, heparin, heparan sulfate and keratan sulfate. Defects in GAG degradation cause the lysosomal storage diseases known as mucopolysaccharidoses, while the anticoagulant drug heparin is itself a highly sulfated GAG.
