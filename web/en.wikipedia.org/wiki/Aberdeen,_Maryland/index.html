Aberdeen, Maryland

Aberdeen is a city in Harford County, Maryland, United States. The population was about 16,000 at the 2020 census. It lies on the northwestern shore of Chesapeake Bay along the Interstate 95 corridor between Baltimore and Wilmington, and is best known as the neighbour of Aberdeen Proving Ground, the United States Army's oldest active proving ground.

History

The area was first settled by English colonists in the seventeenth century. The modern town grew up around a Baltimore and Ohio Railroad station in the nineteenth century and was named by Scottish settlers after Aberdeen, Scotland. It was incorporated in 1892. The establishment of Aberdeen Proving Ground in 1917, on farmland condemned by the federal government at the start of American involvement in the First World War, transformed the town, providing employment and anchoring its economy to military testing and research; the nearby Edgewood Arsenal (now the Edgewood Area of the proving ground) was a centre of chemical weapons research.

Geography and transport

Aberdeen covers roughly 6.8 square miles on the coastal plain near the head of Chesapeake Bay, drained by creeks flowing into the Bush River. It is served by an Amtrak and MARC Penn Line station on the Northeast Corridor, by Interstate 95, and by U.S. Route 40, giving it a distinctly rail-and-highway-oriented character.

Attractions

Ripken Stadium, home of the Aberdeen IronBirds minor-league baseball team, is named for the family of Cal Ripken Jr., who grew up in the area. The former U.S. Army Ordnance Museum's collection was long displayed at the proving ground before its relocation to Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia.
