Figure skating at the 1976 Winter Olympics

Figure skating at the 1976 Winter Olympics took place at the Olympiahalle in Innsbruck, Austria, from 4 to 13 February 1976. Four events were contested: men's singles, ladies' singles, pair skating, and — for the first time at the Olympic Games — ice dance. Innsbruck hosted after Denver, the original host city, withdrew; the city had previously staged the 1964 Winter Olympics.

Highlights

Britain's John Curry won the men's title with a balletic free skate widely considered one of the finest of the era, receiving several perfect 6.0 marks for artistic impression. American Dorothy Hamill won the ladies' event and became a cultural phenomenon; her short "wedge" haircut was widely copied, and she turned professional to great commercial success after the Games. Irina Rodnina won her second consecutive Olympic pairs gold, this time with Alexander Zaitsev, on her way to three straight Olympic titles. In ice dance's Olympic debut, Lyudmila Pakhomova and Alexander Gorshkov of the Soviet Union became the first Olympic champions in the discipline.

Format

Singles and pairs were decided by compulsory figures or a short program combined with free skating, scored under the 6.0 system, in which figures accounted for a large share of the singles result. Ice dance consisted of compulsory dances and a free dance.

Medal table

The Soviet Union topped the figure skating medal table with two golds, two silvers, and no bronzes' fewer events — winning gold in pairs and ice dance. Great Britain, the United States, East Germany, the Netherlands, and Canada also reached the podium.
