Horse hoof

A horse hoof is the lower extremity of each leg of a horse, the equivalent of a human fingernail or toenail. It consists of a hard keratinous hoof wall surrounding the coffin bone (third phalanx), with internal structures including the digital cushion, laminae, frog, and sole that absorb shock and support the animal's weight.

Anatomy

The hoof wall grows continuously from the coronary band. The sensitive laminae interlock with insensitive laminae of the wall to suspend the coffin bone. The frog, a wedge-shaped elastic structure on the underside, aids in circulation and grip. Parts of the hoof include the toe, quarters, heel, bars, and white line.

Care

Hooves require regular trimming every six to eight weeks by a farrier. Horses working on hard surfaces are commonly fitted with horseshoes; others are kept barefoot. Common hoof problems include laminitis, thrush, abscesses, and navicular syndrome. The saying "no hoof, no horse" reflects the structure's importance to equine soundness.
