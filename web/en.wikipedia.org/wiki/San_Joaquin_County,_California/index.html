San Joaquin County is a county in the US state of California, in the Central Valley at the northern end of the San Joaquin Valley and the eastern edge of the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta. Its county seat and largest city is Stockton; other cities include Lodi, Tracy, Manteca, Lathrop, Ripon and Escalon. With roughly 780,000 residents at the 2020 census, it is among California's faster-growing counties, increasingly tied to the San Francisco Bay Area economy.

Geography

The county takes its name from the San Joaquin River, which flows through its western side together with the Mokelumne and Stanislaus rivers. The Delta's channels make the county a centre for boating and irrigated agriculture; the deep-water Port of Stockton connects it by ship channel to San Francisco Bay.

Economy

Agriculture dominates much of the land: the Lodi area is a major wine-grape region (noted especially for old-vine Zinfandel), alongside almonds, cherries, walnuts, tomatoes and dairy. In recent decades, logistics and warehousing have expanded along the Interstate 5 and Highway 99 corridors, and commuters travel over the Altamont Pass to jobs in the Bay Area; Tracy and Lathrop have become commuter cities in the Bay Area's wider orbit.

History

Created in 1850 as one of California's original 27 counties, San Joaquin grew with Gold Rush traffic to the southern mines, the coming of the Central Pacific Railroad, and Delta reclamation. Stockton became an inland port and manufacturing centre for farm machinery, including the Holt tractor works that fed into Caterpillar.
