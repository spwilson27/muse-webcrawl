Suspended sentence

A suspended sentence is a legal term for a judge's delaying of a defendant's serving of a sentence after they have been found guilty, in order to allow the defendant to perform a period of probation. If the defendant does not break the law during that period and fulfils the particular conditions of the probation, the judge usually dismisses the sentence, sparing the defendant imprisonment.

Operation

Courts may suspend part or all of a custodial term, often subject to conditions such as good behaviour, unpaid work, rehabilitation programmes, curfews, or compensation to victims. If the offender commits another offence during the operational period or breaches the conditions, the court can activate the suspended term, in whole or in part, so that it is served in addition to any sentence for the new offence.

Use in different jurisdictions

Suspended sentences are available in many common-law and civil-law systems, though their form varies: in England and Wales the suspended sentence order attaches community requirements to a suspended custodial term, while in many United States jurisdictions judges may impose "suspended imposition of sentence" or "suspended execution of sentence", with different consequences for the defendant's criminal record.
