Robert Baden-Powell

Robert Stephenson Smyth Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell
Born: 22 February 1857, Paddington, London, England
Died: 8 January 1941, Nyeri, Kenya
Occupation: British Army officer, writer
Known for: Founding the Scout Movement and Girl Guides

Robert Stephenson Smyth Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell (22 February 1857 – 8 January 1941), often known as B-P, was a British Army officer and writer best known as the founder of the Scout Movement and, with his sister Agnes and later his wife Olave, of the Girl Guides.

Military career

Baden-Powell was educated at Charterhouse and joined the army in 1876, serving in India and Africa. He specialised in reconnaissance and scouting, and published a manual, Aids to Scouting, in 1899. During the Second Boer War he became a national hero for his successful defence of the town of Mafeking during its 217-day siege (1899–1900), in which he made use of boy cadets for messages and support duties — an experience that influenced his later youth work. He rose to the rank of lieutenant-general and served as Inspector General of Cavalry.

Scouting

In August 1907 Baden-Powell held an experimental camp on Brownsea Island in Dorset to test his ideas for training boys in citizenship, woodcraft, and character through a system of patrols and outdoor activities. In 1908 he published Scouting for Boys in six fortnightly instalments; it became one of the best-selling books of the 20th century and inspired the rapid formation of Scout troops. He retired from the army in 1910 to devote himself to the movement, travelled the world promoting Scouting, and was proclaimed Chief Scout of the World at the first World Scout Jamboree in 1920. With his wife Olave, whom he married in 1912, he also guided the development of the Girl Guides and younger sections — Wolf Cubs (now Cub Scouts) and Rovers.

Later life

Baden-Powell was created Baron Baden-Powell of Gilwell in 1929. In 1939 he settled in Nyeri, Kenya, where he died in 1941; his grave there bears the trail sign for "gone home". Today the Scout and Guide movements he founded count tens of millions of members worldwide.
