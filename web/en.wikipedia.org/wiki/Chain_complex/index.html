Chain complex

In mathematics, a chain complex is a sequence of abelian groups (or modules) connected by homomorphisms, called boundary operators or differentials, such that the composition of any two consecutive maps is zero. Chain complexes are a central tool of homological algebra, used in defining homology and cohomology theories across algebra, geometry and topology.

Definition

A chain complex (A•, d•) is a sequence of modules A0, A1, A2, … and homomorphisms dn : An → An−1 such that dn−1 ∘ dn = 0 for all n. Elements of ker dn are called cycles and elements of im dn+1 are called boundaries; the defining condition says every boundary is a cycle. The quotient group Hn(A) = ker dn / im dn+1 is the n-th homology group of the complex, measuring the failure of every cycle to be a boundary.

Examples

Singular homology

For a topological space, the groups of formal sums of singular simplices, with d given by the alternating sum of face maps, form a chain complex whose homology is the singular homology of the space.

De Rham complex

On a smooth manifold, differential forms with the exterior derivative form a cochain complex (degrees increasing), whose cohomology computes the de Rham cohomology.

Resolutions

In algebra, free and projective resolutions of a module are chain complexes that are exact except at one end; they underpin the derived functors of homological algebra, such as Tor and Ext.

Chain maps and homotopy

A chain map between complexes is a family of homomorphisms commuting with the differentials; chain maps induce maps on homology. Chain-homotopic maps induce the same map on homology, making homology a functor from complexes up to homotopy to graded modules — the starting point of the modern, categorical treatment of the subject developed in the mid-twentieth century.
