Hou Jing

Hou Jing (Chinese: 侯景; died 552), courtesy name Wanjing (萬景), was a Chinese military general and regent of the Eastern Wei, Liang, and his own short-lived Han state during the Northern and Southern dynasties period. His rebellion against Liang — the Hou Jing Disturbance — devastated the southern capital Jiankang (modern Nanjing) and hastened the decline of the Liang dynasty.

Career

Hou Jing rose as a general under the Eastern Wei regent Gao Huan. After Gao Huan's death in 547, fearing the new regent Gao Cheng, Hou surrendered his territory — the provinces south of the Yellow River — first nominally to Western Wei and then to Liang, whose Emperor Wu accepted him despite warnings. When a Liang relief army was defeated in 548, Hou rebelled, crossed the Yangtze, and besieged the palace city of Jiankang, which fell in 549; Emperor Wu starved to death under Hou's control.

Hou dominated the Liang court through puppet emperors before deposing Emperor Jianwen and taking the throne himself in 552 as emperor of Han. Within months he was defeated by the Liang generals Wang Sengbian and Chen Baxian, fled, and was killed by his own attendants. The rebellion left the south impoverished and depopulated and set the stage for the founding of the Chen dynasty.
