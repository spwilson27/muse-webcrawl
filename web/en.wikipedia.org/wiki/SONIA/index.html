SONIA (the Sterling Overnight Index Average) is the principal interest rate benchmark for overnight unsecured lending in pound sterling. It is calculated from actual transactions in the sterling money markets and is administered by the Bank of England, which took over as administrator in April 2018. SONIA is the recommended successor to sterling LIBOR, publication of which ceased at the end of 2021 for most settings.

Calculation

SONIA is computed as the volume-weighted trimmed mean of interest rates paid on eligible overnight unsecured deposits. Reporting firms submit details of their transactions to the Bank of England each business day; transactions in the lowest and highest 25 percent by rate are trimmed, and the rate is published the following business morning to four decimal places. A compounded SONIA index, launched in August 2020, allows users to compute compounded rates over any period.

Reform and transition

Following the LIBOR manipulation scandal and the Financial Conduct Authority's announcement that it would no longer compel panel banks to submit LIBOR after 2021, the Working Group on Sterling Risk-Free Reference Rates selected a reformed SONIA as the sterling risk-free rate. From April 2018 the benchmark was broadened to capture a wider range of overnight deposit transactions, roughly tripling the volume underpinning it. Markets transitioned loans, bonds, derivatives and mortgages to SONIA-based rates; the first SONIA-linked residential mortgage and SONIA bond issues followed during the transition period.
