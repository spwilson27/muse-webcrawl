Bardet–Biedl syndrome

Bardet–Biedl syndrome (BBS) is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder classified as a ciliopathy, because the genes involved encode proteins of the primary cilium and its basal body. It is named for Georges Bardet and Arthur Biedl, who described the condition in the 1920s. Prevalence is roughly 1 in 140,000–160,000 in northern European populations, and higher in some isolated communities such as the Bedouin of Kuwait and the population of Newfoundland.

Clinical features

The principal features are rod–cone dystrophy causing progressive vision loss beginning with night blindness in childhood, truncal obesity, postaxial polydactyly (extra fingers or toes), learning difficulties, hypogonadism in males, and renal abnormalities, which are the leading cause of early mortality. Diagnosis is clinical, based on combinations of primary and secondary features, and confirmed by genetic testing; more than twenty BBS genes have been identified, many encoding components of the "BBSome", a protein complex that traffics cargo within cilia.

Management

There is no cure; treatment is supportive, including low-vision services, weight and metabolic management, monitoring of kidney function, and educational support. In 2022 the drug setmelanotide, a melanocortin-4 receptor agonist, was approved in the United States for chronic weight management in patients with BBS, the first therapy targeting the syndrome's hyperphagia.
