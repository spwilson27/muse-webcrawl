The London Assembly is a 25-member elected body of the Greater London Authority (GLA), established in 2000 under the Greater London Authority Act 1999. Its principal role is to hold the Mayor of London to account: it scrutinises the Mayor's decisions, budget, and policies, investigates issues of importance to Londoners through committees, and can amend the Mayor's annual budget by a two-thirds majority.

Composition and elections

The Assembly is elected at the same time as the Mayor, every four years, by the additional member system. Fourteen members represent "super-constituencies"—groupings of two or three London boroughs—and are elected by first-past-the-post; eleven further "London-wide" members are allocated proportionally from party lists so that the overall result reflects Londoners' second votes. Any registered London voter may stand. Assembly Members (AMs) elect a Chair and Deputy Chair annually, and much of their work is done in cross-party committees covering transport, policing, health, housing, planning, the environment, and the economy. The Assembly holds ten Mayor's Question Time meetings a year, at which the Mayor must answer members' questions in public.

The Assembly also confirms (or may object to) certain mayoral appointments, such as the chair of Transport for London and members of the London Fire and Emergency Planning Authority. Its formal powers are weaker than those of a full legislature—it cannot initiate most policy—but its investigations and reports carry political weight, and it represents London alongside the Mayor as the elected voice of the capital in the GLA's two-part structure.
