Île d'Oléron

Île d'Oléron is an island in the Atlantic Ocean off the west coast of France, in the Charente-Maritime department. With an area of about 174 square kilometres, it is the second-largest French island after Corsica (excluding overseas territories). It has been connected to the mainland by a 3-kilometre road bridge since 1966. The island is famed for its oysters — the Marennes-Oléron basin is France's premier oyster-farming area — as well as beaches, pine forests, and mild climate.

Geography

The island is about 30 kilometres long and 8 kilometres wide at its broadest. Its western coast faces the open Atlantic, while its sheltered eastern shore borders the Marennes basin, where oyster parks occupy the shallow bays at low tide. The Chassiron lighthouse marks the northern point.

History

Oléron gave its name to the Rôles d'Oléron, the medieval sea laws promulgated under Eleanor of Aquitaine in the 12th century that influenced maritime law across Europe. The island changed hands during the Wars of Religion and was a point of embarkation where many Huguenots left France. Its citadel at Le Château-d'Oléron was fortified under Richelieu and Vauban.

Economy

Oyster farming dominates: oysters fattened in the former salt-marsh claires of Marennes-Oléron acquire a characteristic green tinge and fine flavour. Tourism, fishing, salt production, and viticulture (vin de pays charentais) also contribute.
