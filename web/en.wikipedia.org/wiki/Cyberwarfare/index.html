Cyberwarfare
Domain: Cyberspace (fifth domain of war)
Methods: Espionage, sabotage, DDoS, propaganda
Notable: Stuxnet (2010), NotPetya (2017)

Cyberwarfare is the use of computer technology by states or state-sponsored groups to attack the information systems of other nations, causing damage comparable to physical warfare, or for espionage and sabotage.

Types

Operations include cyber espionage, attacks on critical infrastructure (power grids, finance, transport), distributed denial-of-service attacks, defacement and influence operations. Attribution is difficult, shaping doctrine and deterrence.

Notable incidents

The Stuxnet worm (discovered 2010) physically damaged Iranian centrifuges. Estonia (2007) and Georgia (2008) suffered large DDoS campaigns; Ukraine's power grid was attacked in 2015 and 2016; NotPetya (2017) caused billions in global damage. Many states maintain dedicated commands, such as United States Cyber Command.

Law and debate

The Tallinn Manual examines how international law applies. Experts debate whether "cyber war" as such has occurred, or whether operations remain below the threshold of armed conflict — a grey zone explored also in discussions of ransomware proxies.
