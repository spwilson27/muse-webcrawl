Athaliah

Reign: c. 841–835 BC
Predecessor: Ahaziah of Judah
Successor: Joash of Judah
House: House of Omri (by birth)

Athaliah, the only queen regnant of Judah in the biblical account.

Athaliah (Hebrew: עֲתַלְיָה, ʻĂṯalyā) was the daughter of King Ahab and Queen Jezebel of Israel and, according to the Hebrew Bible, queen regnant of Judah for six years in the 9th century BC — the only woman to rule Judah in her own right. Her story is told in 2 Kings 8–11 and 2 Chronicles 22–23.

Background

Athaliah married Jehoram, king of Judah, in a dynastic alliance between the northern kingdom of Israel and Judah. On Jehoram's death their son Ahaziah succeeded, but he was killed after one year by Jehu during Jehu's purge of the house of Ahab in Israel.

Reign

Learning of her son's death, Athaliah seized the throne of Judah and, according to the biblical narrative, ordered the destruction of the royal family of the house of David to secure her position. One infant, Joash, was hidden by his aunt Jehosheba in the Temple in Jerusalem and survived. Athaliah's reign is associated in the text with the worship of Baal, which her family's influence had promoted in Judah.

Overthrow and death

In the seventh year, the high priest Jehoiada staged a coup: he presented the now seven-year-old Joash to the Temple guards and Levites, proclaimed him king and had him anointed. Hearing the noise of the celebration, Athaliah entered the Temple crying "Treason!" She was seized, taken outside the Temple precincts and put to death at the Horse Gate of the palace, and the temple of Baal in Jerusalem was demolished.

In culture

Athaliah's story inspired Jean Racine's final tragedy Athalie (1691), for which Mendelssohn later composed incidental music, and Handel's oratorio Athalia (1733). She is conventionally portrayed in biblical tradition as a usurper and villain, though some modern historians note the account was written from the perspective of her enemies.
