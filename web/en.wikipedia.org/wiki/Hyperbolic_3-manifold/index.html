Hyperbolic 3-manifold

In mathematics, more precisely in topology and differential geometry, a hyperbolic 3-manifold is a manifold of dimension 3 equipped with a hyperbolic metric, that is a Riemannian metric which has all its sectional curvatures equal to −1. It is generally required that this metric be also complete: in this case the manifold can be realised as a quotient of three-dimensional hyperbolic space by a discrete group of isometries.

Hyperbolic 3-manifolds are quotients of hyperbolic 3-space.

Importance

Hyperbolic geometry turned out to be especially fruitful for the study of 3-manifolds, largely through the work of William Thurston, who showed in the late 1970s that hyperbolic structures exist on a wide class of 3-manifolds. Thurston's geometrization conjecture, proved by Grigori Perelman using Ricci flow, states that every closed 3-manifold can be decomposed into pieces each admitting one of eight geometric structures, of which the hyperbolic structure is the most common and the richest.

Volume

Unlike in dimension 2, where hyperbolic surfaces of finite volume can have arbitrarily small or large volume, the volumes of finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds form a well-ordered subset of the real numbers (a result of Thurston and Jørgensen). The smallest-volume closed hyperbolic 3-manifold is the Weeks manifold, with volume approximately 0.9427. By Mostow rigidity, the hyperbolic structure on a finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold is unique, so geometric invariants such as volume are in fact topological invariants.

Examples

Many knot and link complements in the 3-sphere admit complete hyperbolic structures of finite volume; the first example discovered was the complement of the figure-eight knot. Families of examples also arise from arithmetic groups, Dehn surgery on hyperbolic knots, and regular ideal polyhedra.
