Nezahualpilli (1464–1515) was the ruler of the Mesoamerican city-state of Texcoco, succeeding his father Nezahualcoyotl in 1472 at the age of eight. He reigned for 43 years until shortly before the Spanish conquest of Mexico.

Reign

Chosen from among Nezahualcoyotl's many children, reputedly for his intelligence, Nezahualpilli continued his father's legal reforms — his code was said to be so strict that it applied even to his own sons. He expanded Texcoco's palaces and temples, patronised poetry, and was famed as a seer; tradition holds that he warned the Mexica ruler Moctezuma II of ominous portents preceding the arrival of the Spanish.

Succession

His death in 1515 triggered a disputed succession among his sons — Cacama, Coanacochtzin and Ixtlilxochitl II — that split Texcoco during the conquest and contributed to the fall of the Aztec Empire.
