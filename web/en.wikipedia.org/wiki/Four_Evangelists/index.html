Four Evangelists

In Christian tradition, the Four Evangelists are Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, the authors traditionally credited with the four canonical gospels of the New Testament. The word evangelist derives from the Greek euangelion, "good news."

Symbols

From early Christian art onward, each evangelist has been depicted by a symbol drawn from the visions of Ezekiel and Revelation (the tetramorph): Matthew by a winged man or angel, Mark by a winged lion, Luke by a winged ox or calf, and John by an eagle. These symbols appear in countless medieval illuminated manuscripts, including the Book of Kells, in church carving, stained glass and heraldry.

The gospels

Matthew, Mark and Luke are called the Synoptic Gospels because of their similar content and structure; John's gospel is more theological in character. Modern scholarship generally regards the gospels as anonymous works of the late first century, though the traditional attributions remain foundational to Christian devotion and liturgy, which commemorate the evangelists with individual feast days.
