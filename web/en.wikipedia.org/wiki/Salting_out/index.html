Salting out

Salting out (also known as salt-induced precipitation, salt fractionation, anti-solvent crystallization, or precipitation crystallization) is a purification method that utilizes the reduced solubility of certain molecules in a solution of very high ionic strength. Salting out is typically used to precipitate large biomolecules, such as proteins or DNA. Because salt concentration needed for protein precipitation varies from protein to protein, a particular salt concentration can be used to selectively precipitate a target protein. This process is also used to concentrate dilute solutions of proteins.

Increasing salt concentration competes for water molecules, reducing protein solubility until the protein precipitates.

Principle

Salt compounds dissociate in aqueous solutions. The added ions compete with the protein for the water molecules that form its hydration shell; as salt concentration rises, fewer water molecules remain available to keep the protein dissolved, hydrophobic patches on the protein surface become exposed, and protein–protein interactions increase until aggregates form and precipitate. At low salt concentrations the opposite effect, salting in, slightly increases solubility.

The effectiveness of different salts follows the Hofmeister series. Ammonium sulfate is the salt most commonly used because of its high solubility, low cost, and stabilizing effect on many proteins; its precipitation does not generally denature them, and the salt can be removed afterwards by dialysis.

Fractionation

In ammonium sulfate precipitation, the saturated-solution percentage at which a protein precipitates is characteristic. By raising the salt concentration stepwise and collecting each precipitate by centrifugation, a crude fractionation of a protein mixture is achieved — a classic early step in enzyme purification protocols.

Applications

  • Protein purification and concentration in biochemistry.
  • DNA precipitation in extraction protocols.
  • Separation of soap in its manufacture, the origin of the technique's name.
  • Recovery of proteins from fermentation broths in biotechnology.
