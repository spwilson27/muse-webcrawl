Wangfujing

Wangfujing (Chinese: 王府井; pinyin: Wángfǔjǐng, literally "Prince's Mansion Well") is a famous shopping district and street in Dongcheng District, Beijing, China. Much of the main street is pedestrianised, and the area is one of the capital's oldest and best-known commercial centres.

History

The street's name dates to the Ming dynasty, when the mansions (wangfu) of imperial princes stood in the area and a well (jing) was discovered there. In the late Qing dynasty a market developed, and after 1949 the street became home to flagship state stores, including the Beijing Department Store, opened in 1955. Following renovations in the 1990s the street was pedestrianised and lined with department stores, shopping malls such as Oriental Plaza, hotels and the Wangfujing Snack Street, known for skewers and local snacks.

Landmarks

Near Wangfujing stand St Joseph's Church (the East Church, originally built in the 17th century and rebuilt in 1904), the Beijing People's Art Theatre on nearby streets, and the long-established Gongdelin vegetarian restaurant and Shengxifu hat store among its "time-honoured brands". The street is served by Wangfujing station on Beijing Subway Line 1 and Line 8.
