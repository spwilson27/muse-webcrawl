Rab (island)

Rab (Italian: Arbe; Latin: Arba) is an island in the Adriatic Sea, part of Primorje-Gorski Kotar County in northwestern Croatia. The island is 22 kilometres (14 mi) long and covers an area of 93.6 square kilometres (36 sq mi). Its main town, also called Rab, is known for the four church bell towers that define its skyline.

Geography

Rab lies in the Kvarner Gulf, southeast of the larger island of Cres and north of Pag. The northeastern side of the island is mostly barren and rocky, exposed to the cold bora wind, while the southwestern side is covered in green pine forests — one of the last oak and holm-oak forests in the Mediterranean. The island has a mild Mediterranean climate with more than 2,400 hours of sunshine per year.

History

The island was inhabited by Illyrians from about 350 BC and became a Roman municipality under Augustus, who granted it walls and the title of felix Arba. In the Middle Ages Rab was ruled in turn by Byzantium, Croatian kings, Venice, and Hungary. The founder of San Marino, Saint Marinus, is said to have been a stonemason from Rab who left the island in the fourth century. During the Second World War, Italian forces established the Rab concentration camp near Kampor, in which thousands of Slovene and Croat internees died.

Tourism

Rab has a long tradition of organised tourism dating to the nineteenth century and is also known as one of the birthplaces of naturism: in 1936 King Edward VIII and Wallis Simpson famously swam nude at Kandarola beach with official permission. The medieval summer festival Rabska fjera revives the town's medieval crafts and traditions each July.
