Phase matching

Phase matching is a technique in nonlinear optics that allows interactions between light waves to build up constructively over the length of a nonlinear medium. Because most nonlinear optical processes depend on the phases of the interacting waves, efficient frequency conversion — such as second-harmonic generation, sum-frequency generation, and parametric amplification — requires that the phase relationship between the waves be maintained as they propagate.

FieldNonlinear optics
Key quantityWavevector mismatch Δk
Common methodsBirefringent phase matching; quasi-phase matching

Principle

In a dispersive material, waves of different frequency travel at different phase velocities. The generated wave therefore slips in phase relative to the driving polarization, and after one coherence length the energy flow reverses. Perfect phase matching, Δk = 0, removes this oscillation so the generated field grows monotonically with propagation distance.

Techniques

Birefringent phase matching uses the birefringence of crystals such as BBO or KTP to compensate dispersion by choosing polarization and propagation directions. Quasi-phase matching (QPM) instead periodically reverses the sign of the nonlinear coefficient, as in periodically poled lithium niobate (PPLN), resetting the phase slip every coherence length.

Applications

Phase matching is essential in frequency doublers for laser pointers, optical parametric oscillators, entangled-photon sources for quantum optics, and wavelength converters in telecommunications.
