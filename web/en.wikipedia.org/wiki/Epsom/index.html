Epsom is a town in the borough of Epsom and Ewell in Surrey, England, about 14 miles (22 km) south-west of central London. It lies on the dip slope of the North Downs, just outside the boundary of Greater London, and forms part of the London commuter belt. The town is best known worldwide for Epsom Downs Racecourse, home of the Derby, and as the source of Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate), first identified in its mineral springs.

History

Epsom appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Evesham, held by Chertsey Abbey. Its fortunes changed in the early seventeenth century when mineral springs were discovered on Epsom Common — traditionally credited to a herdsman, Henry Wicker, in 1618. The water, rich in magnesium sulfate, gained a reputation as a purgative and made Epsom one of England's earliest spa towns. Visitors included Samuel Pepys and Nell Gwyn, and for a time Epsom rivalled Bath and Tunbridge Wells in fashion, though its popularity as a spa faded in the eighteenth century as horse racing on the Downs grew.

Racing on Epsom Downs is recorded from the seventeenth century. The Oaks Stakes was first run in 1779 and the Derby Stakes in 1780; the Derby became the most famous flat race in the world and gave its name to countless imitations. In 1913 the Derby was the scene of a defining moment of the suffragette campaign, when Emily Davison stepped in front of the King's horse Anmer, suffering injuries from which she died. The arrival of the railway in the mid-nineteenth century, and later suburban development, transformed Epsom into a residential commuter town while the Downs remained protected open land under the Epsom and Walton Downs Conservators.

Geography and governance

The town centre sits in a valley between the chalk Downs to the south — crowned by the racecourse grandstands — and Epsom Common to the west, a local nature reserve of woodland and acid grassland. Epsom is administered jointly with neighbouring Ewell in the borough of Epsom and Ewell, and its parliamentary constituency is Epsom and Ewell.

Transport and education

Epsom railway station is served by Southern and South Western Railway trains to London Waterloo, London Victoria, and London Bridge. The town is home to the University for the Creative Arts' Epsom campus, Laine Theatre Arts, and several independent schools, and the surrounding area hosts the headquarters of a number of national organizations.
