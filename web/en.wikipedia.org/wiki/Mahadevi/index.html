Mahadevi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mahadevi (Sanskrit: महादेवी, IAST: Mahādevī), literally "the Great Goddess", is the supreme goddess in Shaktism — the goddess-centric tradition of Hinduism. In Shakta theology, Mahadevi is the ultimate reality (Brahman) itself, the source and sum of all other goddesses (devis), who are regarded as her manifestations, aspects or portions.

Theology

The Devi Mahatmyam, the principal scripture of goddess worship, proclaims that all female deities — including Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Parvati and Kali — are forms of the one Mahadevi. In the Devi Bhagavata Purana, she is identified with Bhuvaneshvari and Adi Parashakti, the primordial cosmic energy (shakti) from which the universe and the Trimurti themselves arise. In the Tantric tradition the ten Mahavidyas are her great manifestations of cosmic knowledge.

Forms and worship

Mahadevi is worshipped under countless regional and personal forms, from the fierce Chamunda and Kali to the benevolent Annapurna and Lalita Tripurasundari. Festivals such as Navaratri and Durga Puja celebrate her victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura and her role as the protective mother of the universe. Conceptions of Mahadevi vary by tradition: Shaivas may identify her with Parvati, Vaishnavas with Lakshmi, while Shaktas regard all of these as subordinate expressions of the Great Goddess herself.
