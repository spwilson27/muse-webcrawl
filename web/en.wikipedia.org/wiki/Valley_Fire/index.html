Valley Fire

LocationLake County, California, United States
Date12 – 22 September 2015
Area burned76,067 acres (30,783 ha)

The Valley Fire was a wildfire that burned in Lake and Napa counties in northern California in September 2015. Ignited near Cobb on the afternoon of 12 September, it spread with extreme speed under hot, dry, windy conditions, destroying large parts of the communities of Middletown, Hidden Valley Lake, and Cobb.

Fire behaviour

Driven by strong winds and low humidity, the fire grew to tens of thousands of acres within hours of ignition, producing a massive evacuation of more than 20,000 people. At the time it ranked among the most destructive wildfires in California history, destroying 1,955 structures, including 1,281 homes. Four people died, and several firefighters were seriously injured when they were overrun by flames on the first day.

Cause and aftermath

An investigation by Cal Fire determined the fire was caused by a faulty hot tub electrical installation at a residence near Cobb. The fire burned 76,067 acres before it was fully contained. Recovery efforts in Middletown and surrounding communities continued for years, and the disaster prompted renewed attention to evacuation planning and defensible space in California's wildland–urban interface.
