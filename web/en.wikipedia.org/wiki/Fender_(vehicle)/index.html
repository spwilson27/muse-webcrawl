Fender (vehicle)

A fender is the part of an automobile, motorcycle or other vehicle body that frames a wheel well. Its purpose is to prevent sand, mud, rocks, liquids and other road spray from being thrown into the air by the rotating tyre. In British English the equivalent term is wing.

Design

On early automobiles, fenders were separate curved panels mounted over the wheels, often flowing into running boards. As car bodies became fully enclosed during the 1940s and 1950s, fenders were increasingly integrated into the bodywork as "ponton" styling. Modern vehicles typically have the front fenders as bolted-on panels forming part of the front clip, while rear fenders are usually integral with the quarter panels.

Other uses

Bicycles and motorcycles use similar guards, called mudguards in British English, to protect the rider from spray. The term fender is also applied to frames fitted around boat docks and to the bumpers of some vehicles.
