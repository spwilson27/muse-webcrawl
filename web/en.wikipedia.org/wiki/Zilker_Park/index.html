Zilker Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zilker Metropolitan Park is a recreational area in south Austin, Texas, at the juncture of Barton Creek and the Colorado River (Lady Bird Lake). It comprises over 350 acres (142 ha) of publicly owned land and is the city's flagship park, sometimes called Austin's "crown jewel." The park is named for Andrew Jackson Zilker, who donated the land to the city in stages between 1917 and 1934.

Attractions

The park contains many attractions, including Barton Springs Pool, a three-acre spring-fed swimming pool that maintains an average temperature of about 68–70 °F (20–21 °C) year-round. Other features include the Zilker Botanical Garden, the Austin Nature and Science Center, the Umlauf Sculpture Garden, and the Zilker Zephyr miniature railway. The park's Great Lawn hosts major events such as the Austin City Limits Music Festival and the Zilker Park Kite Festival, one of the longest-running kite festivals in the United States.

History

The land around Barton Springs was prized for centuries, first by Indigenous peoples and later by settlers. Zilker's donations ensured the tract remained public parkland. A large tract was also set aside as a preserve along Barton Creek. Today Zilker Park is listed in the National Register of Historic Places and remains the centerpiece of Austin's park system.
