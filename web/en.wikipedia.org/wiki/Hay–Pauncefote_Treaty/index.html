Hay–Pauncefote Treaty

Signed: 18 November 1901, Washington, D.C. Parties: United States and United Kingdom. The treaty abrogated the Clayton–Bulwer Treaty of 1850 and cleared the way for an American-built Panama Canal.

The Hay–Pauncefote Treaty is a treaty signed by the United States and the United Kingdom on 18 November 1901. Named after its negotiators, U.S. Secretary of State John Hay and British ambassador Lord Pauncefote, it nullified the Clayton–Bulwer Treaty of 1850, under which neither power could build a Central American canal without the other, and gave the United States the right to construct and control a canal through the Isthmus of Panama alone.

Terms

The treaty granted the United States the sole right to build, regulate and manage a canal across the isthmus. In return, the canal was to be permanently neutral and open to vessels of all nations on equal terms, following the model of the Suez Canal convention. Fortification of the canal was not explicitly prohibited, an omission that later allowed the United States to fortify the Panama Canal Zone.

Negotiation history

An earlier version, signed in February 1900, was amended by the U.S. Senate in ways Britain could not accept, and negotiations were restarted. The successful 1901 text reflected Britain's desire to concentrate its navy in European waters amid rising German naval power and the strain of the Boer War, and to cultivate American friendship. Two years later the United States signed the Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty with the newly independent Panama and began construction of the Panama Canal, opened in 1914.
