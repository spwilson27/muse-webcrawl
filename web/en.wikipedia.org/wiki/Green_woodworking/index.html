Green woodworking

[Image: Green woodworking]

Green woodworking (also spelled greenwood working) is a form of woodcraft or practical carpentry that works unseasoned or "green" timber into finished items. Because freshly felled wood is soft and easy to shape, green woodworking traditionally relies on hand tools such as axes, knives, drawknives, and pole lathes.

Techniques

Green wood splits readily along the grain, allowing stock to be cleft rather than sawn. Practitioners exploit differential shrinkage: components such as chair legs are dried before assembly so that joints tighten as the wetter surrounding wood shrinks around them. Common products include stools, chairs (notably Windsor and ladder-back forms), spoons, bowls, and hurdles.

Revival

The craft was championed in Britain by Mike Abbott and in the United States by Roy Underhill and Peter Follansbee, and is now widely taught as a heritage craft.
