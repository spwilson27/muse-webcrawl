Scanlation

Scanlation (a portmanteau of scan and translation) is the practice of scanning, translating and editing comics — most commonly Japanese manga, but also Korean manhwa and Chinese manhua — from one language into another by fan groups, and distributing the result, usually online and without authorisation from the copyright holders.

Process

A scanlation group typically divides labour among a raw provider who scans or obtains the source chapter, a translator, a proofreader, a cleaner/redrawer who removes the original text and repairs artwork, and a typesetter who inserts the translated script. Releases were historically distributed through IRC and dedicated websites; aggregator sites later made scanlations accessible to mass audiences.

History and legal status

Fan translation of manga dates to the 1970s, but scanlation expanded dramatically with broadband internet in the 2000s, often providing the only access to series not licensed abroad. The practice exists in a legal grey area: it is copyright infringement, though the community traditionally observed norms such as dropping series once officially licensed in the target language. Publishers initially tolerated scanlation as demand-creating, but by the 2010s many pursued takedowns and launched simultaneous official digital releases. Debate continues over scanlation's role in popularising manga globally versus its effect on creators' incomes.
