The Jackson State Tigers football team represents Jackson State University in Jackson, Mississippi, in college football. The Tigers compete in the NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision (FCS) as a member of the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC). Home games are played at Mississippi Veterans Memorial Stadium.

History

Jackson State's program, dating to 1911, became a powerhouse among historically black colleges under coaches such as John Merritt and W. C. Gorden, who led the Tigers from 1976 to 1991 and won multiple SWAC titles. The program produced an exceptional number of professional players for its level, including Pro Football Hall of Famers Walter Payton, Lem Barney, Jackie Slater and Robert Brazile, as well as wide receiver Jimmy Smith.

The Tigers have won numerous SWAC championships and several Black college national championships. From 2020 to 2022 the team was coached by Pro Football Hall of Famer Deion Sanders, whose tenure brought unprecedented national attention, top FCS recruiting classes, and consecutive SWAC championships in 2021 and 2022, with quarterback Shedeur Sanders starring before both departed for Colorado. T. C. Taylor, a former Jackson State player, succeeded Sanders as head coach.

Culture

The program is famous for the Sonic Boom of the South, Jackson State's marching band, one of the most celebrated in HBCU football, and for large home crowds that regularly rank among the best in the FCS. Rivalries include the Soul Bowl against Alcorn State and games against Grambling State and Southern.
