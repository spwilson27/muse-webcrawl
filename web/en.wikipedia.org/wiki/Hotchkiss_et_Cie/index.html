Hotchkiss et Cie

Hotchkiss et Cie
Founded1867, Saint-Denis, France
FounderBenjamin B. Hotchkiss
Defunct1954 (arms); cars to 1955
ProductsMachine guns, artillery, automobiles

Hotchkiss et Cie was a French armaments and automobile manufacturer founded in 1867 by the American inventor Benjamin Berkeley Hotchkiss. Based at Saint-Denis near Paris, the company first made artillery and the revolving cannon bearing Hotchkiss's name, and became internationally known for its machine guns, above all the gas-operated Hotchkiss Mle 1914, the standard heavy machine gun of the French Army in the First World War, and the lighter Hotchkiss Mle 1909 adopted by Britain and the United States.

Automobiles

From 1903 Hotchkiss built luxury cars, noted for quality engineering and the slogan-quality of its Parisian chassis fitted with bespoke coachwork. Models such as the AM80 and the Grégoire, designed with aeronautical engineer J. A. Grégoire, carried the marque into the 1950s. The company also built military vehicles, including the Hotchkiss H35 light tank used in 1940.

Decline

Post-war taxation of large cars crippled the French luxury market. Hotchkiss merged with Delahaye in 1954, ending car production under its own name, though armoured vehicles and the licence-built Hotchkiss M201 jeep continued. The arms business passed into successor French state arsenals.
