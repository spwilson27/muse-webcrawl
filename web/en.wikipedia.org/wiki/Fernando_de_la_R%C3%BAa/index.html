Fernando de la Rúa (15 September 1937 – 9 July 2019) was an Argentine politician of the Radical Civic Union who served as President of Argentina from December 1999 until his resignation in December 2001 amid the country's severe economic and social crisis.

Early career

A lawyer from Córdoba, de la Rúa was elected senator in 1973 and ran as vice-presidential candidate in that year's second election. After the return of democracy in 1983 he again served as senator, and in 1996 he became the first elected Chief of Government of Buenos Aires, winning the 1999 presidential election at the head of the Alliance coalition against the Peronist Eduardo Duhalde.

Presidency and fall

His government inherited a recession, rising debt and the rigid peso–dollar convertibility regime, which it chose to defend. Austerity measures, the resignation of Vice President Carlos Álvarez in 2000, and the deposit freeze known as the corralito in December 2001 triggered mass protests and riots. After declaring a state of siege and facing deadly repression in Buenos Aires, de la Rúa resigned on 21 December 2001 and famously left the Casa Rosada by helicopter. He was later tried over a Senate bribery scandal and acquitted in 2013.
