T-bone steak

T-bone steak
TypeCut of beef
SourceShort loin of cattle
MusclesStrip steak (longissimus) and tenderloin (psoas major), separated by a T-shaped lumbar vertebra
Related cutsPorterhouse steak, bistecca alla fiorentina

The T-bone steak is a cut of beef taken from the short loin of the animal. It consists of two steaks on either side of a T-shaped lumbar vertebra: the larger strip steak (from the longissimus muscle) on one side and the smaller, tender beef tenderloin (from the psoas major) on the other. The cut combines the flavour of the strip with the tenderness of the filet.

T-bone vs. porterhouse

The porterhouse steak is cut from the rear of the short loin and contains a larger section of tenderloin; under U.S. Department of Agriculture rules a steak may be labelled porterhouse only if the tenderloin portion is at least 1.25 inches (32 mm) wide at its widest point, while a T-bone requires only 0.5 inch (13 mm). The celebrated Tuscan bistecca alla fiorentina is essentially a large T-bone/porterhouse cut from Chianina or Maremmana cattle, traditionally grilled rare over wood embers.

Cooking

Because the two muscles cook at different rates and the bone insulates the meat near it, T-bones are a challenge to cook evenly; grilling and broiling are the most common methods, often with the tenderloin side positioned slightly farther from the hottest part of the fire. Note that in some jurisdictions T-bones were temporarily restricted during the BSE ("mad cow") era because the cut includes part of the vertebral column.
