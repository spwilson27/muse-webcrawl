Phanar

Phanar or Fanar (Greek: Φανάρι, Phanári; Turkish: Fener) is a historic quarter of Istanbul, Turkey, on the southern shore of the Golden Horn, within the Fatih district. It takes its name from the Greek word for a lantern or lighthouse, a reference to a column topped with a lantern that stood there in Byzantine times.

History

After the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople in 1453, Phanar became the home of much of the city's remaining Greek population. From 1600 the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, the spiritual centre of Eastern Orthodox Christianity, was installed at the Church of St George in Phanar, where it remains today; by extension, "the Phanar" is often used as a metonym for the Patriarchate itself.

In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries the wealthy Greek families of the quarter, known as the Phanariots, rose to great influence in the Ottoman Empire. Educated and multilingual, they served as dragomans (interpreters) to the Sultan, and from the early eighteenth century were appointed hospodars — rulers — of the Danubian principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia. Their stone mansions (konaks) along the Golden Horn, some still standing, reflect that prosperity.

Landmarks

The quarter contains the Church of St George, seat of the Ecumenical Patriarch; the Phanar Greek Orthodox College, a striking red-brick building founded in 1454 and known as the "Red Castle"; the Bulgarian St Stephen Church, the famous prefabricated iron church on the shore of the Golden Horn; and the Church of St Mary of the Mongols, the only Byzantine church in Istanbul never converted to a mosque.

The Greek population of Phanar declined drastically during the twentieth century, especially after the Istanbul pogrom of 1955 and subsequent emigration, but the Patriarchate and a small community remain.
