FIFA (Fédération Internationale de Football Association — International Federation of Association Football) is the international self-regulatory governing body of association football, beach soccer and futsal. Headquartered in Zurich, Switzerland, it was founded in Paris on 21 May 1904 by the national associations of Belgium, Denmark, France, the Netherlands, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland. FIFA now comprises 211 national associations — more than the United Nations — organised into six regional confederations: AFC (Asia), CAF (Africa), CONCACAF (North and Central America), CONMEBOL (South America), OFC (Oceania) and UEFA (Europe).

Role

FIFA is responsible for the organisation and promotion of football's major international tournaments, notably the FIFA World Cup, which it has run since 1930, and the FIFA Women's World Cup (since 1991). It works with IFAB, which maintains the Laws of the Game. Its motto is "For the Game. For the World."

History and controversies

FIFA expanded rapidly after 1974 under president João Havelange and his successor Sepp Blatter, turning the World Cup into a multi-billion-dollar event. The organisation was severely damaged by the 2015 corruption scandal, in which US and Swiss authorities indicted senior officials over bribery in the awarding of hosting and media rights; Blatter resigned and was succeeded in 2016 by Gianni Infantino, who oversaw the expansion of the World Cup to 48 teams from 2026.
