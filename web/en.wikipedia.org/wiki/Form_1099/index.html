Form 1099

Form 1099 series
Issued byInternal Revenue Service (United States)
PurposeInformation return reporting non-wage income
Common variants1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, 1099-INT, 1099-DIV, 1099-K, 1099-R

Form 1099 is one of several Internal Revenue Service (IRS) tax forms used in the United States to prepare and file an information return that reports various types of income other than wages, salaries and tips — which are instead reported on Form W-2. Businesses and financial institutions file copies with the IRS and furnish copies to the payment recipient, allowing the IRS to match reported income against individual tax returns.

Variants

The 1099 series contains many variants, each identified by a suffix. Form 1099-MISC reports miscellaneous income such as rents and prizes; Form 1099-NEC, reintroduced for tax year 2020, reports nonemployee compensation paid to independent contractors; 1099-INT reports interest income; 1099-DIV dividends and distributions; 1099-B broker proceeds from securities sales; 1099-R distributions from pensions and retirement accounts; 1099-K payment-card and third-party network transactions; and 1099-SA and other specialised forms cover health savings accounts and additional categories.

Filing

Payers generally must furnish recipient copies by the end of January following the tax year and file with the IRS shortly thereafter, electronically or on paper using transmittal Form 1096. In everyday American usage, workers paid "on a 1099" are independent contractors rather than employees, responsible for their own self-employment taxes — a classification with significant legal and tax consequences that is periodically litigated.
