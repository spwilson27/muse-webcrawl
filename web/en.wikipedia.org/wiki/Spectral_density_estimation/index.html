Spectral density estimation

In statistical signal processing, the goal of spectral density estimation (SDE) or simply spectral estimation is to estimate the spectral density (also known as the power spectral density) of a signal from a sequence of time samples of the signal. Intuitively speaking, the spectral density characterizes the frequency content of the signal. One purpose of estimating the spectral density is to detect any periodicities in the data, by observing peaks at the frequencies corresponding to these periodicities.

Techniques

Estimation methods are broadly divided into nonparametric and parametric approaches.

  • Periodogram — the squared magnitude of the discrete Fourier transform of the data; simple but statistically inconsistent, with variance that does not vanish as data increase.
  • Welch's method — averages periodograms of overlapping windowed segments, reducing variance at the cost of frequency resolution.
  • Multitaper (Thomson, 1982) — applies several orthogonal data tapers (Slepian sequences) and averages the resulting eigenspectra, giving good leakage resistance and variance control.
  • Parametric methods — fit a model such as an autoregressive (AR), moving-average, or ARMA process and derive the spectrum from the model coefficients; examples include the Yule–Walker and Burg methods, which can give sharp spectral peaks from short records.
  • Subspace methods — such as MUSIC and ESPRIT, estimate frequencies of sinusoids in noise from the eigendecomposition of a covariance matrix.

Applications

Spectral estimation is fundamental in communications, radar, sonar, seismology, oceanography, speech and audio analysis, biomedical signal processing, and econometrics, wherever the frequency content of a stochastic process must be inferred from a finite, noisy record.
