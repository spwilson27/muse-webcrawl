Tuskegee Airmen

The Tuskegee Airmen were a group of African American military pilots and airmen who fought in World War II — primarily the 332nd Fighter Group and 477th Bombardment Group of the United States Army Air Forces — trained at Tuskegee Army Air Field, Alabama.

Service

Known as the Red Tails for their distinctive P-51 Mustang markings, the 332nd escorted bombers over Europe with an distinguished record. Some 992 pilots graduated at Tuskegee; 355 deployed overseas, and many were killed or captured.

Legacy

Their performance helped pave the way for President Truman's 1948 desegregation of the US armed forces. In 2007 the Airmen were awarded the Congressional Gold Medal collectively. See 332nd Fighter Group.
