Saint Kevin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Kevin]

Saint Kevin (Irish: Cóemgen, modern Irish Caoimhín; c. 498 – 3 June 618) was an Irish saint, founder and first abbot of Glendalough in County Wicklow. He is one of the patron saints of the Archdiocese of Dublin, and his feast day is 3 June.

Life

Kevin was born near present-day Rathdrum to a family of Leinster nobility. He was educated by monks, including Saint Petroc, and from an early age showed a love of solitude and nature. After his ordination he lived as a hermit for seven years in a hollow tree and later in a small cave above the Upper Lake at Glendalough — the Bronze Age tomb now known as "St Kevin's Bed" — subsisting on wild apples, sorrel, and, in legend, food brought to him by an otter.

Drawn by followers, Kevin founded the monastery of Glendalough ("glen of the two lakes") around the middle of the 6th century. It became one of the most important monastic cities of Ireland, famous for learning and craftsmanship, and survived as a pilgrimage site even after Viking raids and its eventual decline.

Legends

Many stories illustrate Kevin's closeness to nature. During Lent, while praying with arms outstretched, a blackbird is said to have laid an egg in his palm, and Kevin remained motionless until the chick hatched. He is also credited with a famous, darker tale of casting a woman who pursued him into the lake. Gerald of Wales recorded wonders of Glendalough in the 12th century, and Kevin's reputation as a hermit-saint endured through the "Seven Churches" pilgrimage of Glendalough.

Legacy

Glendalough's round tower, cathedral, and Kevin's Church (with its distinctive stone roof) remain major historic monuments. Kevin is a popular given name in Ireland; the modern form Caoimhín and the anglicised "Kevin" derive from his name, which means "handsome birth" or "gentle birth".
