Wenceslaus I of Bohemia

Wenceslaus I (Czech: Václav I.; c. 1205 – 23 September 1253), called the One-Eyed, was King of Bohemia from 1230 to 1253. The son of Ottokar I and Constance of Hungary, he consolidated Přemyslid royal power, promoted German colonisation and town foundations, and successfully defended Bohemia against the Mongol invasion of Europe in 1241.

Wenceslaus I, King of Bohemia 1230–1253 — not to be confused with Saint Wenceslaus, the earlier duke.

Reign

Crowned in 1228 during his father's lifetime, Wenceslaus succeeded in 1230. He fought his brother Přemysl of Moravia, endured a war with Frederick II of Austria, and lost an eye while hunting — hence his epithet. He encouraged the founding of towns such as Brno's expansion, invited German settlers and miners (the silver of Jihlava enriched the crown), and supported the mendicant orders.

Mongol invasion and later years

In 1241 Mongol raiders who had crushed Poland at Legnica probed Moravia; Wenceslaus's cautious defence kept Bohemia largely intact, a feat celebrated by contemporaries. After the last Babenberg duke of Austria died in 1246, Wenceslaus married his son Ottokar II into the inheritance, laying the ground for Bohemian expansion — though Ottokar first rebelled against his father in 1248. Wenceslaus died in 1253 and was buried in the Convent of St. Agnes in Prague, founded by his saintly sister Agnes of Bohemia.
