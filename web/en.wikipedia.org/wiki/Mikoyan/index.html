Mikoyan

The Russian Aircraft Corporation "MiG", formerly the Mikoyan-and-Gurevich Design Bureau (OKB-155) and generally known as Mikoyan, is a Russian aircraft manufacturer and design bureau founded in 1939 by Artem Mikoyan and Mikhail Gurevich. Its fighter aircraft, known by their "MiG" designation, equipped the air forces of the Soviet Union and dozens of allied states throughout the Cold War and beyond.

History

The bureau's first major design, the MiG-3, served in World War II. The jet age brought its greatest successes: the MiG-15, which shocked Western pilots over Korea; the supersonic MiG-21, the most-produced supersonic fighter in history, built in more than 11,000 examples; the swing-wing MiG-23; the Mach 3 MiG-25 interceptor; and the MiG-29 fourth-generation fighter. Its last major type is the upgraded MiG-35.

Organisation

After the dissolution of the USSR the bureau was reorganised as the Mikoyan Scientific Production Complex and later merged into the United Aircraft Corporation as the Russian Aircraft Corporation "MiG" in 2006. Mikoyan designs are manufactured principally at plants in Moscow, Lukhovitsy and Nizhny Novgorod (Sokol).
