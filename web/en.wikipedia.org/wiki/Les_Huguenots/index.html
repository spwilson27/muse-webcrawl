Les Huguenots
ComposerGiacomo Meyerbeer
LibrettistsEugène Scribe and Émile Deschamps
Premiere29 February 1836, Paris Opéra
GenreGrand opéra in five acts

Les Huguenots is a French opera by Giacomo Meyerbeer, one of the most celebrated exemplars of the grand opéra style. The libretto was written by Eugène Scribe and Émile Deschamps, after Prosper Mérimée's novel Chronique du règne de Charles IX. It premiered at the Paris Opéra on 29 February 1836 and was an immediate and enormous success, launching Meyerbeer to the height of his fame and running to more than 1,000 performances at the Opéra alone by 1906.

Plot

The opera dramatizes the conflict between Catholics and Protestant Huguenots in sixteenth-century France, culminating in the St. Bartholomew's Day massacre of 1572. At its center is the love between the Huguenot nobleman Raoul de Nangis and Valentine, daughter of the Catholic Count of Saint-Bris, who is betrothed to the Count of Nevers. The first act introduces Raoul at Nevers's château, where he falls for an unknown lady (Valentine); the second act, at Chenonceau, presents the famous "bathing scene" of Queen Marguerite de Valois and the duet in which Raoul and Valentine's engagement is arranged and then broken. The third act features the great ensemble of the blessing of the daggers' prelude—actually in Act IV, the "Bénédiction des poignards"—in which Catholic nobles bless the weapons for the massacre. In the final act, Valentine converts to Protestantism to marry Raoul, and the lovers die together, shot by Saint-Bris's men, the Count discovering too late that he has ordered the death of his own daughter.

Music and influence

Highlights include Raoul's romance "Plus blanche que la blanche hermine", the Queen's rondo "O beau pays de la Touraine", the Rataplan chorus, and the great Act IV love duet. Meyerbeer's orchestration—including the early use of the bass clarinet and of the Lutheran chorale "Ein feste Burg" as a Huguenot motif—was enormously influential on Verdi and Wagner. Once among the most performed operas in the world, it fell from the repertory in the twentieth century but has returned in major revivals.
