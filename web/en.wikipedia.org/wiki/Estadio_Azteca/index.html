Estadio Azteca

Estadio Azteca

Location: Coyoacán, Mexico City, Mexico

Capacity: 87,523

Opened: May 29, 1966

Tenants: Club América, Mexico national football team

Architects: Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, Rafael Mijares Alcérreca

Estadio Azteca is a football stadium in the Coyoacán borough of Mexico City, Mexico. Opened in 1966, it is the home of Club América and the Mexico national football team, and with a capacity of 87,523 it is the largest stadium in Mexico and one of the largest in the world. It sits at an altitude of about 2,200 metres (7,200 ft) above sea level.

History

The stadium was designed by architects Pedro Ramírez Vázquez and Rafael Mijares Alcérreca and built between 1962 and 1966 on a field of volcanic rock in Santa Úrsula Coapa. It opened on May 29, 1966, with a friendly match between Club América and the Italian club Torino.

Estadio Azteca is the only stadium to have hosted two FIFA World Cup finals, in 1970 and 1986. The 1970 final saw Brazil, led by Pelé, defeat Italy 4–1 in what is often called the greatest World Cup final. In the 1986 quarter-final between Argentina and England, Diego Maradona scored both the infamous "Hand of God" goal and the celebrated "Goal of the Century" within four minutes. The stadium is scheduled to host matches, including the opening match, of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, becoming the first venue to stage games at three World Cups.

Other events

Beyond football, the stadium has hosted the 1968 Olympic football tournament, NFL games — including the first regular-season NFL game played outside the United States in 2005 — boxing (Julio César Chávez vs. Greg Haugen, 1993, before 132,274 spectators, a world record for boxing attendance), and major concerts by artists such as Michael Jackson, U2, and Paul McCartney. Pope John Paul II celebrated mass there in 1999.
