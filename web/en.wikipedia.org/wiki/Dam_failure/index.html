Dam failure

A dam failure or dam burst is a catastrophic type of structural failure characterised by the sudden, rapid, and uncontrolled release of impounded water. Dam failures are comparatively rare but can cause immense damage and loss of life when they occur, as the energy of the stored water is released as a flood wave downstream.

Causes

Common causes include overtopping during extreme floods, internal erosion ("piping") through the dam body or foundation, foundation defects, landslides into the reservoir, and earthquake damage. Deliberate destruction in wartime — such as the 1943 Dambusters raid on the Möhne and Eder dams — and neglect of maintenance are also factors.

Notable failures

The 1975 Banqiao Dam failure in China, triggered by Typhoon Nina, destroyed a cascade of dams and caused an estimated death toll ranging from tens of thousands to well over 100,000, making it among the deadliest structural failures in history. The 1889 South Fork Dam failure caused the Johnstown Flood in Pennsylvania, killing more than 2,200 people. The near-failure of California's Oroville Dam spillway in 2017 prompted the evacuation of about 180,000 people but no breach. The 1976 Teton Dam collapse in Idaho and the 1963 Vajont disaster in Italy (where a landslide displaced the reservoir over the dam) also rank among the best-documented cases studied by engineers.

Prevention

Modern dam safety programmes involve periodic inspection, instrumentation, spillway capacity upgrades, emergency action plans, and probabilistic risk assessment.
