Shimla

Shimla (also known as Simla, the official name until 1972) is the capital and the largest city of the northern Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. In 1864, Shimla was declared as the summer capital of British India. After independence, the city became the capital of East Punjab and was later made the capital city of Himachal Pradesh. It is the principal commercial, cultural and educational centre of the state. Built across a series of ridges and spurs in the foothills of the Himalayas at a mean altitude of about 2,206 metres (7,238 ft), the city had a population of 169,578 in the 2011 census.

History

The area was under the control of the Bhimsen Thapa regime of Nepal until the Anglo-Nepalese War; it came under British influence after the Treaty of Sugauli (1816), and British officers began building summer residences there in the 1820s for the salubrious climate. In 1864, under Viceroy John Lawrence, Shimla became the official summer capital of the Raj, and for roughly half the year the entire government of India migrated to it. The Kalka–Shimla narrow-gauge railway, opened in 1903 and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, dramatically improved access. Shimla hosted major events of the freedom struggle era, including the Simla Conference of 1945 and the Simla talks of 1945–46 on the Cabinet Mission Plan; in 1972 the Shimla Agreement between India and Pakistan was signed in the city (at Barnes Court) following the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971.

Geography and landmarks

The city sprawls over seven hills — Inverarm, Observatory, Prospect, Summer, Bantony, Elysium, and Jakhoo Hills — and is known for its steep pedestrianised centre: the Mall Road and the adjoining Ridge, vehicles being largely banned in the core. Landmarks include the Gothic Christ Church (consecrated 1857), the neo-Tudor Gaiety Theatre, the former Viceregal Lodge (Rashtrapati Niwas, 1888), now the Indian Institute of Advanced Study, and the Jakhoo Temple dedicated to Hanuman. Shimla's colonial-era architecture gives it a distinctive heritage character, and its snowfall makes it a major domestic tourist destination, as well as a base for the surrounding apple-growing region of Himachal Pradesh.
