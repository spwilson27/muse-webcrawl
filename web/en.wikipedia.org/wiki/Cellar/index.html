Cellar

A wine cellar uses the stable cool temperature underground.

A cellar is a room or space below ground level in a building, used for storage. Unlike a basement, which may be partly above ground and used as living space, a cellar is traditionally wholly or mostly underground and valued for its cool, stable temperature and darkness.

Uses

Historically, cellars stored perishable food before refrigeration: root vegetables, cured meat, cheese, butter, and preserves. A root cellar is a structure, often separate from the house, built into the ground or a hillside for this purpose. Cellars are also used for storing wine and beer, where constant cool temperatures favour ageing, and for coal and other fuel in older houses.

Construction

Traditional cellars have stone or brick walls, vaulted ceilings in larger examples, and small vents for limited air circulation. Modern cellars may be built of poured concrete and fitted with drainage, sump pumps, and insulation, though excess dampness remains a common problem in poorly sealed spaces.
