Mullah Muhammad Omar (c. 1960 – 23 April 2013) was an Afghan religious leader and militant who founded the Taliban movement and ruled Afghanistan as its supreme leader from 1996 to 2001 under the title Amir al-Mu'minin, "Commander of the Faithful". A village mullah and veteran of the mujahideen war against the Soviet Union — in which he lost his right eye — Omar emerged from obscurity in Kandahar in 1994, rallying religious students (taliban) against the warlord anarchy of the Afghan civil war. His forces took Kabul in September 1996 and soon controlled most of the country, imposing a severe interpretation of Islamic law.

Rule and fall

The Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan under Omar was recognised by only three states (Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates). Its rule was notorious for the public enforcement of strict edicts — banning girls' education and most employment of women, destroying the Buddhas of Bamiyan in March 2001, and harbouring Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda. After the September 11 attacks, Omar refused to surrender bin Laden; the US-led invasion of October 2001 drove the Taliban from power within weeks, and Omar fled Kandahar in December 2001.

Insurgency and death

From hiding, Omar remained the symbolic and strategic head of the Taliban insurgency against the Afghan government and NATO forces, with a $10 million US bounty on his head, though he was almost never seen and few photographs of him exist. In July 2015 Afghan officials announced that he had died in April 2013 — reportedly of tuberculosis — a fact the Taliban confirmed while acknowledging it had been concealed; his longtime deputy Akhtar Mansour succeeded him. Omar's burial place remains secret, and he continues to be venerated by the Taliban as their founding leader.
