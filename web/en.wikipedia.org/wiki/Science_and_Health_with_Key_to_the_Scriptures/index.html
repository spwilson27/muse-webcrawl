Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures is the central text of the Christian Science religion, written by Mary Baker Eddy and first published in 1875. Together with the Bible, it serves as the denomination's permanent pastor: passages from both books are read aloud in Christian Science Sunday services worldwide. Eddy described the book as the record of her discovery of what she called the Science of Christianity, and she revised it continually for the rest of her life; more than 400 editions appeared during her lifetime.

Contents

The book sets out the theology of Christian Science: God is infinite Mind, Spirit, and Love; matter, sickness, and sin are errors of mortal belief rather than realities created by God; and healing comes through understanding one's true spiritual identity. Its chapters include "Prayer", "Atonement and Eucharist", "Marriage", "Christian Science versus Spiritualism", "Animal Magnetism Unmasked", "Science, Theology, Medicine", "Physiology", "Footsteps of Truth", "Creation", "Science of Being", "Some Objections Answered", "Christian Science Practice", and "Teaching Christian Science", concluding with a "Key to the Scriptures" — comprising chapters on Genesis and the Apocalypse and a Glossary redefining biblical terms in spiritual terms — and a chapter of testimonies of healing called "Fruitage".

Publication history

Eddy published the first edition in Boston in 1875, financing it with difficulty after rejections from several publishers. Early editions were criticised for literary errors, and Eddy employed editors, most notably James Henry Wiggin in the 1880s, to polish the text while she retained control of its doctrine. The book's copyright became the subject of litigation after Eddy's death in 1910; in 1971 the United States Congress passed a private law extending its copyright, but the law was later struck down as unconstitutional on Establishment Clause grounds, and the work entered the public domain.

Influence

Science and Health has sold millions of copies and has been translated into numerous languages, including Braille. In 1992 it was selected by the National Women's Book Association as one of 75 books by women whose words have changed the world. The book remains the doctrinal foundation of the Church of Christ, Scientist, which Eddy founded in 1879, and is studied weekly by adherents in conjunction with the Bible in the denomination's Bible Lessons.
