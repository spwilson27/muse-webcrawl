Nicene Creed
Adopted325 CE (First Council of Nicaea); expanded 381 CE (Constantinople)
LanguageGreek
PurposeStatement of Christian faith against Arianism

The Nicene Creed is a statement of Christian faith first adopted at the First Council of Nicaea in 325 CE and expanded at the First Council of Constantinople in 381. It is the most widely accepted creed in Christianity, used in the liturgies of the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican and most Protestant churches.

History

The emperor Constantine convened the Council of Nicaea to settle the controversy raised by Arianism, which taught that the Son of God was a created being. The creed affirmed that the Son is "begotten, not made, of one substance (homoousios) with the Father". The expanded version of 381 added a fuller article on the Holy Spirit. The Western church later inserted the word Filioque ("and the Son") into the clause on the Spirit's procession — a change rejected by the East and a contributing cause of the Great Schism of 1054.

Text (English translation)

"We believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all worlds; God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God; begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father, by whom all things were made..." The creed continues with articles on Christ's incarnation, crucifixion and resurrection, the Holy Spirit, the church, baptism, the resurrection of the dead and the life of the world to come.
