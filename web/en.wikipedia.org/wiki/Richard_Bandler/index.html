Richard Bandler

Richard Wayne Bandler (born February 24, 1950) is an American author and trainer in the field of self-help. With linguist John Grinder, he co-created neuro-linguistic programming (NLP), a set of models and techniques for communication and personal change, in the 1970s.

Career

While a student at the University of California, Santa Cruz, Bandler studied the work of therapists Fritz Perls, Virginia Satir, and Milton Erickson, collaborating with Grinder to model their language patterns. Their books The Structure of Magic (1975) and Patterns of the Hypnotic Techniques of Milton H. Erickson laid out the resulting framework. Bandler later developed further approaches he called Design Human Engineering and Neuro-Hypnotic Repatterning.

Criticism and legal matters

NLP's claims have been widely examined by psychologists, and scientific reviews have generally found little empirical support for its central propositions, regarding it as a pseudoscience. Bandler was tried in 1986 for the murder of Corine Christensen and acquitted. He was also involved in extended litigation with Grinder and others over the ownership of NLP intellectual property.
