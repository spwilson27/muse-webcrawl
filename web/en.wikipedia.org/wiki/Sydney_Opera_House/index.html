Sydney Opera House

LocationBennelong Point, Sydney, Australia
ArchitectJørn Utzon; completed by Peter Hall
Opened20 October 1973
Construction1959–1973
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2007)

The Sydney Opera House is a multi-venue performing arts centre on Sydney Harbour, and one of the most famous and distinctive buildings of the 20th century. Its roof is formed of a series of precast concrete "shells" — often described as sails — rising above a granite-clad podium at Bennelong Point.

Design and construction

Danish architect Jørn Utzon won the international design competition in 1957. Construction began in 1959 and was plagued by difficulty: the shell geometry was only solved when Utzon reconceived every shell as a ribbed segment of a single sphere, allowing standardised precast components. Cost overruns and political conflict led to Utzon's resignation in 1966; an Australian team led by Peter Hall completed the interiors. Queen Elizabeth II opened the building on 20 October 1973, at a final cost of A$102 million against an original estimate of A$7 million, funded largely by a state lottery.

Significance

The Opera House hosts over 1,500 performances a year, including Opera Australia, the Sydney Symphony Orchestra, the Australian Ballet and the Sydney Theatre Company. Utzon received the Pritzker Prize in 2003, and in 2007 the building became the youngest UNESCO World Heritage Site and the only one listed in its architect's lifetime.
