The Teatro Real (Royal Theatre) is an opera house in Madrid, Spain. Situated on the Plaza de Isabel II, opposite the Royal Palace, it is one of Spain's principal venues for opera and classical music and is home to the Madrid Symphony Orchestra.

History

King Ferdinand VII ordered the construction of a royal opera house, and the building, designed by Antonio López Aguado and Custodio Moreno, opened on 19 November 1850 with Donizetti's La favorita, on the birthday of Queen Isabella II. The theatre became a leading European stage during the 19th century.

Closure and rebuilding

In 1925 the building was closed because of structural damage caused by Metro tunnelling. After decades of partial use as a concert hall from 1966, it was comprehensively rebuilt as an opera house under architects led by José Manuel González Valcárcel and reopened on 11 October 1997 with Manuel de Falla's La vida breve and El sombrero de tres picos.

The building

The theatre has a capacity of 1,746 seats. Its main stage machinery occupies one of the largest stage complexes in Europe. The building also houses rehearsal rooms, the multipurpose Gayarre Hall and a museum.
