General position

FieldGeometry, algebraic geometry
Related conceptsTransversality, generic property

In algebraic geometry and related fields, general position is a notion of genericity: a configuration of geometric objects is in general position if it satisfies no special or degenerate conditions beyond those required — more precisely, if it lies within an open dense subset of the space of all such configurations.

Examples

A set of points in the plane is in general position if no three of them are collinear; in three-dimensional space, points are in general position if no four are coplanar. Lines in the plane are in general position if no two are parallel and no three meet at a single point. The notion depends on context: the conditions to be avoided are dictated by the problem at hand, and "general position" always means general with respect to a specified class of degeneracies.

Use

Assuming objects are in general position often simplifies proofs and computations, because arguments need only handle typical configurations; the degenerate cases can then be treated separately or shown to form a set of measure zero. In computer science, particularly computational geometry, algorithms frequently assume inputs in general position, and techniques such as symbolic perturbation are used to handle degenerate inputs consistently.
