Robert Alan Dahl (17 December 1915 – 5 February 2014) was an American political theorist and Sterling Professor of Political Science at Yale University. A leading theorist of democracy and power, he is best known for his empirical study of power in New Haven, Who Governs? (1961), and for developing the concept of polyarchy.

Career

Dahl was born in Inwood, Iowa, and raised largely in Juneau, Alaska, where his father was a doctor. He studied at the University of Michigan and took his PhD at Yale in 1940, served in the US Army in Europe during the Second World War, and joined Yale's faculty in 1946, spending almost his entire career there. He was president of the American Political Science Association in 1966–67.

Power and pluralism

In "The Concept of Power" (1957) Dahl gave political science a behavioural definition — A has power over B to the extent that A can get B to do something B would not otherwise do. In Who Governs?, based on fieldwork in New Haven, Connecticut, he argued against elite theory (associated with figures such as Gaetano Mosca): different actors prevailed in different issue areas (education, urban renewal, party nominations), officials were constrained by elections and diverse constituencies, and inequalities of resources were "dispersed" rather than cumulative. This became the classic statement of pluralism.

Democracy and polyarchy

Dahl reserved "democracy" for an ideal of full political equality, and called real-world regimes that combine public contestation with inclusive participation polyarchies, elaborated in Polyarchy: Participation and Opposition (1971) and Democracy and Its Critics (1989). His criteria for the democratic process — effective participation, voting equality, enlightened understanding, control of the agenda, and inclusion — remain standard reference points. Later works, including On Political Equality (2006), grew more critical of inequality and of American constitutional structures.
