Heiti

Heiti (Chinese: 黑體; pinyin: hēitǐ; literally "black type") is a style of Chinese typeface characterised by strokes of even thickness and the absence of decorative serifs, corresponding broadly to the sans-serif styles of Latin typography. Heiti faces are widely used for headlines, signage, user interfaces and on-screen text in Chinese-speaking regions.

History

The style derives from the bold, even-stroke lettering developed in China in the early 20th century, influenced by Japanese Gothic typefaces (goshikku-tai) and by Western grotesque type. With the advent of phototypesetting and then digital fonts, heiti became the default style for screens. Apple popularised the name through its STHeiti and "Heiti SC"/"Heiti TC" fonts bundled with Mac OS X and iOS for Simplified and Traditional Chinese, and Microsoft provides comparable faces such as Microsoft YaHei for Windows.

Characteristics

Heiti characters have strokes of roughly uniform width, squared or slightly rounded terminals, and minimal contrast between thick and thin strokes. This distinguishes them from songti (Ming) styles, which emulate the carved appearance of woodblock printing with pronounced thick–thin contrast and triangular serifs, and from kaiti, which imitates brush calligraphy.

Terminology

The Japanese equivalent style is called gothic (ゴシック体), and in Korean the analogous sans-serif style is godik. In English-language font menus, Chinese heiti faces are often labelled "Gothic".
