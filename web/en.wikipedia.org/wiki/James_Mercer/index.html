James Mercer

James Mercer (26 February 1736 – 31 January 1793) was a Virginia lawyer, judge and political figure. He served as a delegate for Virginia to the Continental Congress in 1779.

Early life

Mercer was born in Marlborough, Stafford County, Virginia, the son of John Mercer, a prominent landowner and legal writer. His brother John Francis Mercer later became Governor of Maryland. James was educated at the College of William and Mary and studied law.

Career

During the American Revolutionary War, Mercer served in the Virginia militia and the Virginia House of Delegates. He was a member of the Virginia convention that ratified the United States Constitution in 1788, where he spoke in favour of amendments. He was elected to the Continental Congress in 1779 but attended only briefly.

After the war, Mercer practised law in Fredericksburg and in 1789 was appointed a judge of the Virginia Court of Appeals, the state's highest court, serving until his death in Richmond in 1793.
