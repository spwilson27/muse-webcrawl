St Georges Terrace

St Georges Terrace is the main street of the central business district of Perth, Western Australia. Running roughly parallel to the Swan River, it forms the principal commercial axis of the city and is home to many of the tallest office buildings in Western Australia, housing mining, banking, legal and government tenants.

History

Laid out in the original 1829 plan of Perth, the Terrace was named after Saint George and developed as the city's most prestigious address, lined successively by government offices, banks, clubs and churches. St George's Cathedral (completed 1888) stands on the Terrace at its junction with Cathedral Avenue, near Government House and the Supreme Court Gardens. Nineteenth-century buildings such as the Perth Town Hall (1870, just off the Terrace) and the Deanery survive among post-war and modernist towers.

Character

Major towers on or near the Terrace include Central Park (the city's tallest building), Brookfield Place, and QV1. Plaques set into the pavement commemorate notable Western Australians in a "Walk of Fame"-style sequence of notable citizens since 1829. The street is a focus for civic events and for the city's finance and resources industries, and is served by the Elizabeth Quay and Perth Underground stations at its western and central ends.
