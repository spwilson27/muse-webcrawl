The Further and Higher Education Act 1992 (c. 13) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reshaped post-16 education in England and Wales. Its best-known effect was to abolish the "binary divide" in British higher education: the polytechnics and certain other institutions were granted university status and the power to award their own degrees, greatly expanding the number of universities. A separate Act of the same name was passed for Scotland.

Provisions

The Act:

  • Removed polytechnics and other designated institutions from local authority control and made them independent higher education corporations.
  • Permitted polytechnics meeting the criteria to adopt university title and award their own degrees, ending the role of the Council for National Academic Awards (CNAA), which was dissolved.
  • Established the Higher Education Funding Council for England (HEFCE) and the Higher Education Funding Council for Wales (HEFCW), replacing the Universities Funding Council and the Polytechnics and Colleges Funding Council.
  • Removed further education colleges from local education authority control, incorporating them as independent bodies, and created the Further Education Funding Councils for England and Wales.

Impact

Thirty-four polytechnics in England, Scotland and Wales became universities in 1992, with others following; these institutions are commonly known as "post-1992" or "new" universities, in contrast to the "pre-1992" universities. The reform accompanied a rapid expansion of participation in higher education. The unified funding regime also intensified competition between institutions, and degree-awarding powers were progressively extended to other colleges in subsequent decades.
