LTE Advanced

LTE Advanced (LTE-A) is a mobile communication standard and a major enhancement of the Long Term Evolution (LTE) standard. Formally submitted as a candidate 4G system to the ITU-T in late 2009 and standardised by the 3GPP in Release 10 (frozen in 2011), LTE Advanced meets—and in key respects exceeds—the requirements of the IMT-Advanced specification, supporting peak download rates of 3 Gbit/s and upload rates of 1.5 Gbit/s in its full configuration.

Key technologies

  • Carrier aggregation (CA): two or more component carriers, each up to 20 MHz, are combined to reach bandwidths of up to 100 MHz.
  • Enhanced MIMO: up to 8×8 spatial multiplexing in the downlink and 4×4 in the uplink.
  • Relay nodes and heterogeneous networks: low-power nodes such as pico- and femtocells improve coverage and capacity, supported by enhanced inter-cell interference coordination (eICIC).
  • Coordinated multipoint (CoMP): coordinated transmission from multiple sites in later releases.

Deployment

The first LTE Advanced networks launched commercially in 2013, notably in South Korea, using carrier aggregation over two 10 MHz carriers. Operators marketed early LTE-A services at peak rates of 150 Mbit/s, with later multi-carrier and higher-order modulation upgrades marketed under names such as "LTE-A Pro" (3GPP Releases 13–14), which added features like Licensed Assisted Access and support for massive MIMO, serving as a bridge to 5G New Radio.

Backward compatibility

LTE Advanced is backward compatible with earlier LTE releases: an LTE-A terminal can operate on a Release 8 network, and a Release 8 terminal can use an LTE-A network, albeit without the advanced features.
