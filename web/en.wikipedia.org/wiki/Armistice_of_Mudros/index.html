Armistice of Mudros

The Armistice of Mudros ended hostilities between the Ottoman Empire and the Allies of World War I. It was signed on 30 October 1918 aboard HMS Agamemnon in Mudros harbour on the Greek island of Lemnos by the Ottoman minister Rauf Bey and the British admiral Somerset Arthur Gough-Calthorpe.

Terms

The armistice required the Ottomans to demobilise their army, surrender warships, evacuate occupied territories in the Caucasus and Arabia, open the Dardanelles and Bosphorus to Allied ships, and allow Allied occupation of strategic points — including any territory in case of disorder in the Armenian provinces.

Consequences

Allied forces occupied Constantinople in November 1918. The armistice was followed by the Treaty of Sèvres (1920), the Turkish War of Independence, and ultimately the Treaty of Lausanne (1923).
