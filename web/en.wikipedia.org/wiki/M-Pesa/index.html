M-PESA

M-PESA mobile money service
TypeMobile money transfer and microfinancing service
LaunchedMarch 2007
OperatorsSafaricom (Kenya), Vodacom and others
OriginKenya

M-PESA is a mobile phone-based money transfer, payments and micro-financing service, launched in 2007 by Vodafone and Safaricom, the largest mobile network operator in Kenya. The name combines "M" for mobile and "pesa", the Swahili word for money. It has since expanded to Tanzania, Mozambique, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Lesotho, Ghana, Ethiopia and other markets.

History

M-PESA was initially developed with funding from the British Department for International Development (DFID) as a way for microfinance borrowers to receive and repay loans using the network of Safaricom airtime resellers. It quickly pivoted into a general-purpose money transfer service, allowing users to deposit cash with local agents, transfer money to other users via SMS-based menus, pay bills and withdraw cash.

How it works

Customers register with an authorized agent, deposit cash, and receive electronic money in an account associated with their mobile phone number. Transfers are made using PIN-secured text messages or a mobile app, and recipients can withdraw cash from agents. The service works on basic feature phones using USSD and SIM toolkit menus, which made it accessible in areas with little banking infrastructure.

Impact

M-PESA is widely credited with dramatically expanding financial inclusion in Kenya, where a large majority of adults came to use mobile money. A 2016 study published in Science estimated that access to M-PESA lifted roughly 194,000 Kenyan households out of extreme poverty. The service became a model for mobile money systems worldwide and a cornerstone of Kenya's fintech sector, spawning merchant payment services such as Lipa na M-PESA and lending products such as M-Shwari and Fuliza.

International expansion

Following its Kenyan success, M-PESA was launched in Tanzania in 2008 and subsequently in numerous African and other markets, with varying degrees of success. Some international ventures, such as those in South Africa, Romania and India, were later discontinued, while the African operations continued to grow, processing billions of transactions annually.
