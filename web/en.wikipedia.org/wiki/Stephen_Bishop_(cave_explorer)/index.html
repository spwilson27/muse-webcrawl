Stephen Bishop (cave explorer)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen Bishop (c. 1821 – 1857) was an American cave explorer and self-taught geologist, enslaved in Kentucky, who became famous as a guide at Mammoth Cave. Bishop is credited with discovering and mapping many miles of the cave's passages during the 1840s and 1850s, and he is regarded as one of the great cave explorers of the nineteenth century.

Life at Mammoth Cave

Bishop was brought to Mammoth Cave in 1838 by his enslaver, attorney Franklin Gorin, who had purchased the cave the previous year. At about seventeen years old, Bishop learned the existing tour routes from the white guide Archibald Miller and quickly began exploring beyond them. In 1839 the cave was sold to Dr. John Croghan of Louisville, and Bishop remained its principal guide.

Explorations

Bishop crossed the cave's "Bottomless Pit" — a deep chasm on the standard tour route — using a cedar sapling as a ladder, becoming the first person to explore the extensive passages beyond, including the area around the underground Echo River. There he discovered eyeless fish and other cave-adapted creatures that drew scientific attention. In 1842 he guided a party that included the singer Jenny Lind's contemporaries and other notables, further raising the cave's profile as a tourist attraction.

From memory, Bishop drew a detailed map of the cave system in 1842, showing some 35 miles of passages. Published in 1845 as an inset to Alexander Clark Bullitt's Rambles in Mammoth Cave, the "Bishop Map" remained the most accurate representation of Mammoth Cave for decades and is one of the earliest published maps credited to an African American.

Freedom and death

Under the terms of Dr. Croghan's will, Bishop was to be freed seven years after Croghan's death; he gained his freedom in 1856 but died the following summer, in 1857, before he could carry out his plan to move to Liberia. He is buried in the Old Guide's Cemetery at Mammoth Cave, where his original headstone — replaced in the 20th century after a national fundraising effort among schoolchildren — marks his grave.
