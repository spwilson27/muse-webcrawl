Younger Memnon

The Younger Memnon is an ancient Egyptian statue, one of two colossal granite heads from the Ramesseum, the mortuary temple of Ramesses II in Thebes. Since 1818 it has been held by the British Museum in London.

Younger Memnon
Subject: Ramesses II
Date: c. 1270 BC, 19th Dynasty
Material: Granite
Location: British Museum, London

Description

The bust, 2.67 m high and weighing about 7.25 tonnes, shows the young Ramesses II wearing the nemes headdress with a uraeus. It is carved from a single block of two-tone granite.

Acquisition

Explorer Giovanni Battista Belzoni, commissioned by Henry Salt, removed the head in 1816 using levers and rollers and shipped it down the Nile to London, where its arrival in 1818 caused a sensation. Its fame helped inspire Percy Bysshe Shelley's sonnet "Ozymandias," published the same year.

Name

"Memnon" was a Greek association of Theban colossi with the mythological hero Memnon; "Younger" distinguishes it from an older/larger statue.
