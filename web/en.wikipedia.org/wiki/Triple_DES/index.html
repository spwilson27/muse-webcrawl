Triple DES

Triple DES (3DES or TDES), officially the Triple Data Encryption Algorithm (TDEA), is a symmetric-key block cipher that applies the DES cipher algorithm three times to each data block. It was developed to extend the useful life of DES, whose 56-bit key had become vulnerable to brute-force attack.

Operation

In its most common form, Triple DES uses three independent 56-bit keys in an encrypt–decrypt–encrypt (EDE) sequence: the plaintext is encrypted with the first key, decrypted with the second, and encrypted again with the third. The middle decryption step provides backward compatibility: setting all three keys equal reduces the construction to single DES. Two-key variants use the first key again for the third operation. With three independent keys the nominal key length is 168 bits, though meet-in-the-middle attacks reduce the effective security to about 112 bits.

History and status

Triple DES was widely deployed in banking and payment systems (for example in EMV cards and ATM networks) and in protocols such as TLS and IPsec. Because of its small 64-bit block size — which enables the Sweet32 attack on long-lived connections — and its slowness in software, it has been deprecated in favour of AES. NIST deprecated Triple DES in 2019 and disallowed its use for encryption after 2023, except for processing already-protected data.
