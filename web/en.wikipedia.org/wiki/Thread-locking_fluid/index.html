Thread-locking fluid

[Hero image placeholder: a small bottle of blue threadlocker being applied to a bolt.]

Thread-locking fluid (also known as threadlocker) is a single-component anaerobic adhesive, applied to the threads of fasteners such as screws and bolts, to prevent loosening, leakage and corrosion. The best-known brand is Loctite, produced by Henkel.

How it works

The fluid is a methacrylate resin that remains liquid in the presence of air but cures into a tough thermoset plastic in the absence of oxygen and in contact with metal ions — conditions found in the engaged threads of an assembled fastener. Once cured, it bonds the mating threads together and seals them against fluids and vibration.

Grades

Products are colour-coded by strength: low-strength (purple) grades allow easy disassembly of small screws; medium-strength (blue) grades permit removal with hand tools and are the most common; high-strength (red) grades are near-permanent and typically require heat to remove; green grades include wicking formulations applied to already-assembled fasteners. Separate retaining compounds secure bearings and cylindrical parts.
