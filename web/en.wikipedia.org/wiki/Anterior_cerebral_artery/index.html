Anterior cerebral artery
SourceInternal carotid artery
SuppliesMedial frontal and parietal lobes

The anterior cerebral artery (ACA) is one of the two terminal branches of the internal carotid artery. It supplies the medial surfaces of the frontal and parietal lobes, the corpus callosum, and parts of the basal ganglia and internal capsule.

Course

The ACA arises at the medial end of the lateral sulcus, passes forward and medially, and joins its fellow across the anterior communicating artery, forming the front of the circle of Willis. It then curves upward and backward over the corpus callosum, giving cortical and central branches.

Clinical significance

Occlusion of the ACA causes weakness and sensory loss predominantly in the opposite leg, along with possible frontal-lobe signs such as abulia and personality change. Aneurysms of the anterior communicating artery complex are a common cause of subarachnoid haemorrhage.
