Kırkpınar

Kırkpınar (Turkish for "forty springs") is a Turkish oil wrestling (yağlı güreş) tournament held annually in Edirne, Turkey, usually in late June or early July. It is widely considered the oldest continuously running sporting competition in the world, with a traditional founding date of 1362, and was inscribed in 2010 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

According to legend, the tournament commemorates an event of 1361–1362 during the Ottoman advance into Thrace under Orhan Gazi's son Süleyman Pasha. Forty Ottoman akıncı (raiders) were said to have wrestled each other for sport near the village of Samona; two brothers, Ali and Selim, wrestled to a standstill for hours and were later found dead, locked in each other's arms. A fig tree and forty springs (kırk pınar) were said to have appeared at the site, giving the venue its name. The first documented tournaments date to the following centuries, and the event has been held in Edirne since the mid-19th century at the Sarayiçi ("within the palace") grounds on an island in the Tunca River.

The tournament

Wrestlers, called pehlivan, compete barefoot on a grass field (er meydanı) wearing only kıspet — heavy leather trousers made from water buffalo or cow hide — and with their bodies covered in olive oil, which makes grips extremely difficult. Bouts are announced by a master of ceremonies (cazgır) who recites rhymed introductions, and are accompanied by davul drums and zurna shawms. Wrestlers are divided into categories by skill, from minik (smallest juniors) up to the baş (head) category. The winner of the head category is the başpehlivan (chief wrestler) of the year and receives the golden belt (altın kemer); a wrestler who wins the title three years in succession keeps the belt permanently.

Cultural significance

Kırkpınar is the most prestigious of Turkey's many oil-wrestling festivals. The event is opened under the patronage of the Kırkpınar Ağası, a sponsor-auction winner who funds the festival and carries ceremonial duties, including hosting the participating wrestlers. Modern festivals run for about a week, with the wrestling itself spread over the final days.
