Michael, also called Saint Michael the Archangel, is an archangel in Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and the Baháʼí Faith. His name, from the Hebrew Mīkhāʼēl, is traditionally read as a rhetorical question — "Who is like God?" — answered implicitly by no one. In Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran traditions he is called Saint Michael; in Islam he is Mīkāʾīl (Mikail).

In scripture

In the Hebrew Bible's Book of Daniel, Michael appears as "one of the chief princes" and the protector of Israel, who will arise at the time of the end. In the New Testament, the Book of Revelation (12:7–9) describes war in heaven in which Michael and his angels fight against the dragon (Satan) and cast him down to earth. The Epistle of Jude refers to Michael contending with the devil over the body of Moses.

In the Qur'an, Mikail is one of the four archangels, associated with providing nourishment and rain. In some traditions he is, with Gabriel (Jibril), the angel who instructed Muhammad.

Roles and veneration

Christian tradition assigns Michael four principal offices: leader of the army of God against the forces of evil; the angel of death who carries souls to heaven; the weigher of souls on his balanced scales; and the supreme guardian of the Church. He is the patron saint of soldiers, police officers, and paramedics, and of numerous places including Kyiv and Brussels. His feast, Michaelmas on 29 September, is shared with the archangels Gabriel and Raphael.

Famous sanctuaries dedicated to Michael include Mont Saint-Michel in Normandy, St Michael's Mount in Cornwall, and the Sanctuary of Monte Sant'Angelo in Italy — sites often positioned along the legendary "Sword of Saint Michael" alignment of monasteries.
