Workplace bullying

Workplace bullying
DefinitionRepeated, health-harming mistreatment of one or more employees
Key researchersHeinz Leymann, Gary Namie, Charlotte Rayner
Related termsMobbing, workplace harassment, abusive supervision
EffectsAnxiety, depression, PTSD symptoms, turnover, lost productivity

Workplace bullying is a persistent pattern of mistreatment from others in the workplace that causes either physical or emotional harm. It typically involves repeated behaviours — such as verbal abuse, humiliation, intimidation, work sabotage, gossip, exclusion and unreasonable or shifting demands — often involving a power imbalance between perpetrator and target. The Swedish psychologist Heinz Leymann pioneered research in the 1980s, using the term "mobbing" for hostile collective campaigns against an employee.

Social exclusion is a common form of workplace bullying

Prevalence and effects

Surveys in many countries suggest that between roughly 10% and 30% of workers experience bullying behaviours, depending on definitions and measurement methods. Targets report elevated rates of anxiety, depression, burnout, sleep disturbance and post-traumatic stress symptoms, and bullying is associated with higher sickness absence and staff turnover, imposing significant costs on organisations.

Responses and law

Organisational responses include anti-bullying policies, grievance procedures, training and mediation, though researchers note that complaints are frequently mishandled. Most jurisdictions have no standalone workplace-bullying statute; victims instead rely on occupational health and safety law, discrimination and harassment law, or constructive-dismissal claims. Sweden adopted an early ordinance against victimisation at work in 1993, and France criminalised moral harassment (harcèlement moral) in 2002. In the United States, the proposed Healthy Workplace Bill has been introduced in many state legislatures but widely enacted nowhere.
