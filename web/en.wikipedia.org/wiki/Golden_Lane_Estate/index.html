The Golden Lane Estate is a notable post-war council housing estate in the City of London, built between 1957 and 1962 on a bomb-damaged site near the Barbican. Designed by the architectural practice Chamberlin, Powell and Bon — who later designed the neighbouring Barbican Estate — it is regarded as a landmark of British modernist housing and an influential model of high-density urban living.

Design competition and construction

The estate was commissioned by the Corporation of London after a celebrated architectural competition in 1951–52 to rebuild a heavily bombed area of the City. The competition was won by Geoffry Powell, one of the many young architects who entered; the winning team formed the practice Chamberlin, Powell and Bon. The estate provided around 560 homes — maisonettes and one-room flats for single people and small households, unusual in prioritising small units — arranged around landscaped courtyards with a swimming pool, tennis courts, community facilities and shops.

Architecture

The centrepiece is Great Arthur House, a 16-storey tower block that on completion was briefly the tallest residential building in Britain; its distinctive yellow chequerboard cladding and rooftop pergola and pool remain emblematic. Lower blocks such as Crescent House and Cullum Welch House use bold primary colours, pilotis and varied maisonette plans influenced by Le Corbusier's Unité d'Habitation. The estate's buildings were listed at Grade II in 1997, with Great Arthur House upgraded to Grade II*.
