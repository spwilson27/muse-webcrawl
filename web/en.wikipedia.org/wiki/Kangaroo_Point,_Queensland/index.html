Kangaroo Point is an inner southern suburb of Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, located on a peninsula formed by a sharp bend of the Brisbane River directly east of the central business district. It is known for the Kangaroo Point Cliffs, a popular recreation area, and for the Story Bridge, which connects the suburb to Fortitude Valley.

History

The point was named for the kangaroos once seen there by early European settlers. Before European settlement the area was used by the Turrbal people. The cliffs were created by convict-era quarrying of the volcanic Brisbane tuff for building stone, used in early Brisbane structures. The suburb developed as a shipbuilding and industrial area, hosting naval stores and the Evans Deakin shipyards, before becoming increasingly residential in the late twentieth century.

Landmarks

The Story Bridge, opened in 1940, is climbed by tourists on guided bridge climbs starting from Kangaroo Point. The heritage-listed St Mary's Anglican Church (1873) stands above the cliffs, and the old Yungaba immigration centre (1885) by the river served as a migrant hostel for decades. The Kangaroo Point Cliffs Park offers rock climbing, abseiling, picnic areas and views across to the city skyline.
