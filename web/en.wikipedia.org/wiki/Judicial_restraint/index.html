Judicial restraint

Judicial restraint is a theory of judicial interpretation that encourages judges to limit the exercise of their own power and to strike down laws only when they clearly violate the constitution. It asserts that judges should hesitate to use judicial review unless a statute is unmistakably unconstitutional, and should defer to the legislature as the democratically accountable branch.

Principles

Restraint is associated with respect for precedent (stare decisis), narrow rulings that avoid deciding more than necessary, and a presumption of constitutionality for legislation. It is often contrasted with judicial activism. Advocates argue restraint protects democratic self-government; critics argue it can leave constitutional rights under-enforced. Figures associated with the philosophy include Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. and Felix Frankfurter.
