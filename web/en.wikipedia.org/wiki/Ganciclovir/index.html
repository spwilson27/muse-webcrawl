Ganciclovir

Ganciclovir, sold under the brand name Cytovene among others, is an antiviral medication used to treat cytomegalovirus (CMV) infections. It is used to treat CMV retinitis in people with weakened immune systems, and to prevent CMV disease in organ transplant recipients. It is often used long term. It is given by injection into a vein or taken by mouth in the related prodrug form valganciclovir.

Common side effects include low white blood cells, low red blood cells, kidney problems, fever, and vomiting. It is not recommended in pregnancy due to possible birth defects. Ganciclovir is an analogue of the nucleoside guanosine. It works by decreasing the ability of the virus to reproduce.

Ganciclovir was patented in 1980 and approved for medical use in 1988. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Ganciclovir is a synthetic analogue of 2′-deoxy-guanosine. It is first phosphorylated to ganciclovir monophosphate by a viral kinase encoded by the cytomegalovirus gene UL97 during infection. Subsequently, cellular kinases catalyze the formation of ganciclovir diphosphate and ganciclovir triphosphate, which is present in 10-fold greater concentrations in CMV or herpes simplex virus (HSV)-infected cells than uninfected cells. Ganciclovir triphosphate is a competitive inhibitor of deoxyguanosine triphosphate (dGTP) incorporation into DNA and preferentially inhibits viral DNA polymerases more than cellular DNA polymerases. In addition, ganciclovir triphosphate serves as a poor substrate for chain elongation, thereby disrupting viral DNA synthesis.

Medical uses

  • Treatment of CMV retinitis in immunocompromised patients, including those with AIDS.
  • Prevention of CMV disease in solid organ transplant and bone marrow transplant recipients.
  • Treatment of CMV colitis, esophagitis, and pneumonitis in immunocompromised patients.
  • Intravitreal implant for local treatment of CMV retinitis.

Adverse effects

Ganciclovir is commonly associated with myelosuppression, including neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, and with adverse effects on the kidneys and central nervous system. Long-term treatment was needed for the prevention of the progression of CMV retinitis in the era before effective antiretroviral therapy.
