Séance

A séance is an attempt to communicate with spirits, typically conducted by a group of people seated around a table with a medium who purports to channel messages from the dead. The word derives from the French séance, meaning a sitting or session.

History

Séances became a popular pastime in the mid-19th century with the rise of Spiritualism, following the fame of the Fox sisters in the United States. Famous attendees included Arthur Conan Doyle, while the magician Harry Houdini campaigned to expose fraudulent mediums who used tricks such as table-turning, hidden accomplices and cold reading.

Techniques

Typical séance features include a darkened room, hand-holding around a table, automatic writing, spirit trumpets, and the production of "ectoplasm". The Ouija board is a related popular device.

Scepticism

Scientists and sceptics attribute séance phenomena to ideomotor effects, suggestion, and deliberate fraud. Controlled investigations have repeatedly found no evidence of genuine spirit communication.
