Energy Performance Certificate

Typical EPC rating chart, from A (most efficient) to G

An Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) is a document that rates the energy efficiency of a building and estimates its energy costs and carbon dioxide emissions. EPCs are required across the European Union under the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive when a building is constructed, sold or rented out, and similar schemes exist in the United Kingdom following Brexit.

Contents

An EPC typically gives the building an asset rating on a scale from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient), displayed as a colour-coded bar chart resembling the labels on household appliances. It includes an estimate of energy use and running costs, a summary of the building's energy performance features (walls, roof, heating, windows, lighting), and recommendations for cost-effective improvements together with the improved rating each measure could achieve. Certificates are produced by accredited energy assessors using standardised calculation methods such as SAP (for dwellings, in the UK) and are generally valid for ten years.

United Kingdom

EPCs were introduced in England and Wales in 2007 as part of Home Information Packs and survived the abolition of those packs in 2010. Since April 2018, the Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards have made it unlawful to let most domestic and non-domestic properties rated below band E, subject to exemptions. Certificates are lodged on a central register and can be looked up by postcode or reference number.

Elsewhere

All EU member states operate EPC schemes under the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive, though rating scales, methods and enforcement vary by country. Related schemes elsewhere include Energy Star and HERS ratings in the United States and NABERS in Australia.
