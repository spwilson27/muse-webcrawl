Hazen–Williams equation

The Hazen–Williams equation is an empirical relationship used in fluid mechanics and civil engineering to calculate the pressure loss (head loss) due to friction in a water pipe, given the pipe's length, diameter, flow rate, and a roughness coefficient.

Formula

In SI units the head loss is hf = 10.67 · L · Q1.852 / (C1.852 · d4.87), where L is pipe length, Q the volumetric flow, d the inside diameter, and C the Hazen–Williams roughness coefficient (higher C meaning a smoother pipe; roughly 130–150 for new plastic or lined pipe, lower for corroded iron).

History and use

The equation was developed from the work of Allen Hazen and Gardner Stewart Williams in the early 20th century. It applies only to water at ordinary temperatures and is widely used in the design of water distribution networks, fire sprinkler systems, and irrigation, though the theoretically based Darcy–Weisbach equation is more general.
