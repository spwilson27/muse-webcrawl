Growth hormone (GH) or somatotropin, also known as human growth hormone (hGH) in its human form, is a peptide hormone that stimulates growth, cell reproduction, and cell regeneration in humans and other animals. It is thus important in human development. GH also stimulates production of IGF-1 and increases the concentration of glucose and free fatty acids.

Growth hormone is a 191-amino acid, single-chain polypeptide synthesized, stored and secreted by somatotropic cells of the anterior pituitary gland. Its release is stimulated by growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) and inhibited by somatostatin, both from the hypothalamus, and it is secreted in pulses, chiefly during deep sleep.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of GH in children causes growth failure and short stature; in adults it is associated with reduced muscle mass and energy. Excess GH before the growth plates close produces gigantism, and in adults acromegaly — enlargement of the hands, feet and jaw — usually from a pituitary tumour.

Recombinant human growth hormone (somatropin) has been produced by genetic engineering since 1985, replacing cadaver-derived hormone, which was withdrawn after cases of Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease. It is approved for GH deficiency and several growth disorders, but is also misused as a performance-enhancing and purported anti-ageing drug, for which it is banned in sport by WADA.
