Over the Rainbow

"Over the Rainbow", also known as "Somewhere Over the Rainbow", is a ballad composed by Harold Arlen with lyrics by Yip Harburg. It was written for the 1939 film The Wizard of Oz, where it was sung by Judy Garland in her starring role as Dorothy Gale. The song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and became Garland's signature song for the rest of her life.

"Over the Rainbow" was ranked the greatest film song of all time by the American Film Institute.

Composition

Arlen and Harburg were contracted to write the songs for the film in 1938. The melody, with its distinctive octave leap on the first two notes ("Some-WHERE"), expressed a child's longing for a place of happiness beyond her drab surroundings. Studio executives initially considered cutting the scene, but it was kept and became the film's emotional centrepiece. The song's verse, omitted from the film, was performed by Garland in some later recordings.

Legacy and recordings

Garland recorded the song for Decca Records in 1939 and performed it throughout her career; she wrote that she considered herself "the proud mother" of the song. It has been covered by thousands of artists. A medley version by Hawaiian musician Israel Kamakawiwoʻole, "Somewhere Over the Rainbow/What a Wonderful World" (1993), performed on ukulele, became an enduring international hit and has been widely used in film and television. In 2001 the song was voted the number one Song of the Century by the Recording Industry Association of America and the National Endowment for the Arts, and the American Film Institute ranked it first on its list of the 100 greatest songs in American cinema. The song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and Garland's 1939 recording was entered into the National Recording Registry in 2016.

Interpretation

Harburg, a politically engaged lyricist, imbued the song with yearning for a better world. It has been interpreted variously as a child's wish for escape, an immigrant's hope for America, and a symbol of the LGBT community, for whom the rainbow became an emblem of pride and hope.
