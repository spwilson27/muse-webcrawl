The American Symphony Orchestra is a New York-based American orchestra founded in 1962 by the conductor Leopold Stokowski. It is known for innovative, thematically organised programming and for performing neglected works of the orchestral repertoire, often in concert versions of rare operas.

History

Stokowski, then 80 years old, founded the orchestra with the aim of making performances accessible and affordable and of employing talented young musicians. He served as music director until 1972. The orchestra continued under several conductors, and in 1992 Leon Botstein became its music director and principal conductor.

Programming

Under Botstein the ASO became noted for thematic concerts that place works in their historical, literary, and political contexts, accompanied by scholarly program essays, and for revivals of rarely performed operas in concert, such as works by neglected composers of the 19th and 20th centuries. Since 1990 the orchestra has been the resident orchestra of the Bard Music Festival at Bard College in Annandale-on-Hudson, New York, and it regularly performs at Carnegie Hall. Its recordings on labels including Telarc and Vanguard document many of its revivals.
