The Triomphant class is a class of four nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines (SSBNs) operated by the French Navy. Known in French as Sous-marin Nucléaire Lanceur d'Engins de Nouvelle Génération (SNLE-NG), the class forms the sea-based component of France's nuclear deterrent, the Force de dissuasion. The lead boat, Le Triomphant, entered service in 1997.

Boats

  • Le Triomphant (S616) — commissioned 1997
  • Le Téméraire (S617) — commissioned 1999
  • Le Vigilant (S618) — commissioned 2004
  • Le Terrible (S619) — commissioned 2010

All four boats are based at Île Longue in Brittany, near Brest, the home port of France's SSBN force.

Design

The Triomphant class was designed to replace the six Redoutable-class boats. The submarines displace about 14,335 tonnes submerged and are 138 metres long. They are driven by a single K15 pressurised-water reactor of 150 MW, with a pump-jet propulsor that contributes to an exceptionally low acoustic signature; French sources credit the class with being quieter than the ambient noise of the ocean in some conditions.

Each boat carries 16 submarine-launched ballistic missiles. The first three boats initially carried the M45 missile; the class has been refitted to carry the longer-range M51, which has a range of more than 8,000 km and carries multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles with TN 75 or TNO warheads. For self-defence the boats have four 533 mm torpedo tubes that can launch F17 torpedoes and Exocet SM39 anti-ship missiles.

Service

The class maintains France's continuous at-sea deterrent: at least one boat is on patrol at all times. On 3–4 February 2009, Le Triomphant collided with the British SSBN HMS Vanguard in the Atlantic while both were on deterrent patrol; both boats returned to base under their own power with no injuries and no damage to their nuclear systems.

The class is planned to be replaced from the mid-2030s by the SNLE 3G third-generation ballistic missile submarines.
