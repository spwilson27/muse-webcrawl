Dame Cicely Mary Strode Saunders (22 June 1918 – 14 July 2005) was an English nurse, social worker, physician and writer, recognised as the founder of the modern hospice movement and a pioneer of palliative care.

Life and career

Saunders trained as a nurse at St Thomas' Hospital during the Second World War, qualified as a social worker after a back injury ended her nursing, and then — encouraged to study medicine so that her ideas on pain control would be heard — qualified as a doctor in 1957. Working at St Joseph's Hospice in Hackney, she developed systematic methods of pain management for the terminally ill, including the regular, around-the-clock administration of oral morphine rather than pain relief given only on demand.

St Christopher's Hospice

In 1967 she founded St Christopher's Hospice in Sydenham, London, the first modern hospice combining clinical care, research and teaching. Its model — expert symptom control, attention to the emotional, social and spiritual dimensions of suffering, which Saunders called "total pain", and support for families — spread worldwide and helped establish palliative medicine as a recognised specialty (in Britain, in 1987).

Honours and legacy

Saunders was appointed DBE in 1979 and a member of the Order of Merit in 1989. She died at St Christopher's in 2005. Her writings and the hospice movement she inspired transformed end-of-life care across the world; she is widely quoted for the principle: "You matter because you are you, and you matter to the end of your life."
