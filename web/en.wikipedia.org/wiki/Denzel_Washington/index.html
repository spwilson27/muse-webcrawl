Denzel Hayes Washington Jr. (born December 28, 1954) is an American actor, director, and producer. Known for his dramatic roles on stage and screen, he has received numerous accolades, including two Academy Awards, three Golden Globe Awards, a Tony Award, and the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Career

Washington gained prominence in the medical drama St. Elsewhere (1982–1988). He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for Glory (1989) and Best Actor for Training Day (2001). His acclaimed performances include Cry Freedom (1987), Malcolm X (1992), Philadelphia (1993), The Hurricane (1999), Remember the Titans (2000), American Gangster (2007), Flight (2012), and Fences (2016), which he also directed. He headlined the Equalizer action trilogy and has championed August Wilson's plays on stage and screen, winning a Tony Award for the Broadway revival of Fences in 2010.

Personal life

Washington was born in Mount Vernon, New York, and studied drama and journalism at Fordham University. He has been married to Pauletta Washington since 1983; they have four children, including actor John David Washington. A devout Christian, he is a longtime supporter of the Boys & Girls Clubs of America.
