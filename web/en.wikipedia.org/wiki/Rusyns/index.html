Rusyns (Rusyn: Русины, Rusynŷ), also known as Carpatho-Rusyns or Ruthenians, are an East Slavic ethnic group native to the Carpathian Mountains region of Eastern Europe, principally in present-day western Ukraine (Transcarpathia), north-eastern Slovakia (the Prešov Region), south-eastern Poland (Lemkivshchyna) and northern Romania (Maramureș).

Identity and recognition

Whether Rusyns form a distinct people or a branch of the Ukrainian nation has long been debated. Slovakia, Poland, Serbia, Croatia, Hungary and Romania recognise Rusyns as a separate national minority; Ukraine generally regards them as an ethnographic subgroup of Ukrainians, while Rusyn activists continue to press for recognition there. The Rusyn language has been codified in Slovakia (1995), Serbia and Poland (Lemko).

History

Rusyns historically adhered to the Byzantine rite, and after the Unions of Brest (1596) and Uzhhorod (1646) most became Greek Catholics. In the 19th century they were part of Austria-Hungary, and a large emigration to the United States and Canada followed. After the First World War their homeland was divided among Czechoslovakia, Poland and Romania; Transcarpathia was annexed by the Soviet Union in 1945, where Rusyn identity was officially suppressed in favour of a Ukrainian one. Poland's Operation Vistula (1947) deported many Lemkos from their ancestral lands.

Culture

Rusyn culture is noted for wooden church architecture, egg-decorating (pysanky techniques), folk song and distinctive dialects. Major institutions include the World Congress of Rusyns and numerous museums and cultural societies in Prešov, Uzhhorod, Krynica and Pittsburgh.
