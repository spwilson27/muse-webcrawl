In special relativity, electromagnetism and wave theory, the d'Alembert operator (denoted by a box: □), also called the d'Alembertian, wave operator, box operator or sometimes the quabla operator, is the generalisation of the Laplacian operator to Minkowski space. It is named after the French mathematician and physicist Jean le Rond d'Alembert.

Definition

In Minkowski space in standard coordinates (t, x, y, z), the d'Alembert operator has the form

□ = ∂μ∂μ = (1/c²) ∂²/∂t² − ∂²/∂x² − ∂²/∂y² − ∂²/∂z² = (1/c²) ∂²/∂t² − ∇²,

where ∇² is the three-dimensional Laplacian and c is the speed of light. (The overall sign depends on the metric signature convention.) It is a Lorentz scalar: it is invariant under Lorentz transformations, which makes it the natural second-order differential operator of special relativity.

Applications

The homogeneous wave equation for a massless field can be written compactly as □φ = 0; for a wave travelling at speed v the equation is (1/v²)∂²φ/∂t² − ∇²φ = 0. The Klein–Gordon equation for a massive scalar field is (□ + m²c²/ħ²)ψ = 0. In electromagnetism, in Lorenz gauge, each component of the four-potential satisfies a wave equation with the four-current as source: □Aμ = μ₀Jμ.

History

D'Alembert introduced the one-dimensional wave equation in 1747 in his work on vibrating strings, one of the first partial differential equations studied. The four-dimensional Lorentz-invariant form, and the box notation, became standard with the development of special relativity and relativistic field theory in the early 20th century.
