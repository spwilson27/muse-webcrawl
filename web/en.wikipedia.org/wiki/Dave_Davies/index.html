David Russell Gordon Davies (born 3 February 1947) is an English guitarist, singer and songwriter, best known as the lead guitarist and a founding member of the rock band the Kinks, which he formed with his older brother Ray Davies in 1963.

[ Hero image: Dave Davies performing on stage with his guitar ]

The Kinks and the distorted guitar sound

Davies is credited with creating one of the most influential sounds in rock history: frustrated with the clean tone of his amplifier, he slashed its speaker cone with a razor blade, producing the raw, distorted riff of the Kinks' 1964 breakthrough single "You Really Got Me". The record reached number one in the United Kingdom and helped define the guitar-driven sound later adopted by hard rock, heavy metal, and punk. As the Kinks' lead guitarist he also sang lead vocals on several of his own compositions, including "Death of a Clown" (a solo UK top-five hit in 1967), "Strangers", and "Living on a Thin Line".

Solo work and later life

Davies released solo albums beginning with Dave Davies (AFL1-3603) in 1980 and has published an autobiography, Kink (1996). His relationship with Ray was famously volatile, and the Kinks ceased performing in 1996. In 2004 Davies suffered a stroke, from which he recovered sufficiently to resume touring and recording, releasing albums such as Rippin' Up Time (2014). He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Kinks in 1990.
