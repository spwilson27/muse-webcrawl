Henry George Liddell (6 February 1811 – 18 January 1898) was a British classical scholar and clergyman, Dean of Christ Church, Oxford, and co-author, with Robert Scott, of the monumental Greek–English Lexicon known as "Liddell and Scott."

Life and work

Educated at Christ Church, Oxford, Liddell became headmaster of Westminster School in 1846 and Dean of Christ Church in 1855, a post he held until 1891. With Scott he produced the Lexicon (first edition 1843), based on Passow's German work, which remained the standard Greek–English dictionary for over a century. He also wrote a History of Rome and edited Aristotle.

Family

Liddell was the father of Alice Liddell, the inspiration for Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland. Carroll (Charles Dodgson) was a lecturer at Christ Church, and the famous boat trip on which the story began took place in 1862 with Liddell's daughters.
