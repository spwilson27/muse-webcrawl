Kahuzi-Biega National Park

Kahuzi-Biega National Park
Location: South Kivu, Democratic Republic of the Congo
Area: about 6,000 km²
Established: 1970
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1980)

Kahuzi-Biega National Park is a protected area near Bukavu in South Kivu province, Democratic Republic of the Congo. Named after its two dormant volcanic peaks, Mount Kahuzi (3,308 m) and Mount Biega (2,790 m), the park is one of the last refuges of the eastern lowland gorilla (Grauer's gorilla).

Wildlife

The park spans lowland rainforest in the west and montane forest in the east, harbouring eastern lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, forest elephants, bongos and a rich bird fauna. Its gorilla population suffered severely during the Congo wars from poaching and habitat encroachment.

Conservation

Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980, the park was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1997 owing to armed conflict, illegal mining and settlement within its boundaries. Park rangers of the Institut Congolais pour la Conservation de la Nature continue protection work under difficult conditions.
