Rural electrification

Rural electrification is the process of bringing electrical power to rural and remote areas. Because low population density makes the cost per connection of grid extension high, rural electrification typically requires public subsidy or dedicated programmes. Electricity in rural areas enables lighting, irrigation pumping, refrigeration of food and medicines, communications, and small industry, and is strongly associated with improvements in income, education and health.

History

In the United States, private utilities largely bypassed farms in the early 20th century; the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a New Deal measure, created the Rural Electrification Administration, which lent to farmer-owned cooperatives and raised farm electrification from about 10% in the 1930s to over 90% by the 1950s. Similar state-led programmes followed in Europe and, after decolonisation, in Asia and Africa; China's township and village electrification and India's village electrification drives (which declared all villages electrified in 2018) are among the largest.

Technologies

Grid extension remains the dominant approach where feasible. For remote communities, off-grid solutions — solar home systems, mini-grids combining solar, wind, micro-hydro or diesel, and battery storage — have become increasingly cost-effective and are central to efforts to reach the roughly 700 million people who still lacked electricity in the early 2020s, most of them in sub-Saharan Africa.
