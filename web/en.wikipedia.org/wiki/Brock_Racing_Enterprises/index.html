Brock Racing Enterprises (BRE) is an American racing team and design business founded in 1965 by the automotive designer and driver Peter Brock. Brock had previously worked at General Motors and at Shelby American, where he designed the Shelby Daytona Cobra Coupe that won the FIA GT championship in 1965. After leaving Shelby, he established BRE to design and race cars for clients.

History

An early project was the sleek Hino Samurai GT prototype for the Japanese Hino company. Brock's most famous campaigns came with Datsun (Nissan): BRE-prepared Datsun 2000 roadsters, and then the Datsun 240Z and Datsun 510 saloon driven by John Morton, won consecutive SCCA national championships in 1970–71 (C Production) and the Trans-Am Series 2.5-litre championship in 1971–72 respectively. The red, white and blue BRE livery became iconic in American sports-car racing and helped establish Japanese cars in the US market.

Later work

BRE's racing programme wound down in the mid-1970s, and Brock pursued ventures including Ultralite hang-glider manufacturing and photography. In later years BRE was revived to support heritage racing and limited-edition tributes, including continuation and homage versions of its famous Datsun race cars, while Peter Brock remained active as a designer, journalist and judge at concours events.
