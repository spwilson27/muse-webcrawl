Trial frame

A trial frame is an adjustable spectacle frame used by optometrists and ophthalmologists during subjective refraction to determine a patient's corrective lens prescription. The frame holds trial lenses in front of the patient's eyes so that spherical, cylindrical, and prismatic corrections can be combined and refined while the patient reads an eye chart.

Design

A typical trial frame is made of lightweight metal or plastic and is adjustable in several dimensions: the bridge and nose pads adapt to the patient's face, the temple arms vary in length, and the lens cells can be rotated to set the axis of cylindrical lenses. Most frames provide cells for several lenses on each side, including slots in front of and behind the principal lens plane, and graduated scales for measuring axis and lens position.

Use

During an eye examination the practitioner inserts lenses from a trial lens set — a case containing lenses of graded powers — and asks the patient to compare choices ("which is clearer, one or two?"). Although the phoropter has largely replaced the trial frame in many clinics, trial frames remain essential for verifying a final prescription in free space, for examining children and patients with unusual facial anatomy, for low-vision work, and for checking progressive or multifocal corrections under natural viewing conditions.
