Full score

A full score is a form of sheet music that shows all the instrumental and vocal parts of a musical work aligned vertically on staves, so that a conductor can read every part at once and direct an ensemble. It contrasts with a single part, which contains only the music for one player, and with a reduced or short score, which compresses the work onto a few staves.

Layout and conventions

In an orchestral full score, instruments are conventionally ordered from top to bottom by family: woodwinds, brass, percussion, and strings, with voices typically placed between the brass and strings in choral-orchestral works. Transposing instruments are usually written at their sounding pitch transposition (as the players read them), though some modern scores are written "in C" with all parts at concert pitch. Large scores for opera or ballet may run to hundreds of pages.

Uses

Full scores are used by conductors in performance and rehearsal, and by scholars and students for the study of orchestration and composition. Reduced formats derived from the full score include the miniature (study) score and the piano-vocal score used in rehearsing opera and choral works.
