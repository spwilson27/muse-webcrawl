Nippon Steel Corporation (日本製鉄株式会社, Nippon Seitetsu Kabushiki-gaisha) is Japan's largest steelmaker and one of the world's leading producers of crude steel. The modern company was formed in 1970 by the merger of Yawata Iron & Steel and Fuji Iron & Steel, and took its earlier name Nippon Steel & Sumitomo Metal after absorbing Sumitomo Metal Industries in 2012, before reverting to the Nippon Steel name in 2019.

History

The company's roots reach to the state-owned Yawata Steel Works, which began operation in 1901 in Kitakyushu and became the core of Japan's modern steel industry, and to the pre-war zaibatsu-linked producers consolidated into Japan Iron & Steel in 1934. The 1970 merger creating the original Nippon Steel was among the largest in Japanese corporate history and made the firm the world's biggest steelmaker of its era. Following the 2012 Sumitomo merger, the group expanded overseas, including a joint venture with ArcelorMittal that acquired Essar Steel in India (renamed AM/NS India).

U.S. Steel acquisition

In December 2023 Nippon Steel agreed to acquire United States Steel for about $14.9 billion. The deal became a major political issue in the 2024 U.S. presidential election; President Joe Biden blocked it on national-security grounds in January 2025. Under President Donald Trump, a revised arrangement was approved in June 2025 that gave the U.S. government a "golden share" with certain governance and investment oversight rights, and the acquisition was completed, making U.S. Steel a subsidiary while keeping its headquarters in Pittsburgh.

Operations

Nippon Steel operates large integrated works across Japan — including Kimitsu, Kashima, Nagoya, Wakayama, Oita, and Muroran — producing high-grade steels for automobiles, shipbuilding, energy, and construction, alongside engineering, chemicals, and materials subsidiaries. The company has committed to carbon-neutral steelmaking by 2050, investing in hydrogen-based reduction and large electric arc furnaces.
