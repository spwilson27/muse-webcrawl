Greco-Roman wrestling

Greco-Roman wrestling is a style of wrestling practised worldwide and contested at the Olympic Games. Unlike freestyle wrestling, it forbids holds below the waist: wrestlers may not trip, sweep, or use their legs to attack or defend, and all holds must be applied to the upper body.

History

The style developed in 19th-century Europe — particularly France, where it was promoted as an evocation of ancient classical wrestling — and spread internationally under the name "French wrestling" before becoming known as Greco-Roman. It was included in the first modern Olympic Games at Athens in 1896 and has featured at every Summer Olympics since 1908.

Rules

Bouts are won by pinning the opponent's shoulders to the mat (a fall) or by scoring points for takedowns, throws, exposures of the opponent's back, and reversals. High-amplitude throws score the most points, and the style is noted for spectacular suplexes and lifts executed from standing clinches and from the forced par terre (ground) position. International competition is governed by United World Wrestling.

Notable wrestlers

The most celebrated Greco-Roman wrestler is Russia's Aleksandr Karelin, who won three Olympic gold medals and went undefeated in international competition for thirteen years. Cuba's Mijaín López surpassed all wrestlers by winning gold in the same event at five consecutive Olympics (2008–2024), a record across both wrestling styles.
