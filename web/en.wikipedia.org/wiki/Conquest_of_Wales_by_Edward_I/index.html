The conquest of Wales by Edward I of England was the military annexation of the last independent Welsh principalities — Gwynedd and its dependencies — in two campaigns, the first war of 1277 and the second war of 1282–83. It ended native Welsh rule and brought all of Wales under English domination, confirmed by the Statute of Rhuddlan in 1284.

Background

By the mid-13th century Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, Prince of Gwynedd and Wales, had united most of the country under his authority. His refusal to pay homage to Edward I, and his shelter of English rebels, gave Edward a pretext for war.

The campaigns

In 1277 Edward invaded with a large army, systematically encircling Snowdonia while a fleet cut off Anglesey, Wales's breadbasket; the Treaty of Aberconwy reduced Llywelyn to Gwynedd alone. The harsher second war began in 1282 after Dafydd ap Gruffudd attacked English garrisons. Llywelyn was killed in an ambush at Cilmeri in December 1282, Dafydd was captured in 1283 and executed, and Wales was pacified.

Legacy

Edward secured the conquest with a ring of great castles — Caernarfon, Conwy, Harlech and Beaumaris — and in 1301 granted his son, the future Edward II, the title Prince of Wales, a practice continued for heirs to the English (later British) throne ever since.
