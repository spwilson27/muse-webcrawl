Train Protection & Warning System

The Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) is a train protection system used on the British railway network and in some other countries. Introduced nationally from the late 1990s, it automatically applies a train's brakes if the train passes a signal at danger (a SPAD) or approaches a signal, buffer stop or speed restriction too fast, mitigating accidents where the driver has made an error.

How it works

TPWS uses pairs of radio-frequency loops placed between the rails. An overspeed sensor system near the approach to a signal arms and triggers on timers set by the train's speed: if the train passes the second loop too soon, the system demands a brake application. A train stop system at the signal itself applies the brakes if the train passes at danger. The driver's TPWS panel shows the demand, and the brakes cannot be released until the system resets after a delay.

Background

Development of TPWS followed a series of accidents and official inquiries, including the Clapham Junction crash of 1988 and the Southall (1997) and Ladbroke Grove (1999) collisions, the latter two involving signals passed at danger. Full Automatic Train Protection was judged too costly and slow to fit across the network, and TPWS was adopted as an interim system estimated to prevent the majority of SPAD-related accidents, pending the European Train Control System (ETCS).

Limitations

TPWS cannot stop a train that passes a signal at danger at high speed within the overlap beyond the signal, and it provides no protection against some other failure modes. It is therefore regarded as a risk-reduction measure rather than complete train protection.
