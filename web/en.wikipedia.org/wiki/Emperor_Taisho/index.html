Emperor Taishō (大正天皇, personal name Yoshihito; 1879–1926) was the 123rd Emperor of Japan, reigning from 1912 to 1926 during the period known as Taishō democracy.

Life and reign

The son of Emperor Meiji, Yoshihito suffered meningitis in infancy and lifelong ill health, both physical and mental. Crown prince from 1889, he married Kujō Sadako (Empress Teimei) in 1900; they had four sons, including Hirohito. His reign saw Japan fight on the Allied side in the First World War, the growth of party cabinets, universal male suffrage (1925), the 1918 rice riots and the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923.

As his health declined, Hirohito became regent in 1921. Taishō died of a heart attack at Hayama in December 1926, ending the era named "Great Righteousness."
