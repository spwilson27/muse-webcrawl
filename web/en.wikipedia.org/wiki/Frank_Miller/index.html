Frank Miller (born 27 January 1957) is an American comic book creator, screenwriter and film director known for his comic book stories and graphic novels such as his run on Daredevil and the subsequent Daredevil: Born Again, the graphic novel The Dark Knight Returns and its sequel, The Dark Knight Strikes Again, the hard-boiled crime series Sin City, and the historical work 300. He is widely credited, along with contemporaries such as Alan Moore, with bringing a darker, more adult tone to American mainstream comics in the 1980s.

Career

Raised in Vermont, Miller broke into comics in the late 1970s with short work for Gold Key, DC and Marvel. His breakthrough came as artist, then writer-artist, of Marvel's Daredevil (1979–1983), where he introduced the assassin Elektra and the ninja clan the Hand, drew on film noir and martial-arts cinema, and transformed a second-tier title into a bestseller. Ronin (1983–84), a samurai science-fiction series for DC, showcased his experimentation with colour and Japanese manga influences.

In 1986 DC published Batman: The Dark Knight Returns, depicting an aging Bruce Wayne returning to crime-fighting in a dystopian Gotham; together with Watchmen it redefined the superhero genre and remains one of the best-selling graphic novels ever. Miller followed with Batman: Year One (1987, with David Mazzucchelli) and, at Dark Horse, Hard Boiled and the noir pastiches of Sin City (from 1991), rendered in stark black and white. 300 (1998), with colourist Lynn Varley, retold the Battle of Thermopylae.

Film

Miller co-directed the film adaptation of Sin City (2005) with Robert Rodriguez and served as executive producer on 300 (2007); he directed The Spirit (2008) solo and co-directed Sin City: A Dame to Kill For (2014). In the 2010s he returned to Batman with The Dark Knight III: The Master Race and other projects, and his later political statements and works, such as the controversial Holy Terror (2011), attracted criticism.

Style and influence

Miller's artwork features heavy black areas, chiaroscuro and expressionist anatomy influenced by Will Eisner, Hugo Pratt and Japanese gekiga. His writing favours terse, hard-boiled narration, themes of lone vigilantes against corrupt systems, and moral ambiguity. He has won numerous Eisner and Harvey Awards and was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 2015.
