Deck (building)

[Image: A wooden residential deck]
A raised timber deck attached to a house

In architecture, a deck is a flat surface capable of supporting weight, similar to a floor, but typically constructed outdoors, often elevated from the ground, and usually connected to a building. The term is a generalization from the deck of a ship. Decks provide outdoor living space and are commonly made of wood, composite materials, or other weather-resistant materials.

Construction

A typical residential deck consists of a substructure of posts, beams and joists supporting deck boards laid across the top. Posts are usually set on concrete footings below the frost line. Joists are commonly spaced 12 to 16 inches apart and support the decking. Ledgers attach the deck to the house, and proper flashing is essential to prevent water damage to the building. Guard rails are required by building codes where the deck surface is more than a specified height above grade — commonly 30 inches in the United States.

Materials

Traditionally decks were built of naturally rot-resistant woods such as redwood, western red cedar and ipe, or of pressure-treated pine. Since the early 1990s, wood-plastic composite decking has gained a large share of the market; it resists rot and insects and needs no staining, though it can be hotter underfoot and more expensive. PVC and aluminium decking are also available. Tropical hardwoods raise sustainability concerns, and certification schemes address illegal logging.

Related structures

Decks differ from patios, which sit directly on the ground, and from balconies and verandas, which are typically roofed or enclosed. Raised decks often incorporate stairs, built-in seating, pergolas and planters. Rooftop decks are built over flat roofs with pedestal systems that protect the roofing membrane.
