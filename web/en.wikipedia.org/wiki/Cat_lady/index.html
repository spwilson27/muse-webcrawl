Cat lady

A cat lady is a cultural archetype or stock character, most often depicted as a woman, usually middle-aged or elderly, who keeps many pet cats. The term can be used pejoratively, suggesting eccentricity, spinsterhood, and social isolation, but in recent decades it has also been reclaimed affectionately by people — of any gender, though "cat lady" remains the common phrase — who identify proudly with their devotion to cats.

The stereotype

In its negative form, the stereotype portrays the cat lady as a lonely, unkempt recluse whose excessive devotion to her cats substitutes for human relationships. The image is connected to historical associations between women, cats, and witchcraft, and to the portrayal of spinsters in literature and popular culture. Extreme cases shade into concerns about animal hoarding, a psychological condition in which a person keeps more animals than they can adequately care for.

In popular culture

Famous fictional examples include the "Crazy Cat Lady" (Eleanor Abernathy) in The Simpsons, a dishevelled woman who throws cats at passers-by, and various characters in film and television who embody the trope either for comedy or pathos. The character Catwoman in Batman stories plays on related associations, though in a glamorous register. Social media communities have embraced the term positively, and "cat person" identities are now widely expressed in merchandise, hashtags, and online culture.

Reclamation

Writers and commentators have challenged the stereotype as a sexist double standard, noting that men who keep many dogs or other animals are rarely mocked in equivalent terms. Surveys of cat owners have found little evidence that they are lonelier or more anxious than other pet owners, and the self-applied label "cat lady" is increasingly a badge of humour and pride rather than shame.
