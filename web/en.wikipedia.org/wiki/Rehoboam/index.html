Rehoboam (Hebrew: רְחַבְעָם, Rəḥaḇʿām) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the first monarch of the Kingdom of Judah after the split of the united kingdom of Israel. He was a son of and the successor to Solomon and a grandfather of David.

Division of the kingdom

After Solomon's death, Rehoboam travelled to Shechem to be crowned. The northern tribes, led by Jeroboam, asked him to lighten the heavy labour and taxation imposed by Solomon. Rejecting the advice of the older counsellors, Rehoboam answered harshly — "My little finger is thicker than my father's waist" — whereupon the ten northern tribes seceded and formed the separate Kingdom of Israel under Jeroboam, leaving Rehoboam ruling only Judah and Benjamin.

Reign

Rehoboam fortified cities in Judah and reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem. In his fifth year, Pharaoh Shishak (Shoshenq I) of Egypt invaded and plundered the Temple, according to 1 Kings 14. Rehoboam was succeeded by his son Abijah. In the New Testament, Rehoboam appears in the genealogy of Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew.
