The British Deer Society (BDS) is a British charity dedicated to the welfare, study and humane management of deer in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1963, it is the principal organisation concerned with deer in Britain, where six species are established in the wild: red deer, roe deer, fallow deer, sika deer, muntjac and Chinese water deer.

Work

The Society promotes evidence-based deer management, balancing deer welfare against the impacts of growing deer populations on woodland, agriculture, biodiversity and road safety. It conducts and funds research, runs national deer surveys, and provides training and qualifications for deer managers and stalkers, including the Deer Stalking Certificates (DSC1 and DSC2).

Publications and membership

The BDS publishes a quarterly journal, Deer, and a range of guidance on deer law, species identification and best practice in management. Membership is drawn from stalkers, landowners, conservationists, vets and members of the public with an interest in deer. The Society has been chaired by a series of prominent figures in British conservation and field sports.
