Lawrence, Massachusetts

Downtown Lawrence along the Merrimack River

Lawrence is a city in Essex County, Massachusetts, United States, on the Merrimack River. It was one of the great textile manufacturing centres of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, built as a planned industrial city beginning in 1845 by the Essex Company and named after textile magnate Abbott Lawrence. As of the 2020 census, the city had a population of about 89,000, making it one of the most densely populated cities in New England, and it has a large Latino population, particularly Dominican and Puerto Rican communities.

History

Lawrence was founded as an industrial city powered by the Merrimack River; the Great Stone Dam, completed in 1848, was among the largest dams in the world at the time. Its woollen and cotton mills — including the vast Wood Worsted Mill — drew successive waves of immigrants from Ireland, French Canada, Italy, Poland, Lithuania, and elsewhere. The city is famous as the site of the 1912 Lawrence textile strike, the "Bread and Roses" strike, led largely by immigrant women workers and organised with the Industrial Workers of the World; the strike won wage increases across the New England textile industry.

Decline and later years

The textile industry declined after the Second World War as production moved south and overseas, and Lawrence experienced decades of economic difficulty. In 2018, the city and neighbouring communities were affected by the Merrimack Valley gas explosions, when over-pressurised gas lines caused fires and explosions that killed one person and displaced thousands.

Geography and culture

Lawrence covers roughly 7.4 square miles, almost entirely urban. It is home to a noted heritage of mill architecture, the Lawrence History Center, and the annual Semana Hispana (Hispanic Week) festival. The poet Robert Frost spent part of his youth in Lawrence and published his first poems while living there.
