Tibetan antelope

The Tibetan antelope (Pantholops hodgsonii), also called the chiru, is a medium-sized bovid native to the Tibetan plateau. It is the sole species in the genus Pantholops. Males have long, nearly vertical black horns and a distinctive black face and legs, while females are hornless.

Distribution and habitat

The chiru inhabits high-altitude alpine steppe and semi-desert of the Changtang region at elevations of roughly 3,700 to 5,500 metres, across the Tibet Autonomous Region, Qinghai and Xinjiang, with a small population in Ladakh, India. Females undertake long seasonal migrations to traditional calving grounds, one of the notable mammal migrations of Asia.

Shahtoosh and conservation

The species was hunted nearly to extinction for its extremely fine underfur, known as shahtoosh, which is woven into luxury shawls. Because the animals cannot be shorn alive commercially and the fibre is collected from killed animals, the international trade in shahtoosh is banned under CITES, and the species is protected in China and India. Populations, once reduced to perhaps 75,000 or fewer in the 1990s, have recovered substantially under anti-poaching protection centred on reserves such as Changtang and Hoh Xil (Kekexili); the species was the mascot Yingying among the Fuwa of the 2008 Beijing Olympics. Its IUCN status improved from Endangered to Near Threatened in 2016 as numbers rebounded.
