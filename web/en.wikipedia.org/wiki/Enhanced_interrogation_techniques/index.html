Enhanced interrogation techniques

Used byCIA (2002–2007)
Key reportSenate Intelligence Committee study (2014)

"Enhanced interrogation techniques" is a euphemism for a programme of systematic coercive interrogation methods authorised by the United States government after the September 11 attacks and applied by the Central Intelligence Agency to detainees held at black sites and Guantanamo Bay. The techniques included waterboarding, sleep deprivation, stress positions, confinement in small boxes, slapping and walling, and exposure to cold.

Background and controversy

The programme was authorised through legal memoranda from the Office of Legal Counsel in 2002. Critics, including many legal scholars, medical organisations, and allied governments, characterised the techniques as torture prohibited by the UN Convention Against Torture and the Geneva Conventions, while officials defended them as lawful and effective. Psychologists James Mitchell and Bruce Jessen, contracted to design the programme, adapted methods from the military's SERE resistance training.

Aftermath

President Barack Obama banned the techniques by executive order in January 2009. The Senate Intelligence Committee's 2014 report concluded that the programme was more brutal and less effective than the CIA had represented, findings the agency disputed. No senior officials were prosecuted, though the programme remains a subject of litigation and historical debate.
