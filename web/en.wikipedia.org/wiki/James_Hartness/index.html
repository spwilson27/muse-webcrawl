James Hartness (September 3, 1861 – February 2, 1934) was an American mechanical engineer, inventor, and manufacturer who served as the 47th governor of Vermont from 1921 to 1923. He is credited with major innovations in the machine-tool industry, including the turret lathe designs produced by the Jones & Lamson Machine Company of Springfield, Vermont.

Engineering career

Largely self-educated, Hartness worked as a machinist and foreman before settling in Springfield in 1889, where he joined the Jones & Lamson Machine Company, eventually becoming its president. His flat turret lathe greatly increased the speed and precision of turning operations and became a standard machine in American manufacturing. He held numerous patents and wrote on optics and astronomy as well as engineering; he also built an early equatorial telescope mount, the Hartness turret telescope, at his Springfield home.

Hartness was an early advocate of aviation, helping to establish Springfield's airport — Vermont's first — and serving as the first chairman of the Vermont Aeronautics Commission.

Governor of Vermont

A Republican, Hartness served a single two-year term as governor, from 1921 to 1923, focusing on administrative efficiency, highway improvement, and the promotion of industry and aviation in the state.

Legacy

He died in Springfield in 1934. Hartness State Airport near Springfield bears his name, and his Springfield estate, including its observatory and underground passageways, is preserved as a historic inn.
