Iron peak

The iron peak is a maximum in the curve of atomic abundance and nuclear binding energy in the vicinity of iron and nickel, elements with mass numbers around 56–62. Nuclei in this region have the highest binding energy per nucleon of any elements, meaning they are the most tightly bound and stable nuclei. The peak in binding energy per nucleon occurs at nickel-62, followed by iron-58 and iron-56.

Explanation

Nuclear fusion reactions release energy when they produce nuclei lighter than the iron peak, because the product is more tightly bound. Conversely, fission of nuclei much heavier than iron releases energy. As a result, iron-peak nuclei are effectively "nuclear ash": fusion in stellar cores cannot proceed beyond the iron peak with a net release of energy, which is why massive stars develop inert iron cores whose collapse triggers core-collapse supernovae.

Nucleosynthesis

Iron-peak elements are produced in the final stages of stellar nucleosynthesis, principally during silicon burning in massive stars and in thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernovae, largely as radioactive nickel-56, which decays through cobalt-56 to iron-56. The abundance peak of iron and its neighbours in the cosmos reflects this origin and the great stability of these nuclei.
