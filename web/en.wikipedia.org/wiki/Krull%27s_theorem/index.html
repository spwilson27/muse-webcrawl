Krull's theorem

Krull's principal ideal theorem
FieldCommutative algebra
Named afterWolfgang Krull

Krull's theorem usually refers to the principal ideal theorem (Hauptidealsatz) of commutative algebra, proved by Wolfgang Krull in 1928. It states that a minimal prime ideal over a principal ideal in a Noetherian ring has height at most one.

Generalised form

More generally, a minimal prime over an ideal generated by n elements has height at most n. The theorem is fundamental to dimension theory: it underlies the result that polynomial rings and power series rings have the expected Krull dimension.

Related results

The name "Krull's theorem" is also attached to Krull's intersection theorem and to the Krull–Schmidt theorem. The principal ideal theorem also implies that in a Noetherian local ring the dimension is bounded by the minimal number of generators of an m-primary ideal.
