The spectacled bear (Tremarctos ornatus), also known as the Andean bear, is a species of bear native to the Andes Mountains in northern and western South America. It is the only living species of the genus Tremarctos and the only surviving member of the short-faced bear subfamily (Tremarctinae). It is the last remaining short-faced bear and the only bear species native to South America.

Description

The spectacled bear is named for the pale beige or ginger-coloured markings around its eyes, which in some individuals resemble spectacles; the markings vary greatly and may be reduced or absent. The coat is otherwise black or dark brown. Males are considerably larger than females. The bear has a shortened face and powerful jaw muscles suited to a largely herbivorous diet, and plantigrade feet with long claws for climbing.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges through the Andes of Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, and possibly northwestern Argentina, occupying cloud forests and other habitats between roughly 250 and 4,750 m elevation. It is largely arboreal for feeding, building platforms in trees, though it also forages on the ground.

Diet and behaviour

Spectacled bears are omnivores with a strongly plant-based diet: bromeliads (especially Puya), cactus fruits, palm hearts, bamboo, orchid bulbs, and fruit are staples, supplemented by insects, rodents, carrion, and occasionally livestock, which brings them into conflict with farmers. They are generally solitary and shy.

Conservation

The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, with a population estimated at fewer than 18,000, threatened by habitat loss and fragmentation of Andean cloud forest and by killing in retaliation for crop and livestock damage. The spectacled bear is the real-life species that inspired the fictional Paddington Bear, who comes from "Darkest Peru."
