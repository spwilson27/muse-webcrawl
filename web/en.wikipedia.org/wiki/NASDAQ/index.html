Nasdaq

The Nasdaq Stock Market (/ˈnæzdæk/; originally an acronym for the National Association of Securities Dealers Automated Quotations) is an American stock exchange based in New York City. It is the second-largest stock exchange in the world by market capitalisation of shares traded, behind the New York Stock Exchange. The exchange platform is owned by Nasdaq, Inc., which also owns several other stock exchanges in the United States and Europe.

History

Nasdaq was founded in 1971 by the National Association of Securities Dealers (NASD) and began operating on 8 February 1971 as the world's first electronic stock market, initially as a quotation system rather than a full exchange. It attracted many young technology companies; listings such as Microsoft (1986) and Apple (1980) helped define its tech-heavy identity. The exchange was central to the dot-com boom and bust of 1999–2000. In 2000–2001 the NASD spun Nasdaq off as a publicly traded company. Nasdaq later acquired the operator of the Nordic exchanges (OMX) and the Philadelphia and Boston stock exchanges.

Indices

The best-known benchmark is the Nasdaq Composite, which includes nearly all Nasdaq-listed stocks, and the Nasdaq-100, comprising the 100 largest non-financial companies on the exchange; the latter is tracked by the popular QQQ exchange-traded fund.

Listing tiers

Companies list on one of three tiers — Global Select Market, Global Market and Capital Market — with progressively less stringent financial requirements. Trading is fully electronic, conducted through market makers and electronic communication networks, with hours of 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time plus pre- and post-market sessions.
