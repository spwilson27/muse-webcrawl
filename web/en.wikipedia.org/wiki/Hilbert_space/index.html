Hilbert space

A Hilbert space is a real or complex inner product space that is also a complete metric space with respect to the distance function induced by the inner product. Named after David Hilbert, Hilbert spaces generalize Euclidean space to finite or infinite dimensions and are a cornerstone of functional analysis.

Definition

A Hilbert space H is a vector space equipped with an inner product ⟨·,·⟩ such that the norm ‖x‖ = √⟨x,x⟩ makes H complete: every Cauchy sequence converges to an element of H. Completeness guarantees that geometric arguments based on orthogonal projection work as in Euclidean geometry — notably, every closed convex set has a unique nearest point, and every closed subspace has an orthogonal complement.

Examples

  • Euclidean space ℝn and ℂn with the standard inner product.
  • The sequence space ℓ² of square-summable sequences.
  • The function space L² of square-integrable functions, central to Fourier analysis.

Applications

In quantum mechanics, the state of a physical system is represented by a vector (or ray) in a Hilbert space, and observables by self-adjoint operators. Hilbert spaces also underlie the theory of partial differential equations, signal processing, ergodic theory, and reproducing kernel methods in machine learning.

Orthonormal bases

Every Hilbert space possesses an orthonormal basis; in the separable case this is a countable sequence e₁, e₂, … such that every vector x expands as x = Σ ⟨x, eₙ⟩ eₙ, generalizing Fourier series.
