Symphony No. 9 (Beethoven)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The autograph of the Ninth: Schiller's Ode to Joy chorus blazing across the finale's pages]

The Symphony No. 9 in D minor, Op. 125, is the final completed symphony by Ludwig van Beethoven, composed between 1822 and 1824. It was first performed in Vienna on 7 May 1824. The symphony is regarded by many critics and musicologists as a masterpiece of Western classical music and one of the supreme achievements in the history of music.

The Ninth was revolutionary: its finale introduces four vocal soloists and a chorus into a symphony, setting Friedrich Schiller's poem 'An die Freude' ('Ode to Joy') in a hymn to universal brotherhood. Beethoven, by then almost totally deaf, 'conducted' the premiere while the actual direction was given by Michael Umlauf; the contralto Caroline Unger famously turned him around to see the applause he could not hear. The 'Ode to Joy' theme later became the anthem of the European Union, and the symphony's length is legendarily said to have influenced the capacity of the compact disc.
