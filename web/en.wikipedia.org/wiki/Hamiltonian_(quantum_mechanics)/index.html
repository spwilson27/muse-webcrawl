Hamiltonian (quantum mechanics)

[Image: The Hamiltonian operator at the heart of the Schrödinger equation]

Overview

In quantum mechanics, the Hamiltonian of a system is an operator corresponding to the total energy of that system, including both kinetic and potential energy. It usually takes the form Ĥ = p̂²/2m + V(x̂) for a particle of mass m, and it determines the system's time evolution through the time-dependent Schrödinger equation, iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ. It is named after the Irish mathematician William Rowan Hamilton, whose classical Hamiltonian dynamics it quantises.

Role

The Hamiltonian's eigenfunctions are the stationary states of the system and its eigenvalues their allowed energies — solving Ĥψ = Eψ is the central task of most quantum-mechanical calculations, from the hydrogen atom to quantum chemistry and condensed matter. For open and driven systems the formalism extends to density operators and effective Hamiltonians; in quantum field theory the Hamiltonian generates time translations and anchors canonical quantisation.
