Senate Square

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Senate Square (Russian: Сенатская площадь), formerly known as Decembrists' Square (Площадь Декабристов) from 1925 to 2008, and Peter's Square before 1925, is a historic city square in Saint Petersburg, Russia. It is situated on the left bank of the Bolshaya Neva, in front of Saint Isaac's Cathedral.

History

The square took its name from the building of the Governing Senate, erected on its western side in the early 19th century. To the east stands the Admiralty building, while the southern edge is dominated by Saint Isaac's Cathedral, completed in 1858.

Senate Square is best known for two things. The first is the Bronze Horseman, the famous equestrian monument to Peter the Great commissioned by Catherine the Great and unveiled in 1782; created by the French sculptor Étienne Maurice Falconet, it depicts Peter astride a rearing horse on a massive granite pedestal known as the Thunder Stone.

The second is the Decembrist revolt of 14 December 1825 (Old Style), when some 3,000 soldiers led by liberal army officers assembled on the square to protest the accession of Nicholas I, demanding a constitutional monarchy. The revolt was suppressed by artillery fire loyal to the new emperor, and many of its leaders were executed or exiled to Siberia.

Name changes

In 1925, to mark the centenary of the revolt, the square was renamed Decembrists' Square. On 29 July 2008 the Saint Petersburg city government restored the historical name Senate Square.
