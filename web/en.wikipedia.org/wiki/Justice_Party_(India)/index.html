Justice Party (India)

Founded1916
Dissolved1944 (transformed into Dravidar Kazhagam)
FoundersT. M. Nair, P. Theagaraya Chetty
RegionMadras Presidency, British India

The Justice Party, officially the South Indian Liberal Federation, was a political party in the Madras Presidency of British India. Founded in 1916 by T. M. Nair and P. Theagaraya Chetty, it represented the interests of non-Brahmin communities and became a foundational influence on the Dravidian movement in Tamil politics.

History

The party emerged from the Non-Brahmin Manifesto of 1916, which protested Brahmin dominance in government employment and education. Under the dyarchy system introduced by the Government of India Act 1919, the Justice Party won the first Madras elections of 1920 and formed several ministries during the 1920s and early 1930s.

Policies

Justice ministries introduced communal representation in public services, expanded education, and passed pioneering legislation including the Hindu Religious Endowments Act. The party's social-reform agenda laid groundwork for later Dravidian parties.

Decline and legacy

The party declined in the 1930s amid internal splits and the rise of the Indian National Congress, losing the 1937 elections heavily. In 1944, under E. V. Ramasamy (Periyar), it was reorganised as the Dravidar Kazhagam, from which the DMK and AIADMK later emerged.
