Delilah

Delilah (Hebrew: דְּלִילָה, Dəlīlā) is a woman in the Book of Judges in the Hebrew Bible. She is the lover of Samson, the Israelite judge famed for his superhuman strength, and is bribed by the lords of the Philistines to discover the secret of his power.

Biblical narrative

According to Judges 16, Samson fell in love with Delilah in the valley of Sorek. The Philistine rulers offered her eleven hundred pieces of silver from each of them if she would find out where Samson's great strength lay. Three times Samson deceived her — with fresh bowstrings, new ropes, and weaving his hair into a loom — and three times the ambush failed. Finally, after she pressed him daily, he revealed that his hair had never been cut, because he was a Nazirite. Delilah lulled him to sleep on her knees and had his seven locks shaved; his strength left him, and the Philistines blinded and imprisoned him.

Interpretation

Delilah's ethnicity is not stated in the text, though she is often assumed to be a Philistine. In later tradition she became an archetype of the treacherous seductress and a byword for betrayal for money. Her story has been retold in art, literature, opera (Saint-Saëns' Samson and Delilah), and film.
