Pyrgi Tablets

The Pyrgi Tablets are three inscribed gold plates found in 1964 during excavations at Pyrgi, the ancient port of the Etruscan city of Caere (modern Cerveteri) in Italy. Dating to about 500 BCE, two of the tablets are written in Etruscan and one in Phoenician (Punic), describing the dedication of a shrine to the goddess Uni — identified with the Phoenician Astarte — by Thefarie Velianas, ruler of Caere.

Significance

Because the Phoenician text is not an exact translation of the Etruscan ones, the tablets are often called a "bilingual" only loosely, but they remain one of the most important sources for understanding the Etruscan language, allowing scholars to confirm the meaning of a number of Etruscan words and grammatical forms.

Location

The tablets are now held by the National Etruscan Museum at Villa Giulia in Rome.
