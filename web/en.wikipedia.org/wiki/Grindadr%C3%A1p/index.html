Grindadráp

LocationFaroe Islands
TypeDrive hunt of pilot whales
Target speciesMainly long-finned pilot whale
Records fromAt least 1584
Legal basisFaroese regulation; tradition predates modern law

Grindadráp (from the Faroese words grindur, a school of pilot whales, and dráp, killing) is a type of drive hunting that involves beaching long-finned pilot whales and other small cetaceans in the Faroe Islands. The practice, dating from the time of the first Norse settlements, is regulated by Faroese authorities and carried out by local communities.

Process

When a school of pilot whales is spotted, boats form a semicircle and drive the animals toward an authorised beach, where they are stranded and killed with spinal-cord lances designed to sever the spinal cord quickly. The meat and blubber are distributed among participants and the local community, for whom whaling has historically been an important food source on islands with little arable land.

Scale and controversy

Faroese records of whale drives date back to 1584 and are among the longest-running wildlife statistics in the world. Animal-rights organisations and some governments criticise the hunt as cruel and unnecessary, while Faroese authorities argue it is sustainable, communal, and culturally significant. In September 2021, an unusually large catch of over 1,400 Atlantic white-sided dolphins prompted a Faroese government review and the introduction of an annual catch limit for that species.

Health advisories

Because pilot whale meat and blubber accumulate mercury and PCBs, Faroese health authorities have advised limiting consumption, especially for pregnant women and children.
