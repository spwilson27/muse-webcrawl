The Battle of the Pyramids, also known as the Battle of Embabeh, was a major engagement fought on 21 July 1798 during the French invasion of Egypt. The French Army in the Orient, under Napoleon Bonaparte, used divisional squares — infantry formed in large hollow squares with artillery at the corners — to repel charges by the Mamluk cavalry while advancing on Cairo. The victory opened the way to the French occupation of Egypt.

French infantry squares under attack by Mamluk cavalry, with the pyramids in the background.

Background

Napoleon's expedition landed at Alexandria on 1 July 1798, aiming to strike at British interests and the route to India. Marching south along the Nile through desert heat, the French met the army of the Mamluk beys Murad and Ibrahim near the village of Embabeh, across the river from Cairo and within sight of the Giza pyramids — some 15 kilometres away. Before the battle Napoleon is said to have addressed his men: "Soldiers, from the summit of these pyramids, forty centuries contemplate you."

The battle

The Mamluk force, perhaps 25,000 men, combined superb feudal cavalry with fellahin infantry levies, but was deficient in modern artillery. Napoleon arranged his five divisions in hollow squares. Murad Bey's cavalry charged repeatedly and broke against the volleys and bayonets of the squares; French divisions then advanced and took the entrenched camp at Embabeh. Thousands of Mamluks were killed or drowned in the Nile, while French losses were under 300 men.

Aftermath

Cairo was occupied days later. The triumph was soon overshadowed by Nelson's destruction of the French fleet at the Battle of the Nile (1 August 1798), which stranded Napoleon's army in Egypt, where it remained until 1801.
