Traffic flow

In mathematics and transportation engineering, traffic flow is the study of interactions between travellers (including pedestrians, cyclists, drivers, and their vehicles) and infrastructure (including highways, roadways, tunnels, bridges, and traffic control devices), with the aim of understanding and developing an optimal transport network with efficient movement of traffic and minimal congestion problems.

Fundamental quantities

Traffic flow is described by three main variables: flow (q), the number of vehicles passing a point per unit time; density (k), the number of vehicles per unit length of road; and speed (v). They are related by the fundamental relation q = k × v. The fundamental diagram of traffic flow plots these relationships: flow rises with density up to a critical density at which capacity is reached, after which congestion causes both flow and speed to fall. Empirical models of the speed–density relation include the Greenshields model; macroscopic flow models include the Lighthill–Whitham–Richards (LWR) model, while microscopic models such as car-following and cellular automaton models describe individual vehicles.

Phenomena

Traffic flow theory explains phenomena such as shockwaves (the propagation of braking waves backward through a stream of traffic), phantom traffic jams that arise without any visible bottleneck, capacity drop at merges, and the benefits of ramp metering and variable speed limits in smoothing flow.
