Australian Antarctic Territory

Australian Antarctic Territory
Claimed: 1933 (transfer from United Kingdom)
Area: approx. 5,896,500 km²
Administered by: Australian Antarctic Division
Research stations: Mawson, Davis, Casey

The Australian Antarctic Territory (AAT) is a part of East Antarctica administered by Australia, consisting of all the islands and territory south of 60°S and between 45°E and 160°E, except for Adélie Land (between 136°E and 142°E), which divides the territory into Western AAT and Eastern AAT. It is the largest Antarctic claim of any nation, covering nearly 5.9 million square kilometres — about 42 per cent of the continent.

History

The claim originated in British exploration and was consolidated by the BANZARE voyages of 1929–1931 led by Sir Douglas Mawson, who proclaimed British sovereignty over the lands he visited. The United Kingdom transferred the territory to Australia by an Order in Council of 1933, and Australia's acceptance legislation brought the claim into effect in 1936. Under the Antarctic Treaty of 1959, to which Australia is an original signatory, territorial claims are held in abeyance: they are neither recognised nor disputed, and Australia's claim is recognised only by a few countries, including the United Kingdom, France, Norway and New Zealand.

Administration and stations

The territory is administered from Hobart, Tasmania, by the Australian Antarctic Division. Australia maintains three permanent research stations on the AAT coast — Mawson (established 1954, the oldest continuously operating station south of the Antarctic Circle), Davis (1957) and Casey (1969) — together with the inland skiway at Wilkins Runway and seasonal facilities. Typically fewer than a few hundred people, mostly scientists and support staff, are present in the territory at any time.

Environment

The AAT includes parts of the East Antarctic Ice Sheet, some of the coldest and windiest places on Earth; Mawson station is famous for its katabatic winds. The region supports research in glaciology, climate science, astronomy, and the study of wildlife such as emperor and Adélie penguins and Weddell seals.
