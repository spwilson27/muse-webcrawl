Video Cassette Recording

Video Cassette Recording (VCR) is an early domestic videotape format introduced by Philips in 1971. Sharing its initials with the generic term "videocassette recorder", the VCR format used square cassettes with coaxially mounted reels holding half-inch chromium dioxide tape, and offered recording times of one hour — long enough for a typical television programme, unlike competing short-format systems.

The first model, the N1500, included a built-in tuner and a timer, and sold in the United Kingdom for nearly £600. A longer-play version, marketed in North America and other markets, followed in 1977. Grundig developed a variant, VCR-LP and later the SVR (Super Video Recording) format, which extended recording time up to five hours on a single cassette.

Decline

Despite technical quality, the Philips format was displaced in the consumer market by the rival VHS system from JVC and Betamax from Sony, and Philips eventually abandoned VCR in favour of Video 2000, launched in 1979. The coaxial-reel cassette design remains a landmark in the history of home video as the first truly practical home videorecording system.
