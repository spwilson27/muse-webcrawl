2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal

The 2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal was a controversy surrounding the judging of the pairs figure skating competition at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, Utah. The Russian pair Elena Berezhnaya and Anton Sikharulidze were awarded the gold medal ahead of Canadians Jamie Salé and David Pelletier despite visible judging irregularities, amid allegations that the result had been arranged as part of a vote-trading deal.

A second medal ceremony was held at which Salé and Pelletier received gold medals.

The competition

In the free skate on 11 February 2002, Berezhnaya and Sikharulidze made a minor error on a jump landing, while Salé and Pelletier skated cleanly. Nevertheless the judges placed the Russians first by five votes to four, sparking outrage in the North American media and a formal complaint from Skate Canada. French judge Marie-Reine Le Gougne was alleged to have said she had been pressured by the president of the French skating federation, Didier Gailhaguet, to vote for the Russians as part of a bargain intended to benefit the French ice dance team.

Outcome

On 15 February the International Olympic Committee and the International Skating Union announced that the Canadians would be awarded duplicate gold medals, while the Russian pair retained theirs. Le Gougne and Gailhaguet were suspended from the sport for three years and barred from the 2006 Winter Olympics. Later allegations linked the affair to organised crime figure Alimzhan Tokhtakhounov, who was charged in the United States with conspiring to fix the pairs and ice dance events, though he was never tried there on those charges.

Reform

The scandal destroyed confidence in the traditional 6.0 judging system and led directly to its replacement by the ISU Judging System, introduced from the 2004–05 season, with anonymous judging at the time and detailed component scores intended to make results more transparent and less susceptible to bloc voting.
