The Ireland national rugby union team represents the island of Ireland — both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland — in men's international rugby union. The team is governed by the Irish Rugby Football Union and plays its home matches at the Aviva Stadium in Dublin.

History

Ireland played its first international in 1875, losing to England. The team competes annually in the Six Nations Championship, which it has won outright on multiple occasions, achieving Grand Slams in 1948, 2009, 2018, and 2023. Ireland contests the Rugby World Cup every four years; despite frequently entering as one of the world's top-ranked sides, the team has famously never progressed beyond the quarter-finals.

Playing style and identity

Uniquely, the team draws players from across the political divide on the island, and uses the specially commissioned anthem "Ireland's Call" alongside (in the Republic) the national anthem at home games. Famous Irish players include Brian O'Driscoll, widely regarded as one of the greatest centres in the sport's history, fly-halves Ronan O'Gara and Johnny Sexton, and lock Paul O'Connell.

Honours

  • Six Nations Championship titles, including Grand Slams in 1948, 2009, 2018, and 2023
  • Triple Crowns against the other Home Nations
  • Victories over the All Blacks, first achieved in Chicago in 2016
