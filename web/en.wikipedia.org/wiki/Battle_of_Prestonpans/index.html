The Battle of Prestonpans, also known as the Battle of Gladsmuir, was fought on 21 September 1745 near Prestonpans in East Lothian. It was the first significant engagement of the Jacobite rising of 1745 and ended in a stunning victory for the Jacobite army of Charles Edward Stuart over the government force of Sir John Cope.

Background

Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") landed in Scotland in July 1745 and raised the Jacobite standard at Glenfinnan. The government commander in Scotland, Sir John Cope, marched north but failed to engage, leaving Edinburgh to fall to the Jacobites. Cope then shipped his army to Dunbar and advanced west to meet the Jacobites on ground east of the capital.

The battle

Cope's army stood behind a marsh and ditch system it believed unassailable. During the night, guided by a local sympathiser through the Riggonhead defile, the Jacobites crossed the bog and deployed undetected on the government's flank. At dawn the Highlanders charged. The government artillery fired briefly before its gunners fled; the inexperienced government dragoons panicked and rode off, and the infantry, overwhelmed by the ferocity of the Highland charge, broke within minutes. The battle lasted barely fifteen minutes. Colonel James Gardiner was mortally wounded fighting near his own house at Bankton.

Aftermath

Government losses were some 300–500 killed and around 1,400–1,700 captured; Jacobite casualties were fewer than 100. The victory gave Charles control of Scotland and enormous prestige, encouraging the invasion of England that followed in November — the advance that reached Derby before the fateful decision to retreat. Cope was exonerated by a court of enquiry, which blamed the conduct of his troops, and the rout was mocked in the Jacobite song "Hey, Johnnie Cope, Are Ye Waking Yet?"
