Europa (consort of Zeus)

In Greek mythology, Europa was a Phoenician princess, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre, whose abduction by Zeus in the form of a white bull gave the continent of Europe its name.

Myth

According to the best-known version, Zeus, enamoured of Europa, transformed himself into a tame white bull and mingled with her father's herds near the shore where she gathered flowers with her companions. Europa, charmed by the bull's gentleness, climbed onto its back; Zeus immediately ran into the sea and swam with her to the island of Crete. There he revealed his true identity, and Europa became the first queen of Crete.

Offspring and legacy

By Zeus, Europa bore three sons: Minos, Rhadamanthus and Sarpedon. Minos and Rhadamanthus became judges of the dead in the underworld. Her brother Cadmus was sent to search for her and, failing to find her, founded Thebes. The tale is told by Ovid in the Metamorphoses and alluded to in Homer's Iliad. Europa appears on the Greek two-euro coin, and the myth has been a frequent subject of European painting from Titian to Rembrandt.
