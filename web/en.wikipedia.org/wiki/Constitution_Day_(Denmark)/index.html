Date: 5 June
Danish: Grundlovsdag

Constitution Day (Denmark)

Constitution Day (Danish: Grundlovsdag) is a public holiday in Denmark celebrated on 5 June to commemorate the signing of the Danish constitution on 5 June 1849 by King Frederick VII, which ended the absolute monarchy and established Denmark as a constitutional monarchy. The revised constitutions of 1915 and 1953 were also signed on 5 June.

Celebrations

The day is marked by political speeches and open-air meetings organized by political parties, trade unions, and local associations, a tradition associated with the statesman Steffen Møller. Because 5 June is also Father's Day in Denmark, and because the holiday falls near the summer solstice, celebrations are typically modest. Shops close at noon or remain closed under Danish closing-hours rules, though the day is not a full statutory holiday for all employees.

History

Before 1849, Constitution Day demonstrations were sometimes occasions of political agitation under absolutism. The 1915 constitution, signed on the same date, extended voting rights to women and servants, and the 1953 constitution abolished the upper house and permitted female succession to the throne.
