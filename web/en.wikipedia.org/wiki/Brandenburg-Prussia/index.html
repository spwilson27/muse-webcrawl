Brandenburg-Prussia (German: Brandenburg-Preußen) is the historiographic designation for the early modern realm of the Brandenburgian Hohenzollerns between 1618 and 1701. Based in the Margraviate of Brandenburg, the main branch of the Hohenzollerns established itself in the Duchy of Prussia in 1618 and moved its cast of rulers there over time.

History

The personal union began in 1618 when John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg, inherited the Duchy of Prussia from his father-in-law, Duke Albert Frederick, who died without male heirs. The two territories remained legally separate: Brandenburg was a fief of the Holy Roman Empire, while Ducal Prussia was a fief of the Kingdom of Poland.

Under the "Great Elector" Frederick William (reigned 1640–1688), the composite state was consolidated. By the Treaty of Wehlau (1657) and the Treaty of Oliva (1660), Poland renounced sovereignty over Ducal Prussia, making the Hohenzollerns fully sovereign there. Frederick William built a standing army, welcomed Huguenot refugees after the Edict of Potsdam (1685), and strengthened the central administration.

Kingdom of Prussia

His successor, Frederick III, used the sovereign status of Prussia to crown himself "King in Prussia" as Frederick I on 18 January 1701 in Königsberg, with the assent of Emperor Leopold I in return for military support in the War of the Spanish Succession. From that point the composite state is generally known as the Kingdom of Prussia.
