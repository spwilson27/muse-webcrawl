Scouting America is the flagship membership organisation of the Scout movement in the United States. It was founded in 1910 as the Boy Scouts of America (BSA) and adopted the name Scouting America in February 2025, reflecting the organisation's admission of girls and young women to all of its programmes. It is one of the largest youth organisations in the US and is a member of the World Organization of the Scout Movement.

History

The organisation was incorporated by Chicago publisher W. D. Boyce, inspired by the British Scouting movement of Robert Baden-Powell, and received a congressional charter in 1916. Membership peaked in the 1970s at more than four million youth. From the 1990s the BSA faced litigation and public controversy over its exclusion of gay members and leaders and of atheists, policies that were progressively reversed: gay youth were admitted from 2014, gay adults from 2015, transgender boys from 2017, and girls into Cub Scouts (2018) and the renamed Scouts BSA programme (2019). In 2020 the organisation filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the face of tens of thousands of historical sexual-abuse claims, emerging in 2023 with a settlement trust of more than $2 billion for survivors.

Programmes

Scouting America administers age-based programmes: Cub Scouts (ages 5–10), Scouts BSA (11–17), and the co-educational Venturing, Sea Scouts and Exploring programmes (14–20). Its highest rank is Eagle Scout, attained by roughly 4% of participants. The organisation operates four national high-adventure bases — Philmont Scout Ranch (New Mexico), Sea Base (Florida), Northern Tier (Minnesota/Ontario) and the Summit Bechtel Reserve (West Virginia), the last hosting the national jamboree. The Order of the Arrow is its national honour society.

Values

Members subscribe to the Scout Oath ("On my honor I will do my best to do my duty to God and my country...") and the twelve points of the Scout Law: trustworthy, loyal, helpful, friendly, courteous, kind, obedient, cheerful, thrifty, brave, clean and reverent.
