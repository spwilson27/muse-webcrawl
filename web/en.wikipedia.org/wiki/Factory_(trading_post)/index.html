Factory (trading post)

A factory in the context of early modern trade was an establishment where factors — commercial agents — transacted business on behalf of a merchant company in a foreign country. Such factories were central to Portuguese, Dutch, English and French overseas commerce from the fifteenth to the nineteenth century.

History

The term derives from the Portuguese feitoria. Portuguese factories were established along the coasts of Africa, India and Brazil, often fortified, serving as warehouses, markets and administrative centres. The model was adopted by the Dutch East India Company, the English East India Company and others.

Functions

Factories stored goods, negotiated with local rulers and merchants, collected products such as spices, textiles, gold and enslaved people, and provided lodging, defence and repair facilities for ships. Some, such as those in India and at Canton in China, evolved into major colonial settlements and ports.

Legacy

Many modern cities grew around former factories. The word later shifted in English to mean a place of manufacture during the Industrial Revolution.
