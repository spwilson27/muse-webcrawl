Mount Rainier

[Image: Mount Rainier looming over Tacoma and Puget Sound]
Mount Rainier looming over Tacoma and Puget Sound

Mount Rainier is a large active stratovolcano in the Cascade Range of the Pacific Northwest, in Washington state, about 95 kilometres southeast of Seattle. At 4,392 metres it is the most topographically prominent peak in the contiguous United States and the most heavily glaciated peak in that region, with 26 major glaciers.

Rainier is considered one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the world because of its height, frequent earthquakes, active hydrothermal system and the lahar (mudflow) hazard to the densely populated Puyallup valley below; an eruption's mudflows could reach Puget Sound suburbs. The mountain, called Tahoma or Tacoma by Indigenous peoples, is the centrepiece of Mount Rainier National Park (established 1899) and famously 'looms' over Seattle on clear days — locals say 'the mountain is out'.
