Yeshiva University is a private Orthodox Jewish research university in New York City, with four campuses in the city and one in Israel. Founded in 1886, it combines Jewish scholarship with secular academic study under the philosophy of Torah Umadda — the synthesis of Torah learning and worldly knowledge — and is the flagship institution of Modern Orthodoxy in the United States.

History

The university traces its origins to Yeshivat Etz Chaim, an elementary school founded on the Lower East Side in 1886, and to the Rabbi Isaac Elchanan Theological Seminary (RIETS), established in 1897 as a rabbinical school. The institutions merged in 1915 under Bernard Revel, who became the combined institution's first president and in 1928 moved it to Washington Heights, where Yeshiva College — the undergraduate liberal arts college for men — opened the same year. Stern College for Women followed in 1954, and graduate and professional schools in law (Cardozo), medicine (Albert Einstein College of Medicine, later separated as an independent institution), psychology (Ferkauf), social work (Wurzweiler), and Jewish studies (Revel) were added over the following decades.

Academics and student life

Undergraduates in the men's and women's colleges pursue a dual curriculum of secular majors and Jewish studies. The university sponsors the Yeshiva University Museum and fields intercollegiate athletic teams known as the Maccabees, whose men's basketball team drew national attention with a long winning streak in 2021. Notable features of campus life include daily Talmud study and a year of study in Israel undertaken by most entering students.
