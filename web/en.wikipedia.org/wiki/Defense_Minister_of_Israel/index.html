The Defense Minister of Israel is the political head of the Israeli Ministry of Defense and one of the most senior positions in the Israeli cabinet, second in importance only to the Prime Minister. The minister is the civilian authority over the Israel Defense Forces, although the Prime Minister is formally the supreme commander, and works alongside the Chief of the General Staff, the IDF's professional head.

History

The post was created with the State of Israel in 1948, and the first minister was Prime Minister David Ben-Gurion, who held both portfolios for most of his tenure — a pattern repeated by several later premiers, including Levi Eshkol, Menachem Begin, Yitzhak Rabin, Ehud Barak and Benjamin Netanyahu. The ministry, headquartered at HaKirya in Tel Aviv, directs defence policy, military procurement and planning, supervision of the occupied territories through COGAT, and Israel's large defence industry.

Long-serving and notable ministers include Moshe Dayan (1967–1974), who resigned after the Yom Kippur War; Shimon Peres; Ezer Weizman; Ariel Sharon, who resigned during the Lebanon War after the Kahan Commission report; Yitzhak Rabin, who returned to the post as Prime Minister in 1992; and Yoav Gallant, dismissed in November 2024 during the Gaza war and succeeded by Israel Katz.
