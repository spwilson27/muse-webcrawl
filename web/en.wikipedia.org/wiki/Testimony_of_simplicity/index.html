Testimony of simplicity

The testimony of simplicity is a shorthand description of the behaviour most commonly associated with the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers). It forms part of the Quaker testimonies, alongside the testimonies of peace, integrity, equality and community, which together express the outward, practical expression of Quaker faith.

A plain Quaker meeting room, reflecting the testimony of simplicity

Meaning

For Quakers, simplicity is not primarily about frugality or plain dress for its own sake. Rather, it is understood as a spiritual discipline: by keeping one's material life uncluttered, a person may be better able to discern and follow the leading of the Inner Light. Simplicity is closely related to the Quaker peace testimony, because competition for wealth and status is seen as a source of conflict, and to the testimony of equality, because ostentatious display erects barriers between people.

Historical practice

Historically, Friends expressed simplicity through plain dress (avoiding fashionable ornament, bright dyes and elaborate fabrics), plain speech (using "thee" and "thou" to all people regardless of rank, and refusing titles of honour), and simplicity in worship, gravestones and architecture. Meeting houses were built without steeples, pulpits or decoration, so that nothing would distract from silent worship.

Modern interpretation

Contemporary Quakers tend to interpret the testimony less as a set of rules and more as a set of questions: Is my life crowded with things that do not matter? Do my possessions own me? Am I honest about the environmental and social costs of my consumption? Many Friends today connect simplicity with sustainability and care for the Earth.
