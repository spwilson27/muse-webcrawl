FIS Ski Flying World Championships

The FIS Ski Flying World Championships is a biennial ski flying competition arranged by the International Ski Federation (FIS) since 1972. The championships are contested on the world's largest ski jumping hills, ski flying hills with a construction point of 185 metres or more, and crown world champions in individual and (since 2004) team events.

History

The first Ski Flying World Championships were held in 1972 at Planica, then in Yugoslavia, where Switzerland's Walter Steiner became the first world champion. The event has since rotated among the handful of certified ski flying venues: Planica (Slovenia), Oberstdorf (Germany), Vikersund (Norway), Harrachov (Czech Republic), Tauplitz/Bad Mitterndorf (Austria), and Ironwood (United States, which hosted in 1970 as a precursor "international ski flying week" and never again after the championship era relocated permanently to Europe).

The championships are held every two years, in even-numbered years alternating with the Winter Olympics cycle. The individual competition originally took place over two days with four jumps (later reduced formats have been used), and a team event for national teams of four was added at Planica in 2004, won by Norway.

Venues

  • Letalnica bratov Gorišek, Planica, Slovenia — the most frequent host
  • Heini-Klopfer-Skiflugschanze, Oberstdorf, Germany
  • Vikersundbakken, Vikersund, Norway — site of the current world record distance
  • Kulm, Tauplitz/Bad Mitterndorf, Austria
  • Čerťák, Harrachov, Czech Republic

Champions and records

Successful ski flying nations include Norway, Austria, Germany, Slovenia, Finland, and Japan. The ski flying world record, recognised by the FIS, has been pushed steadily outward at Vikersund and Planica; in March 2015 Anders Fannemel jumped 251.5 metres at Vikersund, and in March 2017 Robert Johansson reached 252 metres at the same hill, while Stefan Kraft set a mark of 253.5 metres there the same weekend. Domen Prevc extended the record to 254.5 metres at Planica in March 2025.

Weather and wind play an outsized role in ski flying, and championship programmes have frequently been shortened or cancelled in part — the 2022 event in Vikersund saw Karl Geiger defend his title, and in 2024 at Tauplitz, Stefan Kraft won the individual gold. The 2020 championships in Planica were postponed by the COVID-19 pandemic from March to December, an unusual winter-season start.
