Trinomial nomenclature

Trinomial nomenclature is a system of naming in taxonomy, particularly in zoology and botany, in which the scientific name of an organism consists of three parts: the genus, the specific epithet, and a third epithet denoting a rank below species — most commonly a subspecies in zoology or a variety or form in botany.

Zoology

In zoological nomenclature, governed by the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN), the trinomial name of a subspecies combines the genus name, the specific name, and the subspecific name. For example, the Bengal tiger is Panthera tigris tigris, and the Siberian tiger is Panthera tigris altaica. The first (nominotypical) subspecies repeats the specific epithet. No other ranks below species are recognized in the Code, so infraspecific names below the subspecies level have no formal standing.

Botany

In botanical nomenclature, governed by the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants, ranks below species include subspecies (subsp.), variety (var.), subvariety (subvar.), form (f.), and subform (subf.). A trinomial such as Brassica oleracea var. capitata (cabbage) identifies a variety within a species. Unlike zoology, botany routinely uses ranks lower than subspecies.
