Hückel's rule

Hückel's rule, proposed by the German chemist Erich Hückel in 1931, predicts whether a planar, cyclic, fully conjugated molecule will exhibit aromaticity: such a molecule is aromatic when its π-electron count equals 4n + 2 for a non-negative integer n (0, 1, 2, …).

Examples

Benzene, with 6 π electrons (n = 1), is the canonical aromatic compound. The cyclopentadienyl anion (6 π electrons) and the tropylium cation (6 π electrons) also satisfy the rule, whereas cyclobutadiene, with 4 π electrons, is antiaromatic and highly reactive.

Criteria

For the rule to apply, the molecule must be cyclic, planar (or nearly so), and conjugated around the whole ring, with a p orbital on every ring atom. Hückel's rule arises from the pattern of energy levels in his molecular orbital treatment of cyclic π systems.
