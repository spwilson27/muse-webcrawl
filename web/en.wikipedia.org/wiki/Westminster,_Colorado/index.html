Westminster is a home rule municipality in Adams and Jefferson counties in the U.S. state of Colorado, a northwestern suburb of Denver. With a population of about 116,000 at the 2020 census, it is one of the larger cities in the Denver metropolitan area.

History

Settlement in the area began during the Pike's Peak gold rush era; the community was first known as DeSpain Junction and later Harris Park, and was renamed Westminster in 1891 after Westminster Castle, a local landmark built as a Presbyterian university. The city incorporated in 1911 and grew rapidly as a post-war suburb.

Geography and landmarks

Westminster offers notable views of the Front Range and is known for its extensive trail and open-space system. The Westminster Castle (the "Pillar of Fire"), a red sandstone building on the National Register of Historic Places, remains a local symbol.
