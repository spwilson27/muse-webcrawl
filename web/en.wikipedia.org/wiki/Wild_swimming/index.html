Wild swimming

Wild swimming is swimming outdoors in natural waters — rivers, lakes, lochs, tarns and the sea — outside of pools and designated lidos. The movement grew in Britain in the 2000s, popularised by Roger Deakin's Waterlog (1999) and groups such as the Outdoor Swimming Society.

Practice

Swimmers range from brief cold-water dippers to distance and winter ice swimmers. Claimed benefits include mood elevation and cold adaptation; risks include cold-water shock, currents, weirs, poor water quality and hypothermia, so acclimatisation, exit plans and never swimming alone are standard advice.

Access

In England and Wales, access to inland waters is legally limited; campaigns sought a right to swim akin to Scotland's outdoor access code. Water-quality concerns about sewage discharges made wild swimming a prominent environmental-political topic in the 2020s.
