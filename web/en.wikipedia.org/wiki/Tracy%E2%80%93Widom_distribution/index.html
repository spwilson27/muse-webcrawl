Tracy–Widom distribution

The Tracy–Widom distribution is a family of probability distributions in random matrix theory describing the fluctuations of the largest eigenvalue of a random Hermitian matrix. It was introduced by Craig Tracy and Harold Widom in the 1990s, and plays a role analogous to the normal distribution in classical statistics: it is the universal limiting law for the extreme eigenvalues of large random matrices.

Definition

For a large random matrix from the Gaussian unitary ensemble, the largest eigenvalue concentrates near the edge of the semicircle law, with fluctuations on a scale of N−2/3. Tracy and Widom showed that the rescaled limiting distribution can be expressed through the Hastings–McLeod solution of the Painlevé II equation, q″(s) = s q(s) + 2 q(s)3: the distribution function is the Fredholm determinant F2(s) = exp(−∫s∞ (x−s) q(x)2 dx).

Applications

The distribution appears far beyond random matrices: in the statistics of the longest increasing subsequence of a random permutation, in growth processes of the KPZ universality class (such as the corner growth model), in certain queuing and directed polymer problems, and in the analysis of large data matrices, where the Tracy–Widom law for sample covariance matrices underpins tests for the number of significant principal components.
