Shepparton is a city located on the floodplain of the Goulburn River in northern Victoria, Australia. It forms part of the Greater Shepparton area together with the neighbouring town of Mooroopna, and is a major regional centre for agriculture and food processing in the Goulburn Valley.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Yorta Yorta and Bangerang peoples. European settlement began in the 1840s around a river crossing point; the town took its name from settler Sherbourne Sheppard. The opening of irrigation schemes in the late 19th and early 20th centuries transformed the district into one of Australia's most productive fruit-growing regions.

Economy

Shepparton is renowned for fruit growing — peaches, pears, apples and tomatoes — supported by extensive channel irrigation from the Goulburn River. The SPC cannery, established in the early 20th century, became an iconic Australian food brand based in the city. Dairy farming, manufacturing and health and education services are also significant employers.

Attractions

Local features include Victoria Park Lake, the Shepparton Art Museum (SAM), and the colourful Moooving Art exhibition of painted fibreglass cows celebrating the region's dairy industry. The city is also a gateway to the Murray River to the north.
