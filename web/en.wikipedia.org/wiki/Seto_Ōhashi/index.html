Seto Ōhashi

Great Seto Bridge
CrossesSeto Inland Sea
LocaleKurashiki, Okayama and Sakaide, Kagawa, Japan
Total length13.1 km (8.1 mi)
OpenedApril 10, 1988

The Seto Ōhashi, officially the Great Seto Bridge (Japanese: 瀬戸大橋), is a series of double-deck bridges connecting Okayama and Kagawa prefectures in Japan across the Seto Inland Sea. It is one of the three routes of the Honshū–Shikoku Bridge Project linking the islands of Honshū and Shikoku.

Structure

The crossing is not a single bridge but a sequence of six bridges — including cable-stayed, suspension and truss spans — hopping between five small islands in the sea, with a total length of about 13.1 kilometres. The upper deck carries four lanes of the Seto-Chūō Expressway, while the lower deck carries the JR Shikoku Honshi-Bisan railway line, with provision in the design for future Shinkansen tracks.

History

The need for a fixed link was dramatised by the 1955 sinking of the ferry Shiun Maru, in which 168 people, many of them schoolchildren, died. Construction of the bridge began in 1978 and took nearly ten years, at a cost of about US$7 billion. The bridge opened to traffic on April 10, 1988, providing the first road and rail connection between Honshū and Shikoku.
