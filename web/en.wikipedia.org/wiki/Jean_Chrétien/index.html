Joseph Jacques Jean Chrétien (born January 11, 1934) is a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the 20th prime minister of Canada from 1993 to 2003. A Liberal, he was first elected to the House of Commons in 1963 and held numerous cabinet posts under Lester B. Pearson and Pierre Trudeau, including minister of Indian affairs, president of the Treasury Board, and minister of finance, before serving briefly as deputy prime minister under John Turner in 1984. He won three consecutive majority governments as Liberal leader.

Early life and cabinet career

Chrétien was born in Shawinigan, Quebec, the 18th of 19 children of Wellie Chrétien and Marie Boisvert; partial facial paralysis from Bell's palsy in childhood left him with a characteristic crooked smile and speech pattern, which he turned into a political asset as "the little guy from Shawinigan". After studying law at Université Laval and practising in Shawinigan, he entered Parliament in 1963. As minister of Indian affairs and northern development (1968–1974) he co-authored the controversial 1969 White Paper on Indigenous policy, later withdrawn. He served as minister of finance (1977–1979), the first francophone to hold the post, and as minister of justice and attorney general (1980–1982) he was a central figure in the patriation of the Constitution and the negotiation of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, including the notwithstanding clause compromise known as the "Kitchen Accord".

Leadership and premiership

After losing the 1984 Liberal leadership to John Turner, Chrétien left Parliament in 1986, returning to win the leadership in 1990. He led the Liberals to a landslide in the 1993 election, with the "Red Book" platform, and won re-election in 1997 and 2000. With finance minister Paul Martin he eliminated the federal budget deficit through spending cuts and tax measures (the "Program Review"), recording a string of surpluses. His government introduced the Clarity Act (2000) following the near-loss of the 1995 Quebec referendum, kept Canada out of the 2003 invasion of Iraq, ratified the Kyoto Protocol, and legalised medicinal marijuana while beginning the process that led to same-sex marriage. His later years in office were marked by a bitter rivalry with Martin and the emerging sponsorship scandal.

Retirement

Chrétien stepped down as prime minister on December 12, 2003, succeeded by Paul Martin. He returned to legal practice, published memoirs (Straight from the Heart and My Years as Prime Minister), and remained an elder statesman of the Liberal Party, occasionally advising successors and campaigning in later elections.
