Dinitrogen tetroxide

FormulaN2O4
Molar mass92.011 g/mol
Melting point−11.2 °C
Boiling point21.69 °C
AppearanceColourless liquid; brown gas in equilibrium with NO2

Dinitrogen tetroxide, commonly known as nitrogen tetroxide (NTO), is the chemical compound N2O4. It is a useful reagent in chemical synthesis and is best known as a storable oxidiser in rocket propulsion, where it is paired with hydrazine-family fuels in hypergolic propellant combinations.

Structure and equilibrium

Dinitrogen tetroxide exists in equilibrium with nitrogen dioxide: N2O4 ⇌ 2 NO2. The colourless tetroxide dissociates increasingly into the reddish-brown dioxide as temperature rises, giving the liquid and gas their characteristic colour. The N–N bond in N2O4 is unusually long and weak.

Production

Industrially, nitrogen tetroxide is produced by the catalytic oxidation of ammonia (the Ostwald process), in which ammonia is oxidised to nitric oxide, then to nitrogen dioxide, which dimerises on cooling and is absorbed or condensed as N2O4.

Applications

NTO has been used as the oxidiser in many launch vehicles and spacecraft propulsion systems, including the Titan rockets, the Space Shuttle's orbital manoeuvring system, and numerous satellites, usually combined with monomethylhydrazine or unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine. It is highly toxic and corrosive, reacting with water to form nitric and nitrous acids.
