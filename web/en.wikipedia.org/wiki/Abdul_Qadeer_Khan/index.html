Abdul Qadeer Khan (Urdu: عبد القدیر خان; 1 April 1936 – 10 October 2021), usually known as A. Q. Khan, was a Pakistani nuclear physicist and metallurgical engineer who founded Pakistan's uranium enrichment programme and the Khan Research Laboratories at Kahuta, which produced the fissile material for Pakistan's first nuclear weapons tests in 1998. He later confessed to running an international network that sold centrifuge technology to Iran, Libya and North Korea.

Early life and career in Europe

Khan was born in Bhopal and emigrated to Pakistan in 1952. He studied metallurgy in Karachi and at universities in Berlin and Delft, earning a doctorate in Leuven in 1972. Working in the Netherlands for a subcontractor of the URENCO enrichment consortium, he had access to designs for gas centrifuges; after India's 1974 nuclear test he offered his services to Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto and returned to Pakistan in 1975, bringing centrifuge knowledge with him. The Netherlands later convicted him in absentia of espionage (the conviction was overturned on a technicality).

Kahuta and the bomb

In 1976 Khan took charge of the Engineering Research Laboratories at Kahuta (renamed Khan Research Laboratories in 1981), which mastered uranium enrichment by the centrifuge route while the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission pursued plutonium. Kahuta's work underpinned the devices tested at Chagai on 28 May 1998, after which Khan was celebrated in Pakistan as a national hero, the "father of the Pakistani bomb".

Proliferation network and fall

From the late 1980s Khan's network supplied centrifuge designs, components and expertise abroad. After Libya's 2003 disclosure exposed the network, Khan confessed on national television on 4 February 2004, accepting sole responsibility; President Pervez Musharraf pardoned him the next day but he remained under house arrest until a court eased the restrictions in 2009. He died in Islamabad in October 2021 of COVID-19 complications and received a state funeral.
