The California Community Colleges is a postsecondary education system in the US state of California. The system includes 116 colleges organised into 73 districts, serving roughly two million students, and is the largest system of higher education in the United States and one of the largest in the world. Together with the University of California and the California State University, it forms California's three-tier public higher education system defined by the 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education.

Mission

Under the Master Plan, the community colleges were charged with providing open-access education: transfer preparation for the state's universities, vocational and career-technical education, remedial and basic-skills instruction, and lifelong learning, with admission open to any adult capable of profiting from instruction. Most colleges also offer associate degrees and certificates, and a growing number offer bachelor's degrees in specific applied fields.

History

The system grew from legislation in 1907 authorising high schools to offer postgraduate courses, with Fresno City College (founded 1910) among the oldest. The 1960 Master Plan confirmed the colleges as the broad-access tier of California higher education. Tuition remains comparatively low, and for many students the colleges are the principal route into the UC and CSU systems through transfer programmes such as the Associate Degree for Transfer.
