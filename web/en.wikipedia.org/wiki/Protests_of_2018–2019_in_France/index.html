Protests of 2018–2019 in France

The protests of 2018–2019 in France were a series of protest movements that swept France beginning in late 2018, most prominently the yellow vests movement (gilets jaunes), named after the high-visibility vests worn by participants. The protests began in November 2018 in opposition to a planned rise in fuel taxes, and broadened into a wide-ranging challenge to the presidency of Emmanuel Macron over the cost of living, fiscal fairness and political representation.

Background

The movement emerged from an online petition by Priscillia Ludosky against fuel prices and was amplified through social media without formal leadership, unions or parties. It drew much of its support from peri-urban and rural France, where car ownership is essential. On 17 November 2018, the first nationwide day of action ("Act I"), some 280,000 people blocked roads and roundabouts across the country.

Course of events

Weekly Saturday protests followed, numbered as successive "Acts". Demonstrations in Paris in early December 2018 saw the heaviest rioting in decades, with damage around the Arc de Triomphe and clashes with police. The government suspended and then cancelled the fuel-tax increase, and on 10 December Macron announced measures worth about €10 billion, including a €100 monthly rise for minimum-wage earners and tax-free overtime. In early 2019 the government launched a two-month "Great National Debate".

Demands and legacy

Demands ranged from higher purchasing power and the restoration of the wealth tax to institutional reforms such as the citizens' initiative referendum (RIC). Protest numbers dwindled through spring 2019, and the weekly acts faded by mid-2019, though smaller actions continued into the pension-reform strikes of late 2019. The period was marked by controversy over police use of crowd-control weapons, with numerous serious injuries reported among protesters and journalists. The movement is widely seen as a turning point of Macron's first term, reshaping French politics and protest culture.
