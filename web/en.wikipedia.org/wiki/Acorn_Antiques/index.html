Acorn Antiques

Acorn Antiques is a parody soap opera created by the British comedian Victoria Wood, first broadcast in 1985 as a recurring sketch in her series Victoria Wood: As Seen on TV. Set in an antiques shop in the fictional town of Manchesterford, the sketches affectionately spoofed low-budget television soaps — especially ITV's Crossroads — with wobbling sets, fluffed lines left uncorrected, melodramatic plotting, and visibly cheap production values.

Characters

Wood played the shop owner Boadicea "Bo" Ratcliffe; Julie Walters starred as the elderly, stooped tea-lady Mrs Overall, whose macaroons and deadpan asides ("soup's made with the last of the dripping, Miss Babs") made her one of British comedy's beloved characters. Celia Imrie played the glamorous Miss Babs, and Duncan Preston the shop assistant Mr Clifford. Rosie Collins and others completed the small cast, with storylines parodying soap conventions — secret wills, amnesia, long-lost relatives — deliberately resolved with absurd abruptness.

Musical

In 2005 Wood adapted the sketch into Acorn Antiques: The Musical!, which premiered at the Theatre Royal Haymarket in London, directed by Trevor Nunn, with Walters and Imrie reprising their roles. The musical — half straight staging of the "soap", half a show about the company staging it — received Olivier Award nominations, and Julie Walters won the Olivier Award for Best Supporting Performance in a Musical. A touring version, directed by Wood, followed.

Legacy

Mrs Overall was later named among Britain's favorite comedy characters, and the sketches are frequently cited among the greatest British television comedy of the 1980s. A spoof documentary and DVD releases collected the original episodes, and the phrase "Acorn Antiques" remains shorthand in British media criticism for threadbare television production.
