The Loxahatchee River (Seminole for "river of turtles") is a 7.6-mile-long (12.2 km) river near the southeast coast of Florida. It flows from Riverbend Park in Palm Beach County eastward through Jonathan Dickinson State Park and empties into the Atlantic Ocean at Jupiter Inlet. In 1985 it was designated a National Wild and Scenic River, the first river in Florida to receive that designation.

Course and ecology

The river's northwest fork, the federally designated wild and scenic segment, winds through a subtropical cypress swamp lined with pond cypress, sabal palms and old-growth forest. Freshwater, brackish and saltwater reaches support manatees, alligators, otters, ospreys and a wide variety of fish. The portion within Jonathan Dickinson State Park is a popular canoeing and kayaking route, noted for the low cypress canopy that arches over the narrow channel.

History

The name derives from the Seminole words lowchow (turtle) and hatchee (river). The area saw action during the Second Seminole War; the Battle of Loxahatchee was fought near the river in January 1838. The river is also associated with Trapper Nelson, the "Wildman of the Loxahatchee," who lived along its banks from the 1930s until his death in 1968 and whose homestead is preserved in the state park.

Recreation

Canoe and kayak rentals at Riverbend Park and within Jonathan Dickinson State Park allow paddlers to travel the full wild and scenic segment. The river is tidal near its mouth, and its lower reaches are used by recreational boaters traveling between the Intracoastal Waterway and Jupiter Inlet.
