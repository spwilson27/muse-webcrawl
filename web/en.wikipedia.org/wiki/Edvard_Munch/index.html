Edvard Munch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Edvard Munch in old age: gaunt face, grey beard, dark clothes, standing among large canvases in his studio

Edvard Munch (12 December 1863 – 23 January 1944) was a Norwegian Symbolist painter, printmaker and an important forerunner of Expressionism. His childhood was overshadowed by illness, bereavement and the dread of inheriting the mental illness that ran in his family; themes of anxiety, sickness, death and troubled love pervade his work.

His best-known painting, The Scream (1893), with its figure clutching its face on a bridge under a blood-red sky, has become an icon of modern art expressing universal anxiety. Other key works include Madonna, The Sun, Death in the Sickroom and the monumental Frieze of Life series. One of the four versions of The Scream sold at auction in 2012 for nearly $120 million, then a record for a painting.
