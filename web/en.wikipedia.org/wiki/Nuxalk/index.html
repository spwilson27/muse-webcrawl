Nuxalk

The Nuxalk (pronounced roughly "nu-halk") are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast whose homeland centres on the Bella Coola Valley and the inlets of the central coast of present-day British Columbia, Canada. They were historically known as the Bella Coola. The Nuxalk are notable as a linguistic isolate among the peoples of the coast: their language is Salishan, surrounded on all sides by Wakashan and Athabaskan languages.

Territory and society

Traditional Nuxalk territory encompassed the Dean Channel, Burke Channel, and the Bella Coola (North Bentinck Arm) inlet, with permanent villages of large cedar plank houses along the rivers. Salmon — especially eulachon, rendered for its oil — sustained dense settlement. Society was ranked, with hereditary chiefs (smayusta), and ceremonial life included the Sisyawk and Alkw winter dances, featuring elaborate masks representing supernatural beings.

History

In 1793 Alexander Mackenzie reached the Pacific overland through Nuxalk territory, guided by Nuxalk people; his arrival is commemorated as the first recorded crossing of North America north of Mexico. Smallpox epidemics in the 19th century devastated the population, and surviving villages amalgamated at the present site of Bella Coola (Q'umk'uts'). The Nuxalk Nation today governs from Bella Coola and maintains stewardship of its territory, including the defence of the Great Bear Rainforest region.
