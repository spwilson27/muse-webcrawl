The Gotthard railway (German: Gotthardbahn) is a Swiss transalpine railway line running from Immensee in central Switzerland to Chiasso on the Italian border, passing beneath the Saint-Gotthard Massif through the original Gotthard Tunnel. Opened in 1882, it is famous for its spiral loops and dramatic mountain scenery, and remains a major European freight and passenger corridor.

History

The line was built by the Gotthard Railway Company under the direction of engineer Louis Favre, with financial support from Switzerland, Germany and Italy. Its centrepiece, the 15 km Gotthard Tunnel between Göschenen and Airolo, was the longest railway tunnel in the world when it opened in 1882; some 200 workers died during construction, and Favre himself died in the tunnel. The line was nationalised into the Swiss Federal Railways in 1909 and electrified in the 1920s.

Route

To gain height on the northern ramp, the line uses spiral tunnels near Wassen, where the village church can be seen three times from different levels. South of the summit tunnel it descends the Leventina valley with further spirals at Giornico.

Gotthard Base Tunnel

On 1 June 2016 the 57 km Gotthard Base Tunnel, the world's longest railway tunnel, opened as part of the AlpTransit (NRLA) project, largely replacing the old mountain route for fast passenger and heavy freight trains. The original line continues to serve regional and tourist traffic.
