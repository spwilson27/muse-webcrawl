Master sergeant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Master sergeant is a senior non-commissioned officer rank in several armed forces. In the United States military it is used in the Army, Air Force, Marine Corps and Space Force, though the grade and duties differ by service.

United States

In the US Air Force and Space Force, master sergeant (MSgt) is the pay grade E-7, the first of the senior non-commissioned officer grades, above technical sergeant and below senior master sergeant. Air Force master sergeants are expected to be technical experts and supervisors; those appointed to positions of special leadership responsibility may serve as "first sergeants", wearing a diamond device in their insignia. In the US Army, master sergeant is pay grade E-8, the technical-expert counterpart of the first sergeant at the same grade, wearing three chevrons above three rockers with a lozenge removed from the first sergeant's insignia. In the US Marine Corps, master sergeant is likewise grade E-8, on the technical staff-noncommissioned-officer track parallel to the first sergeant and leading to master gunnery sergeant; master sergeants provide technical leadership in their military occupational specialty rather than holding command responsibility.

Other countries

The rank, or close equivalents, exists in the Philippines (the title survived from the US colonial period), Singapore and Israel's defence forces (as rav samal, though structured differently), and in numerous air forces modelled on British or American practice.

History

The rank entered the US Army in 1920 when Congress consolidated the enlisted grade structure, drawing on earlier quartermaster and technical specialist sergeants. The Air Force adopted it as an independent service after 1947; its grade was raised from E-6 to E-7 in 1958 when the new supergrades E-8 and E-9 were created.
