Wadi Suq culture

The Wadi Suq culture defines human settlement in the United Arab Emirates and Oman in the period from 2,000 to 1,300 BCE. It takes its name from a wadi, or seasonal watercourse, west of Sohar in Oman and follows on from the Umm al-Nar culture. Although archaeologists have traditionally tended to view the differences in human settlements and burials between the Umm Al Nar and Wadi Suq periods as the result of major external disruption (climate change, the collapse of trade or threat of war), contemporary opinion has moved towards a gradual change in society.

Burials

Evidence of increased mobility among the population points to a trend towards a more nomadic existence, although Wadi Suq period graves are important in the UAE. Particularly located at Jebel Buhais, Jebel Emalah, and Bidaa bint Saud, some fifty burial sites are known throughout the Emirates. Long chambered tombs are characteristic of the period, replacing the circular collective tombs of Umm al-Nar.

Artefacts

Wadi Suq era metalwork displays greater levels of sophistication, including leafy-shaped daggers with ornate handles, decorated vessels and small, fine swords. Pottery becomes less sophisticated and chlorite (soapstone) vessels decline in frequency, replaced by metal and other wares.
