Aileron

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aileron on the trailing edge of a wing

An aileron (French for "little wing" or "fin") is a hinged flight control surface, usually forming part of the trailing edge of each wing of a fixed-wing aircraft. Ailerons are used in pairs to control the aircraft in roll (rotation around the longitudinal axis): when the pilot moves the control stick or yoke sideways, one aileron is deflected upward and the other downward, increasing lift on one wing and decreasing it on the other so that the aircraft banks.

Operation

Deflecting an aileron changes the camber of that section of the wing. The downward-deflected aileron increases lift on its wing, while the upward-deflected aileron decreases lift on the opposite wing, producing a rolling moment. Aileron deflection also creates a yawing moment opposite to the direction of the turn, known as adverse yaw, which is counteracted with the rudder or by differential aileron geometry, in which the upward-moving aileron travels farther than the downward-moving one, adding drag on the rising wing. Frise-type ailerons achieve a similar drag increase mechanically.

History

Lateral control was the last of the three axes to be satisfactorily solved by early aviators. The Wright brothers used wing-warping on their Flyer of 1903 and fought a famous patent battle over lateral control. Ailerons were described as early as 1868 in a patent by Matthew Piers Watt Boulton, and were used practically by designers including Robert Esnault-Pelterie and Henri Farman; Glenn Curtiss's use of ailerons in place of wing-warping led to the landmark patent litigation with the Wrights.

Related surfaces

On large or fast aircraft, ailerons may be supplemented or combined with spoilers (spoilerons), and combined with flaps as flaperons or with elevators as elevons on delta-winged aircraft. At high speeds, aileron reversal can occur when the control input twists the wing enough to negate the intended effect, which is why large jets use separate inboard and outboard ailerons and rely more on spoilers at speed.
