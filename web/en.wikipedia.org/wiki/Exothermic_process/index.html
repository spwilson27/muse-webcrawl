Exothermic process

An exothermic process is a thermodynamic process or reaction that releases energy from the system to its surroundings, usually in the form of heat, but also in the form of light, electricity, or sound. The term comes from the Greek exo ("outwards") and thermē ("heat").

In chemistry, an exothermic reaction has a negative change in enthalpy (ΔH < 0): the products have less energy than the reactants, and the difference is released. Familiar examples include combustion, the thermite reaction, many oxidation reactions, respiration, and the setting of cement. The opposite is an endothermic process, which absorbs heat.

Exothermic processes are also important in physics, for example in nuclear fission and fusion, where mass-energy is released, and in phase transitions such as condensation and freezing.
