Volunteering

Volunteering is generally considered an altruistic activity in which an individual or group freely gives time and labour, often for community service. Many volunteers are specifically trained in the areas they work in, such as medicine, education or emergency rescue; others serve as needed without special training, as after a natural disaster. Volunteers typically work through non-profit organisations, charities, faith groups or public bodies, without payment — though some receive stipends, training or in-kind support.

Types

Categories include formal volunteering through organisations (charities, hospitals, sports clubs), informal helping of neighbours and community, skills-based and pro bono professional work, virtual volunteering online, volunteering abroad through programmes such as the Peace Corps and UN Volunteers, episodic and micro-volunteering, and corporate employee volunteering. Schools and courts sometimes require service hours ("voluntourism" and mandated service blur the edge of voluntariness and are debated).

Scale and benefits

The United Nations estimates around one billion people worldwide volunteer each year, contributing time with an economic value comparable to a major industry. Studies associate volunteering with improved mental and physical health, social connection and employability for the volunteer, alongside the direct benefits to recipients and organisations. Governments measure it through national surveys and social-capital statistics.

Motivations and challenges

Motives range from altruism and religious duty to career development, social belonging and personal experience after a life event. Organisations must recruit, screen, train and retain volunteers, manage safeguarding and insurance, and match roles to motivations. Debates include whether unpaid labour displaces paid jobs, the ethics of "voluntourism" in the developing world, and disparities in who can afford to volunteer.
