Johann de Kalb

Johann de Kalb (29 June 1721 – 19 August 1780) was a Franconian-born French military officer who served as a major general in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. He is remembered as a hero of the American Revolution and a martyr of the Battle of Camden.

Early career

Born Johann Kalb in Hüttendorf, in the Margraviate of Bayreuth, he served in the French army during the War of the Austrian Succession and the Seven Years' War, rising to lieutenant colonel. He secretly visited the American colonies in 1768 on a mission for the French foreign minister.

American Revolution

De Kalb returned to America in 1777 with the Marquis de Lafayette, and was commissioned a major general in the Continental Army. He served at Valley Forge and in the campaigns in the middle states.

Death at Camden

In 1780, de Kalb was second in command of the southern army under Horatio Gates. At the Battle of Camden in South Carolina on 16 August 1780, he was mortally wounded while rallying his troops, reportedly receiving eleven wounds. He died three days later. Congress voted him a monument, and numerous American towns and counties are named for him.
