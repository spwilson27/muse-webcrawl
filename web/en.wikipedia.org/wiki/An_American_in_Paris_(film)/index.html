An American in Paris is a 1951 American musical film inspired by the 1928 orchestral composition An American in Paris by George Gershwin. Starring Gene Kelly, Leslie Caron, Oscar Levant, Georges Guétary, and Nina Foch, the film is set in Paris and was directed by Vincente Minnelli from a script by Alan Jay Lerner. The music is by George Gershwin, with lyrics by his brother Ira, with additional music by Johnny Green and Saul Chaplin.

The story follows Jerry Mulligan, an exuberant American expatriate and aspiring painter living in Paris, who is "discovered" by an influential heiress interested in more than his art. Jerry in turn falls for Lise, a young French girl already engaged to a cabaret singer. The film climaxes with a celebrated 17-minute ballet sequence set to Gershwin's tone poem, featuring Kelly and Caron against sets designed in the styles of French painters such as Raoul Dufy, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Maurice Utrillo, Henri Rousseau, and Toulouse-Lautrec.

Plot

Jerry Mulligan (Gene Kelly) is a former American soldier who has remained in Paris after the Second World War to pursue painting. He shares a cramped apartment building with his friend Adam Cook (Oscar Levant), a struggling concert pianist. At a café, Jerry meets Milo Roberts (Nina Foch), a wealthy American heiress who offers to sponsor his art — and makes clear her romantic interest. Meanwhile Jerry glimpses Lise Bouvier (Leslie Caron), a young perfume-shop clerk, and falls in love with her, not knowing she is engaged to Henri Baurel (Georges Guétary), the cabaret singer who protected her family during the war. The romantic tangle resolves at a masked ball and in the extended closing ballet, a fantasy in which Jerry imagines winning Lise's heart before she returns to him in reality.

Music

The score draws heavily on the Gershwin songbook, including "'S Wonderful", "I Got Rhythm", "Embraceable You", "Love Is Here to Stay", and "I'll Build a Stairway to Paradise", alongside excerpts from the Concerto in F and the full tone poem An American in Paris that accompanies the final ballet.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial and critical success. At the 24th Academy Awards it won six Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Story and Screenplay, Best Art Direction (Color), Best Cinematography (Color), Best Costume Design (Color), and Best Scoring of a Musical Picture. Gene Kelly received an honorary Academy Award for his versatility as actor, singer, director, dancer, and choreographer. The film is frequently ranked among the greatest movie musicals ever made and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
