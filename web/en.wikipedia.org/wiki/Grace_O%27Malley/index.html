Grace O'Malley (Irish: Gráinne Mhaol Ní Mháille; c. 1530 – c. 1603), also known as "The Pirate Queen", was an Irish chieftain of the Ó Máille clan, a celebrated seafarer, trader and military leader in 16th-century Ireland. She commanded a fleet of ships operating from Clew Bay on Ireland's west coast and defied English attempts to extend their authority over Connacht.

Early life

O'Malley was born into the seafaring Ó Máille family, whose motto was "powerful by land and sea". She took control of the family fleet after her father's death and built a formidable maritime enterprise based on trade, fishing rights, and plunder of passing ships.

Meeting with Elizabeth I

In 1593, after her sons and half-brother were captured by the English governor of Connacht, Richard Bingham, O'Malley travelled to London and met Queen Elizabeth I at Greenwich. Speaking in Latin, she negotiated the release of her family and secured promises regarding her lands — a remarkable diplomatic encounter between two powerful women of the age.

Legacy

O'Malley remains a prominent figure in Irish folklore and popular history, celebrated in songs, plays and novels as a symbol of Irish resistance and female leadership.
