Tennessee Senate

Tennessee Senate
Type: Upper house of the Tennessee General Assembly
Seats: 33
Term length: 4 years
Meeting place: Tennessee State Capitol, Nashville

The Tennessee Senate is the upper house of the Tennessee General Assembly, the state legislature of Tennessee. It is composed of 33 members, each elected from a single-member district of roughly equal population, serving staggered four-year terms.

Leadership

The Senate's presiding officer is the Speaker of the Senate, who under the Tennessee Constitution also serves as Lieutenant Governor of Tennessee — making Tennessee one of the states in which the lieutenant governor is chosen by the legislature rather than elected statewide. The Speaker appoints committee chairs and members and exercises considerable influence over legislation. Day-to-day floor leadership is exercised by the majority and minority leaders chosen by the party caucuses.

Qualifications and functions

Senators must be at least 30 years old, U.S. citizens, residents of Tennessee for three years, and residents of their district's county for one year before election. The Senate shares lawmaking and budgetary powers with the House of Representatives. It sits as the court of impeachment for state officials charged by the House, and — unlike in many states — gubernatorial appointments in Tennessee generally do not require Senate confirmation except where specifically provided by law.

Composition

Since the early 2010s the Senate has had a Republican supermajority, reflecting the state's broader partisan realignment. Elections for odd-numbered districts occur in presidential election years and even-numbered districts in midterm years, owing to the staggered structure.
