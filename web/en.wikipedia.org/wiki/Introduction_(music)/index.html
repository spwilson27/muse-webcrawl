Introduction (music)

An introduction ("intro") is a passage or section which opens a movement or a separate piece of music. In popular music, this is often called an intro. The introduction establishes melodic, harmonic, or rhythmic material related to the main body of the piece, prepares its key and mood, and captures the listener's attention.

In classical music

Many symphonies and sonatas of the Classical period begin with a slow introduction before the main fast movement — famous examples include Haydn's "London" symphonies, Mozart's "Linz" and "Prague" symphonies, and Beethoven's Pathétique Sonata and several symphonies. Such introductions are often harmonically exploratory, lingering on dominant harmony to heighten expectation. Operatic overtures and preludes serve a related purpose at the scale of a whole work.

In popular music

Song intros may be instrumental hooks, riffs, or atmospheric passages before the first verse; in the streaming era, intros have tended to shorten so that vocals arrive sooner. Intros can also foreshadow the chorus melody or establish the beat in dance music for mixing by DJs.

Related forms

A brief introduction of a few bars attached to a song is sometimes called a "verse" in early popular-song terminology. Contrasting sections include the bridge, pre-chorus, and coda, which closes a piece as the introduction opens it.
