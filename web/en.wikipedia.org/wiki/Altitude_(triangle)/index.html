Altitude (triangle)

In geometry, an altitude of a triangle is a line segment through a vertex perpendicular to the line containing the opposite side (the base). The length of the segment is also called the altitude, and equals the distance from the vertex to its base. Every triangle has three altitudes.

Orthocenter

The three altitudes (extended as lines) are concurrent at a single point, the orthocenter of the triangle. The orthocenter lies inside the triangle for acute triangles, at the right-angle vertex for right triangles, and outside for obtuse triangles, in which case some altitudes fall outside the figure. The orthocenter, centroid and circumcenter are collinear on the Euler line.

Properties

The area of a triangle is half the product of a base and the corresponding altitude, A = ½bh. In a right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse divides it into segments whose geometric mean equals the altitude (the geometric mean theorem). The feet of the altitudes form the orthic triangle, and each altitude is related to the sides and circumradius R by formulas such as ha = bc / 2R.
