Vietnamese Martyrs

The Vietnamese Martyrs, also known as the Martyrs of Vietnam, or the Martyrs of Indochina, are saints of the Catholic Church who were canonized by Pope John Paul II on 19 June 1988. The group of 117 martyrs represents the many Christians who were killed in persecutions in Vietnam between 1745 and 1862 under the Nguyễn dynasty.

Background

Christianity was introduced to Vietnam by Portuguese, Spanish, and French missionaries in the 17th century and spread widely. Successive emperors, most notably Minh Mạng, Thiệu Trị, and Tự Đức, issued edicts against the foreign religion, which they viewed as a threat to Confucian order and imperial authority. Between the 17th and 19th centuries, estimates of the total number of Vietnamese Christians martyred range from 130,000 to 300,000.

The 117

The canonized group comprises 96 Vietnamese and 21 foreign missionaries: 11 Spaniards (mostly Dominicans) and 10 Frenchmen (mostly of the Paris Foreign Missions Society). They include bishops, priests, catechists, and laypeople — among them Andrew Dũng-Lạc, a Vietnamese priest executed in 1839, whose name the group is often associated with; their collective feast day is 24 November. Among them are notable figures such as Théophane Vénard, a French missionary executed in 1861.

Veneration

The canonization ceremony in St. Peter's Square was one of the largest of John Paul II's pontificate. The martyrs are venerated especially in Vietnam and in Vietnamese diaspora communities worldwide, with numerous parishes named "Vietnamese Martyrs" in the United States, Canada, France, and Australia.
