Critical linguistics

Critical linguistics is an approach to discourse analysis that examines how language encodes ideology, power relations, and social inequality. It emerged in the late 1970s at the University of East Anglia, in work by Roger Fowler, Gunther Kress, Robert Hodge, and Tony Trew, notably the book Language and Control (1979).

Approach

Drawing on Michael Halliday's systemic functional linguistics, critical linguistics analyses grammatical choices — such as transitivity, modality, nominalisation, and passivisation — in texts like news reports, arguing that these choices present particular versions of social reality as natural and inevitable.

Legacy

Critical linguistics laid the groundwork for critical discourse analysis (CDA), developed by scholars including Norman Fairclough and Teun van Dijk, while itself being criticised for over-interpreting texts and relying on the analyst's readings rather than audience reception.
