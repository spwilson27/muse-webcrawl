Red mud

Red mud, now more commonly referred to as bauxite residue, is an industrial waste product generated in the production of alumina (aluminium oxide) from bauxite ore using the Bayer process. It typically contains iron oxides — which give it its red colour — together with residual aluminium compounds, silica, titania and caustic soda.

Production and storage

Between roughly 1 and 1.5 tonnes of red mud are produced per tonne of alumina. Global production exceeds 150 million tonnes per year, and total stockpiles amount to several billion tonnes. The residue is strongly alkaline (pH 10–13) and is usually stored in impoundment ponds or dry stacks near refineries.

Hazards and uses

The failure of a red mud reservoir at Ajka, Hungary, in October 2010 released about one million cubic metres of caustic sludge, killing ten people and devastating nearby villages. Research into uses of red mud includes recovery of iron, titanium and rare-earth elements, and applications in cement, bricks, road base and soil remediation.
