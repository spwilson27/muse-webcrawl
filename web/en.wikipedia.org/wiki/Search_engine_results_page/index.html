Search engine results page

A search engine results page (SERP) is a webpage generated by a search engine in response to a user's query. The SERP displays a list of results, typically including organic search results, paid advertisements, and specialized "SERP features" such as featured snippets, knowledge panels, image packs, video carousels, local map packs, and "People also ask" boxes.

Components

Organic results

Organic results are listings the search engine's index and ranking algorithms deem most relevant to the query. Each result traditionally shows a title, a URL or breadcrumb path, and a descriptive snippet. Improving a page's position among organic results is the goal of search engine optimization (SEO).

Paid results

Advertisers bid on keywords through auction systems such as Google Ads; their text ads appear above, below, or beside organic results and are labeled as sponsored. Advertisers are charged on a pay-per-click basis.

SERP features

Modern SERPs include rich elements beyond the traditional "ten blue links": featured snippets that answer a query directly on the page, knowledge panels drawing on knowledge graphs, local packs for geographic queries, top stories, shopping results, and video or image previews. These features can satisfy queries without a click, giving rise to so-called "zero-click searches."

Variation

SERPs vary by user location, language, device, search history, and the engine's interpretation of intent (navigational, informational, or transactional). The same query may produce a different page for different users, a phenomenon known as personalization.

Importance in marketing

Because click-through rates fall steeply with ranking position, occupying prominent SERP real estate — in organic results, ads, or features — is central to digital marketing strategy and analytics.
