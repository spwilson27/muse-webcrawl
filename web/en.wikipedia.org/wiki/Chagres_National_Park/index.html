Chagres National Park

Chagres National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Chagres) is a protected area in Panama, spanning the provinces of Panamá and Colón. Created in 1985, it covers approximately 129,000 hectares (about 1,290 km² or 320,000 acres) of tropical rainforest, mountains, and the watershed of the Chagres River. The park protects the principal water source of the Panama Canal and supplies drinking water to Panama City and Colón.

Geography

The park lies between the Panama Canal watershed's eastern mountains, with elevations rising to about 1,007 metres at Cerro Jefe. It takes in most of the basin of the Chagres River and the artificial Alajuela Lake (Lake Madden), created in the 1930s to regulate water for the canal. The upper Chagres and its tributaries — including the Pequení, Boquerón, and San Juan rivers — drain some of the wettest terrain in the canal watershed, receiving up to 4,000 mm of rain annually.

Ecology

Chagres National Park protects lowland and premontane tropical moist forest. Its fauna includes jaguars, Baird's tapirs, white-lipped peccaries, harpy eagles, and more than 500 species of birds, making it an important birdwatching destination. The forests are essential to regulating streamflow, limiting sedimentation of the canal, and sequestering carbon.

Human presence

Within and around the park live campesino farmers and Indigenous Emberá communities, several of which — such as the villages along the upper Chagres reachable by dugout canoe — receive visitors for cultural and ecotourism activities. The historic Camino de Cruces trail, a colonial-era cobbled route used to carry goods and gold across the isthmus, passes through the park.

Conservation

The park was established primarily to safeguard the Panama Canal watershed: deforestation in the basin threatens the canal's water supply and increases siltation. Management is supported by Panamanian environmental authorities and conservation organisations such as the Fundación Chagres, which promotes reforestation and sustainable livelihoods for local communities.
