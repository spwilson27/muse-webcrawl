Sanford Meisner (August 31, 1905 – February 2, 1997) was an American actor and acting teacher who developed an approach to acting instruction that is now known as the Meisner technique. While with the Group Theatre, Meisner disagreed with Lee Strasberg's deep reliance on affective memory exercises; instead he developed a system rooted in improvisation, listening, and reacting truthfully in the moment, famously defining acting as "living truthfully under imaginary circumstances."

Life and career

Meisner was born in Brooklyn, New York, the eldest of four children of Jewish immigrants. He initially studied piano at the Damrosch Institute of Music before turning to acting. He joined the Theatre Guild and then, in 1931, became a founding member of the Group Theatre alongside Harold Clurman, Lee Strasberg, and Cheryl Crawford, appearing in productions including Awake and Sing! and Waiting for Lefty.

From 1935, Meisner taught at the Neighborhood Playhouse School of the Theatre in New York, becoming its head of acting and building one of the most influential acting programs in the United States. His signature exercise, "Repetition", trains actors to focus attention on their partner and respond spontaneously. His students included Robert Duvall, Grace Kelly, James Caan, Diane Keaton, Jeff Goldblum, and many others. Late in life he co-founded the Meisner/Carville School of Acting in Los Angeles and on the island of Bequia.

Legacy

The Meisner technique remains one of the most widely taught acting methods worldwide. Meisner's teaching was documented in the book Sanford Meisner on Acting (1987), compiled by Dennis Longwell from class recordings.
