Renfe, officially Renfe Operadora, is Spain's state-owned railway operating company. It runs most of the country's passenger trains — including the AVE high-speed network, among the largest in the world — and substantial freight traffic. The name derives from the initials of the Red Nacional de los Ferrocarriles Españoles (National Network of Spanish Railways), created in 1941 when the Franco government nationalised Spain's broad-gauge railway companies.

History

For decades RENFE held a total monopoly of Spanish rail transport, operating over a network largely built to the distinctive Iberian broad gauge (1,668 mm). In preparation for European liberalisation, the company was split on 1 January 2005: infrastructure passed to the new administrator Adif, while Renfe Operadora retained train operations. Since then, private competitors — Ouigo España and Iryo — have entered the high-speed market under open-access rules, the first such competition on a European high-speed network.

Services

Renfe's flagship is the AVE (Alta Velocidad Española), which began in 1992 between Madrid and Seville and now links most major Spanish cities at speeds up to 310 km/h; the low-cost brand Avlo launched in 2021. Alvia trains use variable-gauge axles to run on both high-speed and conventional lines. The company also operates regional Media Distancia trains and the Cercanías commuter networks of Madrid, Barcelona, and other cities, and since 2023 has run cross-border services into France.
