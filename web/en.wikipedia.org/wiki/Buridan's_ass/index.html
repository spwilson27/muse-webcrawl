Buridan's ass

Buridan's ass is an illustration of a paradox in philosophy, cast in the form of a hypothetical situation wherein an ass (donkey) is placed precisely midway between two identical stacks of hay. Having no reason to prefer one stack to the other, the animal cannot make a rational choice and so starves to death. The paradox is named after the 14th-century French philosopher Jean Buridan, whose philosophy of moral determinism it satirizes.

History

Although named for Buridan, the argument does not appear in his surviving works in this exact form; similar discussions occur in Aristotle's De Caelo, in the Persian philosopher al-Ghazali, and in Dante's Paradiso. Buridan himself discussed the related case of a dog choosing between two equal portions of food, and held that the will can suspend judgment when faced with two apparently equal goods — the point the satire distorts.

Discussion

Some philosophers argue the paradox dissolves once it is recognized that an agent can simply choose arbitrarily when reasons are tied, and that choosing either option is better than choosing neither. Others connect the problem to decision theory, game theory, and to later treatments of situations in which two courses of action are exactly equal in utility. The paradox is also invoked in political contexts to describe deadlocks, and in computer science the concept of "Buridan's principle" describes the failure modes of decision-making circuits, such as arbiters in digital electronics, given arbitrarily little time to decide.
