Texas Legislature

The Texas Legislature is the state legislature of the U.S. state of Texas. It is a bicameral body composed of a 31-member Senate and a 150-member House of Representatives. The legislature meets at the Texas State Capitol in Austin. Unusually among U.S. states, it convenes in regular session for only 140 days every other year, in odd-numbered years, though the governor may call special sessions.

Structure

Senators serve four-year terms and representatives two-year terms. The Senate is presided over by the lieutenant governor of Texas — one of the most powerful offices in the state — while the House elects its own speaker. Legislators receive modest salaries (traditionally $7,200 per year plus a per diem), making Texas a "citizen legislature" whose members usually hold outside occupations.

History

The first legislature of the Republic of Texas met in 1836. After annexation by the United States in 1845, the legislature evolved under a series of constitutions, the current one dating to 1876, which tightly constrains the body's powers and meeting time.
