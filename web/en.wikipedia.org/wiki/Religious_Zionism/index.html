Religious Zionism

Religious Zionism (Hebrew: ציונות דתית, Tziyonut Datit) is an ideology that combines Zionism and Orthodox Judaism. Its adherents are also referred to as Dati Leumi (National Religious). Religious Zionism holds that the Land of Israel was promised to the Jewish people by God, and that the establishment of the State of Israel has religious significance as part of the process of redemption.

History

The Mizrachi movement, founded in 1902 by Rabbi Yitzchak Yaacov Reines, was the first organised expression of religious Zionism, participating in the Zionist movement while maintaining Torah observance. Rav Abraham Isaac Kook, the first Ashkenazi chief rabbi of Mandatory Palestine, developed a theology that saw even secular Zionist pioneers as unwitting instruments of redemption. After the Six-Day War of 1967, the movement, influenced by Rav Zvi Yehuda Kook, became strongly associated with the settlement of the West Bank (Judea and Samaria), and the Gush Emunim movement was founded in 1974 to promote it.

Institutions and politics

The movement is associated with the Bnei Akiva youth movement, a network of religious state schools and yeshivot hesder combining Torah study with military service, and political parties including the historical National Religious Party (Mafdal) and its successors, such as the Jewish Home and the Religious Zionist Party.
