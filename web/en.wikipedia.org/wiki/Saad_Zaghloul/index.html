Saad Zaghloul

Born: 1859, Ibyana, Kafr El Sheikh, Egypt
Died: 23 August 1927 (aged 67–68), Cairo, Egypt
Office: Prime Minister of Egypt (1924)
Party: Wafd Party

Saad Zaghloul (Arabic: سعد زغلول; 1859 – 23 August 1927) was an Egyptian revolutionary and statesman. A central figure in Egypt's nationalist movement, he led the delegation — the Wafd — that demanded Egyptian independence from British rule, and he served as Prime Minister of Egypt in 1924.

Zaghloul was educated at Al-Azhar and trained in law. He became a judge, then Minister of Education (1906–1910) and Minister of Justice (1910–1912), and in 1913 was elected vice-president of the Legislative Assembly.

The Wafd and the 1919 revolution

At the end of the First World War, Zaghloul and three associates formed a delegation (al-Wafd) to demand that Egypt be allowed to present its case for independence at the Paris Peace Conference. The British refusal, and Zaghloul's arrest and deportation to Malta in March 1919, triggered the nationwide Egyptian revolution of 1919. Popular pressure forced his release, and in 1922 Britain unilaterally declared a limited Egyptian independence, though reserving key powers.

Prime Minister and later life

Zaghloul founded the Wafd Party, which won a landslide in the 1924 elections under the new constitution, and he became Egypt's first popularly backed prime minister. He resigned in November 1924 amid a crisis with Britain following the assassination of Sir Lee Stack, Sirdar of the Egyptian Army. He later served as speaker of parliament until his death in 1927.

His Cairo home, known as Beit El-Umma (House of the Nation), became a museum, and his mausoleum, the "House of Saad", is a major landmark. His wife Safiya Zaghloul was herself a prominent nationalist figure.
