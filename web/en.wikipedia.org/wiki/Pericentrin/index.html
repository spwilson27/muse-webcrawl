Pericentrin

Pericentrin

Gene: PCNT (human)

Location: Chromosome 21q22.3

Function: Centrosome scaffold protein

Associated disorders: MOPD II, Seckel syndrome

Pericentrin is a large coiled-coil protein that forms part of the pericentriolar material of the centrosome, the cell's main microtubule-organising centre. Encoded by the PCNT gene in humans, it acts as a scaffold anchoring other centrosomal complexes, including the γ-tubulin ring complex that nucleates microtubules, and is required for proper organisation of the mitotic spindle and cell division.

Clinical significance

Biallelic mutations in PCNT cause microcephalic osteodysplastic primordial dwarfism type II (MOPD II), a disorder of severe prenatal and postnatal growth restriction, microcephaly and skeletal abnormalities, and have also been linked to Seckel syndrome. Because of the gene's location on chromosome 21, pericentrin has been studied in connection with the cellular phenotypes of Down syndrome; it is also a known autoantigen in some autoimmune diseases.
