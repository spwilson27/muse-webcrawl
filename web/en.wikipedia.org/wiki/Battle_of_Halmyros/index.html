The Battle of Halmyros, also known as the Battle of the Cephissus, was fought on 15 March 1311 between the Frankish forces of Walter V of Brienne, Duke of Athens, and the mercenaries of the Catalan Company.

Background

Walter had hired the Catalan Company to fight his wars in Greece, then dismissed them without full pay. When the Catalans refused to leave, Walter assembled a large army of Frankish knights and marched against them in Thessaly.

The battle

The Catalans took up a position behind marshy ground flooded by a diverted stream. The Frankish heavy cavalry charged, became bogged down in the mud, and were slaughtered by the Catalan infantry and their Turkish auxiliaries. Walter and nearly all his knights were killed.

Aftermath

The Catalans took over the Duchy of Athens, which they ruled for more than 70 years under the nominal sovereignty of the Crown of Aragon.
