Catalogue raisonné

A catalogue raisonné is a comprehensive, annotated listing of all the known artworks by an artist, providing for each work details such as title, date, medium, dimensions, provenance, exhibition history, and scholarly commentary. The French term means "reasoned catalogue".

PurposeComplete scholarly record of an artist's oeuvre
FieldsTitle, date, medium, dimensions, provenance, literature
UsersScholars, museums, auction houses, authenticators

Function

Catalogues raisonnés are foundational tools of art history and the art market. Inclusion in the recognised catalogue can authenticate a work and greatly affect its value, while exclusion can render it unsaleable. Committees of scholars, artist foundations and estates often maintain the definitive lists, sometimes over decades of research.

Notable examples

Famous examples include the catalogues of Rembrandt's paintings, Wildenstein's volumes on Monet and other Impressionists, and the multi-volume catalogues of Picasso's paintings and drawings. Many are now published or updated online as digital catalogues raisonnés, allowing new attributions and provenance research to be incorporated continuously.
