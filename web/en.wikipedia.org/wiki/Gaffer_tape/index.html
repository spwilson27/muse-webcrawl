Gaffer tape

Gaffer tape (also called gaffa tape or gaff tape) is a heavy cotton cloth pressure-sensitive tape with strong adhesive properties. It is widely used in theatre, photography, film and television production, and live events, where cables and equipment must be secured temporarily without leaving residue.

Matte black gaffer tape is a staple of film and stage crews.

Description

Gaffer tape is named after the gaffer, the head electrician on a film set. Unlike duct tape, to which it is often compared, gaffer tape is made with a cloth backing and a synthetic rubber adhesive designed to be removed cleanly from surfaces such as cables and stage floors. It is typically matte, to avoid reflecting light on set, and can be torn by hand along the weave.

Uses

  • Taping down cables across floors and stages
  • Marking positions ("spike marks") for actors and set pieces
  • Attaching gels and diffusion to lighting instruments
  • Quick repairs and labelling of equipment cases

The tape usually comes in 48 mm (2-inch) rolls in black, white, and grey, though many colours are available for colour-coding.
