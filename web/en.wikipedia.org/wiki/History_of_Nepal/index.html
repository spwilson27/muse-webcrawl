History of Nepal

The history of Nepal encompasses the history of the Himalayan country between India and Tibet, from early kingdoms in the Kathmandu Valley through its unification under Prithvi Narayan Shah in the 18th century, the Rana autocracy, and the transition from monarchy to a federal democratic republic in 2008.

Early kingdoms

The Kathmandu Valley was ruled in turn by Gopala, Kirata and Licchavi dynasties; the Licchavi period (c. 400–750 CE) produced early inscriptions, coinage and art. Lumbini, in the southern Terai, is revered as the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, in the 6th or 5th century BCE. From the 12th century the Malla kings ruled the valley, which split into the rival city-kingdoms of Kathmandu, Bhaktapur and Patan, each famed for its temples and palaces.

Unification and the Shah monarchy

Prithvi Narayan Shah, king of the small state of Gorkha, conquered the Kathmandu Valley in 1768–69 and made Kathmandu his capital, founding the modern unified kingdom. His successors expanded Nepal's territory from the Sutlej to Sikkim. War with the British East India Company (1814–1816) ended with the Treaty of Sugauli, under which Nepal lost about a third of its territory but preserved its independence; Gurkha soldiers began serving in the British army.

The Rana period

In 1846 Jung Bahadur Rana seized power after the Kot massacre and made the premiership hereditary in his family, reducing the Shah kings to figureheads for 104 years. The Ranas kept Nepal isolated but recruited Gurkha regiments for Britain and codified the legal system. The regime fell in 1951, restoring King Tribhuvan, and Nepal held its first general election in 1959. King Mahendra dissolved parliament in 1960 and imposed the party-less Panchayat system, which lasted until the 1990 People's Movement restored multiparty democracy.

Civil war and republic

Maoist insurgents launched a "People's War" in 1996 that claimed some 17,000 lives. In 2001 Crown Prince Dipendra killed King Birendra and much of the royal family before turning the gun on himself. King Gyanendra's direct rule from 2005 provoked mass protests in 2006; a comprehensive peace accord ended the civil war, and in 2008 the Constituent Assembly abolished the monarchy and declared Nepal a federal democratic republic. A new constitution was promulgated in 2015, the year a devastating earthquake killed nearly 9,000 people.
