Cytochrome P450 2C19 (abbreviated CYP2C19) is an enzyme protein encoded in humans by the CYP2C19 gene. It is a member of the cytochrome P450 mixed-function oxidase system, found mainly in the liver, and it metabolizes a clinically important fraction of commonly prescribed drugs, including several proton pump inhibitors, antidepressants, the antifungal voriconazole, and the antiplatelet prodrug clopidogrel, which CYP2C19 helps activate.

CYP2C19 is a hepatic enzyme central to the metabolism of many drugs.

Pharmacogenetics

CYP2C19 is highly polymorphic. Loss-of-function alleles (notably *2 and *3) produce poor metabolizers, who are common in East Asian populations (roughly 15–20%) and less common in Europeans (about 2–5%). Poor metabolizers activate clopidogrel poorly, increasing cardiovascular risk after stent placement, and FDA labelling recommends considering alternative therapy for them. Conversely, the gain-of-function *17 allele produces rapid and ultrarapid metabolizers with lower exposure to drugs such as escitalopram.

The Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) publishes genotype-guided dosing guidelines for CYP2C19 substrates including clopidogrel, voriconazole, and several antidepressants.
