The Côte de Nuits is the northern half of the Côte d'Or wine region of Burgundy, France — a narrow strip of limestone hillside vineyards running roughly from Dijon in the north to Corgoloin, just south of Nuits-Saint-Georges. It is the most celebrated source of Pinot noir in the world.

Geography and appellations

While its southern twin, the Côte de Beaune, is known for both red and white wines, the Côte de Nuits is overwhelmingly red: about 95% of production is Pinot noir. The region contains 24 of Burgundy's 33 red grand cru vineyards, including Chambertin, Musigny, Clos de Vougeot, and the vineyards of Vosne-Romanée — Romanée-Conti and La Tâche among them, producing some of the most expensive wines on earth.

Villages

From north to south the principal communes are Marsannay, Fixin, Brochon, Gevrey-Chambertin, Morey-Saint-Denis, Chambolle-Musigny, Vougeot, Vosne-Romanée (with Flagey-Echézeaux), and Nuits-Saint-Georges, which gives the Côte its name. Wines not entitled to a village appellation may be sold as Côte de Nuits-Villages.

Terroir

East- and southeast-facing slopes of Jurassic limestone and marl, at 250–350 metres altitude, provide the drainage, exposure, and poor soils in which Pinot noir ripens slowly and develops its characteristic perfume and structure.
