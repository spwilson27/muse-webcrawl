Xterm

xterm is the standard terminal emulator for the X Window System. It provides DEC VT102/VT220 and Tektronix 4010/4014 compatible terminals for programs that cannot interface directly with the window system. First released in 1984 as part of X10, xterm predates much of the modern Unix desktop and remains widely used.

History

Development began at MIT in the summer of 1984 under Project Athena; Mark Vandevoorde wrote the original as a standalone terminal for the VS100 display. It became part of the X Window System distribution and has been continuously maintained, for many years by Thomas Dickey. Its escape-sequence behavior became a de facto reference: many other emulators describe themselves by their compatibility with xterm.

Features

xterm supports 256-color and true-color operation, UTF-8, scrollback, mouse reporting, and extensive configuration through X resources and command-line options. It deliberately provides few built-in GUI amenities beyond a simple scrollbar and menus, in keeping with the Unix philosophy. Related variants and descendants include rxvt, urxvt, and terminal emulators bundled with desktop environments such as GNOME Terminal and Konsole.
