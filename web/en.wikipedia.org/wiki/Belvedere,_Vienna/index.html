Belvedere, Vienna

Location: Landstraße, Vienna, Austria

Built: 1714–1723

Architect: Johann Lukas von Hildebrandt

Commissioned by: Prince Eugene of Savoy

The Belvedere is a historic building complex in Vienna, Austria, consisting of two Baroque palaces — the Upper Belvedere and the Lower Belvedere — set in a landscaped park. It was built as a summer residence for Prince Eugene of Savoy by the architect Johann Lukas von Hildebrandt. The complex is now a museum housing one of Austria's most important art collections.

History

The Lower Belvedere was completed in 1716 and the Upper Belvedere, intended mainly for festivities and display, in 1723. After Prince Eugene's death in 1736 the estate passed to the Habsburgs. In the later 18th century the imperial picture gallery was installed in the Upper Belvedere and opened to the public. In 1955 the Austrian State Treaty, restoring Austria's independence after the Second World War, was signed in the Marble Hall of the Upper Belvedere.

The museum

The Belvedere museum holds the world's largest collection of paintings by Gustav Klimt, including The Kiss and Judith, alongside major works by Egon Schiele, Oskar Kokoschka, and artists from the medieval period to the present. The Lower Belvedere and Orangery host temporary exhibitions, and the Baroque gardens, with their fountains, cascades, and sphinxes, connect the two palaces.
