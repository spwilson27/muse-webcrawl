Catalan independence movement

The Catalan independence movement is a social and political movement seeking the independence of Catalonia from Spain, usually including the wider Catalan-speaking territories known as the Catalan Countries. Its principal symbol is the Estelada flag.

[Image: images/catalan-independence-demonstration.jpg — Mass demonstration in Barcelona with Estelada flags]

History

Catalan nationalism developed in the 19th century, shaped by language, culture and Catalonia's distinct institutions, lost after the War of the Spanish Succession (1714). Autonomy was restored under the Second Republic, suppressed under Franco, and re-established by the 1979 Statute after Spain's democratic transition. Support for independence surged in the 2010s after the Constitutional Court struck down parts of the revised 2006 Statute, amid the financial crisis.

The 2017 crisis

After a consultation in 2014, the Catalan government held a referendum on 1 October 2017 that Spanish courts had suspended; it was marred by police intervention. On 27 October the Catalan Parliament issued a unilateral declaration of independence; Spain imposed direct rule under Article 155, dissolved the Parliament, and prosecuted Catalan leaders for rebellion and sedition — several fled abroad, others were imprisoned and later pardoned (2021), with an amnesty law following in 2024.

Positions

Parties have included Junts, Esquerra Republicana and the CUP on the pro-independence side, against unionist parties favouring remaining in Spain; opinion in Catalonia remains divided.
