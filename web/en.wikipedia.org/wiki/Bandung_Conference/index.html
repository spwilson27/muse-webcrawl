Bandung Conference

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bandung Conference (the Asian–African Conference) was a meeting of 29 Asian and African states held in Bandung, Indonesia, from 18 to 24 April 1955, most of them newly independent. Convened by the Colombo Powers (Burma, Ceylon, India, Indonesia and Pakistan) and graced by figures including Sukarno (host), Nehru, Zhou Enlai, Nasser and Nkrumah's representatives, it proclaimed solidarity among the decolonising world and a refusal to be conscripted into the Cold War's blocs — the seed of the Non-Aligned Movement (formally founded at Belgrade in 1961).

Its final communiqué endorsed the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence (Panchsheel) and ten Bandung principles echoing the UN Charter, and condemned colonialism “in all its manifestations” (a compromise formula covering Soviet as well as Western empires, after debate driven by pro-Western delegates). Bandung's practical output was modest but its symbolic output immense: the “Third World” acquired a stage, a vocabulary and a diplomatic caucus (the Afro-Asian group at the UN), and China's Zhou Enlai used it to present Beijing as a reasonable Asian power (“seek common ground while reserving differences”). Commemorations (the 50th and 60th anniversaries, 2005 and 2015, with Asian-African summits) keep the “Bandung spirit” in diplomatic circulation.
