Stone–Čech compactification

The Stone–Čech compactification of a topological space X, denoted βX, is the largest (most general) compact Hausdorff space into which X can be embedded as a dense subspace, characterised by a universal property: every continuous map from X to a compact Hausdorff space extends uniquely to βX.

Construction

One construction embeds X into a product of unit intervals indexed by all bounded continuous real functions on X and takes the closure. For discrete spaces such as the natural numbers, βN can be understood via ultrafilters; its remainder βN \ N has cardinality 2^(continuum) and plays a central role in set theory and algebra (e.g. the Hindman–Ramsey theory of ultrafilters).

History

Introduced by Marshall Stone (1937) and Eduard Čech (1937), it is the archetypal compactification in general topology.
