"Sleeping Beauty" (French: La Belle au bois dormant) is a classic fairy tale about a princess who is cursed by an evil fairy to sleep for a hundred years after pricking her finger on a spindle, and who can only be awakened by a prince's kiss. It is classified as Aarne–Thompson tale type 410.

Versions

Elements of the story appear in the 14th-century romance Perceforest. Giambattista Basile's "Sun, Moon, and Talia" (Pentamerone, 1634) is the earliest known literary version, a darker tale in which the sleeping Talia is found by a king. Charles Perrault's version in Histoires ou contes du temps passé (1697) introduced the christening fairies and the hundred-year sleep, and adds a second half in which the prince's ogress mother threatens his wife and children. The Brothers Grimm's "Little Briar Rose" (1812) ends with the awakening and wedding.

Synopsis (Perrault/Grimm form)

A king and queen invite fairies to their daughter's christening; an uninvited fairy curses the child to die by a spindle prick, softened by another fairy to a century of sleep. Despite the banning of spindles, the princess pricks her finger at sixteen and the whole castle sleeps with her, surrounded by a thorn forest. After a hundred years a prince finds his way through and wakes her with a kiss.

Adaptations

The tale inspired Tchaikovsky's ballet The Sleeping Beauty (1890), one of his three great ballets, and Walt Disney's animated film Sleeping Beauty (1959), whose villain Maleficent became one of Disney's most famous creations.
