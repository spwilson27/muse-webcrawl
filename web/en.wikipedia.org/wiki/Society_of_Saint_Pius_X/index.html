The Society of Saint Pius X (SSPX; Latin: Fraternitas Sacerdotalis Sancti Pii X) is an international traditionalist Catholic priestly fraternity founded in 1970 by Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre, a leading traditionalist voice at the Second Vatican Council.

The society is known for rejecting many of the ecclesiastical reforms both influenced or institutionalized by the Second Vatican Council, especially aspects of liturgy and ecclesiology, and for retaining the Tridentine Mass and pre-conciliar practices.

History

Lefebvre founded the society at Fribourg, Switzerland, with the approval of Bishop François Charrière, and established its seminary at Écône. In 1976 Lefebvre was suspended a divinis after ordaining priests despite Vatican warnings. In 1988 Lefebvre consecrated four bishops without papal mandate, an act the Vatican declared incurred automatic excommunication for Lefebvre and the bishops; the society's canonical status has remained irregular since.

Relations with Rome

In 2009 Pope Benedict XVI remitted the excommunications of the four surviving bishops. Talks between the society and the Holy See have continued intermittently, and Pope Francis granted SSPX priests faculties for confession (2015) and provisions for marriages (2017), while the society still lacks full canonical recognition.

Size and activities

The society reports roughly 700 priests, several seminaries (including St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary in Virginia, USA), and chapels in more than 60 countries.
