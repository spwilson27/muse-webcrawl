Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani

Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Arabic: ابن حجر العسقلاني), full name Shihāb al-Dīn Abū al-Faḍl Aḥmad ibn Nūr al-Dīn ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (18 February 1372 – 2 February 1449), was a medieval Egyptian scholar of Islam, a Shafi'i jurist and hadith master who authored some 150 works on hadith, history, biography, Quranic exegesis and jurisprudence. He is regarded as one of the greatest scholars of Islamic history and was given the honorific title Shaykh al-Islām and Amīr al-Mu'minīn fī al-Ḥadīth ("Commander of the Faithful in Hadith").

Life

Born in Cairo in 1372, Ibn Hajar was orphaned young and raised by guardians who directed him to study. He memorized the Quran by age nine and travelled widely — to Mecca, Damascus, Jerusalem, Yemen and the Hijaz — in search of hadith. He served as chief judge (qadi) of Egypt on several occasions under the Mamluk sultans and taught at major Cairene madrasas until his death in 1449.

Works

His most celebrated work is Fath al-Bari ("Victory of the Creator"), a vast multi-volume commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari that took some 25 years to complete and remains the most authoritative commentary on that collection. Other major works include Bulugh al-Maram, a manual of hadith relating to legal rulings; al-Isaba fi tamyiz al-sahaba, a biographical dictionary of the Companions of Muhammad; Tahdhib al-Tahdhib, on hadith narrators; and Nukhbat al-Fikar, a foundational primer on hadith terminology.

Legacy

Ibn Hajar's scholarship shaped Sunni hadith studies for centuries, and his works remain standard references in Islamic scholarship and education worldwide.
