Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto (Kyoto, Uji and Otsu Cities)

The Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto (Kyoto, Uji and Otsu Cities) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site comprising seventeen component sites in three cities: Kyoto and Uji in Kyoto Prefecture and Ōtsu in Shiga Prefecture, Japan. The sites include thirteen Buddhist temples, three Shinto shrines and one castle, inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1994.

Description

Kyoto — as Heian-kyō the imperial capital and residence of the emperor from 794 until the mid-19th century — was the centre of Japanese culture for more than a millennium. The inscribed properties illustrate the development of Japanese wooden architecture, particularly religious architecture, and of Japanese garden design, which influenced landscape gardening worldwide.

Component sites

The seventeen sites are: Kamigamo Shrine, Shimogamo Shrine, Tō-ji, Kiyomizu-dera, Enryaku-ji (on Mount Hiei in Ōtsu), Daigo-ji, Ninna-ji, Byōdō-in (in Uji), Ujigami Shrine (Uji), Kōzan-ji, Saihō-ji (the moss temple), Tenryū-ji, Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion), Ginkaku-ji (the Silver Pavilion), Ryōan-ji (famed for its rock garden), Nishi Honganji, and Nijō Castle.

Inscription

UNESCO inscribed the property in 1994 under criteria (ii) and (iv), recognising Kyoto as a testimony to the cultural development of Japan and the sites as outstanding examples of religious and secular architecture and garden design from the 8th to the 19th centuries. The area's survival was aided by Kyoto's exemption from large-scale bombing during the Second World War.
