Opus is a lossy audio coding format developed by the Xiph.Org Foundation and standardized by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), designed to efficiently code speech and general audio in a single format, while remaining low-latency enough for real-time interactive communication and low-complexity enough for low-end embedded processors. Opus replaces both Vorbis and Speex for new applications, and several blind listening tests have ranked it higher-quality than any other standard audio format at any given bitrate until transparency is reached, including MP3, AAC, and HE-AAC.

Features

Opus supports constant and variable bitrate encoding from 6 kbit/s to 510 kbit/s (or up to 256 kbit/s per channel for multi-channel), frame sizes from 2.5 ms to 60 ms, and five sampling rates from 8 kHz (with 4 kHz bandwidth) to 48 kHz (with 20 kHz bandwidth, the human hearing range). It combines the speech-oriented SILK codec (developed by Skype) with the low-latency CELT codec (from Xiph.Org), switching or blending between them depending on content and bitrate. An Opus stream can be carried in an Ogg container (".opus"), in WebM, or directly over RTP.

Adoption

Opus is the mandatory-to-implement audio codec for WebRTC and is used by WhatsApp, Discord, Zoom, and other voice applications, by the HTML5 <audio> element in browsers, and as YouTube's preferred audio codec alongside AAC. It is defined in RFC 6716 (2012) and its payload format for RTP in RFC 7587. In listening tests it consistently outperforms AAC and MP3 at low and medium bitrates, particularly for speech.
