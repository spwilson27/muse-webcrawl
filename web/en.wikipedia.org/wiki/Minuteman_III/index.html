LGM-30 Minuteman

The LGM-30 Minuteman is an American land-based intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) in service with the Air Force Global Strike Command of the United States Air Force. The Minuteman III variant, first deployed in 1970, is the only land-based ICBM in the United States arsenal and represents the land leg of the U.S. nuclear triad, alongside submarine-launched and bomber-delivered weapons.

History

The original Minuteman I entered service in 1962 during the Cuban Missile Crisis; Minuteman II followed in 1965. Minuteman III, introduced in 1970, was the first U.S. ICBM deployed with multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs), carrying up to three warheads, together with improved guidance and penetration aids. Arms-control agreements later reduced loadings; the deployed force is currently downloaded to a single warhead per missile.

Deployment

About 400 Minuteman III missiles are deployed in hardened silos operated by missile wings at F. E. Warren Air Force Base (Wyoming), Malmstrom Air Force Base (Montana), and Minot Air Force Base (North Dakota), with launch controlled by two-officer crews in underground launch control centres. Unarmed test launches are conducted periodically from Vandenberg Space Force Base, California.

Modernisation and replacement

Extensive life-extension programmes have replaced the missiles' propellant, guidance systems, and reentry vehicles. The Minuteman III is planned to be replaced by the LGM-35 Sentinel ICBM, under development by Northrop Grumman, beginning around the end of the 2020s, though the programme has undergone cost-driven restructuring.
