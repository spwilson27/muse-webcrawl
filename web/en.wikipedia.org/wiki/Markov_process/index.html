Markov process

A Markov process or Markov chain is a stochastic process describing a sequence of possible events in which the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event. Informally, this may be thought of as "what happens next depends only on the state of affairs now". A process satisfying this condition is said to have the Markov property: the conditional probability distribution of future states of the process depends only upon the present state, not on the sequence of events that preceded it.

Pr(Xn+1 = x | X1 = x1, …, Xn = xn) = Pr(Xn+1 = x | Xn = xn)

Markov processes are named after the Russian mathematician Andrey Markov, who introduced them in 1906. They are the basis of Markov chain Monte Carlo methods and have applications across physics, chemistry, biology, economics, finance, computer science, and speech and image recognition.

Types

Markov processes are classified by their state space and time index. A Markov chain has a discrete state space, and may evolve in discrete time (a discrete-time Markov chain, DTMC) or continuous time (CTMC). Processes with continuous state spaces include Markov diffusion processes such as Brownian motion. A process that is not Markovian on its given state space can often be made so by expanding the definition of the state.

Properties

Transition probabilities

For a discrete-time chain, the probabilities of moving between states are collected in a transition matrix. Powers of the matrix give multi-step transition probabilities, as formalized by the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations. Chains may be classified by whether states are recurrent or transient, and whether the chain is irreducible or periodic.

Stationary distributions

A stationary distribution is a probability distribution over states that remains unchanged under the transition dynamics. Ergodic chains possess a unique stationary distribution to which the chain converges from any starting state — a fact exploited in ranking algorithms such as PageRank and in sampling methods.

Applications

Markov processes model queues, population dynamics, chemical reaction networks, random walks, and asset prices. Hidden Markov models, in which the state is only partially observable, underpin speech recognition and bioinformatics. Markov decision processes extend the framework to systems with controllable actions and rewards, forming a foundation of reinforcement learning.
