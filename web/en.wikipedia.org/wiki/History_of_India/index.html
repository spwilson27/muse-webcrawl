History of India

The history of India begins with evidence of human activity in the subcontinent tens of thousands of years ago and encompasses one of the world's oldest urban civilisations, the rise of major religions including Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism, a succession of great empires, and the modern Republic of India.

Ancient India

The Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 3300–1300 BCE) built planned cities such as Mohenjo-daro and Harappa with sophisticated drainage and trade reaching Mesopotamia. After its decline, Vedic culture developed in the northern plains; by the 6th century BCE the mahajanapadas — early kingdoms and republics — had arisen, alongside the teachings of the Buddha and Mahavira. The Maurya Empire (c. 322–185 BCE) united most of the subcontinent under Chandragupta and his grandson Ashoka, whose edicts spread Buddhism after his conversion. The Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE) presided over a classical age of science, mathematics — including the development of the decimal system and the concept of zero — art and literature.

Medieval India

The medieval centuries saw regional kingdoms such as the Cholas, who projected power across South and Southeast Asia, and the arrival of Islam, first through trade and then through the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526). In the Deccan, the Vijayanagara Empire flourished as a Hindu bulwark in the south.

Mughal Empire and its decline

Babur founded the Mughal Empire in 1526. Under Akbar and his successors it came to rule most of the subcontinent, producing a rich synthesis of Persian and Indian culture, monumental architecture such as the Taj Mahal, and one of the world's largest economies. Mughal decline in the 18th century opened the way for the Maratha Confederacy, regional powers, and European trading companies.

Colonial India

The British East India Company expanded from coastal trading posts to territorial rule after the Battle of Plassey (1757); following the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the Crown assumed direct rule as the British Raj. Colonial rule integrated India into global markets and built railways and universities, but also deindustrialised textile production and presided over devastating famines. The Indian National Congress (founded 1885) and, from the 1920s, Mohandas Gandhi's mass non-violent movement pressed for independence.

Independence and the Republic

India gained independence on 15 August 1947, partitioned into India and Pakistan amid mass displacement and violence. Under its 1950 constitution, India became a secular democratic republic; Jawaharlal Nehru, its first prime minister, pursued planned industrialisation and non-alignment in foreign policy. Since economic liberalisation in 1991, India has become one of the world's fastest-growing major economies, while continuing to grapple with poverty, communal tensions and regional disputes, including over Kashmir.
