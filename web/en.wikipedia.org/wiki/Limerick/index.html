Limerick (Irish: Luimneach, meaning "bare spot of ground") is a city in western Ireland, in County Limerick, at the head of the Shannon Estuary where the River Shannon meets the Atlantic Ocean. It is the third-largest city in the Republic of Ireland and the principal city of the Mid-West region. Founded by the Vikings in the ninth century, Limerick is one of Ireland's oldest cities.

History

The Norse established a settlement at Limerick around 812, and it grew into a Viking kingdom before being absorbed into the Gaelic kingdoms. The Normans refounded the city in the twelfth century, building King John's Castle on King's Island. Limerick is famously the "Treaty City": after the Williamite War, the Treaty of Limerick (1691) ended the siege of the city, but its terms — guarantees for Irish Catholics — were soon broken, a grievance remembered in Irish history as the "broken treaty". The Treaty Stone, on which the treaty is said to have been signed, stands on the west bank of the Shannon. The Georgian terraces of Newtown Pery were laid out in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and the city later became known through Frank McCourt's memoir Angela's Ashes.

Economy and culture

Limerick's economy centres on the Shannon region's industry, technology and services, with the Raheen business park and nearby Shannon Free Zone hosting multinational employers. The University of Limerick, founded in 1972, and the Technological University of the Shannon are major institutions. Thomond Park is the home of Munster Rugby, and the city is a noted sporting centre. Landmarks include St Mary's Cathedral (founded 1168), the Hunt Museum, and King John's Castle.
