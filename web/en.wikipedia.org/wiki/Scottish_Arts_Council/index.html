The Scottish Arts Council (Scottish Gaelic: Comhairle Ealain na h-Alba) was a Scottish public body responsible for the funding, development, and promotion of the arts in Scotland. It distributed funding from the Scottish Government and from the National Lottery to arts organisations and individual artists across drama, dance, literature, music, and the visual arts.

History

The council originated in 1947 as the Scottish Committee of the Arts Council of Great Britain. Under the Royal Charter of 1967 the committee became a semi-autonomous body, the Scottish Arts Council, and after the devolution of arts funding in 1994 it became a fully separate national body when the Arts Council of Great Britain was split into separate councils for England, Scotland, and Wales. Its decisions were taken at "arm's length" from government by a council of appointed members advised by specialist committees.

For many years the council was based at 12 Manor Place in Edinburgh's West End. It funded Scotland's national performing companies alongside regional theatres, galleries, festivals, and publishing, and it ran award schemes for writers and artists.

Abolition

Following a long-running review of Scotland's cultural agencies, the Scottish Government announced that the Scottish Arts Council would be merged with Scottish Screen, the film agency. The merged body, Creative Scotland, was established in 2010 under the Public Services Reform (Scotland) Act 2010, and the Scottish Arts Council ceased to exist on 1 July 2010.
