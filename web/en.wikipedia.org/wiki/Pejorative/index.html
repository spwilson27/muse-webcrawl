Pejorative

A pejorative word or grammatical form is one that expresses contempt, disapproval, or low opinion, or conveys that something is done poorly. A term used pejoratively functions as a slur or dysphemism when intended to demean its referent. Pejoration, the opposite of amelioration, is also the name of the historical-linguistic process by which a word's meaning acquires negative connotations over time.

Linguistic aspects

Pejorative force may be inherent in a word (such as slurs) or may arise from context, tone, or usage contrasting a neutral synonym — compare "cheap" with "inexpensive," or calling a plan a "scheme." Many languages have pejorative suffixes or diminutives used contemptuously. Pejoration is a common pathway of semantic change: English "silly" once meant blessed or innocent, "villain" denoted a farmhand, and "knave" a boy, before each acquired its modern negative sense.

Reclamation

Some pejorative terms are reclaimed by the groups they targeted, losing or reversing their sting within in-group use; well-known examples include "queer" in LGBTQ communities. Whether a term remains pejorative often depends on the speaker, audience, and setting, which makes pejoration a frequent subject of debate in lexicography, law, and public discourse.

Related concepts

Pejoratives contrast with melioratives (terms of approval) and with euphemisms, which soften negative meanings. In argument, "pejorative" is sometimes used loosely for sweeping critical characterisations — for example, dismissing a claim as "mere rhetoric."
