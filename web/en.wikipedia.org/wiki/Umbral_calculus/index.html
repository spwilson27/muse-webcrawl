Umbral calculus

Umbral calculus is a branch of mathematics dealing with polynomial sequences and identities obtained by treating indices as if they were exponents. The name, from the Latin umbra ("shadow"), was coined in the 19th century for techniques used by John Blissard and others, which long seemed to work "magically" without rigorous justification.

Overview

In classical umbral notation, an identity such as the binomial expansion for Bernoulli numbers is written by lowering indices to exponents — replacing Bn by Bn — manipulating the expression algebraically, then lowering exponents back to indices. Nineteenth-century mathematicians used such tricks to derive many identities but could not explain why they worked.

Modern foundations

In the 20th century Gian-Carlo Rota and collaborators put the subject on a rigorous footing using linear functionals on polynomial spaces: the "umbra" is a linear operator that evaluates sequences, and polynomial sequences of binomial type are characterized by their action under shift operators. The theory now connects combinatorics, special functions and finite operator calculus.
