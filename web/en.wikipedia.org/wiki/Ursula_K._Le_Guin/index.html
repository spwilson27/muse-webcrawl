Ursula K. Le Guin

Ursula Kroeber Le Guin (October 21, 1929 – January 22, 2018) was an American author best known for her works of speculative fiction, including the Earthsea fantasy series and the Hainish science-fiction cycle. She is regarded as one of the greatest writers of the genre and a major figure in American letters.

Ursula K. Le Guin
Born: October 21, 1929, Berkeley, California
Died: January 22, 2018, Portland, Oregon
Notable works: A Wizard of Earthsea, The Left Hand of Darkness, The Dispossessed

Life

Daughter of anthropologist Alfred Kroeber and writer Theodora Kroeber, she studied at Radcliffe and Columbia, married historian Charles Le Guin, and settled in Portland, Oregon. Anthropology, Taoism, and anarchism deeply shaped her fiction.

Works

The Left Hand of Darkness (1969) explored gender on a planet of ambisexual people; The Dispossessed (1974) contrasted an anarchist moon with its capitalist home world; both won Hugo and Nebula Awards. A Wizard of Earthsea (1968) began her beloved fantasy sequence. Later works include Lavinia and the essay collection The Wave in the Mind.

Legacy

Le Guin won eight Hugo Awards, six Nebulas, and the National Book Foundation Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters (2014).
