Short-term rental

A short-term rental (STR) is a furnished residential property rented for short periods, typically by the night or week and usually for fewer than 30 consecutive days. Short-term rentals are an alternative to hotels and are commonly marketed through online platforms such as Airbnb, Vrbo, and Booking.com. The property may be a whole home, an apartment, or a private room in an occupied dwelling, and the owner is usually called a host.

History

Renting furnished rooms and holiday homes on a short-term basis long predates the internet — seaside boarding houses and vacation cottages are early examples. The market was transformed after 2008 by online platforms that standardized search, payment, reviews, and host insurance, rapidly expanding supply in major tourist cities and leading to the professionalization of many listings managed by property companies.

Regulation

Many cities regulate short-term rentals in response to concerns about housing affordability, neighbourhood noise and nuisance, lost hotel tax revenue, and safety. Common measures include registration and licensing, caps on the number of nights a property may be rented per year (for example 90 nights in London and Amsterdam-style 30-night limits), a requirement that the property be the host's primary residence, zoning restrictions, and in some places — most notably New York City's Local Law 18 of 2023 — rules that effectively restrict most whole-apartment rentals. Hosts and platforms dispute the size of the effect on housing supply, while researchers have found associations between STR growth and higher local rents and house prices.

Economics and criticism

Supporters argue short-term rentals let residents earn income, spread tourism spending beyond hotel districts, and make use of otherwise vacant space. Critics argue they remove long-term housing from the market, raise rents, change residential neighbourhood character, and create unfair competition for hotels that face stricter regulation. Municipal governments have responded with varying mixes of taxation agreements with platforms and enforcement against illegal listings.
