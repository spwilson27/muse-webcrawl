39 categories of activity prohibited on Shabbat

Shabbat candles mark the beginning of the day of rest

The thirty-nine categories of activity prohibited on Shabbat (Hebrew: lamed-tet avot melakhot) are the principal classes of "work" (melakha) forbidden by Jewish law on the Sabbath. They are enumerated in the Mishnah (Shabbat 7:2) and were traditionally derived from the labours required to construct the Tabernacle in the wilderness.

The list

The Mishnah lists the categories in an order following the production of bread and garments: sowing, ploughing, reaping, binding sheaves, threshing, winnowing, selecting, grinding, sifting, kneading, baking; shearing wool, washing it, beating it, dyeing it, spinning, weaving, making loops, weaving threads, separating threads, tying, untying, sewing, tearing; trapping, slaughtering, flaying, curing hide, scraping, cutting, writing, erasing, building, demolishing, extinguishing a fire, kindling a fire, striking a final blow (completing an object), and carrying out from one domain to another.

Derivatives

Each category (av, "father") generates derivative prohibitions (toladot, "offspring") that share its essential character; for example, cooking is a derivative of baking, and digging a hole is a derivative of ploughing. Rabbinic decrees and the concept of muktzeh add further restrictions. Modern applications — most famously electricity — are debated by halakhic authorities in terms of which category they fall under.

Exceptions

Saving a human life (pikuach nefesh) overrides the prohibitions, so medical treatment and emergency work are permitted and indeed required when life is at risk.
