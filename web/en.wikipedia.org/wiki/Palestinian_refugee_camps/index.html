Palestinian refugee camps

Palestinian refugee camps are camps established after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War to accommodate Palestinian refugees who fled or were expelled during the 1948 Palestinian exodus, and after the 1967 Six-Day War. The United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) recognises 68 camps in Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, the West Bank, and the Gaza Strip, housing a substantial share of the more than five million registered Palestinian refugees.

History

The first camps were set up by the Red Cross and then UNRWA from 1949, initially as tent cities that gradually became dense concrete neighbourhoods. Large camps include Jabalia and Rafah in Gaza, Baqa'a in Jordan, Ain al-Hilweh in Lebanon, and Yarmouk in Syria, which before the Syrian civil war housed over 100,000 people.

Conditions and status

Most camps suffer from overcrowding, poor infrastructure, and high unemployment, and residents' legal status varies sharply by host country — from Jordanian citizenship for most refugees in Jordan to severe occupational restrictions in Lebanon. The camps remain at the centre of the unresolved Palestinian right of return debate in Israeli–Palestinian negotiations.
