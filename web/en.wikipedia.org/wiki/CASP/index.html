CASP

CASP (Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction) is a community-wide, worldwide experiment for protein structure prediction that has taken place every two years since 1994. CASP provides research groups with an opportunity to objectively test their structure prediction methods and delivers an independent assessment of the state of the art in protein structure modelling to the research community and software users.

History

CASP was established in 1994 by John Moult and co-workers, inspired by earlier blind assessments of protein modelling. The first experiments were held at Asilomar, California. Predictions are made on protein sequences whose experimental structures have been determined but not yet released publicly, so predictors work genuinely "blind". Independent assessors then compare the submitted models with the experimental structures using numerical measures such as GDT (Global Distance Test) and lDDT.

Categories

Over the years CASP has included categories for template-based modelling, free modelling (later renamed template-free or ab initio modelling), refinement, accuracy estimation, oligomeric structures, complexes, and (from CASP15) RNA structures. A related continuous benchmark, CAMEO, runs year-round using the same principles.

CASP14 and AlphaFold

In CASP14 (2020), DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 system achieved median accuracies competitive with experimental methods for many targets, a result widely described as solving the protein folding problem in its single-chain form. AlphaFold's developers Jumper and Hassabis later shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. CASP15 (2022) and CASP16 (2024) extended the challenge to protein complexes, nucleic acids and ligands.
