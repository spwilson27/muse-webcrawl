The Killers (1946 film)

The Killers is a 1946 American film noir directed by Robert Siodmak and starring Burt Lancaster (in his film debut) and Ava Gardner. Partly based on Ernest Hemingway's 1927 short story of the same name, it was produced by Mark Hellinger and released by Universal Pictures.

Plot

Two hit men arrive in a small town and murder Pete Lund, a gas station attendant known as "the Swede," who makes no attempt to escape. Insurance investigator Jim Reardon pieces together the Swede's past through a series of flashbacks: the ex-boxer Ole Anderson fell for the treacherous Kitty Collins, took part in a payroll robbery, was double-crossed, and went to prison for a crime Kitty committed — a chain of betrayal that leads inexorably to his doom.

Production

The film's celebrated opening — the killers' arrival in the diner — adapts Hemingway's story nearly verbatim; the remainder, elaborated by screenwriter Anthony Veiller (with uncredited contributions from John Huston and Richard Brooks), invents the backstory in a flashback structure often compared to Citizen Kane. Miklós Rózsa's score features a four-note motif later famous as the Dragnet theme.

Reception and legacy

The Killers was a critical and commercial success, nominated for four Academy Awards including Best Director. It is considered a quintessential film noir and a landmark of the genre. A loose remake was directed by Don Siegel in 1964. In 2008 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
