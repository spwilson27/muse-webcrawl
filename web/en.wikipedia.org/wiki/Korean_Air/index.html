Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. (Korean: 대한항공, Daehan Hanggong), operating as Korean Air, is the flag carrier and largest airline of South Korea. Founded in 1969 when the Hanjin Group acquired the state-owned Korean Air Lines, it operates an international network from its hub at Incheon International Airport, serving well over a hundred destinations worldwide, and is one of the largest transpacific carriers and air cargo operators. Korean Air is a founding member of the SkyTeam alliance (2000) and in 2024 completed its long-negotiated acquisition of a controlling stake in rival Asiana Airlines, with a full merger into a single mega-carrier planned over subsequent years.

History

The airline began as the government-owned Korean Air Lines in 1962; privatised into Hanjin in 1969, it took over routes and expanded across the Pacific in the 1970s, introducing Boeing 747s on transpacific services. Through the 1980s–90s Korean Air grew into a global carrier, and from the 1970s it has also operated a major cargo division; the airline is also known in Korea as one of the country's key corporate ambassadors, sponsoring visa-free travel campaigns and national promotion. Founder Cho Choong-hoon built the carrier; his son Cho Yang-ho chaired it from 1999 until his death in 2019, succeeded by Cho Won-tae.

Safety record and reform

Korean Air suffered a string of fatal accidents in the 1980s and 1990s, most notoriously the shooting down of Flight 007 by a Soviet interceptor in 1983 (269 killed) and the crash of Flight 801 at Guam in 1997 (229 killed), as well as the 1999 crash of cargo Flight 8509 in England. Analyses blamed in part a steep cockpit hierarchy discouraging first officers from challenging captains. A comprehensive safety overhaul after 1999 — external audits, retraining, English-only cockpit communication and recruitment of foreign check pilots — transformed the airline's record; it has operated without a fatal passenger accident since 1999, and the reform story became a widely taught case study in crew resource management.

Destinations and fleet

Korean Air serves an extensive network across Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania and beyond from Incheon, with a fleet centred on Boeing 747-8s, 777-300ERs, 787 Dreamliners and A380s, plus A321neos and 737s for regional routes, and a large freighter fleet of 747-8Fs and 777Fs. Its frequent-flyer programme is Skypass.

Asiana merger

In November 2020, amid the COVID-19 collapse in travel, Korean Air announced it would acquire Asiana Airlines. After years of competition reviews in Korea, the EU, the US and elsewhere — with slot and route remedies, including transferring routes to low-cost carriers — Korean Air acquired a controlling stake of about 63.9% in Asiana in December 2024, making it a subsidiary; full integration of the two brands, fleets and frequent-flyer programmes is to follow over the following years, creating one of the world's larger airline groups.
