Kaibutsu-kun

Kaibutsu-kun (Japanese: 怪物くん, "Little Monster") is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Fujiko A. Fujio. It was serialized in Shōnen magazine publications beginning in 1965 and became one of the author's best-known works, later adapted into anime television series, a live-action drama, and feature films.

Plot

The story follows Kaibutsu-kun (Tarō Kaibutsu), the young prince of Monsterland, who is sent to the human world — specifically to Japan — to train and learn how to be a proper ruler. He is accompanied by three loyal retainers: Dracula, Wolfman (Franken's counterpart in some versions), and Franken, who help him navigate human society. Together they befriend a young human boy, Hiroshi, and become involved in a series of comedic adventures involving monsters, spirits, and everyday human problems.

Characters

  • Kaibutsu-kun (Tarō Kaibutsu) — the spoiled but good-hearted prince of Monsterland, who can stretch his limbs and change his shape.
  • Dracula — a vampire retainer who must drink tomato juice instead of blood while in the human world.
  • Wolfman — a werewolf retainer, perpetually hungry.
  • Franken — a gentle, immensely strong Frankenstein's monster.
  • Hiroshi — the human boy who befriends the group.

Media

The manga was first adapted into an anime series in 1968–1969 by Tokyo Movie, and a second, more widely distributed color anime ran from 1980 to 1982. A live-action television drama starring Satoshi Ohno of Arashi aired on Nippon TV in 2010, followed by a 3D feature film, Kaibutsu-kun: The Movie (2011), which was shot partly in India and enjoyed considerable box-office success in Japan.

Legacy

Kaibutsu-kun remains a nostalgic fixture of Japanese pop culture, with its characters appearing in merchandising and crossovers with other Fujiko Fujio creations such as Doraemon.
