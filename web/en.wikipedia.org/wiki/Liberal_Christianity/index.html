Liberal Christianity

Liberal Christianity, also known as liberal theology, is a movement that seeks to interpret and reform Christian teaching in light of modern knowledge, science and ethics. It grew out of the Enlightenment and the application of historical-critical methods to the Bible in the 18th and 19th centuries, and it emphasises reason and experience alongside scripture and tradition.

Characteristics

Liberal theologians typically read the Bible as a human witness to divine revelation rather than as literally inerrant text, accept evolutionary science, and stress the ethical teachings of Jesus, social justice and the "fatherhood of God and brotherhood of man." Influential figures include Friedrich Schleiermacher, Albrecht Ritschl, Adolf von Harnack, and in the 20th century Harry Emerson Fosdick and Paul Tillich.

Criticism and reaction

Liberal theology was a principal target of the Fundamentalist–Modernist controversy in American Protestantism, and it was sharply criticised by neo-orthodox theologians such as Karl Barth for accommodating faith to culture. It remains influential in mainline Protestant denominations and in much academic theology.
