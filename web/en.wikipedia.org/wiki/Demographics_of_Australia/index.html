The demographics of Australia covers basic statistics, most populous cities, and ethnic and religious affiliations of the population of Australia. At the 2021 census, the population was recorded as 25,422,788. Australia's population has quadrupled since the end of the First World War, much of this increase from immigration. Australia is one of the world's most urbanised countries, with the great majority of its population concentrated in the coastal cities of the east and south-east.

Australia's population is concentrated along the eastern and south-eastern coasts.

Population history

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have inhabited Australia for tens of thousands of years. European settlement began in 1788 with the arrival of the First Fleet at Sydney Cove. The gold rushes of the 1850s drove rapid growth, and at Federation in 1901 the population was about 3.8 million. Post-war immigration from 1945 onwards transformed the population, first from Britain and Europe, and after the dismantling of the White Australia policy in the 1970s, increasingly from Asia and the rest of the world.

Urbanisation

More than two-thirds of Australians live in the greater metropolitan areas of the state and territory capitals. The largest cities are Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide, each with well over a million inhabitants.

Ancestry and immigration

At recent censuses the most commonly reported ancestries were English, Australian, Irish, Scottish and Chinese. Roughly 30 per cent of residents were born overseas; the largest migrant groups were born in England, India, China and New Zealand. About 3.2 per cent of the population identified as Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander at the 2021 census.

Languages

English is the de facto national language, spoken at home by about 72 per cent of the population. Other commonly spoken languages include Mandarin, Arabic, Vietnamese and Cantonese. More than 250 Indigenous languages were spoken before colonisation; many are now endangered, though revival efforts continue.

Religion

Christianity remains the largest religious group but is declining; at the 2021 census about 44 per cent identified as Christian, while nearly 39 per cent reported no religion. Islam, Hinduism and Buddhism are the largest non-Christian faiths.
