The Israel Museum (Hebrew: מוזיאון ישראל) in Jerusalem is Israel's largest cultural institution and its national museum of art and archaeology. Founded in 1965, it houses encyclopaedic collections — including the Dead Sea Scrolls in the Shrine of the Book — spanning from prehistory to contemporary art.

Collections

The museum's Samuel and Saidye Bronfman Archaeology Wing displays finds from the Land of Israel, including the Bar Kokhba letters and the Heliodorus stele. The Jack, Joseph and Morton Mandel Wing for Jewish Art and Life holds ritual objects and reconstructed synagogue interiors from around the world. The Edmond and Lily Safra Fine Arts Wing covers European, Israeli and contemporary art, and the Billy Rose Art Garden, designed by Isamu Noguchi, contains a notable collection of modern sculpture. The museum also holds the world's most extensive collections of Holy Land archaeology and Judaica, and operates the Rockefeller Archaeological Museum in East Jerusalem and the Ticho House.

Shrine of the Book

The white-domed Shrine of the Book, designed by Armand Bartos and Frederic Kiesler, houses the Dead Sea Scrolls — including the Great Isaiah Scroll — together with the Aleppo Codex. Its shape echoes the lids of the jars in which the scrolls were found at Qumran. Nearby stands a large-scale model of Jerusalem in the Second Temple period.

History

The museum was established under the leadership of Jerusalem mayor Teddy Kollek, with major donations from the Bronfman family and others. A comprehensive renewal campaign completed in 2010 reorganised the main wings and entrances while preserving the original modernist campus design by Alfred Mansfeld and Dora Gad.
