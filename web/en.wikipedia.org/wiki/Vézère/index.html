Vézère

The Vézère is a 211-kilometre river in southwestern France, a right-bank tributary of the Dordogne. It rises in the Massif Central on the plateau of Millevaches and flows through Corrèze and Dordogne departments, joining the Dordogne at Limeuil.

The Vézère valley

The river's lower valley is world-famous for its concentration of prehistoric sites, many inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979 as the "Prehistoric Sites and Decorated Caves of the Vézère Valley." They include the Lascaux cave paintings near Montignac, the Cro-Magnon rock shelter at Les Eyzies — which gave its name to early modern humans — and the engravings of La Madeleine, Combarelles, and Font-de-Gaume.

Tourism and geography

The limestone cliffs, cave dwellings such as La Roque Saint-Christophe, and medieval villages along the river make the valley a major destination for heritage tourism and canoeing, within the Périgord Noir region.
