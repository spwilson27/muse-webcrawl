Western Yugur (also called Yoğur or Sarı Yoğur, "Yellow Uyghur") is a Turkic language spoken by part of the Yugur people in Sunan Yugur Autonomous County in Gansu Province, China. It is unusual among Turkic languages for having preserved many features of Old Uyghur, the language of the medieval Uyghur Khaganate, from which the Western Yugurs are partly descended.

Classification and features

Western Yugur belongs to the Siberian Turkic group but is an outlier geographically, separated from its Siberian relatives by thousands of kilometres. It has developed a tone-like system of preaspiration distinctions and conserves archaic vocabulary and morphology. The other Yugur language, Eastern Yugur, is Mongolic rather than Turkic, reflecting the mixed origins of the Yugur ethnic group.

Status

With only a few thousand speakers, and younger generations shifting to Mandarin Chinese, Western Yugur is considered severely endangered. It has no widely used written form; scholarly work uses phonetic transcription.
