486958 Arrokoth (provisional designation 2014 MU69, formerly nicknamed Ultima Thule) is a trans-Neptunian object in the Kuiper belt. It is a contact binary of two planetesimals joined at a narrow neck. On 1 January 2019 NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past it, making Arrokoth the most distant object ever visited by a spacecraft, at about 6.5 billion kilometres (43.4 AU) from the Sun.

Discovery and naming

Arrokoth was discovered in 2014 during a Hubble Space Telescope search for a post-Pluto target for New Horizons. The name Arrokoth, a word meaning "sky" in the Powhatan language, was formally approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2019, replacing the team's informal nickname Ultima Thule.

Characteristics

The object is a flattened, bilobed body about 36 km long, formed when two smaller bodies — nicknamed Wenu and Weeyo — spiralled together and merged gently at a speed of only a few metres per second. Its surface is notably red, coloured by tholins produced by radiation processing of organic compounds and ices; methanol ice has been detected. Because Arrokoth has likely remained almost undisturbed in the cold classical Kuiper belt since the Solar System formed, it is considered a pristine planetesimal — a preserved building block of the planets.

New Horizons flyby

New Horizons passed within about 3,500 km of Arrokoth, returning images and spectra that revealed a surprisingly smooth surface with few craters, uniform colour between the lobes, and a very slow formation merger — evidence favouring formation by gravitational collapse of a cloud of pebbles rather than violent collisions.
