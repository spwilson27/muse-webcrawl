Paweł Aleksander Pawlikowski (born 15 September 1957) is a Polish filmmaker. He garnered early acclaim for a string of documentaries made for the BBC in the 1990s and achieved wide recognition for the feature films Last Resort (2000) and My Summer of Love (2004). His film Ida (2013) won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film in 2015 — the first Polish film to do so — and Cold War (2018) won him the Best Director prize at Cannes and three Academy Award nominations, including Best Director.

Life

Pawlikowski was born in Warsaw. His mother was a ballet dancer who later taught English literature; his father was a doctor. He left Poland at fourteen, living in Germany and Italy before settling in Britain, where he studied literature and philosophy at Oxford. After teaching, he joined the BBC, making documentaries on post-communist Eastern Europe, including From Moscow to Pietushki (1990), Dostoevsky's Travels (1991), Serbian Epics (1992), and Tripping with Zhirinovsky (1994).

Feature films

His narrative features in Britain, Last Resort and My Summer of Love — the latter a BAFTA winner notable for performances by Paddy Considine, Emily Blunt, and Natalie Press — established his reputation. Personal tragedy interrupted his career; he returned with The Woman in the Fifth (2011) and then, working again in Poland and in black and white, made Ida, about a young novice nun in 1960s Poland who discovers she is Jewish. Cold War, a doomed love story spanning the postwar decades in Poland and Paris, inspired partly by his parents' relationship, consolidated his standing as one of Europe's leading directors.

Style

Pawlikowski's films are marked by spare, elliptical storytelling, carefully composed static frames, and a blending of documentary observation with stylised imagery.
