Computer graphics

A ray-traced rendering.

Computer graphics deals with generating images and art with the aid of computers. The field encompasses the algorithms and representations used to synthesize, manipulate, and display visual information: 2D drawing and image processing, 3D modeling and rendering, animation, and interactive techniques. Major applications include video games, film visual effects, computer-aided design, medical imaging, scientific visualization, and graphical user interfaces.

History

The discipline began in the 1950s–60s with displays such as Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad (1963). Key developments included hidden-surface algorithms and shading models in the 1970s (Gouraud and Phong shading), the founding of studios and research groups that produced landmark imagery, and the growth of dedicated graphics hardware (GPUs). Pixar's Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature film; by the 2000s real-time 3D graphics had become ubiquitous in games and interfaces.

Core concepts

Images may be represented as raster (pixel) or vector data. 3D scenes are typically modeled as meshes of polygons or with parametric surfaces, transformed by matrices through a rendering pipeline, and shaded using lighting models; global illumination methods such as ray tracing and radiosity simulate light transport for photorealism. Textures, bump mapping, and physically based rendering refine surface appearance, while animation uses keyframes, simulation, and motion capture.

Subfields

  • Rendering (real-time and offline)
  • Geometric modeling and procedural generation
  • Animation and simulation (cloth, fluids, particles)
  • Image processing and computational photography
  • Visualization and human–computer interaction
