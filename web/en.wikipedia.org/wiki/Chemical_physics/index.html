Chemical physics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Molecular energy-level diagram of the kind used in spectroscopy]

Chemical physics is a branch of physics that studies chemical processes from the point of view of physics, applying the theories and methods of atomic and molecular physics, condensed matter physics, and quantum mechanics to chemical systems. Whereas physical chemistry traditionally approaches chemistry through thermodynamics, kinetics, and bulk properties, chemical physics tends to focus on the quantum-mechanical description of individual molecules and their interactions.

Scope

Typical topics include molecular spectroscopy and the determination of molecular structure; reaction dynamics, including the study of elementary reactions with crossed molecular beams and ultrafast (femtosecond) laser techniques; the quantum theory of atoms and molecules and intermolecular forces; and the physics of surfaces, clusters, and nanomaterials. The field overlaps heavily with quantum chemistry and computational chemistry, which model chemical systems by solving approximations to the Schrödinger equation.

History and journals

The discipline crystallised in the early twentieth century as quantum mechanics was applied to the chemical bond — notably in the work of Heitler and London on the hydrogen molecule (1927). The Journal of Chemical Physics, founded in 1933, remains a flagship publication of the field, alongside journals such as Chemical Physics Letters and Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics.
