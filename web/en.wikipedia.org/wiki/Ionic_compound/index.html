Ionic compound

[Image: The cubic lattice of sodium chloride — the archetypal ionic compound]

Overview

In chemistry, an ionic compound is a chemical compound composed of ions held together by electrostatic forces termed ionic bonding. The compound is overall neutral, consisting of positively charged cations and negatively charged anions — typically a metal and a nonmetal, such as sodium chloride (NaCl), or polyatomic ions such as ammonium nitrate.

Properties

Ionic compounds form extended crystal lattices rather than discrete molecules, have high melting and boiling points, are hard and brittle, and conduct electricity when molten or dissolved (as electrolytes) but not as solids, since the ions are locked in place. They are often soluble in water. The strong lattice energy explains their stability; the Fajans rules describe the (partial) covalent character that polarising ions introduce. Salts are the largest class of ionic compounds.
