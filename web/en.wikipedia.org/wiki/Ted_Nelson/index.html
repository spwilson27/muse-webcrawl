Ted Nelson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Ted Nelson
BornTheodor Holm Nelson, 17 June 1937, New York City
Known forCoining 'hypertext' and 'hypermedia'; Project Xanadu
Notable workComputer Lib/Dream Machines (1974)
[Image: Portrait photograph of Ted Nelson in the 1970s with long hair, beside a copy of Computer Lib/Dream Machines]

Theodor Holm Nelson (born 1937) is an American pioneer of information technology, philosopher and sociologist. He coined the terms hypertext and hypermedia in the 1960s, and founded Project Xanadu in 1960 — the first hypertext project, whose ambitions long predated, and in some respects still exceed, the World Wide Web that eventually succeeded.

Nelson's Xanadu design insisted on features the web omitted: links that never break and point both ways, transclusion (quotation by live reference, with provenance), version management, and built-in micropayments so authors would be compensated when their work was quoted. His self-published 1974 book Computer Lib/Dream Machines — two books back to back — evangelised personal computing with the rallying cry 'You can and must understand computers NOW'.

Xanadu itself became legendary for delay: development began in 1960, passed through many teams and a period under Autodesk's funding, and a version of the code was released only in 2014, decades after the simpler, 'worse is better' web of Tim Berners-Lee had won. Nelson has remained a sharp critic of the web's one-way, breakable links and of computing conventions — files, hierarchies, the desktop metaphor — that he regards as historical accidents frozen in place.
