Mean aerodynamic chord

The mean aerodynamic chord (MAC) is a characteristic chord length used in aerodynamics to represent the aerodynamic properties of a wing of arbitrary planform. It is the chord of an equivalent rectangular wing that produces approximately the same pitching moments and lift characteristics as the actual wing, and it provides the reference length against which an aircraft's center of gravity position and longitudinal static margin are measured—commonly expressed as a percentage of MAC (%MAC).

Definition

For a wing of area S and span b, with local chord c(y) varying along the spanwise coordinate y, the mean aerodynamic chord is defined as:

MAC = (2 / S) ∫ c(y)² dy, integrated over the half-span.

Because the integrand weights the chord by its own length, the MAC emphasizes the portions of the wing where chord is largest—usually near the root for tapered wings. The longitudinal position of the MAC's leading edge (LEMAC) must also be computed so that moments and the center of gravity can be referenced to it.

Use in practice

Aircraft loading and balance documentation specifies the allowable center-of-gravity range in %MAC: for example, a transport aircraft might have a CG envelope of roughly 15–35% MAC across its loading conditions. The static margin—the distance between the CG and the neutral point, divided by MAC—determines longitudinal static stability; a positive static margin means the aircraft tends to return to its trimmed attitude after a disturbance.

Related quantities

The standard mean chord (SMC) is simply wing area divided by span, a purely geometric average that, unlike the MAC, ignores the chord distribution's aerodynamic weighting. For a rectangular wing, MAC and SMC coincide.
