Trichuriasis, also known as whipworm infection, is an infection by the parasitic worm Trichuris trichiura (human whipworm). If the infection is with only a few worms, there are often no symptoms. In those infested with many worms, there may be abdominal pain, tiredness and diarrhoea. The diarrhoea sometimes contains blood. Infections in children may cause poor intellectual and physical development. Low red blood cell levels may occur due to loss of blood.

Cause and transmission

The disease usually spreads when people eat food or drink water that contains the eggs of the worms. This may occur when contaminated vegetables are not fully cleaned or cooked. Often the eggs are found in soil in areas where human faeces are used as fertilizer or where open defecation takes place. The characteristic barrel-shaped eggs require two to four weeks in warm, moist soil to embryonate and become infective. Adult worms live in the caecum and ascending colon, with their thin anterior ends threaded into the mucosa.

Signs and symptoms

Light infections are usually asymptomatic. Heavy infections can cause Trichuris dysentery syndrome, with chronic bloody diarrhoea, tenesmus, iron-deficiency anaemia, growth stunting in children and, in severe cases, rectal prolapse.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by identifying the characteristic lemon- or barrel-shaped eggs with bipolar plugs in stool samples under the microscope. Treatment is with anthelmintics such as mebendazole or albendazole, typically over three days; cure rates for single doses are relatively low, and combination therapy with ivermectin improves efficacy.

Epidemiology and prevention

Trichuriasis is one of the soil-transmitted helminthiases and a neglected tropical disease, infecting several hundred million people worldwide, mostly in tropical and subtropical regions with poor sanitation. Prevention involves improved sanitation, hand washing, and wearing shoes; mass drug administration in endemic areas is a mainstay of control programmes coordinated by the World Health Organization.
