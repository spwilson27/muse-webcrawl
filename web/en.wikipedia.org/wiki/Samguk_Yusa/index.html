Samguk Yusa

The Samguk Yusa (삼국유사; 三國遺事), or Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms, is a collection of legends, folktales and historical accounts of the Three Kingdoms of Korea (Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla), together with material on earlier and later periods. It was compiled in the late 13th century by the Buddhist monk Iryeon (1206–1289) during the Goryeo dynasty. Unlike the official, Confucian-oriented history Samguk Sagi (1145), the Samguk Yusa preserves myths and Buddhist tales, and is the earliest extant source for the Dangun founding legend of Korea.

Contents

The work is organised into nine sections: chronicles of kings and wonders of the Three Kingdoms; records of marvels; the rise of Buddhism and tales of temples and pagodas; accounts of eminent monks; hymns (including some of the oldest surviving hyangga poems, an invaluable source for Old Korean language); and sections on filial piety and recluses. It records the legend of Dangun Wanggeom, who is said to have founded Gojoseon in 2333 BC, born of the union of the heavenly prince Hwanung and a bear transformed into a woman — a myth central to Korean national identity.

Transmission and significance

No autograph copy survives; the oldest extant printed editions are from the early Joseon period (the earliest fragment is dated 1394). Because it supplements the official history with popular tradition and Buddhist material, the Samguk Yusa is considered one of the two indispensable primary sources for ancient Korean history and mythology, and it has been designated part of Korea's documentary heritage.
