River Orwell

The River Orwell flows through the county of Suffolk in England from Ipswich to Felixstowe, where it meets the sea. Above Ipswich the river is known as the River Gipping, the source of the name of the borough; below the town the tidal section takes the name Orwell.

Course and crossings

The estuary runs for about 11 miles (18 km) from Ipswich to the North Sea, joining with the River Stour at Harwich harbour. It is spanned near Ipswich by the Orwell Bridge, a concrete box-girder bridge carrying the A14, completed in 1982 and among the longest of its kind in the country. The picturesque banks — wooded on the south shore around Nacton and Pin Mill — are within the Suffolk Coast and Heaths Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Use and associations

The Orwell is a celebrated sailing river, with marinas at Ipswich, Woolverstone, Suffolk Yacht Harbour and Pin Mill, the setting of Arthur Ransome's novel We Didn't Mean to Go to Sea. The river also gave the writer Eric Blair his pen name: he took "George Orwell" from the river while living at Southwold. At its mouth, the Port of Felixstowe is Britain's busiest container port.
