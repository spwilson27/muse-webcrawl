Book discussion club

A book discussion club is an organization, often subscription-based, in which members receive books and discuss them, or a reading group in which participants regularly meet to talk about a shared text. The term has been applied both to commercial subscription clubs that distribute selected books by mail and to community reading groups.

Subscription clubs

The Book-of-the-Month Club, founded in the United States in 1926 by Harry Scherman, popularized the model in which a panel of judges selected a featured book each month and shipped it to subscribers unless they declined it. Similar enterprises, such as the Literary Guild, and later specialty and children's clubs, shaped middlebrow reading culture and the marketing of books through much of the 20th century.

Reading groups

In its community sense, a book discussion club is equivalent to a book club or reading group: a small group that agrees on a title, reads it, and meets to discuss it, often with the aid of publisher-supplied discussion guides. Libraries frequently support such clubs by supplying multiple copies of chosen titles in "book club kits."
