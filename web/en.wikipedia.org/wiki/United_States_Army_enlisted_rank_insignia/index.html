United States Army enlisted rank insignia

The chart below shows the current enlisted insignia of the United States Army, worn by soldiers in the pay grades E-1 through E-9. Enlisted soldiers are divided into junior enlisted (E-1 to E-4), noncommissioned officers (E-5 to E-9), and senior noncommissioned officers.

Insignia design

Noncommissioned officer insignia consist of upward chevrons, with curved "rockers" below indicating senior grades, and a star or other device in the center for the most senior ranks. The Sergeant Major of the Army, the senior enlisted adviser to the Chief of Staff, wears a distinctive insignia incorporating the Army's coat of arms.

History

Army chevrons date to the early 19th century. During the Civil War, elaborate insignia distinguished noncommissioned officers of different branches. The modern system of chevrons pointing upward was standardized in 1902, and the specialist ranks evolved through the twentieth century; most technical specialist grades were abolished in 1985, leaving the single Specialist grade at E-4.
