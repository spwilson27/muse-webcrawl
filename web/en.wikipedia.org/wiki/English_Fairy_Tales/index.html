English Fairy Tales

English Fairy Tales
Editor: Joseph Jacobs
Illustrator: John D. Batten
Published: 1890
Publisher: David Nutt
Genre: Folklore collection

English Fairy Tales is a collection of forty-one folk and fairy tales compiled by the folklorist Joseph Jacobs and published in 1890, with illustrations by John D. Batten. It was followed in 1894 by More English Fairy Tales, containing a further forty-four stories. Together the two volumes established the canon of English fairy tales for generations of readers and included the versions of "Jack and the Beanstalk", "Henny Penny", "The Three Little Pigs" and "Jack the Giant Killer" by which they are best known today.

Background

Jacobs, an Australian-born scholar active in the Folk-Lore Society, modelled his work on the Brothers Grimm and on Andrew Lang's fairy books, but aimed to give England its own collection, complaining that English children read French and German tales while their native stories went uncollected. He gathered material from chapbooks, earlier antiquarian collections and oral sources, rewriting the tales in a lively, colloquial style intended to be read aloud, while appending scholarly notes on sources and parallels.

Contents

The 1890 volume includes "Tom Tit Tot" (the English Rumpelstiltskin), "Molly Whuppie", "Cap o' Rushes" (a Cinderella variant), "Dick Whittington and His Cat", "The Golden Arm", "Childe Rowland" and "Mr. Fox". Jacobs deliberately included comic and cumulative tales alongside wonder tales, and his notes — often longer than the tales themselves — remain a resource for folklorists.

Influence

The collections went through many editions and were central to the late Victorian folklore revival. Jacobs's accessible retellings shaped the standard English versions of tales that might otherwise have been lost, and his editorial methods influenced later collectors of national tale traditions.
