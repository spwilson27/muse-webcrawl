Heegaard Floer homology

Heegaard Floer homology is a topological invariant of closed 3-manifolds, introduced by Peter Ozsváth and Zoltán Szabó in the early 2000s. It is the homology of a chain complex built from a Heegaard diagram of the manifold, counting holomorphic disks in a symmetric product of the Heegaard surface, and is a three-dimensional analogue of the Lagrangian Floer homology developed by Andreas Floer. Its Euler characteristic recovers the Reidemeister torsion of the manifold.

Variants

The theory comes in several versions, conventionally written HF∞, HF+, HF− and the "hat" version ĤF, related by exact sequences. Each is defined relative to a spinc structure on the manifold. Associated constructions include knot Floer homology (an invariant of knots, due to Ozsváth–Szabó and independently Jacob Rasmussen), link Floer homology, and versions for manifolds with boundary used in gluing theorems.

Applications

Heegaard Floer homology has had wide-ranging applications in low-dimensional topology. It detects the genus of knots and the Thurston norm of 3-manifolds, detects fibred manifolds and knots, obstructs certain manifolds from bounding rational homology balls, and provides obstructions to knots being slice, including information about the concordance group. Ozsváth and Szabó used it to prove that the Floer homology of a 3-manifold determines its Heegaard genus bounds and, relatedly, it gives a proof of the Poincaré homology sphere's properties in surgery theory.

Relation to other theories

Heegaard Floer homology is conjecturally — and in many cases demonstrably — related to Seiberg–Witten Floer homology and embedded contact homology; Kutluhan, Lee and Taubes proved the equivalence with Seiberg–Witten Floer cohomology. It is one of the principal tools of modern contact and symplectic topology in dimension three.
