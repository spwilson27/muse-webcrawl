United States Code

U.S. Code
First edition: 1926
Titles: 54
Prepared by: Office of the Law Revision Counsel

The United States Code (U.S.C.) is the official compilation and codification of the general and permanent federal statutes of the United States. It is prepared by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel of the United States House of Representatives and is published every six years, with annual cumulative supplements.

Structure

The Code is divided into 54 titles covering broad subject areas, such as Title 11 (Bankruptcy), Title 18 (Crimes and Criminal Procedure), Title 26 (Internal Revenue Code), Title 35 (Patents), and Title 42 (The Public Health and Welfare). Each title is divided into chapters and sections; the standard citation form is, for example, 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

Codification

When Congress enacts a law, it is first published as a slip law and then in the United States Statutes at Large. The Office of the Law Revision Counsel then classifies its provisions into the Code. Some titles have been enacted into positive law, making the Code itself legal evidence of the law; for other titles, the Statutes at Large control in case of discrepancy.

History

Before 1926, federal statutes were compiled in the Revised Statutes of 1873–74. The first edition of the United States Code was published in 1926, and new editions have followed at six-year intervals, supplemented annually.
