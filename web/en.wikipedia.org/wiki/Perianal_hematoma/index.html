Perianal hematoma

A perianal hematoma is a localized collection of blood outside a blood vessel under the skin around the edge of the anus. It is often loosely called a thrombosed external hemorrhoid, although it is not a true hemorrhoid: it results from the rupture of a small vein draining the anus rather than from engorgement of the hemorrhoidal plexus.

Signs and symptoms

The condition presents as a small, tense, bluish, painful lump at the anal margin, typically developing suddenly after straining, heavy lifting, or prolonged sitting. Pain is usually greatest in the first two to three days.

Treatment

Many perianal hematomas resolve on their own within about a week with conservative measures such as warm baths, stool softeners, and analgesia. When seen early and pain is severe, a clinician may drain the clot under local anesthetic through a small elliptical excision, which generally gives faster relief than simple incision or waiting. Recurrence is possible, and repeated episodes may prompt further evaluation.
