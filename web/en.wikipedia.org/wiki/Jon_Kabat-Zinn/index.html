Jon Kabat-Zinn

Jon Kabat-Zinn (born Jon Kabat, June 5, 1944) is an American professor emeritus of medicine and the creator of the Stress Reduction Clinic and the Center for Mindfulness in Medicine, Health Care, and Society at the University of Massachusetts Medical School. Kabat-Zinn was a student of Zen Buddhist teachers and integrated Buddhist mindfulness practices with Western science and medicine. In 1979 he founded the eight-week Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) program, which has been widely adopted in hospitals and clinics around the world.

Early life and education

Kabat-Zinn was born in New York City in 1944, the son of Elvin Kabat, a biomedical scientist, and Sally Kabat, a painter. He graduated from Haverford College in 1964 and earned a PhD in molecular biology in 1971 from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he studied under Salvador Luria. While at MIT he began practicing yoga and meditation, studying with teachers including Philip Kapleau and Thich Nhat Hanh.

Career

In 1979, Kabat-Zinn founded the Stress Reduction Clinic at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center, where he developed the Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction program: an eight-week course teaching patients with chronic pain and stress-related disorders to use mindfulness meditation to cope with suffering. He deliberately framed the practice in secular, scientific terms so it could be studied and used within mainstream medicine.

He founded the Center for Mindfulness in Medicine, Health Care, and Society in 1995. His research documented benefits of MBSR for chronic pain, anxiety, psoriasis, and other conditions, and MBSR became a model for mindfulness-based interventions now offered at hundreds of medical centers worldwide.
