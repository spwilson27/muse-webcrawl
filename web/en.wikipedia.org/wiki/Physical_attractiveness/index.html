Physical attractiveness

Physical attractiveness is the degree to which a person's physical features are considered aesthetically pleasing or beautiful. The term often implies sexual attractiveness or desirability, but can also be distinct from either. Judgements of attractiveness draw on facial and bodily features, grooming and dress, and are influenced by both shared biological preferences and cultural norms.

Perceptions of beauty combine universal cues with cultural standards.

Determinants

Research in evolutionary psychology associates attractiveness with cues of health and fertility. Frequently studied factors include facial symmetry and averageness, clear skin, sexual dimorphism (masculinity or femininity of features), body proportions such as waist–hip ratio, height, hair, and signs of youth. Preferences also vary with ecology, fashion and individual experience, and within-culture agreement on who is attractive is nonetheless high.

Social effects

Attractive people tend to be judged more positively on unrelated traits — the "halo effect" or "what is beautiful is good" stereotype — and studies report advantages in hiring, earnings, sentencing and social interaction, sometimes called a "beauty premium". These advantages raise ethical questions about lookism and discrimination.

Cultural variation

Standards of beauty differ across societies and eras: ideals of body weight, skin tone, hair, cosmetics and adornment (including practices such as tattooing, scarification and body modification) have varied widely. Global media have been argued both to homogenise standards and to diversify them.
