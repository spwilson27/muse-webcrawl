The One-Straw Revolution: An Introduction to Natural Farming (Japanese: 自然農法・わら一本の革命) is a 1975 book by Japanese farmer and philosopher Masanobu Fukuoka. Translated into more than 25 languages, it is a foundational text of the natural farming and permaculture movements.

Content

Fukuoka describes the system of farming he developed on his farm on the island of Shikoku, based on what he called "do-nothing farming": no tilling, no chemical fertilisers, no prepared compost, no weeding by tillage or herbicides, and no dependence on machinery or pesticides. Grain is grown by broadcasting seed in balls of clay (seed balls) among standing crops, with straw from the previous crop returned to the fields as mulch. Fukuoka reported yields comparable to those of conventional Japanese farms.

Beyond its practical farming advice, the book is a philosophical manifesto, drawing on Zen Buddhism and Taoism, criticising industrial agriculture and the fragmented, analytical character of modern scientific knowledge. Fukuoka argues that nature, left largely to itself with minimal human intervention, produces abundance, and that farming should serve spiritual as well as material cultivation.

Influence

The book's English translation, edited by Larry Korn and published in 1978, strongly influenced the development of permaculture; its co-originator Bill Mollison visited Fukuoka's farm and incorporated his ideas. The work remains widely read among advocates of organic and regenerative agriculture.
