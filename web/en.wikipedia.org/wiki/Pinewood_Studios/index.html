Pinewood Studios is a British film and television studio located in Iver Heath, Buckinghamshire, England, about 18 miles (29 km) west of central London. It is one of the oldest and largest film studios in the United Kingdom and is closely associated with the James Bond franchise, most of whose films have been shot there.

History

The studio was built in 1935–36 by construction magnate Charles Boot on the Heatherden Hall estate, financed with the involvement of J. Arthur Rank, whose Rank Organisation came to control it. The first film completed was London Melody (1937). During the Second World War the studios were requisitioned, among other things as a base for the Crown Film Unit.

Stages

Pinewood has more than twenty stages. The most famous is the 007 Stage, built in 1976 for The Spy Who Loved Me and for decades one of the largest sound stages in the world; it burned down in 1984 and again in 2006 and was rebuilt each time. The studio also has a permanent underwater filming stage and large backlots.

Productions

Besides Bond films, notable productions include the Carry On series, Superman (1978), Alien (1979), Batman (1989), and many recent Marvel and Star Wars films. The Pinewood Group has expanded to other sites in the UK and internationally.
