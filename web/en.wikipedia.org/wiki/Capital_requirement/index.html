Capital requirement

Capital requirements
Framework: Basel Accords (Basel III)
Minimum CAR: 8% of risk-weighted assets

A capital requirement (also known as regulatory capital or capital adequacy) is the amount of capital a bank or other financial institution must hold as required by its financial regulator, usually expressed as a capital adequacy ratio of equity to risk-weighted assets.

Purpose

Requirements ensure institutions can absorb a reasonable level of loss, protect depositors, and maintain financial stability without becoming insolvent.

Basel framework

Under Basel III, banks must hold total capital of at least 8% of risk-weighted assets, including Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) of at least 4.5%, plus a capital conservation buffer of 2.5%, countercyclical buffers, and surcharges for systemically important banks. Tier 1 and Tier 2 capital differ in loss-absorbing capacity.

Criticism

Economists debate whether risk weighting invites gaming, whether requirements are procyclical, and whether much higher equity requirements — proposed by Anat Admati and others — would be safer at little social cost.
