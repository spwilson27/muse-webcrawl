Snail darter controversy

The snail darter and the Tellico Dam construction site

The snail darter controversy was a famous legal and political dispute in the United States over the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (ESA). The discovery of the snail darter (Percina tanasi), a small fish, in the Little Tennessee River halted completion of the Tennessee Valley Authority's Tellico Dam and produced a landmark Supreme Court ruling, Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill (1978).

Background

In 1973 zoologist David Etnier discovered the previously unknown snail darter in the Little Tennessee River, near the nearly complete Tellico Dam. The fish was listed as an endangered species in 1975, and its only known habitat was designated critical habitat. Law student Hiram Hill and other plaintiffs sued the TVA, arguing that finishing the dam would violate the ESA by destroying the species' habitat.

Supreme Court ruling

In TVA v. Hill, 437 U.S. 153 (1978), the Supreme Court held 6–3 that the plain language of the ESA required halting the dam, whatever the sunk costs: Congress had intended to halt and reverse the trend toward species extinction "whatever the cost." The case became the classic example of the ESA's strict protections.

Aftermath

Congress responded by amending the ESA to create the Endangered Species Committee (the "God Squad"), which in 1979 nevertheless denied the dam an exemption. Later that year Senator John Culver and Representative John Duncan attached a rider to an appropriations bill exempting Tellico Dam from the Act, and President Jimmy Carter signed it. The dam was completed and its gates closed in 1979. Snail darters were transplanted to other rivers, additional populations were discovered, and the species recovered: it was downlisted to threatened in 1984 and removed from the endangered species list entirely in 2022, with Tellico Lake coexisting with surviving populations.
