Hoa Lư

Hoa Lư is a district of Ninh Bình Province in the Red River Delta region of Vietnam. It is famous as the location of the ancient capital of Vietnam in the 10th and early 11th centuries, under the Đinh and Early Lê dynasties.

History

In 968, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh unified the country after the turmoil following the collapse of Chinese rule and founded the Đinh dynasty, choosing Hoa Lư as the capital of the new kingdom of Đại Cồ Việt. The site, ringed by limestone mountains and rivers, offered natural defences. The capital remained at Hoa Lư under the Early Lê dynasty until 1010, when Lý Công Uẩn moved it to Thăng Long (modern Hanoi).

Heritage

Today the ancient citadel area contains temples dedicated to the emperors Đinh Tiên Hoàng and Lê Đại Hành, and lies close to the Tràng An landscape complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its karst scenery and archaeological remains.
