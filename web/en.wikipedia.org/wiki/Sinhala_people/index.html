Overview

The Sinhala people are an Indo-Aryan ethnic group and the largest ethnic group in Sri Lanka, native to the island. They speak Sinhala, an Indo-Aryan language (in the Indic branch) whose presence in an island off Dravidian South India records their ancestors' migration from northern India — a story mythologised in the chronicle Mahāvaṃsa as the coming of Vijaya from Sinhapura around the traditional date of the Buddha's death (543 or 483 BC).

History and society

Sinhala civilisation built the hydraulic kingdoms of Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa, with vast irrigation tanks and dagobas; Theravada Buddhism, brought (by tradition) by Mahinda, son of Ashoka, in the 3rd century BC, is central to Sinhala identity and constitutionally foremost in Sri Lanka. Centuries of South Indian invasions and the Tamil kingdoms of the north and east framed a long, complex coexistence with Sri Lankan Tamils, which collapsed into civil war (1983–2009) between the state and the LTTE — its legacies remain unresolved in politics today. Most Sinhalese are Buddhist, with a significant Catholic and Christian minority on the coasts.
