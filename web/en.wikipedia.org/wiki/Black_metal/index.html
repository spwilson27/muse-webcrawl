Black metal

Stylistic originsThrash metal, death metal, hardcore punk, first-wave heavy metal
Cultural originsEarly 1980s (first wave); early 1990s Norway (second wave)
Typical elementsFast tempos, shrieked vocals, tremolo-picked guitars, blast beats, lo-fi production, corpse paint
SubgenresSymphonic black metal, atmospheric black metal, blackgaze, war metal, NSBM (controversial)

Black metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music. Common traits include fast tempos, high-pitched shrieked vocals, heavily distorted guitars played with tremolo picking, blast-beat drumming, raw (lo-fi) recording, and unconventional song structures. Lyrics and imagery frequently deal with anti-Christian, Satanic, pagan, occult, and misanthropic themes, and performers often appear in corpse paint.

First wave

The term comes from Venom's 1982 album Black Metal. The first wave of the 1980s comprised bands such as Venom (England), Bathory (Sweden), Hellhammer and Celtic Frost (Switzerland), Mercyful Fate (Denmark), and Sarcófago (Brazil), whose raw thrash- and death-influenced sound and dark imagery laid the genre's foundations.

Second wave

In the early 1990s, a Norwegian scene crystallised around bands including Mayhem, Darkthrone, Burzum, Immortal, Emperor, Satyricon, and Gorgoroth, defining the canonical black metal sound and aesthetic. The scene gained international notoriety for a series of church arsons (including the Fantoft Stave Church in 1992), and for violent crimes, notably the 1993 murder of Mayhem guitarist Euronymous by Varg Vikernes of Burzum. The Helvete record shop in Oslo served as the scene's hub.

Development and subgenres

From the mid-1990s the genre diversified: symphonic black metal (Emperor, Dimmu Borgir, Cradle of Filth — the latter two achieving commercial success), atmospheric and ambient styles, folk-influenced pagan metal, the depressive subgenre, and later fusions such as blackgaze (Alcest, Deafheaven). Black metal scenes have since developed worldwide, from France, Poland, and Greece to the United States, while the genre's imagery and controversy have made it one of metal's most studied subcultures.
