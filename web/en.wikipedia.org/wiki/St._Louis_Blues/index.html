St. Louis Blues

The St. Louis Blues are a professional ice hockey team based in St. Louis, Missouri. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Central Division in the Western Conference. The franchise was founded in 1967 as one of the six teams of the 1967 NHL expansion, and the team plays its home games at the Enterprise Center. The team is named after the famous W. C. Handy song "Saint Louis Blues".

History

The Blues were one of the strongest expansion teams in their early years, reaching the Stanley Cup Finals in each of their first three seasons (1968, 1969, 1970), though they were swept each time. The club endured financial instability in the late 1970s but became a consistent playoff team in the 1980s and 1990s, featuring stars such as Brett Hull, Adam Oates, Al MacInnis, and Chris Pronger, and winning the Presidents' Trophy in 1999–2000.

2019 Stanley Cup

After the longest wait in NHL history at the time, the Blues won their first Stanley Cup in 2019, defeating the Boston Bruins in seven games. Remarkably, the team had been in last place in the league standings in early January 2019 before a remarkable turnaround under interim coach Craig Berube and rookie goaltender Jordan Binnington. Ryan O'Reilly won the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP. The victory song "Gloria", adopted midseason, became the team's anthem.
