John André

Born: 2 May 1750, London, England

Died: 2 October 1780 (aged 30), Tappan, New York

Cause of death: Execution by hanging

Allegiance: Great Britain

Rank: Major

John André (2 May 1750 – 2 October 1780) was a British Army officer who served as head of the British intelligence service in America during the American Revolutionary War. He was hanged as a spy by the Continental Army for his role in Benedict Arnold's plot to surrender the fort at West Point.

Career

Born in London to a Genevan merchant family, André joined the British Army in 1771 and served in Canada and the colonies. A talented artist, poet and linguist, he became a favourite in occupied Philadelphia society and, from 1779, adjutant-general and intelligence chief to Sir Henry Clinton in New York.

Capture and death

In September 1780 André travelled up the Hudson to meet Arnold and receive the plans of West Point. Forced to return overland in civilian dress, he was captured near Tarrytown with the papers hidden in his boot. A board of officers convicted him of espionage despite widespread sympathy for his charm and honour. Washington refused requests that he be shot as a soldier rather than hanged as a spy, and André was executed at Tappan on 2 October 1780. In 1821 his remains were moved to Westminster Abbey, where a monument commemorates him.
