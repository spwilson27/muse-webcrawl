Cordillera Occidental (Colombia)

Country: Colombia

Highest point: Cerro Tatamá

Elevation: approx. 4,100 m (13,500 ft)

Length: about 1,200 km (north–south)

Parent range: Andes

The Cordillera Occidental ("Western Cordillera") is the westernmost of the three main ranges into which the Andes split in Colombia, the others being the Cordillera Central and the Cordillera Oriental. It runs roughly north–south for about 1,200 kilometres from the Nudo de los Pastos in the department of Nariño, near the Ecuadorian border, to the Nudo de Paramillo in northern Antioquia and Córdoba, where it subsides into the lowlands of the Caribbean plain.

Geography

The range separates the valley of the Cauca River (to its east, between it and the Cordillera Central) from the Pacific coastal lowlands drained by rivers such as the San Juan, Atrato (in its northern basin), Patía, and Mira. It is lower on average than the Central Cordillera, with most summits between 2,000 and 4,100 metres; among its highest points are Cerro Tatamá (about 4,100 m), the Farallones de Cali, and the Farallones de Citará. The Western Cordillera passes through the departments of Nariño, Cauca, Valle del Cauca, Chocó, Risaralda, and Antioquia.

Climate and ecology

The western slopes facing the Pacific intercept some of the heaviest rainfall in the world in the Chocó region, sustaining extremely biodiverse lowland and montane rainforests, including the Chocó–Darién moist forests ecoregion and cloud forests rich in orchids, frogs, and endemic birds. Protected areas wholly or partly in the range include Tatamá National Natural Park, Farallones de Cali National Natural Park, and Paramillo National Natural Park, as well as the Munchique and Las Orquídeas parks.

Geology

The Cordillera Occidental consists largely of Cretaceous oceanic basement rocks — basalts and deep-marine sediments of an accreted oceanic plateau related to the Caribbean large igneous province — uplifted during the ongoing collision of the Panama–Chocó block and the Nazca Plate with South America. It forms part of Colombia's gold- and platinum-bearing western metallogenic belt; placer deposits in Chocó rivers were historically major sources of platinum.
