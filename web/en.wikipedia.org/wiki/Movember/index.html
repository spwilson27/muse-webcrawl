Movember (a portmanteau of moustache and November) is an annual event in which participants grow moustaches during the month of November to raise awareness of men's health issues, including prostate cancer, testicular cancer and men's mental health and suicide prevention. The Movember movement is run by the Movember Foundation, a charity founded in Australia in 2003.

History

The idea began in 2003 in Melbourne, where Travis Garone and Luke Slattery joked about reviving the moustache in fashion and challenged friends to grow one for charity. In the first year, thirty men took part. By 2004 the group had formalised fundraising for prostate cancer in Australia, and the campaign spread to New Zealand, then to North America and Europe from 2007 in partnership with prostate cancer charities. It has since run in more than twenty countries.

How it works

Participants — known as "Mo Bros" and "Mo Sisters" — register at the start of November, begin clean-shaven, and grow and groom a moustache for the month, collecting sponsorship. The moustache is meant as a conversation starter about men's health. The foundation also runs the "Move" physical-activity challenge for those who cannot or prefer not to grow a moustache. Rules traditionally prohibit beards and goatees — the moustache must stand alone.

Impact

Movember has raised well over a billion Australian dollars cumulatively since its founding and funds research and support programmes through partner organisations worldwide, as well as its own global projects in prostate cancer research, testicular cancer and suicide prevention. It helped popularise the model of humorous, low-barrier participatory fundraising later imitated by many other campaigns.
