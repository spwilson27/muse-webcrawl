Jabber

Jabber is the original name of the Extensible Messaging and Presence Protocol (XMPP), an open instant messaging technology developed by Jeremie Miller beginning in 1998 and launched in 1999. The Jabber open-source community built decentralised messaging servers that anyone could run, in deliberate contrast to the proprietary, centralised networks of the era.

Development and standardisation

The Jabber protocol was submitted to the Internet Engineering Task Force, and in 2004 its core was standardised as XMPP in RFCs 3920 and 3921. The Jabber Software Foundation became the XMPP Standards Foundation, which maintains hundreds of protocol extensions. "Jabber" remains widely used as a synonym for XMPP, and a Cisco product line (Cisco Jabber) carries the name under licence from the original trademark holder.

Use

Major early services such as Google Talk were built on XMPP and federated with public Jabber servers. Although most large consumer platforms later moved to closed protocols, XMPP and Jabber clients remain in use in open communities, gaming (early League of Legends chat), and enterprise and military messaging.
