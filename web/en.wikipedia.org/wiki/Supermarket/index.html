A supermarket is a self-service shop offering a wide variety of food, beverages, and household products, organised into sections. Larger and with a wider selection than traditional grocery stores, supermarkets typically have departments for fresh produce, meat, dairy, baked goods, and frozen foods, alongside non-food items such as cleaning supplies and toiletries.

History

The first true supermarket is generally credited to Piggly Wiggly, founded by Clarence Saunders in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1916, which introduced self-service shopping — customers selected their own goods from open shelves instead of handing a list to a clerk. The format expanded rapidly during the 1930s with chains such as King Kullen and Safeway, aided by the spread of automobiles, home refrigerators, and mass-produced packaged goods. After the Second World War, suburban supermarkets anchored shopping centres across the developed world.

Operations

Supermarkets buy in bulk and compete on thin margins, using store brands, loyalty programmes, and prominent placement of high-margin goods. Layouts typically route shoppers past fresh departments around the perimeter with packaged staples in central aisles. Very large stores combining a supermarket with a full department store are called hypermarkets; major global operators include Walmart, Tesco, Carrefour, and Aldi.
