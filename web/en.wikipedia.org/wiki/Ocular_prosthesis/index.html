Ocular prosthesis

Ocular prosthesis (placeholder image)

An ocular prosthesis, commonly known as an artificial eye or glass eye, is a type of craniofacial prosthesis that replaces an absent natural eye following an enucleation, evisceration, or orbital exenteration. The prosthesis fits over an orbital implant and under the eyelids. Though often referred to as a glass eye, the ocular prosthesis roughly takes the shape of a convex shell and is made today of medical-grade plastic acrylic.

History

Evidence of ocular prostheses dates to ancient Egypt and Rome. Venetian glassblowers developed glass eyes in the sixteenth century, and German makers in the nineteenth century perfected enamel glass eyes. Cryolite glass remains used by some makers, but most prostheses since the Second World War have been made of polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) acrylic, which is durable and well tolerated.

Fitting and care

A specialist called an ocularist custom-fits and hand-paints the prosthesis to match the remaining eye, matching the iris colour and the veining of the sclera. A related prosthesis is the scleral shell, a thin device worn over a damaged but retained eye. Prostheses are polished periodically and usually replaced every few years; they do not restore vision but improve appearance and help maintain the shape of the eye socket.
