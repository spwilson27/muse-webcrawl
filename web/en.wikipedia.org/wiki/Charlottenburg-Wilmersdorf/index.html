Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf is the fourth borough (Bezirk) of Berlin, Germany, formed on January 1, 2001, by the merger of the former boroughs of Charlottenburg and Wilmersdorf as part of Berlin's administrative reform. It covers much of the historic western city center and is among Berlin's most affluent districts, known for its elegant pre-war apartment buildings, shopping boulevards, and cultural institutions.

Geography and districts

The borough comprises seven localities (Ortsteile): Charlottenburg, Wilmersdorf, Schmargendorf, Grunewald, Westend, Charlottenburg-Nord, and Halensee. The Grunewald forest, one of Berlin's largest woodland areas, lies in the borough's southwest, while its eastern edge meets the Zoo station area in the historic heart of West Berlin.

History

Charlottenburg grew around Charlottenburg Palace, begun in 1695 for Sophie Charlotte, wife of Elector Frederick III of Brandenburg. The town of Charlottenburg was independent until its incorporation into Greater Berlin in 1920. Wilmersdorf, first mentioned in the 13th century, likewise became a prosperous residential suburb. During the Cold War, both boroughs lay in West Berlin, and the Kurfürstendamm (Ku'damm) avenue served as the showcase commercial center of the western half of the divided city.

Sights and institutions

Landmarks include Charlottenburg Palace and its gardens, the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church (Gedächtniskirche), the Deutsche Oper Berlin, the Olympic Stadium in Westend, and the Kurfürstendamm with the historic KaDeWe department store (just across the borough boundary in Schöneberg). The Technical University of Berlin and the Berlin University of the Arts are located in Charlottenburg.
