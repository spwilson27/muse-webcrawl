Dharmakaya

The dharmakāya (Sanskrit: धर्मकाय, "truth body" or "reality body") is one of the three bodies (trikāya) of a buddha in Mahāyāna Buddhism. The dharmakāya constitutes the unmanifested, "inconceivable" aspect of a buddha, out of which all buddhas and all phenomena arise and to which they return after dissolution. It is regarded as the embodiment of truth itself and of the Dharma, beyond form, attributes and dualistic concepts.

In Mahāyāna thought, the dharmakāya is the formless, universal body of truth underlying all buddhas.

The three bodies

According to the trikāya doctrine, a buddha manifests in three ways: the dharmakāya, the ultimate truth body; the saṃbhogakāya, the body of enjoyment, visible to advanced bodhisattvas in pure lands; and the nirmāṇakāya, the emanation body appearing in the world, such as the historical Gautama Buddha. The dharmakāya is often equated with emptiness (śūnyatā), suchness (tathātā), and the buddha-nature present in all beings.

Development of the concept

In early Buddhism, "dharma body" referred mainly to the Buddha's teachings and qualities as a body of doctrine. In Mahāyāna sutras such as the Lotus Sutra, the idea expanded metaphysically: the Buddha's true body is eternal and universal, identical with reality itself. Later traditions elaborate this differently — in Vajrayāna and Dzogchen, realising the dharmakāya is the goal of practice, experienced as the empty, luminous nature of mind.
