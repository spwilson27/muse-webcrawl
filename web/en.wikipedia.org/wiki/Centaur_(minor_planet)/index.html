Centaur (minor planet)

In planetary astronomy, a centaur is a small Solar System body that orbits the Sun between Jupiter and Neptune and crosses their orbits. Centaurs are named after the mythological centaurs because of their dual nature: they behave with characteristics of both asteroids and comets. Their orbits are dynamically unstable, with lifetimes of a few million years, and they are thought to be escaped Kuiper belt objects in transition toward becoming short-period comets.

Characteristics

Centaurs typically have diameters between about 10 and a few hundred kilometres. The largest known is 10199 Chariklo, about 250 km across, which was found in 2013 to possess a system of rings — the first rings discovered around a minor planet. The first centaur identified was 2060 Chiron, discovered in 1977; Chiron later developed a cometary coma and is also designated as comet 95P/Chiron. Another, 60558 Echeclus, has shown large cometary outbursts. Centaur surfaces range from neutral grey to very red, reflecting differences in exposure of ices and organic material.

Origins and fate

Numerical simulations show that centaurs originate in the Kuiper belt and scattered disc, leaking inward under the influence of Neptune. Once among the giant planets, close encounters scatter them further; roughly half are eventually ejected from the Solar System, while others are thrown inward to become Jupiter-family comets, collide with planets, or disintegrate.

Notable centaurs

  • 2060 Chiron — the first discovered, and also a comet.
  • 10199 Chariklo — the largest, with two narrow rings.
  • 5145 Pholus — an extremely red object.
  • 8405 Asbolus — shows a fresh impact feature in Hubble observations.
