Lochia

Lochia
Field: Obstetrics
Phase: Postpartum period
Duration: Typically 4–6 weeks
Stages: Rubra, serosa, alba

In the field of obstetrics, lochia is the vaginal discharge after giving birth, containing blood, mucus, and uterine tissue. Lochia discharge typically continues for four to six weeks after childbirth, a period known as the postpartum period or puerperium.

Stages

Lochia progresses through three stages. Lochia rubra, lasting roughly the first three to four days, is red and consists mainly of blood, decidua and trophoblastic debris. Lochia serosa, from about day four to day ten, becomes pinkish-brown and serous, containing less blood and more leukocytes and cervical mucus. Lochia alba, the final stage, is a whitish-yellow discharge of leukocytes and mucus that can continue for several more weeks as the uterus completes involution and the placental site heals.

Clinical significance

A foul odour, excessively heavy bleeding, large clots, or discharge that returns to bright red after fading can indicate complications such as endometritis (infection of the uterus) or retained products of conception, and warrant medical assessment.
