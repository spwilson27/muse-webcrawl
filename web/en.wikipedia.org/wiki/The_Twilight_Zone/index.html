The Twilight Zone is an American media franchise based on the anthology television series created and presented by Rod Serling, which ran on CBS from 1959 to 1964. Each episode presents a standalone story in which ordinary characters find themselves in disturbing or inexplicable situations — the eponymous "Twilight Zone" — usually concluding with a surprise twist and a moral. Serling wrote or co-wrote most of the original series' episodes, alongside writers including Richard Matheson, Charles Beaumont, and Ray Bradbury.

The original series

Serling, frustrated by network censorship of his socially conscious television dramas, turned to science fiction and fantasy as a vehicle for commentary on subjects such as racism, war, conformity, and McCarthyism. Famous episodes include "Time Enough at Last", "The Monsters Are Due on Maple Street", "Nightmare at 20,000 Feet", "Eye of the Beholder", "To Serve Man", and "It's a Good Life". Serling's on-screen introductions and closing narrations became iconic. The series won Emmy and Hugo awards and is consistently ranked among the greatest television series of all time.

Revivals and legacy

The franchise has been revived several times: a 1983 feature film, Twilight Zone: The Movie; a 1985–89 television revival; a 2002 revival hosted by Forest Whitaker; and a 2019–20 revival developed and hosted by Jordan Peele. The name has passed into the language as a term for surreal or uncanny experiences, and the series has been widely parodied and homaged. A stage production and the Disney theme-park attraction The Twilight Zone Tower of Terror also draw on the franchise.
