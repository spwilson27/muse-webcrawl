Saint Vitus (Italian: San Vito; Sicilian: Santu Vitu) was a Christian saint from Sicily, martyred as a boy during the Diocletianic Persecution around 303 AD. He is counted among the Fourteen Holy Helpers and is the patron saint of dancers, actors, and those suffering from epilepsy and nervous disorders.

Legend

According to tradition, Vitus was the son of a pagan Sicilian senator. Converted to Christianity as a child by his tutor Modestus and nurse Crescentia, he fled with them to Lucania after performing miracles and refusing to renounce his faith. Summoned to Rome, he is said to have cured the Emperor Diocletian's son of an evil spirit, but refused to sacrifice to the gods. The three were tortured — tradition says Vitus was thrown into a cauldron of boiling oil and emerged unharmed — and eventually died of their sufferings in Lucania.

Cult

Vitus's cult spread widely in medieval Europe. His relics were translated to the Abbey of Corvey in Saxony in 836, and he became one of the most popular saints of the German lands; Prague's St Vitus Cathedral, founded by Saint Wenceslaus, holds his arm relic. The nervous movement disorder chorea was called "Saint Vitus's dance", and in the late Middle Ages outbreaks of dancing mania were associated with his shrines. He is patron of Prague, Sicily, and of dancers and entertainers.
