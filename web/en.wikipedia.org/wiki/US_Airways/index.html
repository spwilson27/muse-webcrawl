US Airways

US Airways was a major American airline that operated from 1937 until 2015, when it merged with American Airlines. It was originally founded in Pittsburgh as a mail delivery company under the name All American Aviation, and later became Allegheny Airlines and then USAir before adopting the US Airways name in 1997. At its peak the airline served nearly 200 destinations in the Americas and Europe and was a member of the Star Alliance and later Oneworld.

History

All American Aviation began airmail service in 1939 and passenger service as Allegheny Airlines in 1953. After deregulation it expanded rapidly, rebranding as USAir in 1979 and acquiring Piedmont Airlines and Pacific Southwest Airlines (PSA). The airline suffered a series of fatal accidents in the late 1980s and 1990s, and filed for bankruptcy twice in the early 2000s. In 2005 it merged with America West Airlines, keeping the US Airways name; the combined company was led from America West's Tempe headquarters by Doug Parker.

Miracle on the Hudson

On 15 January 2009, US Airways Flight 1549, an Airbus A320 piloted by Captain Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger and First Officer Jeffrey Skiles, struck a flock of geese shortly after takeoff from New York's LaGuardia Airport and ditched in the Hudson River. All 155 people aboard survived, an event widely known as the "Miracle on the Hudson".

Merger with American

In December 2013 US Airways merged with American Airlines' parent company to form the American Airlines Group, the world's largest airline. The US Airways brand was retired on 17 October 2015, when the reservation systems were combined and the final flight, Flight 1939, landed in Philadelphia.
