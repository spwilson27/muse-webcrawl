Legal metrology

Legal metrology is the branch of metrology concerned with the application of legal requirements to measurements and measuring instruments. Its purpose is to ensure accuracy and fairness wherever measurements affect consumers, trade, health, safety or the environment — for example in shop scales, fuel dispensers, water and electricity meters, and pre-packaged goods.

Activities

Legal metrology authorities carry out type approval of instrument designs, initial and subsequent verification of individual instruments, and market surveillance. Instruments that pass are marked and may be sealed against tampering. National weights and measures inspectorates enforce these rules in the marketplace.

International organisation

The International Organization of Legal Metrology (OIML), founded in 1955 and headquartered in Paris, develops model regulations and recommendations (such as OIML R 76 for non-automatic weighing instruments) that member states use to harmonise their national laws, underpinning mutual recognition of test results.
