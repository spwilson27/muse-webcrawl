The Hunger Games is a 2008 dystopian young-adult novel by the American writer Suzanne Collins. It is the first book in The Hunger Games trilogy, followed by Catching Fire (2009) and Mockingjay (2010). The story is narrated by 16-year-old Katniss Everdeen, who lives in the post-apocalyptic nation of Panem in North America.

Plot

The wealthy Capitol rules twelve impoverished districts and, as punishment for a past rebellion, compels each district to send one boy and one girl — "tributes" — to fight to the death in a televised spectacle, the Hunger Games. When her younger sister Prim is chosen in the lottery for District 12, Katniss volunteers in her place. Alongside fellow tribute Peeta Mellark, who professes his love for her as a survival strategy, Katniss fights through the arena, and the two survive by threatening joint suicide with poisonous berries, defying the Capitol and igniting unrest in the districts.

Themes and inspiration

Collins has cited the Greek myth of Theseus and the Minotaur, Roman gladiatorial games, and modern reality television and war reporting as influences. The novel explores poverty, oppression, propaganda, and the effects of violence and trauma.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was a critical and commercial success, spending over three years on the New York Times bestseller list. The 2012 film adaptation, starring Jennifer Lawrence as Katniss, grossed over $690 million worldwide and launched a major film franchise. A prequel novel, The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, appeared in 2020.
