Massively multiplayer online role-playing game

A crowded hub area typical of MMORPGs

A massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG) is a video game that combines aspects of a role-playing video game and a massively multiplayer online game. Large numbers of players — hundreds to millions — interact within a persistent virtual world hosted on servers, developing characters through quests, combat, crafting and social play.

History

The genre descends from text-based multi-user dungeons (MUDs) of the late 1970s and 1980s. Graphical pioneers included Neverwinter Nights (1991), Meridian 59 (1996) and Ultima Online (1997), which popularized the form. EverQuest (1999) established the 3D subscription model, and World of Warcraft (2004) became the genre's most successful title, peaking at over 12 million subscribers. Other major titles include Final Fantasy XIV, Guild Wars 2, EVE Online, RuneScape and The Elder Scrolls Online.

Common features

MMORPGs typically feature persistent worlds that continue when a player logs off, character progression through levels and equipment, player-versus-environment quests and dungeons, player-versus-player combat, guilds and group "raids", virtual economies and in-game trading, and regular content expansions. Business models have shifted from monthly subscriptions toward free-to-play designs supported by microtransactions and optional subscriptions.

Cultural impact

The genre has influenced online culture, spawned academic study of virtual economies and communities, and generated notable events such as massive in-game battles in EVE Online and the "Corrupted Blood" incident in World of Warcraft, which was studied by epidemiologists.
