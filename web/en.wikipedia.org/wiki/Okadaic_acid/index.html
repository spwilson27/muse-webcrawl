Okadaic acid

Okadaic acid (C44H68O13) is a marine toxin produced by dinoflagellates such as Dinophysis and Prorocentrum. It accumulates in shellfish and is the principal cause of diarrhetic shellfish poisoning (DSP) in humans who consume contaminated bivalves.

History

Okadaic acid was first isolated from the black sponge Halichondria okadai, from which it takes its name, and its structure was determined in 1981. It was later shown to be produced by dinoflagellate algae and to pass up the food chain into filter-feeding shellfish.

Mechanism of action

Okadaic acid is a potent and selective inhibitor of the serine/threonine protein phosphatases PP1 and PP2A. By blocking dephosphorylation, it causes hyperphosphorylation of many cellular proteins, disrupting processes including cytoskeletal organization and ion secretion in intestinal cells — the basis of its diarrhetic effect. It is also a well-known tumor promoter in experimental models.

Uses in research

Because of its specific inhibition of protein phosphatases, okadaic acid is a widely used tool in cell biology for studying phosphorylation-dependent signalling pathways.
