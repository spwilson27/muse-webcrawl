Epona

Epona
Major cult centreGaul, Roman cavalry regions
SymbolsHorse, mare, foal, cornucopia, patera
Festivals18 December (in Roman calendar tradition)
EquivalentsRhiannon (Welsh), Macha (Irish, by association)

In Gallo-Roman religion, Epona was a protector of horses, ponies, donkeys, and mules. She was a goddess of fertility — symbolised by the patera, cornucopia, ears of grain and the presence of foals in some sculptures — and is particularly associated with horse breeding. Epona, her horses, or both appear in art and epigraphy across the Roman Empire, notably in Gaul, the Danube provinces and Britain.

Cult

Epona was the only Celtic deity ultimately worshipped in Rome itself, where her festival day was given as 18 December in a rural calendar. Her cult was carried by cavalry units, muleteers, stable hands and others who worked with horses; small shrines were set up in stables, and she was invoked for the protection of animals and travellers. Inscriptions to her are common from military contexts along the Rhine and Danube frontiers and in Roman Britain.

Iconography

Epona is usually depicted seated side-saddle on a mare, sometimes with a foal, or seated between two horses, distributing grain or fruit from a patera or cornucopia. Literary references are sparse — she is mentioned by Juvenal, Apuleius and Minucius Felix — so knowledge of her cult rests mainly on the archaeology of altars, reliefs and bronze figurines.
