Being and Time

Sein und Zeit (1927), Heidegger's magnum opus.

Being and Time (German: Sein und Zeit) is the 1927 magnum opus of the German philosopher Martin Heidegger and a key document of twentieth-century continental philosophy. Although composed hastily to secure Heidegger's promotion to a professorship, and left unfinished relative to its plan, it has had a pervasive influence on existentialism, hermeneutics, deconstruction and other schools.

Content

The book revives the ancient question of the meaning of Being, which Heidegger argues Western philosophy has forgotten. Its method is phenomenological and hermeneutic: the question is approached through an analysis of Dasein — the kind of being that human beings have — whose existence is always an issue for it. Famous analyses include being-in-the-world, the distinction between equipment that is "ready-to-hand" and objects "present-at-hand", care (Sorge) as the being of Dasein, being-toward-death, conscience, and temporality as the horizon for understanding Being.

Reception

Only the first two divisions of the projected first part were published. The work shaped the thought of Sartre, Merleau-Ponty, Gadamer, Arendt, Levinas and Derrida, among many others, while also drawing criticism for its dense neologisms and, later, for its author's involvement with Nazism.
