WOMADelaide

Location: Botanic Park, Adelaide, South Australia

First held: 1992

Frequency: Annual (originally biennial)

Genre: World music, arts and dance festival

WOMADelaide is an annual four-day festival of world music, arts and dance held in Botanic Park in Adelaide, South Australia, over the March long weekend. It is the Australian edition of the WOMAD (World of Music, Arts and Dance) festivals founded by Peter Gabriel in 1982.

History

The festival was first held in 1992 as part of the Adelaide Festival of Arts, and was staged biennially until 2003, when it became an annual event. It is presented by the WOMADelaide Foundation and managed by Arts Projects Australia. The event grew from modest beginnings to attract audiences of around 90,000–100,000 attendees across the four days, making it one of Australia's flagship outdoor festivals.

Programming

Performances take place across multiple stages set among the Moreton Bay fig trees of Botanic Park. Programming spans traditional and contemporary music from Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas and Oceania, alongside dance, visual arts, workshops, artist conversations ("Artists in Conversation"), the "Taste the World" cooking demonstrations, a KidZone, and the Planet Talks environmental discussion series. The festival is noted for its sustainability practices, including tree-planting programs to offset carbon emissions and extensive recycling and composting.

Impact

WOMADelaide is regarded as a major contributor to South Australia's cultural calendar and tourism economy. In 2021, owing to the COVID-19 pandemic, it was staged in a modified format at King Rodney Park with a largely Australian line-up and seated, ticketed concerts.
