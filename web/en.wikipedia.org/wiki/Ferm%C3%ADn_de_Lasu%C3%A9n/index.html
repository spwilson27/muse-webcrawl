Fermín de Lasuén (June 7, 1736 – June 26, 1803) was a Spanish Franciscan missionary to Alta California in the 18th century, the second president and head of the Franciscan missions there, and the founder of nine of the twenty-one Spanish missions in California. He succeeded Junípero Serra as president of the missions in 1784.

Life

Lasuén was born in Vitoria, Álava, in the Basque region of Spain, and joined the Franciscan order as a young man. He sailed to New Spain in 1759 and served for a time at missions in the Sierra Gorda of Mexico before moving to Baja California in 1768 and then to Alta California in 1773, where he assisted Serra.

Missions founded

As president of the missions (1784–1803), Lasuén founded nine missions: Santa Barbara (1786), La Purísima Concepción (1787), Santa Cruz (1791), Soledad (1791), San José (1797), San Juan Bautista (1797), San Miguel Arcángel (1797), San Fernando Rey de España (1797), and San Luis Rey de Francia (1798). He also systematised mission administration and architecture. He died at Mission San Carlos Borromeo de Carmelo in 1803 and is buried there.

Assessment

Lasuén is generally described as a more administrative and diplomatic mission president than Serra. As with the whole mission system, his work is also assessed in light of its severe impact on California's Native peoples, who suffered disease, forced labour, and cultural suppression at the missions.
