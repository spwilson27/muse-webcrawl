Little Turtle (Miami-Illinois: Mihšihkinaahkwa; c. 1747 – July 14, 1812) was a sagamore and war chief of the Miami people. He is best known as the military leader of the Western Confederacy of Native American tribes during the Northwest Indian War, in which he inflicted two of the worst defeats ever suffered by the United States Army at Native hands.

Early life

Little Turtle was born near the Eel River in what is now Whitley County, Indiana, the son of the Miami chief Acquenacque. He rose to prominence through military ability during the American Revolutionary War, fighting against American forces including the campaign of Augustin de la Balme in 1780.

Northwest Indian War

In October 1790, Little Turtle's forces defeated General Josiah Harmar's expedition near Kekionga (present-day Fort Wayne). On November 4, 1791, the Confederacy destroyed the army of General Arthur St. Clair — "St. Clair's Defeat," still proportionally the worst defeat in U.S. Army history, with over 600 soldiers killed.

Little Turtle recognized that the Confederacy could not indefinitely resist the United States. He advised negotiation before the Battle of Fallen Timbers (1794), where General Anthony Wayne's Legion defeated the Confederacy. In 1795 he signed the Treaty of Greenville, ceding much of present-day Ohio.

Later life

After the treaty Little Turtle became an advocate of peace and accommodation, meeting Presidents George Washington and John Adams in Philadelphia, where Washington presented him with a ceremonial sword. He opposed the younger Tecumseh's renewed resistance movement. He died in 1812 near Fort Wayne and was buried with full military honors by the United States Army.

Legacy

Little Turtle is remembered as one of the most capable Native American military leaders in U.S. history. A branch of the Allen County Public Library in Fort Wayne and various sites in Indiana bear his name.
