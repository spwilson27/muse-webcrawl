James F. Byrnes

Born: May 2, 1882, Charleston, South Carolina, U.S.

Died: April 9, 1972 (aged 89), Columbia, South Carolina, U.S.

Offices: U.S. Secretary of State (1945–1947); Associate Justice of the Supreme Court (1941–1942); Governor of South Carolina (1951–1955)

James Francis Byrnes (May 2, 1882 – April 9, 1972) was an American judge and politician from South Carolina. He served in all three branches of the federal government and, as Secretary of State under Harry S. Truman, helped shape the early Cold War.

Early career

Byrnes entered the U.S. House of Representatives in 1911 and the Senate in 1931, becoming a close ally of President Franklin D. Roosevelt. In 1941 Roosevelt appointed him to the Supreme Court, but he resigned after only a year to direct the Office of Economic Stabilization and the Office of War Mobilization — earning the nickname "Assistant President".

Secretary of State

Appointed Secretary of State by Truman in 1945, Byrnes participated in the Potsdam Conference, helped negotiate the postwar settlements in Europe, and delivered the 1946 Stuttgart speech promising to restore German self-government. He resigned in 1947 as relations with the Soviet Union deteriorated.

Later years

Byrnes served as Governor of South Carolina from 1951 to 1955, where he opposed school desegregation and promoted the state's "equalization" school-building program in an unsuccessful effort to preserve segregation after Brown v. Board of Education.
