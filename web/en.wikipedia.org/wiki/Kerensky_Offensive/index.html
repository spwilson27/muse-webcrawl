Kerensky Offensive

Date1–19 July 1917 (main phase)
LocationGalicia, Eastern Front
CombatantsRussian Provisional Government vs. German Empire and Austria-Hungary
ResultCentral Powers victory; collapse of Russian offensive

The Kerensky Offensive (also known as the June Offensive in Russia, or the July Offensive in Western accounts) was the last major offensive mounted by Russia in the First World War. Launched on 1 July 1917 by the Russian Provisional Government under Minister of War Alexander Kerensky, it aimed to honour Russia's commitments to its Allies and to rally the army and the country behind the government after the February Revolution. The offensive ended in disaster, accelerating the disintegration of the Russian Army and the collapse of the Provisional Government.

Background

Following the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II in March 1917, the Provisional Government resolved to continue the war. The army, however, was undermined by desertion, soldiers' committees, and war-weariness. Kerensky toured the front exhorting troops to fight, and General Aleksei Brusilov, the new commander-in-chief, prepared an offensive in Galicia toward Lemberg (Lviv), assisted by attacks on the northern and western fronts.

The offensive

After a massive artillery bombardment, Russian forces initially broke through Austro-Hungarian lines, advancing up to about 30 kilometres and capturing thousands of prisoners and the towns of Halicz and Kalusz. German reinforcements, however, stiffened resistance. Russian units increasingly refused orders, and discipline collapsed. On 19 July the Germans and Austro-Hungarians counterattacked, routing the Russians, who abandoned Tarnopol and much of eastern Galicia before the front stabilised along the Zbruch river. Russian casualties were heavy — estimates vary widely, from tens of thousands to over 100,000.

Consequences

The failure shattered the prestige of the Provisional Government and of Kerensky, who became prime minister in July amid the crisis. The fiasco fuelled support for the Bolsheviks, contributed to the Kornilov affair in August, and effectively ended Russia's participation in the war as an effective fighting force; the Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution and signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with the Central Powers in March 1918.
