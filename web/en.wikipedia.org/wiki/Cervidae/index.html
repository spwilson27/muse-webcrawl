Cervidae is the family of ruminant mammals commonly known as deer. It comprises about 55 species in two subfamilies: Cervinae (Old World deer, including red deer, fallow deer, sambar and muntjacs) and Capreolinae (New World deer, including white-tailed deer, moose, reindeer, elk and roe deer). Deer are native to all continents except Australia and Antarctica, and have been widely introduced elsewhere.

Characteristics

Deer are even-toed ungulates with two-toed hooves and a four-chambered, ruminating stomach. Males of most species grow antlers of bone that are shed and regrown annually; reindeer are unusual in that females also grow antlers, while Chinese water deer have tusks instead. Sizes range from the southern pudú of about 6–9 kg to the moose at up to 800 kg.

Ecology and humans

Deer inhabit habitats from tundra to tropical rainforest and are important herbivores and prey animals; several species are domesticated or farmed (reindeer) or prized game animals (red deer, white-tailed deer). Overabundant deer populations in the absence of predators can damage forests and agriculture. Extinct members include the giant Irish elk (Megaloceros giganteus).
