Theistic Satanism, otherwise referred to as religious Satanism, spiritual Satanism, or traditional Satanism, is an umbrella term for religious groups that consider Satan to be an objectively existing deity, supernatural entity, or spiritual being worthy of worship and supplication, whom individuals may contact and convene with. It stands in contrast to atheistic Satanism—such as the LaVeyan Satanism of the Church of Satan—in which Satan is treated as a symbol of individualism and rebellion rather than a literal being.

Beliefs and practices

Theistic Satanists may conceive of Satan through the lens of the Christian or Jewish scriptures, through Gnostic or dualist frameworks, or through various occult and Left-Hand Path traditions influenced by writers such as Michael Aquino's Temple of Set (which venerates the Egyptian god Set rather than the biblical Satan). Practices vary widely and may include ritual magic, prayer, devotion, and seasonal observances. Many adherents reinterpret Satan as a liberator figure who offered humanity knowledge, echoing Romantic literary treatments by writers such as Milton, Byron, and Shelley.

History and scholarship

Scholars of religion note that organised groups openly worshipping Satan as a deity are largely a modern phenomenon, distinct from the witch-trial-era accusations of diabolism. The 1980s–1990s "Satanic Panic" generated widespread claims of criminal satanic cults, which official investigations (such as the FBI's 1992 Lanning report) found to be unsubstantiated. Academic study of contemporary Satanism has been advanced by researchers including Massimo Introvigne, Jesper Aagaard Petersen, and Ruben van Luijk.
