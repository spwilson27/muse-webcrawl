Dances with Wolves

Dances with Wolves is a 1990 American epic Western film starring, directed, and produced by Kevin Costner in his feature directorial debut. It is a film adaptation of the 1988 novel of the same name by Michael Blake, which tells the story of Union Army Lieutenant John J. Dunbar (Costner), who travels to a remote Western frontier post and a nearby Lakota village, where he becomes sympathetic to the Native Americans' way of life.

Plot

During the American Civil War, Dunbar, recovering from a wound, requests a posting on the western frontier and finds Fort Sedgwick abandoned. Alone except for his horse Cisco and a wolf he calls Two Socks, he makes contact with the neighbouring Lakota Sioux, learns their language, and is given the name "Dances with Wolves". He falls in love with Stands With a Fist, a white woman raised by the tribe, and gradually turns against his own army, which eventually captures him as a traitor; freed by the Lakota, he leaves the tribe to spare them reprisals.

Production

The film was shot mainly in South Dakota, with much dialogue in Lakota subtitled in English — unusual for a mainstream Hollywood production. Costner partly financed the budget of about $22 million himself after studios balked at the three-hour running time.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success, grossing over $424 million worldwide, and was widely credited with reviving the Western genre. At the 63rd Academy Awards it won seven Oscars from twelve nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director. It is praised for its comparatively sympathetic portrayal of Native Americans, though some critics have noted it retains a "white saviour" perspective. A four-hour extended version was also released.
