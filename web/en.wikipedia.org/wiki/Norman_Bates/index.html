Norman Bates

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Norman Bates is a fictional character created by American author Robert Bloch as the main antagonist in his 1959 novel Psycho. He was portrayed by Anthony Perkins in Alfred Hitchcock's 1960 film adaptation of the novel, and in its sequels. Freddie Highmore portrayed a younger version in the television series Bates Motel (2013–2017).

Character biography

Bates is a young man who runs the Bates Motel, a small roadside establishment near the town of Fairvale, California (in the novel, the town is unnamed and the motel sits off an old highway). He suffers from dissociative identity disorder: after murdering his mother Norma and her lover in a fit of jealous rage, he preserved her corpse, treated it as if she were still alive, and developed a second personality — "Mother" — into which he slips, dressing in her clothes and speaking in her voice.

In the film, "Mother" commits the famous shower murder of Marion Crane, a secretary who stops at the motel while fleeing after embezzling money, and later kills the private investigator Milton Arbogast. At the climax, investigator Sam Loomis subdues Norman, and a psychiatrist explains his condition; the final shot shows Norman fully submerged in the "Mother" personality.

Inspiration

Bloch based Bates in part on Wisconsin murderer and grave robber Ed Gein, whose crimes were discovered in 1957, shortly before the novel was written, although Bloch said he only learned of the parallels with Gein's case later.

Legacy

Norman Bates is routinely ranked among cinema's greatest villains; the American Film Institute placed him second on its list of the top 50 movie villains in 2003, behind Hannibal Lecter. The character has been referenced and parodied extensively in popular culture.
