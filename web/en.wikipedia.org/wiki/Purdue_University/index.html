Purdue University

Purdue University is a public land-grant research university in West Lafayette, Indiana, founded in 1869 after businessman John Purdue donated land and money to establish a college of science, technology and agriculture bearing his name.

Academics

Purdue is organised into colleges including Engineering, Agriculture, Science, Liberal Arts, the Krannert School of Management and the Polytechnic Institute. It is noted for engineering and aviation — it operates its own airport and was an early leader in flight training — and for agriculture and pharmacy. Its students are called Boilermakers.

People

Alumni include numerous astronauts, among them Neil Armstrong, the first person on the Moon, and Eugene Cernan, the last; Purdue is sometimes called the "Cradle of Astronauts".
