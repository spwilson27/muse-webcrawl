The Đinh dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Đinh) was the first imperial dynasty of Vietnam following the country's recovery of independence from China, ruling from 968 to 980. Its founder, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, ended the decades of fragmentation known as the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords and proclaimed himself emperor under the name Đinh Tiên Hoàng.

Founding

Rising from Hoa Lư in modern Ninh Bình province, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh — legendarily a buffalo herder who drilled his playmates with reed banners — defeated the rival warlords one by one and unified the country. In 968 he took the imperial title, named the state Đại Cồ Việt, established his capital at Hoa Lư, and organised a court on the Chinese imperial model, while sending tributary embassies to secure recognition from the Song dynasty.

End of the dynasty

In 979 Đinh Tiên Hoàng and his eldest son were assassinated by a court official, leaving the throne to his six-year-old son Đinh Toàn. Facing invasion by Song China, the court placed the general Lê Hoàn on the throne in 980, founding the Early Lê dynasty. Lê Hoàn went on to defeat the Song invasion of 981, preserving the independence the Đinh had established.
