Balaji Baji Rao

Balaji Baji Rao (8 December 1720 – 23 June 1761), also known as Nana Saheb I, was the 10th Peshwa of the Maratha Confederacy. He was appointed as Peshwa in 1740 upon the death of his father, the famed Baji Rao I. During his tenure, the Maratha territory reached its zenith, extending across most of the Indian subcontinent.

Early years

Balaji Baji Rao was the eldest son of Baji Rao I and Kashibai. Chhatrapati Shahu I appointed him Peshwa at the age of nineteen. After Shahu's death in 1749, Balaji consolidated power at Pune, effectively making the Peshwa the real head of the Maratha state, while the Chhatrapati at Satara became a figurehead under the terms of the Treaty of Sangola (1750).

Expansion and administration

Under Balaji Baji Rao, Maratha armies campaigned from Bengal to Punjab; in 1758 Maratha forces under his brother Raghunathrao captured Attock on the Indus. He reformed administration and finance, patronised learning and building at Pune, and encouraged commanders such as Malhar Rao Holkar, Ranoji Scindia, and the Bhonsles. His cousin Sadashivrao Bhau became his de facto deputy and commander in the north.

Third Battle of Panipat and death

The expansion brought the Marathas into conflict with Ahmad Shah Abdali of Afghanistan. In 1760 Balaji sent a great army north under Sadashivrao Bhau, accompanied by his son Vishwasrao. The army was destroyed at the Third Battle of Panipat on 14 January 1761; both Bhau and Vishwasrao were killed. Shattered by the loss, Balaji Baji Rao died a few months later, on 23 June 1761, at Pune. He was succeeded by his second son, Madhavrao I.
