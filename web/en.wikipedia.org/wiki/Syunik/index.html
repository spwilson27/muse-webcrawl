Syunik

Country: Armenia

Capital: Kapan

Area: 4,506 km²

Population: approx. 140,000

Syunik (Armenian: Սյունիք) is the southernmost province (marz) of Armenia. It borders Azerbaijan to the east and west (including the Nakhchivan exclave) and Iran to the south. Its capital and largest city is Kapan. Syunik is the largest Armenian province by area and one of the most sparsely populated.

Geography and history

The province is mountainous, dominated by the Zangezur Mountains. Historically, Syunik was one of the provinces of the ancient Kingdom of Armenia and later a medieval principality and kingdom whose rulers resisted successive invasions. The medieval Tatev Monastery, reached today by the Wings of Tatev — one of the world's longest reversible aerial tramways — is among Armenia's most famous religious sites. The region also contains the Bronze Age stone circle of Karahunj.

Economy and strategic importance

Mining is central to the economy: the Kajaran copper-molybdenum mine is one of the largest in the country, and Zangezur's copper-molybdenum combine is a major employer. Syunik carries Armenia's only border crossing with Iran at Meghri–Agarak and the road linking Armenia to Iran, giving the province outsized strategic significance, especially in debates over transport corridors following the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War.
