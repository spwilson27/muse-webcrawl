Lateralization of brain function

Some functions are specialised to one cerebral hemisphere.
FieldNeuroscience, neuropsychology
Key areasBroca's area, Wernicke's area

The lateralization of brain function is the tendency for some neural functions or cognitive processes to be specialised to one side of the brain or the other. The two cerebral hemispheres, connected mainly by the corpus callosum, show both structural and functional asymmetries.

Language

Language is the best-known lateralised function: in roughly 95% of right-handed people and a majority of left-handed people, production and comprehension depend predominantly on the left hemisphere, particularly Broca's area (speech production) and Wernicke's area (comprehension). Damage to these areas produces characteristic aphasias.

Other functions

The right hemisphere is relatively more involved in visuospatial processing, face recognition, attention and aspects of emotional prosody, while the left is relatively more involved in sequential and analytical processing. Popular accounts exaggerate these differences into a dichotomy of "left-brained" and "right-brained" personalities, which neuroscientists regard as a myth: most tasks recruit both hemispheres, and individuals do not fall into hemisphere-dominant types.

Evidence

Evidence comes from lesion studies, split-brain (commissurotomy) patients studied by Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga, the Wada test, dichotic listening and neuroimaging.
