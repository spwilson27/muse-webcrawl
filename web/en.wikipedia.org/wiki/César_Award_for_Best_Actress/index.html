César Award for Best Actress

The César Award for Best Actress (French: César de la meilleure actrice) is one of the César Awards, presented annually by the French Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma to honour the best performance by an actress in a leading role in a French film of the preceding year. The award was first given in 1976.

History

The first winner was Romy Schneider for L'important c'est d'aimer (1975). Isabelle Adjani holds the record with five Best Actress wins, for Possession, One Deadly Summer, Camille Claudel, La Reine Margot and Skirt Day. Other multiple winners include Isabelle Huppert, Nathalie Baye, Yolande Moreau and Sandrine Bonnaire. Winners are chosen by the members of the Academy from a shortlist of nominees.

Selection

Nominees are selected in a first round of voting by the Academy's membership of film professionals; the winner is decided in a second round. The award is presented at the annual César ceremony, usually held in Paris in February or March.
