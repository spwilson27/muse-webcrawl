Fort Boyard (fortification)

LocationPertuis d'Antioche strait, Charente-Maritime, France
Built1801–1857
Dimensions68 m × 31 m; walls 20 m high
MaterialsStone on artificial shoal foundation

Fort Boyard (pronounced [fɔʁ bwajaʁ]) is a fort located between the Île-d'Aix and the Île d'Oléron in the Pertuis d'Antioche straits, on the west coast of France. It was built to protect the mouth of the Charente river and the arsenal of Rochefort from British naval attack.

Construction

A fort on the Boyard bank had been proposed under Louis XIV, but Vauban advised against it. Construction finally began under Napoleon in 1801, with stone blocks laid on the shoal at low tide; work was halted by British attacks and resumed only decades later. The fort was completed in 1857: an oval fortress 68 metres long and 31 metres wide, with tiered gun galleries around a central courtyard, housing a garrison of about 250 men.

Obsolescence and later use

Advances in artillery made the fort militarily obsolete almost as soon as it was finished — its guns could not outrange modern warships. It served as a military prison, notably for Communards after 1871, and was abandoned in 1913. After decades of decay it was restored and became famous worldwide as the setting of the television game show Fort Boyard, first broadcast in France in 1990 and adapted in many countries.
