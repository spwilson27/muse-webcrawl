Farm

A farm (also called an agricultural holding) is an area of land devoted primarily to agricultural processes with the primary objective of producing food and other crops; it is the basic facility in food production. The name is used for specialized units, including arable farms, vegetable farms, fruit farms, dairy, pig and poultry farms, and land used for the production of natural fibres, biofuel, and other commodities.

History

Farming dates to the Neolithic period, when agriculture first developed around 12,000 years ago. Farming was the driving force behind the domestication of plants and animals and the rise of settled societies. Farms developed across the Fertile Crescent, East Asia, Mesoamerica, and other centres of domestication, and spread with migrating peoples.

Types of farms

Farms may be owned and operated by a single individual, family, community, corporation, or company, and produce one or many types of produce. Types include subsistence farms, which support a single family with little surplus; commercial farms oriented toward markets; intensive farms using high inputs of labour, fertilizer, and capital; and organic farms avoiding synthetic chemicals. Specialized forms include ranches for grazing livestock, orchards, vineyards, plantations, fish farms, and dairy farms.

Modern farming

Modern farms make extensive use of mechanization, synthetic fertilizers and pesticides, irrigation, and selectively bred varieties, greatly increasing yields. These practices have also raised concerns about environmental impact, soil degradation, water use, and animal welfare. In developed countries, farms have become fewer and larger, and farm employment has declined sharply as a share of the workforce.
