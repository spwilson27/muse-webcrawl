Koevoet (Afrikaans for "crowbar", pronounced [ˈkuːfut]), formally the South West African Police Counter-Insurgency Unit (SWAPOL-COIN), was a paramilitary counter-insurgency unit of the South African Police active during the South African Border War in northern South West Africa (now Namibia). It was officially designated Operation K.

History

Koevoet was founded in 1979 by Major-General Hans Dreyer, drawing on lessons from Rhodesia's Selous Scouts and South Africa's earlier police operations. The unit combined white South African police officers with black Namibian constables and highly skilled Ovambo trackers, operating in Casspir mine-resistant vehicles to follow insurgent tracks and engage SWAPO's armed wing, PLAN. It became one of the most operationally active South African units of the war, credited with a large share of PLAN casualties in the operational area.

Controversy and disbandment

The unit was also repeatedly accused of human rights abuses, including torture and extrajudicial killings of prisoners and civilians. Under United Nations Security Council Resolution 435, Koevoet was to be confined to base and then disbanded ahead of Namibian independence; it was formally disbanded in 1989, though its demobilised members and the management of their demobilisation remained contentious issues during the transition.
