Stage lighting instrument

A stage lighting instrument (also called a lantern in the UK) is a device used in theatrical lighting design to produce illumination on a stage. Instruments are hung from battens, trusses or lighting bars, or placed on the floor, and are controlled through dimmers or directly by a lighting console using protocols such as DMX.

A row of stage lighting instruments on a batten

Components

A conventional instrument consists of a housing, a lamp (increasingly replaced by an LED source), a reflector, and a lens system. Colour is added with gel filters or colour-mixing LED engines, and the beam can be shaped with shutters, barn doors, gobos (patterns) and irises.

Types

The main families are: ellipsoidal reflector spotlights (profiles), which produce a hard-edged, focusable beam and can project gobos; Fresnel lanterns, with a soft-edged wash and adjustable beam angle; PAR cans, simple sealed-beam fixtures producing an intense oval beam; cyclorama and flood lights for even background washes; strip lights; and moving lights (intelligent fixtures) whose pan, tilt, colour and beam can be remotely controlled. Followspots, operated by hand to track a performer, form a further specialist class.

Use

Lighting designers select and position instruments to model the stage picture, establish time and mood, and direct the audience's attention, following functions of light first systematised by designers such as Stanley McCandless.
