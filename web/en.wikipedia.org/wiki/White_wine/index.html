White wine is a wine fermented without skin contact, so that the grape skins do not colour the juice. It can be made from green, golden, or even black grapes (as in blanc de noirs champagne), and its colour ranges from straw-yellow through yellow-green to gold. White wine has been consumed for at least 4,000 years.

Production

Grapes are pressed promptly after harvest and the juice is separated from the skins before fermentation, unlike red wine, which ferments on the skins. Fermentation usually takes place in stainless steel at cool temperatures to preserve fresh fruit aromas, though some styles — notably many Chardonnays — are fermented and aged in oak barrels and undergo malolactic fermentation for a rounder texture.

Styles

White wines range from bone-dry (Muscadet, many Rieslings) through off-dry to lusciously sweet (Sauternes, Trockenbeerenauslese). Sparkling white wines include Champagne, Prosecco, and Cava. White wines are generally served chilled and are commonly paired with fish, poultry, and lighter dishes.
