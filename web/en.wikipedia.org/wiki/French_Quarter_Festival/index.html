French Quarter Festival

LocationFrench Quarter, New Orleans, Louisiana, U.S.
First held1984
WhenFour days in April
AttendanceOver 750,000 (pre-pandemic years)

The French Quarter Festival is a free, annual music festival held in early April in New Orleans, Louisiana. It is the largest showcase of Louisiana music in the world: over four days, hundreds of performances take place on more than twenty stages throughout the French Quarter, featuring jazz, brass bands, Cajun and zydeco, funk, gospel, blues and other local styles. Local restaurants operate food booths in Jackson Square and elsewhere, leading the festival to bill itself as "the world's largest jazz brunch."

History

The festival was founded in 1984, when New Orleans staged the Louisiana World Exposition; Mayor Dutch Morial promoted a festival to draw visitors back downtown after the fair. It has grown steadily, drawing hundreds of thousands of attendees and generating a substantial economic impact for the city. The event is organised by the nonprofit French Quarter Festivals, Inc., which also produces Satchmo SummerFest and other celebrations.

Features

Stages are concentrated at Jackson Square, Woldenberg Riverfront Park, Bourbon Street, Royal Street and the New Orleans Jazz Museum at the Old U.S. Mint. Free and open to the public, the festival is regarded as a more locally oriented counterpart to the larger New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival held later in the spring.
