December murders

Fort Zeelandia in Paramaribo, Suriname, the site of the killings.

The December murders (Dutch: Decembermoorden) were the torture and killing of fifteen prominent Surinamese men on 7 and 8 December 1982 by soldiers of the military regime of Dési Bouterse. The victims — lawyers, journalists, academics, trade unionists, soldiers and businessmen who had criticised or opposed the military government — were seized from their homes in Paramaribo and shot at Fort Zeelandia, the headquarters of the regime.

Background

Suriname had been ruled by a military council since the sergeants' coup of February 1980, led by Bouterse. By late 1982, opposition was growing, fuelled by a strike movement and prominent public criticism of the regime. On the night of 7–8 December 1982, soldiers arrested sixteen men. One, the union leader Freddy Derby, was released; the other fifteen were killed. The regime claimed the men had been shot while trying to escape after plotting a coup; the wounds on the bodies, described by relatives and investigators, indicated torture and execution.

The victims included the lawyers John Baboeram, Kenneth Gonçalves, Eddy Hoost and Harold Riedewald; the journalists Bram Behr, Leslie Rahman, Frank Wijngaarde, Jozef Slagveer and André Kamperveen, who was also a former minister and FIFA vice-president; the university lecturer Gerard Leckie; the businessman Robby Sohansingh; and the soldiers Jiwansingh Sheombar and Surindre Rambocus.

Aftermath and trial

The killings shocked the Netherlands, Suriname's former colonial ruler, which suspended development aid, and they isolated the Bouterse regime internationally. For decades the murders were not prosecuted in Suriname. A trial before a military court finally began in 2007, with Bouterse — by then elected president of Suriname — as the principal suspect. After years of delay, including a controversial amnesty law in 2012 that was later overturned, Bouterse was convicted in 2019 and sentenced to twenty years in prison. After appeals, the conviction was upheld in December 2023; Bouterse went into hiding and died in December 2024 without serving his sentence. The murders remain the most notorious crime of Suriname's military era and are commemorated annually.
