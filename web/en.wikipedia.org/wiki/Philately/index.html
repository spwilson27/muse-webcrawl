Philately

Philately is the collection and study of postage stamps and postal material. It includes the design, production, and use of stamps, covers, postmarks, and related items, as well as the activity of collecting them. The word was coined by the French collector Georges Herpin in 1864, from the Greek philos (friend) and ateleia (exemption from tax), referring to the stamp as proof of payment.

History

Stamp collecting began almost immediately after the issue of the world's first adhesive postage stamp, the British Penny Black of 1840. By the 1860s, dealers, catalogues, and philatelic journals had appeared, and the hobby — once called "the hobby of kings" — spread worldwide. Major rarities include the British Guiana 1c magenta (1856), the Swedish Treskilling Yellow, and the American Inverted Jenny (1918).

Branches

Philately encompasses traditional philately (stamps and their production), postal history (routes, rates, and markings), airmail collecting, topical or thematic collecting (stamps on a subject such as birds or space), postal stationery, revenues, and modern specialties such as first-day covers. Collectors study paper, perforations, watermarks, printings, and errors to attribute and value items; catalogues such as Stanley Gibbons, Scott, Michel, and Yvert et Tellier standardize listings.

Organizations and market

The Fédération Internationale de Philatélie (FIP), founded in 1926, coordinates international exhibitions, while national federations and thousands of local clubs support collectors. Though stamp collecting declined as a mass hobby in many countries, the market for rare items remains strong, with major rarities selling for millions of dollars at auction.
