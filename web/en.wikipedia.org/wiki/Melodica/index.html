The melodica is a handheld free-reed instrument with a musical keyboard on top, played by blowing air through a mouthpiece that fits into a hole in the side of the instrument. Pressing a key allows air to flow past the corresponding reed, producing a pitch. Most melodicas have two or three octaves, commonly 32 or 37 keys.

History

Although similar instruments had existed earlier, the modern melodica was popularised by the German manufacturer Hohner in the 1950s and 1960s, initially as an educational instrument for children. Comparable instruments include the Yamaha "Pianica" and the Suzuki "Melodion". The instrument's pitch range and easy learning curve made it a staple of school music classes in many countries.

Playing technique

The melodica is usually played with the right hand on the keyboard and the left hand holding the instrument, blowing through either a short mouthpiece or a flexible tube that lets the instrument rest on a table. Dynamics are controlled by breath pressure. The instrument's sound is similar to that of a harmonica or a small accordion.

Use in popular music

The melodica gained fame in popular music through Jamaican reggae and dub artist Augustus Pablo, whose playing on records of the 1970s — notably the album East of the River Nile — made the instrument's reedy, haunting tone a signature of his style and a touchstone of dub. It has also been used by artists in genres from indie rock to jazz, and is the principal instrument of musician Jon Batiste's performances in some contexts and of various YouTube-era multi-instrumentalists.
