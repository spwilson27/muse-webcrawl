Becherovka (Czech pronunciation: [ˈbɛxɛrofka]) is a herbal bitters liqueur produced in Karlovy Vary (Carlsbad) in the Czech Republic. Sold since 1807, it is flavoured with a secret blend of some twenty herbs and spices — prominently cinnamon and ginger among them — and has a characteristic bittersweet, warming taste. It is traditionally drunk neat and ice-cold, as a digestif, and is jokingly called the "thirteenth spring" of the spa town, whose twelve mineral springs are famous. Its alcohol content is 38%.

History

The recipe is credited to Josef Vitus Becher, a Karlovy Vary apothecary who in 1805 received (or developed, the stories vary) an herbal tincture formula with the visiting English physician Christian Frobrig, and began selling the "English bitter" drops commercially in 1807. His son Johann (Jan) Becher built the business, registering the brand and the now-famous flattened green bottle. The formula has remained a closely guarded family and company secret, said to be known to only two people at a time; the herbs are mixed in a special room (the "Drogikamr") and macerated, then matured in oak. After 1945 the Becher family was expelled and the firm nationalised; it was privatised in the 1990s–2001 and eventually acquired by Pernod Ricard, which markets Becherovka internationally and as the base of the "Beton" cocktail (Becherovka and tonic).

Variants

Besides the Original, the company has produced variants including Becherovka Lemond, Cordial and the aperitif KV 14. The Jan Becher Museum in Karlovy Vary occupies the historic factory and displays the liqueur's production history.
