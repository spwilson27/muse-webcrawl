Metrorail (South Africa)

Metrorail
Operator: PRASA
Areas: Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Eastern Cape
Type: Commuter rail

Metrorail is the commuter rail service in the major metropolitan areas of South Africa, operated by the Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa (PRASA). It runs services in Gauteng (including Johannesburg and Pretoria), the Western Cape (Cape Town), KwaZulu-Natal (Durban) and the Eastern Cape (Gqeberha and East London), and is the backbone of public transport for millions of lower-income commuters.

Network

The Metrorail networks comprise hundreds of stations over more than 2,000 km of track, overwhelmingly electrified at 3 kV DC on the 1,067 mm (Cape) gauge. Historically the service carried well over a million passengers a day. Travel classes historically included Metro (third class) and Metro Plus (first class) carriages on the same trains.

Challenges and renewal

From the 2010s, Metrorail suffered severe decline from vandalism, cable theft, arson attacks on trains, and under-investment; many lines in Cape Town and Gauteng were suspended entirely around 2019–2021. PRASA has since been rebuilding the network and introducing new blue-and-grey electric multiple units built at a plant in Nigel, Gauteng, with services progressively restored on priority corridors.
