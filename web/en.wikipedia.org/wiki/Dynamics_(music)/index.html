Dynamics (music)

In music, the dynamics of a piece are the variation in loudness between notes or phrases. Dynamics are indicated by specific musical notation, often in some detail, although dynamic markings still require interpretation by the performer depending on musical context.

Markings

The principal dynamic markings, from quietest to loudest, are: ppp (pianississimo), pp (pianissimo), p (piano, soft), mp (mezzo-piano), mf (mezzo-forte), f (forte, loud), ff (fortissimo), and fff (fortississimo). Gradual changes are indicated by crescendo (growing louder) and diminuendo or decrescendo (growing softer), often drawn as hairpins. Accents such as sfz (sforzando) mark sudden emphasis on a single note or chord.

History

Explicit dynamic markings became common in the 18th century as instruments such as the piano — named for its ability to play piano and forte — displaced the harpsichord. In modern and avant-garde scores, composers sometimes use verbal instructions or graphical indications in place of traditional Italian terms.
