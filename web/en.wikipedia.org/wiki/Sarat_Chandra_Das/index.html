Sarat Chandra Das

Sarat Chandra Das (18 July 1849 – 13 January 1917) was an Indian scholar of Tibetan language and culture, traveller and explorer, best known for his two journeys to Tibet in 1879 and 1881–82 and for compiling a standard Tibetan–English dictionary. Working within the world of British India's frontier intelligence, he was one of the celebrated "pundit" explorers who surveyed the Himalayan and trans-Himalayan regions.

Early life

Born in Chittagong in Bengal, Das studied engineering in Calcutta but took a post as headmaster of the Bhutia Boarding School at Darjeeling, where he learned Tibetan under Lama Ugyen Gyatso, who accompanied him on his travels.

Journeys to Tibet

In 1879 Das travelled via Nepal and the Tashilhunpo monastery near Shigatse, seat of the Panchen Lama. On his second, longer journey of 1881–82 he reached Lhasa, then closed to most outsiders, gathering manuscripts, maps and ethnographic information. His narrative was published posthumously in part as Journey to Lhasa and Central Tibet (1902), edited by William Woodville Rockhill. Because his work served British intelligence, its exposure caused hardship for his Tibetan hosts, a controversy debated by later historians.

Scholarship

Das's Tibetan–English Dictionary (1902) remained a standard reference for decades. He wrote on Tibetan religion, folklore and history, founded the Buddhist Text Society in 1892, and published the journal of that society. His Darjeeling home, "Lhasa Villa", hosted many scholars and travellers.
