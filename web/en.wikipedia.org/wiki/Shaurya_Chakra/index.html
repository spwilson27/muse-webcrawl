The Shaurya Chakra is an Indian military decoration awarded for valour, courageous action or self-sacrifice while not engaged in direct action with the enemy. It may be awarded posthumously, and is third in the order of precedence of peacetime gallantry awards, after the Ashoka Chakra and the Kirti Chakra. The decoration may be awarded to officers and men and women of all ranks of the Army, the Navy and the Air Force, of the Territorial Army, Militia and other lawfully constituted forces, as well as to members of nursing services and to civilians.

The Shaurya Chakra medal is circular and made of bronze, suspended from a green ribbon divided by three vertical orange stripes.

History

The award was instituted by the President of India on 4 January 1952 as the "Ashoka Chakra, Class III," the third class of a three-tier peacetime gallantry decoration. The statutes were revised and the decoration renamed the "Shaurya Chakra" on 27 January 1967. Before 1967 the award was known as Ashoka Chakra, Class III.

Design

The medal is circular in shape and of bronze. On the obverse, the Lion Capitol of Ashoka is embossed in the centre, surrounded by a wreath of lotus. On the reverse, the words "Shaurya Chakra" are embossed in Hindi and English on the upper and lower edges respectively, separated by two lotus designs. The ribbon is green in colour, divided into four equal parts by three vertical orange stripes.

Eligibility

The following categories of persons are eligible: officers, men and women of all ranks of the Army, Navy and Air Force, of any of the Reserve Forces, of the Territorial Army, Militia and of any other lawfully constituted forces; members of the Nursing Services of the Armed Forces; and civilian citizens of either sex in all walks of life, along with members of Police Forces including Central Para-Military Forces and the Railway Protection Force. The decoration is awarded for gallantry otherwise than in the face of the enemy.
