Khazar Correspondence

The Khazar Correspondence is a set of documents, which are alleged to date from the 950s or 960s, and to be letters between Hasdai ibn Shaprut, foreign secretary of the Caliph of Córdoba, and Joseph Khagan of the Khazars. The Correspondence is one of only a few sources in which Khazar leaders themselves speak, and it offers important information about the Khazar Khaganate, its conversion to Judaism, its wars and its geography.

Contents

In his letter, Hasdai, having heard reports of an independent Jewish kingdom in the east, asks the Khazar king for information about his country, its people, its government and the genealogy of its rulers, and how the Khazars came to profess Judaism. Joseph's reply recounts the conversion of his ancestor Bulan after a debate between representatives of Judaism, Christianity and Islam, traces the Khazar royal line, describes the extent of the Khaganate and its tributaries, and invites Hasdai to visit.

A related document, the so-called Schechter Letter (or Cambridge Document), also recovered from the Cairo Geniza, gives a Khazar Jewish account of the conversion and of wars against Rus' and Byzantine forces, mentioning a ruler named Aaron and a campaign against Constantinople.

Transmission and authenticity

The correspondence survives in two versions of Joseph's reply (a shorter and a longer one) and was first published in the sixteenth century by Isaac Akrish in Kol Mevasser. Manuscripts are held in the British Library and in Oxford, while the Schechter Letter was identified by Solomon Schechter in the Cairo Geniza in 1912. Some twentieth-century scholars questioned the documents' authenticity, but most specialists accept them as genuine, or as containing a genuine tenth-century core.
