Verein für Socialpolitik

The Verein für Socialpolitik (Association for Social Policy) is a German-speaking association of economists, founded in 1872 in Eisenach and still active today as one of the largest professional associations of economists in Europe.

History

The Verein was founded by economists of the German historical school — including Gustav von Schmoller, Lujo Brentano and Adolph Wagner — who argued that economic policy should address the "social question" of industrial workers' conditions. Critics dubbed them Kathedersozialisten ("socialists of the chair"). The association's research and advocacy helped prepare the ground for Bismarck's pioneering social insurance legislation of the 1880s. Max Weber and Joseph Schumpeter were among its prominent members in the early twentieth century, when the Verein hosted the famous Werturteilsstreit (value-judgement dispute) over whether social science should make policy judgements.

Today

The modern VfS organises an annual conference attended by well over a thousand economists, supports working groups in fields from econometrics to economic history, and works to promote economics in the German-speaking world.
