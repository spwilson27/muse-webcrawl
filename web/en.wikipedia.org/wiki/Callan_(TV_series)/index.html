Callan (TV series)

Callan is a British action-drama television series created by James Mitchell, first broadcast on ITV in 1967 and running until 1972, with a later television film in 1974. Edward Woodward starred as David Callan, a reluctant assassin working for "the Section", a shadowy British intelligence unit run by the man codenamed Hunter.

Premise

Callan, an ex-soldier and ex-convict, is coerced into carrying out assassinations and dirty work. His only confidant is Lonely (Russell Hunter), a smelly petty burglar who supplies information and weapons. The series was notable for its bleak, morally ambiguous tone, its hanging-bare-bulb title sequence imagery, and its depiction of intelligence work as sordid rather than glamorous.

Production and legacy

Produced by ABC Weekend TV and then Thames Television, the series began with a 1967 Armchair Theatre play, "A Magnum for Schneider". A feature film followed in 1974 remaking that story. Callan is frequently cited among the finest British television dramas and made Woodward a star.
