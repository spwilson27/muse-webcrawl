Sir Arthur Ernest Streeton (8 April 1867 – 1 September 1943) was an Australian landscape painter and a leading member of the Heidelberg School, the Australian impressionist movement of the late nineteenth century. His sunlit, blue-and-gold depictions of the Australian bush — painted largely en plein air — helped define a national image of the Australian landscape in the decades around Federation.

Early life and the Heidelberg School

Streeton was born near Geelong, Victoria, and grew up in Melbourne, where he worked as a lithographer's apprentice while studying drawing at night. He came under the influence of Tom Roberts and joined the artists' camps at Box Hill and Eaglemont, near Heidelberg outside Melbourne, where painters worked outdoors to capture the light and colour of the Australian summer. At the famous 9 by 5 Impression Exhibition of 1889 — so named for works painted on nine-by-five-inch cigar-box lids — the twenty-two-year-old Streeton was a sensation, and his Golden Summer, Eaglemont (1889) was later sold to the National Gallery, London, the first Australian-born artist's work to enter its collection; it is now in the National Gallery of Australia.

Career

In the 1890s Streeton painted in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, producing works such as Fire's On (1891), depicting the Lapstone Tunnel excavation, and the panoramic The Purple Noon's Transparent Might (1896), painted from the Hawkesbury River country. He settled in London in 1897, exhibiting at the Royal Academy, and during the First World War served as an official war artist with the Australian Imperial Force in France. Knighted in 1937, he spent his later years at Olinda in the Dandenong Ranges, where he also became a prominent voice in newspaper debates on art and conservation.

Legacy

Streeton's landscapes remain among the most reproduced and valuable in Australian art; his works regularly feature among the highest prices achieved for Australian paintings at auction, and his name is commemorated in streets, a Melbourne suburb (via the Heidelberg milieu), and an Australian postage stamp.
