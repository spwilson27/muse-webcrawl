Sweetheart Abbey

Sweetheart Abbey (Latin: Dulce Cor) is a ruined Cistercian abbey in the village of New Abbey, Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland. It was founded in 1273 by Dervorguilla of Galloway in memory of her husband, John de Balliol, who had died in 1268. According to tradition, Dervorguilla carried her husband's embalmed heart in an ivory casket with her wherever she went, and when she died in 1290 she was buried alongside him, with the heart placed upon her bosom. The monks named the abbey Dulce Cor — "sweet heart" — in her honour.

Architecture and history

Built of local red sandstone in the Early English Gothic style, the abbey church survives substantially to roof height, including its impressive central tower and large east window, making it one of the most complete Cistercian ruins in Scotland. The precinct wall built of granite boulders also largely survives. The abbey was damaged during the Anglo-Scottish wars of the early 14th century and restored under Archibald Douglas, Lord of Galloway. Monastic life ended with the Scottish Reformation in 1560, after which the buildings were quarried for stone until they came under state care in the early 20th century. The site is now managed by Historic Environment Scotland.
