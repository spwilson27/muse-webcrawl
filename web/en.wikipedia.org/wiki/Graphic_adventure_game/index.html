Graphic adventure game

A classic point-and-click adventure scene

A graphic adventure game (sometimes simply adventure game) is a video game genre in which the player explores an interactive story, solves puzzles and interacts with characters and environments, typically without the reflex- or action-based challenges of other genres. Graphic adventures developed from text adventures and interactive fiction, replacing typed commands with graphical scenes and, later, point-and-click interfaces.

History

Early graphical adventures such as Sierra On-Line's Mystery House (1980) and the King's Quest series (from 1984) combined pictures with a text parser. Lucasfilm Games popularised the pure point-and-click interface with Maniac Mansion (1987) and its SCUMM engine, followed by acclaimed titles such as The Secret of Monkey Island (1990) and Grim Fandango (1998). Myst (1993) became one of the best-selling PC games of its era.

Decline and resurgence

Commercial interest in the genre waned in the late 1990s as real-time 3D action games dominated, but graphic adventures persisted through European studios and, from the late 2000s, a revival led by episodic games from Telltale Games such as The Walking Dead (2012), and by independent developers using digital distribution.
