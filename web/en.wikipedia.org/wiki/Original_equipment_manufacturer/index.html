Original equipment manufacturer

An original equipment manufacturer (OEM) is a company that produces parts and equipment that may be marketed by another manufacturer. The term is used in several distinct senses. In its older and still common usage, an OEM produces components that are incorporated into another company's finished product — for example, a maker of braking systems supplying a car manufacturer. In a second usage, particularly in the computer and automotive aftermarket trades, "OEM" describes the maker of the original part fitted to a product, contrasting its parts with aftermarket replacements.

OEM components are incorporated into products sold under another company's brand.

Usage in industry

The relationship between OEMs and the firms that resell or integrate their products is sometimes described via the term value-added reseller (VAR), which purchases OEM products, adds features or services, and sells the result as a turnkey offering. In computing, large brands such as computer manufacturers typically assemble systems from OEM components — processors, memory, displays — while some firms, known as original design manufacturers (ODMs), both design and manufacture products that are then branded by another company, as in much of the laptop and smartphone industries.

Automotive parts

In the automotive sector, "OEM parts" are components made by the original supplier to the vehicle manufacturer, often sold in the manufacturer's packaging at a premium over equivalent aftermarket parts. Debate surrounds quality differences, warranties and the right of independent repairers to obtain OEM parts and information — an issue connected with the wider right-to-repair movement.

Software

OEM software licences are licences sold with hardware at reduced cost, traditionally tied to the machine on which the software was first installed. Operating systems preinstalled on new computers are the classic example; such licences typically cannot legally be transferred to another machine.
