Hernando de Soto

Bornc. 1500, Jerez de los Caballeros, Spain
Died21 May 1542, Mississippi River (Guachoya)
OccupationConquistador, explorer
Known forExpedition through southeastern North America, reaching the Mississippi River

Hernando de Soto (c. 1500 – 1542) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador who led the first European expedition deep into the modern southeastern United States.

Early career

De Soto served in Panama and Nicaragua and with Francisco Pizarro in the conquest of Peru, amassing wealth before returning to Spain.

The southeastern expedition

Landing in Florida in 1539 with some 600 men, he marched through present-day Georgia, the Carolinas, Tennessee, Alabama and Mississippi in search of gold, fighting battles such as Mabila (1540). In May 1541 his party reached and crossed the Mississippi River — the first Europeans documented to do so. He died of fever on its banks in May 1542; Luis de Moscoso led survivors to Mexico in 1543.

Impact

The expedition found no gold but spread disease and violence that devastated Native American societies, while its chronicles preserve invaluable descriptions of Mississippian cultures.
