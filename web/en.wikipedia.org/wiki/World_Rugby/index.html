World Rugby

Founded1886 (as IRFB)
HeadquartersDublin, Ireland
SportRugby union and rugby sevens

World Rugby is the world governing body for the sports of rugby union and rugby sevens. Founded in 1886 as the International Rugby Football Board (IRFB) by the unions of Ireland, Scotland and Wales, it later became the International Rugby Board (IRB) and adopted its present name in 2014. It is headquartered in Dublin, Ireland.

Responsibilities

World Rugby sets and enforces the Laws of the Game, sanctions international fixtures, maintains the world rankings, and organises the Rugby World Cup—first held in 1987 and now one of the largest sporting events in the world—as well as the Women's Rugby World Cup, the Rugby World Cup Sevens and the annual Sevens Series. It also oversees rugby's Olympic participation in the sevens format.

Membership and governance

The body has over one hundred member unions organised through regional associations in Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania. Its council, executive committee and chairman govern the sport; the organisation has worked to grow the game in emerging markets and, since rugby union turned openly professional in 1995, to manage the sport's commercial and welfare standards, including player safety and concussion protocols.
