Kiyosi Itô

Kiyosi Itô (伊藤 清, Itô Kiyosi, 7 September 1915 – 10 November 2008) was a Japanese mathematician known for his pioneering work in stochastic analysis. He is regarded as the founder of the theory of stochastic differential equations, and Itô calculus is named after him.

Life and career

Itô was born in Hokusei, Mie Prefecture, and studied mathematics at the University of Tokyo. From 1939 to 1943 he worked as a statistician for the Statistics Bureau of the Cabinet, where he developed his foundational ideas in his spare time. He later became a professor at Kyoto University. His 1942 paper "On Stochastic Processes" laid the groundwork for modern probability theory.

Contributions

Itô introduced the Itô integral, a way of integrating functions with respect to Brownian motion, and proved Itô's lemma, a fundamental result in stochastic calculus widely used in mathematical finance, including in the derivation of the Black–Scholes model. His work earned him numerous honours, including the inaugural Carl Friedrich Gauss Prize in 2006, the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1987, and the Kyoto Prize in 1998.
