Tail rotor

TypeHelicopter component
FunctionCounteracts main-rotor torque; provides yaw control
AlternativesFenestron, NOTAR, tandem and coaxial rotors

A tail rotor is a smaller rotor mounted vertically (or near-vertically) at the tail of a conventional single-main-rotor helicopter. Its thrust counteracts the torque produced by the main rotor, which would otherwise cause the fuselage to spin in the opposite direction, and by varying its pitch the pilot controls yaw.

Design and operation

The tail rotor is driven from the main transmission by a drive shaft running along the tail boom, usually through an intermediate and tail gearbox. The pilot's anti-torque pedals vary the blade pitch, increasing or decreasing sideways thrust. Tail rotors typically have two to six blades and turn much faster than the main rotor. Loss of tail-rotor effectiveness — through mechanical failure or aerodynamic conditions such as weathercock instability and vortex ring state — is a recognised emergency, countered by procedures including autorotation.

Alternatives

Other anti-torque solutions include the ducted-fan fenestron (pioneered by Aérospatiale), the NOTAR system using the Coandă effect (MD Helicopters), and configurations that eliminate torque imbalance altogether: tandem rotors, coaxial rotors, intermeshing rotors, and tiltrotors in helicopter mode.
