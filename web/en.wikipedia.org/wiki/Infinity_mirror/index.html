Infinity mirror

Infinity mirror
ComponentsOne-way (two-way) mirror, ordinary mirror, light source
EffectApparently endless receding reflections

An infinity mirror is a configuration of two or more parallel or nearly parallel mirrors, creating a series of smaller and smaller reflections that appear to recede to infinity. Often a front mirror is a one-way (partially silvered) mirror, and lights — commonly a frame of LEDs — are placed between the mirrors; viewed from the front, the repeated reflections seem to tunnel endlessly into the distance.

How it works

Light from the internal source reflects back and forth between the fully reflective rear mirror and the partially reflective front mirror. At each bounce a fraction of the light passes through the front mirror to the viewer, while the rest continues, producing a geometric series of progressively dimmer images. Because real mirrors absorb some light, the tunnel fades after tens of visible repetitions. Even a slight angle between the mirrors makes the reflected path curve, creating spiralling or sweeping effects.

Uses

Infinity mirrors are used in decorative lighting, signage, dance floors, and novelty clocks and tables. In art they are famously central to Yayoi Kusama's Infinity Mirror Rooms, immersive installations in which mirrored chambers filled with lights or objects surround the viewer; similar effects are produced in kaleidoscopes and in the facing mirrors of lifts and dressing rooms.
