Iraqi insurgency

The Iraqi insurgency followed the 2003 invasion and toppling of Saddam Hussein.

An Iraqi insurgency began shortly after the 2003 invasion of Iraq and lasted throughout the ensuing Iraq War, which ended in 2011. The insurgency was directed against the U.S.-led Coalition forces, the multinational force, and the new Iraqi government, and involved a shifting coalition of Sunni Arab nationalist, Ba'athist, and Islamist groups — including al-Qaeda in Iraq — as well as Shia militias such as the Mahdi Army.

Origins

The insurgency took root following the dissolution of the Iraqi army and de-Ba'athification decrees issued by the Coalition Provisional Authority in May 2003, which left hundreds of thousands of armed, unemployed men alienated from the new order. Centred initially in the Sunni Triangle around Baghdad, Fallujah, Ramadi, and Tikrit, insurgents employed guerrilla tactics, roadside bombs (IEDs), snipers, and suicide attacks.

Course

Major episodes included the battles of Fallujah in 2004, the sectarian civil war of 2006–2007 ignited by the al-Askari Mosque bombing, and the combined effect of the 2007 troop surge and the Anbar Awakening in sharply reducing violence. A separate phase of insurgency continued after the U.S. withdrawal in 2011, when the Islamic State of Iraq exploited Sunni grievances and the Syrian civil war, culminating in its explosive 2014 offensive as ISIS.
