Military spending

World military expenditure surpassed $2.7 trillion in 2024, according to SIPRI.

Military spending, also known as military expenditure, is the amount of financial resources a state dedicates to maintaining and developing its armed forces. Global military spending is tracked principally by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI) and the International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS).

Trends

World military expenditure fell after the Cold War — the so-called "peace dividend" — reaching a low point in the late 1990s, then rose after 2001 with the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, plateaued in the 2010s, and climbed steeply after Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022. SIPRI estimated world spending at $2.44 trillion in 2023 and approximately $2.72 trillion in 2024, close to 2.5 percent of world GDP.

Measures

Spending is compared in absolute terms, per capita, and as a share of government expenditure or GDP. Absolute figures favor large economies — the United States, China, and Russia lead — while spending as a share of GDP is highest among states facing acute security threats, such as Ukraine, Israel, and the Gulf monarchies. NATO's 2-percent-of-GDP guideline is a prominent political benchmark.

Debate

Economists debate the opportunity costs and growth effects of military spending: critics contrast it with social expenditure ("guns versus butter"), while supporters argue it provides security, technological spillovers, and employment.
