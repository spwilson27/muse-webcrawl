Track ballast

Track ballast forms the trackbed upon which railroad ties (sleepers) are laid. It is packed between, below, and around the ties. It is used to bear the load from the railroad ties, to facilitate drainage of water, and to hold the track in place as trains roll over it. Ballast also reduces noise, holds down vegetation, and allows for adjustment of track position during maintenance.

Construction

Ballast typically consists of crushed stone — hard, angular rocks such as granite, basalt, or limestone — with individual stones roughly 28 to 63 mm in size. The angular shape helps the stones interlock and resist movement. A typical ballast bed is at least 150 mm thick beneath the ties, extending wider than the ties themselves to form "shoulders" that resist lateral movement of the track.

Maintenance

Over time, ballast becomes fouled by dirt, coal dust, and broken-down stone, losing its drainage and load-spreading ability. It is restored by tamping — packing ballast under ties with a tamping machine — and periodically by ballast cleaning or complete renewal. Fouled ballast can lead to wet spots, mud pumping, and loss of track geometry.

Alternatives

On some high-speed and urban lines, ballastless (slab) track is used instead, with rails fastened directly to a concrete or asphalt base; it costs more to build but requires less maintenance and keeps a more precise geometry.
