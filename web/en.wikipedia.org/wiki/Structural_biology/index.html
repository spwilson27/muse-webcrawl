Structural biology

Structural biology is a branch of molecular biology, biochemistry, and biophysics concerned with the molecular structure of biological macromolecules — especially proteins and nucleic acids — how they acquire the structures they have, and how alterations in their structures affect their function. Determining three-dimensional structures is central to understanding mechanisms of enzymes, receptors, and molecular machines, and to rational drug design.

Methods

The principal experimental techniques are X-ray crystallography, which produced the first protein structures (myoglobin and haemoglobin, recognised by the 1962 Nobel Prize to Kendrew and Perutz) and the DNA double helix evidence; nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, which studies smaller proteins in solution; and cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM), whose "resolution revolution" in the 2010s enabled near-atomic structures of large complexes such as the ribosome. Structures are deposited in the Protein Data Bank (PDB), founded in 1971.

Prediction

Computational prediction long lagged experiment until deep-learning systems — notably DeepMind's AlphaFold 2, which effectively solved the protein folding problem at CASP14 in 2020 — made accurate predicted structures available for hundreds of millions of proteins, transforming the field.
