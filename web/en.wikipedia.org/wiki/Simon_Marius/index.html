Simon Marius (Latinized form of Simon Mayr; 20 January 1573 – 26 December 1624) was a German astronomer, court mathematician to the Margraves of Brandenburg-Ansbach. He is known for his telescopic observations made independently of Galileo Galilei.

Observations

Marius observed Jupiter's four largest moons from late 1609 and published his results in Mundus Iovialis (1614). He gave the moons the mythological names by which they are still known — Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — reportedly on the suggestion of Johannes Kepler. Galileo accused Marius of plagiarism, and the priority dispute damaged Marius's reputation for centuries, though modern scholarship accepts that his observations were independent (though slightly later than Galileo's). Marius also made one of the earliest telescopic records of the Andromeda Galaxy (1612, which he compared to "a candle flame seen through horn"), observed sunspots, and noted the phases of Venus.
