WebCrawler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of the WebCrawler homepage from 1995 with its spider mascot on a web]

WebCrawler was a metasearch engine established in 1994, notable as the first web search engine to provide full-text search — indexing the actual words of pages rather than only their titles. It was developed by Brian Pinkerton at the University of Washington and launched on 20 April 1994 with a database of pages from about 4,000 websites.

WebCrawler's spider icon and clean operation made it one of the essential early-web landmarks, at times so popular it had to ask users to come back at night. It was acquired by AOL in 1995 and sold to Excite in 1997, passing through the portal era as a brand; it survives as a metasearch front end aggregating results from Google, Bing and others.

Its historical place is as the proof of concept for modern web search: that a crawler, an index and a query box could tame the whole web — the template AltaVista scaled and Google perfected.
