Eucommia

KingdomPlantae
OrderGarryales
FamilyEucommiaceae
GenusEucommia Oliv.
SpeciesE. ulmoides

Eucommia is a genus of small trees native to China. It is the only genus in the family Eucommiaceae and contains a single living species, Eucommia ulmoides, the hardy rubber tree. The genus is much older than its single survivor suggests: fossils of Eucommia are widespread across the Northern Hemisphere, known from Eocene deposits in North America and Europe, indicating that it was formerly a common component of northern temperate forests before becoming restricted to central China.

Description

Eucommia ulmoides is a deciduous tree growing to about 15–20 metres. Its glossy, elm-like leaves — reflected in the epithet ulmoides, "elm-like" — contain latex: when a leaf is torn, strands of rubbery white threads stretch between the pieces. The tree is dioecious, with inconspicuous wind-pollinated flowers and winged samara fruits.

Uses

The bark, known as dùzhòng (杜仲), has been used for over two thousand years in traditional Chinese medicine, typically for back and joint complaints and to support the kidneys and liver. Modern research has examined its lignans and iridoids, such as pinoresinol diglucoside, for blood-pressure-lowering effects. The latex itself is a source of gutta-percha-like rubber (trans-polyisoprene), and the tree is planted in China as a potential alternative rubber source. Unusually cold-hardy for a rubber-bearing plant, it is also grown in the West as an ornamental shade tree.
