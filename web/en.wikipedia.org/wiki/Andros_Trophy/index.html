Andros Trophy

The Andros Trophy (French: Trophée Andros) is a French national ice racing championship held each winter, principally at ski resorts in the Alps and Pyrenees. Founded in 1990 by racing driver Max Mamers together with the Andros food company, it became the world's best-known ice racing series, attracting leading rally and circuit drivers including multiple Formula One participants and WRC champions.

History

The series grew out of winter races organized at Serre Chevalier in the 1980s. From its first championship in 1990, the Trophée Andros combined specially prepared four-wheel-drive, four-wheel-steering cars with studded tyres on short ice circuits. Its televised spectacle and off-season timing made it popular with professional drivers: Alain Prost, Olivier Panis, Sébastien Loeb and others have competed, and Yvan Muller won the championship a record ten times. Jean-Philippe Dayraut won six titles, and Jean-Baptiste Dubourg won five consecutive titles from 2016.

Cars and format

In the top class, silhouette cars with tubular chassis, mid-mounted engines of roughly 350 horsepower, all-wheel drive and all-wheel steering compete in qualifying heats and finals over a winter calendar of rounds at venues including Val Thorens, L'Alpe d'Huez, Andorra, Isola 2000 and Super Besse. A parallel motorcycle class, the Pilot Bike, and ladies' and sprint-car categories have featured in the programme.

Electric era

From the 2019–20 season the championship's top cars became fully electric, racing as the e-Trophée Andros with identical electric silhouette cars, making it one of the first major motorsport series to switch entirely to electric power. The change was promoted as a response to environmental concerns about racing on melting alpine ice.
