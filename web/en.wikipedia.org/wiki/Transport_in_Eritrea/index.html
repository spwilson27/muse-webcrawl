Transport in Eritrea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transport in Eritrea comprises highways, airports and seaports, as well as various forms of public and private transport. Eritrea's transport infrastructure was heavily damaged during the thirty-year war of independence from Ethiopia (1961–1991) and the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000, and has been only partially rebuilt since.

Railways

The single Eritrean Railway line runs 118 km between the Red Sea port of Massawa and the capital, Asmara, climbing from sea level to about 2,394 m through steep gradients, tunnels and viaducts. Built by the Italians between 1887 and 1932 with a narrow gauge of 950 mm, the line fell into disuse during the war and was restored after independence using local labour and vintage steam locomotives; it operates mainly tourist and limited services. Proposals to extend it toward Bisha and Sudan have not been realised.

Roads

Eritrea has roughly 4,000 km of roads, of which a minority are paved. The most important highway descends the escarpment from Asmara to Massawa; other main routes link Asmara with Keren, Barentu and the Sudanese border, and with the southern port of Assab along the coast. Long-distance buses and minibuses are the principal form of public transport.

Ports and airports

The natural deep-water ports of Massawa and Assab historically handled most of Ethiopia's trade, but that traffic ceased after the 1998–2000 border war, sharply reducing port activity. Asmara International Airport is the country's main airport; Eritrean Airlines and foreign carriers have served it only intermittently, and smaller airstrips exist at Massawa, Assab and other towns.
