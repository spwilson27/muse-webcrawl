Sweet and sour pork

Sweet and sour pork (Chinese: 糖醋里脊 / 咕嚕肉; pinyin: tángcù lǐjǐ / gūlū ròu) is a Chinese dish consisting of pieces of pork that are battered, deep-fried, and served in a sweet and sour sauce. It is a classic of Cantonese cuisine and one of the best-known Chinese dishes internationally.

Preparation

The pork — typically tenderloin or shoulder — is cut into bite-sized pieces, marinated, coated in a batter or starch, and deep-fried until crisp. The sauce is made from sugar, vinegar (often rice vinegar or ketchup in Westernised versions), and soy sauce, thickened with cornstarch, and commonly includes bell peppers, onion, and pineapple chunks.

History and variants

The dish originates in Guangdong province. The Cantonese version known as gu lou yuk (咕嚕肉, "ancient meat") was adapted for Western palates in Chinese restaurants abroad during the 20th century, often with a brighter red sauce made with ketchup. Related versions exist in Korean-Chinese cuisine (tangsuyuk) and Japanese-Chinese cuisine (subuta). A "sweet and sour" preparation is also applied to chicken, fish, and spare ribs.
