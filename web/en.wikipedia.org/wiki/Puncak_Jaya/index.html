Puncak Jaya

Puncak Jaya (Indonesian: [ˈpuntʃak ˈdʒaja]; literally "Glorious Peak"), sometimes called Mount Carstensz or the Carstensz Pyramid, is a mountain in the Sudirman Range of the highlands of Papua, Indonesia. At 4,884 metres (16,024 ft) above sea level, it is the highest summit of Oceania, the highest mountain in Indonesia, and the highest island mountain in the world. It is also the highest point between the Himalayas and the Andes, and one of the Seven Summits.

Geography and geology

Puncak Jaya is a limestone peak in the rugged central cordillera of New Guinea, within Lorentz National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its summit was once capped by the Carstensz Glacier and neighbouring ice fields; these equatorial glaciers have retreated dramatically since the 19th century and are expected to disappear entirely within years. The enormous Grasberg gold and copper mine, one of the world's largest, lies a few kilometres west of the peak.

History

The peak is named for the Dutch explorer Jan Carstenszoon, who sighted its glaciers in 1623 and was ridiculed in Europe for claiming snow near the equator. The first ascent was made in 1962 by an expedition led by the Austrian mountaineer Heinrich Harrer, with Phillip Temple, Russell Kippax, and Albertus Huizenga. Under Indonesian rule the mountain was renamed Puncak Jaya. Access has often been complicated by permit requirements and unrest in Papua.

Climbing

Although modest in elevation, Puncak Jaya is technically demanding: the standard route involves rock climbing up to grade 5.8 on the sharp summit ridge, including tyrolean traverses between pinnacles. It is frequently considered, with Mount Vinson, among the most logistically difficult of the Seven Summits to reach.
