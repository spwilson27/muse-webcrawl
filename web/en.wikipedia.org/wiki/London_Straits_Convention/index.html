London Straits Convention
Signed: 13 July 1841
Subject: Turkish Straits

The London Straits Convention of 1841 was an international treaty signed by the major European powers and the Ottoman Empire regulating navigation through the Dardanelles and the Bosphorus.

Terms

It affirmed the Ottoman rule closing the Straits to all foreign warships in peacetime, internationalising the arrangement and replacing the bilateral Treaty of Hunkar Iskelesi between Russia and the Ottomans.

Significance

The convention was an early example of multilateral regulation of an international waterway and remained influential until replaced by later settlements, culminating in the Montreux Convention of 1936.
