Shalimar (perfume)

Shalimar is a perfume originally created by the French house of Guerlain in 1921 and launched in 1925. Composed by Jacques Guerlain, it is regarded as the archetypal "oriental" (now often termed "amber") fragrance, built around vanilla, bergamot, iris, and resinous notes, and it remains one of the most influential perfumes of the 20th century.

History

Legend holds that Jacques Guerlain created Shalimar by adding a large dose of ethylvanillin to a bottle of Jicky (1889), discovering the accord that defines the perfume. It was presented in 1925 at the International Exhibition of Modern Decorative and Industrial Arts in Paris, where its bottle — designed by Raymond Guerlain and made by Baccarat, with a distinctive fan-shaped blue stopper inspired by the basins of the Shalimar Gardens — won a prize. The name evokes the Shalimar Gardens of Lahore, associated with the love story of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal.

Composition

Shalimar opens with bright citrus (bergamot, lemon, cedar) over a floral heart of iris, rose, jasmine, and patchouli, settling into a warm, powdery base of vanilla, tonka bean, opoponax, incense, and leather. Its generous use of vanillin made it the template for the oriental family.

Legacy and flankers

Shalimar has remained in production for a century, a bestseller and reference fragrance for perfumers. Guerlain has released many flankers and editions, including Shalimar Parfum Initial (2011) and Shalimar Souffle de Parfum, as well as limited millésime editions. It was inducted into the Fragrance Foundation's Hall of Fame.
