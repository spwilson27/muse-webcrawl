A Dynkin system, named after the Russian mathematician Eugene Dynkin and also called a λ-system, is a collection of subsets of a set Ω satisfying a set of closure properties weaker than those of a σ-algebra. Dynkin systems arise naturally in measure theory and probability as the collections of sets on which two measures agree.

Definition

A collection D of subsets of Ω is a Dynkin system if: (1) Ω itself belongs to D; (2) D is closed under complementation — if A is in D, so is its complement; and (3) D is closed under countable unions of pairwise disjoint sets. Equivalently, one may require closure under proper differences and increasing countable unions (a monotone class formulation).

The π-λ theorem

The central result is Dynkin's π-λ theorem: if a Dynkin system contains a π-system (a collection closed under finite intersections), then it contains the σ-algebra generated by that π-system. Consequently, any Dynkin system that is also a π-system is a σ-algebra. The theorem is a standard tool for proving that properties of measures — such as uniqueness of extension, or that two probability measures agreeing on a π-system agree on the generated σ-algebra — hold in full generality.

Applications

Typical applications include showing that two probability measures agreeing on intervals agree on all Borel sets, proving that the image of a random variable has a well-defined distribution, and establishing independence criteria in probability theory.
