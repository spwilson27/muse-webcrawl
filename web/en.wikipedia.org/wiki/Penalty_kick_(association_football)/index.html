Penalty kick (association football)

A penalty kick (commonly known as a penalty or a spot kick) is a method of restarting play in association football, in which a player is allowed to take a single shot at the goal while it is defended only by the opposing team's goalkeeper. It is awarded when a player commits a foul punishable by a direct free kick inside their own penalty area, or commits a handball offence inside that area.

The kick is taken from the penalty mark, 11 metres (12 yards) from the goal line. All other players must remain outside the penalty area and at least 9.15 metres from the mark until the kick is taken. The goalkeeper must stay on the goal line until the ball is kicked.

Penalty kicks are a decisive element of the game: in open play they are converted roughly three-quarters of the time. They are also used in the penalty shoot-out that decides drawn knockout matches, in which teams take alternating kicks from the mark.

The penalty kick was introduced in 1891, following a proposal by Irish goalkeeper and businessman William McCrum.
