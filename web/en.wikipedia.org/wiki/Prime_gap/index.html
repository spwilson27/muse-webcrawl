Prime gap

A prime gap is the difference between two successive prime numbers. The n-th prime gap, denoted gn, is the difference between the (n+1)-th and the n-th prime numbers, i.e. gn = pn+1 − pn. The first prime gaps are 1, 2, 2, 4, 2, 4, 2, 4, 6, 2, 6, ... (for example, between 2 and 3 the gap is 1; between 3 and 5 it is 2; between 7 and 11 it is 4).

Simple results

Prime gaps grow without bound: for any positive integer m, the consecutive integers (m+1)! + 2, ..., (m+1)! + (m+1) are all composite, giving a gap of at least m. On the other hand, by results about primes in short intervals, maximal gaps below x are far smaller than x itself; the prime number theorem suggests that typical gaps near x are of size about log x.

Numerical results

Large prime gaps are found by computation. A gap is called a first occurrence or record prime gap if it is larger than all gaps between smaller primes. As of recent large-scale searches, the largest known prime gaps exceed 1,400 in width and occur between primes with thousands of digits.

Conjectures

Cramér's conjecture proposes that maximal gaps are on the order of (log p)², while Legendre's conjecture — that there is always a prime between consecutive squares — would imply gaps of size at most about 2√p. Neither has been proven.
