Hill 60 (Ypres)

Hill 60
Location: near Zwarteleen, south-east of Ypres, Belgium
Type: battlefield memorial site
Elevation: about 60 m above sea level

Hill 60 is a World War I battlefield and memorial site situated some 3 miles (4.8 km) south-east of Ypres, Belgium. It takes its name from the contour line marking its height of about 60 metres above sea level. The hill was not natural: it was formed from spoil excavated during the construction of the Ypres–Comines railway cutting in the nineteenth century, with a second heap, known as "the Caterpillar", on the opposite side of the line.

Strategic importance

Because of its slight elevation over the surrounding Flanders plain, Hill 60 gave whoever held it observation over Ypres and the Zillebeke area. Together with nearby positions such as Zwarteleen and Sanctuary Wood, it formed part of the Ypres Salient's most contested ground.

Battles of 1915

German troops occupied the hill in late 1914. On 17 April 1915 British tunnellers fired six mines beneath the position and infantry captured the summit at small initial cost. Fierce counter-attacks followed, and in May 1915 the Germans retook the hill after releasing chlorine gas, one of the early gas attacks on the Western Front. The fighting involved desperate close combat in which several Victoria Crosses were won.

Underground war

For the rest of the war the hill was honeycombed with tunnels and mine galleries dug by British, Canadian, Australian and German tunnelling companies. The 1st Australian Tunnelling Company, whose work included the deep mines fired at the Battle of Messines in June 1917, maintained the defences of Hill 60; its story formed the basis of the 2010 film Beneath Hill 60, centred on Captain Oliver Woodward. The hill itself remained in German hands until the Messines offensive.

Memorial

After the war Hill 60 was preserved as a battlefield site. Its scarred, cratered surface, concrete bunkers and memorials — including one to the 1st Australian Tunnelling Company — survive, and the ground is maintained as a memorial to the soldiers of all nations who fought there. The adjacent Caterpillar crater, blown in 1917, is now a pond.
