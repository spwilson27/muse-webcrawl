The Untouchables (film)

The Untouchables is a 1987 American crime film directed by Brian De Palma, produced by Art Linson, and written by David Mamet. It stars Kevin Costner, Charles Martin Smith, Andy García, Robert De Niro and Sean Connery, and follows Prohibition-era federal agent Eliot Ness and his small team of incorruptible officers — the "Untouchables" — in their pursuit of gangster Al Capone in Chicago.

Plot

After his first raid fails through corruption, Ness recruits veteran Irish-American beat cop Jimmy Malone (Connery), rookie sharpshooter George Stone (García) and accountant Oscar Wallace (Smith). Their raids damage Capone's bootlegging empire, but Wallace and Malone are murdered by Capone's enforcer Frank Nitti. Ness nonetheless secures the evidence and testimony that convict Capone of tax evasion, sending him to prison. The film's set pieces include the Potemkin-inspired baby-carriage shootout on the steps of Union Station.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success. Connery won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his performance, and Ennio Morricone's score was widely praised.
