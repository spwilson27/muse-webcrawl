Lend-Lease

Lend-Lease, formally the Lend-Lease Act (Public Law 77-11), was a United States program under which the U.S. supplied the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, China, France, and other Allied nations with food, oil, and materiel — including warships, warplanes, weapons, and vehicles — between 1941 and 1945 during the Second World War. In total, about $50.1 billion (equivalent to roughly $700 billion in modern terms) in supplies was shipped, about 17 percent of total U.S. war expenditure.

Background

After the fall of France in 1940, Britain stood nearly alone against Germany but was running out of cash to pay for American supplies under the "cash-and-carry" policy. Prime Minister Winston Churchill appealed to President Franklin D. Roosevelt, who proposed "lending" supplies in return for later consideration — comparing it to lending a garden hose to a neighbour whose house is on fire. The Act was signed on 11 March 1941, months before the U.S. formally entered the war.

Scale and recipients

The largest recipient was the British Empire, receiving about $31.4 billion, followed by the Soviet Union with about $11.3 billion, France with $3.2 billion, and China with $1.6 billion. Supplies to the USSR flowed through the Persian Corridor, the Arctic convoys to Murmansk and Arkhangelsk, and the Pacific route to Vladivostok. Lend-Lease provided the Red Army with hundreds of thousands of trucks, locomotives, aviation fuel, food, and communications equipment, greatly improving Soviet mobility and logistics.

Reverse Lend-Lease and settlement

Allies also provided goods and services to the United States — bases, food, and supplies worth about $7.8 billion, known as Reverse Lend-Lease. The program ended abruptly in September 1945 after Japan's surrender. Britain's remaining debt was settled with a long-term loan in 1946; the Soviet Union's settlement was disputed for decades, and Russia continued repayments into the 21st century.

Significance

Lend-Lease was a decisive factor in the Allied victory and a major step in the United States becoming the "arsenal of democracy". It marked the end of American neutrality in practice and the beginning of the post-war pattern of American military aid programs.
