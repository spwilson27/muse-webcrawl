"Beautiful Dreamer" is a parlor song by the American composer Stephen Foster, published posthumously in 1864, shortly after his death. Written in a lilting triple metre (9/8 in the original), it is a serenade addressed to a sleeping beloved — "Beautiful dreamer, wake unto me" — and is among the most enduring of Foster's songs, unusual among his late works in its tenderness and freedom from the minstrel idiom of his earlier hits.

History

Foster sold or left the song among the manuscripts published in the year of his death; its first edition (William A. Pond & Co., 1864) billed it as "the last song ever written" by Foster, a common marketing claim of the time, though the manuscript is dated two years earlier. Some scholars have read the song in the tradition of Foster's laments for the dead — the "dreamer" being a beloved who will not wake — while others take it simply as a lover's serenade. Either way its melody, built on broken-chord arpeggios over a simple harmony, made it a staple of the Victorian parlour.

Recordings and cultural use

The song has been recorded by countless singers, from early recording pioneer Edward M. Favor to Bing Crosby, Roy Orbison and many others, and arranged for everything from string quartet to barbershop chorus. It appears frequently in film and television as period colour and as a lullaby, and its melody is widely recognised even by listeners who cannot name it. Together with "Jeanie with the Light Brown Hair" and "Hard Times Come Again No More," it represents the sentimental ballad style of Foster's final years.
