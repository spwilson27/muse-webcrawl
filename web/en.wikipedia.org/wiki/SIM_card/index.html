A SIM card (subscriber identity module) is an integrated circuit operating system stored on a removable smart card used in mobile devices such as mobile phones and computers. SIM cards store information used to authenticate and identify a subscriber to a mobile telephone network, most importantly the IMSI number and its related authentication key. SIM cards can also store contact information and text messages.

History

The SIM was initially specified by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute for the GSM standard. The first SIM card was made in 1991 by the Munich smart card maker Giesecke & Devrient, which sold the first 300 cards to the Finnish network operator Radiolinja. Originally the size of a credit card, SIMs progressively shrank through the mini-SIM (2FF), micro-SIM (3FF) and nano-SIM (4FF) formats.

eSIM and iSIM

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is soldered into the device and can be provisioned remotely with operator profiles, eliminating the physical card. Many modern smartphones support eSIM, and some models sold in the United States are eSIM-only. The iSIM integrates the SIM function directly into the device's main processor.

Security

Access to the SIM is protected by a personal identification number (PIN), with a PUK code used to unlock the card after repeated incorrect entries. The SIM performs mutual authentication with the network, using cryptographic keys that never leave the card.
