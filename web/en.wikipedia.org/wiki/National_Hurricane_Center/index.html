National Hurricane Center

AbbreviationNHC
ParentNOAA / National Weather Service
LocationFlorida International University, Miami, Florida

The National Hurricane Center (NHC) is the division of the United States National Weather Service responsible for tracking and predicting tropical cyclones in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific basins. Based on the campus of Florida International University in Miami, it issues the watches, warnings, forecasts and storm names familiar across the Caribbean, Gulf Coast and eastern seaboard during the June-to-November hurricane season.

Operations

NHC hurricane specialists work in shifts around the clock during active weather, analysing satellite imagery, reconnaissance aircraft data (from NOAA and Air Force Reserve "Hurricane Hunters"), radar and computer models. Every six hours they issue advisories on each active storm with a forecast track — the famous "cone of uncertainty" — intensity forecast, and associated hazards: storm surge, rainfall flooding and tornadoes. The centre also issues Tropical Weather Outlooks discussing disturbances that might develop. Storms reaching sustained winds of 39 mph are named from rotating six-year lists maintained by the World Meteorological Organization; names of especially deadly or costly storms, such as Katrina, Sandy and Ian, are retired.

History

Federal hurricane forecasting in the U.S. dates to the Weather Bureau offices of the late nineteenth century; the Miami office became the focal point after the great hurricanes of the 1920s and 1930s, and the modern centre was formally designated in the 1960s. Forecast accuracy has improved dramatically: three-day track errors have shrunk by roughly two-thirds since the 1990s, and the experimental storm surge inundation maps added in the 2010s reflect a growing emphasis on water, not wind, as the deadliest hurricane hazard.
