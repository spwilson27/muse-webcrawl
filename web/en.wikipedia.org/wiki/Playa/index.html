Playa

A playa (Spanish for "beach") is a dry lake bed: a flat-bottomed depression found in interior desert basins that is at least temporarily filled with water after rain, forming a shallow, ephemeral lake. Playas are also known as dry lakes, salt pans (when covered in salt deposits), alkali flats, or sabkhas in Arabic-speaking regions.

Formation

Playas form in closed (endorheic) drainage basins where water flows inward but has no outlet to the sea. In arid climates, evaporation exceeds inflow, so water collects only briefly after storms. As the water evaporates, it leaves behind dissolved minerals—especially sodium chloride (common salt), gypsum, and other evaporites—forming bright, flat crusts. Because sediment settles evenly in still water, playa surfaces are among the flattest natural landforms on Earth, sometimes varying by only a few centimetres over many kilometres.

When dry, the clay-rich surface typically cracks into polygonal mudcracks. Wind can scour loose material from playas, contributing to dust storms and to the excavation of deflation basins; conversely, playas can trap wind-blown sediment.

Examples

Famous playas include the Bonneville Salt Flats in Utah (remnant of Pleistocene Lake Bonneville), the Black Rock Desert playa in Nevada, Racetrack Playa in Death Valley—known for its "sailing stones"—and Rogers Dry Lake at Edwards Air Force Base in California. Large salt pans elsewhere include the Salar de Uyuni in Bolivia, the world's largest salt flat, and the Etosha Pan in Namibia.

Human uses

The extreme flatness and hardness of dry playas make them useful as natural runways and proving grounds. Rogers Dry Lake served as a landing site for the Space Shuttle and for experimental aircraft, and the Bonneville Salt Flats host land-speed record attempts. The Black Rock Desert playa hosts the annual Burning Man festival. Playas and salt pans are also mined for lithium-rich brines, borax, and other evaporite minerals, and they provide important, if temporary, habitat for migratory birds and specialized invertebrates such as brine shrimp when flooded.
