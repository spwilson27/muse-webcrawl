King's College, Cambridge

Founded: 1441
Founder: Henry VI
Location: King's Parade, Cambridge

King's College, formally The King's College of Our Lady and Saint Nicholas in Cambridge, is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1441 by King Henry VI, it is renowned for its chapel, its choir, and its position on King's Parade in the centre of Cambridge.

History

Henry VI founded the college shortly after founding Eton College, intending the two to be linked, with scholars progressing from Eton to King's. The college's early progress was interrupted by the Wars of the Roses, and its famous chapel took over a century to complete, being finished under the Tudors. Originally admitting only Etonians, the college opened its fellowships and scholarships more widely in the 19th century, admitted women in 1972, and is known for a tradition of academic excellence and a relatively informal, progressive ethos.

Buildings

King's College Chapel, begun in 1446 and completed in 1544, is one of the greatest surviving examples of late Perpendicular Gothic architecture, with the largest fan vault in the world and celebrated stained glass. Other notable buildings include the Gibbs Building (1720s), the riverside lawns of the Backs, and the college bridge over the River Cam.

Choir and alumni

The Choir of King's College, founded in the 15th century, is famous worldwide for the annual Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols broadcast from the chapel on Christmas Eve. Notable alumni include Alan Turing, John Maynard Keynes, Rupert Brooke, E. M. Forster, and M. R. James.
