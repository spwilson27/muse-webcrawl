Lakshmana Temple, Khajuraho

The Lakshmana Temple is a Hindu temple in Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, India, dedicated to the god Vishnu. Built around 930–950 CE by the Chandela king Yashodharman, it is one of the earliest and best-preserved temples of the Khajuraho Group of Monuments, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Architecture

The temple stands on a high platform (jagati) flanked by four subsidiary shrines, and follows the Nagara style: a panchayatana layout with an entrance porch, hall, vestibule, and sanctum crowned by a tall shikhara. Its walls are covered with intricate sculpture, including scenes of daily life, battle, dance, and the erotic carvings for which Khajuraho is famous.

Dedication

An inscription records that Yashodharman (Lakshmanavarman) installed an image of Vaikuntha Vishnu in the temple, giving the shrine its name.
