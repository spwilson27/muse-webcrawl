Patent ductus arteriosus

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is a medical condition in which the ductus arteriosus fails to close after birth. In the fetus, the ductus arteriosus connects the pulmonary artery to the aorta, allowing blood to bypass the unventilated lungs. Normally it closes within days of birth; when it remains open (patent), blood flows abnormally between the aorta and pulmonary artery.

Signs and symptoms

A small PDA may cause no symptoms. Larger shunts can cause a continuous machine-like heart murmur, bounding pulses, poor feeding, breathlessness and failure to thrive in infants, and over time can lead to heart failure and pulmonary hypertension. PDA is especially common in premature infants.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include prematurity, congenital rubella syndrome, genetic conditions such as Down syndrome, and birth at high altitude. The closure of the ductus depends on falling prostaglandin levels and rising oxygen after birth, which is why prostaglandin inhibitors can promote closure.

Treatment

Treatment options include watchful waiting, medication with NSAIDs such as indomethacin or ibuprofen, catheter-based closure with a coil or occluder device, and surgical ligation. Management in very premature infants remains debated, as many PDAs close spontaneously.
