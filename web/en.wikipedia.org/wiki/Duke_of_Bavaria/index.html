Duke of Bavaria

The Duke of Bavaria was the ruler of the Duchy of Bavaria, one of the stem duchies of the Kingdom of Germany and later a principality of the Holy Roman Empire. The title existed in various forms from the sixth century, when Bavaria was ruled by the Agilolfing dynasty under Frankish overlordship, until 1623, when Duke Maximilian I was elevated to the rank of Elector and the ducal title was subsumed into the electoral dignity.

History

The first documented duke was Garibald I of the Agilolfings, around 555. After Charlemagne deposed Tassilo III in 788, Bavaria was administered by Frankish prefects before the duchy was revived in the tenth century amid the collapse of Carolingian authority. The Welf duke Henry the Lion received Bavaria in 1156, but in 1180 Frederick Barbarossa stripped him of it and enfeoffed Otto of Wittelsbach. The Wittelsbach family then ruled Bavaria continuously for more than seven centuries, dividing it at times into Upper and Lower Bavaria and into the cadet duchies of Bavaria-Munich, Bavaria-Landshut, Bavaria-Ingolstadt, and Bavaria-Straubing, before reunification under Albert IV in 1505.

The head of the House of Wittelsbach today, Franz Bonaventura Adalbert Maria, is styled by royalists as Duke of Bavaria, a courtesy title also borne by Jacobite supporters who regard him as heir to the Stuart claim.
