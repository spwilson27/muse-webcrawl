Lake Eyasi is a shallow, seasonal salt lake at the base of the Serengeti Plateau in northern Tanzania, southwest of the Ngorongoro Crater. It occupies the Eyasi-Wembere branch of the East African Rift and is drained by no outlet; it may dry out almost completely in the dry season.

Environment

The lake is fed by the Sibiti River and smaller streams from the surrounding highlands. Its shores are lined with doum palms and acacias, and hippos gather in pools at the river mouths during dry periods. Lesser flamingos visit in numbers when algal conditions are right. Onion farming by irrigation is practised around the northern shore.

People

The lakeshore is home to the Hadza (Hadzabe), one of the last remaining hunter-gatherer peoples of Africa, whose click language is a linguistic isolate. The Datoga, pastoralist neighbours and occasional rivals of the Hadza, also live in the region. In 1934 the German explorer Ludwig Kohl-Larsen excavated at the lake and recovered the fragmented "Eyasi skull," an archaic human fossil attributed to early Homo sapiens.
