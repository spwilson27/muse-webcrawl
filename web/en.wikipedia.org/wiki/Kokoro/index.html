Kokoro (こころ, "heart") is a 1914 novel by Japanese author Natsume Sōseki, regarded as one of the greatest works of modern Japanese literature.

Plot

The novel is in three parts. A young student befriends an older man he calls "Sensei" in Tokyo and Kamakura, sensing a hidden sorrow. The second part recounts the narrator's return home to his dying father. In the third, Sensei's long testament letter reveals his past: his betrayal, amid a love triangle over the woman he later married, of his friend K, who took his own life — a guilt that has haunted him, and that drives his own suicide as the Meiji era ends with Emperor Meiji's death and General Nogi's junshi.

Themes

Kokoro explores guilt, egoism, loneliness, and the transition from Meiji to modern Japan. It remains a fixture of Japanese school curricula and has been translated many times, notably by Edwin McClellan and Meredith McKinney.
