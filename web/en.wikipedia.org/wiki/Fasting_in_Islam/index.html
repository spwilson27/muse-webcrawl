Fasting in Islam

Fasting in Islam (Arabic: ṣawm) is the practice of abstaining, usually from food, drink, sexual activity and sinful behaviour, from dawn until sunset. Fasting during the month of Ramadan is one of the Five Pillars of Islam.

Ramadan fast

Muslims who are physically able fast each day of Ramadan from the pre-dawn meal (suḥūr) until the sunset meal (ifṭār). The Qur'an prescribes fasting in Surah Al-Baqarah and exempts the ill, travellers, pregnant or menstruating women, the elderly and young children, who may make up days later or feed the poor.

Rules and exemptions

Actions that break the fast include intentional eating, drinking and marital relations during daylight. Involuntary vomiting, forgetful eating and medical necessity are treated differently in jurisprudence (fiqh). The fast ends each evening, and Ramadan culminates in Eid al-Fitr.

Other fasts

Voluntary fasts include Mondays and Thursdays, the Day of Arafah, Ashura, the six days of Shawwal, and the white days (13th–15th of each lunar month). Fasting is forbidden on Eid days.

Spiritual purpose

Fasting is intended to cultivate God-consciousness (taqwa), self-discipline, gratitude and empathy for the poor. Communities emphasise charity (zakat and sadaqah), night prayers (tarawih) and Qur'an recitation during Ramadan.
