Three Sisters (play)

Written byAnton Chekhov

GenreDrama

Three Sisters (Russian: Три сёстры, Tri syostry) is a play by the Russian author and playwright Anton Chekhov. It was written in 1900 and first performed in 1901 at the Moscow Art Theatre, directed by Konstantin Stanislavski. The play is a naturalistic study of the decay of the privileged class in Russia and the search for meaning in the modern world, and it is regarded as one of Chekhov's greatest works.

Plot

The three Prozorov sisters — Olga, a schoolteacher; Masha, unhappily married; and Irina, the youngest — live with their brother Andrey in a provincial garrison town, having moved from Moscow eleven years earlier with their late father, a general. They dream endlessly of returning to Moscow, but year by year the dream recedes: Andrey marries the grasping Natasha, who gradually takes over the house; Masha falls in love with the married officer Vershinin; Irina accepts the earnest Baron Tuzenbach, who is killed in a duel by the unstable Solyony on the eve of their wedding. As the regiment departs, the sisters resolve to endure and work, without ever leaving.

Themes

The play's action is famously indirect: events occur offstage or dissolve into conversation, while time, decay and unrealised longing carry the drama. Its recurring motifs include the contrast between imagined and actual lives, the meaning of work, and the hope that future generations may understand what the present generation cannot.

Reception and legacy

Early audiences were sometimes baffled by the play's lack of conventional plot, but Three Sisters has become a cornerstone of the modern repertoire, performed worldwide and translated and adapted many times, including notable English versions by Michael Frayn and others. The cry "To Moscow! To Moscow!" has become a byword for unattainable longing.
