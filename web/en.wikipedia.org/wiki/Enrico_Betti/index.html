Enrico Betti (21 October 1823 – 11 August 1892) was an Italian mathematician, remembered for his 1871 paper on the properties of spaces now known as Betti numbers. He also worked on the theory of equations, giving early expositions of Galois theory, and on elasticity, where Betti's reciprocal theorem bears his name.

Biography

Betti was born in Pistoia, Tuscany, and graduated from the University of Pisa in 1846. He taught at Pistoia and Florence before returning to Pisa, where he became professor in 1859 and later director of the Scuola Normale Superiore. In 1858 he toured Europe with Francesco Brioschi and Felice Casorati, meeting Bernhard Riemann, whose ideas strongly influenced his later topological work. Betti also participated in the Italian wars of independence, fighting at the battles of Curtatone and Montanara, and later served in the Italian parliament and senate, and as undersecretary of state for education.

Work

Betti's most famous contribution came in his 1871 paper Sopra gli spazi di un numero qualunque di dimensioni, in which he introduced the invariants now called Betti numbers, describing the connectivity of a space in each dimension — work developed independently of, and partly anticipating, the homology theory later formalised by Henri Poincaré. In elasticity, Betti's theorem (1872) states a reciprocity relation between the work of two systems of forces acting on an elastic body, generalising earlier results of Maxwell.
