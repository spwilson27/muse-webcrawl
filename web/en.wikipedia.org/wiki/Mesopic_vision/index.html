Mesopic vision

Mesopic vision is the vision of the eye under intermediate light levels — brighter than the purely rod-driven scotopic vision of very low light, but dimmer than the cone-driven photopic vision of daylight. Mesopic conditions range roughly from 0.001 to 3 cd/m² of luminance, and are typical of twilight and many night-time lighting situations, including road lighting.

Physiology

In mesopic vision both rods and cones contribute to perception, and the balance shifts with light level. Spectral sensitivity moves progressively toward shorter wavelengths as luminance falls — the Purkinje effect — so blue-green surfaces appear relatively brighter and reds duller as light dims. Visual acuity and colour discrimination are poorer than in photopic vision but better than in scotopic vision.

Photometry

Because the standard photopic and scotopic luminosity functions do not describe mesopic sensitivity, the CIE published a recommended system for mesopic photometry (CIE 191:2010) that blends the two functions according to adaptation luminance. Mesopic photometry is applied in road and exterior lighting design, where it can change the apparent effectiveness of light sources with different spectra, such as metal halide or LED lamps versus high-pressure sodium.
