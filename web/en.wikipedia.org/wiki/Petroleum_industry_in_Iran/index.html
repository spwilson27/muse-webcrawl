Petroleum industry in Iran

Proven reserves: approx. 209 billion barrels (among the world's largest)

Key company: National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC)

Historic centre: Abadan refinery (1912)

The petroleum industry in Iran is one of the oldest in the Middle East and central to the country's economy and modern political history. Iran holds some of the world's largest proven reserves of crude oil and natural gas, and hydrocarbons have long supplied the bulk of its export earnings and a large share of government revenue.

Origins: D'Arcy and the Anglo-Persian Oil Company

In 1901 the Shah granted a vast concession to William Knox D'Arcy. After years of fruitless drilling, oil was struck at Masjed Soleyman on 26 May 1908 — the first commercial discovery in the Middle East. The Anglo-Persian Oil Company (later Anglo-Iranian, then BP) was founded in 1909, and the great refinery at Abadan, for decades the largest in the world, entered service in 1912.

Nationalisation and revolution

Resentment of British control culminated in 1951 when Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh nationalised the industry, creating the National Iranian Oil Company. Britain organised a boycott and embargo; in 1953 Mosaddegh was overthrown in a coup backed by the US and UK, after which a consortium of Western companies operated Iranian fields until the Iranian Revolution of 1979 returned full control to NIOC.

Modern era

The Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) battered Khuzestan's oil facilities. Since the 1990s, US and international sanctions — tightened over Iran's nuclear programme and reimposed after 2018 — have restricted exports, investment and access to technology, cutting output from peaks above 6 million barrels per day in the 1970s to fluctuating levels around 2.5–3.5 million barrels per day in recent years. Giant fields include Ahvaz, Marun and Gachsaran onshore, and the South Pars/North Dome gas field, shared with Qatar, the largest natural gas field on Earth. Most crude exports leave via the Kharg Island terminal in the Persian Gulf.
