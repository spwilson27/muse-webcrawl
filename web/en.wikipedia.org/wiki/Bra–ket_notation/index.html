Bra–ket notation

Bra–ket notation, also called Dirac notation, is a notation for linear algebra and linear operators on complex vector spaces, widely used in quantum mechanics. It was introduced in 1939 by the physicist Paul Dirac, who split the word "bracket" into a left part, the bra ⟨A|, and a right part, the ket |B⟩, so that their combination ⟨A|B⟩ denotes an inner product. A ket represents a vector (such as a quantum state) in a Hilbert space, while a bra represents the corresponding dual vector — a linear functional.

Usage in quantum mechanics

In quantum mechanics a physical state is represented by a ket |ψ⟩. The bra ⟨ψ| is its Hermitian conjugate. The inner product ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is a complex number whose squared modulus gives the probability of finding the system in state |φ⟩ when it is in state |ψ⟩ (the Born rule). Operators act on kets from the left, and expectation values take the compact form ⟨ψ|Â|ψ⟩. The outer product |ψ⟩⟨φ| is an operator, and an operator can be resolved with the completeness relation Σ |i⟩⟨i| = Î, the identity — a device that makes many calculations in perturbation theory and scattering almost mechanical.

Relation to matrix notation

In a finite-dimensional space a ket is a column vector and a bra is a row vector (the conjugate transpose of the ket). The inner product is then ordinary matrix multiplication of the row by the column, and the outer product yields a matrix. Dirac notation is basis-independent: labels inside bras and kets may denote eigenvalues, so that position and momentum eigenstates can be written |x⟩ and |p⟩, with the wavefunction recovered as ψ(x) = ⟨x|ψ⟩.

History

Dirac introduced the notation in a 1939 paper and developed it in the third edition of his Principles of Quantum Mechanics (1947). It supplanted the clumsier notation of Hilbert space theory in most physics practice and is now standard in quantum mechanics, quantum optics, and quantum computing, where a ket |0⟩ or |1⟩ denotes a qubit basis state.
