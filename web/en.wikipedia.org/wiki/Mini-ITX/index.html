Mini-ITX

Mini-ITX
Developer: VIA Technologies
Released: 2001
Size: 6.7 × 6.7 in (170 × 170 mm)
Slots: One expansion slot

Mini-ITX is a low-power motherboard form factor developed by VIA Technologies, measuring 6.7 × 6.7 inches (170 × 170 mm). First announced in 2001, it was designed for small, quiet, energy-efficient computers such as home-theatre PCs, embedded systems and thin clients.

Design

A Mini-ITX board retains a single expansion slot (conventionally PCI, later PCI Express x16 on many boards) and uses standard ATX mounting holes and power connector positions, so Mini-ITX boards fit in many ATX and microATX cases, as well as the small dedicated Mini-ITX enclosures. Early boards carried soldered VIA C3/Eden processors and could run fanlessly; the format was later adopted by other manufacturers with sockets for Intel and AMD chips.

Use

Because of its compact size and low power draw, Mini-ITX became popular for home servers, network-attached storage builds, retro and arcade machines, car PCs, industrial control and hobbyist small-form-factor gaming rigs. Still smaller successors in VIA's line included Nano-ITX (120 mm) and Pico-ITX (100 × 72 mm).
