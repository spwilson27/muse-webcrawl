Andy Palacio

Andy Vivian Palacio (December 2, 1960 – January 19, 2008) was a Belizean punta rock musician and government official. A leading advocate for the Garifuna people and their language and music, he achieved international acclaim with his final album, Wátina (2007), recorded with the Garifuna Collective, which was named one of the best world music albums of its year and won the 2007 WOMEX Award.

Early life

Palacio was born in the coastal Garifuna village of Barranco in southern Belize. He grew up speaking Garifuna and immersed in the traditional music and rituals of his people. Before turning to music full-time, he worked as a schoolteacher and later as a cultural officer and administrator, including service as deputy administrator of Belize's National Institute of Culture and History (NICH).

Music career

Palacio rose to popularity in the 1980s and 1990s in Belize and the Garifuna diaspora with punta rock, a modernized form of traditional punta rhythm popularized by Pen Cayetano. Albums such as Til Da Mawnin! (1997) made him one of Belize's most beloved performers. In the 2000s he turned toward the roots of Garifuna music, working with producer Ivan Duran of Stonetree Records to record the elder paranda musicians and, ultimately, the Garifuna Women's Project and his own album Wátina (2007).

Wátina and recognition

Wátina, recorded with the Garifuna Collective, was widely acclaimed internationally — named one of Amazon's and the BBC's top world music albums of 2007 and awarded the WOMEX Award that year. Palacio used his platform to advocate for the preservation of the Garifuna language, which UNESCO had proclaimed in 2001 (together with Garifuna music and dance) a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

Death and legacy

Palacio fell ill in January 2008 and died in Belize City on January 19, 2008, at age 47, of a stroke and heart attack. Belize declared a period of national mourning and gave him a state funeral. He is remembered as the most prominent cultural ambassador of the Garifuna people.
