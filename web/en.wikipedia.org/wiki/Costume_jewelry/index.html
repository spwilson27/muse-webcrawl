Costume jewelry

Costume jewelry is typically made from inexpensive base metals and imitation stones.

Costume jewelry, also known as fashion jewelry, is jewelry manufactured as ornamentation to complement a particular fashionable costume or garment, as opposed to "real" (fine) jewelry, which is made of more expensive, precious materials and is usually considered an investment. Originally, costume jewelry came into being to allow a wide audience to copy the look of fine jewels, and it is typically made from base metals, glass, plastic, or synthetic stones rather than precious metals and gems.

History

The term "costume jewelry" dates from the early 20th century and reflects the use of the word "costume" to refer to what is now called an "outfit". Mass-produced costume jewelry flourished in the 1920s and 1930s, when department stores and manufacturers such as Miriam Haskell, Coco Chanel, and Elsa Schiaparelli popularized bold, affordable pieces. Chanel famously mixed genuine and faux pearls, declaring that it was a disgrace to walk around with millions around one's neck because one happened to be rich.

During the Second World War, sterling silver was commonly used because other metals were needed for the war effort. In the 1950s, makers such as Trifari, Coro, Napier, and Monet produced fashionable pieces for the middle-class market, and Hollywood films popularized sparkling "statement" designs.

Materials and manufacture

Costume jewelry is generally made from base metals such as brass, copper, steel, pewter, or pot metal, often plated with gold, silver, or rhodium. Stones are typically glass, crystal (such as Swarovski crystal), cubic zirconia, acrylic, or plastic. Because the materials are inexpensive, designs can follow rapidly changing fashion trends and be produced in large quantities.

Collecting

Vintage costume jewelry is widely collected. Signed pieces by notable designers and makers—such as Miriam Haskell, Schreiner, Eisenberg, and Juliana—can command substantial prices. Collectors value craftsmanship, design, and provenance, and many mid-century pieces are prized for their hand-set stones and intricate metalwork.
