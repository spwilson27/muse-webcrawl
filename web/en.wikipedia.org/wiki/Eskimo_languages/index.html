Eskimo languages

The Eskimo languages are a branch of the Eskimo–Aleut language family spoken across the Arctic regions of northern Canada, Alaska, Greenland, and the Chukotka Peninsula of far-eastern Russia. Together with the Aleut language, they form the Eskimo–Aleut family. The Eskimo branch divides into two main groups: the Yupik languages of Alaska and Siberia, and the Inuit language continuum, which stretches from northwestern Alaska across Arctic Canada to Greenland.

Classification

Yupik languages include Central Alaskan Yup'ik, the most widely spoken Indigenous language of Alaska; Alutiiq (Pacific Yupik); Central Siberian Yupik, spoken on St. Lawrence Island and in Chukotka; and the moribund Naukan and Sirenik varieties of Siberia.

The Inuit languages form a dialect continuum whose main named varieties include Iñupiaq in northern Alaska, Inuvialuktun and Inuktitut in Canada, and Greenlandic (Kalaallisut), the official language of Greenland and the Eskimo language with the most speakers, at roughly 57,000.

Features

Eskimo languages are polysynthetic and ergative: complex words are built from a root followed by long strings of derivational suffixes ("postbases") and an inflectional ending, so that a single word can express the content of an entire English sentence. They have relatively small consonant and vowel inventories but permit long consonant clusters. Vowel length and gemination are phonemic.

The popular claim that Eskimo languages have an exceptionally large number of words for snow derives from the polysynthetic structure, which allows unbounded derivation; linguists note that the number of distinct roots for snow is comparable to English, though the derivational possibilities are far greater.

Status

Most Eskimo languages are endangered to varying degrees, under pressure from English, French, Danish, and Russian. Greenlandic and Inuktitut have official status and comparatively strong speaker bases; several Yupik varieties and the Siberian languages are severely endangered, with small and ageing speaker populations.
