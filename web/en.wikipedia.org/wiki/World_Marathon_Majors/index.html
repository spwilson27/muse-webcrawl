The World Marathon Majors (WMM), sponsored as the Abbott World Marathon Majors, is a championship-style competition for marathon runners that groups together the world's largest and most prestigious annual marathons. Founded in 2006 by the directors of five races, the series awards points to elite athletes across the member events and awards a special medal to recreational runners who finish all of the Majors.

Member races

The series began with the Boston Marathon, London Marathon, Berlin Marathon, Chicago Marathon and New York City Marathon. The Tokyo Marathon joined in 2013, creating the "six majors". In November 2024 the Sydney Marathon was announced as the seventh Major, debuting in the series in 2025, with races such as Cape Town and Shanghai under evaluation for further expansion. The Olympic and World Championships marathons have also counted toward elite series scoring in some cycles.

Elite series and champions

Elite runners earn points based on finishing positions in the Majors, with each series cycle historically culminating in a US$1 million total purse split between the men's and women's champions, later restructured. Dominant series winners include Kenya's Eliud Kipchoge, widely regarded as the greatest marathoner of all time, and Mary Keitany, while wheelchair series titles have been dominated by athletes such as Marcel Hug and Manuela Schär of Switzerland. In 2023 Kelvin Kiptum set the men's world record of 2:00:35 at Chicago, and in 2024 Ruth Chepng'etich ran 2:09:56 there, the first women's sub-2:10 marathon.

Six Star Finishers

For non-elite runners the Majors are famous for the Six Star Finisher programme: participants who complete Tokyo, Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago and New York each receive a Six Star Medal and entry in the series' Hall of Fame. Since the programme's launch in 2016, tens of thousands of runners from more than a hundred countries have earned the medal, and the system's expansion to seven races has prompted the introduction of further multi-star recognition.
