Physician

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A physician, medical practitioner (British English), medical doctor, or simply doctor is a health professional who practises medicine, which is concerned with promoting, maintaining or restoring health through the study, diagnosis, prognosis and treatment of disease, injury, and other physical and mental impairments. Physicians may focus their practice on certain disease categories, types of patients, and methods of treatment — known as specialties — or they may assume responsibility for the provision of continuing and comprehensive medical care to individuals, families, and communities — known as general practice.

Education and regulation

Becoming a physician requires extensive university education: typically an undergraduate medical degree (such as the MBBS in the British tradition or the graduate-entry MD in the United States), followed by supervised residency or foundation training and licensure by a national or state regulator. Admission to medical school is highly competitive, and training involves anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology and prolonged clinical apprenticeships. Most jurisdictions require continuing professional development and periodic revalidation to remain in practice.

Work and roles

Physicians take medical histories, examine patients, order and interpret diagnostic tests, prescribe medications, perform procedures and surgery, and counsel patients on prevention. They work in hospitals, clinics, private practice, public health, research and medical education, often within multidisciplinary teams. Specialties range from family medicine, internal medicine and paediatrics to surgery, psychiatry, radiology and pathology, with dozens of recognised subspecialties.

History

The profession descends from the physician-philosophers of antiquity — Hippocrates in Greece, Galen in Rome, and later figures such as Avicenna in the Islamic world — through the university medical schools of medieval Europe to the scientific medicine of the 19th and 20th centuries, shaped by germ theory, anaesthesia, imaging and evidence-based practice. Ethical codes, from the Hippocratic Oath to the Declaration of Geneva, frame the physician's duties to patients.
