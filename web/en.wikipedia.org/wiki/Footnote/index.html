Footnote

A footnote is an additional piece of information printed at the bottom of a page, below the main text, keyed to a reference mark (such as a number or asterisk) in the text. Footnotes typically give citations for sources, but they may also contain supplementary commentary, explanations or digressions that would interrupt the flow of the main text.

Use

In scholarly publishing, footnotes are a standard vehicle for citing sources and documenting evidence, particularly in history, law and the humanities. Legal scholarship in particular is known for extensive footnoting. Style guides such as The Chicago Manual of Style prescribe footnote formats, while other disciplines prefer parenthetical citations or endnotes — notes collected at the end of a chapter or book rather than at the foot of each page.

History

The printed footnote developed alongside the printed book; early printers used marginal notes and commentaries before conventions settled on notes at the foot of the page. Footnotes have occasionally been used as a literary device in their own right, most famously in works where the notes form a parallel narrative, such as Vladimir Nabokov's Pale Fire.

In digital media

In electronic documents and web pages, footnotes may be implemented as hyperlinks, pop-up annotations or collapsible sections, since the notion of a "foot of the page" does not transfer directly to scrolling text.
