Switched-On Bach is the debut album by American composer Wendy Carlos, released in October 1968 by Columbia Records. Produced with Benjamin Folkman, it consists of pieces by Johann Sebastian Bach performed on a Moog modular synthesiser. The album was a surprise commercial and critical success, reaching the top 10 of the US Billboard chart and winning three Grammy Awards.

Production

Because the Moog was monophonic, every line of Bach's counterpoint had to be recorded separately and assembled on multitrack tape, a painstaking process taking months. Tracks include the Sinfonia to Cantata No. 29, "Air on the G String," and the first movement of the Brandenburg Concerto No. 3.

Impact

The album proved that synthesisers could make serious, musical recordings, transforming public perception of electronic music and driving demand for Moog instruments. It became one of the best-selling classical albums of its era and was followed by sequels including The Well-Tempered Synthesizer and Switched-On Bach II.
