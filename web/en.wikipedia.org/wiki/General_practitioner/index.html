General practitioner

A general practitioner (GP) is a medical doctor who treats acute and chronic illnesses and provides preventive care and health education to patients of all ages. GPs form the foundation of primary care in many health systems and act as the first point of contact for people seeking medical attention.

Role

General practitioners diagnose and manage a broad range of conditions, prescribe medication, perform minor procedures, and refer patients to specialists or hospital services when needed. Because they often care for the same patients over many years, GPs emphasize continuity of care and knowledge of the patient's history and family context.

Training

In the United Kingdom, doctors complete medical school and a two-year foundation program before three years of GP specialty training, leading to membership of the Royal College of General Practitioners. In the United States, the equivalent primary care role is usually filled by family medicine or internal medicine physicians who complete a residency after medical school.

Practice settings

GPs work in solo or group practices, community health centers and increasingly in multidisciplinary primary care teams. Most consultations concern common problems such as respiratory infections, musculoskeletal pain, mental health issues and the long-term management of conditions like diabetes and hypertension.
