Whiskey sour

Whiskey sour
TypeCocktail
Primary alcoholWhiskey (usually bourbon)
ServedOn the rocks or straight up
Standard garnishMaraschino cherry and/or orange or lemon slice
IBA specified ingredients45 ml bourbon, 25 ml lemon juice, 20 ml sugar syrup, optional egg white
PreparationShake with ice, strain into a sour glass or old fashioned glass

The whiskey sour is a classic mixed drink containing whiskey (often bourbon), lemon juice, sugar and optionally a dash of egg white. It belongs to the sour family of cocktails — a base spirit, citrus juice and a sweetener — which descends from the practice of mixing spirits with citrus aboard British naval vessels to combat scurvy and make stored water safe. It is an official cocktail of the International Bartenders Association (IBA), listed among "The Unforgettables".

History

Sours were being consumed by the mid-19th century, and the whiskey sour was described in print by 1862 in Jerry Thomas's How to Mix Drinks, the first bartender's guide. One of the earliest known newspaper mentions of the drink dates to 1870 in a Waukesha, Wisconsin newspaper. Bartenders later debated the virtues of lemon versus lime and of different sweeteners; the modern canon settled on lemon juice and simple syrup, with the egg white — shaken first without ice in a "dry shake" to emulsify — giving the finished drink its characteristic silky foam.

Variations

The Boston sour is a whiskey sour made with egg white. A whiskey sour with a float of dry red wine is a New York sour. The family extends to the amaretto sour, the pisco sour (with pisco and lime, claimed by both Peru and Chile), the daiquiri (rum and lime) and the white lady / sidecar styles of sour. Bottled sour mix — a sweetened, shelf-stable substitute for fresh lemon and sugar — became common in the mid-20th century but is eschewed by craft bartenders in favour of fresh juice.
