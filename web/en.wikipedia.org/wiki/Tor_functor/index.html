Tor functor

In homological algebra, the Tor functors are the derived functors of the tensor product of modules. For a ring R and modules A and B, the groups ToriR(A, B) for i ≥ 0 measure the failure of the tensor product to be exact. The name comes from "torsion", since the first examples concern torsion subgroups of abelian groups.

Definition

Take a projective (or free) resolution of A, tensor it with B, and take homology: Tori is the i-th homology group of the resulting complex. The construction is balanced — resolving either variable gives the same result. Tor0 is simply A ⊗ B; Tori vanishes for all i ≥ 1 when either module is flat.

Examples and applications

For abelian groups, Tor1Z(Z/n, B) is the subgroup of elements of B annihilated by n. Tor appears in the universal coefficient theorem for homology, the Künneth formula, and change-of-rings spectral sequences, and it detects relations (syzygies) between generators of a module.
