Recurvirostridae

The Recurvirostridae are a family of wading birds in the suborder Charadrii. It includes the avocets and stilts, long-legged shorebirds that feed in shallow brackish or saline wetlands. The family comprises about nine or ten species in three genera.

Description

Members of the family range from about 30 to 46 cm in length. Avocets have distinctive slender bills that curve upward (recurved), which they sweep from side to side in shallow water to detect invertebrate prey. Stilts have extremely long, thin legs and straight, needle-like bills, and include the black-winged stilt, which has one of the longest leg-to-body proportions of any bird.

Genera and species

  • Recurvirostra — avocets: pied avocet, American avocet, red-necked avocet, Andean avocet
  • Himantopus — stilts: black-winged stilt, black-necked stilt, black stilt, and others
  • Cladorhynchus — the banded stilt of Australia

Distribution and habitat

The family is cosmopolitan, found on all continents except Antarctica, typically around salt lakes, estuaries, lagoons, and flooded fields. The black stilt of New Zealand is critically endangered.
