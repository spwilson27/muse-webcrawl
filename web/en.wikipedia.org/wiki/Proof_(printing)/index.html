Proof (printing)

In printing and publishing, a proof is a preliminary version of a publication, made for checking and correcting before the final print run. Proofs allow authors, editors, and printers to catch errors in text, layout, and colour before the expense of full production.

Types

Historically, galley proofs were long columns of typeset text printed from a galley tray before being divided into pages; page proofs show the text in its final page layout. In modern prepress workflows, proofs may be digital ("soft proofs" on a calibrated monitor) or printed on a proofer that simulates the final press output, especially for checking colour accuracy in process-colour work.

Proofreading

Corrections on proofs are traditionally marked with standardized proofreading symbols. A corrected proof approved for printing may be marked "press OK" or signed off as ready for the press run.
