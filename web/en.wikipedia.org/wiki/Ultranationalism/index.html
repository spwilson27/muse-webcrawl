Ultranationalism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ultranationalism is an extreme form of nationalism in which a country asserts that its interests and values are superior to those of all others and must be pursued at their expense. In political science the term is commonly used for nationalist ideologies that are authoritarian, xenophobic or expansionist, and it is frequently associated with fascist movements of the interwar period and with later radical-right politics.

Where ordinary nationalism asserts a nation's right to self-government, ultranationalism subordinates the individual wholly to the nation, treats internal and external enemies as existential threats, and often sacralises national rebirth (palingenesis) — Roger Griffin's influential definition of fascism centres on this “palingenetic ultranationalism”. Critics of the term note that it is usually applied by opponents and that the line between strong patriotism, national conservatism and ultranationalism is contested.

Characteristics

Features commonly listed include glorification of the military and of a charismatic leader; hostility to ethnic, religious or political minorities portrayed as alien; rejection of international institutions and compromise; and myths of national humiliation and rebirth. Interwar examples include the regimes and movements of Fascist Italy, Nazi Germany and Imperial Japan, and smaller movements across Europe.
