Crossover dribble

A crossover dribble is a basketball maneuver in which a player dribbling the ball switches it rapidly from one hand to the other, changing direction in order to evade a defender. A sharp crossover can unbalance the defender — in extreme cases causing the defender to stumble or fall, an outcome popularly known as an "ankle breaker".

Technique

The basic crossover is executed by bouncing the ball in front of the body from the dominant hand to the opposite hand while stepping in the new direction. Variations include the between-the-legs crossover, the behind-the-back crossover, the double crossover, and the hesitation or "in-and-out" fake. Players such as Tim Hardaway, Allen Iverson and Kyrie Irving became famous for their crossover moves; Iverson's crossover against Michael Jordan in 1997 is among the most replayed highlights in NBA history.

Rules

The move must be performed without carrying the ball: the dribbler's hand must remain on top of or to the side of the ball; placing the hand underneath and hesitating constitutes a carrying violation.
