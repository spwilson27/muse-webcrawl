Ātman (Hinduism)

Ātman
SanskritĀtman (आत्मन्)
MeaningSelf, soul, essence
Related conceptsBrahman, samsara, moksha

Ātman is a Sanskrit word meaning the "self" or "soul" — the true, eternal essence of an individual, distinct from the material body and the changing mind. It is a central concept in Hindu philosophy, first elaborated in the Upanishads, and plays a comparable role in Jain and some Buddhist discussions (where its existence is famously denied under the doctrine of anātman, "no-self").

In the Upanishads

The early Upanishads (c. 800–500 BCE) explore ātman as the innermost reality of the person. The Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya Upanishads develop the celebrated teaching tat tvam asi ("thou art that"), and Advaita-oriented passages identify the individual ātman with Brahman, the ultimate reality underlying the universe: ayam ātmā brahma, "this self is Brahman."

Schools of interpretation

  • Advaita Vedanta (Shankara) – ātman is identical with Brahman; realising this identity is liberation (moksha).
  • Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja) – individual selves are real and eternally distinct modes of Brahman.
  • Dvaita (Madhva) – ātman is eternally distinct from God (Vishnu).
  • Samkhya and Yoga – the self (purusha) is pure consciousness, distinct from matter (prakriti).

Rebirth and liberation

In Hindu thought the ātman transmigrates through samsara, the cycle of birth and death governed by karma, until liberation is attained. Because the ātman is held to be unborn and undying, the Bhagavad Gita teaches that weapons cannot cut it, fire cannot burn it, and water cannot wet it.
