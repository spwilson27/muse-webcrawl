Iran–Israel proxy conflict


Period: 1985–present (often dated from 1979/1982)
Region: Middle East

The Iran–Israel proxy conflict, also called the Iran–Israel cold war, is an ongoing indirect confrontation between Iran and Israel, fought largely through proxies, covert operations, and strikes on allied forces rather than open war between the two states. It developed after the 1979 Iranian Revolution ended the previously friendly relations between the two countries, and intensified from the 1982 Lebanon War onwards.

Course

Iran built a network of allied armed groups — often called the "Axis of Resistance" — including Hezbollah in Lebanon, Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad in Gaza, Shia militias in Iraq and Syria, and the Houthis in Yemen. Israel has conducted a long "campaign between the wars" of airstrikes against Iranian and Hezbollah targets in Syria, is widely believed to have carried out assassinations and sabotage against Iran's nuclear programme (including the Stuxnet operation with the United States), and fought wars with Hamas and Hezbollah. The conflict escalated into direct exchanges in 2024, with Iranian missile and drone attacks on Israel in April and October and Israeli strikes on Iran, following the Gaza war that began in October 2023.

Background

Under the Shah, Iran and Israel maintained close strategic and economic ties under Israel's "periphery doctrine". The Islamic Republic adopted an ideology of opposition to Israel, which Iranian leaders have framed as a religious and anti-imperialist duty, while Israeli governments regard Iran's nuclear programme and regional proxies as an existential threat.
