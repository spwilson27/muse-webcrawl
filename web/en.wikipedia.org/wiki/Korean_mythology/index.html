Korean mythology is the body of myths and legends originating in the Korean Peninsula, preserved in early chronicles such as the Samguk Yusa (13th century) and in the orally transmitted narrative songs of Korean shamanism (muism). It blends native shamanic tradition with elements absorbed from Buddhism, Confucianism, and Taoism.

Foundation myths

The most famous foundation myth tells of Dangun Wanggeom, legendary founder of Gojoseon in 2333 BCE. Hwanung, son of the heavenly king Hwanin, descended to Mount Taebaek; a bear and a tiger prayed to become human, and the bear, enduring a trial of eating only mugwort and garlic in darkness, became the woman Ungnyeo, who bore Dangun. Other kingdoms had their own founders: Jumong for Goguryeo, born from an egg and a peerless archer; Bak Hyeokgeose for Silla, hatched from a shining egg; and Suro for Geumgwan Gaya.

Shamanic mythology

Shamanic narratives, sung by mudang during rituals (gut), preserve myths of gods not found in written chronicles: the goddess Samshin, who grants children; the ten kings of the underworld who judge the dead; and epics such as the Jeju bon-puri about the origins of gods and heroes. Household gods (gashin) guard each part of the home — the kitchen, the gate, the yard, and the outhouse.

Cosmology and creatures

Traditional cosmology divides the world into the heavens, the earth, and the underworld (Jeoseung), with the dead guided by psychopomps such as the jeoseung saja. Creatures include the four-direction guardians (Azure Dragon, White Tiger, Vermilion Bird, Black Tortoise), nine-tailed foxes (gumiho), goblin-like dokkaebi, and water dragons (imugi) that may ascend to heaven.
