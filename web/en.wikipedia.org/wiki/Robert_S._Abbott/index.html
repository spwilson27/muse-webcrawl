Robert S. Abbott

Robert Sengstacke Abbott
Born: 24 December 1870, St. Simons Island, Georgia, United States
Died: 29 February 1940, Chicago, Illinois, United States
Occupation: Newspaper publisher, editor, lawyer
Known for: Founding the Chicago Defender (1905)

Robert Sengstacke Abbott (24 December 1870 – 29 February 1940) was an American lawyer and newspaper publisher who founded The Chicago Defender in 1905, which grew into the most influential African-American newspaper in the United States and played a major role in encouraging the Great Migration of Black Southerners to northern cities.

Early life

Abbott was born on St. Simons Island, Georgia, to formerly enslaved parents. He was educated at Hampton Institute, where he learned printing, and later moved to Chicago, earning a law degree from Kent College of Law in 1899. Finding his legal practice limited by racial discrimination, he turned to publishing.

The Chicago Defender

Abbott founded the Defender in 1905 with an initial investment of 25 cents, printing early editions in his landlord's room. The paper combined militant civil-rights advocacy with sensational journalism, campaigning against lynching and Jim Crow, and was distributed throughout the South by Pullman porters, who carried bundles of the paper on trains. The Defender exhorted Southern Black readers to move north — its "Great Northern Drive" of 1917 promoted migration to Chicago — and by the 1920s claimed a national circulation in the hundreds of thousands, making Abbott one of the first Black millionaire publishers in America. He later converted to the Baháʼí Faith.

Legacy

Abbott died in Chicago in 1940; the Defender continued under his nephew John H. Sengstacke. His home in Chicago, the Robert S. Abbott House, is a National Historic Landmark, and he is remembered as a pioneering figure of the Black press.
