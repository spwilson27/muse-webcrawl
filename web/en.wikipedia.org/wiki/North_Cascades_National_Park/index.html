North Cascades National Park

North Cascades National Park is an American national park in the state of Washington. Established on October 2, 1968, the park protects a rugged section of the Cascade Range noted for its jagged alpine peaks, deep valleys, and the largest concentration of glaciers of any national park in the contiguous United States — more than 300 glaciers feed its rivers and lakes.

Geography

The park covers about 504,781 acres (204,278 ha) and is divided into northern and southern units, separated by the Skagit River valley and the Ross Lake National Recreation Area. Together with Ross Lake and Lake Chelan National Recreation Areas, it forms the North Cascades National Park Service Complex. State Route 20, the North Cascades Highway, threads through the complex along the Skagit River, providing the principal road access. Prominent features include turquoise Diablo Lake, dammed for hydroelectric power by Seattle City Light; Cascade Pass; and peaks such as Mount Shuksan, Mount Triumph, and Forbidden Peak.

History

Indigenous peoples, including ancestors of the Upper Skagit and other tribes, used the mountain country for thousands of years. Prospectors and miners entered the range in the late nineteenth century, and hydroelectric development on the Skagit followed in the early twentieth century. After decades of debate between conservationists and logging interests, Congress established the park in 1968. In 1988 the Stephen Mather Wilderness was designated over most of the park, named for the first director of the National Park Service.

Ecology and recreation

Elevations range from lowland rainforest along the Skagit to alpine tundra, supporting old-growth western red cedar and Douglas-fir forests, black bears, mountain goats, gray wolves, and one of the most diverse plant ecosystems in North America. The park is comparatively lightly visited, drawing hikers, climbers, and backpackers to hundreds of miles of trails. The gateway community of Marblemount lies west of the park, and the isolated village of Stehekin, at the head of Lake Chelan, is reachable only by boat, trail, or floatplane.
