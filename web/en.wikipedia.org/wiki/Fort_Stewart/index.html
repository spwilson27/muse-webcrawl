Fort Stewart

Fort Stewart is a United States Army post in northeastern Georgia, covering parts of Liberty, Long, Bryan, Evans, and Tattnall counties. At roughly 280,000 acres it is the largest Army installation east of the Mississippi River. It is the home of the 3rd Infantry Division.

History

The post was established in June 1940 as Camp Stewart, an anti-aircraft artillery training center, named for Daniel Stewart, a Revolutionary War general and grandfather of a U.S. president's family line from Liberty County. During World War II it also held German and Italian prisoners of war. It was redesignated Fort Stewart in 1956.

The 3rd Infantry Division moved to Fort Stewart in 1996 and deployed from there to spearhead the 2003 invasion of Iraq, driving on Baghdad. Hunter Army Airfield, near Savannah, Georgia, is a sub-installation and home to aviation and Ranger units.

Environment

The reservation includes longleaf pine forests, wetlands, and the Canoochee River, supporting endangered species such as the red-cockaded woodpecker. Its ranges and maneuver areas make it a premier armored training site.
