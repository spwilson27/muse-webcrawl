Bilateral symmetry

FieldBiology, morphology
CladeBilateria

Bilateral symmetry is a body plan in which an organism can be divided into mirror-image left and right halves along a single plane. It characterises the Bilateria, the great clade containing most animals, including humans.

Features

Bilateral animals have distinct anterior–posterior and dorsal–ventral axes, which favour directional movement and cephalisation — the concentration of sense organs and nervous tissue at the head end. Bilateral symmetry contrasts with the radial symmetry of cnidarians and ctenophores.

Evolution

The bilaterian body plan appeared in the Ediacaran–Cambrian interval over 550 million years ago and underlies the diversification of most animal phyla.
