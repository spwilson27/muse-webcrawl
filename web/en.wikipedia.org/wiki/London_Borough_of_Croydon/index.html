London Borough of Croydon

The London Borough of Croydon is a London borough in south London, part of Outer London. It covers an area of about 87 km2 (33.6 sq mi) and is the largest London borough by population, with roughly 390,000 residents. The local authority is Croydon London Borough Council, based since 2022 under a directly elected mayor. Croydon is the principal town.

History

The borough was formed in 1965 under the London Government Act 1963 by merging the County Borough of Croydon with the Coulsdon and Purley Urban District, both formerly in Surrey. Croydon's town centre was radically redeveloped in the 1960s and 1970s with high-rise offices, earning it the nickname "mini-Manhattan", and it is served by the Tramlink light-rail network opened in 2000. The borough was affected by rioting in August 2011. In November 2020 the council issued a Section 114 notice effectively declaring bankruptcy, and issued further notices in subsequent years amid efforts to stabilise its finances.

Governance

Croydon Council provides services including education, housing, planning, waste, and social care, while the Greater London Authority handles strategic functions. Since May 2022 the borough has had a directly elected executive mayor, Jason Perry (Conservative), the first holder of the office.

Transport

East Croydon station is a major rail hub with fast services to London Victoria and London Bridge and to Gatwick Airport; Tramlink connects Croydon with Wimbledon, Beckenham, Elmers End, and New Addington.
