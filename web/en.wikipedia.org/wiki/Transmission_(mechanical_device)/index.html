Transmission (mechanical device)

Transmission
Also calledGearbox
TypesManual, automatic, CVT, dual-clutch, automated manual
FunctionMatch engine speed/torque to wheel demand via gear ratios

A transmission (also called a gearbox) is a mechanical device that provides speed and torque conversions from a rotating power source to another device using gear ratios. In motor vehicles, the transmission adapts the output of the internal combustion engine to the drive wheels: the engine runs in a relatively narrow band of speeds, while the wheels must operate from a standstill to maximum speed, so the transmission offers multiple gears (and reverse) between them.

Types

Manual transmissions require the driver to select gears and operate a clutch; modern ones have five to seven forward speeds. Automatic transmissions use planetary gearsets with a torque converter and shift hydraulically or electronically, now with up to ten speeds. Continuously variable transmissions (CVTs) use belts or chains over variable pulleys for a seamless range of ratios. Dual-clutch transmissions preselect gears on two clutches for very fast shifts. Most battery-electric vehicles use a single-speed reduction gear because electric motors deliver torque over a wide speed range.

Other applications

Transmissions are also used on pedal bicycles (derailleur and hub gears), machine tools, agricultural machinery, wind turbines and marine propulsion, anywhere a prime mover's speed and torque must be matched to a load. This article concerns the mechanical device; for the broader engineering concept, see Transmission (mechanics).
