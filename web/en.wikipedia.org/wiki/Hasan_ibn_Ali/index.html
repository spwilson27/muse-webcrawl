Hasan ibn Ali (Arabic: الحسن بن علي, romanized: al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī; c. 625 – 670) was an Alid political and religious leader. The eldest son of Ali and Fatimah and a grandson of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, Hasan briefly ruled as caliph from January until August 661, following his father's assassination. He is the second Imam in Shia Islam, succeeding Ali and preceding his brother Husayn.

Life

Hasan was born in Medina and was famously cherished by his grandfather Muhammad, who called him and Husayn "the two masters of the youth of Paradise". He took part in the events of his father's caliphate and, after Ali was assassinated in Kufa in 661, was acclaimed caliph there. Faced with a stronger Syrian army under Mu'awiya I, dwindling support, and an attempt on his own life, Hasan negotiated a peace treaty (the Hasan–Mu'awiya treaty) under which he abdicated in return for terms that included a general amnesty and that the succession after Mu'awiya should be decided by consultation; he then retired to Medina.

Death

Hasan died in Medina around 670, aged about 45. Many sources, particularly Shia ones, hold that he was poisoned at Mu'awiya's instigation, allegedly by his wife Ja'da bint al-Ash'ath; modern historians treat the report with varying degrees of caution. His attempt to be buried beside Muhammad's tomb in the Prophet's Mosque was blocked, and he was interred in the al-Baqi cemetery.

Legacy

Hasan is venerated in both Sunni and Shia Islam as a member of the Ahl al-Bayt; Sunnis count him among the rightly guided caliphs for the brief period of his rule. He is remembered for generosity, piety, and for preventing further civil war — sometimes called "the Peacemaker" — while Shia tradition also emphasises his forbearance under injustice.
