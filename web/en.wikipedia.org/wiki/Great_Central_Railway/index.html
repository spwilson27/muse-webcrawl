Great Central Railway

[Image: Great Central Railway]

The Great Central Railway in England was formed when the Manchester, Sheffield and Lincolnshire Railway changed its name in 1897, anticipating the opening in 1899 of its London Extension. On 1 January 1923, the company was grouped into the London and North Eastern Railway.

London Extension

The London Extension ran from Annesley, Nottinghamshire, through Nottingham, Leicester, Rugby and Aylesbury to a new terminus at Marylebone in London. Built under chairman Sir Edward Watkin, who dreamed of a Channel Tunnel link, it was the last main line built in Britain until High Speed 1, and was engineered to a generous loading gauge.

Closure and preservation

The line was run down in the 1960s under the Beeching cuts and closed as a through route in 1966–69. Sections survive: the Great Central Railway heritage line between Loughborough and Leicester North, and the Nottingham Heritage Railway at Ruddington.
