Theory of relativity

The theory of relativity, developed chiefly by Albert Einstein, comprises special relativity (1905), concerning space, time and motion at constant velocity, and general relativity (1915), a theory of gravitation.

Key ideas

Special relativity rests on the constancy of the speed of light and the equivalence of physical laws in inertial frames, implying time dilation, length contraction and mass–energy equivalence. General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.

Evidence

Predictions confirmed by observation include the bending of light by the Sun, the precession of Mercury's orbit, gravitational redshift, gravitational waves and the operation of GPS satellites.
