A Single Man is a 2009 American drama film based on the 1964 novel of the same name by Christopher Isherwood. It is the directorial debut of fashion designer Tom Ford, who co-wrote the screenplay with David Scearce. The film stars Colin Firth as George Falconer, a depressed British university professor living in Los Angeles who contemplates suicide after the death of his long-time partner.

Plot

Set on 30 November 1962, in the shadow of the Cuban Missile Crisis, the film follows a single day in the life of George Falconer, a 52-year-old English professor. Eight months earlier his partner of sixteen years, Jim, died in a car accident; excluded from the funeral by Jim's family, George has lived in numb grief ever since. He resolves to kill himself that evening and spends the day putting his affairs in order. Encounters with a Spanish hustler, with his student Kenny — who shows an unusual interest in him — and a dinner with his closest friend Charley, a divorcée who still carries a torch for him, gradually reawaken his appreciation of life. Having finally decided to live, George suffers a fatal heart attack at home; in his last moments he imagines Jim beside him.

Production

Ford financed the film largely himself after difficulty securing backing, and shot it in 21 days in Los Angeles. The production design by Dan Bishop and Ford's direction deliberately use shifts in colour saturation to reflect George's emotional state: the world is desaturated in his depression and floods with colour when he connects with others. The score is by Abel Korzeniowski, with additional music by Shigeru Umebayashi.

Reception

The film premiered at the 66th Venice International Film Festival, where Firth won the Volpi Cup for Best Actor. Firth went on to win the BAFTA Award for Best Actor and received an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor. The film was broadly praised for its visual elegance and Firth's restrained performance, and it grossed roughly $25 million against a $7 million budget.
