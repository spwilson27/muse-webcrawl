Testimony (Quaker)

In the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers), the word testimony describes the ways in which Friends bear witness to their beliefs in their everyday lives. A testimony is not a creed or a statement of doctrine but a lived expression of faith — an outward behaviour arising from an inward spiritual conviction. Throughout Quaker history, Friends have recognised a number of historic testimonies which remain influential today.

The testimonies

The best-known testimonies are often summarised by the mnemonic SPICES:

  • Simplicity — avoiding excess, superfluity and ostentation
  • Peace — the peace testimony: refusing military service and working to remove the causes of war
  • Integrity — truthfulness and honesty in all dealings, including the refusal of oaths
  • Community — mutual care and responsibility within and beyond the Society
  • Equality — recognising "that of God in everyone"; historically expressed in opposition to slavery and in plain address without titles
  • Stewardship (or Sustainability) — care for the Earth and its resources

Historically, testimonies were also expressed through plain dress and plain speech — using "thee" and "thou" and numbered days and months rather than names derived from pagan deities. Most of these specific forms have lapsed among liberal Friends, but the underlying principles remain central to Quaker practice. The testimonies are described and commended in each yearly meeting's book of discipline, such as Quaker Faith and Practice in Britain.
