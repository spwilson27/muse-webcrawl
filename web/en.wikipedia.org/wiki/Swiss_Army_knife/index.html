Swiss Army knife

The Swiss Army knife (SAK; German: Schweizer Armeemesser) is a multi-tool pocketknife manufactured by Victorinox. The term "Swiss Army knife" was coined by American soldiers after World War II, who had difficulty pronouncing the German name. The knife is noted for its distinctive red handle bearing the Victorinox or (formerly) Wenger cross-and-shield emblem.

History

In 1891 the Swiss Army decided to equip every soldier with a folding knife for opening canned food and maintaining the Schmidt–Rubin rifle. Karl Elsener's company, later Victorinox, won the contract, and in 1897 Elsener patented the officer's and sports knife with additional tools including a corkscrew and second blade. Victorinox and Wenger were for over a century the two official suppliers; Victorinox acquired Wenger in 2005 and integrated its knife lines. Since 2008 the issue knife has been the green-handled Soldier Knife 08 with a serrated locking blade.

Design

Typical models combine a main blade with tools such as a can opener, screwdrivers, awl, scissors, tweezers and toothpick. Elaborate models such as the SwissChamp include dozens of implements. The phrase "Swiss Army knife" is widely used metaphorically for a versatile, multi-purpose tool.
