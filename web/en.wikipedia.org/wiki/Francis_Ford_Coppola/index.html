Francis Ford Coppola

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Francis Ford Coppola on the set of Apocalypse Now in the Philippines: megaphone in hand, helicopters and smoke beyond]

Francis Ford Coppola (born 7 April 1939) is an American film director, producer and screenwriter. He is considered one of the major figures of the New Hollywood film-making movement of the 1960s and 1970s. He is widely considered one of the greatest directors of all time, recipient of five Academy Awards and the Palme d'Or twice.

Coppola's towering achievement is The Godfather trilogy (1972, 1974, 1990), adapted from Mario Puzo's novel; the first two parts are routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made, and Part II was the first sequel to win Best Picture. Between them came The Conversation (1974) and the ordeally-produced Vietnam epic Apocalypse Now (1979). Later work ranged from Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992) to the self-financed Megalopolis (2024).
