Luigi Nono
Born29 January 1924, Venice, Italy
Died8 May 1990, Venice
OccupationComposer

Luigi Nono (29 January 1924 – 8 May 1990) was an Italian avant-garde composer of classical music. A leading figure of the post-war European avant-garde and a prominent member of the Darmstadt School, he combined serial technique with explicit Marxist political commitment.

Life and music

Nono studied with Gian Francesco Malipiero in Venice and with Bruno Maderna, who introduced him to serialism. In 1955 he married Nuria Schönberg, daughter of Arnold Schoenberg. His breakthrough choral work Il canto sospeso (1955–56), setting letters of condemned resistance fighters, established his reputation. His opera Intolleranza 1960 sparked a riot at its Venice premiere.

Through the 1960s and 1970s Nono brought music into factories and working-class venues and experimented with electronics, notably at the Experimental Studio in Freiburg. Works such as Como una ola de fuerza y luz and the opera Al gran sole carico d'amore continued his political engagement. His late music turned inward and quiet: Fragmente—Stille, An Diotima (1979–80) for string quartet, the opera Prometeo (1984), and No hay caminos, hay que caminar… Andrej Tarkovskij (1987) explore silence, microtones, and deep listening.
