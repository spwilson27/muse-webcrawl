American whiskey is whiskey produced in the United States. The primary types produced in the United States are bourbon whiskey, rye whiskey, rye malt whiskey, malt whiskey, wheat whiskey, Tennessee whiskey, and corn whiskey. All of these are made from mashes with specified proportions of grain, distilled to no more than 80% alcohol by volume (160 proof) for most styles, and aged in oak containers, generally new charred oak barrels for the straight whiskeys.

Regulations

American whiskey is governed by the Federal Standards of Identity for Distilled Spirits (27 CFR § 5). To be labelled "straight" whiskey, the spirit must be aged in oak for at least two years, distilled from a fermented mash of grain, and contain no added colouring or flavouring beyond what the regulations permit. Bourbon must be made from at least 51% corn and aged in new charred oak containers.

History

Distilling in America began with settlers adapting European techniques to New World grains, especially corn and rye. In 1791, the federal excise tax on whiskey provoked the Whiskey Rebellion in western Pennsylvania, suppressed by President George Washington. The Bottled-in-Bond Act of 1897 established quality standards. Prohibition (1920–1933) devastated the industry, but American whiskey revived after repeal and, in 1964, bourbon was recognised by Congress as a "distinctive product of the United States."

Types

Bourbon whiskey

Bourbon is made primarily from corn (at least 51%), distilled to no more than 160 proof, entered into the barrel at no more than 125 proof, and aged in new charred oak. It is most associated with Kentucky but may be made anywhere in the United States.

Rye whiskey

Rye whiskey is made from at least 51% rye grain and follows similar ageing rules, yielding a drier, spicier spirit historically associated with Pennsylvania and Maryland.

Tennessee whiskey

Tennessee whiskey is similar to bourbon but undergoes the Lincoln County Process, filtering the new spirit through sugar maple charcoal before ageing, as practised by Jack Daniel's and George Dickel.

Corn whiskey

Corn whiskey is made from a mash of at least 80% corn and is either unaged or aged in used or uncharred barrels.
