Masseter

Masseter
Origin: Zygomatic arch
Insertion: Ramus of mandible
Nerve: Masseteric branch of mandibular nerve (V3)
Action: Elevation of the mandible (closing the jaw)

The masseter is a thick, somewhat quadrilateral muscle of mastication, consisting of superficial and deep parts. It arises from the zygomatic arch and inserts into the lateral surface of the ramus of the mandible.

Function

The masseter elevates the mandible, closing the mouth for biting and chewing; its superficial fibres also help protrude the jaw. It is among the strongest muscles in the body by force exerted.

Clinical significance

Masseter hypertrophy can occur with bruxism. The muscle is examined in trigeminal nerve assessment, and its enlargement may be treated with botulinum toxin in cosmetic and therapeutic practice.
