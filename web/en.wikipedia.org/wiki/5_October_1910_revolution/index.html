The 5 October 1910 revolution was a coup d'état organised by the Portuguese Republican Party together with elements of the army and navy, which deposed King Manuel II and proclaimed the First Portuguese Republic, ending more than 770 years of monarchy in Portugal. The day is now a national holiday in Portugal.

Background

Republican sentiment had grown since the 1890 British Ultimatum humiliated the monarchy, and the 1908 assassination of King Carlos I and Crown Prince Luís Filipe in Lisbon badly weakened the throne. Manuel II's political manoeuvring failed to stabilise the regime, while the republican movement, supported by the secretive Carbonária society, prepared an uprising.

Events

The revolt began on 3 October 1910. Although several army units wavered, naval ships in the Tagus — including the cruiser Adamastor — bombarded the royal Necessidades Palace, and republican forces held the Rotunda in Lisbon against loyalist attacks. Manuel II fled into exile in England. On 5 October the Republic was proclaimed from the balcony of Lisbon's city hall, with a provisional government under Teófilo Braga.

Aftermath

The new republic introduced a constitution (1911), separated church and state, and extended education reforms, but suffered chronic instability — dozens of governments — until the military coup of 28 May 1926 ushered in dictatorship.
