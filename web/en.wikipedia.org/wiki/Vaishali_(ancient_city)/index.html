Vaishali (ancient city)

Vaishali (Pali: Vesālī) was an ancient city in present-day Bihar, India, and the capital of the Licchavi republic, one of the earliest known republican states (a gaṇasaṅgha) in the world. It flourished in the 6th century BCE, in the time of the Buddha and Mahavira.

Significance

Vaishali is sacred in both Buddhism and Jainism. The Buddha visited the city many times, delivered important sermons there, admitted women to the monastic order at Vaishali, and announced his approaching parinirvana in its vicinity. Mahavira, the 24th Jain Tirthankara, was born at Kundagrama, a suburb of Vaishali, and spent much of his early life there. The Licchavi confederacy, governed by an assembly of clan chiefs, resisted the expansion of Magadha under Ajatashatru.

Archaeology

Excavations at Basarh and Kolhua have revealed a fortified citadel (Raja Vishal ka Garh), an Ashokan pillar with a single lion capital, a large stupa said to contain the Buddha's relics, and the tank Abhishek Pushkarini used for the coronation of Licchavi rulers.
