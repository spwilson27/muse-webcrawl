Principality

A principality (or princedom) is a monarchical state ruled or reigned over by a prince, or by a monarch with another title ranking below that of king. The term derives from the Latin princeps, "first" or "chief".

Historical development

Principalities were a common form of polity in feudal Europe, often as sub-units of larger kingdoms and empires, especially within the Holy Roman Empire, whose princes (Fürsten) held imperial immediacy. Examples include the principalities of Wales, and the Danubian Principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia under Ottoman suzerainty.

Modern principalities

Only a handful of sovereign principalities survive. In Europe they are Andorra (a co-principality), Liechtenstein, and Monaco. Their heads of state bear the title Prince (or, in Andorra's case, two co-princes, one being the French head of state and the other the Bishop of Urgell).
