Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC)
Type: Crown corporation
Established: 1946
Headquarters: Ottawa, Ontario
Role: housing finance and policy

The Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC; French: Société canadienne d'hypothèques et de logement) is a federal Crown corporation and Canada's national housing agency. Created in 1946 to house returning Second World War veterans, it is best known for insuring residential mortgages, which allows lenders to offer buyers with small down payments access to mortgage financing.

Functions

CMHC provides mortgage loan insurance (mandatory for most purchases with less than 20% down), guarantees securities backed by insured mortgages (supporting the Canada Mortgage Bond program), funds housing programs under the National Housing Strategy, and conducts housing market research and data analysis used across the industry.

History

Originally Central Mortgage and Housing Corporation, it built housing directly, seeded planned communities and public housing, and underwrote much of postwar suburban Canada. Renamed in 1979, it later withdrew from direct construction in favour of insurance, securitisation, and targeted program funding.
