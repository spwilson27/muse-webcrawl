Jon Postel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Jon Postel
BornJonathan Bruce Postel, 6 August 1943, Altadena, California
Died16 October 1998 (aged 55), Santa Monica, California
Known forRFC Editor; IANA; SMTP, IP standards
[Image: Portrait photograph of Jon Postel with his trademark beard and sandals, in the 1990s]

Jonathan Bruce Postel (6 August 1943 – 16 October 1998) was an American computer scientist who made many significant contributions to the development of the Internet, particularly with respect to standards. He is known principally as the editor of the Request for Comments (RFC) document series — a role he held for nearly three decades — and as the founder and steward of the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA).

At UCLA and then at USC's Information Sciences Institute, Postel was at the centre of the ARPANET community: he wrote or edited the specifications for IP, TCP's companions, and mail, and personally maintained the registries of protocol numbers, port numbers and address allocations on which the whole system depended. He is the author of the robustness principle that bears his name — 'Postel's law': be conservative in what you do, be liberal in what you accept from others — the design ethic of interoperable networking.

In January 1998 Postel briefly demonstrated how much authority rested in one trusted person by redirecting eight of the twelve root name servers to IANA as a test — an act that alarmed the US government and hastened the creation of ICANN, into which IANA was folded weeks before his death from complications of heart surgery in October 1998. Vint Cerf's memorial RFC 2468, 'I Remember IANA', remains the RFC series' most famous obituary.
