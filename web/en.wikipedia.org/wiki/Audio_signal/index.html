Audio signal

An audio signal is a representation of sound, typically using either a changing level of electrical voltage for analogue signals, or a series of binary numbers for digital signals. Audio signals have frequencies in the audio frequency range of roughly 20 to 20,000 Hz, which corresponds to the lower and upper limits of human hearing. Audio signals can be synthesised directly, or may originate at a transducer such as a microphone, musical instrument pickup, phonograph cartridge, or tape head. Loudspeakers or headphones convert an electrical audio signal back into sound.

Audio signals can be characterised by parameters such as bandwidth, nominal level, and power level in decibels. The relationship between different reference levels is described by standards for programme levels, which vary between analogue and digital systems and between regions.

Signal flow

In a typical recording or sound reinforcement system, audio signals flow from sources (microphones and instruments) through preamplifiers, mixing consoles and signal processors, to power amplifiers and loudspeakers. Along the way, signals may be balanced or unbalanced, and are carried at different nominal levels: microphone level (a few millivolts), instrument level, and line level (about 0.3–2 volts depending on the standard). Professional line level is commonly +4 dBu, while consumer equipment uses −10 dBV.

Digital audio signals

Digital audio signals are produced by sampling an analogue signal at a regular rate (the sample rate, e.g. 44,100 samples per second for Compact Disc audio) and quantising each sample to a fixed number of bits. Digital signals can be transmitted over interfaces such as AES3, S/PDIF, ADAT, and USB, and are the form in which audio is stored in files and processed in digital audio workstations. The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem states that a signal can be perfectly reconstructed if the sample rate is more than twice its highest frequency.

Channels and specifications

Audio signals may be monophonic (one channel), stereophonic (two channels), or multichannel, as in surround sound formats. Important quality measures include frequency response, signal-to-noise ratio, dynamic range, and total harmonic distortion.
