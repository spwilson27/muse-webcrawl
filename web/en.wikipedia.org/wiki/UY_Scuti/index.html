UY Scuti

UY Scuti is a red supergiant star in the constellation Scutum, near the galactic center direction in the Scutum–Centaurus Arm of the Milky Way. It is a pulsating semiregular variable with a period of about 740 days and is one of the largest known stars by radius: early estimates of roughly 1,700 solar radii made it famous as the largest known star, large enough that placed at the center of the Solar System its surface would extend beyond the orbit of Jupiter.

Properties

UY Scuti lies roughly 5,000–6,000 light-years from Earth. Its distance, and therefore its luminosity and radius, is difficult to pin down precisely; subsequent studies using Gaia data and revised distances have produced smaller radii, on the order of 900 solar radii, comparable to other well-known red supergiants such as Betelgeuse and Antares. Its luminosity is estimated at several hundred thousand times that of the Sun, making it also one of the most luminous cool supergiants known.

Classification and future

The star has spectral type around M2–M4 and is losing mass through a dusty stellar wind, with surrounding circumstellar material detected in infrared observations. Like all red supergiants, UY Scuti is in a late stage of stellar evolution and is expected to end its life as a core-collapse (Type II) supernova within the next few hundred thousand years, leaving behind a neutron star or black hole.

Fame

Media coverage in the 2010s popularized UY Scuti as "the largest star in the universe"; astronomers caution that size records depend on measurement technique and that stars such as Stephenson 2-18 may rival or exceed it, while radio and interferometric measurements of different stars are not always directly comparable.
