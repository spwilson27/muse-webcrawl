Movie star

A movie star (also called a film star) is a famous and accomplished actor, particularly one regarded as a celebrity whose name and image draw audiences to films. Movie stars are a product of the studio system of classical Hollywood cinema, in which studios cultivated, contracted and publicised their leading performers as branded personalities.

Early stars such as Mary Pickford, Douglas Fairbanks and Charlie Chaplin became among the most famous people in the world in the silent era. The sound era produced stars such as Clark Gable, Marilyn Monroe, Humphrey Bogart and Audrey Hepburn, whose box-office appeal largely determined a film's financing and marketing. In contemporary cinema the dominance of franchise films and global markets has changed star economics, though highly paid, globally recognised actors — and analogous stars in industries such as Bollywood and Chinese cinema — remain major cultural figures.
