The Phantom of the Opera

This article is about the stage musical and its source novel in general usage; the title is shared by Gaston Leroux's 1910 novel, Andrew Lloyd Webber's musical, and several films.

The Phantom of the Opera is a musical with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber, lyrics by Charles Hart, and additional lyrics and book by Richard Stilgoe. It is based on the 1910 French novel of the same name by Gaston Leroux. The plot follows Christine Daaé, a beautiful young soprano at the Paris Opéra, who becomes the obsession of a mysterious, disfigured musical genius — the Phantom — living in the subterranean labyrinth beneath the opera house.

History

The musical opened at Her Majesty's Theatre in London's West End in 1986, starring Michael Crawford as the Phantom and Sarah Brightman as Christine, with designs by Maria Björnson and direction by Hal Prince. The Broadway production opened in 1988 and won seven Tony Awards, including Best Musical. It became the longest-running show in Broadway history, playing 13,981 performances before closing in April 2023; the London original ran 33 years before closing in 2020, with a reduced-orchestra revival reopening in 2021. Worldwide, the show has grossed well over $6 billion, making it the most financially successful stage production in history.

Score and spectacle

The score includes "The Phantom of the Opera", "The Music of the Night", "All I Ask of You", "Think of Me", "Angel of Music", and "Masquerade". Signature staging includes the chandelier that rises over the audience in the prologue and crashes at the end of Act I, the underground lake crossed by boat through candlelit mist, and the Phantom's half-mask — one of the most recognizable images in theatre.

Adaptations

Leroux's novel has been filmed repeatedly, most famously in the 1925 silent film starring Lon Chaney. A film adaptation of the musical was released in 2004, directed by Joel Schumacher and starring Gerard Butler and Emmy Rossum. Lloyd Webber's sequel, Love Never Dies, opened in London in 2010.
