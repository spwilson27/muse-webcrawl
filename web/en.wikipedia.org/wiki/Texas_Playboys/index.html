The Texas Playboys were an American musical group led by fiddler and bandleader Bob Wills, and the leading exponents of western swing, a style that fused country fiddling with big-band jazz, blues and mariachi influences. With Wills at the helm, the band was one of the most popular dance bands in the United States during the 1930s and 1940s.

Bob Wills and the Texas Playboys in performance

History

Wills formed the band in 1934 after leaving the Light Crust Doughboys, settling in Tulsa, Oklahoma, where the Playboys broadcast daily on radio station KVOO and played nightly at Cain's Ballroom — still known as "the house that Bob built." Their recording of "San Antonio Rose" (1940) sold over a million copies and became their signature song. Other hits included "Take Me Back to Tulsa", "Faded Love" and "Time Changes Everything".

The band's classic line-up featured fiddles, steel guitar (played by Leon McAuliffe), electric guitar (Eldon Shamblin), piano, bass, drums and a horn section — an unusually large and versatile ensemble for a "country" act. Vocalist Tommy Duncan was the band's long-time frontman.

Legacy

After Wills's death in 1975, former members kept the Texas Playboys name alive for concerts and recordings. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1999 in the early influences category, and western swing's revival has kept their recordings in print.
