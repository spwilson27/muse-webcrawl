Praefectus annonae

The praefectus annonae ("prefect of the provisions") was a senior official of ancient Rome responsible for the annona — the grain supply and distribution system that fed the city of Rome. The office was created by the emperor Augustus, who took personal control of the capital's food supply after a severe shortage in AD 6–7; the first permanent prefect was appointed around AD 8–14.

Duties

The prefect supervised the import of grain from provinces such as Egypt, Africa Proconsularis and Sicily, organised shipping fleets and the corps of grain measurers and boatmen, maintained warehouses (horrea) in Rome and at the ports of Ostia and Portus, and oversaw the monthly distribution of free or subsidised grain (later bread) to registered citizens. In later periods the office also managed supplies of pork, olive oil and wine.

Status

The post was one of the highest open to men of the equestrian order, ranking second only to the prefects of Egypt and the Praetorian Guard in prestige. Holders served indefinite terms at the emperor's pleasure. The prefect had a staff of officials and exercised jurisdiction over disputes concerning the grain trade.

Prefects of the annona are attested into late antiquity, when Rome's supply system was gradually wound down and a parallel office served Constantinople.
