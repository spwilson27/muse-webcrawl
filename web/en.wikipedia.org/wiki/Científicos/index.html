Científicos

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The científicos (Spanish for "scientists") were a group of technocratic advisers to Mexican president Porfirio Díaz during the later part of his regime, the Porfiriato (roughly 1892–1911). Influenced by positivism and contemporary European ideas of scientific politics, they favoured order, economic modernisation and foreign investment, administered — in their view — "scientifically" rather than through democratic politics.

Composition and influence

The circle crystallised around José Yves Limantour, Secretary of Finance from 1893, and included figures such as Justo Sierra, Rosendo Pineda, Pablo Macedo and Francisco Bulnes. Under Limantour the científicos balanced the federal budget, renegotiated the foreign debt, put Mexico on the gold standard and encouraged the railway, mining and banking expansion that characterised the Porfirian boom — gains accompanied by land concentration, labour repression and deepening inequality.

Politics and decline

The group was less a formal party than a faction within the regime, opposed by the more military "Reyista" circle around General Bernardo Reyes. Critics — including the liberal opposition that grew into the movement of Francisco I. Madero — attacked the científicos as an oligarchy that had turned Mexico over to foreign capital. With the outbreak of the Mexican Revolution in 1910 and Díaz's fall in 1911, the group dissolved into exile.

Legacy

In Mexican historiography the científicos remain a byword for technocratic, authoritarian modernisation: admired for fiscal and administrative competence, blamed for the social costs of Porfirian growth.
