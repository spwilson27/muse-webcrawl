Titanic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

RMS Titanic was a British ocean liner, the largest ship afloat at the time of her maiden voyage, that sank on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg in the North Atlantic. Of the estimated 2,224 people aboard, more than 1,500 died, making it one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters in history.

Construction and voyage

Built by Harland and Wolff in Belfast for the White Star Line, Titanic was the second of the Olympic-class liners, with her sister ships Olympic and Britannic. She departed Southampton on 10 April 1912, calling at Cherbourg and Queenstown (Cobh), bound for New York under Captain Edward Smith. At 23:40 on 14 April the ship struck an iceberg, buckling hull plates and opening compartments to the sea; the flooding exceeded her design limits, and the vessel broke apart and sank in the early hours of 15 April. The Cunard liner Carpathia rescued 705 survivors from lifeboats.

Aftermath

The disaster shocked the world and prompted major reforms: the first International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) in 1914 mandated sufficient lifeboats for all aboard, continuous wireless watch and the International Ice Patrol. The wreck, lying about 3,800 metres deep some 600 km southeast of Newfoundland, was located in 1985 by a joint French–American expedition led by Robert Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel. Titanic remains an enduring cultural subject, most famously in James Cameron's 1997 film Titanic.
