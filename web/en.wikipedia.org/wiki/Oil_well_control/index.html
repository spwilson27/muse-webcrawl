Oil well control

Field: Petroleum engineering, drilling

Purpose: Prevention and management of uncontrolled formation fluid influx

Key equipment: Blowout preventer, choke manifold, mud system

Oil well control is the discipline concerned with maintaining control of subsurface pressures during the drilling, completion, and workover of oil and gas wells, so that formation fluids do not enter the wellbore unintentionally. An uncontrolled influx — a kick — can escalate into a blowout, one of the most dangerous and costly accidents in the petroleum industry, as in the 2010 Deepwater Horizon disaster.

Principles

Primary well control is hydrostatic: the column of drilling mud in the well exerts pressure equal to or slightly greater than the formation pore pressure, holding formation fluids in place. Drilling crews monitor mud pit volumes, flow rates, and pump pressures for signs of a kick, such as increased return flow or a rising pit level while the pumps are off.

Secondary control and killing the well

If an influx occurs, the crew shuts in the well using the blowout preventer (BOP) — a stack of rams and an annular preventer that seal around the drill string or close the open hole. Pressures are recorded, and the influx is circulated out under control through a choke manifold while heavier "kill weight" mud displaces the original mud, restoring hydrostatic overbalance. Standard procedures include the driller's method and the wait-and-weight (engineer's) method, each with different circulation sequences. Barriers, testing regimes, and crew certification in well control (such as IWCF and IADC WellSharp courses) are mandated by regulators worldwide, with periodic drills and BOP pressure tests.
