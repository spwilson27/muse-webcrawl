The Astrodome, formally the Harris County Domed Stadium and later the Reliant Astrodome, is a multi-purpose domed stadium in Houston, Texas. Opened in 1965, it was the world's first fully enclosed, air-conditioned multi-purpose sports stadium and was hailed on opening as the "Eighth Wonder of the World". It was the home of the Houston Astros of Major League Baseball for 35 years and of the Houston Oilers of the NFL until 1996.

History

The stadium was championed by Judge Roy Hofheinz to lure and keep major league sport in Houston's hot, humid climate. Its clear Lucite roof panels initially allowed natural grass to grow, but glare caused outfielders to lose fly balls; painting the panels solved the glare and killed the grass, which led directly to the installation of artificial turf in 1966 — marketed as "AstroTurf", a product that reshaped playing surfaces worldwide. The Dome also pioneered luxury suites and giant animated scoreboards.

Notable events

Beyond baseball and football, the Astrodome hosted the 1968 "Game of the Century" college basketball match between Houston and UCLA, the 1973 "Battle of the Sexes" tennis match between Billie Jean King and Bobby Riggs, Evel Knievel jumps, WrestleMania X-Seven (2001), and concerts by Elvis Presley and others. In 2005 it sheltered thousands of evacuees from New Orleans after Hurricane Katrina.

Decline and preservation

After the Astros moved to a new ballpark in 2000 and the Oilers left for Tennessee, the Dome fell into disrepair and lost its certificate of occupancy in 2009. Repeated redevelopment plans failed at the ballot box or in funding, but the building was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2014 and designated a State Antiquities Landmark, and Harris County has continued to debate proposals to convert it into event space and parking within the NRG Park complex, beside its successor, NRG Stadium.
