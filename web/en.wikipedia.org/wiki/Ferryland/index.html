Ferryland

Ferryland is a town in the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador, on the eastern coast of the Avalon Peninsula, about 65 kilometres south of St. John's. It is notable as the site of one of the earliest permanent English settlements in North America.

History

The harbour was used by migratory fishermen from the 16th century. In 1621 George Calvert, later Lord Baltimore, established the Colony of Avalon at Ferryland under a royal charter, building a mansion house, forge and other structures. Calvert himself resided there in 1628–29 before moving on to found Maryland. The settlement continued under the Kirke family and was raided by the Dutch in 1673 and attacked by French forces in the 1690s.

Archaeology

Excavations since the 1980s have uncovered substantial remains of the 17th-century colony, including parts of the waterfront, a cobbled street and thousands of artefacts. The site is interpreted at the Colony of Avalon museum, and Ferryland is also known for its lighthouse picnics and summer theatre.
