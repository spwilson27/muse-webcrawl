Patricia Highsmith (born Mary Patricia Plangman; January 19, 1921 – February 4, 1995) was an American novelist and short story writer widely known for her psychological thrillers, including her series of five novels featuring the character Tom Ripley. She wrote 22 novels and numerous short stories, and her work has been adapted for film more than twenty times.

Life and career

Highsmith was born in Fort Worth, Texas, and raised in New York City, graduating from Barnard College in 1942. Her first novel, Strangers on a Train (1950), was adapted by Alfred Hitchcock in 1951. Writing as Claire Morgan, she published The Price of Salt (1952), a lesbian romance with an unusually hopeful ending, later republished under her own name as Carol and filmed in 2015.

The Talented Mr. Ripley (1955) introduced the charming, amoral con man and murderer Tom Ripley, who returned in four sequels; the novel won the Grand Prix de Littérature Policière and has been filmed several times, notably in 1999. Highsmith lived most of her adult life in Europe — England, France, and finally Switzerland — where she was more celebrated than in her native country. Graham Greene called her "the poet of apprehension." She died of aplastic anemia and lung cancer in Locarno in 1995.
