Guy Debord

Guy Louis Debord (28 December 1931 – 30 November 1994) was a French Marxist theorist, philosopher, filmmaker, critic of consumer society, founder and chief theorist of the Situationist International (1957–1972). His best-known work, The Society of the Spectacle (1967), is a critique of advanced capitalism and mass media that influenced the events of May 1968 in France and later movements.

Life

Debord was born in Paris and raised largely in southern France and Italy. As a young man he gravitated to the avant-garde Letterist movement of Isidore Isou, then broke away to form the Letterist International, developing the practices of dérive (drifting through urban space) and détournement (the rerouting of existing cultural material). In 1957 these groups merged into the Situationist International, which Debord dominated until its dissolution in 1972.

The Society of the Spectacle

Published in 1967, La Société du spectacle comprises 221 theses arguing that modern capitalist society replaces authentic lived experience with representation: "All that once was directly lived has become mere representation." The book became a touchstone of the May 1968 uprisings and of later anti-consumerist and media criticism. Debord followed it with Comments on the Society of the Spectacle (1988), describing an "integrated spectacle."

Films and later life

Debord made several films, including Hurlements en faveur de Sade (1952) and a film version of The Society of the Spectacle (1973); he withdrew his films from circulation in 1984 after the assassination of his friend and producer Gérard Lebovici. Afflicted by illness and alcohol-related polyneuritis, he died by suicide in 1994.
