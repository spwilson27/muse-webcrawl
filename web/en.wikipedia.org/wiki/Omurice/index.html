Omurice or omu-rice (オムライス, omu-raisu) is a Japanese dish consisting of an omelette wrapped around fried rice, commonly topped with ketchup. The name is a portmanteau of the Japanese katakana for "omelette" (omu) and "rice" (raisu). It is a staple of yōshoku — Western-influenced Japanese cooking — and a favourite children's dish in family restaurants.

History

Omurice is generally dated to the turn of the 20th century in Tokyo's Ginza district. The restaurant Rengatei (est. 1895) is often credited with an early version served to its staff, and the Hokkyokusei restaurant in Osaka claims a 1925 origin inspired by a customer with digestive trouble who was served a soft omelette over rice.

Preparation

The rice is typically pan-fried with diced chicken, onion and ketchup, seasoned, then enveloped in a thin sheet of fried egg or topped with a soft, creamy omelette that is sliced open at the table so the curds spill over the rice. A "tornado" variant swirls the egg in the pan with chopsticks.

Variations

Demi-glace sauce is a common alternative to ketchup. In maid cafés, servers draw pictures or messages in ketchup on the omelette. Omurice is also extremely popular in South Korea (omuraiseu) and appears in Taiwan and elsewhere in East Asia.
