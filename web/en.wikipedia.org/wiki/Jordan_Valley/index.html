Jordan Valley

Location: Israel, Jordan, Palestine (West Bank), Syria
Length: About 105 km (Sea of Galilee to Dead Sea)
Formed by: Jordan Rift Valley (Great Rift Valley system)
Lowest point: Shore of the Dead Sea, c. 430 m below sea level

The Jordan Valley (Arabic: غور الأردن, Ghor al-Urdun; Hebrew: עמק הירדן, Emek HaYarden) is the valley in which the Jordan River flows, in the Middle East. It forms part of the larger Jordan Rift Valley, which runs from the river's sources at Mount Hermon southward through the Sea of Galilee to the Dead Sea—the lowest land elevation on Earth—and onward, in the wider rift system, to the Red Sea. Unlike most river valleys, the Jordan Valley lies largely below sea level.

Geography

The valley floor, the Ghor, is bordered by steep fault escarpments rising to the highlands of the West Bank and Galilee on the west and the Transjordanian plateau on the east. Within the Ghor lies a lower, badland-like flood plain, the Zor, through which the river meanders. The hot, dry climate and irrigation from the river, springs, and east-side wadis make the valley a major agricultural region, producing dates, bananas, citrus, and winter vegetables; Jericho, one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, sits in an oasis on the valley floor.

History and politics

Inhabited since prehistoric times, the valley figures prominently in biblical history. Since 1967, the portion on the West Bank has been under Israeli occupation, with Palestinian communities and Israeli settlements sharing the region; its final status, water resources, and borders are central to Israeli–Palestinian and Israeli–Jordanian negotiations. The 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty set the boundary largely along the river.
