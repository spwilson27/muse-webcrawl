Golden Age (metaphor)

A Golden Age is a period of time considered the peak in the history of a country or people when the greatest achievements were made. The term originated from early Greek and Roman poets, who used it to refer to a period when mankind lived in peace and harmony because they did not have to work to feed themselves, for the earth provided food in abundance.

Mythological origin

The concept first appears in the works of the ancient Greek poet Hesiod, whose poem Works and Days describes successive ages of mankind: the Golden Age, the Silver Age, the Bronze Age, the Heroic Age, and finally the present Iron Age. Men of the Golden Age lived without sorrow, toil, or old age, and the earth yielded its fruits without labour. The Roman poet Ovid later retold the scheme in his Metamorphoses, association the golden race with the reign of Saturn.

Use as a historical metaphor

By extension, "golden age" denotes any era regarded in retrospect as a high point of achievement, prosperity, or cultural flourishing. Examples frequently cited include the Golden Age of Athens under Pericles in the fifth century BC, the Dutch Golden Age of the seventeenth century, the Golden Age of Islam (roughly the eighth to thirteenth centuries), the Spanish Golden Age (Siglo de Oro), and the Golden Age of Piracy in the early eighteenth century.

In popular culture

The term is also applied to eras of media and entertainment, such as the Golden Age of Hollywood, the Golden Age of Television, the Golden Age of Comic Books, and the golden age of hip hop. Such labels are often retrospective and contested, since the "goldenness" of an age typically reflects the perspective of those looking back on it.
