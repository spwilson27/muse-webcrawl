Hands-free

Hands-free describes equipment that can be used without the hands, or with only limited manual operation. The term is most commonly applied to mobile telephony: a hands-free device — a wired or Bluetooth headset, earpiece, or built-in car system — allows a telephone conversation without holding the handset, typically using a microphone, speaker and voice commands.

Technology

Hands-free operation in vehicles is usually delivered through the Bluetooth Hands-Free Profile (HFP), which connects a phone to the car's audio system and steering-wheel controls, or through smartphone integration platforms that mirror phone functions on the dashboard display. Voice recognition handles dialling, messaging and navigation prompts. Outside telephony, the term covers voice-controlled assistants, foot- or sensor-operated fixtures, and accessibility devices.

Driving laws and safety

Many jurisdictions prohibit holding a phone while driving but permit hands-free use; some restrict novice drivers further, and a number of countries ban all phone use, handheld or not, for certain categories of driver. Research by road-safety bodies generally finds that the cognitive distraction of a conversation persists even when the phone is not held, so hands-free use reduces manual and visual distraction but does not eliminate risk — a point reflected in safety campaigns in several countries.

Other uses

The term is also used for hands-free faucets and doors, surgical and industrial equipment operable without touch, and hands-free breast pumps and baby carriers. In computing, "hands-free" profiles and voice control are important accessibility features.
