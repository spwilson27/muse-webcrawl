Circle packing

In geometry, circle packing is the study of the arrangement of circles — of equal or varying sizes — on a given surface such that no overlapping occurs and so that all circles touch one another or lie as densely as possible. The associated packing density of an arrangement is the proportion of the surface covered by the circles.

Packing in the plane

For equal circles in the infinite plane, the densest arrangement is the hexagonal (triangular lattice) packing, in which the centres of the circles form a triangular lattice and each circle touches six others. Its density is π/(2√3) ≈ 0.9069. Joseph Louis Lagrange proved in 1773 that this is the densest lattice packing, and Axel Thue gave early proofs (1890–1910) that it is optimal among all packings; a fully rigorous elementary treatment was completed by László Fejes Tóth in 1940.

Packings in containers

A classic family of problems asks for the densest way, or the arrangement of the largest equal circles, that fit inside a given container — a larger circle, a square, a rectangle or an equilateral triangle. Many cases have been solved exactly for small numbers of circles, while others are known only numerically and are recorded in references such as Packomania. Packing unequal circles, as in an Apollonian gasket formed by repeatedly filling gaps with tangent circles, leads to fractal structures connected with Descartes' circle theorem.

Circle packing theorem

The term also refers to the Koebe–Andreev–Thurston theorem, which states that for any planar graph there exists a packing of circles in the plane whose tangency graph is the given graph. This result links combinatorics, geometry and complex analysis, and underlies a discrete theory of conformal maps developed by William Thurston.

Applications

Packing problems model the arrangement of atoms in crystals, the layout of cylindrical goods (cans, pipes, cables), and problems in communications and materials science. In three dimensions the analogue is sphere packing, settled by the Kepler conjecture.
