Meir Dizengoff (Hebrew: מאיר דיזנגוף; 25 February 1861 – 23 September 1936) was a Zionist leader, industrialist and politician, and one of the founders of Tel Aviv, serving as its first mayor.

Early life

Dizengoff was born in the village of Ekimovtsy in Bessarabia, in the Russian Empire. He studied chemical engineering in Paris and became involved in early Zionist activity. After involvement with the Odessa Committee and various industrial ventures, including glass production, he immigrated to Ottoman Palestine in 1905, settling in Jaffa, where he founded the Geula company, which bought land for Jewish settlement.

Founding and mayor of Tel Aviv

In 1909, Dizengoff was among the 66 founding families of Ahuzat Bayit, the Jewish neighbourhood outside Jaffa that, renamed Tel Aviv in 1910, became the first modern Hebrew city. Dizengoff chaired its council and, when Tel Aviv received township status in 1921, became its first mayor, serving until 1925 and again from 1928 until his death in 1936. He championed rapid urban development, planning and cultural life, envisioning Tel Aviv as a modern European-style city.

Legacy

Dizengoff's home on Rothschild Boulevard was donated to the city and became the Tel Aviv Museum of Art; it was in this building that David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel on 14 May 1948, and it now houses Independence Hall. Dizengoff Street, one of Tel Aviv's main thoroughfares, and Dizengoff Square bear his name; the colloquial Hebrew expression lehizdangef ("to Dizengoff oneself", i.e., to stroll and show oneself on the street) preserves his association with the city's boulevard culture.
