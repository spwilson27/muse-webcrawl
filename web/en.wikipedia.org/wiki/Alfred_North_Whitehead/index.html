Alfred North Whitehead

Alfred North Whitehead OM FRS
Born: 15 February 1861, Ramsgate, Kent, England
Died: 30 December 1947 (aged 86), Cambridge, Massachusetts
Fields: Mathematics, philosophy of science, metaphysics
Known for: Principia Mathematica (with Russell), process philosophy

Alfred North Whitehead (15 February 1861 – 30 December 1947) was an English mathematician and philosopher. He created the modern school of thought known as process philosophy, which has been applied in a wide variety of disciplines, including ecology, theology, education, physics, biology, economics and psychology.

Life and career

Whitehead studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he later taught mathematics. With his former student Bertrand Russell he co-wrote Principia Mathematica (1910–1913), an attempt to ground mathematics in logic and one of the most influential works of modern logic. He also wrote on algebra and on relativity, proposing an alternative theory of gravitation.

In 1924, at age 63, he moved to Harvard University as professor of philosophy, beginning a second career. There he developed a comprehensive metaphysical system based on "actual occasions" of experience rather than static material substance.

Philosophy

His major philosophical work, Process and Reality (1929), based on his Gifford Lectures, argues that reality consists of processes and events in relation, captured in his phrase that the world is made of "drops of experience." Other works include Science and the Modern World (1925), Religion in the Making (1926), The Aims of Education (1929) and Adventures of Ideas (1933). He famously characterised European philosophy as "a series of footnotes to Plato."

Influence

Process thought influenced process theology (Charles Hartshorne, John B. Cobb Jr.), environmental philosophy and educational theory, and has seen renewed interest in metaphysics and philosophy of science.
