Anterior cingulate cortex

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) is the frontal part of the cingulate cortex, which wraps around the corpus callosum in the medial frontal lobe. It is implicated in a wide variety of functions, including error detection, conflict monitoring, decision-making, emotion, empathy and the experience of pain.

Structure and connections

The ACC is commonly divided into a dorsal (cognitive) division, connected with the prefrontal cortex and motor areas, and a ventral (affective) division, connected with the amygdala, insula and hypothalamus. It corresponds roughly to Brodmann areas 24, 32 and 33 and contains distinctive large spindle-shaped von Economo neurons, also found in the insula and associated with social cognition.

Functions

The dorsal ACC is activated when a person makes errors or faces conflicting responses — the source of the error-related negativity signal in EEG — and is a core node of the salience network. The ACC responds to the affective unpleasantness of pain as well as to social exclusion. Abnormal ACC activity has been reported in depression, obsessive–compulsive disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder and chronic pain; surgical cingulotomy targeting the ACC has been used as a last-resort treatment for severe depression, OCD and intractable pain.
