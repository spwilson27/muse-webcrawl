Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway

The Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway (often called the Tenn-Tom) is a 234-mile (377 km) artificial waterway in the United States connecting the Tennessee River with the Tombigbee River and hence the Gulf of Mexico at Mobile, Alabama. Built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers between 1972 and 1985, it provides a shorter route for commercial navigation between the interior river system and the Gulf.

Construction

The project required excavating some 310 million cubic yards of earth—more than the Panama Canal—including the Divide Cut through the divide between the two river basins. Ten locks raise and lower barges a total of about 341 feet. Authorised in 1946 but long delayed by cost and environmental disputes, it remains one of the largest earth-moving projects in history.

Use

Traffic consists mainly of bulk commodities such as coal, timber and aggregates; volumes have generally fallen below original projections, a point raised by critics since its approval.
