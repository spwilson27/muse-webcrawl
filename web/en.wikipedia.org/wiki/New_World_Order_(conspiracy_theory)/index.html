New World Order (conspiracy theory)

The New World Order (NWO) is a conspiracy theory which hypothesizes a secretly emerging totalitarian world government. The common theme is that a secretive power elite with a globalist agenda is conspiring to eventually rule the world through an authoritarian one-world government — replacing sovereign nation-states — and an all-encompassing propaganda campaign whose ideology hails the establishment of the New World Order as the culmination of history's progress.

The Eye of Providence, frequently cited in New World Order theories

Origins of the term

The phrase "new world order" has been used in politics to describe periods of dramatic change, notably after the First and Second World Wars and at the end of the Cold War, when U.S. President George H. W. Bush used it in a 1990 speech about post–Cold War cooperation. Conspiracy theorists reinterpreted such usage as evidence of a plot, drawing on older themes involving groups such as the Illuminati, Freemasons, and various alleged secret societies.

Themes

Theories vary widely but commonly allege that political and financial elites, acting through organizations such as the Bilderberg Group, the Trilateral Commission, the Council on Foreign Relations, or the United Nations, are preparing a single global currency, a cashless society, mass surveillance, and population control. Some versions incorporate religious prophecy, extraterrestrials, or other fringe elements. Symbols on the United States one-dollar bill, including the Eye of Providence and the Latin phrases Novus ordo seclorum and Annuit cœptis, are frequently cited as supposed proof.

Spread and influence

The theory gained prominence in the United States in the 1990s through militia movements, talk radio, and writers and broadcasters, and spread further with the internet and social media. Scholars and journalists generally describe New World Order beliefs as a form of conspiracism that can fuse older prejudices with contemporary anxieties about globalization, and note that the theory's flexibility allows unrelated events to be absorbed as confirmation.
