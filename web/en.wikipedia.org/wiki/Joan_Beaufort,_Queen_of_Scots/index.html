Joan Beaufort (c. 1404 – 15 July 1445) was Queen of Scotland from 1424 to 1437 as the wife of King James I. She served briefly as regent of Scotland during the minority of her son, James II, after her husband's assassination.

Marriage to James I

Joan was the daughter of John Beaufort, 1st Earl of Somerset, and Margaret Holland, and a niece of King Henry IV of England. James I of Scotland, held captive in England for eighteen years, is said in tradition to have fallen in love with her after seeing her in the garden below his window at Windsor—a romance celebrated in his poem The Kingis Quair, in which she is generally identified as the lady described. They married at Southwark in February 1424, and Joan's dowry formed part of the arrangements for James's release and return to Scotland. She was crowned queen at Scone in May 1424.

Regency and later life

Joan was wounded trying to protect the king when he was assassinated at Perth in February 1437. As mother of the six-year-old James II, she became a leading figure in the regency government, though real power was contested between her, Archibald Douglas, 5th Earl of Douglas (as Lieutenant-General), and Sir Alexander Livingstone. In 1439 she married James Stewart, known as the Black Knight of Lorn, a match that weakened her political position; she was briefly arrested by Livingstone's faction the same year. Joan died at Dunbar Castle on 15 July 1445 and was buried beside her first husband in the Carthusian priory at Perth.
