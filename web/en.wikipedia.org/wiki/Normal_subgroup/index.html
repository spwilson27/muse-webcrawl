Normal subgroup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In abstract algebra, a normal subgroup (also known as an invariant subgroup or self-conjugate subgroup) is a subgroup that is invariant under conjugation by members of the group of which it is a part. In other words, a subgroup N of the group G is normal if and only if gNg−1 = N for all g in G. The usual notation for this relation is N ◁ G.

Definitions

The following conditions are equivalent for a subgroup N of G:

  • For all g in G, gNg−1 = N.
  • For all g in G, gN = Ng — the left and right cosets coincide.
  • N is a union of conjugacy classes of G.
  • There is a homomorphism from G whose kernel is exactly N.

Importance

Normal subgroups are important because they — and only they — can be used to construct quotient groups from a given group. If N is normal in G, the set of cosets G/N forms a group under the operation induced from G; this construction underlies the first isomorphism theorem. Kernels of group homomorphisms are always normal, and every normal subgroup arises as a kernel. In an abelian group every subgroup is normal. A group with no normal subgroups other than the trivial subgroup and itself is called simple; the classification of finite simple groups is one of the great achievements of 20th-century mathematics.

History

The concept emerged from Galois theory: Évariste Galois was the first to realise that certain subgroups (corresponding to irreducible factors of a polynomial's resolvent) have the invariance property, which Camille Jordan later formalised in his Traité des substitutions (1870).
