Siege of Baghdad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Siege of Baghdad may refer to several sieges of the city of Baghdad, the Abbasid capital on the Tigris River in present-day Iraq, over the centuries. Baghdad, founded in 762 by the caliph al-Mansur, was repeatedly attacked as the seat of the Abbasid Caliphate and later rulers of Mesopotamia.

Notable sieges

  • Siege of Baghdad (812–813) — during the Fourth Fitna, the civil war between the caliphs al-Amin and al-Ma'mun; forces of al-Ma'mun under Tahir ibn Husayn blockaded and captured the city, and al-Amin was killed.
  • Siege of Baghdad (865) — during the Anarchy at Samarra, in the civil war between al-Musta'in and al-Mu'tazz.
  • Siege of Baghdad (945) — the Buyid emir Mu'izz al-Dawla captured the city, reducing the Abbasid caliphs to figureheads under Buyid control.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1136) — the Seljuk sultan Mas'ud besieged the caliph al-Mustarshid's successor al-Rashid.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1258) — the most famous of all: the Mongol army of Hulagu Khan captured and devastated Baghdad, ending the Abbasid Caliphate in Iraq.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1393 and 1401) — Timur (Tamerlane) twice captured the city; the second siege ended in a great massacre.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1534) — the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent took the city from Safavid Persia.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1638) — Sultan Murad IV recaptured Baghdad from the Safavids in the final Ottoman–Safavid war.
  • Siege of Baghdad (1733) — Nader Shah of Persia besieged Ottoman Baghdad but was forced to withdraw.
