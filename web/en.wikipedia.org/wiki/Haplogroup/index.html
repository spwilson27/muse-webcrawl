A haplogroup is a group of similar haplotypes that share a common ancestor with a single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) mutation. Because haplogroups trace descent along a single line of inheritance, they are used in population genetics and genetic genealogy to study the ancestry and migrations of human populations.

Y-DNA and mtDNA haplogroups

The two most studied systems are Y-chromosome DNA haplogroups, inherited father-to-son and tracing the patrilineal line, and mitochondrial DNA haplogroups, inherited from the mother and tracing the matrilineal line. Neither recombines (the Y largely, mtDNA entirely), so mutations accumulate in a nested hierarchy that can be reconstructed as a phylogenetic tree rooted in "Y-chromosomal Adam" and "Mitochondrial Eve" respectively.

Nomenclature and uses

Haplogroups are designated by letters and alphanumeric subclade codes (for example Y-DNA haplogroup R1b, mtDNA haplogroup H). Their present-day geographical distributions, combined with ancient DNA, have been used to reconstruct prehistoric migrations such as the peopling of the Americas and the spread of Indo-European languages, though single-line haplogroups represent only a tiny fraction of a person's total ancestry.
