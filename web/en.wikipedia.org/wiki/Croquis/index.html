Croquis

Croquis drawing is a quick and sketchy drawing of a live model. The word is French for "sketch". Croquis drawings are usually made in a few minutes, after which the model changes pose or leaves, and another croquis is drawn.

Practice

The short duration of the pose forces the artist to omit detail and concentrate on the essential gesture, movement and proportions of the figure. Croquis is a standard exercise in art schools and life-drawing classes worldwide: a session typically begins with a series of poses lasting from thirty seconds to ten minutes, warming up the artist's hand and eye before longer studies.

Because the aim is speed and spontaneity rather than finish, croquis are usually executed in pencil, charcoal, ink or other quick media on inexpensive paper. Practitioners credit regular croquis practice with improving observation, line confidence and the ability to capture fleeting poses from life or memory. Fashion designers also use croquis — the term additionally denotes the stylised elongated figure templates over which garments are sketched.
