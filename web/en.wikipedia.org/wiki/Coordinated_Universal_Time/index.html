Overview

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the world's primary time standard, the basis from which civil time zones are reckoned as fixed offsets. It took over in the 1960s–70s from Greenwich Mean Time, the astronomical time of the Greenwich meridian.

UTC runs at the rate of atomic clocks — it is derived from International Atomic Time — but is kept within 0.9 seconds of Earth's rotation by the occasional insertion of a leap second, announced about six months ahead; 27 were added between 1972 and the most recent in 2016. International bodies have agreed to relax that link by 2035, letting UTC drift from solar time rather than continue leap seconds.
