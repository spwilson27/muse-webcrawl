Meyers Manx

The Meyers Manx is a dune buggy designed by engineer, artist, boat builder, and surfer Bruce F. Meyers (1926–2021) and produced by his Fountain Valley, California company, B. F. Meyers & Co., from 1964 to 1971. It is widely credited with popularizing the dune buggy and launching the off-road recreation industry.

The Manx is a small, open-wheel recreational vehicle built on a shortened Volkswagen Beetle chassis, combining the Beetle's air-cooled flat-four engine, transaxle, and suspension with a one-piece fiberglass body distinguished by its high-mounted curved fenders, low sides, and lack of doors, roof, or hood in the conventional sense.

History

Meyers, a pioneer of fiberglass boat building, built the first prototype, "Old Red", in 1964 using his experience in fiberglass construction. The car attracted attention in the California beach and desert scene. In 1967 a Manx driven by Vic Wilson and Ted Mangels won the inaugural Mexican 1000 (the forerunner of the Baja 1000) off-road race, beating motorcycles and trucks and cementing the buggy's reputation. The Manx was named to the cover of Car and Driver and became a cultural icon; Steve McQueen drove a modified Manx in the film The Thomas Crown Affair (1968).

Copies and decline

The Manx was so popular that it was widely imitated; Meyers estimated that more than 250,000 buggies resembling his design were made by hundreds of companies. Although Meyers won a patent case, the flood of copies undermined the business, and production of the original Manx ended around 1971 after roughly 6,000–7,000 genuine kits. In 2000 Meyers revived the brand as Meyers Manx, Inc., producing updated versions such as the Manxter 2+2, the Kick-Out, and the Manxter DualSport.

Electric revival

In 2022 the company, under new ownership, unveiled the Meyers Manx 2.0 EV, an all-electric revival designed by Freeman Thomas, with rear-wheel drive, independent rear suspension, and a claimed range of up to 300 miles with the larger of two battery options. Bruce Meyers was inducted into the Off-Road Motorsports Hall of Fame, and a genuine Manx was added to the Historic Vehicle Association's national register, with Old Red displayed at the Petersen Automotive Museum and documented for the Library of Congress.
