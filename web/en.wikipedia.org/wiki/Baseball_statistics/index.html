Baseball statistics

Baseball statistics play an important role in evaluating the performance of a player or team in the game of baseball, and the sport has accumulated an exceptionally rich statistical record since the nineteenth century. Statistics are recorded for batting, pitching, baserunning and fielding, and are summarised in the traditional box score.

Traditional measures

For batters, the classic "counting" and rate statistics include games played, at bats, runs, hits, doubles, triples, home runs, runs batted in (RBI), walks and strikeouts. The most famous rate measure is the batting average (hits divided by at bats), alongside on-base percentage and slugging percentage, which combine into OPS. Pitchers are evaluated by wins, losses, innings pitched, earned run average (ERA) and strikeouts.

Sabermetrics

The analytical movement known as sabermetrics, popularised by Bill James and by the 2002 Oakland Athletics depicted in Moneyball, developed advanced measures such as OPS+, wins above replacement (WAR), WHIP, FIP and weighted on-base average (wOBA), aiming to isolate a player's true contribution from context. Modern player-tracking systems such as Statcast have added measurements of exit velocity, launch angle, spin rate and sprint speed.
