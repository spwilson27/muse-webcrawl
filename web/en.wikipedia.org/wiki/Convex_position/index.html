Convex position

In discrete geometry, a set of points in the plane is in convex position if every point of the set is a vertex of the set's convex hull — equivalently, no point of the set lies inside the convex hull of the others, and no point lies in the interior of the polygon formed by the rest.

Combinatorial geometry

Sets in convex position are the extremal configurations of the Erdős–Szekeres theorem, which states that any set of (r−1)(s−1)+1 points in general position contains r points in convex position or s collinear points; the classical Erdős–Szekeres conjecture concerns the smallest number of points that forces a convex k-gon. Related questions ask for the largest empty convex polygons (containing no other points of the set) that a point set must contain.

Counting and randomness

The number of points in convex position is a key parameter in output-sensitive convex hull algorithms. For random point sets, the expected number of points on the hull — and hence potentially in convex position — grows only polylogarithmically or as a small power of n, depending on the distribution.

Higher dimensions

The notion generalizes to higher dimensions: points are in convex position if each is a vertex of their convex hull, i.e. a vertex of the convex polytope they span.
