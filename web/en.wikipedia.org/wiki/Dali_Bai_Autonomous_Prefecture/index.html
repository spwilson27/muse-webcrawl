Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture (Chinese: 大理白族自治州; pinyin: Dàlǐ Báizú Zìzhìzhōu; Bai: Darl·lit Baif·cuf zirl·zirl·zox) is an autonomous prefecture of western Yunnan Province, China. It administers an area of about 29,500 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 3.3 million at the 2020 census. The prefectural seat is Xiaguan, in Dali City, on the southern shore of Erhai Lake.

Geography

The prefecture lies between the Cangshan mountain range to the west and Erhai Lake to the east, at an elevation around 2,000 metres, giving it a mild, spring-like climate for much of the year. The historic old town of Dali, seat of the medieval Nanzhao and Dali kingdoms, stands between the mountains and the lake and is one of China's best-known tourist destinations.

Demographics

The prefecture is named for the Bai people, its largest recognised minority group, who form roughly a third of the population; Han Chinese form about half, with Yi, Hui, Lisu, Achang and other groups also present. The Bai language and culture, including the March Fair (Sanyue Jie) festival and traditional tie-dye crafts of Zhoucheng, are prominent features of the region.

Transport and economy

Dali is connected to Kunming by high-speed rail (opened 2018) and expressway, and Dali Fengyi Airport provides domestic flights. Tourism, agriculture (including tea, walnuts and dairy), marble quarrying and renewable energy are major economic activities.
