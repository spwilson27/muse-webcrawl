German reunification

Date: 3 October 1990
German: Deutsche Wiedervereinigung
Process: East Germany accedes to Federal Republic
Holiday: Day of German Unity

German reunification (German: Deutsche Wiedervereinigung) was the process in 1990 by which the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) joined the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany), ending 45 years of division after the Second World War, with reunified Berlin as capital.

Background

Mass protests in East Germany in autumn 1989, emigration via Hungary, and the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989 opened the way. Chancellor Helmut Kohl's ten-point programme, East Germany's first free elections (March 1990), monetary union (July 1990) and the Two Plus Four Treaty with the wartime Allies settled external aspects, including a united Germany's NATO membership.

Unification and consequences

On 3 October 1990 the five re-established eastern Länder acceded under Article 23 of the Basic Law. Economic transformation via the Treuhand privatisation agency brought rapid deindustrialisation and unemployment, and economic and social gaps between east and west have persisted, alongside Ostalgie and ongoing political divergence.
