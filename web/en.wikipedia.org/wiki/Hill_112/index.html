Hill 112

Hill 112 is a wooded elevation rising to 112 metres south-west of Caen in Normandy, France, located between the rivers Odon and Orne near the villages of Baron-sur-Odon and Esquay-Notre-Dame. During the Battle of Normandy in the summer of 1944 it was one of the most bitterly contested positions on the British front, dominating observation over the surrounding plain.

Battle of Normandy

British forces first reached the hill during Operation Epsom in late June 1944, when the 11th Armoured Division seized the crest, only to be forced back across the Odon by German counter-attacks. In Operation Jupiter (10–11 July), the 43rd (Wessex) Infantry Division fought to retake the hill at heavy cost; the position changed hands repeatedly and was not fully secured until early August, during the fighting around Operation Bluecoat and the drive to Mont Pinçon.

Significance

Both sides recognised the hill's value: from its summit observers could direct artillery fire over a wide area, and German commanders described it as a key to the defence of Caen. The intensity of the fighting is reflected in memorials on the site, including a Churchill tank monument to the 43rd Wessex Division and an arboretum of remembrance trees planted on the slopes.
