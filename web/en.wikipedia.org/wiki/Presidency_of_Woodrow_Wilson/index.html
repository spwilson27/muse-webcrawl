Presidency of Woodrow Wilson

Term: 4 Mar 1913 – 4 Mar 1921
Party: Democratic
Vice Presidents: Thomas R. Marshall
Predecessor: William Howard Taft
Successor: Warren G. Harding

The presidency of Woodrow Wilson began on 4 March 1913, when Woodrow Wilson was inaugurated as the 28th president of the United States, and ended on 4 March 1921.

Domestic policy — New Freedom

Wilson enacted the Underwood tariff reduction, the Federal Reserve Act (1913), the Federal Trade Commission Act and Clayton Antitrust Act (1914), the Federal Farm Loan Act, and the Adamson Act. His first term saw the ratification context for income tax (16th Amendment, 1913). Later reforms included women's suffrage (19th Amendment, 1920) and Prohibition (18th Amendment). His administration segregated federal offices, a major stain on his record.

Foreign policy and World War I

After neutrality and intervention in Mexico, the US entered World War I in April 1917 after German unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram. Wilson's Fourteen Points (1918) guided the Paris Peace Conference; he championed the League of Nations and Treaty of Versailles, but the Senate refused ratification.

Final years

Wilson suffered a disabling stroke in October 1919. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1919. Espionage and Sedition Acts wartime repression also mark his presidency.
