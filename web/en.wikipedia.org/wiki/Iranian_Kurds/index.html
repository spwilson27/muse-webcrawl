Iranian Kurds

Iranian Kurds are Kurds who live in Iran, concentrated in the country's west and northwest — principally Kurdistan Province, Kermanshah Province, Ilam Province, and West Azerbaijan Province, with significant populations in Lorestan and in cities such as Tehran. Estimates of their population commonly range between roughly 8 and 12 million, making them one of Iran's largest ethnic minorities.

Language and religion

Iranian Kurds speak several Kurdish dialects, chiefly Sorani (Central Kurdish) in the north and Southern Kurdish (including Kalhori and Laki) further south; some communities also speak Gorani (Hawrami). A majority of Iranian Kurds are Sunni Muslims, in contrast to Iran's predominantly Shia population, though there are Shia Kurdish communities, especially around Kermanshah, as well as adherents of Yarsanism (Ahl-e Haqq).

History

After the Second World War, the short-lived Republic of Mahabad (1946) established a Kurdish-led statelet around Mahabad in the northwest under Qazi Muhammad, supported by the Soviet Union; it collapsed within a year when Iranian troops reoccupied the area. Following the 1979 Iranian Revolution, armed conflict erupted between Kurdish movements seeking autonomy — principally the Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (KDPI) and Komala — and the Islamic Republic, causing heavy fighting into the 1980s. Kurdish parties have since pursued political goals amid periodic crackdowns, cross-border tensions with Turkey and Iraq, and recurring protests, including those following the death of Mahsa (Jina) Amini, a Kurdish woman from Saqqez, in police custody in 2022.
