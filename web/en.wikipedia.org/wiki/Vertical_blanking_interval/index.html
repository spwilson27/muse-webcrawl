Vertical blanking interval

In raster scan displays, the vertical blanking interval (VBI), also known as the vertical interval or VBLANK, is the time between the end of the last visible line of a frame or field and the beginning of the first visible line of the next. During this interval the electron beam of a cathode-ray tube returns from the bottom of the screen to the top to begin the next scan, and no picture information is displayed.

Analogue television

In analogue television systems the VBI occupies a number of scan lines at the start of each field — about 20 lines in the 525-line NTSC system and a similar number in PAL. Because these lines are not displayed, they came to be used to carry ancillary data: teletext pages, closed captions (in North America, caption data on line 21), timecode, test signals, and copy-protection data such as Macrovision. Time-of-day services and programme delivery control signals have also been broadcast in the VBI.

Computing

In computer graphics, software synchronizes drawing with the vertical blanking interval (vertical synchronization, or VSync) to prevent screen tearing, updating the frame buffer only while the display is between frames. Many home computers and video game consoles historically performed animation and game logic during the vertical blank, the only time the video hardware could be safely reprogrammed.
