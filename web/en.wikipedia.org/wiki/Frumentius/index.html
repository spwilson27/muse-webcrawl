Frumentius (c. 300 – c. 383) was a Phoenician Christian missionary from Tyre who brought Christianity to the Kingdom of Aksum in Ethiopia and became its first bishop. In Ethiopia he is honoured as Abba Salama, "Father of Peace", and regarded as the founder of the Ethiopian Church.

Journey to Aksum

According to the church historian Rufinus, Frumentius travelled as a boy with his brother Aedesius in the company of the philosopher Meropius of Tyre on a voyage to India. On the return journey their ship was attacked at a Red Sea port — probably Adulis — and the crew killed; the two boys were spared and taken to the court of the king of Aksum at Aksum. There Frumentius rose to become secretary and treasurer, and after the king's death he served as tutor to the young heir, Ezana.

Bishop of Aksum

Granted leave to return home, Frumentius instead went to Alexandria and reported to Patriarch Athanasius that Ethiopia was ripe for evangelisation. Athanasius consecrated Frumentius himself as bishop of Aksum (around AD 328, or possibly later, c. 346). Returning to Ethiopia, Frumentius baptised converts, built churches, and — by tradition — won the conversion of King Ezana himself, establishing Christianity as the religion of the Aksumite state.

Legacy

Frumentius is credited with helping establish the close historic bond between the Ethiopian Church and the Coptic Patriarchate of Alexandria, which appointed Ethiopia's bishops for nearly 1,600 years. A letter from the (Arian) emperor Constantius II to the Aksumite court, preserved by Athanasius, asks in vain that Frumentius be sent to Alexandria to be examined by an Arian bishop.
