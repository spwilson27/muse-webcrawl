Bella ciao

"Bella ciao" (Italian for "Goodbye, beautiful") is an Italian folk song associated worldwide with anti-fascist resistance. Its best-known version was the anthem of the Italian partisans fighting Nazi occupation and the Italian Social Republic in 1943–1945: a fighter bids farewell to his beloved, expecting death and asking to be buried in the mountains beneath a flower. The song has since been recorded in dozens of languages and adopted by protest and solidarity movements around the world.

Origins

The melody's origins are debated; related tunes circulated among the mondine, the women who worked seasonally in the rice paddies of the Po Valley, whose version laments the hardships of labour ("Alla mattina appena alzata / o bella ciao..."). The partisan text emerged during the Resistance, though how widely it was actually sung by partisans during the war, as opposed to popularised afterwards, is questioned by historians.

Spread and modern use

After the war the song was performed at folk festivals and spread internationally through left-wing movements, appearing in versions by artists such as Yves Montand. In the 2010s it reached a new global audience as a signature tune of the television series Money Heist (La Casa de Papel), and it is sung at protests from climate marches to anti-war demonstrations. In Italy it is performed every 25 April, Liberation Day, and remains a potent — and sometimes contested — symbol of the Resistance.
