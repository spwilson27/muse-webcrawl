Grýla

Grýla is a female troll or ogress in Icelandic folklore, one of the oldest figures of the Icelandic Christmas tradition. She lives in the mountains and descends at Christmas time to hunt naughty children, whom she carries away in her sack to boil in her cauldron.

Folklore

Grýla is named in medieval sources including Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and in later folk tales and poems (Grýlukvæði). Descriptions accumulated grotesque details over the centuries—multiple heads, hooves, horns, and as many as fifteen tails in one famous poem. She is the mother of the thirteen Yule Lads (Jólasveinar), who visit Icelandic homes in the run-up to Christmas, and the owner of the Yule Cat (Jólakötturinn), a huge feline that eats those who receive no new clothes for Christmas. Her husband is the lazy troll Leppalúði.

Modern tradition

In 1746, Icelandic authorities discouraged the use of Grýla to frighten children, but she remains a fixture of Icelandic Christmas celebrations, appearing in seasonal events and media.
