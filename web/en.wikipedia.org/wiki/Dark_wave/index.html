Dark wave

Dark wave is a music genre that emerged from the late-1970s new wave and post-punk movement, characterised by dark, introspective lyrics, minor-key melodies, and atmospheric synthesisers.


Stylistic origins: New wave, post-punk, gothic rock
Cultural origins: Late 1970s, Europe

History

The term was applied to acts such as Siouxsie and the Banshees, Joy Division, The Cure, Bauhaus, and Cocteau Twins. In Germany the Neue Deutsche Welle underground produced related sounds. By the 1980s–90s, labels like 4AD and Projekt Records fostered ethereal wave, neoclassical dark wave, and cold wave substyles with artists including Dead Can Dance, Clan of Xymox, and Deine Lakaien.

Characteristics

Dark wave blends synthesisers, drum machines, and guitars with melancholic vocals, drawing on romanticism, existentialism, and gothic literature. It overlaps with goth subculture while remaining broader in sound.
