Tate St Ives

Opened1993
ArchitectsEvans & Shalev

Tate St Ives is an art gallery in St Ives, Cornwall, England, exhibiting work by modern British artists with links to the St Ives area. The Tate also took over management of another museum in the town, the Barbara Hepworth Museum and Sculpture Garden, in 1980. Tate St Ives was built on the site of a former gasworks overlooking Porthmeor Beach and opened in 1993.

History

St Ives became a magnet for artists in the twentieth century; Ben Nicholson and Christopher Wood visited in 1928 and met the naive painter Alfred Wallis, and during the Second World War Nicholson, Barbara Hepworth and Naum Gabo settled in the town, founding the St Ives School of modernism. The gallery celebrates this heritage with displays of works by Hepworth, Nicholson, Peter Lanyon, Patrick Heron, Terry Frost and others, alongside contemporary exhibitions.

Building and extension

The original building, designed by Eldred Evans and David Shalev, features a rotunda entrance and a large window framing the sea view. The building was closed in 2015 for a major expansion, designed by Jamie Fobert Architects, which doubled the gallery space by excavating a new wing into the cliff; it reopened in October 2017. The refurbished gallery was shortlisted for the RIBA Stirling Prize and won the Art Fund Museum of the Year award in 2018.
