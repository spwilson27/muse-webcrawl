Planxty was an Irish folk group formed in Dublin in 1972, generally credited with revolutionising Irish traditional music by marrying it to folk-revival musicianship and sophisticated ensemble arrangements. The original line-up — Christy Moore (guitar, bodhrán), Andy Irvine (mandolin, bouzouki), Dónal Lunny (bouzouki, guitar) and Liam O'Flynn (uilleann pipes, tin whistle) — took its name from "planxty", a word used by the harper Turlough O'Carolan in tune titles honouring patrons, such as "Planxty Irwin".

Career

The band formed after working together on Moore's 1972 album Prosperous. Their debut album Planxty (1973, sometimes called "the Black Album") and The Well Below the Valley (1973) paired virtuoso pipe and string interplay with songs such as Irvine's "The West Coast of Clare". Exhaustion and management problems split the original group by 1975. A re-formed Planxty (1978–1983), at times including Matt Molloy, explored Balkan metres and more experimental material. In 2003 the original four reunited for concerts filmed and recorded to wide acclaim (released as Planxty Live 2004, with concerts in 2004–05).

Influence

Planxty's combination of uilleann pipes with bouzouki and mandolin counter-melodies created the template for the modern Irish folk band, and its members seeded further influential groups: Lunny went on to form The Bothy Band and Moving Hearts, Moore became Ireland's pre-eminent folk singer, Irvine co-founded Patrick Street, and O'Flynn collaborated widely as a solo piper.
