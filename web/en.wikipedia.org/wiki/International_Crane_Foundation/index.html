International Crane Foundation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Whooping cranes at the International Crane Foundation, Baraboo, Wisconsin

The International Crane Foundation (ICF) is a non-profit conservation organisation dedicated to the conservation of cranes and the ecosystems, watersheds and flyways on which they depend. It was founded in 1973 by ornithologists George Archibald and Ron Sauey and is headquartered on a 225-acre site near Baraboo, Wisconsin, in the United States.

History

Archibald and Sauey, who met as graduate students at Cornell University, established the foundation on the Sauey family farm. ICF is notable for maintaining a captive collection that has included all fifteen of the world's crane species, and for pioneering techniques in captive breeding and reintroduction, including costume-rearing of chicks so that they do not imprint on humans.

Work

The foundation works worldwide on crane conservation, including efforts for the critically endangered Siberian crane, the whooping crane in North America, and the grey crowned crane in Africa. Its site in Wisconsin is open to visitors and displays living cranes of all fifteen species, the only place in the world where this is possible.
