Chiodos was an American post-hardcore band formed in Davison, Michigan, in 2001. Originally known as the Chiodos Bros., the group took its name from the Chiodo brothers, the special-effects artists behind the cult horror film Killer Klowns from Outer Space. The band became known for its theatrical blend of post-hardcore, emo, and progressive rock, built around the dramatic falsetto of vocalist Craig Owens and the classical-influenced keyboard work of Bradley Bell.

History

Formation and early years (2001–2004)

Chiodos formed while its members were attending high school in Davison, Michigan. After releasing early demos and the EP The Heartless Control Everything (2003), the band signed with Equal Vision Records and began touring heavily in the American Midwest and beyond.

All's Well That Ends Well and breakthrough (2005–2006)

The band's debut full-length album, All's Well That Ends Well, was released in July 2005. Driven by songs such as "Baby, You Wouldn't Last a Minute on the Creek" and "The Words 'Best Friend' Become Redefined," the record established Chiodos as a leading act in the mid-2000s post-hardcore scene. The album's literary and Shakespearean references, elaborate song titles, and piano-driven arrangements set the band apart from its peers.

Bonewars and mainstream success (2007–2009)

Bonewars, released in September 2007, debuted at number five on the Billboard 200, an unusually high chart position for the genre. Singles included "Lexington (Joey Pea-Pot with a Monkey Face)" and "The Undertaker's Thirst for Revenge Is Unquenchable." The band headlined tours and appeared on the Warped Tour and at major festivals. In 2009, Owens was dismissed from the band amid internal tensions.

Lineup changes and Illuminaudio (2010–2012)

Brandon Bolmer, formerly of Yesterday's Rising, replaced Owens for the band's third album, Illuminaudio (2010). Drummer Tanner Wayne also joined during this period. In 2012 both Bolmer and Wayne departed, and Owens returned to the group, with original drummer Derrick Frost also rejoining.

Devil and breakup (2013–2016)

Chiodos signed with Razor & Tie and released Devil in April 2014, produced by David Bottrill. Thomas Erak of The Fall of Troy joined as lead guitarist. After a period of reduced activity, Owens announced in November 2016 that the band had ended, and he continued with solo and other projects.

Musical style

Chiodos combined the aggression of post-hardcore with progressive rock structures, orchestral keyboard textures, and vaudevillian or cabaret flourishes. Owens's wide vocal range — from screams to a high tenor and falsetto — and Bell's piano parts were signature elements. Critics frequently grouped the band with contemporaries such as crabcore-era acts, Emarosa, and Dance Gavin Dance.
