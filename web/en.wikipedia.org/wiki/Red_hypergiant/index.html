Red hypergiant

Red hypergiants are extremely massive and luminous red supergiant stars, classed as luminosity class 0 or Ia+, that show evidence of very high mass-loss rates. They are among the largest stars known by volume, and among the most luminous, with extended atmospheres and circumstellar material ejected in past outbursts.

Properties

Red hypergiants have radii of roughly 1,000 solar radii or more, effective temperatures in the range of about 3,500–4,500 K, and luminosities of several hundred thousand times that of the Sun. Their spectra and infrared excesses reveal slow, dense winds and sometimes detached shells of gas and dust from earlier eruptions. Well-known examples include VY Canis Majoris, VX Sagittarii, S Persei and NML Cygni.

Evolution

Red hypergiants descend from stars with initial masses of roughly 25–40 solar masses that have evolved off the main sequence and shed much of their envelopes. Their cores are expected eventually to collapse, producing a type II supernova, possibly after evolving back toward hotter temperatures.
