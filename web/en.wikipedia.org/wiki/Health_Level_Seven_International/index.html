Health Level Seven International

Health Level Seven International (HL7) is a not-for-profit, ANSI-accredited standards developing organisation dedicated to providing a comprehensive framework and related standards for the exchange, integration, sharing and retrieval of electronic health information. It develops and maintains the HL7 family of standards, including HL7 version 2, version 3, the Clinical Document Architecture (CDA) and FHIR.

History

HL7 was founded in 1987 to develop a standard for hospital information system communication. It held its first meetings at the University of Pennsylvania. The organisation's name derives from the application layer — the seventh level — of the OSI reference model. In the 1990s the organisation internationalised, adding affiliated organisations in countries around the world, and adopted the name Health Level Seven International. Its headquarters are in Ann Arbor, Michigan, United States.

Structure and membership

HL7's members include healthcare providers, government stakeholders, payers, pharmaceutical companies, vendors, consulting firms and patients. Development work is carried out by volunteer work groups, and standards are approved through a consensus balloting process conforming to ANSI procedures. International affiliates adapt and promote the standards in their own countries and contribute requirements to the international process.

Standards

  • HL7 version 2.x — the widely implemented messaging standard for clinical and administrative data exchange.
  • HL7 version 3 — model-driven standards based on the Reference Information Model.
  • CDA — Clinical Document Architecture for structured clinical documents.
  • FHIR — Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources, a web-based interoperability standard first published in the 2010s.
  • CQL, CDS Hooks and other standards supporting clinical quality measurement and decision support.

HL7 also runs educational programmes, certification testing and implementation-a-thons ("connectathons") to test interoperability between systems.
