The Chrysler Imperial was the flagship luxury automobile of the Chrysler Corporation, introduced in 1926 and produced, with interruptions, as a Chrysler model until 1954 and again from 1990 to 1993. From 1955 to 1975 and from 1981 to 1983, Imperial was marketed as a separate marque above Chrysler, competing with Cadillac and Lincoln.

Early years

The first Imperial of 1926 established Chrysler's luxury credentials, setting speed records at Daytona. In the 1930s, the Imperial line included the streamlined Airflow models and custom-bodied cars by LeBaron and Derham. The 1940s and early 1950s Imperials offered innovations such as fully automatic transmission (via the torque-converter-based Fluid Torque Drive), power steering, power brakes, and air conditioning.

Imperial as a separate marque

From 1955, Imperial became a standalone division. Under Virgil Exner's "Forward Look" styling, the 1957–1963 Imperials featured soaring tailfins, curved glass, and the 392 and later 413 cubic-inch Hemi and wedge V8 engines. Imperials of this era are noted for their durability, torsion-bar suspension, and features like swivelling seats and the Imperial's signature free-standing headlamps of 1961–1963. A 1964 redesign by Elwood Engel produced the slab-sided, formal styling shared with contemporary Lincolns.

Later years and revival

Declining sales in the 1970s ended the Imperial marque after 1975; a badge-engineered revival appeared for 1981–1983, promoted with Frank Sinatra editions. The Chrysler Imperial name returned once more for 1990–1993 as a luxury version of the front-wheel-drive Chrysler New Yorker platform, with a V6 engine and electronic instrumentation, before being discontinued.
