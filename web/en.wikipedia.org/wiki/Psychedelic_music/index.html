Psychedelic music

Psychedelic music (sometimes called psychedelia) is a wide range of popular music styles influenced by 1960s psychedelic culture, a subculture of people who used psychedelic drugs such as LSD. It emerged in the mid-1960s among folk and rock musicians in the United States and the United Kingdom, creating the subgenres of psychedelic folk, psychedelic rock, acid rock, and psychedelic pop before declining in the early 1970s.

Characteristics

Psychedelic music often features exotic instrumentation such as sitars and tablas, electronic effects including feedback, phasing, flanging, and reversed tape, extended improvisational solos, and surreal or whimsical lyrics referencing dreams, visions, and altered states of consciousness. Studio experimentation was central, with producers treating the recording studio itself as an instrument.

History

The style crystallized around 1965–1967 with recordings by the Byrds, the Beatles (notably Revolver and Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band), the Beach Boys (Pet Sounds), Pink Floyd, Jefferson Airplane, the Grateful Dead, and the Jimi Hendrix Experience. The 1967 "Summer of Love" and festivals such as Monterey Pop brought the music to a mass audience.

Legacy

Psychedelic techniques influenced progressive rock, krautrock, funk, and electronic music. Later revivals and offshoots include neo-psychedelia, psychedelic soul, space rock, stoner rock, and the electronic dance subgenres of psychedelic trance (psytrance) and psybient.
