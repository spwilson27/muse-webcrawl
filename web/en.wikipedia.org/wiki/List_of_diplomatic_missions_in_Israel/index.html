List of diplomatic missions in Israel

This is a list of diplomatic missions in Israel. Around 90 countries maintain embassies in Israel. Because of the disputed status of Jerusalem, most embassies have historically been located in Tel Aviv and its metropolitan area, with many ambassadors residing in Herzliya. After the United States moved its embassy to Jerusalem in May 2018, a small number of countries followed, including Guatemala, Honduras, Kosovo and Papua New Guinea; several other states maintain branch offices or cultural centres in Jerusalem.

Tel Aviv

  • Argentina, Australia, Austria, Belarus, Belgium, Brazil, Bulgaria, Canada, Chile, China, Colombia, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Dominican Republic, Egypt, Estonia, Ethiopia, Finland, France, Georgia, Germany, Ghana, Greece, Hungary, India, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Latvia, Lithuania, Mexico, Moldova, Myanmar, Nepal, Netherlands, Nigeria, Norway, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Russia, Serbia, Singapore (consulate-general historically), Slovakia, Slovenia, South Korea, Spain, Sri Lanka, Sweden, Switzerland, Thailand, Turkey, Ukraine, United Kingdom, Uruguay, Uzbekistan, Vietnam, Zambia and others.

Egypt (since 1980) and Jordan (since 1994) were for many years the only Arab states with embassies; they were joined following the 2020 Abraham Accords by the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Morocco, which opened embassies or liaison offices in Tel Aviv.

Other missions

Many countries maintain consulates-general in Jerusalem — including the United Kingdom, France, Italy, Greece, Spain, Sweden, Belgium, Turkey and (historically) the United States consulate — which primarily serve the Palestinian territories and do not function as embassies to Israel. The Holy See maintains an apostolic nunciature in Tel Aviv–Jaffa and an apostolic delegation in Jerusalem. Taiwan operates the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Tel Aviv.
