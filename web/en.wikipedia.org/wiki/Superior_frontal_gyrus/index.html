Superior frontal gyrus

Part ofFrontal lobe
ArteryAnterior cerebral artery
LocationSuperior part of the frontal lobe

The superior frontal gyrus occupies the upper portion of the frontal lobe.

The superior frontal gyrus (SFG) is a gyrus — a ridge on the cerebral cortex — that forms the superior part of the frontal lobe of the human brain. It makes up about one third of the frontal lobe and is bounded laterally by the superior frontal sulcus and medially by the interhemispheric fissure.

Anatomy

The superior frontal gyrus contains parts of Brodmann areas 6, 8, 9, and 10. Its posterior portion includes medial parts of the premotor cortex, while its medial surface extends down toward the cingulate sulcus. The paracentral lobule lies on its medial surface at the posterior end.

Function

The superior frontal gyrus is implicated in higher cognitive functions, including working memory, attention, and aspects of self-awareness. Research has associated the region with laughter — electrical stimulation of a small area of the left superior frontal gyrus has been reported to evoke laughter accompanied by a sense of merriment. The gyrus also participates in motor planning through its premotor and supplementary motor connections.
