Trentino (Ladin: Trentin) is an autonomous province of Italy, officially the Autonomous Province of Trento (Italian: Provincia autonoma di Trento). Together with South Tyrol it forms the autonomous region of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol. Its capital is the city of Trento. The province covers an area of about 6,212 km² in the southern Alps and Dolomites of north-eastern Italy, with a population of roughly 540,000.

Geography

Trentino is almost entirely mountainous, crossed north to south by the valley of the Adige River. It borders Lombardy to the west, Veneto to the east and south, and South Tyrol to the north. Major valleys include the Val di Sole, Val di Non, Valsugana and Val di Fiemme; the northern shore of Lake Garda lies within the province. Notable mountain groups include the Brenta Dolomites, the Adamello-Presanella range and the Cevedale group.

History

For centuries the area was dominated by the Prince-Bishopric of Trento within the Holy Roman Empire. It passed to the Austrian Empire in the early 19th century and, as an Italian-speaking land under Habsburg rule, became a focus of Italian irredentism. It was annexed by Italy after the First World War (Treaty of Saint-Germain, 1919). After the Second World War, special autonomy statutes (1948 and 1972) granted the province broad legislative powers, making it one of Italy's most autonomous and prosperous territories.

Economy and culture

The economy combines high-quality agriculture — especially apples (Val di Non) and wine — with tourism in the Dolomites ski resorts (such as Madonna di Campiglio and Canazei) and the northern Lake Garda towns. The population is mostly Italian-speaking, with Ladin-speaking minorities in the Val di Fassa and German-speaking communities in the Val dei Mòcheni and Luserna, all enjoying protected status.
