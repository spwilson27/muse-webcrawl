The Princely House of Thurn and Taxis (German: Fürstenhaus Thurn und Taxis) is a family of German nobility that is part of the Briefadel. It was a key player in the postal services in Europe during the 16th century, until the end of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806, when it ran the Imperial Reichspost and later the Thurn-und-Taxis Post.

History

The family, originally the Tasso family of Bergamo in Lombardy, rose through the operation of postal routes for the Venetian Republic and the Habsburgs. Franz von Taxis is credited with founding the first regular postal system in Europe around 1490, connecting Innsbruck with Brussels and other imperial centres. In 1695 the family was raised to princely rank by Emperor Leopold I, and in the 18th century it moved its seat to Frankfurt and later to Regensburg, where it received St. Emmeram's Abbey as its residence.

Postal monopoly

The Thurn and Taxis postal monopoly, held as an imperial fief, made the family one of the wealthiest in Europe. After the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire, the family continued to run postal services in several German states until Prussia bought out the remaining rights in 1867.

The family today

The head of the house is Albert, 12th Prince of Thurn and Taxis. The family remains among the largest private landowners in Germany, with extensive forestry holdings, and its palace in Regensburg hosts the annual Thurn and Taxis Festival.
