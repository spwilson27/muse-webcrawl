Rajaraja I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The vimana tower of the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur at dusk]

Rajaraja I (947 – 1014), born Arulmoli Varman, was a Chola emperor (r. 985–1014) who raised the Chola dynasty of Tamil South India from a regional power to the dominant state of southern India and a maritime empire. His conquests took the Pandya and Chera countries, northern Ceylon (Sri Lanka), the Maldives and the Karnataka marches; his inscriptions — the first in India to record a king's deeds in a standardised historical introduction (meikeerthi) — document a rebuilt administration of revenue surveys, local assemblies and a powerful navy. His crowning monument, the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur (completed 1010), with its 66-metre vimana tower capped by an 80-tonne cupola, is among the supreme achievements of Indian architecture.

Conquest and administration

Rajaraja's early campaigns destroyed the combined Pandya–Ceylon fleet at Kandalur (c. 988) and annexed northern Ceylon as a Chola province administered from Polonnaruwa; the Western Chalukyas were humbled, Vengi (the eastern Deccan) made a protectorate. At home his great land survey (c. 1000) graded and assessed the Tamil country village by village — the inscriptions on his temple's walls recording grants, staff, dancers and revenues in archival detail that makes the Chola state the best-documented of early medieval India. The village assemblies (sabha, ur) whose workings his inscriptions preserve remain a byword in Indian administrative history.

The temple and the heir

The Brihadisvara (Rajarajeshwara) Temple was both devotion and manifesto: its tower the tallest in India, its bronze Nataraja tradition and frescoes (rediscovered under later Nayak paint) marking the classical peak of Tamil art. Rajaraja's son Rajendra I, associated as co-regent from 1012, extended the empire to the Ganges and Southeast Asia — the father's navy and administration made the son's audacity possible. Rajaraja's name ('king of kings') was chosen by himself; history has not corrected it.
