FIFA Women's World Cup

The FIFA Women's World Cup is an international association football competition contested by the senior women's national teams of the members of the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA), the sport's global governing body. The competition has been held every four years since the inaugural tournament in 1991.

The FIFA Women's World Cup Trophy.

History

The first tournament was held in China in 1991 and was won by the United States. The field expanded over time from 12 teams to 16 in 1999, 24 in 2015, and 32 in 2023, when the tournament was co-hosted by Australia and New Zealand and won by Spain. The United States is the most successful nation, with four titles (1991, 1999, 2015, 2019), followed by Germany with two.

Format

Qualification tournaments are held within FIFA's six continental zones. The finals tournament features group-stage matches followed by a knockout phase of the round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place match, and the final.
