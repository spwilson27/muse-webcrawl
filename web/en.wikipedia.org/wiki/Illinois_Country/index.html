Illinois Country

Illinois Country (Pays des Illinois)
RegionUpper Mississippi valley (present-day Illinois, Missouri, Indiana)
Colonial powerFrance (c. 1673–1763), then Britain and Spain
Main settlementsKaskaskia, Cahokia, Ste. Genevieve, St. Louis, Vincennes

The Illinois Country (French: Pays des Illinois) was a vast district of New France centred on the confluence of the Mississippi, Missouri and Ohio rivers, in what is now the American Midwest. Named for the Illiniwek (Illinois) peoples who inhabited it, the region was a frontier of French settlement famed for its fertile "American Bottom" floodplain, its fur trade, and its French Creole villages.

French period

French explorers Louis Jolliet and Jacques Marquette passed through in 1673, and missions and trading posts followed. Settlements at Cahokia (1699) and Kaskaskia (1703) grew into farming communities whose grain surpluses were shipped down the Mississippi to Louisiana, earning the region the name "breadbasket of Louisiana". From 1718 the Illinois Country was administered as part of French Louisiana. Its French inhabitants (habitants) practised open-field agriculture in long lots, lived under the Coutume de Paris, and held enslaved Africans and Native Americans. Fort de Chartres, rebuilt in stone in the 1750s, was the seat of government.

After 1763

By the Treaty of Paris (1763) France ceded the east bank of the Mississippi to Britain and the west bank to Spain. Many French families crossed the river to found St. Louis (1764). During the American Revolution, George Rogers Clark's Virginia militia captured Kaskaskia and Cahokia in 1778, and Virginia organised the region as Illinois County. Under the Northwest Ordinance of 1787 the area became part of the United States, though its French-speaking, Catholic communities retained their distinct culture for generations.

Legacy

The Illinois Country preserves some of the oldest European-built structures in the interior of North America, including the Cahokia Courthouse and the Church of the Holy Family, and its French colonial heritage is commemorated in the Fort de Chartres State Historic Site.
