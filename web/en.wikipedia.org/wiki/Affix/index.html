Affix

In linguistics, an affix is a morpheme that is attached to a word stem to form a new word or word form. Affixes are bound morphemes: they cannot stand alone as words. They may be derivational, creating a new lexeme (e.g. happy → unhappy, teach → teacher), or inflectional, marking grammatical categories such as tense, number or case (e.g. walk → walked, cat → cats).

Types by position

Affixes are classified by where they attach. A prefix precedes the stem (un-, re-, pre-); a suffix follows it (-ness, -ed, -ly). An infix is inserted inside a stem (rare in English, common in languages such as Tagalog); a circumfix surrounds the stem in two parts (as in the German past participle ge-...-t); and a transfix interleaves with a consonantal root, as in Semitic languages.

Distribution

Agglutinative languages such as Turkish, Finnish and Swahili make especially heavy use of affixation, stacking many affixes onto a single stem. English uses a moderate inventory, mostly prefixes and suffixes inherited from Germanic and borrowed from Latin and Greek.
