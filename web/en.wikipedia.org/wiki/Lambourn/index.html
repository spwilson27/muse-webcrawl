Lambourn is a large village and civil parish in West Berkshire, England. It lies in the valley of the River Lambourn, a chalk stream, on the Lambourn Downs within the North Wessex Downs Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The village is best known as a major centre of British horse racing training.

Geography

Lambourn is situated about 13 miles (21 km) northwest of Newbury and 11 miles (18 km) northeast of Swindon, close to the county boundary with Wiltshire and Oxfordshire. The parish includes the hamlets of Eastbury, Bockhampton, Upper Lambourn and Lambourn Woodlands. The surrounding downland, with its short springy turf on chalk, is ideal for training racehorses, and the parish has extensive gallops.

Horse racing

Lambourn is nicknamed the "Valley of the Racehorse" and, together with Newmarket, is one of the two principal centres of racehorse training in England. Horses have been trained on the downs around Lambourn since the 18th century, and the village today supports dozens of training yards and a population in which a large share of employment depends on the racing industry. Well-known trainers based in Lambourn have included Nicky Henderson and Barry Hills. There is no racecourse in Lambourn itself; trained horses travel to courses such as nearby Newbury.

History

The area has a long history of settlement, with prehistoric sites on the downs including the Lambourn Seven Barrows Bronze Age cemetery northwest of the village. The parish church of St Michael and All Angels is a substantial Norman and later building. Lambourn was a market town in the medieval period and was served by the Lambourn Valley Railway from 1898 until its closure in the 1960s; the route is recalled by the Lambourn Valley Railway heritage association and the Lambourn station site.
