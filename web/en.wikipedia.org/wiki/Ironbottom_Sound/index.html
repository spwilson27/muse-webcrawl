Ironbottom Sound

Ironbottom Sound (alternatively Iron Bottom Sound) is the name given by Allied sailors to the stretch of water at the southern end of the Slot between Guadalcanal, Savo Island and Florida Island in the Solomon Islands. The name derives from the dozens of ships and aircraft sunk there during the Guadalcanal campaign of the Second World War.

Battles

The sound was the scene of a series of fierce night surface actions in 1942, including the Battle of Savo Island, the Battle of Cape Esperance, the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal and the Battle of Tassafaronga. More than fifty major warships — American, Australian and Japanese cruisers, destroyers and transports — were lost in these waters.

Geography

Its official name is Sealark Sound. The wrecks now rest at depths of several hundred metres and have become sites of interest for historians and deep-water exploration; several, including HMAS Canberra and the Japanese battleship Kirishima, have been located by survey expeditions.
