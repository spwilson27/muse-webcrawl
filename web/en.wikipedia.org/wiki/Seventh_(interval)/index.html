Seventh (interval)

In music theory, a seventh is a musical interval encompassing seven staff positions (see interval number) — the distance between two notes whose letter names are seven apart, such as C and the B above it. Because the letter span covers seven positions, the interval is called a seventh even though the exact size in semitones varies by type.

Forms

The most common sevenths are:

  • Major seventh — 11 semitones (e.g. C to B).
  • Minor seventh — 10 semitones (e.g. C to B♭).
  • Diminished seventh — 9 semitones (e.g. C to B𝄫), enharmonically equivalent to a major sixth.
  • Augmented seventh — 12 semitones, enharmonically equivalent to an octave, and very rare in practice.

Role in harmony

The seventh above the root is a defining component of seventh chords, such as the dominant seventh chord, whose tension (particularly the tritone between the third and seventh of the chord) drives resolution in common-practice harmony. The minor seventh is also fundamental to much blues, jazz and popular music. The interval of a seventh is generally classified as a dissonance in strict counterpoint, requiring resolution, typically by step down to the sixth or, in the case of the major seventh as chord root motion, up to the octave.

The inversion of a seventh is a second: a major seventh inverts to a minor second, and a minor seventh inverts to a major second.

In tuning systems

In just intonation, the minor seventh may be tuned as 7:4 (the "harmonic seventh"), 16:9, or 9:5 depending on context, while the major seventh is commonly 15:8. In 12-tone equal temperament these are approximated by 10 and 11 semitones respectively.
