Cassini–Huygens, commonly called Cassini, was a joint NASA–ESA–ASI space mission that sent a probe to study the planet Saturn and its system of rings and moons. It comprised the NASA-built Cassini orbiter and the ESA-built Huygens lander, which separated and landed on the moon Titan.

Mission profile

Launched in 1997 aboard a Titan IVB/Centaur, Cassini used gravity assists from Venus, Earth and Jupiter before entering Saturn orbit in 2004. In January 2005, Huygens descended through Titan's atmosphere and landed on its surface — still the most distant landing ever made — returning images of rounded ice pebbles and river-like channels.

Discoveries

Cassini revealed water-ice geysers erupting from the south pole of Enceladus, evidence of a subsurface ocean there and on Titan; it mapped Titan's hydrocarbon lakes and seas, tracked giant storms on Saturn, measured the rings' composition and age, and discovered several new moons. The mission's final phase, the "Grand Finale" in 2017, threaded the gap between Saturn and its rings before the spacecraft burned up in Saturn's atmosphere to avoid contaminating potentially habitable moons.

Legacy

Over nearly 20 years, Cassini travelled about 7.9 billion kilometres and returned more than 450,000 images, transforming understanding of the Saturn system. It is named after astronomers Giovanni Domenico Cassini and Christiaan Huygens.
