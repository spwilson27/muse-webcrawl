Trichobezoar

A trichobezoar is a bezoar — a mass found trapped in the gastrointestinal system — formed from ingested hair. Because hair is indigestible and resists peristalsis, swallowed strands accumulate in the stomach, becoming matted with mucus and food into a dense, often stomach-shaped mass.

Causes and symptoms

Trichobezoars are most often associated with trichotillomania (compulsive hair pulling) and trichophagia (compulsive hair eating), and occur predominantly in young women and girls. Symptoms may include abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, early satiety, weight loss, and a palpable abdominal mass; complications include gastric ulceration, obstruction, and perforation. When a tail of hair extends from the stomach into the small intestine, the condition is known as Rapunzel syndrome, which can cause severe obstruction.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is typically by imaging (ultrasound, CT scan) and confirmed by endoscopy, which may also remove small trichobezoars. Large or complicated cases generally require surgical removal (gastrotomy or laparoscopy). Long-term treatment addresses the underlying psychiatric condition, usually with behavioural therapy such as habit reversal training, to prevent recurrence.
