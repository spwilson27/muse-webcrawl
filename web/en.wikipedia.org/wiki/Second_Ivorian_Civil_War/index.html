Second Ivorian Civil War

The Second Ivorian Civil War (2010–2011) was a civil conflict in Côte d'Ivoire (Ivory Coast) arising from the disputed presidential election of 2010. Incumbent president Laurent Gbagbo refused to step down after the Independent Electoral Commission and international observers declared his challenger, Alassane Ouattara, the winner of the November 2010 run-off election.

Background

Côte d'Ivoire had been divided since the First Ivorian Civil War of 2002–2007, with rebels holding the north and the government the south. Elections, repeatedly postponed, were finally held in 2010. After the run-off, the electoral commission declared Ouattara the winner with 54.1% of the vote, but the Constitutional Council, headed by a Gbagbo ally, annulled results from the north and proclaimed Gbagbo the winner.

Conflict

Both men claimed the presidency and formed rival governments. Fighting escalated in March 2011, when pro-Ouattara forces — the Republican Forces of Côte d'Ivoire — launched an offensive from the north and west, seizing most of the country within days, including the political capital Yamoussoukro and the port of San Pedro. Loyalist forces held on in Abidjan, where a battle for the city raged for weeks.

Fall of Gbagbo

After United Nations and French forces struck Gbagbo's heavy weapons in early April 2011, pro-Ouattara fighters stormed the presidential residence on 11 April and arrested Gbagbo. He was later transferred to the International Criminal Court in The Hague to face charges of crimes against humanity; he was acquitted in 2019, a verdict upheld on appeal in 2021. Ouattara was sworn in as president in May 2011 and has governed Côte d'Ivoire since.
