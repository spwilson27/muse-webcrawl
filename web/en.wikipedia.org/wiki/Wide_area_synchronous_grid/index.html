Wide area synchronous grid

A wide area synchronous grid (also known as an interconnection in North America) is a regional-scale or larger electric power grid in which all connected generators operate in synchrony at the same frequency, so that the entire region shares a single alternating current frequency at any instant.

The principal synchronous grids of the world

Major grids

The largest synchronous grids include the Continental Europe grid operated through ENTSO-E, serving some 450–600 million people; the Eastern Interconnection and Western Interconnection of North America; the IPS/UPS grid of Russia and neighbouring states; and China's regional grids, progressively linked by high-voltage DC lines. India unified its regional grids into a single national synchronous grid in 2013, one of the largest in the world by connected load.

Characteristics

Synchronisation allows power to be traded across vast areas and lets generators share reserves, improving efficiency and reliability, but it also means disturbances can propagate: major cascading blackouts (such as the 2003 Northeast blackout and the 2006 European disturbance) have spread within synchronous areas. Adjacent grids are often linked by high-voltage direct current (HVDC) connections, which transfer power without requiring synchrony. System operators continuously balance generation and demand to hold frequency within tight limits around 50 or 60 Hz.
