Emperor of China

The Emperor of China (Chinese: 皇帝; pinyin: Huángdì) was the title given to the sovereign ruler of imperial China from the establishment of the title by Qin Shi Huang in 221 BC until the fall of the Qing dynasty in 1912. The emperor was considered the Son of Heaven (tianzi) and ruled under the Mandate of Heaven over "all under heaven" (tianxia).

History

Before the Qin unification, the rulers of China used the titles wang (king) and, in legendary tradition, huang (sovereign) and di (divine ruler). When the king of Qin completed the unification of China in 221 BC, he combined the two archaic titles to create the new title huangdi, proclaiming himself the First Emperor (Qin Shi Huang). The title was used continuously, with interruptions during periods of division, for more than two thousand years.

Through successive dynasties — Han, Sui, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing — the emperor stood at the apex of a vast bureaucratic state staffed by scholar-officials selected increasingly through the imperial examination system. Imperial authority was in practice mediated by the civil bureaucracy, eunuchs, and the imperial family, and many emperors were figureheads; nevertheless the institution remained the symbolic center of the Chinese political order.

Power and role

The emperor was in theory an absolute ruler: lawgiver, supreme judge, commander of the armies, and chief ritual officiant of the state cult. His legitimacy depended on the Mandate of Heaven, which was understood to be forfeited by misrule, natural disasters, or successful rebellion — a doctrine that retrospectively justified dynastic change. The emperor's person was surrounded by elaborate court ritual, and the imperial colour was yellow; the imperial dragon was his emblem.

End of the institution

The last emperor, Puyi, abdicated on 12 February 1912 following the Xinhai Revolution, ending more than two millennia of imperial rule. A brief restoration attempt in 1917 lasted only twelve days. Puyi later served as the ruler of the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo and died in the People's Republic of China in 1967.
