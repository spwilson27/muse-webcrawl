Intramembranous ossification

Precursor tissueMesenchyme (no cartilage intermediate)

Intramembranous ossification is one of the two essential processes during fetal development of the gnathostome (excluding chondrichthyans such as sharks) skeletal system by which rudimentary bone tissue is created. Unlike endochondral ossification, which is the other process by which bone tissue is created, cartilage is not present during intramembranous ossification. It is also an essential process during the natural healing of bone fractures and the rudimentary formation of bones of the head.

Formation of bone tissue

Mesenchymal stem cells within the embryonic mesenchyme condense into clusters and differentiate directly into osteoblasts under the control of the transcription factor RUNX2 (CBFA1). Groups of these cells form a center of ossification, where osteoblasts secrete osteoid — an unmineralized matrix rich in type I collagen. The osteoid then calcifies as calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) is deposited, trapping some osteoblasts within the matrix, where they mature into osteocytes.

Formation of woven and lamellar bone

The first bone laid down is immature woven bone, a network of randomly oriented trabeculae (spicules) that grows outward from the ossification center. Continued appositional growth and remodeling by osteoblasts and osteoclasts replace woven bone with organized lamellar bone. In flat bones, this produces the adult structure of two compact cortical plates (the inner and outer tables of the skull) sandwiching spongy diploë.

Bones formed

  • Flat bones of the skull: frontal, parietal, and parts of the occipital and temporal bones
  • Facial bones, including the maxilla and most of the mandible
  • The medial part of the clavicle (the lateral part forms endochondrally)

Clinical significance

Defects in intramembranous ossification cause several skeletal dysplasias. Haploinsufficiency of RUNX2 produces cleidocranial dysplasia, with open fontanelles, hypoplastic clavicles, and dental abnormalities. The growing margins of intramembranous skull bones meet at sutures and fontanelles, whose premature fusion (craniosynostosis) restricts skull growth.
