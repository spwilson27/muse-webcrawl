Death growl

A death growl, also known as simply a growl or occasionally as death metal vocals, is a vocal style most commonly employed by death metal vocalists, and also used in other styles of heavy metal music such as grindcore, metalcore, and doom metal. Death growls are sometimes criticized for being difficult to decipher, but they provide an aesthetic and sonic extremity that is a defining characteristic of death metal.

Growls can be obtained with various voice effects, but the primary technique is generally a form of guttural, low-pitched vocalization produced using the false vocal folds and other structures of the throat rather than the true vocal cords alone. Growls have been used for a variety of effects in music beyond metal, from the early rock and roll recordings of artists such as Howlin' Wolf to novelty and avant-garde works.

Technique

Growling is produced by vibrating the false vocal folds (vestibular folds) together with adducted true vocal cords and a lowered larynx, allowing a deep, rumbling tone. Vocalists commonly describe shaping the mouth and tongue to colour the sound, and the pitch of a growl can be raised or lowered substantially. Because the technique places strain on the throat, performers often emphasize warming up, hydration, and proper breath support to avoid damage. Some singers use cupped hands or microphone technique to further deepen the perceived tone.

History

Precursors of the death growl can be heard in early blues and rock music, where singers such as Howlin' Wolf used rough, growled delivery, and in the shrieked and snarled vocals of early heavy metal and hardcore punk. In the 1980s, bands such as Possessed, Death, and Morbid Angel pushed extreme metal vocals toward a lower, more bestial register. Vocalists such as Chuck Schuldiner, David Vincent, and Glen Benton helped popularize the style. Kam Lee of Massacre and Jeff Becerra of Possessed are often cited among its earliest practitioners.

Styles and variations

  • Low growl — a deep, guttural register typical of brutal death metal.
  • Mid-range growl or scream — common in melodic death metal and metalcore.
  • Pig squeal — an extreme, high-pitched squealed variant associated with deathcore and goregrind.
  • Guttural "bree bree" — an inhaled or exhaled squealing technique used in slam death metal.

Growled vocals are often contrasted with clean singing in genres such as melodic death metal and gothic metal, where a "beauty and the beast" pairing of a female soprano and a male growler became a recognized arrangement.
