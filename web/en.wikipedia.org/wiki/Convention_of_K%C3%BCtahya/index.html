Convention of Kütahya

The Convention of Kütahya, also known as the Peace of Kütahya, was an agreement signed in May 1833 that ended the Egyptian–Ottoman War of 1831–1833. It was concluded between the Ottoman Empire and Muhammad Ali of Egypt after Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha had advanced deep into Anatolia, defeating the Ottoman army at the Battle of Konya in December 1832.

Terms

Under the convention, Muhammad Ali received Syria and the district of Adana in Anatolia, in addition to his existing rule over Egypt, the Hejaz, and Crete. Ibrahim Pasha was confirmed as governor of Syria. In return, the Egyptian forces withdrew from the remaining Ottoman territory and Muhammad Ali formally remained a vassal of Sultan Mahmud II.

Background

Muhammad Ali had demanded Syria as compensation for his assistance to the Sultan during the Greek War of Independence. When the demand was refused, he invaded Syria in 1831. Egyptian victories alarmed the European powers; Russia intervened diplomatically and militarily, leading to the Treaty of Hünkâr İskelesi later in 1833.

Aftermath

The settlement left the underlying rivalry unresolved. Tensions over Syria and Adana led to a second Egyptian–Ottoman war in 1839, which ended with European intervention and the Convention of London in 1840.
