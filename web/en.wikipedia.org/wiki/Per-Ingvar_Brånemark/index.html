Per-Ingvar Brånemark

Per-Ingvar Brånemark (3 May 1929 – 20 December 2014) was a Swedish physician and research professor, widely regarded as the father of modern dental implantology. While studying blood circulation in bone at Lund University in the 1950s, he discovered that titanium chambers implanted in bone became permanently fused to the living tissue — a phenomenon he named osseointegration.

Recognising the clinical potential of this discovery, Brånemark developed titanium dental implants anchored directly in the jawbone. The first patient, Gösta Larsson, received such implants in 1965 and used them successfully for the rest of his life. After initial professional scepticism, the technique was accepted internationally, and Brånemark's system transformed restorative dentistry.

The osseointegration principle was later applied beyond dentistry to bone-anchored hearing aids, facial prostheses, and limb prosthetics for amputees. Brånemark received numerous honours, including the European Inventor Award for lifetime achievement in 2011.
