Chinese goose

TypeDomestic goose breed
AncestorSwan goose (Anser cygnoides)
FeaturesProminent basal knob on the bill; long, upright neck
Color varietiesBrown (grey) and white

The Chinese goose is a breed of domestic goose descended from the wild swan goose (Anser cygnoides). It is recognizable by the prominent knob at the base of its bill and its long, slender, swan-like neck held very upright.

Characteristics

Chinese geese are among the most prolific layers of the domestic geese, with females capable of laying 50–100 eggs in a season. They are active foragers, valued as "weeder geese" in agriculture, and are notably vocal, making them effective watch animals.

History

The breed was developed in China and reached Europe and North America by the 18th and 19th centuries; George Washington kept Chinese geese at Mount Vernon. It is recognized in brown and white varieties by poultry associations.
