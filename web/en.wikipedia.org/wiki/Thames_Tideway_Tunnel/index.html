Thames Tideway Tunnel


Inside the Tideway "super sewer" during construction

The Thames Tideway Tunnel, marketed as the Tideway and nicknamed the "super sewer", is a 25-kilometre (16 mi) deep-level sewer tunnel running under the tidal section (Tideway) of the River Thames through central London. It was built to capture the tens of millions of tonnes of untreated sewage that overflow into the Thames each year from London's Victorian combined sewer system.

Background

London's sewer network, designed by Joseph Bazalgette after the Great Stink of 1858, was built for a population of about 4 million. With the city's population having more than doubled, even light rainfall caused the combined sewers to discharge diluted raw sewage through combined sewer overflows into the river. Following years of study and pressure including the threat of EU fines, the tunnel scheme was approved in 2014.

Construction

Construction began in 2016. The main tunnel is 7.2 m in internal diameter, running from Acton in the west to Abbey Mills in the east at depths of roughly 35 m to 65 m, following the river for much of its course. It connects 34 of the most polluting combined sewer overflows and transfers flows to the Lee Tunnel and Beckton Sewage Treatment Works. Tunnel boring machines named after notable women — including Millicent, Ursula, Rachel and Selina — excavated the drives. Tunnelling was completed in 2022, and the system entered operation in 2024–25.

Delivery and finance

The tunnel was delivered by Bazalgette Tunnel Limited, trading as Tideway, a special-purpose company owned by a consortium of investors, under a licence from Ofwat. The costs, initially estimated at £4.2 billion, are recovered through customer bills.
