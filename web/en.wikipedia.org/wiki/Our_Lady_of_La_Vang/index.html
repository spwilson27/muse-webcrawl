Our Lady of La Vang

Our Lady of La Vang (Vietnamese: Đức Mẹ La Vang) refers to a reported Marian apparition at a time when Catholics were persecuted and killed in Vietnam. The Catholic Church in Vietnam believes the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared in 1798 in La Vang, a forest area near Quảng Trị, to comfort persecuted Christians who had fled there.

According to tradition, the apparition appeared as a lady in an áo dài holding a child, flanked by two angels, and told the faithful to boil leaves from the surrounding trees for medicine. The name La Vang is associated with the cries heard in the forest or with the leaves used for healing.

A basilica and pilgrimage centre now stand at the site. Our Lady of La Vang is regarded as the patroness of Vietnamese Catholics, and shrines to her exist throughout the Vietnamese diaspora, including major pilgrimage celebrations in the United States.
