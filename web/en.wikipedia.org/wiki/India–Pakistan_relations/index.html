India–Pakistan relations are the bilateral ties between the Republic of India and the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. Since both nations gained independence from British rule in August 1947, their relationship has been shaped by the violent Partition of India, the disputed territory of Kashmir, and a series of wars and crises. Both states are nuclear powers.

Partition and the first war

The Radcliffe Line divided British India into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan amid mass migration and killings that displaced perhaps 15 million people. The princely state of Jammu and Kashmir's accession to India in October 1947 triggered the first war, ended by a UN-brokered ceasefire that left Kashmir divided along what became the Line of Control.

Later wars and crises

Full-scale war returned in 1965, again over Kashmir, and in 1971, when India intervened in East Pakistan's civil war, leading to the creation of Bangladesh. The 1999 Kargil conflict followed within a year of both states' nuclear tests. Terrorist attacks — including the 2001 Parliament attack and the 2008 Mumbai attacks — repeatedly derailed dialogue, as did the 2019 Pulwama–Balakot crisis.

Water, trade, and culture

Despite hostility, the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 has survived wars, allocating the rivers of the Indus system. Cross-border trade remains small relative to potential; cricket matches and Bollywood and Pakistani music retain large cross-border audiences. Periodic peace initiatives — bus services, the composite dialogue, and backchannel diplomacy — have alternated with freezes in relations.
