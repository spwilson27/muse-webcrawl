Flogging

Flogging, flagellation, or whipping is the act of beating the human body with special implements such as whips, rods, switches, the cat o' nine tails, or the sjambok. Typically it is imposed on an unwilling subject as a punishment; alternatively it can be undergone voluntarily, as in religious self-flagellation or in consensual sadomasochistic activity. Flogging was one of the most common corporal punishments in history, used in judicial, military, naval, penal, educational, and domestic settings.

History

Whipping appears in ancient Egyptian, Persian, Greek, and Roman practice; in Rome, flagellation with the flagrum was a preliminary to crucifixion and a punishment for slaves and soldiers. Navies made especially heavy use of it: the Royal Navy's cat o' nine tails was administered for breaches of discipline until its suspension in the nineteenth century, and the United States Navy abolished flogging in 1850. Armies likewise abandoned it during the nineteenth century (Britain in 1881). In many slave societies, whipping was the standard instrument of coercion, and its brutality is extensively documented in abolitionist literature.

Modern practice

Judicial corporal punishment by flogging or caning is retained in a number of countries, including Singapore and Malaysia (caning), Brunei, and states applying Islamic criminal law where flogging is prescribed as a hudud or ta'zir punishment, such as Saudi Arabia, Iran, and Aceh in Indonesia. International human rights bodies classify judicial flogging as cruel, inhuman, or degrading punishment contrary to the Convention against Torture and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, and campaign for its abolition.

Religious practice

Self-flagellation has been practiced in some Christian traditions — notoriously by the medieval Flagellant movement during the Black Death, and still, in subdued form, by some penitential confraternities — and in Shia Islam during Ashura commemorations, though many religious authorities discourage or forbid self-harm.

Medical effects

Flogging can cause lacerations, hematomas, infection, nerve damage, and, with severe beatings, kidney failure from muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) and death.
