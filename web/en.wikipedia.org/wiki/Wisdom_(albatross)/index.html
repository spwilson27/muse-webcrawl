Wisdom is a wild female Laysan albatross (Phoebastria immutabilis) and the oldest known wild bird in the world, as well as the oldest confirmed banded bird. She nests on Midway Atoll in the North Pacific Ocean. Wisdom was first banded in 1956 by the biologist Chandler Robbins as an adult of breeding age — at least five or six years old at the time — which places her hatching around 1951 or earlier.

Wisdom has become famous for continuing to return to Midway each breeding season and successfully raising chicks well into her seventies, an extraordinary span for a wild bird. She is estimated to have flown millions of miles over the Pacific during her lifetime and to have raised dozens of chicks; Laysan albatrosses mate for life, and Wisdom has outlived several mates. Among her best-known mates was Akeakamai, with whom she nested for many years.

Biography

Wisdom was banded by Chandler Robbins of the US Fish and Wildlife Service in 1956 at the Midway Atoll naval air station colony. Robbins encountered her again in 2002 when re-banding birds at Midway, recognizing the original band number, by which time she had become a scientific celebrity. She survived the 2011 Tōhoku tsunami, which killed large numbers of nesting albatrosses on Midway.

Wisdom's returns and breeding successes have been regularly documented by the US Fish and Wildlife Service, which issues announcements when she arrives at Midway each autumn. In December 2024, at an estimated age of at least 74, Wisdom was observed with a new mate and laid an egg — making headlines worldwide as the oldest known wild bird to lay an egg. The egg subsequently hatched, and the chick became Wisdom's latest recorded offspring.

Significance

Wisdom serves as an emblem of seabird longevity and resilience, and of the value of long-term bird banding studies: her known history exists only because Robbins banded her in 1956 and because banding at Midway has continued since. Her story has been the subject of children's books, including "Wisdom, the Midway Albatross" by Darcy Pattison. At the same time, the colony she belongs to faces threats from plastic pollution, lead paint contamination, introduced predators, and sea-level rise.
