True Grit is a 2010 American Western film directed, written, produced, and edited by the Coen brothers. It is the second adaptation of Charles Portis's 1968 novel of the same name, which was previously filmed in 1969 starring John Wayne. The film stars Jeff Bridges as Deputy U.S. Marshal Reuben J. "Rooster" Cogburn and Hailee Steinfeld as Mattie Ross, with Matt Damon, Josh Brolin, and Barry Pepper in supporting roles.

Plot

Fourteen-year-old Mattie Ross arrives in Fort Smith, Arkansas, to claim the body of her father, who was murdered by his hired hand, Tom Chaney. Determined to see Chaney captured and hanged, Mattie hires the hard-drinking, one-eyed marshal Rooster Cogburn — because, she is told, he has "true grit" — and insists on accompanying him into the Choctaw Nation. They are joined by Texas Ranger LaBoeuf, who is pursuing Chaney for another murder. The pursuit leads to a confrontation with the outlaw gang of "Lucky" Ned Pepper, in whose company Chaney rides.

Production

The Coens described their film as a more faithful adaptation of the novel, which is narrated by Mattie in old age, than the 1969 version. Steinfeld, aged 13 at casting, was chosen from an open casting call attended by some 15,000 girls. Filming took place in New Mexico and Texas in 2010.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing more than $252 million worldwide. It was nominated for ten Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for Bridges, and Best Supporting Actress for Steinfeld, though it won none. It is regarded as one of the most acclaimed Westerns of the 21st century.
