The Jeep CJ (for "Civilian Jeep") is a series of small, open-body off-road vehicles built from 1945 to 1986. Descended directly from the World War II Willys MB, the CJ line established the civilian Jeep as an icon of American motoring and the template for the modern SUV.

Models

  • CJ-2A (1945–49): the first civilian Jeep, essentially a militarized MB with tailgate and side-mounted spare.
  • CJ-3A (1949–53) and CJ-3B (1953–68): the high-hood 3B accommodated the taller F-head Hurricane engine.
  • CJ-5 (1955–83): the longest-running model, with rounded fenders and a longer wheelbase; over 600,000 built.
  • CJ-6 (1956–75): long-wheelbase CJ-5.
  • CJ-7 (1976–86): longer wheelbase, plastic moulded dashboard, and optional automatic transmission and Quadra-Trac full-time four-wheel drive.
  • CJ-8 Scrambler (1981–85): pickup variant of the CJ-7.

American Motors replaced the CJ with the square-headlamp Jeep Wrangler (YJ) in 1987.
