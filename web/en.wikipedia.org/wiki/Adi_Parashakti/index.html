Adi Parashakti

TraditionShaktism (Hinduism)
Meaning"Primordial Supreme Power"
TextsDevi Bhagavata Purana, Devi Mahatmya, Lalita Sahasranama
ManifestationsDurga, Kali, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Tripura Sundari

Adi Parashakti (Sanskrit: आदि पराशक्ति, "Primordial Supreme Power") is the supreme goddess in the Shakta tradition of Hinduism. She is regarded as the ultimate reality — the original, primordial energy from which the universe and all deities arise. In Shaktism, she is identified with Mahadevi, the great goddess who encompasses all other goddesses as her forms and manifestations.

Theology

According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, before creation there existed only Adi Parashakti, a formless, divine energy. From her emerged the Trimurti — Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva — together with their consorts Saraswati, Lakshmi, and Parvati, who are themselves her manifestations. She is described as both nirguna (without attributes) in her ultimate aspect and saguna (with attributes) when she assumes form for the sake of her devotees.

Forms

Adi Parashakti manifests in countless forms, including the fierce goddesses Durga and Kali, the benevolent Lakshmi and Saraswati, and Tripura Sundari, who is central to the Sri Vidya tradition. In the Lalita Sahasranama, the thousand names of the goddess celebrate her as the mother of the universe, the destroyer of demons, and the bestower of liberation (moksha).

Worship

Devotees worship Adi Parashakti in temples across India, especially during Navaratri, when her nine forms (Navadurga) are honoured. Important centers of her worship include the Shakti Peethas, sites associated with the fallen body parts of the goddess Sati.
