Summer solstice

Summer solstice
Also calledMidsummer; June solstice (Northern Hemisphere)
Date20 or 21 June (Northern Hemisphere); 21 or 22 December (Southern Hemisphere)
SignificanceLongest day and shortest night of the year; beginning of astronomical summer
Related toWinter solstice, equinoxes, Midsummer festivals

The summer solstice is the solstice on which the Sun reaches its highest position in the sky and the day with the longest period of daylight occurs. It happens twice yearly, once in each hemisphere: around 20–21 June in the Northern Hemisphere, when the North Pole is tilted most directly toward the Sun, and around 21–22 December in the Southern Hemisphere. At the summer solstice the Sun's declination reaches its extreme (+23.44° in June), and the Sun appears at its northernmost (or, in December, southernmost) rising and setting points on the horizon.

Astronomy

Solstices result from Earth's axial tilt of about 23.44° relative to its orbital plane. On the June solstice, the Sun stands directly over the Tropic of Cancer at local noon; within the Arctic Circle there are 24 hours of daylight (the "midnight sun"), while the Antarctic Circle experiences polar night. Although the solstice brings the longest day, the warmest weather typically lags by several weeks because land and oceans take time to heat up — a phenomenon called seasonal lag.

Cultural significance

Solstices have been marked since the Neolithic era; monuments such as Stonehenge and the Goseck circle are aligned to solstitial sunrise or sunset. The June solstice anchors Midsummer celebrations across northern Europe — Swedish Midsommar, Finnish Juhannus, Danish Sankt Hans, and Baltic Jāņi — traditionally involving bonfires, maypoles, dancing and gatherings through the bright night. The Christian feast of Saint John the Baptist (24 June) absorbed many of these customs. In East Asia the solstice (Chinese xiàzhì) is one of the 24 solar terms.

Names and dates

The word "solstice" derives from Latin sol (sun) and sistere (to stand still), describing the apparent pause in the Sun's daily movement along the horizon before it reverses direction. The exact date varies between 20 and 22 June because of the difference between the calendar year and the tropical year, corrected by leap years.
