Irrigation scheduling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Centre-pivot irrigation on a field

Irrigation scheduling is the process of determining when to irrigate crops and how much water to apply. Good scheduling aims to supply water in the right amount at the right time to maximise yield and quality while minimising water use, energy costs, nutrient leaching and waterlogging.

Methods

Scheduling methods include: soil-moisture monitoring with tensiometers, capacitance probes or neutron probes; plant-based indicators such as canopy temperature or stem water potential; and weather-based water-balance ("checkbook") approaches, in which crop evapotranspiration is estimated from reference evapotranspiration and crop coefficients, and the soil water deficit is tracked and replenished before it stresses the crop.

Practice

Modern scheduling increasingly uses automated sensors, telemetry and decision-support software, and integrates with deficit-irrigation strategies that deliberately allow mild stress at drought-tolerant growth stages to save water. Scheduling is a central element of precision agriculture and of water management under scarcity.
