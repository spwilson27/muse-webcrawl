The Shogakukan Manga Award (小学館漫画賞, Shōgakukan Mangashō) is one of Japan's most prestigious awards for serialized manga. Sponsored by the publisher Shogakukan, it has been presented annually since 1956, initially for works published in the previous year. Winners receive a statuette and a cash prize of one million yen.

Categories

The award originally recognised general works, and dedicated categories developed over time. The current structure comprises the children's category (kodomo), boys' category (shōnen), girls' category (shōjo), and general category. Special prizes are occasionally awarded for lifetime achievement or works that do not fit the regular categories. Winning works need not be published by Shogakukan itself.

Notable winners

Classic winners include Osamu Tezuka's Astro Boy era works and later titles such as Doraemon by Fujiko F. Fujio; Urusei Yatsura and Ranma ½ by Rumiko Takahashi; Detective Conan by Gosho Aoyama; 20th Century Boys by Naoki Urasawa; Fullmetal Alchemist by Hiromu Arakawa (shōnen category, 2003); and in recent years titles such as Mob Psycho 100, Kaguya-sama: Love Is War, and Frieren: Beyond Journey's End.
