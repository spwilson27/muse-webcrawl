Sunset

Sunset
DefinitionMoment the trailing limb of the Sun disappears below the horizon in the evening
Preceded byGolden hour, afternoon
Followed byCivil, nautical and astronomical twilight (dusk)

Sunset (or sundown) is the disappearance of the Sun below the horizon of the Earth (or other astronomical object in the Solar System) due to its rotation. As viewed from everywhere on Earth, equinox sunset is centred at the cardinal direction of west, while at other times of year it shifts north or south of west depending on latitude and season.

Astronomical definition

The time of sunset is defined in astronomy as the moment when the trailing edge (upper limb) of the Sun's disk disappears below the horizon. Because atmospheric refraction raises the apparent position of the Sun by about 0.6°, the Sun is already geometrically below the horizon when it visually sets. Sunset is followed by twilight in reverse order to dawn: civil, nautical and astronomical dusk, after which full night begins.

Colours

At sunset, sunlight travels through a far greater thickness of atmosphere than at noon, and Rayleigh scattering removes most of the blue light, leaving the characteristic red, orange and pink hues (sometimes called alpenglow on mountains). Clouds, dust, pollution and volcanic aerosols can intensify or alter these colours. Rarely, a green flash may be visible at the final instant of sunset over a clear ocean horizon.

Variation and occurrence

The earliest sunset of the year occurs before the winter solstice (in early December for the Northern Hemisphere) because of the equation of time. In polar regions there may be no sunset for weeks in summer (the midnight sun) or no sunrise in winter. Sunset timing is essential in many religious calendars: it marks the start of the day in the Jewish and Islamic traditions, the breaking of the Ramadan fast at Maghrib, and the observance of the Sabbath in Judaism.
