Bergen County is the most populous county in the U.S. state of New Jersey. Located in the northeastern corner of the state, directly across the Hudson River from Manhattan, it forms part of the New York metropolitan area. As of the 2020 census, the county had a population of 955,732. Its county seat is Hackensack.

History

Bergen County was created in 1683 as one of the four original counties of the Province of East Jersey, and took its name from the Bergen township settlement, among the earliest European settlements in New Jersey, itself probably named after Bergen in the Netherlands or Bergen op Zoom. The county was a strategically contested area during the American Revolutionary War; Fort Lee, on the Palisades above the Hudson, was evacuated in November 1776 during Washington's retreat across New Jersey.

Geography

The county covers about 247 square miles, of which roughly 234 are land. The eastern border is formed by the Hudson River and the cliffs of the Palisades; the western part of the county rises into the foothills of the Ramapo Mountains. The Hackensack and Passaic rivers drain much of the county. Bergen County contains 70 municipalities, more than any other New Jersey county, including the boroughs famous for their "boroughitis" era of incorporations in the 1890s.

Transportation and economy

The George Washington Bridge, connecting Fort Lee to Manhattan, is among the busiest motor-vehicle bridges in the world. The county is also served by the New Jersey Turnpike, the Garden State Parkway, and several NJ Transit rail lines. It is a major retail centre — Paramus hosts several large shopping malls — and is known for its blue laws restricting Sunday retail sales, retained in much of the county and throughout Paramus especially.
