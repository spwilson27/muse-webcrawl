Andrew Cuomo

Andrew Mark Cuomo (born December 6, 1957) is an American politician and lawyer who served as the 56th governor of New York from 2011 until his resignation in 2021. A member of the Democratic Party, he previously served as the United States Secretary of Housing and Urban Development from 1997 to 2001 under President Bill Clinton, and as attorney general of New York from 2007 to 2010. He is the eldest son of Mario Cuomo, the 52nd governor of New York.

Early life and education

Cuomo was born in Queens, New York City, to Mario Cuomo and Matilda (née Raffa). He attended Fordham University, graduating in 1979, and earned his Juris Doctor from Albany Law School in 1982. He worked on his father's successful 1982 campaign for governor and afterward served as an unpaid advisor and campaign manager in his father's later campaigns.

Housing career and HUD

In 1986 Cuomo founded Housing Enterprise for the Less Privileged (HELP), an organization providing housing and services to homeless families. In 1993 he joined the Clinton administration as assistant secretary of Housing and Urban Development for community planning and development, and in 1997 he was confirmed as secretary of HUD, a post he held until the end of the Clinton presidency in 2001. At HUD he pressed for enforcement of fair housing laws and campaigned against gun violence by firearms manufacturers.

Attorney general of New York

After a failed 2002 bid for the Democratic nomination for governor, Cuomo was elected attorney general of New York in 2006 and took office in 2007. His tenure included investigations of student loan industry practices, the pollution of the state pension fund by pay-to-play schemes, and the 2008 financial crisis bonuses at Merrill Lynch and AIG.

Governor of New York

Cuomo was elected governor in 2010 and re-elected in 2014 and 2018. His administration legalized same-sex marriage in New York with the Marriage Equality Act of 2013, passed the SAFE Act gun control law, raised the state minimum wage toward $15, enacted paid family leave, and launched large infrastructure projects including the replacement Tappan Zee Bridge (named for his father) and the renovation of LaGuardia Airport. His daily televised briefings during the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 won national attention and an Emmy Award, though his administration later faced criticism over nursing home policies and the reporting of COVID-19 deaths.

Resignation and later life

In August 2021, following a report by Attorney General Letitia James concluding that he had sexually harassed numerous women, Cuomo announced his resignation, effective August 24, 2021. He was succeeded by Lieutenant Governor Kathy Hochul. Criminal charges related to one allegation were later dropped. In 2025, Cuomo ran for mayor of New York City, losing the Democratic primary to Zohran Mamdani.
