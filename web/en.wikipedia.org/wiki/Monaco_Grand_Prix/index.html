The Monaco Grand Prix (French: Grand Prix de Monaco) is a Formula One motor racing event held annually on the Circuit de Monaco, in late May or early June. Run since 1929, it is widely considered to be one of the most important and prestigious automobile races in the world, and is one of the races — along with the Indianapolis 500 and the 24 Hours of Le Mans — that form the Triple Crown of Motorsport.

The circuit

The circuit has been called "an exceptional location of glamour and prestige". It runs through the streets of Monte Carlo and La Condamine, past Monaco Harbour. The tight, twisting course, with its elevation changes, the tunnel section, and famous corners such as Sainte-Dévote, Casino Square, Mirabeau, the Grand Hotel Hairpin, Rascasse and the Swimming Pool complex, makes it one of the most demanding tracks in Formula One; overtaking is notoriously difficult, so qualifying position is crucial. Nelson Piquet famously likened driving the circuit to "riding a bicycle in your living room".

History

The first race was held in 1929, organised by Antony Noghès under the auspices of the Automobile Club de Monaco, and won by William Grover-Williams in a Bugatti. The event became part of the Formula One World Championship in 1950. Graham Hill became known as "Mr. Monaco" for his five wins in the 1960s, a record later surpassed by Ayrton Senna, who won six times, including five consecutively from 1989 to 1993. Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton are among the multiple winners since.
