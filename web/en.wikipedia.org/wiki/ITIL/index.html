ITIL

ITIL (formerly the Information Technology Infrastructure Library) is a framework of best practices for delivering IT services. It provides a systematic approach to IT service management (ITSM) that can help organizations manage risk, strengthen customer relations, establish cost-effective practices and build stable IT environments. ITIL was originally developed by the UK government's Central Computer and Telecommunications Agency (CCTA) in the 1980s; since 2021 it has been owned by PeopleCert.

History

The first version of ITIL consisted of a library of dozens of books covering specific areas of ITSM practice. ITIL v2 (2000–2006) consolidated the guidance into two core volumes, Service Support and Service Delivery. ITIL v3 (2007, refreshed in 2011) organized practice around a service lifecycle of five stages: service strategy, service design, service transition, service operation and continual service improvement. ITIL 4, released in 2019, replaced the lifecycle with a service value system and value chain, adding a greater focus on agile, Lean and DevOps ways of working, and a set of 34 management practices.

Key processes and practices

Well-known ITIL practices include incident management, problem management, change enablement (change management), service level management, service catalogue management, availability management, capacity management and IT service continuity management. ITIL also popularised concepts such as the service desk, known error database and configuration management database (CMDB).

Certification

Individuals can be certified at Foundation, Practitioner, Intermediate, Expert and Master levels under ITIL v3, and Foundation through Managing Professional and Strategic Leader streams under ITIL 4. Organizations themselves are not certified to ITIL, though the related ISO/IEC 20000 standard provides an auditable specification for ITSM.
