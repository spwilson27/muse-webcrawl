Thesis

For other uses, see Thesis (disambiguation). For the closely related doctoral document, see Dissertation.


A bound thesis deposited in a university library after a successful examination.

A thesis (plural theses), or dissertation (abbreviated diss.), is a document submitted in support of candidature for an academic degree or professional qualification, presenting the author's original research and findings. In some contexts the word "thesis" or a cognate is used for part of a bachelor's or master's course, while "dissertation" is normally applied to a doctorate. This usage varies between countries and universities.

The word derives from the Greek θέσις (thésis), "something put forth", an intellectual proposition; the related term dissertation comes from the Latin dissertātiō, "discussion".

Structure and composition

Although conventions differ by discipline and institution, a research thesis commonly contains:

  • a title page and abstract;
  • an introduction setting out the research questions or hypotheses;
  • a literature review situating the work in existing scholarship;
  • a description of methodology;
  • presentation of results or findings;
  • analysis and discussion;
  • conclusions, with a bibliography or list of references.

Required lengths vary enormously: an undergraduate thesis may run to some tens of pages, a master's thesis typically to around a hundred, and a doctoral thesis may extend to several hundred pages. Increasingly, theses may be presented "by publication", incorporating published journal articles as chapters, or as a monograph.

Examination

Assessment usually involves an examination committee drawn from the awarding institution and external experts. For doctoral degrees this commonly includes a public defence or oral examination (viva voce), in which the candidate answers questions about the thesis and the wider field. Outcomes range from unconditional acceptance, through passes subject to minor or major corrections, to resubmission or failure. Once accepted, copies of the thesis are typically deposited in the university library and in national or subject repositories, many of them now openly accessible online.

Terminology by country

  • United Kingdom and Commonwealth: "dissertation" is often used for the extended project of a bachelor's or taught master's degree, and "thesis" for the research doctorate (PhD).
  • United States: the usage is reversed — "dissertation" is the doctoral document, while "thesis" more often denotes the master's-level work.
  • Germany: the doctoral work is the Dissertation or Doktorarbeit; a Habilitationsschrift is required for the further qualification of habilitation.
  • France: the thèse de doctorat is publicly defended (soutenance) before a jury.

History

The practice descends from the disputations of medieval universities, in which a candidate defended a set of theses against opponents before the assembled masters. With the rise of the research university in the 19th century — notably the Berlin model and the PhD degree developed in Germany and the United States — the written thesis became the principal evidence of a candidate's capacity for independent original research.
