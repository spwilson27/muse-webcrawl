Dimension theory (algebra)

Dimension theory in algebra, specifically commutative algebra, is the study of the dimension of commutative rings and modules, above all the Krull dimension: the supremum of the lengths of chains of prime ideals.

Main results

For a Noetherian local ring, Krull's Hauptidealsatz (principal ideal theorem) states that a minimal prime over an ideal generated by n elements has height at most n. Dimension theory relates the dimension of a local ring to its system of parameters and to the Hilbert–Samuel function. Regular local rings are catenary, universally catenary, and Cohen–Macaulay.

Examples

A field has dimension 0; the integers have dimension 1; a polynomial ring in n variables over a field has dimension n. Affine varieties inherit dimension from their coordinate rings, linking the algebraic theory to the geometric dimension of algebraic varieties.
