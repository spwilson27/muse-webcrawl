Justice Against Sponsors of Terrorism Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: United States Capitol]

The Justice Against Sponsors of Terrorism Act (JASTA) is a United States federal law enacted in 2016 that narrows sovereign immunity protections for foreign states in American courts. It allows victims of terrorist attacks on U.S. soil to sue foreign governments alleged to have supported the attacks, even where the state is not officially designated a state sponsor of terrorism.

Legislative history

JASTA was supported strongly by families of victims of the September 11 attacks, who wished to pursue claims against Saudi Arabia. President Barack Obama vetoed the bill in September 2016, warning of reciprocal risks to the United States abroad, but Congress overrode the veto — the only override of Obama's presidency — by overwhelming margins in both chambers.

Effects

The Act amended the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act and the Anti-Terrorism Act, enabling consolidated 9/11 litigation against Saudi Arabia to proceed in federal court in New York. It prompted debate about sovereign immunity principles and foreign relations.
