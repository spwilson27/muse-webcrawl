Connaught Tunnel

The Connaught Tunnel is a railway tunnel in the Royal Docks area of east London, England, passing beneath the Connaught Passage between the Royal Victoria Dock and the Royal Albert Dock. It was built in 1878 for the North Woolwich line's approach to the docks and is about 550 metres (roughly 600 yards) long. The tunnel takes its name from the passage of water it crosses under, close to the later Connaught Bridge and ExCeL London exhibition centre.

History

The tunnel was constructed by cut-and-cover and immersed works beneath the dock passage, which linked the two great dock basins of the Port of London, allowing shipping to pass between them above the railway. It carried passenger services between Stratford, North Woolwich and (later) the branch serving the docks area for well over a century as part of what became the North London line. The line through the tunnel closed to passengers in 2006 when the Stratford–North Woolwich section was shut to make way for new rail projects, including the extension of the Docklands Light Railway.

Reuse by Crossrail

The disused tunnel was refurbished and brought back into use by Crossrail. Because the structure had been submerged in places and its lining was in poor condition, engineers surveyed it by draining and laser-scanning, then strengthened the tunnel by filling sections of the surrounding ground and enlarging the bore — including cutting away the crown and lining — to accommodate the larger Elizabeth line trains and their overhead electrification. It is notable that, exceptionally for the Crossrail project, Victorian infrastructure was reused rather than bored anew. Connaught Tunnel now carries Elizabeth line services between Custom House and Woolwich on the Abbey Wood branch, which opened in 2022.
