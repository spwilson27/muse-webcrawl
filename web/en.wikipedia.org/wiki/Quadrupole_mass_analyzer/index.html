Quadrupole mass analyzer

The quadrupole mass analyzer (or quadrupole mass filter) is one type of mass analyzer used in mass spectrometry. As the name implies, it consists of four cylindrical rods, set parallel to each other. Ions are separated based on the stability of their trajectories in oscillating electric fields (a combination of radio-frequency and direct-current voltages) applied to the rods.

Principle of operation

Opposite rods are connected electrically, and a radio frequency (RF) voltage with a DC offset is applied between the two pairs. For a given RF/DC ratio, only ions of a specific mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) have stable trajectories and pass through the filter to the detector; all other ions are deflected into the rods. Scanning the voltages sweeps the transmitted m/z, producing a mass spectrum.

Applications

Quadrupole analyzers are inexpensive, robust, and compact, and are widely used in residual gas analysers, GC-MS and LC-MS instruments. In triple quadrupole mass spectrometers, three quadrupoles in series enable sensitive targeted quantitation through selected reaction monitoring.
