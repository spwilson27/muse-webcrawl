Short-track speed skating

Short-track speed skating is a form of competitive ice speed skating in which skaters race in packs on an oval ice rink measuring 111.111 metres (364.54 ft) in circumference—typically the size of an Olympic hockey rink. Because races involve multiple skaters jostling for position, crashes are common, and strategy and positioning matter as much as raw speed.

History

The sport developed in North America in the early 20th century, where mass-start races on small rinks became popular in the United States and Canada. The International Skating Union recognized short track in 1967; the first World Championships were held in 1981 (with earlier competitions retroactively recognized), and it became a full Olympic sport at the 1992 Albertville Winter Games after appearing as a demonstration event in Calgary in 1988.

Events

Individual events are held over 500 m, 1000 m, and 1500 m for men and women, along with team relays (5000 m for men, 3000 m for women) and a mixed team relay added at the 2022 Beijing Olympics. Skaters advance through heats; a unique feature is the advancement of skaters impeded by fouls. Dominant nations include South Korea, Canada, China, and the Netherlands. Famous skaters include Apolo Anton Ohno, Viktor Ahn, Choi Min-jeong, and Suzanne Schulting.
