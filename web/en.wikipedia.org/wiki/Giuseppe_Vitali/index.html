Giuseppe Vitali (26 August 1875 – 29 February 1932) was an Italian mathematician who worked in mathematical analysis, especially measure theory and the theory of functions of a real variable.

Life

Vitali studied at the Scuola Normale Superiore in Pisa, where Giuseppe Peano's ideas and the new set-theoretic methods strongly influenced him, though ill health and financial difficulties interrupted his studies and he graduated later from the University of Pisa. He taught in secondary schools while pursuing research, and only late in life obtained university chairs, at Modena (1923), Padua (1924) and Bologna (1930).

Work

In 1905 Vitali published the first example of a non-measurable set of real numbers — the Vitali set — constructed using the axiom of choice, showing that no translation-invariant measure can assign a length to every set of reals. He is also known for the Vitali covering theorem, a fundamental combinatorial tool in measure theory used to prove the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, and for a convergence theorem for sequences of integrable functions bearing his name. His work came close to the notion of absolute continuity, whose definitive characterisation is associated with the Vitali–Lebesgue theorem.
