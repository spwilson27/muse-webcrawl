Henry Alfred Kissinger (born Heinz Alfred Kissinger; 27 May 1923 – 29 November 2023) was an American diplomat, political scientist, and statesman who served as United States Secretary of State and National Security Advisor under Presidents Richard Nixon and Gerald Ford. Born in Germany to a Jewish family that fled Nazi persecution in 1938, he became a naturalised U.S. citizen and a Harvard academic before entering government.

Statecraft

A leading practitioner of Realpolitik, Kissinger orchestrated the opening of relations with the People's Republic of China (1971–72), pursued détente with the Soviet Union including the SALT I arms control agreement, conducted "shuttle diplomacy" in the Middle East after the 1973 Yom Kippur War, and negotiated the Paris Peace Accords that ended direct U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War — for which he was jointly awarded the 1973 Nobel Peace Prize, a decision that remains controversial. His record is fiercely debated: supporters credit him with reshaping Cold War diplomacy, while critics condemn his roles in the bombing of Cambodia, the 1973 Chilean coup, and support for authoritarian regimes.

Later life

After leaving government, Kissinger founded the consulting firm Kissinger Associates, wrote numerous books including Diplomacy (1994), On China (2011), and World Order (2014), and remained an influential, and polarising, commentator on foreign affairs until his death at the age of 100.
