Kalgoorlie

Kalgoorlie is a city in the Goldfields–Esperance region of Western Australia, located about 595 kilometres (370 mi) east-northeast of Perth. Together with the nearby town of Boulder, with which it merged to form the City of Kalgoorlie–Boulder, it is a major centre of the Australian gold mining industry.

History

Kalgoorlie was founded after Paddy Hannan discovered gold in the area in 1893, sparking one of Australia's great gold rushes. The rich strip of mines known as the "Golden Mile" became one of the most productive goldfields in the world. The town's growth depended on water delivered by the Goldfields Water Supply Scheme, engineered by C. Y. O'Connor and completed in 1903, which pumps water from near Perth through a 530 km pipeline.

Economy

Mining remains the basis of the local economy. The Super Pit, created by consolidating many underground workings into a single open-cut mine, is one of Australia's largest gold mines, measuring roughly 3.5 km long and over 600 m deep. Kalgoorlie is also a rail junction, served by the Trans-Australian Railway and the Indian Pacific and Prospector passenger trains.
