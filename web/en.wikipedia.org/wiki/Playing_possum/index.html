Playing possum

Playing possum is an idiomatic phrase meaning to pretend to be asleep, unconscious, or dead, or more generally to feign inactivity or ignorance in order to deceive an opponent or avoid an unwelcome situation. It takes its name from the defensive behaviour of the Virginia opossum, which, when threatened and unable to flee or fight, lapses into an involuntary, death-like state. Biologists call the behaviour thanatosis or tonic immobility, and it occurs in many animals besides opossums.

The opossum's defence

When a Virginia opossum (Didelphis virginiana) is confronted by a predator and escape fails, it may hiss, bare its teeth, and drool; if the threat persists, it falls onto its side with mouth open, tongue lolling, eyes staring, and body limp or curled, while its heart rate and breathing slow and it may emit a foul-smelling fluid from its anal glands that mimics the smell of decay. The trance is involuntary — a last-resort physiological response to extreme fear rather than a conscious performance — and can last from a few minutes to several hours. Many predators lose interest in prey that appears already dead, and carrion-avoiding hunters may be actively repelled.

Thanatosis in other animals

Death-feigning is widespread in the animal kingdom: hognose snakes roll over with mouths agape, many beetles, weevils, and other insects drop and lie still when disturbed, tonic immobility can be induced in chickens and sharks, and some fish and amphibians perform similar displays. In some species the behaviour is used offensively or sexually rather than defensively — for example, certain predatory cichlid fish feign death to lure scavenging prey within reach.

Figurative use

The expression entered American English in the nineteenth century, in a culture where opossums were familiar game animals whose trick of "playing dead" frustrated hunters and dogs. It is applied to people and organisations that stay quiet or conceal their intentions while danger passes — a candidate lying low during a scandal, or a company feigning weakness in negotiation. The related phrase "playing dead" is used synonymously, but "playing possum" carries the specific suggestion of cunning passivity rather than panic.
