Gelfond–Schneider theorem

The Gelfond–Schneider theorem, established independently by Alexander Gelfond in 1934 and Theodor Schneider in 1935, is a result in transcendental number theory that completely solves Hilbert's seventh problem.

Statement

If a and b are algebraic numbers with a ≠ 0 and a ≠ 1, and b is not a rational number, then ab is a transcendental number.

Consequences

Corollaries include the transcendence of 2√2 (the Gelfond–Schneider constant), of eπ (Gelfond's constant) and of √2√2. The theorem generalizes earlier results of Hermite on e and Lindemann on π.
