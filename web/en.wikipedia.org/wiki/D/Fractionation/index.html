Fractionation

Fractionation is a separation process in which a certain quantity of a mixture (gas, solid, liquid, enzyme, suspension, or isotope) is divided during a phase transition into a number of smaller quantities (fractions) in which the composition varies according to a gradient. Fractions are collected based on differences in a specific property of the individual components.

Types

A common trait in fractionations is the need to find an optimum between the amount of fractions collected and the purity in each fraction. Fractionation makes it possible to isolate more than two components in a mixture in a single run. Techniques include fractional distillation, fractional freezing, and various forms of chromatography.

Isotope fractionation

Isotope fractionation describes processes that affect the relative abundance of isotopes, often because lighter isotopes react or diffuse slightly faster than heavier ones. Equilibrium fractionation arises from differences in bond strength between isotopologues, while kinetic fractionation occurs in unidirectional processes such as evaporation and biological uptake. Measuring isotope ratios produced by fractionation is a central tool of isotope geochemistry, used to reconstruct past climates, trace water sources, and study metabolic pathways.

Other applications

In medicine, fractionation refers to dividing a total dose of radiation therapy into multiple smaller treatments. In chemistry and industry, fractional crystallization separates substances by differences in solubility. Plasma fractionation separates blood plasma proteins such as albumin and immunoglobulins for therapeutic use.
