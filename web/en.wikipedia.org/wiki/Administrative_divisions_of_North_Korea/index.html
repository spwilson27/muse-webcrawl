Administrative divisions of North Korea

The administrative divisions of North Korea are organized into three hierarchical levels. At the highest level are nine provinces, together with directly governed cities and special cities administered at provincial level — the capital Pyongyang, the special city of Rason, and the directly governed cities of Nampo and Kaesong. These are subdivided into cities (si), counties (kun), and districts, and at the lowest level into towns (ŭp), neighborhoods (tong), villages (ri), and workers' districts (rodongjagu).

Provinces

The nine provinces are North Hamgyong, South Hamgyong, North Pyongan, South Pyongan, Kangwon, Chagang, Ryanggang, North Hwanghae, and South Hwanghae. Most descend from the provinces of Japanese colonial Korea and the older Eight Provinces of Korea, with Chagang created in 1949 and Ryanggang in 1954. Provincial governments are organs of the central state under the country's unitary system, headed by chief secretaries of the provincial party committees and people's committee chairmen.

Second and third levels

Provincial-level units are divided into cities and counties; Pyongyang itself is divided into districts (kuyŏk) functioning analogously to counties. Counties and cities are subdivided into towns (the county seat), villages — the traditional rural unit — and, in urban areas, neighborhoods (tong); mining and industrial communities may be organized as workers' districts. Village and neighborhood levels host the lowest organs of administration and of the inminban neighborhood-unit system of social organization.

Special administrative regions

North Korea has created several special zones with distinct administrative arrangements, including the Kaesong Industrial Region near the border with South Korea, the Rason Economic and Trade Zone (formerly Rajin-Sonbong) in the far northeast, the Sinuiju Special Administrative Region (established on paper in 2002 but never fully implemented), and the Mount Kumgang tourist region. Their boundaries and status have shifted with political conditions, including the suspension of inter-Korean projects.

Changes over time

Boundary changes have been frequent: counties are split, merged, renamed, or promoted to cities as populations shift, and provincial-level status has been granted and withdrawn — Kaesong and Nampo have alternated between provincial and provincial-level administration, and Chongjin and Hamhung, though large, remain within their provinces. South Korean sources and the North Korean constitution do not always agree on the current list, and South Korea officially claims the territory using its own five-province committee for the north.
