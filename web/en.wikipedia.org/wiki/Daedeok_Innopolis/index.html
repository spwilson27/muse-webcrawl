Daedeok Innopolis

Daedeok Innopolis, formerly known as Daedeok Science Town, is a research and development district in the Yuseong District of Daejeon, South Korea. Established by the government beginning in 1973, it is the country's principal science cluster, often described as the "Silicon Valley of Korea".

Overview

The complex covers about 70 km² and hosts dozens of government-funded research institutes, universities, and hundreds of technology companies and startups, employing tens of thousands of researchers. Major residents include the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute (ETRI), the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute (KAERI), and the Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science (KRISS).

History

Construction of the science town began in the 1970s under President Park Chung-hee to concentrate national research capability outside Seoul. In 2005 it was redesignated a special R&D zone under the name Daedeok Innopolis, with incentives for technology commercialisation and venture companies.
