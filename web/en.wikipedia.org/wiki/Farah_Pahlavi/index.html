Farah Pahlavi

Farah Pahlavi (born Farah Diba, 14 October 1938) is the widow of Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, the last Shah of Iran, and was the Shahbanu (empress consort) of Iran from 1967 until the Iranian Revolution of 1979.

Early life and marriage

Farah Diba was born in Tehran into an upper-class family; her father was an officer in the Imperial Iranian Army. She studied architecture in Paris, where she was introduced to the Shah. They married on 21 December 1959. In 1967 the Shah crowned her Shahbanu — the first empress crowned in Iran since the seventh century — and named her as regent in the event of his death before their son came of age.

Role as empress

Farah took an active public role unusual for an Iranian consort, championing education, healthcare, women's rights and the arts. She founded institutions including Farah Pahlavi University for women and supported the Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art, whose collection of Western modern art she helped assemble. She also worked on heritage preservation and rural development through organisations such as the Farah Pahlavi Foundation.

Exile

The royal family left Iran in January 1979 as the revolution gathered force. After the Shah's death in Cairo in 1980, Farah settled principally in the United States and France. Her younger son Ali Reza and daughter Leila both died in 2011 and 2001 respectively. She remains a prominent figure among the Iranian diaspora and monarchist community.
