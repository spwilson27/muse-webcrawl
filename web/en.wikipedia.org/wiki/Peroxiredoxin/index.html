Peroxiredoxin

Peroxiredoxin
EC number1.11.1.15
ReactionReduction of hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides
SubfamiliesPrx1, Prx2, Prx3, Prx4, Prx5, Prx6 (mammals)
CofactorThioredoxin (for most types)

Peroxiredoxins (Prxs, EC 1.11.1.15) are a ubiquitous family of antioxidant enzymes that also control cytokine-induced peroxide levels and thereby mediate signal transduction in mammalian cells. Peroxiredoxins reduce hydrogen peroxide and alkyl hydroperoxides, using a reactive cysteine residue at their active site. They are among the most abundant proteins in many cells and are considered major components of the cellular defence against oxidative stress.

Classification

Peroxiredoxins are classified by the number and position of their catalytic cysteine residues. Typical 2-Cys peroxiredoxins (Prx1–Prx4 in mammals) contain both a peroxidatic cysteine and a resolving cysteine, which form an intermolecular disulfide during catalysis. Atypical 2-Cys peroxiredoxins (Prx5) form an intramolecular disulfide, while 1-Cys peroxiredoxins (Prx6) rely on external thiols such as glutathione.

Mechanism

In the catalytic cycle, the peroxidatic cysteine (CP) attacks the peroxide substrate and is oxidised to a sulfenic acid (CP–SOH). The resolving cysteine then condenses with the sulfenic acid to form a disulfide bond, releasing water. The disulfide is subsequently reduced by thioredoxin, which is itself regenerated by thioredoxin reductase and NADPH. Under high peroxide loads, the sulfenic acid can be further oxidised to sulfinic acid, temporarily inactivating the enzyme — a step reversed by sulfiredoxin.

Biological roles

Beyond detoxifying peroxides, peroxiredoxins regulate hydrogen-peroxide-mediated signalling, protect against oxidative damage in red blood cells (where Prx2 is the third most abundant protein), and are implicated in cancer, neurodegeneration, and inflammation. Peroxiredoxin 3 is localised to mitochondria, while peroxiredoxin 4 is found in the endoplasmic reticulum and secreted.
