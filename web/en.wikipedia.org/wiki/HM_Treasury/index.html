HM Treasury (His Majesty's Treasury) is the United Kingdom's economics and finance ministry. It is responsible for developing and executing the government's public finance and economic policy: setting the framework for taxation and public spending, controlling departmental expenditure, overseeing the financial services sector, and managing the national debt. The Treasury is consistently regarded as one of the most powerful departments of the British state.

Organisation

The department is headed politically by the Chancellor of the Exchequer, who is also Second Lord of the Treasury; the Prime Minister is nominally First Lord. Below the Chancellor sit the Chief Secretary to the Treasury, who negotiates spending settlements, and several junior ministers. The senior civil servant is the Permanent Secretary; James Bowler has held the post since October 2022. Key events in the Treasury's calendar are the Budget and the Spending Reviews, which allocate budgets across departments. Since 2010 the independent Office for Budget Responsibility has produced the official economic and fiscal forecasts accompanying Budgets.

History

The Treasury traces its origins to the medieval Exchequer, which audited royal accounts, and to the office of Lord High Treasurer, placed in commission from 1714. The modern department took shape in the nineteenth century, when William Gladstone's reforms strengthened Treasury control over departmental spending, and "Treasury control" became a constitutional convention of British government. Notable episodes in its history include the return to the gold standard in 1925, the collapse of the pound's position in the ERM in 1992, and the grant of operational independence to the Bank of England in 1997, which transferred interest-rate decisions from the Chancellor to the Bank's Monetary Policy Committee. The Treasury's headquarters, Government Offices Great George Street at 1 Horse Guards Road, overlooks Parliament Square.
