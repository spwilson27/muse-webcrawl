Sanjiangyuan

Sanjiangyuan (Chinese: 三江源, "Source of Three Rivers") is a vast region of the Tibetan Plateau in southern Qinghai province, China, where three of Asia's great rivers — the Yangtze, the Yellow River, and the Lancang (Mekong) — have their headwaters. Covering roughly 360,000 square kilometres at an average elevation above 3,500 metres, it is one of the most important water-source regions on Earth, supplying fresh water to hundreds of millions of people downstream.

Ecology

The Sanjiangyuan is a high-altitude landscape of alpine meadows, wetlands, lakes, and snow-capped mountains. Its wetlands and grasslands support iconic Tibetan Plateau wildlife including the snow leopard, Tibetan antelope (chiru), wild yak, kiang (wild ass), Tibetan gazelle, and black-necked crane. The region's marshes store large quantities of carbon and regulate the flow of the three rivers.

Protection

China established the Sanjiangyuan National Nature Reserve in 2000 and later designated the area as a pilot national park in 2016; Sanjiangyuan National Park was formally established in 2021 as one of China's first five national parks and one of the largest in the world. Conservation programmes have included grazing restrictions, ecological-migration resettlement of some herding families, and employment of local Tibetan residents as park rangers. Degradation of the grasslands — partly attributed to climate change and overgrazing — and the shrinking of plateau glaciers remain major concerns for the river sources.

People

The region's population is predominantly Tibetan, traditionally semi-nomadic pastoralists herding yaks and sheep across the high grasslands.
