The Golden Horseshoe is a secondary region of Southern Ontario, Canada, lying at the western end of Lake Ontario, with outer boundaries stretching south to Lake Erie and north to Lake Scugog, Lake Simcoe and Georgian Bay. It is named for the horseshoe shape of its densely populated arc around the lake's western shore. The region is the most populous and industrialised in Canada, containing the Greater Toronto Area and the cities of Hamilton, St. Catharines–Niagara, Waterloo Region, Guelph, Barrie, Peterborough and Oshawa.

Definition and extent

The core Golden Horseshoe runs from Niagara Falls and the Niagara Peninsula eastward along Lake Ontario through Hamilton, Burlington, Oakville, Mississauga, Toronto, and Durham Region to about Oshawa and Clarington. The Ontario government's Growth Plan defines the larger Greater Golden Horseshoe, an area of about 31,500 km² including the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area plus surrounding counties such as Simcoe, Dufferin, Wellington, Waterloo, Brant, Haldimand, Northumberland, Peterborough and Kawartha Lakes.

Economy and significance

With more than nine million people — roughly a quarter of Canada's population — the Golden Horseshoe is the country's principal manufacturing, finance and technology corridor. It hosts Toronto's financial district, major automotive plants in Oshawa, Oakville, Brampton and Alliston, steelmaking in Hamilton, and the Waterloo Region technology cluster. The region is connected by the Highway 401 corridor, the Queen Elizabeth Way, and GO Transit commuter rail and bus services. The Greenbelt, established in 2005, protects a band of farmland and natural areas encircling the built-up Horseshoe.
