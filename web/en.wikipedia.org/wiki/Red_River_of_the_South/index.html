Red River of the South

The Red River, or Red River of the South, is a major river in the Southern United States. It rises in the Texas Panhandle and flows eastward about 2,190 km (1,360 miles), forming the border between Texas and Oklahoma and part of the border between Texas and Arkansas, before joining the Atchafalaya and Mississippi rivers in Louisiana.

Course and name

The river's waters carry reddish sediment eroded from the red sandstone and siltstone of its basin, giving it the color reflected in its name. Its forks unite in southwestern Oklahoma; the river then flows past Wichita Falls, Denison (where Denison Dam forms Lake Texoma), Texarkana, and Shreveport, Louisiana. In Louisiana the river was once blocked by the "Great Raft," a huge natural log jam cleared in the 19th century, which had sustained Caddo Lake.

History

The Red River formed an international boundary between the United States and Spanish (later Mexican) territory after the Adams–Onís Treaty of 1819, and the precise location of the border along the river's forks was disputed into the 20th century. The river remains legally and culturally symbolic of the Texas–Oklahoma division, giving its name to the Red River Rivalry football game.
