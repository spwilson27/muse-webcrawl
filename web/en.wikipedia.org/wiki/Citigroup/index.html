Citigroup

Citigroup Inc., or Citi, is an American multinational investment bank and financial services company based in New York City. The company was formed by the merger of banking giant Citicorp and financial conglomerate Travelers Group in 1998, then the largest merger in history. It is one of the Big Four banks of the United States and is designated a systemically important bank.

History

Citicorp traces to the City Bank of New York (1812), which became First National City Bank and pioneered international branch banking and consumer innovations including early credit cards. The 1998 Travelers merger, led by Sandy Weill and John Reed, combined banking, insurance and Salomon Smith Barney brokerage, anticipating repeal of Glass–Steagall by the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act (1999). Jamie Dimon was a senior Travelers/Citi executive before his 1998 departure.

Financial crisis and after

Citi suffered enormous mortgage losses in 2008 and received $45 billion in TARP funds plus government guarantees on risky assets, repaid in 2009–2010. Since then it has shed hundreds of billions in non-core assets, exited many consumer markets abroad, and restructured under CEOs Michael Corbat and Jane Fraser, the first woman to lead a major Wall Street bank. It competes with Wells Fargo and JPMorgan Chase.
