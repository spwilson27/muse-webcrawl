Formant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In speech science and acoustic phonetics, a formant is a concentration of acoustic energy at a particular frequency in the speech wave. Formants are the distinctive frequency components of the acoustic signal produced by speech, and they arise from resonances of the vocal tract. They are denoted F1, F2, F3, and so on, in order of increasing frequency.

Vowels

The identity of a vowel is determined primarily by the frequencies of the first two formants: F1 correlates inversely with tongue height, and F2 with tongue frontness and lip rounding. Vowel diagrams plotting F1 against F2 correspond closely to the traditional vowel quadrilateral. Formants also distinguish speakers, and the perceptually important "singing formant" is a clustering of higher formants that helps trained singers project over an orchestra.

Analysis

Formants are visualised on spectrograms as dark bands and measured using techniques such as linear predictive coding. They are fundamental to speech synthesis, speech recognition, and the acoustic description of speech sounds.
