Participatory action research

Participatory action research (PAR) is an approach to research in which the people affected by the issue under study participate as co-researchers in designing questions, gathering and analysing data, and acting on the findings. It emphasises collective inquiry, reflection and social change rather than research done on subjects by outside experts.

Origins

PAR draws on Kurt Lewin's action research of the 1940s, on Orlando Fals Borda's work with Colombian communities, and on Paulo Freire's pedagogy of critical consciousness. It became prominent in development studies, public health, education and community organising, especially in the Global South.

Principles

Participants are treated as experts in their own lives; the research proceeds in cycles of planning, action, observation and reflection; and the goal is practical transformation — empowerment and change in the conditions studied — alongside knowledge. Critics question its generalisability and rigour by conventional academic standards, while proponents argue it produces knowledge that is more valid for, and usable by, the community involved.
