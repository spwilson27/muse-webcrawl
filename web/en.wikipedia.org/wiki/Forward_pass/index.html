Forward pass

In several forms of football, a forward pass is a throwing of the ball in the direction that the offensive team is trying to move, towards the defensive team's goal line. The legal and widespread use of the forward pass distinguishes gridiron football (American and Canadian) from rugby football, from which it evolved, where the ball may only be passed backwards or laterally. The forward pass is normally made by the quarterback to an eligible receiver, and its development in the early twentieth century transformed gridiron football from a low-scoring running game into an open, high-scoring sport.

History

Early American football prohibited passing the ball forwards. Following a brutal 1905 season in which numerous players were killed, President Theodore Roosevelt pressed for reforms; the 1906 rules legalised the forward pass but with severe restrictions — including a loss of possession on an incompletion — that discouraged its use. Saint Louis University coach Eddie Cochems is credited with the first systematic passing offence; his player Bradbury Robinson threw the first legal forward pass in September 1906, and in 1913 Notre Dame's Knute Rockne and quarterback Gus Dorais famously used the pass to upset Army 35–13, popularising the play nationally. Rules were progressively liberalised — notably in 1933, when the NFL allowed passing from anywhere behind the line of scrimmage and the ball was slimmed to ease throwing — ushering in the modern passing game.

Rules

Under NFL rules, a team may throw at most one forward pass per down, and it must be thrown from behind the line of scrimmage. Only eligible receivers (those wearing eligible numbers and not "covered up" on the line) may catch it. If the pass is not caught it is incomplete and the down ends; if caught by the defence it is an interception; if it crosses the line of scrimmage or is thrown forwards illegally, penalties apply. A pass thrown backwards or parallel is a lateral, which may be thrown any number of times and is a live ball if dropped. Pass interference rules protect receivers from early contact by defenders, and intentional grounding rules penalise a quarterback who throws the ball away merely to avoid a sack.

Terminology

Common forward passes include the screen pass, play-action pass, Hail Mary, and routes such as the slant, out, post and go route. Statistics such as passing yards, completion percentage, touchdown passes and passer rating measure passing performance.
