John Stith Pemberton

John Stith Pemberton (July 8, 1831 – August 16, 1888) was an American pharmacist and Confederate States Army veteran who is best known as the inventor of Coca-Cola. In May 1886, he developed an early version of a beverage that would later become Coca-Cola, but sold its rights to the drink shortly before his death in 1888.

Life

Pemberton served as a lieutenant colonel in the Confederate army and was wounded at the Battle of Columbus in 1865; his subsequent morphine addiction motivated his search for a medicinal alternative. In Atlanta he sold patent medicines, including 'Pemberton's French Wine Coca', which he reformulated as a non-alcoholic syrup when Atlanta adopted prohibition.

Coca-Cola

On May 8, 1886 the syrup, mixed with carbonated water at Jacobs' Pharmacy, was first sold as Coca-Cola. Bookkeeper Frank M. Robinson named it and penned its famous script logo. Ill and in debt, Pemberton sold off portions of his interest; after his death Asa Candler consolidated control and built The Coca-Cola Company, whose later formula change produced New Coke.
