Marylebone Cricket Club

Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) is a cricket club founded in 1787 and based since 1814 at Lord's Cricket Ground, which it owns, in St John's Wood, London. The club was formerly the governing body of cricket in England and Wales and, as the sport's legislator, held considerable global influence. It remains the custodian and arbiter of the Laws of Cricket.

History

MCC grew out of the White Conduit Club, whose members engaged Thomas Lord to find a private ground. The club adopted its name from the Marylebone area and first played at Lord's original ground in Dorset Square. In 1788 MCC published a revised code of the Laws of Cricket, beginning nearly two centuries in which it governed the game and selected England touring teams; England teams into the 1970s toured under the MCC name and colours.

Modern role

Governance of English cricket passed to the ECB and international cricket to the ICC in 1993, but MCC retains copyright in the Laws and issues new editions, most recently the 2017 Code. The club has around 18,000 full members, a famous waiting list, and its distinctive "egg and bacon" colours of red and gold. Women were admitted as members from 1998, after a long campaign. MCC plays hundreds of matches annually, runs the MCC Young Cricketers scheme, and promotes the Spirit of Cricket.

Lord's

Lord's, often called the "Home of Cricket", hosts the club, the Middlesex county side, a museum and the world's oldest sporting library.
