The 1974 Cypriot coup d'état was a military coup carried out on 15 July 1974 by the Cypriot National Guard and the paramilitary organisation EOKA B, ordered by the ruling military junta of Greece. The coup deposed the democratically elected President of Cyprus, Archbishop Makarios III, and installed the Greek Cypriot nationalist Nikos Sampson as president. The coup's objective was enosis — the union of Cyprus with Greece.

The coup of 15 July 1974 precipitated the Turkish invasion of Cyprus five days later.

Background

Cyprus had gained independence from Britain in 1960 under a constitution balancing its Greek Cypriot majority and Turkish Cypriot minority, with Greece, Turkey and the United Kingdom as guarantor powers. Intercommunal violence erupted in 1963–64, and relations between Makarios and the Greek junta deteriorated sharply after 1967. Athens resented Makarios's independent foreign policy and his reliance on support from the communist AKEL party, while Makarios sought to disarm the EOKA B gunmen who campaigned for enosis.

The coup

On the morning of 15 July 1974, National Guard units commanded by Greek officers attacked the presidential palace in Nicosia. Makarios escaped and was evacuated by the British; he later addressed the UN Security Council. Sampson, a former EOKA gunman and newspaper publisher, was proclaimed president. Fighting continued for several days between coup forces and Makarios loyalists.

Consequences

On 20 July 1974, Turkey, invoking its rights as a guarantor power, invaded Cyprus. The Sampson regime collapsed within days, Sampson resigned on 23 July, and constitutional government was restored under Glafkos Clerides as acting president. The invasion and a second Turkish offensive in August left the island effectively partitioned, a division that persists today as the Cyprus problem. The collapse of the Sampson regime also contributed to the fall of the Greek junta on 24 July 1974.
