SteamVR

The Valve Index kit, a SteamVR-based system.

SteamVR is a virtual reality platform and software framework developed by Valve Corporation. First announced at the 2015 Game Developers Conference alongside the HTC Vive, it provides the runtime, drivers, and user environment that connect VR applications on the Steam store to a wide range of headsets. Rather than being tied to one device, SteamVR implements Valve's OpenVR API so that games written once can run on hardware from multiple manufacturers.

Technology

SteamVR's signature technology is Lighthouse positional tracking: base stations sweep the room with infrared light, and photodiodes on the headset and controllers measure the timing of the sweeps to compute their position and orientation to sub-millimeter accuracy over room scale. Lighthouse 2.0 base stations, introduced with later hardware, support larger play areas and more than two stations. SteamVR also supports inside-out tracked headsets (such as Windows Mixed Reality devices) through vendor plug-ins, motion smoothing and asynchronous reprojection to maintain frame rates, and the SteamVR Home and dashboard environments for launching content from within VR.

Hardware

Supported headsets include the HTC Vive (2016), Vive Pro, and Vive Cosmos lines; the Oculus Rift and Quest families (via Oculus and Link software); Windows Mixed Reality headsets; Pimax headsets; and Valve's own Valve Index (2019), which paired a high-refresh-rate display with "Knuckles" controllers capable of individual finger tracking. Valve's flagship VR game, Half-Life: Alyx (2020), was designed for SteamVR and is widely credited with raising expectations for VR production values. Valve also co-developed the OpenXR standard through the Khronos Group, which has gradually superseded vendor-specific APIs including OpenVR for new applications.
