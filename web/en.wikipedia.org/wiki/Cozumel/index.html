Cozumel (Spanish: Isla Cozumel) is an island and municipality in the Caribbean Sea off the eastern coast of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, opposite Playa del Carmen and close to the Yucatán Channel. The municipality, part of the state of Quintana Roo, also includes enclaves on the mainland. The island is about 48 km (30 mi) long and 16 km (10 mi) wide, and is Mexico's largest Caribbean island and third largest island overall.

History

The name Cozumel derives from the Yucatec Maya Kùutsmil, "Island of the Swallows". The island was a sacred centre of the Maya goddess Ixchel, and pilgrims — especially women seeking fertility blessings — travelled to its shrines. The first Spanish expedition to reach the island was that of Juan de Grijalva in 1518; Hernán Cortés stopped at Cozumel in 1519 at the start of his expedition to the mainland. Smallpox brought by the expeditions devastated the Maya population.

During the colonial era the island was largely depopulated, serving at times as a refuge for pirates. Resettlement began during the Caste War of Yucatán in the 1840s. In the 20th century the island developed a modest economy based on chicle and fishing before tourism transformed it.

Tourism and diving

Cozumel is one of the world's best-known scuba diving destinations. The coral reefs on its western and southern shores — including Palancar Reef and Colombia Reef — form part of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System, the second-largest barrier reef in the world. Jacques Cousteau's 1961 visit and film are credited with publicising the island's underwater attractions. Much of the southern reef area is protected within the Cozumel Reefs National Park, established in 1996. The island is also a major cruise ship port of call, and most visitors arrive at the principal town, San Miguel de Cozumel, which is served by Cozumel International Airport and by ferries from Playa del Carmen.

Geography and wildlife

The island is flat and composed of limestone, with cenotes (natural sinkholes) formed by water percolating through the rock. It hosts endemic species such as the Cozumel racoon (pygmy raccoon), the Cozumel thrasher and the Cozumel emerald hummingbird. The island lies in the path of Atlantic hurricanes; Hurricane Wilma in 2005 caused severe damage to the reefs and infrastructure.
