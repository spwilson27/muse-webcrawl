Robocall

A robocall is a telephone call that delivers a pre-recorded message using an automated dialling system (autodialer), as if from a robot. Robocalls are used for legitimate purposes — public-service announcements, school closures, appointment reminders, debt collection and political campaigning — but the term is most associated with unwanted telemarketing and telephone scams, which account for billions of calls per year in the United States alone.

Regulation

In the United States, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act of 1991 (TCPA) restricts autodialled and pre-recorded calls, particularly to mobile phones, and the Federal Trade Commission's Telemarketing Sales Rule prohibits most pre-recorded sales calls to consumers without written consent. The National Do Not Call Registry (2003) reduced legitimate telemarketing but not illegal calls, many of which originate overseas and use spoofed caller ID. The TRACED Act (2019) pushed carriers to implement the STIR/SHAKEN caller-ID authentication framework, which US carriers were required to deploy by 2021.

Scams

Common robocall scams include IRS impersonation, Social Security fraud, fake warranty offers, tech-support fraud and health-insurance schemes. Scammers frequently spoof local numbers ("neighbour spoofing") to raise answer rates, and a single answered call can mark a number as active for further targeting. Regulators advise consumers not to answer unknown numbers, not to press keys or speak to the system, and to report illegal calls.

Countermeasures

Beyond STIR/SHAKEN, countermeasures include carrier-level call labelling and blocking, third-party spam-blocking apps, the FCC's Robocall Mitigation Database, and enforcement actions and fines against illegal callers and the voice providers that carry their traffic. Providers such as Hiya and Truecaller maintain spam databases. Despite these measures, illegal robocalls remain in the billions annually, though volumes of some categories have declined.
