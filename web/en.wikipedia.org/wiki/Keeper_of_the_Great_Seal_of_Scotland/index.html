Keeper of the Great Seal of Scotland

Keeper of the Great Seal of Scotland
Office holder ex officio: First Minister of Scotland (since 1999)
Original role: custodian of the Great Seal of the Kingdom of Scotland

The Keeper of the Great Seal of Scotland is the custodian of the Great Seal of Scotland, historically one of the great officers of state of the Scottish kingdom. Since Scottish devolution the office has been held ex officio by the First Minister of Scotland under the Scotland Act 1998.

History

The Great Seal was used to authenticate the most solemn acts of the Scottish Crown. In earlier centuries its keeper was often the Lord Chancellor of Scotland or a senior churchman or magnate. After the Union of 1707 a separate Great Seal for Scotland continued in use for Scottish business, and the office was held by various peers and officials; for long periods in the 19th and 20th centuries the Secretary (of State) for Scotland acted in connection with the seal while the formal keepership passed among officials.

Modern role

The Scotland Act 1998 provides that the First Minister is Keeper of the Scottish Seal (the successor to the Great Seal as used for acts of the Scottish Government). Documents requiring authentication, including royal proclamations and commissions for Scotland, are passed under the seal, whose practical custody lies with the office of the Registers of Scotland.
