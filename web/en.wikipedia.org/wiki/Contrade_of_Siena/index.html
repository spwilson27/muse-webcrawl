Contrade of Siena

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The contrade of Siena are the seventeen districts (contrade, singular contrada) into which the historic centre of Siena, Tuscany, Italy is divided. Each contrada is a small city-within-a-city, with its own territory, church, museum, fountain, motto, patron saint, colours, and animal or symbolic emblem. The contrade are famous worldwide for their fierce mutual rivalries, which culminate in the Palio di Siena, the bareback horse race held twice each summer in the Piazza del Campo.

History and identity

The contrade originated in the Middle Ages as neighbourhood organisations that supplied troops to the city's military companies, provisioned festivals, and provided mutual aid. From an original number of as many as 59, they were reduced to the present seventeen in 1729, when Violante Beatrice of Bavaria, governor of Siena, fixed their boundaries by decree. The surviving contrade are: Aquila (Eagle), Bruco (Caterpillar), Chiocciola (Snail), Civetta (Little Owl), Drago (Dragon), Giraffa (Giraffe), Istrice (Crested Porcupine), Leocorno (Unicorn), Lupa (She-Wolf), Nicchio (Seashell), Oca (Goose), Onda (Wave), Pantera (Panther), Selva (Forest), Tartuca (Tortoise), Torre (Tower), and Valdimontone (Ram). Contrada identity is ascribed at birth and baptism — new members are ceremonially "baptised" at the contrada fountain — and structures members' social life year-round through festivals, dinners, and alliances and rivalries that structure Palio strategy.

The Palio

Only ten contrade compete in each Palio (2 July and 16 August): the seven that did not run in the corresponding race the previous year, plus three drawn by lot. Horses are assigned by lottery and blessed in the contrada church. Victory brings year-long prestige; each contrada also maintains a formal principal rival (for example, Chiocciola–Tartuca and Istrice–Lupa) and a web of allies, which shapes jockeys' negotiations and race tactics.
