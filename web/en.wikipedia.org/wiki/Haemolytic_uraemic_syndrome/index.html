Haemolytic uraemic syndrome

Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS) is a group of blood disorders characterized by low red blood cells, acute kidney failure, and low platelets. It is a form of thrombotic microangiopathy.

Signs and symptoms

Initial symptoms often include bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain, vomiting and fever, followed by pallor, decreased urination, swelling, bruising and confusion as kidney failure develops.

Causes

Typical HUS most often follows infection with Shiga toxin-producing Escherichia coli (STEC), especially O157:H7, often from undercooked beef or contaminated produce, as in the 2011 German outbreak. Atypical HUS results from dysregulation of the complement system, often genetic, and can be triggered by pregnancy, drugs, or other illness. Pneumococcal HUS follows Streptococcus pneumoniae infection.

Treatment

Treatment is supportive, including fluids, blood transfusion, and dialysis. Antibiotics are generally avoided in STEC HUS. Atypical HUS is treated with the complement inhibitor eculizumab or ravulizumab.

Epidemiology

HUS is most common in young children and is a leading cause of acute kidney failure in childhood.
