King of Tyre

The King of Tyre was the ruler of Tyre, the Phoenician city-state on the Mediterranean coast of present-day Lebanon. The list of its kings is preserved chiefly through the history of Menander of Ephesus, quoted by the Jewish historian Josephus, who drew on Tyrian court records.

Notable kings

The most famous king of Tyre was Hiram I (c. 980–947 BCE), contemporary of David and Solomon of Israel, who supplied cedar, craftsmen and gold for the building of the Temple in Jerusalem and joined Solomon in maritime expeditions to Ophir. In the 6th century BCE, Ithobaal III was king when Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon besieged Tyre for thirteen years. Ethbaal (Ithobaal I), a priest of Astarte who usurped the throne, was the father of Jezebel, queen of Israel.

Later history

After the Babylonian siege, Tyre was governed for a period by judges (suffetes) and restored kings under Persian suzerainty. The monarchy effectively ended when Alexander the Great stormed the island city in 332 BCE after a seven-month siege, having built a causeway from the mainland that permanently joined the island to the coast. The most important source for the royal list is Josephus's Against Apion, which preserves the succession from Abibaal, father of Hiram, down to Pygmalion, whose sister Dido (Elissa) is said to have fled Tyre and founded Carthage.
