Lighting control console

A lighting control console (also called a lighting board or lighting desk) is an electronic device used in theatrical and entertainment lighting design to control multiple stage lights from a single position. The operator, seated at the console, can set levels, record cues, and play back sequences during a performance.

History

Early lighting control relied on resistance dimmers and autotransformers operated by hand. Preset boards with two or more scenes of faders appeared in the mid-20th century, and computerised memory consoles spread from the 1970s onward, allowing hundreds of cues to be stored and recalled with a single "GO" button.

Modern consoles

Modern consoles communicate with dimmers and fixtures using the DMX512 protocol and network-based systems such as sACN and Art-Net. Memory consoles designed for moving lights and LED fixtures provide encoders, touchscreens, and parameter-based programming. Well-known console families include the ETC Eos series and the grandMA range, widely used in theatre, concerts, and live events.
