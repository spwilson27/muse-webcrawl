Mantua (Italian: Mantova [ˈmantova]; Lombard and Latin: Mantua) is a city and comune in Lombardy, northern Italy, nearly surrounded on three sides by lakes formed by the Mincio River. It is renowned as a cradle of the Italian Renaissance, the seat of the Gonzaga dynasty, and the city near which the Roman poet Virgil was born (at Andes, modern Pietole).

History

Founded in Etruscan and Roman times, Mantua became a free commune in the Middle Ages and was ruled from 1328 to 1707 by the Gonzaga family, first as signori, then marquesses, then dukes. Under Isabella d'Este and her successors the court attracted artists such as Andrea Mantegna, whose frescoes in the Palazzo Ducale's Camera degli Sposi (1474) are masterpieces of the early Renaissance, as well as Leon Battista Alberti (architect of San Sebastiano and Sant'Andrea), Giulio Romano (builder of the Palazzo Te), and the composer Claudio Monteverdi, whose opera L'Orfeo premiered there in 1607. After the ruinous War of the Mantuan Succession and the sack of 1630, the duchy passed to Austria in 1707.

Sights

Principal sights include the Palazzo Ducale (the vast Gonzaga palace complex), Palazzo Te, the Basilica of Sant'Andrea by Alberti (housing Mantegna's tomb), the Cathedral, and the Teatro Bibiena. Mantua and Sabbioneta were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008 as outstanding examples of Renaissance town planning.

Culture

Mantua hosts the Festivaletteratura, one of Italy's leading literary festivals. In Verdi's opera Rigoletto the city is the setting of the decadent Gonzaga court, and Shakespeare set scenes of The Taming of the Shrew in the Mantua area; Romeo buys his poison from an apothecary in Mantua in Romeo and Juliet.
