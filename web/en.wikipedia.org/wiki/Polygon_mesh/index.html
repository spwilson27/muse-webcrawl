Polygon mesh

A polygon mesh is a collection of vertices, edges, and faces that defines the shape of a polyhedral object in 3D computer graphics and solid modelling. Faces usually consist of triangles (a triangle mesh), quadrilaterals, or other simple polygons, and together approximate the surface of a modelled object.

A low-polygon mesh; denser meshes approximate smooth surfaces more closely.

Elements

A vertex is a point in space, often carrying additional data such as a normal vector, colour, or texture coordinates. An edge connects two vertices, and a face is a closed set of edges forming a flat polygon. Meshes may also include groups, smoothing information, and material assignments.

Representations

Meshes can be stored as indexed face sets (a vertex list plus faces that reference it), or in connectivity-rich structures such as the winged-edge and half-edge representations, which make local traversal efficient. Common file formats include OBJ, STL (the standard for 3D printing), PLY, and the glTF format. Triangle meshes dominate because GPUs are optimised to render them.

Operations and quality

Modelling operations include extrusion, subdivision (e.g. Catmull–Clark), decimation, and Boolean combination. A "watertight" mesh with consistent face orientation encloses a volume and can be manufactured; holes, non-manifold edges, and flipped normals are common defects repaired by mesh-processing tools. Mesh density trades visual fidelity against rendering cost, motivating level-of-detail techniques.
