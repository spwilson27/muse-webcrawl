IEEE 802

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

IEEE 802 is a family of IEEE standards dealing with local area networks (LANs) and metropolitan area networks (MANs). The standards are maintained by the IEEE 802 LAN/MAN Standards Committee (LMSC), formed in February 1980 — hence the number "802," from the year and month of its first meeting. The family specifies services and protocols for the physical and data link layers of the OSI model, including the logical link control (802.2) sublayer common to the various media access control standards below it.

Working groups

StandardSubject
802.1Higher-layer LAN protocols: bridging, network management, security (802.1X)
802.2Logical link control (dormant)
802.3Ethernet (CSMA/CD)
802.4 / 802.5Token bus / Token ring (disbanded)
802.11Wireless LAN (Wi-Fi)
802.15Wireless personal area networks (incl. 802.15.1 Bluetooth, 802.15.4)
802.16Broadband wireless access (WiMAX)
802.22Wireless regional area networks (TV white space)

Among the most influential are 802.3 Ethernet, which dominates wired networking, and 802.11, marketed under the Wi-Fi trademark. The 802 standards are freely available through the IEEE Get Program six months after publication.
