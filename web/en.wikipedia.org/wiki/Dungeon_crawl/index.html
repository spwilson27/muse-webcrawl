Dungeon crawl

A dungeon crawl is a type of scenario in fantasy role-playing games and video games in which heroes navigate a labyrinthine environment — most often an underground complex such as a dungeon, cave system, or ruined fortress — fighting monsters, avoiding traps, solving puzzles, and collecting treasure. The format provides a structured setting in which a game master or game program can present a series of encounters within clearly bounded spaces.

History

The dungeon crawl originated with the earliest role-playing games. Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson's Dungeons & Dragons (1974) grew out of campaigns such as Arneson's Blackmoor and Gygax's Castle Greyhawk, in which play centred on descending ever deeper into multi-level dungeons, with difficulty and rewards increasing by level. Published modules such as Tomb of Horrors (1978) codified the form. In video games, dungeon crawls were adapted by early computer role-playing games including Wizardry and Ultima, and by roguelikes such as Rogue (1980), which procedurally generated the dungeon. Later works from Diablo to modern "dungeon crawler" board games and first-person grid-based games continue the tradition.

Characteristics

Typical features include a party of specialised adventurers (fighter, thief, magic-user, cleric), mapping of explored areas, resource management of light, food, and spells, random or placed monster encounters, and a "boss" or goal object on the deepest level. Critics sometimes use the term pejoratively for play emphasising combat and loot over narrative, but the dungeon crawl remains one of the most durable structures in gaming.
