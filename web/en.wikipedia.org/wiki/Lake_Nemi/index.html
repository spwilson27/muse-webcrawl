Lake Nemi

Lake Nemi (Italian: Lago di Nemi, Latin: Nemorensis Lacus) is a small circular volcanic lake in the Lazio region of Italy, about 30 km (19 mi) south of Rome. It takes its name from the town of Nemi, which overlooks it from the crater rim. In antiquity the lake was sacred to the goddess Diana and was known as Speculum Dianae, the "Mirror of Diana".

Geography

The lake occupies a volcanic crater in the Alban Hills (Colli Albani). It has a surface area of about 1.67 km² and a maximum depth of around 33 metres. On its western shore lies the town of Genzano di Roma; Nemi stands above the northeastern shore. The lake is drained by an ancient artificial emissary tunnel, attributed to the Roman period, which regulates its level.

Antiquity

On the lake's northern shore stood the Sanctuary of Diana Nemorensis, one of the most important cult sites of ancient Latium. Its priest, the Rex Nemorensis ("King of Nemi"), was a runaway slave who won his office by slaying his predecessor in single combat — a custom famously described by Sir James George Frazer at the opening of The Golden Bough (1890).

The Nemi ships

The lake is celebrated for the two enormous Roman pleasure barges — the Nemi ships — built under the emperor Caligula in the 1st century AD. Fitted with marble, mosaics, plumbing and statuary, they sank and lay on the lake bed for nearly two millennia. Between 1928 and 1932 the lake was partially drained and the ships recovered and housed in a purpose-built museum on the shore. The hulls were destroyed by fire in 1944 during the Second World War; models and surviving artefacts remain on display.
