Engelbert Humperdinck (born Arnold George Dorsey, 2 May 1936) is a British pop singer. Described by various sources as "one of the finest middle-of-the-road balladeers around", he achieved international fame in the 1960s and 1970s with hit singles including "Release Me", "The Last Waltz", "After the Lovin'" and "A Man Without Love". He has sold over 140 million records worldwide.

Early life

Dorsey was born in Madras, British India, to British parents; his father served in the British Army and his mother was of German descent. The family moved to Leicester, England when he was ten. He began playing saxophone and performing in nightclubs before signing with Decca Records in 1958 under his birth name, with little success.

Career

In the mid-1960s he adopted the stage name Engelbert Humperdinck, after the 19th-century German composer, at the suggestion of manager Gordon Mills. His 1967 recording of "Release Me" reached number one in the UK and famously kept the Beatles' "Strawberry Fields Forever"/"Penny Lane" double A-side from the top spot. Follow-up hits "There Goes My Everything" and "The Last Waltz" established him as a major star, particularly in the United States, where his smooth style and matinee-idol looks made him a leading performer in Las Vegas and on television.

His ballad "After the Lovin'" (1976) was a US Top Ten hit and earned a Grammy nomination. In 2012 he represented the United Kingdom at the Eurovision Song Contest with "Love Will Set You Free". He continues to tour worldwide into his late eighties, and in 2021 he was appointed MBE.
