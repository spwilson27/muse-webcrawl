The Urdd National Eisteddfod (Welsh: Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Urdd Gobaith Cymru) is an annual Welsh-language youth festival of literature, music and performing arts organised by Urdd Gobaith Cymru, the Welsh youth movement. Held during the late-May school half-term and rotating among locations in Wales, it is billed as Europe's largest youth festival, attracting tens of thousands of visitors and around 15,000 competitors.

History

The first Urdd Eisteddfod was held at Corwen in 1929, a few years after Ifan ab Owen Edwards founded the Urdd in 1922 to give Welsh children cultural activities in their own language. It has grown into one of the principal institutions of Welsh-language culture, broadcast extensively by S4C and BBC Radio Cymru.

Competitions

Competitors, from primary-school age to 25, first compete in local (cylch) and regional (rhanbarth) rounds before reaching the national festival. Competitions include solo and choral singing, cerdd dant, folk dancing, recitation, drama, instrumental playing, literature and art. The premier literary honours mirror those of the senior National Eisteddfod: a Chair for poetry, a Crown, and a medal for prose, awarded in ceremonies led by the Gorsedd of the Bards' youth counterpart.

Significance

The festival is a rite of passage for Welsh-speaking schoolchildren and a training ground for performers; many prominent Welsh musicians, actors and broadcasters first competed at the Urdd Eisteddfod. Its mascot, Mistar Urdd — a red, white and green triangular character introduced in 1976 — is a widely recognised emblem of Welsh youth culture.
