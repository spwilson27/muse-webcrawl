Kurt Matthias Robert Martin Hahn (5 June 1886 – 14 December 1974) was a German educator. He founded several innovative schools and educational initiatives worldwide, including Schule Schloss Salem in Germany, Gordonstoun in Scotland, the Outward Bound movement, the Duke of Edinburgh's Award scheme, and the first of the United World Colleges, Atlantic College. His educational ideas emphasised character development, experiential learning, service to the community, and physical challenge.

Early life and Salem

Hahn was born in Berlin to Jewish parents and studied at the universities of Kiel, Heidelberg, Freiburg and Göttingen, and later at Christ Church, Oxford. During the First World War he worked in the German Foreign Office. In 1920, together with Prince Max of Baden, he founded Schule Schloss Salem, a boarding school intended to educate a new German elite guided by responsibility and service.

Exile and Gordonstoun

After publicly opposing the Nazi regime, Hahn was briefly imprisoned in 1933 and then exiled to Britain. In 1934 he founded Gordonstoun School in Moray, Scotland, applying the Salem principles. Advising on the problems he saw in modern youth — declines in fitness, initiative, craftsmanship, self-discipline and compassion — Hahn developed short residential courses at Aberdyfi in Wales during the Second World War, which grew into Outward Bound.

Later initiatives

Hahn helped inspire the Duke of Edinburgh's Award (launched 1956) and co-founded Atlantic College in Wales, opened in 1962 as the first United World College. His maxim "Plus est en vous" ("There is more in you") summarised his belief that young people are capable of far more than they suppose. He retired to Hermannsberg in Germany, where he died in 1974.
