Information design

Information design is the practice of presenting information in a way that fosters efficient and effective understanding. It involves preparing information so that it can be used with efficiency and effectiveness by people, combining skills from graphic design, technical writing, linguistics, psychology and usability. Information designers work on documents, signs, forms, diagrams, interfaces and data visualisations.

History

The discipline has roots in nineteenth-century statistical graphics, the wayfinding and diagrammatic work of designers such as Otto Neurath with his ISOTYPE pictograms, and Harry Beck's topological London Underground map. The term came into general use in the 1970s, and figures such as Edward Tufte, with The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983), shaped its modern theory.

Principles

Good information design puts the user's needs first: content is structured, labelled and visualised so that readers can find, understand and act on it. It is tested with real users and judged by performance rather than by decoration.
