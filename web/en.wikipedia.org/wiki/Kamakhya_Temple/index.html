Kamakhya Temple

ReligionHinduism (Shaktism, Tantra)
DeityKamakhya (a form of the goddess)
LocationNilachal Hill, Guwahati, Assam, India
ArchitectureNilachal style, beehive-shaped shikhara
FestivalAmbubachi Mela

The Kamakhya Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to the goddess Kamakhya, located atop Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Assam, India. It is the foremost of the 51 Shakti Peethas and one of the most important centers of Tantric practice and goddess worship in India.

Legend

According to tradition, the temple marks the spot where the yoni (womb/genitalia) of the goddess Sati fell to earth when her body was dismembered by Vishnu's discus after her self-immolation. Uniquely, the sanctum contains no image of the goddess: worship centers on a natural rock fissure kept moist by an underground spring, symbolizing the goddess's creative power. Kamakhya is also associated with the goddess Kali and the ten Mahavidyas, who have subsidiary shrines on the hill.

History

The site is ancient, possibly originating as a local tribal (Kirata) shrine that was absorbed into Hinduism. The temple was destroyed during invasions in the early 16th century and rebuilt in 1565 by the Koch king Nara Narayana and his brother Chilarai. The present structure combines earlier Nagara elements with a distinctive beehive-shaped dome characteristic of the Nilachal style, decorated with sculptured panels and figures.

Ambubachi Mela

The temple's most famous festival is the annual Ambubachi Mela, held during the monsoon month of Ashadha (June). It celebrates the goddess's annual menstruation: the temple closes for three days, and the Brahmaputra is said to turn red. When the temple reopens, devotees receive pieces of cloth believed to be blessed by the goddess. The festival draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims, sadhus, and Tantric practitioners.

Worship

Kamakhya is a major center of Tantra, and animal sacrifice — chiefly of goats and buffalo — remains part of worship, though human sacrifice is said to have occurred in earlier centuries. The temple complex includes shrines to the ten Mahavidyas and other deities scattered over Nilachal Hill.
