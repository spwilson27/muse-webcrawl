Burlington is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Vermont and the county seat of Chittenden County. It lies on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain, about 45 miles (72 km) south of the Canadian border. Despite its modest size — a population of about 44,700 at the 2020 census, with roughly 225,000 in its metropolitan area — Burlington is Vermont's principal economic and cultural centre, home to the University of Vermont and the state's largest hospital.

History

Burlington was chartered in 1763 by New Hampshire governor Benning Wentworth and settled after the American Revolutionary War, with Ethan Allen among its early landowners; his homestead is preserved as a historic site. The city grew in the 19th century as a lumber and shipping port on Lake Champlain, connected by the Champlain Canal to the Hudson River, and later as a rail and manufacturing centre. The University of Vermont was founded in 1791, the nation's fifth college.

Geography and climate

The city is built on a hillside sloping down to the lake, with views across to the Adirondack Mountains of New York. Burlington has a humid continental climate with cold, snowy winters moderated somewhat by the lake, and warm summers. The waterfront, once industrial, has been redeveloped with parks, a bike path along the lake, and public access points.

Economy and culture

Major employers include the University of Vermont, the UVM Medical Center, and technology and food companies; Ben & Jerry's was founded in a converted Burlington gas station in 1978. Church Street Marketplace, a pedestrianised four-block shopping and dining district, anchors downtown. The city is known for progressive politics — Bernie Sanders served as its mayor from 1981 to 1989 before representing Vermont in Congress — and for an active arts, music, and craft-beer scene. Burlington's municipal electric department has reported running the city on 100 percent renewable electricity since 2014.

Government

Burlington is governed by a mayor and a twelve-member city council elected from wards and districts. Progressive Party and Democratic candidates have dominated recent city politics. The city operates its own electric utility and airport (Patrick Leahy Burlington International Airport, in neighbouring South Burlington).
