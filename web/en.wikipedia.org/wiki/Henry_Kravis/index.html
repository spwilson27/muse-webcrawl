Henry Kravis

Henry Roberts Kravis (born January 6, 1944) is an American businessman, investor, and philanthropist, best known as a co-founder of Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. (KKR), one of the world's largest private equity firms. He is regarded as a pioneer of the leveraged buyout (LBO).

Career

Born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, Kravis studied economics at Claremont McKenna College and earned an MBA from Columbia Business School. At Bear Stearns he worked under corporate-finance mentor Jerome Kohlberg Jr., alongside his cousin George R. Roberts. In 1976 the three left to found KKR, developing the leveraged buyout model using "bootstrap" acquisitions such as Houdaille Industries. KKR's $25 billion buyout of RJR Nabisco in 1989 — then the largest in history — was chronicled in the book and film Barbarians at the Gate.

Philanthropy and public life

Kravis has been a major benefactor of Claremont McKenna College (including the Kravis Center and the Henry Kravis Leadership Institute), the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and Mount Sinai Hospital. He has served as a co-chair of the Council on Foreign Relations and is a prominent figure in New York cultural and economic institutions.
