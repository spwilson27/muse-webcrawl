HTTP cookie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of a browser cookie consent banner with Accept and Reject buttons]

An HTTP cookie (also called a web cookie, Internet cookie or browser cookie) is a small block of data created by a web server while a user is browsing a website and placed on the user's computer or other device by the user's web browser. Cookies are designed to be a reliable mechanism for websites to remember stateful information — because HTTP itself is stateless, forgetting everything between requests.

Cookies were invented in 1994 by Lou Montulli at Netscape Communications for an online shopping cart, and shipped in Netscape Navigator; the name derives from 'magic cookies', a term already used by programmers for exchanged data tokens (and ultimately from fortune cookies). A server sets a cookie with a Set-Cookie header; the browser returns it on later requests to matching domains, enabling logins, carts, preferences and analytics.

The controversial form is the third-party cookie, set by a domain other than the one being visited — the mechanism that let advertising networks follow users across sites and build profiles. Privacy law (the EU ePrivacy Directive and GDPR, hence the ubiquitous consent banners) and browser policy pushed back: Safari and Firefox block third-party cookies by default, and Google Chrome spent years announcing, delaying and ultimately revising their removal — one of the defining privacy struggles of the web's third decade.
