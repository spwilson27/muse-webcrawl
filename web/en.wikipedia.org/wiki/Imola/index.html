Imola

The Rocca Sforzesca, a landmark of Imola

Imola (Romagnol: Jômla) is a city and comune in the Metropolitan City of Bologna, located on the river Santerno, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy. The city is traditionally considered the western entrance to the historical region Romagna. It has a population of around 69,000.

The city is best known outside Italy for the Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari, commonly called the Imola Circuit, which hosted the San Marino Grand Prix and later the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix in Formula One.

History

The city was anciently called Forum Cornelii, after its founder, the Roman dictator Lucius Cornelius Sulla, who founded it about 82 BC on the Via Aemilia. It was an agricultural and trade centre known for its ceramics. In the Middle Ages it was contested by Lombards, Byzantines, and papal forces, and in the Renaissance it passed between local dynasties such as the Manfredi, before being seized by Cesare Borgia in 1499; Leonardo da Vinci, in Borgia's service, drew a famous map of the city. Caterina Sforza famously defended the Rocca Sforzesca fortress.

Main sights

Landmarks include the Rocca Sforzesca, a 13th–15th-century fortress now housing a museum; Imola Cathedral, dedicated to Saint Cassian; the Palazzo Tozzoni; and the medieval Palazzo Comunale. The circuit park contains memorials to Ayrton Senna and Roland Ratzenberger, who died during the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix weekend.
