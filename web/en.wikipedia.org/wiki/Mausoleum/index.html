A mausoleum is an external free-standing building constructed as a monument enclosing the interment space or burial chamber of a deceased person or people. The term derives from the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the tomb of Mausolus, the 4th-century BC satrap of Caria in present-day Turkey, which was counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and was so famous that "mausoleum" became the generic word for a grand tomb.

History

Monumental tombs date to antiquity, from Egyptian pyramids to Roman imperial tombs such as the Mausoleum of Augustus and Hadrian's mausoleum (now Castel Sant'Angelo) in Rome. Mausolea were widely built in the Islamic world — for example the Taj Mahal in Agra, itself the mausoleum of Mumtaz Mahal — and in East Asia, including the imperial tomb complexes of China and the Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi in Kazakhstan.

Modern mausolea

In the 20th century, mausolea housed the embalmed leaders of communist states, including Lenin's Mausoleum in Moscow, the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum in Nanjing, and the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun in Pyongyang. Mausolea also became common in cemeteries in Europe and North America as family burial buildings, ranging from neoclassical temple forms to Gothic Revival chapels, and as large community mausolea with stacked crypts.

Notable examples

Famous examples include the Taj Mahal, the Pantheon in Paris, Grant's Tomb in New York, Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum in Hanoi, and the Mazar-e-Quaid in Karachi.
