Arsaces II (from Greek Ἀρσάκης; in Parthian 𐭀𐭓𐭔𐭊 Aršak) was king of the Parthian Empire from c. 211 BC to 191 BC. He was the son and successor of Arsaces I, founder of the Arsacid dynasty and of the empire itself.

Reign

Little is known directly of Arsaces II's rule. He inherited a young state carved out of the Seleucid Empire's northeastern provinces (Parthia and Hyrcania). In 209 BC the Seleucid king Antiochus III invaded Parthia on his eastern anabasis, occupying the capital Hecatompylos. According to Justin, Arsaces II fought a hard battle before retreating to Hyrcania, and the two kings concluded a peace and alliance in 206 BC, in which Antiochus recognised Parthian rule — a settlement that let the Arsacid state survive and later expand under Mithridates I.

Coinage and legacy

Arsaces II's silver drachms continue his father's type: the obverse shows the king in a soft cap, while the reverse depicts a seated archer — an image that became dynastic iconography for centuries, and every subsequent Parthian king took the throne name Arsaces. He was succeeded by Priapatius, whose relationship to him (son or nephew) is uncertain in the sources.
