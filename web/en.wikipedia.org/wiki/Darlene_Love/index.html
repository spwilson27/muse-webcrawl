Darlene Love

Darlene Love (born Darlene Wright; July 26, 1941) is an American singer and actress. She was the lead singer of the girl group the Blossoms and one of the most celebrated backing vocalists of the 1960s, singing on many of Phil Spector's "Wall of Sound" productions, including the Crystals' "He's a Rebel." Her recording of "Christmas (Baby Please Come Home)" became a seasonal standard.

Career

Love began singing in church and joined the Blossoms as a teenager. As a session singer she worked with Sam Cooke, Elvis Presley, Frank Sinatra, Dionne Warwick, and the Beach Boys, and was a member of Bob B. Soxx & the Blue Jeans. Under Phil Spector's Philles label she recorded under her own name and, uncredited, as lead vocalist of the Crystals. Her performance of "Christmas (Baby Please Come Home)" on Spector's 1963 album A Christmas Gift for You became her signature song; she performed it annually on David Letterman's late-night shows from 1986 to 2014.

Later work and honors

After years away from the spotlight, Love returned as an actress, playing Trish Murtaugh in the Lethal Weapon film series, and as a Broadway performer in Leader of the Pack, Grease, and Hairspray. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2011. Her story was featured in the Academy Award-winning documentary 20 Feet from Stardom (2013). Her 2015 album Introducing Darlene Love was produced by Steven Van Zandt.
