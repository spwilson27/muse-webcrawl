Amoeba

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An amoeba (plural amoebas or amoebae), often called an amoeboid, is a type of cell or unicellular organism which has the ability to alter its shape, primarily by extending and retracting pseudopods. Amoebae do not form a single taxonomic group; instead, they are found in every major lineage of eukaryotic organisms.

History

The earliest record of an amoeboid organism was produced in 1755 by August Johann Rösel von Rosenhof, who named his discovery "der kleine Proteus" ("the little Proteus"), after the shape-shifting Greek god. The genus Amoeba, typified by Amoeba proteus, became a model organism for the study of cell movement and phagocytosis.

Movement and feeding

Amoebae move by cytoplasmic streaming: the cell pushes its membrane outward into temporary projections called pseudopods ("false feet"), anchored by the cytoskeleton, and the body flows into them. They feed by phagocytosis, surrounding bacteria, algae, and other small organisms with pseudopods and enclosing them in food vacuoles where enzymes digest them. Freshwater species possess a contractile vacuole that expels excess water.

Reproduction

Most amoebae reproduce asexually by binary fission, the nucleus and cytoplasm dividing into two daughter cells. Under harsh conditions many form dormant cysts.
