The Hubbard Medal is awarded by the National Geographic Society for distinction in exploration, discovery and research. It is named after Gardiner Greene Hubbard, the first president of the Society, and is the organisation's highest honour.

History

The first Hubbard Medal was awarded in 1906 to Robert Peary for his Arctic explorations. Recipients have included Roald Amundsen, Charles Lindbergh, and the crew of Apollo 8. Anne Morrow Lindbergh received the medal in 1934 for her work as co-pilot and radio operator on her husband's survey flights.
