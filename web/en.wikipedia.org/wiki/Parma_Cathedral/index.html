Parma Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Parma; Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta) is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Parma, Emilia-Romagna, Italy, dedicated to the Assumption of the Virgin Mary. It is an important Italian Romanesque cathedral, and is especially known for the illusionistic fresco in its dome, the Assumption of the Virgin by Correggio (Antonio Allegri), one of the most celebrated works of Renaissance painting.

History

An earlier basilica on the site was damaged by fire; construction of the present cathedral began under Bishop Cadalo (later antipope Honorius II) after 1059, and the building was consecrated by Pope Paschal II in 1106. An earthquake in 1117 caused damage and repairs. The façade, with its loggia tiers, was completed in the 12th century, and the bell tower was built in the late 13th century. The octagonal Baptistery of Parma, in pink Verona marble by Benedetto Antelami, stands adjacent and was begun in 1196.

Art and architecture

The interior is a Latin cross with a nave and two aisles, a raised presbytery over a large crypt, and a dome on an octagonal drum. Benedetto Antelami carved the Deposition relief (1178) now in the south transept and the episcopal throne. The dome fresco by Correggio (1526–1530) depicts the Virgin ascending amid a swirling host of angels, viewed from below with daring foreshortening that influenced Baroque ceiling painting. Other frescoes include works by Lattanzio Gambara and members of the Farnese-era schools; the aisles were painted by various Renaissance artists.
