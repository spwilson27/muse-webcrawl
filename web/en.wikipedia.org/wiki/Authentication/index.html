Authentication (from Greek authentikos, "real, genuine") is the act of proving an assertion, such as the identity of a computer system user. It is the process of verifying that someone or something is who or what they claim to be. Authentication is distinct from identification (establishing an identity) and from authorisation (determining what an authenticated party is permitted to do).

Factors

Authentication methods are commonly grouped into three categories: knowledge factors (something the user knows, such as a password or PIN), possession factors (something the user has, such as a security token, smart card, or phone), and inherence factors (something the user is or does, such as a fingerprint, face, or voice). Combining factors from different categories yields multi-factor authentication, which substantially reduces the risk of impersonation; two-factor authentication (2FA) is now widely deployed for online accounts.

Methods and protocols

Digital authentication systems range from simple password checks to cryptographic challenge–response protocols and public-key infrastructures in which a certificate authority vouches for identities. Protocols such as Kerberos, OAuth and OpenID Connect, and hardware-backed standards such as FIDO2/WebAuthn, underpin modern single sign-on and passkey systems. Message authentication codes and digital signatures provide authentication of data origin and integrity.

Authentication of objects

Beyond computing, authentication applies to artworks, documents, antiques and consumer goods: experts examine provenance, materials, seals, holograms and other security features to distinguish genuine items from counterfeits.
