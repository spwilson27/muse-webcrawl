Juneau is the capital city of the U.S. state of Alaska, located on the Gastineau Channel in the Alaska Panhandle. It is the second-largest city in the United States by area and has served as Alaska's capital since 1906, when the government was moved from Sitka.

History

The area was a fishing ground of the Auke and Taku Tlingit peoples for centuries. In 1880, gold was discovered in Gold Creek by Joe Juneau and Richard Harris, guided by the Tlingit chief Kowee, and a mining town quickly grew. The settlement was named for Juneau in 1881. Hard-rock gold mines, notably the Alaska-Juneau and Treadwell mines, drove the economy until the mid-20th century.

Geography and access

Surrounded by steep mountains, the Mendenhall Glacier and the Juneau Icefield, Juneau has no road connection to the rest of the continent; it is reachable only by air and sea, including the Alaska Marine Highway ferry system. Its downtown sits beneath Mount Juneau and Mount Roberts.

Economy

Government is the largest employer, followed by tourism — more than a million cruise ship passengers visit in summer — and fishing and mining. Juneau is also home to the Alaska State Capitol and the University of Alaska Southeast.
