Automatic writing

Also calledPsychography
ContextsSpiritualism, Surrealism, psychology

Automatic writing, or psychography, is a claimed psychic ability allowing a person to produce written words without consciously writing. In Spiritualism it is attributed to spirits or the subconscious; in psychology it is explained by the ideomotor effect and dissociative states.

History

Automatic writing was popular in 19th-century séances; mediums such as Pearl Curran (claiming the spirit “Patience Worth”) produced novels. Psychiatrist Pierre Janet studied it clinically. In the 1920s Surrealists André Breton and Philippe Soupault used automatic writing — notably Les Champs magnétiques (1919) — to bypass rational control and access the unconscious.

Explanations and criticism

Sceptics attribute the phenomenon to the subconscious mind and ideomotor action, with no scientific evidence for spirit authorship. Related practices include Ouija board use and free writing.
