Zhongnanhai (Chinese: 中南海; pinyin: Zhōngnánhǎi, "Central and Southern Lakes") is a former imperial garden complex in Beijing, west of the Forbidden City, which serves as the headquarters of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the State Council of the People's Republic of China. The name is used as a metonym for China's top leadership, much as "the Kremlin" or "the White House" is used elsewhere.

History

The grounds were developed as imperial gardens from the Jin and Yuan dynasties onward, taking their name from two of three connected lakes (the third, Beihai, is now a public park). Emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties built palaces, temples, and pavilions around the lakes, and the Empress Dowager Cixi resided there in the late Qing. Yuan Shikai made it his presidential palace after 1912.

Modern role

After 1949 Zhongnanhai became the residential and office compound of the highest CCP and state leaders; Mao Zedong and Zhou Enlai both lived and worked there. Key buildings include Huairen Hall, used for major party meetings, and the offices of the Politburo Standing Committee. The compound is closed to the public and heavily guarded; its main southern entrance is the Xinhua Gate, guarded by sentries and bearing slogans in Mao's calligraphy.
