Kerman (Persian: کرمان) is the capital city of Kerman Province in south-eastern Iran, and the largest and most developed city in the province. Situated on a high plateau at about 1,755 metres (5,758 ft) above sea level, it is surrounded by mountains and desert and has a dry climate with hot summers and cold winters. It is known historically for its carpets, pistachios and its role as a trading centre between central Iran and the Persian Gulf.

History

Kerman was founded probably in the third century AD under the Sassanian king Ardashir I, who is said to have built the city and its castle. It passed through Arab, Seljuk, Mongol and Safavid rule, serving as a flourishing commercial and textile centre famous for wool shawls and carpets. In 1794 the city suffered a notorious catastrophe when Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar captured it after a siege; in revenge for the inhabitants' support of his rival, the last Zand ruler Lotf Ali Khan, he ordered the blinding of thousands of the city's men. Under the Qajars and in the twentieth century Kerman was rebuilt and grew into a regional capital.

Sights and economy

The historic core contains the Ganjali Khan complex, built under the Safavids around 1631, with a bazaar, bathhouse (now an anthropology museum), mosque, madrasa and caravanserai, and the Jameh Mosque of Kerman, founded in the fourteenth century. Kerman carpets, woven in the city and province for centuries, are among the most famous Persian carpet styles, and the province is Iran's leading pistachio-producing region. Nearby lies the city of Mahan, with the Shazdeh Garden and the shrine of Shah Nimatullah Wali.
