Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Mahdi (Arabic: محمد بن الحسن المهدي, born c. 29 July 869) is believed by Twelver Shia Muslims to be the twelfth and final Imam and the promised Mahdi, a messianic figure who, it is believed, will return at the end of time to restore justice. According to Twelver belief he has been in a state of occultation since 874 CE and will reappear before the Day of Judgement.

Birth and early life

He was the son of the eleventh Imam, Hasan al-Askari, and, in most Shia accounts, of Narjis, a woman of Byzantine or Nubian origin described in tradition as a descendant of one of the apostles of Jesus. Shia sources state that his birth in Samarra was kept secret because the Abbasid caliphs sought to eliminate the awaited son, and that the child was seen only by a small circle of the Imam's companions. Sunni scholars have historically differed over whether the historical Hasan al-Askari left a son at all.

Occultation

On his father's death in 874, the young Imam entered the Minor Occultation (al-Ghayba al-Sughra), during which he communicated with his followers through four successive deputies (sufara): Uthman ibn Sa'id, his son Muhammad ibn Uthman, Husayn ibn Ruh, and Ali ibn Muhammad al-Samarri. In 941 a final letter (tawqi') announced the Major Occultation, which continues to the present day; during it, no direct representative is appointed, and Shia jurists exercise general deputyship. The Jamkaran Mosque near Qom and the shrine complex at Samarra are among the sites most associated with him.

Beliefs about his return

Twelver tradition holds that the Mahdi will reappear (an event awaited as intizar) accompanied by Jesus (Isa), fill the world with justice after it has been filled with oppression, and rule before the end of time. His titles include al-Qa'im ("the one who rises") and Sahib al-Zaman ("lord of the age"). Belief in a coming Mahdi is also widespread in Sunni Islam, though Sunnis do not generally identify him with a son of Hasan al-Askari in occultation; other Shia branches, such as the Ismailis and Zaydis, hold differing views of the imamate and the Mahdi.
