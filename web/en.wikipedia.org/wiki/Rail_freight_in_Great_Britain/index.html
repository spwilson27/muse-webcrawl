Rail freight in Great Britain

The rail freight transport market in Great Britain comprises freight train operation by private freight operating companies over the network managed by Network Rail. Freight traffic peaked in the mid-20th century under British Rail, declined as coal traffic fell and road haulage grew, and stabilised in the 21st century around intermodal container traffic, construction aggregates, and remaining bulk flows.

Following the privatisation of British Rail in the 1990s, freight operations passed largely to English, Welsh & Scottish Railway (now DB Cargo UK), which was later joined by Freightliner, GB Railfreight, Direct Rail Services, and Colas Rail. Traffic is dominated by intermodal services linking ports such as Felixstowe, Southampton, and London Gateway with inland terminals, plus aggregates and cement for construction; coal, once the largest commodity, has largely disappeared with the closure of coal-fired power stations.

Statistics and policy

Rail freight in Britain moves on the order of 15–20 billion net tonne kilometres per year. Government policy has set growth targets for rail freight, citing its lower emissions per tonne-kilometre compared with road transport, and has supported schemes such as gauge enhancement for larger containers and the Strategic Freight Network.
