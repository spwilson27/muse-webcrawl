Talavera pottery

Talavera pottery is a Mexican and Spanish pottery tradition named after the Spanish city of Talavera de la Reina. In Mexico, authentic Talavera is produced only in the city of Puebla and the nearby communities of Atlixco, Cholula and Tecalí, using the same methods introduced in the 16th century. It is a form of maiolica — tin-glazed earthenware with a milky white base glaze, most characteristically painted in blue, though yellow, green, orange, black and mauve are also traditional.

History

Spanish potters, some of them from Talavera de la Reina, settled in Puebla in the 16th century and combined European maiolica techniques with Chinese porcelain motifs arriving via the Manila galleons and with indigenous Mexican craft traditions. Puebla became the leading pottery centre of New Spain; guild ordinances from 1653 regulated materials, methods and quality to protect the trade. Talavera tiles (azulejos) came to cover the facades and domes of Puebla's churches, convents and houses — most famously the "Casa de los Muñecos" and the kitchen of the Santa Rosa convent — and Puebla ware was exported throughout Spanish America.

Production

Traditional Talavera is made from two local clays, black and white, which are mixed, washed, kneaded by foot, shaped by hand or on a wheel, and fired. The pieces are dipped in a tin-based glaze that fires to an ivory white, then painted by hand with mineral pigments before a second firing. The entire process can take three to six months. Because the glazes historically used lead, modern certified pieces for food use are made lead-free.

Protection

"Talavera" is a protected designation of origin in Mexico, administered under regulations issued in the 1990s; only workshops in the designated zone that follow the traditional process may use the name, and certified pieces are marked and signed. In 2019, the artisanal Talavera processes of Puebla and Tlaxcala, together with those of Talavera de la Reina and El Puente del Arzobispo in Spain, were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
