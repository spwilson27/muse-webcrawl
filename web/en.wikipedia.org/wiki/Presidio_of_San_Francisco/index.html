The Presidio of San Francisco (originally El Presidio Real de San Francisco) is a park and former U.S. Army military fort on the northern tip of the San Francisco Peninsula, part of the Golden Gate National Recreation Area. It was founded in 1776 as a Spanish colonial fortress and remained an active military post under Spanish, Mexican, and American rule for more than two centuries.

History

Under the U.S. Army, the Presidio served as a headquarters for the defence of the West Coast and a staging point for Pacific operations, including during the Spanish–American War and both world wars; Letterman Army Hospital treated generations of soldiers there. The Army departed in 1994, transferring the post to civilian management.

The Presidio today

The Presidio is now managed by the Presidio Trust, a federal agency required to be financially self-sustaining. It contains historic buildings leased to residents and organisations, museums, hiking and cycling trails, beaches such as Crissy Field, the Main Parade Ground, and views of the Golden Gate Bridge.
