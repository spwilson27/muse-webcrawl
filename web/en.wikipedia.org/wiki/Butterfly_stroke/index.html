Butterfly stroke

The butterfly stroke, characterised by the simultaneous over-water arm recovery and the undulating dolphin kick.

The butterfly stroke (often shortened to "fly") is a swimming stroke swum on the chest, with both arms moving symmetrically, accompanied by the butterfly kick (also known as the dolphin kick). While other styles like the breaststroke, front crawl, or backstroke can be swum adequately by beginners, the butterfly is regarded as the most difficult stroke, requiring very good technique as well as strong muscles. It is the newest competitive swimming style, first swum in 1933 and formalised as a separate stroke in 1953, and the second fastest after the front crawl.

Technique

The butterfly technique combines a simultaneous arm pull and recovery with a wave-like body undulation driven by the dolphin kick. The arms move together: after entering the water in front of the shoulders, they sweep outward, catch the water, and pull back in a keyhole-shaped path past the hips, then recover over the surface with straight, relaxed arms. The legs kick together in two dolphin kicks per arm cycle — one as the hands enter the water and a larger one during the pull. The body rises and falls in a wave motion initiated from the chest, and breathing is typically taken by lifting the head and chin forward as the arms exit the water, commonly every stroke or every two strokes.

History

Butterfly developed within breaststroke competition. In the early 1930s swimmers such as Henry Myers and the German Erich Rademacher began recovering their arms over the water to reduce resistance, and in 1934 David Armbruster of the University of Iowa refined the overarm technique while his swimmer Jack Sieg developed the fishtail (dolphin) kick. FINA initially treated the stroke as a breaststroke variant — at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics several finalists swam butterfly with a breaststroke kick — before recognising butterfly as a distinct stroke with its own rules in 1953; it entered the Olympic programme at Melbourne in 1956.

Rules

Under FINA rules, both arms must be brought forward together over the water and moved simultaneously throughout, the legs must kick simultaneously with no alternating or breaststroke (scissors/frog) kick, and at turns and the finish both hands must touch the wall simultaneously. Not more than 15 metres may be swum underwater after the start and each turn.

Competition

Butterfly is raced at 50, 100, and 200 metres, and forms one leg of the individual medley and medley relay. Notable butterfly swimmers include Michael Phelps, who dominated the 100 m and 200 m events across four Olympic Games, Ian Thorpe, Sarah Sjöström, and Kristóf Milák.
