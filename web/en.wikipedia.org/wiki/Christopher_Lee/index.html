Sir Christopher Frank Carandini Lee (May 27, 1922 – June 7, 2015) was an English actor and singer whose screen career lasted nearly seven decades. He first became a star as Count Dracula in the Hammer horror films of the late 1950s and 1960s, and late in life reached new generations as the wizard Saruman in The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit film trilogies and as Count Dooku in Star Wars: Episode II and III.

Early life and war service

Lee was born in London to a British officer father and an Italian-English mother. During World War II he served in the Royal Air Force and as an intelligence officer with the Special Operations Executive, operating behind enemy lines in North Africa and Italy — experiences he seldom discussed.

Acting career

After small roles from 1946, Lee's breakthrough came with Hammer's The Curse of Frankenstein (1957) and Dracula (1958), in which his tall, commanding presence as the Count defined the character for a generation; he played Dracula nine more times. He also starred in The Wicker Man (1973) and as the Bond villain Scaramanga in The Man with the Golden Gun (1974). Peter Jackson cast him as Saruman in The Fellowship of the Ring (2001) — the only member of the cast to have met Tolkien — and George Lucas cast him as Count Dooku. He was knighted in 2009 for services to drama and charity.

Music

A trained bass singer, Lee released symphonic heavy-metal albums in his eighties, including Charlemagne: By the Sword and the Cross, becoming the oldest artist to chart a heavy-metal single.
