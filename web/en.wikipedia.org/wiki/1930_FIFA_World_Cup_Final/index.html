The 1930 FIFA World Cup Final was the deciding match of the first FIFA World Cup, played on 30 July 1930 at the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo. Hosts Uruguay defeated Argentina 4–2 to become the first world champions.

Match summary

Pablo Dorado gave Uruguay an early lead, but Argentina replied through Carlos Peucelle and Guillermo Stábile to lead 2–1 at half-time. Uruguay rallied in the second half with goals from Pedro Cea, Santos Iriarte, and Héctor Castro to win 4–2. The two teams famously disputed which ball to use, and a different ball was used in each half.

Aftermath

The victory, following Uruguay's Olympic football titles of 1924 and 1928, confirmed the hosts as the dominant football nation of the era. The following day was declared a national holiday in Uruguay, while defeat soured football relations across the Río de la Plata for years.
