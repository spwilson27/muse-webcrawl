Dinghy racing is a competitive sport using sailing dinghies — small, open sailboats typically crewed by one or two sailors. It is one of the most popular forms of yacht racing worldwide and is governed internationally by World Sailing. Races are usually sailed over triangular or windward–leeward courses marked by inflatable buoys.

Overview

Dinghies are raced in recognised classes, each with its own rules about hull shape, sail area and crew weight. Popular classes include the Laser (ILCA), the 420, the 470, the Optimist for junior sailors, and the high-performance 49er skiff. Because dinghies are light and responsive, racing places a premium on boat handling, tactics and reading wind shifts.

Race format

Most dinghy races use a starting sequence of sound and flag signals, after which the fleet races around a set course and finishes in order. Regattas typically consist of a series of races, with each boat's worst result often discarded. Scoring follows the low-point system: first place scores one point, second two points, and so on.

Olympic sailing

Several dinghy classes have featured in the Olympic Games, including the Finn (until 2020), the 470, the 49er and 49erFX, and the ILCA 6 and ILCA 7 singlehanders. Olympic dinghy racing is contested in a series of fleet races followed by a double-points medal race for the top ten boats.
