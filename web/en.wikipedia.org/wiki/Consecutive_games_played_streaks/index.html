Consecutive games played streaks

[Image placeholder: Ripken 2131 celebration]

Consecutive games played streaks — often called "iron man" streaks — are records of athletes appearing in game after game without missing one, prized as a measure of durability. The most famous is in Major League Baseball, where such streaks acquire a special status in record books.

Cal Ripken Jr. of the Baltimore Orioles broke Lou Gehrig's long-standing record on 6 September 1995, playing his 2,131st consecutive game, and voluntarily ended the streak at 2,632 games in 1998 — playing every inning of every game for stretches of it, including a streak of 8,243 consecutive innings. In Japanese professional baseball, Sachio Kinugasa played 2,215 consecutive games.

Other sports

In the National Hockey League, Doug Jarvis played 964 consecutive games (1975–1987), a mark later broken by Keith Yandle (989) and then Phil Kessel, who passed 1,000 consecutive games in 2022–23. In the National Football League, quarterback Brett Favre started 297 consecutive regular-season games (321 including playoffs), while punter Jeff Feagles played in 352 consecutive games. In the NBA, A. C. Green played 1,192 consecutive games between 1986 and 2001.
