Evidence

Evidence is anything presented in support of an assertion. In science and philosophy, evidence is information that supports or undermines a hypothesis or belief; in law, it is the body of material admitted in proceedings to help prove or disprove facts at issue. Strong evidence increases confidence that a claim is true, while weak or irrelevant material does not.

In philosophy and science

Philosophers debate what evidence consists of — sense experiences, observations, or propositions — and how it justifies belief. In Bayesian terms, evidence for a hypothesis is an observation that is more likely if the hypothesis is true than if it is false. Scientific practice prizes empirical evidence gathered by observation and experiment, and bodies of work are often ranked in "hierarchies of evidence", with systematic reviews of controlled trials near the top in medicine.

In law

Legal systems regulate evidence through rules governing relevance, hearsay, privilege, and the chain of custody of physical exhibits. Evidence in court may be testimonial, documentary, or demonstrative, and the standard of proof ranges from the balance of probabilities in civil cases to proof beyond reasonable doubt in criminal trials. Forensic science supplies much physical evidence, from fingerprints to DNA profiles.
