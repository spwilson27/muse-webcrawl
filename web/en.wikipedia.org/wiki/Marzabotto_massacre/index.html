The Marzabotto massacre, also known as the Monte Sole massacre, was a series of killings of Italian civilians carried out by Nazi German troops in the area around Marzabotto, south of Bologna, between 29 September and 5 October 1944 during the Second World War. Troops of the 16th SS Panzergrenadier Division "Reichsführer-SS", commanded on the ground by SS-Sturmbannführer Walter Reder, murdered hundreds of civilians — estimates range from at least 770 to as many as 1,830 — in reprisal for local support of the Stella Rossa partisan brigade and the Italian resistance. It was the largest massacre of civilians committed by the Waffen-SS in Italy during the war.

Background

After the Allied advance stalled at the Gothic Line in the autumn of 1944, German forces launched anti-partisan sweeps in the Apennines. The Monte Sole area, between the Reno and Setta valleys, was a stronghold of the Stella Rossa ("Red Star") partisan brigade. Civilians were rounded up in villages, churches and cemeteries — including at Casaglia, Caprara and San Martino — and killed with machine guns and grenades; farmhouses were burned.

Aftermath and trials

Walter Reder was tried by an Italian military court in Bologna in 1951 and sentenced to life imprisonment; he was released in 1985, provoking outrage. In 2007, an Italian military tribunal in La Spezia convicted ten former SS members in absentia to life imprisonment for the massacres. The area is now preserved as the Monte Sole Historical Park, with a Peace School and memorials at the ruined churches.
