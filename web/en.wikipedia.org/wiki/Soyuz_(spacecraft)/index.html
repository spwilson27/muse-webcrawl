Soyuz (spacecraft)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Soyuz MS approaching the ISS.

Soyuz is a series of Soviet and Russian spacecraft designed by the Korolev design bureau in the 1960s. First flown in 1966 and crewed from 1967, it has become the longest-serving and most-flown crewed spacecraft design in history, and for a decade after the Space Shuttle's retirement in 2011 it was the only vehicle carrying crews to the International Space Station.

The spacecraft has three parts: a spherical orbital module at the front, a bell-shaped descent module in the middle — the only section that returns through the atmosphere — and a cylindrical service module with solar panels at the rear. Soyuz launches on the Soyuz rocket from Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan, leased by Russia, and lands under parachutes on the Kazakh steppe, using small rockets to soften the touchdown.

History

The programme suffered two fatal accidents early on: Soyuz 1 in 1967, which killed Vladimir Komarov, and Soyuz 11 in 1971, whose three-man crew died from a cabin depressurisation — the only deaths to have occurred above the Kármán line. Since then the design has flown for decades without a fatality, through versions including Soyuz-T, Soyuz-TM, Soyuz-TMA and the current Soyuz MS.
