Direct flight

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the aviation industry, a direct flight is a flight between two points by an airline with no change in flight number, which may include one or more intermediate stops. A direct flight is therefore not necessarily a nonstop flight: passengers may remain on the aircraft during stops, or be required to disembark and reboard the same flight number, but they do not change planes.

Terminology

The term is a frequent source of confusion for travellers, because "direct" in airline usage differs from everyday usage:

  • Nonstop flight — a flight between two points with no intermediate stops at all.
  • Direct flight — a single flight number between origin and destination; the aircraft may stop en route, and in some cases even change aircraft ("change of gauge") while keeping the same flight number.
  • Connecting flight — travel involving two or more different flight numbers, requiring a change of aircraft at an intermediate airport.

Travel industry guides therefore often advise passengers seeking the shortest journey to look for "nonstop" rather than "direct" services. Direct, same-plane services were historically common on multi-stop long-haul routes; with the growth of hub-and-spoke networks and longer-range aircraft, most long-haul "direct" flights marketed today are in fact nonstop.
