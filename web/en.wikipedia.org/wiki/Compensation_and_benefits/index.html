Compensation and benefits

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Components of a typical total compensation package

Compensation and benefits (often abbreviated C&B) is a sub-discipline of human resources concerned with everything an employee receives in return for their work. Compensation refers to direct financial payments — wages, salaries, bonuses, and commissions — while benefits are indirect, non-cash forms of reward such as health insurance, retirement plans, and paid time off. Together they make up an employee's total rewards or total compensation package.

Components

Direct compensation

  • Base pay — a fixed salary or hourly wage.
  • Variable pay — bonuses, commissions, profit sharing, and incentive pay tied to performance.
  • Equity compensation — stock options, restricted stock units, and employee stock purchase plans.

Benefits

  • Health benefits — medical, dental, and vision insurance.
  • Retirement benefits — pension plans and employer-sponsored savings plans such as the 401(k) in the United States.
  • Paid time off — vacation, public holidays, sick leave, and parental leave.
  • Protection benefits — life insurance, disability insurance, and unemployment insurance contributions.
  • Perquisites — company cars, phones, gym memberships, and other perks.

Purpose and design

Employers design compensation and benefits programs to attract, retain, and motivate employees while controlling labour costs and complying with employment law. Specialists survey market pay rates, set salary ranges and pay grades, and evaluate jobs to maintain internal equity — the perception that pay is fair relative to other roles in the organization — and external competitiveness. Many jurisdictions legally mandate certain benefits, such as social security contributions, minimum paid leave, or workers' compensation insurance.

Theory

Compensation design draws on ideas from economics and organizational psychology, including expectancy theory, equity theory, and the principal–agent problem. Research on topics such as pay transparency, performance-related pay, and the gender pay gap is commonly discussed within the field. The growing use of flexible or "cafeteria" benefit plans allows employees to choose among benefit options within a set budget.
