Isopoda is an order of crustaceans within the class Malacostraca. Its roughly 10,000 described species occupy marine, freshwater and terrestrial habitats and include the familiar woodlice (pillbugs and sowbugs), deep-sea giant isopods, and parasitic forms such as fish lice and tongue-eating isopods.

Anatomy

Isopods have dorsoventrally flattened bodies (in most groups) with seven pairs of similar walking legs — the name derives from Greek for "equal feet". Unlike many crustaceans they lack a carapace covering the thorax, and females brood their eggs in a ventral marsupium. Size ranges from microscopic parasitic species under a millimetre to Bathynomus giganteus, which can exceed 30 cm.

Ecology

Most isopods are scavengers or detritivores; woodlice are important decomposers of leaf litter, while many marine species graze algae or prey on invertebrates. Parasitic suborders (Epicaridea, Gnathiidea, Cymothoida in part) feed on fish, crustaceans or other hosts, sometimes drastically altering host physiology. Terrestrial isopods (Oniscidea) are among the few crustacean groups fully adapted to land, though they remain dependent on moisture.

Evolution

The isopod fossil record extends to the Carboniferous period, more than 300 million years ago, and molecular studies place them among the peracarid crustaceans.
