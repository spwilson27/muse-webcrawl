Headroom

Headroom may refer to the margin between a normal operating level and a maximum level in several fields.

Audio and electronics

In audio engineering, headroom is the amount by which the signal-handling capabilities of an audio system exceed a designated reference level (such as 0 VU or −18 dBFS). It is a safety margin that allows transient peaks above the nominal level to pass without clipping or distortion. In digital audio, where 0 dBFS is an absolute ceiling, recording is typically done with average levels well below full scale so that peaks retain headroom. Related concepts include dynamic range and the peak-to-average ratio of a signal.

Photography and composition

In photography and film composition, headroom is the space between the top of a subject's head and the top of the frame. Too much or too little headroom is generally considered a compositional fault; conventional practice places the eyes near the upper third of the frame.

Other uses

  • The vertical clearance inside a vehicle, vessel or room — the distance between a seated person's head and the ceiling or roof.
  • In computing and project planning, spare capacity deliberately left unused as a buffer.
