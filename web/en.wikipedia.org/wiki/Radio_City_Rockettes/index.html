The Radio City Rockettes are an American precision dance company. Founded in 1925 in St. Louis, they have, since 1932, performed at Radio City Music Hall in New York City. Until 2015, they also had a touring company. They are best known for the Radio City Christmas Spectacular, an annual Christmas show, and for performing annually since 1957 at the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York.

The Rockettes are renowned for their perfectly synchronized choreography, including their famous eye-high leg kicks performed in an unbroken line, and classic numbers such as the "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers", which has been part of the Christmas Spectacular since 1933.

History

The troupe was founded by Russell Markert in St. Louis, Missouri, inspired by the precision dance lines of the British Tiller Girls and the Ziegfeld Follies. Originally called the Missouri Rockets, the group came to New York in 1927 to perform at the Roxy Theatre, where they were renamed the Roxyettes. When Radio City Music Hall opened in 1932, the dancers moved there and became the Rockettes.

Markert insisted on exacting standards: dancers were required to be between specified heights so that the line appeared uniform, and choreography emphasized geometric precision. The Rockettes have since become an American cultural institution, performing for presidents, at Super Bowl halftime shows, and at presidential inaugurations.

Auditions and training

Hundreds of dancers audition each year for a limited number of places. Dancers must be proficient in tap, jazz, and ballet, and must meet height requirements measured in stocking feet, traditionally between 5 feet 6 inches and 5 feet 10.5 inches. Rehearsals for the Christmas Spectacular begin months in advance, and the company may perform multiple shows per day during the holiday season.
