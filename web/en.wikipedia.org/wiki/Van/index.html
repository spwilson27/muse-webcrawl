Van

A van is a type of road vehicle used for transporting goods or people. It usually has a boxy shape taller than a typical car, with an enclosed cargo or passenger compartment. Depending on the type, it may be larger or smaller than a pickup truck and SUV, and bigger than a common car.

Types

Common categories include the panel van or cargo van, an enclosed vehicle for freight; the minivan, designed for passengers with sliding doors and flexible seating; the step van, with a high interior allowing the driver to stand and walk to the cargo area, used for deliveries; and campervans and conversion vans fitted for leisure or specialist use. Electric vans have become common for urban delivery fleets.

History

The word "van" originated as a contraction of "caravan". Early motor vans of the late 19th and early 20th centuries were often coachbuilt boxes on car chassis. Mass-produced vans such as the Ford Transit (1965), the Volkswagen Type 2, and the Dodge Ram Van defined the modern one-box design. Minivans emerged in the 1980s with models such as the Plymouth Voyager, becoming a major family vehicle segment.

Uses

Vans are used by tradespeople, parcel and postal services, shuttle and ambulance services, trades and rental fleets, and as the basis for minibuses and recreational vehicles.
