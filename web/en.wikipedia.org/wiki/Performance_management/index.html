Performance management

Performance management (PM) is the process of ensuring that a set of activities and outputs meets an organization's goals in an effective and efficient manner. It can focus on the performance of an organisation, a department, an employee, or the processes in place to manage particular tasks. Performance management standards are generally organised and disseminated by senior leadership and are the responsibility of managers at all levels.

Employee performance management

At the individual level, performance management typically involves setting expectations and goals, providing regular feedback and coaching, reviewing achievements — traditionally through an annual appraisal — and linking outcomes to development plans, rewards or corrective action. Frameworks such as management by objectives, the balanced scorecard and objectives and key results (OKRs) are used to align individual goals with organisational strategy.

Criticism and evolution

Annual appraisals have been criticised as infrequent, backward-looking and prone to bias, and many organisations have moved toward continuous feedback, check-ins and calibration processes. Critics also note that forced ranking systems can damage collaboration, and that poorly designed metrics can distort behaviour.

Organisational performance

Business performance management applies similar ideas at the enterprise level, using key performance indicators (KPIs), dashboards and planning cycles to monitor strategy execution and resource allocation.
