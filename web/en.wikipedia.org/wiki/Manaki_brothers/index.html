Manaki brothers

The Manaki brothers — Yanaki (1878–1954) and Milton (1882–1964) — were Aromanian photography and cinema pioneers, the first filmmakers in the Balkans and in the Ottoman Empire. They are best known for the Manaki Film Festival held in their honour in Bitola, and for their extensive photographic and film record of life in the southern Balkans in the early 20th century.

Life and work

The brothers were born in Avdella, a village in the Pindus mountains of Ottoman Macedonia. Yanaki studied painting and calligraphy and opened a photographic studio in Ioannina in 1898, later relocating with Milton to Monastir (modern Bitola), which became the centre of their work. In 1905 Yanaki purchased a Bioscope No. 300 film camera in London, and the brothers began filming scenes of everyday life, including their 114-year-old grandmother Despina spinning and weaving — regarded as the first film shot in the Balkans.

Over the following decades they documented visits by Ottoman sultans, Balkan Wars scenes, village festivals, weddings, and the changing political life of the region. Their surviving archive comprises hundreds of short films and more than 17,000 photographs, preserved in archives in North Macedonia and elsewhere.

Legacy

The International Cinematographers' Film Festival "Manaki Brothers", founded in 1979 and held annually in Bitola, is named after them. In 2012 North Macedonia organised events marking the centenary of their film work. Their photographs and films are a major source for the social history of the late Ottoman Balkans.
