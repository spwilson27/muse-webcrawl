30 St Mary Axe, previously known as the Swiss Re Building and informally known as The Gherkin, is a commercial skyscraper in London's primary financial district, the City of London. It was completed in December 2003 and opened in April 2004. With 41 floors, it is 180 metres tall and stands on the former site of the Baltic Exchange and Chamber of Shipping, which were extensively damaged in a 1992 Provisional IRA bombing.

Design

Designed by Foster + Partners, with former partner Ken Shuttleworth, the building's distinctive bullet-like form is generated by a circular plan that widens through the middle floors and tapers toward the top. A diagrid structural shell — a lattice of intersecting steel diagonals — carries the loads, allowing column-free floors and six triangular light wells that spiral up the building, drawing daylight deep into the plan and enabling natural ventilation for much of the year. Its energy use is about half that of a comparable conventional tower of its era.

History

The tower was commissioned by the reinsurance company Swiss Re, which sold it in 2007 for £630 million, then a record price for a London office building. After the purchasing vehicles defaulted, the building was put into receivership and sold in November 2014 to the Brazilian Safra Group for over £700 million. The top floor houses a private dining club and bar beneath the glazed dome, with panoramic views over the City.

Recognition

In 2004 the building won the Royal Institute of British Architects' Stirling Prize — the first time the prize was awarded unanimously — and it has been voted among London's most popular modern buildings in public polls. Its silhouette appears in numerous films and television programmes and has made it one of the most recognisable landmarks of the London skyline.
