Baiju Noyan (Mongolian: Байжу ноён; died c. 1259) was a Mongol commander in Persia, Anatolia and the Caucasus in the 13th century. He succeeded Chormaqan as the supreme Mongol commander in western Asia under Ögedei Khan and later Möngke Khan.

Career

Baiju served under Chormaqan in the Mongol conquest of Iran and Transcaucasia in the 1230s. On Chormaqan's death around 1241, Baiju took command. In 1243 he invaded the Sultanate of Rum in Anatolia and crushed the Seljuk army at the Battle of Köse Dağ, reducing the sultanate to a Mongol vassal. He campaigned repeatedly in Georgia, Armenia and Anatolia, suppressing revolts and enforcing tribute.

Baiju was at times displaced or checked by Mongol officials sent from the imperial court — notably in 1246, when the regent Töregene Khatun appointed Eljigidei over him — but he recovered his command under Möngke and quarrelled with the Seljuks and with the princes of Georgia who resisted Mongol taxation. In 1256 he was ordered to yield supreme command in Persia to Hülegü, whose advance into Iran superseded Baiju's authority; Baiju nevertheless participated in the campaign that culminated in the sack of Baghdad in 1258.

Death

Sources differ on the circumstances of his end. He is generally held to have been executed around 1259–1260, reportedly after Hülegü charged him with inadequate service or sorcery connected with the death of one of Hülegü's sons. His name survives in Persian, Armenian and Georgian chronicles as one of the principal agents of Mongol domination in the region for two decades.
