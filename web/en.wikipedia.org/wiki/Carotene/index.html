Carotene

Carotene is a class of unsaturated hydrocarbons with the formula C40H56, found in plants as orange, red and yellow photosynthetic pigments. The two principal isomers, α-carotene and β-carotene, differ in the position of a double bond in one terminal ring. Carotenes are the most widely recognised members of the larger carotenoid family and are distinguished from xanthophylls by containing no oxygen atoms.

Occurrence and function

Carotenes are synthesised by plants, algae and some fungi and bacteria, and occur in leaves (where their colour is masked by chlorophyll), roots such as carrots, sweet potatoes, and fruits including mangoes and pumpkins. In photosynthesis they act as accessory pigments, absorbing blue-green light, and as antioxidants that protect the photosynthetic apparatus from photo-oxidative damage.

Nutrition

β-Carotene is the principal dietary precursor of vitamin A: an enzyme in the intestinal mucosa cleaves it centrally to yield retinal, which the body converts to retinol. α-Carotene and β-cryptoxanthin have lesser provitamin A activity, while lycopene has none. Excessive intake produces the benign yellowing of the skin known as carotenemia. Industrially, carotene is used as a food colorant (E160a).
