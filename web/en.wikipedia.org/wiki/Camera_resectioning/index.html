Camera resectioning (also called camera calibration in some contexts, or space resection in photogrammetry) is the process of determining the parameters of a camera — principally its position and orientation in space (the extrinsic parameters) — from correspondences between known three-dimensional points in a scene and their two-dimensional projections in an image. It is the inverse of forward projection: instead of computing where scene points appear, one recovers where the camera must have been.

Problem formulation

Under the pinhole camera model, a 3D point X projects to an image point x according to x = K[R | t]X, where K contains the intrinsic parameters (focal length, principal point, skew) and [R | t] is the camera pose. Resectioning estimates these unknowns from a set of correspondences. The minimal case — the Perspective-Three-Point (P3P) problem — uses three points and can yield up to four solutions; additional points disambiguate. With known intrinsics, three points suffice in principle; with unknown intrinsics, more correspondences are required.

Methods

  • Direct Linear Transformation (DLT): treats the projection matrix entries as unknowns in a linear system solved from six or more correspondences, followed by decomposition into K, R, and t.
  • PnP algorithms: Perspective-n-Point methods such as EPnP and iterative Levenberg–Marquardt refinement minimise reprojection error efficiently for large point sets.
  • RANSAC-based estimation: robust variants tolerate outlier correspondences, essential in automated matching from image features.

Applications

Resectioning underlies aerial and close-range photogrammetry (where ground control points fix the camera's position for mapping), augmented reality (tracking a device's pose against a known model), robot and vehicle localisation, motion capture, and visual effects, where virtual cameras must be matched to live-action footage. In structure-from-motion pipelines, resectioning incrementally registers new images to a growing 3D reconstruction.
