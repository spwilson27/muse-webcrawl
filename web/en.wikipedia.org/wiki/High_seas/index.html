High seas


The high seas (in dark blue)

The high seas are the parts of the world's oceans that are not included in the territorial sea or internal waters of any state. Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982), the high seas comprise all parts of the sea not included in the exclusive economic zone (EEZ), territorial sea, internal waters, or archipelagic waters of a state — roughly 64% of the ocean's surface and about 95% of its volume.

Freedoms of the high seas

Article 87 of UNCLOS provides that the high seas are open to all states and include freedoms of navigation, overflight, laying of submarine cables and pipelines, construction of artificial islands and installations, fishing, and scientific research. No state may validly purport to subject any part of the high seas to its sovereignty. Ships on the high seas are generally subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of their flag state. Traditional universal-jurisdiction exceptions include piracy, unauthorized broadcasting, and the slave trade.

Governance and conservation

Living resources of the high seas are managed through regional fisheries management organisations, with varying success; problems such as illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, deep-sea mining at polymetallic nodule fields, and plastic pollution remain acute. In June 2023, UN member states adopted the High Seas Treaty (the BBNJ agreement) to protect marine biodiversity in areas beyond national jurisdiction, including by creating marine protected areas on the high seas.
