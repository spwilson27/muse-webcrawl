Mediterranean diet

The Mediterranean diet is a dietary pattern inspired by the traditional eating habits of people living around the Mediterranean Sea, particularly in Greece, southern Italy, and Spain, as they were observed in the mid-20th century. It is characterised by abundant plant foods, olive oil as the principal source of fat, moderate fish and poultry, low red meat and sweets, and wine in moderation with meals. In 2010 UNESCO inscribed the Mediterranean diet on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Components

  • High consumption of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds
  • Olive oil as the main added fat
  • Moderate fish and seafood; moderate dairy (chiefly cheese and yogurt)
  • Low to moderate poultry and eggs; infrequent red meat and sweets
  • Herbs and spices in place of salt; water as the main drink

History and research

The concept was popularised by American physiologist Ancel Keys, whose Seven Countries Study (begun in 1958) associated the dietary patterns of Crete and southern Italy with low rates of coronary heart disease. Subsequent research, including the PREDIMED trial in Spain, linked Mediterranean-style diets supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts to reduced incidence of major cardiovascular events, and observational studies associate the pattern with lower risks of type 2 diabetes and cognitive decline.

Criticisms

Definitions vary between studies, and the historical diet of the region has changed substantially since the 1960s, with modern Mediterranean populations consuming more processed food. Critics also note that early studies were observational and that socioeconomic factors may confound the associations.
