X-Men: First Class

Director: Matthew Vaughn
Released: 2011
Starring: James McAvoy, Michael Fassbender, Jennifer Lawrence, January Jones, Kevin Bacon
Budget: ~$140–160 million
Box office: ~$353 million

X-Men: First Class is a 2011 American superhero film directed by Matthew Vaughn, based on the Marvel Comics characters. It is the fifth instalment of the X-Men film series and a prequel set primarily in 1962 during the Cuban Missile Crisis.

Plot

The film follows young Charles Xavier (James McAvoy) and Erik Lehnsherr (Michael Fassbender) before they became Professor X and Magneto. Erik hunts Sebastian Shaw (Kevin Bacon), the former Nazi scientist who murdered his mother; Xavier, working with the CIA and the shape-shifter Raven (Jennifer Lawrence), recruits young mutants to stop Shaw and his Hellfire Club from triggering nuclear war between the United States and the Soviet Union. The climax off the coast of Cuba splits the mutants into two camps: Xavier's school-bound X-Men and Erik's Brotherhood.

Production and reception

Vaughn drew on 1960s James Bond films for the production design. The film was praised for its performances, style and emotional core, and is often ranked among the best entries in the franchise. It revitalised the series and was followed by the sequel X-Men: Days of Future Past (2014).
