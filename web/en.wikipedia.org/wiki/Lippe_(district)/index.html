Lippe (German: Kreis Lippe) is a Kreis (district) in the east of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It corresponds closely to the territory of the historic Principality and Free State of Lippe, one of the smallest constituent states of the German Empire and the Weimar Republic, which retained its independence until it was incorporated into North Rhine-Westphalia in 1947.

Location of Lippe within North Rhine-Westphalia.

Geography

The district lies between the Teutoburg Forest in the west and the Weser river in the east. Much of its northern and central part belongs to the Lippe hill country, while the south reaches into the foothills of the Egge Mountains. Neighbouring districts include Herford, Minden-Lübbecke, Schaumburg, Hamelin-Pyrmont, Holzminden, Höxter, Paderborn and Gütersloh, as well as the city of Bielefeld.

History

The counts (later princes) of Lippe ruled the region from the twelfth century from their residence at Detmold. The Free State of Lippe joined the new state of North Rhine-Westphalia in 1947 after negotiations in which the Lippian government secured guarantees preserving landmarks such as the district's own symbols. The modern district was formed in 1973 by merging the former districts of Detmold and Lemgo.

Towns and municipalities

The largest towns are Detmold (the capital), Lemgo, Bad Salzuflen, Lage, Horn-Bad Meinberg and Blomberg. The district is known for the Hermannsdenkmal monument near Detmold, commemorating Arminius's victory in the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest.
