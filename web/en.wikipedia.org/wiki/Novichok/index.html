Novichok agents
TypeNerve agents (organophosphorus compounds)
DevelopedSoviet Union / Russia, 1971–1993 (Foliant programme)
Known agentsA-230, A-232, A-234, A-242, A-262 and others

Novichok (Russian: Новичок, "newcomer" or "novice") is a family of nerve agents developed in the Soviet Union and Russia between 1971 and 1993 under the secret Foliant programme. They are organophosphorus compounds that inhibit acetylcholinesterase, like the earlier V- and G-series agents, and are regarded as among the most deadly chemical weapons ever made; some were designed to defeat NATO protective equipment and detection methods, and several were developed as binary weapons, whose two less-toxic precursors are mixed before use.

Development and disclosure

Knowledge of the programme reached the West largely through two scientists at the State Research Institute of Organic Chemistry and Technology (GosNIIOKhT): Vil Mirzayanov, who revealed the programme in the early 1990s and later emigrated to the United States, and Leonid Rink. The best-known agents include A-230, A-232 and A-234, as well as the earlier "Substance 33" and the binary agent known as Novichok-5. Exact structures were long disputed among chemists, since only limited information was published.

Poisoning incidents

Novichok came to international attention with the poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal in Salisbury, England, on 4 March 2018. The former Russian intelligence officer and his daughter survived after intensive treatment; a police officer, Nick Bailey, was also seriously poisoned. In nearby Amesbury, Dawn Sturgess died after exposure to the same agent (identified by the OPCW as the A-234 type) from a discarded container, and her partner Charlie Rowley survived. The United Kingdom blamed Russia, which denied involvement; the affair led to the coordinated expulsion of over 150 Russian diplomats by Western countries.

In August 2020 the Russian opposition leader Alexei Navalny fell ill on a flight in Siberia; laboratories in Germany, France and Sweden, and the OPCW, identified a Novichok-type cholinesterase inhibitor in his samples. Navalny survived after treatment in Berlin. A 1995 incident in Moscow, the murder of banker Ivan Kivelidi, has also been linked to a Novichok-class agent.

Control

Although the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) bans nerve agents generally, Novichok compounds were not originally named in its schedules. In November 2019 the CWC's Conference of the States Parties voted to add Novichok families to Schedule 1 — the first expansion of the treaty's annexes — with the change taking effect in June 2020.
