Acid phosphatase

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Acid phosphatases are a group of enzymes that catalyse the hydrolysis of phosphate monoesters at an acidic pH, releasing a free phosphate ion and an alcohol. They are a subclass of the phosphatases (EC 3.1.3.2) and are distinguished from alkaline phosphatases, which act most efficiently at alkaline pH. Acid phosphatases are widely distributed in nature, occurring in many animal and plant species, in bacteria and fungi, and in numerous human tissues including the prostate, liver, spleen, bone and blood cells.

Classification and types

Several distinct forms of acid phosphatase are recognised in humans. Prostatic acid phosphatase (PAP), encoded by the ACP3 gene, is produced in large quantities by the prostate gland and was historically used as a serum marker for prostate cancer before being largely superseded by prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing. Tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP, ACP5) is expressed by osteoclasts and activated macrophages and serves as a marker of bone resorption. Lysosomal acid phosphatase (ACP2) functions within lysosomes, where the internal pH is naturally acidic. Erythrocyte acid phosphatase (ACP1) is a low-molecular-weight form found in red blood cells and was one of the earliest genetic polymorphisms studied in human populations.

Reaction and mechanism

The general reaction catalysed is the cleavage of a phosphomonoester bond: R–O–PO32− + H2O → R–OH + H2PO4−. Most acid phosphatases use a conserved histidine residue as a nucleophile, forming a covalent phosphohistidine intermediate that is subsequently hydrolysed. Their pH optimum typically lies between 4 and 6.

Clinical significance

Elevated serum acid phosphatase was once an important diagnostic test for metastatic prostate cancer. TRAP levels reflect osteoclast activity and are raised in conditions of increased bone turnover. Deficiency of lysosomal acid phosphatase causes a rare lysosomal storage disorder. Acid phosphatase staining in forensic science — the Brentamine test — has been used as a presumptive test for semen, since seminal fluid is rich in prostatic acid phosphatase.
