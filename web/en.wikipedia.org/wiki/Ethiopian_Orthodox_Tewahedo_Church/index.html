The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is an Oriental Orthodox Christian church and the largest church in Ethiopia, with a history reaching back to the conversion of the Kingdom of Aksum in the 4th century AD. The name Tewahedo ("being made one") expresses its miaphysite Christology: the belief that Christ's divine and human natures are united in one nature.

History

Christianity was introduced to the Aksumite court by Frumentius, a Syrian shipwreck survivor who became tutor to the young king Ezana and later the first bishop (Abune) of Ethiopia. For over 1,600 years the Ethiopian church's primate was appointed by the Coptic Patriarch of Alexandria; it became autocephalous in 1959, when the first Ethiopian-born patriarch was consecrated.

The church preserved distinctive practices influenced by its long isolation and its reading of the Old Testament: observance of Saturday as well as Sunday as holy days, dietary laws, male circumcision, and the centrality of replicas of the Ark of the Covenant (tabots) in every church. Church tradition holds that the original Ark rests in the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion at Axum.

Canon and literature

The church's biblical canon is the broadest in mainstream Christianity, including books such as 1 Enoch and Jubilees, preserved completely only in Ge'ez, the classical liturgical language. Ethiopia's rock-hewn churches at Lalibela, carved in the 12th–13th centuries, are among its most famous monuments.

Organisation and practice

The church is led by a Patriarch and a Holy Synod, with archdioceses in Ethiopia and a worldwide diaspora. It observes a long fasting calendar — observant members fast on roughly 200 days a year, abstaining from animal products on Wednesdays, Fridays, and during Lent. The Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church became independent after Eritrea's independence in the 1990s.
