The Iron Guard (Romanian: Garda de Fier) was a Romanian militant revolutionary fascist movement and political party founded in 1927 by Corneliu Zelea Codreanu as the Legion of the Archangel Michael. It combined ultranationalism, Orthodox Christian mysticism and virulent antisemitism, and became notorious for political assassinations, pogroms and paramilitary violence.

History

Codreanu built the Legion around "nests" of devoted members, work camps and a cult of sacrifice and death. The movement's political wing, Everything for the Fatherland, won about 15.6% of the vote in the 1937 elections, becoming the third-largest party. King Carol II suppressed the movement in 1938; Codreanu was arrested and killed in custody that November.

Under Horia Sima, the Guard joined General Ion Antonescu in the National Legionary State in September 1940, sharing power for several months. In January 1941 the Legion rebelled against Antonescu; during the failed coup its members carried out the Bucharest pogrom, murdering more than 120 Jews. Antonescu, with Hitler's approval, crushed the rebellion and the Guard was outlawed.

Ideology and legacy

Scholars describe the Iron Guard as a distinctive form of clerical fascism, fusing Romanian Orthodoxy with revolutionary nationalism and a cult of martyrdom. After 1989, small neo-Legionary groups in Romania have attempted to revive its symbols, while mainstream commemoration remains contested.
