ACiD Productions (ANSI Creators in Demand) is an underground digital art group founded in 1990 that specialised in ANSI and ASCII text art for the bulletin board system (BBS) scene. One of the longest-lived and most influential groups of the computer artscene, ACiD defined the look of the dial-up BBS era and distributed its work in monthly collections called artpacks.

History

ACiD was formed in 1990 by five members — RaD Man, Shadow Demon, Grimm, The Beholder and Phantom — at a time when ANSI screens were the graphical interface of the modem underground. The group's monthly artpacks, distributed as ZIP archives through BBS networks, bundled ANSI screens, logos for individual boards, VGA graphics, music and demo code. ACiD also developed key scene tools, including the drawing program ACiDDraw, which made sophisticated ANSI artwork easier to produce and became a standard editor for the medium.

Artpacks and conventions

The group's main release series, internally labelled "ACiD Spec", bundled hundreds of works by dozens of artists per issue in peak years. Works typically carried the file extension .ANS and exploited the IBM PC text mode: 16 foreground colours, block characters (the "high ASCII" shading glyphs) and the iCE colour extension for bright backgrounds. ACiD helped codify conventions still used by the text art community, and its releases are treated as historical artefacts of pre-web online culture.

Preservation

Founder RaD Man (Christian Wirth) later became a prominent historian of the scene, organising the artpacks.org and Sixteen Colors archives that preserve tens of thousands of ANSI artworks, and championing text art within the broader demoscene — recognised by UNESCO in several countries as intangible cultural heritage. ACiD Productions members continue to release artpacks sporadically, decades after the group's founding.
