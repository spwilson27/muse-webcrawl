Acer rubrum

KingdomPlantae
OrderSapindales
FamilySapindaceae
GenusAcer
SpeciesA. rubrum

Acer rubrum, the red maple, also known as swamp maple, water maple or soft maple, is one of the most common and widespread deciduous trees of eastern and central North America. It is famed for the brilliant red colour of its autumn foliage, as well as its red flowers, petioles, twigs and seeds.

Description

The red maple is a medium-sized tree, usually 18 to 27 metres tall, with a trunk up to about 1.5 metres in diameter. Its leaves are palmately lobed, typically with three to five lobes with serrated margins and sinuses shaped like a V. The flowers appear in early spring, generally before the leaves.

Distribution and ecology

The species ranges from Newfoundland west to Minnesota and south to Florida and eastern Texas. It tolerates a wide range of soils and moisture conditions, from dry ridges to swamps, making it one of the most abundant tree species in eastern North America.

Cultivation

Red maple is widely planted as a shade and street tree; popular cultivars include 'October Glory' and 'Red Sunset', selected for dependable fall colour.
