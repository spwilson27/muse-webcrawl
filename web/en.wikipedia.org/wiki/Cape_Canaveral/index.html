Cape Canaveral

Cape Canaveral hosts Cape Canaveral Space Force Station and is adjacent to NASA's Kennedy Space Center.

Cape Canaveral (Spanish: Cabo Cañaveral) is a cape in Brevard County, Florida, in the United States, near the centre of the state's Atlantic coast. Officially renamed Cape Kennedy from 1963 to 1973 in memory of President John F. Kennedy, it is the site of Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, from which most American crewed and robotic spaceflights of the twentieth century were launched, and lies adjacent to NASA's Kennedy Space Center on Merritt Island.

History

The name Cañaveral, meaning "canebrake" or place of reeds, was given by Spanish explorers in the early sixteenth century. The cape's combination of a southerly latitude, sparse population and open ocean to the east — allowing rockets to launch over water and gain speed from Earth's rotation — led the United States to select it in the late 1940s as its principal missile testing and space launch site. The first rocket launch, a modified V-2 called Bumper 8, took place in July 1950.

Spaceflight

From Cape Canaveral's launch complexes flew America's first satellite (Explorer 1, 1958), first astronaut (Alan Shepard, 1961), the Mercury and Gemini missions, and uncrewed probes to the Moon and planets. Apollo Moon launches departed from the adjacent Kennedy Space Center, which is often popularly conflated with the cape; the region is still widely marketed as Florida's "Space Coast". Today the cape hosts launches by the U.S. Space Force and commercial providers such as SpaceX and United Launch Alliance.

Geography

The cape is a broad promontory formed by currents and sediment along the barrier-island coast. It is separated from Merritt Island by the Banana River. The city of Cape Canaveral lies just south of the cape itself.
