Lythraceae

Lythraceae is a family of flowering plants in the order Myrtales, comprising roughly 620 species in about 28 genera of herbs, shrubs, and trees, mostly tropical but extending into temperate regions. The family is named for the genus Lythrum, which includes the purple loosestrifes.

Characteristics

Members typically have simple, opposite leaves and flowers with a distinctive floral tube (hypanthium) and often crumpled petals. The family includes the pomegranate (Punica, now usually placed in Lythraceae as subfamily Punicoideae), henna (Lawsonia inermis), crape myrtles (Lagerstroemia), water chestnut relatives, and cupheas. Purple loosestrife (Lythrum salicaria) is a notorious invasive plant in North American wetlands. Mangrove genera such as Sonneratia were formerly placed in their own families but are included in modern classifications based on molecular evidence.
