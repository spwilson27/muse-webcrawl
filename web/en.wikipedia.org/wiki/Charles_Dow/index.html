Charles Dow

Charles Henry Dow (November 6, 1851 – December 4, 1902) was an American journalist who co-founded Dow Jones & Company with Edward Jones and Charles Bergstresser. He was the founder and first editor of The Wall Street Journal and the creator of the Dow Jones Industrial Average, the first widely followed stock market index. His editorials laid the groundwork for Dow theory, a foundation of modern technical analysis.

Life and career

Dow was born on a farm in Sterling, Connecticut, and lost his father at age six. Without a college education, he worked as a labourer and then as a reporter for newspapers in Massachusetts and Rhode Island, where he developed a talent for clear writing on business and history. By 1880 he had moved to New York and was reporting on mining stocks for the Kiernan News Agency.

Dow Jones and the Journal

In 1882 Dow, Edward Jones, and Charles Bergstresser founded Dow Jones & Company, which delivered financial news bulletins to Wall Street firms. In 1889 the firm launched The Wall Street Journal, with Dow as its first editor. On May 26, 1896, Dow published the first Dow Jones Industrial Average, computed from twelve industrial companies; it succeeded his earlier rail-heavy average of 1884.

Dow theory

In a series of editorials, Dow argued that the market moves in trends, that averages discount everything, and that price movements must be confirmed across indices. He never formalized these ideas into a "theory" himself; followers such as William Peter Hamilton and Robert Rhea systematized them after his death.
