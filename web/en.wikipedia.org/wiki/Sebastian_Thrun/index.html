Sebastian Thrun (born 14 May 1967) is a German-American computer scientist, entrepreneur and educator known for his work in robotics, probabilistic methods in artificial intelligence, and online education. He led the development of the autonomous vehicle Stanley, which won the 2005 DARPA Grand Challenge, founded Google's self-driving car project (later Waymo), and co-founded the online education company Udacity.

Academic career

Thrun studied computer science, economics and medicine at the University of Hildesheim and received his doctorate from the University of Bonn in 1995. He held faculty positions at Carnegie Mellon University, where he co-directed the Robot Learning Laboratory, and at Stanford University, where he directed the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL) from 2003 to 2011. His research centred on probabilistic robotics — applying Bayesian statistics to robot perception, localisation and mapping — and his textbook Probabilistic Robotics (2005), written with Wolfram Burgard and Dieter Fox, became a standard reference in the field.

Autonomous vehicles

In 2005, Thrun led the Stanford Racing Team whose modified Volkswagen Touareg, Stanley, completed the 131-mile DARPA Grand Challenge course in the Mojave Desert, winning the $2 million prize and a place in the Smithsonian. He subsequently moved to Google, where he founded and led the self-driving car project whose test fleet pioneered autonomous driving on public roads; the project later became the Alphabet subsidiary Waymo.

Udacity and later ventures

In 2011 Thrun and Peter Norvig offered Stanford's artificial intelligence course freely online, attracting around 160,000 students and catalysing the massive open online course (MOOC) movement. In 2012 he co-founded Udacity, a for-profit online education company offering technology "nanodegree" programmes. He later co-founded Kitty Hawk, an electric aviation company backed by Larry Page, serving as its chief executive.

Recognition

Thrun was named one of Popular Science's "Brilliant 5" and has been listed among Foreign Policy's Top 100 Global Thinkers. He is a member of the National Academy of Engineering and the German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina.
