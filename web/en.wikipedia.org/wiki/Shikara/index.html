Shikara

A shikara is a type of wooden boat found on Dal Lake and other water bodies in Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir, India. Shikaras are used for a variety of purposes, including transporting people and goods, fishing, and harvesting aquatic vegetation, and are an iconic part of tourism and daily life in the Kashmir Valley.

Description

Shikaras are typically built of deodar cedar wood, which resists water damage, and range from about 25 to 41 feet in length. The boats have a distinctive flat bottom and pointed ends, and passenger shikaras are fitted with a canopy supported on wooden pillars and cushioned seating. They are propelled by a boatman using a single paddle, often standing or sitting at the stern.

Uses

In addition to ferrying tourists on sightseeing rides among the houseboats and floating gardens of Dal Lake, shikaras serve local residents as taxis and cargo carriers. Floating markets, in which vendors sell vegetables and flowers from shikaras in the early morning, are a well-known feature of Dal Lake. Shikara races are also held as part of local festivals and sporting events.
