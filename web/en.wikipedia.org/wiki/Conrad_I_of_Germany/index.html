Conrad I (c. 881 – 23 December 918), sometimes called Conrad the Younger, was king of East Francia from 911 to 918. He was the first king not of the Carolingian dynasty: when the last East Frankish Carolingian, Louis the Child, died without an heir, the magnates elected Conrad, duke of Franconia, in the hope that a weaker, non-hereditary king could preserve the balance among the stem duchies.

Reign

Conrad's reign was beset by rebellion: the dukes of Saxony, Bavaria and Swabia each pursued independence, and he relied increasingly on the church for support. He failed to repel Magyar invasions, and lost Lotharingia to West Francia. Wounded in battle against the Bavarians in 915, he never recovered his authority. On his deathbed in 918, recognising his rival Henry the Fowler of Saxony as the stronger man, Conrad is traditionally said to have sent him the royal insignia — the story told by the chronicler Widukind — and Henry succeeded him in 919, founding the Ottonian line.
