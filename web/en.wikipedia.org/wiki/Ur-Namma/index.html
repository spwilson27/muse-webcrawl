Ur-Namma

Reignc. 2112–2095 BC (middle chronology)
PredecessorUtu-hengal (of Uruk)
SuccessorShulgi
DynastyThird Dynasty of Ur

Ur-Namma (also rendered Ur-Nammu) was a Sumerian ruler who founded the Third Dynasty of Ur and reigned for about eighteen years in the late third millennium BC. Originally a governor (and possibly military commander) under Utu-hengal of Uruk, who had expelled the Gutians from Sumer, Ur-Namma established his own kingship at Ur and reunified southern Mesopotamia, inaugurating the period known as the Neo-Sumerian or Ur III era.

Reign and achievements

Ur-Namma is celebrated as a builder and lawgiver. He undertook extensive construction programmes, including temple towers (ziggurats) at Ur, Eridu, Uruk, and Nippur; the great Ziggurat of Ur remains the most famous surviving monument of his reign. He restored canals, city walls, and trade routes, and deposit tablets record his administrative reforms.

His name attaches to the Code of Ur-Namma, the oldest known surviving law code — predating the Code of Hammurabi by roughly three centuries. Preserved on fragmentary tablets, its prologue credits Ur-Namma with establishing justice, and its provisions prescribe monetary fines for bodily injuries rather than the "eye for an eye" retaliation characteristic of later codes.

Death

Ur-Namma appears to have died in battle, an event lamented in the Sumerian poem "The Death of Ur-Namma", which describes his descent to the underworld and the gifts he presented to its deities. He was succeeded by his son Shulgi, under whom the Ur III state reached its greatest extent.
