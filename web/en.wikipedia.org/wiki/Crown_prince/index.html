Crown prince

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A crown prince (or crown princess) is the male (or female) heir apparent to a throne in a monarchy. The term derives from the crown as the symbol of royal authority and generally denotes the person who stands first in the line of succession and will become king or queen on the death or abdication of the reigning monarch, provided the succession is not altered. In many monarchies the heir bears a specific traditional title instead of, or alongside, "crown prince".

The crown, symbol of the succession awaiting the heir apparent.

Titles by country

  • United Kingdom: the heir is traditionally created Prince of Wales (and in Scotland is Duke of Rothesay, among other titles).
  • Spain: Prince or Princess of Asturias.
  • Netherlands: Prince or Princess of Orange.
  • France (historical): the Dauphin; the title came from the Dauphiné, ceded to the crown on condition the heir bear its arms.
  • Russian Empire: the Tsesarevich.
  • Japan: the Crown Prince (Kōtaishi) is the heir to the Chrysanthemum Throne under the Imperial Household Law.
  • Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states: the Crown Prince is often also deputy prime minister or prime minister and wields substantial executive power.
  • German states (historical): Kronprinz; Austria's heirs were styled Archduke and Crown Prince.

Role

A crown prince typically has no constitutional power while heir, though he or she may deputise for the monarch, undertake official and ceremonial duties, and receive training for kingship through military service, education and charitable foundations. Because the succession is his or her defining prospect, crown princes have historically been political flashpoints — centres of opposition to an unpopular monarch, or victims of suspicion, as in the long waits of some heirs. In several monarchies the position is not automatic by primogeniture: in Saudi Arabia the king appoints the crown prince with the approval of the Allegiance Council, and Malaysia's elective monarchy has no crown prince at the federal level.

Famous crown princes

Notable crown princes who never reigned include Edward the Black Prince (died before Edward III), Frederick, Prince of Wales (died before George II), and Crown Prince Rudolf of Austria, whose 1889 death at Mayerling altered the Habsburg succession, passing the heir's position ultimately to Archduke Franz Ferdinand, whose assassination in 1914 precipitated the First World War. Others, like Frederick III of Germany, reigned only briefly (99 days in 1888).
