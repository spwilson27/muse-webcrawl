Arthur (Or the Decline and Fall of the British Empire), often referred to simply as Arthur, is the seventh studio album by the English rock band the Kinks, released on 10 October 1969. Written by Ray Davies, it is a concept album loosely based on the life of Davies's brother-in-law, Arthur Anning, who had emigrated from England to Australia in the 1960s. Through the story of Arthur, a working-class Londoner, the album satirises the fading British Empire, post-war austerity, consumerism and the class system.

Background

The album was originally conceived as the soundtrack to a television play of the same name, to be written by Davies with novelist Julian Mitchell and produced by Granada Television. The TV project collapsed at a late stage, leaving the songs to be released as a standalone album. Davies developed the character of Arthur from his sister Rose's husband, who had emigrated to Australia — a departure lamented in the song "Some Mother's Son" and dramatised in "Australia," which sardonically advertises the new country as a land of opportunity.

Music and themes

Songs such as "Victoria" (a wry celebration of the Victorian Empire), "Yes Sir, No Sir," "Mr. Churchill Says," "Shangri-La" (the album's centrepiece, portraying Arthur's modest suburban dream house) and "Arthur" itself combine music-hall influences, brass-band touches and sharp social observation. A single of "Victoria" was released in advance, and "Shangri-La" and "Drivin'" also appeared as singles.

Reception and legacy

On release Arthur received strong reviews, particularly in the American music press, though its sales were modest. It is now regarded as one of the Kinks' finest albums and one of the great British concept albums, frequently appearing on lists of the greatest albums of all time. In 2019, for its fiftieth anniversary, the album was adapted into a BBC radio drama and reissued in an expanded deluxe edition.
