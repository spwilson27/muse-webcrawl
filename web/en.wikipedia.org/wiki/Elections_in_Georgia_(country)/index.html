Elections in Georgia, the country in the South Caucasus, are held at the national level for the Parliament and the presidency, and at the local level for municipal councils (sakrebulo) and mayors. Georgia is a parliamentary republic: the government is formed by the majority in Parliament.

Parliamentary elections

The 150-member Parliament was long elected by a mixed system combining party-list proportional seats with single-member majoritarian districts. Following constitutional reforms adopted in 2017 and implemented fully in 2024, parliamentary elections are now held entirely by proportional representation with a 5% threshold. Parliamentary terms are four years.

Presidential elections

The president, the largely ceremonial head of state, was directly elected in 1991–2018. Under the 2017 constitution, from 2024 the president is chosen for a five-year term by a 300-member electoral college of parliamentarians and local representatives.

History and conduct

Georgia's post-Soviet elections have included landmark peaceful transfers of power, notably in 2012, as well as periods of disputed results and protests, including around the 2003 Rose Revolution, which followed a flawed parliamentary election. Elections are administered by the Central Election Commission and routinely observed by OSCE/ODIHR missions.
