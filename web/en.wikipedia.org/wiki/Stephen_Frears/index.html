Stephen Arthur Frears (born 20 June 1941) is an English film and television director and producer. One of the most versatile directors of modern British cinema, he moved from BBC television drama and low-budget Channel 4 films in the 1980s to Hollywood successes, and is best known for My Beautiful Laundrette (1985), Dangerous Liaisons (1988), The Queen (2006) and Philomena (2013). He has twice been nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director.

Early life and career

Frears was born in Leicester, the son of a general practitioner, and studied law at Trinity College, Cambridge. He entered film as an assistant to Lindsay Anderson and Karel Reisz, directed his first feature, Gumshoe (1971), with Albert Finney, and then spent a decade directing acclaimed television plays for the BBC, including work with Alan Bennett and Stephen Poliakoff.

Breakthrough and Hollywood

Made for Channel 4, Hanif Kureishi's My Beautiful Laundrette (1985) became an international success and launched Daniel Day-Lewis. Frears followed it with Prick Up Your Ears (1987) and the period drama Dangerous Liaisons (1988), which won three Academy Awards. His American films include The Grifters (1990), earning his first Oscar nomination, High Fidelity (2000), Dirty Pretty Things (2002) and Mrs Henderson Presents (2005).

Later work

The Queen (2006), written by Peter Morgan, dramatised the royal family's response to the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, and won Helen Mirren the Academy Award for Best Actress; Frears received his second Best Director nomination. Later films include Philomena (2013) with Judi Dench, Florence Foster Jenkins (2016), Victoria & Abdul (2017) and The Lost King (2022). In television, his A Very English Scandal (2018) won widespread acclaim and multiple awards.
