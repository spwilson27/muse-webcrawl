The Casa de Contratación (full name: Casa y Audiencia de Contratación de las Indias, "House and Court of Trade of the Indies") was one of the most important institutions of the Spanish Empire. Founded by Queen Isabella I in Seville in 1503, it controlled all trade, shipping, navigation training, and emigration between Spain and its American colonies for nearly three centuries.

Functions

Every ship sailing to the Indies had to be licensed, inspected, and registered at the Casa; all goods were taxed and all bullion recorded, with the crown taking its fifth (quinto real) of precious metals. The Casa licensed and examined pilots through its chief pilot (piloto mayor) — a post first held by Amerigo Vespucci from 1508 — maintained the secret master map, the padrón real, ran a school of navigation, and acted as a court of law for commercial disputes. It also vetted settlers, since emigration to the Indies required royal permission and proof of Catholic "purity of blood."

History

The Casa first met in the royal Alcázar of Seville and later in the Lonja (exchange) built beside the cathedral, whose archive became the Archivo General de Indias. Seville's monopoly of the Indies trade, conducted through the annual treasure fleets, made it one of Europe's richest ports. In 1717 the Casa and the trade moved to Cádiz, whose harbour better suited deep-draught ships. As Bourbon reforms liberalised colonial trade (notably the comercio libre of 1778), the Casa lost its purpose and was abolished in 1790.
