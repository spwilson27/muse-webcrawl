Cardinal direction

The four cardinal directions, or cardinal points, are the four main compass directions: north, east, south and west, commonly denoted by their initials N, E, S and W. They are defined by the Earth's rotation axis and the apparent motion of the Sun: north points toward the geographic North Pole, south toward the South Pole, and east and west lie at right angles to them along the direction of rotation and against it, respectively.

A compass rose: cardinal points (N, E, S, W) with the ordinal points (NE, SE, SW, NW) between them.

Definitions

True north is the direction along the Earth's surface toward the geographic North Pole, found from the stars — Polaris in the northern hemisphere — or by gyrocompass. A magnetic compass instead indicates magnetic north, toward the north magnetic pole; the angle between the two, magnetic declination, varies by place and time. East is the direction in which the Sun rises at the equinoxes and the direction of the Earth's rotation as seen from above the North Pole; west is its opposite.

Ordinal and secondary directions

The directions midway between the cardinal points are the ordinal (or intercardinal) directions: northeast, southeast, southwest and northwest. Bisecting again yields the eight secondary intercardinal points (north-northeast, east-northeast, etc.), and traditional mariner's compasses divided the circle into 32 named points. Directions are also expressed numerically as azimuths or bearings in degrees clockwise from north.

Cultural aspects

The four directions structure language and custom worldwide. Many languages name them from the Sun's path (words for south derived from "midday", east from "dawn"). In Chinese tradition the directions correlate with the five elements and four symbolic animals; in many Indigenous North American traditions the four directions have ceremonial and medicinal significance. Cardinal directions also anchor everyday geography — city grids such as Manhattan's are laid out by them — and adjectives derived from them (boreal, oriental, austral, occidental) appear in older English usage.
