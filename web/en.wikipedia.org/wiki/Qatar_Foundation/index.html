The Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development (Arabic: مؤسسة قطر) is a state-led non-profit organisation in Qatar, founded in 1995 by then-Emir Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani and his wife Sheikha Moza bint Nasser, who has chaired it since its founding. It is the principal vehicle of Qatar's strategy to build a knowledge-based economy beyond hydrocarbons, and its flagship project is Education City, a 14-square-kilometre campus on the outskirts of Doha.

Education City

Education City hosts branch campuses of international universities — including Georgetown, Carnegie Mellon, Texas A&M, Northwestern, Cornell's medical school (Weill Cornell Medicine-Qatar) and HEC Paris — alongside the home-grown Hamad Bin Khalifa University. The foundation also operates Qatar Academy schools, the Qatar National Library, and hosts the World Innovation Summit for Education (WISE), launched in 2009.

Science, culture and sponsorship

The foundation funds research through the Qatar National Research Fund and the Qatar Science & Technology Park, and supports cultural institutions such as the Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art. Internationally it became widely known as the first commercial shirt sponsor of FC Barcelona (2011–2013), part of a broader Qatari sports-sponsorship strategy. Its initiatives were affected by the 2017–2021 Qatar diplomatic crisis, during which neighbouring states blockaded Qatar, but the foundation continued to expand, and Education City served as a hub during the 2022 FIFA World Cup.
