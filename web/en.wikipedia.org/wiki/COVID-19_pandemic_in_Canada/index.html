COVID-19 pandemic in Canada

The COVID-19 pandemic in Canada is part of the worldwide pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The first confirmed case in Canada was announced on January 25, 2020, involving a traveller who had recently returned from Wuhan, China, to Toronto. The federal government and all ten provinces declared states of emergency in March 2020 as community transmission spread.

Timeline

Through spring 2020, long-term care and retirement homes suffered the majority of deaths, particularly in Ontario and Quebec; the Canadian Armed Forces were deployed to assist in care homes in both provinces. Provinces imposed lockdowns, school closures and gathering limits in successive waves driven by the original strain, Alpha, Delta, and — from late 2021 — the highly transmissible Omicron variant, which produced Canada's largest case counts.

Vaccination

Canada authorised the Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine in December 2020 and began immunisation on December 14, 2020. After a slow start caused by supply delays, Canada achieved one of the world's highest vaccination rates by mid-2021, with more than 80% of the eligible population fully vaccinated. Vaccine mandates for federal employees and travellers, and provincial vaccine passports, were introduced in late 2021 and largely lifted during 2022.

Protests and aftermath

In early 2022, the "Freedom Convoy" protests against vaccine mandates occupied downtown Ottawa for several weeks, prompting the federal government to invoke the Emergencies Act for the first time since its enactment. The Public Health Agency of Canada ended most remaining federal border measures in October 2022.
