Billy Elliot the Musical is a coming-of-age stage musical based on the 2000 film Billy Elliot. The music is by Elton John, and the book and lyrics are by Lee Hall, who wrote the film's screenplay. The musical premiered at the Victoria Palace Theatre in London's West End in 2005 and ran until 2016.

Plot

Set in a northern English mining town during the UK miners' strike of 1984–85, the story follows Billy, the motherless son of a coal miner, who stumbles into a ballet class and discovers a passion for dance that collides with his family's expectations and his community's struggles. Supported by his dance teacher, Mrs Wilkinson, Billy auditions for the Royal Ballet School in London while the strike — and his father's resistance — crumble around him.

Productions

The original London production was directed by Stephen Daldry and choreographed by Peter Darling, as with the film. It won four Laurence Olivier Awards including Best New Musical. The Broadway production opened in 2008 and won ten Tony Awards, including Best Musical; the three boys who originated the title role — Trent Kowalik, David Álvarez and Kiril Kulish — jointly won the Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical. Productions followed in Australia, North America on tour, South Korea, the Netherlands and elsewhere, and a filmed performance, Billy Elliot the Musical Live, was screened in cinemas in 2014.

Reception

The musical was praised for its political backdrop, its score (including the numbers "Electricity", "Expressing Yourself" and "Once We Were Kings") and the demanding child leads, whose casting required extensive searches and a purpose-built training programme nicknamed "Billy School".
