Women in China

The status of women in China has changed profoundly over the past century, from the Confucian family order and practices such as foot binding, through the reforms of the early People's Republic, to today's high rates of female education and labour-force participation alongside persistent inequalities.

History

Traditional society emphasised filial piety and the 'three obediences'. The May Fourth Movement and the 1950 Marriage Law attacked arranged marriage and concubinage; Mao-era campaigns proclaimed that 'women hold up half the sky' and mobilised women into the workforce.

Contemporary issues

Modern debates include gender imbalance linked to the former one-child policy, workplace discrimination, the low share of women in top political leadership, domestic violence law, and feminist activism both online and offline.
