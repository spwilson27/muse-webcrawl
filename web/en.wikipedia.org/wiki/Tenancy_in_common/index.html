Tenancy in common

A tenancy in common (TIC) is a form of concurrent ownership of property in which each co-owner holds a distinct, undivided share that may be unequal and may be freely transferred or inherited. Unlike a joint tenancy, there is no right of survivorship: on a tenant in common's death, their share passes to their estate or heirs, not to the surviving owners.

Features

Each tenant in common has the right to possess the whole property regardless of share size. Shares can be created at different times and by different transfers. Co-owners may seek partition—division or sale of the property—if they cannot agree on its use.

Uses

Tenancy in common is common among unmarried co-buyers, business partners and investors, and in some cities for converting apartment buildings into separately saleable units.
