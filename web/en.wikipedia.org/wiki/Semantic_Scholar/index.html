Semantic Scholar

Semantic Scholar is a free, AI-powered research tool and academic search engine for scientific literature, developed at the Allen Institute for AI (AI2) in Seattle. It was publicly released in November 2015 and indexes well over 200 million publications drawn from publisher partnerships, web crawls, and sources such as PubMed, arXiv and the Association for Computational Linguistics.

Features

Unlike conventional keyword search engines, Semantic Scholar uses machine learning and natural language processing to extract meaning from papers: it identifies citations and figures, generates one-sentence TLDR summaries of papers, distinguishes highly influential citations from incidental ones, and builds author and topic pages. Its Semantic Reader augments PDFs with inline definitions and summary cards.

Data and API

The project publishes the Semantic Scholar Academic Graph (S2AG), a large knowledge graph of papers, authors, venues and citations, and offers public APIs and datasets used by researchers studying the science of science. Its corpus and citation data underpin tools for literature discovery, research alerts and bibliometric analysis.
