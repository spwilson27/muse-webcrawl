Susanne Wenger

Susanne Wenger MFR (4 July 1915 – 12 January 2009), also known by the Yoruba name Adunni Olorisha, was an Austrian-Nigerian artist and priestess of the Yoruba religion. She is principally known as the founder of the New Sacred Art movement and the driving force behind the revival and protection of the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove in Osogbo, Nigeria, which was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.

Life

Wenger was born in Graz, Austria, and trained in art in Graz and Vienna. She spent time in Paris before moving to Nigeria in 1950 with her husband, the linguist Ulli Beier, taking a post connection with the University of Ibadan. She settled in Osogbo, learned Yoruba, and became deeply involved in Yoruba religion, being initiated as a priestess of Obatala and later of Osun. Struck by the decay of the Osun grove, she worked with local artists and worshippers to rebuild its shrines using monumental cement sculptures, a project that continued for the rest of her life and founded the New Sacred Art school of artists.

Work and legacy

Her own work spans sculpture, batik, drawing, and painting, fusing European modernist training with Yoruba mythology. The grove she defended became both a living religious site and a centre of contemporary Nigerian art, and Nigerian artists formed in her circle include Adebisi Akanji and Twins Seven Seven. She remained in Osogbo until her death there in 2009.
