Dagger

A dagger is a fighting knife with a very sharp point and usually two sharp edges, designed primarily for thrusting or stabbing. Daggers have been used throughout human history in close combat confrontations and as ceremonial objects; many ancient cultures decorated daggers as symbols of wealth and status.

History

Stone and flint daggers date to the Neolithic period, followed by copper, bronze and iron examples across the ancient world. Well-known types include the Roman pugio, the medieval rondel and bollock daggers, the Scottish dirk, the Japanese tantō, and the Kris of Southeast Asia. In the Renaissance, the main-gauche or "left-hand dagger" was used with a rapier.

Modern use

Today daggers survive mainly as military fighting knives, ceremonial dress items (for example with Scottish Highland dress), and collectors' pieces. The word "dagger" is also used typographically for the obelus symbol (†), marking footnotes or obsolete names.
