Nha Trang is a coastal city and the capital of Khánh Hòa Province on the south-central coast of Vietnam. It is one of the country's best-known beach resorts, set on a wide crescent bay dotted with islands and backed by mountains, with a long palm-lined beachfront promenade. The city is a major centre for domestic and international tourism, fishing and aquaculture, and is home to Nha Trang University and Vietnam's Institute of Oceanography, founded in 1922.

History

The area was part of the Hindu-Buddhist kingdom of Champa; the Po Nagar Cham towers, built between the 7th and 12th centuries on a hill north of the Cái River and dedicated to the goddess Yan Po Nagar, remain the city's most famous historic site and are still used for worship. The town grew under French colonial rule as a seaside resort — the bacteriologist Alexandre Yersin, discoverer of the plague bacillus, lived and worked here from the 1890s and is buried nearby; the Pasteur Institute he founded still operates. During the Vietnam War the United States maintained a major presence, including at Cam Ranh Bay to the south. Nha Trang's beach was designated a town in 1937 and it became a provincial city in 1977; in 2009 it was recognised as a Class-1 city.

Tourism and economy

Tourism dominates the modern economy. Attractions include the 6-kilometre city beach, offshore islands such as Hòn Mun (a marine protected area popular for diving and snorkelling) and Hòn Tre, linked to the mainland by a long sea-crossing cable car and home to the Vinpearl resort and amusement park; mud baths; the Long Sơn Pagoda with its giant white Buddha; and Dam Market. The city hosted the Miss Universe 2008 pageant and the recurring Sea Festival. It is served by Cam Ranh International Airport, about 30 km south, and by the North–South Railway. Nha Trang is also noted for seafood, swiftlet (edible bird's nest) products from offshore islands, and fish sauce.

Climate

Nha Trang has a tropical savanna climate with a long dry season from January to August and a short wet season in autumn. Sheltered by surrounding mountains, it receives less rainfall than much of central Vietnam and enjoys about 300 days of sunshine a year, with average temperatures around 26–27 °C.
