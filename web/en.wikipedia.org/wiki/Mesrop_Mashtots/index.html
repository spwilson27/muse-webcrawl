Mesrop Mashtots

Bornc. 362, Hatsik, Taron, Kingdom of Armenia
Died17 February 440, Vagharshapat
Known forCreation of the Armenian alphabet
Venerated inArmenian Apostolic Church; Roman Catholic Church

Mesrop Mashtots (Armenian: Մեսրոպ Մաշտոց, Mesrop Maštocʿ; 362 – 17 February 440) was an Armenian linguist, composer, theologian, and statesman. He is venerated as a saint in the Armenian Apostolic Church and is best known for inventing the Armenian alphabet around 405 CE, a foundational event in Armenian cultural and national history.

Monument to Mesrop Mashtots in Yerevan

Life

Mesrop was born in the village of Hatsik in the province of Taron and educated in Greek, Syriac, and Persian. He served as a secretary and soldier at the royal court before embracing the monastic life and becoming a missionary in the border regions of Armenia. Confronted with the difficulty of teaching the Christian scriptures to Armenians in foreign languages and scripts, he conceived the need for a native alphabet. With the support of Catholicos Sahak Partev and King Vramshapuh, he developed the alphabet — tradition holds after a vision — settling its letter forms around 405, possibly with the help of the Greek calligrapher Rufanos of Samosata.

The alphabet and its legacy

The Armenian alphabet originally had 36 letters (two more were added in the Middle Ages). Mesrop and Sahak then organised schools and a great programme of translation, first rendering the Bible into Armenian — the "Queen of Translations" — followed by works of the Church Fathers and Greek learning, inaugurating the Golden Age of Armenian literature. Armenian tradition also credits Mesrop with a role in creating or inspiring the Georgian and Caucasian Albanian scripts, though this is debated by scholars. His biography was written by his pupil Koryun in the Life of Mashtots, the earliest surviving original work of Armenian literature. He is buried in Oshakan, where a church bearing his name stands over his tomb.
