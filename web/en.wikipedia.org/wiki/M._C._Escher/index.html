Maurits Cornelis Escher (17 June 1898 – 27 March 1972) was a Dutch graphic artist who made mathematically inspired woodcuts, lithographs and mezzotints. His work features impossible constructions, explorations of infinity, interlocking tessellations and architecture that defies gravity and perspective.

Life and work

Escher was born in Leeuwarden, the youngest son of a civil engineer, and studied at the School for Architecture and Decorative Arts in Haarlem. He travelled extensively in Italy and Spain; his visit to the Alhambra in Granada, with its Moorish tilings, profoundly influenced his interest in the regular division of the plane. From the late 1930s he concentrated on tessellations, transformations (as in the Metamorphosis series and Sky and Water) and impossible objects.

Famous works include Drawing Hands (1948), Relativity (1953), with its multiple gravity-defying staircases, Ascending and Descending (1960), based on the Penrose stairs, and Waterfall (1961), based on the Penrose triangle. Although he had little formal mathematical training, Escher corresponded with mathematicians including Roger Penrose and H. S. M. Coxeter, and his work became celebrated by scientists as well as the general public; Douglas Hofstadter's book Gödel, Escher, Bach (1979) further popularised his art.
