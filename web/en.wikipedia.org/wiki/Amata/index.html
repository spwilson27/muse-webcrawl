Amata
Appears in: Virgil's Aeneid
Spouse: Latinus
Children: Lavinia

Amata is a figure in Roman mythology, appearing in Virgil's epic poem the Aeneid. She was the wife of King Latinus of Latium and the mother of Lavinia, and according to tradition she was also an aunt of Turnus, the Rutulian prince.

Role in the Aeneid

Amata strongly opposed the marriage of her daughter Lavinia to the Trojan exile Aeneas, favouring Turnus instead. Inspired by the Fury Allecto, sent by Juno, she was driven into a frenzy, hid Lavinia in the mountains, and stirred the Latin women to protest the Trojan alliance. Her resistance helped ignite the war between the Trojans and the Latins that fills the second half of the poem. When she believed Turnus had been killed, Amata hanged herself in despair — an act that deepened Latinus's grief and weakened Latin resistance.

Interpretation

Commentators regard Amata as one of Virgil's most tragic figures: a queen whose maternal and dynastic concerns, manipulated by divine hostility, contribute to the destruction of her household even as Rome's founding proceeds.
