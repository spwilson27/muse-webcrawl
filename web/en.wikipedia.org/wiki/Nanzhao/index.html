Nanzhao

Nanzhao
Capital: Taihe (near modern Dali)
Existed: 738 – 902 CE
Preceded by: Six Zhao principalities
Succeeded by: Dachanghe, then Dali Kingdom

Nanzhao (Chinese: 南詔, "southern prince") was a dynastic state and empire that flourished in what is now southwestern China and Southeast Asia during the 8th and 9th centuries CE. Centred on the Erhai Lake basin around modern Dali in Yunnan, it was founded in 738 when Piluoge, chief of the southernmost of the six zhao (principalities), unified them with the support of Tang-dynasty China and received the title of King of Yunnan.

Expansion

Nanzhao soon broke with the Tang, allying with the Tibetan Empire and defeating Tang armies sent against it in 751 and 754. At its height in the mid-9th century, Nanzhao controlled Yunnan, parts of Sichuan, Guizhou and northern Vietnam, and campaigned as far as Chengdu (taken briefly in 829) and Hanoi. Its armies also raided the Pyu city-states of upper Burma.

Culture and decline

The kingdom was strongly Buddhist, influenced both by Tang esoteric Buddhism and by Southeast Asian traditions, and left notable monuments such as the pagodas of Dali. The ethnicity of its ruling elite — variously associated with the ancestors of the Bai and Yi peoples — remains debated. Nanzhao collapsed in 902 amid court coups; after brief successor regimes, Duan Siping founded the Dali Kingdom in 937.
