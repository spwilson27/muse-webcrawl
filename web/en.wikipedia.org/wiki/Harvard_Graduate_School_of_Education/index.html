Harvard Graduate School of Education

The Harvard Graduate School of Education (HGSE) is the education school of Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1920, it was the first school to grant the EdD degree and is one of the leading graduate schools of education in the United States.

History

HGSE grew out of Harvard's Division of Education and was formally established in 1920 under dean Henry Holmes. Its faculty has included figures who reshaped American education research and practice, and centres such as Project Zero — long associated with Howard Gardner — have conducted widely cited work on teaching and learning.

Programmes

The school offers a one-year master's degree (EdM) with concentrations in areas such as learning design, education policy, and human development, as well as the research-oriented PhD (administered jointly with the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences) and the practice-focused Doctor of Education Leadership (EdLD).
