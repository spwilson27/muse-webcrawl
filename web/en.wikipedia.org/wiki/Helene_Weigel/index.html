Helene Weigel

Helene Weigel (German pronunciation: [heˈleːnə ˈvaɪɡl̩]; 12 May 1900 – 6 May 1971) was an Austrian-German actress and theatre director, one of the outstanding interpreters of her husband Bertolt Brecht's plays and, from 1956 until her death, artistic director of the Berliner Ensemble, the theatre company the two of them founded in East Berlin.

Life and career

Born in Vienna into a Jewish family—her father was a textile company director and her mother ran a toy shop—Weigel trained as an actress and appeared in Frankfurt and Berlin in the 1920s, including in plays by Arnolt Bronnen and early works of Brecht, whom she married in 1929. With the Nazi seizure of power in 1933 the couple fled Germany, living in Denmark, Sweden, Finland and the United States; Weigel, whose Jewish background and communism made stage work impossible in Germany, worked little during exile, which she later described as years of enforced silence.

Returning to Berlin after the war, she played the title role in the landmark 1949 production of Mother Courage and Her Children, whose success led to the founding of the Berliner Ensemble. Her performance as the canteen-woman Courage—gritty, unsentimental and capped by the famous silent scream—became definitive. Other celebrated roles included Pelagea Vlassova in The Mother, the title role in Antigone, and Volumnia in Coriolanus. As intendant after Brecht's death in 1956 she preserved and toured the company's model productions while also admitting new work. She was a member of the Volkskammer of the GDR and died in Berlin in 1971; she is buried beside Brecht in the Dorotheenstadt Cemetery.
