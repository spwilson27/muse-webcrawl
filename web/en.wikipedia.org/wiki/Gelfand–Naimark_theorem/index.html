Gelfand–Naimark theorem

FieldFunctional analysis
Proved byIsrael Gelfand, Mark Naimark
Year1943
ConcernsC*-algebras

In mathematics, the Gelfand–Naimark theorem is a foundational result in functional analysis, proved by Israel Gelfand and Mark Naimark in 1943. It states that every C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to a concrete C*-algebra of bounded operators on some Hilbert space.

Statement

More precisely, for every C*-algebra A there exists a Hilbert space H and an injective *-homomorphism from A into the algebra B(H) of bounded linear operators on H that preserves norms. The standard proof proceeds via the Gelfand–Naimark–Segal (GNS) construction, which builds a representation from a state on the algebra, and then takes a direct sum over all states to obtain a faithful representation.

Significance

The theorem justifies the abstract axiomatic definition of a C*-algebra: no abstract C*-algebra exists that cannot be realised concretely as operators on a Hilbert space. Together with the commutative Gelfand–Naimark theorem, which identifies commutative C*-algebras with algebras of continuous functions on locally compact Hausdorff spaces, it underlies the philosophy that noncommutative topology is the study of general C*-algebras. It is also a cornerstone of the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics, where observables are modelled by operators on Hilbert space.
