Smoot–Hawley Tariff Act

Smoot–Hawley Tariff Act
Enacted: 17 June 1930
Sponsors: Sen. Reed Smoot, Rep. Willis C. Hawley
Signed by: President Herbert Hoover

The Smoot–Hawley Tariff Act, formally the Tariff Act of 1930, was United States legislation that raised tariffs on more than 20,000 imported goods to record levels. Sponsored by Senator Reed Smoot and Representative Willis C. Hawley, it was signed by President Herbert Hoover on 17 June 1930, despite a petition from over a thousand economists urging a veto.

Effects

Other countries retaliated with their own tariffs, and world trade collapsed during the early Great Depression — US imports and exports fell by more than half between 1929 and 1933. Economists debate how much of the Depression's severity Smoot–Hawley caused; the consensus is that it worsened and prolonged the downturn and damaged international cooperation, even if the Depression's main causes were monetary and financial.

Legacy

The Act became a byword for harmful protectionism. The Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act of 1934 began its reversal, shifting US policy toward negotiated tariff reductions that culminated in the GATT and later the WTO.
