Disk (mathematics)

FieldGeometry, topology
DefinitionRegion of a plane bounded by a circle
Areaπr²
BoundaryCircle

In geometry, a disk (also spelled disc) is the region in a plane bounded by a circle. A disk is said to be closed if it contains the circle that constitutes its boundary and open if it does not. The closed disk of radius r centred at a point C consists of all points at distance at most r from C.

Formulas

The area of a closed disk of radius r is πr². In Cartesian coordinates, the open disk centred at (a, b) with radius R is the set of points (x, y) satisfying (x − a)² + (y − b)² < R²; the closed disk uses ≤ in place of <.

Topology

In topology, the unit disk is a fundamental object: it is homeomorphic to any convex bounded subset of the plane and serves as the local model of a surface. The Poincaré disk model uses the interior of a disk as a model of hyperbolic geometry. Higher-dimensional analogues are balls, bounded by spheres.
