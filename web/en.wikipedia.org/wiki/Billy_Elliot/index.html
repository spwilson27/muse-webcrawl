Billy Elliot is a 2000 British coming-of-age comedy-drama film directed by Stephen Daldry and written by Lee Hall. Set in County Durham during the 1984–85 miners' strike, it stars Jamie Bell as the title character, an 11-year-old coal miner's son who discovers a passion for ballet against the wishes of his widowed father (Gary Lewis) and older brother, both striking miners. Julie Walters plays the ballet teacher, Mrs Wilkinson, and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress.

Plot

Billy, reluctantly attending boxing lessons, instead joins a ballet class held in the same hall and proves a natural talent. Concealing the lessons from his family, he eventually comes into conflict with his father, who wants him to be a boxer. When the family sacrifices to support Billy, and with money raised by the striking community, Billy auditions for the Royal Ballet School in London, culminating in an audition scene in which he describes dancing as being "like electricity". The film ends with Billy as an adult principal dancer in Matthew Bourne's Swan Lake, watched by his father and brother.

Reception and adaptations

The film was a critical and commercial success, earning three Academy Award nominations (director, supporting actress, original screenplay) and winning BAFTAs including Outstanding British Film. In 2005 it was adapted as Billy Elliot the Musical, with music by Elton John and book and lyrics by Lee Hall, which became a major West End and Broadway success, winning ten Tony Awards including Best Musical.
