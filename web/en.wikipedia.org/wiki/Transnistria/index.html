Transnistria, officially the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic (PMR), is an unrecognized breakaway state that is internationally recognized as part of Moldova. It controls a narrow strip of territory on the eastern bank of the Dniester River, together with the city of Bender (Tighina) on the western bank. Its capital and largest city is Tiraspol. No United Nations member state recognizes its independence; it is supported politically, economically, and militarily by Russia, which maintains troops on its territory.

History

The territory east of the Dniester formed part of the Moldavian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic within the Ukrainian SSR from 1924. In 1940 the Soviet Union combined most of Bessarabia with part of that autonomous republic to create the Moldavian SSR. As the USSR dissolved, fears in the Russophone east that Moldova might unite with Romania, together with language laws privileging Moldovan (Romanian), led local leaders to proclaim the Pridnestrovian Moldavian SSR in 1990. Tensions escalated into the Transnistria War of 1992, in which several hundred people were killed before a ceasefire — brokered with the involvement of the Russian 14th Army — froze the conflict. A Russian military presence has remained ever since, alongside a contingent guarding a large Soviet-era ammunition depot at Cobasna.

Status and negotiations

Moldova regards Transnistria as an autonomous territorial unit with special status within its borders. Negotiations in the "5+2" format — Moldova and Transnistria as parties; the OSCE, Russia, and Ukraine as mediators; the EU and US as observers — have produced limited practical agreements but no final settlement. A 2006 referendum in Transnistria endorsed independence and future association with Russia, but it was not recognized internationally. The European Court of Human Rights has held Russia responsible for violations in the region on the basis of its effective control.

Economy and society

Transnistria inherited much of Soviet Moldova's heavy industry, including the steel mill at Rîbnița and the Moldavskaya GRES power station at Dnestrovsc, which supplies electricity to Moldova proper. The region issues its own currency, the Transnistrian ruble, and its own passports, license plates, and stamps, none widely recognized. The Sheriff conglomerate dominates much of the economy and is influential in politics. The population is a mix of Russians, Moldovans/Romanians, and Ukrainians in roughly equal proportions, and Russian is the dominant lingua franca. The region's status became more precarious after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, which cut transit routes and deepened its dependence on gas supplies subsidized through Russian arrangements.
