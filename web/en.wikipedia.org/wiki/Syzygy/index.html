Syzygy

In astronomy, a syzygy (from Ancient Greek συζυγία, "yoked together") is a roughly straight-line configuration of three or more celestial bodies in a gravitational system. The word is most often used for the alignment of the Sun, Earth, and the Moon or a planet.

Types

An eclipse occurs at syzygy: a solar eclipse when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth at new moon, and a lunar eclipse when the Earth lies between the Sun and the full Moon. A transit and an occultation are also syzygies, as are the conjunctions and oppositions of planets. Syzygy of the Sun, Earth, and Moon is responsible for spring tides, when tidal ranges are greatest.

Usage

The term is also applied to configurations of the Galilean moons of Jupiter and is used loosely in computing and popular culture for any notable alignment.
