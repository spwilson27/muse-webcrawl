Charlie Munger

Charles Thomas Munger (January 1, 1924 – November 28, 2023) was an American businessman, investor, and philanthropist. He was vice chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, the conglomerate controlled by Warren Buffett, who described him as his closest partner and right-hand man. Munger was also chairman of Wesco Financial Corporation from 1984 to 2011 and of the Daily Journal Corporation.

Early life and career

Munger grew up in Omaha, a few blocks from Warren Buffett's later home, and as a teenager worked at Buffett & Son, the grocery store owned by Buffett's grandfather. He studied mathematics at the University of Michigan, served as a meteorologist in the U.S. Army Air Corps during the Second World War, and graduated from Harvard Law School in 1948 without an undergraduate degree. He co-founded the Los Angeles law firm Munger, Tolles & Olson in 1962, then left law to run his own investment partnership (1962–1975), which compounded at about 19% annually.

Berkshire Hathaway

Munger, who had known Buffett since 1959, became Berkshire's vice chairman in 1978. He is credited with moving Buffett away from buying mediocre companies at bargain prices toward buying excellent companies at fair prices — "forget what you know about buying fair businesses at wonderful prices; instead, buy wonderful businesses at fair prices." The two men's annual-meeting question-and-answer sessions became famous for Munger's blunt wit and his advocacy of multidisciplinary "mental models" and a "latticework" of knowledge; his talks were collected in Poor Charlie's Almanack (2005).

Philanthropy and death

Munger gave hundreds of millions of dollars to universities, notably the University of Michigan and Stanford, often for student residences he helped design. He died in Santa Barbara on November 28, 2023, a month short of his 100th birthday.
