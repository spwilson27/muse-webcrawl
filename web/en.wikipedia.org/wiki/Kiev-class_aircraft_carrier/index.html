Kiev-class aircraft carrier

The Kiev class (Soviet designation Project 1143 Krechyet) was a class of four heavy aircraft-carrying cruisers built for the Soviet Navy in the 1970s and 1980s: Kiev, Minsk, Novorossiysk and Baku (later Admiral Gorshkov). Uniquely among aircraft carriers, the Kiev class combined a heavy anti-ship missile armament on the foredeck with an angled flight deck aft operating Yak-38 VTOL fighters and helicopters; the Soviet Union officially classed them as "heavy aircraft-carrying cruisers" partly to comply with the Montreux Convention's restrictions on carriers passing the Turkish Straits.

Design

Unlike Western carriers, the Kiev class were primarily guided-missile cruisers that also operated aircraft: the bow carried eight (later twelve) large P-500 Bazalt anti-ship cruise missiles, surface-to-air missile launchers and anti-submarine weapons, while the angled deck and island supported flight operations. Their Yak-38 fighters, hampered by limited range and payload, were mainly used for reconnaissance and limited strike roles; helicopters performed anti-submarine and search-and-rescue duties.

Fates

After the Cold War the first three ships were retired and sold: Kiev and Minsk became floating theme parks in China, and Novorossiysk was scrapped in South Korea. Admiral Gorshkov was extensively rebuilt with a full-length flight deck and ski jump and sold to India, commissioning in 2013 as INS Vikramaditya.
