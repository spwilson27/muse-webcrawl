Jaffa (Hebrew: יָפוֹ, Yafo; Arabic: يَافَا, Yāfā), also called Japho or Joppa in older sources, is an ancient Levantine port city on the Mediterranean coast, now part of Tel Aviv-Yafo, Israel. Among the oldest continuously used ports in the world, Jaffa has been settled for some 4,000 years; tradition associates it with Japheth, son of Noah, and with the biblical story of Jonah, who is said to have set sail from here. For millennia the port served as the maritime gateway to Jerusalem, and Jaffa oranges became one of the most famous citrus brands in the world.

History

Jaffa appears in Egyptian records of the 15th century BCE and was conquered by Pharaoh Thutmose III, an episode retold in the Egyptian tale of the taking of Joppa. It changed hands among Philistines, Israelites, Phoenicians, Persians, Greeks and Romans; the apostle Peter's vision at the house of Simon the Tanner is set here in the Book of Acts. Muslim armies took Jaffa in the 7th century; Crusaders and Ayyubids fought over it repeatedly, and Richard the Lionheart refortified it after the Treaty of Jaffa of 1192. Under Ottoman rule it revived as the principal port of Palestine. Napoleon captured Jaffa in 1799, in a siege followed by the massacre of prisoners and a notorious plague among his troops. In the 19th century Jaffa grew into a prosperous commercial city; its orange groves expanded rapidly, and the first railway in the region (to Jerusalem) opened in 1892.

20th century

In 1909, Jewish families from Jaffa founded the garden suburb of Ahuzat Bayit, which became Tel Aviv. Jaffa was a centre of Palestinian political and cultural life under the British Mandate, and a flashpoint in the riots of 1921 and the revolt of 1936–39, when the British blew up parts of the old city. During the 1948 war, most of Jaffa's roughly 70,000 Arab inhabitants fled or were expelled, largely by sea; the city surrendered to Israeli forces on 13 May 1948. In 1950 Jaffa was unified with Tel Aviv as one municipality.

Present day

Old Jaffa is now an arts and tourism quarter of galleries, restaurants and the flea market (Shuk HaPishpeshim), with landmarks including the clock tower (1900), St Peter's Church, the Mahmoudiya Mosque, the zodiac alleys of the old city, and the fishing port, still active. The neighbourhood of Ajami retains much of Jaffa's remaining Arab population, and the city is a focus of debates over gentrification and shared urban space.
