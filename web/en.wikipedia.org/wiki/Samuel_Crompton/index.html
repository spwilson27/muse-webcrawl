Samuel Crompton

Samuel Crompton
Born: 3 December 1753, Bolton, Lancashire, England
Died: 26 June 1827 (aged 73), Bolton
Known for: Spinning mule

Samuel Crompton (3 December 1753 – 26 June 1827) was an English inventor and pioneer of the spinning industry. Building on the work of James Hargreaves and Richard Arkwright, he invented the spinning mule, a machine that revolutionised the textile industry worldwide and became a cornerstone of the Industrial Revolution.

Early life

Crompton was born at 10 Firwood Fold, Bolton, the eldest son of George and Betty Crompton. The family supplemented its income from farming with handloom weaving and spinning. After his father's death, Samuel contributed to the family's resources by spinning yarn on Hargreaves's spinning jenny. The defects of the jenny — particularly the frequent breaking of the thread — impressed upon Crompton the need for improvement.

The spinning mule

For about five years Crompton worked in secret at Hall i' th' Wood, perfecting his machine. Completed in 1779, the mule combined the moving carriage of the jenny with the rollers of Arkwright's water frame, producing yarn that was finer, stronger, and more even than any previously spun. He called it a "mule" because it was a hybrid of the two machines; it was also known at first as the "muslin wheel" because it allowed spinners to produce fine muslin-quality yarns.

Loss of the invention

Unable to afford a patent and fearful of machine-breakers, Crompton was persuaded to disclose his invention to local manufacturers in return for promises of payment that largely went unfulfilled. The mule was rapidly adopted — by 1812 there were between four and five million mule spindles in use. Parliament eventually granted him £5,000 in 1812, far less proportionate to the fortune others made from his invention. He died in 1827 and was buried in the churchyard of St Peter's, Bolton.

Legacy

A statue of Crompton was erected in Bolton in 1862, one of the first public statues raised by popular subscription to an inventor. The original mule is preserved by the Bolton Museum.
