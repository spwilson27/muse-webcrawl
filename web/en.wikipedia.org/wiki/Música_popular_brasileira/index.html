Música popular brasileira

Música popular brasileira (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈmuzikɐ popuˈlaʁ bɾaziˈlejɾɐ]; literally "Brazilian popular music"), better known by the acronym MPB, is a trend or movement in Brazilian popular music that emerged in the mid-1960s, after the bossa nova boom. MPB is not a single genre but an umbrella for a generation of singer-songwriters who mixed traditional Brazilian rhythms — above all samba — with foreign influences such as jazz, rock, and folk, and with literate, often politically engaged lyrics.

MPB grew out of televised song festivals of the 1960s and came to define Brazil's singer-songwriter tradition.

History

The movement crystallised in 1965–66 around the televised music festivals of TV Record and TV Excelsior, where songs such as "Arrastão" (performed by Elis Regina) and "Disparada" (Geraldo Vandré) won mass acclaim. Artists including Chico Buarque, Gilberto Gil, Caetano Veloso, Edu Lobo, Milton Nascimento, Elis Regina, Gal Costa, and Maria Bethânia became the movement's leading figures. In 1967–68 the related Tropicália movement radicalised MPB by embracing electric guitars, concrete poetry, and pop art; Gil and Veloso were imprisoned and exiled by the military dictatorship in 1969.

Characteristics

MPB is marked by sophisticated harmony inherited from bossa nova, poetic lyrics drawing on modernist literature, and a fluid fusion of regional Brazilian styles — samba, baião, maracatu, frevo — with international pop. Under the dictatorship (1964–1985) much MPB carried coded protest, and its leading artists became symbols of cultural resistance.

Legacy

MPB remains a loose umbrella term applied to subsequent generations of Brazilian singer-songwriters, from Djavan and Ivan Lins to Marisa Monte and Lenine. It is one of Brazil's most successful cultural exports and is often taught as the country's canonical popular-music tradition.
