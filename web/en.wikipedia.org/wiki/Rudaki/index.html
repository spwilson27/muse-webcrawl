Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Rudaki (Persian: ابوعبدالله جعفر بن محمد رودکی; c. 858 – 941), better known as Rudaki, was a Persian poet, singer, and musician who is regarded as the first great literary genius of the Modern Persian language. He is considered the founder of classical Persian literature and is often called the "father of Persian poetry".

Rudaki was born in the village of Rudak (also spelled Rodak), near Samarkand, in what is now Tajikistan. According to tradition he was blind, although some modern scholars dispute whether he was blind from birth or blinded later in life. Despite this, his poetry displays a vivid knowledge of colour and visual detail.

Life

Rudaki was summoned to the court of the Samanid emir Nasr II (914–943) in Bukhara, where he became the court poet and a close companion of the ruler. He amassed great wealth and prestige — sources describe him travelling with a retinue of two hundred slaves — but after the fall of his patron, the vizier Abu'l-Fadl Bal'ami, Rudaki fell from favour, was dismissed from court, and spent his final years in poverty in his native village, where he died in 941.

Works

Rudaki composed poetry in nearly every major form, including the qasida (ode), ghazal (lyric), masnavi (rhyming couplets), and rubai (quatrain). Of his enormous reported output — tradition credits him with over a million verses — only a small fraction, roughly a thousand lines, survives. His most famous undertaking was the versification of the Kalila wa Dimna, a collection of animal fables of Indian origin, into Persian verse; the work is now lost apart from scattered quotations.

His best-known surviving poem is the qasida beginning "The scent of the river Muliyan comes to me" (Buy-i Ju-yi Muliyan), said to have persuaded Nasr II, lingering in Herat, to return at once to Bukhara.

Legacy

Rudaki is a national cultural hero in Tajikistan, where the main avenue of Dushanbe and numerous institutions bear his name. The year 2008 was proclaimed the Year of Rudaki by UNESCO in honour of the 1150th anniversary of his birth. He is also celebrated in Iran and Afghanistan as a founding figure of New Persian literature.
