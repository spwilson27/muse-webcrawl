Mars (mythology)

In ancient Roman religion and mythology, Mars was the god of war and also an agricultural guardian, a combination characteristic of early Rome. He was the son of Jupiter and Juno, father of Romulus, and equivalent in many respects to Greek Ares, though more dignified and popular among Romans.

AbodeMount Olympus / Campus Martius
ParentsJupiter and Juno
Greek equivalentAres
SymbolsSpear, shield, wolf, woodpecker

Cult

Mars was second only to Jupiter in the Roman pantheon. The month Martius (March), named for him, opened the campaigning season with festivals. The Campus Martius, Field of Mars, and the temple of Mars Ultor in Augustus's forum were central to his worship.

Mythology

With Rhea Silvia, Mars fathered Romulus and Remus, founders of Rome, giving Romans divine ancestry. His consort associations include Nerio, Bellona, and Venus.
