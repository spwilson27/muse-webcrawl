Edam (Dutch pronunciation: [eːˈdɑm]) is a town in the northwest Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. Combined with Volendam, it forms the municipality of Edam-Volendam. Edam is famous worldwide for the cheese of the same name — round cheeses traditionally coated in red paraffin wax — and for its picturesque canals, drawbridges, and historic cheese market square.

History

The town originated around a dam (dam) built on the Ye (IJe) river where it flowed into the Zuiderzee — hence "Ye-dam," Edam. Edam received city rights in 1357 from Count William V of Holland. Its harbour made it a trading and shipbuilding town: Edam prospered on herring fishing, trade with the Baltic, and cheese. In 1526 Emperor Charles V granted Edam the right to hold a weekly cheese market and to levy weighing fees, a privilege that underpinned the town's economy for centuries; its shipyards also built vessels for the Dutch merchant fleet, including, in the seventeenth century, ships associated with voyages of exploration.

The damming of the town's sea access in the seventeenth century (with a lock built after flood damage) ended its role as a major port, and Edam settled into life as a market town serving the surrounding dairy polders. The old cheese market ceased commercial operations in 1922, but was revived in the twentieth century as a traditional re-enactment for visitors: on Wednesday mornings in July and August, porters in white uniforms carry cheeses on wooden barrows to the scales of the 1778 weigh house (Kaaswaag) in a ceremony recalling the historic market.

Sights

The historic centre preserves many seventeenth- and eighteenth-century houses, tea houses along the canals, and characteristic wooden drawbridges. Landmarks include the Grote Kerk (St. Nicolaaskerk), a large late-Gothic church with notable stained glass; the small leaning Carillon tower (Speeltoren, 1560s) of the demolished Church of Our Lady; the Edams Museum, housed partly in a merchant's house of c. 1540 with a floating cellar; and the Kaaswaag with its cheese-market square. The town sits within cycling distance of Volendam and Marken and is a popular day trip from Amsterdam, some 20 km to the south.

Edam cheese

Edam cheese is a semi-hard cow's-milk cheese from the region, traditionally sold in spheres with flattened ends, coated in red wax for export. Mild, slightly salty and nutty, young Edam slices easily; aged Edam hardens and intensifies. Alongside Gouda, it defined the Dutch cheese trade for centuries: the durable, wax-coated wheels kept well aboard ships, which carried the cheese's fame — and its name — around the world.
