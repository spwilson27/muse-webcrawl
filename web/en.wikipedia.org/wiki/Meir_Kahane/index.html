Meir Kahane

Meir David HaKohen Kahane (August 1, 1932 – November 5, 1990) was an American-born Israeli Orthodox rabbi, writer, and politician. He founded the Jewish Defense League (JDL) in the United States in 1968 and the Kach party in Israel. Kahane advocated a form of religious Zionism often termed Kahanism, which called for a theocratic Jewish state and the transfer of Arabs from Israel and the occupied territories. His views were widely condemned as racist; Kach was barred from the Knesset in 1988 under anti-racism legislation, and organizations linked to him were later designated as terrorist by Israel, the United States, and others.

Early life

Kahane was born in Brooklyn, New York, to an Orthodox Jewish family. He studied at the Mir Yeshiva, was ordained as a rabbi, and also earned degrees from Brooklyn College and New York University. As a young man he was active in the Betar Zionist youth movement.

Jewish Defense League

In 1968 Kahane founded the Jewish Defense League in New York, initially to protect elderly and poor Jews in changing neighbourhoods, and later campaigning on behalf of Soviet Jewry. The JDL was associated with violent tactics, and Kahane was convicted in the United States in connection with conspiracy to manufacture explosives, receiving a suspended sentence.

In Israel

Kahane emigrated to Israel in 1971 and founded the Kach movement. After several failed attempts, he was elected to the Knesset in 1984, serving a single term before his party was banned from the 1988 elections. On 5 November 1990, after speaking in New York City, Kahane was assassinated by El Sayyid Nosair, an Egyptian-born American citizen.
