The Four Tops, whose original line-up stayed together for over four decades

The Four Tops are an American vocal quartet from Detroit, Michigan, who helped define the Motown sound of the 1960s. Remarkably, their original line-up — Levi Stubbs, Abdul "Duke" Fakir, Renaldo "Obie" Benson, and Lawrence Payton — performed together from 1953 until Payton's death in 1997, a run of 44 years without a personnel change.

Career

Signed to Motown in 1963, the group worked with the songwriting team Holland–Dozier–Holland and reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 with "I Can't Help Myself (Sugar Pie Honey Bunch)" (1965) and "Reach Out I'll Be There" (1966), the latter often called their signature song. Other major hits include "Baby I Need Your Loving," "Bernadette," "Standing in the Shadows of Love," and "It's the Same Old Song." After leaving Motown in the 1970s they scored further hits such as "Ain't No Woman (Like the One I've Got)."

Honours

The Four Tops were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2009.
