The Königsallee (literally "King's Avenue"), affectionately known as the Kö, is an urban boulevard in Düsseldorf, Germany, famous for its canal, chestnut-lined banks, and concentration of luxury shops, banks, and hotels.

Layout

The avenue runs north–south through the Stadtmitte district. A landscaped canal — the Stadtgraben, fed by the Düssel river — occupies its centre, crossed by ornate bridges and overlooked by the Triton Fountain at its northern end. The eastern side is lined with flagship stores of international luxury brands, while the western side is characterised by banks, offices, and hotels.

History

The boulevard was laid out after the demolition of Düsseldorf's fortifications in the early 19th century and received its present name in 1851, following an incident in which King Frederick William IV of Prussia was pelted with horse manure; the city council renamed the street "King's Avenue" in apology. Today it is one of Germany's most prestigious retail addresses.
