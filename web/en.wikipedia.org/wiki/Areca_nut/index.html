Areca nut

Areca nuts, whole and sliced

The areca nut is the fruit of the areca palm (Areca catechu), which grows in much of the tropical Pacific, South Asia, Southeast Asia and parts of eastern Africa. It is commonly chewed together with betel leaf in a preparation known as betel nut or paan, a practice with a history of several thousand years in Asia. The nut is also known as betel nut, although it is the leaf of the betel vine (Piper betle), a different plant, that supplies the "betel" part of the name.

Use

In India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, southern China, Taiwan, Malaysia, Indonesia, Papua New Guinea and the Philippines, slices or shreds of the nut are wrapped in a betel leaf with slaked lime and often other flavourings such as cardamom, cloves, catechu and tobacco. Chewing produces a mild stimulant effect and a sense of well-being, turns the saliva red and stains the teeth.

The principal active alkaloid in the nut is arecoline, which acts on the central nervous system in a manner broadly comparable to nicotine.

Health effects

Regular chewing of areca nut is strongly associated with oral cancer and oral submucous fibrosis. The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies betel quid with and without tobacco, and the areca nut itself, as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1). Habitual use is also linked to dependence, and it contributes to poor oral health and stained, damaged teeth. Several governments have run public-health campaigns to discourage the practice.

Cultivation and trade

The areca palm is cultivated commercially across South and Southeast Asia. India is the largest producer and consumer of the nut. The fruit is an orange-yellow drupe; the nut is the seed inside, harvested at varying ripeness, then dried, cured or boiled depending on regional preferences. In Taiwan, betel nut has historically been sold at distinctive roadside stalls, while in Papua New Guinea the trade in "buai" is an important part of the informal economy.
