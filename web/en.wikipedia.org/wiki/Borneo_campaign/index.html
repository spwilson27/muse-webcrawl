Borneo campaign

Date1 May – late 1945
LocationBorneo, South East Asia
CombatantsAustralia, United States, and allies vs. Imperial Japan
ResultAllied victory

The Borneo campaign of 1945 was the last major Allied campaign of the South West Pacific Area in the Second World War. A series of amphibious landings by Australian forces, supported by American air and naval units, sought to liberate Japanese-held British and Dutch Borneo and secure its oil fields and bases.

Background

Japanese forces had occupied Borneo since early 1942, exploiting the oilfields at Miri, Seria, and Balikpapan. By 1945 the island had been bypassed by the main Allied advance, but its resources and its position astride routes toward Java made it a target for the final offensives of the war.

Operations

The campaign began on 1 May 1945 with the landing on Tarakan Island, followed in June by landings in Brunei Bay and at Labuan (Operation Oboe Six), and on 1 July by the landing at Balikpapan — the largest amphibious operation undertaken by Australian forces during the war. Australian I Corps, commanded by Lieutenant General Leslie Morshead, conducted the operations with extensive United States naval and air support.

Aftermath

Fighting continued until the Japanese surrender in August 1945. The campaign has been the subject of historical debate: some critics argued that by 1945 the operations had limited strategic value, while defenders point to the liberation of prisoners and civilians and the recovery of the oilfields. Australian casualties numbered roughly 2,000 killed and wounded across the campaign.
