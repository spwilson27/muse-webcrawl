iBeacon

iBeacon is a protocol developed by Apple and introduced at the Apple Worldwide Developers Conference in 2013. It uses Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) proximity sensing to transmit a universally unique identifier that compatible applications can pick up, enabling smartphones to determine their position within a building or other micro-location and to trigger location-based actions.

Operation

An iBeacon transmission consists of a 16-byte UUID together with "major" and "minor" values (two bytes each) that allow a deployment to be subdivided—for example, one UUID for a whole retail chain, a major value per store, and a minor value per department. Receiving devices estimate distance from signal strength, classing devices as immediate, near, or far. Beacons are typically small, inexpensive, battery-powered transmitters with a range of up to about 70 metres.

Uses

Applications include indoor navigation, museum and exhibition guides, retail offers and contactless payments, event check-in, and asset tracking. Apple Stores themselves deployed iBeacons to greet customers and provide product information. Google's rival Eddystone format was announced in 2015.
