Spirit (rover)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spirit (rover)
OperatorNASA / JPL
Launch10 June 2003
Landing4 January 2004, Gusev Crater
Mission length6 years (planned 90 days)
Spirit in the Columbia Hills (artist's concept).

Spirit, formally Mars Exploration Rover-A (MER-A), was the first of NASA's twin Mars Exploration Rovers, landing in Gusev Crater on 4 January 2004 for what was planned as a 90-day mission. It worked for nearly six years, crossing a plain, climbing the Columbia Hills, and finding some of the strongest evidence that Mars was once wet and geologically violent.

Spirit's discoveries included goethite and hematite minerals formed in water, volcanic rocks altered by steam, and — when a stuck wheel dragged a trench through the soil in 2007 — bright silica deposits nearly pure enough to suggest ancient hot springs or fumaroles, environments where microbial life thrives on Earth.

End of mission

The dragging wheel (a failed right-front wheel from 2006) eventually mired Spirit in soft sand at a site called Troy in 2009. Unable to reach a sun-facing winter position, it fell silent in March 2010 as winter cold gripped the site, and NASA ended recovery attempts in 2011. Spirit's legacy, with Opportunity, is the proof that twin, long-lived rovers could rewrite a planet's history.
