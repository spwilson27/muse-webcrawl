Stephen Samuel Wise

Stephen Samuel Wise (17 March 1874 – 19 April 1949) was an American Reform rabbi and Zionist leader, one of the most influential Jewish figures in the United States in the first half of the 20th century. Born in Budapest and raised in New York, he was ordained in 1893 and served congregations in New York and Portland, Oregon, before founding the Free Synagogue in New York City in 1907.

Leadership and activism

Wise was a founder or leader of the American Jewish Congress, the Zionist Organization of America, and the World Jewish Congress (1936), which he headed until his death. He founded the Jewish Institute of Religion in 1922 to train rabbis. A confidant of Presidents Woodrow Wilson and Franklin D. Roosevelt, he advocateded for social justice, civil liberties and labor rights, and helped found the NAACP and the ACLU.

World War II era

In 1942 Wise publicly confirmed — after receiving the Riegner telegram — that Nazi Germany was systematically murdering European Jews, and he pressed the Roosevelt administration, controversially and with limited success, for rescue action.
