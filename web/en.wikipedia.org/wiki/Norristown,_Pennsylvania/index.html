Norristown, Pennsylvania

Norristown is a borough in Montgomery County, Pennsylvania, United States, and its county seat. Located on the Schuylkill River about six miles (10 km) northwest of Philadelphia, it had a population of roughly 35,000 at the 2020 census. It is formally styled the Municipality of Norristown.

History

The land was purchased from William Penn's family connections and named for Isaac Norris, an early landowner and colonial statesman. When Montgomery County was created in 1784, Norristown became its seat. The borough grew in the 19th century as a milling and manufacturing center on the Schuylkill, aided by the Schuylkill Canal and later railroads; textile mills, iron works, and marble quarries were major industries.

Postwar suburbanization drew residents and commerce to surrounding townships, and Norristown experienced economic decline, but recent decades have brought redevelopment efforts, a growing arts scene, and significant Latino immigration that has reshaped the borough's culture and businesses.

Transportation

Norristown Transportation Center is one of SEPTA's busiest suburban hubs, serving the Manayunk/Norristown regional rail line, the Norristown High Speed Line to Upper Darby, and numerous bus routes. The Schuylkill River Trail passes through the borough, and Elmwood Park, with its zoo, is a principal local attraction.
