Iapetus Ocean
Existed: c. 600–400 million years ago
Between: Laurentia, Baltica, Avalonia
Named for: Titan Iapetus, father of Atlas

The Iapetus Ocean was an ocean that existed in the late Neoproterozoic and Palaeozoic eras, between the palaeocontinents of Laurentia, Baltica and Avalonia, in roughly the position of the modern southern North Atlantic. It is named after the Titan Iapetus, father of Atlas — the Atlantic Ocean's mythological namesake — making Iapetus the Atlantic's geological 'father'.

History

The ocean opened around 600 million years ago as the supercontinent Rodinia (or Pannotia) rifted apart. It reached maximum width — perhaps 5,000 km — in the Cambrian and Ordovician, hosting island arcs and distinct trilobite faunas on its opposing shores, evidence used by J. Tuzo Wilson in 1966 in a foundation paper of plate tectonics. Subduction progressively closed it: collisions produced the Caledonian orogeny (Scotland, Scandinavia), the Taconic and Acadian orogenies (Appalachians) as Avalonia and then Gondwana accreted. By the Devonian, about 400 million years ago, Iapetus had vanished and the continents had merged toward Pangaea.

Traces

Its suture runs through the British Isles — roughly along the Solway–Shannon line separating different basement and fossil provinces — and through Newfoundland and the northern Appalachians. See Plate tectonics and Caledonian orogeny.
