Emperor Seimu

Emperor Seimu (成務天皇, Seimu-tennō), also known as Wakatarashihiko no Sumeramikoto, was the 13th legendary emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. Very little is known about Seimu, and historians regard him as a legendary figure whose existence cannot be verified; conventional dates assign his reign to AD 131 to 191.

Legendary narrative

Seimu is recorded in the Kojiki (712) and Nihon Shoki (720) as a son of Emperor Keikō and Yasakairihime. He was named crown prince in 121 and succeeded his father in 131. The chronicles credit him with administrative reforms: he is said to have appointed the first provincial governors (kuni no miyatsuko and agatanushi) and to have fixed the boundaries of provinces, districts, and villages, an early step in organising the country's local administration.

Life and legacy

According to the traditional account, Seimu had no children, and he therefore named his nephew, Tarashinakatsuhiko — the son of the legendary hero Yamato Takeru — as his heir, the first instance in the tradition of succession passing outside a direct father-to-son line. His imperial tomb (misasagi) is conventionally identified with a kofun in the Saki district of Nara, known as the Sakinotatanami no misasagi.

No firm dates can be assigned to the lives or reigns of the emperors of this period; the names and sequence of the early emperors were not confirmed as "traditional" until the reign of Emperor Kanmu in the late eighth century.
