The Catholic Church in South Korea is part of the worldwide Catholic Church in communion with the Pope in Rome. Uniquely, the Korean church was founded by Korean lay scholars rather than foreign missionaries: in 1784 the scholar Yi Seung-hun was baptised in Beijing and returned to baptise others, forming a lay-led community that survived repeated persecutions.

History

Joseon authorities, alarmed by Catholic rejection of Confucian ancestral rites, launched persecutions in 1801, 1839, 1846 and 1866; the last, the Byeongin Persecution, killed some 8,000 Korean Catholics and nine French missionaries. Andrew Kim Taegon, ordained in 1845 as the first Korean priest, was martyred in 1846. Religious freedom followed the treaty era of the 1880s. During the 20th century the church grew steadily, and it played a visible role in the pro-democracy movement of the 1970s–80s, with Cardinal Stephen Kim Sou-hwan a prominent voice.

Recent history

Pope John Paul II visited in 1984, canonising 103 Korean martyrs in Seoul, and again in 1989. Pope Francis visited in 2014, beatifying 124 more martyrs. In 2021 Pope Francis appointed Bishop Lazarus You Heung-sik as prefect of the Congregation for the Clergy, the first Korean to head a Vatican dicastery, and created him cardinal in 2022. Seoul was announced as host of World Youth Day 2027.

Demographics

Catholics number roughly six million — around 11 percent of South Korea's population — and the church is notable for steady growth, a high proportion of adult converts, and strong lay organisations. The church in North Korea is suppressed; the Diocese of Pyongyang persists formally with an apostolic administrator based in Seoul.
