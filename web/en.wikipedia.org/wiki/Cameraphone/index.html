Cameraphone

A cameraphone (or camera phone) is a mobile telephone that incorporates a digital camera, allowing the user to take photographs — and later video — and to send them to others, typically via the cellular network. Cameraphones became widespread in the early 2000s and eventually displaced most standalone compact cameras.

History

The first commercial cameraphone is generally considered to be the Sharp J-SH04, released in Japan by J-Phone in November 2000; it could transmit photographs electronically over the mobile network. In South Korea, Samsung released the SCH-V200 in June 2000, which contained a camera but required a computer to retrieve the images. Kyocera's VP-210 of 1999, a videophone, is also sometimes cited.

Spread

Cameraphones spread rapidly after 2002, when Nokia's 7650 and Sony Ericsson's T68i (with a clip-on camera) reached Western markets. Improvements in sensor resolution, autofocus, flash and image processing followed quickly. The arrival of the smartphone, particularly the iPhone from 2007, made the cameraphone the default camera for most consumers.

Impact

The ubiquity of cameraphones transformed photography, citizen journalism and social media, making photographic documentation of news events by bystanders routine. It also raised debates about privacy, photography bans in sensitive locations, and the decline of the dedicated camera industry.
