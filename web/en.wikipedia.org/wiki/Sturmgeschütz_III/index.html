Sturmgeschütz III

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type: Assault gun / tank destroyer
Place of origin: Nazi Germany
In service: 1940–1945 (Germany)
Number built: approx. 10,000
Crew: 4
Main armament: 7.5 cm StuK 40 L/48 (late models)

The Sturmgeschütz III (StuG III) was an assault gun produced by Germany during the Second World War. Built on the chassis of the Panzer III tank, it was the most-produced fully tracked German armoured fighting vehicle of the war, and the second-most produced German armoured combat vehicle of any type after the Sd.Kfz. 251 half-track.

Development

The StuG concept originated in 1936, when then-Colonel Erich von Manstein proposed infantry-support assault artillery — a turretless armoured vehicle mounting a gun capable of destroying bunkers and fortifications in direct support of infantry attacks. Daimler-Benz developed the vehicle using the Panzer III chassis, and the first production vehicles entered service in 1940, seeing action in the Battle of France.

Design

Lacking a turret, the StuG III had a low silhouette and a fixed casemate superstructure in which the gun was mounted with limited traverse. Early versions carried the short-barrelled 7.5 cm StuK 37 L/24 howitzer for infantry support. As the war progressed and German forces encountered heavily armoured Soviet tanks, the design was up-gunned with the long-barrelled 7.5 cm StuK 40 L/43 and later L/48 guns, transforming it into an effective tank destroyer. Later models received side skirts (Schürzen), spaced armour, a machine gun for close defence, and in some cases a remote-controlled machine-gun mount.

Combat history

StuG IIIs served on all fronts and were credited with destroying large numbers of enemy tanks; their low profile made them well suited to ambush. They were operated chiefly by the artillery arm in Sturmgeschütz detachments. Finland used StuG IIIs with considerable success during the Continuation War, and vehicles were also supplied to several Axis-allied states. After the war, captured and surplus examples served for years in countries such as Syria.

Variants

Production models ran from Ausführung A through the definitive Ausf. G, of which some 7,700 were built. Related vehicles included the Sturmhaubitze 42, which mounted a 10.5 cm howitzer, and the flamethrower-equipped StuG III (Flamm).
