Information Age

The Information Age, also called the Computer Age or Digital Age, is the historical period beginning in the late 20th century characterised by a rapid shift from economies based on traditional industry to economies based on information technology. In this era, information and knowledge became central economic resources, and the creation, storage, transmission, and manipulation of data were transformed by the transistor, the personal computer, and the Internet.

Origins

The foundations were laid with the invention of the transistor in 1947, the integrated circuit in the late 1950s, and Claude Shannon's mathematical theory of communication (1948). Economists such as Fritz Machlup and Marc Porat measured the growing share of "information work" in advanced economies, and sociologist Daniel Bell described the coming of a "post-industrial society" in 1973. The microcomputer revolution of the late 1970s and 1980s, and the opening of the Internet and World Wide Web to the public in the 1990s, made computing and connectivity mass phenomena.

Characteristics

Defining features include the digitisation of media and records, near-instant global communication, the growth of knowledge work and service industries, e-commerce, social media, and an unprecedented volume of data — trends later extended by mobile computing, cloud services, and artificial intelligence. The Information Age has reshaped education, politics, entertainment, and personal life, while raising new problems: misinformation, privacy erosion, cybercrime, the digital divide, and the concentration of power in large technology platforms.
