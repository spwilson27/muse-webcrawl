Imagery intelligence

Imagery intelligence (IMINT), sometimes also called photo intelligence (PHOTINT), is an intelligence gathering discipline which collects information via satellites and aerial photography. IMINT is one of the principal intelligence disciplines alongside signals intelligence (SIGINT), human intelligence (HUMINT) and measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT).

History

Aerial photographic reconnaissance began in the First World War, when cameras were carried aloft in aircraft and balloons. In the Second World War, dedicated reconnaissance units — such as the Allied photographic interpreters at RAF Medmenham — analysed millions of images, identifying targets such as V-weapon sites. During the Cold War, high-altitude aircraft such as the U-2 and SR-71 and the CORONA series of reconnaissance satellites made imagery a cornerstone of strategic intelligence; U-2 photographs of Soviet missile sites in Cuba triggered the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962. The United States organisation responsible for imagery analysis became the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA).

Sources and methods

Modern IMINT sources include electro-optical imaging satellites, synthetic-aperture radar satellites that can image through cloud and at night, infrared sensors, and uncrewed aerial vehicles providing full-motion video. Imagery analysts (historically "photo interpreters") examine imagery for military, political, economic and infrastructure information, producing annotated reports and target materials. Open-source imagery from commercial satellites has increasingly supplemented classified collection.
