Meme stock

Meme stock
Origin of term2021, from "meme" + "stock"
Key examplesGameStop, AMC Entertainment, BlackBerry, Bed Bath & Beyond
Associated forumr/wallstreetbets

A meme stock is a stock that gains popularity among retail investors through the internet and social media. This popularity is generally based on online memes shared on platforms such as Reddit's r/wallstreetbets forum and on social media services. Investors in meme stocks are often young, individual investors. The prices of such stocks can become substantially overvalued relative to companies' fundamentals, and trading is typically volatile and speculative.

The January 2021 episode

The phenomenon erupted in January 2021, when coordinated buying by retail investors drove an explosive short squeeze in GameStop, a heavily shorted video-game retailer. The share price rose from under $20 to an intraday high near $483 within weeks, inflicting large losses on short-selling hedge funds such as Melvin Capital. Trading restrictions imposed by retail brokerages, including Robinhood, amid clearinghouse collateral demands provoked outrage, class-action lawsuits, and congressional hearings in the United States.

Characteristics and subsequent waves

Other companies treated as meme stocks include AMC Entertainment, BlackBerry, Nokia, Koss, and Bed Bath & Beyond. Later episodes — including spikes in 2024 following renewed social-media activity by figureheads of the movement — showed that meme-stock surges recur, driven by forum activity, options trading, and short interest rather than changes in company prospects. Regulators and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission examined the events, publishing a staff report on the January 2021 market volatility.
