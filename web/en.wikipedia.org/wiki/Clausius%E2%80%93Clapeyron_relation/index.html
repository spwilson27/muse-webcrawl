Clausius–Clapeyron relation

The Clausius–Clapeyron relation, named after Rudolf Clausius and Benoît Paul Émile Clapeyron, specifies the temperature dependence of pressure, most importantly for vapour pressure at a discontinuous phase transition between two phases of matter of a single constituent.

Equation

For a phase transition at temperature T and pressure P, the slope of the coexistence curve is dP/dT = L / (T ΔV), where L is the latent heat of the transition and ΔV the change in specific volume. For vaporisation, approximating the vapour as an ideal gas gives the commonly used integrated form relating saturation vapour pressure to temperature.

Applications

The relation is fundamental in meteorology, where it implies that the water-holding capacity of the atmosphere increases by roughly 7% per degree Celsius of warming, and in chemistry and materials science for phase diagrams.
