Trisagion

Angels singing the thrice-holy hymn, after Isaiah 6

The Trisagion (Greek: Τρισάγιον, "Thrice Holy") is a standard hymn of the Divine Liturgy in most of the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches:

Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.

The hymn is sung three times and is addressed to the Holy Trinity. Its name derives from the triple repetition of the Greek word hagios, "holy", echoing the song of the seraphim in Isaiah 6:3.

History and legend

According to tradition, during the reign of the Emperor Theodosius II in the 5th century, Constantinople was shaken by a violent earthquake. As the people processed in prayer, a child was said to have been lifted into the air and to have heard angels singing the hymn, teaching the words to the people; when the hymn was sung, the earthquake ceased.

Use in liturgy

In the Byzantine Rite the Trisagion is sung at the Divine Liturgy after the Little Entrance, at Matins and Vespers, and in many other services. During certain feasts it is replaced by other hymns: "As many as have been baptised into Christ" at Christmas, Theophany, Pascha and Pentecost, and "Before Thy Cross we bow down" on feasts of the Cross.

In the Roman Rite, a form of the Trisagion appears in the Reproaches sung on Good Friday, alternating Greek and Latin verses.
