Rain garden

A rain garden capturing runoff from a road and roof.

A rain garden is a designed depression in the landscape, planted with grasses and flowering perennials, that collects rainwater runoff from impervious surfaces such as roofs, driveways, and streets and allows it to soak into the ground (infiltration) rather than flow into storm drains.

Function

Rain gardens are a form of low-impact development and bioretention. As water ponds briefly (typically draining within 24–48 hours) and filters through an engineered soil mix, pollutants such as nutrients, metals, and hydrocarbons are removed by soil, microbes, and plant uptake. This reduces flooding, erosion, and pollution of receiving waters and recharges groundwater.

Design

A typical rain garden is dug 10–20 cm deep with a porous planting soil over a gravel or sand layer, located at least a few metres from building foundations. Native plants tolerant of both wet and dry conditions — such as sedges, rushes, and wildflowers — are favoured, and the garden also provides habitat for pollinators.

Adoption

Rain gardens were popularised in Prince George's County, Maryland, in the 1990s as an alternative to conventional stormwater ponds, and are now widely promoted by municipalities for managing stormwater from individual lots. See also bioretention and stormwater.
