Blockade

[Hero image: British warships enforcing the blockade of Brest during the Napoleonic Wars]

A blockade is the act of actively preventing a country or region from receiving or sending out food, supplies, weapons, or communications, and sometimes people, by military force. A blockade differs from an embargo or sanction, which are legal barriers to trade rather than physical interdiction. It is also distinct from a siege, which is usually directed at a specific fortified place; a blockade may target an entire coast or frontier.

Types

  • Naval blockade — the use of warships to seal the ports and coasts of an enemy, as in Britain's blockade of Napoleonic France or the Union blockade of the Confederacy during the American Civil War.
  • Land blockade — closing land frontiers, as in Napoleon's Continental System directed against British trade.
  • Aerial blockade — interdiction enforced by air power, sometimes termed an air blockade.
  • Close and distant blockades — a close blockade stations ships immediately off enemy ports; a distant blockade keeps the enemy fleet confined by controlling choke points far away.

International law

Under the customary law codified in the Declaration of Paris (1856) and the London Declaration (1909), a blockade must be declared and notified, must be effective (maintained by a force sufficient to prevent access), and must be applied impartially to vessels of all nations. A blockade that does not meet these conditions is a "paper blockade" and is not legally binding on neutrals. Blockading forces may visit, search, capture, and in some circumstances sink vessels attempting to breach the blockade ("blockade runners"). The 1994 San Remo Manual restated much of this law for modern naval warfare.

History

Notable blockades include the British blockade of France during the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars; the Union "Anaconda Plan" blockade of the Confederate States; the British distant blockade of Germany in both World Wars; the Allied blockade of Germany after the First World War armistice; the Cuban Missile Crisis naval "quarantine" of 1962; and the Berlin Blockade of 1948–49, defeated by the Berlin Airlift.

Blockade running

Blockade running — the attempt to evade a blockade to deliver cargo — has been practised by fast steamers during the American Civil War, by "bananas boats" and Q-ship rivals in wartime, and by sanctions-busting vessels in the modern era.
