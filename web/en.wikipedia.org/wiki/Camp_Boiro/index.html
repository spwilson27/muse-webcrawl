Camp Boiro

Camp Boiro was a concentration and extermination camp in Conakry, Guinea, operated under the regime of President Ahmed Sékou Touré from the 1960s until 1984. Named after a police inspector killed in 1969, it became the most notorious prison of Touré's Guinea.

History

Thousands of Guineans accused of plotting against the regime — real or fabricated "plots" — were arrested, tortured, and executed or starved at the camp, which stood on the grounds of the presidential guard barracks near Donka. Victims were subjected to the "black diet" (total deprivation of food and water). Estimates of those who died there and in Guinea's wider repression run into the tens of thousands.

Aftermath

After Touré's death in 1984, the succeeding military government released surviving prisoners. Victims' associations have preserved the memory of the camp and pressed for recognition and justice.
