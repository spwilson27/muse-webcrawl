European Southern Observatory

The European Organisation for Astronomical Research in the Southern Hemisphere, commonly the European Southern Observatory (ESO), is an intergovernmental astronomy organisation supported by 16 member states, founded in 1962. It operates major observatories in northern Chile's Atacama Desert.

Founded1962
HeadquartersGarching, Germany
SitesLa Silla, Paranal, Chajnantor (ALMA)
Key facilitiesVery Large Telescope; Extremely Large Telescope (under construction)

Facilities

ESO runs La Silla (its first observatory), Paranal — home of the four 8.2 m Very Large Telescope units and the VISTA and VST survey telescopes — and partners in ALMA on the Chajnantor plateau. The 39-metre Extremely Large Telescope is under construction on Cerro Armazones, expected to be the world's largest optical/near-infrared telescope.

Discoveries

ESO telescopes contributed to the first image of an exoplanet, the tracking of stars around the Milky Way's central black hole (work recognised by the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics), discovery of Proxima Centauri b, and measurements of the accelerating universe.
