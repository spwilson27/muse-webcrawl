Wikitext

Placeholder image: wikitext source of an article.

Wikitext, also known as wiki markup or wikicode, is a lightweight markup language used to write pages in wiki websites, most prominently the MediaWiki software that powers Wikipedia. It consists of simplified syntax intended to be easier to write than HTML, and is converted by the wiki software into HTML for display. Unlike Markdown, wikitext has never had a single formal specification and historically was defined by the behaviour of the MediaWiki parser.

Features

Wikitext provides syntax for headings (== Heading ==), bold and italic text ('''bold''', ''italic''), internal links ([[Article]]) and external links, lists, images, and tables. Its most powerful features are templates — reusable snippets transcluded with double braces, such as {{Infobox}} — along with parser functions, magic words, and category tags. Combined, these allow complex, structured article layouts to be produced from simple text.

History and standardisation

Wiki markup descends from the markup of Ward Cunningham's original WikiWikiWeb (1995) and UseModWiki, from which MediaWiki derived. Repeated efforts to standardise wiki markup (including the Creole project) had limited adoption. The Wikimedia Foundation later developed Parsoid, a bidirectional wikitext/HTML parser, to support the VisualEditor graphical interface while retaining wikitext as the storage format.
