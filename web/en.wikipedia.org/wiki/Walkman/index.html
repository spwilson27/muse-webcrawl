Walkman

The Walkman is a brand of portable audio players manufactured and marketed by Sony since 1979. The original Walkman, released in Japan on 1 July 1979, was a portable cassette player that changed listening habits by allowing people to carry recorded music with them and listen through lightweight headphones.

History

The Walkman was developed from Sony's earlier Pressman portable tape recorder. Sony co-founder Masaru Ibuka wanted a device to listen to music on long flights, and engineer Nobutoshi Kihara adapted the Pressman by removing the recording function and adding stereo playback. Chairman Akio Morita championed the product despite internal scepticism.

The first model, the TPS-L2, was a blue-and-silver cassette player with two headphone jacks, allowing two people to listen at once, and a "hotline" button that lowered the volume so listeners could converse. It launched in Japan in 1979 and in the United States in 1980, where it was initially marketed as the "Soundabout" before the Walkman name took over globally.

Impact and evolution

The Walkman became a cultural phenomenon of the 1980s; the word "walkman" entered dictionaries as a generic term for personal stereos. Sony sold over 200 million cassette Walkmans. The line expanded to include the Discman (CD Walkman), MiniDisc players, and later flash-memory and hard-drive digital players. Production of cassette Walkmans ended in 2010, though digital Walkman models continue, positioned as high-resolution audio players.

The Walkman is credited with popularising private, mobile music listening—a shift later amplified by the iPod and streaming smartphones.
