The guembri (also spelled gimbri, and known in Morocco as the sintir or hajhuj) is a three-stringed skin-covered bass plucked lute used by the Gnawa people of Morocco. Its body is carved from a single hollowed-out log and covered on the playing side with camel skin. Two of its strings run over a movable bridge while a shorter drone string is fixed higher on the neck; the instrument is played by plucking and by striking the skin soundboard percussively.

Role in Gnawa music

The guembri is the central instrument of Gnawa ritual music, played by the master musician (maâlem) who leads the all-night lila ceremonies of healing and trance, accompanied by iron castanets (qraqeb) and chanting. The instrument's deep, buzzing bass lines are held to invoke the spirits (mluk) honoured in the ritual. Gnawa musicians such as Mahmoud Guinia and Hassan Hakmoun brought the guembri to international audiences, and it has influenced fusion projects blending Gnawa music with jazz, blues, and rock.

Origins

The Gnawa descend in part from West African soldiers and enslaved people brought across the Sahara, and the guembri is related to West African lutes such as the ngoni and the kora family, adapted in the Maghreb to local materials and ritual use.
