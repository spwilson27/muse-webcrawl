Lloyd Stowell Shapley (June 2, 1923 – March 12, 2016) was an American mathematician and Nobel Prize-winning economist. He contributed to the fields of mathematical economics and especially game theory. Shapley is generally considered one of the most important contributors to the development of game theory since the work of von Neumann and Morgenstern. With Alvin E. Roth, Shapley won the 2012 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences "for the theory of stable allocations and the practice of market design."

Life and career

Shapley was born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, one of the five children of astronomers Harlow Shapley and Martha Shapley. He was drafted into the United States Army in 1943 and served as a weather observer in China, receiving the Bronze Star for breaking a Japanese weather code. After the war he studied mathematics at Harvard University and then joined the RAND Corporation in 1948. He received his PhD from Princeton University in 1953, where his contemporaries included John Nash. After a period back at RAND, he joined the faculty of the University of California, Los Angeles in 1981, where he remained professor emeritus.

Contributions

Shapley value

The Shapley value (1953) is a solution concept in cooperative game theory that assigns to each player a payoff equal to their average marginal contribution across all possible orderings of players. It is uniquely characterised by axioms of efficiency, symmetry, additivity and the null-player property, and is widely used in cost allocation, voting power analysis and, more recently, explaining predictions of machine-learning models (SHAP values).

Stable matching

With David Gale, Shapley developed the deferred-acceptance algorithm (the Gale–Shapley algorithm) in their 1962 paper "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage", founding the theory of stable matching that underlies modern market design, including school choice and medical residency matching.

Other work

  • The core of a cooperative game (with Donald Gillies)
  • Stochastic games, introduced in a 1953 paper proving existence of stationary equilibria
  • The Bondareva–Shapley theorem on the non-emptiness of the core
  • The Shapley–Folkman lemma and the Aumann–Shapley pricing theory
  • Potential games; the Gale–Shapley model of the housing market (Shapley–Scarf)

Honours

Shapley was a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, a Fellow of the Econometric Society, and received the John von Neumann Theory Prize in 1981. He shared the 2012 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Alvin Roth.
