Empress Suiko

Empress Suiko (推古天皇, Suiko-tennō; 554 – 15 April 628) was the 33rd monarch of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. She reigned from 593 until her death in 628, and was the first of eight women in Japanese history to reign as empress regnant. Her reign, conducted together with her nephew and regent Prince Shōtoku and the powerful statesman Soga no Umako, saw foundational reforms and the flourishing of Buddhism and continental culture in Japan's Asuka period.

Empress Suiko is remembered with her regent Prince Shōtoku as a founder of the Japanese state.

Accession

Born Princess Nukatabe, a daughter of Emperor Kinmei, she became a consort of her half-brother Emperor Bidatsu and, after his death, of the court. In 592 the emperor Sushun was assassinated by agents of Soga no Umako, the dominant clan leader. To stabilize the court, the Soga-backed Nukatabe was enthroned in 593, taking the name Suiko — reportedly at first reluctant to accept. She appointed her nephew Prince Umayado, better known as Prince Shōtoku, as regent (sesshō).

Reign and reforms

The Suiko–Shōtoku government implemented the Twelve Level Cap and Rank System (604), a merit-influenced court ranking based on Confucian virtues, and promulgated the Seventeen-article Constitution (604) — really a set of moral and administrative precepts for officials drawing on Confucian and Buddhist ideals. Buddhism, recently arrived from Baekje, was officially promoted: temples such as Hōryū-ji and Shitennō-ji were built, and missions were sent to Sui China (from 600/607), famously including the 607 embassy of Ono no Imoko carrying a letter addressed from "the Son of Heaven in the land where the sun rises" to "the Son of Heaven in the land where the sun sets" — asserting Japanese equality with China. The court also adopted the Chinese calendar and encouraged Chinese-style administration.

Death and succession

Shōtoku died in 622 and Umako in 626; Suiko herself died in 628 without clearly designating an heir, sparking a succession dispute in which Umako's son Soga no Emishi prevailed, installing Emperor Jomei. Suiko's mausoleum is traditionally identified in Osaka Prefecture.
