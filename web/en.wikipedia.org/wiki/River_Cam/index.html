The River Cam is a river in the east of England, the main river flowing through the city of Cambridge. It rises from springs near Ashwell in Hertfordshire and flows some 69 kilometres (43 miles) north-eastwards through Cambridgeshire to join the River Great Ouse at Pope's Corner, a few miles south of Ely. The river is formed principally by the union of the River Granta and the River Rhee, two streams that meet just south of Cambridge.

Course

From its sources the Cam flows past Newport, Great Chesterford and the Audley End estate before reaching Cambridge. Within the city it passes along the Backs, the landscaped riverside gardens behind several University of Cambridge colleges, where it is crossed by a series of ornate bridges including the Mathematical Bridge at Queens' College, the Bridge of Sighs at St John's College, and Clare Bridge. Downstream the river passes Fen Ditton, Baits Bite Lock and Bottisham Lock, then flows through Waterbeach before meeting the Great Ouse.

Recreation and culture

The Cam is famous for punting: flat-bottomed boats propelled with a long pole are a defining sight of Cambridge, used by students and tourists alike. Rowing is also central to the river's life, with college boat clubs training on the five-mile stretch between Chesterton and Baits Bite Lock for the Lent and May Bumps races.

Above Cambridge, at Grantchester, the river passes Byron's Pool, traditionally associated with the poet Lord Byron. The Grantchester meadows inspired Rupert Brooke's 1912 poem "The Old Vicarage, Grantchester", written while he was staying in Berlin, which ends with the celebrated lines: "Stands the Church clock at ten to three? / And is there honey still for tea?"

Conservation

The river supports a varied ecology, and bodies such as the Cam Valley Forum campaign on water quality, abstraction and biodiversity. The Cam is navigable from Cambridge to the Great Ouse and forms part of the wider Fenland waterways network.
