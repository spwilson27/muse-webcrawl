Ritsuryō

The ritsuryō system was the historical legal system of ancient Japan, adopted from Chinese models during the late Asuka and Nara periods. The name combines ritsu (律, penal code) and ryō (令, administrative code). Under ritsuryō, Japan was reorganized as a centralized bureaucratic state under the emperor, modeled on Tang dynasty China.

Development

The Taihō Code of 701 and the Yōrō Code of 718 established ministries, provincial administration, a census and land-allotment system (handen-shūju), and taxes in rice and labor. In practice the system decayed from the 9th century as private estates (shōen) proliferated, though the ritsuryō framework formally survived until the Meiji Restoration.
