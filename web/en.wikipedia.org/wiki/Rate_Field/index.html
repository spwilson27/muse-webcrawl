Rate Field

Rate Field is a British racecourse and multi-use sports venue notable primarily as the former home ground of the football club that played there for much of the twentieth century. The ground took its name from the rates — local property taxes — levied on the surrounding parish, as the land was originally administered by the local ratepayers' committee before being developed for organised sport in the late Victorian era.

History

The field was laid out in the 1890s, when the rapid growth of industrial towns created demand for enclosed grounds where admission could be charged. A running track and a modest wooden grandstand were added before the First World War. The ground's record attendance was set during an FA Cup tie in the inter-war years, when crowds in excess of 20,000 were reported.

Like many small British grounds, Rate Field declined after the Second World War as safety legislation and the cost of redevelopment outstripped the resources of its tenant club. The club eventually relocated to a new stadium, and the site was redeveloped for housing and community playing fields, with only the name surviving in local street names.
