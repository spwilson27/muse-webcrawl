Preta

Preta (Sanskrit: प्रेत, "departed one"; Pali: peta), often translated as hungry ghost, is a class of supernatural beings in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and related traditions. Pretas are the spirits of the dead who, because of greed, miserliness, jealousy or other karmic defilements in life, are reborn into a state of perpetual, unfulfillable craving. They are typically depicted with enormous distended bellies and necks as thin as needles, so that the food they crave can never satisfy them.

In Buddhism

In Buddhist cosmology the preta realm (pretaloka) is one of the six realms of rebirth, below the human realm. The Pali Petavatthu, a collection of fifty-one stories, describes individual pretas and the deeds that led to their condition — refusing alms to monks, stealing offerings, or dying consumed by attachment. Pretas are said to suffer from hunger and thirst for centuries; some can eat only filth, others find food turning to fire or blades in their mouths, and some are tormented by heat, cold and fear. Compassionate humans can relieve them by making offerings and transferring the merit of good deeds to the dead, a practice central to the Ghost Festival.

In East Asia

The concept travelled with Buddhism into China, where hungry ghosts are called èguǐ (餓鬼), and Japan (gaki). The Ullambana tradition, based on the story of the monk Maudgalyayana (Mulian) rescuing his mother from the preta realm, gave rise to the Ghost Festival held in the seventh lunar month, when families offer food, incense and paper goods to wandering spirits. In Tibetan Buddhism, rituals involving the burning of effigies and the offering of torma cakes address hungry ghosts, and the preta realm is pictured among the six realms on the bhavacakra, the wheel of life.

In Hinduism and Jainism

In Hindu tradition, a preta is the spirit of a recently deceased person that has not yet received the proper funerary rites (śrāddha); offerings of rice balls (piṇḍa) during the mourning period help the preta pass on to join the ancestors (pitṛ). Jain texts describe pretas among the infernal and ghostly beings populating their elaborate cosmology, likewise bound by karma.

Cultural influence

The image of the hungry ghost has become a widely used metaphor in modern writing on addiction and consumerism: a being driven by insatiable appetite that consumption can never relieve, notably in the work of the physician Gabor Maté, whose book In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts popularised the term in addiction medicine.
