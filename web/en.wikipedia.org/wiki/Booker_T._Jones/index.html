Booker Taliaferro Jones Jr. (born November 12, 1944) is an American musician, songwriter, record producer, and arranger, best known as the frontman of the band Booker T. & the M.G.'s, which was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992. His Hammond organ playing defined the sound of Stax Records and of 1960s soul music.

Career

A child prodigy on piano, oboe, saxophone, and trombone, Jones joined the Stax Records house band while still in high school. In 1962 his group Booker T. & the M.G.'s — with Steve Cropper, Donald "Duck" Dunn, and Al Jackson Jr. — scored an international hit with the instrumental "Green Onions." The band backed nearly every Stax artist, including Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, and Albert King (whose album Born Under a Bad Sign Jones co-wrote and arranged), and had further hits with "Hip Hug-Her" and "Time Is Tight." Jones studied music composition at Indiana University while continuing to record on weekends.

Later work

Jones produced Bill Withers's debut album Just As I Am (1971) and Willie Nelson's Stardust (1978). His later albums include Potato Hole (2009), recorded with the Drive-By Truckers and Neil Young, which won the Grammy for Best Pop Instrumental Album, and The Road from Memphis (2011), which won Best R&B Album. He received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award with the M.G.'s in 2007 and published the memoir Time Is Tight in 2019.
