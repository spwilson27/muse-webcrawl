Saint-Étienne

Saint-Étienne is a city in east-central France, in the Loire department of the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, about 60 km southwest of Lyon. With roughly 170,000 inhabitants in the commune and over 500,000 in its metropolitan area, it is one of France's major cities, built on arms manufacture, coal mining, ribbon weaving and bicycles.

History

Named for Saint Stephen, the town grew from the 16th century as a centre of arms production — the state manufacture d'armes supplied the French army for centuries — and of silk ribbon weaving. Coal from the Loire basin powered its industrial expansion in the 19th century; France's first railway line (1827) ran from Saint-Étienne to Andrézieux. The mail-order firm Manufrance, the Casino supermarket group (founded here in 1898) and a pioneering bicycle industry defined its 20th-century economy. Like other coal and steel towns, it suffered deindustrialisation from the 1970s.

Culture and sport

The city has reinvented itself around design: it hosts the Cité du design and an international design biennale, and is a UNESCO Creative City of Design. Its Le Corbusier site at Firminy, including a church completed in 2006, is among the largest architectural ensembles by the architect. AS Saint-Étienne, "les Verts", is one of France's most storied football clubs, with a record-tying ten league titles won mostly in the 1960s and 1970s and a famous European Cup final appearance in 1976.
