Itô calculus

Itô calculus, named after Japanese mathematician Kiyosi Itô, extends methods of calculus to stochastic processes such as Brownian motion (see Wiener process). It has important applications in mathematical finance and stochastic differential equations.

The central concept is the Itô stochastic integral, a generalization of the ordinary Riemann–Stieltjes integral in which the integrand and integrator are stochastic processes. Unlike an ordinary integral, the Itô integral is defined with respect to the left endpoint of each subinterval, a choice that preserves the martingale property of the result and makes the integral non-anticipating — crucial for modelling quantities that cannot depend on future information.

Itô's lemma

The fundamental theorem of Itô calculus is Itô's lemma, the stochastic analogue of the chain rule. For a process dXt = μt dt + σt dBt and a twice-differentiable function f(t, x), it states:

df(t, Xt) = (∂f/∂t + μt ∂f/∂x + ½σt² ∂²f/∂x²) dt + σt ∂f/∂x dBt.

The extra second-order term, absent from ordinary calculus, appears because Brownian motion has non-zero quadratic variation. Itô's lemma is the key computational tool behind the derivation of the Black–Scholes equation in mathematical finance.

Applications

Itô calculus is used to model systems evolving randomly in time, including stock prices (geometric Brownian motion), interest rates, physical systems subject to thermal noise, and population dynamics. An alternative integral, the Stratonovich integral, uses the midpoint convention and obeys ordinary chain-rule behaviour; the two are related by a correction term involving quadratic covariation.
