SerDes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A SerDes (pronounced "sir-deez"), short for serializer/deserializer, is a pair of functional blocks commonly used in high-speed communications to compensate for limited input/output. These blocks convert data between serial data and parallel interfaces in each direction. A SerDes pair allows parallel data to be transmitted over a single serial link — or a small number of differential pairs — instead of a wide parallel bus, reducing pin count, board area, and skew problems.

Operation

The serializer receives parallel data, often at a relatively low clock rate, encodes it (frequently with 8b/10b, 64b/66b, or 128b/130b line codes), and transmits it as a high-speed serial bit stream with an embedded clock. At the receiver, a clock and data recovery (CDR) circuit extracts the timing from the incoming stream, and the deserializer reconstructs the parallel data.

Applications

SerDes technology underlies most modern high-speed chip-to-chip and backplane interfaces, including PCI Express, SATA, USB 3, Ethernet physical layers (such as 10GBASE-KR and later standards), Fibre Channel, InfiniBand, HDMI, and JESD204 data-converter interfaces. Data rates have climbed from a few hundred megabits per second in early devices to 112 Gbit/s per lane and beyond using PAM4 modulation.

Techniques

High-speed serial links rely on equalization (such as continuous-time linear equalizers, feed-forward equalization, and decision-feedback equalization) to counteract channel loss, on clock and data recovery with phase-locked or phase-interpolator loops, and on jitter and bit-error-rate testing during design validation.
