Telepresence


A high-end telepresence conferencing suite

Telepresence refers to a set of technologies that allow a person to feel as if they were present, to give the appearance of being present, or to have an effect, at a place other than their true location. The term was popularised by computer scientist Marvin Minsky in a 1980 essay on teleoperation, and relates to the psychological sense of "presence" studied in media research — the illusion of "being there" in a mediated environment.

Video telepresence

In business usage, telepresence describes high-end videoconferencing systems designed to simulate face-to-face meetings: life-size high-definition displays, matched furniture and lighting, spatial audio and carefully aligned cameras that preserve eye contact. Cisco introduced such a system in 2006, with competitors including Polycom and HP following. These immersive rooms were marketed as a way to cut executive travel costs, and demand for remote presence surged again during the COVID-19 pandemic, though largely through simpler software conferencing.

Telepresence robots

A second form gives a remote user a mobile robotic body: a screen, camera and microphone mounted on a wheeled base that the user drives around an office, school or hospital, speaking and seeing through the machine. Such robots are used for remote work, telemedicine rounds and allowing sick or distant students to attend class.

Broader concept

Researchers distinguish levels of presence — from the suspension of disbelief in a good video call to full virtual reality immersion — and study factors such as latency, field of view and sensory fidelity. Telerobotics for hazardous environments and space exploration is considered the functional extreme of telepresence: operating on a distant environment as if physically there.
