Viktor Ambartsumian

Born18 September 1908, Tiflis, Russian Empire
Died12 August 1996 (aged 87), Byurakan, Armenia
FieldsAstrophysics
Known forStellar associations, inverse Compton scattering

Viktor Amazaspovich Ambartsumian (18 September 1908 – 12 August 1996) was a Soviet and Armenian astrophysicist and science administrator. He was one of the founders of theoretical astrophysics. Ambartsumian worked on the physics of stars and nebulae, stellar astronomy, the dynamics of stellar systems, and the cosmogony of stars and galaxies. He proposed the existence of stellar associations — groups of young stars expanding from a common origin — and argued that star formation is still occurring in the galaxy, contrary to the prevailing view that all stars formed at roughly the same time.

Ambartsumian founded the Byurakan Observatory in Armenia

Career

Born in Tiflis (now Tbilisi) into an Armenian family, Ambartsumian studied at Leningrad State University and published his first scientific paper at the age of 16. He worked at Pulkovo Observatory and Leningrad before returning to Armenia, where he founded the Byurakan Astrophysical Observatory in 1946 and directed it for decades. He was president of the International Astronomical Union from 1961 to 1964 and twice president of the International Council of Scientific Unions.

Contributions

Among his contributions, Ambartsumian studied radiative transfer and formulated principles of invariance used in the theory of inverse Compton scattering, examined the properties of active galactic nuclei — proposing in the late 1950s that galactic nuclei contain massive compact objects with explosive activity — and developed a statistical theory of fluctuations in brightness of the Milky Way. He received numerous honours, including two Stalin Prizes, two titles of Hero of Socialist Labour, and the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society.
