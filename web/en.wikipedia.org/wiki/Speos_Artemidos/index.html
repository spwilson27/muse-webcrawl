Speos Artemidos

The Speos Artemidos (Greek for "Grotto of Artemis") is an ancient Egyptian rock-cut temple in Middle Egypt, south of Beni Hasan in Minya Governorate. It was built principally under the 18th-Dynasty pharaoh Hatshepsut (c. 1479–1458 BC) and dedicated to the lioness goddess Pakhet, whom the Greeks later identified with Artemis.

Description

The temple is cut into the limestone cliffs on the east bank of the Nile. It consists of a portico with pillars, a transverse hall, and a sanctuary. An important inscription of Hatshepsut on the portico architrave boasts of restoring temples ruined under the Hyksos rulers, and is a key text for her reign. The sanctuary was completed under Seti I, who also added reliefs.

Pakhet

Pakhet, "she who scratches", was a local lioness goddess of the desert margins near Beni Hasan, where the cliffs contained many cat burials. The valley in which the temple stands was known in Greek as the Speos Artemidos, and nearby is the grotto of Artemis with burials of sacred cats.
