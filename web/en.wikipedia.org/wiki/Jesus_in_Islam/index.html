In Islam, Jesus (Arabic: عيسى ابن مريم, romanized: ʿĪsā ibn Maryam, lit. 'Jesus, son of Mary') is believed to be the penultimate prophet and messenger of God and the Messiah. He is also considered one of the five messengers of firm resolve (ulu al-azm). Muslims believe Jesus was born of a virgin birth, performed miracles by God's permission, and was neither crucified nor killed, but was raised alive to heaven by God.

Quranic narrative

Jesus is mentioned in the Quran 25 times, more often by name than Muhammad. The Quran affirms the virgin birth to Mary, who has a chapter (surah) named for her. Jesus is described as speaking from the cradle, breathing life into clay birds, healing the blind and the leper, and raising the dead—all "by God's leave". He is called the Messiah, a word and spirit from God, and a sign for mankind.

Crucifixion

The Quran (4:157) states that Jesus was not killed or crucified, but that "it was made to appear so". Most classical commentators held that another person was made to resemble Jesus, or that he was taken up directly; interpretations vary. Islam rejects the doctrine that Jesus is God or the Son of God, emphasising strict monotheism (tawhid).

Second coming

Though not stated explicitly in the Quran, Islamic tradition based on hadith holds that Jesus will return at the end of time, descending in Damascus, defeating the false messiah (al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl), and establishing justice before dying a natural death.
