O Lucky Man!

O Lucky Man! is a 1973 British comedy-drama film directed by Lindsay Anderson and starring Malcolm McDowell as Mick Travis, an ambitious young coffee salesman whose picaresque journey through Britain exposes him to corruption, exploitation, and absurdity. It is the middle film of Anderson's "Mick Travis trilogy", following If.... (1968) and preceding Britannia Hospital (1982).

Plot and style

Travis rises and falls repeatedly: he is arrested on suspicion of espionage, subjected to medical experiments, becomes the assistant of a corrupt tycoon, is imprisoned, and emerges determined to reform, only to encounter further disillusionment. The narrative is episodic and satirical, punctuated by songs performed on screen by Alan Price and his band, who act as a kind of Greek chorus commenting on the action. Many actors appear in multiple roles, reinforcing the film's view of a closed social system.

Reception

The film premiered at the 1973 Cannes Film Festival and divided critics, but it has since been re-evaluated as one of the major British films of its era — an ambitious, sprawling state-of-the-nation satire in the tradition of Voltaire's Candide, which partly inspired it.
