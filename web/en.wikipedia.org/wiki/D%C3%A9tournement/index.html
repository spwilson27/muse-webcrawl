Détournement

Détournement (French for "rerouting" or "hijacking") is a technique developed in the 1950s by the Letterist International and later the Situationist International, in which existing artistic or media elements are turned against their original meaning to create something new.

Practice

Guy Debord and Gil Wolman set out the method in their 1956 "User's Guide to Détournement". Advertisements, films, comic strips and classical artworks were re-captioned, re-edited or overpainted so that the spectacle's own materials criticised the spectacle — the capitalist media culture the Situationists opposed.

Legacy

Détournement influenced punk graphics, culture jamming, Adbusters-style subvertising, remix and meme culture, and sampling in music. Its counterpart, récupération, describes how the mainstream absorbs and defuses such subversion.
