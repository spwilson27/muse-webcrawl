Parasite (2019 film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Kim family's semi-basement window at street level, feet and flooded street beyond — the film's defining image of class geography]

Parasite (Korean: 기생충, Gisaengchung) is a 2019 South Korean black comedy thriller directed by Bong Joon-ho, who co-wrote with Han Jin-won. The poor Kim family schemes its way, member by member, into the employ of the wealthy Park family — tutor, art therapist, driver, housekeeper — until a visitor in a rainstorm brings the secret living beneath the Parks' architect-designed house to light, and comedy curdles into violence.

It premiered at Cannes in May 2019, winning the Palme d'Or unanimously, and in February 2020 became the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture, also taking Best Director, Original Screenplay and International Feature. Its worldwide gross exceeded $250 million on a budget of about $11 million.

Design and themes

Bong's class fable is built into the film's geography: the Kims' semi-basement (banjiha) below the street, the Parks' hilltop modernist house (a full set built for the film), and below that the bunker — a vertical diagram of Seoul's inequality, flooded in the storm that is a cleansing inconvenience uphill and a catastrophe downhill. The scholar's rock passed between the families condenses the film's argument about aspiration and its curses, stated visually rather than in words.

Reception

Parasite's sweep capped a decade of Korean cinema's rise (the Korean Wave in film) and re-centred world cinema's geography; critics placed it at or near the top of decade lists, and it led the 2022 Sight and Sound-era reassessments of recent cinema. Bong's command — genre machinery running at full entertainment speed in the service of social X-ray — became the era's model of popular auteurism.
