Indiegogo

Indiegogo is an American crowdfunding website founded in 2008 in San Francisco, California. The platform allows people to solicit funds for an idea, charity, or start-up business and is one of the first and largest reward-based crowdfunding sites, alongside its chief competitor Kickstarter. Campaigns raise money through fixed-goal (all-or-nothing) or flexible funding, in which creators keep contributions even if they miss their target.

History

Indiegogo was founded by Slava Rubin, Danae Ringelmann and Eric Schell, drawing on their experience with an earlier fundraising venture for theatre and on frustration with traditional gatekeepers of capital. It launched at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2008 with a focus on film projects and expanded to accept campaigns in nearly every category and most countries. The company raised venture financing from investors including Kleiner Perkins and later diversified into product launch services, the "InDemand" programme letting successful campaigns continue raising funds after their deadline, and a marketplace for crowdfunded products.

Model and notable campaigns

Indiegogo charges a platform fee — historically 5% of funds raised — on top of payment processing fees. Successful campaigns include the Flowbee-adjacent wave of hardware hits such as the Sondors Electric Bike, the "Solar Roadways" project (which raised over $2 million in 2014), and numerous film, music and charitable efforts. Unlike Kickstarter, Indiegogo historically permitted campaigns without prototypes and offered equity crowdfunding for a time under U.S. Regulation Crowdfunding rules.

Criticism

The flexible-funding model and light vetting have drawn criticism when high-profile products failed to ship or delivered late; Indiegogo responded with trust-and-safety initiatives, guaranteed-shipping badges and, in the 2020s, more selective "Indiegogo Select" programmes.
