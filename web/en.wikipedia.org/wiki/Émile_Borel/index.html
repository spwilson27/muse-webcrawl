Félix Édouard Justin Émile Borel (7 January 1871 – 3 February 1956) was a French mathematician and politician. Along with René-Louis Baire and Henri Lebesgue, he was among the pioneers of measure theory and its application to probability theory. The concepts of Borel sets, the Borel measure, Borel summation, and the Heine–Borel theorem bear his name; he also made early contributions to game theory and wrote influentially on probability and philosophy of science.

Career

Borel entered the École Normale Supérieure at the top of his class and took his doctorate in 1893. He taught at Lille and then at the École Normale and the Sorbonne, where he held the chair of theory of functions from 1909. He founded the modern French school of function theory and, in the 1920s, created the Institut Henri Poincaré in Paris as a centre for probability and mathematical physics.

In his 1895–1898 work he founded the theory of Borel summability of divergent series, and his 1909 book Éléments de la théorie des probabilités brought the notion of countably additive measure into probability. His "infinite monkey theorem" — that an event of sufficiently small probability never occurs in practice — became a famous illustration of probability in human affairs.

Politics and later life

Borel served as a Radical-Socialist deputy from 1924 to 1936 and briefly as Minister of Marine in 1925 under Paul Painlevé. During the Second World War he worked in the French Resistance and was awarded the Croix de Guerre. He was president of the French Academy of Sciences and won the CNRS Gold Medal in 1954. Lunar crater Borel is named after him.
