Mate (drink)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mate (Spanish: mate; Portuguese: chimarrão) is a traditional South American caffeine-rich infused drink made by steeping the dried leaves of the yerba mate plant (Ilex paraguariensis) in hot water. It is the national drink of Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay, and is also widely consumed in southern Brazil, parts of Chile and Bolivia, and in the Levant, where it was introduced by returning emigrants.

Preparation and consumption

Mate is traditionally served in a hollowed calabash gourd (also called a mate or cuia) and drunk through a metal straw called a bombilla (Portuguese bomba), whose perforated lower end filters out the leaf fragments. The dried leaves (yerba) are packed into the gourd, hot — but not boiling — water is poured over them, and the infusion is drunk and refilled repeatedly.

Drinking mate is a deeply social ritual. A single gourd is commonly prepared by one person (the cebador) and passed from drinker to drinker in a circle, each finishing the gourd before it is refilled and passed on; accepting or politely declining carries social meaning, and saying "gracias" traditionally signals that one has had enough. In the Southern Cone it is drunk at all hours, at home, at work, in parks and while travelling, and many people carry a thermos of hot water to refill the gourd through the day.

History

The drink originates with the indigenous Guaraní people, and was adopted and spread by Spanish colonists and Jesuit missionaries, who established yerba plantations in the missions of Paraguay in the 17th century. It later became the defining drink of the gauchos of the pampas and spread throughout the region.

Variations

In Paraguay, mate is commonly drunk cold as tereré, infused with cold water and often medicinal herbs, and is considered the country's national drink. Sweetened mate, mate cocido (brewed like tea, sometimes with milk), and mate served in a cup rather than a gourd are also common. Roasted yerba variations are popular in Brazil. Mate contains caffeine comparable to coffee in effect, along with theobromine, giving it a stimulating character reflected in its marketing worldwide as an ingredient in energy drinks.

Health

Yerba mate contains antioxidants and various nutrients, but drinking very hot mate has been associated in studies with an increased risk of oesophageal cancer, a risk attributed mainly to the temperature of the beverage; the International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies drinking very hot beverages above about 65 °C as probably carcinogenic.
