Homicide: Life on the Street

Homicide: Life on the Street
Genre: Police procedural drama
Created by: Paul Attanasio
Based on: Homicide: A Year on the Killing Streets by David Simon
Starring: Andre Braugher, Richard Belzer, Yaphet Kotto
Network: NBC
Original run: 31 January 1993 – 21 May 1999
Seasons: 7; Episodes: 122

Homicide: Life on the Street is an American police drama television series chronicling the work of a fictional version of the Baltimore Police Department's Homicide Unit. It ran for seven seasons and 122 episodes on NBC from 1993 to 1999, followed by a television film in 2000.

The show was based on David Simon's non-fiction book Homicide: A Year on the Killing Streets (1991), about a year Simon spent with the unit. Many storylines and characters were adapted from real cases.

Premise and style

Filmed on location in Baltimore with hand-held cameras and naturalistic editing, the series was noted for multiple overlapping storylines, morally ambiguous investigations, and an emphasis on the toll of the work. The squad room board listing open cases in red and closed cases in black became iconic.

Cast

The ensemble included Yaphet Kotto as Lt. Al Giardello, Andre Braugher as Det. Frank Pembleton, Richard Belzer as Det. John Munch — a character who later appeared in Law & Order: Special Victims Unit and many other series — Kyle Secor, Melissa Leo, Daniel Baldwin, Ned Beatty, and later Jon Seda and Michael Michele.

Reception and legacy

Despite modest ratings, the series won critical acclaim, Peabody Awards, and Writers Guild and Emmy Awards. It is frequently cited as a precursor to Simon's later series The Wire.
