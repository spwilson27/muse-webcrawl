The pulmonary arteries are the arteries carrying blood from the heart to the lungs. They are exceptional in that, unlike most arteries, they carry deoxygenated blood: their function is to deliver the entire cardiac output to the pulmonary capillaries, where carbon dioxide is released and oxygen is taken up before the blood returns to the left atrium via the pulmonary veins.

Anatomy

The short pulmonary trunk arises from the right ventricle, passes posterior to the aorta, and divides at the level of the fifth thoracic vertebra into the right and left pulmonary arteries. The right pulmonary artery passes horizontally beneath the carina to the hilum of the right lung; the left pulmonary artery, shorter, enters the left lung at its hilum. Within the lungs the arteries branch alongside the bronchi, eventually forming the dense capillary network around the alveoli.

Clinical significance

Pulmonary embolism — blockage of a pulmonary artery, usually by a thrombus from a deep vein — is a common and potentially fatal emergency. Pulmonary arterial hypertension is raised pressure in the pulmonary circulation, causing right-heart strain; it can be idiopathic or secondary to lung disease or left-heart disease. In newborns, failure of the ductus arteriosus to close can mimic pulmonary artery abnormalities, and congenital defects such as pulmonary atresia or pulmonary stenosis narrow or close the pulmonary outflow.
