Apophatic theology
Also calledNegative theology; via negativa
ApproachSpeaking of God only by negation — what God is not
Key figuresPseudo-Dionysius, Gregory of Nyssa, Meister Eckhart, Maimonides

Apophatic theology, also known as negative theology or the via negativa, is a theological approach that attempts to describe God — or ultimate reality — only by negation: by saying what God is not, rather than what God is. Its premise is that the divine transcends all human concepts and language, so affirmative statements about God are inevitably inadequate.

History

Roots lie in Plato's and Plotinus's reflections on the ineffable One. In Christianity the approach was developed by the Cappadocian fathers and given classic form by the 5th–6th-century writer Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite in The Mystical Theology. It became central to Eastern Orthodox theology and influenced Western mystics such as Meister Eckhart and the anonymous author of The Cloud of Unknowing, as well as scholastics like Thomas Aquinas in their use of analogy.

Analogues in other traditions

Parallels exist beyond Christianity: Maimonides argued for negative attributes in Judaism; Islamic theology debates God's attributes in related terms; and the Upanishadic formula neti neti ("not this, not this") expresses a similar strategy in Hindu thought, as does the Buddhist denial of inherent self-nature.
