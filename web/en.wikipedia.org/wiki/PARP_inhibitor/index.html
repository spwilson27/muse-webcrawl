PARP inhibitor

PARP inhibitors are a group of pharmacological inhibitors of the enzyme poly ADP ribose polymerase (PARP), developed for the treatment of several cancers. They exploit the concept of synthetic lethality: cancer cells with defects in homologous recombination DNA repair — most notably mutations in the BRCA1 or BRCA2 genes — depend on PARP-mediated repair of single-strand breaks, so inhibiting PARP selectively kills them while sparing normal cells.

Mechanism of action

PARP enzymes, chiefly PARP1, detect and help repair single-strand DNA breaks. PARP inhibitors block this repair; unrepaired single-strand breaks are converted to double-strand breaks during replication. Normal cells repair these by homologous recombination, but BRCA-deficient tumour cells cannot, and accumulate lethal DNA damage. Several PARP inhibitors also "trap" PARP on DNA, an effect that contributes to their potency.

Approved drugs

  • Olaparib — the first PARP inhibitor approved (2014), for BRCA-mutated ovarian cancer; later extended to breast, pancreatic, and prostate cancers.
  • Rucaparib — approved for ovarian and prostate cancers.
  • Niraparib — approved as maintenance treatment for ovarian cancer.
  • Talazoparib — approved for BRCA-mutated HER2-negative breast cancer.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include nausea, fatigue, anaemia and other haematologic toxicities; rare but serious risks include myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukaemia.
