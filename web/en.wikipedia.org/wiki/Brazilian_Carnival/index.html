The Brazilian Carnival (Portuguese: Carnaval do Brasil) is an annual festival held from the Friday before Ash Wednesday until noon on Ash Wednesday, marking the beginning of Lent. It is the largest carnival celebration in the world, drawing millions of participants to street parades, samba school competitions, balls and neighbourhood block parties (blocos) across Brazil.

History

Brazilian Carnival descends from the Portuguese Entrudo, a rowdy pre-Lenten festivity brought by colonists, blended over the 19th and 20th centuries with African-Brazilian musical and religious traditions. In Rio de Janeiro, Afro-Brazilian communities developed samba and the first samba schools in the 1920s–30s, transforming the festival into organised competitive parades.

Regional celebrations

Rio de Janeiro is famous for the samba school parades at the Sambadrome, where the top schools of the Special Group compete with elaborate floats, thousands of costumed dancers and original samba-enredo songs. Salvador in Bahia hosts vast street circuits with trio elétrico trucks and axé music; Olinda and Recife are known for frevo, maracatu and giant puppets; São Paulo holds its own major sambadrome parades.

Music and dance

Samba, samba-enredo, marchinhas, frevo and axé dominate the soundtrack, performed by large percussion ensembles (baterias). Costumes, floats and choreography are prepared by communities year-round, and the festival is a major generator of tourism and seasonal employment.
