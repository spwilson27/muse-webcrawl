Francis Russell "Frank" O'Hara (March 27, 1926 – July 25, 1966) was an American writer, poet, and art critic. A curator at the Museum of Modern Art in New York City, he was a central figure of the New York School of poetry, alongside John Ashbery, Kenneth Koch, and James Schuyler.

Life

O'Hara was born in Baltimore and raised in Grafton, Massachusetts. He served in the United States Navy during the Second World War, then studied at Harvard University on the G.I. Bill, where he roomed with the artist Edward Gorey. After earning a master's degree in English at the University of Michigan, he moved to New York in 1951 and took a job at the Museum of Modern Art, rising to the position of curator.

He died in 1966 after being struck by a dune buggy on Fire Island.

Poetry

O'Hara's poetry is marked by spontaneity, wit, and an intimate, conversational tone that he called "Personism." Many of his best-known poems were written quickly during lunch breaks and published in Meditations in an Emergency (1957) and, posthumously, Lunch Poems (1964) — the latter issued during his lifetime — which includes "The Day Lady Died," an elegy for Billie Holiday, and "Ave Maria." His Collected Poems appeared in 1971 and won the National Book Award.

Art criticism

As a critic and curator, O'Hara championed the Abstract Expressionists, writing monographs and essays on artists including Jackson Pollock and Robert Motherwell, and organizing exhibitions that introduced contemporary American art abroad.
