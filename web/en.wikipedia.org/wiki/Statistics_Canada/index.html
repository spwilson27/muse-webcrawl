Statistics Canada

Statistics Canada (French: Statistique Canada), formed in 1971, is the agency of the Government of Canada commissioned with producing statistics to help Canadians better understand their country — its population, resources, economy, society, and culture. Its headquarters are located in Ottawa.

The agency is led by the Chief Statistician of Canada, currently André Loranger, who serves as deputy head. Statistics Canada's mandate comes from the Statistics Act, which assigns it the task of collecting, compiling, analysing, abstracting and publishing statistical information relating to commercial, industrial, financial, social and general activities and condition of the people of Canada. It also conducts the national Census of Population every five years, which is the world's largest peacetime undertaking of its kind.

History

Canada's first census was conducted in 1666 by the intendant of New France, Jean Talon. For several decades after Confederation in 1867, a census was held every ten years, and specialized statistical work was scattered among various government departments. In 1918 the Dominion Bureau of Statistics was created under the Statistics Act of that year, centralizing statistical production; its first chief was Robert Coats. The bureau introduced the five-year census cycle for the Prairie provinces and, after the Second World War, greatly expanded economic surveys.

In 1971 the Dominion Bureau of Statistics was renamed Statistics Canada, part of a broader reorganization of the federal government. The agency moved to the Tunney's Pasture campus in Ottawa. Outgoing chief statistician Munir Sheikh resigned in 2010 over the government's decision to replace the mandatory long-form census with a voluntary National Household Survey, an episode that drew international attention to questions of statistical independence; the mandatory long form was restored for the 2016 census.

Activities

Statistics Canada publishes a wide range of statistical products, including the daily release schedule known as The Daily, the Consumer Price Index, the Labour Force Survey, the census of population, gross domestic product estimates, and international trade statistics. Most products are released under the Statistics Canada Open Licence. The agency also maintains geographic standards such as the Standard Geographical Classification and provides data through online tables and the Canadian Socio-economic Information Management System (CANSIM).
