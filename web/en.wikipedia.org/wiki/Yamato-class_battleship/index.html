Yamato-class battleship

OperatorsImperial Japanese Navy
ShipsYamato, Musashi, Shinano (converted to carrier)
DisplacementAbout 65,000 tonnes (standard)
Armament9 × 46 cm (18.1 in) guns

The Yamato-class battleships were a class of battleships built for the Imperial Japanese Navy during the late 1930s and early 1940s. They were the largest and most heavily armed battleships ever constructed, mounting 46 cm main guns — the largest calibre ever fitted to a warship.

Design and construction

Designed under conditions of great secrecy after Japan withdrew from the naval treaty system, the class was intended to give individual superiority over American battleships. Yamato was commissioned in December 1941 and Musashi in August 1942. A third ship, Shinano, was converted on the slipway into an aircraft carrier; a fourth hull was scrapped incomplete.

War service

Neither battleship ever fought the surface duel for which it was designed. Musashi was sunk by American carrier aircraft in the Sibuyan Sea during the Battle of Leyte Gulf in October 1944. Shinano was torpedoed by the submarine USS Archerfish in November 1944, days after commissioning. Yamato was sunk by carrier aircraft on 7 April 1945 during Operation Ten-Go, a one-way mission toward Okinawa.
