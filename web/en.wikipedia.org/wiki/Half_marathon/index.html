Half marathon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Half marathon

A half marathon is a road running event of 21.0975 kilometres (13 miles 192.5 yards) — exactly half the distance of a marathon. It is a popular distance for recreational and competitive runners, offering a significant endurance challenge that requires less training and recovery time than a full marathon. Participation in half marathons grew rapidly in the early 21st century, and in many countries more runners finish half marathons each year than marathons.

Training and participation

Typical training plans for beginners run 10 to 14 weeks, building weekly mileage toward long runs of 16–19 kilometres. World Athletics recognises world records for the distance; the men's record has been lowered repeatedly by East African runners and stood under 57 minutes in the mid-2020s (Jacob Kiplimo ran 56:42 in Barcelona in 2025), while the women's record is held by Letesenbet Gidey (1:02:52, Valencia 2021) in a mixed-gender race. Separate records are kept for women-only races.

Notable races

Major half marathons include the Great North Run in England — among the world's largest, with tens of thousands of participants — as well as races in Valencia, Lisbon, Copenhagen, Ras Al Khaimah and the World Athletics Half Marathon Championships, held since 1992. Many marathons offer a half marathon held on the same day or weekend.
