HD Radio

HD Radio (HDR) is a trademarked digital radio broadcast technology developed by iBiquity Digital Corporation (now part of Xperi) and adopted as the standard for digital AM and FM broadcasting in the United States. The system transmits digital audio alongside existing analog signals using in-band on-channel (IBOC) technology, allowing stations to offer CD-quality sound on FM, additional multicast channels (HD2, HD3), and data services such as song titles, album art and traffic information, without requiring new spectrum.

Technology

HD Radio places digital sidebands on either side of the host analog carrier. In FM hybrid mode the digital signal occupies the edges of the channel and uses COFDM modulation with the proprietary HDC codec, a descendant of AAC. Receivers blend to analog if the digital signal is lost. FM stations can divide their digital bitrate among a main channel and up to three additional multicast program streams.

Adoption

The FCC authorised HD Radio in 2002, and commercial broadcasts began in 2004. Thousands of US stations broadcast in HD, and the technology is factory-fitted in many cars; however, consumer awareness has remained modest compared with satellite radio and streaming. Critics have cited increased interference to adjacent stations on AM, where hybrid digital operation has since largely been abandoned by broadcasters.
