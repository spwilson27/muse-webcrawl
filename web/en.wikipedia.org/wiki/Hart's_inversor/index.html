Hart's inversor

Hart's inversor is a mechanical linkage that converts rotary motion into perfect straight-line motion without sliding guides. It was invented and published in 1874–75 by Harry Hart. Together with the Peaucellier–Lipkin linkage, it was one of the first mechanisms shown to produce an exact straight line.

Geometry

The inversor is based on an antiparallelogram linkage: a crossed quadrilateral with opposite sides equal. Points fixed on the bars of the antiparallelogram remain collinear with the crossing point, and the product of their distances from it is constant, giving a geometric inversion. Constraining one point to move on a circle through the centre of inversion makes a second point trace an exact straight line.

Variants

Hart devised two forms, known as Hart's first and second inversors; the first uses a six-bar arrangement with an A-frame appearance. Straight-line mechanisms found practical use in early steam engines and precision instruments.
