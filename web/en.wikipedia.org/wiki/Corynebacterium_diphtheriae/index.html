Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Corynebacterium diphtheriae is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped bacterium, the causative agent of diphtheria. It is also known as the Klebs–Löffler bacillus, having been identified by German bacteriologists Edwin Klebs and Friedrich Löffler in 1883–1884.

Characteristics

The organism is club-shaped (Greek koryne, "club"), non-motile, non-spore-forming and aerobic. Four biotypes are recognised: gravis, mitis, intermedius and belfanti. Cultures show characteristic dark colonies on tellurite media, and stained cells display metachromatic (Babes–Ernst) granules.

Pathogenicity

Only strains lysogenised by a specific bacteriophage carrying the tox gene produce diphtheria toxin, an AB exotoxin that ADP-ribosylates elongation factor EF-2, halting host protein synthesis. The toxin damages the respiratory mucosa — forming the characteristic grey pseudomembrane that can obstruct the airway — and can injure the heart and nerves.

Prevention and treatment

Diphtheria is prevented by vaccination with diphtheria toxoid (as in the DPT and Td vaccines). Treatment of disease requires diphtheria antitoxin plus antibiotics such as erythromycin or penicillin.
