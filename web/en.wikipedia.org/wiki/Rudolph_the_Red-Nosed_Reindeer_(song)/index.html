Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (song)

"Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer"
Single by:Gene Autry
Released:1949
Songwriter:Johnny Marks

"Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer" is a song by songwriter Johnny Marks, based on the 1939 story Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer published by the Montgomery Ward Company. Gene Autry's recording hit No. 1 on the U.S. charts the week of Christmas 1949, and it sold 2.5 million copies the first year, eventually selling a claimed 25 million copies, remaining the second best-selling Christmas song of all time until the 1980s.

History

The story of Rudolph was written by Robert L. May, an employee of Montgomery Ward, as a promotional booklet in 1939. May's brother-in-law, Johnny Marks, a radio producer and songwriter, adapted the story into a song. Although several singers initially declined to record it, Gene Autry was persuaded, reportedly by his wife, and his version became an enormous hit.

The song relates how Rudolph, mocked by the other reindeer for his glowing red nose, is chosen by Santa Claus to lead his sleigh on a foggy Christmas Eve.

Other recordings

The song has been recorded by many artists, including Bing Crosby, Dean Martin, The Jackson 5, and Destiny's Child. Autry recorded several sequels and follow-ups over his career. Marks went on to write other Christmas songs featured in the 1964 Rankin/Bass television special Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer, including "The Most Wonderful Day of the Year" and "A Holly Jolly Christmas".
