Floor area ratio

Floor area ratio (FAR) is the ratio of a building's total floor area (gross floor area) to the size of the land upon which it is built. It is often used as one of the regulations in city planning along with the building-to-land ratio. The terms can also refer to limits imposed on such a ratio through zoning.

Calculation

FAR is computed by dividing the total floor area of the building (counting all floors, with certain exemptions that vary by jurisdiction) by the area of the lot. An FAR of 1.0 could mean a one-storey building covering the whole lot, or a four-storey building covering a quarter of the lot.

Purpose

Cities use FAR limits to control building bulk and density, manage infrastructure loads, and protect light and air. Critics argue that restrictive FAR limits constrain housing supply, and some cities grant FAR bonuses for public amenities, affordable housing, or preservation transfers of development rights.
