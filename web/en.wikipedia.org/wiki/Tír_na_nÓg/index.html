Tír na nÓg

Tír na nÓg (Old Irish: "Land of the Young") is one of the best-known names for the Celtic Otherworld in Irish mythology — an island paradise of eternal youth, beauty, health and joy, located far to the west across the sea.

TypeOtherworld island
TraditionIrish mythology

Oisín in Tír na nÓg

In the most famous tale, the hero Oisín, son of Fionn mac Cumhaill, is wooed by Niamh of the Golden Hair, who carries him on a white horse across the waves to Tír na nÓg. There they marry and he lives in bliss for what seems three years — actually three hundred. Homesick, he returns to Ireland on the same horse, warned never to dismount; when the saddle girth breaks and he touches Irish soil, the centuries fall upon him and he becomes a blind old man.

Features

Time passes differently in the Otherworld; its inhabitants, often identified with the Tuatha Dé Danann, do not age or sicken. Other Otherworld names include Mag Mell, Emain Ablach, and Annwn in Welsh tradition.

In culture

Tír na nÓg recurs in Irish literature, W. B. Yeats's poetry, film, and games as a symbol of lost youth and longing.
