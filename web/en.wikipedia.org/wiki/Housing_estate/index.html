Housing estate

A housing estate is a group of homes and other buildings built together as a single development. The term is especially common in the United Kingdom, where it is used for both public housing built by local authorities (council estates) and privately developed residential areas, and in Hong Kong, where large public housing estates house a substantial share of the population.

United Kingdom

British housing estates trace their origins to nineteenth-century model villages and garden suburbs, and expanded greatly after the First World War with "homes fit for heroes". After 1945, councils built millions of homes — low-rise cottage estates, mixed developments, and in the 1960s, high-rise tower blocks using industrialised building methods. The Housing Act 1980 gave tenants the "right to buy", transferring much of the stock into private ownership, while remaining council and housing-association estates have been the subject of regeneration programmes and debates about social housing provision.

Hong Kong and Asia

In Hong Kong, public housing estates — vast clusters of high-rise blocks housing roughly a third of the population — were built from the 1950s onwards, beginning with resettlement estates after the Shek Kip Mei fire of 1953, and are administered by the Hong Kong Housing Authority. Similar large-scale estate housing exists in Singapore (HDB towns) and in many cities of mainland China.

Other regions

In continental Europe, large post-war estates include the French grands ensembles and HLM developments and the prefabricated Plattenbau estates of East Germany and Eastern Europe. In the United States, comparable developments are usually called housing projects (public housing) or subdivisions and planned communities (private housing).
