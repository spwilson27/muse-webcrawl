Latent Dirichlet allocation

Latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA) is a Bayesian network and generative statistical model for collections of discrete data such as text corpora. Introduced by David Blei, Andrew Ng and Michael I. Jordan in 2003, it is one of the most widely used topic models.

Model

LDA assumes each document is a mixture of a small number of topics, and each topic is a distribution over words. In the generative process, for each document a topic distribution is drawn from a Dirichlet prior with parameter alpha; for each word position, a topic is drawn from that distribution, and then a word is drawn from the topic's word distribution, itself drawn from a Dirichlet prior beta. Only the words are observed; topics are latent variables inferred from the data.

Inference

Exact inference is intractable, so algorithms such as collapsed Gibbs sampling and variational Bayes are used. Libraries such as MALLET and gensim made LDA a standard tool in natural language processing, digital humanities and recommender research.

Applications and limits

LDA has been applied to scientific literature mapping, news analysis, genetics (where an analogous model describes population admixture) and software engineering. Criticisms include sensitivity to hyperparameters, the bag-of-words assumption ignoring word order, and topics that can be hard to interpret.
