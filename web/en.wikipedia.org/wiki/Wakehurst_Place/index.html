Wakehurst

Location: Ardingly, West Sussex, England

Area: About 500 acres (200 ha)

Managed by: Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew

Home of: The Millennium Seed Bank

Wakehurst, previously known as Wakehurst Place, is a house and botanic garden in West Sussex, England, near the village of Ardingly. The estate, of some 500 acres of woodland, reservoirs and formal and naturalistic gardens, has been managed by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew since 1965 and is often described as "Kew's country garden." The Elizabethan mansion at its centre was built around 1590 for Edward Culpeper.

The estate

The gardens were developed most extensively in the 20th century, first by Lord Wakehurst and then by Sir Henry Price, who purchased the estate in 1938 and spent 25 years creating its renowned plant collections. Price bequeathed Wakehurst to the National Trust, which leased it to Kew. The grounds include the Himalayan Glade, the Southern Hemisphere Garden, the Bog Garden, lakes and a large area of ancient woodland forming part of the Loder Valley nature reserve. Wakehurst's tree collections include notable specimens of Wollemi pine and dawn redwood.

Millennium Seed Bank

Wakehurst is home to the Millennium Seed Bank, the largest ex situ plant conservation project in the world by number of species. Housed in the Wellcome Trust Millennium Building, opened in 2000, its underground vaults store billions of seeds from tens of thousands of wild plant species, collected through partnerships in scores of countries, as an insurance against extinction and for research and restoration. In 2023 the seed bank passed the milestone of storing seeds of more than 40,000 species.
