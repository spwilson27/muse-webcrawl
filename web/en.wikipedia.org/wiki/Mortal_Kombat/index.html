Mortal Kombat is an American media franchise centred on a series of fighting video games originally developed by Midway Games in 1992. The development of the first game was originally based on an idea that Ed Boon and John Tobias had of making a video game starring martial-arts actor Jean-Claude Van Damme, but as that idea fell through, a fictional fighting tournament universe was created instead.

The original game became infamous for its realistic digitized sprites — actors filmed and inserted into the game — and for its graphic violence, most famously the finishing moves called Fatalities, in which the victor gruesomely kills the defeated opponent. The controversy surrounding the home version's release contributed to the United States congressional hearings on video game violence and the creation of the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) in 1994.

Gameplay

Players select from a roster of fighters — among the most recognisable are Scorpion, Sub-Zero, Liu Kang, Raiden, Sonya Blade and Johnny Cage — each with special moves performed through combinations of directional inputs and buttons. Later games added stage hazards, weapons, the X-Ray and Fatal Blow systems, and extensive single-player story modes. The series is noted for its secret characters, hidden fights, and its lore of realms including Earthrealm, Outworld, and the Netherrealm.

Legacy

The franchise has sold tens of millions of copies and spawned films (1995's Mortal Kombat and a 2021 reboot), animated and live-action television series, comic books, and a card game. It remains one of the best-selling fighting game franchises of all time and a defining title of 1990s arcade culture.
