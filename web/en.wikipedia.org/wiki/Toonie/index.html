Toonie

The toonie is the Canadian two-dollar coin, introduced by the Royal Canadian Mint on February 19, 1996. Its name is a blend of "two" and "loonie", the nickname of the one-dollar coin it joined in circulation.

The toonie is bimetallic: a nickel outer ring surrounds an aluminium bronze centre. The reverse depicts a polar bear standing on an ice floe, designed by artist Brent Townsend, while the obverse bears the portrait of the reigning monarch. The coin replaced the two-dollar banknote, which was withdrawn in the interest of durability and cost.

Design and features

The original issue was famous in numismatic circles because early minting problems occasionally caused the centre to pop out — coins separated this way were jokingly dubbed "moonies". In 2012 the mint updated the coin with a steel core, lighter weight, and security features including laser micro-engraving and edge lettering, to deter counterfeiting. Commemorative editions have marked events such as the 75th anniversary of D-Day and the discovery of insulin.
