Hanbok

Hanbok (Korean: 한복) is the traditional clothing of Korea. It is characterised by vibrant colours, simple lines without pockets, and a graceful silhouette. The term literally means "Korean clothing", and in North Korea the garment is called joseonot. Hanbok is worn today mainly on holidays, festivals and ceremonial occasions such as weddings and ancestral rites.

Components

A woman's hanbok consists of a short blouse or jacket (jeogori) worn with a full, high-waisted wrap skirt (chima), often with an underskirt (sokchima) and embroidered shoes (kkotshin). A man's hanbok comprises a jeogori with loose trousers (baji) tied at the ankles, sometimes with a vest (jokki) or overcoat (durumagi). Both are fastened with long decorative ribbons (goreum) and may be accessorised with a norigae pendant.

History

The basic structure of hanbok dates back to the Three Kingdoms period (57 BC – 668 AD), as seen in Goguryeo tomb murals. Designs evolved through the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties, when Confucian values influenced colour and decoration according to social status, age and occasion. Daily wear declined during the 20th century as Western clothing became standard, but hanbok remains a potent symbol of Korean cultural identity, and contemporary designers produce modernised versions for everyday use.
