Akinetic mutism is a medical term describing patients who tend neither to move (akinesia) nor speak (mutism). It results from severe frontal lobe injury, particularly damage to the anterior cingulate cortex and the circuits that initiate motivated behaviour. Patients appear awake, may follow objects with their eyes, but show profound apathy and do not spontaneously speak or move.

History

The clinical picture was described in association with tumours of the third ventricle and frontal lesions in the early twentieth century; the term was introduced by Cairns and colleagues in 1941, reporting a patient with a cyst of the third ventricle who alternated between normal behaviour and a mute, motionless state as the cyst changed size.

Causes

Causes include bilateral anterior cerebral artery strokes, tumours near the third ventricle, hydrocephalus, meningitis, and damage following rupture of anterior communicating artery aneurysms. The condition lies on a spectrum with abulia, a milder form of diminished motivation.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause (for example, draining hydrocephalus or removing a tumour). Dopaminergic agents such as bromocriptine and other stimulants have been reported to improve responsiveness in some cases.
