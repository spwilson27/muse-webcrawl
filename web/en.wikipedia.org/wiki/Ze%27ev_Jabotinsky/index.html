Ze'ev Jabotinsky

Ze'ev Jabotinsky

Born: October 18, 1880, Odessa, Russian Empire

Died: August 3, 1940, New York City, U.S.

Known for: Founder of Revisionist Zionism

Ze'ev Jabotinsky (born Vladimir Yevgenyevich Zhabotinsky; 1880–1940) was a Russian-born Zionist leader, author, journalist and orator who founded the Revisionist Zionist movement and helped create the Jewish Legion during the First World War.

Life and career

Raised in Odessa, Jabotinsky worked as a foreign correspondent before turning to Zionist activism after the 1903 Kishinev pogrom. During the First World War he campaigned successfully for Jewish battalions in the British Army. He founded the Haganah's rival self-defence ethos in Jerusalem and was imprisoned by the British in 1920.

Revisionist Zionism

In 1925 he founded the Union of Revisionist Zionists, advocating a Jewish state on both banks of the Jordan, mass immigration, and a more assertive stance toward the British Mandate and Arab opposition, famously argued in his 1923 essay "The Iron Wall". He also founded the Betar youth movement in 1923.
