Hyphen-minus

The hyphen-minus (-) is a character used in digital documents and computing to represent several similar characters, most importantly the hyphen and the minus sign, as well as dashes in some contexts. It is the most commonly typed dash-like character and appears on every standard keyboard. In Unicode it is U+002D, inherited from ASCII, where it occupies position 45.

Hyphen-minus
UnicodeU+002D
ASCII45 (decimal)
UsesHyphen, minus sign, dash substitute

History

Typewriters had a single key for hyphen and minus, and early character encodings retained the compromise: ASCII included only one such character, and printers and programmers used it as hyphen, minus sign and dash alike. Unicode later added distinct characters—the hyphen (U+2010), en dash (U+2013), em dash (U+2014) and minus sign (U+2212)—but the hyphen-minus remains dominant in plain text and programming.

Uses

The hyphen-minus serves as a hyphen in compound words, as a minus sign in arithmetic and programming, to mark command-line options (for example -h or --help), in ranges and in hyphenation. Two hyphen-minuses are often typed as a substitute em dash, and many word processors auto-convert this to a true dash.
