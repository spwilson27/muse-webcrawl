Santillana del Mar

Santillana del Mar is a historic town in Cantabria, northern Spain, renowned for its well-preserved medieval old town. Despite its name ("of the sea"), it lies a few kilometres inland. A popular joke calls it the town of three lies: it is neither holy (santa), flat (llana), nor by the sea (mar).

History and sights

The town grew around the monastery of Santa Juliana, whose collegiate church, the Colegiata de Santa Juliana, is a fine Romanesque building of the 12th century. Cobbled streets are lined with stone mansions and palaces from the 14th to 18th centuries, such as the Casa del Águila y la Parra and the Palacio de Velarde. The philosopher Jean-Paul Sartre described Santillana as the prettiest village in Spain.

Altamira

Near the town lies the Cave of Altamira, famous for its Upper Paleolithic polychrome paintings of bison and other animals, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Access to the original cave is heavily restricted; a museum and replica (the Neocave) receive visitors.
