British Expeditionary Force (World War II)

British Expeditionary Force (BEF)
Active: 1939–1940
Country: United Kingdom
Commander: Lord Gort
Size: ~390,000 at peak

The British Expeditionary Force (BEF) was the contingent of the British Army sent to France in 1939–1940 during the Second World War, commanded by General Lord Gort, supporting the French against Nazi Germany.

History

Deployed after Britain's declaration of war in September 1939, the BEF grew to 10 divisions. During the German offensive of May 1940 (Fall Gelb), it was cut off and driven back to Dunkirk, from where Operation Dynamo (26 May–4 June 1940) evacuated about 338,000 British and Allied troops to England, abandoning most heavy equipment. A second BEF sent later in June was also evacuated (Operation Aerial).
