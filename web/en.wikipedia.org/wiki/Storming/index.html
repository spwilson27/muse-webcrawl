Storming

Storming is a military term describing a rapid, violent assault on a fortified position, in which attackers attempt to take the position by direct force rather than by siege. Historically, storming was used against castles, fortresses, and entrenched defensive lines, where troops would rush across open ground under fire to scale walls or breach defences with ladders, battering rams, or explosives.

History

In siege warfare, a commander would typically order a storm only after artillery or mining had opened a practicable breach in the defences. Troops assigned to the first wave of a storm were sometimes called the "forlorn hope", because their chances of survival were low; survivors were often promised promotion or reward. Famous storms include the storming of Badajoz and Ciudad Rodrigo during the Peninsular War, and the storming of the Bastille during the French Revolution, which had more political than military significance.

Modern usage

In modern warfare, the term is applied to rapid assaults on buildings or strongpoints by infantry, and metaphorically to the forcible seizure of buildings, as in the storming of a government building by a crowd. See also siege and assault.
