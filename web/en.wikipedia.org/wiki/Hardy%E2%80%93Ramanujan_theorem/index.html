In mathematics, the Hardy–Ramanujan theorem, proved by G. H. Hardy and Srinivasa Ramanujan in 1917, states that the normal order of the number ω(n) of distinct prime factors of a number n is log log n.

Statement

Roughly speaking, this means that most numbers have about log log n distinct prime factors. More precisely, for any function that grows more slowly than log log n, almost all numbers n have ω(n) within that tolerance of log log n. The theorem was the founding result of probabilistic number theory.

Significance

The theorem was later strengthened by the Erdős–Kac theorem, which shows that ω(n) is asymptotically normally distributed with mean and variance log log n. The 1917 paper by Hardy and Ramanujan is widely regarded as the beginning of the study of the statistical behaviour of arithmetic functions.
