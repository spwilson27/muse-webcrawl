Horatio Gates


Horatio Gates, American general

Horatio Lloyd Gates (July 26, 1727 – April 10, 1806) was a British-born American soldier who served as a general in the Continental Army during the early years of the American Revolutionary War. He is best known for commanding the American army that won the decisive Battles of Saratoga in 1777, a victory that helped bring France into the war, and for his disastrous defeat at the Battle of Camden in 1780.

Early life and British service

Gates was born in Maldon, Essex, England. He obtained a commission in the British Army and served in North America during the French and Indian War, fighting under General Edward Braddock and being wounded in the disastrous Battle of the Monongahela in 1755. After the war he found promotion slow and, frustrated, sold his commission and settled in Virginia in 1772, where he became friendly with George Washington.

Revolutionary War

When the Revolution began, Gates was appointed adjutant general of the Continental Army and organised its early administration. Given command in the north, his army received the surrender of British general John Burgoyne at Saratoga in October 1777, although subordinates such as Benedict Arnold and Daniel Morgan played major roles in the fighting. Gates was later implicated in the Conway Cabal, an alleged intrigue against Washington. In 1780 he took command in the South and suffered a crushing defeat at Camden, South Carolina, where his army collapsed; he rode far from the battlefield, an act that ruined his reputation, and he was replaced by Nathanael Greene.

Later life

Gates retired to his Virginia plantation, where he freed his slaves. In 1790 he moved to New York City, served a single term in the New York state legislature, and died in 1806.
