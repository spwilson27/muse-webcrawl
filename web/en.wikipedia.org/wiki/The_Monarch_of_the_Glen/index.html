The Monarch of the Glen (1851) is an oil-on-canvas painting of a red deer stag completed in 1851 by the English painter Sir Edwin Landseer. It is one of the most famous images associated with Scotland, and the stag in the painting has been widely reproduced and used as a symbol in advertising, including for the Glenfiddich whisky brand and the Hartford insurance company.

History

Landseer was commissioned to paint a series of three panels for the Refreshment Rooms of the Houses of Parliament. The project fell through, but Landseer completed one of the works, The Monarch of the Glen, which he sold privately. The painting depicts a majestic red deer stag with twelve points on its antlers standing in a misty Highland landscape, surveying the glen around him. The work passed through several owners; it was displayed in the Pears soap company's collection and later held by the whisky firm Dewar's, which used it in advertising. In 2016–17 the painting was acquired by the National Galleries of Scotland following a public fundraising campaign, and it is displayed at the Scottish National Gallery in Edinburgh.

Significance

The painting helped popularize romantic images of the Scottish Highlands and the sport of deer stalking during the Victorian era, when Queen Victoria's enthusiasm for Balmoral fueled a fashion for all things Highland. Landseer, a favorite animal painter of the Queen, also designed the lion sculptures at the base of Nelson's Column in Trafalgar Square, London.

Other uses of the title

The title has been reused for a 1941 novel by Compton Mackenzie, the BBC television drama Monarch of the Glen (2000–2005) set on a Highland estate, and various works celebrating Highland culture.
