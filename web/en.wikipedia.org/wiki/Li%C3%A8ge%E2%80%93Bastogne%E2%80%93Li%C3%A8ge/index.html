Liège–Bastogne–Liège, sometimes called La Doyenne ("the oldest"), is a one-day professional cycling race held annually in Belgium. First run in 1892, it is one of the five Monuments of cycling and the oldest of them, and it traditionally closes the spring classics season as part of the Ardennes classics.

Course

The race runs from Liège south to Bastogne and back, roughly 250–260 km through the hills of the Ardennes. Its signature climbs, or côtes, include the Côte de la Redoute, the Côte des Forges and the Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons; since 2019 the finish has returned to central Liège after decades ending in Ans.

History

The race was created in 1892 as an amateur event and became professional in 1908. Eddy Merckx holds the record with five victories (1969, 1971, 1972, 1973, 1975). Other multiple winners include Moreno Argentin and Alejandro Valverde with four each. A women's edition has been held since 2017.

Character

Unpredictable spring weather and the relentless series of short, steep climbs make the race a test of endurance and positioning, typically won by strong climbers and Grand Tour riders rather than sprinters.
