Alp Arslan, second sultan of the Seljuk Empire.

Alp Arslan (c. 1029 – 15 December 1072), born Muhammad bin Dawud Chaghri, was the second sultan of the Seljuk Empire and the great-grandson of Seljuk, the eponymous founder of the dynasty. His name means "heroic lion" in Turkish. He is best known for his decisive victory over the Byzantine Empire at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071, which opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement and is often taken as the beginning of the Turkish presence in Anatolia.

Early life and accession

Alp Arslan was the son of Chaghri Beg, one of the two founding brothers of the Seljuk enterprise, and governed Khurasan during his father's life. On the death of his uncle Tughril, the first sultan, in 1063, Alp Arslan secured the succession after defeating a rival claimant, and consolidated the empire's territories from Central Asia to Mesopotamia.

Reign

Assisted by his able vizier Nizam al-Mulk, Alp Arslan campaigned on all frontiers of the empire: in the Caucasus, where he conquered Ani and Kars from Byzantine and Armenian control; in Syria, where he took Aleppo; and in the east, where he campaigned against the Karakhanids in Transoxiana. Facing the Byzantine emperor Romanos IV Diogenes' attempt to recover eastern Anatolia, Alp Arslan met him near Manzikert, north of Lake Van, on 19 August 1071. The Seljuk victory — aided by the defection of part of the Byzantine force and the capture of the emperor himself — was a turning point in Byzantine history. Romanos was treated honourably, ransomed, and released, though he was deposed and blinded on his return.

Death

In 1072, during a campaign across the Amu Darya (Oxus), Alp Arslan was mortally wounded by a captured fortress commander whom he had ordered executed; the man drew a dagger and stabbed the sultan, who died four days later. He was succeeded by his son Malik-Shah I, under whom, guided by Nizam al-Mulk, the empire reached its greatest extent.
