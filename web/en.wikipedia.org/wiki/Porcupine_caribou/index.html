Porcupine caribou

Porcupine caribou
Scientific name: Rangifer tarandus granti
Range: Northern Alaska, Yukon, Northwest Territories

The Porcupine caribou (Rangifer tarandus granti) is a subspecies of the reindeer or caribou found in Alaska, Yukon and the Northwest Territories. It is named after the Porcupine River, which runs through a large part of the range of this herd.

The herd undertakes one of the longest land migrations of any mammal, travelling roughly 2,400 km (1,500 mi) between winter ranges in the boreal forest and calving grounds on the Arctic coastal plain, including the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge. The population has fluctuated widely; it peaked at about 218,000 animals in 2017, before declining in later counts.

Management and controversy

The "1002 area" of the Arctic Refuge coastal plain, a key calving ground, has long been the focus of controversy over proposed oil and gas development. The Gwich'in people of Alaska and Canada depend on the herd for subsistence and regard the calving grounds as sacred, while the Inupiat community of Kaktovik has supported development.
