Overview

Mount Tarawera is a volcano on the North Island of New Zealand, about 24 kilometres south-east of Rotorua, within the Okataina volcanic centre. It consists of a series of rhyolitic lava domes fissured down the middle by its violent eruption of 1886, one of the largest and most destructive volcanic eruptions in New Zealand's recorded history.

The 1886 eruption

Shortly after midnight on 10 June 1886 a series of earthquakes heralded the eruption. Basaltic magma broke through the rhyolite domes and a fissure roughly 17 kilometres long opened across the mountain and the neighbouring Lake Rotomahana basin. Ash and scoria buried the Māori villages of Te Ariki, Moura and Te Wairoa, and around 120 people were killed. Official tolls were lower, but many accounts place the deaths at 120 or more, making it New Zealand's deadliest volcanic disaster.

The eruption destroyed the celebrated Pink and White Terraces (Otukapuarangi and Te Tarata), silica sinter formations on Lake Rotomahana that had been a major nineteenth-century tourist attraction, sometimes called the Eighth Wonder of the World. Lake Rotomahana was blasted into a new, much larger crater lake, and the steaming Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley formed in the south-western part of the fissure.

Later history

The buried village of Te Wairoa has been partly excavated and is a popular visitor attraction. Guided walking tours operate on the mountain itself, which is administered with the local iwi, Tūhourangi. In 2017, survey teams announced evidence that a portion of the Pink Terraces may have survived, buried on the shoreline of the new Lake Rotomahana, though the question remains unsettled.
