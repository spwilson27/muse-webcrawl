Postpartum depression

Postpartum depression (PPD), also called postnatal depression, is a type of mood disorder associated with childbirth, which can affect both parents. Symptoms may include extreme sadness, low energy, anxiety, crying episodes, irritability, and changes in sleeping or eating patterns. Onset is typically within the first few weeks after childbirth but can occur at any time during the first year. PPD can negatively affect the newborn child as well as the parent.

Distinction from baby blues

PPD is more severe and longer-lasting than the common "baby blues", a mild and transient state of tearfulness and anxiety that affects many new mothers and resolves within about two weeks without treatment. A rarer and much more serious condition, postpartum psychosis, is a psychiatric emergency.

Causes and treatment

The exact cause is unknown, but hormonal changes, sleep deprivation, genetic vulnerability, and prior depression are believed to contribute. Risk factors include a history of depression, stressful life events, and lack of social support. Screening often uses the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale. Treatment may include counselling, support groups, and antidepressant medication; in 2019 brexanolone became the first drug approved in the United States specifically for postpartum depression, followed by zuranolone in 2023. Fathers can also experience depression in the perinatal period, at lower but significant rates.
