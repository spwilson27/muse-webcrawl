Winter Hexagon

Not to be confused with the Winter Triangle within it, or with Saturn's polar hexagon.

Winter Hexagon
Type: Asterism
Vertex stars: Sirius, Rigel, Aldebaran, Capella, Pollux, Procyon
Visibility: Winter evenings (Northern Hemisphere)

The Winter Hexagon, sometimes called the Winter Circle, is a large asterism formed by six of the brightest stars in the northern winter sky, each from a different constellation: Sirius in Canis Major, Rigel in Orion, Aldebaran in Taurus, Capella in Auriga, Pollux in Gemini, and Procyon in Canis Minor. Within the hexagon lies an eighth bright star, Betelgeuse in Orion, which forms the smaller Winter Triangle with Sirius and Procyon.

Location and visibility

The hexagon spans a large region of sky — its stars range from Sirius at declination about −17° up to Capella near +46° — and it is prominent on winter evenings in the Northern Hemisphere, gradually crossing the meridian through the season. Observers in the Southern Hemisphere see most of the same stars high in their summer sky, where the pattern is sometimes called the Summer Hexagon.

Contents

The Milky Way runs through the hexagon, and within its outline lie many notable deep-sky objects, including the Orion Nebula, the Pleiades (near Aldebaran), the Rosette Nebula and the Crab Nebula. The asterism is a useful guidepost for locating winter constellations and for star-hopping to fainter sights.
