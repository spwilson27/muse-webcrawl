Local Government Act 1894

Local Government Act 1894
Citation: 56 & 57 Vict. c. 73
Royal assent: 5 March 1894
Government: Gladstone / Rosebery
Territory: England and Wales

The Local Government Act 1894 (56 & 57 Vict. c. 73) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reformed local government in England and Wales outside the county boroughs. It created a system of urban and rural districts with elected councils, and established civil parish councils, completing the framework begun by the Local Government Act 1888, which had created county councils.

Provisions

The Act converted the existing sanitary districts into urban districts and rural districts, each governed by an elected district council that took over responsibilities for public health, housing, water supply and roads from the former boards. It created elected parish councils in rural parishes with more than 300 inhabitants, transferring civil functions from the church vestries, and parish meetings in smaller parishes.

The Act was notable for widening the local franchise: there was no property qualification for parish councillors, and married women were eligible both to vote in local elections and to sit on parish and district councils — decades before women gained the parliamentary vote.

Legacy

The structure of urban and rural districts created in 1894 survived largely intact until the reorganisation of local government in 1974.
