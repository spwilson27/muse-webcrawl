Siddhar

A Siddhar (Tamil: cittar) is a saint in Tamil tradition who is believed to have attained siddhis, supernatural powers and spiritual perfection, through meditation, alchemy, and yogic practice. The Siddhars form one of the most distinctive currents of Tamil religious culture, blending Shaivism, medicine, alchemy, and mysticism.

History

The Siddhar tradition is traditionally said to comprise eighteen principal Siddhars, though lists vary. Figures such as Agastya, Bogar, Thirumoolar, and Patanjali (in his Tamil aspect) are counted among them. Thirumoolar's Tirumantiram is one of the foundational texts of Tamil Shaivism and is regarded as a Siddhar work. The Siddhars are associated with the development of Siddha medicine, one of India's traditional systems of healing, which uses herbal, mineral, and metallic preparations.

Beliefs and practices

Siddhars sought liberation and physical immortality through disciplines including breath control (pranayama), meditation, and the preparation of elixirs. Their writings often use a coded, symbolic "twilight language" that conceals esoteric meanings. Many Siddhar texts are critical of caste hierarchy and empty ritual, emphasising direct inner experience of the divine.

Legacy

The Siddhar tradition remains influential in Tamil Nadu, where Siddhar shrines attract devotees and Siddha medicine is still practised and formally taught. The Palani hills, associated with Bogar, are a major pilgrimage centre.
