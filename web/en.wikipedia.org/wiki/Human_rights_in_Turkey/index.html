Human rights in Turkey


Ankara, Turkey.

Human rights in Turkey are protected by a variety of international law treaties, which take precedence over domestic legislation, according to Article 90 of the 1982 Constitution. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) was not signed by Turkey until 2000. The issue of human rights, however, remains controversial in Turkey; international organizations including Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have documented issues regarding freedom of the press, freedom of expression, the rights of Kurdish and other minorities, torture and ill-treatment in detention, and the independence of the judiciary.

History

In the wake of the 1980 military coup, human rights abuses became systematic, prompting Turkey's application for European Union membership to be long delayed over the issue. Reforms in the early 2000s, including the abolition of the death penalty, improved the situation, but after the failed coup attempt of 15 July 2016, a two-year state of emergency saw mass arrests and dismissals of public officials, journalists, and academics, drawing widespread international criticism.

Specific issues

Frequently raised concerns include the treatment of the Kurdish population and restrictions in the southeast, violence against women, the rights of refugees (Turkey hosts millions of Syrian refugees), press freedom, and the prosecution of journalists and opposition politicians.
