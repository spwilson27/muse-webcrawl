Film scanner

A film scanner is a device designed to scan photographic film directly into a computer without the intermediate use of a print. It digitises negatives and slides at high resolution, capturing detail, dynamic range, and colour depth from the original film.

Operation

The film is held in a holder or carrier and illuminated, usually by an LED or cold-cathode light source, while a CCD or CMOS sensor records the image. Dedicated film scanners typically offer higher optical resolution and greater Dmax (maximum density) than flatbed scanners with transparency adapters, making them better suited to dense slide film. Many models include infrared dust-and-scratch removal (such as Digital ICE), which detects defects on a separate infrared channel and removes them in software; the technique does not work with most black-and-white films.

Use

Film scanners became widespread in the 1990s and 2000s as photographers and archives transitioned to digital workflows. Brands such as Nikon, Minolta, Canon, and Epson produced popular models. Motion-picture film scanners, a professional counterpart, scan cinema film frame by frame for digital intermediates and restoration.
