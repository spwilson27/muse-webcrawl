John Robert Wooden (14 October 1910 – 4 June 2010) was an American basketball coach and player. Nicknamed the "Wizard of Westwood", he won ten NCAA national championships in a 12-year period as head coach of the UCLA Bruins — including a record seven in a row — a feat unmatched in college basketball.

Playing career

Wooden was an All-American guard at Purdue University, where he helped the Boilermakers win the 1932 national championship and was named college player of the year. He was one of the first players inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame (1960), later becoming the first person inducted as both player and coach (1973).

UCLA dynasty

After coaching at Indiana State, Wooden took over at UCLA in 1948 and built a dynasty on fast-break basketball, fundamentals and discipline. His teams won titles in 1964, 1965, 1967, 1968, 1969, 1970, 1971, 1972, 1973 and 1975, and at one point won 88 consecutive games. He coached stars including Lew Alcindor (Kareem Abdul-Jabbar), Bill Walton and Gail Goodrich. Wooden retired after the 1975 championship with a career record of 664 wins and 162 losses.

Philosophy and legacy

Wooden distilled his coaching philosophy into the "Pyramid of Success", a set of virtues headed by competitive greatness, and became as celebrated for aphorisms and moral instruction as for tactics. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2003. He died in Los Angeles in 2010, aged 99.
