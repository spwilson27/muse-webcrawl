Knut (pronounced [ˈknuːt]; 5 December 2006 – 19 March 2011) was a polar bear born in captivity at the Berlin Zoological Garden. Rejected by his mother after his twin brother died, he was raised by zookeepers and became a worldwide media sensation — a phenomenon dubbed "Knutmania" — drawing record crowds to the zoo, spawning merchandise, songs, books, and a film, and serving as a mascot in public debate about climate change and the conservation of polar bears.

Early life

Knut was born on 5 December 2006 to Tosca, a 20-year-old former East German circus bear. Tosca rejected both cubs for unknown reasons; the twin died of an infection after four days, and keepers rescued Knut after he had spent 44 hours alone on a rock. Zookeeper Thomas Dörflein became his surrogate parent, sleeping beside the cub's crate and feeding him from a bottle. When the crustacean-free, hand-rearing approach was criticised by an animal-rights activist who argued the cub should be allowed to die, public sympathy for Knut surged and made international headlines.

Celebrity

Knut made his public debut on 23 March 2007 before hundreds of journalists. Visitor numbers doubled at the Berlin Zoo; shares in the zoo rose sharply; Knut appeared on the cover of Vanity Fair (German edition) photographed by Annie Leibovitz alongside Leonardo DiCaprio, was the subject of a children's book, a hit song ("Knut Is Cute"), plush toys by Steiff, and the 2008 animated film Knut and His Friends. German environment minister Sigmar Gabriel formally adopted Knut as a mascot for a 2008 biodiversity conference in Bonn.

Later life and death

As Knut grew into a large adult, media attention faded. Keeper Dörflein's sudden death from a heart attack in September 2008 was a major story in Germany. Knut was later housed with female bears, including his mother. On 19 March 2011, aged four, Knut collapsed and died in his enclosure in front of hundreds of visitors; an autopsy found significant changes in his brain — later research attributed the death to anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, an autoimmune disease, the first such case reported in a non-human. A bronze statue of Knut was unveiled at the zoo in 2012, and his mounted pelt is displayed at Berlin's Natural History Museum.
