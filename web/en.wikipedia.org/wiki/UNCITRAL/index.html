UNCITRAL

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations Commission on International Trade Law (UNCITRAL) is the core legal body of the United Nations system in the field of international trade law, established by the General Assembly in 1966 and based in Vienna. Its membership (60 states elected by the Assembly for six-year terms, as expanded in 2002) works through working groups open to all member states and observers, producing conventions, model laws and guides that harmonise commercial law across legal traditions.

Its instruments structure modern commerce: the Vienna Sales Convention (CISG, 1980) governing international sales in some 95 states; the New York Convention's complement, the Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (1985, amended 2006), adopted in more than 80 jurisdictions; model laws on electronic commerce (1996), insolvency (1997), secured transactions and public procurement; and the Hamburg and Rotterdam rules on carriage of goods. UNCITRAL's method — technocratic, consensus-based, soft-law heavy — is itself studied as a model of global lawmaking, and its working groups (on arbitration, insolvency, digital trade, investor-state dispute reform) remain active sites of legal diplomacy.
