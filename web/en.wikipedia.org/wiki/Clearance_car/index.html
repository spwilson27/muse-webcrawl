Clearance car

A clearance car is a type of railroad maintenance of way vehicle used to check that the structure gauge of a railway line — the clearance profile through tunnels, bridges, station platforms, and other lineside structures — is sufficient for trains to pass safely. Clearance cars carry an array of measuring devices, historically including physical feeler arms, templates, or "whiskers" set to the outline of the loading gauge; modern cars use laser and optical scanning systems to build a precise profile of obstructions along the track.

Purpose

Clearance measurements are needed when a line is newly built, after engineering work that might have encroached on the gauge, and before running vehicles of a new or larger profile. If an obstruction is found within the required envelope, it must be removed or the operation of the rolling stock restricted. Clearance cars are also used to survey routes for out-of-gauge or exceptional loads.

History

Early clearance cars were simple frames built to the maximum permitted outline of a train, pushed slowly along the line so that any part of the frame striking a structure revealed an infringement. In the twentieth century these were supplemented and eventually replaced by instrumented cars, and today high-speed laser measurement trains can record tunnel and platform profiles at normal line speeds.
