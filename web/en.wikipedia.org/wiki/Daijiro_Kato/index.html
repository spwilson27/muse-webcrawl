Daijiro Kato

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Daijiro Kato (Japanese: 加藤 大治郎, Katō Daijirō; 4 July 1976 – 20 April 2003) was a Japanese Grand Prix motorcycle road racer. He won the 250cc World Championship in 2001 in one of the most dominant seasons in the class's history, and was regarded as one of the most talented riders of his generation before his death following a crash at the 2003 Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka.

Career

Kato was born in Saitama, Japan, and began riding pocket bikes at the age of three. He made his Grand Prix debut as a wildcard in the 125cc class in 1996, finishing third, and won his home 250cc race at Suzuka as a wildcard in both 1997 and 1998. He joined the world championship full-time in 2000 with the Gresini Honda team, winning four races and the Rookie of the Year award.

In 2001 Kato dominated the 250cc championship, winning 11 of 16 races—a record for the class—and securing the title with a round to spare. He moved up to the premier MotoGP class in 2002, finishing seventh in the standings with two podium finishes aboard Honda machinery.

Death

On 6 April 2003, during the season-opening Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka Circuit, Kato crashed heavily at the high-speed Casio Triangle chicane. He sustained severe head, neck and chest injuries and fell into a coma. He died in hospital two weeks later, on 20 April 2003, aged 26. The accident led to major safety changes at Suzuka, which was removed from the MotoGP calendar, and prompted criticism of the handling of the incident by race marshals.

Kato's racing number, 74, was retired from use in the premier class in his honour, and he was inducted into the MotoGP Hall of Fame as a Legend in 2003.
