Jane Seymour Fonda (born 21 December 1937) is an American actress and activist. Recognised as a film icon, she is the recipient of numerous accolades, including two Academy Awards, two British Academy Film Awards, seven Golden Globe Awards, and a Primetime Emmy Award. The daughter of actor Henry Fonda, she made her screen debut in 1960.

Career

Fonda rose to prominence in the 1960s with films such as Cat Ballou (1965) and Barbarella (1968). She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for Klute (1971) and Coming Home (1978), and gave acclaimed performances in They Shoot Horses, Don't They?, Julia, The China Syndrome, On Golden Pond and 9 to 5. In the 1980s she became a fitness phenomenon with her bestselling workout videos. She returned to prominence in the 2010s co-starring in the Netflix series Grace and Frankie (2015–2022).

Activism

Fonda was a prominent opponent of the Vietnam War; her 1972 visit to Hanoi, during which she was photographed on a North Vietnamese anti-aircraft gun, earned her the nickname "Hanoi Jane" and lasting controversy, for which she has repeatedly expressed regret. She has subsequently campaigned on feminist causes, against the Iraq War, and on climate change, including her "Fire Drill Fridays" protests in Washington, D.C., beginning in 2019.

Personal life

She was married to director Roger Vadim, activist Tom Hayden, and media magnate Ted Turner. Her memoir My Life So Far was published in 2005.
