Tupolev Tu-95

The Tupolev Tu-95 (NATO reporting name: Bear) is a large, four-engine turboprop-powered strategic bomber and missile platform developed by the Soviet Union during the Cold War. First flown in 1952, it remains in service with Russia, making it one of the longest-serving military aircraft designs.

Design

The Tu-95 is powered by four Kuznetsov NK-12 turboprops driving contra-rotating propellers, giving it a top speed of about 925 km/h and intercontinental range. The NK-12 remains the most powerful turboprop ever flown, and the aircraft's swept wing (35 degrees) was unusual for a propeller-driven design. Its noise signature is famously loud.

Variants and service

Variants include the Tu-95MS cruise-missile carrier, the Tu-142 maritime patrol aircraft, and the Tu-114 airliner derivative. A modified Tu-95V dropped the Tsar Bomba, the largest nuclear weapon ever tested, in 1961. Russian Tu-95MS aircraft continue to fly long-range patrols and have launched cruise missiles in combat operations.
