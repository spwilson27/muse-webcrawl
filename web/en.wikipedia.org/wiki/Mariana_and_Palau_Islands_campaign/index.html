The Mariana and Palau Islands campaign, also known as Operation Forager, was an offensive launched by United States forces against Imperial Japanese forces in the Mariana Islands and Palau in the Pacific Ocean between June and November 1944 during the Pacific War. The campaign was part of the American "island hopping" strategy and aimed to seize airfields from which the new B-29 Superfortress bombers could strike the Japanese home islands.

The Marianas

The US Fifth Fleet under Admiral Raymond Spruance, with ground forces under Lieutenant General Holland Smith, first attacked Saipan on 15 June 1944. The Japanese Combined Fleet sortied to intervene, leading to the Battle of the Philippine Sea (19–20 June), a decisive American carrier victory remembered as the "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot". Saipan was declared secure on 9 July, at heavy cost, including the mass suicides of Japanese civilians at Marpi. Tinian was captured between 24 July and 1 August, and Guam, invaded on 21 July, was secured by 10 August. The fall of Saipan caused the collapse of the Japanese government of Hideki Tojo.

The Palaus

In September 1944, US forces landed on Peleliu (15 September) and Angaur in the Palau Islands. Peleliu, expected to fall in days, instead saw some of the fiercest fighting of the war, as the Japanese employed a new defence-in-depth strategy in caves and bunkers; the battle lasted until 27 November. Ulithi atoll was seized unopposed and became a major fleet anchorage.

Aftermath

The capture of the Marianas placed B-29 bases within range of Japan; the first raid on Tokyo from Saipan took place in November 1944. Nearly 10,000 Americans and more than 60,000 Japanese died in the campaign. The airstrips on Tinian later served as the departure point for the atomic bomb missions against Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.
