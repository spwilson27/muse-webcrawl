The China Academy of Art (Chinese: 中国美术学院) is a leading Chinese art academy based in Hangzhou, Zhejiang. Founded in 1928 as the National Academy of Art by the educator Cai Yuanpei with the painter Lin Fengmian as its first president, it was the first comprehensive art academy in modern China.

History and programmes

The academy was established on the shore of West Lake with the aim of integrating Chinese and Western art. It relocated several times during the war with Japan and subsequent upheavals, returning to Hangzhou in the 1940s–50s and taking its present name in 1993. It offers programmes in fine art (including Chinese painting and calligraphy), design, architecture, film and animation, and art theory, and awards China's first doctorates in fine arts. Its Xiangshan campus, designed in part by the Pritzker Prize-winning architect Wang Shu, a professor at the academy, is noted for its use of recycled materials and traditional rooflines.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include Pan Tianshou, Wu Guanzhong, Zao Wou-Ki (who studied there), and Wang Shu. The academy also hosts the China Design Museum.
