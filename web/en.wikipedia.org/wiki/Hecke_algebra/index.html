Hecke algebra

The name Hecke algebra is used for several related algebras in mathematics, all ultimately connected with the work of Erich Hecke. Depending on context it may refer to:

  • the algebra of Hecke operators acting on modular forms;
  • the Iwahori–Hecke algebra, a deformation of the group algebra of a Coxeter group; or
  • the Hecke algebra of a locally compact group, the convolution algebra of bi-invariant functions with respect to a compact subgroup.

Hecke operators

Hecke introduced operators Tn acting on spaces of modular forms. These operators commute with each other and are self-adjoint with respect to the Petersson inner product, so modular forms can be chosen to be simultaneous eigenfunctions — Hecke eigenforms. Their eigenvalues encode deep arithmetic information: for the Ramanujan tau function, the eigenvalue of Tp is τ(p), and the multiplicativity of these eigenvalues reflects the Euler product of the associated L-function. Hecke algebras of double cosets generalise this construction to automorphic forms on other groups.

Iwahori–Hecke algebras

Given a Coxeter group W with generators s, its Iwahori–Hecke algebra Hq(W) is an algebra over a ring containing a parameter q, with generators Ts satisfying the braid relations of W together with the quadratic relation (Ts − q)(Ts + 1) = 0. At q = 1 it reduces to the group algebra of W. These algebras arose in the representation theory of finite groups of Lie type, where they govern the representations of the group generated by a Borel subgroup's double cosets, and were computed in generality through the work of Iwahori, Matsumoto, Kazhdan and Lusztig.

Iwahori–Hecke algebras of the symmetric group are intimately connected with quantum groups and knot theory: the Jones polynomial can be constructed from their representations, and the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials arose from their representation theory.

Hecke algebras of groups

For a locally compact group G and a compact open subgroup K, the Hecke algebra consists of compactly supported K-bi-invariant functions under convolution. Its representation theory corresponds to the representations of G generated by their K-fixed vectors, a framework central to the study of representations of p-adic groups and the Langlands program.
