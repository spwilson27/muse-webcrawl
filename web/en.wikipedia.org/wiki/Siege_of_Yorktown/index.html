The siege of Yorktown (28 September – 19 October 1781), also known as the Battle of Yorktown, was the decisive final engagement of the American Revolutionary War. A combined force of American Continental Army troops led by General George Washington and French Army troops led by the Comte de Rochambeau, supported by a French fleet under the Comte de Grasse, surrounded and besieged the British army of Lieutenant General Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia, forcing its surrender and effectively ending major combat in the war.

Surrender of Lord Cornwallis by John Trumbull, depicting the British capitulation of 19 October 1781.

Background

In 1781 Cornwallis moved his army to the port of Yorktown on the Chesapeake Bay to rest and refit, expecting support from the Royal Navy. Washington, learning that Admiral de Grasse was sailing from the West Indies with a large fleet, marched his allied army south from New York in a celebrated feat of logistics and deception. De Grasse defeated the British fleet at the Battle of the Chesapeake (5 September), sealing Cornwallis's fate by cutting off relief and escape by sea.

The siege and surrender

Following formal siege operations on the Vauban model, allied engineers dug successive parallels toward the British lines while artillery bombarded the town. On the night of 14 October, American light infantry under Alexander Hamilton stormed Redoubt No. 10 and French troops took Redoubt No. 9, allowing the allies to complete their second parallel and render the British position untenable. After a failed breakout attempt across the York River, Cornwallis opened negotiations and surrendered on 19 October 1781; over 7,000 British soldiers became prisoners. Tradition holds that the British band played "The World Turned Upside Down." News of the disaster destroyed British political will to continue the war, leading to the Treaty of Paris (1783), in which Britain recognised American independence.
