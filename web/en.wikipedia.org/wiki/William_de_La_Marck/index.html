William de La Marck (1446 – 18 June 1485), nicknamed the Wild Boar of the Ardennes, was a nobleman and adventurer of the House of La Marck who terrorised the Prince-Bishopric of Liège in the late fifteenth century. He is notorious for the murder of the prince-bishop Louis de Bourbon in 1482.

Life

A younger son of the counts of Marck, William carved out a lordship around Sedan and Bouillon and waged a long feud against Louis de Bourbon, Prince-Bishop of Liège, over family claims and influence in the bishopric. Backed at times by Louis XI of France, he seized Liège in 1482, and the bishop was killed — by William himself, according to tradition.

Downfall

William installed his son as bishop but could not hold the city against Maximilian of Austria, regent of the Burgundian Netherlands. Captured in 1485 after further intrigues, he was taken to Maastricht and beheaded.

In literature

William appears as a villain in Sir Walter Scott's novel Quentin Durward (1823), which popularised his nickname, the Wild Boar of the Ardennes.
