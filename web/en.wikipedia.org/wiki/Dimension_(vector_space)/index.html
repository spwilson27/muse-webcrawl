Dimension (vector space)

A basis of a vector space: the dimension counts the vectors in any basis.

In mathematics, the dimension of a vector space V is the cardinality (number of vectors) of a basis of V over its base field. It is sometimes called the Hamel dimension (after Georg Hamel) to distinguish it from other notions of dimension.

Definition

A basis of a vector space is a set of vectors that is linearly independent and spans the space. A fundamental theorem of linear algebra states that all bases of a given vector space have the same cardinality, so the dimension is well defined. If this cardinality is finite, the space is called finite-dimensional, and the dimension is a natural number; otherwise the space is infinite-dimensional and the dimension is an infinite cardinal.

Examples

  • The real coordinate space Rn has dimension n, with the standard basis of unit vectors.
  • The complex numbers C form a vector space of dimension 1 over themselves, but dimension 2 over the real numbers, with basis {1, i}.
  • The space of all polynomials in one variable is infinite-dimensional, with basis {1, x, x2, …}.

Properties

Every subspace of a vector space of dimension n has dimension at most n. For a linear map between finite-dimensional spaces, the rank–nullity theorem relates the dimensions of the image and kernel: dim(image) + dim(kernel) = dim(domain). Two vector spaces over the same field are isomorphic if and only if they have the same dimension.
