Geared turbofan

A geared turbofan is a type of turbofan aircraft engine in which a planetary gearbox between the fan and the low-pressure shaft allows each to rotate at its optimum speed. In a conventional turbofan the fan, low-pressure compressor and low-pressure turbine are rigidly connected.

Because the fan can turn more slowly while the low-pressure spool turns faster, geared turbofans can use larger-diameter fans with higher bypass ratios, improving propulsive efficiency and reducing fuel burn and noise.

Examples

The best-known example is the Pratt & Whitney PW1000G family ('GTF'), which powers the Airbus A320neo family, A220 and Embraer E-Jet E2. The Rolls-Royce UltraFan design also uses a power gearbox.
