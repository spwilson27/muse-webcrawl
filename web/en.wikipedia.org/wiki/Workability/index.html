Workability

Workability is a property of freshly mixed materials—most commonly concrete, mortar, soil or asphalt—that describes the ease with which the material can be mixed, placed, compacted and finished without segregating. A workable concrete, for example, flows and consolidates readily around reinforcement yet holds together as a uniform mass.

Concrete workability

Workability of concrete is governed mainly by water content, aggregate grading and shape, cement content, and chemical admixtures. It is most often measured with the slump test, in which the subsidence of a standard cone of fresh concrete is recorded; other methods include the Vebe consistometer, the compacting factor test, and flow-table tests for self-compacting concrete. Increasing water raises workability but weakens hardened concrete through a higher water–cement ratio, so plasticisers are used instead to gain flow without extra water.

Other materials

In soil mechanics and ceramics, workability relates to the moisture range over which a soil or clay can be shaped, described by the Atterberg limits. In asphalt technology it describes the ease of mixing and compaction of hot mix at given temperatures.
