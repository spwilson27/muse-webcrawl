Yamato Colony, Florida

Founded1905
LocationPresent-day Boca Raton, Palm Beach County, Florida
FounderJo Sakai

The Yamato Colony was a Japanese agricultural settlement founded in 1905 in what is now Boca Raton, Florida. Named after an ancient term for Japan, the colony was established by Jo Sakai and a small group of Japanese settlers recruited to grow pineapples and other crops on land promoted along the Florida East Coast Railway.

History

At its peak the colony numbered only a few dozen families farming land between present-day Yamato Road and the surrounding area. Pineapple blight, competition, and economic downturns gradually shrank the settlement through the 1920s and 1930s. In 1942, following the outbreak of war between the United States and Japan, the remaining land was taken for a military airfield (now Boca Raton Airport), ending the colony. One settler, George Sukeji Morikami, remained in the area; his land later became Morikami Park and the Morikami Museum and Japanese Gardens in Delray Beach.

Legacy

Yamato Road in Boca Raton preserves the colony's name, and the Morikami Museum interprets its history as one of the earliest Japanese-American communities in the southeastern United States.
