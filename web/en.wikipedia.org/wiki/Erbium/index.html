Erbium

Erbium
Symbol: Er
Atomic number: 68
Atomic mass: 167.259 u
Category: Lanthanide
Electron configuration: [Xe] 4f12 6s2
Melting point: 1770 K (1497 °C)
Boiling point: 3141 K (2868 °C)
Density: 9.066 g/cm3

Erbium is a chemical element; it has symbol Er and atomic number 68. It is a silvery-white solid metal that belongs to the lanthanide series and is a rare-earth element. Its properties are such that it can be found in nature only in chemical combination with other elements. Despite the name, erbium is not particularly rare, being more abundant than many transition metals.

History

Erbium was discovered in 1843 by Swedish chemist Carl Gustaf Mosander, who separated the mineral yttria into three components, one of which he named erbia after the village of Ytterby in Sweden. Pure metallic erbium was not isolated until 1934, when Wilhelm Klemm and Heinrich Bommer achieved its reduction.

Characteristics

A trivalent element, pure erbium metal is silvery-white, soft, and malleable. It is fairly stable in air compared with the other lanthanides, though it does oxidize slowly. When dissolved in acids, erbium forms pink-colored Er(III) salts, whose characteristic color makes erbium compounds useful as pigments.

Applications

Erbium-doped optical fibers are widely used as amplifiers in fiber-optic telecommunications, because erbium amplifies light at the wavelengths most commonly used for long-distance fiber transmission. Erbium-doped lasers are used in medicine and dentistry, and erbium compounds are used as a soft pink colorant in glazes and glass.
