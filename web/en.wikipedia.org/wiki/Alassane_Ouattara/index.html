Alassane Dramane Ouattara (born 1 January 1942) is an Ivorian politician and economist who has served as President of Ivory Coast (Côte d'Ivoire) since 2011. An economist by training, he worked at the International Monetary Fund and as governor of the Central Bank of West African States before becoming Prime Minister under President Félix Houphouët-Boigny in 1990.

Early life and career

Ouattara was born in Dimbokro and studied in Upper Volta (now Burkina Faso) and the United States, earning a PhD in economics from the University of Pennsylvania in 1972. He joined the IMF in 1968, later moving to the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO), where he became governor in 1988. Appointed Prime Minister in 1990, he implemented structural adjustment programmes during a period of economic crisis.

Path to the presidency

After Houphouët-Boigny's death in 1993, Ouattara's eligibility for the presidency was contested on grounds of nationality — the doctrine of Ivoirité was used to bar him from the elections of 1995 and 2000. He won the 2010 presidential election, but the incumbent Laurent Gbagbo refused to step down, triggering the 2010–2011 Ivorian crisis, which ended when Gbagbo was arrested in April 2011. Ouattara was sworn in the following month.

Presidency

Ouattara was re-elected in 2015 and, controversially, in 2020 after a constitutional reset allowed him a third term, a move boycotted by much of the opposition. His governments have overseen rapid economic growth, large infrastructure projects, and relative stability, alongside continuing debates over reconciliation and political freedoms.
