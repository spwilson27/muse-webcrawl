ZOMO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ZOMO (Polish: Zmotoryzowane Odwody Milicji Obywatelskiej), known in English as the Motorized Reserves of the Citizens' Militia, were paramilitary-police formations during the communist era in the People's Republic of Poland. They were the most infamous of Poland's police forces.

History

ZOMO units were created on 24 December 1956, in the aftermath of the Poznań protests, as highly mobile riot-control forces equipped with trucks, armored carriers, water cannon, and later tear gas launchers. Recruits were conscripts who served their military duty in the units and were subjected to intensive political indoctrination; membership brought privileges such as better pay and housing.

ZOMO became notorious for brutal suppression of dissent: the 1970 coastal protests, the imposition of martial law in December 1981, the pacification of the Wujek coal mine (in which nine miners were killed), and beatings of Solidarity demonstrators through the 1980s. Their dark-blue camouflage uniforms and visored helmets made them a symbol of communist repression. ZOMO was disbanded on 7 September 1989, after the fall of communism in Poland, and replaced by the OPP riot units of the modern Policja.
