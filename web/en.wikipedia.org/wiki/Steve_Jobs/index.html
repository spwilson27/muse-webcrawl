Steve Jobs

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Steve Jobs, portrait photograph in black turtleneck, holding an iPhone]

Steven Paul Jobs (1955–2011) was an American businessman, inventor, and investor best known for co-founding Apple Computer with Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne in 1976. As Apple's CEO he oversaw the Macintosh, iPod, iPhone, and iPad, products that reshaped personal computing, music, and mobile phones.

Born in San Francisco and adopted by Paul and Clara Jobs of Mountain View, he dropped out of Reed College, worked at Atari, and travelled to India seeking spiritual enlightenment before starting Apple in the Los Altos garage mythos.

Career

Apple's Macintosh (1984) popularised the graphical user interface pioneered at Xerox PARC. Forced out in 1985 after a boardroom clash, Jobs founded NeXT; its software became the basis of macOS after Apple bought NeXT in 1997, returning him to lead Apple. He also bought Pixar in 1986 for $10 million; it became the leading animation studio (Toy Story, 1995) and was sold to Disney in 2006 for about $7.4 billion, making him Disney's largest individual shareholder.

Back at Apple he pruned products, launched the iMac (1998), iPod (2001), iPhone (2007 — 'reinventing the phone'), App Store (2008), and iPad (2010), driving Apple toward becoming the world's most valuable company. Known for blistering product focus, keynote showmanship ('one more thing'), and a demanding management style, he also championed design minimalism and end-to-end control.

Death and legacy

Diagnosed with a rare pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour in 2003, Jobs resigned as CEO in August 2011 and died on 5 October 2011, aged 56. Walter Isaacson's biography, published weeks later, became a bestseller. Jobs's influence on technology design and consumer electronics remains widely acknowledged, alongside ongoing debate about his management methods and labour practices in Apple's supply chain.
