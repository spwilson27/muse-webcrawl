Mental chronometry

Mental chronometry is the scientific study of processing speed or reaction time on cognitive tasks to infer the content, duration, and temporal sequencing of mental operations. Research in this area commonly explores the time course of information processing in the nervous system.

History

The field traces to Franciscus Donders (1868), whose subtraction method compared simple, choice, and discrimination reaction times to estimate the duration of mental stages. Saul Sternberg's additive-factors method later allowed researchers to infer processing stages without assuming pure insertion.

Methods and findings

Chronometric tasks include simple and choice reaction time, the Sternberg memory-scanning task, mental rotation (Shepard and Metzler), and visual search. Reaction-time distributions, speed–accuracy trade-offs, and correlations between elementary task speed and general intelligence are major research topics.
