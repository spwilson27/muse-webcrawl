Sudden cardiac death
SpecialtyCardiology
DefinitionUnexpected death from cardiac causes within a short time of symptom onset
Leading cause (older adults)Coronary artery disease
Immediate treatmentCPR and defibrillation

Sudden cardiac death (SCD) is the sudden, unexpected death due to loss of heart function, most often resulting from a lethal heart rhythm disturbance such as ventricular fibrillation. It typically occurs within one hour of the onset of symptoms in a person with known or unknown cardiac disease, and is distinguished from a heart attack, though heart attacks can trigger it.

Causes

In adults over 35, coronary artery disease accounts for the great majority of cases. In younger people, causes include inherited or structural conditions such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, long QT syndrome, Brugada syndrome, and congenital coronary anomalies. In athletes, commotio cordis — a blow to the chest — is a rare additional cause.

Prevention and treatment

Survival depends on immediate cardiopulmonary resuscitation and early defibrillation with an automated external defibrillator. People at high risk may receive an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD). Screening of athletes and management of cardiovascular risk factors reduce incidence.
