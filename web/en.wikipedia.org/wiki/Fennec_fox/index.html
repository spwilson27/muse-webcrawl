Fennec fox

Fennec fox (Vulpes zerda)
Family: Canidae
Range: Sahara and North Africa
Mass: about 1–1.9 kg
Conservation status: Least Concern

The fennec fox (Vulpes zerda) is a small crepuscular fox native to the deserts of North Africa, from Morocco and Mauritania to northern Sinai. It is the smallest species of canid, distinguished by its extraordinarily large ears — the largest ear-to-body ratio of any fox.

Description

Fennecs weigh roughly 1 to 1.9 kg and stand about 20 cm at the shoulder. Their ears, up to 15 cm long, dissipate heat and provide acute hearing capable of detecting prey moving underground. A cream-coloured coat reflects sunlight by day and insulates on cold desert nights; thick fur covers even the soles of the feet, protecting against hot sand. Kidneys adapted to water scarcity let the fennec survive indefinitely without free water.

Behaviour and diet

Fennecs live in family groups of up to ten in dens dug in sand, sometimes extensive tunnel systems. They are opportunistic omnivores, eating rodents, birds, eggs, reptiles and insects, and can leap vertically to catch prey. They breed once a year, with litters of two to five kits born after a gestation of about 50 days.

Humans and conservation

The fennec is the national animal of Algeria, and its name is the nickname of the Algerian national football team, Les Fennecs. It is hunted for fur and trapped for the exotic pet trade locally, but the IUCN lists it as Least Concern, with stable populations across most of the Sahara.
