A flashcard or flash card is a card bearing information on both sides, which is intended to be used as an aid in memorisation. Each flashcard bears a question or prompt on one side and its answer on the other. Flashcards are frequently used to memorise vocabulary, historical dates, formulae or any subject matter that can be learned in question-and-answer form, and they exploit the psychological spacing effect when reviewed on expanding schedules.


Paper flashcards for vocabulary study

Use and evidence

Using flashcards engages active recall — attempting to retrieve an answer before checking — which research consistently finds superior to passive re-reading for long-term retention (the "testing effect"). The Leitner system, proposed by Sebastian Leitner in 1972, sorts cards into boxes by how well the learner knows them, so that difficult cards recur frequently and mastered ones are reviewed rarely. This principle of spaced repetition is built into modern flashcard software, whose scheduling algorithms present each card shortly before it would be forgotten.

Digital flashcards

Programs and apps such as Anki, SuperMemo and Quizlet allow cards to include images, audio and formatted text, synchronise decks across devices, and share decks made by other learners. They are especially popular with medical students and language learners, who may maintain decks of tens of thousands of cards. Two-sided physical cards remain common in schools and for young children learning reading and arithmetic.
