Grashof number

The Grashof number (Gr), named after the German engineer Franz Grashof, is a dimensionless number in fluid dynamics and heat transfer that approximates the ratio of the buoyancy force to the viscous force acting on a fluid. It is the natural-convection analogue of the Reynolds number and is used to characterise flow driven by temperature or concentration differences rather than by external means such as a fan or pump.

Definition

For a vertical surface the Grashof number is defined as

Gr = g β (Ts − T∞) L³ / ν²

where g is gravitational acceleration, β is the coefficient of thermal expansion, Ts is the surface temperature, T∞ is the bulk fluid temperature, L is a characteristic length, and ν is the kinematic viscosity. For mass transfer, an analogous form uses concentration differences and the density variation they produce.

Interpretation

The Grashof number indicates whether natural-convection flow is laminar or turbulent: for vertical plates the transition occurs at roughly Gr ≈ 109. Combined with the Prandtl number it forms the Rayleigh number, Ra = Gr · Pr, which governs the onset of convection. When the ratio Gr/Re² is much greater than one, buoyancy dominates and forced convection can be neglected; when much less than one, the reverse holds.
