Voice of America

Voice of America (VOA) is an American state-funded international broadcaster, founded in 1942. It is the largest U.S. international broadcaster, producing radio, television and digital programming in dozens of languages for audiences abroad, under the U.S. Agency for Global Media.

History

VOA began broadcasting in German in February 1942 to counter Nazi propaganda during World War II, opening with the pledge that "the news may be good or bad; we shall tell you the truth." During the Cold War it broadcast to the Soviet bloc despite jamming; its 1976 Charter, signed into law, requires its news to be accurate, objective and comprehensive. The Smith–Mundt Act long restricted domestic dissemination of its material, a ban relaxed in 2013.

Operations

Headquartered in Washington, D.C., VOA has operated services in more than 40 languages — including English, Mandarin, Persian, Russian and Spanish — reaching a weekly audience estimated in the hundreds of millions, and is complemented by sister networks such as Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty.
