ThyssenKrupp AG (stylised thyssenkrupp) is a German multinational industrial engineering and steel production conglomerate headquartered in Essen. It was formed in 1999 through the merger of Thyssen AG and Friedrich Krupp AG Hoesch-Krupp, two companies whose histories reach back to the early industrialisation of the Ruhr. With revenues in the tens of billions of euros, it is one of Germany's largest industrial groups and one of the world's largest steel producers.

The thyssenkrupp Quarter in Essen, opened in 2010.

History

The Krupp firm, founded in Essen in 1811, grew into the largest armaments and steel concern of the German Empire, while Thyssen, founded in 1891 by August Thyssen, built an integrated coal, steel, and engineering empire in Duisburg. Both companies were deeply involved in German rearmament and used forced labour during the Second World War; Alfried Krupp was convicted at Nuremberg in the Krupp trial. After decades of post-war expansion and diversification, the two rivals merged in 1999 after Thyssen's hostile takeover attempt in 1997 prompted a negotiated union.

Operations

The group's businesses include steel production (Steel Europe), materials distribution, automotive components, industrial plant engineering, and marine systems — thyssenkrupp Marine Systems builds submarines and naval vessels, including the Type 209 and fuel-cell Type 212A submarines. Its elevator division, once the world's third-largest lift maker, was sold in 2020 to a private-equity consortium for €17.2 billion and became TK Elevator, in one of the largest European buyouts of the period.

Restructuring

In the 2010s and 2020s the group underwent repeated restructuring: a planned steel joint venture with Tata Steel was blocked by the European Commission in 2019, the group was relegated from the DAX index to the MDAX, and it has pursued sales and spin-offs of divisions while investing in green steel production using hydrogen at Duisburg.
