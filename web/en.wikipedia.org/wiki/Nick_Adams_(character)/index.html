Nick Adams is a fictional character, the semi-autobiographical protagonist of a series of short stories by American writer Ernest Hemingway. Adams appears in two dozen stories and vignettes written between the early 1920s and the 1930s, many of them collected posthumously in The Nick Adams Stories (1972).

Character

Nick grows up in northern Michigan, the son of a doctor, and the stories trace his development from boyhood through adolescence, his service and wounding as a soldier in the First World War, his return home, his marriage and early fatherhood. Critics regard him as Hemingway's alter ego: the stories are generally read as a Bildungsroman tracking the education of a young man in violence, nature, fishing and writing.

Notable stories

  • "Indian Camp" — the young Nick witnesses his father perform an emergency caesarean.
  • "The Doctor and the Doctor's Wife"
  • "The End of Something" and "The Three-Day Blow"
  • "The Battler" — Nick encounters a punch-drunk ex-boxer.
  • "Big Two-Hearted River" — the returned soldier fishes alone, a landmark of Hemingway's "iceberg" style.
  • "The Killers" — Nick warns a boxer of two hitmen.
  • "A Way You'll Never Be" — Nick, shell-shocked, on the Italian front.
  • "Fathers and Sons" — the adult Nick reflects on his father.

Analysis

Scholars have debated whether Nick is one continuous character or several; Hemingway confirmed in letters that the stories concern the same person. The Nick Adams sequence is central to studies of Hemingway's omission technique and his portrayal of war trauma, sometimes read today as an early literary depiction of post-traumatic stress.
