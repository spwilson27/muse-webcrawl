Polarization identity

In linear algebra, a branch of mathematics, the polarization identity is any one of a family of formulas that express the inner product of two vectors in terms of the norm of a normed vector space. If a norm arises from an inner product, then the identity can be used to reconstruct that inner product entirely from the norm.

Formulas

For a real inner product space, the identity reads ⟨x, y⟩ = (1/4)(‖x + y‖² − ‖x − y‖²). For a complex inner product space, additional terms involving ‖x + iy‖² and ‖x − iy‖² recover both the real and imaginary parts of ⟨x, y⟩. The identity is closely related to the parallelogram law: a normed space is an inner product space if and only if its norm satisfies the parallelogram law (the Jordan–von Neumann theorem), in which case the inner product is given by polarization.

Applications

Beyond inner product spaces, polarization identities appear in the theory of quadratic forms, where a symmetric bilinear form can be recovered from its associated quadratic form, and in functional analysis and quantum mechanics, where transition probabilities (norms) determine the underlying amplitudes.
