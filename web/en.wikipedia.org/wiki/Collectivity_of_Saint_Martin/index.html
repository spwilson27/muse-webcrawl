The Collectivity of Saint Martin (French: Collectivité de Saint-Martin) is an overseas collectivity of France occupying the northern part of the island of Saint Martin in the Lesser Antilles, Caribbean. The southern part of the island forms Sint Maarten, a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Saint Martin's capital is Marigot.

History

The island has been partitioned between France and the Netherlands since the Treaty of Concordia in 1648. For most of the modern era the French side was administered as part of the overseas department of Guadeloupe. In a 2003 referendum, voters on the French side chose to become a separate overseas collectivity, a status that took effect in 2007 with its own territorial council and president.

Politics and status

As an overseas collectivity under Article 74 of the French Constitution, Saint Martin has a degree of legislative autonomy in areas such as taxation and tourism, while France retains responsibility for defence, justice, and currency. It is an outermost region of the European Union and uses the euro, unlike Dutch Sint Maarten.

Geography and economy

The territory covers about 53 square kilometres of hills, lagoons, and beaches, with a population of roughly 30,000. Tourism is the mainstay of the economy, drawing visitors to duty-free shopping, beaches, and the shared international airport on the Dutch side. The island was severely damaged by Hurricane Irma in 2017 and has undergone extensive reconstruction.
