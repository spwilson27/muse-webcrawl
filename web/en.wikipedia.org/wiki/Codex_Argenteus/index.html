Codex Argenteus

The Codex Argenteus (Latin for "Silver Book") is a sixth-century illuminated manuscript that originally contained Bishop Ulfilas's fourth-century translation of the Bible into the Gothic language. It is the primary surviving source for Gothic, an extinct East Germanic language, and is preserved at the Carolina Rediviva library of Uppsala University in Sweden. Of the original 336 folios, 188 survive, plus a final leaf discovered in Speyer in 1970.

Description

The codex was written on high-quality vellum dyed purple, with the text inscribed in silver ink — and the sacred names and certain passages in gold — a luxury format befitting a royal Ostrogothic patron, possibly Theodoric the Great himself. It contains most of the four Gospels in the Western order (Matthew, John, Luke, Mark), written in two columns in the Gothic alphabet devised by Ulfilas, with decorated arcades at the foot of pages reproducing the Eusebian canon tables.

History

After the fall of the Ostrogothic kingdom, the manuscript's whereabouts are obscure until it appeared at Werden Abbey in the Ruhr. It passed to Emperor Rudolf II's collection in Prague, was taken as war booty by Swedish troops in 1648 at the end of the Thirty Years' War, and, after a period in the hands of the Dutch collector Isaac Vossius, was bought back and in 1669 donated to Uppsala University by Magnus Gabriel De la Gardie, bound in a solid silver binding. In 2011 it was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.
