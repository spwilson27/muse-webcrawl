Double eagle

Double eagle may refer to several things:

Coins

A double eagle is a gold coin of the United States with a denomination of $20. The coins are made from 90% gold and 10% copper alloy, and contain just under one troy ounce of pure gold. Regular-issue double eagles were minted from 1850 to 1907 (the Liberty Head design) and from 1907 to 1933 (the Saint-Gaudens design, widely considered the most beautiful American coin). The 1933 double eagle is among the most famous and valuable coins in the world: nearly all were melted before release, and one specimen sold at auction in 2021 for $18.9 million.

Golf

In golf, a double eagle (also called an albatross in Britain) is a score of three strokes under par on a single hole — for example, holing out in two on a par-5. It is far rarer than a hole in one. Gene Sarazen's double eagle at the 1935 Masters Tournament, struck with a 4-wood at the 15th hole of Augusta National, is the most famous in the sport's history and is credited with popularising the event.

Heraldry

The double-headed eagle (or double eagle) is a heraldic charge associated historically with the Byzantine Empire, the Holy Roman Empire, the Serbian monarchies, and the Russian Empire, whose coat of arms features a double-headed eagle. It also appears in the arms and flags of Albania and Montenegro.
