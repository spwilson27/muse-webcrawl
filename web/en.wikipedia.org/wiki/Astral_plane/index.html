Astral plane

The astral plane, also called the astral dimension or astral world, is a plane of existence postulated by classical, medieval, oriental, and esoteric philosophies and mystery religions. It is believed to be the world of celestial spheres, traversed by the soul in its astral body on the way to being born and after death, and is generally said to be populated by angels, spirits, and other immaterial beings.

History

The idea has roots in Plato's cosmology and Neoplatonism, in which the soul descends through the celestial spheres, and in the subtle-body doctrines of Indian religions. In the late 19th century the concept was systematised by Theosophy, especially in the writings of Helena Blavatsky and later C. W. Leadbeater and Annie Besant, who described the astral plane as one of seven planes of existence, intermediate between the physical and mental planes, and as the realm of emotions and desires (corresponding to the Hindu kama-loka).

Astral projection

The astral plane is closely associated with astral projection — the purported ability to separate an "astral body" from the physical body and travel at will — and with experiences reported during dreams, meditation, and near-death experiences. Such ideas influenced 20th-century occult writers and remain common in New Age belief; they are not supported by scientific evidence.
