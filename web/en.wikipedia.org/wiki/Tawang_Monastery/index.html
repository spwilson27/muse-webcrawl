Tawang Monastery, known in Tibetan as Gaden Namgyal Lhatse, is the largest monastery in India and the second largest in the world after the Potala Palace in Lhasa. Located at an elevation of about 3,000 metres in the Tawang district of Arunachal Pradesh, it was founded in 1680–1681 by Merak Lama Lodre Gyatso in accordance with the wishes of the 5th Dalai Lama, Ngawang Lobzang Gyatso. Tawang is also the birthplace of the 6th Dalai Lama, Tsangyang Gyatso.

Description

The fortified complex, some 65 buildings enclosed by a 610-metre compound wall, houses around 300 monks of the Gelug school. Its three-storey assembly hall (dukhang) contains an 8-metre-high gilded statue of Shakyamuni Buddha, and its library preserves a valuable collection of manuscripts, including handwritten editions of the Kangyur and Tengyur, some written in gold ink.

History and significance

The name Tawang means "chosen by horse", recalling the legend that Merak Lama's horse led him to the site. The monastery historically administered the Monpa people of the region. After the Sino-Indian War of 1962, during which the 14th Dalai Lama had passed through Tawang while fleeing Tibet in 1959, the monastery has remained an important centre of Tibetan Buddhism in exile and a focus of the territorial dispute between India and China, which claims the district as part of "South Tibet".
