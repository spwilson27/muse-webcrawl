Court of Master Sommeliers


The Master Sommelier diploma

The Court of Master Sommeliers is an educational organisation established in 1977 to encourage improved standards of beverage service, particularly wine and wine service, in hotels and restaurants. It is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious — and difficult — wine qualifications in the world, with only a few hundred people worldwide having earned the title of Master Sommelier since the first examinations.

History

The Court's origins lie in the United Kingdom, where the Institute of Masters of Wine and the Wine & Spirit Trade Association had long examined wine professionals. The first Master Sommelier diploma exam was held in the United Kingdom in 1969; the Court of Master Sommeliers itself was established in 1977, and separate chapters were later formed in the Americas, Europe, and Asia-Pacific.

Examinations

Certification proceeds through four levels: Introductory Sommelier, Certified Sommelier, Advanced Sommelier, and Master Sommelier. The final Master Sommelier Diploma Examination consists of three parts — theory, blind tasting of six wines in 25 minutes, and a practical service examination — all of which must be passed within three consecutive attempts years. Candidates must identify grape varieties, regions, and vintages of the tasting wines by sight, smell, and taste alone.

In popular culture

The Court and its examination are the subject of the documentary films Somm (2012) and its sequels, which follow candidates preparing for the diploma examination.
