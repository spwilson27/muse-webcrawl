Munda people

The Munda people are an Austroasiatic-speaking ethnic group of the Indian subcontinent. They are mainly found in the states of Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, Bihar, Assam and Madhya Pradesh in India, with smaller communities in Bangladesh and Nepal.

The Munda are one of the largest Adivasi (Scheduled Tribe) groups in India. Major subgroups include the Santhal, Ho, Munda proper, Bhumij, Korku and others. They speak languages of the Munda branch of the Austroasiatic family, including Mundari, Santhali, Ho and Korku.

History

Scholarly research suggests Munda-speaking peoples migrated into eastern India in prehistoric times, possibly from Southeast Asia. They have a long history of agrarian settlement, village councils and festivals such as Mage Parab and Sarhul celebrating nature and ancestors.

Culture

Traditional Munda society is organised around the village, with a headman (munda) and priest (pahan). Music, dance, and the worship of sacred groves (sarna) are central. In modern times many Munda have been active in movements for tribal rights, land protection and recognition of the Sarna religion.
