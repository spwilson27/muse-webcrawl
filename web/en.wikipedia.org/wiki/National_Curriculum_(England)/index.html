National Curriculum (England)

Introduced1988 (Education Reform Act)
Applies toMaintained schools in England, ages 5–16
StructureKey Stages 1–4
Latest major revision2014

The National Curriculum is a set of subjects and attainment standards used by primary and secondary schools in England so that all children learn the same essential knowledge. Introduced by the Education Reform Act 1988, it is compulsory in local-authority-maintained schools; academies and free schools are not legally bound by it, though most broadly follow it.

Structure

The curriculum is organised into four Key Stages tied to age: Key Stage 1 (ages 5–7), Key Stage 2 (7–11), Key Stage 3 (11–14) and Key Stage 4 (14–16), the last culminating in GCSE examinations. Core subjects are English, mathematics and science; foundation subjects include art and design, citizenship, computing, design and technology, geography, history, languages, music and physical education. Religious education and relationships and sex education are also required under separate provisions. Statutory assessments — SATs — take place at the end of Key Stages 1 and 2, though the Key Stage 1 tests became non-statutory in the 2020s.

Revisions and debate

The curriculum has been revised repeatedly: the Dearing review slimmed an overloaded original in the 1990s, and a major reform under Education Secretary Michael Gove produced a new, more demanding curriculum taught from September 2014, emphasising factual knowledge, grammar, mental arithmetic and — controversially — a canon of English literature. Critics have argued over workload, the narrowing of the curriculum by performance measures such as the EBacc, and the exemption of academies, which educate most secondary pupils.
