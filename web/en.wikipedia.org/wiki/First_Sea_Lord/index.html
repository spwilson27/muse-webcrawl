First Sea Lord

First Sea Lord and Chief of the Naval Staff Service: Royal Navy
Formation: 1828 (as First Naval Lord); title First Sea Lord from 1904
Member of: Defence Council, Chiefs of Staff Committee
Reports to: Chief of the Defence Staff
Residence/office: Ministry of Defence, London

The First Sea Lord and Chief of the Naval Staff (1SL/CNS) is a statutory position in the British Armed Forces, usually held by an admiral. As the professional head of the Royal Navy, the First Sea Lord is responsible for the fighting effectiveness, efficiency, and morale of the Naval Service, and is a member of the Defence Council and the Chiefs of Staff Committee.

History

The office evolved from the senior naval lord on the Board of Admiralty; from 1828 the title First Naval Lord was used, and in 1904, during the reforms of Admiral Sir John Fisher, it was restyled First Sea Lord. Fisher himself became the most famous early holder, driving the introduction of HMS Dreadnought and reform of the fleet. From 1923 the First Sea Lord also held the title Chief of the Naval Staff. After the Admiralty was merged into the Ministry of Defence in 1964, the role continued as that of the Royal Navy's professional chief.

Responsibilities

The First Sea Lord commands the Royal Navy through Navy Command Headquarters, advises the government on naval matters, and is accountable for the service's personnel, equipment, and readiness. The holder is supported by the Second Sea Lord (responsible for personnel) and the Fleet Commander.

Notable holders

Notable First Sea Lords include John Fisher, Louis Mountbatten (later Earl Mountbatten of Burma), and Sir Henry Leach, who held the post during the 1982 Falklands War.
