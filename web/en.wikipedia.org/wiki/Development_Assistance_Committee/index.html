The DAC is a committee of the OECD, based in Paris.

The Development Assistance Committee (DAC) is a forum of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) whose members are the world's major providers of development aid. It works to coordinate official development assistance (ODA), set standards for aid reporting and effectiveness, and promote policies that contribute to sustainable development and poverty reduction in developing countries.

History

The DAC began in January 1960 as the Development Assistance Group, an informal forum of aid donors, and became a committee of the newly founded OECD in 1961. It has since grown to include more than 30 members, including the European Union, which participates as a member in its own right.

Functions

The DAC defines and monitors ODA — government aid designed to promote the economic development and welfare of developing countries — and maintains the DAC List of ODA Recipients. It conducts peer reviews of members' aid programmes roughly every five to six years, collects and publishes the definitive statistics on aid flows, and issues recommendations on topics such as untying aid, aid effectiveness, and assistance to fragile states.

The 0.7% target

DAC members have endorsed the United Nations target of providing 0.7% of gross national income as ODA, though only a minority of members consistently meet it. The DAC average has generally been around 0.3% of combined GNI.
