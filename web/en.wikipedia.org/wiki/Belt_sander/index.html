Belt sander

A belt sander is a power tool used for shaping and finishing wood and other materials. It consists of an electric motor driving a pair of drums, around which a continuous loop of sandpaper — the sanding belt — moves at high speed. Belt sanders are used for aggressive material removal, levelling surfaces, stripping paint and smoothing rough lumber.

Types

Hand-held belt sanders are moved over the workpiece and are common in carpentry and floor sanding. Stationary belt sanders (including combination belt/disc sanders and wide-belt sanders used industrially) keep the belt fixed while the work is fed against it. Large drum-and-belt floor sanders are used to refinish hardwood floors.

Use

Belts are available in grits from very coarse (around 40) to fine (around 120 and above). Because belt sanders remove material quickly and in the direction of the belt's travel, they are typically used with the grain and followed by finer tools such as random orbital sanders. Dust collection via a bag or vacuum port is standard on modern models.

History

The portable electric belt sander was developed in the early 20th century; the first portable model is credited to the 1920s, and the tool became a workshop staple after the Second World War. Humorously, modified belt sanders are raced in "belt sander drag racing" events.
