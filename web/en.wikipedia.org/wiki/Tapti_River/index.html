Tapti River

The Tapti, one of the major west-flowing rivers of peninsular India

The Tapti River (also spelled Tapi) is a river in central India, flowing westward between the Satpura Range to the north and the Ajanta–Satmala hills to the south. It is one of the few major rivers of peninsular India that flow from east to west, emptying into the Arabian Sea. The river is about 724 kilometres (450 miles) long.

Course

The Tapti rises in the Satpura Range near Multai in the Betul district of Madhya Pradesh, at an elevation of about 752 metres. It flows west through Madhya Pradesh, then through Maharashtra — where it passes the city of Burhanpur and forms part of the Khandesh region — and into Gujarat, where it passes Surat before discharging into the Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay) of the Arabian Sea near Dumas.

Basin and tributaries

The Tapti basin covers about 65,000 square kilometres across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat. Its principal tributaries include the Purna (the largest), Girna, Waghur, Bori and Panzara rivers from the south, and the Arunavati and Aner from the north. The basin is bounded by the Satpura Range in the north and the Western Ghats and Ajanta hills in the south, and the river occupies a rift valley (graben) between them, like its northern neighbour the Narmada.

Significance

The fertile black-cotton-soil plains along the Tapti support cotton, banana and sugarcane cultivation, particularly in the Khandesh region of Maharashtra. The Ukai Dam in Gujarat, completed in the 1970s, provides irrigation, hydroelectric power and flood control, and supplies water to Surat, a major centre of the diamond and textile industries. In Hindu tradition the river is considered sacred, and legend associates it with Tapati, daughter of the sun god Surya.
