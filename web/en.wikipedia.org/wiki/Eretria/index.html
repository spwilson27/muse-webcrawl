Ancient city-state on the island of Euboea

Eretria is a town in Euboea, Greece, facing the coast of Attica across the South Euboean Gulf. It was an important Greek polis in the 6th and 5th centuries BC, mentioned by many famous writers and actively involved in significant historical events. The modern town, with a population of several thousand, lies near the site of the ancient city and is connected by ferry to Oropos on the mainland.

Ancient history

Eretria was one of the earliest and most prosperous Greek cities. In the 8th century BC it fought Chalcis, its neighbour, in the Lelantine War over the fertile Lelantine Plain. Eretrians founded colonies in Italy and the northern Aegean and played a pioneering role in Greek colonisation and early alphabetic writing.

In 499 BC Eretria supported the Ionian Revolt against Persia, sending ships to aid Miletus. In revenge, the Persian expedition of 490 BC sacked and burned Eretria and deported its inhabitants before going on to defeat at the Battle of Marathon. The city was rebuilt and later joined the Delian League; it revolted from Athens in 446 BC and again in 411 BC, after which the Eretrians massacred the Athenian garrison. Under Macedonian and later Roman rule the city gradually declined.

Archaeology

Excavations, begun in the 19th century and continued by the Swiss School of Archaeology in Greece, have uncovered the acropolis, temples, the theatre, gymnasium and fine houses with mosaics, including the House of the Mosaics of the 4th century BC. Finds are displayed in the Archaeological Museum of Eretria.
