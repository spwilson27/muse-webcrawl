Whithorn

Whithorn (Scottish Gaelic: Taigh Mhàrtainn) is a small town in the Machars peninsula of Dumfries and Galloway, south-west Scotland. It is traditionally regarded as the cradle of Christianity in Scotland, associated with St Ninian in the late 4th and early 5th centuries.

History

According to tradition, St Ninian built a church at Whithorn — the Candida Casa, "white house" — around 397 AD, from which he evangelised the southern Picts. Whatever the precise date, archaeological excavation has confirmed an important early Christian settlement here from the 5th and 6th centuries, making Whithorn one of the earliest Christian sites in Britain.

In the 12th century Fergus, Lord of Galloway, refounded the church as an Augustinian priory; Whithorn Priory became a major centre of pilgrimage, visited by Scottish kings including Robert the Bruce and James IV, and by Mary, Queen of Scots. The pilgrimage route to St Ninian's shrine and to the cave associated with the saint on the shore nearby is being revived today as the Whithorn Way.

The town today

Whithorn is a quiet town of stone-built houses around its priory ruins. The Whithorn Trust visitor centre displays carved stones from the site, including the Latinus Stone, one of the earliest Christian monuments in Scotland, and interprets the excavations that revealed the town's early medieval past.
