DCI-P3

DCI-P3, also known as Digital Cinema Initiative — P3, is a color space defined by the Digital Cinema Initiatives (DCI) organization for digital movie projection. It was published by DCI in 2007, and the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers (SMPTE) later codified related specifications, including the SMPTE EG 432-1 and RP 431-2 standards.

Characteristics

DCI-P3 uses a white point of approximately 6300 K for theatrical projection (the "DCI white point") and a gamma of 2.6 in the cinema specification. Its gamut is roughly 25 percent larger than sRGB, with notably more saturated greens and reds achieved using monochromatic red and green primaries. A variant, Display P3, developed by Apple and standardized by the ICC, adapts the same primaries and white point to displays by using the D65 white point and an sRGB-like transfer function; it is widely used in smartphones, tablets, and computer monitors, and the web platform exposes it through CSS Color 4.

Adoption

Originally confined to digital cinemas, the P3 gamut became common in consumer devices from the mid-2010s onward, and it is the required color gamut for HDR content mastered to several streaming specifications, often with the Rec. 2020 container gamut and P3 primaries ("P3-D65").
