Potential enlargement of the European Union

EU member states and countries seeking membership.

There are currently a number of states recognised as candidates for membership of the European Union, and further states are potential candidates. Enlargement is governed by Article 49 of the Treaty on European Union and the Copenhagen criteria, which require stable democratic institutions, the rule of law, a functioning market economy and the capacity to take on the obligations of membership, including adoption of the EU's body of law, the acquis communautaire.

Current candidates

Recognised candidate countries include Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Turkey and Ukraine. Montenegro and Serbia are the most advanced in accession negotiations, having opened many negotiating chapters; Albania and North Macedonia began talks in 2022 after years of delay. Ukraine and Moldova were granted candidate status in June 2022, four months after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and accession talks with both were opened in 2024. Georgia received candidate status in 2023, though its process subsequently stalled amid democratic backsliding concerns. Turkey has been a candidate since 1999 and in negotiations since 2005, but its process has been effectively frozen.

Potential candidates and other states

Kosovo, whose independence is not recognised by all EU members, applied for membership in 2022 and is regarded as a potential candidate. Other European states, including Iceland (which withdrew its application in 2015), Norway and Switzerland, have chosen association through the European Economic Area or bilateral agreements rather than membership. The United Kingdom, which joined in 1973 and left in 2020, is the only former member state.

Process and debate

Accession requires unanimous agreement of existing members at every stage, screening of national law against the acquis, and ratification of an accession treaty. Since the 2004 enlargement, which admitted ten countries, debate within the EU has weighed the geopolitical case for further enlargement against concerns about absorption capacity, institutional reform and the rule of law in candidate states.
