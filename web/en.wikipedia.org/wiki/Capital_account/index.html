Capital account

Image placeholder: The capital account within the balance of payments.

In macroeconomics and international finance, the capital account is one of the two primary components of the balance of payments, the other being the current account. Whereas the current account reflects a nation's net income, the capital account reflects net change in national ownership of assets.

Definition

In the International Monetary Fund's Balance of Payments Manual, the capital account in the narrow sense records capital transfers and the acquisition and disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets, such as patents, copyrights, and rights to natural resources. What many older treatments called the "capital account" — the net flow of investment into and out of a country, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, and other investment — is now classified as the financial account. In common usage, however, "capital account" is still often used in this broader sense.

Capital account openness

A surplus in the capital (and financial) account means money is flowing into the country, financing a current account deficit; a deficit means money is flowing out. Governments may restrict these flows through capital controls, or promote free movement through capital account liberalisation — a policy step that the IMF historically encouraged but which was reassessed after the Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 highlighted the risks of volatile flows.
