Dallas Cowboys

The Dallas Cowboys are a professional American football team based in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) East division. Nicknamed "America's Team," the Cowboys are consistently ranked among the most valuable sports franchises in the world.

History

Founded in 1960 by Clint Murchison Jr., the Cowboys rose to prominence under head coach Tom Landry, who led the team for 29 seasons (1960–1988), winning Super Bowls VI and XII and pioneering innovations such as the 4–3 flex defense and the shotgun formation revival. Businessman Jerry Jones bought the team in 1989 and, with coach Jimmy Johnson, built a dynasty that won three Super Bowls in the 1990s behind Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith and Michael Irvin.

The team has played at AT&T Stadium in Arlington since 2009. Despite a championship drought since the 1995 season, the Cowboys remain the NFL's most valuable and widely followed franchise, with their cheerleaders and star logo among the most recognisable brands in American sport.
