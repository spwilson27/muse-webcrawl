Severe Tropical Cyclone Yasi was a powerful and destructive tropical cyclone that made landfall in northern Queensland, Australia, in the early hours of 3 February 2011. One of the strongest cyclones ever to strike Australia, Yasi reached Category 5 on the Australian scale with a huge circulation spanning hundreds of kilometres.

History

Yasi developed from a tropical low near Fiji on 26 January 2011 and moved west-southwest across the Coral Sea, intensifying rapidly. Tens of thousands of residents of Cairns, Townsville, and smaller coastal towns evacuated or sheltered as the storm approached; hospitals in Cairns were evacuated. Yasi crossed the coast near Mission Beach, between Cairns and Townsville, shortly after midnight on 3 February, with a storm surge inundating some coastal areas, though lower than the worst fears because landfall came at low tide.

Impact

The towns of Tully, Mission Beach, and Cardwell suffered severe damage; thousands of homes were damaged or destroyed and power was cut to some 200,000 people. The storm devastated the region's banana and sugarcane crops — a large majority of Australia's banana crop was lost, driving prices up nationwide. Total damage was estimated at about A$3.5 billion, then making Yasi the costliest tropical cyclone in Australian history. One death was attributed to the storm, from asphyxiation by generator fumes in a sealed room. The name Yasi was retired and replaced by Yvonna.
