Mark 41 Vertical Launching System

The Mark 41 Vertical Launching System (Mk 41 VLS) is a shipborne missile canister launching system used by the United States Navy and many allied navies. Derived from the Aegis Combat System, it provides the ability to fire a wide variety of missiles rapidly against air, surface, subsurface and land targets.

Design

The system consists of modules of eight cells each; US Navy ships typically carry 32, 64, 96 or 122 cells. Missiles are stored in sealed canisters and “hot-launched”, igniting their motors within the cell with exhaust vented through an uptake. The launcher’s flexibility lets a ship mix weapons load-outs by mission, with some missiles quad-packed four to a cell.

Missiles fired

The Mk 41 can fire Standard Missile 2, Standard Missile 3 and Standard Missile 6 air-defence missiles, the Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile, Tomahawk land-attack cruise missiles, and the Vertical Launch ASROC anti-submarine weapon. It first entered service aboard USS Bunker Hill in 1986 and has been adopted by more than a dozen navies; land-based versions form the Aegis Ashore missile defence sites.
