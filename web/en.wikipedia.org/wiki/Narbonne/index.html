Narbonne

Narbonne (Occitan: Narbona) is a commune in the Aude department of the Occitanie region in southern France, near the Mediterranean Sea. A prosperous port in antiquity and the first Roman colony in Gaul, it now has a population of roughly 55,000.

History

Rome founded Colonia Narbo Martius in 118 BC on the Via Domitia, the road linking Italy to Spain. Narbo became capital of the province of Gallia Narbonensis and one of the great ports of the western Mediterranean. After Roman decline it passed to the Visigoths, was briefly held by Muslim forces (719–759), and later was ruled by its viscounts and archbishops. The silting of its harbour and the diversion of the Aude in the fourteenth century ended its maritime importance.

Landmarks include the vast, unfinished Gothic cathedral of Saint-Just-et-Saint-Pasteur, begun in 1272, the Archbishops' Palace, the Roman granary museum (Horreum) and the Canal de la Robine, a branch of the Canal du Midi and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is also known for rugby — RC Narbonne — and as the birthplace of singer Charles Trenet.
