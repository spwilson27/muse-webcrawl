Addams Family Values is a 1993 American supernatural black comedy film directed by Barry Sonnenfeld and written by Paul Rudnick, based on the characters created by cartoonist Charles Addams. A sequel to The Addams Family (1991), it stars Anjelica Huston as Morticia Addams, Raul Julia as Gomez Addams, Christopher Lloyd as Uncle Fester, and Christina Ricci as Wednesday Addams. Joan Cusack plays Debbie Jellinsky, a serial killer who marries Fester intending to murder him for his fortune.

Plot

Gomez and Morticia welcome a third child, baby Pubert. Fearing for the baby's safety from his jealous siblings Wednesday and Pugsley, the family hires a nanny, Debbie Jellinsky, who sets her sights on Uncle Fester. Debbie marries Fester and tries — repeatedly and unsuccessfully — to kill him, while Wednesday and Pugsley, sent to a summer camp, stage a revolt against its relentlessly cheerful counsellors. In the climax, Debbie attempts to electrocute the whole family, but baby Pubert intervenes and redirects the current onto her.

Reception

The film received positive reviews and is frequently cited as superior to its predecessor, with critics praising Rudnick's witty script and the Thanksgiving play sequence at the summer camp, in which Wednesday's speech about the treatment of Native Americans has become a widely shared cultural moment. Despite favourable reviews, it underperformed at the box office, grossing about $48.9 million against a $47 million budget. It was Raul Julia's final theatrical film released before his death in 1994.
