Richard Ira Bong (24 September 1920 – 6 August 1945) was a United States Army Air Forces officer and Medal of Honor recipient in World War II. He was the United States' most successful fighter pilot and top flying ace of the war, credited with shooting down 40 Japanese aircraft, all in the Lockheed P-38 Lightning twin-engine fighter.

Early life

Bong was born in Superior, Wisconsin, the eldest of nine children of Swedish immigrant parents, and grew up on a farm near Poplar, Wisconsin. Fascinated by the aircraft that flew over the farm, he studied at Superior State Teachers College and enlisted in the Army Air Corps in 1941, earning his wings in 1942.

Combat career

Posted to the South West Pacific, Bong joined the 49th Fighter Group's 9th Fighter Squadron in New Guinea, flying the P-38 Lightning. He scored his first victories in December 1942 and rapidly built his tally, developing a distinctive gunnery style that relied on closing to very short range. In April 1944 he surpassed Eddie Rickenbacker's First World War record of 26 victories; General Douglas MacArthur personally presented him with the Medal of Honor in December 1944, citing his "conspicuous gallantry and intrepidity in action above and beyond the call of duty". Although assigned as a gunnery instructor, Bong repeatedly sought and received permission to fly combat missions, reaching 40 confirmed victories by the end of 1944.

Death

Returned to the United States, Bong married Marjorie Vattendahl in February 1945 and became a test pilot for Lockheed in Burbank, California, evaluating the new P-80 Shooting Star jet fighter. On 6 August 1945 — the day of the Hiroshima atomic bombing — his P-80's fuel pump failed shortly after take-off; he bailed out too low for his parachute to deploy and was killed. He was 24.

Legacy

Bong is commemorated by the Richard I. Bong Veterans Historical Center in Superior, Wisconsin, by Bong State Recreation Area in Wisconsin, and by Bong Air Force Base, an unfinished Strategic Air Command base named for him. His restored P-38 is displayed at the EAA Aviation Museum in Oshkosh.
