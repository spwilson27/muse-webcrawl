Harmonia axyridis

Harmonia axyridis is a large lady beetle (ladybird) species, commonly known as the harlequin ladybird, Asian lady beetle, or multicoloured Asian lady beetle. Native to eastern Asia, it is one of the most variable beetle species in the world, with elytra ranging from pale yellow-orange to black and bearing from zero to more than twenty spots. Introduced to North America and Europe as a biological control agent for aphids and scale insects, it has become one of the world's most invasive insects.

Description

Adults are 5.5–8.5 mm long, dome-shaped, and oval. The most reliable identification feature is the pronotum: white or cream with a black "M"- or "W"-shaped marking. Colour forms include the orange-red succinea form with variable spots, and melanic forms that are mostly black with red patches. Larvae are spiny, black with orange markings. When threatened, the beetles exude a bitter, foul-smelling fluid ("reflex bleeding") that can stain surfaces and trigger allergies in some people.

Distribution and invasiveness

The species is native to central and eastern Asia, from Siberia and Korea to China and Japan. It was first released in the United States in 1916 (and repeatedly thereafter) to control pecan aphids; established populations were found in Louisiana in 1988. It reached South America in the 1990s, Europe around 2001 (where it spread rapidly after commercial releases), and Africa (South Africa) by 2004. In Britain, where it arrived in 2004, it spread faster than almost any other recorded invasive species.

Impacts

H. axyridis outcompetes and preys upon native ladybirds, contributing to declines of species such as the two-spot ladybird in Britain and North America. In autumn, large aggregations enter buildings to overwinter, becoming a household nuisance. The beetles also contaminate wine grapes — crushed beetles impart "ladybug taint" to wine — and can bite humans harmlessly when handled. Paradoxically, it remains a valued predator of aphid pests in agriculture even as its ecological costs are documented.
