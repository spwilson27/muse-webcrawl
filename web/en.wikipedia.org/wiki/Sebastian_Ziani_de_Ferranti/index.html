Sebastian Ziani de Ferranti

Ferranti in later life.

Sebastian Pietro Innocenzo Adolfo Ziani de Ferranti (9 October 1864 – 13 January 1930) was a British electrical engineer and inventor of Italian descent. A pioneer of alternating-current (AC) power generation and distribution, he designed the Deptford Power Station — the first modern high-voltage power station — and founded the company that became Ferranti, a major British electrical engineering firm.

Born: 9 October 1864, Liverpool, England

Died: 13 January 1930 (aged 65), Zurich, Switzerland

Occupation: Electrical engineer, inventor

Known for: AC power systems, Deptford Power Station, Ferranti effect

Honours: Fellow of the Royal Society; president of the IEE (1910–11); Faraday Medal (1924)

Early work

Ferranti showed inventive talent from boyhood, building his own dynamo as a teenager. In 1882 he went into business with Alfred Thompson, and his alternator design was taken up by Sir William Thomson (Lord Kelvin). By his early twenties he was engineer to the Grosvenor Gallery power station in London's Bond Street, one of the first public electricity supplies.

Deptford and the AC system

For the London Electric Supply Corporation, Ferranti designed the Deptford Power Station (opened 1889–1891), which generated at 10,000 volts and transmitted AC several miles to distribution substations in central London — an audacious scale that vindicated high-voltage AC transmission over Edison's low-voltage DC. The Ferranti effect, the rise of voltage at the lightly loaded end of a long transmission line, is named after his observations of the phenomenon.

Later life

Ferranti founded S. Z. de Ferranti Ltd in 1885; the firm later became Ferranti, prominent in transformers, meters, defence electronics and early computers (the Ferranti Mark 1 of 1951 was among the first commercially available computers). He served as president of the Institution of Electrical Engineers in 1910–11 and continued inventing — including work on steam turbines and domestic appliances — until his death.
