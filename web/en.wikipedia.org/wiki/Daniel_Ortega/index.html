Daniel Ortega

José Daniel Ortega Saavedra (born 11 November 1945) is a Nicaraguan politician who has served as President of Nicaragua since 2007, having previously led the country from 1979 to 1990 as coordinator of the Sandinista junta and then as president.

Early life and revolution

Born in La Libertad, Chontales, Ortega joined the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN) as a young man, took part in guerrilla activity against the Somoza dictatorship, and was imprisoned for several years after a bank robbery intended to fund the movement. After the Sandinista victory in 1979 he became the leading figure of the revolutionary government, which faced the Contra insurgency backed by the United States during the Iran–Contra era.

Later career

Ortega lost the 1990 election to Violeta Chamorro and spent years in opposition before winning the presidency again in 2006. His subsequent governments have been accused by critics and international organisations of authoritarianism, particularly after the violent suppression of protests in 2018 and the imprisonment of opposition candidates before the 2021 election; his wife, Rosario Murillo, serves as vice president and co-president under later constitutional changes.
