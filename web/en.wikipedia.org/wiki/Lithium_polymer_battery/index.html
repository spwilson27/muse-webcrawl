A lithium polymer battery, or more correctly a lithium-ion polymer battery (abbreviated as LiPo, LIP, or Li-poly), is a rechargeable battery of lithium-ion technology using a polymer electrolyte instead of a liquid electrolyte. Highly conductive semisolid (gel) polymers form this electrolyte. LiPo cells are usually manufactured in a flexible pouch format, which makes them lighter than rigid cylindrical or prismatic cells and allows them to be made in a wide variety of shapes.

Design and chemistry

LiPo cells work on the same intercalation principle as other lithium-ion cells: lithium ions move between a graphite or lithium-metal-oxide anode and a metal-oxide cathode (such as lithium cobalt oxide or lithium iron phosphate) during discharge and back during charging. The distinguishing feature is the microporous separator soaked with a gel polymer electrolyte based on polymers such as poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVdF) or poly(methyl methacrylate). The pouch is a laminated aluminium foil envelope, sealed at the edges.

Applications

Because they are thin, light and easily shaped, LiPo batteries dominate smartphones, tablets, ultra-thin laptops and wearable devices. High-discharge cells are standard in radio-controlled aircraft, drones and multirotors, where their ability to deliver bursts of tens of amps at low weight is critical, and they are also used in some electric vehicles and power tools (often in pouch or soft-pack configurations).

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include low profile, low weight, flexible shapes and absence of free liquid electrolyte. Disadvantages include lower energy density than the best cylindrical cells at equal cost, swelling with age or abuse (gas generation inside the pouch), sensitivity to puncture and overcharging, and a need for protection circuitry and careful storage charge (around 3.8 V per cell) for longevity.

Safety

Like all lithium-ion batteries, damaged or overcharged LiPo cells can undergo thermal runaway and catch fire; hobby charging practice recommends fireproof charging bags and supervision. Properly handled, they are considered as safe as other lithium-ion formats.
