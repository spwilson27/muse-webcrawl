Orfeo ed Euridice

Orfeo ed Euridice
Composer: Christoph Willibald Gluck
Libretto: Ranieri de' Calzabigi
Premiere: 5 October 1762, Burgtheater, Vienna
Language: Italian (French version 1774)

Orfeo ed Euridice (Orpheus and Eurydice) is an opera composed by Christoph Willibald Gluck to a libretto by Ranieri de' Calzabigi, first performed in Vienna in 1762. It is the first of Gluck's "reform" operas, in which he sought to replace the convoluted plots and vocal display of opera seria with noble simplicity, music that serves the drama, and a unified treatment of chorus, ballet, and solo singing.

Plot

The opera tells the myth of Orpheus, who descends into the underworld to retrieve his dead wife Euridice. Granted permission to lead her back on condition that he not look at her until they reach the world above, he falters under her pleas; he looks, and she dies again. As Orfeo prepares to kill himself, Amore (Cupid) intervenes and restores Euridice to life, in a happy ending characteristic of the work's Enlightenment spirit.

Music and legacy

Famous numbers include Orfeo's lament "Che farò senza Euridice?" ("What shall I do without Euridice?"), the Dance of the Blessed Spirits, and the Furies' chorus "Chi mai dell'Erebo". Gluck revised the work in French for Paris in 1774 (Orphée et Eurydice), expanding the role of ballet and rewriting Orfeo for a tenor. The opera remains Gluck's most popular work and a landmark in operatic reform, influencing composers from Mozart to Wagner.
