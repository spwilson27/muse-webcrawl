Nicola Sturgeon

Nicola Ferguson Sturgeon (born 19 July 1970) is a Scottish politician who served as First Minister of Scotland from November 2014 to March 2023 and as leader of the Scottish National Party (SNP). A member of the Scottish Parliament since its creation in 1999, she was previously deputy first minister under Alex Salmond and Scotland's health secretary.

Early life and career

Sturgeon was born in Irvine, Ayrshire, and educated at Greenwood Academy and the University of Glasgow, where she studied law. She worked as a solicitor in Glasgow before entering politics, joining the SNP as a teenager. Elected to the Scottish Parliament in 1999 for the Glasgow region, she became an MSP for Glasgow Southside in 2007 and served as Cabinet Secretary for Health and Wellbeing (2007–2012) and for Infrastructure, Investment and Cities (2012–2014).

First Minister

Following the SNP's defeat in the 2014 independence referendum and Salmond's resignation, Sturgeon became leader unopposed. She led the SNP to a landslide at the 2015 UK general election, winning 56 of Scotland's 59 seats, and remained first minister after the 2016 and 2021 Scottish elections. Her government pursued a second independence referendum, particularly after the Brexit vote, in which Scotland voted to remain in the EU. Sturgeon won international notice for her daily briefings during the COVID-19 pandemic. She announced her resignation in February 2023, citing the toll of public life, and was succeeded by Humza Yousaf.

Later events

In 2023 Sturgeon was arrested and released without charge as part of Police Scotland's Operation Branchform investigation into SNP finances; her husband, former SNP chief executive Peter Murrell, was charged with embezzlement in 2024. She announced she would stand down as an MSP at the 2026 election.
