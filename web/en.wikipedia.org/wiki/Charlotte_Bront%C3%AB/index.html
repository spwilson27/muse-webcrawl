Charlotte Brontë

Born21 April 1816, Thornton, Yorkshire, England
Died31 March 1855, Haworth, Yorkshire
Pen nameCurrer Bell
Notable worksJane Eyre; Villette; Shirley

Charlotte Brontë (21 April 1816 – 31 March 1855) was an English novelist and poet, the eldest of the three Brontë sisters whose novels became classics of English literature. She wrote under the pen name Currer Bell.

Life

Charlotte grew up at the parsonage in Haworth, where she and her siblings invented elaborate fictional worlds. After the deaths of her sisters Emily and Anne and her brother Branwell, she achieved fame and moved in London literary circles, befriending Elizabeth Gaskell, who later wrote her biography.

Works

Her novel Jane Eyre (1847) was a publishing sensation, praised for its intensity and its critique of Victorian social conventions. Her other novels include Shirley (1849), Villette (1853) and the posthumously published The Professor. She married Arthur Bell Nicholls in 1854 and died the following year, probably from complications of pregnancy.
