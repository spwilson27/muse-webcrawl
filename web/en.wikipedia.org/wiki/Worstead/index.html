Worstead

Country: England
County: Norfolk
District: North Norfolk
Known for: Worsted cloth

Worstead is a village and civil parish in the English county of Norfolk, about 13 miles (21 km) north of Norwich. It is famous as the birthplace of worsted cloth, a smooth woollen yarn and fabric that takes its name from the village.

History

In the Middle Ages, Flemish weavers who settled in Norfolk from the 12th century onwards developed the local wool trade. Worstead's weavers produced a fine, tightly twisted yarn quite distinct from the woollens of other regions, and "worsted" became the generic name for the fabric wherever it was made. The village grew prosperous on the trade, reflected in its large parish church of St Mary, built largely in the 14th and 15th centuries.

The village today

Worstead holds an annual festival celebrating its weaving heritage, with demonstrations of spinning and weaving and stalls in the village centre. The parish includes the hamlets of Bengate, Briggate, Lyngate and Meeting House Hill. Worstead railway station, on the Bittern Line between Norwich and Sheringham, serves the village.
