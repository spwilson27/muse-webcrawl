The King and I is the fifth stage musical by the team of Richard Rodgers and Oscar Hammerstein II. It is based on Margaret Landon's novel Anna and the King of Siam (1944), itself derived from the memoirs of Anna Leonowens, governess to the children of King Mongkut of Siam in the early 1860s.

Plot

Widowed schoolteacher Anna Leonowens arrives in Bangkok with her young son to teach the King's many children. She and the King clash repeatedly — over her promised house, his autocratic ways, and the treatment of the slave Tuptim — even as mutual respect and affection grow between them. The musical ends with the King dying after a crisis of conscience, as his son, Prince Chulalongkorn, begins to abolish the custom of prostration.

Productions

The original 1951 Broadway production starred Gertrude Lawrence as Anna and Yul Brynner as the King, the role that defined Brynner's career; he reprised it in the 1956 film version opposite Deborah Kerr and in stage revivals for decades, winning Tony and Academy Awards. The score includes "Getting to Know You", "I Whistle a Happy Tune", "Hello, Young Lovers", and "Shall We Dance?". Major revivals include the 2015 Broadway production directed by Bartlett Sher, which won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Musical.

Controversy

The work has long been criticised in Thailand for its inaccurate and, to many Thais, insulting portrayal of King Mongkut; the stage musical and the films based on the story have been banned there. Historians likewise note that Leonowens's memoirs are partly fictionalised.
