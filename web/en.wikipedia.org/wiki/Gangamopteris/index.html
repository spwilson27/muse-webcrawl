Gangamopteris

Gangamopteris is a genus of fossil seed-plant leaves belonging to the Glossopteridales, the dominant flora of the supercontinent Gondwana during the Permian period. Like its better-known relative Glossopteris, Gangamopteris is known almost entirely from isolated leaves, which were borne by woody plants — probably small trees or shrubs — that grew in the cool-temperate, seasonally dark high-latitude environments of the southern continents.

Morphology

Leaves of Gangamopteris are tongue-shaped to spatulate, typically several centimetres to tens of centimetres long. The genus is distinguished from Glossopteris principally by the absence of a prominent midrib: the veins emerge from the base of the leaf and spread in a fan-like fashion, repeatedly forking and anastomosing (joining) to form a reticulate (net-like) pattern, with a bundle of veins running through the centre of the blade rather than a single thick midvein. The leaf base is wedge-shaped, and leaves are often found in pavement-like accumulations representing autumnal leaf fall — evidence of a deciduous habit adapted to polar light seasonality.

Distribution and significance

Gangamopteris has a slightly earlier and more restricted range than Glossopteris, appearing in the late Carboniferous to early Permian of South America (notably Brazil and Argentina), India, Africa, Australia and Antarctica. Together with Glossopteris, its distribution across now widely separated southern continents was cited by Alfred Wegener and later geologists as key palaeobotanical evidence for continental drift and the former unity of Gondwana: identical freshwater-flora assemblages could not plausibly have crossed oceans. The genus was first described by John McClelland in 1850 from Indian material and later monographed by Ottokar Feistmantel.

Classification

Gangamopteris is a form genus for leaves; the whole plant that produced them, and its reproductive structures, remain incompletely known, though associated glossopterid fructifications are assumed on floristic association.
