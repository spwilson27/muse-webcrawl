Orange chicken

Orange chicken (Chinese: 橙子雞) is an American Chinese dish of Hunanese origin. The variety of orange chicken most commonly found at North American Chinese restaurants consists of chopped, battered and fried chicken pieces coated in a sweet orange-flavored chili sauce, which thickens or caramelizes to a glaze. Orange chicken is the signature dish of the Panda Express restaurant chain.

Origin

Orange chicken is claimed to have originated in the Hunan province of China, where a dish of chicken with dried tangerine or orange peel (chenpi) is traditional. The American version was created in 1987 by chef Andy Kao for the Panda Express chain in Hawaii, adapting the peel-based flavor profile to a sweeter sauce made with orange juice for American tastes. Panda Express has stated that it sells tens of millions of pounds of the dish annually, making it the chain's most popular item.

Preparation

Boneless, skinless chicken (usually dark meat) is cut into bite-sized pieces, coated in a batter or cornstarch dredge, and deep-fried until crisp. The sauce combines orange juice and zest, soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar or honey, garlic, ginger, and dried red chilies, thickened with cornstarch. The fried chicken is tossed in the hot sauce until coated and is typically served over steamed white rice, sometimes garnished with sesame seeds or scallions.

Relation to other dishes

The dish is closely related to General Tso's chicken, which uses a similar sweet-and-spicy glaze without orange. Its older Chinese relative, tangerine chicken (陳皮雞), uses dried tangerine peel and is considerably less sweet and more bitter-aromatic. Lemon chicken, popular in Hong Kong-style and overseas Chinese cooking, follows the same battered-and-glazed template with a lemon sauce.
