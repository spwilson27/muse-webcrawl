Hantzsch–Widman nomenclature

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hantzsch–Widman nomenclature, also called the extended Hantzsch–Widman system, is a type of systematic chemical nomenclature used in organic chemistry to name heterocyclic parent hydrides having no more than ten ring members. It is named after the chemists Arthur Hantzsch and Oskar Widman, who independently proposed similar systems in 1887 and 1888. Some common heterocyclic names, such as furan, pyrrole and pyridine, predate the system and are retained.

How names are formed

A Hantzsch–Widman name combines a prefix ("a" nomenclature) indicating the heteroatom(s) — such as oxa- for oxygen, thia- for sulfur, aza- for nitrogen, with elision of the final "a" where required — with a stem that encodes the size of the ring and whether it is saturated or contains the maximum number of non-cumulative double bonds. Stems include -ir- (3-membered), -et- (4), -ol-/-ole (5), -in-/-ine (6), -ep- (7), -oc- (8), -on- (9) and -ec- (10), with endings adjusted for the heteroatom and saturation level; for example, the saturated three-membered nitrogen ring is aziridine and the six-membered oxygen ring with maximum unsaturation is pyran.

Rules and priority

When several different heteroatoms are present, their prefixes are cited in a fixed priority order (O > S > N > P > Si > B, etc.), and numbering is chosen to give locants to the heteroatoms in that order. The system is codified in the IUPAC Blue Book and is the standard method for naming monocyclic heterocycles of 3–10 members that lack trivial names.
