Mohammad Yaqub Khan

Mohammad Yaqub Khan (1849 – 15 November 1923) was Emir of Afghanistan from February to October 1879. The son of Sher Ali Khan, he is best remembered for signing the Treaty of Gandamak during the Second Anglo-Afghan War, which ceded control of Afghanistan's foreign policy to the British; the treaty's unpopularity and the subsequent killing of the British envoy in Kabul led to his abdication and exile.

Early life

Yaqub Khan was governor of Herat under his father, Emir Sher Ali. Relations between father and son were strained — Sher Ali favoured his younger son Abdullah Jan as heir — and Yaqub was imprisoned in Kabul for several years in the 1870s. When Sher Ali fled Kabul before the advancing British in 1878 and died at Mazar-i-Sharif in February 1879, Yaqub was released and proclaimed emir.

Reign and Gandamak

Seeking to end the war, Yaqub negotiated with Sir Louis Cavagnari and signed the Treaty of Gandamak on 26 May 1879. Afghanistan ceded districts including Khyber and Michni passes, agreed to receive a British Resident in Kabul, and surrendered control of its foreign relations in return for a subsidy and protection. Cavagnari's mission arrived in July but was massacred on 3 September 1879 when mutinous Afghan regiments stormed the Residency. British forces under General Roberts reoccupied Kabul, and Yaqub — suspected of complicity and in any case unable to control events — abdicated on 12 October. He spent the rest of his life in exile in India, dying at Shimla in 1923.
