Vajrayana

Vajrayana (Sanskrit: 'Diamond Vehicle' or 'Thunderbolt Vehicle'), also called Mantrayana, Tantric Buddhism, or Esoteric Buddhism, is a form of Mahayana Buddhism that developed in medieval India and spread to Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan, Mongolia, and parts of East Asia (Japanese Shingon). It adds esoteric practices — mantra, mudra, mandala, and deity yoga — aimed at attaining Buddhahood swiftly.

Vajrayana
Origin: India, c. 5th–7th centuries CE
Regions: Tibet, Himalayas, Mongolia, Japan (Shingon)
Key practices: Guru yoga, deity yoga, mantra, empowerment (abhisheka)

Features

Vajrayana rests on Mahayana philosophy (emptiness, bodhicitta) but employs skillful means (upaya): initiation by a qualified guru (lama), visualization of oneself as a meditation deity (yidam), recitation of mantras such as Om mani padme hum, and use of ritual implements — the vajra (diamond sceptre) and bell symbolizing method and wisdom.

History

Tantric texts (tantras) emerged in India from about the 5th century; masters such as Padmasambhava carried the teachings to Tibet in the 8th century. Tibetan Buddhism's four schools — Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, and Gelug (of the Dalai Lamas) — are Vajrayana, as is Bhutan's state religion.
