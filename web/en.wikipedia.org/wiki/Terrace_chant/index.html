Terrace chant

A terrace chant is a song or chant sung by football (soccer) supporters in the terraces and stands of a stadium. Football chants are traditions of fan culture, used to encourage the home team, celebrate players and managers, mock opponents, or express club identity and local pride. New lyrics are typically set to well-known melodies from hymns, folk songs, music hall numbers and pop hits.

Supporters singing on the terraces

History

Organised singing at British football matches grew with the sport itself in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Chants became elaborate in the 1960s, when Liverpool's Kop famously adopted "You'll Never Walk Alone" from the musical Carousel (via the Gerry and the Pacemakers hit of 1963), now one of the most famous football anthems in the world. Other celebrated examples include West Ham United's "I'm Forever Blowing Bubbles", Manchester City's "Blue Moon" and Stoke City's "Delilah".

Characteristics

Terrace chants are frequently humorous, topical and ephemeral: songs honouring a star player may be devised within days of his arrival, while chants directed at rivals can be cutting, self-deprecating or deliberately provocative. Some chants have attracted controversy and disciplinary action for offensive, sectarian or discriminatory content, and governing bodies have campaigned against such songs. Elsewhere in the world, organised supporter groups — ultras in Europe and South America — choreograph elaborate chanting, drumming and tifo displays.
