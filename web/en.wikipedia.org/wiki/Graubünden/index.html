Graubünden (German: [ɡʁaʊˈbʏndn̩]; Romansh: Grischun; Italian: Grigioni), also known in English as the Grisons, is the largest and easternmost canton of Switzerland. It is the country's only trilingual canton, with German, Romansh, and Italian all holding official status, and is the last stronghold of the Romansh language.

Geography

The canton occupies 7,105 square kilometres of the central Alps and contains some 150 valleys radiating from the headwaters of the Rhine, Inn, and Ticino rivers. Its landscape includes the Bernina and Albula ranges, the Swiss National Park in the Engadine — Switzerland's only national park — and famous resorts including Davos, St. Moritz, Arosa, and Klosters. The Rhaetian Railway's Albula and Bernina lines are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, noted for viaducts such as the Landwasser Viaduct.

History

The region corresponds to the Roman province of Raetia. In the late Middle Ages its valley communities formed three leagues — the League of God's House, the Grey League (from which the canton takes its name, "Grey Leagues"), and the League of the Ten Jurisdictions — which together formed the Free State of the Three Leagues, an associate of the Swiss Confederacy. The area became a full Swiss canton under Napoleon's Act of Mediation in 1803.

Economy and culture

Tourism dominates the economy, anchored by Davos — host of the World Economic Forum's annual meeting — and St. Moritz, twice host of the Winter Olympics. The canton is also known for hydroelectric power, its Bündnerfleisch (air-dried beef), and a distinctive Romansh and Walser cultural heritage.
