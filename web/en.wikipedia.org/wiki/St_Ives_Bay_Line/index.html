St Ives Bay Line

The St Ives Bay Line is a 4.25-mile (6.84 km) railway line in Cornwall, England, United Kingdom, running from St Erth to St Ives. Services are operated by Great Western Railway and the line is designated as a community rail line. It is widely regarded as one of the most scenic branch lines in Britain, running along the cliffs and beaches of St Ives Bay.

History

The line was opened on 1 June 1877 by the Great Western Railway; it was the last new line in Britain to be built to the broad gauge of 7 ft 1/4 in before being converted to standard gauge in 1892. Originally built to serve the busy fishing port and mining district of St Ives, the line survived the Beeching cuts of the 1960s largely because of growing tourist traffic.

Route and stations

From St Erth, where it connects with the Cornish Main Line, the route calls at Lelant Saltings (a park-and-ride station opened to serve St Ives), Lelant, Carbis Bay, and terminates at St Ives station, which overlooks Porthminster Beach. Trains run at least every half hour in the summer season, and the line carries more than 700,000 passengers a year, making it an essential service for reducing road congestion in St Ives.
