The World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS) is the global association supporting the female-oriented and female-only Guiding and Scouting organizations in 152 countries. It was formed in 1928 and has its headquarters in London, United Kingdom. It is the counterpart of the World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM). WAGGGS is organized into five regions: Africa, Arab, Asia Pacific, Europe and Western Hemisphere.

History

Girl Guiding was founded following the appearance of girls at the 1909 Crystal Palace Scout Rally, after which Robert Baden-Powell asked his sister Agnes Baden-Powell to organize a separate movement for girls; his wife Olave Baden-Powell later became World Chief Guide. An International Council was formed in 1919 to coordinate national Guide associations, and at the fifth International Conference in Hungary in 1928 the council was replaced by the World Association. Olave Baden-Powell was a central figure in its early development.

Mission and work

WAGGGS states its mission as enabling girls and young women to develop their fullest potential as responsible citizens of the world. It runs leadership development programmes, advocates on issues affecting girls and young women at forums such as the United Nations, and coordinates global campaigns, including work on violence against girls, body confidence and climate action. Its members celebrate World Thinking Day on 22 February each year.

World centres

The association maintains world centres where members from around the world can stay and take part in programmes: Our Chalet in Adelboden, Switzerland (opened 1932); Our Cabaña in Cuernavaca, Mexico; Sangam in Pune, India; Pax Lodge in London; and the travelling centre Kusafiri, based in Africa.
