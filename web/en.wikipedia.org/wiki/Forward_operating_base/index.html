Forward operating base

A forward operating base (FOB) is any secured forward military position, commonly a military base, used to support tactical operations. FOBs range from austere combat outposts to large logistics hubs with airfields, hospitals, and hardened defenses.

Characteristics

A FOB is established in or near a theatre of operations to shorten supply lines and sustain units operating at distance from a main operating base. Defenses typically include perimeter walls or Hesco barriers, guard towers, entry control points, and indirect-fire protection. Amenities vary widely: large bases in Iraq and Afghanistan had dining facilities, gyms, and base exchanges, while small outposts relied on delivered supplies and field sanitation.

Use in recent conflicts

The term became ubiquitous during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, when coalition forces maintained hundreds of FOBs — such as FOB Salerno in Khost and Camp Leatherneck in Helmand — to support counterinsurgency operations dispersed among the population. Smaller positions were called combat outposts (COPs) or patrol bases. As those wars wound down, most FOBs were transferred to host-nation forces or dismantled.
