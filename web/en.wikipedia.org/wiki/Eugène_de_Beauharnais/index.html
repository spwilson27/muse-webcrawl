Eugène Rose de Beauharnais (3 September 1781 – 21 February 1824) was a French nobleman, statesman and military commander who served during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Napoleonic Wars. He was the son of Joséphine de Beauharnais by her first husband, Alexandre de Beauharnais, and thus the stepson of Napoleon Bonaparte, who adopted him in 1806. Napoleon appointed him Viceroy of the Kingdom of Italy in 1805, a post he held until the fall of the Empire in 1814.

Early life

Eugène was born in Paris, the son of Viscount Alexandre de Beauharnais, who was guillotined during the Reign of Terror in 1794. His mother Joséphine married Napoleon in 1796. Eugène served as an aide-de-camp to his stepfather in the Italian and Egyptian campaigns, and rose quickly through the ranks, fighting at Marengo in 1800 and later in the War of the Third Coalition.

Viceroy of Italy

In June 1805 Napoleon named the 23-year-old Eugène Viceroy of the newly created Kingdom of Italy. From Milan he administered the kingdom energetically: he introduced the Napoleonic Code, reformed taxation and conscription, suppressed brigandage, and supervised major public works. Although nominally subordinate to Napoleon, who retained the crown, Eugène proved a capable and conscientious ruler, and was widely respected in Italy. In 1806 he married Princess Augusta of Bavaria, a match arranged by Napoleon; by all accounts the marriage was a happy one.

Later campaigns

Eugène commanded the Army of Italy in the War of the Fifth Coalition, defeating Archduke John of Austria at the Battle of Raab in 1809 before joining the main army at Wagram. In the Russian campaign of 1812 he commanded the IV Corps, distinguishing himself at Borodino and conducting a skilful retreat, notably at the Battle of Maloyaroslavets. After Murat's defection he took command of the remnants of the Grande Armée in Germany in 1813, then returned to defend Italy until Napoleon's abdication in 1814.

Later life

After the fall of Napoleon, Eugène settled in Bavaria under the protection of his father-in-law, Maximilian I Joseph, who made him Duke of Leuchtenberg and Prince of Eichstätt. He lived quietly in Munich, building the Palais Leuchtenberg, and died there in 1824 at the age of 42. His descendants married into several European royal houses; his daughter Joséphine became Queen of Sweden and Norway as the wife of Oscar I, and another daughter, Amélie, became Empress of Brazil.
