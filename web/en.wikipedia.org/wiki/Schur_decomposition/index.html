Schur decomposition

In the mathematical discipline of linear algebra, the Schur decomposition or Schur triangulation, named after Issai Schur, is a matrix decomposition that allows an arbitrary complex square matrix to be expressed as the product of a unitary matrix, an upper triangular matrix, and the conjugate transpose of the unitary matrix. Specifically, if a square matrix A over the complex numbers can be written as A = QTQ−1 (equivalently A = QTQ*), where Q is unitary and T is upper triangular, this is a Schur decomposition of A.

Properties

The diagonal entries of the triangular factor T are the eigenvalues of A, arranged in any desired order by appropriate choice of Q. The decomposition always exists over the complex numbers, in contrast to eigendecomposition, which requires the matrix to be diagonalizable. If A is a normal matrix (A*A = AA*), then T is diagonal and the Schur decomposition coincides with the spectral decomposition.

Real Schur form

For real matrices, a real variant exists in which Q is orthogonal and T is block upper triangular with 1×1 blocks for real eigenvalues and 2×2 blocks corresponding to complex-conjugate eigenvalue pairs.

Computation

The Schur decomposition is computed numerically by the QR algorithm, and it underlies many algorithms for eigenvalues, matrix functions, and the solution of Sylvester and Lyapunov equations. A generalized Schur decomposition applies to pairs of matrices.
