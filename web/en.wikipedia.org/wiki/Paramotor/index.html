Paramotor

A paramotor is the powered paragliding apparatus consisting of a motor, propeller, harness and cage (the paramotor unit) worn on the pilot's back, combined with a paraglider wing above. The term also refers to the sport itself, powered paragliding. Because the engine provides thrust, the pilot can take off from flat ground by running a few steps, climb at will and fly in calm conditions — typically at 25–70 km/h — without needing a hill or tow.

Equipment

Most units use a lightweight two-stroke petrol engine of around 80–190 cc driving a propeller of about 1.25 metres, producing enough thrust for level flight and climb; electric paramotors are also available. The complete unit usually weighs 20–30 kg. Wheeled variants called paratrikes attach the motor to a three-wheeled cart for pilots who cannot or prefer not to foot-launch. Flights can last several hours on a tank of fuel, and paramotors are among the simplest and cheapest powered aircraft to own and operate.

Regulation and safety

Regulation varies by country; in the United States, paramotors generally fall under the ultralight rules (FAR Part 103) with no licence required, while some other countries require training certification. Training with an instructor is strongly recommended; principal hazards are low-altitude engine failure, wing collapses in turbulence, and propeller and line entanglement risks on the ground.
