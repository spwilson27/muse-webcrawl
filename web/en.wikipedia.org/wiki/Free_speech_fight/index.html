Free speech fight

Free speech fights are struggles over free speech involving the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW) in the early 20th century United States. When local authorities passed ordinances banning street speaking or arrested IWW organisers, union members would flood into the town from across the country, deliberately violate the ordinance by speaking in public, and fill the jails, making enforcement prohibitively expensive until the ordinance was repealed or the right to speak was conceded.

Background

The IWW, founded in 1905, relied heavily on street meetings and soapbox oratory to organise migratory and unskilled workers who had no access to halls or the press. Employers and municipal authorities frequently responded with ordinances prohibiting public speaking on city streets, which were enforced selectively against union speakers.

Major fights

Free speech fights took place in dozens of cities. Among the most notable were those in Missoula, Montana (1909), Spokane, Washington (1909–1910), Fresno, California (1910–1911), Aberdeen, South Dakota (1911–1912), and San Diego, California (1912), where the campaign met organised vigilante violence, including the kidnapping and beating of orator Ben Reitman. In Spokane, hundreds of Wobblies were jailed; several died from prison conditions, but the city ultimately repealed its ordinance and compensated some of the arrested speakers.

Tactics

The characteristic tactic was mass defiance: one speaker after another would mount a soapbox, begin speaking (often by reading the Declaration of Independence or the Constitution), be arrested, and be replaced by the next. Supporters filled the jails, sang union songs, refused bail, and demanded jury trials, overwhelming local courts and budgets. Newspapers outside the affected towns often sympathised with the speakers, generating publicity for the union.

Legacy

The free speech fights helped establish practical precedents for public protest and civil disobedience in the United States, and they remain a celebrated episode in the IWW's history. Their methods anticipated later civil rights era tactics of deliberately filling jails to expose and defeat unjust laws.
