Nammalvar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nammalvar (Tamil: நம்மாழ்வார், romanized: Nammāḻvār, lit. "Our Alvar"; also known as Sadakopan and Maran) was one of the twelve Alvar saints of Tamil Nadu, India, who are known for their affiliation to the Vaishnava tradition of Hinduism. He is regarded as the foremost among the twelve Alvars, and his works are considered the essence of the Vedas rendered in Tamil.

Traditional accounts place Nammalvar in the early centuries of the common era, often dated to around the 8th or 9th century CE in modern scholarship. He is said to have been born in Alvartirunagari (Kurugur), a town on the banks of the Tamiraparani River in present-day Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu.

Legend

According to tradition, Nammalvar was born a silent, extraordinary child who neither cried nor nursed, and who sat in meditation beneath a tamarind tree in the temple of Adinatha at Kurugur for sixteen years, until the saint Madhurakavi Alvar, following a divine light from the south, found him and posed a riddle: "If the small is born in the dead, what will it eat and where will it lie?" Nammalvar broke his silence with the answer, "That it will eat, and there it will rest," interpreted to mean that the soul, embodied, partakes of the world. Madhurakavi became his devoted disciple and recorded and propagated his verses.

Works

Nammalvar's four works, forming part of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, comprise 1,102 verses — the largest contribution of any single Alvar:

  • Tiruvaymoli ("words of divine mouth") — 1,102 verses, regarded as the Tamil rendering of the Sama Veda and the crown of the Divya Prabandham.
  • Tiruviruttam — 100 verses, associated with the Rig Veda.
  • Tiruvasiriyam — 7 verses, associated with the Yajur Veda.
  • Periya Tiruvantati — 87 verses, associated with the Atharva Veda.

His hymns are marked by intense devotional longing (bhakti) for Vishnu, especially in the form of Krishna, and by philosophical reflections on the soul's relationship to God that became foundational for the Sri Vaishnava tradition. Commentators such as Ramanuja drew heavily on the Tiruvaymoli, which is recited daily in Vishnu temples.

Legacy

The ten-day Ra Pathu and Pagal Pathu festivals at Srirangam centre on the recitation of Nammalvar's Tiruvaymoli, and the saint's ascent to Vaikuntha is celebrated as Moksham at the Alvartirunagari temple. He is depicted in temple bronzes seated beneath the tamarind tree, one of the most recognisable images of Tamil devotional art.
