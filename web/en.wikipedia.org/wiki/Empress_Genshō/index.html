Empress Genshō

Empress Genshō (元正天皇, Genshō-tennō, 680 – 22 May 748) was the 44th monarch of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. She reigned from 715 to 724 and was the fifth of eight women to ascend the Chrysanthemum Throne as empress regnant. She was a daughter of Prince Kusakabe and Empress Genmei, and the elder sister of Emperor Monmu.

Reign

Genshō succeeded her mother Genmei in 715 because Crown Prince Obito (later Emperor Shōmu) was still a minor. Her reign saw the completion of the Nihon Shoki in 720, one of Japan's earliest official histories, and the compilation of the Fudoki provincial gazetteers ordered by her mother. In 720 the Hayato people rebelled in southern Kyushu and were suppressed. Under Fujiwara no Fuhito and Prince Nagaya, the court also revised the legal codes, producing the Yōrō Code.

Abdication and later life

In 724 Genshō abdicated in favour of her nephew Shōmu, receiving the title of Grand Empress Dowager. She lived in retirement for another 24 years, dying in 748. Her tomb is at Nahoyama no nishi no misasagi in Nara.
