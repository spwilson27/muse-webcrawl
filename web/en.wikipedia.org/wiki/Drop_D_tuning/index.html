Drop D tuning


Drop D is widely used on electric guitar in rock and metal.

Drop D tuning is an alternative guitar tuning in which the lowest (sixth) string is tuned down a whole step from E to D, giving the open pitches D–A–D–G–B–E. Only one string differs from standard tuning, making it the simplest of the drop tunings.

Characteristics

Lowering the sixth string extends the guitar's range by two semitones, deepens the bass and forms a root-fifth-octave D power chord across the three lowest strings. Because the lowest three strings are then tuned in fourths-plus-octave, power chords can be played or transposed with a single finger barred across them, enabling fast riffing. The open D string also favours songs in D major and D minor and makes the tuning common in folk and fingerstyle playing as well as heavy music.

Usage

Drop D has a long history in blues, country and folk fingerpicking, and became a staple of hard rock and metal from the 1980s onward; it underpins songs such as Led Zeppelin's "Moby Dick", Rage Against the Machine's "Killing in the Name", Tool's "Schism", Foo Fighters' "Everlong" and much of the nu-metal repertoire. Related tunings lower the whole instrument from the drop D pattern (drop C, drop B) or apply the same idea to the fifth string (double drop D).
