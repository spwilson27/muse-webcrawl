Battle of Junín

The Battle of Junín was fought on 6 August 1824 in the high pampas of Junín, Peru, during the Peruvian War of Independence. Patriot cavalry under Simón Bolívar defeated the royalist cavalry of José de Canterac. The unusually brief engagement — fought entirely with lance and sabre, without a shot fired, according to tradition — opened the final campaign that ended Spanish rule in South America.

Course of the battle

Bolívar, advancing into the Peruvian highlands, encountered Canterac's cavalry on the plain near Lake Junín at over 4,100 metres altitude. The royalist squadrons charged and initially drove back part of the patriot cavalry — Colombian, Venezuelan and Peruvian horsemen, including the Hussars of Peru. A patriot squadron under Major José Andrés Rázuri, acting on (and famously embroidering) Bolívar's orders, counter-attacked the royalist rear at the decisive moment, turning near-defeat into victory. The melee lasted only about 45 minutes.

Aftermath

Canterac retreated south in disorder, abandoning the initiative. Bolívar handed the army to Antonio José de Sucre, who destroyed the remaining royalist army at Ayacucho on 9 December 1824, securing the independence of Peru and Upper Peru (Bolivia). In honour of the battle, the patriot Hussars of Peru were renamed the "Hussars of Junín", a regiment that still exists in the Peruvian Army.
