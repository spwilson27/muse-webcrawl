Western culture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Western culture, also known as Western civilization, Occidental culture, or Western society, is the set of social norms, ethical values, traditional customs, belief systems, political systems, artifacts and technologies of the Western world. The term applies mainly to European countries and to countries whose cultures are strongly influenced by European immigration, such as the Americas and Australasia.

Origins

Western culture is generally traced to the heritage of ancient Greece and Rome, Judaism and Christianity. Key influences include Greek philosophy and democracy, Roman law and engineering, the Christian church, the medieval universities, and the transformations of the Renaissance, the Reformation, the Scientific Revolution and the Enlightenment. Industrialization and colonialism spread Western institutions, languages and technologies worldwide during the 19th and 20th centuries.

Characteristics

Features commonly associated with Western culture include liberal democracy, the rule of law, individualism, human rights, capitalism, rationalism and secularism, together with artistic traditions such as the Western classical music canon and the Latin alphabet. Scholars debate the coherence and boundaries of the concept, noting both its internal diversity and the influence of exchange with other cultures.
