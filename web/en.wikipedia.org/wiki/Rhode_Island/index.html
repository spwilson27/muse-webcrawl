Rhode Island

CapitalProvidence
AreaSmallest US state
Founded1636 (colony)
[Image: The Newport mansions and Narragansett Bay]
The Newport mansions and Narragansett Bay

Rhode Island, officially the State of Rhode Island, is the smallest state in the United States by area (about 4,000 km² including its bays), in New England. Its capital and largest city is Providence.

Overview

Founded by Roger Williams in 1636 as a refuge of religious liberty, the colony built wealth on trade — including, shamefully, a leading role in the colonial slave trade — and then on textile mills that opened the American Industrial Revolution (Slater Mill, 1793, on the Blackstone River). Narragansett Bay cuts deep into the state; Newport's Gilded Age mansions and sailing, Block Island, and the WaterFire installations in Providence are modern icons. Its official name dropped the words "and Providence Plantations" by referendum in 2020. Despite its size it has two senators like every state and a famously machine-like local politics.
