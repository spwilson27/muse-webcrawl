The United Monarchy (Hebrew: הממלכה המאוחדת) is the name given in the Hebrew Bible to the kingdom of Israel and Judah under the reigns of Saul, David, and Solomon, said to have existed from roughly the late 11th century to about 930 BCE. According to the biblical narrative, the monarchy ended after Solomon's death, when the northern tribes seceded under Jeroboam to form the Kingdom of Israel, leaving Solomon's son Rehoboam ruling the southern Kingdom of Judah.

Biblical account

The books of Samuel and Kings describe Saul, a Benjaminite, as the first king, anointed by the prophet Samuel. After Saul's fall, David—first ruling from Hebron over Judah, then over all Israel from Jerusalem—expanded the kingdom through wars against the Philistines, Arameans, Moabites, Ammonites, and Edomites. His son Solomon is portrayed as ruling a prosperous trading empire, building the First Temple in Jerusalem, and forming alliances through diplomacy and marriage, though his heavy taxation and forced labour sowed the discontent that split the kingdom under his successor.

Historicity

The scale and even the existence of a united monarchy are debated among archaeologists and historians. The Tel Dan stele provides extra-biblical evidence for a "House of David." Excavations at sites such as Khirbet Qeiyafa and the fortified cities attributed to Solomon (Hazor, Megiddo, Gezer) are cited by "maximalist" scholars in support of a substantial 10th-century polity, while "minimalist" scholars, notably Israel Finkelstein with his "low chronology," argue that Jerusalem was then a small highland settlement and that the grand united kingdom is largely a later literary construction of Judahite scribes. The debate remains a central controversy in Levantine archaeology.
