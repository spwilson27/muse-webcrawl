Elias Boudinot (Cherokee: ᎦᎴᏂᏍᏙᏗ Gallegina Uwati, "Buck Watie"; c. 1802 – June 22, 1839) was a writer, newspaper editor, and orator of the Cherokee Nation. He founded and was the first editor of the Cherokee Phoenix, the first Native American newspaper, published in both Cherokee and English, and was the first Native American to publish writing in English under his own name.

Early life and education

Born Buck Watie near present-day Calhoun, Georgia, he was educated at mission schools and at the Foreign Mission School in Cornwall, Connecticut. He took the name Elias Boudinot from the New Jersey statesman and patron of the school. In Connecticut he married Harriet Gold, a union that caused scandal locally; after her death he married Delight Sargent.

Cherokee Phoenix

In 1828 at New Echota, the Cherokee capital, Boudinot began editing the Cherokee Phoenix, printed on a press using Sequoyah's Cherokee syllabary. Through the paper he promoted Cherokee literacy and argued the Nation's case against removal, though he resigned the editorship in 1832 as his views diverged from those of Principal Chief John Ross.

Treaty of New Echota and death

Concluding that removal was inevitable, Boudinot became a leader of the "Treaty Party" and signed the Treaty of New Echota (1835), by which the Cherokee ceded their eastern lands — a treaty the Cherokee majority repudiated. After removal to Indian Territory, Boudinot was assassinated on June 22, 1839, along with other treaty signers, under a Cherokee law that made unauthorized land cession a capital offense.
