Singular they

Singular they is the use of the pronoun they or its inflected forms (them, their, theirs, themselves, or themself) as a gender-neutral singular pronoun in English. It typically occurs with an antecedent of indeterminate gender, such as "somebody", "anyone" or "each student", and increasingly with specific individuals whose gender is unknown, unstated, or non-binary.

History

Singular they has been attested in English writing since the 14th century, appearing in works by Chaucer, Shakespeare, Austen and many others. From the 18th century onward, prescriptive grammarians objected to the usage and promoted generic he instead; school grammars and style books long discouraged singular they. Despite this, it remained common in speech and informal writing.

Modern acceptance

Since the early 21st century, major style guides and dictionaries have increasingly accepted singular they. The Associated Press Stylebook and the Chicago Manual of Style permit it in many contexts, and the American Dialect Society named singular they its Word of the Year for 2015; Merriam-Webster did likewise in 2019, also recording its use for non-binary people.

Usage

With definite but unknown referents ("Someone left their umbrella") the usage is nearly universal. With quantified antecedents ("Every student should bring their book") it is widespread. Its newest use, for a specific known person who uses they/them pronouns, is accepted by many speakers and style guides, though verb agreement remains plural in standard usage: "they are", not "they is".
