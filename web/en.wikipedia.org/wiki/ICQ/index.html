ICQ

ICQ was a cross-platform instant messaging and VoIP client that originated the modern era of consumer instant messaging. The name is a play on the phrase "I seek you". It was developed in 1996 by the Israeli company Mirabilis, founded by Yair Goldfinger, Arik Vardi, Sefi Vigiser, and Amnon Amir.

History

ICQ was released in November 1996 and grew explosively, reaching millions of users. In June 1998, America Online (AOL) acquired Mirabilis for about $407 million. Users were identified by a User Identification Number (UIN), issued sequentially; the earliest five-digit UINs later became coveted status symbols. At its peak around 2001, ICQ had over 100 million registered accounts. AOL sold ICQ in 2010 to Digital Sky Technologies (later the Mail.ru Group, later VK), which operated it as ICQ New. VK announced the service's shutdown effective 26 June 2024.

Features

ICQ pioneered one-to-one chat with presence indicators, contact lists, offline message delivery, multi-user chat, emoticons, and file transfer. Its "uh-oh!" notification sound became an iconic sound of the early consumer internet.
