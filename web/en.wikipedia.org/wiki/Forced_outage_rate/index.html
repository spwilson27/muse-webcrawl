Forced outage rate

In electrical power generation, the forced outage rate (FOR) is a measure of the reliability of a generating unit. It expresses the proportion of time a unit is unavailable because of an unplanned (forced) outage, relative to the total time the unit was either in service or forced out of service.

Definition

The forced outage rate is calculated as the forced outage hours divided by the sum of service hours and forced outage hours, usually expressed as a percentage over a defined period. A related measure, the equivalent forced outage rate (EFOR), also accounts for partial deratings in which a unit can run only at reduced capacity.

Use

Forced outage rates are used by utilities, system operators and regulators in capacity planning and reliability studies, such as loss-of-load probability calculations. Industry data are collected by bodies such as the North American Electric Reliability Corporation through its Generating Availability Data System (GADS). Lower forced outage rates indicate more dependable generating plant; rates vary by technology, age and fuel type.
