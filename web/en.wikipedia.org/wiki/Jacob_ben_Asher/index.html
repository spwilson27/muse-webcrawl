Jacob ben Asher

Jacob ben Asher (c. 1269 – c. 1343), also known as the Ba'al ha-Turim ("Master of the Columns") after his main work, was an influential medieval rabbi and authority on Jewish law (halakha). He was born in Cologne, in the Holy Roman Empire, and moved with his family to Toledo, Spain.

Life

Jacob was the son of Asher ben Jehiel, the Rosh, one of the leading rabbinic authorities of his age, who fled Germany for Spain in 1303. Jacob lived most of his life in Toledo. Unlike his father, he never held an official rabbinic position and is said to have lived in poverty, supporting himself through his writing.

Works

His most famous work is the Arba'ah Turim ("Four Columns"), a comprehensive code of Jewish law organized into four sections: Orach Chayim (daily life, prayer, and holidays), Yoreh De'ah (dietary and ritual laws), Even Ha'ezer (marriage and family law), and Choshen Mishpat (civil and financial law). This four-part structure later formed the framework of Joseph Karo's Shulchan Aruch, the most authoritative code of Jewish law.

Jacob also wrote a commentary on the Torah, notable for its use of gematria (numerical interpretation of Hebrew letters), and a commentary on the Talmud. He died in Toledo around 1343.
