A drop or droplet is a small column of liquid, bounded completely or almost completely by free surfaces. A drop may form when liquid accumulates at the lower end of a tube or other surface boundary, producing a hanging drop called a pendant drop. Drops may also be formed by the condensation of a vapor or by atomization of a larger mass of liquid.

Surface tension

Liquid forms drops because the liquid exhibits surface tension. A simple way to form a drop is to allow liquid to flow slowly from the lower end of a vertical tube of small diameter. The pendant drop grows until its weight exceeds the force of surface tension holding it to the tube, at which point it detaches. The size of the drop that falls is governed by Tate's law, which relates the drop's weight to the tube radius and the liquid's surface tension and density.

Optics

Because of the different refractive index of water and air, rainbows and other optical phenomena occur in drops. A spherical drop also acts as a lens, which is why water droplets can magnify objects behind them and, in concentrated sunlight, can focus light enough to scorch surfaces.

As a unit of measure

The drop is an approximated unit of measure of volume equal to the amount dispensed as one drop from a dropper or drip chamber. It is often used in administering liquid medicines. The metric drop is defined as exactly 0.05 mL, so that 20 drops equal one millilitre. The imperial drop is about 0.0592 mL and the US drop about 0.0616 mL, though in practice drop size varies with the liquid, the dropper, and the speed of dripping, making it an imprecise unit.
