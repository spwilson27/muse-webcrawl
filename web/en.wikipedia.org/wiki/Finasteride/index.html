Finasteride

Finasteride, sold under the brand names Proscar and Propecia among others, is a medication used to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and male pattern hair loss. It is taken by mouth.

Medical uses

Finasteride is used for the treatment of symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia in men with an enlarged prostate, and for the treatment of androgenetic alopecia (male pattern baldness) in men. It is a 5α-reductase inhibitor.

Mechanism of action

Finasteride works by inhibiting the enzyme 5α-reductase type II, which converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). By blocking this conversion, finasteride lowers DHT levels in the scalp and prostate by roughly 70%, which shrinks the prostate and slows or reverses hair loss.

Side effects

Common side effects include decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, and ejaculation disorders. Finasteride can cause birth defects in a developing male fetus, so women who are or may become pregnant should not handle crushed or broken tablets. There has been debate over a reported "post-finasteride syndrome" of persistent sexual and other symptoms after stopping the drug.

History

Finasteride was developed by Merck and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 1992 for BPH (as Proscar, 5 mg) and in 1997 for hair loss (as Propecia, 1 mg). It is available as a generic medication and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
