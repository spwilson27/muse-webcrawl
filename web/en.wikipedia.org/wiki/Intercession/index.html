Intercession

Intercession or intercessory prayer is the act of praying on behalf of others. An intercessor is one who takes the place of another or pleads another's case, especially in prayer. The practice appears in many religions and is a standard component of Christian worship, where it includes prayers for the sick, for rulers, for the dead in some traditions, and for the world at large.

In Christianity

The Bible contains many examples of intercession: Abraham pleading for Sodom, Moses interceding for Israel, and the letters of Paul urging prayer "for all people". Christ is described as the supreme intercessor who "always lives to make intercession" for believers (Hebrews 7:25). In Roman Catholic, Orthodox and some other traditions, the saints — above all the Virgin Mary — are asked to intercede with God, a practice grounded in the communion of saints; this was a major point of contention in the Protestant Reformation, whose traditions generally restrict prayer to God directly while retaining intercessory prayer by the living for one another. Liturgical intercessions, such as the Prayer of the Faithful at Mass and the Great Litany, remain central to many services.

In other traditions

In Islam, intercession (shafa'ah) on the Day of Judgement is attributed principally to the Prophet Muhammad, by God's permission, and is a debated topic among schools of theology. Concepts of advocacy before the divine also appear in Judaism, with Moses and the patriarchs as archetypal intercessors. Studies of whether intercessory prayer affects medical outcomes have produced no conclusive evidence, though the practice remains pastorally significant to believers.
