The Olivet Discourse is the name given to the teaching of Jesus recorded in the Synoptic Gospels (Matthew 24–25, Mark 13, and Luke 21) and delivered on the Mount of Olives near the end of his ministry. Prompted by the disciples' questions about the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple and the end of the age, it is the longest continuous discourse in Mark and one of the major discourses in Matthew.

Its content includes predictions of wars, persecutions and false prophets, the "abomination of desolation", the coming of the Son of Man, and parables of watchfulness, including the Parable of the Ten Virgins and the Parable of the Talents, concluding with the judgment of the sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:31–46).

Context

Jesus delivers the discourse privately to his disciples after leaving the Temple, following his prediction that not one stone of it would be left upon another. The disciples ask when these things will happen and what sign will precede his coming and the end of the age.

Content

The discourse warns against deception, describes "birth pangs" of calamity, foretells persecution, urges endurance, speaks of the gospel being preached to all nations, and describes cosmic signs accompanying the return of the Son of Man. Parabolic material stresses readiness, since the day and hour are unknown.

Interpretations

Interpretations divide broadly among preterist readings (fulfilment largely in the Roman destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70), futurist readings (events still to come), historicist readings (a sweep of church history), and idealist readings (timeless spiritual principles). Many interpreters combine approaches, treating AD 70 as a type of a final judgment.
