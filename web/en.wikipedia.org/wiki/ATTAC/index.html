ATTAC (the Association for the Taxation of Financial Transactions and for Citizens' Action) is an international alter-globalisation movement founded in France in 1998. It grew from an editorial by Ignacio Ramonet in Le Monde diplomatique proposing a tax on speculative currency transactions — the "Tobin tax" proposed by economist James Tobin — and the creation of an association to promote it.

Aims and activities

ATTAC advocates democratic control over financial markets, a financial transaction tax, cancellation of developing-world debt, fair trade and the defence of public services against privatisation. Its national chapters — particularly strong in France, Germany and Norway — combine popular education, publishing and street protest, and ATTAC activists were prominent in the protests surrounding G8 and WTO summits and in the World Social Forum movement.

Influence

The financial transaction tax ATTAC championed entered mainstream policy debate after the 2008 financial crisis, with the European Commission proposing an EU-wide FTT in 2011. Critics have at times accused parts of the movement of protectionism or of harbouring conspiracy theorists, disputes which the organisation has publicly addressed.
