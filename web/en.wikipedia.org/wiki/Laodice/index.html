Laodice

Laodice (Greek: Λαοδίκη, "justice of the people") is a Greek female name borne by numerous women of the Hellenistic world, especially queens and princesses of the Seleucid and Pontic dynasties. Cities named Laodicea were typically founded in honour of a Laodice by her husband or son.

Notable bearers

  • Laodice, wife of Antiochus, general of Philip II, and mother of Seleucus I Nicator, founder of the Seleucid Empire; several cities of Laodicea were named for her.
  • Laodice I, first wife of Antiochus II Theos; her repudiation in favour of Berenice under the marriage settlement with Ptolemy II helped trigger the Third Syrian War (the "Laodicean War") after Antiochus's death in 246 BC.
  • Laodice III, daughter of Mithridates II of Pontus and wife of Antiochus III the Great.
  • Laodice IV, daughter of Antiochus III, who married successively her brothers Antiochus, Seleucus IV and Antiochus IV.
  • Laodice of Pontus, sister of Mithridates VI Eupator.

Other uses

In Greek mythology, Laodice is named among the daughters of Priam and Hecuba of Troy. The name is also borne by figures in the Iliad tradition styled "fairest of the daughters of Priam". For the many Hellenistic cities called Laodicea — including Laodicea on the Lycus in Phrygia and Laodicea ad Mare in Syria — see the Laodicea articles.
