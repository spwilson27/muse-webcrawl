Multivariable calculus

Multivariable calculus (also known as multivariate calculus) is the extension of calculus in one variable to functions of several variables: the differentiation and integration of functions involving multiple variables, rather than just one. It is a standard part of undergraduate mathematics, physics, and engineering curricula, and provides the language for describing surfaces, fields, and optimisation in higher dimensions.

A function of two variables defines a surface; its partial derivatives give the slopes in the x and y directions.

Limits and continuity

Limits in several variables are more subtle than in one variable, because a point can be approached along infinitely many paths; a limit exists only if the function tends to the same value along every path. Continuity is defined analogously, and continuous functions on closed bounded sets attain maxima and minima.

Partial differentiation

The partial derivative of a function f(x, y, …) with respect to one variable treats the others as constants. The collection of first partial derivatives forms the gradient vector ∇f, which points in the direction of steepest increase. Second derivatives are arranged in the Hessian matrix, used to classify critical points. For vector-valued functions, the Jacobian matrix generalises the derivative, and the total derivative gives the best linear approximation.

Multiple integration

Double and triple integrals extend integration to volumes and higher-dimensional regions. Fubini's theorem allows them to be computed as iterated integrals. Changes of variables — such as to polar, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates — simplify many problems, with the Jacobian determinant accounting for the scaling of the volume element.

Vector calculus theorems

The fundamental theorems of vector calculus relate integrals over regions to integrals over their boundaries: the gradient theorem, Green's theorem, Stokes' theorem, and the divergence theorem. These results underpin classical field theories such as electromagnetism and fluid dynamics.

Applications

Multivariable calculus is used in physics (fields, potentials), economics (optimisation with several goods), computer graphics, machine learning (gradient descent), and statistics.
