First mile

In logistics and supply chain management, the first mile is the first stage of the movement of goods, in which products are transported from a supplier, manufacturer, or seller to a distribution centre, fulfilment centre, or carrier hub. It is the counterpart of the "last mile", the final delivery of goods to the end customer, and sits at the start of the supply chain rather than the end.

Description

First-mile operations typically include collecting goods from factories, farms or merchants, consolidating shipments, packaging and labelling, documentation and customs preparation for export, and handing freight over to line-haul transport. In e-commerce, "first mile" often refers to the pickup of parcels from sellers and their transfer into the delivery network. Because errors or delays in the first mile propagate through everything downstream, businesses track first-mile performance closely; digitisation of first-mile logistics — booking, tracking and proof of pickup — has been a focus for logistics technology companies.

Relation to other terms

The term is also used in telecommunications and energy by analogy, although in telecommunications the consumer-side connection is more commonly called the last mile. In supply chains the sequence runs: first mile (origin to consolidation point), middle mile (between warehouses and distribution centres), and last mile (to the end customer).
