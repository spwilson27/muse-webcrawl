Jomsvikings

The Jomsvikings were a legendary order of Viking mercenaries or brigands of the 10th and 11th centuries. According to the Icelandic sagas, particularly the Jómsvíkinga saga, they were based at Jomsborg, a stronghold on the southern coast of the Baltic Sea, and followed a strict code of conduct that forbade showing fear or fleeing before an enemy of equal or inferior strength.

Legendary history

The sagas credit Palnatoki (Palna-Toki) with founding the order. Members were admitted only after tests of strength and were bound by strict rules: no women or children were allowed within the fortress, disputes were settled by the community, and all booty was shared. The Jomsvikings are said to have fought at the Battle of Hjörungavágr against Norwegian forces, where they were defeated.

Historicity

The historical existence of the order as described is debated; Jomsborg is often identified with the town of Wolin in modern Poland, though the sagas' accounts likely mix fact with later literary invention.
