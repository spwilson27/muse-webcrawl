Harris Tweed

Harris Tweed (Scottish Gaelic: Clò Mòr, "the big cloth") is a tweed cloth handwoven by islanders at their homes in the Outer Hebrides of Scotland, finished in the Outer Hebrides, and made from pure virgin wool dyed and spun in the Outer Hebrides. This definition is protected by the Harris Tweed Act 1993.

History

For centuries the islanders of Lewis, Harris, Uist, and Barra wove cloth for their own use and for trade. In the 1840s Lady Catherine Herbert, wife of the Earl of Dunmore, promoted the cloth in London society, and "Harris tweed" became fashionable for sporting wear among the landed gentry. To protect the product from imitations, the Harris Tweed Association was founded in 1909 and registered the Orb certification trademark — the oldest British certification mark still in use.

Production

Wool is dyed at island mills using a wide palette of colours, blended, carded, and spun into yarn. The yarn is delivered to crofter-weavers, who weave it on single-width treadle looms at their homes. The woven cloth returns to the mill for finishing, inspection, and stamping with the Orb mark by the Harris Tweed Authority. Annual production is on the order of a million metres, used for jackets, suits, accessories, and interiors worldwide.
