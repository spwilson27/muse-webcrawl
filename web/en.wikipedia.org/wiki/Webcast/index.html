Webcast

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A live corporate webcast in progress.

A webcast is a media presentation distributed over the Internet using streaming media technology to a large number of simultaneous listeners or viewers. A webcast may be distributed live or on demand; it is essentially "broadcasting" over the Internet. The term, a blend of "web" and "broadcast," came into use in the mid-1990s as streaming technology made real-time audio and video delivery practical.

History

Early webcasts included the first live rock concert broadcast online (Severe Tire Damage, 1993, over the MBone), RealAudio streams from 1995, and landmark events such as the 1999 Victoria's Secret fashion show webcast and the Woodstock '99 streams, which drew millions of viewers and strained early infrastructure. Corporate webcasting grew through the 2000s for earnings calls, product launches, and conferences, and was normalised during the COVID-19 pandemic, when lectures, religious services, concerts, and public meetings moved online en masse.

Technology

Webcasts are encoded at the source (typically to H.264 or newer codecs), distributed through content delivery networks using adaptive streaming protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH, and played in browsers or apps. Large-scale webcasts rely on multicast-style distribution at the CDN layer to scale to millions of viewers, and commonly include live chat, Q&A, slides synchronisation, and captioning. Related terms include webinar (an interactive seminar), podcast (pre-recorded episodic audio or video distributed by subscription), and livestream.

Uses

Webcasts are used for corporate communications and investor relations, education and training, government proceedings, sports and entertainment events, and religious services. They reduce travel costs and geographic barriers, and recordings can be archived for on-demand viewing.
