Svan language

Svan (Svan: lušnu nin; Georgian: სვანური, svanuri) is a Kartvelian language spoken by the Svans, an ethnic subgroup of Georgians, in the mountainous region of Svaneti in northwestern Georgia. With perhaps 30,000 speakers, it is by far the most divergent of the Kartvelian languages and is classified by UNESCO as endangered.

History

Svan is generally considered the first language to have split from Proto-Kartvelian, possibly as early as the second millennium BC, and the isolation of Svaneti's high valleys preserved many archaic features lost in Georgian, Mingrelian, and Laz. Unlike Georgian, Svan never developed a literary tradition of its own; Georgian serves as the language of schooling, media, and writing, which is a major driver of language shift.

Features

Svan has one of the largest consonant inventories of the family and a rich vowel system that, in the Upper Bal dialect, includes long vowels and umlauted vowels, giving many more vowel qualities than Georgian's five. It retains complex consonant clusters and ejective stops typical of Kartvelian. Verbs are polypersonal, agreeing with subject and objects, with an elaborate screeve (tense-aspect-mood) system. The four dialects — Upper and Lower Bal, Lashkh, and Lentekh — differ substantially, sometimes impeding mutual intelligibility.

Status

Most Svans are bilingual in Georgian, and younger generations in towns such as Mestia and Lentekhi increasingly shift to Georgian. Svan survives most strongly in remote villages and in a rich oral tradition of songs, epics, and ritual poetry. Documentation and revitalisation efforts include dictionaries, text collections, and local cultural initiatives, but there is no official status or standard orthography for the language; it is occasionally written with adapted Georgian script.
