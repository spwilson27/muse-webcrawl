Secondary sector of the economy

The secondary sector of the economy comprises industries that transform raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods. It includes manufacturing, construction, and utilities such as electricity generation and water supply. In the three-sector model of economic development, it sits between the primary sector (extraction of raw materials) and the tertiary sector (services).

Activities

Secondary industries take the outputs of agriculture, mining, forestry, and fishing and convert them into usable products: milling grain into flour, refining crude oil into fuels and plastics, smelting ore into steel, assembling vehicles and electronics, and building houses, roads, and infrastructure. The sector ranges from small workshops and craft production to large automated factories.

Role in development

Industrialisation — the growth of the secondary sector — has historically been the engine of economic development, raising productivity and incomes. As economies mature, employment typically shifts from agriculture to manufacturing and later to services, a pattern described by economists such as Colin Clark and Allan Fisher. In many advanced economies the manufacturing share of employment has declined since the late 20th century through automation and offshoring (deindustrialisation), while in much of Asia manufacturing expanded rapidly. Construction remains a major, largely non-tradable part of the sector everywhere.

Examples

  • Food and beverage processing
  • Textiles and clothing manufacture
  • Chemical, pharmaceutical, and steel industries
  • Motor vehicle and machinery production
  • Building and civil engineering construction
