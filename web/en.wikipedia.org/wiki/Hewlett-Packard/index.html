The Hewlett-Packard Company, commonly shortened to Hewlett-Packard or HP, was an American multinational information technology company headquartered in Palo Alto, California. Founded in 1939 in a one-car garage by Bill Hewlett and David Packard — an origin later celebrated as the "birthplace of Silicon Valley" — HP grew from an electronic test-equipment maker into one of the world's largest technology companies, spanning instruments, computing, printing, and services. On 1 November 2015 the original company split into two publicly traded successors: HP Inc. (personal computers and printers) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (servers, storage, networking, and services).

History

Hewlett and Packard, Stanford University electrical engineering graduates mentored by Frederick Terman, formalised their partnership on 1 January 1939 with $538 of capital; a coin toss decided the order of names in "Hewlett-Packard". Their first financially successful product was the Model 200A precision audio oscillator, one of which the Walt Disney Studios bought (as the improved 200B) to test sound equipment for the film Fantasia. During and after the Second World War, HP became a leading maker of electronic test and measurement instruments, famed for an egalitarian management culture — the "HP Way" — codified around trust, decentralisation, and management by walking around.

HP entered computing with the HP 2116A minicomputer (1966) and produced the first programmable desktop calculator (HP 9100A, 1968) and the first handheld scientific calculator (HP-35, 1972). The company became a giant in printers from the 1980s — the LaserJet (1984) and DeskJet (1988) defined their markets — and in personal computers. Under CEO Carly Fiorina, HP acquired Compaq in 2002 in a contested $25 billion merger, briefly becoming the world's largest PC maker. Later acquisitions included EDS (2008, services) and the ill-fated Autonomy (2011). In 1999 HP had already spun off its original instruments business as Agilent Technologies.

The 2015 split

Announced in October 2014, the separation divided the company along its two natural lines: the PC and printing business became HP Inc., retaining the HP name and logo, while the enterprise hardware and services business became Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE). Both entered the Fortune 500, and the original company's name formally passed into history.
