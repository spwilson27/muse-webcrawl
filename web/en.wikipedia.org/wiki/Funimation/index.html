Funimation

Funimation was an American entertainment company best known for dubbing and distributing Japanese anime in North America. Founded in 1994 by Gen Fukunaga and his wife Cindy in Silicon Valley, California, the company achieved its breakthrough by acquiring the North American rights to the Dragon Ball franchise, which became a major success on Cartoon Network's Toonami block.

History

The company was sold to the Navarre Corporation in 2005 and later bought back by an investor group led by Fukunaga in 2011. Funimation launched one of the first dedicated anime streaming services and simulcast programs in partnership with Japanese broadcasters. In 2017, Sony Pictures Television acquired a majority stake in the company.

Merger with Crunchyroll

In 2019, Funimation formed a joint venture with Aniplex under Sony, and in 2021 Sony's Crunchyroll completed the acquisition of Crunchyroll from AT&T, bringing the two rival anime streamers under common ownership. Funimation's library was progressively migrated to Crunchyroll, and the Funimation streaming service was shut down in April 2024, with the Funimation brand retired in favor of Crunchyroll.

Notable releases

Beyond Dragon Ball, Funimation dubbed and distributed series including Yu Yu Hakusho, Fullmetal Alchemist, One Piece (from 2007), Fruits Basket, My Hero Academia, and Attack on Titan.
