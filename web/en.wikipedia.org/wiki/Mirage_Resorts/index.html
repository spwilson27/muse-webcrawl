Mirage Resorts

Mirage Resorts was an American hotel and casino company founded by developer Steve Wynn. Originating with Wynn's Golden Nugget properties, the company — first known as Golden Nugget Companies and renamed Mirage Resorts in the early 1990s — developed a series of landmark Las Vegas Strip resorts that reshaped the modern casino industry before being acquired by MGM Grand Inc. in 2000 for about US$6.4 billion, forming MGM Mirage.

History

Wynn took control of the Golden Nugget in downtown Las Vegas in 1973 and built the company around it, opening a Golden Nugget in Atlantic City in 1980 (later sold). Its defining project was The Mirage, a Polynesian-themed megaresort that opened on the Las Vegas Strip on 22 November 1989 at a then-unprecedented cost of about US$630 million; famous for its erupting volcano and Siegfried & Roy show, it is credited with triggering the 1990s Las Vegas building boom.

Subsequent Mirage Resorts developments included Treasure Island (1993), the Beau Rivage in Biloxi, Mississippi (1999), and the Bellagio (1998), at about US$1.6 billion then the most expensive hotel ever built, noted for its lake and Fountains of Bellagio.

Acquisition

In 2000, after an initially unsolicited approach, MGM Grand Inc. acquired Mirage Resorts in a deal valued at roughly US$6.4 billion including assumed debt. The combined company took the name MGM Mirage (later MGM Resorts International). Wynn left to found Wynn Resorts, opening Wynn Las Vegas in 2005.
