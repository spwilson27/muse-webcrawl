The Purple Heart (PH) is a United States military decoration awarded in the name of the President to those wounded or killed while serving, on or after 5 April 1917, with the U.S. military. With its forerunner, the Badge of Military Merit, which took the form of a heart made of purple cloth, the Purple Heart is the oldest military award still given to U.S. military members.

The Purple Heart is a heart-shaped medal within a gold border, 1 3⁄8 inches (35 mm) wide, containing a profile of General George Washington. Above the heart appears a coat of arms of George Washington. The medal is suspended from a purple and white ribbon.

History

Badge of Military Merit

The original Purple Heart, designated as the Badge of Military Merit, was established by George Washington—then the commander-in-chief of the Continental Army—by an order from his Newburgh, New York, headquarters on 7 August 1782. The badge was a purple heart of cloth or silk, edged with silver lace, awarded for "singularly meritorious action." Only three soldiers are known to have received it during the Revolutionary War: Sergeants Daniel Bissell, William Brown, and Elijah Churchill.

Revival

The medal went unawarded for nearly 150 years. In 1927, Army Chief of Staff General Charles Pelot Summerall directed that a draft bill be sent to Congress to revive it, and on 22 February 1932—the 200th anniversary of Washington's birth—President Herbert Hoover signed an executive order re-establishing the Purple Heart. The first medals were distributed to veterans of the First World War.

Criteria

The Purple Heart is awarded to members of the armed forces of the U.S. who are wounded by an instrument of war in the hands of the enemy, or posthumously to the next of kin in the name of those who are killed in action or die of wounds received in action. Since 1997, civilians have no longer been eligible; wounded civilian employees are instead recognized with the Defense of Freedom Medal.

Notable recipients include John F. Kennedy, wounded in the sinking of PT-109; John Kerry; John McCain; and Tammy Duckworth. The most Purple Hearts awarded to a single person is ten, held by several servicemen including Curtis G. Williams and William G. White.
