The Flemish Region (Dutch: Vlaams Gewest), usually simply referred to as Flanders, is one of the three regions of Belgium — alongside the Walloon Region and the Brussels-Capital Region. It occupies the northern part of the country and covers an area of 13,625 km², with a population of about 6.7 million, the majority of Belgium's inhabitants. Its official language is Dutch.

Politics and institutions

Uniquely in Belgium, the institutions of the Flemish Region were merged with those of the Flemish Community from the start: the Flemish Parliament and Flemish Government exercise both regional powers (economy, environment, transport, agriculture) and community powers (education, culture, language, welfare) for the Flemish Region and for Dutch-speakers in Brussels. The region comprises five provinces: Antwerp, Limburg, East Flanders, Flemish Brabant and West Flanders.

Economy and geography

Flanders is densely populated and highly urbanised, forming part of the "Flemish Diamond" of cities — Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent and Leuven. The port of Antwerp is one of Europe's largest, and the region's economy is export-oriented, with strengths in chemicals, logistics, food processing and technology. The flat coastal plain and polders in the west give way to the rolling hills of the Flemish Ardennes in the south.
