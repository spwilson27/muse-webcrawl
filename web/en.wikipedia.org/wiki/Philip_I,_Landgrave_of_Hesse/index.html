Philip I, Landgrave of Hesse (13 November 1504 – 31 March 1567), called the Magnanimous, was a German prince and champion of the Protestant Reformation. One of the most important political leaders of the early Reformation, he founded the first Protestant university, protected reformers, and co-founded the Schmalkaldic League, the defensive alliance of Protestant princes and cities.

Reign and Reformation

Philip succeeded as a child in 1509 and was declared of age in 1518. He suppressed the Knights' Revolt of Franz von Sickingen and helped crush the Peasants' War of 1525. After hearing reforming preaching he adopted Lutheranism in 1524–26 and introduced the Reformation into Hesse, secularising monasteries and using their revenues for social purposes. In 1527 he founded the University of Marburg, the first Protestant university, and in 1529 hosted the Marburg Colloquy, at which Luther and Zwingli famously failed to agree on the Eucharist.

Politics and the bigamy scandal

Philip was a founder of the League of Schmalkalden (1531) and its most energetic organiser. His leadership was compromised by his bigamous marriage: in 1540, still married to Christine of Saxony, he married Margarethe von der Saale with the private approval of Luther, Melanchthon and Bucer. When the secret marriage became public it caused a scandal that forced Philip to seek the Emperor Charles V's pardon, weakening the Protestant cause. After the Schmalkaldic War, Philip surrendered in 1547 and spent five years in imperial captivity, until the Peace of Passau (1552).

Released, he spent his last years rebuilding Hesse and promoting Protestant unity. On his death in 1567 Hesse was divided among his four legitimate sons into Hesse-Kassel, Hesse-Marburg, Hesse-Rheinfels and Hesse-Darmstadt — a partition that shaped German politics for centuries.
