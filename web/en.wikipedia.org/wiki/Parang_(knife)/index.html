Parang (knife)

The parang is a type of large knife or short sword/machete used across the Malay Archipelago — in Malaysia, Indonesia, Brunei, and the southern Philippines. It is a workhorse jungle tool, used for cutting paths through vegetation, clearing brush, processing wood, and general camp work.

Design

A typical parang has a blade around 25–45 cm (10–18 in) long, broad and typically thicker and heavier toward the tip, concentrating weight for chopping. Unlike a Latin machete, a well-made parang often has a "convex" edge profile and a blade whose heaviest section lies in the front third. Handles are traditionally wood or horn, sometimes with rattan binding, and the blade is carried in a wooden sheath.

Variants

Regional variants include the Dayak parang ilang (mandau) of Borneo, the golok of western Java and the Malay Peninsula, and numerous local forms differing in blade curvature and decoration. Parangs were also adopted by British and Commonwealth forces as jungle equipment during the Malayan Emergency, and a modernized "parang" pattern machete remains associated with jungle survival training.
