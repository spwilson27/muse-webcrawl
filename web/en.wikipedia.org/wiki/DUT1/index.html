DUT1

DUT1 (sometimes written DU) is the difference between Universal Time (UT1), which is defined by Earth's rotation, and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), which is defined by a uniform atomic time scale:

DUT1 = UT1 − UTC

Purpose

Because Earth's rotation is irregular and gradually slowing, UT1 drifts with respect to UTC. Leap seconds are inserted into UTC to keep the difference small; by convention |DUT1| is kept below 0.9 seconds. The value of DUT1 is predicted and published by the International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS) in its Bulletin A, and final values appear in Bulletin B.

Broadcast

Some time signal broadcasts, such as WWV and CHU, encode DUT1 in their transmissions (to a resolution of 0.1 second) so that navigators using celestial observations can recover UT1 from UTC. DUT1 is used in astronomy and celestial navigation where the precise rotational phase of the Earth matters.
