Zettelkasten

Zettelkasten (German for "slip box") is a system of note-taking and personal knowledge management in which individual notes, written on separate slips of paper or cards, are given unique identifiers and linked to one another, allowing a body of knowledge to grow organically through accumulation and cross-referencing.

History

Slip-box methods date back centuries — the commonplace book and card indexes of early modern scholars are antecedents. The system's best-known practitioner was the German sociologist Niklas Luhmann (1927–1998), who built a Zettelkasten of some 90,000 linked notes over his career and credited it as his "second memory" and research partner, with which he produced dozens of books and hundreds of articles.

Method

In Luhmann's method, notes are written in one's own words, kept atomic (one idea per note), assigned a unique branching identifier, and filed so that related notes are adjacent or explicitly cross-referenced. New notes are placed in conversation with existing ones, so knowledge develops through links rather than through a fixed topical hierarchy. In the digital era, the method has been adopted in personal-knowledge software that supports wiki-style linking and unique note IDs.
