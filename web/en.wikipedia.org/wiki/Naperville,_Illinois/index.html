Naperville is a city in DuPage and Will counties in the U.S. state of Illinois. Located 28 miles (45 km) west of Chicago, it is a suburb in the Chicago metropolitan area. With a population of 149,540 at the 2020 census, it is the fourth-most populous city in Illinois.

History

Naperville was founded in 1831 by Joseph Naper, who arrived with his family and friends aboard the schooner Telegraph and built a settlement on the DuPage River known as Naper's Settlement. It served briefly as the seat of DuPage County from 1839 to 1868. The arrival of the Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad in 1864 spurred growth, and Naperville remained a largely rural community until the suburban boom of the 1960s.

Economy and recognition

The construction of the East-West Tollway (I-88) research corridor attracted corporate offices and research facilities, including Bell Labs (later Nokia) and BP research centers. Naperville has repeatedly ranked among the best places to live in the United States in national surveys, and its Naperville Public Library system has been ranked among the nation's best.

Attractions

The downtown Riverwalk, built in 1981 for the city's sesquicentennial, runs along the DuPage River. Other landmarks include Naper Settlement, an outdoor history museum, the Millennium Carillon bell tower, and Centennial Beach, a flooded quarry used as a swimming facility.
