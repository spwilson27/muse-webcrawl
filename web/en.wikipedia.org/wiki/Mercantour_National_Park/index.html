Mercantour National Park (French: Parc national du Mercantour) is a national park in the French Alps, created in 1979. It covers parts of the Alpes-Maritimes and Alpes-de-Haute-Provence departments and borders Italy's Maritime Alps Natural Park, with which it forms a large transboundary protected area.

Landscape and wildlife

The park is named for the Mercantour massif, whose highest summit, Mont Gélas, rises to 3,143 metres. Its valleys support chamois, ibex, marmots and golden eagles, and wolves returned naturally from Italy in the 1990s, the first confirmed in France for decades. The flora includes many endemic species, reflecting the park's position between Alpine and Mediterranean climates.

Valley of Marvels

The park's Vallée des Merveilles (Valley of Marvels) contains tens of thousands of Bronze Age petroglyphs carved into rocks around Mont Bégo, one of Europe's most important open-air rock-art sites.
