Surigao City

Surigao City (Surigaonon: Dakbayan nan Surigao; Cebuano: Dakbayan sa Surigao) is a component city and the capital of the province of Surigao del Norte, Philippines. Located at the north-easternmost tip of Mindanao, facing the Surigao Strait, it is a major transport hub: its port connects Mindanao by ferry to the Visayas and to nearby islands such as Siargao and Dinagat. According to recent censuses the city has a population of roughly 170,000.

Geography and history

The city consists of a mainland portion and 17 surrounding islands, with a total land area of about 245 square kilometres. Spanish records from the 18th century describe Surigao as a fortified settlement; it became a town in 1871 and the capital of the old Surigao province. During the Second World War the nearby Surigao Strait was the scene, in October 1944, of the last battleship-versus-battleship action in naval history, part of the Battle of Leyte Gulf. Surigao became a city on 31 August 1970 under Republic Act No. 6134.

Economy and transport

The economy rests on the port, fishing, retail trade and mining-related services for the nickel-rich province; the city is the gateway for tourists bound for Siargao Island's surfing beaches. Surigao Airport offers flights to Manila and Cebu. In February 2017 the city was struck by a magnitude 6.7 earthquake that damaged buildings and infrastructure.
