Meiyu

The meiyu (梅雨), also known as the baiu in Japan and jangma in Korea, is the rainy season of East Asia, occurring in late spring and early summer. It is caused by a quasi-stationary front — the meiyu front — that forms where warm, moist air from the south meets cooler air from the north, bringing weeks of persistent rain, drizzle and overcast skies to China, Taiwan, Japan and Korea.

Meiyu

Rain during meiyu season

Chinese: 梅雨 (méiyǔ, "plum rain")
Japanese: 梅雨 (baiu, tsuyu)
Korean: 장마 (jangma)
Season: May–July

Name

The Chinese name means "plum rain": the season coincides with the ripening of plums in the Yangtze basin. In Japan the same characters are read baiu or tsuyu. The season is also sometimes called the "mould rain" in older English writing, referring to the damp conditions that encourage mould growth.

Progression

The front typically establishes itself over South China in May, then migrates northward through June and July, reaching the Yangtze valley, Korea and Japan in turn, before dissipating as the summer monsoon advances. The rainfall is vital for rice cultivation across the region, but the season also brings serious flooding: the 2020 China floods, the 2018 Japan floods and many other disasters occurred during meiyu/baiu periods.
