Regular polytope

In mathematics, a regular polytope is a polytope whose symmetry group acts transitively on its flags; equivalently, all of its constituent elements (cells, faces, edges and vertices) are congruent and regular, arranged symmetrically around each element of any given dimension.

Classification

In two dimensions there are infinitely many regular polygons. In three dimensions there are exactly five convex regular polyhedra, the Platonic solids: the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron. Together with the four Kepler–Poinsot star polyhedra, these make nine regular polyhedra.

In four dimensions there are six convex regular polychora (the 5-cell, 8-cell or tesseract, 16-cell, 24-cell, 120-cell and 600-cell) plus ten regular star polychora. In five and higher dimensions there are only three convex regular polytopes: the simplex, the hypercube and the cross-polytope.

Notation

Regular polytopes are denoted by Schläfli symbols of the form {p, q, r, ...}. The concept was systematically developed in the 19th century by Ludwig Schläfli and in the 20th century by H. S. M. Coxeter.
