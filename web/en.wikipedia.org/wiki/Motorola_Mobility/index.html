Motorola Mobility LLC, marketed as Motorola, is an American consumer electronics manufacturer headquartered in Chicago. It was formed on 4 January 2011 when Motorola, Inc. split into two independent companies: Motorola Mobility, which took the mobile phone and home (set-top box) businesses, and Motorola Solutions, which took the enterprise and public-safety businesses. Motorola Mobility thus inherited the handset business founded by Motorola in the twentieth century — creator of the first commercial handheld mobile phone, the DynaTAC (1983), and of the iconic RAZR flip phone (2004).

Google ownership

In August 2011, Google announced it would acquire Motorola Mobility for $12.5 billion, motivated principally by its portfolio of roughly 17,000 patents (with thousands more pending), which Google wanted to defend the Android ecosystem in litigation. The deal closed in May 2012. Under Google, Motorola launched the Moto X (2013), assembled in Texas and marketed on customisation and contextual features, and the Moto G, a highly praised budget smartphone. Google sold Motorola's home business to Arris Group in 2013.

Lenovo ownership

In January 2014, Google agreed to sell Motorola Mobility to Lenovo for about $2.91 billion, retaining most of the patent portfolio and the Advanced Technology and Projects (ATAP) group; the sale closed in October 2014. Lenovo initially ran Motorola alongside its own phone business and in 2017 consolidated its smartphone efforts under the Motorola brand. The product range spans the budget Moto E and mid-range Moto G lines, the flagship Edge series, and foldable revivals of the Razr brand (from 2019) with flexible displays. Motorola has become a top-three smartphone vendor in the United States and the leading vendor in parts of Latin America, and re-entered the top tier of global vendors in the 2020s.

Heritage

Motorola Mobility markets itself on the heritage of Motorola's mobile inventions: the first car radios, the Handie-Talkie of the Second World War, the DynaTAC, the StarTAC clamshell (1996), and the RAZR V3 (2004), which sold over 130 million units.
