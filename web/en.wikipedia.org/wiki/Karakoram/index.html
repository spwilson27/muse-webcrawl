Karakoram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Karakoram range: a sea of jagged snow peaks and huge glaciers, including the Baltoro]
The Karakoram, Pakistan.

The Karakoram is a mountain range in the Kashmir region, spanning the borders of Pakistan, India and China, and the northeast corner of Afghanistan's Wakhan Corridor. It is part of the greater Himalayas complex though a separate range. The Karakoram contains more peaks over 8,000 m in close proximity than anywhere else — including K2, the world's second-highest mountain — and the longest glaciers outside the polar regions.

Geography

The range runs about 500 km northwest–southeast. Its great glaciers include the Siachen (about 76 km), Biafo, Baltoro and Batura. The Karakoram Highway, one of the world's highest paved international roads, links Pakistan and China over the Khunjerab Pass (about 4,693 m).

Name

'Karakoram' is a Turkic name meaning 'black gravel', originally applied to a trading pass. The range's peaks were designated with the prefix K in the Great Trigonometrical Survey — hence K2.
