Juncus

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Poales
Family: Juncaceae
Genus: Juncus L.
Type species: Juncus acutus

Juncus is a genus of monocotyledonous flowering plants, commonly known as rushes. It is the largest genus in the family Juncaceae, containing around 300 species distributed worldwide, especially in temperate regions and in wet habitats such as marshes, bogs and lakeshores.

Description

Rushes are perennial, rarely annual, grass-like herbs with slender, usually leafless or few-leaved stems. The small, inconspicuous flowers are typically brown or green, borne in clusters, and the fruit is a many-seeded capsule. Familiar species include the soft rush (Juncus effusus), used for weaving mats and rushlights, and the sharp rush (Juncus acutus).

Ecology and uses

Juncus species are important in wetland ecology, stabilising banks and providing habitat for wildlife, and are used in constructed wetlands and phytoremediation to filter water. In Japan the soft rush is cultivated for the tatami mat surface known as igusa.
