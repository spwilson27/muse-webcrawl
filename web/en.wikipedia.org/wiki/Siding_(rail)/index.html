Siding (rail)

A siding, in rail terminology, is a low-speed track section distinct from a running line or main line. Sidings are used for stabling trains, storing rolling stock, loading and unloading freight, and allowing slower trains to be overtaken.

Types

A passing loop (or passing siding) on a single-track line lets opposing trains cross. Private sidings connect factories, mines, and warehouses directly to the network. Other forms include refuge sidings, yard sidings, and headshunts used for shunting movements.

Operation

Access to a siding is controlled by points (switches) and signalling; on modern railways, movements are often governed by track warrants or centralised traffic control.
