Break-even (economics)

A break-even chart: the break-even point is where total revenue equals total cost.

The break-even point (BEP) in economics, business and cost accounting is the point at which total cost and total revenue are equal: there is no net loss or gain, and one has "broken even". A profit or a loss has not been made, although opportunity costs have been "paid" and capital has received the risk-adjusted, expected return.

Calculation

In the linear cost–volume–profit model, the break-even quantity is computed by dividing total fixed costs by the contribution margin per unit — the selling price per unit minus the variable cost per unit. The break-even point in sales value is the break-even quantity multiplied by the price, or equivalently total fixed costs divided by the contribution margin ratio.

Applications

Break-even analysis is used by managers and entrepreneurs to determine how much output must be sold to cover costs, to set prices, and to assess the margin of safety — the amount by which actual sales exceed the break-even level. The analysis assumes fixed costs, prices and unit variable costs remain constant, which limits its realism for long-run or multi-product settings.
