Nuclear physics

Nuclear physics is the field of physics that studies atomic nuclei and their constituents and interactions. It grew from the discovery of radioactivity (1896), Rutherford's nuclear model (1911) and the identification of the proton and neutron, and underlies nuclear power, nuclear weapons, nuclear medicine and much of astrophysics.

Core questions include nuclear structure (shell model, liquid-drop model), nuclear reactions and decay, and the strong nuclear force binding nucleons. Beyond applied topics, nuclear physics probes the origin of the elements (nucleosynthesis in stars and supernovae) and exotic states of matter such as the quark–gluon plasma created in heavy-ion colliders.

Relation to other fields

Nuclear physics shades into particle physics at high energies — historically, cosmic-ray and nuclear experiments seeded particle physics — and into atomic physics at the electron scale. Facilities include particle accelerators, research reactors and underground detectors for neutrinos and dark matter.
