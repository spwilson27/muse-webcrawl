USB

Universal Serial Bus (USB) is an industry standard that allows users to create connections between electronic devices and their peripherals. It specifies the architecture, in particular the physical interfaces and communication protocols to and from hosts, such as personal computers, to and from peripheral devices, e.g. displays, keyboards and mass storage devices, or hubs that connect additional devices. A USB network is a host-controlled network that requires a host controller, usually a personal computer.

History

USB was designed in the mid-1990s to standardize the connection of peripherals to personal computers, replacing a multitude of earlier interfaces such as serial ports, parallel ports, and proprietary connectors. The USB Implementers Forum (USB-IF) was formed in 1995; version 1.0 of the specification was released in January 1996, offering speeds of 1.5 Mbit/s (Low Speed) and 12 Mbit/s (Full Speed).

Connectors

Original connectors included Type-A and Type-B plugs and receptacles, later joined by Mini and Micro variants widely used on mobile phones. The reversible USB-C (Type-C) connector, introduced in 2014, has become the dominant form factor and is used for data, video (via DisplayPort Alt Mode), and power delivery up to 240 W under the USB Power Delivery specification. In the European Union, USB-C became the mandated common charging port for most portable electronics.

Architecture

USB is a host-centric, tiered-star bus. A host controller schedules all transactions; devices are enumerated at connection and assigned addresses. Communication uses endpoints and pipes, with transfer types including control, bulk, interrupt, and isochronous. USB On-The-Go and Dual-Role-Data allow devices such as smartphones to act as hosts for peripherals.
