USMLE

The United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) is a multi-part professional examination for medical licensure in the United States. It is sponsored by the Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB) and the National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME), and passing its three Steps is required for both U.S. medical graduates and international medical graduates who wish to practice medicine in the United States.

The Steps

Step 1 assesses the basic sciences — anatomy, biochemistry, physiology, pharmacology, pathology, microbiology, and behavioral sciences — usually taken after the second year of medical school. It became pass/fail in January 2022. Step 2 CK (Clinical Knowledge) tests clinical science and patient care, usually taken in the fourth year; the former Step 2 CS clinical-skills exam was discontinued in 2021. Step 3, taken during residency, assesses the application of medical knowledge and the management of patients in ambulatory settings over two days of testing.

Scoring and role in residency selection

Step 2 CK and Step 3 are reported with three-digit scores, with a passing standard around 200–214 depending on the Step. Before its conversion to pass/fail, the Step 1 score was one of the most heavily weighted factors in residency selection, a practice criticized for distorting medical education; following the change, program directors shifted emphasis to Step 2 CK scores.

International graduates

International medical graduates must be certified by the Educational Commission for Foreign Medical Graduates (ECFMG), which requires passing Step 1 and Step 2 CK and meeting pathways requirements that replaced Step 2 CS, before entering U.S. residency programs through the Match.
