Solanaceae


Common name: Nightshade family
Genera: About 98
Species: About 2,700
Order: Solanales
Distribution: Worldwide, centred in South America

The Solanaceae, or nightshades, are a family of flowering plants that ranges from annual and perennial herbs to vines, lianas, epiphytes, shrubs, and trees, and includes a number of agricultural crops, medicinal plants, spices, weeds, and ornamentals. Many members contain potent alkaloids.

Important genera and crops

The family includes some of the world's most important food plants: the potato, tomato, eggplant (aubergine), and peppers (genus Capsicum) in the genus Solanum and its relatives. Other economically significant members include tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum), petunias, and the medicinal and poisonous plants belladonna (Atropa belladonna), henbane, datura, and mandrake. Many originated in the Americas and spread worldwide after the Columbian exchange.

Characteristics

Flowers are typically five-petalled and radially or slightly bilaterally symmetric, with five stamens; the fruit is a berry or capsule. Leaves are alternate, often with an uneven base. Members characteristically produce steroidal or tropane alkaloids such as solanine, nicotine, atropine, and capsaicin, which serve as chemical defences.
