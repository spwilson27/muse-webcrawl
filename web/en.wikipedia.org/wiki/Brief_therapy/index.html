Brief therapy

[Image placeholder: psychotherapy session]

Brief therapy (also brief psychotherapy, planned short-term therapy, and time-limited treatment) is an umbrella term for a variety of approaches to psychotherapy. It differs from other schools of therapy in that it emphasizes (1) a focus on a specific problem and (2) direct intervention. In brief therapy, the therapist takes responsibility for working more proactively with the client in order to treat clinical and subjective conditions more rapidly.

Characteristics

Brief therapies are usually completed within a small number of sessions — commonly between one and twenty — rather than extending over months or years. Rather than exploring the client's entire history or personality structure, the work concentrates on presenting problems, current patterns, and achievable goals. Sessions are structured, and homework assignments between sessions are common.

Approaches

Schools and techniques commonly described as brief therapies include:

  • Solution-focused brief therapy, developed by Steve de Shazer, Insoo Kim Berg and colleagues, which builds on exceptions and the client's own resources and preferred future
  • Brief psychodynamic therapy, which compresses psychoanalytic ideas into a focused, time-limited format
  • Strategic and systemic family therapy, associated with the Mental Research Institute (MRI) in Palo Alto and the Milan school
  • Cognitive behavioural therapy, which is typically short-term and structured
  • Interpersonal therapy, a time-limited treatment developed for depression

Rationale and evidence

The growth of brief therapy was driven by research showing that much of the benefit of psychotherapy occurs early in treatment, by clients' preferences, and by the cost constraints of managed care and public health systems. Studies of the "dose–effect" relationship suggest roughly half of patients recover within about 13–18 sessions. Brief therapies have an evidence base for many common conditions, including depression, anxiety disorders, and relationship problems, though longer treatment may be needed for complex or chronic presentations.
