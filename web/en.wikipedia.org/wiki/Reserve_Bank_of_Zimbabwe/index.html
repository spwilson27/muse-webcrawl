The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) is the central bank of Zimbabwe, headquartered in the capital Harare. It was founded in 1964, succeeding the Bank of Rhodesia and Nyasaland, and is established under the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe Act. Its functions include formulating and implementing monetary policy, issuing currency, regulating banks, and holding the nation's gold and foreign exchange reserves.

History

The bank became notorious internationally during the 2000s, when its money creation under Governor Gideon Gono financed government deficits and drove one of the worst hyperinflations ever recorded: official annual inflation peaked at 231 million percent in July 2008, and the Zimbabwean dollar was effectively abandoned in 2009 in favour of a multi-currency regime dominated by the US dollar. Trillion-dollar banknotes from the period became collectors' items. A new Zimbabwean dollar was introduced in 2019, and in April 2024 the bank launched the Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG), a currency unit said to be backed by gold and foreign reserves.
