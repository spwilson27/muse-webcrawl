Alaska Native Language Center

The Alaska Native Language Center (ANLC) is a research institute at the University of Alaska Fairbanks dedicated to the documentation, study and revitalisation of the Indigenous languages of Alaska. Established by the Alaska State Legislature in 1972 — the first such centre in the United States devoted to Native languages — it maintains an archive of several thousand documents, recordings and manuscripts covering all twenty Alaska Native languages, and publishes dictionaries, grammars and teaching materials developed with Native speakers.

Work

Under founding director Michael Krauss, the Center produced the landmark 1980 map of Alaska's Indigenous languages and conducted the surveys that charted their rapid decline; Krauss's 1992 warning that most of the state's languages would become extinct without intervention helped spur immersion and master-apprentice programmes. Languages documented include Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Siberian Yupik, Aleut (Unangam Tunuu), Alutiiq, Tlingit, Haida and the Athabascan family. The Center supports the University of Alaska's degree programmes in Native languages and community-based projects such as the Yup'ik immersion schools in Bethel. Its archive of roughly 15,000 items is a principal repository for scholars and tribal programmes. See also Michael Krauss, Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act and University of Alaska Fairbanks.
