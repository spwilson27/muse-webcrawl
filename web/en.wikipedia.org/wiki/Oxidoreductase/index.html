Oxidoreductase

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In biochemistry, an oxidoreductase is an enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of electrons from one molecule, the reductant (electron donor), to another, the oxidant (electron acceptor). This group of enzymes usually utilizes NADP+ or NAD+ as cofactors. Transmembrane oxidoreductases create electron transport chains in bacteria, chloroplasts and mitochondria, including complex I, complex III and complex IV of cellular respiration.

Classification

Oxidoreductases are classified as EC 1 in the EC number classification of enzymes. They are subdivided into about 22 subclasses according to the donor group acted upon, including enzymes acting on CH–OH groups (EC 1.1), aldehyde or oxo groups (EC 1.2), and NADH or NADPH (EC 1.6). Familiar examples include dehydrogenases, oxidases, reductases, peroxidases, catalases and oxygenases.

Examples

Important oxidoreductases include alcohol dehydrogenase, glucose oxidase, cytochrome c oxidase, laccase, and the enzymes of the photosynthetic and respiratory electron transport chains. Cofactors such as FAD, FMN, haem and metal ions frequently participate in the electron transfer.
