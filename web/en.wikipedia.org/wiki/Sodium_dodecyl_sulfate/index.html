Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) or sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS), sometimes written sodium laurilsulfate, is an organic compound with the formula CH3(CH2)11OSO3Na and structure H3C−(CH2)11−O−SO3− Na+. It is an anionic surfactant used in many cleaning and hygiene products. This compound is the sodium salt of the 12-carbon alcohol dodecanol, esterified by sulfuric acid.

Production

SDS is prepared by sulfation of dodecanol (lauryl alcohol) with sulfur trioxide or chlorosulfonic acid, followed by neutralization with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate. The dodecanol may be derived from coconut or palm kernel oil, or synthesized petrochemically.

Applications

Cleaning and hygiene

SDS is a common ingredient in laundry detergents, dishwashing liquids, shampoos, and toothpastes, where its surfactant and foaming properties help remove oils and dirt. In toothpaste it also disperses active ingredients, though it has been associated in some users with recurrent mouth ulcers.

Laboratory use

In molecular biology, SDS is used to denature proteins for SDS-PAGE (polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis). SDS coats proteins with a uniform negative charge roughly proportional to their length, so that electrophoretic separation occurs primarily by molecular mass. It is also used to lyse cells during nucleic-acid extraction.

Safety

SDS is a skin and eye irritant, particularly in concentrated or prolonged contact, but is considered safe in typical consumer-product concentrations by regulatory bodies.
