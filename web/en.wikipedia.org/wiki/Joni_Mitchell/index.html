Joni Mitchell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Joni Mitchell in 1976 with her guitar, in the Hejira years: long blonde hair, beret, mid-conversation at a recording session]

Roberta Joan 'Joni' Mitchell (born Roberta Joan Anderson, 7 November 1943) is a Canadian-American singer-songwriter and painter, widely regarded as one of the greatest songwriters of the twentieth century. From the folk clubs of Toronto and Detroit she produced a sequence of albums — Blue (1971) above all, regularly ranked among the finest ever made — that turned confession into high art, then moved through jazz (Court and Spark, 1974; Hejira, 1976; Mingus, 1979) with a harmonic language no guitarist has replicated, in part because she played in some fifty self-devised tunings.

Her songs include 'Both Sides, Now' (written 1967, a standard in two centuries), 'A Case of You', 'River', 'Big Yellow Taxi' and 'Woodstock' — the anthem of the festival she missed, made famous by Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young. She designed most of her album covers from her own paintings.

From folk to jazz

Born in Fort Macleod, Alberta, and raised in Saskatoon (polio at nine left her with the weakened hand that shaped her guitar style), Mitchell married folksinger Chuck Mitchell, took his name, and in 1967 moved to New York and then Laurel Canyon. David Crosby produced her debut; Judy Collins's hit with 'Both Sides, Now' made her reputation as a writer before her own albums did. Blue's emotional exposure — to lovers including Graham Nash and James Taylor — remains the benchmark of the confessional mode.

Later years and return

The jazz years alienated parts of her audience and are now critically ascendant; the eighties brought synth-pop experiments and painting. A brain aneurysm in 2015 nearly silenced her; her surprise appearance at the Newport Folk Festival in 2022, and subsequent concerts, became events of public rejoicing. Honours include ten Grammy Awards, the Gershwin Prize (2023) and Kennedy Center Honors (2021).
