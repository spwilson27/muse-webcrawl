National Centre for the Performing Arts (China)

LocationXicheng District, Beijing, China
ArchitectPaul Andreu
Construction began2001
Opened2007
Capacity5,452 seats (all halls)

The National Centre for the Performing Arts (NCPA; Chinese: 国家大剧院), sometimes colloquially called the "Giant Egg", is an arts centre in Beijing, China, housing an opera house, concert hall, theatre and art exhibition halls within a single enormous ellipsoid dome of titanium and glass surrounded by an artificial lake. It sits immediately west of Tiananmen Square and the Great Hall of the People, close to the Forbidden City.

Architecture

The building was designed by French architect Paul Andreu. Construction began in December 2001, and the inaugural concert was held in December 2007. The exterior is a semi-ellipsoid shell of titanium plates and glass, about 212 metres long and 144 metres wide, rising from an artificial lake so that the structure appears to float. Visitors enter through an underwater corridor beneath the lake. Inside are three main performance halls: an opera hall seating about 2,400, a concert hall seating about 2,000, and a theatre seating about 1,000, together with a smaller multi-function theatre.

Controversy and reception

The design was controversial from the start: critics argued that its ultra-modern, egg-like form clashed with the historic surroundings of central Beijing, and the cost — roughly 3 billion yuan — was publicly debated. Andreu defended the design as a deliberate contrast, a serene island of culture amid the bustle of the modern city. Despite the arguments, the NCPA quickly became one of Beijing's most recognisable landmarks and its resident companies, including its own orchestra and chorus, anchor a busy calendar of Chinese and international productions.
