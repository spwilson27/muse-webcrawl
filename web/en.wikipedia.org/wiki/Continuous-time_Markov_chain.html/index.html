Continuous-time Markov chain

A continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) is a continuous-time stochastic process that undergoes transitions from one state to another on a state space; it satisfies the Markov property — the distribution of future states depends only on the present state, not the past.

Definition

A CTMC is characterised by a transition rate matrix (the infinitesimal generator), whose entries give the rates at which transitions between states occur. The holding time in each state is exponentially distributed with a parameter equal to the total exit rate, after which a jump to another state occurs. The evolution of the state probabilities is governed by the Kolmogorov forward and backward equations.

Applications

Continuous-time Markov chains model queueing systems, population processes, chemical kinetics, reliability, finance and epidemiology (such as compartmental models of disease spread).
