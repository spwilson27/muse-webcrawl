Parmenion (also Parmenio; Greek: Παρμενίων; c. 400 – 330 BC) was a Macedonian general in the service of Philip II of Macedon and his son Alexander the Great. A veteran commander of great experience, he was Alexander's second-in-command and commanded the left wing of the Macedonian army in Alexander's great battles against the Persian Empire.

Career under Philip II

Parmenion rose to prominence under Philip II, winning a notable victory over the Illyrians in 356 BC. He became one of Philip's most trusted generals and was sent with an advance force into Asia Minor in 336 BC to prepare the invasion of Persia, shortly before Philip's assassination.

Service under Alexander

Under Alexander, Parmenion held the left wing at the battles of Issus (333 BC) and Gaugamela (331 BC), and played a key role in the army's operations and logistics. Ancient sources often portray him as a cautious counterweight to Alexander's boldness — most famously advising (unsuccessfully) a night attack before Gaugamela and acceptance of Darius III's peace offer, prompting Alexander's alleged remark that he would accept, "were I Parmenion".

Death

In 330 BC, after the arrest and execution of Parmenion's son Philotas for alleged conspiracy, Alexander ordered Parmenion's assassination at Ecbatana, where the old general guarded the royal treasury and supply lines, fearing he might seek revenge. His death is generally regarded by historians as a ruthless political precaution rather than a response to any real disloyalty.
