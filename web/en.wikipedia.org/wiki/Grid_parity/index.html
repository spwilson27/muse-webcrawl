Grid parity

Grid parity occurs when an alternative energy source can generate electricity at a cost less than or equal to the price of electricity from the conventional power grid. The term is most commonly applied to solar photovoltaic power, comparing the levelised cost of electricity from a solar installation with the retail ("socket parity") or wholesale ("busbar parity") price of grid electricity.

Development

Because photovoltaic module prices fell steadily for decades — following the learning-curve pattern known as Swanson's law — grid parity was reached first in sunny regions with high retail electricity prices, such as Hawaii, California, Italy and parts of Australia, during the 2010s, and has since spread to many more markets. Reaching parity is considered a milestone because it means rooftop solar can compete without subsidies: owners save money by consuming their own generation rather than buying from the grid, though the value of exported power, feed-in tariffs, net metering rules and the intermittency of solar all complicate the simple parity comparison.
