Black September Organization

The Black September Organization (BSO) was a Palestinian militant organisation founded in 1970, taking its name from the "Black September" conflict in Jordan that year, when King Hussein's forces expelled the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO). The group carried out attacks against Israeli, Jordanian, and Western targets in the early 1970s and is best known for the Munich massacre at the 1972 Summer Olympics.

Origins

After the Jordanian army's September 1970 offensive killed thousands of Palestinians and drove the PLO to Lebanon, Black September emerged — widely regarded as linked to Fatah, though the relationship was officially denied — to avenge the expulsion. Its first major operation was the assassination of Jordanian prime minister Wasfi Tal in Cairo in November 1971.

Munich massacre

On 5 September 1972, eight BSO members infiltrated the Olympic Village in Munich, killed two members of the Israeli Olympic team, and took nine hostage, demanding the release of Palestinian prisoners. A botched German rescue attempt at Fürstenfeldbruck airbase ended with all nine hostages, five attackers, and a police officer dead. The events were broadcast live worldwide and transformed Olympic security.

Aftermath

Israel responded with Operation Wrath of God, a years-long campaign assassinating Palestinians it held responsible. Black September also attempted or carried out other attacks, including the 1973 Khartoum diplomatic assassinations, before fading by the mid-1970s as the PLO moved toward diplomacy after the 1973 Arab–Israeli war.
