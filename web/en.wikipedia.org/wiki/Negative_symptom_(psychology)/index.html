Negative symptom (psychology)

In psychology and psychiatry, negative symptoms are the absence or diminution of normal functions and behaviours. They are a core feature of schizophrenia but also occur in other psychotic and neurological conditions. The term contrasts with positive symptoms, which are experiences or behaviours that are added, such as hallucinations and delusions.

Principal symptoms

The negative symptoms are commonly grouped into five domains, sometimes called the "five A's":

  • Avolition – reduced motivation and self-initiated purposeful activity
  • Alogia – poverty of speech
  • Anhedonia – reduced ability to experience pleasure
  • Asociality – reduced social interaction and interest
  • Affective blunting – diminished facial expression, eye contact and expressive gestures

Assessment and treatment

Negative symptoms are assessed with rating scales such as the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), the Scale for the Assessment of Negative Symptoms (SANS) and newer instruments. They are generally more resistant to antipsychotic medication than positive symptoms and are strongly associated with poorer functional outcomes, making them a major focus of treatment research.
