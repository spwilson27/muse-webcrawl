Xfinity

Xfinity is the trade name of Comcast Cable Communications, LLC, a subsidiary of Comcast Corporation, used to market consumer cable television, internet, telephone, and wireless services provided by the company. The brand was first introduced in 2010; prior to that, these services were marketed primarily under the Comcast name.

History

Comcast announced the Xfinity rebranding in February 2010 and rolled it out beginning in April of that year, initially in eleven markets including Boston, Philadelphia, Chicago and Seattle. The rebranding accompanied the rollout of expanded digital services under Project Infinity, including more high-definition channels, faster internet tiers, and the X1 entertainment platform introduced in 2012, which combined live TV, on-demand content and later streaming applications in a voice-controlled interface.

Services

Xfinity provides cable television (Xfinity TV), broadband internet — where it is the largest cable internet provider in the United States — digital telephone service (Xfinity Voice), home security (Xfinity Home), and, since 2017, Xfinity Mobile, a mobile virtual network operator running on Verizon's network combined with Xfinity Wi-Fi hotspots. The Flex streaming device and the Xfinity Stream app extend television services to broadband-only customers.

Criticism

The brand and its parent have frequently ranked poorly in consumer satisfaction surveys of American cable and internet providers, drawing criticism for pricing practices, data caps in some markets, and customer service. Comcast has also been a central figure in debates over net neutrality in the United States.
