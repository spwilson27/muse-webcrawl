Sight and Sound poll

The Sight and Sound poll is a decennial survey of film critics and scholars conducted by the British film magazine Sight and Sound, published by the British Film Institute (BFI), to determine the greatest films of all time. First held in 1952, the poll has taken place every ten years and is widely regarded as one of the most authoritative canons in film culture.

History

The first poll, in 1952, collected ballots from 63 critics and placed Vittorio De Sica's Bicycle Thieves (1948) at the top. From 1962 onwards, Orson Welles's Citizen Kane (1941) topped every poll for fifty years, until the 2012 poll, in which Alfred Hitchcock's Vertigo (1958) displaced it. The 2022 poll, the largest ever with 1,639 participants, placed Chantal Akerman's Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du commerce, 1080 Bruxelles (1975) at number one — the first film directed by a woman to top the critics' list.

Methodology

Invited voters each submit a ballot of ten films, ranked or unranked depending on the edition. Since 1992 a separate directors' poll has also been held. In 2012 the directors' choice was Yasujirō Ozu's Tokyo Story (1953); in 2022 the directors voted 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) first.

Criticism

The poll has been criticised for its reliance on an established canon and for historically under-representing women directors and non-Western cinema. The 2022 edition broadened its electorate considerably and produced the most diverse list in the poll's history.
