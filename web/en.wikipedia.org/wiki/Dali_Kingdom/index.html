Dali Kingdom

Capital: Yangjumie (present-day Dali)
Established: 937
Disestablished: 1253
Succeeded by: Mongol Empire / Yuan dynasty Yunnan
Religion: Buddhism

The Dali Kingdom, also known simply as Dali, was a Bai-led dynastic state centred in modern Yunnan, China, that existed from 937 until 1253, when it was conquered by the Mongol Empire. Its capital was the city of Dali, now Dali City.

History

Dali was founded by Duan Siping in 937 after the collapse of the preceding Nanzhao kingdom and a sequence of short-lived regimes. The Duan clan — many of whose rulers abdicated to become Buddhist monks — governed for over three centuries. The kingdom practised a distinctive form of esoteric Buddhism (Azhali Buddhism), and its art, including the famous Dali scroll painting of Buddhist figures, shows strong local character.

Mongol conquest

In 1253, Mongol forces under Uriyangkadai, campaigning as part of Möngke Khan's strategy to envelop the Song dynasty, crossed the Jinsha River and took Dali. The Duan family was retained as hereditary native officials (tusi) under Yuan rule, governing Yunnan as vassals for centuries thereafter.
