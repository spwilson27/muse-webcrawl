Reclining figure

A reclining figure is a work of art, usually sculpture, depicting a human or animal figure lying on its side or back. The motif is ancient — seen in pre-Columbian chacmool statues and Roman river gods — but is especially associated with the English sculptor Henry Moore, whose reclining figures became a central theme of modernist sculpture.

Henry Moore

Moore made his first reclining figure in 1924 and returned to the theme throughout his career, inspired by the Toltec-Maya chacmool figure. His reclining forms explore the interplay of mass and void, often incorporating holes and hollows that echo landscapes.

Other traditions

Reclining figures appear in Etruscan funerary art, classical sarcophagi, Buddhist iconography (the reclining Buddha), and modern works by artists such as Fernando Botero.
