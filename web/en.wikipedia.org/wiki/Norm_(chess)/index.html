Norm (chess)

In chess, a norm is a high level of performance in a tournament that counts toward earning a FIDE title such as Grandmaster (GM) or International Master (IM). To be awarded the Grandmaster title, a player must achieve three GM norms in tournaments meeting specified conditions, totalling at least 27 games, and must also have reached a FIDE rating of 2500 at some point. For the International Master title, three IM norms and a rating of 2400 are required.

Requirements

A norm performance requires the player to achieve a performance rating above a threshold (2600 for a GM norm, 2450 for an IM norm) in a single event, against a field with a sufficient proportion of titled players and players from other federations, under classical time controls. The tournament must include a minimum number of rounds and opponents holding GM or IM titles, and no more than a certain share of the player's games may be against untitled players. Norms are certified by the tournament arbiter and submitted to FIDE.

Norm tournaments

Because round-robin events with strong invited fields offer the most predictable route to a norm, "norm tournaments" are regularly organised — sometimes controversially, as fields can be arranged to favour the norm-seekers. Closed events such as those in the Hungarian First Saturday series became famous venues for earning norms.
