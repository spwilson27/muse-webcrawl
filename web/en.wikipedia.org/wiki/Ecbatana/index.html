Ecbatana

Ecbatana (Old Persian: Hagmatāna; Greek: Ἐκβάτανα) was an ancient city in Media in western Iran, identified with modern Hamadan. It was the capital of the Median Empire and later served as a summer residence of the Achaemenid Persian kings, valued for its cool climate at the foot of Mount Alvand.

History

According to Herodotus, Ecbatana was founded by Deioces, the first king of the Medes, who surrounded it with seven concentric walls, each painted a different colour, with the royal palace and treasury at the centre. While this description is likely legendary, the city was certainly a major royal centre. Cyrus the Great captured Ecbatana in 550 BCE, taking the Median kingdom into his new Persian Empire. Under the Achaemenids it housed archives — the Book of Ezra records a scroll of Cyrus's decree being found there — and under the Parthians and Sasanians it remained an important city on the road between Mesopotamia and the Iranian plateau.

Alexander the Great occupied Ecbatana in 330 BCE in pursuit of Darius III; his closest friend Hephaestion died there the following year. The city's treasures were carried off to finance Alexander's campaigns.

Archaeology

Because modern Hamadan sits directly atop the ancient city, excavation has been limited. Investigations at Tepe Hagmatana (Ecbatana hill) have uncovered mud-brick fortifications, columned halls and artefacts of the Median and Achaemenid periods, and finds are displayed in the Hegmataneh Museum. The nearby Ganjnameh inscriptions of Darius I and Xerxes I are carved in the Alvand foothills.
