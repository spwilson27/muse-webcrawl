The Badinter Arbitration Committee, formally the Arbitration Commission of the Peace Conference on Yugoslavia, was a body established by the Council of Ministers of the European Community on 27 August 1991 to provide legal advice on questions arising from the breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. It was chaired by Robert Badinter, a former French Minister of Justice and president of the Constitutional Council, and comprised presidents of European constitutional courts.

Opinions

Between 1991 and 1993 the Commission issued fifteen opinions and related decisions. Among the most influential:

  • Opinion No. 1 (29 November 1991) held that Yugoslavia was "in the process of dissolution", rather than facing secession by some republics.
  • Opinion No. 2 addressed the rights of Serb populations in Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina, holding that they were entitled to minority and human rights protections but not to change borders unilaterally.
  • Opinion No. 3 held that the internal boundaries between the republics could not be altered except by agreement and would become international frontiers protected by international law, applying the principle of uti possidetis juris.
  • Opinions Nos. 4–7 assessed whether Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Macedonia, and Slovenia met the EC's conditions for recognition, famously finding that Croatia's protection of minorities was incomplete and that recognition of Bosnia should depend on a referendum.
  • Opinion No. 8 (4 July 1992) concluded that the dissolution of Yugoslavia was complete and that the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro) was a new state that could not simply continue the old federation's legal personality — a position echoed by the UN Security Council in Resolution 777.

Influence

The Badinter opinions are leading modern authorities on statehood, recognition, self-determination, and the transformation of internal boundaries into international borders on the dissolution of a federation; they have been widely cited in later cases, including debates surrounding Kosovo and Crimea.
