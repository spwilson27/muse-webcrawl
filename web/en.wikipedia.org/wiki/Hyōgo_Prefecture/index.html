Hyōgo Prefecture (兵庫県, Hyōgo-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Kobe, one of Japan's principal ports. Hyōgo is unusual among Japanese prefectures in facing two seas: the Sea of Japan to the north and the Seto Inland Sea to the south, and it also administers Awaji Island, the largest island in the Inland Sea.

Geography and cities

The prefecture covers about 8,400 km² and has a population of roughly 5.4 million. Beyond Kobe (about 1.5 million people), major cities include Himeji, Nishinomiya, Amagasaki, Akashi and Takarazuka. The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge, one of the world's longest suspension bridges, links Kobe to Awaji Island. The northern coast (the San'in area around Toyooka and the Kinosaki hot springs) contrasts with the dense Hanshin industrial corridor in the south-east.

History

Present-day Hyōgo corresponds to the former provinces of Harima, Tajima, Awaji and parts of Tanba and Settsu. Kobe's port was opened to foreign trade in 1868 and developed into a cosmopolitan trading city with foreign settlements at Kitano. On 17 January 1995 the Great Hanshin earthquake (magnitude 6.9) struck the prefecture, killing more than 6,400 people, most of them in Kobe, and destroying large parts of the city's housing and port infrastructure; recovery took a decade. Himeji Castle, a surviving feudal castle, was among the first Japanese sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993.

Economy and culture

Heavy industry (steel, shipbuilding, machinery), the port of Kobe, sake brewing in the Nada district, Kobe beef from Tajima cattle, and tourism (Arima Onsen, Kinosaki Onsen, Himeji) anchor the economy. The all-female Takarazuka Revue is headquartered in Takarazuka city.
