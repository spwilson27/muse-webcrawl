Anchorage (maritime)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ships at anchor in a designated anchorage area

An anchorage is a location at sea where ships can lower anchors — as distinct from a mooring, where vessels attach to a fixed buoy or structure. Anchorages are commonly situated close to ports, in sheltered water with a suitable seabed (typically mud or sand) that allows the anchor to hold.

Types and regulation

Charts mark designated anchorage areas, often assigned by a port authority or vessel traffic service, where ships wait for berths, conduct cargo transfers, quarantine inspections, bunkering or crew changes. Some anchorages are set aside for vessels carrying explosives or dangerous cargo. Famous large anchorages include those off Singapore, Rotterdam, Gibraltar and Istanbul, where dozens of vessels may wait at anchor simultaneously.

Seamanship

Choosing an anchorage requires regard to depth, swinging room as wind and tide change, shelter, holding ground and proximity to hazards. The master must maintain an anchor watch and be ready to get under way; dragging anchor in heavy weather is a recognised navigational risk addressed in the COLREGs and local port rules.
