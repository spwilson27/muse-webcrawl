Architectural style

An architectural style is a set of characteristics and features that make a building or structure recognizable, including elements such as form, materials, construction methods, ornament, and regional or historical context. Styles emerge from the cultural, technological, and economic conditions of their time and place.

Field: Architecture / Art history
Examples: Gothic, Baroque, Neoclassical, Modernist, Art Deco

Development

Styles often develop gradually and overlap; historians classify buildings into styles such as Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, Rococo, Neoclassical, and the various revival and modern movements of the 19th and 20th centuries, including Art Nouveau, Bauhaus, International Style, Brutalism, and Postmodernism. Vernacular architecture reflects local traditions rather than named styles.

Study

The classification of architecture into styles parallels periodization in art history and helps scholars date buildings, trace influences, and understand the ideas of their creators.
