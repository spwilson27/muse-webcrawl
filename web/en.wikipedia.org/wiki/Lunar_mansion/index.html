Lunar mansion

A lunar mansion is a segment of the ecliptic (the path the Moon travels in the sky) through which the Moon passes during its roughly 27.3-day orbit around the Earth. Lunar mansions are a feature of several traditional astronomical and astrological systems, where the Moon's daily position was used for calendrical and divinatory purposes.

Systems

In Indian astronomy and astrology, the mansions are the 27 or 28 nakshatras, beginning with Ashvini, each associated with a ruling deity and used in the Jyotisha tradition. In Arabic astronomy, the 28 manāzil al-qamar ("mansions of the Moon") served as a lunar calendar and influenced medieval European astrology. Chinese astronomy divides the Moon's path into 28 xiù grouped into the Four Palaces (Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Black Tortoise). Each mansion spans roughly 13 degrees of the ecliptic, and particular bright stars mark the mansions in each culture's system.

Use

Because the Moon returns to the same mansion every sidereal month, lunar mansions provided a practical framework for tracking time, predicting lunar positions, and electing auspicious moments for activities in astrology.
