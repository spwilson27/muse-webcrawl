Kenny Loggins

Placeholder image: Kenny Loggins in concert.

Kenneth Clark Loggins (born January 7, 1948) is an American singer, songwriter and guitarist. He first gained fame as one half of the duo Loggins and Messina (1971–1976) before launching a successful solo career, and became especially known for a run of hit movie soundtrack songs in the 1980s, including "Footloose" and "Danger Zone".

Loggins and Messina

Paired with producer-artist Jim Messina, Loggins released albums such as Sittin' In (1971), featuring "Danny's Song" and "House at Pooh Corner", and the live and studio records that made the duo one of the top-selling acts of the early 1970s.

Solo career and soundtracks

Loggins's solo albums Celebrate Me Home (1977), Nightwatch (1978, containing "Whenever I Call You 'Friend'" with Stevie Nicks) and Keep the Fire (1979) established his soft-rock success. In the 1980s he recorded signature soundtrack hits: "I'm Alright" (Caddyshack), "Footloose" and "I'm Free (Heaven Helps the Man)" (Footloose), and "Danger Zone" and "Playing with the Boys" (Top Gun). "Footloose" was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Later work

Loggins won a Grammy Award for co-writing "What a Fool Believes" with Michael McDonald, released children's music including Return to Pooh Corner (1994), and later formed the supergroup Blue Sky Riders. He published a memoir, Still Alright, in 2022.
