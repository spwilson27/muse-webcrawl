Al Thani

House of Thani (Arabic: آل ثاني)
Country: Qatar
Founded: c. 1825
Founder: Thani bin Mohammed
Current head: Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, Emir of Qatar

The House of Thani (Arabic: آل ثاني, romanized: Āl Thānī) is the ruling family of Qatar. The dynasty is descended from Thani bin Mohammed, a sheikh of the Al Maadid clan of the Banu Tamim tribe, who settled at Doha in the mid-19th century. Members of the family have ruled Qatar since the mid-1800s and continue to dominate its government and economy.

History

The Al Thani rose to prominence under Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani (r. 1847–1876), who unified the Qatari tribes and established Doha as the effective capital. His son Jassim bin Mohammed Al Thani is regarded as the founder of modern Qatar, resisting Ottoman and Bahraini claims and winning recognition of Qatari autonomy; his victory at the Battle of al-Wajbah (1893) is commemorated as Qatar National Day. Under British protectorate arrangements confirmed by treaty in 1916, the family ruled Qatar until independence in 1971, proclaimed by Sheikh Ahmad bin Ali Al Thani.

Modern rulers

Sheikh Khalifa bin Hamad deposed his cousin in 1972 and ruled until 1995, when his son Hamad bin Khalifa seized power in a bloodless coup. Hamad transformed Qatar using its natural gas wealth, founded Al Jazeera, and abdicated in 2013 in favour of his son Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, the current emir.
