Nationality

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nationality is the legal status of belonging to a particular nation, in the sense of a sovereign state. It is the bond that gives a state jurisdiction over a person and the person the state's protection; in most states its principal form is citizenship, though the terms differ in nuance (in the United States, “national” includes non-citizen nationals of outlying possessions; in international law “nationality” is the generic term).

Nationality is acquired at birth by descent (Jus sanguinis) or by place of birth (Jus soli), and later by naturalisation, marriage in some systems, or state succession. International law leaves the grant of nationality largely to states but limits it — the Nottebohm case (ICJ, 1955) required a “genuine link” for nationality to be opposable to other states — and prohibits arbitrary deprivation and statelessness. Nationality determines rights to enter and remain, diplomatic protection, military obligations, and (with residence) most political rights.
