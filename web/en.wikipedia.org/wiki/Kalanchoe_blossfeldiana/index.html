Kalanchoe blossfeldiana

Kalanchoe blossfeldiana is a herbaceous and commonly cultivated houseplant of the genus Kalanchoe native to Madagascar. It is known by the English common names flaming Katy, Christmas kalanchoe, florist kalanchoe and Madagascar widow's-thrill.

Description

The plant forms a compact, bushy mound of glossy, scallop-edged succulent leaves up to about 45 cm (18 in) tall. In late autumn and winter it produces clusters of small four-petalled flowers held above the foliage. The wild species has bright red flowers, but cultivated varieties have been bred in shades of pink, orange, yellow and white, including double-flowered forms.

Cultivation

K. blossfeldiana is a short-day plant: flowering is triggered by long nights, and commercial growers manipulate day length to bring plants into bloom for sale, especially around Christmas. It requires bright light, well-drained soil and infrequent watering, and is frost-tender, so it is grown as a houseplant in temperate climates. The species was described in 1934 and named for the German botanist and nurseryman Robert Blossfeld, who introduced it to the horticultural trade.
