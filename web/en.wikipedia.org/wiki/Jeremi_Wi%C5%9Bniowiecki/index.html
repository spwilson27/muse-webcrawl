Born: August 17, 1612, Łubnie, Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Died: August 20, 1651 (aged 39), Pawołocz
Title: Voivode of Ruthenia

Jeremi Wiśniowiecki

Prince Jeremi Michał Korybut Wiśniowiecki (1612–1651), known by his nickname "Hammer on the Cossacks", was a notable member of the aristocracy of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Prince at Wiśniowiec, Łubnie and Chorol, and Voivode of Ruthenia from 1646.

Biography

Wiśniowiecki was one of the wealthiest magnates of the Commonwealth, owning vast estates in Ukraine, including much of the Left-bank region around Łubnie. Orphaned young, he was raised by his uncle Konstanty Wiśniowiecki and educated at Jesuit colleges and abroad in Italy, France, and the Netherlands. Though born into an Orthodox family, he converted to Roman Catholicism in 1632.

Military career

He fought in the Smolensk War against Russia, in campaigns against the Ottoman Empire and the Crimean Tatars, and against rebellious Cossacks. During the Khmelnytsky Uprising of 1648 he became the principal Polish commander resisting Bohdan Khmelnytsky, defending Zbaraż in 1649 and fighting at the Battle of Berestechko in 1651, shortly before his death, likely from cholera, that same year.

Legacy

Wiśniowiecki is a major character in Henryk Sienkiewicz's novel With Fire and Sword (1884), where he is portrayed heroically; Ukrainian historiography has traditionally viewed him far more negatively because of the harsh suppression of Cossack revolts on his estates.
