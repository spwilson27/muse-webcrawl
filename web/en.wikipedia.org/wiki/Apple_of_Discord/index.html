Apple of Discord

The Apple of Discord (Ancient Greek: μῆλον τῆς Ἔριδος) is a golden apple dropped by Eris, the goddess of strife and discord, at the wedding of Peleus and Thetis in Greek mythology. Inscribed "for the fairest" (τῇ καλλίστῃ), it sparked a vanity-fuelled dispute among the goddesses Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite that eventually led to the Trojan War. In common usage, an "apple of discord" is any small matter that precipitates a larger dispute.

The myth

According to the myth, Zeus held a banquet celebrating the marriage of Peleus and Thetis, the future parents of Achilles. Eris, goddess of discord, was not invited because of her troublemaking nature. Arriving uninvited, she threw into the gathering a golden apple inscribed "for the fairest". Three goddesses claimed it: Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. Zeus, unwilling to judge among them, appointed Paris, a Trojan prince, as arbiter.

The Judgement of Paris

Each goddess offered Paris a bribe: Hera offered kingship and dominion, Athena offered wisdom and victory in battle, and Aphrodite offered the love of the most beautiful mortal woman, Helen of Sparta. Paris awarded the apple to Aphrodite. Helen, however, was already married to Menelaus of Sparta; Paris's subsequent abduction (or elopement) with Helen provoked the Achaean expedition against Troy, causing the ten-year Trojan War narrated in the Iliad and the surrounding epic cycle. The episode thus frames the war as arising from divine vanity and a single contested prize.

Legacy

The story was a favourite subject of ancient and Renaissance art — the Judgement of Paris was painted by Rubens, Cranach, and many others — and the phrase "apple of discord" entered European languages as an idiom for a nucleus of contention. In modern Discordianism, a satirical religion founded in the twentieth century, the golden apple of Eris is a central symbol. The myth is also the narrative source of the word eris in botanical and zoological names honouring strife or rivalry.
