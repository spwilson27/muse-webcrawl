Portable Network Graphics (PNG, officially pronounced "ping") is a raster-graphics file format that supports lossless data compression, transparency and a wide colour gamut. It was created in 1995–96 as an improved, patent-free replacement for GIF, and is one of the most widely used image formats on the World Wide Web.

History

The immediate spur was Unisys's announcement in December 1994 that it would enforce its patent on the LZW compression used by GIF, charging licence fees to software developers. In early 1995 an online group of graphics experts, coordinated largely by Thomas Boutell, designed a successor format in a matter of weeks on the comp.graphics Usenet groups. The World Wide Web Consortium recommended PNG 1.0 on 1 October 1996; it later became an ISO/IEC standard (ISO/IEC 15948). The original backronym "PNG's Not GIF" survives as a recursive joke.

Technical features

PNG images begin with an eight-byte signature (89 50 4E 47 0D 0A 1A 0A) and are organised as a series of chunks, each labelled with a four-letter type: IHDR (header), PLTE (palette), IDAT (image data) and IEND (end), plus many optional ancillary chunks carrying text metadata, gamma and colour-profile information. Pixel data are first processed line by line by one of five predictive filters (None, Sub, Up, Average, Paeth) and then compressed with the DEFLATE algorithm, a patent-free method shared with ZIP and gzip. PNG supports greyscale, truecolour (up to 16 bits per channel) and indexed-colour images, a full alpha channel for partial transparency, and optional two-dimensional interlacing (Adam7) for progressive display. It does not natively support animation — a gap filled by the related MNG format and, more successfully, by the Firefox-backed APNG extension.

Use

PNG is the standard choice for web graphics needing sharp lines or transparency — logos, icons, diagrams and screenshots — and for archival masters, though for photographs its lossless files are far larger than JPEG's lossy ones. All modern browsers and image editors support it, and optimisers such as pngcrush and optipng can shrink files by recompressing and trying every filter combination.
