Crudo
Course: Appetiser
Place of origin: Italy
Main ingredients: Raw fish or shellfish, olive oil, citrus, sea salt

In Italian cuisine, crudo (Italian for "raw") is a dish of raw fish or shellfish dressed with olive oil, sea salt, and citrus such as lemon, sometimes with herbs or chilli. Unlike ceviche, the fish is not marinated long enough to be denatured by acid; unlike Japanese sashimi, it is seasoned before serving.

Preparation

Sushi-grade fish — sea bass, tuna, salmon, scallops or prawns — is sliced or diced shortly before serving, dressed lightly so its flavour dominates. Regional coastal traditions in Puglia, Sicily and Liguria include eating freshly caught seafood raw, pesce crudo.

Related dishes

Comparable preparations include French-style tartare, Hawaiian poke, Peruvian tiradito, and carpaccio, originally of raw beef. Food-safety guidance stresses freshness, cold chain and parasite-control freezing.
