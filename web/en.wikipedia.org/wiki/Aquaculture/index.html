Aquaculture

Marine fish farming, such as these salmon cages, is a major branch of aquaculture.

Aquaculture (less commonly spelled aquiculture), also known as aquafarming, is the controlled cultivation ("farming") of aquatic organisms such as fish, crustaceans, mollusks, algae and other organisms of value. It involves cultivating freshwater, brackish water and saltwater populations under controlled or semi-natural conditions, and can be contrasted with commercial fishing, which is the harvesting of wild fish.

Scale and species

Aquaculture is the fastest-growing food production sector. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization, world aquaculture production exceeded 90 million tonnes of aquatic animals in the early 2020s, and farmed production surpassed capture fisheries as the main source of aquatic animals for human consumption for the first time in 2022. China is by far the largest producer. Major farmed species include carp, tilapia, Atlantic salmon, shrimp and prawns, oysters, mussels and scallops; seaweed farming accounts for a large additional tonnage of aquatic plants.

Methods

Systems range from extensive ponds — such as the carp ponds of Central Europe and the rice-fish systems of Asia — to intensive operations: cages and net pens in lakes and coastal waters, raceways, and land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) that filter and reuse water. Mariculture refers to cultivation in the marine environment. Integrated multi-trophic aquaculture combines species at different trophic levels, so that the waste of fed species becomes food for extractive species such as shellfish and seaweed.

Issues

Criticisms concern the use of wild-caught fish in feed (fishmeal and fish oil), disease and parasite transfer to wild stocks (notably sea lice on salmon), escapes of farmed fish, antibiotic use, and habitat damage, such as the clearing of mangroves for shrimp ponds. Certification schemes such as the Aquaculture Stewardship Council aim to set environmental and social standards. Selective breeding, alternative feeds from insects and algae, and offshore farming are active areas of development.
