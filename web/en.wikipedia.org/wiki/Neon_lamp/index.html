Neon lamp

A neon lamp (also neon glow lamp) is a miniature gas-discharge lamp. The lamp typically consists of a small glass capsule containing a mixture of neon and other gases at low pressure, with two electrodes. When sufficient voltage is applied, the gas ionises and produces a glow discharge, emitting the orange-red light characteristic of neon. Small neon lamps such as the NE-2 were widely used as indicator lamps in electronic equipment and as components in relaxation oscillators; larger, elaborately shaped neon tubes are used in advertising signage — see neon sign.

History

Neon discharge was first observed in Geissler tubes in the 19th century. After William Ramsay's isolation of neon in 1898, Georges Muse developed commercial neon lamps and demonstrated them in Paris in 1910, founding the modern neon-sign industry. Miniature glow lamps for electrical use followed in the early decades of the 20th century.

Operation

A neon lamp has a striking (breakdown) voltage of roughly 65–90 volts, depending on gas mix and geometry. Once struck, the voltage across the lamp falls to a lower maintaining voltage (around 55–65 V for an NE-2), so a series resistor is required to limit current. Below the striking voltage the lamp draws essentially no current, which made it useful for detecting the presence of mains voltage. With a capacitor and resistor, the lamp's negative-resistance characteristic can form a simple relaxation oscillator that produces flashes or sawtooth waveforms; arrays of glow lamps were also used in early logic and counting circuits and in the Nixie tubes used as numeric displays.

Applications

Neon glow lamps served as power indicators in appliances, test probes for electricians, and voltage regulators (in special voltage-reference types). Their low cost and direct operation from mains voltage kept them common through the late 20th century, though LEDs have largely replaced them.
