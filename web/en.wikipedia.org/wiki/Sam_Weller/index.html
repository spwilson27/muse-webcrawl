Sam Weller

Created by: Charles Dickens

First appearance: The Pickwick Papers (1836)

Occupation: Boots at an inn; later valet and servant to Mr Pickwick

Family: Tony Weller (father)

Sam Weller is a fictional character in The Pickwick Papers (1836), the first novel by Charles Dickens. He is the cockney servant and devoted companion of the novel's protagonist, Samuel Pickwick, and is famed for his wit, loyalty and characteristic "Wellerisms".

Role in the novel

Sam is first encountered as the "boots" — the odd-job man and shoe-cleaner — at the White Hart Inn in the Borough, London. Pickwick engages him as his personal servant, and Sam becomes his master's protector and comic interpreter of the world, guiding him through scrapes, lawsuits and travels across England.

Sam's father, Tony Weller, is a coachman whose warnings against marriage — especially to widows — form a running joke. Sam himself eventually marries Mary, Mrs Winkle's servant, with Mr Pickwick's blessing.

Wellerisms

Sam's speech is studded with aphoristic comparisons known as Wellerisms, typically of the form "…as the X said when…": for example, "Business first, pleasure afterwards, as King Richard the Third said when he stabbed the other king in the Tower." His habit of reversing the spelling of v and w ("wery", "ven") marked his cockney dialect in print.

Reception

Sam Weller's introduction in the novel's fourth number transformed the sales of The Pickwick Papers, turning a modest success into a publishing phenomenon. He remains one of Dickens's most popular comic creations, and his name entered popular culture through songs, stage adaptations and merchandise of the period.
