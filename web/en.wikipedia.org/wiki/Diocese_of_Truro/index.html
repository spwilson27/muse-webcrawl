The Diocese of Truro is a Church of England diocese in the Province of Canterbury covering Cornwall, the Isles of Scilly and a small part of Devon (two parishes across the Tamar). It was created on 15 December 1876 from the Cornish archdeaconry of the Diocese of Exeter, restoring a Cornish bishopric a thousand years after the early medieval see at St Germans was absorbed into Crediton and then Exeter.

History

The first bishop, Edward White Benson (1877–1883), later Archbishop of Canterbury, drove the building of Truro Cathedral, the first entirely new Anglican cathedral in England since the Reformation: designed by John Loughborough Pearson in Gothic Revival style, it incorporated the surviving south aisle of the old parish church of St Mary and was completed in 1910. Benson also devised the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, first celebrated at Truro in 1880. The diocese is served by the Bishop of Truro and the suffragan Bishop of St Germans; its parishes include the historic deaneries of Penwith, Kerrier, Carnmarth, Powder, Pydar, East, West and Trigg.

Cornish identity

The diocese has a distinctive Cornish character, with the Cornish language used occasionally in services and diocesan materials, and a long tradition of Celtic Christianity drawn from the Cornish saints. Recent bishops include Tim Thornton (2008–2017) and Philip Mounstephen (2018–2023), the latter leaving to become Bishop of Winchester.
