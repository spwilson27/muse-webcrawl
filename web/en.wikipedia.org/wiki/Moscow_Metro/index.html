Moscow Metro

The Moscow Metro (Russian: Московский метрополитен) is a rapid transit system serving Moscow, Russia, and neighbouring towns in Moscow Oblast. Opened in 1935 with one 11-kilometre line and 13 stations, it was the first underground railway system in the Soviet Union. The system now extends to more than 300 stations and over 500 km of track (including the Moscow Central Circle and monorail), making it one of the largest metro networks in the world, and it carries several million passengers daily.

History

The first line, the Sokolnicheskaya line between Sokolniki and Park Kultury, opened on 15 May 1935. Construction under Lazar Kaganovich emphasised grandeur: early stations were conceived as "palaces of the people", decorated with marble, mosaics, chandeliers and statuary — famous examples include Mayakovskaya, Kropotkinskaya and Ploshchad Revolyutsii, whose bronze figures commuters rub for luck. During the Second World War, stations served as air-raid shelters and command posts.

Network

The traditional network consists of numbered lines radiating from the centre, intersected by the Koltsevaya (Circle) line (Line 5), completed in 1954. Since 2016 the Moscow Central Circle (Line 14) and, from 2023, the Big Circle Line (Line 11) — at 70 km the longest circle line in the world — have greatly expanded capacity. Trains run at intervals as short as 90 seconds at peak times.

Architecture and culture

The Metro's Stalin-era stations are renowned works of socialist realism and art deco design, and newer stations continue a tradition of elaborate decoration. The system is a major symbol of Moscow and a popular subject of literature and tourism.
