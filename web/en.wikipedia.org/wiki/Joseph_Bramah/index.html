Joseph Bramah


Joseph Bramah
Born: 13 April 1748, Barnsley, Yorkshire, England
Died: 9 December 1814 (aged 66), London
Occupation: Engineer, inventor, locksmith
Known for: Bramah lock, hydraulic press, beer pump

Joseph Bramah (13 April 1748 – 9 December 1814) was an English inventor and locksmith. He is best known for having improved the flush toilet and for inventing the Bramah lock and, with William George Armstrong often credited later, pioneering work on the hydraulic press.

Life and work

Born in Barnsley, Bramah was apprenticed as a carpenter and cabinet maker before moving to London, where he set up a locksmith business. In 1784 he patented the Bramah lock, a tubular pin tumbler design so secure that the company offered a reward to anyone who could pick it; the "Challenge Lock" displayed in their Piccadilly shop window remained unpicked for 67 years, until Alfred Charles Hobbs opened it in 1851 after 51 hours of work spread over 16 days.

Inventions

Bramah's many patents included a siphon flush toilet valve, a beer engine for dispensing draught beer, a machine for numbering banknotes, a planing machine, and in 1795 the hydraulic press, which became fundamental to heavy industry. His workshop employed Henry Maudslay, whose screw-cutting lathe and standardised machine tools helped launch precision engineering.

Legacy

The company he founded in 1784 continued making locks and pumps for two centuries. Bramah died in London in 1814 after contracting pneumonia while supervising experiments in Norfolk.
