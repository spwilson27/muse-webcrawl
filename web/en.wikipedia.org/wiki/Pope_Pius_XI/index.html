Pope Pius XI

Born: Ambrogio Damiano Achille Ratti, 31 May 1857, Desio
Died: 10 February 1939, Vatican City
Papacy: 6 Feb 1922 – 10 Feb 1939
Predecessor: Benedict XV
Successor: Pius XII

Pope Pius XI (Italian: Pio XI), born Ambrogio Damiano Achille Ratti (31 May 1857 – 10 February 1939), was head of the Catholic Church from 1922 to 1939. He was the first sovereign of Vatican City State after its creation in 1929.

Early career

A scholar and librarian of the Ambrosian Library and Vatican Library, he served as nuncio to Poland and titular archbishop before being made Archbishop of Milan and cardinal in 1921.

Papacy

He signed the Lateran Treaty (1929) with Mussolini, resolving the Roman Question and creating Vatican City. He issued Quadragesimo anno (1931) on social justice, Mit brennender Sorge (1937) condemning Nazism, and Divini Redemptoris against communism. He founded Vatican Radio and promoted Catholic Action and missions.

He canonised many saints including Therese of Lisieux, Bernadette Soubirous, and Thomas More.

Death

He died in February 1939, months before World War II.
