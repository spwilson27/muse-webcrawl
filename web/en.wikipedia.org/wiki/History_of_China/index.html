History of China

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Great Wall, built and rebuilt by successive dynasties.

The history of China spans several millennia across a wide expanse of East Asia. Chinese civilisation originated in the Yellow River valley, and its recorded history is conventionally organised by dynasties, from the legendary Xia through the Shang and Zhou to the imperial system founded by Qin Shi Huang in 221 BCE and lasting until 1912, followed by the Republic of China and, from 1949, the People's Republic of China on the mainland.

Ancient China and the early dynasties

Neolithic cultures such as Yangshao and Longshan preceded the Shang dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BCE), source of the earliest Chinese writing on oracle bones. The Zhou dynasty (1046–256 BCE) developed the Mandate of Heaven doctrine and saw the flourishing of Confucianism, Daoism and Legalism in the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods.

Imperial China

Qin Shi Huang unified China in 221 BCE, standardising script, currency and measures. The Han dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE) consolidated the bureaucratic empire and opened the Silk Road. After centuries of division, the Sui and Tang dynasties reunified the country; Tang China (618–907) was a cosmopolitan golden age. The Song dynasty (960–1279) saw commercial revolution, printing and early gunpowder weapons, before Mongol conquest created the Yuan dynasty (1271–1368). The Ming (1368–1644) built the Forbidden City and sent Zheng He's treasure fleets overseas. The Qing dynasty (1644–1912), ruled by the Manchu Aisin Gioro clan, expanded China to its greatest territorial extent but weakened under internal rebellion and foreign pressure in the 19th century.

Republic and People's Republic

The 1911 Revolution ended the monarchy; the Republic of China struggled with warlordism, Japanese invasion (1937–1945) and civil war, which the Communists under Mao Zedong won in 1949. The PRC launched the Great Leap Forward (1958–1962) and the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976), then under Deng Xiaoping began market reforms in 1978 that produced decades of rapid growth, making China the world's second-largest economy.
