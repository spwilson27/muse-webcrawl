Multimorbidity

Multimorbidity is the coexistence of two or more long-term (chronic) health conditions in a single individual. Unlike comorbidity, which is defined in relation to an index disease, multimorbidity treats all conditions together without privileging one as primary.

Epidemiology

Multimorbidity becomes more common with age and is strongly associated with socioeconomic deprivation, in which it can occur 10–15 years earlier than in affluent groups. Common clusters involve cardiometabolic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease, often combined with musculoskeletal pain, depression, or chronic lung disease.

Clinical challenges

People with multimorbidity face polypharmacy, with risks of drug interactions and adverse effects; fragmented care among multiple specialists; and treatment burdens from numerous appointments and self-management demands. Clinical guidelines written for single diseases may conflict when applied together, so care increasingly emphasises prioritising the patient's goals and reviewing medications regularly.

Impact

Multimorbidity is associated with reduced quality of life, disability, higher healthcare use, and increased mortality, making it a central concern for health systems serving ageing populations.
