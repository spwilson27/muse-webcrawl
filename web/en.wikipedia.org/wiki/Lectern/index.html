Lectern

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lectern is a reading desk with a slanted top, on which documents or books are placed as support for reading aloud, as in a scripture reading, lecture, or sermon. Lecterns are usually made of wood and may be either free-standing or attached to a wall. The word derives from the Latin lectus, the past participle of legere, "to read".

Types

In churches, the lectern holds the Bible or lectionary from which lessons are read; traditional Anglican and Catholic lecterns are often fashioned as an eagle with outstretched wings, symbolising John the Evangelist and the spread of the Gospel. A lectern differs from a pulpit, which is used for preaching, and from an ambo, a term used in early and Eastern churches for a raised platform. Modern lecterns in lecture theatres and conference rooms may incorporate microphones, lights, and controls for audiovisual equipment, and are commonly (if loosely) called podiums.

Academic use

The image of a scholar standing at a high desk gave rise to the word lecture. Adjustable and table-top lecterns are also used by musicians, speakers, and people who work standing.
