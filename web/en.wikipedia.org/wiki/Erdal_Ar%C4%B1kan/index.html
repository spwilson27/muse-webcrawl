Erdal Arıkan (born 1958) is a Turkish professor of electrical and electronics engineering at Bilkent University in Ankara. He is best known as the inventor of polar codes, the first family of error-correcting codes proved to achieve the Shannon capacity of symmetric binary-input channels with low-complexity encoding and decoding.

Career

Arıkan received his B.S. degree from the California Institute of Technology in 1981, and his M.S. and Ph.D. degrees from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1982 and 1985. After a period at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, he returned to Turkey and joined Bilkent University.

Polar codes

In a landmark 2009 paper, Arıkan introduced channel polarization, a phenomenon in which copies of a channel split into effectively noiseless and completely noisy sub-channels as the block length grows. Codes constructed from this effect, called polar codes, were adopted in the control channels of the 5G New Radio standard by the 3GPP in 2016, bringing Arıkan wide international recognition.

Honours

He is an IEEE Fellow and received the 2018 IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal for his work on polar coding.
