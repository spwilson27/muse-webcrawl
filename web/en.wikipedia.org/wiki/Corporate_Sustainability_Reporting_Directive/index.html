Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD, Directive (EU) 2022/2464) is a European Union law that requires large companies and listed companies to disclose information on their environmental and social impacts, and on how sustainability issues affect their business. It substantially expands and replaces the earlier Non-Financial Reporting Directive.

Directive

Citation: Directive (EU) 2022/2464

Adopted: 14 December 2022

In force: 5 January 2023

Replaces: Non-Financial Reporting Directive

Requirements

Companies in scope must report in their management reports according to the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) developed by EFRAG, applying the principle of "double materiality": they must describe both the company's effects on people and the environment and the financial risks and opportunities sustainability issues create for the company. Reports are subject to limited assurance by an auditor or independent assurance provider, and must be published in a machine-readable electronic format.

Scope and timetable

The directive was estimated to cover around 50,000 companies, including large EU undertakings and listed companies, with obligations phasing in from financial year 2024 for companies already under the old directive. Subsequent "Omnibus" proposals in 2025 sought to delay and narrow some obligations.
