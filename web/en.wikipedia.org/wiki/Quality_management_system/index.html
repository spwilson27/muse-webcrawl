Quality management system

A quality management system (QMS) is a collection of business processes and procedures focused on consistently meeting customer requirements and enhancing their satisfaction. It is aligned with an organisation's purpose and strategic direction and is typically documented in a quality manual, procedures, work instructions and records.

Principles

Modern quality management rests on principles articulated by figures such as W. Edwards Deming, Joseph Juran and Kaoru Ishikawa, and codified in the ISO 9000 family of standards: customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, a process approach, continual improvement, evidence-based decision making and relationship management. The Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle is its characteristic method of improvement.

Standards and certification

ISO 9001 is the world's most widely used QMS standard, with over a million certified organisations; sector-specific variants include IATF 16949 for the automotive industry, AS9100 for aerospace and ISO 13485 for medical devices. Certification by an accredited third-party auditor is voluntary but often required by customers.
