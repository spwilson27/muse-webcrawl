Media of Greece

The media of Greece refers to mass media outlets based in Greece. Television broadcasting began in 1966, operated by the state broadcaster ERT, and private television was introduced in 1989. Greek media are characterized by a historically close relationship between media owners, political parties, and business interests, a feature often described by the Greek term diaplokí ("entanglement").

Television and radio

ERT, the public broadcaster, was abruptly closed by the government in June 2013 during the debt crisis and reopened in June 2015. Major private channels include Mega, ANT1, Alpha, Star, Skai, and Open TV. Radio is dominated by music and news/talk stations in Athens, with ERT and private networks broadcasting nationally.

Press

Historically influential newspapers include Kathimerini, Ta Nea, To Vima, and Eleftherotypia (closed in 2011). Newspaper circulation has declined sharply with the financial crisis and the shift to online news, and Greece has ranked poorly in recent years in the World Press Freedom Index, prompting debate about media independence and state advertising.
