The Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum in Nashville, Tennessee, honours country music's most significant figures and preserves the genre's history. Inductees are elected by the Country Music Association; the museum, operated by the Country Music Foundation, is one of the world's largest music research centres.

History

The Hall of Fame was created in 1961; its first inductees were Jimmie Rodgers, Fred Rose and Hank Williams. The museum first opened on Music Row in 1967 and moved in 2001 to a large purpose-built downtown building whose design echoes musical forms — its windows resemble piano keys and its rotunda recalls a drum kit. The rotunda displays the bronze plaques of all members.

Induction

New members are announced annually and formally inducted at the Medallion Ceremony. Categories include a rotating "modern era" and "veterans era" artist, plus non-performer, recording/touring musician, and songwriter spots. Inductees span from the Carter Family and Bill Monroe to Patsy Cline, Johnny Cash, Dolly Parton and Garth Brooks — over 150 members in total.

Museum

Exhibits trace country music from folk roots through honky-tonk, the Nashville sound and contemporary country, displaying instruments (including Maybelle Carter's Gibson guitar), costumes and Elvis Presley's gold Cadillac. The museum also runs Hatch Show Print, the historic RCA Studio B tours and a major archive and oral-history programme. See country music and Grand Ole Opry.
