J'Accuse…!

"J'Accuse…!" (French: "I accuse…!") is an open letter by the novelist Émile Zola, published on the front page of the Paris newspaper L'Aurore on 13 January 1898. Addressed to President Félix Faure, it accused the French military and government of wrongfully convicting Captain Alfred Dreyfus of treason and of covering up the real culprit — the most famous intervention in the Dreyfus affair.

Content

Written after the acquittal of the actual traitor Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy, the letter named officers accused of forging evidence, framing Dreyfus (a Jewish artillery captain), and obstructing justice — culminating in a litany of "J'accuse" statements. Zola deliberately provoked a libel trial so the evidence could be aired in open court.

Impact

The letter galvanized the Dreyfusard cause, split French society, and sold some 300,000 copies of the paper's issue. Zola was convicted of criminal libel in February 1898 and fled to England; he returned in 1899. Dreyfus was retried, pardoned, and finally fully exonerated in 1906. "J'accuse" has since become a generic term for a bold public denunciation of injustice.
