Pertuzumab

Pertuzumab, sold under the brand name Perjeta, is a monoclonal antibody used in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy to treat HER2-positive breast cancer. It was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2012. Developed by Genentech (Roche), it was the first agent in its class known as HER dimerization inhibitors.

Mechanism

Pertuzumab binds to domain II of the extracellular portion of HER2, blocking its dimerization with other HER family receptors, particularly HER3, and thereby inhibiting downstream signaling. Because it binds a different epitope from trastuzumab, the two antibodies are complementary.

Clinical use

The CLEOPATRA trial showed that adding pertuzumab to trastuzumab and docetaxel significantly prolonged progression-free and overall survival in metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer. It is also approved in neoadjuvant and adjuvant settings (APHINITY trial). A fixed-dose subcutaneous combination with trastuzumab and hyaluronidase (Phesgo) is also available. Common adverse effects include diarrhea, neutropenia, fatigue, and infusion reactions; cardiac monitoring is required because of the risk of left-ventricular dysfunction.
