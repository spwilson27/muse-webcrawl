Blockhouse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A blockhouse is a small fortification, usually consisting of one or more rooms with loopholes, designed to be defended by a small garrison against a numerically superior enemy. The term is derived from structures built to block access routes such as roads, mountain passes and frontiers.

History

Wooden blockhouses were common on the North American frontier from the 17th to the 19th century; their overhanging upper storeys let defenders fire down on attackers at the walls. During the Second Boer War the British built some 8,000 stone and concrete blockhouses linked by barbed wire to guard railways and restrict Boer commando movement. In the Second World War, massive concrete blockhouses protected German U-boat pens and V-weapon launch sites.

Modern uses

The word is also used for the fortified control buildings at rocket launch complexes, sited close to the launch pad, and for hardened shelters generally. See also Fortification and Bunker.
