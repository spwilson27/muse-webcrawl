Llywelyn ab Iorwerth

Reign: 1195 – 11 April 1240

Title: Prince of Aberffraw, Lord of Snowdon

Spouse: Joan, Lady of Wales

Llywelyn ab Iorwerth (c. 1173 – 11 April 1240), also known as Llywelyn the Great (Welsh: Llywelyn Fawr), was a King of Gwynedd in north Wales and eventually ruler of all Wales. By a combination of war and diplomacy he dominated Wales for 45 years and came closer than any previous ruler to unifying the country under a single prince.

During Llywelyn's youth, Gwynedd was ruled by two of his uncles, who split the kingdom between them. Llywelyn began a campaign to win control in 1194 and by 1200 had taken sole control of Gwynedd. He made a treaty with King John of England the following year and married John's illegitimate daughter Joan in 1205. Relations with John deteriorated in 1211, when John invaded Gwynedd, but Llywelyn recovered his lands by allying with the other Welsh princes and exploiting the baronial revolt that produced Magna Carta.

Consolidation of power

At the Council of Aberdyfi in 1216, the other Welsh princes recognised Llywelyn's leadership and divided conquered lands under his arbitration. In 1218 he concluded the Treaty of Worcester with the new king, Henry III, which confirmed him in possession of the lands he held. For the remainder of his reign he fought a series of campaigns against the Marcher Lords and occasional English invasions, while carefully maintaining his position as the dominant prince in Wales.

Succession and legacy

Llywelyn worked to secure the succession of his legitimate son Dafydd, obtaining recognition from the English crown and the papacy, which displaced the traditional Welsh law of partible inheritance among sons. He died on 11 April 1240 at the Cistercian abbey of Aberconwy, which he had founded. His achievement in uniting Wales, though it did not long survive him, made him one of the most revered figures in Welsh history, remembered as Llywelyn Fawr, "Llywelyn the Great".
