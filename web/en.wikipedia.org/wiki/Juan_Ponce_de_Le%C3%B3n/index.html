Juan Ponce de León

Bornc. 1474, Santervás de Campos, Castile
DiedJuly 1521, Havana, Cuba
OccupationExplorer, conquistador, governor of Puerto Rico

Juan Ponce de León (c. 1474–1521) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador known for leading the first official European expedition to Florida. He served as the first governor of Puerto Rico and is popularly associated with the legend of the Fountain of Youth.

Early career

Ponce de León sailed to the Americas with Christopher Columbus's second voyage in 1493 and took part in the conquest of Hispaniola. He later explored and colonized Puerto Rico, founding the settlement of Caparra in 1508 and serving as its governor.

Expedition to Florida

In 1513 he led an expedition north from Puerto Rico and landed on the east coast of a land he named La Florida, because of its lush vegetation and because it was discovered during the Easter season (Pascua Florida). During the voyage his ships encountered the powerful Gulf Stream current.

Death

In 1521 Ponce de León returned to Florida with settlers to establish a colony, but the expedition was attacked by Calusa warriors and he was wounded, reportedly by a poisoned arrow. He died of his wounds in Havana, Cuba.

Fountain of Youth legend

Later chroniclers linked Ponce de León to a search for a miraculous Fountain of Youth, though historians consider the story largely a posthumous legend with little basis in his documented expeditions.
