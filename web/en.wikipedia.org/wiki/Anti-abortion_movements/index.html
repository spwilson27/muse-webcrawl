Anti-abortion movements

Anti-abortion movements, also self-described as pro-life movements, are involved in the abortion debate, advocating against the practice of abortion and its legality. Many anti-abortion movements began as countermovements in response to the legalization of elective abortions. Participants typically hold that human embryos and fetuses have a right to life, and that abortion is morally wrong.

History

Organized opposition to abortion dates to nineteenth-century campaigns by physicians and religious bodies that led to abortion restrictions in many Western countries. Modern movements took shape in the 1960s and 1970s as jurisdictions liberalized abortion laws: the United States saw the founding of the National Right to Life Committee (1968) and the annual March for Life following Roe v. Wade (1973), which remained a central mobilizing event until the decision was overturned by Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization in 2022.

Activities and positions

Activities include lobbying, electoral campaigning, public demonstrations, sidewalk counseling outside clinics, and support for crisis pregnancy centers. Positions vary: some adherents oppose abortion in nearly all circumstances, while others accept exceptions for rape, incest, fetal anomalies, or threats to the life of the pregnant woman. A minority of activists have resorted to violence, which mainstream organizations condemn. The Catholic Church and many evangelical and Orthodox bodies are prominent institutional supporters, though the movement also includes secular adherents.
