HMS Belfast

ClassTown-class light cruiser
Launched17 March 1938
Commissioned5 August 1939
Decommissioned24 August 1963
StatusMuseum ship in London

HMS Belfast is a Town-class light cruiser of the Royal Navy, launched in 1938 and now preserved as a museum ship on the River Thames in London. She was the first Royal Navy ship named after the capital of Northern Ireland and one of the largest and most powerful light cruisers of her time.

Second World War

Early in the Second World War Belfast struck a magnetic mine in the Firth of Forth in November 1939, suffering severe damage that kept her under repair for three years. Returning to service in 1942, she escorted Arctic convoys to the Soviet Union and in December 1943 took part in the Battle of the North Cape, in which the German battleship Scharnhorst was sunk. On D-Day, 6 June 1944, she fired some of the first shots of the Normandy bombardment in support of British and Canadian landings, and she later served in the Far East as the war ended.

Later service

Belfast saw action again during the Korean War, conducting shore bombardments between 1950 and 1952. After a modernisation refit she made a final series of overseas commissions before paying off in 1963. Efforts to save her from scrap led by the Imperial War Museum succeeded, and she opened to the public in October 1971, moored in the Pool of London near Tower Bridge, where she remains a branch of the Imperial War Museum.
