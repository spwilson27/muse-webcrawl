Thai solar calendar

The Thai solar calendar (Thai: ปฏิทินสุริยคติ, patithin suriyakhati) is the official civil calendar of Thailand. Adopted by King Vajiravudh (Rama VI) in 1912, it is a version of the Gregorian calendar in which the years are counted in the Buddhist Era (BE), which is 543 years ahead of the Christian/Common Era. A year CE is converted to BE by adding 543; thus 2025 CE is 2568 BE.

History

Thailand traditionally used a lunisolar calendar for religious and agricultural purposes, with years counted from various eras including the Chula Sakarat (Minor Era) beginning in 638 CE. In 1889 King Chulalongkorn (Rama V) introduced the Rattanakosin Era for official documents. King Vajiravudh replaced this with the Buddhist Era in 1912 and aligned the calendar with the solar year. Until 1941 the Thai New Year began on 1 April; Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram moved the start of the official year to 1 January, so the year 2483 BE was shortened to nine months.

Month names ending in -khom have 31 days, those ending in -yon have 30 days, and February (-phan) is the exception. Leap years follow the Gregorian rule.

Usage

Buddhist Era dates appear on Thai government documents, newspapers, coinage and official signage. The traditional New Year festival, Songkran (13–15 April), remains the major public holiday even though the official year now begins on 1 January. A separate Thai lunar calendar continues to govern Buddhist holy days and festivals.
