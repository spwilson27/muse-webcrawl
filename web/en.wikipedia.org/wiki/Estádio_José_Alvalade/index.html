Estádio José Alvalade

Estádio José Alvalade is a football stadium in Lisbon, Portugal, home of Sporting Clube de Portugal. Opened on 6 August 2003, it was built for UEFA Euro 2004 and replaced the earlier stadium of the same name that stood nearby. The stadium is named after José Alvalade, the founder of Sporting CP.

History

The stadium was inaugurated on 6 August 2003 with a friendly match in which Sporting defeated Manchester United 3–1, a game notable for the performance of young winger Cristiano Ronaldo, who was signed by United shortly afterwards. During Euro 2004 the stadium hosted five matches, including a semi-final.

Design

Designed by architect Tomás Taveira, the stadium is recognizable for its colorful tiled exterior and mosaic patterns. It is part of the Alvalade XXI complex, which includes a shopping centre, cinema, club museum, and other facilities. In 2005 it hosted the UEFA Cup Final, in which CSKA Moscow defeated Sporting 3–1.
