Isaias Afwerki (born 2 February 1946) is an Eritrean politician who has been the president of Eritrea since its independence in 1993. He led the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) to victory in the Eritrean War of Independence in 1991 and remains the country's sole head of state, presiding over a one-party government in which national elections have never been held.

Path to power

Born in Asmara, Isaias studied engineering briefly in Addis Ababa before joining the Eritrean Liberation Front in 1966 and receiving military training in China. He co-founded the EPLF, which by the late 1970s eclipsed its rivals, and directed the long guerrilla war against Ethiopia's Derg regime. In May 1991 EPLF forces liberated Asmara; a 1993 referendum confirmed independence, and Isaias became president.

Presidency

The 1998–2000 border war with Ethiopia devastated both countries and was followed in 2001 by a crackdown: dissident officials (the "G-15") and independent journalists were arrested and held incommunicado, private newspapers were closed, and the ratified 1997 constitution was never implemented. Indefinite national service conscription, extended far beyond its statutory 18 months, has driven large numbers of Eritreans to emigrate. A United Nations commission of inquiry reported in 2015–2016 that crimes against humanity had been committed in Eritrea since 1991; the government rejected the findings. In 2018 Isaias signed a peace declaration with Ethiopia's prime minister Abiy Ahmed, ending two decades of no-war-no-peace, though Eritrean forces later took part in the Tigray War (2020–2022) alongside Ethiopia's federal government.
