List of chess grandmasters

The title of Grandmaster is awarded to world-class chess players by FIDE, the international chess federation. FIDE first awarded the title in 1950 to 27 players; since then well over 1,500 players have earned it, including a growing number who achieved it as teenagers. This article provides a summary and links to lists of grandmasters by country.

Requirements

Under current FIDE rules, a player generally must achieve a published rating of at least 2500 and earn three grandmaster "norms" — performances at a 2600+ level in qualifying tournaments — although winners of certain events (such as the World Junior Championship or the Women's World Championship) may qualify directly.

History

The 1950 inaugural class included the world's leading players such as Mikhail Botvinnik, Max Euwe, and Samuel Reshevsky. Bobby Fischer became, at 15 in 1958, the youngest grandmaster of his era; the record for youngest ever has since fallen repeatedly, with Abhimanyu Mishra earning the title at 12 years and 4 months in 2021. Judit Polgár became the first woman to qualify for the open grandmaster title by norms in 1991; Nona Gaprindashvili was the first woman awarded it, in 1978.

Numbers by country

Russia and its predecessor the Soviet Union long produced the most grandmasters; the United States, India, China, and Ukraine also count large numbers. India's total has grown rapidly since Viswanathan Anand became its first grandmaster in 1988.
