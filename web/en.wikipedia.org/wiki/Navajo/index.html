Navajo

The Navajo (Navajo: Diné) are a Native American people of the Southwestern United States, the second-most populous federally recognised tribe in the US, with over 399,000 enrolled members. The Navajo Nation, in the Four Corners region of Arizona, New Mexico and Utah, is the largest reservation in the United States, covering about 27,000 square miles.

History and culture

Navajo language is a Southern Athabaskan language. The people are known for weaving, silversmithing, and the Navajo Code Talkers of World War II. In 1864 many Navajo were forced on the Long Walk to Bosque Redondo; a treaty in 1868 allowed their return. The Navajo Nation government is based in Window Rock, Arizona.
