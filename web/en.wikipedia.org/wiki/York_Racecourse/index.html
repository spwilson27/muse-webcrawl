York Racecourse

LocationKnavesmire, York, England
Owned byYork Racecourse Knavesmire LLP / City of York Council (land)
Date opened1731 (racing on the Knavesmire)
Notable racesJuddmonte International, Yorkshire Oaks, Nunthorpe Stakes, Ebor Handicap

York Racecourse is a horse racing venue in York, England, situated on the Knavesmire south-west of the city centre. It is one of Britain's premier flat-racing tracks and hosts the four-day Ebor Festival each August.

History

Racing on the Knavesmire dates to 1731, on land long used for public gatherings and, earlier, executions — the highwayman Dick Turpin was hanged nearby in 1739. The course developed through the 18th and 19th centuries; the Ebor Handicap was first run in 1843.

Meetings and major races

The season runs from May to October and includes the Dante Festival, the John Smith's Cup meeting, and the Ebor Festival featuring the Juddmonte International Stakes, the Yorkshire Oaks, the Nunthorpe Stakes, and the Ebor Handicap, among Europe's richest handicap races. Royal Ascot was staged at York in 2005 while Ascot was redeveloped.

The course

The track is a flat, left-handed, roughly U-shaped course about two miles in extent, regarded as a fair, galloping test suited to strong-travelling horses.
