"Disco 2000" is a song by the British band Pulp, released as the third single from their 1995 album Different Class. Written by Jarvis Cocker and the band, it is one of Pulp's best-known songs and a staple of the Britpop era.

Content

The lyric, built around a chugging guitar riff reminiscent of Laura Branigan's "Gloria", tells of the narrator's childhood infatuation with a girl named Deborah, whose family moved away; the two agree to meet again as adults at a fountain "down the road" in the year 2000. The song was inspired by a real Deborah — Deborah Bone, a childhood friend of Cocker in Sheffield — whose story attracted press attention when the song became a hit.

Reception

The single reached number 7 on the UK Singles Chart. Its video and the song's narrative of class, memory and suburban romance fit the themes of Different Class, the Mercury Prize-winning album that made Pulp one of the biggest British bands of the mid-1990s.
