Alogia

Alogia (from Greek a-, "without", and logos, "speech") is poverty of speech: a general lack of additional, unprompted content seen in normal speech. It is considered a negative symptom of schizophrenia and may also appear in major depression, autism, and other conditions.

Characteristics

People with alogia give brief, concrete replies to questions, speak spontaneously less than expected, and show a diminished amount of speech whose content is impoverished. Alogia can make conversation difficult and is associated with poor functional outcomes.

Causes and assessment

Alogia is thought to reflect dysfunction in language-production and thought processes, possibly involving the frontal lobes. Clinicians assess it as part of scales such as the Scale for the Assessment of Negative Symptoms (SANS).

Distinction

Alogia differs from aphasia, which is a language disorder caused by brain damage affecting the ability to produce or comprehend language; alogia is a reduction in speech output rather than a loss of language ability itself.
