Constitution of Brazil

The Constitution of the Federative Republic of Brazil (Portuguese: Constituição da República Federativa do Brasil) is the supreme law of Brazil, promulgated on 5 October 1988. It is the country's seventh constitution (the eighth if the 1969 amendment is counted as a separate text) and was drafted by a National Constituent Assembly elected in 1986, following the end of the military dictatorship that had ruled Brazil since 1964. Because of its extensive catalogue of rights and guarantees, it is often called the "Citizen Constitution" (Constituição Cidadã), a phrase associated with the assembly's president, Ulysses Guimarães.

The 1988 Constitution was promulgated on 5 October 1988, restoring and entrenching democratic rule in Brazil.

History

Brazil's first constitution was granted by Emperor Pedro I in 1824, after independence from Portugal. The republic proclaimed in 1889 adopted a federal, presidential constitution in 1891 modelled partly on that of the United States. Further constitutions followed in 1934, 1937 (under Getúlio Vargas's Estado Novo), 1946 (after redemocratisation), and 1967 under the military regime, heavily amended in 1969. The movement for direct presidential elections (Diretas Já) and the transition to civilian rule in 1985 led to the convocation of the Constituent Assembly, which sat from February 1987 and drew on extensive public input, including popular amendments signed by millions of citizens.

Structure

The Constitution contains a preamble, 250 articles organised into nine titles, and a set of transitory constitutional provisions. The titles cover fundamental principles; fundamental rights and guarantees; the organisation of the state; the organisation of the branches of government (legislative, executive and judiciary); defence of the state and democratic institutions; taxation and the budget; the economic and financial order; the social order; and general provisions.

Fundamental rights

Article 5 sets out a long list of individual and collective rights and duties — equality before the law, freedom of expression and religion, inviolability of the home and of correspondence, due process guarantees, and the writs of habeas corpus, mandado de segurança and habeas data. Later articles add social rights to education, health, work, housing, social security and an ecologically balanced environment, along with protections for children, the elderly and indigenous peoples, whose rights over traditionally occupied lands are recognised.

Organisation of the state

The Constitution establishes Brazil as a federal, presidential republic composed of the Union, the states, the Federal District and the municipalities — unusually, municipalities are themselves federative entities. Certain matters are entrenched as "eternal clauses" (cláusulas pétreas) that no amendment may abolish: the federal form of state; the direct, secret, universal and periodic vote; the separation of powers; and individual rights and guarantees.

Amendments

The Constitution has been amended well over one hundred times since 1988. Amendments require approval by three-fifths of the members of each house of the National Congress in two rounds of voting. Notable amendments include the 1997 change permitting re-election of the president, governors and mayors for one consecutive term, and the 2016 spending-cap amendment limiting the growth of federal expenditure.
