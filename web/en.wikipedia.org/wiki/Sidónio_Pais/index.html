Sidónio Bernardino Cardoso da Silva Pais (1 May 1872 – 14 December 1918) was a Portuguese politician, military officer, diplomat and academic who served as President of the Republic in 1918, a position he combined with that of Prime Minister following a military coup in December 1917. Known as the "President-King" (Presidente-Rei), he led the so-called "New Republic" (República Nova), an authoritarian regime that anticipated later conservative movements in Portugal.

Early life and career

Pais was born in Caminha, in northern Portugal. He joined the army and was educated at the Military Academy, later becoming a professor of mathematics at the University of Coimbra, where he also served as vice-rector. A republican from an early age, he was elected to parliament after the proclamation of the Portuguese Republic in 1910 and served briefly as Minister of Public Works and then Minister of Finance. He was afterwards Portugal's minister plenipotentiary (ambassador) in Berlin until Portugal's entry into the First World War in 1916.

Coup and government

On 5 December 1917, Pais led a military revolt against the government of Afonso Costa, whose Democratic Party dominated Portuguese politics and whose management of the war effort had become deeply unpopular. After three days of fighting in Lisbon, the coup succeeded. Pais dissolved Congress, ruled by decree, and in 1918 promulgated a new constitutional law that strengthened presidential powers. In April 1918 he was elected President of the Republic by direct popular vote — the only president of the First Portuguese Republic chosen in this way.

His regime combined populist and authoritarian features: it courted the Catholic Church, restored relations with the Vatican, modified the law separating church and state, and sought support among monarchists and conservative republicans, founding the National Republican Party as its political vehicle. At the same time, it harshly repressed strikes and opposition movements.

Assassination

Pais survived an assassination attempt in December 1918 but was shot dead on 14 December 1918 at Lisbon's Rossio railway station by José Júlio da Costa, a left-wing activist. His death plunged the country into renewed instability; within weeks a monarchist uprising in the north proclaimed the short-lived Monarchy of the North.

Legacy

Pais is remembered as a charismatic and controversial figure whose personalist regime marked a turning point in the First Republic, and whose cult of personality foreshadowed aspects of the later Estado Novo.
