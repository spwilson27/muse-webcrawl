COVID-19 vaccine

A COVID-19 vaccine is a vaccine intended to provide acquired immunity against severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the virus that causes coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19). The first vaccines were authorized for emergency use in December 2020, less than a year after the viral genome was published — an unprecedented speed made possible by prior research on SARS and MERS coronaviruses, mRNA platform technology, and large public investment such as Operation Warp Speed in the United States.

Vaccine typemRNA, viral vector, protein subunit, inactivated virus
TargetSARS-CoV-2 spike protein
First authorizationDecember 2020 (Pfizer–BioNTech, UK)
Doses administeredMore than 13 billion worldwide (by 2024)

Types

  • mRNA vaccines — Pfizer–BioNTech (Comirnaty) and Moderna (Spikevax): lipid nanoparticles deliver mRNA encoding the spike protein.
  • Viral vector vaccines — Oxford–AstraZeneca (Vaxzevria), Johnson & Johnson/Janssen, and Sputnik V: a modified adenovirus carries the spike gene.
  • Inactivated vaccines — Sinopharm (BBIBP-CorV) and Sinovac (CoronaVac), widely used in China and many other countries.
  • Protein subunit vaccines — Novavax (Nuvaxovid), using recombinant spike protein with an adjuvant.

Effectiveness and safety

Clinical trials showed very high efficacy (around 94–95%) for the mRNA vaccines against symptomatic disease from the original strain. Protection against infection wanes over months and was reduced by the Delta and Omicron variants, but protection against hospitalization and death has remained substantial, especially with booster doses updated to match circulating variants. Rare adverse events include anaphylaxis, myocarditis/pericarditis after mRNA vaccines (mainly in young males), and thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome (TTS) after adenoviral vector vaccines, which led several countries to restrict or withdraw the AstraZeneca and J&J vaccines.

Deployment

More than 13 billion doses were administered globally. Coverage was highly unequal: high-income countries achieved high coverage early, while many low-income countries remained below 10–20 percent coverage well into 2022 despite the COVAX sharing initiative. Vaccination is credited with preventing tens of millions of deaths in the first years of the pandemic.
