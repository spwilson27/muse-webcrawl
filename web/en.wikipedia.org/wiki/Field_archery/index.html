Field archery

Field archery is any archery discipline that involves shooting at outdoor targets of varying and often unmarked distances, typically in woodland or rough terrain. It differs from target archery, which is shot on a flat range at known distances with large round targets. Field archery tests the archer's ability to judge distance, shoot uphill and downhill, and adapt to natural light and weather.

Formats

In World Archery field competition, a course of 24 targets is laid out over natural terrain, with 12 targets at marked distances and 12 at unmarked distances ranging from about 5 to 60 metres depending on the bow division. Targets are round with black and yellow scoring zones. In the United States, the National Field Archery Association (NFAA) uses 28-target rounds with animal-shaped and round targets at marked distances. The International Field Archery Association (IFAA) sanctions rounds including field, hunter and animal rounds. 3D archery, shooting at life-sized foam animal replicas, is a closely related format.

Equipment

Archers compete in recurve, compound, barebow, longbow, traditional and instinctive divisions. Field archers often favour lighter tackle for long days on the course, and many use range-estimation techniques rather than rangefinders, which are generally prohibited in competition.

History

Organized field archery grew in the mid-20th century out of bowhunting practice traditions, particularly in the United States with the founding of the NFAA in 1939, and in Britain under the English Field Archery Association. World Archery has held field archery world championships since 1969, and the discipline features in the World Games.
