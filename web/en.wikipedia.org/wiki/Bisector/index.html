Bisector

FieldGeometry
TypesLine segment bisector, angle bisector, perpendicular bisector

A bisector is a line, ray, or segment that divides another geometric figure into two equal parts. The term is most commonly applied to the bisection of a line segment and of an angle, two of the classical constructions of Euclidean geometry.

Line segment bisector

A bisector of a line segment passes through the segment's midpoint, dividing it into two equal lengths. The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the line perpendicular to it through its midpoint; every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the segment's endpoints, and conversely every point equidistant from the two endpoints lies on it. The perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle meet at the circumcenter, the centre of the triangle's circumcircle.

Angle bisector

An angle bisector is a ray from the vertex of an angle that divides it into two congruent angles. Every point on an angle bisector is equidistant from the two sides of the angle. The internal bisectors of a triangle's three angles meet at the incenter, the centre of the incircle. The angle bisector theorem states that the bisector of an angle in a triangle divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides.

Constructions

Both segment and angle bisection are among the constructions achievable with an unmarked straightedge and compass; angle trisection, by contrast, was proved impossible with those tools in general. In analytic geometry, bisectors of pairs of lines can be expressed as loci of points equidistant from the lines.
