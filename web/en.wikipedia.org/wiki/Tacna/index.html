Tacna

Country: Peru

Region: Tacna

Elevation: 552 m

Population: approx. 280,000 (metro)

Tacna is a city in southern Peru and the capital of the Tacna Region. It lies on the Caplina River in an arid coastal valley about 35 km north of the border with Chile, at an elevation of about 552 metres. It is Peru's southernmost major city and a hub of cross-border trade and services with northern Chile.

History

The area was inhabited in pre-Columbian times and incorporated into the Spanish colonial system after the conquest. Tacna was formally founded in the colonial period and grew as an agricultural and commercial centre.

During the War of the Pacific (1879–1884), Chilean forces defeated the Peruvian-Bolivian allies at the Battle of Tacna (Alto de la Alianza) on 26 May 1880, and Chile occupied the province. Under the Treaty of Ancón (1883) Tacna and Arica were to be administered by Chile for ten years pending a plebiscite that was never held. The question embittered relations for decades until the Treaty of Lima (1929) returned Tacna to Peru while Arica remained Chilean. Because of this history, Tacna is celebrated in Peru as the "Ciudad Heroica" (Heroic City).

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Parabolic Arch (Arco Parabólico) in the main plaza, erected in honour of the heroes of the War of the Pacific; the neoclassical Tacna Cathedral; the Alto de la Alianza battlefield memorial south of the city; and the railway museum and the historic Tacna–Arica railway, one of Peru's oldest international rail links.

Economy

Tacna's economy rests on commerce — boosted by the ZOFRATACNA duty-free zone — agriculture in the surrounding valleys (olives, grapes, and oregano are notable products), mining in the wider region, and growing shopping and medical tourism from Chile.
