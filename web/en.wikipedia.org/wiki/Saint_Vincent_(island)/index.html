Saint Vincent (island)

Saint Vincent is a volcanic island in the Caribbean, the largest island of the nation of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. It lies in the Lesser Antilles island arc, between Saint Lucia to the north and Grenada to the south. The island covers about 344 square kilometres (133 sq mi) and has a population of roughly 100,000, centred on the capital, Kingstown.

Geography

Saint Vincent is mountainous and heavily forested. Its highest point is the active volcano La Soufrière (1,234 m / 4,049 ft), whose eruptions — notably in 1902, 1979, and April 2021 — have repeatedly displaced northern communities. The windward (Atlantic) coast is rugged, while the leeward (Caribbean) coast has sandy bays and most of the settlements.

History

The island was inhabited by the Island Caribs (Kalinago), who resisted European colonisation for longer than on most Caribbean islands. France and Britain contested Saint Vincent through the 18th century; it became a British colony in 1783. The Carib leader Joseph Chatoyer was killed during the Second Carib War of 1795–96, after which many Caribs were deported to Roatán. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines became independent on 27 October 1979.

Economy

Agriculture, especially banana production, and tourism are the main economic activities. The island's dramatic scenery served as a principal filming location for Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl (2003).
