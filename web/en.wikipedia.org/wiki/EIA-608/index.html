EIA-608

EIA-608, also known as "line 21 captions" and made widespread by its predecessor the CEA-608 and ANSI/CTA-608 standards, is a standard used in North America for encoding closed captioning data into the vertical blanking interval (VBI) of NTSC television broadcasts — specifically on line 21 of the video signal. The standard was developed by the Electronic Industries Alliance (EIA) and the National Captioning Institute. Its digital successor for ATSC broadcasting is EIA-708.

Data channels

EIA-608 provides up to four caption/text services carried in two fields of line 21. In the NTSC broadcast, field 1 carries data channels CC1 and CC2 (captions) plus text services T1/T2; field 2 carries CC3, CC4, T3, T4, and Extended Data Services (XDS). CC1 is the primary, near-universally used caption channel, with synchronized captions in the broadcast's primary language; CC3 is commonly used for a second language (often Spanish in the United States). XDS carries metadata such as program name, length, rating (content advisory), network, and time-of-day.

Technical details

The data rate of line 21 is 503.5 bit/s (480 bit/s usable), sending two bytes per video field (one byte pair per frame in each field), which limits captions to roughly 32 characters per caption frame and requires caption text to be edited to fit the stream. Characters are encoded in a mostly-ASCII 7-bit set with special characters, accents, and musical-note symbols provided by control codes in a "special/extended character" repertoire. Caption presentation modes include pop-on (complete captions appearing at once — the norm for prerecorded programming), roll-up (scrolling, used for live events), and paint-on.

Regulation and legacy

In the United States, the Television Decoder Circuits Act of 1990 required caption decoders in all larger televisions sold from 1993, and FCC rules mandated captioning of nearly all new programming, making EIA-608 captions ubiquitous. With the digital television transition, EIA-608 streams are preserved as "608-compatible" caption packets embedded within EIA-708 digital caption data, and they are routinely converted to web formats such as WebVTT and SRT in streaming media.
