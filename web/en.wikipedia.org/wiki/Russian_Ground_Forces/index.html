The Russian Ground Forces (Russian: Сухопутные войска) are the land forces of the Russian Federation, formed from elements of the Soviet Army following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991–92. They are the largest and most varied branch of the Russian Armed Forces in personnel terms.

Organisation

The Ground Forces are organised through Russia's military districts, each controlling combined-arms armies composed of motor rifle and tank divisions and brigades, together with artillery, air defence, and support formations. Their principal arms include the Motor Rifle Troops, Tank Troops, Rocket Forces and Artillery, Air Defence Troops, and special troops such as reconnaissance, engineer, and signals units.

History

After 1992 the Ground Forces fought in the First and Second Chechen Wars, underwent reforms under Defence Minister Anatoly Serdyukov after the 2008 Russo-Georgian War (shifting from divisions to brigades), and partly reversed those reforms thereafter. Since 2014 forces have been involved in the war in Donbas and, from February 2022, in the full-scale invasion of Ukraine, where they have sustained very heavy casualties and equipment losses.
