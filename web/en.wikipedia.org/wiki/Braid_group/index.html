Braid group

In mathematics, the braid group on n strands (denoted Bn), also known as the Artin braid group after Emil Artin, is the group whose elements are equivalence classes of n-braids (e.g. under ambient isotopy), and whose group operation is composition of braids. Braid groups were introduced explicitly by Artin in the 1920s–1940s, although implicit work dates to Adolf Hurwitz.

Presentation

Bn has a presentation with generators σ1, …, σn−1 (σi crosses strand i over strand i+1) subject to the braid relations: σiσj = σjσi for |i − j| ≥ 2, and σiσi+1σi = σi+1σiσi+1 (the Yang–Baxter relation). Forgetting crossings gives a homomorphism onto the symmetric group Sn; its kernel is the pure braid group Pn.

Applications

Braid groups are central to knot theory (every knot is the closure of a braid — Alexander's theorem; Markov's theorem characterises when two braids close to the same link). Representations of braid groups underlie the Jones polynomial and its generalisations. They have applications in algebraic geometry (fundamental groups of configuration spaces), mathematical physics (anyons and topological quantum computing), cryptography (braid-group-based protocols), and robotics (motion planning).
