Apollo 13

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Apollo 13's damaged service module, photographed after jettison.

Apollo 13 was the seventh crewed mission in the Apollo program and was intended to be the third Moon landing. Launched on 11 April 1970 with James Lovell, Jack Swigert and Fred Haise aboard, the mission was aborted after an oxygen tank in the service module exploded two days into the flight, crippling the spacecraft.

Swigert's report to Houston — 'Okay, Houston, we've had a problem here' — began one of the most famous rescue efforts in spaceflight history. The crew used the Lunar Module Aquarius as a 'lifeboat', rationing power and water and improvising a carbon-dioxide filter from plastic bags and duct tape, while engineers in Houston worked around the clock on procedures to bring them home. A free-return trajectory swing around the Moon put the crew on course for Earth.

Outcome

Apollo 13 splashed down safely in the South Pacific on 17 April 1970. The mission was classified a 'successful failure' because of the experience gained in rescuing the crew. An investigation traced the explosion to damaged wiring and a thermostat failure in oxygen tank two during pre-flight testing. The story was dramatised in the 1995 film Apollo 13.
