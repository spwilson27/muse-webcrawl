Gertrude Stein

Gertrude Stein (February 3, 1874 – July 27, 1946) was an American novelist, poet, playwright, and art collector. Born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, she moved to Paris in 1903, where the salon she hosted with her brother Leo Stein, and later with her lifelong partner Alice B. Toklas, became a centre of the modernist avant-garde.

Paris salon

At 27 rue de Fleurus, Stein and her brother amassed one of the first major collections of modern art, buying works by Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse, and others. Picasso painted her portrait in 1906. After Leo left in 1913, Stein's salon continued to draw writers; she mentored and coined the phrase "Lost Generation" for the expatriate writers of the 1920s, including Ernest Hemingway and F. Scott Fitzgerald.

Writing

Stein's writing is known for repetition, unusual syntax, and a deliberate playfulness with language. Her most famous line, "Rose is a rose is a rose is a rose," comes from the poem "Sacred Emily" (1913). The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas, written in Toklas's voice, was her greatest commercial success.

Later life and death

Stein and Toklas remained in France through the Second World War, living much of the time in the village of Bilignin. Stein died of stomach cancer in 1946 and is buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris.
