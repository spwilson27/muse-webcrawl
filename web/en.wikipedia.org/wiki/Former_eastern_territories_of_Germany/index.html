Former eastern territories of Germany

The former eastern territories of Germany (German: ehemalige deutsche Ostgebiete) are those provinces of the German Reich that lay east of the Oder–Neisse line and were transferred to Poland and the Soviet Union after the Second World War. They include East Prussia, most of Silesia, eastern Pomerania, and the eastern part of Brandenburg (the Neumark).

Background

These lands had been part of German states for centuries, though with mixed German, Polish, and other populations in many regions. East Prussia, separated from the rest of Germany after 1919 by the Polish Corridor, contained Königsberg, the coronation city of the Prussian kings. Breslau in Silesia and Stettin in Pomerania were major German cities.

Transfer in 1945

Under the Potsdam Agreement of 1945, the territories east of the Oder and Lusatian Neisse were placed under Polish administration, and northern East Prussia, including Königsberg (renamed Kaliningrad), was annexed by the Soviet Union. Between 1944 and 1950, some 12–14 million Germans fled or were expelled from these and other Eastern European territories — the largest forced migration in European history — amid great hardship and loss of life. Polish and, in the north, Russian settlers repopulated the regions.

Legal status and recognition

West Germany long treated the transfers as provisional, pending a final peace settlement, and expellee organisations kept the question politically alive. The Federal Republic recognised the Oder–Neisse line de facto in the Treaty of Warsaw (1970). Final confirmation came with German reunification: the Two Plus Four Treaty and the German–Polish Border Treaty of 1990 definitively recognised the border, closing the territorial question.
