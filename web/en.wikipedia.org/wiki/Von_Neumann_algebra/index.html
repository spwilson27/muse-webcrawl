Von Neumann algebra

In mathematics, a von Neumann algebra or W*-algebra is a *-algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology, and contains the identity operator. It is a special type of C*-algebra.

They were originally called "rings of operators" and were introduced by John von Neumann, together with Francis Murray, in the 1930s and 1940s, motivated in part by the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics. Von Neumann algebras are one of the principal objects of study in functional analysis.

Definitions

There are three common ways to define von Neumann algebras. A von Neumann algebra may be defined as a *-subalgebra of the algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology and contains the identity. Equivalently, by the von Neumann bicommutant theorem, a *-subalgebra containing the identity is a von Neumann algebra if and only if it equals its own double commutant. A third, more abstract definition characterizes von Neumann algebras as C*-algebras that admit a predual, i.e. that are dual Banach spaces in a compatible way.

Factors and classification

A von Neumann algebra whose center consists only of scalar multiples of the identity is called a factor. Von Neumann showed that every von Neumann algebra decomposes into an integral (or direct sum) of factors, so classification focuses on factors. Factors are divided into types I, II, and III, based on the structure of their projection lattices. Type I factors are analogous to the algebras of all bounded operators on a Hilbert space; type II factors admit a dimension function taking non-integer values; and type III factors, whose existence surprised early researchers, have no nontrivial semifinite trace. The theory later became central to Alain Connes's work in noncommutative geometry, for which he received the Fields Medal in 1982.
