SoundJam MP

Developer: Casady & Greene

Initial release: 1998

Platform: Mac OS

Type: Media player, MP3 encoder

Fate: Acquired by Apple; became the basis of iTunes

SoundJam MP was an early MP3 player and encoder for the Macintosh, developed by Jeff Robbin and Bill Kincaid and published by Casady & Greene from 1998. It allowed users to play MP3 files, rip audio CDs into MP3 format, organize a music library, edit ID3 tags, and synchronize with early portable players such as the Rio PMP300.

Features

SoundJam offered a skinnable interface, playlists, an equalizer, visualizations and support for streaming audio. A companion version, SoundJam MP Plus, added support for portable MP3 players and additional encoding options. The software was a popular Macintosh entry in the late-1990s MP3 boom.

Acquisition by Apple

In 2000 Apple Inc., seeking a jukebox application for the Mac, acquired SoundJam MP from Casady & Greene and hired Robbin and Kincaid. Apple simplified and redesigned the interface, added CD-burning support, and re-released the software as iTunes in January 2001. iTunes became the management software for the iPod (launched later in 2001) and the foundation of Apple's digital music business; Robbin went on to lead the iTunes team for many years.
