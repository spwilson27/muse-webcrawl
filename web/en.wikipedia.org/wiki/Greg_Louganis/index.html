Greg Louganis

Gregory Efthimios Louganis (born January 29, 1960) is an American former Olympic diver widely regarded as one of the greatest divers of all time. He won gold medals at the 1984 and 1988 Summer Olympics on both the springboard and the platform, the only man and the second diver in history to sweep the diving events in consecutive Olympic Games.

Career

Adopted as an infant and raised in California, Louganis took up diving as a child and won a silver medal on the 10-metre platform at the 1976 Montreal Olympics aged sixteen. The American boycott of the 1980 Moscow Games denied him a likely gold, but he dominated the sport through the 1980s, winning five world championship titles. At the 1988 Seoul Olympics he struck his head on the springboard during the preliminary rounds, suffering a concussion, yet returned to win the springboard gold and then the platform title in one of the most celebrated comebacks in Olympic history.

Personal life and legacy

Louganis came out as gay in 1994 and in his 1995 autobiography Breaking the Surface revealed that he was HIV-positive and had known his status before the Seoul Games, which caused controversy over his not disclosing the head injury bleeding incident to officials. The book became a bestseller and was adapted into a television film. He has since worked as an actor, author, motivational speaker, sports commentator and mentor to divers, and as an activist for HIV awareness and LGBT rights.
