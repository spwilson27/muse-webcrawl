Gaston of Foix, Duke of Nemours


The funerary monument of Gaston of Foix, Milan.

Gaston de Foix, duc de Nemours (10 December 1489 – 11 April 1512), also known as the "Thunderbolt of Italy" (il fulmine d'Italia), was a French military commander during the Italian Wars, noted for the brilliance of his campaigns. A nephew of King Louis XII of France and grandson of Gaston IV of Foix and Eleanor of Navarre, he inherited the County of Foix and was made Duke of Nemours in 1507.

Italian campaign

In 1511, aged only 21, Gaston was given command of the French army in Italy in the War of the League of Cambrai. In a campaign of barely three months he relieved Bologna, defeated a Spanish army at Isola della Scala, retook Brescia (where he was wounded), and advanced on Ravenna. On 11 April 1512 he won a crushing victory over the Spanish and Papal army at the Battle of Ravenna — one of the bloodiest battles of the era, notable for its heavy use of artillery — but was killed at the age of 22 while pursuing the fleeing enemy.

Reputation

Contemporaries and historians have compared his meteoric career to that of Alexander the Great. Machiavelli and later writers mourned his death as a decisive turning point: after Ravenna, French fortunes in Italy collapsed, and within a year France had lost Milan. His tomb in Milan, sculpted by Agostino Busti (il Bambaia), is a masterpiece of Renaissance funerary art.
