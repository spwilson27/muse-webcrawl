Xenophon

Xenophon of Athens (c. 430 – c. 354 BC) was a Greek military leader, philosopher, and historian. At the age of 30, Xenophon was elected commander of the Ten Thousand, Greek mercenaries who marched into Persia to support Cyrus the Younger.

Xenophon
Born: c. 430 BC, Athens
Died: c. 354 BC, Corinth
Occupation: Historian, soldier, philosopher
Notable works: Anabasis, Hellenica, Cyropaedia, Memorabilia

Life

A student of Socrates, Xenophon joined Cyrus's expedition in 401 BC. After Cyrus was killed at Cunaxa and the Greek generals were executed, Xenophon helped lead the Ten Thousand on their famous retreat to the Black Sea — the cry "Thalatta! Thalatta!" ("The sea! The sea!") marking their arrival. Exiled from Athens, he lived at Scillus under Spartan protection, later moving to Corinth.

Works

His works include the Anabasis, Hellenica (continuing Thucydides), Cyropaedia (a fictional biography of Cyrus the Great), Memorabilia and Apology defending Socrates, Oeconomicus, and treatises on horsemanship and hunting. His prose was prized in antiquity for its clarity — "Attic honey."

Legacy

Xenophon influenced Roman leaders and Renaissance writers; his Cyropaedia shaped the mirror-for-princes genre.
