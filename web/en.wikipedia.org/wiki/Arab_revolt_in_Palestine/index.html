The 1936–1939 Arab revolt in Palestine was a nationalist uprising by Palestinian Arabs in Mandatory Palestine against British colonial rule and mass Jewish immigration. Beginning with a six-month general strike in 1936, the revolt escalated into armed rebellion in 1937–1939 before being suppressed by large British forces.

Causes and course

Grievances included rising Jewish immigration under the British Mandate, land purchases that displaced Arab tenant farmers, and frustration after the killing of the preacher Izz ad-Din al-Qassam in 1935. The Arab Higher Committee, led by Haj Amin al-Husseini, coordinated the strike and later the armed bands (fasa'il) operating from the countryside.

Suppression and outcome

Britain deployed tens of thousands of troops, imposed collective punishments and worked with Jewish auxiliary forces. The revolt weakened Palestinian society militarily and politically on the eve of the 1948 war, but it also prompted the 1939 White Paper, which limited Jewish immigration and land sales.
