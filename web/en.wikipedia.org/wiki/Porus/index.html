Porus

Porus (Ancient Greek: Πῶρος, Pôros; in Indian sources often identified with a king of the Puru lineage, possibly "Puru" or a name rendered as Poros) was an ancient Indian king whose kingdom, Paurava, lay between the Jhelum (Hydaspes) and Chenab (Acesines) rivers in the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent. He is famous for his stubborn resistance to Alexander the Great at the Battle of the Hydaspes in 326 BC — the last major battle of Alexander's campaigns.

Battle of the Hydaspes

In May 326 BC Alexander crossed the flooded Hydaspes by a daring night maneuver upstream and defeated Porus's army, which included some 200 war elephants. Porus, fighting from elephant-back and wounded, refused to flee. Greek accounts by Arrian, Plutarch, and Diodorus describe how the captured king, asked by Alexander how he wished to be treated, replied "Like a king." Impressed, Alexander restored and even enlarged his kingdom, and Porus became a loyal ally and satrap.

Aftermath and death

Porus remained a Macedonian client ruler until his assassination sometime between 321 and 315 BC by the Macedonian agent Eudemus during the wars of the Diadochi. His stand at the Hydaspes became a lasting symbol of courageous kingship in Indian and Greek literature alike.

In culture

The exchange between Alexander and Porus is retold in Handel's opera Alessandro and in numerous Indian films, plays, and the 2017–18 Indian television series Porus.
