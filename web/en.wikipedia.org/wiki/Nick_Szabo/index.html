Nick Szabo is an American computer scientist, legal scholar and cryptographer known for pioneering research into digital contracts and digital currency. He coined the concept of smart contracts in the 1990s and in 1998 designed "bit gold", a decentralised digital currency mechanism widely regarded as a direct precursor of Bitcoin.

Smart contracts

In papers beginning in 1994, Szabo proposed translating contractual clauses into code embedded in hardware and software so that breaches would be made prohibitively expensive or automatically enforced — taking the humble vending machine as the primitive ancestor of the idea. The concept became foundational for blockchain platforms such as Ethereum.

Bit gold

Bit gold, proposed publicly in 2005 (and described as designed in 1998), combined proof-of-work puzzles chained into a Byzantine-fault-tolerant public registry of ownership — anticipating key elements of Bitcoin's architecture, though it relied on a federated set of servers rather than full decentralisation. Because of these parallels, Szabo has repeatedly been speculated to be Satoshi Nakamoto, the pseudonymous creator of Bitcoin; he has denied this.

Other work

Szabo's blog Unenumerated covers law, economics, history and technology, including his influential essay "Shelling Out: The Origins of Money", which explores the anthropological history of collectibles as proto-money. In 2017 he served as chief scientist (and earlier consultant) for blockchain projects, and he remains a prominent commentator on cryptocurrency and legal institutions.
