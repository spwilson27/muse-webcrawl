Radio nan Gàidheal broadcasts from Stornoway in the Outer Hebrides, the heartland of Scottish Gaelic.

BBC Radio nan Gàidheal is a Scottish radio station owned and operated by the BBC, broadcasting mainly in Scottish Gaelic. Its name means "Radio of the Gaels". It is the principal Gaelic-language radio service, carrying news, current affairs, traditional and contemporary Gaelic music, sport, drama and programmes for learners, and forms part of the BBC Alba brand together with the Gaelic television channel of the same name.

History

Gaelic programmes had featured on BBC Radio Scotland and its predecessors as opt-outs since the 1930s. A dedicated Gaelic service, Radio nan Eilean ("Radio of the Isles"), was established in the Western Isles in 1979. On 1 October 1985 the BBC unified its Gaelic output into a Scotland-wide service under the name Radio nan Gàidheal, progressively expanding its hours until it became a full-time network complementing BBC Radio Scotland. The station's main base is in Stornoway on the Isle of Lewis, with further production in Inverness and Glasgow.

Programming

The schedule includes the morning news and magazine programme Aithris na Maidne, lunchtime news Aithris an Fheasgair in the evening, live coverage of shinty and Highland sport, the annual Royal National Mòd, and music programmes spanning Gaelic song, piping and contemporary Scottish acts. When not carrying its own programmes, the station may simulcast BBC Radio Scotland at certain hours. Radio nan Gàidheal serves around 55,000–65,000 Gaelic speakers and is considered a vital support for the language's survival, alongside BBC Alba television and the Gaelic learning service.
