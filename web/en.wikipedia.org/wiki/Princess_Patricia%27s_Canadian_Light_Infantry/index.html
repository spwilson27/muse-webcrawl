Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry

Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI)
Active: 1914–present
Country: Canada
Branch: Canadian Army
Type: Light infantry
Garrison: Edmonton, Alberta; Shilo, Manitoba

Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI, generally referred to as the Patricia's) is one of the three Regular Force infantry regiments of the Canadian Army. The regiment is named for Princess Patricia of Connaught, daughter of the then Governor General of Canada.

History

The regiment was raised in August 1914 at the outbreak of the First World War, financed largely by Montreal businessman Andrew Hamilton Gault. It was the first Canadian infantry unit to arrive in France, in December 1914, fought at Ypres, the Somme, Vimy Ridge, and Passchendaele, and later served in the Second World War in Sicily, Italy, and Northwest Europe.

Modern service

PPCLI battalions fought in Korea — notably earning a U.S. Presidential Unit Citation at Kapyong in 1951 — served on peacekeeping missions, and deployed repeatedly to Afghanistan. The regiment today comprises three regular battalions and the affiliated 4th Battalion, The Loyal Edmonton Regiment, in the reserve force.
