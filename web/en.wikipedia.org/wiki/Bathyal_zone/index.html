Bathyal zone

The bathyal zone or bathypelagic zone is the part of the open ocean that extends from a depth of 1,000 to 4,000 metres (3,300 to 13,100 ft) below the surface. It lies between the mesopelagic zone above and the abyssopelagic zone below. Sunlight does not penetrate to these depths, so the zone is in perpetual darkness apart from light produced by organisms themselves through bioluminescence.

Conditions

Temperatures in the bathyal zone range from about 4 °C near the top to near freezing at greater depths, and hydrostatic pressure rises to several hundred atmospheres. There is no primary production by photosynthesis; food webs depend on organic matter sinking from the sunlit surface — "marine snow" — supplemented at hydrothermal vents and cold seeps by chemosynthesis.

Fauna

Despite the harsh conditions, the bathyal zone supports a diverse fauna, including anglerfish, gulper eels, giant isopods, vampire squid, rattails (grenadiers) and many bioluminescent fishes and invertebrates. Animals here often have large eyes reduced or absent, slow metabolisms, and soft, watery bodies adapted to scarcity of food. Sperm whales and other deep-diving predators hunt in this zone.
