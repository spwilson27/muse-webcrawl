Graduated driver licensing

[Image: Graduated driver licensing]

Graduated driver licensing (GDL) systems are designed to provide new drivers of motor vehicles with driving experience and skills gradually over time in low-risk environments. There are typically three steps or stages: a learner's permit, an intermediate or provisional licence, and a full licence.

Stages

During the learner stage, driving is permitted only under supervision. The intermediate stage allows unsupervised driving but with restrictions such as limits on night driving and the number of young passengers. After meeting time and record requirements, the novice graduates to a full licence.

Effectiveness

GDL was first introduced in New Zealand in 1987 and has since been adopted across the United States, Canada, and Australia. Studies associate GDL programmes with substantial reductions — commonly 20 to 40 per cent — in crash rates among the youngest drivers.
