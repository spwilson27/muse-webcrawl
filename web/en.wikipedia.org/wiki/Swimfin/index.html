Swimfin

Swimfins, or simply fins, are fin-like accessories worn on the feet to increase propulsion through the water in swimming, bodyboarding, bodysurfing, float-tube fishing, kneeboarding, riverboarding, snorkelling, scuba diving and other water activities. They work by enlarging the effective surface area of the foot, allowing the wearer to push more water with each kick.

History

Early concepts for swim fins date to Leonardo da Vinci's sketches of webbed gloves, and Benjamin Franklin made a pair of wooden hand fins as a boy. Modern rubber swim fins were developed in the 1930s: the Frenchman Louis de Corlieu patented a design in 1933, and Owen Churchill's fins, developed after observing Tahitian swimmers, were used by the US Navy in the Second World War. The Italian firm Cressi became a major producer in the post-war period.

Types

  • Full-foot fins enclose the heel and are worn barefoot, typical for snorkelling and warm-water diving.
  • Open-heel fins have an adjustable strap and are worn over neoprene boots, common in cold-water scuba diving.
  • Split fins have a longitudinal slit in the blade intended to reduce resistance.
  • Monofins join both feet in a single wide fin, used in finswimming and freediving.
  • Short fins are used in swim training to build leg strength and refine kick technique.

Use

Fins increase speed and efficiency, reduce the effort needed to remain at the surface, and help divers manoeuvre with heavy equipment. Styles vary by activity: long blades for freediving efficiency, stiff blades for technical diving, and small, stiff fins for bodyboarding.
