National anthem

A national anthem is a patriotic musical composition symbolising and evoking a nation or its people. National anthems rose to prominence in Europe during the 19th century, as nation-states consolidated, though some are older: the Dutch "Wilhelmus", dating to the 16th century, is often considered the oldest.

Use

Anthems are played at state ceremonies, sporting events — notably the Olympic Games and international football — and in some countries in schools and before public performances. Usually only one verse is performed officially.

Notable anthems

Famous examples include France's "La Marseillaise" (1792), the United States' "The Star-Spangled Banner" (lyrics 1814, adopted 1931), and the United Kingdom's "God Save the King". Some anthems have no official lyrics, such as Spain's "Marcha Real".
