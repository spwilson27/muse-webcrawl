Belzec extermination camp

Location: near Bełżec, occupied Poland

Operated: March – December 1942 (gassing operations)

Operation: Operation Reinhard

Victims: approx. 434,500 – 600,000 murdered

Belzec was a Nazi German extermination camp built by the SS for the secretive Operation Reinhard, the plan to murder the Jews of German-occupied Poland. It operated from March 1942 near the village of Bełżec in the Lublin district of the General Government. The camp, along with Sobibor and Treblinka, existed for the sole purpose of killing deportees in gas chambers; at least 434,500 Jews were murdered at Belzec, and the true figure may have approached 600,000. Roma and Poles were also among the victims.

Operation of the camp

The camp was small, only a few hundred metres across, divided into a reception area and a killing area connected by a fenced path called the "tube". Transports arrived by rail; victims were told they were at a transit camp and were driven into gas chambers disguised as showers, where they were killed with carbon monoxide exhaust from engines. Commandants included Christian Wirth and Gottlieb Hering. Almost no prisoners survived: Rudolf Reder, one of only two known survivors of the camp, later testified about it.

Closure and memorial

Gassing operations ceased in December 1942. In 1943 the bodies were exhumed and burned, the camp was dismantled, and the site was planted over with trees to conceal the crime. After the war the grounds were neglected for decades and looted by grave robbers. A new memorial and museum, designed with a massive stone-and-slag field covering the site, was opened in 2004.
