Scutum

The scutum (Latin for "shield") was the large, curved rectangular shield used by Roman legionaries. The classical scutum of the early Empire was a semi-cylindrical body shield about 1.2 metres tall, made of layers of wood glued together like plywood, covered with canvas and hide, and fitted with an iron or bronze boss (umbo) protecting a central horizontal grip.

Use in battle

Its curved shape wrapped around the body, deflecting blows and missiles while leaving the soldier's sword arm free for short thrusts with the gladius. Legionaries could interlock scuta in the testudo (tortoise) formation to shelter from arrows, and used the shield offensively to punch and push in close combat. Shields were painted with unit insignia such as Jupiter's eagle and lightning bolts.

Development

Earlier Roman shields included the round clipeus and oval shields adopted from Celtic and Iberian peoples. The curved rectangular scutum became standard by the late Republic; in the later Empire it gradually gave way again to flat oval and round shields as army organisation and tactics changed.
