Linux Mint

Linux Mint is a community-driven Linux distribution based on Ubuntu (which in turn is based on Debian), bundled with a variety of free and open-source applications. First released in 2006 by Clément Lefèbvre, it aims to provide a modern, elegant, and comfortable operating system that is powerful and easy to use.

Features

Mint's flagship desktop environment is Cinnamon, developed in-house, with MATE and Xfce editions also available. The distribution includes home-grown tools (the "X-Apps" and utilities such as the Update Manager and Timeshift for system snapshots), full multimedia support out of the box, and a conservative update policy emphasizing stability. A separate edition, LMDE (Linux Mint Debian Edition), is based directly on Debian as a contingency against Ubuntu's future.

Reception

Linux Mint is consistently among the most popular desktop Linux distributions, frequently recommended for users migrating from Windows because of its familiar layout and preinstalled software.
