Holy Qurbana

TypeEucharistic liturgy
RitesEast Syriac Rite; West Syriac Rite
ChurchesSyro-Malabar Church, Syriac Orthodox Church, Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, Assyrian Church of the East, Chaldean Catholic Church, Maronite Church

The Holy Qurbana (Syriac: ܩܘܼܪܒܵܢܵܐ ܩܲܕܝܼܫܵܐ, Qurbānā Qaddišā, "Holy Offering"), also called the Holy Qurobo in the West Syriac tradition, is the Eucharistic liturgy celebrated by churches of the Syriac tradition, including the Saint Thomas Christians of India. The word qurbana derives from the Aramaic root meaning an offering or sacrifice, cognate with the Hebrew korban.

East Syriac tradition

In the East Syriac Rite — used by the Assyrian Church of the East, the Chaldean Catholic Church, and the Syro-Malabar Church — the principal anaphora is that of the Holy Apostles, attributed to Addai and Mari, disciples of St Thomas the Apostle. The Anaphora of Addai and Mari is remarkable among ancient Eucharistic prayers for lacking an explicit Institution narrative in its oldest form, a feature whose validity was formally recognized by the Holy See in 2001. Alternative anaphoras attributed to Theodore of Mopsuestia and Nestorius are used on certain feasts.

West Syriac tradition

In the West Syriac Rite, celebrated by the Syriac Orthodox Church, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, and the Maronite Church among others, the service is called the Holy Qurobo and most commonly uses the Anaphora of St James, the liturgy associated with the ancient Church of Jerusalem. The West Syriac tradition possesses a large repertoire of anaphoras, of which more than seventy have been catalogued.

Structure

The celebration typically includes an introductory service of preparation and vesting, the Liturgy of the Word with readings from the Old and New Testaments, the offering of incense, the Creed, the anaphora with its prayers of epiclesis invoking the Holy Spirit, the fraction and commingling of the consecrated elements, the Lord's Prayer, and Holy Communion, followed by prayers of thanksgiving and dismissal.
