"Macarena" is a Spanish dance song by Latin pop duo Los del Río, about a woman of the same name. Appearing on their 1993 album A mí me gusta, it was an international hit in 1995 and 1996, driven by a remix by the Bayside Boys with partly English lyrics. The song got the group ranked the "#1 Greatest One-Hit Wonder of All Time" by VH1 in 2002, and it remains one of the best-selling singles and most recognizable dance songs in pop history.

The refrain tells of Macarena, whose boyfriend Vitorino has left to join the army; the English-language version portrays her as a flirtatious partygoer. The song's title refers to a neighbourhood and a Virgin venerated in Seville, La Macarena — also the name of one of the writers' daughters.

Success and the dance

The Bayside Boys remix reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in August 1996 and stayed there for 14 weeks; the single spent a record 60 weeks on the chart overall. It topped charts in dozens of countries, including Australia, France, Germany, and Italy. The accompanying choreographed dance — a simple sequence of arm movements, hip sways, and quarter turns — became a cultural phenomenon: it was performed at the 1996 Democratic National Convention, at Super Bowl XXXI celebrations, and by the victorious United States women's gymnastics team at the Atlanta Olympics era festivities. At Yankee Stadium in 1996, some 50,000 people danced it simultaneously, setting a record. It has since become a fixture at weddings, parties, and sporting events worldwide.

Later versions

Los del Río recorded numerous versions and remixes over the decades, including a Christmas version and collaborations such as "Macarena" re-recordings with Gente de Zona (2016, "Más Macarena"). In 2019–2024 the song found new audiences through social media revivals, and Tyga sampled it in "Ayy Macarena" (2019).
