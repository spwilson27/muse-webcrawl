Predicate transformer semantics


Edsger W. Dijkstra, founder of the calculus

Predicate transformer semantics is a formal framework for describing the semantics of computer programs, introduced by Edsger W. Dijkstra in his 1975 papers and his book A Discipline of Programming (1976). A program is characterised by two predicate transformers: the weakest precondition, wp(S, R), and the weakest liberal precondition, wlp(S, R).

Weakest precondition

The weakest precondition wp(S, R) is the weakest condition on the initial state guaranteeing that executing statement S terminates in a state satisfying postcondition R. Dijkstra imposed healthiness conditions on legitimate predicate transformers, including monotonicity and the law of the excluded miracle.

Applications

The calculus underlies program derivation, formal verification and refinement calculi used in safety-critical software development.
