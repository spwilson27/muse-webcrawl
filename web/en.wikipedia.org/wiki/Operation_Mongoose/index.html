Operation Mongoose, also known as the Cuban Project, was a covert operation of the United States government aimed at removing Fidel Castro's Communist government from power in Cuba. Authorised by President John F. Kennedy in November 1961, following the failure of the Bay of Pigs invasion, the programme was run by Air Force Brigadier General Edward Lansdale as Chief of Operations, working from the CIA's large JM/WAVE station in Miami, Florida.

Planning

Lansdale's plan envisaged a phased programme of political action, economic warfare, sabotage and support for internal resistance, intended to culminate in an open revolt inside Cuba by October 1962 that would justify, and possibly be supported by, direct American military intervention. Attorney General Robert F. Kennedy took a close personal interest in the project and pressed for results. Dozens of schemes were proposed, ranging from sabotage of Cuban industry and crops to propaganda operations; a related set of proposals, Operation Northwoods — which contemplated staged attacks to manufacture a pretext for invasion — was presented to the President in March 1962 and rejected.

Activities

CIA teams conducted maritime raids, sabotage missions and infiltration of agents into Cuba, though internal resistance remained weak and Cuban security penetrated many networks. The operation coincided with, and formed part of the context for, the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962. As part of the crisis settlement, Kennedy pledged not to invade Cuba, and Mongoose operations were ordered halted; the programme was formally wound down, although lower-level covert activity against Cuba continued for years.

Legacy

Details of Operation Mongoose emerged publicly through the Church Committee investigations of 1975–76 into US intelligence activities, including revelations about assassination plots against Castro run in parallel channels. Historians have debated how far the operation's pressure on Cuba helped push Havana toward accepting Soviet missiles, contributing to the very crisis it was overtaken by.
