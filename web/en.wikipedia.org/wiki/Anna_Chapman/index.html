Anna Chapman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anna Vasilyevna Chapman (Russian: А́нна Васи́льевна Ча́пман; born 23 February 1982 as Anna Kushchenko) is a Russian media personality and former intelligence operative. She became internationally known in 2010 when she was arrested in the United States as part of the "Illegals Program", a network of Russian sleeper agents, and was returned to Russia in a prisoner exchange. She subsequently built a career in Russian media and business.

Early life and career

Chapman was born in Volgograd. Her father was a diplomat, and she has said he worked for the KGB. She studied economics in Moscow and moved to London in the early 2000s after marrying Alex Chapman, a British citizen, whose surname she kept after their divorce. She worked in finance-related roles in London and later in New York, where she ran an online real-estate company.

Arrest and deportation

On 27 June 2010, Chapman was arrested by the FBI along with nine others accused of acting as unregistered agents of Russia under "deep cover" in the United States. She pleaded guilty to conspiracy to act as an agent of a foreign government and on 8 July 2010 was deported to Russia as part of a spy swap — the largest between the two countries since the Cold War — in which Russia released four prisoners convicted of espionage for the West. Her photograph and biography received extensive media coverage worldwide.

Later career

Back in Russia, Chapman became a public figure: she hosted a television programme about unsolved mysteries, worked in banking and youth politics, modelled, and pursued business ventures, benefiting from her celebrity status. Her case has been cited in discussions of modern espionage and of the media treatment of intelligence scandals.
