Idiophone

Idiophone
ClassificationPercussion (Hornbostel–Sachs category 1)
ExamplesBell, gong, xylophone, marimba, mbira, triangle, cymbals

An idiophone is any musical instrument that creates sound primarily by the vibration of the instrument itself, without the use of air flow (as in aerophones), strings (chordophones), membranes (membranophones), or electricity (electrophones). Idiophones form the first of the five categories of the Hornbostel–Sachs classification of instruments, published by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs in 1914.

Types

Hornbostel–Sachs divides idiophones by the way they are played:

  • Struck idiophones — xylophones, metallophones, gongs, bells, triangles, cymbals, and the steelpan, sounded by hitting.
  • Plucked idiophones (lamellaphones) — the mbira (thumb piano), the Jew's harp, and the music box comb.
  • Friction idiophones — the glass harmonica and nail violin, sounded by rubbing.
  • Shaken idiophones — maracas, rattles, and shakers.
  • Scraped idiophones — the güiro and washboard.
  • Stamping idiophones — stamping tubes and sticks struck against the ground.

Materials and distribution

Idiophones are made of materials that resonate when struck — most commonly metal, wood, stone, or shell — and are found in virtually every musical culture. Many of the earliest known instruments, such as bone and stone rattles and slit drums, are idiophones. In the Western orchestra they form much of the percussion section.
