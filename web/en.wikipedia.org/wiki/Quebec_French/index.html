Quebec French (French: français québécois) is the predominant variety of French spoken in Canada and the most widespread variety of Canadian French. It differs from the French of France in pronunciation, vocabulary, and certain grammatical features, reflecting its origins in the regional dialects of 17th- and 18th-century settlers from northern and western France and subsequent isolation after the British Conquest of 1760.

Characteristics

Quebec French preserves features lost in standard European French, such as the distinction between /a/ and /ɑ/ (pâte vs. patte) and affrication of /t/ and /d/ before high front vowels (tu pronounced "tsu"). Its vocabulary includes archaisms (char for car), nautical terms, and distinctive words such as dépanneur (corner store), magasiner (to shop), and tuque (knit cap), alongside carefully regulated borrowings and calques from English.

Status

Quebec French is the official language of Quebec under the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977). The vernacular register, sometimes called joual, occupies a complex sociolinguistic position as both a badge of identity and a subject of debate over standards.
