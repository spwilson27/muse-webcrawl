2019–20 Australian bushfire season

Fire details
DateJune 2019 – March 2020
LocationAustralia, especially New South Wales and Victoria
Area burnedRoughly 18–24 million hectares
Fatalities33 directly; hundreds more attributed to smoke

The 2019–20 Australian bushfire season, known as the Black Summer, was a period of unusually intense bushfires across Australia, fuelled by record-breaking temperatures, prolonged drought and strong winds. Fires burned from mid-2019 through the Southern Hemisphere summer and were largely extinguished by heavy rain in February 2020.

Scale and impact

The fires destroyed more than 5,900 buildings, including over 2,400 homes, and burned an estimated 18.6 million hectares or more. Thirty-three people died directly in the fires, including nine firefighters, while smoke exposure was linked to several hundred additional deaths. Smoke blanketed Sydney, Canberra and Melbourne for weeks and was carried across the Pacific to South America. Ecologists estimated that nearly three billion animals were killed or displaced.

Causes

Australia had just experienced its hottest and driest year on record. A strong positive Indian Ocean Dipole and a rare sudden stratospheric warming event over Antarctica contributed to the extreme heat and dryness, against the background of long-term climate warming trends.

Response

Thousands of firefighters, including overseas crews from North America and New Zealand, fought the blazes, and the Australian Defence Force was mobilised in its largest domestic deployment. A royal commission and multiple inquiries examined causes, preparedness and recovery.
