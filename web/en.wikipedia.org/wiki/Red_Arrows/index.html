The Red Arrows, officially known as the Royal Air Force Aerobatic Team, is the aerobatics display team of the Royal Air Force. Based at RAF Waddington in Lincolnshire, the team was formed in late 1964 and has since flown close to 5,000 displays in nearly 60 countries. The Red Arrows are regarded as one of the world's premier aerobatic display teams and are a prominent symbol of the United Kingdom.

History

Before 1964 several RAF squadrons maintained their own display teams, including the Black Arrows of No. 111 Squadron, famous for a 22-aircraft loop in 1958. The Red Arrows consolidated display flying into a single team, initially flying the Folland Gnat — chosen for its agility and low running costs. In 1979–80 the team re-equipped with the BAE Hawk, which it still flies; the Hawks are painted in the distinctive red scheme with a Union Flag tail fin, and are fitted with smoke pods using diesel and dye to produce the signature red, white and blue smoke trails.

The display

The team flies nine aircraft in signature formations such as Diamond Nine, the Arrow, the Swan and the heart-shaped Cupid, and performs opposition passes by the "Synchro Pair". Display pilots — call-signed Red 1 to Red 9 — are selected from front-line fast-jet squadrons and serve three-year tours; Red 1, the team leader, flies the front position. Around 100 support staff, the "Blues", maintain the aircraft, including the "Circus" of travelling engineers who ride in the rear seats on transits.

Incidents

The team has suffered several fatal accidents, including the 2011 crash at Bournemouth Air Festival that killed Flight Lieutenant Jon Egging, and a 2018 crash at RAF Valley that killed engineer Corporal Jonathan Bayliss. In 2022–2024 reports of a toxic culture and inappropriate behaviour within the team led to investigations, dismissals and an official apology from the RAF.
