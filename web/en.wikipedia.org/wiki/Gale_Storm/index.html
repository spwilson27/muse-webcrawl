Gale Storm


Gale Storm (born Josephine Owaissa Cottle; April 5, 1922 – June 27, 2009) was an American actress and singer.

Gale Storm (born Josephine Owaissa Cottle; April 5, 1922 – June 27, 2009) was an American actress and singer. She was best known for her starring roles in two television series, My Little Margie and The Gale Storm Show, both of which were highly popular during the 1950s.

Early life

Storm was born in Bloomington, Texas. Her middle name, "Owaissa," is a Native American word meaning "bluebird." Orphaned as a teenager, she won a talent contest — the Gateway to Hollywood contest — in 1940, which led to a contract with RKO Radio Pictures and a new stage name.

Career

Storm appeared in numerous B-movies during the 1940s, including It Happened in Flatbush and The Dude Goes West. In 1950 she made her television debut. Her role as the trouble-prone Margie Albright in My Little Margie (1952–1955) made her a household name, and she followed it with The Gale Storm Show: Oh! Susanna (1956–1960), in which she played cruise director Susanna Pomeroy.

Storm also had a successful recording career. Her 1955 recording of "I Hear You Knockin'," originally by Smiley Lewis, reached number two on the Billboard charts and sold over a million copies. Other hits included "Dark Moon" (1957) and "Never Leave Me."

Personal life and death

Storm was married to actor Lee Bonnell, with whom she had four children. She died on June 27, 2009, at a convalescent hospital in Danville, California, at the age of 87. She has three stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame — for radio, television, and records.
