Antiviral drug

Antiviral drug
ExamplesAciclovir, oseltamivir, remdesivir, tenofovir, sofosbuvir
TargetsHIV, influenza, herpesviruses, hepatitis B and C, SARS-CoV-2

Antiviral drugs are a class of medication used for treating viral infections. Unlike most antibiotics, which destroy bacteria, antivirals do not kill their target pathogen; instead they inhibit its replication at some stage of the viral life cycle — entry into the cell, uncoating, genome replication, assembly, or release. Because viruses replicate inside host cells using host machinery, finding targets that harm the virus without harming the host is difficult, and most viruses have few or no effective antiviral treatments; broad-spectrum antivirals are rare compared with broad-spectrum antibiotics.

Major classes and uses

Combination antiretroviral therapy (ART) transformed HIV into a manageable chronic condition using classes such as nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (e.g. tenofovir), protease inhibitors, and integrase inhibitors. Herpesviruses are treated with nucleoside analogues such as aciclovir and valaciclovir. Influenza can be treated with neuraminidase inhibitors such as oseltamivir, and the polymerase inhibitor baloxavir. Direct-acting antivirals such as sofosbuvir can cure most hepatitis C infections, while hepatitis B is managed with tenofovir or entecavir. During the COVID-19 pandemic, remdesivir and the oral antivirals nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) and molnupiravir were deployed against SARS-CoV-2.

Resistance and limitations

Viruses can evolve resistance, particularly when drugs are used singly or courses are incomplete, which is why HIV and hepatitis treatments use combinations. Most antivirals work best when given early in infection, and for many common viral illnesses — including the common cold — no specific antiviral exists, so care is supportive.
