Conservatoire

A conservatoire (also conservatory, conservatorium, or Konservatorium) is a school or university devoted to education in the performing arts, principally music, and in some cases dance and drama. Conservatoires typically offer performance-focused training alongside music theory, music history, and composition.

History

The term derives from the Italian conservatorio, which originally referred to orphanages and charitable institutions in Naples and Venice — such as the Conservatorio di Santa Maria di Loreto (founded 1537) — where children were sheltered ("conserved") and given musical training. Vivaldi taught at the Ospedale della Pietà in Venice. The Paris Conservatoire, founded in 1795, became the model for the modern institution, followed by schools in Prague (1811), Vienna (1819), Leipzig (1843, founded by Mendelssohn), and later throughout Europe and the Americas.

Education

Students usually specialize in an instrument or voice, receiving one-to-one tuition, ensemble experience in orchestras, choirs and chamber groups, and academic coursework. Many conservatoires award undergraduate and postgraduate degrees in association with universities. Famous examples include the Juilliard School in New York, the Royal Academy of Music in London, and the Moscow Conservatory.
