National identification number

Many countries issue identity documents bearing a unique national identification number.

A national identification number, national identity number, or national insurance number is a number used by the governments of many countries as a means of tracking their citizens, permanent residents, and temporary residents for the purposes of work, taxation, government benefits, health care, and other governmentally related functions. Such numbers are usually assigned at birth or upon reaching legal age, and are intended to remain unique to an individual for life.

Uses

National identification numbers serve as a primary key across government databases, simplifying the administration of taxes, social security, elections, and public health. In many countries the number is required to open a bank account, obtain a driving licence, or access medical care. Where the number encodes personal data — such as date of birth or sex, as in the Swedish personnummer or the Chinese resident identity card number — it can also function as a convenient identifier in everyday transactions.

Examples by country

  • United States — the Social Security number (SSN), introduced in 1936 for the Social Security programme, is a de facto national identifier, though officially restricted in use.
  • United Kingdom — the National Insurance number is used for tax and benefits; there is no general identity card.
  • India — Aadhaar, a 12-digit number linked to biometric data, is the world's largest biometric ID system, administered by the Unique Identification Authority of India.
  • Sweden — the personal identity number (personnummer), introduced in 1947, is used universally in dealings with authorities and many private services.
  • South Korea — the resident registration number encodes birth date, sex, and place of registration.
  • Brazil — the CPF (Cadastro de Pessoas Físicas) number is used for tax and identification purposes.
  • Estonia — the personal code underpins the country's e-government and digital identity system.

Privacy and security concerns

Critics warn that universal identifiers facilitate surveillance, profiling, and identity theft, since a single compromised number can unlock many services. Some countries, such as Germany, have deliberately avoided a single universal number for historical and constitutional reasons, using sector-specific identifiers instead. Debates over India's Aadhaar programme, including a landmark 2018 Supreme Court judgment upholding it with restrictions, illustrate the tension between administrative efficiency and privacy rights.
