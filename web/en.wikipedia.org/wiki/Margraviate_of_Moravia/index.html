The Margraviate of Moravia (Czech: Markrabství moravské; German: Markgrafschaft Mähren) was one of the Lands of the Bohemian Crown within the Holy Roman Empire, and later a crown land of the Austrian Empire and of Cisleithanian Austria-Hungary. It existed as a distinct political entity from 1182 until the dissolution of Austria-Hungary in 1918. Its territory corresponds broadly to the historical region of Moravia in the present-day Czech Republic, with capitals first at Olomouc and later at Brno.

History

Moravia, originally part of the ninth-century Great Moravian empire, came under the rule of the Bohemian Přemyslid dukes around the turn of the eleventh century, governed by junior members of the dynasty in appanages at Brno, Olomouc and Znojmo. In 1182 Emperor Frederick Barbarossa elevated Moravia to a margraviate immediately subject to the emperor, though it soon returned to the Bohemian orbit. The margrave title was thereafter frequently held by the Bohemian king's heir or by the king himself.

Moravia suffered devastating Mongol incursions in 1241, was contested during the wars between Ottokar II and Rudolf of Habsburg, and flourished under the Luxembourg kings. During the Hussite Wars of the fifteenth century it was a battleground and a stronghold of the Utraquists. After the Battle of White Mountain (1620) Moravia was firmly re-Catholicised and governed from Vienna as part of the hereditary lands, though it retained its own diet and land tables. Industrialisation in the nineteenth century made Brno a textile centre — the "Moravian Manchester".

With the collapse of the Habsburg monarchy in 1918, Moravia became part of the new state of Czechoslovakia, losing its separate status when it was merged into the province of Moravia-Silesia in 1928.
