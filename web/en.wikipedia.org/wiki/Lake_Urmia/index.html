Lake Urmia (Persian: دریاچه ارومیه) is a hypersaline lake in northwestern Iran, between the provinces of East Azerbaijan and West Azerbaijan. It was once the largest lake in the Middle East and one of the largest salt lakes in the world, a UNESCO biosphere reserve supporting brine shrimp (Artemia urmiana) and migratory flamingos and pelicans on its many islands.

Shrinkage

From the 1990s, dam construction on its inflowing rivers, agricultural water diversion, groundwater pumping, drought, and a causeway built across the lake combined to shrink Urmia dramatically; by 2015 it had lost around 90% of its surface area, exposing salt flats whose wind-blown dust threatened surrounding cities and farmland. The disaster became a symbol of Iran's national water crisis.

Restoration

The Urmia Lake Restoration Program, launched in 2013 with international support, worked to release environmental flows from dams, improve irrigation efficiency, and transfer water from the Zab River. Wet winters in 2019–2020 raised the lake substantially, though subsequent drought years reversed part of the recovery, and the lake's long-term future remains precarious.
