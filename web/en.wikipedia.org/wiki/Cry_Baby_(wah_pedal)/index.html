Cry Baby (wah pedal)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cry Baby is a wah-wah pedal for electric guitar, manufactured by Dunlop Manufacturing, Inc. First introduced in 1966, it is among the best-selling guitar effects pedals of all time and has become the generic reference point for the wah sound.

History

The pedal originated with the Thomas Organ Company, whose engineer Brad Plunkett developed the mid-range boost circuit in 1966 while working on a solid-state amplifier. Marketed as the "Clyde McCoy" pedal by Vox in Britain and as the Cry Baby by Thomas Organ in the United States — named for the "cry baby" sound the pedal made, echoing a muted trumpet's wah — the effect was popularised by guitarists such as Eric Clapton and Jimi Hendrix. After the various rights changed hands, Jim Dunlop's company acquired the Cry Baby name and has manufactured the pedal since the early 1980s.

Design

The pedal is a treadle-operated band-pass filter: rocking the footplate sweeps the resonant peak of the filter up and down in frequency, producing a vowel-like "wah" effect. A switch under the toe engages or bypasses the effect. The classic circuit uses an inductor-based filter and a potentiometer driven by a rack-and-pinion from the treadle. Dunlop produces many variants, including artist signature models for players such as Slash, Kirk Hammett, Zakk Wylde, and John Petrucci, as well as mini and bass versions.
