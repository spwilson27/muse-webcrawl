Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), or squamous cell cancer, is a cancer arising from squamous epithelial cells. It occurs in many organs — including the skin, lips, mouth, oesophagus, lungs, cervix, and anus — and is among the most common of all cancers. Cutaneous SCC is the second most common skin cancer after basal cell carcinoma, with incidence rising worldwide owing to ultraviolet exposure, tanning, and ageing populations.

Causes and risk factors

The dominant cause of skin SCC is cumulative ultraviolet (UV) radiation, so tumours arise mainly on sun-exposed sites such as the face, ears, scalp, and backs of the hands; fair skin, outdoor work, and tanning beds increase risk. Other factors include immunosuppression (organ-transplant recipients develop SCC at many times the normal rate), human papillomavirus (HPV) infection — central to cervical and many oropharyngeal and anal SCCs — tobacco and alcohol (head, neck, lung, and oesophageal SCC), chronic inflammation and non-healing wounds or scars (Marjolin's ulcer), ionising radiation, and arsenic exposure.

Presentation and diagnosis

Cutaneous SCC typically presents as a firm, red, scaly or crusted nodule or plaque that may ulcerate and grow over weeks to months; a rapidly growing dome-shaped tumour with a central keratin core is called a keratoacanthoma-type SCC. Diagnosis is confirmed by skin biopsy and histology showing invasive nests of atypical squamous cells and keratin "pearls". Most cutaneous SCCs are cured by complete excision, but a minority — especially large, deep, poorly differentiated tumours and those on the lip or ear or in immunosuppressed patients — can metastasise to lymph nodes and beyond.

Treatment

Standard treatment is surgical removal, with Mohs micrographic surgery preferred for high-risk or cosmetically sensitive sites. Radiotherapy is an alternative or adjunct; cryotherapy, curettage, and topical or photodynamic therapy suit selected superficial lesions. Advanced or metastatic SCC is treated with systemic therapy, notably PD-1 inhibitor immunotherapy (cemiplimab, pembrolizumab), which has substantially improved outcomes. Prevention centres on sun protection and surveillance of precursor lesions such as actinic keratoses and Bowen's disease (SCC in situ).
