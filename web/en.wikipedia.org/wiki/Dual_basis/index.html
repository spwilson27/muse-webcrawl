Dual basis

In linear algebra, given a vector space V with a basis {e1, …, en}, the dual basis is the corresponding basis {ε1, …, εn} of the dual space V*, consisting of linear functionals defined by εi(ej) = δij, the Kronecker delta. Every linear functional on V can then be written uniquely as a combination of the dual basis functionals.

Properties

If v = Σ vi ei is a vector and f ∈ V*, then f(v) = Σ fi vi, where fi = f(ei). Under a change of basis with matrix P, the dual basis transforms by the inverse transpose of P, so that components of vectors (contravariant) and components of functionals (covariant) transform oppositely and their pairing is unchanged. The dual basis construction is a finite-dimensional notion in this exact form; in infinite dimensions the analogous set of functionals need not form a basis.

Reciprocal basis

In an inner product space, the dual basis can be realised inside V itself as the reciprocal basis: vectors ei satisfying ⟨ei, ej⟩ = δij. Reciprocal lattice vectors in crystallography are an important example, used to define the reciprocal lattice for diffraction theory and solid-state physics.
