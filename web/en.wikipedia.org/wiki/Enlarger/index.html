Enlarger

An enlarger is a specialised transparency projector used to produce photographic prints from film or glass negatives, or from transparencies. All enlargers consist of a light source — normally an electric bulb — shining through or reflected by a condenser or diffuser, illuminating a film holder containing the negative, a lens, and an adjustable stand on which the head carrying these parts moves up and down a column above a baseboard on which the photographic paper is placed in an easel.

Light passes through the film, is enlarged by the enlarging lens, and projects an image of the negative onto photographic paper. The size of the print is controlled by adjusting the height of the enlarger head and refocusing; exposure is controlled by the lens aperture and by timing the lamp, often with an electronic timer. Prints are then developed in trays of chemicals in the darkroom under safelight illumination.

Types

Condenser enlargers use a pair of lenses to concentrate light into a collimated beam, giving sharp, high-contrast images that also emphasise dust and scratches. Diffusion enlargers use a diffusing screen or an integrating chamber, producing softer, lower-contrast images more forgiving of negative defects. Colour enlargers have a filter drawer or a dichroic colour head allowing cyan, magenta and yellow filtration to be dialled in for printing colour negatives, and can also be used with variable-contrast black-and-white papers. Common formats range from 35 mm miniature enlargers to large-format machines handling 4×5 inch or 8×10 inch negatives.

History

Solar enlarging cameras — using sunlight as the source — existed from the 1850s for making enlarged prints from small negatives. Artificial-light enlargers spread with amateur roll-film photography in the early twentieth century, and the classic vertical enlarger reached mature form in machines such as the Leitz Focomat and the De Vere and Durst ranges used in professional labs. The diffusion colour head became standard for RA-4 colour printing. With the decline of film photography after 2000, most commercial printing moved to digital minilabs and inkjet printing, but enlargers remain in use among fine-art and hobbyist darkroom photographers.
