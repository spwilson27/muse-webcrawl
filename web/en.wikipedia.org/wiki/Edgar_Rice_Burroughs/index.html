Edgar Rice Burroughs

Edgar Rice Burroughs (September 1, 1875 – March 19, 1950) was an American writer best known for creating Tarzan of the Apes and the Mars adventurer John Carter. A prolific author of pulp fiction, he helped define the adventure and planetary romance genres and built one of the first successful character-franchise businesses in American publishing.

Early life and career

Burroughs worked at a series of jobs — cavalryman, gold prospector, railroad policeman and pencil-sharpener salesman — before turning to writing at age 35, unimpressed by the quality of stories in the pulp magazines he read while supervising salesmen. His first story, "Under the Moons of Mars," was serialised in 1912, introducing John Carter's Barsoom. Later that year Tarzan of the Apes appeared, becoming an enormous success.

Works

Burroughs wrote some two dozen Tarzan books and eleven Barsoom novels, as well as the Pellucidar tales set in a hollow Earth, the Venus (Amtor) series, westerns and historical romances. Tarzan became a multimedia phenomenon in films, comics and merchandise. To control his properties Burroughs incorporated himself as Edgar Rice Burroughs, Inc. — a pioneering step for an author — and bought a California ranch he named Tarzana, around which the Los Angeles neighbourhood of that name grew.

Later life

During the Second World War Burroughs served as a war correspondent in the Pacific, among the oldest accredited correspondents. He died of a heart attack in 1950. His influence on adventure and science fiction — on writers from Ray Bradbury to Arthur C. Clarke — is widely acknowledged, and a crater on Mars is named in his honour.
