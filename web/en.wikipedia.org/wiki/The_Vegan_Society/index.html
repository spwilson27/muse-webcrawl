The Vegan Society

The Vegan Society is a registered charity and the oldest vegan organisation in the world, founded in the United Kingdom in 1944 by Donald Watson and a group of non-dairy vegetarians. The word "vegan" itself was coined at its founding.

History

Watson, a woodwork teacher from Leicester, had argued within the Vegetarian Society that vegetarians who consumed eggs and dairy should form a distinct movement, since egg and dairy production also involve animal exploitation and killing. When the Vegetarian Society declined to create a section, Watson and 23 others founded The Vegan Society in November 1944 — "vegan" taking the first three and last two letters of "vegetarian," "because veganism starts with vegetarianism and carries it through to its logical conclusion." Watson edited its newsletter, The Vegan News, the ancestor of the society's magazine The Vegan.

Definition and work

The society's definition describes veganism as "a philosophy and way of living which seeks to exclude — as far as is possible and practicable — all forms of exploitation of, and cruelty to, animals for food, clothing or any other purpose." The society campaigns, provides nutritional information, and operates the Vegan Trademark (introduced 1990), a sunflower label certifying that products contain no animal ingredients and were not tested on animals. It launched World Vegan Day (1 November) in 1994 and campaigns such as Veganuary-era outreach and "Live Vegan For Less."

Influence

From a handful of wartime members, the society helped seed vegan organisations worldwide; veganism's growth in the 2010s and 2020s — in food retail, restaurants and public awareness — traces much of its vocabulary and institutional roots to the society's founding.
