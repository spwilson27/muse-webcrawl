Gaius Julius Civilis was a hereditary prince of the Batavi, a Germanic people living in the Rhine delta of the Roman province of Germania Inferior, and the leader of the Batavian revolt against Rome in AD 69–70.

Background

Civilis was a Roman citizen and a veteran commander of Batavian auxiliary cohorts who had served Rome for decades. Arrested and sent to Rome in chains on a charge of treason, he was released by Galba and returned to Germania nursing grievances against Roman officials.

The revolt

Taking advantage of the civil wars of the Year of the Four Emperors, Civilis raised the Batavi and allied tribes, initially claiming to fight for Vespasian against Vitellius. Batavian forces destroyed Roman forts and defeated legionary detachments, besieging the legions at Vetera (Xanten). After Vespasian's victory, the revolt continued until the general Cerialis forced negotiations; Civilis's fate thereafter is unknown, as Tacitus's account breaks off. Rembrandt's famous painting The Conspiracy of Claudius Civilis depicts the rebels' oath.
