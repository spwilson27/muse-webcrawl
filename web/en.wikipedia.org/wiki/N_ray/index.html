N rays
Claimed: 1903, by Prosper-René Blondlot
Status: Discredited; classic case of pathological science

N rays were a hypothesized form of radiation described in 1903 by the French physicist Prosper-René Blondlot of the University of Nancy. The rays were said to be emitted by many substances, to increase the brightness of a faintly lit spark or screen, and to be refracted by aluminium prisms. Within a year, some 300 papers by around 100 scientists claimed to detect and study them.

Debunking

Other physicists, particularly outside France, failed to replicate the effects. In September 1904, the American physicist Robert W. Wood visited Blondlot's laboratory and, in the darkened room, secretly removed the aluminium prism essential to the apparatus; Blondlot's assistant continued to report readings as if the rays were still being refracted. Wood's account, published in Nature, effectively ended the episode.

Significance

N rays are now a textbook example of pathological science and experimenter bias: the effect depended on subjective judgements of faint changes in brightness, at the threshold of perception. The affair is frequently compared to later episodes such as polywater and cold fusion.
