Renewable Energy Directive

The Renewable Energy Directive is the legal framework for the development of clean energy across all sectors of the EU economy, supporting cooperation between EU countries towards this goal. The original directive (2009/28/EC) established a common framework for the promotion of energy from renewable sources and set binding national targets for the overall share of energy from renewable sources in gross final consumption of energy; the EU-wide target was 20% by 2020, with a separate 10% target for renewable energy in transport.

Revisions

The recast Renewable Energy Directive (EU) 2018/2001, known as RED II, entered into force in December 2018 and raised the overall EU target to at least 32% renewable energy by 2030. As part of the European Green Deal and the Fit for 55 package, the directive was revised again: Directive (EU) 2023/2413 (RED III) increased the binding target to 42.5% by 2030, with an additional indicative top-up of 2.5%, and introduced sectoral targets and faster permitting procedures for renewables.

Content

The directive sets rules on support schemes, guarantees of origin, sustainability and greenhouse gas emissions saving criteria for biofuels, bioliquids and biomass fuels, and procedures for joint projects between member states. Member states set out their contributions in integrated National Energy and Climate Plans.
