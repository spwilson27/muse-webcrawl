Eupleridae

Image: Fossa (Cryptoprocta ferox) (placeholder)

Eupleridae is a family of carnivorans endemic to Madagascar. It includes the fossa, the falanouc, the Malagasy civet, and several mongooses — all of the island's native terrestrial carnivores. Molecular studies show that these species descend from a single colonisation of Madagascar by an African ancestor roughly 18–24 million years ago, making the family a striking example of adaptive radiation.

Classification

The family is divided into two subfamilies: the Euplerinae, containing the fossa (Cryptoprocta ferox), the falanouc (Eupleres goudotii), and the Malagasy civet (Fossa fossana); and the Galidiinae, the Malagasy mongooses, including the ring-tailed mongoose and the broad-striped Malagasy mongoose. Historically these animals were scattered among the civet and mongoose families, but DNA evidence united them into a single endemic clade.

Characteristics

Euplerids range from the small narrow-striped mongoose, weighing under a kilogram, to the fossa, which at up to 1.8 metres in length (including tail) is Madagascar's largest predator. Diets vary from the fossa's lemur-hunting specialisation to the falanouc's earthworm-based feeding, reflected in its unusual small, non-shearing teeth.

Conservation

Most euplerids are threatened by habitat loss and hunting, and several are listed as vulnerable or endangered. The fossa is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN.
