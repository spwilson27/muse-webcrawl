Indigenous peoples in Canada

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples are recognised in the Constitution Act, 1982.

Indigenous peoples in Canada (also known as Aboriginals) are the Indigenous peoples within the boundaries of Canada. They comprise the First Nations, Inuit, and Métis, three groups recognised by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982. In the 2021 census, some 1.8 million people — about 5% of the population — identified as Indigenous, and the population is growing much faster than the non-Indigenous population.

History

Indigenous peoples have lived in what is now Canada for at least 12,000 years, developing diverse nations, trade networks, and governance systems. European contact from the 16th century brought trade, alliances, epidemics, and progressive colonisation. The Royal Proclamation of 1763 recognised Aboriginal title; the Numbered Treaties (1871–1921) covered much of the west and north. The Indian Act (1876) imposed federal control over status Indians and reserves, and the residential school system removed children from families in a policy the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2008–2015) called cultural genocide.

Peoples

First Nations number over 630 recognised bands speaking more than 50 languages. Inuit live across Inuit Nunangat in the Arctic. The Métis emerged from unions of First Nations women and European fur traders, centred on the Red River and recognised as a distinct people; the Supreme Court's Daniels decision (2016) confirmed federal responsibility for Métis and non-status Indians.

Rights and reconciliation

Landmark cases — Calder (1973), Delgamuukw (1997), Tsilhqot'in (2014) — progressively affirmed Aboriginal title. Comprehensive land claims and self-government agreements, the creation of Nunavut (1999), the federal apology for residential schools (2008), and Canada's adoption of the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples frame an unfinished reconciliation agenda, alongside persistent disparities in water, housing, health, and child welfare.
