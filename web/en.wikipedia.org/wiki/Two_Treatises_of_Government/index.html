Two Treatises of Government

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Two Treatises of Government (full title: Two Treatises of Government: In the Former, The False Principles, and Foundation of Sir Robert Filmer, and His Followers, Are Detected and Overthrown. The Latter Is an Essay Concerning The True Original, Extent, and End of Civil Government) is a work of political philosophy published anonymously in 1689 by John Locke. Written largely during the Exclusion Crisis years earlier and published after the Glorious Revolution, it destroyed the theoretical case for divine-right absolutism and set out the classic liberal account of legitimate government.

Contents

The First Treatise refutes Filmer's Patriarcha line by line, denying that Adam's supposed authority could ground hereditary kingship. The Second Treatise builds afresh: in the state of nature, men are free and equal under the law of nature, holding rights to life, liberty and property ("estates"); property arises when labour is mixed with the common stock (subject to leaving "enough and as good" for others); political society forms by consent to protect these rights through known laws, impartial judges and executive power; government is a trust, and a government that systematically violates it dissolves itself — the people may then resist and constitute a new one, the doctrine that armed 1688 and 1776. Locke's theory of property, consent and resistance remains the reference point of liberal constitutionalism.
