Platform (geology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In geology, a platform is a continental area covered by relatively flat or gently tilted, mainly sedimentary strata, which overlie a basement of consolidated igneous or metamorphic rocks of an earlier deformation. Platforms, together with shields and the basement rocks, constitute the stable interiors of continents known as cratons.

Platforms can be understood as cratons in which the basement rock is buried beneath younger, typically flat-lying sedimentary cover. Where the basement is exposed at the surface the corresponding area is called a shield; where the cover thickens into deep sedimentary basins, those basins form part of the broader platform region.

Characteristics

Platform sedimentary cover is typically a few hundred metres to several kilometres thick. It accumulated in shallow epicontinental seas that repeatedly flooded the stable continental interior during the Phanerozoic. Because the underlying basement has been tectonically quiet for hundreds of millions of years, the cover strata are usually only gently warped into broad domes, arches and basins rather than strongly folded.

Examples

  • The East European Platform, underlying much of European Russia and Ukraine beneath the Russian Plain.
  • The North American Platform, the sedimentary cover of the North American craton extending across the continental interior of Canada and the United States.
  • The Siberian Platform, between the Yenisei and Lena rivers in Russia.
  • The Yangtze Platform in southern China.
