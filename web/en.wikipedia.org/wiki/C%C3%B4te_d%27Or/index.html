Côte-d'Or
Department number: 21
Region: Bourgogne-Franche-Comté
Prefecture: Dijon
Population: ~530,000

Côte-d'Or is a department in eastern France, in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region. Its prefecture and largest city is Dijon. Created in 1790 during the French Revolution, the department takes its name — "golden slope" — from the vineyard-covered escarpment west of Dijon.

Wine

The Côte d'Or escarpment is the heart of Burgundy wine country, divided into the Côte de Nuits in the north and the Côte de Beaune in the south. Its limestone slopes produce some of the world's most celebrated Pinot noir and Chardonnay wines, including grand cru vineyards such as Romanée-Conti.

Geography

The department ranges from the Morvan foothills and the Langres plateau to the Saône plain. The Canal de Bourgogne crosses it, linking the Yonne and Saône rivers.
