Overview

Mbare, originally Harare (after Chief Neharawa, and renamed when the capital took the name Harare in 1982), is the oldest high-density suburb (township) of Harare, Zimbabwe, established in 1907 to house African workers near the colonial capital Salisbury. It is the home of Mbare Musika, the country's largest market for fruit and vegetables and its main long-distance bus terminus, through which much of Zimbabwe's informal trade flows.

Culture and events

Mbare produced the Mbare Chimurenga Choir and was a crucible of the liberation-era urban culture; its hostels (the notorious Matapi flats) and shebeens feature in Zimbabwean literature (Dambudzo Marechera lived its bohemian fringe). In 2005 Operation Murambatsvina demolished informal structures across Zimbabwe — Mbare's market and housing were among the most heavily hit, displacing tens of thousands locally. The suburb remains shorthand in Zimbabwean life for hustle, commerce and street wisdom.
