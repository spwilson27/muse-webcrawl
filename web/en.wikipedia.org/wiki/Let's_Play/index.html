Let's Play

A Let's Play (LP) is a video (or a series of screenshots accompanied by text) documenting the playthrough of a video game, usually including commentary by the player. Let's Plays differ from a walkthrough or strategy guide in their focus on an individual's subjective experience with the game, often with humorous, irreverent, or critical commentary rather than a purely informational one. The format became one of the dominant genres of online video in the 2010s and underpins much of YouTube and livestreaming gaming culture.

History

The term is traced to the Something Awful forums around 2006–2007. A 2006 thread by user slowbeef playing The Immortal is often cited as an early example, and the screenshot-based format — in which a player posted images and narration of a game for an audience that could vote on decisions — was popularised by Michael Sawyer ("slowbeef"), who is credited with coining the term "Let's Play" and founding the genre's conventions. The format migrated from forum threads to video with the growth of YouTube and capture tools, and livestreaming platforms such as Twitch extended it into real-time performance. Creators such as PewDiePie built the largest individual audiences on YouTube largely on Let's Play content, and the genre became a significant marketing channel for game publishers.

Formats

  • Screenshot Let's Plays — the original forum format, using images and written narration, often for strategy or role-playing games.
  • Video Let's Plays — recorded gameplay with voice commentary, sometimes with a facecam picture-in-picture.
  • Live Let's Plays — streamed playthroughs with live audience interaction via chat.
  • Blind Let's Plays — played without prior knowledge of the game, emphasising genuine reaction.
  • Challenge and completionist runs — playthroughs aiming at 100% completion or self-imposed restrictions; related to speedrunning.

Legal status

Let's Plays reproduce large amounts of copyrighted game footage and audio, and their legality has long been debated. Some publishers, most notably Nintendo in its early Creators Program (2015–2018), claimed a share of advertising revenue or issued takedowns, while most publishers came to tolerate or encourage the format under published content-usage policies because of its promotional value. Commentators have argued that transformative commentary can make such videos fair use, but the question has not been definitively settled in court.

Impact

The genre reshaped how games are bought and played: research and industry surveys have found substantial minorities of players who watch games as often as they play them. It also professionalised online video creation, launched media networks and merchandising businesses, and influenced game design toward "streamable" moments and spectator modes.
