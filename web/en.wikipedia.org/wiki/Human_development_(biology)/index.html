Human development (biology)

Human development is the process of growing to maturity. In biological terms, this entails growth from a one-celled zygote to an adult human being. Fertilisation occurs when the sperm cell successfully enters and fuses with an egg cell (ovum). The genetic material of the sperm and egg then combine to form a single cell called a zygote, and the germinal stage of prenatal development commences.

Prenatal development

Prenatal development is divided into three stages. The germinal stage lasts from fertilisation until about two weeks, during which the zygote divides repeatedly and implants in the uterine wall. The embryonic stage runs from the third through the eighth week, when the basic structures of all major organs form from the three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm. The fetal stage extends from the ninth week until birth, characterised by growth and maturation of the organs and body systems.

Infancy and childhood

After birth, the newborn (infant) undergoes rapid physical growth, roughly tripling birth weight in the first year. Motor milestones such as sitting, crawling and walking emerge in a predictable sequence. Childhood is marked by steady growth, the development of language, and increasing cognitive and social capacities.

Adolescence

Adolescence begins with puberty, when hormonal changes trigger the development of secondary sexual characteristics and a growth spurt. Brain development, particularly of the prefrontal cortex, continues into the mid-twenties.

Adulthood and aging

Physical peak is typically reached in the twenties and thirties. Thereafter, gradual changes associated with aging (senescence) occur, including reduced muscle mass, bone density loss and changes in sensory acuity, until death.
