Benjamin O. Davis Jr.

Benjamin Oliver Davis Jr. (December 18, 1912 – July 4, 2002) was a United States Air Force general and commander of the World War II Tuskegee Airmen. He was the first African-American brigadier general in the United States Air Force.

Benjamin O. Davis Jr.
Born: 18 December 1912, Washington, D.C.
Died: 4 July 2002
Service: U.S. Army / U.S. Air Force
Rank: General (four-star, advanced 1998)
Commands: 99th Fighter Squadron, 332nd Fighter Group

Career

Davis graduated from West Point in 1936 despite being shunned by fellow cadets. He commanded the all-Black 99th Fighter Squadron and later the 332nd Fighter Group — the Tuskegee Airmen — in Europe, building an outstanding escort record. After the war he helped integrate the Air Force, served in Korea and Vietnam-era commands, and retired a lieutenant general in 1970. In 1998 President Bill Clinton advanced him to full general.

Legacy

His father, Benjamin O. Davis Sr., was the first African-American general in the U.S. Army. Davis Jr.'s memoir is Benjamin O. Davis Jr.: American (1991).
