Aldeburgh is a coastal town in the county of Suffolk, England, on the North Sea coast north of the River Alde. Once a thriving medieval port, it is best known today as the home of the composer Benjamin Britten and of the Aldeburgh Festival of Music and the Arts, founded in 1948.

History

Aldeburgh was a port of consequence in the 16th century, with a shipbuilding industry that built vessels associated with the Elizabethan age of exploration. Its fortunes declined as the River Alde silted and the shoreline shifted; two-thirds of the old town was lost to the sea. The Tudor Moot Hall (c. 1520) survives as a landmark on the seafront.

Culture

Britten settled in Aldeburgh in 1947 with the tenor Peter Pears. His opera Peter Grimes (1945), set in a Suffolk fishing community inspired by the poet George Crabbe's The Borough, is closely associated with the town. In 1967 the festival moved much of its programme to the Snape Maltings concert hall, a few miles inland. The Red House in Aldeburgh, Britten and Pears's home, is now a museum and archive.

Landmarks

  • The Moot Hall, a timber-framed Tudor building housing a small museum
  • The Martello tower at Slaughden, the largest and northernmost of the chain
  • The Scallop sculpture by Maggi Hambling on the beach, dedicated to Britten
  • Aldeburgh lifeboat station
