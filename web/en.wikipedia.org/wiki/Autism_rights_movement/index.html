Autism rights movement


Image placeholder: autism acceptance event

The autism rights movement, also known as the autistic acceptance movement, is a social movement allied with the disability rights movement that emphasises the concept of neurodiversity, viewing autism as a natural variation of the human brain rather than a disorder to be cured.

History

Activist Jim Sinclair's 1993 essay "Don't Mourn for Us", addressed to parents at an autism conference, is widely regarded as a founding document of the movement. Autistic-led organisations followed, including the Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN), founded in 2006, which popularised the slogan "Nothing about us without us".

Goals and positions

The movement advocates for greater acceptance of autistic behaviours (including stimming), services that improve quality of life rather than enforce conformity, communication rights for non-speaking autistic people, and more autistic involvement in research and policy. Many advocates prefer identity-first language ("autistic person") over person-first language, and commemorate Autism Acceptance Month rather than awareness campaigns they regard as stigmatising. The movement has campaigned against therapies it considers harmful or coercive and against organisations it regards as speaking over autistic people.

Symbols

The rainbow-coloured infinity symbol is commonly used to represent neurodiversity and autistic pride, and red or gold are preferred by many advocates over the blue associated with Autism Speaks' "Light It Up Blue" campaign.
