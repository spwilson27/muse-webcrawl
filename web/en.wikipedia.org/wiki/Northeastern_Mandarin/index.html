Northeastern Mandarin

Northeastern Mandarin (simplified Chinese: 东北官话; traditional Chinese: 東北官話; pinyin: Dōngběiguānhuà, "Northeast Mandarin") is a group of Mandarin dialects spoken across Northeast China (Manchuria), including the provinces of Liaoning, Jilin and Heilongjiang, as well as parts of eastern Inner Mongolia. It has on the order of 80–100 million speakers.

History

The region was largely closed to Han Chinese settlement under the Qing dynasty, in part by the Willow Palisade, and Northeastern Mandarin developed mainly from the dialects of Shandong and Zhili (Hebei) migrants who arrived in large numbers in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — the movement known as Chuǎng Guāndōng ("crashing into Manchuria"). Because it formed recently from similar source dialects, the group is comparatively uniform.

Features

Northeastern Mandarin is closely related to, but distinct from, the Beijing dialect underlying Standard Chinese. Characteristic features include a distinctive vocabulary (for example, words of Manchu and Russian origin), the retention or loss of certain initials compared with the standard, and the merging or redistribution of some entering-tone syllables among the four tones. Subgroups are commonly divided into the Ji–Liao, Ha–Fu and Hei–Song varieties, named after the areas they cover. The dialect is widely recognised in China through Northeastern comedy traditions such as errenzhuan and the sketches of performers from the region.
