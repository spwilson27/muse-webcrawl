U.S. Customs and Border Protection

U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is the largest federal law enforcement agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security. It is charged with regulating and facilitating international trade, collecting import duties, and enforcing U.S. regulations, including trade, customs, and immigration. CBP is one of the largest law enforcement agencies in the United States, with more than 60,000 employees.

History

CBP was established in 2003 when the Homeland Security Act merged the United States Customs Service, the Border Patrol, and inspection functions from the Immigration and Naturalization Service and the Department of Agriculture into a single border agency within the new Department of Homeland Security.

Organization

The agency includes the Office of Field Operations, which staffs officers at ports of entry; the United States Border Patrol, which secures the borders between ports of entry; and Air and Marine Operations, which provides aviation and maritime interdiction. CBP officers inspect travellers and cargo at more than 300 ports of entry across the United States.

Responsibilities

CBP's priorities include preventing terrorists and weapons from entering the United States, stemming illegal immigration and drug smuggling, protecting agriculture and economic interests, and facilitating lawful travel and trade.
