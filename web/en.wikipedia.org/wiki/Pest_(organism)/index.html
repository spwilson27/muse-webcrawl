Pest (organism)

A pest is any organism harmful to humans or human concerns: an animal, plant, fungus, or microorganism that damages crops, livestock, stored goods, buildings, or health, or that is simply a nuisance. The term is anthropocentric — no species is a pest in itself; an organism is a pest in a particular place, at a particular time, from a human point of view.

Categories

Agricultural pests include insects such as aphids, locusts, and boll weevils; plant pathogens such as rusts, mildews, and viruses; and weeds, which are pest plants competing with crops. Household and structural pests include cockroaches, bed bugs, termites, and rodents. Some animals, such as rats and certain birds, are also disease vectors of public-health significance. Invasive species frequently become pests when introduced to new regions without their natural enemies.

Control

Pest control methods include chemical pesticides, biological control using predators, parasites, or pathogens; cultural practices such as crop rotation; mechanical methods such as traps and barriers; and genetic approaches including sterile insect technique. Integrated pest management (IPM) combines these methods, applying chemicals only when pest numbers exceed economic thresholds, in order to limit resistance, environmental damage, and harm to non-target species.
