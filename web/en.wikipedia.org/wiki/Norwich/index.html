Norwich

Country: England
Region: East of England
County: Norfolk
Population: approx. 144,000

Norwich is a cathedral city and district of the county of Norfolk, England, of which it is the county town. It lies on the River Wensum in East Anglia, about 100 miles (160 km) north-east of London. In the Middle Ages it was one of England's largest and most prosperous cities, grown wealthy on the wool trade, and it retains an exceptional stock of medieval buildings, including its Norman cathedral and castle.

History

The site was settled by the Anglo-Saxons by the seventh century, near the earlier Iceni capital at Venta Icenorum. By the time of the Domesday Book (1086) Norwich was one of the largest towns in England. Its wealth rested on worsted cloth, exported through the port of Great Yarmouth; from the sixteenth century, religious refugees from the Low Countries — the "Strangers" — revitalised its textile industry and famously introduced canaries, now the emblem of Norwich City Football Club. In 2012 Norwich became England's first UNESCO City of Literature.

Landmarks

Norwich Cathedral, begun in 1096, is one of the finest Romanesque cathedrals in England, noted for its stone spire — the second tallest in the country — and its large cloister. Norwich Castle, built by the Normans as a royal palace, now houses a museum and art gallery. The city has 57 surviving medieval parish churches within its walls, more than any other city north of the Alps, and the complete medieval street patterns of areas such as Elm Hill survive largely intact.

Education and culture

The University of East Anglia, founded in 1963 on a campus west of the city designed around Denys Lasdun's teaching wall and ziggurat residences, is noted for its creative writing programme. The Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts, designed by Norman Foster, stands on the campus. Norwich is also home to the Norfolk and Norwich Festival, one of the oldest arts festivals in England.
