Akashi (明石市, Akashi-shi) is a city in southern Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, on the coast of the Seto Inland Sea, west of Kobe. The city lies on the 135th meridian east, which defines Japan Standard Time, and is home to the Akashi Municipal Planetarium, the oldest operating planetarium in Japan, which marks the meridian.

Geography

Akashi faces the Akashi Strait, which separates Honshu from Awaji Island. The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge, opened in 1998 and for many years the longest suspension bridge in the world, links the city (at its Tarumi ward neighbour in Kobe) with Awaji Island and onward to Shikoku.

History

Akashi developed as a castle town under the feudal lords of Akashi Domain; Akashi Castle was built in 1619. The town is mentioned in classical literature, including The Tale of Genji, whose hero spends part of his exile on this coast. In 1951, a stampede at a New Year shrine festival killed many people, an event still recalled locally. The Great Hanshin earthquake of January 1995 had its epicentre in the Akashi Strait just off the city, causing severe damage in the region.

Culture and food

Akashi is famous for akashiyaki, soft egg-and-octopus dumplings dipped in dashi broth, considered the local ancestor of the nationally popular takoyaki. The Uonotana market street near the station is known for fresh seafood from the strait, where strong tidal currents produce prized fish and octopus.
