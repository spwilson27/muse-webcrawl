Oswego Canal

LocationCentral New York, United States
Length~38 miles (61 km)
Opened1828 (original); 1917 (modern barge canal)
ConnectsErie Canal at Syracuse area to Lake Ontario at Oswego

The Oswego Canal is a canal in New York State, part of the New York State Canal System, linking the Erie Canal near Syracuse with Lake Ontario at the port of Oswego. It follows the course of the Oswego River and the Oneida River along a corridor used for centuries by the Haudenosaunee and by traders travelling between the Great Lakes and the Hudson valley.

History

The original Oswego Canal opened in 1828, running from the Erie Canal at Salina (Syracuse) north to Oswego. It gave upstate New York a direct water route to Lake Ontario and made Oswego a significant grain and lumber port. The canal was enlarged in the mid-19th century alongside the Erie enlargement. In the early 20th century it was rebuilt as part of the New York State Barge Canal: the modern Oswego Canal, opened in 1917, channelised the Oswego River with a series of locks and dams, passing through Phoenix, Fulton and Minetto.

Present use

Today the canal is used mainly by recreational boaters travelling between the Erie Canal and Lake Ontario. Its eight locks lift and lower vessels roughly 118 feet (36 m) between Three Rivers, where the Oswego meets the Oneida and Seneca rivers, and Oswego Harbor.
