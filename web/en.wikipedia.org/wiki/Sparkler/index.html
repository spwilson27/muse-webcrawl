Sparkler

A sparkler is a type of hand-held firework that burns slowly while emitting bright, intense sparks. It consists of a thin metal wire coated with a slow-burning pyrotechnic composition. Sparklers are popular at celebrations such as Independence Day in the United States, Guy Fawkes Night in the United Kingdom, Diwali in India, and New Year's Eve, and are frequently used at weddings.

Composition

The pyrotechnic coating of a traditional sparkler typically contains a metallic fuel (such as aluminium, iron, or steel powder), an oxidizer (commonly barium nitrate or potassium nitrate), and a binder such as dextrin. The metal particles oxidize as they are thrown off, producing the characteristic shower of sparks. Different metal salts produce different spark colours: iron gives golden-orange sparks, while titanium produces bright white sparks. "Morning glory" sparklers, popular in Asia, use a paper tube instead of a wire and may change colour as they burn.

History

Sparkler-like devices trace their ancestry to the handheld fireworks of ancient China and to the "Cherosiphon" described in Byzantine sources. Modern wire sparklers were developed in Germany in the early 20th century; Franz Jacob Harder is often credited with popularizing the modern design around 1905. German sparklers were exported worldwide under names such as Wunderkerze ("wonder candle").

Safety

Despite their gentle appearance, sparklers burn at extremely high temperatures—hot enough to melt some metals—and are a leading cause of firework-related injuries to young children. Safety authorities recommend adult supervision, keeping sparklers away from the face, hair, and clothing, and immersing used wires in water, since they remain hot after the visible flame goes out. Some jurisdictions restrict or ban their sale.

In culture

Sparklers are associated with festive occasions worldwide. In Japan, slender senkō hanabi sparklers are a traditional summer pastime, appreciated for the brief, delicate spark that forms a glowing droplet. Long-exposure photographs of people "drawing" shapes with sparklers are a popular artistic technique known as light painting.
