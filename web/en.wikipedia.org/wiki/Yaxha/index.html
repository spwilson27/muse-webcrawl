Yaxha (also spelled Yaxhá) is a Mesoamerican archaeological site of the Maya civilization in the Petén lowlands of northeastern Guatemala, on the north shore of Lake Yaxha. It was one of the largest Classic-period Maya cities — behind only Tikal and El Mirador in the region — with a ceremonial core of more than 500 structures, including nine temple pyramids, twin-pyramid complexes, palace acropolises, ballcourts, and a network of causeways (sacbeob) linking it to the satellite centers of Nakum and Naranjo. Its name means "blue-green water" in Yucatec Maya, a reference to the lake.

History

Yaxha was occupied for nearly two and a half millennia, from the Middle Preclassic (c. 1000 BC) into the Postclassic period, with its apogee in the Late Classic (AD 600–900), when its population is estimated in the tens of thousands. Its earliest monumental architecture includes an E-Group astronomical complex of the Preclassic. Through the Classic period Yaxha maneuvered among the great powers of the Petén — Tikal, Calakmul, Caracol, and its near neighbor Naranjo — with which it was joined by causeway and by turns allied and at war; stela inscriptions record its kings, its dynastic ties, and its military vicissitudes, including defeats and royal captures in the 8th century as the region descended into the wars of the Terminal Classic. Uniquely among large Petén centers, Yaxha shows substantial occupation after the Classic collapse, and the lakeside city continued into the Postclassic era, part of the same lake-district world that produced the Itza capital at Tayasal on Lake Petén Itzá.

The site

The site's principal groups are laid out along an east–west axis of causeways above the lakeshore. The North Acropolis and the great pyramid known as Structure 216 (over 30 m tall, the city's highest, famous for its sunset view over the lake) dominate the core; twin-pyramid complexes — the hallmark ritual architecture invented at Tikal — mark the celebration of k'atun endings. The Maler Group, Lincoln Group, and the royal palace complex preserve carved and plain stelae (40 recorded), altars, and remains of a port and canal system on the lake. Since the 1990s, Guatemala's IDAEH and German archaeological missions have excavated and consolidated the site, now protected within the Yaxha–Nakum–Naranjo National Park.

In popular culture

Yaxha reached a wide international audience in 2005 as the principal filming location of the American reality series Survivor: Guatemala — The Maya Empire, whose production camp among the ruins brought both publicity and archaeological debate about the use of protected sites.
