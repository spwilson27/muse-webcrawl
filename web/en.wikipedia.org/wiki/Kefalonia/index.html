Kefalonia

Kefalonia or Cephalonia (Greek: Κεφαλονιά) is the largest of the Ionian Islands in western Greece, with an area of about 773 km² and a population of roughly 36,000. Its capital is Argostoli, in the south-west. The island is mountainous, dominated by Mount Ainos (1,628 m), and is known for the Melissani cave lake, Drogarati cave, Myrtos Beach and its Robola wine.

History

Kefalonia was settled in antiquity by Greek city-states including Sami, whose acropolis still stands. It passed through Roman, Byzantine, Norman, Venetian and brief Ottoman and French hands before joining the modern Greek state in 1864 as part of the Ionian Islands. In September 1943, after the Italian armistice, German forces massacred several thousand Italian soldiers of the Acqui Division on the island — an event later depicted in Louis de Bernières' novel Captain Corelli's Mandolin (1994), set on Kefalonia and filmed there in 2001.

1953 earthquake

The great Ionian earthquake of August 1953 (magnitude 7.2) devastated Kefalonia and neighbouring Zakynthos, destroying nearly every building outside the northern Fiskardo peninsula and prompting mass emigration; the island's population fell by tens of thousands. Rebuilt villages retain a distinctive post-quake architectural character.

Geography and nature

Mount Ainos is a national park covered in Greek fir forest. Loggerhead sea turtles nest around Argostoli's Koutavos lagoon, and the island's sinkholes and underground rivers — seawater famously flows inland from Katavothres near Argostoli to emerge at Melissani on the opposite coast — are classic karst phenomena.
