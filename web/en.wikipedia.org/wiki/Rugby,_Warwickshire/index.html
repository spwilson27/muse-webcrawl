Rugby is a market town in Warwickshire, England, close to the River Avon and near the borders with Leicestershire and Northamptonshire. It is the second-largest town in Warwickshire and is best known internationally as the birthplace of rugby football.

History

Rugby began as a small Anglo-Saxon settlement and is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as "Rocheberie". It remained a small market town for centuries. Rugby School was founded in 1567 by Lawrence Sheriff, a London grocer originally from the town. In the 19th century the town grew rapidly as a railway junction: the London and Birmingham Railway arrived in 1838, and the station became a major interchange on the West Coast Main Line.

Rugby football

The town gave its name to the sport of rugby after, according to tradition, William Webb Ellis, a pupil at Rugby School, picked up the ball and ran with it during a football match in 1823. The Webb Ellis Cup, awarded to the winners of the Rugby World Cup, commemorates him. The town has a museum and the World Rugby Hall of Fame celebrates the game's heritage.

Industry

Rugby became an important engineering centre. Companies associated with the town include British Thomson-Houston (later GEC), jet engine pioneer Frank Whittle carried out development work on his turbojet engine at Rugby in the 1930s, and the town has long-standing cement works. Holography inventor Dennis Gabor also worked in Rugby.

Transport

Rugby railway station lies on the West Coast Main Line with services to London Euston, Birmingham and the north-west. The M1, M6 and A14 roads pass close to the town, and the Oxford Canal runs through the area.
