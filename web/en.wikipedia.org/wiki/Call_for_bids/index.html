A call for bids, also known as an invitation to bid (ITB) or invitation to tender, is a formal solicitation issued by a buyer — commonly a government agency or large organisation — inviting suppliers or contractors to submit competing bids to provide specified goods, services or construction work. It is a central instrument of procurement, intended to promote competition, transparency and value for money.

Process

The buyer publishes a document describing the requirement, specifications, terms and conditions, submission deadline and evaluation criteria. Prospective bidders may attend pre-bid meetings and submit questions; bids are then submitted by the deadline, often sealed, and opened and evaluated according to the stated criteria. In many public systems the contract is awarded to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder when specifications are fully defined; where quality and technical merit matter more, a request for proposal procedure may be used instead.

Legal framework

Public calls for bids are regulated to prevent favouritism and corruption, for example under the WTO Agreement on Government Procurement, EU procurement directives, and national and local procurement statutes. Electronic procurement portals have largely replaced newspaper advertisements as the medium of publication.
