Battle of Balaclava

The Battle of Balaclava, fought on 25 October 1854 during the siege of Sevastopol in the Crimean War, was an attempt by Russian forces under General Pavel Liprandi to break the siege by attacking the British supply base at Balaclava harbour. The battle is remembered for three famous episodes: the "Thin Red Line" of the 93rd Highlanders repelling Russian cavalry, the successful Charge of the Heavy Brigade, and the catastrophic Charge of the Light Brigade.

Course

Russians seized the Turkish-manned redoubts on the Causeway Heights at dawn. When Russian cavalry advanced toward Balaclava village, Sir Colin Campbell's two-deep line of Highlanders — the reporter's "thin red streak tipped with a line of steel" — volleyed them back. Scarlett's Heavy Brigade then routed a much larger Russian cavalry force uphill. Lord Raglan's ambiguous order to recover the redoubt guns then sent Cardigan's Light Brigade down the wrong valley against the main Russian battery, in the war's most famous blunder; French intervention saved the survivors. The Russians celebrated holding the heights; the allies kept their port but lost the Woronzoff road, worsening the winter supply crisis that followed.
