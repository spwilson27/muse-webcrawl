Hugh Todd Naylor Gaitskell CBE (9 April 1906 – 18 January 1963) was a British politician who served as Leader of the Labour Party from 1955 until his sudden death in 1963. A wartime civil servant and economist, he entered Parliament in 1945 as MP for Leeds South and rose rapidly in Clement Attlee's government, becoming Minister of Fuel and Power and then, in 1950, Chancellor of the Exchequer.

Chancellor and opposition leader

As Chancellor during the Korean War, Gaitskell's 1951 budget imposed charges on NHS dentures and spectacles to fund rearmament, provoking the resignation of Aneurin Bevan and a lasting split between Gaitskellites and Bevanites on the Labour right and left. After Labour's 1955 election defeat he defeated Bevan for the leadership. He led the party through the Suez Crisis — fiercely opposing Anthony Eden's invasion of Egypt — and through the 1959 election defeat, after which he unsuccessfully attempted to revise Clause IV of the party constitution, the commitment to public ownership. He is remembered for his pledge to "fight, fight and fight again" to reverse the 1960 party conference vote for unilateral nuclear disarmament, which he succeeded in overturning in 1961, and for opposing British entry into the European Economic Community on the terms negotiated in 1962.

Death

Gaitskell died suddenly in January 1963 of complications of lupus, aged 56, when widely expected to become prime minister at the next election. He was succeeded by Harold Wilson. His unexpected death later attracted conspiracy theories, for which no evidence has emerged.
