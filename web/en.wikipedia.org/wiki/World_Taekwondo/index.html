World Taekwondo (WT), formerly the World Taekwondo Federation (WTF), is the international governing body for the sport of taekwondo and is a member of the Association of Summer Olympic International Federations. It governs the Olympic style of the Korean martial art, distinct from the International Taekwon-do Federation (ITF) tradition.

History

The federation was established on 28 May 1973 at the Kukkiwon in Seoul, South Korea, during the first World Taekwondo Championships, with Un Yong Kim as its first president. Taekwondo appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1988 and 1992 Olympic Games and became a full medal sport at the Sydney 2000 Olympics. In 2017 the organization rebranded as World Taekwondo, in part to distance its initials from an internet slang usage.

Competitions

WT sanctions the biennial World Taekwondo Championships, the World Taekwondo Grand Prix series, the World Taekwondo Poomsae Championships (for forms), junior and cadet championships, and para taekwondo events. Para taekwondo made its Paralympic debut at Tokyo 2020.

Competition format

Sparring (kyorugi) bouts are contested over rounds on an octagonal mat, with points awarded for kicks and punches to the trunk and head; electronic scoring systems in the body protector and headgear register valid strikes. Athletes compete in weight categories, and Olympic taekwondo uses four weight classes per gender.
