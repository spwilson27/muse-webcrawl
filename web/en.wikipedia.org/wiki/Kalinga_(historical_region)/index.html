Kalinga (historical region)

Kalinga is a historical region on the east coast of India, roughly corresponding to modern Odisha and adjoining parts of Andhra Pradesh. It is famous for the Kalinga War fought by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka around 261 BCE.

Kalinga War

Ashoka's conquest of Kalinga was exceptionally bloody; according to his own edicts, 100,000 were killed and 150,000 deported. The carnage is traditionally said to have filled Ashoka with remorse and led to his embrace of Buddhism and a policy of dhamma.

Later history

In the following centuries Kalinga was ruled by dynasties including the Mahameghavahana, Eastern Ganga and Gajapati kingdoms, and its ports traded across the Bay of Bengal with Southeast Asia.
