Aldo Moro (Italian: [ˈaldo ˈmɔːro]; 23 September 1916 – 9 May 1978) was an Italian statesman and one of the longest-serving post-war prime ministers of Italy, leading the country in five governments between 1963 and 1976. A leading figure of the Christian Democracy party, he was kidnapped by the far-left Red Brigades in March 1978 and murdered after 55 days in captivity — one of the defining tragedies of Italy's Years of Lead.

Political career

A law professor from Apulia, Moro entered parliament in 1946 and served as foreign minister and minister of justice. As prime minister he led the "centre-left" coalition that brought the Italian Socialist Party into government. In the 1970s, as president of his party, he promoted the strategy of the "historic compromise" — an opening of dialogue toward the Italian Communist Party — and was preparing a government of national solidarity supported by the Communists when he was seized.

Kidnapping and death

On 16 March 1978 Red Brigades gunmen ambushed Moro's car in Via Fani, Rome, killing his five bodyguards and driver. During his captivity Moro wrote letters pleading for negotiations; the government, backed by most parties, refused to bargain with the terrorists. On 9 May 1978 his body was found in the boot of a car parked in Via Caetani, midway between the headquarters of the Christian Democrats and the Communists.

Legacy

The Moro affair left enduring questions about the role of domestic and foreign interests in the outcome, and it remains a watershed in the history of the Italian Republic.
