Genghis Khan (c. 1162–1227), born Temüjin, was the founder and first khan of the Mongol Empire, the largest contiguous land empire in history. He united the nomadic tribes of the Mongolian steppe and launched invasions that conquered northern China, Central Asia and Persia.

Legacy

Genghis's armies were superbly organised and ruthless — cities that resisted were destroyed — yet his rule brought the Pax Mongolica: secured trade routes (the Silk Road), religious tolerance, a written script for Mongolian and a unified Eurasian order. His descendants ruled China (Yuan dynasty), Persia, and Russia's steppe for generations.
