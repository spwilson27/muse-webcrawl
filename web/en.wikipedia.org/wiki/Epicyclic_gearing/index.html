Epicyclic gearing

Epicyclic gearing
Also calledPlanetary gearing
ComponentsSun gear, planet gears, planet carrier, ring (annulus) gear
UsesAutomatic transmissions, hub gears, hybrid vehicles, wind turbines

Epicyclic gearing, also known as a planetary gear system, is a gear arrangement consisting of one or more planet gears rotating around a central sun gear, all meshing within a fixed or rotating outer ring gear (annulus). The planets are mounted on a movable carrier connected to the output shaft.

Operation

By holding different members stationary, a single planetary set yields several gear ratios, including reverse and direct drive. For example, with the ring gear fixed, input to the sun and output from the carrier gives a speed reduction; holding the carrier makes the planets act as idlers and reverses the output.

History

Epicyclic mechanisms appear in the ancient Antikythera mechanism. James Watt used a sun-and-planet gear to convert reciprocating to rotary motion in his steam engines, circumventing a patent on the crank.

Applications

Planetary gears are compact and can transmit high torque, making them standard in automatic transmissions, bicycle hub gears, electric-vehicle reducers, hybrid power-split devices such as the Toyota Prius transmission, and wind-turbine gearboxes.
