Durga Puja

Durga Puja is an annual Hindu festival originating in the Indian subcontinent that reveres the goddess Durga, celebrating her victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura. It is particularly celebrated in West Bengal, Assam, Tripura, Odisha, Bihar and Bangladesh, during the month of Ashvin (September–October), coinciding with the wider Navaratri festival.

Celebrations

In Kolkata, Durga Puja is a vast public festival: neighbourhood committees erect thousands of elaborate temporary temples (pandals) housing large clay images of Durga slaying Mahishasura, flanked by Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartikeya. The six days from Mahalaya to Vijayadashami include rituals such as bodhon, sandhi puja and the immersion (visarjan) of the images in the Hooghly River. In 2021 UNESCO inscribed "Durga Puja in Kolkata" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Ritual and meaning

The festival marks the triumph of good over evil and honours female divine power (Shakti). It is also a harvest festival and, for Bengalis worldwide, a homecoming: Durga is imagined as a daughter returning to her parental home with her children.
