The John Lewis Partnership is a British company that operates the John Lewis department stores and the Waitrose supermarket chain. It is the largest employee-owned business in the United Kingdom: all permanent staff, known as Partners, are beneficiaries of a trust that holds the business on their behalf.

History

The business began in 1864 when John Lewis opened a draper's shop on Oxford Street in London. His son, John Spedan Lewis, developed the idea of co-ownership and in 1929 and 1950 transferred his controlling interest into trust settlements for the employees, establishing the Partnership's unusual constitution and its principle of sharing profit, knowledge and power. The company acquired the Waitrose grocery business in 1937.

Operations

The Partnership trades through dozens of John Lewis stores — long known for the "Never Knowingly Undersold" price pledge, retired in 2022 — and several hundred Waitrose shops, together with substantial online operations. Partners receive an annual bonus as a share of profits, and elect representatives to the Partnership Council.
