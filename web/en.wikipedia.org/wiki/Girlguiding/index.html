Girlguiding

Founded: 1910

Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

Membership: ~300,000 members and volunteers

Chief Guide: Tracy Foster (from 2023)

Affiliation: WAGGGS

Girlguiding is the operating name of The Guide Association, the national Guiding organisation of the United Kingdom. It is the UK's largest girl-only youth organisation, offering fun, friendship, challenge, and adventure to girls and young women aged 4 to 18 through local units led by trained volunteers. Girlguiding is a founding member of the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS).

History

Guiding began in 1909 when a group of girls gatecrashed the first Scout rally at Crystal Palace demanding "something for the girls." Robert Baden-Powell asked his sister Agnes Baden-Powell to organise a parallel movement, and The Girl Guides Association was formally established in 1910, with Agnes as its first president; their sister-in-law Olave Baden-Powell later became Chief Guide and led the movement's rapid growth. The association received a royal charter in 1922 and adopted the operating name Girlguiding in 2013.

Sections

The programme is divided by age:

  • Rainbows — ages 4–7
  • Brownies — ages 7–10
  • Guides — ages 10–14
  • Rangers — ages 14–18

Adult volunteers (18+) lead units, and young women aged 18–30 can participate in development opportunities. All members make a Promise; its wording has been revised over time to reflect a diverse, inclusive membership, and since 2013 it has pledged members "to be true to myself and develop my beliefs."

Activities

Units meet weekly for games, crafts, outdoor adventure, camps, and skills-builder and interest badges covering topics from survival and aviation to campaigning and coding. Girlguiding runs national activity centres, campaigns on issues affecting girls, and publishes the annual Girls' Attitudes Survey, a major research source on girls' wellbeing and views in the UK.
