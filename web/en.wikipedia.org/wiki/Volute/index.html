Volute

A volute is a spiral, scroll-like ornament used on the capital of an Ionic column, and more generally a spiral curve or form in art, architecture, nature and engineering. The word derives from the Latin voluta, "scroll".

Architecture

In classical architecture the paired volutes of the Ionic order are its defining feature: two spirals curl outward from beneath the abacus on the front and back of the capital. The Corinthian capital incorporates smaller volutes, called caulicoli, and the Composite order combines large Ionic volutes with Corinthian acanthus leaves. Renaissance and Baroque architects used volutes on a larger scale, for example in the great scrolls linking the nave to the lower aisles on church façades such as Santa Maria Novella in Florence and Il Gesù in Rome. Volutes also appear in furniture, stair newels and musical instruments such as the scroll of a violin.

Other uses

In engineering, a volute is the spiral casing of a centrifugal pump or compressor that collects the fluid discharged by the impeller and converts its velocity into pressure. In zoology, "volute" is the name of a family of predatory sea snails (Volutidae) with spiral shells.
