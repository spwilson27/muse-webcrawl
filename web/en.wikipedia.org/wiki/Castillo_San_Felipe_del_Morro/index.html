Castillo San Felipe del Morro

Castillo San Felipe del Morro, also known as El Morro, is a citadel built between the 16th and 18th centuries in San Juan, Puerto Rico. It lies on the northwesternmost point of the islet of Old San Juan, overlooking the entrance to San Juan Bay, and was built to guard the city and the bay against seaborne attack.

History

Construction began in 1539 on a promontory — el morro — at the harbor entrance, starting with a simple tower; the fortress was expanded over some 250 years into a six-level citadel in the bastioned trace style. El Morro withstood attacks by Sir Francis Drake in 1595 and by the Dutch in 1625, and it repelled the British assault of 1797. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, it was bombarded by the United States Navy.

Modern era

The U.S. military used the site through the world wars (as Fort Brooke), and in 1961 it was transferred to the National Park Service. Together with Castillo San Cristóbal and the old city walls, it forms the San Juan National Historic Site, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983. The grounds, with their garitas (sentry boxes), lighthouse, and broad esplanade, are among Puerto Rico's most visited landmarks.
