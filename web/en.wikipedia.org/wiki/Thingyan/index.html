Thingyan is the Burmese New Year festival, celebrated in mid-April as the culmination of the Burmese calendar year. It is a Theravada Buddhist festival cognate with Songkran in Thailand and Laos, Pi Mai in Laos, and similar new year water festivals across South and Southeast Asia.

Customs

The festival's defining activity is the throwing and pouring of water, symbolising the washing away of the past year's sins and misfortunes. Temporary pandals (stages) erected along city streets hose down passing cars and pedestrians, and revellers roam with buckets, cups, and water guns. Traditional observances include releasing fish and birds, offering food to monks, paying respect to elders by washing their hair, and gathering in monasteries to observe the Eight Precepts.

The name Thingyan derives from the Pali saṅkanta, related to Sanskrit saṃkrānti, marking the sun's transit from Pisces to Aries. The festival is followed by Burmese New Year's Day, when merit-making activities predominate and the revelry subsides.
