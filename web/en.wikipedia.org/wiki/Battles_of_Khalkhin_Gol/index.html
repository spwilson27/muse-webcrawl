The Battles of Khalkhin Gol (known in Japan as the Nomonhan Incident) were the decisive engagements of the undeclared Soviet–Japanese border conflicts fought along the border of Mongolia and Japanese-occupied Manchuria in 1939. The conflict was named after the river Khalkha (Khalkhin Gol) that flows through the battlefield. It ended in the defeat of Japan's Kwantung Army by Soviet and Mongolian forces under Georgy Zhukov.

Background

Disputes over the ill-defined border between Mongolia and Manchukuo escalated from cavalry skirmishes in May 1939 into major fighting as both sides reinforced. The Japanese Sixth Army expanded its operations in July, while the Soviet Union built up a large corps under Zhukov, supplied over enormous distances by truck.

The August offensive

After weeks of fighting and elaborate deception measures, Zhukov launched a double envelopment on 20 August 1939. Soviet armour and mechanised infantry, supported by artillery and aircraft, encircled the Japanese forces on the disputed east bank of the river. By 31 August the surrounded Japanese formations were effectively destroyed. A ceasefire was signed in Moscow on 15 September and took effect the next day.

Aftermath

Japanese casualties are disputed but numbered in the tens of thousands. The defeat discredited the "strike north" faction in Tokyo and influenced Japan's decision to sign the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact in 1941 and to expand southward instead of attacking Siberia — a decision of great significance for the Second World War. The battle was Zhukov's first major command and a showcase for Soviet combined-arms tactics.
