German Cathedral, Berlin

LocationGendarmenmarkt, Berlin, Germany
Built1701–1708; tower 1780–1785
ArchitectsMartin Grünberg; Carl von Gontard (tower)
Current useExhibition on German parliamentary history

The German Cathedral (German: Deutscher Dom) is a church building on the Gendarmenmarkt in central Berlin, facing the French Cathedral across the square. Despite its name it has never been a cathedral in the episcopal sense; "Dom" derives from its domed tower. Today it houses the Bundestag's permanent exhibition on the history of parliamentary democracy in Germany.

History

The church was built in 1701–1708 to designs by Martin Grünberg for Berlin's German-speaking Reformed and Lutheran congregations in the new Friedrichstadt district. Between 1780 and 1785 Frederick the Great had Carl von Gontard add the famous domed tower, mirroring the French Cathedral opposite, as part of his embellishment of the Gendarmenmarkt.

War damage and reconstruction

The church burned out in 1943 during the Second World War and stood as a ruin in East Berlin for decades. Reconstruction began in 1983; the exterior was restored and the interior converted into exhibition space. The building reopened in 1996 and since 2002 has hosted the exhibition "Paths of German parliamentary history" (Wege – Irrwege – Umwege).
