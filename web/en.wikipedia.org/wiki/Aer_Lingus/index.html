Aer Lingus is the flag carrier of Ireland. Founded in 1936, it operates scheduled passenger and cargo services from its main hub at Dublin Airport to destinations in Ireland, the United Kingdom, Europe, and North America. The airline's name is an anglicisation of the Irish Aer Loingeas, meaning "air fleet," and its aircraft carry a shamrock on their tail fins.

History

Aer Lingus began operations in May 1936 with a de Havilland Dragon flying between Dublin and Bristol. It grew as Ireland's state airline, inaugurating transatlantic services to New York in 1958 with Lockheed Super Constellations. The Irish government progressively reduced its stake through a stock-market flotation in 2006. After several takeover attempts, the airline was acquired in 2015 by International Airlines Group (IAG), the parent of British Airways and Iberia, while the Irish state retained a minority stake.

Operations

The airline operates a mixed fleet of Airbus A320-family aircraft on short-haul routes and Airbus A330s (supplemented by A321XLRs) on transatlantic routes, where it benefits from US border preclearance facilities at Dublin and Shannon. Its regional services are flown under the Aer Lingus Regional brand. Aer Lingus is not a member of a global airline alliance, though it cooperates with several partner carriers, notably on transatlantic joint ventures.
