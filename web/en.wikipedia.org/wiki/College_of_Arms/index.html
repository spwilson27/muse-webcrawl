College of Arms

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The College of Arms in the City of London.

The College of Arms, or Heralds' College, is a royal corporation consisting of professional officers of arms, with jurisdiction over heraldry and the granting of new coats of arms in England, Wales, Northern Ireland, and some Commonwealth realms. It is part of the Royal Household of the United Kingdom.

History

The college was incorporated by royal charter by King Richard III in 1484 and re-incorporated by Philip and Mary in 1555, when it was granted its present home on the site in the City of London. The building, on what is now Queen Victoria Street, was rebuilt after the Great Fire of London. The college's records, maintained since the early 16th century, form one of the most complete heraldic archives in the world.

Officers

The head of the college is the Earl Marshal, a hereditary office held by the Duke of Norfolk. The officers of arms are the three kings of arms (Garter Principal King of Arms, Clarenceux King of Arms, and Norroy and Ulster King of Arms), six heralds, and four pursuivants, together with several heralds and pursuivants extraordinary. Their historic titles derive from badges and offices of the medieval royal household.

Functions

The college grants new coats of arms by letters patent, records pedigrees and genealogies, settles disputes over the inheritance of arms (in the Court of Chivalry, which last sat in 1954), and advises on matters of precedence, flags, and ceremonial, including state funerals and coronations. Scotland has its own heraldic authority, the Court of the Lord Lyon, which is separate from the college.
