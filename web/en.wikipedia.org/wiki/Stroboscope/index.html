A stroboscope, also known as a strobe, is an instrument used to make a cyclically moving object appear to be slow-moving, or stationary. It consists of either a rotating disk with slots or a lamp such as a flashtube, which produces brief repetitive flashes of light. Usually, the rate of the stroboscope is adjustable to different frequencies. When a rotating or vibrating object is observed with the stroboscope at its vibration frequency (or a submultiple of it), it appears stationary. Thus stroboscopes are also used to measure frequency.

History

Joseph Plateau of Belgium and Simon von Stampfer of Austria independently invented the stroboscope in 1833; Stampfer's device, which he named the Stroboscop, used a slotted disk that let the viewer glimpse a sequence of images, producing the illusion of motion—the same principle behind early animation devices such as the phenakistoscope. In the 1930s Harold Eugene Edgerton at MIT pioneered the electronic flash stroboscope, applying it to high-speed photography and industrial motion study.

Principle of operation

The stroboscopic effect arises because the eye retains an image for a fraction of a second. If the flash rate exactly matches the rotation rate of a marked shaft, the mark appears frozen. If the flash rate is slightly lower, the mark appears to rotate slowly forwards; slightly higher, it appears to drift backwards. By adjusting the flash rate until the object appears stationary, its rotational speed can be measured directly without contact.

Applications

  • Engineering: measuring the speed of rotating shafts, fans and engines; observing vibration and timing marks in automotive engines.
  • Medicine: stroboscopy of the vocal cords to assess vibration during speech.
  • Entertainment: strobe lighting in nightclubs and concerts creates slow-motion visual effects; flashing rates in this context are regulated in some jurisdictions because they can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy.
  • Photography: freezing fast motion such as a droplet splash or a bursting balloon.
