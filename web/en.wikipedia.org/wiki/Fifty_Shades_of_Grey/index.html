Fifty Shades of Grey is a 2011 erotic romance novel by British author E. L. James. It is the first instalment in the Fifty Shades trilogy, which traces the relationship between a college graduate, Anastasia Steele, and a young business magnate, Christian Grey. The novel is notable for its explicitly erotic scenes featuring elements of sexual practices involving BDSM.

Background and publication

James originally wrote the story as Twilight fan fiction under the title Master of the Universe, published episodically online as "Snowqueen's Icedragon". After concerns about its sexual content led her to remove it from fan-fiction sites, she rewrote it as an original work and published it through the small Australian virtual publisher The Writers' Coffee Shop in 2011. Word of mouth, driven by e-book sales, made it a publishing phenomenon; Vintage Books acquired the rights in 2012, and the trilogy sold more than 150 million copies worldwide.

Reception

The novel topped bestseller lists around the world and broke records for paperback sales in the United Kingdom, but received mixed-to-negative reviews for its prose style and depiction of relationships. It sparked wide public discussion of female sexuality and of the mainstreaming of erotica, alongside criticism from commentators and some BDSM practitioners about its portrayal of consent and practice.

Film adaptation

A film adaptation directed by Sam Taylor-Johnson, starring Dakota Johnson as Anastasia and Jamie Dornan as Christian, was released in February 2015 and grossed over $569 million worldwide, followed by adaptations of the two sequels.
