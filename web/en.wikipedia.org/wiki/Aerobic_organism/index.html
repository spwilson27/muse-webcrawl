Aerobic organism

Aerobic organism
Metabolism: Aerobic respiration
Requires: Molecular oxygen
Types: Obligate aerobes, facultative aerobes, aerotolerant organisms

An aerobic organism or aerobe is an organism that can survive and grow in an oxygenated environment, using oxygen as the terminal electron acceptor in cellular respiration.

Types

Obligate aerobes require oxygen and die without it, as do most animals and many fungi and bacteria. Facultative aerobes, such as yeast and E. coli, grow best with oxygen but can survive anaerobically. Aerotolerant organisms do not use oxygen but are not harmed by it, while microaerophiles require low oxygen concentrations.

Energy yield

Aerobic respiration yields far more ATP per glucose molecule than fermentation, which is one reason complex multicellular life depends on it. Its downside is exposure to reactive oxygen species, against which aerobes maintain antioxidant defences.
