Tadodaho

Tadodaho (also rendered Atotarho) was a legendary Onondaga chief and — in the oral tradition of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy) — the last leader to accept the Great Peace preached by the prophet Deganawida, the Peacemaker, and his spokesman Hiawatha. His name became the title of the principal chief of the Onondaga Nation and the hereditary keeper of the council fire of the Confederacy.

Legend

According to the founding epic of the Confederacy, Tadodaho, chief of the Onondaga, was a feared and powerful sorcerer said to have a body twisted and crooked in seven places and snakes writhing in his hair. He opposed the Peacemaker's message of peace and was blamed for the deaths of Hiawatha's daughters. Hiawatha and the Peacemaker travelled to Onondaga and, through ceremony and songs, "combed the snakes" from Tadodaho's hair, straightened his mind and body, and persuaded him to accept the Great Law of Peace. In return, the Onondaga were made keepers of the Confederacy's council fire and of the wampum records, and Tadodaho was given first place among its chiefs.

The title

"Tadodaho" is one of the fifty hereditary chiefly titles (royaner) of the Grand Council of the Haudenosaunee, held by the Onondaga. The holder of the title presides over the Grand Council and is traditionally regarded as the head chief of the Confederacy. Notable modern holders included Leon Shenandoah (Tadodaho from 1967 to 1996) and Sid Hill, who has held the title since 2002 and has represented the Haudenosaunee in international forums, including addressing the United Nations.
