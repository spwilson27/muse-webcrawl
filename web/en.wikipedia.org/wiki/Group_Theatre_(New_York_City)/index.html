Group Theatre (New York City)

Founded1931
Disbanded1941
FoundersHarold Clurman, Cheryl Crawford, Lee Strasberg
LocationNew York City
Notable productionsAwake and Sing!, Waiting for Lefty, Golden Boy

The Group Theatre was a theater collective based in New York City and formed in 1931 by Harold Clurman, Cheryl Crawford, and Lee Strasberg. It was intended to be a base for a new kind of acting: a permanent ensemble of actors trained together in a shared technique — an American adaptation of Stanislavski's system — performing plays that spoke to contemporary social life.

History

The company's early productions, such as The House of Connelly (1931), established its reputation, but its defining works came through its association with playwright Clifford Odets. The one-act Waiting for Lefty (1935), about a taxi drivers' strike, caused a sensation, and Awake and Sing! and Golden Boy followed. Other members included Stella Adler, Sanford Meisner, Elia Kazan, Morris Carnovsky, and Franchot Tone.

Method and schisms

Internal disputes over acting technique — notably Stella Adler's 1934 report from Paris that Stanislavski had moved beyond "affective memory," in contrast to Strasberg's emphasis on it — strained the collective. Financial pressures and diverging careers led to its dissolution in 1941.

Legacy

Despite its short life, the Group Theatre transformed American acting. Its alumni founded the Actors Studio and teaching lineages that produced "the Method," influencing generations of stage and screen performers.
