The 2021 Taliban offensive was a military offensive by the Taliban and its allied militant groups against the government of Afghanistan and its allies that began on 1 May 2021, coinciding with the withdrawal of most United States troops from Afghanistan. It culminated in the fall of Kabul on 15 August 2021 and the restoration of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.

Background

The offensive followed the February 2020 Doha Agreement between the United States and the Taliban, under which US forces would withdraw from Afghanistan in exchange for counterterrorism assurances. In April 2021, President Joe Biden announced that all US combat troops would leave by 11 September 2021, later moved to 31 August. As international forces withdrew, the Taliban accelerated attacks on Afghan National Security Forces positions, particularly in rural areas.

Course of the offensive

During May and June 2021 the Taliban seized dozens of rural districts, often through negotiated surrenders. In July the offensive shifted to provincial capitals and border crossings, including Spin Boldak on the Pakistani border and crossings with Iran and Tajikistan. On 6 August the Taliban captured Zaranj, the first provincial capital to fall. In the following nine days, major cities including Herat, Kandahar, Ghazni, Mazar-i-Sharif and Jalalabad fell in rapid succession.

On 15 August 2021 Taliban fighters entered Kabul as President Ashraf Ghani fled the country. The government collapsed, and the Taliban declared the war over. The last US military aircraft departed Hamid Karzai International Airport on 30 August, ending the nearly twenty-year war.

Aftermath

The offensive prompted a large-scale airlift evacuating more than 120,000 people. Resistance briefly continued in the Panjshir Valley before being subdued in September. The Taliban announced an interim government, and Afghanistan faced a severe humanitarian and economic crisis as international aid was suspended.
