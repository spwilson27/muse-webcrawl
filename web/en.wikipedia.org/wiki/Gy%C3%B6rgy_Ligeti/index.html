Born: 28 May 1923, Târnăveni, Romania
Died: 12 June 2006, Vienna, Austria

György Sándor Ligeti (28 May 1923 – 12 June 2006) was a Hungarian-Austrian composer of contemporary classical music, regarded as one of the most important avant-garde composers of the second half of the 20th century.

Life

Born in Transylvania to a Hungarian Jewish family, Ligeti survived the Holocaust, in which his father and brother were murdered. He studied in Budapest and fled Hungary after the 1956 uprising, working in Cologne with Stockhausen and later settling in Vienna.

Music

Ligeti developed "micropolyphony" — dense webs of sound heard in Atmosphères (1961), the Requiem (1965), and Lux Aeterna (1966), music famously used in Stanley Kubrick's film 2001: A Space Odyssey. Later works, including the opera Le Grand Macabre and his Études for piano, explored polyrhythm and illusion.
