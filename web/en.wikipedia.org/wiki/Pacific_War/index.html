Pacific War

The Pacific War, sometimes called the Asia–Pacific War, was the theatre of the Second World War fought in eastern Asia, the Pacific Ocean, the Indian Ocean and Oceania. It is generally dated from the Japanese invasion of China in 1937, or from Japan's attacks of 7–8 December 1941 on American, British and Dutch possessions, and it ended with the surrender of Japan on 2 September 1945. The principal Allied powers were the United States, China, the United Kingdom and its empire, Australia, the Netherlands and the Soviet Union (from August 1945); they fought the Empire of Japan, supported by its Axis partners after Germany's declaration of war on the United States.

Background

Japan's expansion into Manchuria (1931) and China (1937) brought it into conflict with the Western powers, whose embargoes — above all the American oil embargo of 1941 — pushed Tokyo toward seizing the resources of Southeast Asia. On 7 December 1941 Japan attacked the U.S. Pacific Fleet at Pearl Harbor and simultaneously struck the Philippines, Malaya, Singapore and Hong Kong, bringing the United States and the British Empire into open war with Japan.

Course of the war

In six months Japan overran Southeast Asia and the western Pacific, but its advance was checked at the Coral Sea (May 1942) and decisively reversed at Midway (June 1942), where four Japanese fleet carriers were sunk. The Guadalcanal campaign (1942–43) began a grinding Allied counteroffensive on two axes: MacArthur's forces through New Guinea and the Philippines, and Nimitz's "island hopping" across the Central Pacific through Tarawa, the Marshalls, Saipan and Tinian. Meanwhile British and Indian forces fought in Burma, and China tied down huge Japanese armies.

In 1945 the Allies captured Iwo Jima and Okinawa at great cost, firebombed Japanese cities, and tightened a submarine blockade that strangled Japanese industry. The atomic bombings of Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945) and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria (9 August) precipitated Japan's decision to surrender; the formal instrument was signed aboard USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on 2 September 1945.

Consequences

The war cost the lives of more than twenty million people in Asia, including vast numbers of Chinese civilians, and left Japan occupied and its empire dissolved. War-crimes trials were held at Tokyo, and the conflict reshaped the region: it hastened decolonisation, set the stage for the Chinese Civil War's conclusion, the Korean division and the Cold War alignment of East Asia, and established the United States as the dominant Pacific power.
