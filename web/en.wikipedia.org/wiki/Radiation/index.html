Radiation

Radiation is energy released in the form of moving subatomic particles or electromagnetic waves. Ionising radiation — alpha and beta particles, neutrons, gamma rays and X-rays — carries enough energy to strip electrons from atoms, which is why it can damage tissue and DNA; non-ionising radiation includes ultraviolet (borderline), visible light, infrared, microwaves and radio waves.

Natural background radiation (about 2–3 mSv per year on average, varying by place) comes from cosmic rays, terrestrial rocks, radon gas and our own bodies (potassium-40). Artificial sources include medical imaging and radiotherapy, nuclear power and fallout from past weapons tests. Radiation is measured as activity (becquerel), absorbed dose (gray) and dose equivalent (sievert).
