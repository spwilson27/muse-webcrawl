Plynlimon

The summit of Plynlimon (Pumlumon Fawr)

Plynlimon (Welsh: Pumlumon) is a mountain massif in the Cambrian Mountains of Mid Wales, on the border between Ceredigion and Powys. Its highest point, Pumlumon Fawr, reaches 752 metres (2,467 ft), making it the highest point in Mid Wales and the source of several major rivers.

Geography

The massif is a broad, peaty plateau of several summits, including Pen Pumlumon Fawr and Y Garn. On its slopes rise the River Severn (which begins on the eastern flank and flows through Shrewsbury and Worcester to the Bristol Channel), the River Wye, and the River Rheidol – an unusual concentration of sources that led to the massif being dubbed the "water tower" of Wales. The Nant-y-moch reservoir lies to the north-west and the A44 road crosses the southern flank.

History and legend

The name Pumlumon means "five peaks". Gerald of Wales described the mountain in the 12th century. Welsh tradition associates the giant's chair (Cadair Idris has the better-known version) and other folk tales with the mountain, and Owain Glyndŵr won a victory at the battle of Hyddgen (1401) on its slopes against a Flemish-English force. The area is also noted in the search for the "Beast of Bont", a supposed big cat blamed for sheep kills in the 1980s.

Access

Popular ascents start from Eisteddfa Gurig on the A44 and from Nant-y-moch. The summit carries a trig point and a large cairn, with views on clear days from Snowdonia to the Brecon Beacons and across Cardigan Bay.
