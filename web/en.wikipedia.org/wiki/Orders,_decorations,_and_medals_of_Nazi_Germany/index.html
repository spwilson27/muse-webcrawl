Orders, decorations, and medals of Nazi Germany

Orders, decorations, and medals of Nazi Germany were military, political, and civilian awards created between 1933 and 1945 under the Nazi regime. The system combined revived and re-styled First World War decorations — most famously the Iron Cross, reinstated in 1939 — with a large number of new awards created during the Second World War for combat achievement, wounds, service, and political loyalty.

Military awards

The highest military decoration was the Knight's Cross of the Iron Cross, instituted in 1939, with progressively rarer higher grades including Oak Leaves, Swords, Diamonds, and the single award of Golden Oak Leaves to Hans-Ulrich Rudel. Other major awards included the German Cross in Gold and Silver, the War Merit Cross, wound badges in black, silver, and gold, and campaign shields and cuff titles for particular battles and theatres.

Political and civilian awards

The Nazi Party maintained its own honours, including the Golden Party Badge, the Blood Order for participants in the 1923 Beer Hall Putsch, long-service awards for the NSDAP, SS, and police, and the German Order, the regime's highest award for service to the party and state. Civilian decorations included the Social Welfare Decoration and the Mother's Cross (Ehrenkreuz der Deutschen Mutter), awarded to mothers of large families.

Post-war status

After 1945 the display of Nazi symbols was restricted in Germany. A 1957 federal law permitted veterans to wear de-nazified versions of wartime decorations, with the swastika removed, in specified circumstances.
