Medical education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Medical education is education related to the practice of being a medical practitioner, including the initial training to become a physician (medical school and internship) and additional training thereafter (residency, fellowship, and continuing medical education).

Structure

Systems vary by country. In the United States and Canada, students typically complete an undergraduate degree before a four-year Doctor of Medicine (MD) or Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (DO) program, followed by residency training of three to seven years in a specialty. In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries, medicine is an undergraduate-entry degree of five or six years (MBBS or equivalent), followed by foundation training and specialty training. Many other countries use a six-year undergraduate model.

Curriculum

Modern curricula combine basic sciences (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pathology, pharmacology) with early clinical exposure, clinical clerkships in medicine, surgery, pediatrics, psychiatry, and obstetrics and gynecology, and teaching in communication, ethics, and professionalism. The 1910 Flexner Report reshaped North American medical education around university-based, science-driven training. Recent reforms emphasize problem-based learning, simulation, interprofessional education, and competency-based assessment.

Assessment and licensure

Graduates must pass licensing examinations — such as the USMLE in the United States or the UKMLA in the United Kingdom — and be registered with a regulator before independent practice. Physicians then undertake continuing professional development throughout their careers, which is mandatory in many jurisdictions.
