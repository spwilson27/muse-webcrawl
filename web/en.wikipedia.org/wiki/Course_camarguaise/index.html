Course camarguaise

The course camarguaise is a bloodless form of bullfighting practised in the Camargue and surrounding areas of southern France, mainly in the departments of Bouches-du-Rhône, Gard, Hérault and Vaucluse. Unlike Spanish-style corrida, the bull is not killed; instead, young men called raseteurs attempt to snatch rosettes and tassels (attributs) tied between the bull's horns.

The event

The bull, a small black Camargue breed animal known as a cocardier, enters the arena with a red rosette (cocarde) and tassels attached to its horns, each worth money to the raseteur who removes them with a small hooked comb held between the fingers. The raseteurs, dressed in white, provoke the bull's charge and must vault the arena barriers to escape; the most skilled perform the raset — cutting close across the bull's head — at the last moment.

A course lasts about fifteen to twenty minutes per bull, with several bulls appearing in an afternoon. Between bulls, the capelado ceremony honours famous animals, and celebrated cocardiers are retired to pasture with great ceremony; some, such as the famous bull Goya, have statues erected to them.

Organisation

The sport is governed by the Fédération Française de la Course Camarguaise, founded in 1975. Competitions are ranked, culminating in trophies such as the Trophée des As. The tradition is inscribed in France's inventory of intangible cultural heritage.
