Ticker-tape parade

Shredded paper falling on the Canyon of Heroes, lower Broadway.

A ticker-tape parade is a parade held in a city — classically along the "Canyon of Heroes" on Broadway in New York City — in which the honorees are showered with shredded paper thrown from the windows of surrounding office buildings. Originally the paper was ticker tape, the long paper strips printed by stock ticker machines in the offices of the financial district; today shredded office paper and confetti are used instead. The parades celebrate explorers, aviators, astronauts, returning soldiers, sports champions and visiting heads of state.

Origins

The first such celebration occurred spontaneously on 28 October 1886, during the parade marking the dedication of the Statue of Liberty, when office workers along the route threw ticker tape from the windows, creating a blizzard of paper. The practice was repeated for notable visitors and returning heroes, and the city began formally organising the parades; by the early twentieth century the ticker-tape parade had become New York's signature honour, the route up Broadway from Bowling Green to City Hall acquiring the name Canyon of Heroes.

Famous parades

  • Theodore Roosevelt's return from Africa and Europe (1910)
  • Charles Lindbergh after his solo transatlantic flight (1927)
  • Rear Admiral Richard Byrd and other polar explorers
  • Generals Dwight D. Eisenhower (1945) and Douglas MacArthur (1951) after the Second World War and Korea
  • The Apollo 11 astronauts (1969), and earlier John Glenn (1962)
  • New York championship teams — the Yankees, Mets, Giants and others — whose parades end with a ceremony at City Hall
  • The U.S. women's national soccer team (2015, 2019), the first female team-sport honorees
  • Essential workers during the COVID-19 pandemic (2021), the "Hometown Heroes" parade

The Canyon of Heroes

More than two hundred granite strips are embedded in the sidewalks along the parade route, each commemorating an individual parade and its honoree. The volume of paper involved was once measured in tens of tons, to be swept up by the city's sanitation department; since ticker machines disappeared, the shredded paper is supplied deliberately for the occasion.
