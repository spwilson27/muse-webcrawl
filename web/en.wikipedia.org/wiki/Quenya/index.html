Quenya

Quenya
Created by: J. R. R. Tolkien
Date: From c. 1910–1915
Setting: Middle-earth (Arda)
Writing system: Tengwar, Cirth, Latin

Quenya is one of the fictional languages devised by J. R. R. Tolkien for the Elves of his legendarium, including The Lord of the Rings. Along with Sindarin, it is one of the two most developed of Tolkien's constructed languages. Within the fiction, Quenya is the ancient, formal tongue of the High Elves (the Quendi who reached Aman), preserved as a language of lore and ceremony somewhat as Latin was in medieval Europe.

Development

Tolkien began developing the language, then called Qenya, around 1910–1915, heavily influenced by Finnish — he described discovering Finnish as "like discovering a complete wine-cellar filled with bottles of an amazing wine of a kind and flavour never tasted before." Latin and Greek also shaped its phonology and grammar. He revised the language throughout his life as the surrounding mythology evolved.

Grammar and phonology

Quenya is a highly inflected, agglutinative language. Nouns decline in up to ten cases (including nominative, accusative, genitive, possessive, dative, instrumental, locative, allative, ablative, and a "respective" case) and four numbers (singular, plural, dual, and partitive plural). Verbs conjugate for tense and agree with their subjects through suffixes. Its sound system features phonemic vowel length, five short vowels, and a consonant inventory that avoids voiced plosives except after nasals and liquids.

Texts

The best-known Quenya text in The Lord of the Rings is Galadriel's lament Namárië ("Ai! laurië lantar lassi súrinen..."), the longest passage of Elvish in the book. A modest number of journals, wordlists, and scholarly societies are devoted to Tolkien's languages, and limited original composition in Neo-Quenya continues among enthusiasts, though the surviving corpus is too small for full everyday use.
