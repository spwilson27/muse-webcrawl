The Newport Jazz Festival is an annual American multi-day jazz festival held each summer in Newport, Rhode Island. Founded in 1954 by jazz pianist and promoter George Wein with the backing of socialites Louis and Elaine Lorillard, it was the first outdoor jazz festival of its kind in the United States and became a model for jazz festivals worldwide.

History

The first festival took place in July 1954 at the Newport Casino. The 1956 festival produced one of the most celebrated performances in jazz history: Duke Ellington's band, revitalised by Paul Gonsalves's marathon tenor saxophone solo on "Diminuendo and Crescendo in Blue", a moment that revived Ellington's career. The 1958 festival was filmed as the acclaimed documentary Jazz on a Summer's Day (1960). Performances by Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, Mahalia Jackson and many others yielded landmark live albums.

A riot during the 1960 festival and changing musical tastes brought difficulties, and in 1972 the festival moved to New York City, where it continued under various sponsor names. A Newport edition was revived in 1981, and the festival has since been held at Fort Adams State Park, overlooking Newport Harbor.

Legacy

The Newport Jazz Festival is credited with establishing the outdoor music festival format in the United States and demonstrating the commercial viability of presenting jazz to mass audiences. In 2007 George Wein sold his production company, but the festival continues as a non-profit under the Newport Festivals Foundation, alongside its sibling event, the Newport Folk Festival.
