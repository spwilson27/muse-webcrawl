Turkish State Railways

Turkish State Railways (TCDD)
Founded: 1927
Headquarters: Ankara, Turkey
Network length: over 12,000 km

The Turkish State Railways (Turkish: Türkiye Cumhuriyeti Devlet Demiryolları, abbreviated TCDD) is the state-owned railway company responsible for the ownership and maintenance of railway infrastructure in Turkey, and since a 2017 restructuring its subsidiary TCDD Transport operates passenger and freight trains.

History

TCDD was established in 1927 to take over and unify the railway lines of the former Ottoman Empire that lay within the Republic of Turkey, including lines nationalised from foreign concession companies. It built out the network across Anatolia during the early republican period and for decades operated Turkey's ports-linked freight and long-distance passenger services.

Modernisation

Since the 2000s Turkey has invested heavily in high-speed rail under the Yüksek Hızlı Tren (YHT) brand, beginning with the Ankara–Eskişehir line in 2009 and extending to Konya and Istanbul. Istanbul's Marmaray tunnel under the Bosphorus, opened in 2013, connected the European and Asian networks.
