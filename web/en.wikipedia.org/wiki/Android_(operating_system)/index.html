Android is a mobile operating system based on a modified version of the Linux kernel and other open-source software, designed primarily for touchscreen devices such as smartphones and tablets. It was developed by a consortium led by Google and released under the Android Open Source Project (AOSP). Android is the world's best-selling operating system on smartphones, with a global market share of around 70 percent, and runs on several billion active devices, as well as on televisions, cars, watches and embedded systems.

History

Android Inc. was founded in Palo Alto, California, in 2003 by Andy Rubin, Rich Miner, Nick Sears and Chris White to develop an operating system for digital cameras, later repurposed for mobile handsets. Google acquired the company in 2005. Android was unveiled in November 2007 together with the Open Handset Alliance, a consortium of hardware, software and telecom companies. The first commercial Android device, the HTC Dream (T-Mobile G1), went on sale in October 2008.

Early dessert-themed version names — Cupcake, Donut, Eclair, Froyo, Gingerbread, Honeycomb, Ice Cream Sandwich, Jelly Bean, KitKat, Lollipop, Marshmallow, Nougat, Oreo and Pie — ran alphabetically until Android 10 (2019), after which versions are identified by number alone.

Features and architecture

Android applications are typically written in Kotlin or Java and run on the Android Runtime (ART), which compiles bytecode ahead of time. The system provides a home screen with widgets, notifications, voice-based interaction, and access to app stores such as Google Play. The Linux kernel layer handles hardware drivers, memory management and security; each app runs in its own sandbox with a permissions model mediating access to sensitive data and sensors.

Reception and market position

Android achieved majority global smartphone share by 2011, overtaking Symbian. Its openness allowed manufacturers such as Samsung, Xiaomi and Oppo to build large ecosystems, though fragmentation across versions and vendor modifications has been a persistent criticism. Regulators have scrutinised Google's bundling of its services with the Play Store, notably in the European Commission's 2018 antitrust decision. Google services are absent from Android devices sold in China, where domestic forks dominate.
