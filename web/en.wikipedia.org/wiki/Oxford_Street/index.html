Oxford Street

Oxford Street
Location: City of Westminster, London
Length: 1.2 mi (1.9 km)
From: Tottenham Court Road
To: Marble Arch (Hyde Park)
Known for: Shopping; Christmas lights

Oxford Street is a major road in the City of Westminster in London's West End, running from Tottenham Court Road to Marble Arch via Oxford Circus. With around half a million visitors daily and some 300 shops, it is one of Europe's busiest shopping streets and home to flagship department stores including Selfridges (opened 1909) and the former John Lewis and Debenhams stores.

History

The street follows part of a Roman road, the Via Trinobantina, and in the Middle Ages was the route from London to Oxford, notorious as the path taken by condemned prisoners to the gallows at Tyburn near Marble Arch — the "Tyburn Road". It developed as a shopping street in the late 18th and 19th centuries, with drapers and furniture shops; Selfridges transformed it from 1909 onward. The street was badly bombed in the Blitz, and its Christmas lights, first hung in 1959, became a celebrated annual tradition.

Transport and redevelopment

Oxford Street is served by the Central line stations at Tottenham Court Road, Oxford Circus, Bond Street and Marble Arch; the Elizabeth line added stations at Tottenham Court Road and Bond Street. Long-running proposals to pedestrianize the street have been debated for decades, and Westminster City Council has pursued pavement widening and traffic reduction schemes in the 2020s.
