Comparative physiology

Comparative physiology is a subdiscipline of physiology that studies and exploits the diversity of functional characteristics of various kinds of organisms. It is closely related to evolutionary physiology and environmental physiology.

Approach

Comparative physiologists examine how different organisms solve similar physiological problems — such as thermoregulation, respiration, osmoregulation, and locomotion — in different environments. August Krogh's principle states that for many problems there is an animal ideally suited to study them.

History

The field grew from early natural history and was formalized in the twentieth century by figures such as Knut Schmidt-Nielsen, whose work on animals in extreme environments (camels, desert rodents, diving mammals) became foundational.

Applications

Insights from comparative physiology inform medicine, conservation biology, and understanding of how organisms may respond to climate change.
