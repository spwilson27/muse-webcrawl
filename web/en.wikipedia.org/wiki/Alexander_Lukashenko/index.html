Overview

Alexander Grigoryevich Lukashenko (born 1954) has served as president of Belarus since winning the country's first presidential election in 1994, and is frequently described as "Europe's last dictator" by Western critics — a label he rejects. A former collective farm director and border guard, he campaigned on anti-corruption populism and nostalgia for Soviet stability.

Rule

Lukashenko reversed market reforms, kept much of industry state-owned, and built a security state (the KGB retains its name in Belarus) that suppressed successive protest waves, most massively after the disputed 2020 election, when Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya claimed victory and hundreds of thousands protested; mass arrests, torture allegations and exile of the opposition followed, with verdicts of election falsification by Western governments (Belarusian official results credited him with 80%). Belarus under Lukashenko is closely aligned with Russia — a Union State, and a staging ground (though not a direct belligerent) for the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine. His precise role in post-2020 and wartime developments remains contested between official and opposition accounts.
