Dome (geology)

A dome is a feature in structural geology consisting of symmetrical anticlines that intersect each other at their respective apices, forming an upwarped, roughly circular or elliptical structure in plan view.

Dome
Type: Structural feature
Shape: Circular / elliptical uplift
Opposite: Basin
Examples: Black Hills, Llano Uplift

Formation

Domes can form by igneous intrusion (laccoliths and plutons pushing up overlying strata), by salt diapirism, by regional uplift and isostatic rebound after erosion, or by impact cratering. Strata dip away from the centre in all directions, so the oldest rocks are exposed at the core after erosion.

Examples

Well-known domes include the Black Hills of South Dakota and Wyoming, the Llano Uplift in Texas, the Ozark Dome in Missouri, the Cincinnati Arch region, and salt domes of the Gulf Coast of the United States, many of which trap petroleum and natural gas and are used for storage. The Richat Structure in Mauritania is a deeply eroded dome once mistaken for an impact crater.
