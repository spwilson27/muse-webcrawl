Prebendary

A prebendary is a member of the clergy holding a prebend: a stipend or income drawn from the revenues of a cathedral or collegiate church, historically assigned from a specific estate or portion of the chapter's endowment. A prebend is a type of benefice, and its holder was originally expected to assist in the work of the cathedral.

History

In the medieval church, cathedral chapters were originally communities living on a common fund. From the 11th and 12th centuries the endowment of many chapters was divided into individual prebends, each attached to a particular stall in the choir and carrying defined duties — often largely nominal, so prebends became a common form of clerical income and could be held by absentees or in plurality with other benefices. The number of prebends varied: great churches such as York, Lincoln and St Paul's had dozens.

Modern usage

In the Church of England, the Ecclesiastical Commissioners took over prebendal estates in the 19th century, and the title became honorary. Today cathedrals may appoint honorary prebendaries (often styled prebendary) — clergy or distinguished lay people recognised with a seat in the chapter's stalls but no income or governing role. The title is also used in some other Anglican and Roman Catholic contexts.
