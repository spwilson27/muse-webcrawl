Kobzar

A kobzar (Ukrainian: кобзар, pl. kobzari) was an itinerant Ukrainian blind bard who sang to his own accompaniment on the kobza or bandura, performing epic poems known as dumy, religious songs, and historical ballads.

Tradition

Kobzars flourished from the 16th century onward and were central carriers of Cossack oral history. They were organised into guild-like structures, learned their repertoire through long apprenticeships, and performed at markets, churches, and village gatherings. The tradition was severely persecuted in the Soviet 1930s, when many kobzars were arrested and executed.

Legacy

The name is also inseparably linked with Taras Shevchenko, Ukraine's national poet, whose famous 1840 poetry collection is titled Kobzar.
