Benchmark (crude oil)

Main benchmarksBrent Crude, West Texas Intermediate (WTI), Dubai/Oman
FunctionReference prices for pricing other crude grades
Traded onICE Futures Europe (Brent), NYMEX (WTI)

A benchmark crude oil (or marker crude) is a crude oil grade whose price serves as a reference point for pricing the many other grades of crude oil traded worldwide. Because crude oils differ in density (API gravity), sulfur content and location, most are bought and sold at a premium or discount — a "differential" — to one of a handful of freely traded benchmark grades. Three benchmarks dominate: Brent, West Texas Intermediate and the Dubai/Oman average; roughly two-thirds of the world's internationally traded crude is priced in relation to Brent.

Brent Crude

Brent is a light, sweet crude originally produced from the Brent field in the North Sea and now a basket of North Sea grades (Brent, Forties, Oseberg, Ekofisk and Troll — BFOET). It is traded on ICE Futures Europe in London and is the benchmark for Europe, Africa, the Middle East exports to Europe, and much of Asia. Its waterborne delivery makes it readily tradable globally.

West Texas Intermediate

WTI is a very light, sweet crude benchmark for North America, physically delivered at Cushing, Oklahoma, and traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX). For decades it traded at a small premium to Brent, but the US shale boom and pipeline bottlenecks at Cushing reversed this in the 2010s. In April 2020, amid the COVID-19 demand collapse and full storage at Cushing, the front-month WTI futures contract famously settled at a negative price (about −$37 per barrel).

Dubai/Oman and other markers

The Dubai/Oman average, assessed by price-reporting agencies, prices Middle East crude sold to Asia. Other regional markers include Urals (Russia), Bonny Light (Nigeria), Tapis (Malaysia), Western Canadian Select and the OPEC Reference Basket, a weighted average of member countries' grades used for monitoring rather than trading.
