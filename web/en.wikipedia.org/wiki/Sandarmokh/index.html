Sandarmokh (Russian: Сандармох) is a forest massif near Medvezhyegorsk in the Republic of Karelia, Russia, where an unknown number of victims of Stalin's Great Terror were secretly executed and buried in 1937 and 1938. It is one of the largest known killing fields of the Great Terror.

Executions

Prisoners were brought to the site from Karelian prisons and from the Solovki prison camp in the White Sea. Among the most notorious episodes was the "First Solovki Echelon": 1,116 prisoners shipped from Solovki in late October 1937, of whom 1,111 were shot at Sandarmokh between 27 October and 4 November 1937 by NKVD officer Mikhail Matveyev. The dead included many of the Soviet Union's Ukrainian cultural elite — writers, theatre directors and scholars of the "Executed Renaissance" — as well as clergy, Finns, Karelians and others from at least 58 nationalities. Historians estimate the total number of victims in the tract at over 6,000.

Discovery and memorial

The site was located in 1997 by researchers including Veniamin Ioffe and Yuri Dmitriev, working with the Memorial society, who matched execution documents with the forest's burial pits. A memorial cemetery was opened the same year, with hundreds of wooden posts and monuments erected by victims' families and national communities. An annual Day of Remembrance is held on 5 August.

Controversy

Since the mid-2010s the site has been at the centre of a dispute over historical memory in Russia: Dmitriev was prosecuted on charges widely regarded internationally as politically motivated, and state-backed researchers have promoted an alternative theory that the graves also contain Soviet POWs shot by Finnish forces in 1941–44, a claim Memorial historians say is unsupported by the evidence.
