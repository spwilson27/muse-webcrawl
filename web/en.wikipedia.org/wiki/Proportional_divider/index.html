Proportional divider

A proportional divider is a drafting and measuring instrument with two legs joined at an adjustable pivot, each leg having a point at both ends. By sliding the pivot along the instrument, the user sets a fixed ratio between the distances spanned by the two pairs of points, allowing a measurement taken at one end to be transferred, enlarged or reduced at the other.

Use

Proportional dividers have been used since at least the seventeenth century by artists, cartographers, engravers and architects to copy drawings at a different scale, divide lines into equal parts, and solve proportions of the golden ratio. The pivot position is often read against an engraved scale of ratios or "lines" (lines, circles, plans and solids) inherited from the sector, a related instrument associated with Galileo.

Design

Instruments are typically made of brass or steel, 15–30 cm long, with the pivot mounted on a sliding block secured by a screw. Quality examples include fine adjustment wheels for precise ratio setting.
