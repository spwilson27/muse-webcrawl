Praefectus castrorum

The praefectus castrorum ("prefect of the camp") was a senior officer in a Roman legion, third in command after the legate (legatus legionis) and the senior tribune (tribunus laticlavius). The post was normally held by a veteran soldier promoted from the ranks, typically a former chief centurion (primus pilus), and was the summit of a professional soldier's career.

Duties

The camp prefect was responsible for the construction and maintenance of the legion's camp and fortifications, for equipment, artillery and transport, for drill and discipline, and for the general administration of the unit. In the absence of the legate and senior tribune he could take command of the legion in battle. The position combined the roles of quartermaster, chief engineer and second-in-command.

Career path

Because the post required decades of experience rather than senatorial rank, it was the highest appointment attainable by a man who had risen from the ranks. After serving a term, a praefectus castrorum might be granted equestrian status and further prefectures. The office existed from the Augustan reorganisation of the army through the early imperial period; in the later empire its functions changed as legion structures evolved.
