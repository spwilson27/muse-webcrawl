Sir Peter George Snell KNZM MBE (17 December 1938 – 12 December 2019) was a New Zealand middle-distance runner, one of the most celebrated athletes in the country's history. He won three Olympic gold medals and is the only man since 1920 to have won the 800 metres at two consecutive Olympic Games.

Career

Coached by Arthur Lydiard, Snell emerged as a largely unknown 21-year-old to win the 800 m at the 1960 Rome Olympics in an Olympic record. In January 1962, in Christchurch, he set a world record for the mile, and a week later in Wanganui famously ran 800 m in 1:44.3, a world record that stood as the New Zealand national record for decades. He won both the 880 yards and the mile at the 1962 Commonwealth Games in Perth.

At the 1964 Tokyo Olympics, Snell completed the rare 800 m/1500 m double, winning the 800 m in an Olympic record of 1:45.1 and the 1500 m in 3:38.1, becoming one of the few athletes ever to achieve the Olympic middle-distance double. He retired in 1965.

Later life

Snell later moved to the United States, earned a doctorate in exercise physiology, and built a distinguished academic career at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, researching human performance and cardiac health. He remained active in masters orienteering into old age. Voted New Zealand's Sportsman of the 20th Century, he died in Dallas in December 2019, days short of his 81st birthday.
