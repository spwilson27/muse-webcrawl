Muscidae

The Muscidae are a large family of flies in the order Diptera, often called house flies and their relatives. The family contains over 4,000 described species in more than 100 genera, found worldwide.

The best-known member is the housefly, Musca domestica, a ubiquitous human commensal and mechanical vector of disease. Other important genera include Stomoxys (stable flies), which bite livestock, and Fannia.

Biology

Adults typically have grey thoraxes with dark stripes and feed on decaying organic matter, dung, nectar or blood depending on species. Larvae develop in dung, carrion, compost and other decaying material, and some are predators of other insect larvae. Muscids are of medical, veterinary and forensic importance.
