The Lithuanian Song Festival (Lithuanian: Lietuvių dainų šventė) is a massive traditional song and dance festival held in Lithuania, drawing tens of thousands of singers, folk dancers and folk musicians from across the country and the Lithuanian diaspora. Together with the corresponding Latvian and Estonian festivals, it forms the Baltic song and dance celebrations, proclaimed by UNESCO in 2003 a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity and inscribed in 2008 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

The first Lithuanian Song Day was held in Kaunas in August 1924, gathering about 3,000 singers from 77 choirs, inspired by the earlier Estonian and Latvian festivals. Festivals continued in the interwar republic and, under tight ideological control, during the Soviet occupation, when the repertoire was required to include Soviet songs even as the events quietly sustained national feeling. During the "Singing Revolution" of 1988–1991, mass singing at the festivals and rock marches became a symbol of peaceful resistance. The 2024 festival marked the centenary of the tradition, under the motto "May the green forest grow" (Kad giria žaliuotų), with around 37,000 performers.

Events

The festival week in Vilnius includes the Song Day at Vingis Park, where a choir of some 12,000 or more singers performs on a purpose-built stage; the Dance Day at a stadium, with thousands of dancers forming choreographed patterns; the Folklore Day in the Old Town; an evening of ensembles; kanklės (Lithuanian zither) concerts; brass band performances; and a closing procession of all participants in national costume through the city.

Significance

The song festivals are regarded as one of the central expressions of Lithuanian national identity, uniting local choirs, school groups and emigrant communities in a shared repertoire of folk songs and works by Lithuanian composers such as Juozas Naujalis and Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis.
