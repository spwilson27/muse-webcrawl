Southern Upland Way

Southern Upland Way
Length: 344 km (214 mi)
Location: Southern Scotland
Trailheads: Portpatrick – Cockburnspath
Designation: Scotland's Great Trails
Opened: 1984

The Southern Upland Way is a 344-kilometre (214 mi) coast-to-coast long-distance footpath in southern Scotland. It runs from Portpatrick on the west coast (on the Rhins of Galloway) to Cockburnspath on the North Sea coast in the Scottish Borders. Opened in 1984, it was Britain's first officially recognised coast-to-coast long-distance route, and is one of Scotland's Great Trails.

Route

The way crosses the Southern Uplands through some of Scotland's wildest and least populated hill country, passing through or near Stranraer, New Luce, Bargrennan, St John's Town of Dalry, Sanquhar, Wanlockhead (the highest village in Scotland), Beattock, Ettrick, Lauder, Melrose, and Longformacus. The route combines forest tracks, moorland paths, and minor roads, and reaches its highest point at Lowther Hill (725 m). Waymarking is by the thistle symbol used for Scotland's Great Trails.

Walking the route

Most walkers complete the route in 12 to 16 days. Accommodation in the middle sections is sparse, and the trail has a reputation as the most demanding of Scotland's Great Trails; baggage transfer services and pre-booked pickups are commonly used. The "Waymerks" — a series of small kists hidden along the route containing commemorative coins — were installed as a treasure-hunt feature of the trail. A notable literary connection is with the Covenanters and with John Buchan's The Thirty-Nine Steps, set in the Galloway hills the path crosses.
