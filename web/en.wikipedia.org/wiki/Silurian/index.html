Silurian

The Silurian is a geologic period and system spanning 24.6 million years from the end of the Ordovician Period, at 443.8 million years ago, to the beginning of the Devonian Period, 419.2 million years ago.

Silurian Period
Chronology443.8 ± 1.5 – 419.2 ± 3.2 Ma
EtymologySilures, ancient Welsh tribe
ProposerRoderick Murchison, 1835
EpochsLlandovery, Wenlock, Ludlow, Přídolí

Subdivisions

Murchison named the system for the Silures, a tribe of Roman Britain, based on rocks in Wales and the Welsh Borders. It is divided into four epochs: Llandovery, Wenlock, Ludlow and Přídolí.

Life

Following the end-Ordovician extinction and glaciation, life recovered. Seas teemed with brachiopods, trilobites, graptolites, crinoids and the first coral-stromatoporoid reefs. Eurypterids (sea scorpions) reached giant sizes. Jawed fishes (acanthodians, early gnathostomes) and bony fish appeared, and the first vascular plants – such as Cooksonia – colonised land, along with early terrestrial arthropods and fungi. Climate stabilised into a warm greenhouse with high sea levels.
