Emotional labor

Emotional labor is the process of managing feelings and expressions to fulfil the emotional requirements of a job, in which workers are expected to regulate their emotions during interactions with customers, co-workers, and superiors. The term was coined by sociologist Arlie Hochschild in her 1983 book The Managed Heart.

Concept

Hochschild distinguished surface acting (modifying outward expressions) from deep acting (attempting to change inner feelings). Jobs requiring emotional labor include flight attendants, nurses, teachers, and service workers. Chronic emotional dissonance — the gap between felt and displayed emotion — has been linked to burnout and stress.

Later usage

The term has broadened in popular usage to describe unpaid emotional work in personal relationships, a usage distinct from Hochschild's original wage-labour definition.
