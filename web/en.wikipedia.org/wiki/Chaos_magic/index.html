Chaos magic

Chaos magic, also spelled chaos magick, is a contemporary magical practice that emerged in England in the 1970s. It is built around the idea that belief itself is a tool that can be adopted and discarded at will: practitioners freely borrow techniques from any tradition — ceremonial magic, shamanism, popular culture — treating paradigms as temporary frameworks for producing results rather than as truths.

History

Chaos magic developed from the writings of artist and occultist Austin Osman Spare, whose sigil techniques and concept of "Kia" influenced the founders. In 1976–77 Peter J. Carroll and Ray Sherwin placed advertisements that led to the formation of the Illuminates of Thanateros (IOT), a magical order devoted to chaos magic. Carroll's Liber Null (1978) and Psychonaut (1982), later combined as Liber Null & Psychonaut, became foundational texts, and Phil Hine's Condensed Chaos (1995) popularised the approach for a wider audience.

Beliefs and practices

Central concepts include:

  • Belief as a tool and deliberate "paradigm shifting" between belief systems.
  • Sigils — symbolic representations of a desired outcome, charged in a state of gnosis (altered consciousness) and then forgotten.
  • Servitors — thought-forms created to perform particular tasks, distinguished from more autonomous "egregores".
  • A pragmatic, results-based ethos often summarised by the slogan "nothing is true, everything is permitted".

Influence

Chaos magic has influenced modern occultism, the work of writer Grant Morrison (who explored it in the comic series The Invisibles), and wider popular culture, including the term "chaos magick" entering role-playing games and fiction.
