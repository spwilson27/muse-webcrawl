Silver medal

A silver medal, in sports and other similar areas involving competition, is a medal made of, or plated with, the metal silver awarded to the second-place finisher, or runner-up, of contests or competitions such as the Olympic Games, Commonwealth Games, etc. The outright winner receives a gold medal and the third place a bronze medal.

History

At the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896, winners were actually awarded silver medals, since gold was considered less pure a prize in some traditions; the now-standard gold–silver–bronze sequence for first, second, and third places was established at the 1904 Summer Olympics in St. Louis. Olympic silver medals are required to be made of at least 92.5% pure silver (sterling silver); the gold medal is in fact also mostly silver, plated with gold.

Other uses

Silver medals are also awarded by learned societies, exhibitions, and military and civil decoration systems for distinguished service or achievement below the standard of a gold award. In some academic and artistic competitions, no gold medal may be awarded in a given year if no entry meets the standard, while silver medals are given to the best entries.
