The Christian Science Monitor (CSM), commonly known as The Monitor, is a nonprofit news organization founded in 1908 by Mary Baker Eddy, the founder of the Church of Christ, Scientist, in Boston, Massachusetts. A daily newspaper for most of the twentieth century, it became primarily a web publication in 2009 while continuing a weekly print magazine.

Eddy founded the paper, at the age of 87, partly in response to the sensationalist journalism of her era, declaring that its object was "to injure no man, but to bless all mankind." Despite its name, the Monitor has been a secular newspaper that does not promote the doctrine of Christian Science, apart from a daily religious article published since its founding.

History

The Monitor quickly built a reputation for serious, non-sensational international reporting, with bureaus around the world. During the twentieth century it was especially noted for its coverage of foreign affairs and of the arts. In 2009, facing financial pressures, it became the first major American newspaper to replace its daily print edition with web-based publication, while launching a weekly print magazine.

Honors

The Monitor staff have won seven Pulitzer Prizes, including awards for international reporting and editorial writing, as well as numerous Overseas Press Club awards. Its reporting on topics such as the identification of the Unabomber's brother and the condition of American public schools has been widely recognized.
