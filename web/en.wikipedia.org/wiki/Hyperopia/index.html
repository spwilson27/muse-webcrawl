Hyperopia

Hyperopia
Other namesHypermetropia, long-sightedness, far-sightedness
SpecialtyOphthalmology, optometry
SymptomsBlurred near vision, eyestrain, headaches

TreatmentConvex lenses (glasses, contact lenses), refractive surgery

Hyperopia, also known as long-sightedness or far-sightedness, is a common refractive error of the eye in which distant objects may be seen clearly while near objects appear blurred. It occurs when light entering the eye is focused behind, instead of directly on, the retina, typically because the eyeball is too short from front to back or the cornea and lens have insufficient refractive power.

In hyperopia, light focuses behind the retina; a convex (plus) lens converges the rays so they focus on the retina.

Signs and symptoms

People with hyperopia often have difficulty with near tasks such as reading, and may experience eyestrain, headaches, fatigue and squinting. Young people can frequently compensate by accommodating—contracting the ciliary muscle to increase the lens's power—so mild hyperopia may produce no symptoms until the accommodative ability declines with age, typically from the forties onward (see presbyopia). Significant uncorrected hyperopia in children is associated with convergent strabismus (accommodative esotropia) and amblyopia.

Causes

Hyperopia may be axial (the eyeball is too short), refractive (the cornea is too flat or the lens too weak), or a combination. Most infants are born mildly hyperopic, and the error usually decreases as the eye grows. It has a substantial hereditary component.

Diagnosis and treatment

Hyperopia is diagnosed by an eye examination, often with retinoscopy; in children, cycloplegic refraction (using drops that temporarily paralyse accommodation) reveals the full amount of the error. It is corrected with convex ("plus") lenses in spectacles or contact lenses. Refractive surgery such as LASIK can reshape the cornea to correct mild-to-moderate hyperopia. Severity is measured in dioptres; values around +2.00 D or less are considered low, +2.25 to +5.00 D moderate, and above +5.00 D high.
