Caesarea Maritima

Caesarea Maritima, formerly Straton's Tower, was an ancient port city on the Mediterranean coast of present-day Israel, built by Herod the Great between 22 and 15 BC and named for Caesar Augustus. Its great artificial harbour, Sebastos, made it the administrative capital of Roman Judaea and an early centre of Christianity.

History

Herod built a palace, theatre, amphitheatre, hippodrome, aqueducts, and a temple to Roma and Augustus overlooking a harbour constructed with underwater concrete — a feat of Roman engineering. Pontius Pilate governed from here; an inscription bearing his name was found at the site. The apostle Paul was imprisoned in Caesarea, and Origen and Eusebius later made it a centre of Christian scholarship with a famous library. The city flourished under Byzantium, fell to Muslim armies in 640, was rebuilt by Crusaders, and finally destroyed by the Mamluks in 1265.

Archaeology

Excavations have revealed the harbour works, Herod's palace, the theatre (still used for concerts), and Crusader fortifications. The site is now a national park.
