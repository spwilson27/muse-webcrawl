Stagecoach (1939 film)

Stagecoach is a 1939 American Western film directed by John Ford and starring Claire Trevor and John Wayne in his breakthrough role. The screenplay by Dudley Nichols is an adaptation of Ernest Haycox's 1937 short story "The Stage to Lordsburg". The film follows a group of strangers riding on a stagecoach through dangerous Apache territory.

Plot

A stagecoach carries an unlikely group of passengers from Tonto, Arizona to Lordsburg, New Mexico, through territory where Geronimo and his Apache warriors are on the warpath: Dallas, a prostitute being driven out of town; the alcoholic Doc Boone; army officer's wife Lucy Mallory; gambler Hatfield; banker Gatewood; whiskey drummer Peacock; and marshal Curly, pursuing — then joined by — the escaped outlaw known as the Ringo Kid, who seeks revenge on the Plummer brothers for murdering his family. After the coach is attacked by Apaches and the travellers bond under fire, Ringo confronts the Plummers in Lordsburg and is allowed by Curly to ride off with Dallas to his ranch.

Production and legacy

Ford shot much of the film in Monument Valley on the Arizona–Utah border, establishing the landscape that would define the screen image of the American West. Orson Welles famously claimed to have watched Stagecoach more than forty times while preparing Citizen Kane. The film was nominated for seven Academy Awards, winning for Best Supporting Actor (Thomas Mitchell) and Best Music, Scoring. In 1995 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
