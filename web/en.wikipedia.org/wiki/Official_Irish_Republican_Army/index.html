Official Irish Republican Army

The Official Irish Republican Army (OIRA) was an Irish republican paramilitary organisation that emerged from the split in the Irish Republican Army in December 1969, at the start of the Troubles in Northern Ireland. The rival faction became the Provisional IRA. Its political wing was Official Sinn Féin, which evolved through the Workers' Party of Ireland.

Split and campaign

The split arose from disputes over the IRA leadership's move towards Marxist politics, its failure to defend Catholic areas during the August 1969 riots, and strategy towards Stormont, Westminster and Dublin. The Officials, nicknamed "Stickies" for the adhesive Easter lilies they wore, retained the IRA's traditional name and much of its Dublin leadership under Cathal Goulding.

Ceasefire

The OIRA took part in armed conflict in 1970–72, including feuds with the Provisionals and attacks on British soldiers and the original Bloody Sunday context in Derry. After the Aldershot bombing of February 1972, which killed seven civilian employees, caused public revulsion, the leadership called a ceasefire in May 1972. Thereafter the organisation largely confined itself to feuds — notably with the Irish National Liberation Army, which split from it in 1974 — and alleged criminal activity, while its political wing pursued electoral politics.

Later years

The OIRA never formally disbanded or decommissioned in the manner of the Provisional IRA, though it was reported to have disposed of some weapons in 2010 and has been considered inactive for decades. Its associated party, the Workers' Party, became a significant force in the Republic of Ireland in the 1980s before a major split in 1992.
