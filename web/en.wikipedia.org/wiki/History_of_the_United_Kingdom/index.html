The history of the United Kingdom covers the formation and development of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, created through the union of England, Scotland, Wales, and (in part) Ireland. The UK became the world's foremost industrial and naval power in the 19th century before declining relative to other powers in the 20th.

Formation of the union

The Laws in Wales Acts (1535–42) incorporated Wales into England. The crowns of England and Scotland were united in 1603 under James VI and I, and the Acts of Union 1707 created the Kingdom of Great Britain. The Act of Union 1801 added Ireland, forming the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland.

Industrial Revolution and empire

Britain pioneered the Industrial Revolution, transforming manufacturing, transport, and society. At its greatest extent in the early 20th century, the British Empire ruled roughly a quarter of the world's land and population. Constitutional reform gradually extended the franchise throughout the 19th century.

The 20th century

The First World War was followed by Irish independence (1922), leaving Northern Ireland in the UK. Britain stood against Nazi Germany in the Second World War, and postwar governments created the welfare state and the National Health Service (1948). Decolonization dissolved most of the empire, and Britain joined the European Economic Community in 1973.

Recent history

The late 20th century saw the Falklands War, the Thatcher governments' economic reforms, the Northern Ireland peace process (Good Friday Agreement, 1998), and devolution to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland. In a 2016 referendum the UK voted to leave the European Union, formally departing in 2020. A Scottish independence referendum in 2014 voted to remain in the UK.
