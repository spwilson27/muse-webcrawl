Gothic fashion

Gothic fashion is a clothing style marked by conspicuously dark, mysterious, antiquated and homogeneous features. It is worn by members of the Gothic subculture. Dress, typical of the Gothic movement, is typically dark and morbid, featuring black velvet, lace, fishnet stockings, leather, and tight clothing. A typical Gothic outfit makes use of make-up that emphasizes pale skin, dark eyeliner, and black or deep-red lipstick and nail polish. Style sources range from Victorian mourning dress and Gothic literature to punk and the costuming of horror films.

History

Gothic fashion emerged in the late 1970s and early 1980s around the British gothic rock scene — bands such as Bauhaus, Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Cure and Sisters of Mercy. Early goth style adapted punk's DIY ethos and black clothing, adding elements of glam rock theatricality: backcombed or teased hair, heavy eyeliner worn by all genders, pale foundation, fishnet garments, pointed boots ("winklepickers"), and silver jewellery featuring crosses, ankhs and other occult or religious symbols. Figures such as Siouxsie Sioux and Robert Smith became enduring style icons for the subculture.

During the 1980s and 1990s, designers both inside and outside the subculture developed the look. High fashion periodically drew on gothic motifs — black lace, corsetry, mourning veils — while specialist retailers and mail-order labels supplied the scene. In Japan, the visual kei movement and designers such as Moi-même-Moitié (founded by Mana) developed "gothic Lolita," a doll-like style mixing Victorian and Rococo silhouettes with gothic darkness, which became internationally influential.

Characteristics

Although diverse, gothic fashion is generally recognized by:

  • Color: predominance of black, with deep jewel tones, blood red, purple and white accents
  • Fabrics: velvet, lace, satin, leather, PVC, fishnet and mesh
  • Silhouettes: corsets, long coats, Victorian- and Edwardian-inspired dresses, tight trousers, platform boots
  • Makeup and hair: pale complexion, dark eyeliner and lipstick, dyed black or vividly colored hair, often teased or elaborately styled
  • Accessories: silver jewellery, chokers, crosses, ankhs, pentagrams, religious iconography, lace gloves, parasols and top hats

Substyles

Commentators commonly distinguish numerous substyles, including "trad goth" (the original 1980s look), "romantic goth" (Victorian and medieval fantasy influences), "cyber goth" (neon accents, goggles, synthetic dreads, industrial influences), "gothic Lolita," "deathrock" fashion (more closely tied to punk), "nu-goth" and "pastel goth" (which subvert the palette while retaining motifs). Festivals such as the Wave-Gotik-Treffen in Leipzig and Whitby Gothic Weekend serve as showcases for the breadth of gothic dress.

Influence

Gothic fashion has repeatedly influenced mainstream and high fashion, from runway revivals of Victorian mourning dress to the incorporation of black lace, corsetry and dark romanticism by designers such as Alexander McQueen, John Galliano and Rick Owens. Elements of goth style — black clothing, chokers, heavy eyeliner — periodically cycle into popular fashion, sometimes detached from the subculture itself.
