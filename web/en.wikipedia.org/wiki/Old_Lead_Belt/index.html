Old Lead Belt

The Missouri Mines State Historic Site at Park Hills.

The Old Lead Belt is a historic lead-mining district in southeastern Missouri, in the Saint Francois Mountains region about 70 miles south of St. Louis. For much of the 20th century it was the largest lead-producing district in the world.

History

Lead mining in Missouri began under French colonial rule in the early 18th century. The Old Lead Belt's deep mines, centred on towns such as Flat River (now Park Hills), Bonne Terre, Desloge and Leadwood, boomed after the St. Joseph Lead Company introduced diamond drilling and mechanised mining in the 1860s–1900s.

Decline and legacy

The district's mines closed by the 1970s as production shifted to the newer Viburnum Trend ("New Lead Belt") to the west. Mining left an extensive legacy of chat piles and contaminated sites, several of which became Superfund sites; the Missouri Mines State Historic Site preserves the district's history.
