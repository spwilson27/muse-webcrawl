Electricity sector in Norway

The electricity sector in Norway is dominated by hydroelectricity, which supplies roughly 90 percent of the country's electricity generation — one of the highest shares of renewable electricity in the world. Norway's mountainous terrain and high precipitation make it exceptionally well suited to hydropower, and the country's reservoirs hold about half of Europe's total reservoir storage capacity.

Generation

Total annual production is typically around 150–160 TWh, varying with precipitation. Hydropower accounts for about 90 percent, wind power for most of the remainder (around 10 percent), with small contributions from thermal generation. Norway is usually a net exporter of electricity through interconnectors to Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, Germany and the United Kingdom.

Market and grid

Norway deregulated its power market with the Energy Act of 1990 and participates in the Nordic power exchange Nord Pool. The state-owned Statnett operates the transmission grid, while the state enterprise Statkraft is the largest producer. Electricity prices vary between bidding areas, and Norway's traditionally low prices support energy-intensive industries such as aluminium and ferroalloys, as well as widespread electric heating and one of the world's highest rates of electric vehicle adoption.

History

Large-scale hydropower development began in the early twentieth century, underpinning electrochemical and metallurgical industries. Public ownership at state and municipal level remains extensive, and Norwegian law reserves natural resource rents from hydropower to the public through concession rules and taxation.
