Stikine River

Length: 539 km (335 mi)

Source: Spatsizi Plateau, British Columbia

Mouth: Gulf of Alaska, near Wrangell, Alaska

The Stikine River (pronounced "stick-EEN") is a major river in northern British Columbia, Canada, and southeastern Alaska in the United States. It drains a rugged, largely wilderness area of about 49,800 km2 (19,200 sq mi) and flows generally southwest for 539 km (335 mi) to reach the Pacific Ocean near Wrangell, Alaska.

The river is known for the Grand Canyon of the Stikine, an 83 km (52 mi) gorge through which the river runs between steep basalt walls; the canyon is sometimes described as dangerous and largely unrunable by boat, though expert kayakers have descended parts of it. The Stikine is also an important salmon river and a traditional territory of the Tahltan and Tlingit peoples.

Course

The Stikine rises on the Spatsizi Plateau in northwestern British Columbia, flows north and then southwest through the Coast Mountains, passes the community of Telegraph Creek, and crosses the international boundary into the Alaska Panhandle, emptying into the Gulf of Alaska east of Wrangell. Major tributaries include the Iskut, Tuya, and Chutine rivers.

History

The river's name comes from a Tlingit phrase meaning "the river", possibly "great river". It was explored by fur traders in the 19th century and was a route into the interior during the Stikine Gold Rush of the 1860s and the Cassiar and Klondike gold rushes. The lower river remains an important transportation corridor.
