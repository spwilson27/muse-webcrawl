Mica Dam is a hydroelectric dam spanning the Columbia River 135 kilometres north of Revelstoke, British Columbia, Canada. Completed in 1973 under the terms of the Columbia River Treaty, it was built by BC Hydro and is one of the largest earthfill dams in the world.

Construction and reservoir

The dam is 243 metres high and impounds Kinbasket Lake, a reservoir about 216 kilometres long named after a chief of the Shuswap people. The project flooded a long stretch of the Columbia valley known as the Big Bend, submerging settlements, farmland and part of the historic Big Bend Highway. Construction required a remote work camp — the community of Mica Creek — established to house the workforce.

Mica Generating Station

The underground powerhouse originally housed four generating units; two additional units were added in 2015–2016, bringing total installed capacity to about 2,805 megawatts and making the station one of the largest in British Columbia. Its output, together with the downstream Revelstoke Dam (added later outside the treaty), is central to the province's electricity supply. Under the Columbia River Treaty, the storage provided by Mica also delivers flood control and power benefits in the United States, for which Canada receives a share of downstream benefits known as the Canadian Entitlement.
