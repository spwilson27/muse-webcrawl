Bologna Process

[Image placeholder: Bologna Process]

The Bologna Process is a series of ministerial meetings and agreements between European countries to ensure comparability in the standards and quality of higher-education qualifications. It is named after the University of Bologna, where the Bologna Declaration was signed by education ministers from 29 European countries in 1999. The process created the European Higher Education Area (EHEA), launched in 2010.

Framework

The core features are a three-cycle system of bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees; a common credit system (ECTS); mutual recognition of qualifications under the Lisbon Recognition Convention; and quality-assurance cooperation through bodies such as ENQA. By the 2020s the EHEA had 49 member states, including several non-EU countries.

Criticism

Supporters credit Bologna with improving student mobility and degree comparability. Critics have argued that harmonisation can compress curricula, impose excessive standardisation, and shift universities toward market-oriented models; student protests against reforms occurred in several countries in the 2000s.
