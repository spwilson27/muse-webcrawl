Perfect Dark is a first-person shooter developed and published by Rare for the Nintendo 64, first released in 2000. It is considered the spiritual successor to Rare's acclaimed 1997 shooter GoldenEye 007 and features many of the same gameplay mechanics, while building on them with new features. The game follows Carrington Institute agent Joanna Dark as she uncovers a conspiracy involving the rival dataDyne corporation and two warring extraterrestrial races, the Maians and the Skedar.

Gameplay

As a first-person shooter, Perfect Dark features a wide variety of firearms, most of which have secondary fire functions. The single-player campaign consists of missions with primary, secondary, and bonus objectives that increase with difficulty setting. The game also features a notable emphasis on stealth, gadgets such as the CamSpy camera drone, and diverse environments ranging from corporate towers to an alien spacecraft.

The multiplayer mode is highly customizable, offering numerous game types, arenas, and computer-controlled "simulants" (bots) with adjustable personalities and difficulty. In addition to competitive play, the campaign can be played co-operatively (Counter-Operative mode lets a second player control enemies). The Nintendo 64 version requires the Expansion Pak for access to the full campaign and most multiplayer features.

Development

Development began in 1997 under director Martin Hollis, using an upgraded version of the GoldenEye 007 engine, before Hollis left Rare in 1998. The project then expanded in scope with features—including a planned face-mapping function using the Game Boy Camera—that were later cut. The game shipped in North America on May 22, 2000; by the end of its run it had sold millions of copies, though fewer than its predecessor.

Reception

Perfect Dark received critical acclaim upon release, with reviewers praising its graphics, artificial intelligence, weapon variety, and extensive multiplayer options; criticism focused on the occasionally inconsistent frame rate. It is routinely ranked among the greatest video games of all time, and it won the BAFTA Moving Images Award for 2000.

Legacy

A Game Boy Color companion game was released in 2000, and the prequel Perfect Dark Zero launched with the Xbox 360 in 2005. A remastered version of the original, developed by 4J Studios, was released for Xbox 360 in 2010 through Xbox Live Arcade, and was included in the Rare Replay compilation in 2015. A reboot, developed by The Initiative and Crystal Dynamics, was announced in 2020; the project was cancelled in 2025 amid studio closures at Xbox.
