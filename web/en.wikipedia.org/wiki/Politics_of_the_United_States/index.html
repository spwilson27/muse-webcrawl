Politics of the United States

The politics of the United States function within a framework of a federal democratic republic and presidential system. The President is head of state and government; Congress holds legislative power; the federal judiciary interprets laws. A two-party system dominated by Democrats and Republicans has prevailed since the mid-19th century.

Federal government

Separation of powers among executive, legislative and judicial branches, with federalism dividing authority between federal and state governments. Elections use first-past-the-post in most cases and the Electoral College for president.

Ideology and issues

American politics is shaped by liberal and conservative ideologies, debates over the economy, healthcare, immigration, guns, abortion and civil rights, and by interest groups, media and campaign finance.
