Drought in South Africa

Drought in South Africa is a recurring feature of the country's climate. Much of South Africa is semi-arid, rainfall is highly variable, and droughts are often associated with the El Niño phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation. Severe droughts affect agriculture, water supply for cities, hydroelectric generation and ecosystems across the region.

RegionSouth Africa, southern Africa
CausesRainfall variability, El Niño
Notable events2015–17 national drought; Cape Town water crisis 2017–18

Climate background

Average annual rainfall in South Africa is about 450 mm, well below the world average, and roughly a third of the country receives less than 300 mm. Summer rainfall dominates in the interior and east, while the Western Cape has a Mediterranean winter-rainfall regime, making different regions vulnerable in different seasons.

The 2015–2017 drought

The drought that began in 2015, driven by one of the strongest El Niño events on record, was South Africa's worst in decades. Dam levels fell nationally, maize harvests failed and the country became a net importer of grain, livestock were culled, and several provinces were declared disaster areas.

Cape Town "Day Zero"

From 2015 to 2017 Cape Town's supply dams fell steadily. The city imposed strict water restrictions and warned of "Day Zero", when municipal taps would be turned off and residents would queue at distribution points. Aggressive conservation, restrictions and improved rainfall in 2018 pushed Day Zero back and it was ultimately averted, making the episode a widely studied case of urban water management under climate stress.
