Perioperative

Perioperative care
Field: Surgery, anaesthesiology, nursing
Phases: Preoperative, intraoperative, postoperative

Perioperative care surrounds the surgical procedure itself

Perioperative is a term denoting the period around a patient's surgical procedure — literally, "around the operation". Perioperative care encompasses the whole surgical journey, conventionally divided into three phases, and is delivered by a multidisciplinary team including surgeons, anaesthetists, theatre nurses and practitioners, recovery staff, and allied professionals.

Phases

  • Preoperative — from the decision to operate until the patient enters the operating theatre: assessment and optimisation of comorbidities, consent, fasting, medication management, and anaesthetic planning.
  • Intraoperative — from entry to the operating suite until handover to the recovery unit: anaesthesia, positioning, asepsis, the operation itself, and physiological monitoring.
  • Postoperative — from recovery (post-anaesthesia care) through discharge and rehabilitation: pain control, wound care, early mobilisation, and management of complications.

Practice

Modern perioperative medicine emphasises prehabilitation, enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols, meticulous safety systems such as the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, and the management of perioperative risk in an increasingly elderly and comorbid surgical population. Perioperative nursing and operating department practice are recognised specialties in many countries, and university qualifications in perioperative medicine now exist for physicians of many backgrounds.
