Franco of Cologne

Franco of Cologne (fl. mid-13th century) was a German composer and music theorist who was one of the most influential figures in the development of Western musical notation. His treatise Ars cantus mensurabilis ("The Art of Mensurable Music"), written around 1260, is the first surviving account of a system in which the shapes of written notes themselves indicate their durations.

Ars cantus mensurabilis

In the system known as Franconian notation, note values — the long, breve, and semibreve — are distinguished by their noteheads, stems, and shapes, and can be combined within a mensuration analogous to modern time signatures. Franco also codified rules for rests, ligatures, and consonance and dissonance in counterpoint. This notation displaced earlier modal notation and laid the foundation for the mensural notation of the Ars Nova and, ultimately, for modern Western notation.

Music

Franco is credited with a small number of compositions, including the three-voice motet Munus tibi; motets in the Montpellier Codex and elsewhere show the practical application of his notational system.
