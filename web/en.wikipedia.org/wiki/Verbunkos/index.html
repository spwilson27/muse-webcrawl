Verbunkos

Verbunkos is an 18th-century Hungarian dance and music genre. The name derives from the German Werbung ("recruitment"), because the music originated in dances performed by hussar recruiting parties to attract young men to military service, with Gypsy (Romani) bands providing the music.

Style

A verbunkos typically alternates a slow section (lassú) with a fast one (friss), and is characterised by dotted rhythms, sudden ornaments, cadenza-like runs and the "bokázó" (heel-clicking) rhythm that ends phrases. Its scales and rhythms strongly influenced the later style hongrois heard in Haydn, Schubert, Liszt and Brahms, and the genre is a direct ancestor of the csárdás.

The violinist János Bihari was its most famous early exponent. Béla Bartók and Zoltán Kodály later studied verbunkos as a source of Hungarian national music, distinguishing its composed, urban character from older peasant traditions.
