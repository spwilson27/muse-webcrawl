Aino Maria Marsio-Aalto (born Aino Mandelin; 25 January 1894 – 13 January 1949), known as Aino Aalto, was a Finnish architect and a pioneer of Scandinavian design. She was the first wife and professional partner of Alvar Aalto, with whom she co-founded the design company Artek in 1935, and she is celebrated in her own right for her glassware, furniture, interiors, and architecture.

Career

Aino Mandelin qualified as an architect from the Helsinki University of Technology in 1920 and worked first in the office of Oiva Kallio before joining Alvar Aalto's practice in Jyväskylä in 1924; they married the same year. The Aaltos collaborated closely on the works that made their reputation, including the Paimio Sanatorium, the Viipuri Library, and the Villa Mairea. Attribution between the two partners is often difficult, and Aino's independent role — particularly in interiors, furniture, and the celebrated Aalto exhibitions — has been increasingly recognized by historians.

Design work

Her best-known design is the pressed-glass series Bölgeblick ("wave view"), which won a gold medal at the 1936 Milan Triennale and remains in production by Iittala as the "Aino Aalto" glassware, among the oldest designs still manufactured by the company. As managing director of Artek from 1941 until her death, she steered the firm through the war years. She also designed the interiors and furnishings of numerous Aalto buildings and the children's ward and other elements of the Paimio Sanatorium.

Death and legacy

Aino Aalto died of cancer in Helsinki in 1949, aged 54. Exhibitions and scholarship since the 1990s have substantially reassessed her contribution to modern Finnish architecture and design.
