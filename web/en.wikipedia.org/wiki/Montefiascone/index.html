Montefiascone is a town and comune of the province of Viterbo, in Lazio, central Italy. It stands on a hill on the southeast side of Lake Bolsena, about 15 km north of Viterbo, and is the highest town in Lazio.

History

The area was inhabited in Etruscan times, and the town's name is thought to derive from Mons Faliscorum, "mountain of the Falisci". In the Middle Ages Montefiascone became an important papal possession because of its strategic position on the Via Francigena and Via Cassia. Its fortress, the Rocca dei Papi, hosted several popes, including Urban IV and Martin IV.

Est! Est!! Est!!!

The town is famous for the legend of the wine Est! Est!! Est!!! di Montefiascone. According to the tale, in 1111 a German bishop, Johannes Fugger, sent his servant ahead to mark inns serving good wine with the word "Est" (Latin for "it is [here]"). At Montefiascone the wine was so fine that the servant wrote "Est! Est!! Est!!!" — and the bishop is said to have died from drinking too much of it.

Main sights

Sights include the Cathedral of Santa Margherita, with one of the largest domes in Italy, the Romanesque church of San Flaviano, and the remains of the Rocca dei Papi.
