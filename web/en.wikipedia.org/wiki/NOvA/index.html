NOvA (the NuMI Off-axis νe Appearance experiment) is a particle physics experiment designed to study neutrino oscillation, and in particular the appearance of electron neutrinos in a beam of muon neutrinos. It is operated by the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab) in Illinois, United States.

Design

NOvA uses the NuMI (Neutrinos at the Main Injector) beam produced at Fermilab. Two detectors are placed 14.6 milliradians (0.8 degrees) off the beam axis, which produces a narrow-band neutrino beam peaked at about 2 GeV at the far site. The near detector, weighing about 300 tonnes, sits underground at Fermilab, while the far detector — a 14-kilotonne tracking calorimeter built from PVC cells filled with liquid scintillator — is located at Ash River, Minnesota, 810 km away near the Canadian border. Both detectors measure the composition of the beam so that oscillation effects over the long baseline can be extracted.

Physics goals

The experiment aims to measure the neutrino mass hierarchy (whether the ordering of the neutrino mass states is normal or inverted), to search for CP violation in the lepton sector by comparing neutrino and antineutrino oscillations, and to constrain the mixing angle θ23, including whether it lies in the lower or upper octant. NOvA is a predecessor and complement to the larger DUNE experiment.

History

Construction of the far detector was completed in 2014, and the experiment has taken data in both neutrino and antineutrino beam modes since then. Together with the Japanese T2K experiment, NOvA provides the world's leading long-baseline oscillation data.
