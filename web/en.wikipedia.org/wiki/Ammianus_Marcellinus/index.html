Ammianus Marcellinus (born c. 330, died c. 391–400) was a Roman soldier and historian who wrote the penultimate major historical account surviving from antiquity, the Res Gestae. His work, in Latin, chronicled the history of Rome from the accession of the Emperor Nerva in 96 to the death of Valens at the Battle of Adrianople in 378, although only the sections covering the period 353–378 survive.

Life

Ammianus was born into a wealthy family in Antioch, then a Greek-speaking city of Roman Syria. He joined the army as a young man and served as a protector domesticus (an officer cadet of the imperial guard) under the general Ursicinus, accompanying him on campaigns in Mesopotamia. He was present at the Persian siege of Amida in 359 and narrowly escaped when the city fell. He later joined the Emperor Julian's Persian expedition of 363, witnessing Julian's death and the disastrous retreat that followed. By the 380s he had settled in Rome, where he wrote his history and gave public readings from it to acclaim.

The Res Gestae

The history originally comprised 31 books; the first 13, covering 96–353, are lost. The surviving 18 books (14–31) cover 353–378 in dense, vivid detail and are the principal narrative source for the period. Ammianus modelled himself on Tacitus and presented his work as a continuation of Tacitus's histories. Though a pagan writing under Christian emperors, he is generally regarded as impartial and judicious, praising Julian — his hero — without concealing his faults, and treating Christianity with a notable, if sometimes cool, even-handedness. Modern historians value his eyewitness campaign narratives, his geographical and ethnographic digressions, and his famous description of the Battle of Strasbourg (357) and of the Huns.
