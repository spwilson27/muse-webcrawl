J'Accuse…!

AuthorÉmile Zola
Published13 January 1898
NewspaperL'Aurore, Paris
SubjectThe Dreyfus affair

"J'Accuse…!" ("I accuse…!") is an open letter by the French novelist Émile Zola, published on the front page of the Paris newspaper L'Aurore on 13 January 1898. Addressed to President Félix Faure, the letter accused the French government and army of anti-Semitism and of unlawfully jailing Captain Alfred Dreyfus, a French artillery officer of Jewish descent, for espionage.

Background

Dreyfus had been convicted of treason in 1894 on the basis of a handwritten note — the bordereau — and sentenced to life imprisonment on Devil's Island. Evidence gathered by Lt. Col. Georges Picquart indicated that the real author was Major Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy, but the army suppressed the findings and acquitted Esterhazy in a perfunctory court-martial in January 1898, provoking Zola's intervention.

Contents and consequences

The headline, coined by editor Georges Clemenceau, framed a series of paragraphs each beginning "J'accuse", naming officers and officials Zola charged with obstructing justice. Zola intended to be prosecuted for libel so that the suppressed evidence could be aired in court; he was convicted and fled to England, but the affair split France and led ultimately to Dreyfus's retrial, pardon in 1899 and full exoneration in 1906. The phrase "J'accuse" has since become a generic term for a forceful public accusation of wrongdoing.
