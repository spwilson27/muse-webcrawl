Electric dipole moment

The electric dipole moment is a measure of the separation of positive and negative electrical charges within a system: that is, a measure of the system's overall polarity. The SI unit for the electric dipole moment is the coulomb-metre (C·m), while in atomic physics and chemistry the debye (D) is used for molecular dipole moments.

Definition

For a pair of opposite charges +q and −q separated by a displacement vector d, the electric dipole moment is p = qd, pointing from the negative to the positive charge. For a continuous charge distribution ρ(r), the dipole moment is the integral of rρ(r) over the volume.

Field and potential

Far from the dipole, its electric potential falls off as 1/r² and its field as 1/r³, much faster than for a net charge. The field of a dipole depends both on the distance and on the angle relative to the dipole axis.

Applications

Dipole moments are important in chemistry: polar molecules such as water (1.85 D) have permanent dipole moments arising from electronegativity differences between atoms, which determine properties such as boiling point, solubility, and intermolecular forces. Experimenters also search for permanent electric dipole moments of elementary particles such as the electron and neutron, which would signal violation of CP symmetry beyond the Standard Model; so far none has been observed, constraining many theories.
