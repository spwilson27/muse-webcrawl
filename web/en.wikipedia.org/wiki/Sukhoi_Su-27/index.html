The Sukhoi Su-27 (Russian: Сухой Су-27; NATO reporting name: Flanker) is a Soviet-origin twin-engine supersonic supermaneuverable fighter aircraft designed by Sukhoi. It was intended as a direct competitor to the large American fourth-generation fighters such as the F-15 Eagle, combining long range, heavy armament, and outstanding agility.

Design

The Su-27 features a blended wing-body layout with widely spaced Saturn AL-31F engines, a fly-by-wire control system, and an integral aerodynamic configuration that gives it exceptional manoeuvrability, including the famous "Pugachev's Cobra" manoeuvre publicly demonstrated in 1989. It is armed with a 30 mm GSh-30-1 cannon and up to ten air-to-air missiles, guided by a radar and an infrared search-and-track (IRST) system allowing passive attacks. Two-seat and multirole derivatives include the Su-30, the carrier-based Su-33, the strike Su-34, and the advanced Su-35; China produces related aircraft as the J-11, J-15, and J-16 families.

Operational history

Entering Soviet service in 1985, the Su-27 and its derivatives have served with Russia, Ukraine, China, India (as the Su-30MKI), and numerous other air forces. Flanker variants have seen combat in the Eritrean–Ethiopian War — where Ethiopian Su-27s scored air-to-air victories — and in the Russo-Ukrainian War. The type remains in production and service in upgraded forms.
