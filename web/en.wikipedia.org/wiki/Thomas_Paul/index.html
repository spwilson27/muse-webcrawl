Thomas Paul

Thomas Paul
Born: 1773, Exeter, New Hampshire
Died: 1831, Boston, Massachusetts
Occupation: Baptist minister; founder of the African Baptist Church, Boston

Thomas Paul (1773–1831) was an African American Baptist minister in New England who was one of the first Black preachers in the United States to lead both Black and integrated congregations. He founded the African Baptist Church of Boston, one of the earliest independent Black churches in the country.

Life and ministry

Paul was born free in Exeter, New Hampshire, in 1773 and was ordained as a Baptist minister in 1805. In Boston he gathered a congregation of free Black worshippers who had faced segregation in white churches; in 1806 they dedicated the African Meeting House on Joy Street in Beacon Hill, with Paul as pastor. The African Baptist Church became a centre of the Black community and of abolitionist activism.

Missionary work

In 1823 Paul travelled to Haiti as a missionary with the support of the Massachusetts Baptist Missionary Society, spending several months preaching and organising churches there. After returning to Boston he continued preaching, and also served congregations in New York, including the Abyssinian Baptist Church.

Legacy

Paul died in 1831. The African Meeting House survives as a National Historic Landmark and part of the Museum of African American History in Boston; his nephew, the writer and activist, was the minister and historian also active in abolitionist circles, and his family — including daughter Susan Paul, a teacher and abolitionist — remained prominent in the movement.
