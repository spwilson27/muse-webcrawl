John Amos Comenius (Czech: Jan Amos Komenský; 28 March 1592 – 15 November 1670) was a Moravian philosopher, pedagogue and theologian. He is considered the father of modern education, serving as the last bishop of the Unity of the Brethren (the Moravian Church) before becoming a religious refugee and one of the earliest champions of universal education.

Life

Comenius was born in Moravia and educated at the Herborn Academy and Heidelberg University. After ordination he worked as a teacher and pastor at Fulnek. The Thirty Years' War and the Battle of White Mountain (1620) drove the Brethren into exile; Comenius lost his library and manuscripts, and spent the rest of his life abroad—in Poland (Leszno), England, Sweden, Transylvania and finally Amsterdam, where he died.

Educational thought

Comenius advocated "teaching everything to everyone" (omnes, omnia, omnino): education for all children regardless of sex or class, conducted in the vernacular, proceeding from the senses and from the easy to the difficult. His Didactica Magna (written 1628–32, published 1657) set out a complete system of graded schools from infancy to university. His Janua Linguarum Reserata (1631) revolutionised language teaching, and his Orbis Sensualium Pictus (1658) was the first widely used illustrated textbook for children, remaining in use for two centuries.

Pansophy

Comenius's broader project was "pansophy"—a universal system of knowledge that would harmonise all learning and reform society. His plans for an international college of scholars anticipated later learned academies, and his fame led to invitations from England, Sweden, Hungary and the Dutch Republic.
