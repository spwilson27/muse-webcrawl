The Panhellenic Socialist Movement (Greek: Πανελλήνιο Σοσιαλιστικό Κίνημα, Panellínio Sosialistikó Kínima), known mostly by its acronym PASOK, is a social-democratic political party in Greece. Founded by Andreas Papandreou in 1974, it came to power in 1981 and, alternating with New Democracy, dominated Greek politics for decades.

History

PASOK won the 1981 elections with a programme of social reform, welfare expansion and a more independent foreign policy, and governed through most of the 1980s and again 1993–2004 under Papandreou and Costas Simitis, who took Greece into the eurozone. Under George Papandreou the party won in 2009 but was overwhelmed by the Greek government-debt crisis; its support collapsed in 2012 (a decline sometimes called "Pasokification"). It later participated in the Movement for Change (KINAL) and re-emerged under its own name in the 2020s.
