Paul Sérusier (1864–1927) was a French painter and a leading figure of Post-Impressionism, best known for The Talisman, a small landscape painted under the direction of Paul Gauguin in 1888, and for founding the group Les Nabis ("the prophets") with fellow students at the Académie Julian.

The Talisman and Les Nabis

In the autumn of 1888 at Pont-Aven in Brittany, Gauguin instructed Sérusier to paint the Bois d'Amour as he saw the colors — trees in pure vermilion, shadows in ultramarine — rather than imitate nature. The resulting panel, painted on a cigar-box lid, became a manifesto of Synthetism: flat areas of bold, non-naturalistic color. Back in Paris, Sérusier showed it to friends including Pierre Bonnard, Édouard Vuillard and Maurice Denis, who formed Les Nabis around its example.

Later work

Sérusier painted Breton landscapes, religious subjects and decorative works, drawn increasingly to Catholic faith, theosophy and theories of sacred geometry and color harmony. He taught at the Académie Ranson, published the treatise ABC de la peinture (1921), and spent his later years in Brittany. His work bridges Gauguin's Synthetism and the decorative modernism of the Nabis.
