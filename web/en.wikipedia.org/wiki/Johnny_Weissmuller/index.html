Johnny Weissmuller

Johnny Weissmuller (born János Weißmüller; June 2, 1904 – January 20, 1984) was an American Olympic swimmer, water polo player and actor. One of the world's fastest swimmers in the 1920s, he won five Olympic gold medals for swimming and one bronze medal for water polo, and set dozens of world records. After his swimming career, he became the sixth actor to portray Tarzan, a role he played in twelve feature films from 1932 to 1948 and for which he is best remembered.

Swimming career

Born in Freidorf, in the Banat region of Austria-Hungary (now Romania), Weissmuller emigrated to the United States as an infant and grew up in Chicago. Swimming for the Illinois Athletic Club, he never lost a race in his career: at the 1924 Paris Olympics he won three gold medals (100 m and 400 m freestyle and the 4 × 200 m relay) and a water polo bronze; at Amsterdam in 1928 he added two more golds. He was the first man to swim 100 m freestyle in under a minute.

Film career

Weissmuller's physique brought him to MGM, where Tarzan the Ape Man (1932), co-starring Maureen O'Sullivan as Jane, made him a star. His distinctive Tarzan yell became one of cinema's iconic sounds. After six MGM and six RKO Tarzan films he starred as Jungle Jim in a further series of films and a television series. Declining health and finances marked his later years; he died in Acapulco in 1984. The Tarzan yell was played at his funeral as his coffin was lowered.
