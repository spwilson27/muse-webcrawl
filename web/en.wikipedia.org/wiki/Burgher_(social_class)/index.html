Burgher (social class)

Burgher was a rank or title of a privileged citizen of a medieval or early modern European town. Burghers formed the pool from which city officials could be drawn, and their immediate families formed the social class of the medieval bourgeoisie. Admission to burgher status typically required proof of birth or residence, payment of a fee, and an oath, and conferred rights such as trading, owning property in the town, and voting in civic elections.

Terminology

The word derives from German Bürger and Dutch burger, both from burg, a fortified town or borough. In German-speaking lands a distinction was made between the Großbürger (grand burgher), a small hereditary elite, and ordinary burghers. In the Dutch Republic, burgers (poorters) formed the chartered citizenry of cities such as Amsterdam.

Other uses

In Sri Lanka, "Burgher people" denotes a distinct ethnic group of mixed European — chiefly Portuguese and Dutch — and Sri Lankan descent. In South Africa, the term burgher referred to citizens of the Boer republics, and during the Second Boer War to men liable for commando service. In some contexts the word is also used generically for a respectable middle-class townsman.
