Ptolemy I Soter (c. 367 BC – 282 BC) was a Macedonian Greek general under Alexander the Great and founder of the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt. One of Alexander's most trusted companions and bodyguards (somatophylakes), Ptolemy accompanied the conquest of Persia and distinguished himself in campaigns from Issus to India.

King of Egypt

On Alexander's death in 323 BC, Ptolemy secured the rich satrapy of Egypt in the Partition of Babylon. He famously diverted Alexander's funeral cortege and installed the conqueror's body in Alexandria, legitimising his rule. Through forty years of the Wars of the Diadochi he defended and consolidated Egypt against rivals such as Perdiccas and Antigonus, taking the royal title in 305/304 BC. His epithet Soter ("Saviour") was awarded by the Rhodians for aiding them during Demetrius Poliorcetes' siege of Rhodes.

Legacy

Ptolemy made Alexandria his capital, founded the great Library of Alexandria and Mouseion, promoted the god Serapis, and wrote a history of Alexander's campaigns (now lost) that became a principal source for Arrian. Judicious and pragmatic, he abdicated in favour of his son Ptolemy II in 285 BC — an orderly succession rare among the Diadochi — and died peacefully in 282 BC. Under his son, the Lighthouse of Alexandria, designed by Sostratus of Cnidus, was completed.
