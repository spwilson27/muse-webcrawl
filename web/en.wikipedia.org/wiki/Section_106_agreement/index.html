Section 106 agreement

Affordable housing in new developments is often secured through section 106 agreements

A Section 106 agreement (also known as a planning obligation) is a legal agreement between a local planning authority in England and a developer, made under section 106 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. Such agreements are used to mitigate the impact of a development that would otherwise be unacceptable in planning terms, and they run with the land, binding future owners.

Purpose and content

Planning obligations commonly require the developer to provide affordable housing, contribute to local infrastructure such as schools, roads, public open space and health facilities, or restrict the use of the land in specified ways. They may take the form of a bilateral agreement or a unilateral undertaking offered by the developer, for example at a planning appeal.

Legal tests

Regulations provide that a planning obligation may only constitute a reason for granting planning permission if it is necessary to make the development acceptable in planning terms, directly related to the development, and fairly and reasonably related in scale and kind to the development. Government policy in the National Planning Policy Framework restates these tests.

Relationship with the Community Infrastructure Levy

Since the introduction of the Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL) in 2010, section 106 agreements have been scaled back for general infrastructure funding in areas that charge CIL, but they remain the principal mechanism for securing site-specific mitigation and affordable housing.
