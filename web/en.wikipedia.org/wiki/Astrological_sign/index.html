Astrological sign

In Western astrology, astrological signs are the twelve 30-degree sectors that make up Earth's 360-degree orbit around the Sun. The signs enumerate from the first day of spring (the vernal equinox, in the Northern Hemisphere) known as the first point of Aries. The Western zodiac originated in Babylonian astrology and was later influenced by Hellenistic culture.

Classification

The signs are grouped by element or triplicity — fire, earth, air, and water — and by quality or quadruplicity — cardinal, fixed, and mutable. Each combination of element and quality is unique to one sign. These groupings are used in interpreting a natal chart, in which the "sun sign" is the sector occupied by the Sun at a person's birth.

Tropical and sidereal systems

Western astrology uses the tropical zodiac, anchored to the seasons, while most Hindu astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, anchored to the constellations. Because of the precession of the equinoxes, the two systems have drifted apart by roughly 24 degrees since they were aligned in antiquity.
