ChatGPT is a generative artificial intelligence chatbot developed by OpenAI and released on 30 November 2022. It uses large language models (LLMs) — initially GPT-3.5, later GPT-4 and subsequent models — to generate human-like text in conversation, and can write and debug computer programs, compose text in many genres, answer questions, summarise documents, translate languages and more. By January 2023 it had become the fastest-growing consumer software application in history, reaching an estimated 100 million monthly active users within two months of launch.

Training

ChatGPT is built on OpenAI's GPT family of transformer models, pre-trained on large corpora of text and then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF): human trainers ranked model outputs, and the resulting reward model was used to optimise the policy with proximal policy optimisation. The original release was fine-tuned from GPT-3.5. GPT-4, released in March 2023, added multimodal image input and substantially improved reasoning performance.

Adoption and impact

The service is offered free of charge with usage limits, with paid tiers (ChatGPT Plus, Team and Enterprise) providing access to more capable models and features such as web browsing, image generation with DALL·E, data analysis and custom "GPTs". Its launch intensified an industry-wide race in generative AI, prompted widespread debate about the effects of AI on education, work, misinformation and academic integrity, and led many schools and companies to adopt or ban its use. Later versions added voice interaction, memory, and "reasoning" models (the o-series and GPT-5 family).

Criticism

Critics note the model's tendency to produce plausible but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), possible biases inherited from training data, and concerns about plagiarism, privacy and environmental cost. OpenAI has introduced usage policies, refusal behaviours and transparency measures in response.
