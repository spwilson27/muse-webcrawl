Duchy of Jülich

The Duchy of Jülich (German: Herzogtum Jülich; Dutch: Hertogdom Gulik) was a state of the Holy Roman Empire on the west bank of the lower Rhine, in modern North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, between the Duchy of Cleves and the Electorate of Cologne. Its capital was the town of Jülich, an important fortress on the Rur river.

History

Jülich originated as a county (the Jülichgau) in the early Middle Ages and was raised to a duchy in 1356 under William I. In 1423 it was united with the neighbouring Duchy of Berg, and in 1511 the combined Jülich-Berg passed, with the county of Ravensberg, to the dukes of Cleves, creating the United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, one of the richest and most strategically placed territories in north-west Germany.

When Duke John William died childless in 1609, the succession dispute (the War of the Jülich Succession) drew in the Dutch Republic, France, Spain, and the Emperor, and briefly threatened a general European war. By the Treaty of Xanten (1614) Jülich and Berg went to the Catholic Palatinate-Neuburg line, while Cleves and Mark went to Brandenburg. Jülich remained with Palatinate-Neuburg, then the Electoral Palatinate and Bavaria, until it was annexed by France in the Revolutionary Wars and, in 1815, awarded to Prussia, within whose Rhine Province its fortress town became famous for its citadel and, later, its research centre.
