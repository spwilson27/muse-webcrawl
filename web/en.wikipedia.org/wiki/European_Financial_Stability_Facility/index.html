The European Financial Stability Facility (EFSF) is a special-purpose vehicle created by the member states of the eurozone in 2010 to preserve financial stability in Europe by providing financial assistance to eurozone states in difficulty. It was established in response to the European sovereign debt crisis, following the first Greek bailout in May 2010. The EFSF is backed by guarantees from eurozone governments totalling €780 billion and had an effective lending capacity of €440 billion, raised by issuing bonds and other debt instruments on the capital markets.

Operations

The EFSF was incorporated in Luxembourg on 7 June 2010 and became fully operational in August 2010. Its first bonds were issued in January 2011 to fund, together with the EU's European Financial Stabilisation Mechanism (EFSM), the rescue package for Ireland. It subsequently financed assistance programmes for Portugal (2011) and the second programme for Greece (2012), and lent to Spain in 2012 to recapitalise its banking sector. In total the EFSF disbursed about €174.6 billion to Ireland, Portugal and Greece, in addition to the Spanish bank programme arranged through it.

Expansion and succession

In 2011 eurozone leaders agreed to expand the EFSF's effective capacity and broaden its tools, allowing it to intervene in primary and secondary bond markets, lend pre-emptively, and finance bank recapitalisations. In October 2012 the permanent European Stability Mechanism (ESM) entered into force and took over the role of providing new assistance; the EFSF ceased new lending but continues to exist to manage its outstanding loans, which extend for decades, until they are repaid.
