Football (ball)

A football is the ball used in the sport of football. The name came from the ball's use in the sport, but balls similar in shape and size are used in several kinds of football, including association football (soccer), gridiron football (American and Canadian), rugby football, and Gaelic football. The shape and construction of the football vary by code: some are spherical, while others are spheroid (oval or pointed at the ends).

Association football

The association football ball is spherical and, under Law 2 of the Laws of the Game, must have a circumference of 68–70 cm (27–28 in) and a mass of 410–450 g (14–16 oz) at the start of a match. The classic design, popularized by the Adidas Telstar at the 1970 World Cup, consists of 32 panels — 12 black pentagons and 20 white hexagons — forming a truncated icosahedron. Modern match balls are thermally bonded rather than stitched and use textured synthetic surfaces.

Gridiron football

The American and Canadian football is a prolate spheroid with pointed ends, about 28 cm (11 in) long, with a circumference of roughly 56 cm (22 in) at its widest. It is traditionally made of leather with a prominent set of laces used to improve grip when throwing. Its shape allows it to be thrown in a tight spiral and kicked end over end.

Rugby football

Rugby balls are also prolate spheroids but are larger and less pointed than gridiron balls, with four panels and a blunt profile suited to passing by hand and kicking. Rugby league balls are slightly smaller and more pointed than rugby union balls.
