Othona

Othona was a Roman Saxon Shore fort located near modern Bradwell-on-Sea in Essex, England, at the mouth of the River Blackwater. Listed in the late Roman military document the Notitia Dignitatum, it guarded the Essex coast against Saxon seaborne raiders.

Much of the fort's stone was later robbed for building material, but parts of its walls survive. Around 654 AD, St Cedd, a missionary bishop of the East Saxons, founded a monastery within the old fort's precinct and built the Chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall, one of the oldest largely intact churches in England, which still stands on the seawall site today.

The modern Othona Community, a Christian retreat community founded in the area in the 20th century, takes its name from the Roman fort.
