AIDS activism

AIDS activism refers to the social and political movements that emerged in response to the HIV/AIDS epidemic beginning in the early 1980s. Activists — many of them people living with HIV and members of the gay community most affected in the epidemic's first years — campaigned for faster drug approvals, research funding, anti-discrimination protections, public education, and affordable treatment access worldwide.

Early organizing

Community organizations such as Gay Men's Health Crisis (founded 1982 in New York) provided care and information when government responses lagged. The 1983 Denver Principles, drafted by people with AIDS, asserted their right to participate in decisions affecting their lives. In 1987, ACT UP (AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power) formed in New York and became famous for confrontational direct actions, including protests at the FDA and the New York Stock Exchange, and the "Silence = Death" graphic campaign.

Impact

Activist pressure reshaped clinical trial design and accelerated drug approval processes at the FDA, contributed to price reductions for antiretroviral drugs, and later drove global access campaigns, including the Treatment Action Campaign in South Africa and efforts that led to initiatives such as PEPFAR and the Global Fund.
