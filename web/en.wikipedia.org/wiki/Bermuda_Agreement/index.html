Bermuda Agreement

The 1946 agreement shaped transatlantic aviation for three decades.

The Bermuda Agreement, formally the Agreement between the Government of the United Kingdom and the Government of the United States relating to Air Services between their Respective Territories, was a bilateral air transport agreement signed in Bermuda on 11 February 1946. It was the first major bilateral aviation treaty of the post-war era and became the model — the "Bermuda type" — for hundreds of air service agreements worldwide.

Background

The Chicago Convention of 1944 had failed to agree on an international economic regime for air transport, leaving traffic rights to be negotiated bilaterally. The United States favoured liberal competition and free capacity, while Britain, rebuilding a weakened industry, sought to fix routes, frequencies and fares in advance. The Bermuda conference reconciled the two positions by compromise.

Terms

The agreement exchanged specified routes — including US airline access to London and onward fifth-freedom rights — while providing that capacity should bear a close relationship to traffic demand and be subject to ex post facto governmental review rather than prior control. Fares were to be set by the airlines through the IATA rate-making machinery, subject to approval by both governments. Either side could designate multiple airlines, and designated carriers such as Pan Am and BOAC built their transatlantic networks under its terms.

Bermuda II and legacy

Dissatisfied with the imbalance of the arrangement, the United Kingdom denounced the agreement in 1976; the replacement treaty, Bermuda II (1977), was significantly more restrictive, limiting US carriers' access to Heathrow (the "Heathrow User" restrictions that confined scheduled US services there to Pan Am and TWA) and tightening capacity controls. Bermuda II governed US–UK aviation until the EU–US Open Skies agreement took effect in 2008. Despite its supersession, the original Bermuda principles — route schedules, designation, capacity by review, and IATA tariffs — defined the framework of international aviation for a generation.
