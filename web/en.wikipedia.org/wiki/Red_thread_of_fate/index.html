Red thread of fate

The red thread of fate (simplified Chinese: 红线; traditional Chinese: 紅線; pinyin: hóngxiàn), also referred to as the red thread of marriage and by other names, is an East Asian belief originating from Chinese mythology. It is commonly thought of as an invisible red cord around the finger of those that are destined to meet one another in a certain situation, as they are "their true love".

Legend

According to Chinese legend, the deity in charge of the red thread is Yue Lao (月老), the old lunar matchmaker god. Yue Lao is said to appear under the moon and tie together with a red silk thread all couples predestined to marry. The most famous telling involves a young man named Wei Gu in the Tang dynasty, who was shown his future wife — then a small child — by the old man, and despite Wei Gu's later attempt to have the girl killed, the two eventually married, the wound from the attack confirming her identity.

Variations

In Chinese tradition the cord is tied around the ankles, while in Japanese culture it is bound around the little finger (pinky), and in Korean culture around the thumb. The thread is said to be able to stretch, tangle and contract, but never break. The concept has also spread more loosely to mean destined encounters of any kind, not only romantic ones, and appears widely in East Asian popular culture, including film, anime and manga.
