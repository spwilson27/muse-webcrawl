Pam Grier

Pamela Suzette Grier (born 26 May 1949) is an American actress and singer, celebrated as one of the first female action stars of cinema. She rose to fame in the 1970s with leading roles in blaxploitation films such as Coffy (1973) and Foxy Brown (1974), playing bold, self-reliant heroines who avenged wrongs against their communities.

Career

Born in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, and raised largely in Colorado, Grier was discovered while working as a receptionist and began acting in Roger Corman productions including The Big Doll House (1971). Her AIP films of the mid-1970s made her the genre's defining star. After the blaxploitation cycle faded she worked steadily in film and television, appearing in Miami Vice, Fort Apache, The Bronx, and Above the Law.

Jackie Brown and later work

Quentin Tarantino wrote the title role of Jackie Brown (1997) for Grier, adapting Elmore Leonard's novel Rum Punch; her acclaimed performance earned Golden Globe and Screen Actors Guild nominations and revived her career. She later starred in the series The L Word as Kit Porter and published her memoir, Foxy: My Life in Three Acts, in 2010.
