Google Photos is a photo sharing and storage service developed by Google. It was announced in May 2015 at Google I/O and spun out of the photo features of Google+. The service automatically backs up photos and videos from phones and computers, organises them using machine learning — including face grouping, object and place recognition, and natural-language search — and generates albums, collages, animations, and "Memories" automatically.

History

At launch, Google Photos offered unlimited storage for photos up to 16 megapixels and videos up to 1080p in "high quality" (compressed) form, which made it extremely popular; it reached 500 million users within two years and one billion by 2019. The unlimited tier ended on 1 June 2021, after which uploads began counting against the 15 GB shared Google account storage quota, a change that drew significant criticism.

Features

Features include an Assistant (later "Memories") tab, shared libraries and partner sharing, automatic red-eye and lighting fixes, an integrated editor, printing of photo books, and integration with Google Lens for identifying objects in images. The service has raised recurring discussion about facial recognition and privacy, including an Illinois biometric-privacy settlement.
