The M3 Bradley, officially the M3 Cavalry Fighting Vehicle (CFV), is an American tracked reconnaissance vehicle and a member of the Bradley Fighting Vehicle family. It is closely related to the M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicle, sharing the same chassis, turret, and armament.

Role and design

The M3 is designed to perform armoured reconnaissance and security missions for cavalry units. Externally it is almost identical to the M2, but internally it differs considerably: instead of a squad of infantry, the M3 carries two scouts plus substantially more ammunition — additional 25 mm rounds and extra TOW anti-tank missiles — and extra radios and observation equipment. The firing ports fitted to the M2's troop compartment are deleted on the M3.

The vehicle is crewed by a commander, gunner, and driver, with two scouts carried in the rear. Its aluminium and steel armour protects against small arms and shell splinters, and later variants received explosive reactive armour and improved survivability features.

Service

The Bradley family entered U.S. Army service in the early 1980s and saw extensive combat in the 1991 Gulf War and the 2003 invasion of Iraq, where the M3 performed reconnaissance for armoured formations.
