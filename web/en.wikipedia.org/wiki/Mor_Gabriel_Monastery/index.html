Mor Gabriel Monastery (Syriac: ܕܝܪܐ ܕܡܪܝ ܓܒܪܐܝܠ, Dayro d-Mor Gabriel), also known as Deyrulumur, is the oldest surviving Syriac Orthodox monastery in the world. It is located on the Tur Abdin plateau near Midyat in Mardin Province, south-eastern Turkey, close to the Syrian border. Founded in 397 by Mor Samuel and Mor Simon, it has functioned as a centre of Syriac Christian life for over 1,600 years.

History

The monastery grew rapidly in the Byzantine period, receiving patronage from emperors including Anastasius I, who is credited with building works around 512. It is named after Mor Gabriel, bishop of Tur Abdin (died 668), whose tomb is in the monastery and who is remembered for reviving its community. At its height the monastery housed hundreds of monks and was a seat of bishops and a school of Syriac learning; its church contains notable mosaics and its crypt holds the tombs of thousands of monks and bishops.

The community survived periods of persecution, including the massacres of 1915 (the Sayfo), which devastated the Syriac population of Tur Abdin; the monastery was occupied and its monks dispersed, but it was re-established in the 1920s. Today a small community of monks, nuns, and students remains, and the monastery serves as the spiritual centre of the remaining Syriac Christians of Tur Abdin and of the diaspora, though its land has been subject to long-running legal disputes with neighbouring villages and the Turkish state.
