Engelbert Humperdinck

Engelbert Humperdinck (1 September 1854 – 27 September 1921) was a German composer. He is known widely today for his opera Hansel and Gretel (1893). Humperdinck was a protégé of Richard Wagner, whom he assisted in the production of Parsifal at Bayreuth in 1882.

Life and career

Born1 September 1854, Siegburg, Kingdom of Prussia
Died27 September 1921, Neustrelitz, Germany
OccupationComposer
Notable workHansel and Gretel (1893)

Humperdinck was born at Siegburg in the Rhine Province. He studied at the Cologne Conservatory and won scholarships that allowed him to travel in Italy, where he met Wagner in Naples in 1879. Wagner invited him to Bayreuth, where he assisted with the first production of Parsifal. He subsequently taught at conservatories in Barcelona and Cologne and worked as an editor and critic.

Hansel and Gretel

His most famous work began as incidental songs written for his sister Adelheid Wette's play for her children, based on the Grimm fairy tale. It grew into a full opera, premiered in Weimar on 23 December 1893 under the baton of Richard Strauss, who praised it highly. Combining Wagnerian orchestral technique with simple folk-like melodies, Hansel and Gretel became an enduring success and is now traditionally performed at Christmas.

Other works and legacy

Humperdinck's other operas include Königskinder (1897, revised 1910), whose premiere of the revised version took place at the Metropolitan Opera in New York. He also composed incidental music for plays by Shakespeare and Aristophanes, songs, and choral works. His students included the composer Kurt Weill.
