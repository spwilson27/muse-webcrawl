Avidyā (Buddhism)

Avidyā (Sanskrit: अविद्या; Pali: avijjā) is a Buddhist term usually translated as "ignorance" or "delusion". It is regarded as the root cause of suffering (duḥkha) and the first link in the twelve nidānas of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), the chain that keeps beings cycling through saṃsāra.

Meaning

In Buddhism, avidyā is not simply a lack of information but a fundamental misperception of reality: taking what is impermanent (anitya) as permanent, what is suffering as pleasant, what is without self (anātman) as self, and what is impure as pure. In Mahayana texts it also includes ignorance of emptiness (śūnyatā).

In the Abhidharma

The Abhidharma literature classifies avidyā as one of the primary unwholesome mental factors (kleśa). In Theravada, avijjā heads the list of the ten fetters and is destroyed only at full awakening; in the sequence of insight, dispelling ignorance is synonymous with the arising of knowledge (vijjā).

Overcoming ignorance

The Buddhist path — ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom — is directed at uprooting avidyā. Its complete eradication through realization of the Four Noble Truths and dependent origination marks liberation from rebirth.
