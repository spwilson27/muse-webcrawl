TG4 (Irish: Teilifís na Gaeilge 4) is an Irish free-to-air public service television channel that broadcasts primarily in the Irish language. It was launched on 31 October 1996 as Teilifís na Gaeilge (TnaG) and rebranded as TG4 in 1999. The channel is headquartered in Baile na hAbhann in the Connemara Gaeltacht.

Programming

TG4 is noted for its Irish-language drama and documentary output, including the long-running soap opera Ros na Rún, and for landmark series such as the western An Klondike. It provides live coverage of Gaelic games, rugby and other sports, much of it in Irish. A substantial part of the schedule consists of acquired programming, including children's programmes dubbed into Irish, and the channel also carries the Irish-language news service Nuacht TG4. TG4's creative use of subtitles has made its Irish-language content accessible to viewers with limited Irish.

Organisation

The channel is operated by the statutory body Teilifís na Gaeilge, funded by a combination of licence-fee income (channelled through RTÉ arrangements and direct exchequer funding) and commercial revenue. It is watched by a small but loyal audience and is regarded as a cornerstone of Irish-language media policy.
