Greene County is a county in the US state of New York, located on the west bank of the Hudson River south of Albany. Its county seat is the village of Catskill. The county was formed in 1800 from parts of Albany and Ulster counties and named in honour of Nathanael Greene, a general in the American Revolutionary War.

Geography

The western part of the county is dominated by the high peaks of the Catskill Mountains, including Hunter Mountain, one of the highest summits in the range, while the eastern portion descends to the Hudson River valley. Much of the county lies within the Catskill Park. Rivers include Catskill Creek, Schoharie Creek and Kaaterskill Clove, famous for its waterfalls and for inspiring painters of the Hudson River School.

History

The area was settled by Dutch and Palatine German colonists in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. In the nineteenth century the mountaintop became celebrated as America's first great tourist destination: the Catskill Mountain House, perched above Kaaterskill Clove from 1823, drew visitors from around the country, and artists such as Thomas Cole, who lived in the village of Catskill, made the landscape famous. Tanning, quarrying bluestone, brickmaking and river trade supported the valley towns.

Communities and economy

Towns in the county include Catskill, Athens, Cairo, Coxsackie, Hunter, Windham and Tannersville. Tourism remains central to the economy, with skiing at Hunter Mountain and Windham Mountain, hiking, and second homes. The population was about 48,000 at recent censuses, making Greene County one of the less populous counties in the state.
