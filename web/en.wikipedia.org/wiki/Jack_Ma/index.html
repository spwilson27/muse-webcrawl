Jack Ma Yun (Chinese: 马云; born 10 September 1964) is a Chinese business magnate and philanthropist, best known as the co-founder of Alibaba Group, the e-commerce and technology conglomerate behind Taobao, Tmall and Alipay. He became one of China's richest and most famous entrepreneurs and a global symbol of its internet boom.

Early life

Ma was born in Hangzhou and taught himself English by guiding foreign tourists, acquiring the name "Jack" from a visitor. After repeatedly failing China's college entrance examination, he eventually graduated from Hangzhou Normal University in 1988 and worked as an English teacher. A 1995 trip to the United States introduced him to the internet; on returning home he founded China Pages, an online directory, before working for a government-backed internet venture.

Alibaba

In 1999 Ma and seventeen co-founders launched Alibaba.com from his Hangzhou apartment as a marketplace connecting Chinese manufacturers with overseas buyers. Backed by SoftBank and Yahoo investments, the group created the consumer marketplace Taobao (2003), which out-competed eBay in China, and the payments service Alipay (2004). Alibaba's 2014 New York Stock Exchange IPO, raising about $25 billion, was then the largest in history. Ma stepped down as CEO in 2013 and retired as executive chairman in 2019.

Later years

After a speech in October 2020 criticising Chinese financial regulation, the IPO of affiliate Ant Group was suspended and Chinese authorities launched an antitrust crackdown on the tech sector; Ma largely withdrew from public life, spending time abroad before returning to China. Through the Jack Ma Foundation he funds education and environmental projects, and he remains a UN advocate for small business and the Sustainable Development Goals.
