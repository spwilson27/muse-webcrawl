Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub

Ecoregion: Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub
BiomeTemperate broadleaf and mixed forests; Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub
GeographyRegions with Mediterranean climates on five continents
ClimateHot, dry summers and mild, wet winters

Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub is a biome defined by the World Wide Fund for Nature. The biome is generally characterized by dry summers and rainy winters, although in some areas rainfall may be uniform. Summers are typically hot in low-lying inland locations but can be cool near colder ocean waters. Winters are typically mild to cool in low-lying locations but can be cold in inland and higher locations. All these ecoregions are highly distinctive, collectively harboring 10% of the Earth's plant species.

The biome occurs in five widely separated regions: the Mediterranean Basin, central Chile (the matorral), the Cape fynbos of South Africa, southwestern and southern Australia, and California and the Baja California Peninsula in North America (the chaparral). Vegetation types range from forests to woodlands, savannas, shrublands, and grasslands; "mosaic habitat" landscapes are common, where different vegetation types are interleaved with one another in complex patterns created by variations in soil, topography, exposure to wind and sun, and fire history.

Vegetation

Mediterranean ecosystems are dominated by evergreen sclerophyllous shrubs and trees, whose small, hard, waxy leaves reduce water loss during the dry summer. Characteristic plants include olive, carob, cypress, and stone pine in the Mediterranean Basin; Banksia and eucalypts in Australia; Protea in the fynbos; and chamise, manzanita, and coast live oak in California chaparral. Many plants are fire-adapted, resprouting after burns or germinating from fire-cued seeds.

Ecology and biodiversity

Though Mediterranean-climate regions cover only a small fraction of the Earth's land surface, they support disproportionately high biodiversity and endemism. The Cape Floristic Region and Southwest Australia are recognized as biodiversity hotspots. Fire plays a central role in maintaining many of these ecosystems, and many species depend on periodic burning.

Threats

Mediterranean landscapes are among the most heavily modified by agriculture, grazing, urbanization, and altered fire regimes. Climate change, which is projected to lengthen dry seasons in several Mediterranean regions, poses an additional threat to these ecosystems.
