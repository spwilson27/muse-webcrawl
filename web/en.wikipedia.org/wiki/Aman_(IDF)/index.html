The Military Intelligence Directorate (Hebrew: אגף המודיעין, Agaf HaModi'in), commonly abbreviated Aman, is the central, overarching military intelligence body of the Israel Defense Forces. Aman was created in 1950, when the Intelligence Department was spun off from the IDF's General Staff; it is responsible for providing the government and the armed forces with intelligence assessments, early warning of war, and target intelligence.

Aman is one of the three pillars of the Israeli intelligence community, alongside the Mossad (foreign intelligence) and Shin Bet (internal security). Unlike most comparable Western organisations, Aman both collects and analyses intelligence, and in Israeli practice its national estimates carry great — at times contested — weight with political decision-makers.

History

During Israel's early years, intelligence duties were divided among several bodies. The Intelligence Department of the General Staff was formalised under Isser Be'eri and later reorganised under Yehoshafat Harkabi, who is credited with professionalising Aman in the 1950s. Aman played central roles in the 1956 Suez Crisis, the 1967 Six-Day War — where its assessments and the air force's planning helped achieve surprise — and in the intelligence failure preceding the 1973 Yom Kippur War, after which the Agranat Commission's criticism led to major reforms, including strengthening research pluralism and the creation of a "devil's advocate" function (the Ipcha Mistabra unit) charged with challenging consensus estimates.

Structure

Aman comprises several major components:

  • Research Division — produces strategic and operational intelligence assessments.
  • Collection Division — oversees collection units, including Unit 8200, the IDF's signals-intelligence unit, and Unit 504, which runs human sources.
  • Unit 9900 — visual intelligence (IMINT), including satellite and aerial imagery analysis.
  • Special Operations Division and the elite Sayeret Matkal reconnaissance unit, which is operationally subordinate to Aman.
  • Information Security Department — responsible for military censorship and safeguarding classified information.

Directors

The Director of Military Intelligence (DMI) holds the rank of major general (aluf). Notable directors have included Yehoshafat Harkabi, Meir Amit, Aharon Yariv, Shlomo Gazit, Yehoshua Saguy (in post during the 1982 Lebanon War), Ehud Barak, Uri Sagi, Moshe Ya'alon, Amos Malka, Amos Yadlin, Aviv Kochavi, Herzi Halevi, and Tamir Hayman. Aharon Haliva, who served from 2021, announced his resignation in April 2024, accepting responsibility for the intelligence failures surrounding the October 7, 2023 attack, and was succeeded by Shlomi Binder.
