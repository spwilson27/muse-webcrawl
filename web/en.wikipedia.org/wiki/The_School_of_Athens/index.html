The School of Athens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The School of Athens (1509–11): Plato and Aristotle walking at the centre of a vaulted basilica filled with the philosophers of antiquity]

The School of Athens (Italian: Scuola di Atene) is a fresco by Raphael in the Stanza della Segnatura, Vatican Palace, painted between 1509 and 1511. One of the defining images of the High Renaissance, it gathers the great philosophers and scientists of classical antiquity in a vast, Bramante-like architectural space, symbolising the harmony of philosophy and reason in the room that housed Julius II's library.

At the centre walk Plato — holding his Timaeus, pointing upward, given Leonardo da Vinci's features by tradition — and Aristotle, holding the Ethics, hand levelled toward the earth. Around them are grouped Pythagoras, Heraclitus (a brooding late addition painted as Michelangelo), Euclid bending with compasses, Ptolemy with a globe, Diogenes sprawled on the steps, and at the right edge Raphael's own self-portrait beside his master Perugino.

Commission and programme

The fresco is one of four in the Segnatura cycle representing Theology (Disputation of the Holy Sacrament, facing it), Philosophy, Poetry (the Parnassus) and Justice. Its serene perspective architecture quotes Donato Bramante, Raphael's mentor, who also appears in the picture as Euclid. Identifications of many figures remain conjectural, since no contemporary key survives.

Fame

Long reproduced as the emblem of Renaissance humanism and classical learning itself, the fresco forms with Michelangelo's Sistine ceiling — painted simultaneously a few rooms away — the twin summit of the High Renaissance in Rome. The full-scale cartoon survives in the Pinacoteca Ambrosiana, Milan.
