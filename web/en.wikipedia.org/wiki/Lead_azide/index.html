Lead azide (Pb(N3)2) is the lead salt of hydrazoic acid. It is a highly sensitive primary explosive that has been widely used as an initiator in detonators and blasting caps, because it reliably transitions from deflagration to detonation and can set off less sensitive secondary explosives such as RDX or TNT.

Properties

Lead azide is a white to buff crystalline solid, sparingly soluble in water. It is extremely sensitive to impact, friction, heat, and electrostatic discharge, and must be handled with great care. Its detonation velocity is approximately 5,180 m/s at a density of 4.0 g/cm3. Unlike many other primary explosives, lead azide becomes more dangerous in contact with copper and its alloys, with which it can form the even more sensitive copper azide; for this reason it is typically loaded into aluminium housings.

Preparation

Lead azide is prepared by the metathesis reaction between sodium azide and lead nitrate or lead acetate in aqueous solution. Dextrin or other colloids are often added during precipitation to control crystal growth and reduce sensitivity, producing the material known as dextrinated lead azide.

History and use

First prepared in the laboratory in the early twentieth century, lead azide largely replaced mercury fulminate in detonators because it is more stable in storage, more powerful, and effective in smaller quantities. It remains a standard initiating explosive in military and commercial detonators, though environmental concerns about lead have driven research into lead-free alternatives such as DBX-1 (potassium 5,7-dinitro[2,1,3]-benzoxadiazol-4-yl oxidate).

Safety

Because of its sensitivity, lead azide is manufactured and handled remotely wherever possible, and quantities in process are kept to a minimum. It is also toxic, as are all soluble lead compounds.
