Lancaster, Pennsylvania

Lancaster is a city in and the county seat of Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, United States. With a population of about 58,000 at the 2020 census, it is one of the oldest inland cities in the United States. It lies about 59 miles (95 km) west of Philadelphia.

History

Originally called Hickory Town, the city was renamed by John Wright after the English city of Lancaster. Its symbol, the red rose, comes from the House of Lancaster. Lancaster served as the capital of Pennsylvania from 1799 until 1812, when Harrisburg became the capital. For one day, on 27 September 1777, Lancaster was the capital of the United States when the Continental Congress fled Philadelphia. The Conestoga wagon and the Pennsylvania long rifle were both developed in the area.

Geography and demographics

The city lies in south-central Pennsylvania in the heart of Pennsylvania Dutch Country, an area known for its Amish and Mennonite communities. Lancaster is a centre for tourism, healthcare, education and manufacturing, and is home to Franklin & Marshall College.

Culture

Lancaster Central Market, one of the oldest continuously operating farmers' markets in the United States, and the Fulton Opera House, among the oldest working theatres in the country, are notable landmarks.
