Mintaka (Delta Orionis, abbreviated δ Ori) is a multiple star system about 1,200 light-years from the Sun in the constellation of Orion. Together with Alnilam and Alnitak, it forms Orion's Belt, an asterism known since antiquity in many cultures. Mintaka is the westernmost of the three belt stars. Its name derives from Arabic manṭaqa, meaning "the belt".

Properties

Mintaka lies very close to the celestial equator, so it rises and sets almost exactly due east and west and is visible from nearly all inhabited latitudes. The main system is an eclipsing binary consisting of a bright O-type giant and a B-type companion with an orbital period of about 5.73 days, causing small periodic dips in brightness. Around this pair orbits a third star with a period of roughly 346 years, and further fainter components complete a system of at least five stars. The primary is a massive, luminous blue giant tens of thousands of times more luminous than the Sun.

Observational history

Mintaka was the first star whose spectrum revealed the presence of interstellar matter: in 1904, Johannes Hartmann, studying Mintaka's spectrum at Potsdam, noticed that certain calcium absorption lines did not share the star's periodic Doppler shifts, demonstrating the existence of stationary gas clouds between the stars — a landmark discovery in the study of the interstellar medium.
