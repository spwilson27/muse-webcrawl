Serviced apartment

A serviced apartment is a fully furnished apartment available for short-term or long-term stays, providing amenities similar to a hotel but with additional space, convenience and privacy like a typical rented apartment. Serviced apartments typically include a complete kitchen, separate living and sleeping areas, and hotel-like services such as housekeeping, a reception desk, and sometimes room service, laundries, or fitness facilities.

Characteristics

Unlike hotel rooms, serviced apartments are designed for extended stays of a week to several months. They usually offer more floor space at a lower per-night cost than an equivalent hotel for long stays, and residents can cook their own meals, receive guests, and live more independently. Utilities, broadband internet, and taxes are generally bundled into the rate.

Market

The concept grew out of the extended-stay segment developed in the United States in the 1970s and 1980s for business travellers on long assignments, and expanded internationally with corporate relocation and project-based work. Major users include business travellers, consultants, relocating employees, medical tourists, and families between homes. Serviced apartments are also marketed as aparthotels when operated within a hotel-style building with daily services, and as corporate housing when aimed at the relocation market.

The sector expanded rapidly in the 2010s in cities such as London, Singapore, and Dubai, and membership bodies such as the Association of Serviced Apartment Providers (ASAP) were formed to set quality standards. The rise of short-let platforms blurred the line between serviced apartments and amateur holiday rentals, prompting regulation in some cities.

Comparison with hotels

Serviced apartments generally lack the extensive public facilities of full-service hotels — multiple restaurants, bars, conference suites — but offer greater space, kitchens, and a more residential feel. Rates are often tiered so that longer stays cost substantially less per night, and guests may not earn hotel loyalty points with traditional schemes.
