Two Sessions

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Two Sessions (Chinese: 两会; pinyin: Liǎnghuì) are the annual plenary sessions of the two national-level political bodies of the People's Republic of China: the National People's Congress (NPC) and the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC). They are held concurrently each spring, usually in March, in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing.

Overview

The CPPCC session typically opens a day or two before the NPC session. Together they are the most important event in China's political calendar. During the sessions, the Premier delivers the annual Government Work Report setting out economic targets and policy priorities, the national budget is reviewed, and senior state leaders are confirmed or elected.

Participants

Nearly 3,000 deputies attend the NPC, China's top legislature, while more than 2,000 members attend the CPPCC, an advisory body whose members represent political parties, people's organisations, ethnic groups, and various sectors of society.

Significance

Every five years the sessions mark a leadership transition, electing the President, Premier, and other top officials. In other years they serve as a platform for announcing GDP growth targets, defence spending, and major legislative agendas, and they attract extensive domestic and international media coverage.
