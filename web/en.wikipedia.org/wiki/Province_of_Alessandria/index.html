The Province of Alessandria (Italian: Provincia di Alessandria) is a province in the Piedmont region of northern Italy. Its capital is the city of Alessandria. With an area of roughly 3,560 square kilometres, it is the third-largest province of Piedmont, and it is divided into 187 municipalities (comuni).

Geography

The province occupies the south-eastern corner of Piedmont, bordering Lombardy, Emilia-Romagna and Liguria. Its territory ranges from the flat Po plain in the north to the Monferrato hills — famous for wine production, including Barbera and the sparkling wines of Acqui Terme — and the Ligurian Apennines in the south. The main rivers are the Tanaro, the Bormida and the Scrivia.

Main towns

Besides the capital, the largest towns include Casale Monferrato, Tortona, Novi Ligure, Acqui Terme, Valenza (known for goldsmithing and jewellery), and Ovada.

History

The province was created in 1859 by the Rattazzi decree following the unification of Italy. The city of Alessandria, founded in 1168 by the Lombard League, gives the province its name. Parts of the Monferrato hills in the province form part of the UNESCO World Heritage landscape of the vineyard landscapes of Piedmont.
