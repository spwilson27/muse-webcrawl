Roy O. Disney

Roy Oliver Disney (June 24, 1893 – December 20, 1971) was an American businessman and co-founder of the Walt Disney Company. The elder brother of Walt Disney, Roy handled the company's finances and business operations while Walt led the creative side.

Career

Roy served in the U.S. Navy in the First World War and afterwards worked in banking. In 1923 he and Walt founded the Disney Brothers Cartoon Studio in Los Angeles, which became the Walt Disney Company. Roy's steady financial management — negotiating licensing, studio financing and the pioneering use of television — repeatedly rescued and expanded the business, including the financing of Disneyland in 1955.

Walt Disney World

After Walt's death in 1966, Roy postponed retirement to oversee construction of Walt Disney World in Florida, insisting it be named "Walt Disney World" in his brother's honour. He dedicated the resort at its opening on 1 October 1971 and died less than three months later. A statue of Roy seated with Minnie Mouse stands in the Magic Kingdom's Town Square.
