Underdog

An underdog is a person or team expected by most observers to lose a competition, election or other contest. The term originated in 19th-century dog fighting, where the losing dog was the one underneath, and the winner was the "top dog".

Psychology and culture

Studies of the "underdog effect" show that neutral observers often root for underdogs, identifying with their struggle and perceiving their success as more meaningful. Underdog narratives are a staple of sports and storytelling, from the biblical David and Goliath to celebrated sporting upsets. Political candidates and brands frequently present themselves as underdogs to win sympathy, a tactic sometimes called underdog branding.

In betting

In bookmaking, the underdog is the competitor offered at longer odds; bettors backing an underdog risk the same stake for a larger payout, reflecting the lower assessed probability of victory.
