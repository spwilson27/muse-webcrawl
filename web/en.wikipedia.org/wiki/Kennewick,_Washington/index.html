Kennewick is a city in Benton County, Washington, United States. It is located along the southwest bank of the Columbia River, just southeast of the confluence of the Columbia and Yakima rivers and across from the confluence with the Snake River. Together with Pasco and Richland, Kennewick forms the Tri-Cities metropolitan area. It is the most populous of the three cities.

History

The area was long inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Umatilla and Yakama. The name Kennewick is believed to derive from a Native word meaning "grassy place" or "winter paradise". European-American settlement began in the late 19th century, aided by irrigation projects that transformed the arid shrub-steppe into farmland. The city was incorporated in 1904.

Kennewick Man

Kennewick gained international attention in 1996 with the discovery of "Kennewick Man" (known as the Ancient One by Native American tribes), a set of human remains approximately 8,400 to 9,000 years old found on the bank of the Columbia River. The discovery sparked a long legal and scientific dispute over repatriation under the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act. Genomic studies published in 2015 showed the remains were closely related to modern Native Americans, and in 2017 the remains were reburied by claimant tribes.

Economy

The Tri-Cities economy is driven by agriculture, food processing, wine production, and the Hanford nuclear site and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory near Richland. Kennewick has grown rapidly as a retail and residential centre for the region.
