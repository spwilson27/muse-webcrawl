Kingdom of East Anglia

The Kingdom of East Anglia (Old English: Ēastengla Rīce) was one of the seven traditional kingdoms of the Anglo-Saxon Heptarchy. It covered roughly the modern counties of Norfolk and Suffolk and part of the Cambridgeshire Fens, and was ruled by the Wuffingas dynasty.

History

The kingdom emerged during the 6th century from Anglian settlement in eastern Britain. Its most powerful king, Rædwald (r. c. 599–624), was recognised as a bretwalda (overlord of the southern English kingdoms) according to Bede and the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, and is generally considered the most likely occupant of the great ship burial at Sutton Hoo. Christianity was introduced under Sigeberht in the 630s.

Decline

From the mid-7th century East Anglia fell increasingly under the shadow of Mercia, and King Æthelberht was executed by Offa of Mercia in 794. After the Viking Great Heathen Army killed King Edmund — thereafter venerated as Saint Edmund the Martyr — in 869 or 870, the region became part of the Danelaw. It was reconquered by Edward the Elder of Wessex by 917 and incorporated into the emerging kingdom of England as an earldom.
