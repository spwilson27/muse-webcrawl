Digg is an American social news aggregator. Its original incarnation, founded by Kevin Rose in 2004, allowed users to submit links and vote them up ("digg") or down ("bury"), with the most popular stories reaching the front page. At its height in the late 2000s Digg was one of the most-visited websites in the United States and a defining service of the Web 2.0 era; the so-called "Digg effect" could overwhelm smaller websites with traffic.

History

Digg grew rapidly from 2005, attracting major venture investment and turning down reported acquisition offers, including from Google in 2008. The site was known for its influential community and for episodes such as the 2007 AACS encryption key controversy, in which users revolted against the removal of posts containing an HD DVD processing key, forcing management to relent.

A widely criticised redesign ("v4") in August 2010 alienated the user base, triggering a mass migration to Reddit and a steep decline in traffic. In 2012 the company was broken up: the Digg brand and website were sold to Betaworks, the team was hired by the Washington Post, and patents went to LinkedIn. Betaworks relaunched Digg as a curated news reader. In 2025, founder Kevin Rose, together with Reddit co-founder Alexis Ohanian, reacquired Digg and announced a relaunch aimed at community-driven news in the era of AI-generated content.

Influence

Digg pioneered social news voting at scale and shaped the design of later aggregators; Reddit, which adopted a similar voting model, ultimately eclipsed it. Its rise and fall are frequently studied as a case study in online community management and product redesign risk.
