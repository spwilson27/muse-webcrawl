Lingerie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lingerie (from French lingerie, from linge, "linen") is a category of primarily women's clothing including undergarments, sleepwear, and lightweight robes. The term originally referred simply to underwear made of linen, but by the early twentieth century it had come to denote fashionable, decorative undergarments, often associated with luxury fabrics such as silk, lace, satin, and chiffon.

History

The modern concept of lingerie developed in the late nineteenth century, when reformers and designers such as Lady Duff-Gordon promoted less restrictive, more comfortable undergarments in place of the tight-laced corset. Lightweight chemises, camisoles, and combinations replaced heavier Victorian layers. The invention of nylon in 1938, and later elastane fabrics, transformed the industry by making fitted garments cheaper and more durable. Post-war brands and, later, companies such as Victoria's Secret (founded 1977) turned lingerie into a major fashion retail sector.

Types

Typical lingerie items include brassieres, briefs and knickers, camisoles, chemises, negligées, teddies, bodysuits, slips, suspender (garter) belts, corsets and bustiers, and hosiery. Lingerie is sold from specialist boutiques and department stores to supermarkets; the market is segmented into everyday ("core") garments, fashion ranges, shapewear, and bridal and occasion wear.
