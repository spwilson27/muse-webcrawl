Self-heating can

Placeholder image: a self-heating can of coffee.

A self-heating can is a food or drink container that heats its contents without an external heat source. Most designs use an exothermic chemical reaction — commonly calcium oxide (quicklime) reacting with water to form calcium hydroxide — in a separate compartment at the base of the container. Pressing a button or breaking a seal mixes the reactants, and the heat released warms the food or drink within a few minutes.

History and uses

Self-heating rations were developed for military use, and modern military rations generally employ flameless ration heaters based on magnesium–iron alloys instead. Self-heating cans have been marketed commercially for coffee, tea, soup, and ready meals in Japan, Europe, and elsewhere, aimed at travellers, outdoor users, and convenience markets. Cost, added weight, waste, and variable heating performance have limited their mass adoption.
