Airbus Group

Airbus Group was the corporate name used between 2014 and 2017 by the European multinational aerospace and defence corporation now known simply as Airbus SE. The company was created in 2000 as the European Aeronautic Defence and Space Company (EADS) through the merger of France's Aérospatiale-Matra, Germany's DaimlerChrysler Aerospace (DASA) and Spain's Construcciones Aeronáuticas SA (CASA). Headquartered in Leiden, Netherlands, with operational headquarters in Toulouse, France and Munich, Germany, it became the world's largest manufacturer of commercial airliners, competing principally with Boeing.

An Airbus A380, the flagship product of the Airbus Group era.

History

EADS was formed on 10 July 2000, bringing together the civil Airbus consortium — in which the partners had held shares since 1970 — with military aircraft, helicopter, space and defence electronics businesses. Airbus became a wholly owned subsidiary in 2001, and the Eurocopter helicopter division was a market leader. On 1 January 2014, the group was reorganised into three divisions — Airbus (commercial aircraft), Airbus Defence and Space, and Airbus Helicopters — and renamed Airbus Group. In 2017 the group completed its consolidation by merging into its commercial-aircraft subsidiary and adopting the single name Airbus SE, ending the separate "Group" identity.

Products and divisions

The group's best-known products included the A320 family of narrow-body airliners, the A330 and A350 wide-body jets, and the double-deck A380, the world's largest passenger aircraft. Defence products included the Eurofighter Typhoon, the A400M Atlas military transport and the Ariane space launchers built through its space division. Its helicopter division produced the Tiger attack helicopter and the NH90. See also Airbus, Boeing and Eurofighter Typhoon.
