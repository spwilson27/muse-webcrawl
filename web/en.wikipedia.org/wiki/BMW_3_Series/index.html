The BMW 3 Series is a line of compact executive cars manufactured by the German automaker BMW since 1975. It is the successor to the 02 Series and has been produced across seven generations. The 3 Series is BMW's best-selling model, historically accounting for around 30% of the brand's annual car sales, and is widely regarded as the benchmark sports sedan in its class.

The first generation (E21) was offered only as a two-door sedan and introduced many features that became BMW trademarks, including the driver-oriented, angled dashboard. The E30 generation added four-door sedan, convertible, and Touring (wagon) body styles and hosted the first M3, introduced in 1986 as a touring-car homologation special with a high-revving four-cylinder engine.

Body styles and models

Depending on generation, the 3 Series has been sold as a sedan, coupe, convertible, station wagon ("Touring"), hatchback (Compact and later Gran Turismo), and Gran Coupé. Since the F30 generation, the coupe and convertible models have been marketed as the separate 4 Series. High-performance variants are sold as the M3 (and M4 for the coupe line), and plug-in hybrid and diesel models form a significant part of European sales. In China, long-wheelbase sedan versions have been offered since the F30 generation.

Motorsport

The 3 Series has an extensive racing history. The E30 M3 became the most successful touring car of its era, winning the World, European, German (DTM), British, and Italian touring car championships, among many others. Later generations competed in the DTM, WTCC, and numerous GT and endurance series.
