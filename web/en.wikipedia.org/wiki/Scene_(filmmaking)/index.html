Scene (filmmaking)

Scene
FieldFilm production, screenwriting
Defined byUnity of place and/or continuous time within the narrative
Composed ofShots
Part ofSequences and acts

In filmmaking and video production, a scene is a unit of action that takes place in a single location at a single time, or follows a continuous strand of the story. A scene is made up of a series of shots, and scenes in turn combine into sequences and acts. A change of location or a significant break in the continuity of time generally marks the start of a new scene.

In the screenplay

In screenplay format each scene begins with a slugline (scene heading) stating whether the location is interior or exterior (INT./EXT.), the place, and the time of day — for example, "INT. COFFEE SHOP – DAY". Scenes are numbered in the shooting script for scheduling and budgeting. Screenwriting teachers often describe a well-constructed scene as a small story in itself, in which a character pursues a goal, meets an obstacle, and the scene ends on a change of value or a turning point.

Structure and function

Scenes are the basic building blocks of dramatic structure. Within classical three-act structure, scenes advance the plot, reveal character, or both; editors shape a film's rhythm by the ordering and duration of its scenes. The term overlaps with, but is distinct from, the theatrical scene, which is a division of an act in a play marked by changes in the characters present.

Related units

  • Shot — a single continuous take between cuts.
  • Sequence — a series of scenes forming a distinct narrative episode.
  • Act — a major division of the story's overall structure.
