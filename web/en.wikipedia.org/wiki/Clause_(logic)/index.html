Clause (logic)

In logic, a clause is a disjunction of literals, the building block of conjunctive normal form.

In logic, a clause is a propositional formula formed from a finite collection of literals (atoms or their negations), typically understood as their disjunction. In first-order logic, a clause is a disjunction of literals whose variables are implicitly universally quantified. Clauses are the standard representation used in conjunctive normal form (CNF), where a formula is a conjunction of clauses, and they underpin automated theorem proving by resolution.

Definitions

A literal is an atomic formula or its negation. A (disjunctive) clause is a finite set (or sequence) of literals L1, …, Ln interpreted as the disjunction L1 ∨ … ∨ Ln. Special cases include:

  • The empty clause, written □, containing no literals; it is unsatisfiable and plays the role of falsum in resolution proofs.
  • A unit clause, containing exactly one literal.
  • A Horn clause, containing at most one positive literal; Horn clauses are the logical basis of the programming language Prolog.
  • A definite clause, containing exactly one positive literal.

Sometimes the term is used dually for a conjunction of literals; this conjunctive clause convention appears in some logic programming and circumscription literature, but the disjunctive usage is standard in theorem proving and SAT solving.

Use in automated reasoning

Any first-order formula can be transformed into a set of clauses via Skolemisation and conversion to prenex and conjunctive normal form, at the cost of potential exponential growth (mitigated by definitional, or Tseitin, transformations). The resolution rule then operates on pairs of clauses: from clauses containing complementary literals P and ¬P, it infers a new clause (the resolvent) with those literals removed. Resolution is refutation-complete for first-order clauses, and efficient clause-learning SAT solvers (CDCL) make clause sets the practical input format for problems such as the Boolean satisfiability problem, expressed in the DIMACS format.
