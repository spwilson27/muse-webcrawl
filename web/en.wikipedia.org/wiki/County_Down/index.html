County Down


Image placeholder: County Down

County Down (Irish: Contae an Dúin) is one of the six counties of Northern Ireland and one of the thirty-two traditional counties of Ireland. It lies in the province of Ulster, in the northeast of the island, with a population of roughly 550,000. The county town is Downpatrick, and the largest city is Bangor.

Geography

County Down contains the Mourne Mountains, including Slieve Donard, the highest peak in Northern Ireland, and a long coastline on the Irish Sea and Strangford Lough. The drumlin hills of the county's interior are a characteristic landscape, celebrated in the song "Star of the County Down".

History

The county is associated with Saint Patrick, who is traditionally buried at Downpatrick. Its name derives from dún, the Irish word for a fort. The historic county also included suburbs now part of Belfast, which is divided between County Down and County Antrim.
