Algebra over a field

[Image placeholder: diagram of an algebra over a field]

In abstract algebra, an algebra over a field (often simply an algebra) is a vector space equipped with a bilinear product. Thus an algebra is an algebraic structure consisting of a set together with operations of multiplication and addition and scalar multiplication by elements of an underlying field, where the multiplication is compatible with the vector space structure.

Definition

Let K be a field and let A be a vector space over K equipped with an additional binary operation of multiplication A × A → A. Then A is an algebra over K if the product is bilinear: (x + y)z = xz + yz, x(y + z) = xy + xz, and (ax)(by) = (ab)(xy) for all scalars a, b in K and all elements x, y, z in A. Equivalently, an algebra can be defined as a ring A together with a ring homomorphism from K into the centre of A.

Examples

  • The set of all n × n matrices over a field K forms an associative algebra, as do polynomial rings K[x] and spaces of linear operators on a vector space.
  • The complex numbers form a two-dimensional algebra over the real numbers; the quaternions form a four-dimensional associative division algebra over ℝ, and the octonions a non-associative one.
  • Every field extension of K is an algebra over K; group algebras and Lie algebras (with the Lie bracket as product) are further central examples.

Types and structure

Algebras are classified as associative or non-associative, unital or non-unital, commutative or non-commutative, and finite- or infinite-dimensional. Important classes include division algebras, simple algebras, central simple algebras and Frobenius algebras. The structure theory of finite-dimensional algebras — including the Artin–Wedderburn theorem describing semisimple algebras in terms of matrix algebras over division rings — is a cornerstone of representation theory.
