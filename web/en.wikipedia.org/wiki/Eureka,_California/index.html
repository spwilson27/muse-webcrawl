Eureka (Wiyot: Jaroujiji) is a city and the county seat of Humboldt County, located on the North Coast of California. Situated on Humboldt Bay, about 270 miles (430 km) north of San Francisco, Eureka is the largest coastal city between San Francisco and Portland, Oregon. Its population was 26,512 at the 2020 census. The city is known for its extensive stock of Victorian architecture, centred on the Old Town district, and for its role as a port for the region's timber, fishing and tourism industries.

History

The Eureka area was traditionally inhabited by the Wiyot people. European-American settlement began in 1850 during the California Gold Rush, when the town was founded as a supply point for gold mines in the Trinity Mountains. The name "Eureka", from the Greek for "I have found it", echoes the gold-seekers' cry and also serves as the state motto of California. Eureka was incorporated in 1856 and became the county seat in 1857.

The city's history includes the 1860 massacre of Wiyot people on Indian Island (now Tuluwat Island) by local settlers, an event commemorated annually; in 2019 the city returned the island to the Wiyot Tribe. In 1885, anti-Chinese agitation led to the expulsion of Eureka's Chinese population, one of many such expulsions on the Pacific Coast. Timber drove the economy for over a century: lumber barons built elaborate Victorian mansions, most famously the Carson Mansion (1886), built for lumber magnate William Carson and considered one of the most notable examples of Queen Anne-style architecture in the United States.

Geography and climate

Eureka lies on the eastern shore of Humboldt Bay, the second-largest natural bay in California. It has a mild Mediterranean climate strongly moderated by the Pacific Ocean, with cool summers, frequent fog, and winter rainfall. Temperatures rarely exceed 70 °F (21 °C) or fall below freezing. The city is surrounded by redwood forests, including nearby Redwood National and State Parks.

Economy and culture

With the decline of the timber industry, Eureka's economy rests on government services, healthcare, education (including nearby Cal Poly Humboldt in Arcata), retail, tourism and fishing. Old Town, the original commercial core, contains shops, galleries, museums and restaurants. Annual events include the Kinetic Grand Championship, a race of human-powered amphibious sculptures that begins in Arcata and finishes in Ferndale via Eureka.
