Bona Sforza (2 February 1494 – 19 November 1557) was Queen of Poland and Grand Duchess of Lithuania as the second wife of Sigismund I the Old, and Duchess of Bari and Rossano in her own right. A member of the powerful Italian Sforza family, she was an energetic and influential political figure who worked to strengthen the royal authority and finances of the Polish–Lithuanian state.


Bona Sforza in her later years

Early life and marriage

Bona was the daughter of Gian Galeazzo Sforza, Duke of Milan, and Isabella of Naples. After her father's death and the French conquest of Milan, she was raised in Bari and Naples, where she received a humanist education. In 1518 she married the widowed King Sigismund I of Poland, bringing a large dowry and Italian courtiers to Kraków.

Political activity

As queen, Bona pursued an ambitious programme: she recovered alienated royal lands, reformed the administration of the grand ducal domains in Lithuania, promoted agriculture and drainage, and accumulated a vast personal fortune. She secured the election of her son, the future Sigismund II Augustus, as king during his father's lifetime (1529–1530) — the first such vivente rege election in Poland. In foreign policy she opposed the Habsburgs and sought alliances with France and the Ottoman Empire against them, while opposing the growing power of the Hohenzollerns in Prussia.

Later years and death

After Sigismund I's death in 1548, Bona's relations with her son deteriorated, particularly over his marriage to Barbara Radziwiłł. In 1556 she left Poland for her duchy of Bari, taking enormous wealth with her. She died in Bari in 1557, allegedly poisoned by her secretary on behalf of Habsburg interests; her Italian estates were subsequently absorbed into the Spanish crown after protracted litigation (the famous "Neapolitan sums" dispute).

Legacy

Bona is credited with introducing Italian Renaissance culture, cuisine, and garden vegetables — still called włoszczyzna ("Italian things") in Polish — to Poland. She founded towns, built castles, and remains one of the most prominent queens in Polish history.
