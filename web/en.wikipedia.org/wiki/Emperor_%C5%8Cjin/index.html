Emperor Ōjin

Emperor Ōjin
Reign: traditionally 270–310 AD
Predecessor: Empress Jingū (regent)
Successor: Emperor Nintoku
Deified as: Hachiman

Emperor Ōjin was the 15th legendary Emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. Historians consider him a legendary figure, though a historical king behind the legend may have lived in the late 4th or early 5th century. He is traditionally said to have reigned from 270 to 310.

Legendary narrative

According to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Ōjin was the son of Emperor Chūai and Empress Jingū, born after his mother's legendary conquest of Korea. He is said to have encouraged the introduction of writing and scholarship from the continent.

Worship

Ōjin was deified as Hachiman, the god of war and divine protector of Japan, and is enshrined at Hachiman shrines throughout the country, including Usa Jingū. His supposed tomb is a large keyhole-shaped kofun in Osaka Prefecture.
