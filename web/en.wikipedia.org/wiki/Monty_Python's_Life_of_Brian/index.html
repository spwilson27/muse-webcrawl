Monty Python's Life of Brian, also known as Life of Brian, is a 1979 British comedy film starring and written by the comedy group Monty Python (Graham Chapman, John Cleese, Terry Gilliam, Eric Idle, Terry Jones and Michael Palin). It was directed by Jones. The film tells the story of Brian Cohen (played by Chapman), a young Jewish-Roman man who is born on the same day as—and next door to—Jesus, and is subsequently mistaken for the Messiah.

Following the withdrawal of funding by EMI Films just days before production was scheduled to begin, fellow Python Eric Idle's former bandmate and friend George Harrison, lead guitarist of the Beatles, arranged financing through the formation of his production company Handmade Films.

Plot

Brian Cohen is born in a stable a few doors from the one in which Jesus is born, which initially confuses the three wise men who come to praise the future King of the Jews. Brian grows up into an idealistic young man who resents the continuing Roman occupation of Judea. While listening to Jesus's Sermon on the Mount, Brian becomes infatuated with a young rebel, Judith. His mother, a Jewish Roman, reveals that Brian's father was really a Roman centurion named Naughtius Maximus, which makes Brian part Roman.

Brian joins the People's Front of Judea, one of many fractious and bickering separatist movements, who spend more time fighting each other than the Romans. When the group attempts to kidnap Pontius Pilate's wife, Brian is captured by the Romans and taken to Pilate. An attempt at a witty repartee with his captors fails, and Brian barely escapes. He finds himself in a marketplace of prophets and tries to make a living as a preacher. A crowd begins to follow him, mistaking his words for profound truths.

Brian's following grows rapidly. After a night of passion with Judith, Brian opens his bedroom curtains to find a huge crowd of followers outside his mother's house, proclaiming him the Messiah. When Brian denies their claims, they refuse to believe him. The group tracks Brian to the city square where he is hiding. Judith begs the front to leave Brian alone. Instead, Brian is arrested by Pilate's soldiers and sentenced to crucifixion. In a parody of the crucifixion of Jesus, a huge crowd sings the song "Always Look on the Bright Side of Life" as Brian hangs on the cross.

Cast

Graham Chapman as Brian Cohen, John Cleese as Reg and others, Terry Gilliam as various characters, Eric Idle as Mr Cheeky and Stan/Loretta, Terry Jones as Mandy Cohen (Brian's mother), Michael Palin as Pontius Pilate and others, with Kenneth Colley as Jesus and Neil Innes as a Samaritan.

Production

Directed by Terry Jones, the film was shot on location in Monastir, Tunisia, reusing sets from Franco Zeffirelli's Jesus of Nazareth (1977). The original backer, EMI Films, withdrew at the last minute. George Harrison stepped in, mortgaging his home and office and forming Handmade Films to fund the production, an act the Pythons described as "the most anybody's ever paid for a cinema ticket."

Release and reception

The film was a box office success, the fourth-highest-grossing film in the United Kingdom in 1979, and the highest grossing of any British film in the United States that year. It received positive reviews and was later named the greatest comedy film of all time by several magazines and television networks. However, it was controversial for its combination of comedy and religious themes, and was accused of blasphemy by various religious groups. Some countries, including Ireland and Norway, banned its showing, with a few of these bans lasting decades.

Themes

The Python team denied that the film was intended to mock Jesus himself, who is treated respectfully in the film; their target was religious dogmatism, groupthink, and the tendency of followers to miss the message of the figures they revere. Brian's desperate attempts to dissuade his followers, and their refusal to think for themselves, form the film's central satire, crystallised in his line: "You've all got to work it out for yourselves."

Legacy

The closing song, "Always Look on the Bright Side of Life," written by Eric Idle, became one of the most popular songs at British funerals. The film regularly appears near the top of greatest-comedy polls; in 2006 the UK's Channel 4 viewers ranked it the best comedy film of all time, and in 2011 Total Film readers voted it the greatest comedy film of all time.
