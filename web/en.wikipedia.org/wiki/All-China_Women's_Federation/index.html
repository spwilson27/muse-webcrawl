The All-China Women's Federation (ACWF) is a mass organisation for women in the People's Republic of China, founded on 3 April 1949 and operating under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party. Its founding chairwoman was Cai Chang, and early leaders included Deng Yingchao. It is one of the country's principal "people's organisations" and functions as a bridge between the Party-state and women.

History

The federation was established shortly before the founding of the People's Republic, as the All-China Democratic Women's Federation, and renamed in 1957. It campaigned for implementation of the 1950 Marriage Law, literacy, and women's entry into the workforce. Suspended during the Cultural Revolution, it was revived in 1978 and subsequently worked on legislation including the 1992 Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests and anti-domestic-violence legislation enacted in 2015.

Structure and work

The ACWF maintains a vast hierarchy from the national congress down to village-level women's committees, historically reaching nearly every community in China. Its activities include legal aid, poverty alleviation, micro-loans for women entrepreneurs, and campaigns against trafficking. The federation designates "March 8th Red Banner Holders" and publishes newspapers and magazines for women. Since the 2010s it has also been tasked with promoting official family values and marriage and childbearing among young women.

Criticism

Scholars and feminists have debated the federation's dual role: it has advanced concrete protections for women, but as a party-led body it has also constrained independent feminist activism, notably during the detention of the "Feminist Five" in 2015, when the ACWF remained largely silent.
