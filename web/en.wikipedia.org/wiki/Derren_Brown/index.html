Derren Brown
Born 27 February 1971, Purley, London, England
Occupation: Mentalist, illusionist, author, artist

Derren Brown (born 27 February 1971) is an English entertainer, mentalist, illusionist, and author. He first came to prominence with his television series Mind Control (2000) on Channel 4 and has since created numerous specials and stage shows combining magic, suggestion, psychology, misdirection, and showmanship. Brown describes his act as achieving effects through "psychological" means rather than paranormal powers, and he is a prominent sceptic and atheist who has written critically about psychics and faith healers.

Career

Brown studied law and German at the University of Bristol, where he began performing as a close-up magician and hypnotist. His television breakthrough came in 2000, and subsequent specials included Russian Roulette (2003), in which he appeared to play the game live with a loaded gun; The Heist (2006), in which he persuaded members of the public to commit an armed robbery; and The Experiments (2011), a series exploring crowd behaviour and obedience, which won a BAFTA. Later Netflix specials included The Push (2018), examining social compliance.

Stage work and writing

Brown tours extensively with stage shows such as Something Wicked This Way Comes, Enigma, Svengali, Infamous, Miracle, and Secret — the latter transferring to Broadway in 2019. His books include Tricks of the Mind (2006), Confessions of a Conjuror (2010), Happy (2016), a popular exploration of Stoic philosophy and well-being, and Bootstrapping Your Way to Happiness. He is also a keen painter of caricatures and an accomplished photographer.

Personal life

Brown is gay and has spoken publicly about his Christian upbringing, his subsequent loss of faith, and themes of belief and control that run through his work.
