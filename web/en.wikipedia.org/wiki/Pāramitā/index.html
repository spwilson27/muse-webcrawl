Pāramitā

Pāramitā (Sanskrit; Pali: pāramī) is a term used in Buddhism to describe the "perfections" or virtues cultivated by a bodhisattva on the path to awakening. The word is often glossed as "that which has reached the other shore" — transcendent virtues that carry one across the ocean of saṃsāra.

The six perfections

Mahayana Buddhism lists six pāramitās:

  1. Dāna — generosity, giving
  2. Śīla — virtue, morality, conduct
  3. Kṣānti — patience, forbearance
  4. Vīrya — energy, diligence
  5. Dhyāna — meditation, concentration
  6. Prajñā — wisdom, insight into emptiness

Some later texts, such as the Daśabhūmika Sūtra, expand the list to ten by adding skillful means (upāya), aspiration (praṇidhāna), spiritual power (bala), and knowledge (jñāna).

Theravada pāramīs

In Theravada Buddhism, the ten pāramīs — generosity, morality, renunciation, wisdom, energy, patience, truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness, and equanimity — are qualities the Buddha is said to have perfected over many lifetimes, as recounted in the Jātaka tales.
