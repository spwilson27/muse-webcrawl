Lake Huron

Lake Huron is one of the five Great Lakes of North America. Hydrologically, it is the eastern portion of Lake Michigan–Huron, having the same surface elevation as Lake Michigan, to which it is connected by the 5-mile-wide (8.0 km), 120-foot-deep (37 m) Straits of Mackinac. It is shared on the north and east by the Canadian province of Ontario and on the south and west by the U.S. state of Michigan.

Geography

Lake Huron has a surface area of about 59,600 km² (23,000 sq mi), making it the second-largest of the Great Lakes by surface area and the third-largest lake in the world by that measure. It contains Manitoulin Island, the world's largest lake island, and Georgian Bay, a large bay in its northeastern portion. The lake's shoreline, around 6,157 km (3,827 mi) including islands, is the longest of any Great Lake.

History

The name derives from the Huron (Wyandot) people who lived along its shores. French explorers called it La Mer Douce, "the freshwater sea". The lake is known for its many shipwrecks, including those from the Great Lakes Storm of 1913, and for popular resort towns along its shores.
