Battle of Acentejo

The First Battle of Acentejo, also known as La Matanza de Acentejo ("The Massacre of Acentejo"), was fought on 31 May 1494 on the island of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. It was the worst defeat suffered by Castilian forces during the conquest of the Canaries: an army led by the conquistador Alonso Fernández de Lugo was ambushed and routed in a ravine near Acentejo by Guanche warriors under the mencey (king) Bencomo of Taoro and his brother Tinguaro.

The Barranco de Acentejo, scene of the 1494 ambush.

Background and battle

Lugo had landed on Tenerife in the spring of 1494 to complete the Castilian conquest of the archipelago. After establishing a base at Santa Cruz, he advanced inland toward the Guanche stronghold of the Orotava valley. On 31 May his column — around 1,000 men, including allied Guanches — was drawn into the steep ravine of Acentejo, where Bencomo's warriors attacked from the heights with stones, spears and clubs. The Castilian cavalry and firearms were useless in the confined, wooded terrain. Tinguaro personally led the attack; tradition holds he killed many soldiers in single combat before being slain. Of Lugo's force, several hundred were killed — some accounts put Spanish dead at over 600, roughly half the army — and Lugo himself escaped wounded, disguised by removing his distinctive red clothing.

Aftermath

The disaster forced Lugo to withdraw to Gran Canaria. With fresh funding from Genoese merchants and reinforcements, he returned in 1495; after Guanche resistance was weakened by a plague (the modorra), he defeated them at the Second Battle of Acentejo (25 December 1495) and the Battle of Aguere, completing the conquest of Tenerife in 1496. The modern municipality of La Matanza de Acentejo takes its name from the battle, while its neighbour is called La Victoria de Acentejo, marking where the remnants of the army rallied.
