Formic acid

Formic acid
Systematic name: methanoic acid
Formula: CH2O2 (HCOOH)
Molar mass: 46.03 g/mol
Appearance: colourless, pungent liquid
Melting point: 8.4 °C
Boiling point: 100.8 °C

Formic acid (systematically methanoic acid) is the simplest carboxylic acid, with the formula HCOOH. It occurs naturally in the venom of ant stings and bee stings — its name comes from the Latin formica, "ant" — and in the leaves of stinging nettles.

Properties

Formic acid is a colourless liquid with a pungent odour, miscible with water. It is a stronger acid than acetic acid and also acts as a reducing agent. Concentrated formic acid is corrosive and can decompose to carbon monoxide and water, especially when heated with sulphuric acid.

Production and uses

Industrially it is produced mainly from methanol via methyl formate, and as a by-product of acetic acid manufacture. Formic acid is used as a preservative and antibacterial agent in animal feed, in leather tanning and textile dyeing, in rubber production, and by beekeepers against varroa mites. It has also been studied as a hydrogen storage medium and as a fuel for fuel cells.
