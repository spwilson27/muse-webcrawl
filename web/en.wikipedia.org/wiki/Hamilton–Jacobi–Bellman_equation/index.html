Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation

The Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation is a nonlinear partial differential equation that provides a necessary and sufficient condition for optimality of a control with respect to a loss function. It is at the heart of optimal control theory and is the continuous-time analogue of the discrete dynamic programming principle developed by Richard Bellman.

The equation

For a control system with state x, control u and value function V(x, t), the HJB equation takes the form:

−∂V/∂t = minu { L(x, u) + ∇V · f(x, u) }

where L is the running cost and f describes the dynamics of the system. The solution, if it exists and is sufficiently smooth, gives the optimal cost-to-go, from which the optimal feedback control can be recovered.

Background

The equation combines ideas from the Hamilton–Jacobi equation of classical mechanics (associated with William Rowan Hamilton and Carl Gustav Jacobi) with Bellman's principle of optimality from dynamic programming. Solving the HJB equation is generally difficult because of its nonlinearity and the "curse of dimensionality", so numerical and approximate methods — including viscosity solutions, which allow non-smooth value functions — are widely used.

Applications

HJB equations arise in economics and finance (for example in optimal consumption and portfolio problems), robotics, aerospace guidance, and stochastic control, where the equation gains a second-order diffusion term from the underlying stochastic process.
