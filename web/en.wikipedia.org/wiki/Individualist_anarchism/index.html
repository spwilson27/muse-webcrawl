Individualist anarchism

Individualist anarchism is a tradition of anarchist thought that emphasises the individual and their will over external determinants such as groups, society, traditions and ideologies. Unlike social anarchism, which stresses communal organisation and mass revolution, individualist anarchism centres on the sovereignty of the individual, voluntary association, and — in many of its strands — market exchange free of state-granted privilege.

Origins

The German philosopher Max Stirner's The Ego and Its Own (1844) laid the foundation for egoist anarchism, rejecting all abstractions — God, state, morality, "humanity" — as "spooks" constraining the self-owning individual. In the United States, Josiah Warren's experiments with equitable commerce and "the sovereignty of every individual" (from 1827 onward) made him arguably the first American anarchist, and his ideas were developed by Stephen Pearl Andrews and, most influentially, by Benjamin Tucker, editor of the journal Liberty (1881–1908), who fused Stirner's egoism with the economic critiques of Proudhon and Herbert Spencer.

Varieties

Individualist anarchism encompasses mutualism (associated with Pierre-Joseph Proudhon), free-market anarchism, illegalism (a controversial current in early-1900s France favouring criminal acts as rebellion, exemplified by the Bonnot Gang), anarcho-naturism, and philosophical anarchism. In 20th-century America, figures such as Voltairine de Cleyre, Albert Jay Nock and the essayist Auberon Herbert explored individualist positions, and the tradition influenced later free-market libertarianism, although its adherents maintained opposition to capitalism as they understood it — a system of state-protected monopolies in land, money, tariffs and patents (Tucker's "four monopolies").

Critiques

Social anarchists have criticised individualist anarchism as impractical, insufficiently revolutionary, or indistinguishable from laissez-faire capitalism; individualists have replied that collectivist anarchism risks coercing the individual. The two traditions nevertheless overlapped substantially in the 19th-century anarchist movement.
