Tipping points in the climate system

In climate science, a tipping point is a critical threshold that, when crossed, leads to large, accelerating and often irreversible changes in the climate system. Components of the Earth system that may pass a tipping point are called tipping elements; they include the Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, tropical coral reefs, Arctic sea ice, the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC), the Amazon rainforest and the boreal permafrost. Tipping behaviour results from self-reinforcing feedbacks, so that a small additional forcing can push the element into a qualitatively different state.

Key tipping elements

ElementChange if tipped
Greenland ice sheetCommitted long-term melt; up to ~7 m of sea-level rise over millennia
West Antarctic ice sheetCollapse of marine-based sectors; ~3 m or more of sea-level rise
Tropical coral reefsMass bleaching and die-off at sustained warming above ~1.5 °C
AMOCWeakening or collapse; regional cooling in Europe, shifted rainfall belts
Amazon rainforestDieback to savanna in the east and south; large carbon release
PermafrostAbrupt thaw releasing carbon dioxide and methane

Thresholds and cascades

A major 2022 assessment in Science concluded that several tipping points become possible even at the current level of warming (~1.2 °C) and likely at the Paris Agreement limit of 1.5 °C, including Greenland and West Antarctic ice loss, tropical coral die-off and abrupt permafrost thaw. Tipping elements can interact: meltwater from Greenland may weaken the AMOC, whose changes in turn affect the Amazon and the West African monsoon, raising the prospect of a "tipping cascade".

Implications

Because tipping points involve irreversibility on human timescales and potentially severe impacts, they feature prominently in assessments of climate risk and in arguments for rapid emissions reductions. Researchers also study early-warning signals — such as slowing recovery from perturbations ("critical slowing down") — that might indicate a system approaching its threshold.
