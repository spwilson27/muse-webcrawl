Penetration test

Also calledPentest, ethical hacking
PurposeAuthorised simulated attack on a computer system to find exploitable vulnerabilities
ApproachesBlack box, white box, grey box
StandardsPTES, OWASP Testing Guide, NIST SP 800-115

Penetration testers simulate the tactics of real attackers, with the owner's permission.

A penetration test, colloquially known as a pentest, is an authorised simulated cyberattack on a computer system, performed to evaluate the security of the system. Unlike a vulnerability assessment, which catalogues weaknesses, a penetration test actively attempts to exploit them—chaining flaws, escalating privileges and pivoting through a network—to demonstrate real-world impact.

Methodology

Testing typically proceeds through reconnaissance (gathering information), scanning and enumeration, exploitation, post-exploitation and reporting. Engagements are scoped by the tester's knowledge: black-box tests start with no internal information, white-box tests with full documentation or credentials, and grey-box tests in between. Social engineering and physical intrusion may also be in scope.

History and practice

The concept emerged in the 1960s–70s with US government "tiger teams" probing systems such as Multics. Today penetration testing is a routine element of security programmes and is required by compliance regimes such as PCI DSS. Practitioners use toolkits including Metasploit, Nmap and Burp Suite, and deliver reports rating findings by severity so organisations can remediate them.
