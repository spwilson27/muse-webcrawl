Heine–Borel theorem

Field: Real analysis / topology
Statement: A subset of R^n is compact iff it is closed and bounded.

The Heine–Borel theorem, named after Eduard Heine and Émile Borel, is a fundamental result in real analysis. It states that for a subset S of Euclidean space R^n, S is compact if and only if S is closed and bounded. Equivalently, every open cover of a closed and bounded subset of R^n has a finite subcover.

History

The result was first proved by Eduard Heine in 1872 in the context of uniform continuity arguments, and a related covering result was published by Émile Borel in 1895. The theorem became a cornerstone of the modern definition of compactness.

Proof idea

One direction uses the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem: a bounded sequence in a closed bounded set has a convergent subsequence whose limit lies in the set, giving sequential compactness. The converse follows because unbounded or non-closed sets admit open covers with no finite subcover.

Generalizations

In general metric spaces, compactness is equivalent to completeness plus total boundedness. The Heine–Borel property characterizes finite-dimensional normed spaces: a normed vector space has the property that all closed bounded sets are compact only if it is finite-dimensional.
