Jin dynasty (266–420)

Years266–420
CapitalLuoyang (266–311); Chang'an; Jiankang (from 317)
FounderEmperor Wu (Sima Yan)
LanguageChinese

The Jin dynasty was an imperial dynasty of China that ruled from 266 to 420. It was founded by Sima Yan, son of Sima Zhao and grandson of Sima Yi, who forced the abdication of the last Wei emperor and reunified China in 280 by conquering Eastern Wu.

Western Jin

The first phase of the dynasty, known as the Western Jin (266–316), briefly reunified the empire, but was soon weakened by the War of the Eight Princes and invasions by the Five Barbarians, leading to the fall of Luoyang and Chang'an.

Eastern Jin

After 317, a Jin prince re-established the dynasty at Jiankang (modern Nanjing) in the south, known as the Eastern Jin, which survived until 420, when Liu Yu usurped the throne and founded the Liu Song dynasty.

Culture

The period saw the flourishing of Buddhism, celadon pottery, and the calligraphy of Wang Xizhi.
