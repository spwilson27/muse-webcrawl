Webmail

Webmail (or web-based email) is an email service accessed through a standard web browser rather than a dedicated email client program. Messages are stored on the provider's servers, so mail is available from any computer with internet access and no software configuration.

History

Among the earliest webmail services were Hotmail, launched in 1996, and RocketMail, which became Yahoo Mail; both offered free accounts accessible from anywhere and were rapidly popular. Google's Gmail, launched in 2004 with an unusually large 1 GB of storage and a search-based, conversation-threaded interface, transformed expectations for webmail and made the browser the primary email client for most users. Open-source webmail software such as SquirrelMail and Roundcube is widely used by organisations running their own servers.

Features and considerations

Modern webmail includes spam filtering, virus scanning, calendars, contacts and integration with cloud storage. Compared with client-based access via POP3 or IMAP, webmail centralises data with the provider — convenient for access and backup, but raising dependence on the provider's availability and privacy practices. Security features such as two-factor authentication and phishing warnings are largely implemented at the webmail layer.
