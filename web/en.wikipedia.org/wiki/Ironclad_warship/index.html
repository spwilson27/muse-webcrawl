Ironclad warship

An ironclad was a steam-propelled warship protected by iron or steel armour plates, constructed during the mid-19th century in response to the vulnerability of wooden warships to explosive and incendiary shells. The ironclad era, roughly 1859 to the 1890s, saw navies transform from wooden sailing fleets to armoured steam fleets, before the type evolved into the steel battleship and battlecruiser.

Origins

Armoured floating batteries were used by France and Britain in the Crimean War (1854–56), notably at the Battle of Kinburn. In 1859 France launched Gloire, the first ocean-going ironclad — a wooden hull sheathed in iron — and Britain replied with the all-iron-hulled HMS Warrior (1860). The American Civil War produced the most famous ironclad action: the Battle of Hampton Roads (March 1862), where CSS Virginia fought USS Monitor, whose low freeboard and rotating gun turret set a pattern for monitor-type vessels.

Development

Ironclads combined steam engines with retained sail rigs for long voyages, and their armament evolved from broadside smoothbores to turret-mounted rifled breech-loaders. Key actions of the era included the Battle of Lissa (1866), which spuriously encouraged ramming tactics, and the Battle of Angamos (1879) in the War of the Pacific. By the 1890s, compound and steel armour, quick-firing guns, and uniform designs merged the ironclad into the pre-dreadnought battleship.
