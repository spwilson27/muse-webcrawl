Dáil Éireann
Type: Lower house of the Oireachtas
Seats: 174 Teachtaí Dála (TDs)
Meeting place: Leinster House, Dublin
Presiding officer: Ceann Comhairle

Dáil Éireann (Irish for "Assembly of Ireland", pronounced [d̪ˠaːlʲ ˈeːɾʲən̪ˠ]) is the lower house of the Oireachtas, the legislature of Ireland. It is directly elected at least once every five years under the single transferable vote system of proportional representation. Its powers are similar to those of lower houses elsewhere, and it is by far the dominant branch of the Oireachtas: it nominates the Taoiseach (prime minister) and can dismiss the government by withdrawing confidence.

Composition and functions

Members, called Teachtaí Dála or TDs, represent multi-seat constituencies. Following legislation enacted in 2023, the Dáil elected in November 2024 has 174 members. The Dáil enacts legislation (bills must also pass the Seanad and be signed by the President, though the Seanad can only delay), approves the budget, and holds the government to account through debates and committees.

History

The name was first used by the revolutionary parliament proclaimed in Dublin on 21 January 1919 during the War of Independence, which adopted the Declaration of Independence and a Democratic Programme. The Dáil of the Irish Free State was established under the 1922 constitution, and the present body derives from the Constitution of Ireland of 1937. It has met at Leinster House in Dublin since 1922.
