Hagar

Hagar (Hebrew: הָגָר, Hāgār; Arabic: هَاجَر, Hājar) is a biblical figure in the Book of Genesis. She was an Egyptian slave of Sarah, the wife of Abraham, who gave her to Abraham as a concubine to bear him a child. Hagar is the mother of Ishmael, Abraham's first son. Expelled into the wilderness with Ishmael, she is depicted as encountering God, and she is the only person in the Hebrew Bible who gives God a name — El Roi, "God of seeing". Hagar is also a significant figure in Islam, where her search for water for her son in the desert is commemorated in the ritual of sa'i during the Hajj.

Biblical narrative

According to Genesis 16, the childless Sarah gave her Egyptian maid Hagar to Abraham. Once pregnant, Hagar looked with contempt on her mistress; Sarah treated her harshly, and Hagar fled into the desert, where an angel of the LORD found her by a spring, promised that her son Ishmael would father a great multitude, and told her to return. Genesis 21 recounts that after Isaac's birth and weaning, Sarah demanded that Abraham send Hagar and Ishmael away. In the wilderness of Beersheba, when their water ran out, God opened Hagar's eyes to a well and reaffirmed the promise concerning Ishmael.

In Judaism and Christianity

Rabbinic tradition variously identified Hagar as a daughter of Pharaoh given to Sarah and sometimes equated her with Keturah, whom Abraham married after Sarah's death. In the New Testament, Paul's Epistle to the Galatians (chapter 4) uses Hagar and Sarah allegorically, associating Hagar with Mount Sinai and slavery and Sarah with freedom.

In Islam

In Islamic tradition, Hājar is the wife of the prophet Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and mother of Ismā'īl (Ishmael). Left in the valley of Mecca, she ran between the hills of Safa and Marwah seeking water until the spring of Zamzam appeared. Pilgrims re-enact her search in the sa'i rite. Although not named in the Quran, she is honoured as a matriarch.
