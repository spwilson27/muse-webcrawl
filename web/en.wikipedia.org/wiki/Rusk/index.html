Rusk

TypeTwice-baked bread / biscuit
VariationsSouth African beskuit, Dutch beschuit, Italian fette biscottate

A rusk is a hard, dry biscuit or a slice of twice-baked bread. It is sometimes used as a teething food for babies, and is a traditional staple for sailors, soldiers, and travellers because it keeps for long periods without spoiling.

Varieties

In South Africa, beskuit are buttermilk rusks dunked in coffee or rooibos tea, a tradition from trekboer and wartime rationing. In the Netherlands, beschuit is a light round rusk eaten with toppings, traditionally with muisjes at a baby's birth. German Zwieback, Greek paximadi, Indian tea rusks, and Japanese rusks (often sweetened slices of baguette) are related. British rusks, made by Farley's, are a classic infant food.

History

Twice-baking to preserve bread dates to ancient Egypt, Rome (see Roman Forum), and medieval ship's biscuit / hardtack, prized for durability on long voyages.
