Runaway truck ramp

A runaway truck ramp, runaway truck lane, escape lane, emergency escape ramp or truck arrester bed is a traffic device that enables vehicles suffering braking problems to stop safely. It is typically a long, sand- or gravel-filled lane connected to a steep downhill grade section of a main road, and is designed to accommodate large trucks or buses.

Design

Runaway truck ramps are placed on long, steep descents where brake failure — most often from overheating during prolonged braking — is a serious hazard. Types include:

  • Arrester beds: deep beds of loose, angular gravel parallel to the road, which stop vehicles through rolling resistance;
  • Gravity escape ramps: short uphill lanes that use gravity to decelerate the vehicle;
  • Sand piles: short lanes filled with loose sand;
  • Mechanical arresters: systems of nets or cables that catch and decelerate the vehicle.

Ramps are signed in advance so a driver with fading brakes can steer onto them. After use, a vehicle usually has to be towed out of the arrester bed and the gravel re-graded.

Usage

Studies of ramps on U.S. mountain corridors such as Interstate 70 in Colorado show that individual ramps may be used dozens of times a year, and that they substantially reduce the severity of brake-failure crashes. Drivers face no penalty merely for using a ramp, and officials encourage their use over attempting to continue.
