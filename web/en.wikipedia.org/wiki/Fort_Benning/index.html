Fort Benning

Fort Benning is a United States Army post in the Columbus, Georgia area, straddling the Alabama–Georgia border next to the Chattahoochee River. Established in October 1918 as Camp Benning, the post is the home of the United States Army Maneuver Center of Excellence, the United States Army Armor School, United States Army Infantry School, the Western Hemisphere Institute for Security Cooperation (formerly known as the School of the Americas), elements of the 75th Ranger Regiment, and 3rd Brigade Combat Team, 3rd Infantry Division, as well as other additional tenant units. It covers 182,000 acres (738 km²).

The post was named for Brigadier General Henry L. Benning, a Confederate States Army general. In 2023, following the recommendations of the Congressional Naming Commission, the post was redesignated Fort Moore, in honour of Lieutenant General Hal Moore and his wife Julia Compton Moore. In 2025 the name was changed back to Fort Benning, this time honouring Corporal Fred G. Benning, a World War I soldier awarded the Distinguished Service Cross.

History

Camp Benning was established in October 1918 as a training site for units deploying to World War I; although the war ended before many trainees deployed, the Infantry School was moved there in 1919 and the post became permanent as Fort Benning in 1922. During the 1920s and 1930s it was a centre of infantry doctrine; Dwight D. Eisenhower served there, and George C. Marshall was assistant commandant from 1927 to 1932, where his "Benning revolution" reformed the academic system. During World War II the post expanded greatly, hosting the first parachute training in the US Army — the famous 250-foot jump towers, adapted from amusement-park rides, trained the Parachute School.

The Armor School moved from Fort Knox to Fort Benning in 2011 under the 2005 Base Realignment and Closure decisions, uniting the infantry and armour branches at the new Maneuver Center of Excellence.

Units and activities

Major activities include Basic Combat Training and One Station Unit Training for infantry and armour soldiers, the Airborne School, Ranger School (whose first phase is conducted here), the Officer Candidate School, and the Sniper School. The post hosts the National Infantry Museum nearby and the annual Best Ranger Competition.

Renamings

The 2021 National Defense Authorization Act created a commission to rename Department of Defense assets honouring the Confederacy. Fort Benning — named for Confederate general Henry Benning — was renamed Fort Moore on 11 May 2023. In March 2025, the Army restored the name Fort Benning, dedicating it instead to WWI Corporal Fred G. Benning. A similar double renaming affected Fort Liberty in North Carolina.
