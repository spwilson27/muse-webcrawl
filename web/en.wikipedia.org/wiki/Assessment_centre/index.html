Assessment centre

Type: Personnel selection method

Used for: Recruitment, promotion, development

Origins: 1930s–40s military officer selection

An assessment centre is a process where candidates are examined to determine their suitability for specific types of employment, especially management or military command. Typically, candidates take part in a battery of exercises — group discussions, role plays, presentations, in-tray (in-basket) tasks, case analyses and psychometric tests — while trained assessors observe and rate them against a pre-defined set of competencies.

History

The method developed from officer selection in Germany in the 1930s and was adopted by the British War Office Selection Boards and the American Office of Strategic Services during the Second World War. AT&T's Management Progress Study in the 1950s demonstrated that assessment-centre ratings predicted managerial advancement, and the technique spread widely through large employers.

Design principles

Sound assessment centres use multiple exercises, multiple assessors and multiple measured dimensions, with assessors trained to observe, record, classify and evaluate behaviour before reaching consensus ratings. Meta-analyses report moderate-to-good predictive validity for job performance and promotion, higher than unstructured interviews alone. "Development centres" adapt the same format for feedback and career development rather than selection.
