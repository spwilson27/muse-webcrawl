Mount Meru

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Meru, the cosmic mountain at the centre of the universe in Hindu cosmology.

Mount Meru (Sanskrit: मेरु, Meru), also called Sumeru, is the sacred five-peaked mountain of Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist cosmology, regarded as the centre of all the physical, metaphysical, and spiritual universes. It is the axis mundi — the pole around which the heavens revolve and on whose summit the gods reside. For the related volcano in Tanzania, see Mount Meru (Tanzania).

Description

The Hindu Puranas describe Meru as an immense golden mountain, said in some texts to be 84,000 yojanas high, standing at the centre of the continent of Jambudvīpa, around which the sun, moon, and stars revolve. The rivers of the world, including the Ganges, were said to descend from heaven onto its peak. On its terraces lie the cities of the gods: Brahmā's city crowns the summit, while Indra's heaven, Svarga, sits below.

In the traditions

In Jain cosmology, Meru stands at the centre of the middle world and is the site of the birth celebrations (janma-kalyāṇaka) of the Tīrthaṅkaras. Buddhist texts place Meru at the centre of the world-system, ringed by seven mountain ranges and seas, with the Trāyastriṃśa heaven of Indra on its summit. The mountain shapes sacred architecture across Asia: the towers of Angkor Wat represent its five peaks, and temple stupas, Thai wats, and Tibetan mandalas all model the world upon it.
