Grafton Architects

Grafton Architects is an architecture practice based in Dublin, Ireland, founded in 1978 by Yvonne Farrell, Shelley McNamara, and others. Named after Grafton Street in Dublin, the firm is known for monumental, sculptural public buildings — particularly universities — that carve generous civic space out of heavy masonry and concrete. Its directors received the Pritzker Architecture Prize (2020) and the RIBA Royal Gold Medal (2020), and the practice won the RIBA Stirling Prize in 2021.

History

Grafton was founded by graduates of University College Dublin and for its first three decades worked principally in Ireland — on schools, university buildings, housing, and the Temple Bar district of Dublin. The firm's turning point was winning the competition for Bocconi University in Milan (completed 2008), whose stone-clad mass, pierced by a sunken public "broadsheet" of space, was named the first World Building of the Year at the World Architecture Festival.

Major works

  • Bocconi University, Milan (2008) — faculty building of travertine and glass over a great underground aula.
  • UTEC, Lima (2015) — a rugged concrete "vertical campus" against the cliffs of Barranco; winner of the inaugural RIBA International Prize (2016).
  • Institut Mines-Télécom, Paris-Saclay (2019).
  • Town House, Kingston University, London (2020) — a colonnaded library and dance studios; RIBA Stirling Prize 2021.
  • Marshall Building, London School of Economics (2021) — a branching concrete structure behind a Portland stone facade.
  • ESB Headquarters, Dublin and the Department of Finance, Dublin.

Philosophy and recognition

Farrell and McNamara describe architecture as the "making of space" for human life and speak of buildings as gifts to the city — ideas crystallised in "Freespace", the theme of the 2018 Venice Architecture Biennale, which they directed. Critics place Grafton in a lineage of weighty, place-conscious modernism, praised for achieving monumentality in the service of public generosity.
