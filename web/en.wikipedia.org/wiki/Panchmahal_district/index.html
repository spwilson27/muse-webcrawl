Panchmahal district (also spelled Panch Mahals) is a district of Gujarat state in western India. The name means "five palaces" or "five estates" (panch, five; mahal, palace/estate), recalling the five mahals — Godhra, Dahod, Jhalod, Kalol, and Halol — administered from the region in earlier centuries. The district headquarters is the city of Godhra.

History

The district's most famous site is Champaner, founded in the eighth century and made capital of Gujarat by Mahmud Begada after his conquest of Pavagadh fort in 1484. The Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park, with its mosques, tombs, temples, and fortifications blending Hindu and Islamic architecture, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004. In 2013, the district was reorganized when the new Mahisagar district was carved out of Panchmahal and Kheda.

Geography and economy

Panchmahal lies in eastern Gujarat, bordered by Madhya Pradesh to the east. The terrain includes the Pavagadh hill, part of the Aravalli range, and forests inhabited substantially by tribal (Adivasi) communities, including Bhil groups. Agriculture — maize, rice, pulses, and cotton — dominates the economy, supplemented by industry around Halol and Kalol.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census the district (before bifurcation) had a population of about 2.39 million; after the split, roughly 1.64 million. Scheduled Tribe communities form a large share of the population, and Gujarati is the principal language.
