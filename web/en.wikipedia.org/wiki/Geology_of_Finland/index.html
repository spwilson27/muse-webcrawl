Geology of Finland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The geology of Finland is dominated by an ancient Precambrian crystalline basement, part of the East European Craton and the Baltic (Fennoscandian) Shield, overlain in most places by a thin mantle of Quaternary glacial deposits. Finland's bedrock is among the oldest in Europe, with rocks approaching three billion years in age, and the country contains almost no rocks younger than the Precambrian except for glacial and post-glacial sediments.

Precambrian basement

The eastern and northern parts of the country form part of the Archaean Karelian craton, with gneisses and granite-greenstone belts dating to 2.5–3.1 billion years ago. The central and western regions were assembled during the Paleoproterozoic Svecofennian orogeny (about 1.9 billion years ago), when island arcs and microcontinents accreted to the craton margin, producing vast volumes of granitoid intrusions and metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary belts. Later rapakivi granites, famous for their large round feldspar crystals, intruded about 1.65–1.54 billion years ago in the south.

Glaciation and post-glacial rebound

During the Quaternary ice ages, the Fennoscandian ice sheet repeatedly scoured the bedrock, depositing till, eskers, and drumlins and carving the basins of Finland's tens of thousands of lakes. Since deglaciation the land has been rising by isostatic rebound — up to about 9 mm per year around the Gulf of Bothnia — so the coastline continues to shift and new land emerges, notably in the Kvarken archipelago, a UNESCO World Heritage Site shared with Sweden. The Salpausselkä ridges in the south mark prominent ice-margin positions of the Younger Dryas.

Economic geology

Finland is a significant European producer of nickel, cobalt, gold, chromium, and copper, with major deposits including the Outokumpu copper district, the Kemi chromite mine, the Kevitsa nickel-copper mine, and large gold deposits in Lapland. The shield also hosts important industrial minerals and the deep geological repository for spent nuclear fuel under construction at Olkiluoto (Onkalo).
