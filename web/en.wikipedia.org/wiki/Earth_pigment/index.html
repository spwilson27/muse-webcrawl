Earth pigment

Earth pigments are naturally occurring mineral pigments made from coloured clays and soils whose colour comes from iron oxide and manganese oxide. Used by humans since prehistoric cave painting, they include the ochres, siennas, umbers and terre verte. Earth pigments are valued for their durability, lightfastness and muted, natural tones.

Earth pigments: yellow ochre, raw sienna, burnt sienna and umbers.

Types

  • Ochres: yellow ochre (limonite/goethite), red ochre (haematite) and brown ochre—used since the Paleolithic, as at Blombos Cave.
  • Sienna: raw sienna is a yellowish-brown earth from Tuscany; heating it produces the richer red-brown burnt sienna.
  • Umber: a darker brown earth containing manganese oxide; burnt umber is its calcined form.
  • Terre verte ("green earth"): a dull green pigment from celadonite and glauconite, historically used for flesh underpainting.
  • Venetian red and Indian red: red iron-oxide earths.

Properties and use

Earth pigments consist largely of iron(III) oxide in a clay or silica matrix. They are chemically inert, non-toxic (in their natural form), extremely lightfast and compatible with all painting media—fresco, tempera, oil and watercolour. Calcining (heating) an earth pigment deepens and reddens its colour, giving the "burnt" variants. Because they are excavated rather than manufactured, hue varies by source; synthetic iron oxides are now made to imitate them consistently.
