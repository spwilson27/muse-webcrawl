Principal ideal ring

In a principal ideal ring, every ideal is generated by a single element.

In mathematics, a principal ideal ring, or PIR, is an integral domain in which every ideal is a principal ideal — that is, can be generated by a single element. More generally, the term is sometimes applied to any ring (not necessarily a domain) in which every ideal is principal.

A principal ideal ring that is also an integral domain is closely related to the notion of a principal ideal domain (PID). Every principal ideal domain is a unique factorization domain, but a general principal ideal ring with zero divisors need not have this property.

Examples

  • The ring of integers ℤ is a principal ideal domain, hence a principal ideal ring.
  • Any field is trivially a principal ideal ring, since its only ideals are (0) and (1).
  • The ring ℤ/nℤ of integers modulo n is a principal ideal ring even when n is composite and the ring has zero divisors.
  • A polynomial ring over a field, F[x], is a principal ideal domain.

Properties

Every principal ideal ring is a Noetherian ring, because every ideal is finitely generated (by one element). The structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain has analogues for principal ideal rings, and such rings play a role in the classification of finitely generated abelian groups and modules over ℤ/nℤ.

Quotients and localizations of principal ideal rings are again principal ideal rings. A commutative ring in which every finitely generated ideal is principal is called a Bézout ring when it is a domain; principal ideal rings are the Noetherian special case of this notion.
