Automatic transmission fluid (ATF)

Use: Automatic transmissions, power steering, some manual transmissions
Colour: Typically red or green
Types: Dexron, Mercon, ATF+4, CVT fluid

Automatic transmission fluid (ATF) is a type of hydraulic fluid used in vehicles with automatic transmissions. It is typically coloured red or green to distinguish it from engine oil and other fluids. The fluid is optimised for the special requirements of an automatic transmission, such as valve operation, friction for clutch plates and bands, torque converter operation, gear lubrication and cooling.

History

General Motors introduced the first widely used ATF specifications in the 1940s for its Hydra-Matic transmission. Ford followed with its own specifications. Over time, manufacturer-specific formulations proliferated.

Modern specifications

Major specification families include GM Dexron, Ford Mercon, Chrysler ATF+4, Toyota WS and Honda ATF DW-1. Continuously variable transmissions (CVTs) and dual-clutch transmissions require dedicated fluids that are not interchangeable with conventional ATF.

Properties

ATF contains friction modifiers, detergents, anti-wear agents, corrosion inhibitors and viscosity improvers. Its frictional characteristics are critical: too slippery or too grippy a fluid can cause harsh or slipping shifts.
