Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park is a large wildlife preserve and conservation area in southern Africa. The park straddles the border between South Africa and Botswana and comprises two adjoining parks: the Kalahari Gemsbok National Park in South Africa and the Gemsbok National Park in Botswana. The total area of the park is 38,000 square kilometres (15,000 sq mi). Approximately three-quarters of the park lies in Botswana and one-quarter in South Africa. Kgalagadi means "place of thirst".

History

The Kalahari Gemsbok National Park in South Africa was established on 3 July 1931 mainly to protect the migrating game, especially the gemsbok, from poaching. The Gemsbok National Park in Botswana was proclaimed in 1971. On 12 May 2000 the two parks were formally merged into Africa's first formally declared transfrontier park, or peace park.

Geography and wildlife

The park is located largely within the southern Kalahari Desert. The terrain consists of red sand dunes, sparse vegetation, occasional trees, and the dry riverbeds of the Nossob and Auob rivers. The rivers are said to flow only about once per century, although water flows underground and provides life for grass and camelthorn trees growing in the river beds.

The park has abundant, varied wildlife, and is home to large mammalian predators such as black-maned Kalahari lions, cheetahs, leopards, and spotted and brown hyenas. Migratory herds of large herbivores such as blue wildebeest, springbok, eland, and red hartebeest also live and move seasonally within the park, providing sustenance for the predators. More than 200 species of bird inhabit the park, including the secretarybird, kori bustard, and sociable weaver, whose enormous communal nests are built in camelthorn trees.

Facilities

The park has three main rest camps — Twee Rivieren, Mata Mata, and Nossob — as well as several unfenced wilderness camps. Twee Rivieren, at the confluence of the Auob and Nossob rivers, is the park's administrative headquarters and main entrance gate from South Africa.
