AFL Grand Final

The AFL Grand Final is the championship-deciding match of the Australian Football League (AFL), the elite competition in Australian rules football. It is traditionally held at the Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) on the last Saturday in September, before crowds that routinely approach 100,000, making it one of the largest annual club sporting events in the world.

History

The competition began in 1897 as the Victorian Football League (VFL). Essendon won the first premiership, decided that year by a round-robin finals system; the first true grand final followed in 1898, when Fitzroy defeated Essendon. The VFL was renamed the AFL in 1990 as the competition nationalised. Notable drawn grand finals occurred in 1948, 1977, and 2010, each historically resolved by a replay until the rules were changed in 2016 to decide replays of future draws with extra time.

Traditions

The day features a pre-match entertainment program, the presentation of the premiership cup and medallions, and the Norm Smith Medal for the best player on the ground. Carlton and Essendon share the record for most premierships with sixteen each, followed by Collingwood.
