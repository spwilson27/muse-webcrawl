Australian Football League

The Australian Football League (AFL) is the pre-eminent professional competition in Australian rules football. It was originally named the Victorian Football League (VFL) and was founded in 1896 as a breakaway division of the Victorian Football Association, with its inaugural season in 1897. The competition was renamed the Australian Football League in 1990 following its national expansion.

History

Eight foundation clubs broke away from the VFA in 1896. The league expanded within Victoria, then nationally in the 1980s with the relocation of South Melbourne to Sydney and the admission of teams from Western Australia, South Australia, Queensland, and later a second Sydney club, Greater Western Sydney, and Gold Coast. A Tasmanian team was awarded a licence to join in the late 2020s.

Competition format

Eighteen clubs play a home-and-away season of 23 rounds, followed by a four-week finals series culminating in the AFL Grand Final, traditionally held at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on the last Saturday in September. Player movement is governed by a national draft, a salary cap, and trading periods.

Clubs

Current clubs include Adelaide, Brisbane Lions, Carlton, Collingwood, Essendon, Fremantle, Geelong, Gold Coast, Greater Western Sydney, Hawthorn, Melbourne, North Melbourne, Port Adelaide, Richmond, St Kilda, Sydney, West Coast, and the Western Bulldogs.
