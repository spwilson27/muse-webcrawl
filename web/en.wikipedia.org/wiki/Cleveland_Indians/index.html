Cleveland Indians

The Cleveland Indians were an American professional baseball team based in Cleveland, Ohio, that competed in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League (AL) Central Division. The franchise played under the Indians name from 1915 through the 2021 season, after which it was renamed the Cleveland Guardians. Earlier names included the Cleveland Blues (1901), Bronchos (1902), and Naps (1903–1914), the last in honour of star player-manager Napoleon Lajoie.

History

The club won two World Series championships, in 1920 and 1948. The 1948 team, led by player-manager Lou Boudreau and featuring Larry Doby — the first African American player in the American League — and pitcher Satchel Paige, defeated the Boston Braves. Thereafter the franchise endured one of the longest championship droughts in North American professional sports: after 1948 it reached the World Series in 1954, 1995, 1997, and 2016, losing each time, most painfully in Game 7 of 2016 against the Chicago Cubs, who ended their own 108-year drought.

Notable Indians players included Bob Feller, one of the fastest pitchers in baseball history, as well as Early Wynn, Jim Thome, Manny Ramirez, Omar Vizquel, and Francisco Lindor. The team played at League Park and Cleveland Municipal Stadium before moving to Jacobs Field (later Progressive Field) in 1994.

Name change

The name "Indians" and especially the team's Chief Wahoo logo — a red-faced caricature used from 1947 — drew decades of protest from Native American groups as racist. The logo was retired after the 2018 season, and in December 2020 the club announced it would change its name. On 23 July 2021 the new name, Guardians — taken from the "Guardians of Traffic" statues on the Hope Memorial Bridge near the ballpark — was announced, taking effect after the 2021 season.
