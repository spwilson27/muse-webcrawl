Lyon Arboretum

The Harold L. Lyon Arboretum is a 200-acre (0.8 km2) arboretum and botanical garden managed by the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa located at the upper end of Mānoa Valley in Honolulu, Hawaiʻi. It is the only university botanical garden located in a tropical rainforest in the United States, with over 5,000 tropical and subtropical plant species, one of the largest collections of palms of any botanical garden, and an active research and conservation programme.

History

The site was established in 1918 by the Hawaiian Sugar Planters' Association as the Manoa Arboretum, to test tree species for watershed restoration and possible commercial forestry, under its first director, botanist Harold L. Lyon — who argued successfully for preserving native plants rather than focusing only on introduced species. In 1953 the HSPA deeded the land to the University of Hawaiʻi, and the arboretum was renamed in Lyon's honour in 1957.

Collections and research

The arboretum maintains living collections, a seed conservation laboratory storing seeds of endangered Hawaiian plants, a micropropagation lab, and the Hawaiian Rare Plant Program. Trails wind past ʻAihualama Falls under a canopy of albizia, with themed gardens featuring palms, bromeliads, heliconia, and ethnobotanical plantings. It hosts the Culinary Herb Garden and community education classes and volunteers' plant sales.
