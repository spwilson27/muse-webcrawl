Vanguard 2 (or Vanguard 2E before launch) is an Earth-orbiting satellite launched 17 February 1959 as part of the United States Navy's Project Vanguard. The satellite was designed to measure cloud cover distribution over the daylight portion of its orbit, and it also carried instruments to measure the density of the upper atmosphere through tracking of its orbit.

Background

Project Vanguard was managed by the Naval Research Laboratory as the United States' contribution to the International Geophysical Year (1957–58). After a string of failed attempts and the success of Vanguard 1 in March 1958, the Vanguard 2 launch on a Vanguard SLV-4 rocket from Cape Canaveral placed the 10.75 kg sphere, 50.8 cm (20 in) in diameter, into an elliptical orbit.

Instruments

The satellite carried two photocells mounted at opposite ends of a light baffle, intended to scan the Earth's cloud cover as the satellite spun and moved along its orbit, recording reflectivity onto magnetic tape. It also carried two radio transmitters: a 108.03 MHz transmitter for tracking and a 108 MHz command transmitter. The spin stabilization was undermined when the satellite began to wobble ("precession") because the spent third stage collided slightly with it at separation, so the cloud-cover data were difficult to interpret, though some useful imagery of cloud patterns was recovered.

Legacy

The satellite and the spent upper stage remain in orbit; Vanguard 2's orbit was expected to decay only after roughly 300 years, making it, along with Vanguard 1 and its rocket stage, among the oldest human-made objects still in space. Data from the satellite's orbital decay have been used to refine models of upper-atmospheric density.
