Grant Morrison (born 31 January 1960) is a Scottish comic book writer and playwright known for nonlinear storytelling, countercultural themes, and metaphysical and self-referential ideas. One of the most influential writers in modern comics, they came to prominence in the late 1980s as part of the "British Invasion" of American comics.

Career

After early work for Marvel UK and 2000 AD, Morrison made their name at DC Comics with Animal Man (1988–1990), famous for its metafictional final arc, and a surreal run on Doom Patrol. The graphic novel Arkham Asylum: A Serious House on Serious Earth (1989), with Dave McKean, became one of DC's best-selling books. Their creator-owned series The Invisibles (1994–2000) drew on chaos magic, conspiracy theory, and their own experiences, and is regarded as a landmark of the Vertigo imprint.

Superhero work

Morrison revitalised the Justice League in JLA (1997–2000), relaunched Marvel's New X-Men (2001–2004), and wrote All-Star Superman (2005–2008) with Frank Quitely, widely considered one of the greatest Superman stories. A long run on Batman (2006–2013) introduced Damian Wayne as Robin and culminated in Batman Incorporated. Later work includes the DC event Final Crisis, the multiverse-spanning The Multiversity, and runs on Green Lantern and Wonder Woman.

Other work and recognition

Morrison has written the prose novels Supergods (2011), a history and memoir of superheroes, and Luda (2022), the TV series Happy! and Brave New World, and co-founded the magazine Heavy Metal's revival era output as editor. They were appointed MBE in 2012 for services to film and literature.
