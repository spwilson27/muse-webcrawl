Anna Leonowens

Anna Harriette Leonowens (born Ann Hariett Emma Edwards; 5 November 1831 – 19 January 1915) was an Anglo-Indian travel writer, educator and social activist. She is best known for her memoirs of her years as governess and teacher to the wives and children of Mongkut, King Rama IV of Siam (Thailand), which inspired the novel Anna and the King of Siam (1944) and the musical The King and I.

Life

Born in India to a soldier father, Leonowens spent years concealing her modest, mixed-heritage origins behind a fabricated Welsh upbringing. Widowed young after the death of her husband Thomas Leon Owens in Penang, she ran a school in Singapore before accepting King Mongkut's invitation in 1862 to teach English and other subjects in Bangkok, where she remained until 1867. Her son Louis later became a prominent figure in the Siamese teak trade.

Writings and controversy

Her books The English Governess at the Siamese Court (1870) and The Romance of the Harem (1873) were bestsellers but have long been criticised in Thailand for inaccuracies and exaggeration; the musical and several film adaptations remain effectively banned there for their depiction of the revered King Mongkut. Leonowens later lived in Halifax, Nova Scotia — where she helped found the Victoria School of Art and Design — and Montreal, and campaigned for women's suffrage.
