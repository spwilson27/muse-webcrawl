Mars Science Laboratory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mars Science Laboratory
OperatorNASA / JPL
Launch26 November 2011
Landing6 August 2012, Gale Crater
RoverCuriosity
The sky crane lands Curiosity (artist's concept).

Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) is a NASA mission that delivered the Curiosity rover to Gale Crater on Mars in August 2012 — the largest and most capable rover then sent to another planet, a car-sized nuclear-powered laboratory charged with assessing whether Mars ever offered conditions suitable for microbial life.

Its landing invented a new technique: after a capsule entry and supersonic parachute, a rocket-powered "sky crane" lowered the rover on cables to the surface, because the rover was too heavy for airbags or a legged lander. The manoeuvre worked flawlessly, and Curiosity has been exploring Gale — climbing the foothills of Mount Sharp — for well over a decade since.

Findings

Within its first year Curiosity found rounded pebbles in an ancient streambed and drilled mudstones at Yellowknife Bay that showed Gale once held a freshwater lake with the chemistry and energy sources life would need. It measured radiation levels relevant to future human missions, detected organic molecules in ancient rocks, and continues to track mysterious seasonal methane variations. The mission's landing system evolved into the architecture used again by Perseverance in 2021.
