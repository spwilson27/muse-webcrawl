Politics of Ukraine

The Verkhovna Rada building in Kyiv.

The politics of Ukraine take place in a framework of a semi-presidential representative democratic republic with a multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the President and the Government, with the Cabinet of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. Legislative power is vested in the Verkhovna Rada, the unicameral parliament of Ukraine.

Constitutional framework

The current Constitution of Ukraine was adopted in 1996, following independence from the Soviet Union in 1991. Amendments in 2004 (restored in 2014 after a period of reversal) shifted the balance of power toward parliament, making Ukraine a parliamentary-presidential republic.

Executive

The President of Ukraine is the head of state, elected by popular vote for a five-year term, and is commander-in-chief of the armed forces. The Prime Minister, appointed by parliament with presidential consent, heads the Cabinet of Ministers.

Legislature

The Verkhovna Rada has 450 seats. Electoral systems have alternated between proportional representation and mixed systems combining party lists with single-member constituencies.

Political history

Ukrainian politics since independence has been marked by competition between pro-Western and pro-Russian orientations, large-scale protest movements — the Orange Revolution of 2004 and the Euromaidan protests of 2013–14 — and persistent concerns about corruption and oligarchic influence. Following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and the full-scale invasion launched in February 2022, Ukraine has been governed under martial law, with elections postponed.
