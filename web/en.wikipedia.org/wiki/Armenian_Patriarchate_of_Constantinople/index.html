Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople

The Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople is one of the four ancient sees of the Armenian Apostolic Church, alongside the Catholicosates of Echmiadzin and Cilicia and the Patriarchate of Jerusalem. Its seat is the Church of St Mary (Surp Asdvadzadzin) in the Kumkapı district of Istanbul, Turkey.

History

The patriarchate was established in 1461, when Sultan Mehmed II invited Bishop Hovakim of Bursa to Constantinople and recognised him as patriarch and ethnarch of the Armenians of the Ottoman Empire. Under the Ottoman millet system the patriarch exercised wide civil as well as religious authority over his community.

Modern role

Following the Armenian genocide of 1915 and the foundation of the Turkish Republic, the patriarchate's jurisdiction shrank to the Armenians remaining in Turkey, centred on Istanbul. The patriarch is elected by a general assembly of clergy and lay delegates and must be approved by the Turkish government.
