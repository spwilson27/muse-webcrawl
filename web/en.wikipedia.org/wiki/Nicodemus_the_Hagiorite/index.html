Nicodemus the Hagiorite

Born 1749, Naxos, Greece

Died July 14, 1809, Mount Athos

Venerated in Eastern Orthodox Church

Feast July 14

Nicodemus the Hagiorite (Greek: Ὅσιος Νικόδημος ὁ Ἁγιορείτης; 1749 – July 14, 1809) was a Greek monk and theologian of the Eastern Orthodox Church, venerated as a saint. He was a central figure of the Kollyvades movement, which sought the revival of traditional Orthodox spirituality on Mount Athos, and he is best known for compiling, with Saint Macarius of Corinth, the Philokalia, an anthology of writings on prayer and the contemplative life.

Life

Born Nikolaos Kallivroutsis on the island of Naxos, he was educated there and in Smyrna. In 1775 he went to Mount Athos, where he was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Dionysiou, taking the name Nicodemus. He devoted himself to ascetic life, study of the Church Fathers, and the editing and translation of spiritual texts, often working in seclusion on Athos and at nearby sketes.

Works

Together with Macarius of Corinth, Nicodemus edited the Philokalia, published in Venice in 1782, which gathered texts on the Jesus Prayer and hesychasm from the 4th to the 15th centuries; it became one of the most influential spiritual books in modern Orthodoxy. He also compiled the Pedalion ("Rudder"), a commentary on the canons of the Orthodox Church; adapted Lorenzo Scupoli's Unseen Warfare and Ignatius of Loyola's Spiritual Exercises for Orthodox readers; and wrote the Exomologetarion, a manual for confessors, and lives of saints in the New Martyrologion and Synaxaristes.

Nicodemus was glorified (canonized) by the Ecumenical Patriarchate in 1955, and his feast day is July 14.
