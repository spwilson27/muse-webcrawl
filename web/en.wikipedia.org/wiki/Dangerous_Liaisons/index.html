Dangerous Liaisons

Dangerous Liaisons (Les Liaisons dangereuses) is an epistolary novel by Pierre Choderlos de Laclos, first published in 1782. Told entirely through letters, it depicts the Marquise de Merteuil and the Vicomte de Valmont, two aristocratic ex-lovers who amuse themselves by seducing and destroying others — until their intrigues destroy them in turn. It is regarded as one of the masterpieces of French literature and a penetrating portrait of the decadent aristocracy of the Ancien Régime.

Plot

The Marquise de Merteuil challenges Valmont to seduce the convent-educated Cécile de Volanges as revenge on a former lover; Valmont, meanwhile, pursues the virtuous Madame de Tourvel as a greater conquest. Through manipulation of letters and reputations, both plots succeed with tragic consequences: Cécile is ruined, Tourvel dies of grief after Valmont abandons her, Valmont is killed in a duel with the Chevalier Danceny, and Merteuil — exposed by her own correspondence — is disfigured by smallpox and flees society.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times, notably Stephen Frears's 1988 film Dangerous Liaisons with Glenn Close and John Malkovich, Roger Vadim's 1959 version, the 1999 teen reworking Cruel Intentions, and Christopher Hampton's stage play Les liaisons dangereuses.
