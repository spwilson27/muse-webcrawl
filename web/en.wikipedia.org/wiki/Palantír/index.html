Palantír

A palantír (Quenya: palantíri, "those that watch from afar"; plural "palantíri") is a fictional magical artefact in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing in The Lord of the Rings and related works. The palantíri — also called the Seeing Stones — are indestructible crystal globes used for communication and for seeing events far away, in space and sometimes in time.

Description

The stones are described as perfectly smooth dark spheres, varying from about a foot in diameter to much larger. They were made by the Elves of Valinor, possibly by Fëanor himself. A user of a palantír could see scenes occurring far away and could communicate mind-to-mind with a user of another stone, but the stones showed only what was true — they could, however, mislead by selective presentation.

History in the legendarium

Seven stones were given to the Faithful Númenóreans and brought to Middle-earth by Elendil and his sons, who placed them in towers across Arnor and Gondor: at Amon Sûl, Annúminas, Elostirion, Orthanc, Minas Ithil, Minas Anor (Minas Tirith) and Osgiliath (the master stone, largest of the seven). By the late Third Age several were lost — at sea, or in the Kin-strife — and the stone of Minas Ithil had been captured by Sauron. In The Lord of the Rings, Saruman is ensnared through the Orthanc-stone, Denethor is driven to despair by Sauron through the Anor-stone, and Aragorn wrests the Orthanc-stone to his own will, revealing himself to Sauron.

Name and legacy

The name derives from Quenya palan, "far", and tir, "watch/guard". The data-analysis company Palantir Technologies, co-founded in 2003 by Peter Thiel and Alex Karp, took its name from the stones.
