Tiger 131 is a German Tiger I heavy tank captured by British forces in Tunisia during the Second World War. It is preserved at The Tank Museum in Bovington, Dorset, England, and is famous as the only Tiger I in the world restored to running order. The turret number "131" was painted on by its British captors; its original unit was the 504th German Heavy Panzer Battalion.

Capture

On 24 April 1943, during fighting around Djebel Djaffa in Tunisia, Tiger 131 was hit by rounds from British Churchill tanks of the 48th Royal Tank Regiment. A shot jammed its turret traverse and damaged the gun elevation, and the crew abandoned the largely intact vehicle. It was the first Tiger captured intact by the Western Allies, and it was shipped to Britain, where it was exhibited and closely studied; King George VI and Winston Churchill inspected it in Tunisia before its transport.

Preservation

The tank was transferred to the Tank Museum in 1951. After decades as a static exhibit, it was restored in the 1990s and 2000s, fitted with a Maybach engine recovered from a Tiger II, and returned to running condition in 2003. It appears at the museum's annual Tankfest and starred as the German tank in the 2014 film Fury, the first time a real Tiger I had been used in a feature film since 1946.
