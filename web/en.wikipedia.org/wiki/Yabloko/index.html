Yabloko (Russian: Яблоко, IPA: [ˈjabɫəkə], lit. "apple") is a Russian social-liberal political party, formally the Russian United Democratic Party "Yabloko" (Российская объединённая демократическая партия «Яблоко»). It is one of the oldest political parties in post-Soviet Russia and for much of its history was the principal liberal-democratic opposition force represented in the State Duma.

History

Yabloko originated as an electoral bloc formed in 1993 ahead of the first elections to the State Duma of the Russian Federation. Its name derives from the surnames of its three founders: economist Grigory Yavlinsky, former ambassador Yury Boldyrev, and former deputy prime minister Vladimir Lukin — Yavlinsky–Boldyrev–Lukin. The bloc won 7.86% of the vote in the December 1993 elections and formed a faction in the Duma critical of the government's shock-therapy economic reforms while supporting market reform with stronger social protections.

In the 1995 Duma elections Yabloko won 6.89% and 45 seats. Grigory Yavlinsky ran for president in 1996 and 2000, finishing fourth and third respectively. At the 1999 elections the party won 5.93% and 20 seats. In the 2003 Duma elections, however, Yabloko fell just below the 5% threshold, winning 4.37% and losing its faction, though four deputies won in single-member districts. It failed to return to the Duma in 2007 and 2011, and in 2016 received about 2% of the vote.

The party has maintained representation in a number of regional legislatures, notably in Saint Petersburg, Pskov Oblast, and Karelia, and has held seats on the Moscow City Duma at various times. Long-time party figure Lev Shlosberg led the Pskov regional branch.

Leadership

Grigory Yavlinsky led the party from its founding until 2008, when he was succeeded by Moscow city councillor Sergey Mitrokhin. Emilia Slabunova, a deputy of the Karelian regional assembly, became chair in 2015, and Nikolay Rybakov was elected chair in December 2019.

Ideology and positions

Yabloko advocates a social market economy, the rule of law, protection of civil liberties, and closer relations with the European Union. It opposed the First and Second Chechen Wars, criticised the 2014 annexation of Crimea, and was the only Russian political party with parliamentary history to openly oppose the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, a stance that led to pressure on its members and candidates.
