Glottidia

Glottidia is a genus of brachiopods belonging to the family Lingulidae, a group of inarticulate brachiopods characterised by shells composed of calcium phosphate rather than calcite. Species of the genus are marine burrowing animals found in intertidal and shallow subtidal sediments, principally along the coasts of the Americas.

The best known species is Glottidia pyramidata, which lives in vertical burrows in muddy sand flats along the Gulf coast of Florida and elsewhere. Like other lingulids, it has an elongate, tongue-shaped shell and a long fleshy pedicle used for anchoring in the sediment — a body plan that has remained nearly unchanged since the Cambrian, earning lingulids a reputation as "living fossils".

Fossil species assigned to Glottidia and its close relatives are known from Cenozoic deposits of the Americas, including the renowned Calvert Formation of Maryland.
