John Chadwick (21 May 1920 – 24 November 1998) was an English linguist and classical scholar who, with architect Michael Ventris, deciphered Linear B, the syllabic script of Mycenaean Greece, proving that its language was an early form of Greek. His popular account, The Decipherment of Linear B (1958), remains a classic of scholarly detection.

Life and career

Educated at St Paul's School and Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, Chadwick served during the Second World War in naval intelligence at Bletchley Park and in Alexandria, working on Italian and Japanese codes — experience in cryptanalysis that shaped his later work. After the war he completed his degree, worked on the Oxford Latin Dictionary, and became a lecturer in Classics at Cambridge in 1952, eventually a Fellow of Corpus Christi and Perne Librarian.

Linear B

When Ventris announced in 1952 that Linear B encoded Greek, Chadwick — a specialist in early Greek dialects — recognized the linguistic patterns and became his principal collaborator. Their joint paper, "Evidence for Greek Dialect in the Mycenaean Archives" (1953), and their monumental Documents in Mycenaean Greek (1956) established the decipherment. After Ventris's early death in 1956, Chadwick carried the field forward, revising Documents (1973) and writing The Mycenaean World (1976), a reconstruction of Bronze Age Greek society from the tablets.

Other work

Chadwick also wrote The Decipherment of Linear B (1958; 2nd ed. 1967), a widely read narrative of the decipherment, and the Cambridge Lexicographica studies on the vocabulary of early Greek.
