Clinical chemistry

Clinical chemistry (also known as chemical pathology, clinical biochemistry or medical biochemistry) is the area of chemistry that is generally concerned with analysis of bodily fluids for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. It is an applied form of biochemistry (not to be confused with medicinal chemistry, which involves basic research for drug development).

Scope

The discipline originated in the late 19th century with simple chemical tests for various components of blood and urine. Modern clinical chemistry laboratories use automated analyzers to measure a wide range of analytes, including electrolytes, enzymes, glucose, lipids, hormones, and markers of organ function, in specimens such as serum, plasma, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid.

Common tests

Common panels include the basic metabolic panel (sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, urea, creatinine, and glucose), liver function tests, lipid profiles, and thyroid function tests. Results are interpreted against reference intervals to diagnose and monitor disease.
