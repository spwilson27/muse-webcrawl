Gabriel Fauré

Gabriel Urbain Fauré
Born: 12 May 1845, Pamiers, France
Died: 4 November 1924 (aged 79), Paris, France
Occupation: Composer, organist, pianist, teacher

Gabriel Urbain Fauré (12 May 1845 – 4 November 1924) was a French composer, organist, pianist and teacher. He was one of the foremost French composers of his generation, and his musical style influenced many 20th-century composers. Among his best-known works are his Pavane, Requiem, nocturnes for piano and the songs "Après un rêve" and "Clair de lune".

Life

Born in Pamiers, Ariège, Fauré was sent at age nine to the École Niedermeyer in Paris, where he studied with Camille Saint-Saëns. He spent much of his career as a church organist — eventually at La Madeleine in Paris — and as a teacher, becoming director of the Paris Conservatoire in 1905. His pupils included Maurice Ravel and Nadia Boulanger. In his last years he suffered increasing deafness, yet composed some of his most concentrated chamber works.

Music

Fauré's early music is lyrical and harmonically adventurous within a classical framework; his late style is spare, elusive, and harmonically radical in a quiet way. His Requiem, Op. 48 (1887–1890), in its focus on rest rather than judgement, is among the most beloved choral works in the repertoire. Other major works include the opera Pénélope (1913), the incidental music to Pelléas et Mélisande (1898), the Dolly Suite, thirteen nocturnes and thirteen barcarolles for piano, and two piano quintets.

Legacy

Fauré is regarded as a link between Romanticism and modernism in French music, admired for the elegance and restraint of his style. Aaron Copland called him "a model of musical discretion."
