DVB-H

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DVB-H (Digital Video Broadcasting – Handheld) is one of three prevalent mobile television formats. It is a technical specification for bringing broadcast services to handheld receivers and was formalised as an ETSI standard (EN 302 304) in November 2004. DVB-H was developed by the DVB Project as an extension of the terrestrial DVB-T standard, adapted for the constraints of battery-powered mobile devices and reception while moving.

Technology

DVB-H shares the DVB-T physical layer but adds several features aimed at handheld reception:

  • Time slicing: Data are transmitted in high-rate bursts, allowing the receiver to power down its front end between bursts. This reduces average power consumption by up to about 90 percent, a key requirement for mobile phones.
  • MPE-FEC: An additional layer of forward error correction (multi-protocol encapsulation FEC) improves robustness in mobile and indoor reception conditions, typically by several decibels.
  • IP datacasting: Services are delivered as IP datagrams, enabling television to be integrated with interactive and on-demand services over a separate cellular return channel.
  • Smaller cells: DVB-H networks were often designed with denser transmitter grids than conventional DVB-T to support indoor and in-motion reception.

Trials and deployment

Commercial DVB-H services were launched in several countries during the late 2000s, including Italy (in time for the 2006 FIFA World Cup), Finland, and parts of Asia and Africa. Handsets with built-in DVB-H tuners were produced by manufacturers such as Nokia, Samsung and LG.

Despite early enthusiasm, DVB-H services generally failed to attract large subscriber numbers. The rise of smartphones with 3G and later 4G data connections, which made IP streaming of video widely available without a separate broadcast tuner, undermined the business case, and most DVB-H networks were switched off during the early 2010s. Related technologies included DVB-SH, MediaFLO and T-DMB.
