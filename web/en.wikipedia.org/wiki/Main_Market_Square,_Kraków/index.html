The Main Market Square (Polish: Rynek Główny) in the Old Town of Kraków, Poland, is the principal urban space of the city and the largest medieval town square in Europe, measuring roughly 200 metres on each side. It dates from the 13th century and was laid out when Kraków received its charter under Magdeburg law in 1257.

Landmark buildings

The square is dominated by the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice), rebuilt in Renaissance style in the 1550s and now housing market stalls and a gallery of 19th-century Polish painting. At the eastern side stands St Mary's Basilica, famous for its wooden altarpiece by Veit Stoss and the hourly hejnał bugle call played from its taller tower. Other landmarks include the Town Hall Tower — all that remains of the demolished medieval town hall — and the Adam Mickiewicz Monument, unveiled in 1898.

History

The square was designed as the commercial and administrative heart of the re-founded city after the Mongol invasion of 1241. Markets, public announcements, homage ceremonies, and executions took place here. It forms part of the first UNESCO World Heritage Site listing, granted in 1978.

Events

The square hosts festivals throughout the year, including a celebrated Christmas market and the June Lajkonik parade, in which a costumed Tatar rider processes through the Old Town. A bugle call, the hejnał mariacki, is played hourly from St Mary's tower, breaking off mid-note in memory of a legendary trumpeter shot while sounding the alarm.
