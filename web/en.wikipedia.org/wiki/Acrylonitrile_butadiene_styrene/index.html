Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) is a common thermoplastic polymer valued for its toughness, rigidity and ease of processing. It is a terpolymer made by polymerising styrene and acrylonitrile in the presence of polybutadiene. Typical proportions vary from 15–35% acrylonitrile, 5–30% butadiene and 40–60% styrene.

Properties

The acrylonitrile contributes chemical resistance and heat stability; the butadiene, present as dispersed rubber particles, provides impact toughness even at low temperatures; and the styrene gives rigidity and a glossy, easily moulded surface. ABS is amorphous, with a glass transition temperature around 105 °C. It resists aqueous acids, alkalis and many oils, but is attacked by ketones and some solvents, and it degrades under prolonged ultraviolet exposure unless stabilised.

Manufacture and processing

ABS is produced by emulsion, mass or suspension polymerisation and is readily injection moulded, extruded, vacuum formed and machined. It is one of the most common filaments for fused-deposition 3D printing, printed at temperatures around 220–250 °C.

Applications

Famous uses include LEGO bricks (since 1963), computer keyboard keycaps, automotive trim, protective headgear, luggage, musical instruments such as recorders and clarinets, medical devices and the housings of consumer electronics.

Safety and recycling

ABS is generally regarded as safe in finished form, though fumes released during high-temperature processing or printing should be ventilated. It carries resin identification code 7 and can be recycled, though collection is less widespread than for PET or HDPE.
