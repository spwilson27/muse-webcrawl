Primaquine is a medication used to treat and prevent malaria and to treat Pneumocystis pneumonia. It belongs to the 8-aminoquinoline class of antimalarials and is uniquely effective against the dormant liver stages (hypnozoites) of Plasmodium vivax and Plasmodium ovale, and against the gametocytes of Plasmodium falciparum. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Primaquine is used for the radical cure of vivax and ovale malaria — typically a 14-day course following a blood-stage treatment such as chloroquine — to prevent relapses arising from hypnozoites. A single low dose is recommended by the WHO as a gametocytocide in falciparum malaria to reduce transmission. In combination with clindamycin it is an alternative treatment for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia in people with HIV.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain. The most important risk is haemolytic anaemia in people with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency; testing for G6PD deficiency is recommended before treatment, and primaquine is contraindicated in pregnancy because of the risk to the fetus. It can also cause methaemoglobinaemia.

History

Primaquine was developed in the United States during and after the Second World War from the earlier 8-aminoquinoline pamaquine, and was introduced in the early 1950s; it was used extensively during the Korean War, where relapsing vivax malaria among troops spurred research into G6PD deficiency. The related drug tafenoquine, approved in 2018, offers single-dose radical cure.
