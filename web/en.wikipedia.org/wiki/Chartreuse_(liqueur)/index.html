Chartreuse (liqueur)

Chartreuse is a French herbal liqueur made by Carthusian monks from a secret recipe of 130 plants and herbs. It is produced in two main versions — the stronger, naturally green Chartreuse Verte and the milder, sweeter Yellow Chartreuse — and gives its name to the chartreuse color. The recipe is said to be known in full to only a few monks at any time.

Green and Yellow Chartreuse

History

According to tradition, in 1605 the French diplomat François Annibal d'Estrées gave the Carthusian monks at Vauvert, near Paris, a manuscript for an "elixir of long life." The formula passed to the order's motherhouse, the Grande Chartreuse monastery near Grenoble, where Brother Jérôme Maubec deciphered it; production of the elixir began in 1737. A drinkable liqueur version, Green Chartreuse, was developed, and in 1840 a milder Yellow Chartreuse followed. Expelled from France in 1903, the monks produced Chartreuse in Tarragona, Spain, for decades before returning; distillation has taken place at Aiguenoire, near the monastery, since 2017.

Production

The plant mixture is macerated and distilled, then aged in oak casks. Only Carthusian monks perform the blending, and the order — devoted to silence and prayer — has repeatedly resisted expanding production: in 2023 the monks announced they would cap output to protect their contemplative life, contributing to worldwide shortages and a surge in popularity among bartenders.

Uses

Chartreuse is drunk neat as a digestif, often chilled, and is a classic cocktail ingredient — notably in the Last Word, Bijou, and Champs-Élysées — and in the French tradition of a verte chaude, mixed with hot chocolate in Alpine ski resorts. The strongly flavored Élixir Végétal, at 69% alcohol, is still sold in small wooden bottles.
