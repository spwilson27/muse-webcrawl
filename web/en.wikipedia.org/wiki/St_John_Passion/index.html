St John Passion

The St John Passion (German: Johannespassion), BWV 245, is a sacred oratorio by Johann Sebastian Bach, composed during his first year as Thomaskantor in Leipzig and first performed on Good Friday, 7 April 1724, at the St. Nicholas Church (Nikolaikirche). It sets the Passion narrative from chapters 18 and 19 of the Gospel of John, supplemented by hymn stanzas (chorales) and free poetic arias, to music for soloists, choir and orchestra.

History

Bach composed the work in the winter of 1723–24, shortly after taking up the post of cantor at the Thomasschule and music director of Leipzig's principal churches. The Passion was performed as part of the Good Friday Vespers service, alternating between the Nikolaikirche and Thomaskirche. Bach revised the work several times — versions exist from 1724, 1725, around 1730 and 1749 — substituting arias and chorales and altering instrumentation, which has made reconstructing a single definitive text a matter of continuing scholarly debate.

Structure

The drama is carried by a tenor Evangelist who narrates the Gospel text in recitative. Jesus sings the vox Christi, while other roles — Peter, Pilate, the High Priest — are taken by bass and tenor soloists, and the choir represents the crowds of Jews, soldiers and priests in vivid turba choruses. Reflective arias, many with obbligato instruments such as viola d'amore and lute, invite the listener to meditate on the events. Chorale stanzas frame the drama in the shared voice of the congregation.

The opening chorus, "Herr, unser Herrscher" ("Lord, our master"), with its swirling string figures and dissonant woodwind, is one of Bach's most dramatic movements. Notable arias include "Es ist vollbracht" ("It is accomplished"), sung by the alto after the death of Jesus, in which a vigorous middle section proclaims the victorious hero of Judah.

Comparison with the St Matthew Passion

The St John Passion is shorter and generally considered more urgent and theatrical than the St Matthew Passion, BWV 244, of 1727. Where the Matthew setting is contemplative and expansive, the John setting is taut, concentrating on the trial and crucifixion with stark emotional immediacy.
