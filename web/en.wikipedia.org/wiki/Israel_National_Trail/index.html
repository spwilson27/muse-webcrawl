Israel National Trail

The Israel National Trail (Hebrew: שביל ישראל, Shvil Yisra'el) is a hiking trail that crosses the entire length of Israel, from Kibbutz Dan in the north near the Lebanese border to Eilat on the Red Sea in the south. The trail is approximately 1,025 kilometres (637 miles) long.

History

The trail was inaugurated in 1995 by President Ezer Weizman. Its creation was led by the Society for the Protection of Nature in Israel (SPNI), inspired in part by the Appalachian Trail in the United States. It is marked with a distinctive three-stripe blaze — blue, white, and orange — symbolising the sea, the snow of Mount Hermon, and the desert sands.

Route

From Dan the trail passes through the Galilee, around the Sea of Galilee, over Mount Carmel, along the Mediterranean coast to Tel Aviv, then inland to Jerusalem, and south through the Negev desert, crossing the Makhtesh Ramon crater before reaching the Red Sea mountains near Eilat.

Trail Angels

Residents living along the route who volunteer to host hikers are known as "Trail Angels" (mal'achei hashvil), offering water, lodging, and showers to through-hikers.
