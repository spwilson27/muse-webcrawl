Furfural is an organic compound with the formula C4H3OCHO. It is a colourless liquid with an almond-like odour, although commercial samples are often brown, and it is an important renewable, non-petroleum-based chemical feedstock produced from agricultural by-products rich in pentosans, such as corncobs, oat hulls and sugarcane bagasse.

Production

Furfural is produced by acid-catalysed hydrolysis and dehydration of pentose sugars (chiefly xylose) contained in lignocellulosic biomass, a process commercialized by the Quaker Oats Company in the 1920s. Many agricultural residues contain 10–25% potential furfural by this route, and production is centred in China, South Africa and the Dominican Republic.

Uses

Most furfural is hydrogenated to furfuryl alcohol, used in foundry resins and polymers. Furfural itself serves as a selective solvent in petroleum refining — for extracting aromatics from lubricating oils — and as a precursor to tetrahydrofuran, furoic acid and other furan chemicals. It is toxic in quantity and an irritant, though it also occurs naturally in many cooked foods and beverages.
