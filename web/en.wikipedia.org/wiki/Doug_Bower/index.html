Doug Bower

Doug Bower (born 1942) is an English artist who, together with his friend Dave Chorley, claimed in 1991 to have created many of the crop circles that appeared in the English countryside from the late 1970s onwards. The two men, from Southampton, said they were inspired by the Tully "saucer nest" case in Australia and began making circles in 1978 as a joke, using planks, rope and a sighting device made from a wire loop.

The claim

In September 1991, Bower and Chorley went to the newspaper Today with their claim, demonstrating their technique by making a circle in front of journalists and researchers. They said they had created some 200 circles over 13 years, motivated by fun and by fooling UFO enthusiasts ("cereologists") who attributed the formations to extraterrestrial visitors or paranormal forces. To authenticate their claims in later years they pointed to their early works signed with the initials "DD".

Reception

The confession was widely reported but disputed by some crop circle researchers, who argued that the most complex formations could not be human-made. Nevertheless, the demonstration helped shift mainstream coverage of crop circles toward human hoaxing and land art, and circle-making subsequently developed into an acknowledged art form, with teams creating elaborate commissioned designs.
