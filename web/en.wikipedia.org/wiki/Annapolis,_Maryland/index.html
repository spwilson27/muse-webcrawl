Annapolis, Maryland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Annapolis (/əˈnæpəlɪs/) is the capital of the U.S. state of Maryland and the county seat of Anne Arundel County. Situated on the Severn River where it meets Chesapeake Bay, it is home to the United States Naval Academy and St. John's College. Annapolis served as the temporary capital of the United States in 1783–84.

History

The settlement was founded in 1649 by Puritan exiles from Virginia and was known as Providence and later Anne Arundel's Town. In 1694 it became the colonial capital and was renamed Annapolis in honour of Princess (later Queen) Anne. Governor Francis Nicholson laid out a baroque street plan of circles and radiating avenues centred on the State House and St. Anne's Church. Annapolis prospered as a port for the tobacco trade, and its eighteenth-century brick architecture survives in unusual quantity.

In 1783–84 the Congress of the Confederation met in the Maryland State House, where George Washington resigned his commission as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army in December 1783, and where the Treaty of Paris was ratified in January 1784. The State House's wooden dome, the largest of its kind in the United States, crowns the oldest state capitol in continuous legislative use.

Naval Academy and sailing

The United States Naval Academy was established in 1845 on the grounds of Fort Severn; its campus, chapel, and Bancroft Hall dominate the waterfront. Annapolis is celebrated as a centre of sailing and recreational boating on Chesapeake Bay, hosts major boat shows, and is sometimes styled the "Sailing Capital of the World". The historic district and the neighbouring community of Eastport retain strong maritime traditions.

Government and education

As state capital, Annapolis hosts the Maryland General Assembly, the governor's residence (Government House), and state courts. St. John's College, chartered in 1784 and descended from King William's School (1696), is known for its Great Books curriculum.
