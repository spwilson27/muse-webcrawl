Romanos the Melodist

Romanos the Melodist (Greek: Ῥωμανὸς ὁ Μελωδός; c. 490 – c. 556) was a Byzantine hymnographer, the greatest composer of kontakia — long sung sermons in verse that became the centrepiece of Byzantine church poetry. His works, among them the famous Christmas kontakion "Today the Virgin gives birth", remain in Orthodox liturgical use, and he is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church.

Life

Romanos was born in Emesa in Syria, possibly to a Jewish family, and served as a deacon in Berytus (Beirut) before moving to Constantinople in the reign of Anastasius I, where he was attached to the Church of the Theotokos in the Kyros district. According to his hagiography, the initially untalented singer received the gift of hymnody miraculously: the Virgin Mary appeared to him in a dream on Christmas Eve and gave him a scroll to swallow, after which he mounted the ambo and sang his first kontakion.

Works

About 59 kontakia are generally accepted as genuine from the hundreds once attributed to him. Each is a dramatic, dialogic meditation on a feast or biblical episode — the Nativity, the Passion, the Resurrection, the betrayal of Judas, the lament of the Virgin at the Cross — built of metrical stanzas (oikoi) bound by a refrain and an acrostic often spelling "of the humble Romanos". His Christmas hymn is still sung on 25 December, and the great Akathist to the Theotokos has sometimes, though disputedly, been credited to him.

Legacy

Romanos marks the high point of early Byzantine hymnography and a bridge between Syriac verse homilies and Greek church poetry; scholars regard him as one of the greatest poets of the Greek language in late antiquity.
