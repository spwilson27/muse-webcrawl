Soft story building

A soft story building is a multi-storey building in which one level — typically the ground floor, with open parking, garages, or large windows — is substantially less rigid or strong than the floors above, making it vulnerable to collapse in earthquakes.

Characteristics

Common examples include wood-frame apartment buildings over tuck-under parking, found widely in California cities such as San Francisco and Los Angeles. The open ground floor lacks the shear walls needed to resist lateral forces, so the building can pancake at that level during strong shaking.

Retrofitting

After collapses in the 1989 Loma Prieta and 1994 Northridge earthquakes, several cities enacted mandatory retrofit programmes requiring owners to add steel frames or shear walls to soft-story buildings.
