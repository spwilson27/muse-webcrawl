Legendre symbol

In number theory, the Legendre symbol is a multiplicative function with values in {−1, 0, 1} that is a quadratic character modulo an odd prime number p. Introduced by Adrien-Marie Legendre in 1798 in the course of his attempts to prove the law of quadratic reciprocity, it indicates whether an integer a is a quadratic residue modulo p.

Definition

The Legendre symbol (a/p) is defined for an integer a and an odd prime p not dividing a as +1 if a is a quadratic residue modulo p (i.e., the congruence x² ≡ a (mod p) has a solution), −1 if a is a quadratic non-residue, and 0 if p divides a.

Properties

Euler's criterion gives the explicit formula (a/p) ≡ a(p−1)/2 (mod p). The symbol is completely multiplicative in its numerator: (ab/p) = (a/p)(b/p). Its values for special numerators include (−1/p) = (−1)(p−1)/2 and (2/p) = (−1)(p²−1)/8.

The central result is the law of quadratic reciprocity: for distinct odd primes p and q,

(p/q)(q/p) = (−1)((p−1)(q−1)/4)

which, combined with the supplementary laws for −1 and 2, allows rapid computation of any Legendre symbol by a Euclidean-algorithm-like reduction, without factoring beyond extracting factors of 2.

Computation and generalisations

Legendre symbols are computed efficiently much as in the Euclidean algorithm, and they appear in primality tests such as the Solovay–Strassen test and in the theory of quadratic fields. The symbol was generalised by Jacobi to the Jacobi symbol, which allows composite odd moduli in the denominator, and more broadly to power-residue symbols and characters of finite fields.
