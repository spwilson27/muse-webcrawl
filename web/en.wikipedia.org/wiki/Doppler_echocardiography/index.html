Doppler echocardiography

Doppler echocardiography is a procedure that uses diagnostic ultrasound and the Doppler effect to examine the heart, measuring the direction and speed of blood flow through its chambers and valves.

Doppler echocardiography
Type: Medical imaging
Uses: Valve disease, shunts, pressures
Modes: Pulsed-wave, continuous-wave, colour flow

Principles

Red blood cells reflect ultrasound; the frequency shift of the returning signal reveals their velocity. Pulsed-wave Doppler samples a specific location but aliases at high velocities, continuous-wave Doppler measures very high velocities without a definite location, and colour-flow mapping overlays velocity information on the two-dimensional image. Tissue Doppler measures myocardial motion itself.

Clinical uses

The technique quantifies valvular stenosis and regurgitation (using the simplified Bernoulli equation, ΔP = 4v²), estimates pulmonary artery pressure, detects intracardiac shunts, and assesses diastolic function. It is combined with transthoracic or transoesophageal echocardiography and with stress testing.
