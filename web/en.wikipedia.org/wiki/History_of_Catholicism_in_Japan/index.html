History of Catholicism in Japan

The history of Catholicism in Japan began in 1549 with the arrival of the Jesuit missionary Francis Xavier at Kagoshima. Within decades the church claimed hundreds of thousands of converts.

Persecution

From 1587, and decisively under the Tokugawa shogunate from 1614, Christianity was banned. Missionaries and converts were executed, including the Twenty-six Martyrs of Nagasaki (1597), and surviving "Hidden Christians" (Kakure Kirishitan) practised in secret for over two centuries.

Reopening

After Japan reopened in the 1850s, missionaries returned and discovered hidden Christian communities near Nagasaki in 1865. The ban was lifted in 1873, and the church was re-established openly.
