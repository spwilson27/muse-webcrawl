October Diploma

Franz Joseph I issued the October Diploma in 1860 in an attempt to re-order the empire on federal lines.

The October Diploma (German: Oktoberdiplom) was a constitutional document issued by Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria on 20 October 1860. It was an attempt to end the decade of neo-absolutism that followed the revolutions of 1848 by devolving power to the empire's historic provincial diets and creating an enlarged Imperial Council (Reichsrat) with a say in imperial legislation.

Background

Austria's defeat in the Franco-Austrian War of 1859 and a resulting fiscal crisis exposed the weakness of the centralised Bach system. Under the influence of the Hungarian conservative aristocracy and Minister-President Count Agenor Gołuchowski, Franz Joseph sought a federal settlement that would reconcile the empire's nationalities while preserving dynastic authority.

Provisions

The diploma declared itself an "irrevocable fundamental law of the state." It restored the provincial diets of the crown lands, granted Hungary its historic constitution and a separate chancellery, and gave the Reichsrat competence over taxation, the budget, and commercial and military legislation. Foreign affairs and the army command remained imperial prerogatives.

Reaction and replacement

The compromise satisfied almost nobody: Hungarian liberals demanded full restoration of the laws of 1848, while German liberals and the bureaucracy wanted a centralised parliament. Within months Franz Joseph dismissed Gołuchowski and appointed Anton von Schmerling, whose more centralist February Patent of 1861 effectively superseded the diploma. The search for a settlement continued until the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867.
