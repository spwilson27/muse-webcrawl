RAF Bomber Command

RAF Bomber Command
Active: 14 July 1936 – 1968
Country: United Kingdom
Branch: Royal Air Force
Role: Strategic bombing
Headquarters: RAF High Wycombe

RAF Bomber Command controlled the Royal Air Force's bomber forces from 1936 to 1968. It is most famous for its role in the strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany during the Second World War, in which it mounted a vast night-time offensive against German cities, industry, and military targets, complementing the daylight operations of the United States Army Air Forces.

Second World War

At the outbreak of war in 1939 Bomber Command was equipped largely with twin-engined aircraft of limited range and bomb load, and early daylight raids suffered heavy losses. The Command shifted to night bombing; the Butt Report of 1941 showed how inaccurate that bombing was, leading to the adoption of formal area bombing policy in February 1942. Under Air Chief Marshal Sir Arthur Harris, who took command the same month, and with four-engined heavies such as the Avro Lancaster and Handley Page Halifax, the Command grew enormously: landmark operations included the first "thousand-bomber raid" on Cologne (May 1942), the Dambusters raid of No. 617 Squadron (May 1943), the Hamburg firestorm (July 1943), the Battle of Berlin, support of the Normandy invasion, and the bombing of Dresden (February 1945).

Some 55,573 Bomber Command aircrew were killed during the war, the highest loss rate of any British service branch. The Command's wartime contribution and the morality of area bombing remain subjects of historical debate; official recognition came late, with a Bomber Command Memorial in London unveiled in 2012 and a campaign clasp authorised in 2013.

Cold War

After 1945 Bomber Command operated the V-bomber force — the Valiant, Vulcan, and Victor — which carried Britain's independent nuclear deterrent from 1955 until the role passed to the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines in 1969. In 1968 Bomber Command was merged with Fighter Command to form the new Strike Command.
