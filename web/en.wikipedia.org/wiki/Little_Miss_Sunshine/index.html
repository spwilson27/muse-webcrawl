Little Miss Sunshine

Little Miss Sunshine is a 2006 American tragicomedy road film directed by Jonathan Dayton and Valerie Faris and written by Michael Arndt. It stars Greg Kinnear, Steve Carell, Toni Collette, Paul Dano, Abigail Breslin and Alan Arkin. The film follows the dysfunctional Hoover family as they drive their yellow Volkswagen Type 2 microbus from Albuquerque, New Mexico, to Redondo Beach, California, so that their seven-year-old daughter Olive can compete in the "Little Miss Sunshine" beauty pageant.

Plot

The family includes Richard, a failing motivational speaker obsessed with winning; Sheryl, his overworked wife; Dwayne, their teenage son, who has taken a vow of silence; Frank, Sheryl's brother, a Proust scholar recovering from a suicide attempt; and Edwin, Richard's foul-mouthed father. Along the way the van's clutch fails, Edwin dies suddenly, and the family smuggles his body onward to stay on schedule. Olive's exuberant, inappropriate dance routine — choreographed by her grandfather — scandalises the pageant, but the family joins her on stage in solidarity.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2006 and became a major box-office success, grossing over $101 million against an $8 million budget. At the 79th Academy Awards it won Best Original Screenplay (Arndt) and Best Supporting Actor (Arkin), with nominations for Best Picture and Best Supporting Actress (Breslin, then aged ten).
