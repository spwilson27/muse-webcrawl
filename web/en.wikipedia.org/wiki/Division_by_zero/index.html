Division by zero

In mathematics, division by zero is division in which the divisor (denominator) is zero. Such a division can be formally expressed as a/0, where a is the dividend. In ordinary arithmetic this expression has no meaning, because there is no number which, when multiplied by 0, gives a (assuming a ≠ 0); division by zero is therefore undefined.

Why it is undefined

Division is the inverse of multiplication: a/b = c means a = b × c. If b = 0 and a ≠ 0, no c satisfies the equation, since 0 × c = 0 for every c. If a = 0 as well, every c satisfies it, so there is still no unique result. Defining 1/0 as infinity leads to contradictions within the ordinary field axioms (for instance, ∞ + 1 = ∞ would imply 1 = 0 by cancellation).

Limits and extended systems

In calculus, a quotient whose denominator tends to zero may tend to positive or negative infinity, or fail to converge; such forms are treated by limits rather than by division. Certain extended number systems do give division by zero a value: on the Riemann sphere (projectively extended complex plane), 1/0 = ∞, and in wheel theory and in the IEEE 754 floating-point standard, nonzero values divided by zero yield signed infinities, while 0/0 yields NaN ("not a number").

In computing

Integer division by zero typically triggers an error or exception, while floating-point arithmetic follows IEEE 754 conventions. Historical incidents include the 1997 incident aboard USS Yorktown, whose propulsion was disabled when a database entry caused a division by zero in its control software.
