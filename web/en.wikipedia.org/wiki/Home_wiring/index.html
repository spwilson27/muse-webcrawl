Home wiring

Home wiring is the electrical wiring system installed in a house or other dwelling, distributing mains electricity from the utility service entrance to lighting, socket outlets and fixed appliances. It comprises the service drop or cable, meter, consumer unit or breaker panel, branch circuits, switches, outlets and protective devices, and is governed by national wiring regulations such as the National Electrical Code in the United States and BS 7671 in the United Kingdom.

Systems and practice

Power enters the home through a service entrance and passes through a meter to a distribution board, where circuit breakers or fuses protect separate branch circuits. North American homes typically receive split-phase 120/240 V service, with 120 V for general outlets and lighting and 240 V for large appliances; most other countries supply 230 V single-phase power. Conductors are copper (historically also aluminium in 1960s–70s North American branch wiring, now regarded as a fire risk at terminations). Common cable types include non-metallic sheathed cable (Romex) in North America and twin-and-earth PVC cable in Britain. Circuits are arranged radially (star) or, in British practice, as ring final circuits for socket outlets.

History

Early domestic installations in the 1880s–1900s used exposed knob-and-tube wiring; this gave way to armoured cable, conduit and then insulated cables run within walls. Fuse boxes were progressively replaced by breaker panels, and safety devices have been added over time: earthing (grounding), residual-current devices (RCDs/GFCIs) protecting against electric shock, and arc-fault protection in some jurisdictions. Older systems — knob-and-tube, lead-sheathed cable, degraded rubber insulation — are common targets of modernisation.

Safety

Faulty or overloaded wiring is a major cause of house fires and electrocution; regulations therefore specify conductor sizes, overcurrent protection, earthing, and separation of circuits in wet areas such as bathrooms. In many jurisdictions significant work on home wiring must be performed or certified by a licensed electrician.
