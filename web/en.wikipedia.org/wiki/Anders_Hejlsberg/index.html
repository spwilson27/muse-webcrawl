Anders Hejlsberg

Born: 2 December 1960, Copenhagen, Denmark

Nationality: Danish

Occupation: Systems engineer, Microsoft Technical Fellow

Known for: Turbo Pascal, Delphi, C#, TypeScript

Anders Hejlsberg (born 2 December 1960) is a Danish software engineer who co-designed several popular and commercially successful programming languages and development tools. He is the original author of Turbo Pascal and the chief architect of Delphi. As a Distinguished Engineer and Technical Fellow at Microsoft, he led the design of C# and later created TypeScript.

Early work

Hejlsberg studied engineering at the Technical University of Denmark but did not graduate. In the early 1980s he wrote programs for the Nascom microcomputer, including a Pascal compiler that he later ported to CP/M and MS-DOS. The compiler was licensed to Borland and marketed as Turbo Pascal, released in 1983 to great success.

Borland and Delphi

At Borland, Hejlsberg became chief architect of the Turbo Pascal line and then led the team that developed its successor, Delphi, a rapid application development environment for Windows built around an object-oriented Pascal dialect. Delphi was released in 1995.

Microsoft: C# and TypeScript

In 1996 Hejlsberg moved to Microsoft, where he worked on the J++ language and Windows Foundation Classes before becoming lead architect of C#, the flagship language of the .NET platform, announced in 2000. C# combined elements of C++, Java, and Delphi. In 2012 Microsoft released TypeScript, a superset of JavaScript adding static types, designed under Hejlsberg's leadership and released as open source. He has received numerous honours, including Dr. Dobb's Excellence in Programming Award.
