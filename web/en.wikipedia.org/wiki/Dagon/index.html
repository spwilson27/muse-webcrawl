Dagon

Dagon or Dagan was a god worshipped in ancient Syria and Mesopotamia and, in the Hebrew Bible, presented as the national god of the Philistines. Dagan is attested from the third millennium BCE at sites such as Ebla and Mari, where he was a major deity associated with grain and fertility and regarded as the father of Hadad (Baal).

Etymology

The name is probably derived from a Semitic word for "grain" (Hebrew dagan), though a later folk etymology connected it with dag ("fish"), leading medieval and early modern depictions of Dagon as a merman — part man, part fish. Rabbinic and classical sources differ on which association is original.

In the Bible

In the Book of Judges, Samson destroys a temple of Dagon in Gaza, pulling down its pillars and killing the Philistine lords assembled there. In 1 Samuel, the captured Ark of the Covenant is placed in Dagon's temple at Ashdod, and the statue of Dagon is found fallen and broken before it. Dagon also appears in connection with Beth-dagon and other place names.
