Mérida Initiative

The Mérida Initiative (Spanish: Iniciativa Mérida) was a security cooperation agreement between the United States and Mexico, later extended to countries of Central America, aimed at combating drug trafficking, transnational organised crime and associated money laundering. Named after the city of Mérida, Yucatán, where it was announced in 2007, the initiative channelled several billion dollars of US assistance to Mexico between 2008 and 2021.

Background

The initiative grew out of meetings between US president George W. Bush and Mexican president Felipe Calderón in March 2007. Calderón had launched a militarised campaign against Mexico's drug cartels on taking office in December 2006, and the United States accepted a doctrine of "shared responsibility", acknowledging that US demand for illicit drugs and the southbound flow of firearms fuelled violence in Mexico.

Assistance

Between fiscal years 2008 and 2021, the US Congress appropriated roughly $3.3 billion for the initiative. Funding supplied equipment including helicopters (among them UH-60 Black Hawks), surveillance aircraft, ion scanners and other inspection equipment, as well as training for Mexican police, military personnel, prosecutors and judges. A portion of assistance was conditioned on human-rights requirements under US law.

Four pillars

In 2010 the programme was reorganised under four strategic "pillars": (1) disrupting the capacity of organised criminal groups to operate; (2) institutionalising the rule of law; (3) building a twenty-first-century border; and (4) building strong and resilient communities, including drug-demand reduction and prevention programmes.

Central America and criticism

Related funding for Central America was folded into the Central America Regional Security Initiative (CARSI). Critics argued that the Mérida Initiative over-emphasised military hardware while violence in Mexico continued to climb, and that vetting of Mexican units for human-rights abuses was weak. Supporters pointed to improved intelligence sharing, judicial reform support — including Mexico's transition to oral, adversarial criminal trials — and captures of cartel leaders.

Successor framework

In October 2021 the United States and Mexico announced the Bicentennial Framework for Security, Public Health and Safe Communities, formally replacing the Mérida Initiative with an approach placing greater weight on public health, arms trafficking and the root causes of violence and migration.
