Decontamination

Decontamination (often abbreviated decon) is the process of removing contaminants from an object or area, including chemicals, micro-organisms, and radioactive substances. The purpose is to make people, equipment, and environments safe, preventing the spread of hazardous material and reducing exposure.

Methods

Decontamination methods include physical processes such as washing, scrubbing, vacuuming with HEPA filtration, and absorption; chemical processes such as neutralization, disinfection with bleach solutions, and use of decontaminating foams and gels; and, for equipment and surfaces contaminated with radioactivity, techniques such as chemical dissolution, abrasive cleaning, and simply allowing decay during storage.

Emergency response

In hazardous materials (HAZMAT) and CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) incidents, responders establish decontamination corridors where victims and personnel remove clothing and are washed down, typically with water and soap, before receiving medical care. Mass-casualty decontamination uses tents, showers, and conveyor systems to process large numbers of people quickly.

Medical and laboratory settings

In healthcare, decontamination of instruments precedes disinfection and sterilization, and includes cleaning to remove blood and tissue. Nuclear facilities carry out decontamination of components and whole plants during maintenance and decommissioning.
