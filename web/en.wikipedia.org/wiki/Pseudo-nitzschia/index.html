Pseudo-nitzschia

Pseudo-nitzschia is a genus of marine planktonic diatoms found in oceans worldwide. The cells are needle-shaped and join in stepped, overlapping chains. The genus is notorious because many of its species produce domoic acid, a neurotoxin responsible for amnesic shellfish poisoning in humans and for mass strandings and deaths of seabirds and marine mammals.

Taxonomy and identification

Pseudo-nitzschia was separated from the genus Nitzschia on the basis of its chain-forming habit and valve ultrastructure. The genus contains more than fifty described species, many of which are cryptic — they look nearly identical under the light microscope and can only be distinguished by electron microscopy of their silica frustules or by molecular sequencing. This makes monitoring of toxic blooms difficult, since only some species, and only some strains of those, produce domoic acid.

Domoic acid and harmful blooms

The danger of the genus was recognised after a 1987 outbreak in Prince Edward Island, Canada, traced to cultured mussels contaminated with domoic acid produced by P. multiseries; three people died and over a hundred suffered illness, some with lasting memory loss — hence "amnesic" shellfish poisoning. A huge bloom of P. australis along the west coast of North America in 2015, associated with unusually warm water, closed crab and shellfish fisheries and poisoned sea lions. A 1961 incident in which sooty shearwaters attacked people and objects in Capitola, California — an episode often linked to Alfred Hitchcock's film The Birds — has also been attributed to domoic acid poisoning.
