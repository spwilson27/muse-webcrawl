Euridice (Peri)

Euridice
Composer:Jacopo Peri
Librettist:Ottavio Rinuccini
Premiere:6 October 1600, Palazzo Pitti, Florence
Language:Italian

Euridice is an opera by Jacopo Peri, with additional music by Giulio Caccini, set to a libretto by Ottavio Rinuccini. It is the earliest surviving opera, though Peri's earlier setting of Dafne (1598), the first opera ever composed, survives only in fragments.

Performance history

Euridice was first performed on 6 October 1600 at the Palazzo Pitti in Florence, as part of the festivities celebrating the wedding of Maria de' Medici to King Henry IV of France. Peri himself sang the role of Orfeo. The score was published in 1601 and dedicated to Maria de' Medici.

Synopsis

The libretto follows the Greek myth of Orpheus and Euridice. Unlike the original myth, Rinuccini's version has a happy ending: Orfeo's singing moves Pluto to release Euridice from the underworld, and the reunited lovers celebrate.

Music

The work is written in the new stile recitativo, in which the voice declaims the text over a sparse basso continuo accompaniment, interspersed with choruses and short instrumental ritornelli. Peri aimed to imitate the delivery of ancient Greek tragedy as it was then understood by the Florentine Camerata.
