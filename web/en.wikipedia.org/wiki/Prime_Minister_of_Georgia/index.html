Prime Minister of Georgia

Prime Minister of Georgia
Residence: Tbilisi
Appointer: Parliament of Georgia
Term length: 4 years
Formation: 1918 (first); 1991 (current form)

The Prime Minister of Georgia is the head of government of Georgia. The prime minister chairs the Government of Georgia, directs domestic and foreign policy implementation, and is accountable to the Parliament of Georgia.

Constitutional role

Under the Constitution of Georgia, as revised by the 2017–2018 amendments, most executive power is vested in the prime minister, while the President of Georgia serves largely as a ceremonial head of state. The prime minister is nominated by the political party that secured the best results in parliamentary elections and must win a vote of confidence in parliament.

History

The office was first established in 1918 in the Democratic Republic of Georgia, with Noe Ramishvili as its first holder. After the Soviet period, the post was restored following independence in 1991. The constitutional reforms of 2013 shifted Georgia from a presidential toward a parliamentary system, strengthening the prime minister's role.

Appointment and removal

Following a parliamentary election, the president nominates the candidate put forward by the strongest party or bloc, and parliament confirms the government. The prime minister may be removed by a vote of no confidence or may resign, in which case the whole government departs.
