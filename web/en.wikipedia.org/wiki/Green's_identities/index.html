Green's identities

In mathematics, Green's identities are a set of three identities in vector calculus relating the bulk with the boundary of a region on which differential operators act. They are named after the British mathematician George Green, who discovered them in his 1828 essay An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism.

Green's first identity

This identity is derived from the divergence theorem applied to the vector field F = ψ∇φ. It states that for scalar functions φ and ψ defined on a region U:

∫U (ψ∇²φ + ∇φ·∇ψ) dV = ∫∂U ψ ∂φ/∂n dS

where ∂φ/∂n is the directional derivative of φ in the direction of the outward-pointing normal to the boundary ∂U.

Green's second identity

If φ and ψ are both twice continuously differentiable on U, then:

∫U (ψ∇²φ − φ∇²ψ) dV = ∫∂U (ψ ∂φ/∂n − φ ∂ψ/∂n) dS

This identity is symmetric in φ and ψ. In particular, if φ and ψ are both harmonic functions (satisfying Laplace's equation ∇²φ = 0) in U, then the identity reduces to the statement that the normal derivatives weighted by the functions are equal in a reciprocal sense.

Green's third identity

Green's third identity states that if ψ is a function with a singularity, such as the fundamental solution of Laplace's equation, then an integral representation can express the value of φ at any interior point in terms of boundary values of φ and its normal derivative, plus a volume integral involving ∇²φ. This identity is foundational for boundary integral methods and potential theory.

Applications

Green's identities are used extensively in solving partial differential equations, particularly in deriving uniqueness results for boundary value problems, in establishing the mean value property of harmonic functions, and in the formulation of Green's functions for the Laplacian. They also underpin the theory of boundary element methods in numerical analysis and engineering.
