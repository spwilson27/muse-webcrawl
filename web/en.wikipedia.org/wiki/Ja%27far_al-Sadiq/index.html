Ja'far al-Sadiq

Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Sadiq (c. 702 – 765 CE), commonly known as Ja'far al-Sadiq ("the Truthful"), was a Muslim scholar and the sixth imam in Twelver and Ismaili Shia Islam. He is revered in Shia tradition as a major authority on Islamic law and theology, and the Ja'fari school of jurisprudence is named after him.

Life

Ja'far was born in Medina, the son of Muhammad al-Baqir, the fifth Shia imam, and a descendant of the prophet Muhammad through Ali and Fatima. He lived through the fall of the Umayyad Caliphate and the rise of the Abbasids. Unlike some of his contemporaries, he largely abstained from political revolt, devoting himself instead to teaching. He died in Medina in 765 and is buried in the al-Baqi cemetery.

Teachings

Al-Sadiq taught a wide circle of students in Medina on subjects including jurisprudence (fiqh), theology (kalam), hadith, and natural philosophy. His students are said to have included Abu Hanifa, founder of the Hanafi school, Malik ibn Anas, founder of the Maliki school, and the alchemist Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber), to whom a large body of alchemical writings was attributed. His legal doctrines form the basis of the Ja'fari madhhab followed by Twelver Shias.

Succession and legacy

Disagreement over al-Sadiq's successor after his death contributed to the split between the Twelver and Ismaili branches of Shia Islam: Ismailis recognise his son Isma'il (who predeceased him, according to Twelver accounts) as his designated heir, while Twelvers follow his son Musa al-Kazim. Al-Sadiq is remembered as one of the most influential scholars of the formative period of Islamic thought.
