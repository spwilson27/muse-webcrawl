Council of States (Switzerland)

46 seats; upper house of the Swiss Federal Assembly.

The Council of States (German: Ständerat; French: Conseil des États; Italian: Consiglio degli Stati; Romansh: Cussegl dals Stadis) is the upper house of the Federal Assembly of Switzerland, representing the cantons. Together with the National Council, the lower house representing the people, it forms the federal legislature.

Composition

The council has 46 members: each of the twenty full cantons elects two members, and each of the six former half-cantons (Obwalden, Nidwalden, Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, and Appenzell Innerrhoden) elects one. Members serve four-year terms. Elections are regulated by the cantons; in almost all cantons members are elected directly by popular vote, traditionally at the same time as National Council elections, while in Appenzell Innerrhoden the member is chosen by the Landsgemeinde.

Powers

Unlike many upper houses, the Council of States has exactly the same powers as the National Council: legislation requires the agreement of both chambers, and neither can override the other. Disagreements are resolved through a shuttle procedure and, if necessary, a conciliation conference. The two chambers sit together as the United Federal Assembly to elect the Federal Council, the federal judges, and the Chancellor, and to decide on pardons and jurisdictional disputes.

Presidency

The council elects a president for a one-year term, who presides over its sessions and represents it externally. When the United Federal Assembly meets, the President of the National Council presides, with the President of the Council of States as vice-chair.
