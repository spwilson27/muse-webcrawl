Military–industrial–media complex

The military–industrial–media complex is an extension of the concept of the military–industrial complex that incorporates mass media and entertainment industries into the network of relationships among defence contractors, the armed forces, government and public opinion. The term is used by critics who argue that media organisations, through ownership links, advertising, access journalism and entertainment content such as films made with military cooperation, help shape favourable public attitudes toward defence spending and military interventions.

Background

President Dwight D. Eisenhower warned of the "military–industrial complex" in his farewell address of 1961. Later analysts added Congress (the "iron triangle") and, subsequently, the media, noting arrangements such as the United States Department of Defense's entertainment liaison offices, which provide equipment and personnel to film and television productions in exchange for script influence.

Criticism and debate

Supporters of the concept point to embedded journalism during the Iraq War and revolving-door employment between media, government and defence firms. Critics reply that commercial media often report critically on wars and that influence is difficult to demonstrate directly.
