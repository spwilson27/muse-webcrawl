Duke of Somerset

Duke of Somerset is a title that has been created five times in the peerage of England. It is particularly associated with two families: the Beauforts, who held the title from the 1440s, and the Seymours, who held it from 1547 and hold it today. The incumbent is John Seymour, 19th Duke of Somerset, who succeeded his father in 1984.

History

The title was first created in 1443 for John Beaufort, 3rd Earl of Somerset, a grandson of John of Gaunt. His brother Edmund Beaufort became the second duke and was a leading figure on the Lancastrian side in the Wars of the Roses; he was killed at the First Battle of St Albans in 1455. His son Henry Beaufort, 3rd Duke of Somerset, was executed after the Battle of Hexham in 1464, and the title was forfeited, though it was later briefly restored.

The title was created again in 1499 for Edmund Tudor, the infant son of Henry VII, who died young. A further short-lived creation was made for the king's illegitimate son Henry FitzRoy, who was created Duke of Richmond and Somerset in 1525 and died in 1536.

The most enduring creation came in 1547, when Edward Seymour, elder brother of Jane Seymour and uncle of Edward VI, was created Duke of Somerset. As Lord Protector of England he effectively ruled the country during the young king's minority, until his fall and execution in 1552, after which the title was forfeited. It was restored in 1660 to his descendant William Seymour, and the Seymour family has held it ever since. The family seats have included Maiden Bradley House in Wiltshire and Berry Pomeroy Castle in Devon.

Dukes of Somerset (1547 creation)

Among the holders of the Seymour dukedom were Edward Seymour, 1st Duke (c. 1500–1552), the Lord Protector; Algernon Seymour, 7th Duke, after whose death in 1750 the dukedom passed to a junior branch of the family, the Seymours of Berry Pomeroy; and Percy Seymour, 18th Duke (1910–1984). The heir apparent to the dukedom traditionally uses the courtesy title Lord Seymour.
