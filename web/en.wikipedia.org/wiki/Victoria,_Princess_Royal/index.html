Victoria, Princess Royal (Victoria Adelaide Mary Louisa; 21 November 1840 – 5 August 1901) was German Empress and Queen of Prussia as the wife of German Emperor Frederick III. She was the eldest child of Queen Victoria of the United Kingdom and Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha. Educated by her father in a liberal political environment, she became engaged at age fourteen to Prince Frederick of Prussia, later the German Emperor, and married him in 1858.

Life in Prussia

Victoria ("Vicky") and Frederick shared liberal ambitions to transform Prussia and the German Empire into a constitutional monarchy on the British model, but were largely outmanoeuvred by Chancellor Otto von Bismarck and the conservative court. Frederick reigned only 99 days in the "Year of the Three Emperors" (1888) before dying of laryngeal cancer; Victoria became known as the Empress Frederick. Her relations with her eldest son, Wilhelm II, were strained throughout his reign.

Legacy

A talented painter and patron of the arts and education, Victoria supported nursing reform, girls' education, and the decorative arts in Berlin. Her extensive correspondence with Queen Victoria — thousands of letters, many smuggled to Britain for safekeeping — is a major source for the period. She died of breast cancer at Schloss Friedrichshof, near Kronberg, in August 1901, only months after her mother's death.
