Fujifilm

Fujifilm Holdings Corporation (富士フイルムホールディングス株式会社), commonly known as Fujifilm, is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Tokyo. Founded in 1934 as a photographic film manufacturer, it has diversified into imaging, healthcare, materials and document solutions.


A Fujifilm digital camera

History

The company was established on 20 January 1934 as Fuji Photo Film Co., Ltd., a subsidiary of Daicel, and began producing photographic film at Ashigara, Kanagawa. It expanded into colour film, magnetic media, and the QuickSnap disposable camera. As digital photography displaced film after 2000, Fujifilm survived the collapse of its core market — unlike its rival Kodak — by diversifying into medical imaging, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics (using collagen and antioxidant technology from film chemistry), and flat-panel display materials. In 2006 it adopted a holding company structure, and in 2021 Fuji Xerox became wholly owned and renamed Fujifilm Business Innovation.

Products

Fujifilm is known for its X-series and GFX mirrorless digital cameras, Instax instant cameras and film, photographic paper, medical imaging systems (including computed radiography), endoscopes, and biotechnology contract manufacturing through subsidiaries such as Fujifilm Diosynth Biotechnologies.
