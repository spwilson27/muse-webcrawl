Freshwater whitefish

The freshwater whitefishes are fishes of the subfamily Coregoninae in the salmon family Salmonidae. Alongside the ciscoes and lake whitefish (genus Coregonus), the subfamily includes the inconnu (Stenodus) and the round whitefishes (Prosopium). They are found in cold lakes and rivers across the Northern Hemisphere and support important commercial and subsistence fisheries.

Characteristics

Coregonines are silvery, small-scaled salmonids with small, often toothless mouths adapted to feeding on plankton and benthic invertebrates. An adipose fin is present, as in other salmonids. Body form and gill-raker number vary greatly within lakes, and the taxonomy of Coregonus is notoriously difficult: post-glacial populations evolved distinct forms in individual lakes, and dozens of species have been described, some now extinct.

Distribution and ecology

Whitefishes inhabit deep, cold, well-oxygenated lakes and northern rivers throughout Canada, Alaska, the Great Lakes region, Scandinavia, Russia, and alpine Europe. Most spawn in autumn over gravel shoals. They are key prey for lake trout and other predators, and sensitive indicators of lake health.

Fisheries

Lake whitefish (Coregonus clupeaformis) is one of the most valuable freshwater commercial fishes of North America; ciscoes (lake herring) and the European vendace and powan are also fished. Several Great Lakes cisco species were driven to extinction in the 20th century by overfishing, invasive sea lamprey, and habitat change, and some remaining forms are protected.
