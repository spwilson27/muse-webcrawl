Donella Meadows

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Donella Hager "Dana" Meadows (March 13, 1941 – February 20, 2001) was an American environmental scientist and writer. She was the lead author of the influential 1972 report The Limits to Growth, which used system dynamics modelling to examine the long-term consequences of growth in population and resource use, and a leading populariser of systems thinking.

Life and work

Meadows earned a PhD in biophysics from Harvard in 1968 and joined the faculty of Dartmouth College, where she taught for 29 years. Commissioned by the Club of Rome, she led the MIT-based team that built the World3 model and wrote The Limits to Growth with Dennis Meadows, Jørgen Randers, and William Behrens; the book sold millions of copies and sparked worldwide debate. The team later published updates including Beyond the Limits (1992).

Writing and legacy

For 16 years Meadows wrote "The Global Citizen", a syndicated newspaper column on environment and society, nominated for a Pulitzer Prize. Her essay "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (1999) — ranking interventions from parameters to paradigms — became a classic of systems literature. Her book Thinking in Systems: A Primer was published posthumously in 2008. She founded the Sustainability Institute and lived and farmed in Vermont.
