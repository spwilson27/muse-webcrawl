Super Rugby

Super Rugby Pacific action

Super Rugby is a men's professional rugby union club competition involving teams from Australia, New Zealand, Fiji and the Pacific Islands, with past participation from South Africa, Argentina and Japan. Run by SANZAAR, it began in 1996 as the Super 12 in the sport's first professional season, expanded through Super 14 (2006) and a 15-team Super Rugby (2011), peaked at 18 teams across four continents, and since 2022 has operated as Super Rugby Pacific with 12 teams.

History

The competition was created after rugby union turned professional in 1995, when the southern hemisphere unions formed SANZAR and signed a landmark broadcast deal. The original Super 12 featured five New Zealand, four South African and three Australian provincial/franchise teams. Expansions added Australia's Western Force (2006), the Melbourne Rebels (2011), South Africa's Southern Kings, Argentina's Jaguares and Japan's Sunwolves (2016).

The sprawling 18-team format proved unwieldy, and the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated a split: South African teams departed for Europe's United Rugby Championship, and from 2022 the competition relaunched as Super Rugby Pacific with five New Zealand teams, five Australian teams, the Fijian Drua and Moana Pasifika.

Champions

The Crusaders of Christchurch are the most successful team, winning 13 titles including a run of dominance across multiple eras. Other multiple champions include the Blues (4), the Bulls (3), and the Chiefs, Hurricanes, Waratahs and Reds with titles of their own. Argentina's Jaguares reached the 2019 final, the best result by a team from outside the founding three nations.
