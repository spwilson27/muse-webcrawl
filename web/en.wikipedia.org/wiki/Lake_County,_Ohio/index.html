Lake County is a county in the U.S. state of Ohio. As of the 2020 census, the population was 232,603. Its county seat is Painesville, and its largest city is Mentor. The county was established on March 6, 1840, from land given by Cuyahoga and Geauga counties, and is named for Lake Erie, which forms its northern boundary. It is the smallest county in Ohio by land area.

Lake County is part of the Cleveland, OH metropolitan statistical area. It lies in the historic Connecticut Western Reserve, and was settled largely by migrants from New England in the early 19th century.

History

Kirtland, in the county's southwest, was the headquarters of the Latter Day Saint movement under Joseph Smith from 1831 to 1838; the Kirtland Temple, completed in 1836, was the first temple built by the movement and is preserved today as a historic site. The county's lake plain developed a famous nursery industry — by the early 20th century Lake County styled itself the "nursery capital" — and its Lake Erie shore towns became summer resorts for Clevelanders.

The Perry Nuclear Generating Station, on the lake shore in North Perry, began operation in 1987 and is one of Ohio's two nuclear power plants.

Geography

Most of the county's total area is the surface of Lake Erie itself. On land, the flat lake plain rises southward to a low ridge; the Grand River and Chagrin River cut valleys through the county on their way to the lake. Fairport Harbor is the county's principal port. Lake-effect snow from Lake Erie gives the county heavy winter snowfall, especially in its eastern townships.

Communities

Cities include Eastlake, Kirtland, Mentor, Mentor-on-the-Lake, Painesville, Wickliffe, Willoughby, Willoughby Hills and Willowick. Villages include Fairport Harbor, Grand River, Kirtland Hills, Lakeline, Madison, North Perry, Perry, Timberlake and Waite Hill. Townships include Concord, Leroy, Madison and Painesville.
