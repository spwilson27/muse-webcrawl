Flight ticket

A flight ticket (or airline ticket) is a document issued by an airline or travel agency that confirms an individual's entitlement to a seat on a flight. It serves as evidence of the contract of carriage between the passenger and the airline. Historically printed on multi-part carbon paper with detachable flight coupons, tickets have been almost entirely electronic since IATA mandated 100 per cent e-ticketing in 2008.

Contents

A ticket records the passenger's name, the issuing airline and ticket number, the itinerary (flight numbers, dates, departure and arrival airports and times), the fare basis and booking class, taxes and total fare, form of payment, and conditions such as refundability and baggage allowance. The booking is stored in the airline's reservation system under a passenger name record (PNR) locator, which the traveller uses for check-in; the boarding pass — the document actually needed to board — is issued separately at check-in.

History

Early airline tickets of the 1920s and 1930s were simple handwritten receipts. Standardised IATA ticket stock, with red carbon flight coupons surrendered at each boarding, dominated the jet age. Automated tickets and boarding passes (ATB) with magnetic stripes appeared in the 1980s, followed by the first e-tickets in the mid-1990s; paper coupons disappeared from IATA settlement in June 2008.
