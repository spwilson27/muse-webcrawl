Bloody Mary (cocktail)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Bloody Mary in a tall glass with a celery stalk, lemon wedge and elaborate garnishes]
A Bloody Mary in a tall glass with a celery stalk, lemon wedge and elaborate garnishes

A Bloody Mary is a cocktail containing Vodka, tomato juice and combinations of other spices and flavourings including Worcestershire sauce, hot sauces, garlic, herbs, horseradish, celery, olives, salt, black pepper, lemon juice, lime juice and celery salt. Created in the 1920s–30s and canonised at New York's St. Regis Hotel, it is the archetypal savoury cocktail and a brunch institution, famed (on doubtful evidence) as a hangover cure.

Origins

Paris bartender Fernand Petiot made a vodka-and-tomato drink at Harry's New York Bar in 1921; the name's origin is disputed (a waitress named Mary, Queen Mary I, or others). At the St. Regis's King Cole Bar from 1934, Petiot added the spices that define it; the drink was briefly renamed the Red Snapper when 'bloody' seemed indelicate. The industrial tomato juice of the era — and vodka's American marketing — made it a transatlantic hit; the celery stalk garnish is credited to a 1960s Chicago ambassador-hotel guest improvising a stirrer.

Variations and culture

The family includes the Bloody Maria (tequila), Red Snapper (gin), Virgin Mary and the Canadian Caesar (clamato juice, 1969) — Canada's national cocktail. Modern bars compete in garnish excess, skewering sliders and fried chickens atop the glass, while the drink's reputation as 'the world's most complex cocktail' rests on its balance of acid, salt, heat and umami.
