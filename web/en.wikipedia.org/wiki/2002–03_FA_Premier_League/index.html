The 2002–03 FA Premier League (known as the FA Barclaycard Premiership for sponsorship reasons) was the 11th season of the Premier League. Manchester United won the title, reclaiming it from defending champions Arsenal after a dramatic run-in in which Arsenal's long lead at the top was overturned in the final weeks.

Season summary

Arsenal, who had won the Double in 2001–02, led the table for much of the season and at one stage in March held an eight-point advantage. A collapse in form — including a 2–2 draw at Bolton Wanderers after leading 2–0 and a 2–3 home defeat by Leeds United — allowed Manchester United, inspired by Ruud van Nistelrooy's 25 league goals, to surge past them. United sealed the championship with a game to spare.

Newcastle United and Chelsea took the remaining Champions League places, Chelsea securing fourth place by beating Liverpool on the final day in a match later described as pivotal to the club's future under incoming owner Roman Abramovich. At the bottom, Sunderland finished with a then-record low of 19 points, and West Ham United were relegated with 42 points, the highest total ever recorded by a relegated Premier League side at the time.
