Louis Comfort Tiffany

Louis Comfort Tiffany (February 18, 1848 – January 17, 1933) was an American artist and designer who worked in the decorative arts and is best known for his work in stained glass. He is associated with the Art Nouveau and Aesthetic movements, and was the first Design Director of his father's company, Tiffany & Co., the luxury jewelry and silverware firm founded by Charles Lewis Tiffany.

Early life and painting

Tiffany was born in New York City, the son of Charles Lewis Tiffany, founder of Tiffany & Co. He trained as a painter, studying with George Inness and Samuel Colman, and travelled in Europe and North Africa. His early watercolours and landscapes were exhibited at the National Academy of Design and internationally. In the late 1870s his interests turned toward interior decoration and glass.

Interior design and Associated Artists

In 1879 Tiffany joined with Candace Wheeler, Samuel Colman and Lockwood de Forest to form Louis Comfort Tiffany and Associated American Artists. The firm undertook prestigious commissions, including rooms in the White House for President Chester A. Arthur in 1882. The partnership dissolved in 1885, when Tiffany decided to concentrate on glassmaking.

Favrile glass and Tiffany Studios

Tiffany established his own glassworks and in 1893 built the Tiffany Glass Furnaces at Corona, Queens. There he developed Favrile glass, a type of iridescent art glass whose colour is ingrained in the glass itself, influenced by medieval glass and the work of English makers. His firm, later known as Tiffany Studios, produced celebrated stained-glass windows, mosaics, enamels and the famous Tiffany lamps with leaded-glass shades, many designed by Clara Driscoll and the women of the firm's Women's Glass Cutting Department.

Tiffany's work achieved international fame at the 1900 Paris Exposition Universelle. Major commissions included the curtain of the National Theatre in Mexico City and interiors for wealthy patrons such as Henry Osborne Havemeyer.

Laurelton Hall and later life

Tiffany's own estate, Laurelton Hall at Oyster Bay, Long Island, completed in 1905, was an 84-room Art Nouveau mansion that served as a showcase for his work; much of it was later preserved by the Charles Hosmer Morse Museum of American Art after a fire destroyed the house in 1957. In 1918 Tiffany established the Louis Comfort Tiffany Foundation to support young artists. He died in New York on January 17, 1933.
