Equifax

Equifax Inc. is an American multinational consumer credit reporting agency headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. Together with Experian and TransUnion, it is one of the three largest consumer credit reporting agencies ("credit bureaus") in the United States. Founded in 1899 as the Retail Credit Company, it collects and aggregates information on over 800 million individual consumers and more than 88 million businesses worldwide, and also sells identity theft protection, credit monitoring, and workforce verification services.

History

The company was renamed Equifax in 1975. Its consumer practices were a major impetus for the Fair Credit Reporting Act of 1970. In the 2000s Equifax expanded its "Workforce Solutions" business; its The Work Number database holds employment and income records for a large share of US workers.

2017 data breach

In September 2017 Equifax disclosed a massive data breach in which attackers exploited an unpatched Apache Struts vulnerability to access personal data — names, Social Security numbers, birth dates, addresses, and in some cases driver's licence numbers — of about 147 million people, roughly half the US population, plus consumers in the UK and Canada. The scandal led to the resignation of CEO Richard Smith and a settlement of up to $700 million with US regulators and state attorneys general in 2019.
