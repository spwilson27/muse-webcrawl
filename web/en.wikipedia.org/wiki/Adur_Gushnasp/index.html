Adur Gushnasp
Religion: Zoroastrianism
Location: Takht-e Soleyman, northwestern Iran
Status: Sacred fire temple, ruined

Adur Gushnasp was one of the three great sacred fires of Zoroastrian Iran, associated with the warrior class. Its temple stood at the site now known as Takht-e Soleyman in northwestern Iran.

History

The fire was among the most revered in the Sasanian Empire, alongside Adur Farnbag and Adur Burzen-mihr. Sasanian kings traditionally visited Adur Gushnasp after their coronation, and the temple complex received royal patronage over centuries. The sanctuary was built around a crater lake and enclosed by monumental walls. After the Muslim conquest of Persia the site declined, though the ruins remained imposing and were later associated in legend with Solomon.

Archaeology

Excavations in the 20th century uncovered the fire temple, palace structures and fortifications. Takht-e Soleyman is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
