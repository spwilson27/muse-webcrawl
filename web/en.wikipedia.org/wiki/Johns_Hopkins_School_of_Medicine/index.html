The Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, located in Baltimore, Maryland, is the medical school of Johns Hopkins University. Founded in 1893, it is widely regarded as one of the world's leading medical schools and research institutions, and it profoundly reformed American medical education by requiring a university degree and science prerequisites for admission — standards later adopted nationwide following the Flexner Report of 1910.

History

The school grew out of the bequest of Johns Hopkins, which also founded the university (1876) and Johns Hopkins Hospital (1889). Opening of the medical school was delayed until a fundraising campaign led by Mary Elizabeth Garrett and the "Women's Medical School Fund" supplied the endowment — on the condition that women be admitted on the same terms as men. From the outset the school demanded a bachelor's degree with premedical science, a graded four-year curriculum, laboratory teaching and clinical clerkships at the hospital — a revolutionary model for American medicine.

People and research

The school's founding faculty — William Henry Welch, William Osler, William Stewart Halsted and Howard A. Kelly — created the modern residency system and the first schools of public health and nursing in the Hopkins orbit. Faculty and alumni have included many Nobel laureates; the school is consistently among the top recipients of National Institutes of Health funding, and its researchers have contributed to fields from genetics and neuroscience to immunology and oncology.

Admissions and programmes

The school offers the MD, MD–PhD (Medical Scientist Training Program), and numerous PhD programmes in biomedical sciences. Admission is among the most selective in the United States, with roughly 100–120 new MD students per class.
