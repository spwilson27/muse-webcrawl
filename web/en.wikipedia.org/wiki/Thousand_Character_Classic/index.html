Thousand Character Classic

The Thousand Character Classic (Chinese: 千字文; pinyin: Qiānzìwén) is a Chinese poem composed of 1,000 distinct characters, arranged in 250 lines of four characters each, with no character repeated. It was traditionally used as a primer for teaching Chinese characters to children, who would memorise it while learning to read and write.

Origin

According to tradition, Emperor Wu of the Liang dynasty (r. 502–549) commissioned the scholar Zhou Xingsi to compose the text in a single night, selecting the thousand characters from calligraphy samples of Wang Xizhi. The resulting rhymed, rhythmic poem covers topics from astronomy and geography to history, ethics, government, and daily life, opening with the famous lines "Heaven and Earth, dark and yellow; the cosmos, vast and desolate" (天地玄黃，宇宙洪荒).

Use and diffusion

Together with the Three Character Classic and the Hundred Family Surnames, it formed the traditional "San Bai Qian" set of elementary textbooks used across China for over a millennium. The text spread to Korea (as Cheonjamun) and Japan (as Senjimon), where it likewise served in education. Famous calligraphers through the centuries produced celebrated copies of the text, making it an enduring exercise piece for calligraphy as well as literacy.
