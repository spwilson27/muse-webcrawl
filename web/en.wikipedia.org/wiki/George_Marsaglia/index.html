George Marsaglia

Born: 12 March 1924, Denver, Colorado, U.S.

Died: 15 February 2011, Tallahassee, Florida, U.S.

Fields: Mathematics, statistics, computer science

Institutions: Florida State University, Washington State University

George Marsaglia (12 March 1924 – 15 February 2011) was an American mathematician and computer scientist best known for his work on pseudorandom number generators and for creating the Diehard tests for randomness.

Random number generation

Marsaglia proved fundamental results about the structure of linear congruential generators, famously showing that "random numbers fall mainly in the planes" — the outputs of such generators lie on a limited number of hyperplanes in higher dimensions. He invented several widely used generators, including the multiply-with-carry and lagged Fibonacci methods, the xorshift family was popularized following his work, and the Marsaglia polar method for generating normal deviates (developed with George Box and others) remains standard.

Diehard tests

In 1995 Marsaglia released the Diehard Battery of Tests of Randomness on CD-ROM, a demanding suite of statistical tests that became a benchmark for evaluating pseudorandom number generators. He later developed the Dieharder and TestU01-influenced successors' predecessor suites of tests.
