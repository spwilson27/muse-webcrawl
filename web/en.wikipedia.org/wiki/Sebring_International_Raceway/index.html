Sebring International Raceway is a road racing circuit in Sebring, Florida, United States, built on the runways and taxiways of the former Hendricks Army Airfield, a World War II bomber training base. It is home to the 12 Hours of Sebring, one of the world's great endurance sports car races, first run in 1952 and a fixture of the IMSA SportsCar Championship, forming part of the informal "triple crown" of endurance racing with the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

History

The first race at Sebring, a six-hour event, was held on New Year's Eve 1950, organised by Alec Ulmann. The first 12 Hours followed in 1952, and in 1959 the circuit hosted the first United States Grand Prix of the Formula One World Championship, won by Bruce McLaren. The track's notoriously rough concrete surfaces — a legacy of its airfield origins — make it a punishing test of machinery; teams traditionally regard half-distance at Sebring as harder on a car than a full 24 hours at Le Mans. In 2019 the FIA World Endurance Championship added the 1000 Miles of Sebring, run on the same weekend as the 12 Hours in a "Super Sebring" double-header.

Circuit

The full circuit is 3.74 miles (6.02 km) long with 17 turns, combining old airfield concrete sections with later asphalt. Famous corners include the fast Turn 1, the Hairpin, and the bumpy final Turn 17 (Sunset Bend), which leads onto the pit straight.
