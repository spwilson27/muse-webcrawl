Mercury most commonly refers to:

  • Mercury (planet), the smallest planet in the Solar System and the closest to the Sun, with an orbital period of about 88 Earth days and a heavily cratered surface
  • Mercury (element) (symbol Hg), a heavy, silvery liquid metal, also known as quicksilver, historically used in thermometers and fluorescent lamps and notorious for its toxicity
  • Mercury (mythology), the Roman god of commerce, communication and travellers, counterpart of the Greek Hermes

Other uses

The name is also borne by numerous other subjects, including the Mercury automobile brand formerly produced by Ford, Project Mercury (the first United States human spaceflight programme), the Freddie Mercury stage name of Queen's lead singer, newspapers, record labels, and many places and ships.

Overview of the planet

Mercury (planet) has the most eccentric orbit of the eight planets, experiences the widest temperature swings in the Solar System — from about −180 °C at night to 430 °C by day — and has a disproportionately large iron core. It has been visited by the Mariner 10 and MESSENGER spacecraft, with BepiColombo en route.
