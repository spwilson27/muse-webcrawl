Keratosis

A keratotic lesion on skin

Keratosis is a growth of keratin on the skin or on a mucous membrane stemming from keratinocytes, the prominent cell type in the epidermis. The term covers a family of mostly benign skin conditions characterized by rough, scaly patches or growths.

Types

  • Actinic keratosis (solar keratosis) — rough, scaly patches on sun-exposed skin caused by cumulative ultraviolet exposure. These are considered precancerous because a small proportion progress to squamous cell carcinoma.
  • Seborrheic keratosis — very common benign, waxy, "stuck-on" appearing growths that increase with age; they are not precancerous.
  • Keratosis pilaris — small rough bumps, often on the upper arms and thighs, caused by keratin plugging hair follicles; sometimes called "chicken skin."
  • Hyperkeratosis — a thickening of the stratum corneum, which may be a feature of many conditions.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type. Actinic keratoses may be treated with cryotherapy, topical medications such as fluorouracil or imiquimod, chemical peels, or photodynamic therapy, along with sun protection. Seborrheic keratoses usually need no treatment unless irritated or for cosmetic reasons.
