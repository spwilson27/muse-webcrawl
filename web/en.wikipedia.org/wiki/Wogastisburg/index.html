Wogastisburg

Wogastisburg (castrum Vogastisburg) is the name of a stronghold of the Slavic ruler Samo, mentioned in the Frankish Chronicle of Fredegar in connection with the Battle of Wogastisburg in 631/632. At the battle, Samo's Slavs defeated a Frankish army under King Dagobert I, securing the independence of Samo's tribal union.

Location

The location of Wogastisburg has never been conclusively determined. Candidates proposed by historians and archaeologists include sites in Bohemia, Moravia, Slovakia and the Danube region; a frequently discussed candidate is the hillfort at Rubín near Podbořany in the Czech Republic. The name itself is of Germanic origin, suggesting a fortified place on or near the frontier between the Frankish and Slavic worlds.

The battle

In 631, after Slavic (Wendish) merchants under Samo's protection were robbed and killed, Dagobert I sent three armies against Samo's realm. The main Frankish force was ambushed and defeated at Wogastisburg after a three-day battle, and the Franks were forced to flee, leaving behind all their weapons and tents.
