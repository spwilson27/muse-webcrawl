Kansai Electric Power Company

The Kansai Electric Power Company, Inc. (関西電力株式会社, Kansai Denryoku Kabushiki-gaisha; KEPCO) is an electric utility serving the Kansai region of Japan, including Osaka, Kyoto and Kobe. Headquartered in Kita-ku, Osaka, it is one of Japan's largest electric power companies by generating capacity and revenue.

History

KEPCO was established in 1951 during the reorganisation of the Japanese electricity industry under the Allied occupation, which divided the state-controlled system into nine regional private utilities. The company built large hydroelectric stations on the Kurobe River and later invested heavily in thermal and nuclear generation along Wakasa Bay in Fukui Prefecture.

Operations

KEPCO operates hydroelectric, thermal (LNG, coal and oil) and nuclear power plants, including the Mihama, Takahama and Ōi nuclear stations. Following the 2011 Fukushima disaster, its reactors were taken offline; Takahama and Ōi units were restarted after passing new regulatory standards, amid public debate and litigation. The company also supplies gas and telecommunications services through subsidiaries.

Controversies

In 2019 it was revealed that KEPCO executives had received money and gifts from a former deputy mayor of Takahama, a host town for its nuclear plants, leading to resignations of senior management and a governance scandal.
