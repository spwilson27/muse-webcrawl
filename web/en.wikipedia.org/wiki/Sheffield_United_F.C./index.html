Sheffield United Football Club is a professional football club based in Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England. The club competes in English football's league system and plays its home matches at Bramall Lane. Nicknamed "the Blades" after Sheffield's steel industry and the crossed swords on the club crest, United traditionally play in red and white striped shirts with black shorts.

Bramall Lane, the oldest major stadium in the world still hosting professional football.

History

The club was formed in 1889 as an offshoot of Sheffield United Cricket Club, adopting the "United" name that the cricket club had taken. United joined the Football League in 1892, won promotion to the First Division at the first attempt, and won their only league championship in 1897–98 under secretary John Nicholson. The club also won the FA Cup four times, in 1899, 1902, 1915 and 1925.

After decades largely in the top two divisions, United descended to the Fourth Division in 1981 for a single season before climbing back under managers including Dave Bassett, who achieved successive promotions in 1989 and 1990. Under Neil Warnock the club reached the Premier League in 2006 but was relegated after one season amid the controversy over Carlos Tevez at West Ham United. A six-year spell in League One ended with promotion under Chris Wilder in 2017, followed by Championship promotion in 2019 and a ninth-place Premier League finish in 2019–20. Subsequent seasons saw further relegations and promotions between the Premier League and Championship.

Rivalries

United's fiercest rivalry is with city neighbours Sheffield Wednesday, contested as the Steel City derby. The club's ground, Bramall Lane, began as a cricket ground in 1855 and hosted its first football match in 1862; it is recognised as the oldest major football stadium in the world still in professional use.

Honours

  • Football League First Division champions: 1897–98
  • FA Cup winners: 1899, 1902, 1915, 1925
  • Football League Second Division champions: 1952–53
  • EFL League One champions: 2016–17
