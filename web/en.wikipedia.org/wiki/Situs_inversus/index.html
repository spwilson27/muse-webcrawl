Situs inversus

Situs inversus (also called situs transversus or oppositus) is a congenital condition in which the major visceral organs are reversed or mirrored from their normal positions: the heart is on the right side of the chest (dextrocardia), the liver is on the left, the stomach and spleen on the right, and the lobation of the lungs is reversed.

Signs and symptoms

When the reversal is complete (situs inversus totalis) and the organs are otherwise structurally normal, affected individuals are usually healthy and may be unaware of the condition until it is noticed on imaging or examination. The estimated incidence is about 1 in 10,000 people. The condition matters clinically because symptoms of common emergencies can be mirrored — appendicitis, for example, may cause left-sided pain.

Causes

Left–right asymmetry is established in the early embryo by the motion of cilia at the embryonic node. Situs inversus is frequently associated with primary ciliary dyskinesia; about half of people with that condition have situs inversus, a combination known (with sinusitis and bronchiectasis) as Kartagener syndrome. The condition can also occur without ciliary defects.

Related conditions

Partial forms exist: situs inversus with levocardia (heart in the normal position while abdominal organs are reversed) and situs ambiguus (heterotaxy), in which organ arrangement is neither normal nor neatly mirrored; these are more often associated with congenital heart defects.
