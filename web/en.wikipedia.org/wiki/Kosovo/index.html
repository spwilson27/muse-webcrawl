Kosovo

Pristina, capital of Kosovo

Kosovo, officially the Republic of Kosovo, is a partially recognised state in Southeast Europe, situated in the Balkans. It lies at the centre of the Balkan Peninsula, bordered by Serbia to the north and east, North Macedonia to the southeast, Albania to the southwest and Montenegro to the west. Its capital and largest city is Pristina. Kosovo has a population of roughly 1.8 million, predominantly ethnic Albanians, with Serb, Bosniak, Turkish, Roma and other minorities.

History

The territory was the heartland of the medieval Serbian state and the site of the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, before centuries of Ottoman rule. In the twentieth century it became an autonomous province within Serbia in Yugoslavia. Following the Kosovo War of 1998–99 and NATO intervention, the territory was administered by the United Nations under UN Security Council Resolution 1244. On 17 February 2008 Kosovo unilaterally declared independence from Serbia.

Recognition and status

Kosovo's independence has been recognised by a majority of UN member states at various times, including the United States and most EU members, while Serbia, Russia, China and five EU states do not recognise it. In 2010 the International Court of Justice advised that the declaration of independence did not violate international law. Kosovo is a member of the IMF and World Bank but not of the United Nations.

Geography

Kosovo is landlocked and largely mountainous, ringed by the Šar Mountains in the south, the Accursed Mountains (Bjeshkët e Nemuna) in the west—home to its highest peak, Gjeravica (2,656 m)—and the Kopaonik range in the north. Its plains of Dukagjin and Kosovo Field form the main agricultural lowlands.

Economy

Kosovo has one of Europe's youngest populations. Its economy is driven by services, remittances from a large diaspora, mining (notably lignite and the Trepča complex) and growing information-technology and manufacturing sectors. It unilaterally adopted the euro as its currency.
