Copeland–Erdős constant

The Copeland–Erdős constant is the concatenation of "0." with the base 10 representations of the prime numbers in order. Its value is approximately

CCE = 0.235711131719232931374143…

It is named after Arthur Herbert Copeland and Paul Erdős, who proved in 1946 that it is a normal number in base 10. That is, in its decimal expansion, all possible finite strings of digits appear with the expected limiting frequency. The constant was one of the earliest examples of a number explicitly constructed to be normal.

Properties

The constant is formed analogously to Champernowne's constant, which concatenates the positive integers, but here only the primes are used. Copeland and Erdős showed more generally that concatenating the values of a suitable increasing integer sequence yields a normal number provided the number of terms up to x grows faster than xθ for every θ < 1. The Copeland–Erdős constant is known to be irrational; whether it is normal in other bases or transcendental is a separate question in the study of normal numbers.

Related constants

Similar constructions include the concatenation of composite numbers and the Smarandache–Wellin numbers, formed by concatenating the primes before adding the decimal point as integers.
