Flag of Israel

The flag of Israel consists of a blue Star of David (Magen David) centred between two horizontal blue stripes on a white field. It was adopted on 28 October 1948, five months after the establishment of the State of Israel, and is modelled on the tallit, the Jewish prayer shawl, whose white cloth carries blue stripes.

Design and symbolism

The white field and blue stripes recall the tallit worn during prayer; the colour itself — a deep blue sometimes described in Israeli law simply as dark sky-blue — echoes the dye tekhelet of biblical tradition. The six-pointed Star of David in the centre is an ancient Jewish symbol that became the emblem of the Zionist movement. The standard proportions are 8:11.

History

A flag of essentially this design was displayed as early as 1891 at a Zionist celebration in Boston, and it flew over the First Zionist Congress in Basel in 1897, where David Wolffsohn proposed basing the movement's banner on the tallit: "We have a flag — and it is blue and white. The tallith with which we wrap ourselves when we pray: that is our symbol." During the British Mandate period the design served as the de facto flag of the Jewish community of Palestine.

When Israel declared independence in May 1948, the Provisional Council considered more than a hundred designs before confirming the Zionist banner, formally adopting it that October. The flag's status was entrenched in the Flag, Emblem and National Anthem Law of 1949 and given constitutional rank by the 2018 Nation-State Basic Law.

Use

The flag is flown on public buildings, schools and military installations, and displayed widely on Independence Day (Yom Ha'atzmaut). Israeli law regulates its manufacture and dignified handling; desecration of the flag is an offence. Variants include the naval ensign, which places the star within a triangle on a dark blue field, and the air force ensign.
