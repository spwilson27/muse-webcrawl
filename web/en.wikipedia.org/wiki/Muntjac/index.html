Muntjacs, also known as barking deer or rib-faced deer, are small deer of the genus Muntiacus native to South and Southeast Asia. They are known for their short antlers, prominent facial scent glands, and a bark-like alarm call.

Description

Muntjacs are among the smallest deer, standing roughly 40–65 cm at the shoulder. Males have small, unbranched antlers on long pedicles and upper canine teeth that protrude as small tusks. Both sexes have dark facial markings and large preorbital glands.

Species

The genus includes the Indian muntjac (M. muntjak), Reeves's muntjac (M. reevesi), the giant muntjac (M. vuquangensis), and the leaf muntjac, among others. Reeves's muntjac has been introduced to England, where it is now widespread.

Genetics

Muntjacs are notable among mammals for their extreme variation in chromosome number: the Indian muntjac has the lowest recorded diploid number of any mammal, 2n = 6 in females and 7 in males.
