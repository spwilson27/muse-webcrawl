Carol Reed

Sir Carol Reed (30 December 1906 – 25 April 1976) was an English film director and producer, best known for Odd Man Out (1947), The Fallen Idol (1948), The Third Man (1949), and the musical Oliver! (1968), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Director. He was the first British filmmaker to be knighted for his work in film.

Career

Born in Putney, London, the son of actor-producer Herbert Beerbohm Tree, Reed began as an actor and stage director before joining Ealing Studios and later directing thrillers and dramas in the 1930s and during the Second World War, including the documentary The Way Ahead (1944). His collaborations with novelist Graham Greene produced The Fallen Idol and The Third Man, the latter filmed in postwar Vienna with Orson Welles and Joseph Cotten and famous for its zither score by Anton Karas, tilted camera angles, and shadowy cinematography.

Later work

Later films include Trapeze (1956), The Agony and the Ecstasy (1965), and Oliver!, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture. Reed was renowned for his work with child actors and for expressive location filming.
