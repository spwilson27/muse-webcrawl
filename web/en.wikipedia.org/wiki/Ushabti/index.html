Ushabti

An ushabti (also shabti or shawabti) is a small funerary figurine placed in ancient Egyptian tombs to serve the deceased in the afterlife. The name derives from the Egyptian for 'answerer': when the gods called the dead to work in the fields of the afterlife, the ushabti would answer in their place.

Ushabti
Origin: Middle Kingdom Egypt, c. 2000 BCE
Materials: Faience, wood, stone, bronze
Inscription: Book of the Dead, chapter 6

History

Ushabtis evolved from Middle Kingdom wooden models and reserve figures. By the New Kingdom, sets commonly numbered 365 workers — one for each day — plus 36 overseers with whips. Tutankhamun's tomb contained 413. In the Third Intermediate Period some burials had over 1,000, mass-produced in blue-green faience.

Form

Figures are usually mummiform, arms crossed, holding hoes or seed bags, inscribed with the shabti spell (Book of the Dead chapter 6): 'O shabti, if I am called to do any work in the realm of the dead, answer thou for me.'
