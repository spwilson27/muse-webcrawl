The Archery World Cup is an annual series of archery competitions organised by World Archery (formerly FITA). Launched in 2006, the circuit consists of several stages held around the world during the outdoor season, culminating in a World Cup Final for which the highest-ranked archers of the season qualify.

Format

Each season normally comprises three or four stages hosted in different countries, followed by the final. Archers earn ranking points at each stage based on their finishing positions in qualification and elimination matches. The top seven archers in each category over the season, together with a host-nation representative, advance to the World Cup Final, where the champions are decided in head-to-head elimination matches.

Competitions are held for recurve and compound bows in men's and women's individual, team, and mixed team events. Recurve matches use the set system at 70 metres; compound matches are decided on cumulative score at 50 metres.

History

The World Cup was created to give archery a regular, televised international circuit outside the Olympic Games and the World Archery Championships. Stages have been staged in cities including Shanghai, Antalya, Medellín, Wrocław, Paris, and Yankton. The 2020 season was disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic.

South Korea has been the dominant nation in recurve events, while archers such as Brady Ellison of the United States, Sara López of Colombia, and Mike Schloesser of the Netherlands have been among the most successful compound competitors on the circuit.
