Farming

Farming is the practice of cultivating plants and raising livestock as part of agriculture. It is the primary occupation through which humanity produces food, fibre, fuel, and other products. Farming developed independently in several regions of the world beginning around 12,000 years ago during the Neolithic Revolution, and remains the livelihood of a large share of the world's population, especially in developing countries.

Types

Farming systems are commonly classified by their methods and products. Major types include arable farming (growing crops), pastoral farming (raising animals), mixed farming (both), subsistence farming (growing mainly for the farmer's family), commercial farming (growing for sale), intensive and extensive farming, organic farming, and dryland and irrigated farming. Shifting cultivation and nomadic herding are among the oldest forms still practised.

Practices

Farming practices include ploughing and preparing soil, sowing, irrigation, fertilization, pest and weed control, harvesting, and animal husbandry practices such as grazing, breeding, and milking. Twentieth-century developments — mechanization, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and high-yielding varieties during the Green Revolution — transformed productivity, while also generating debate over sustainability, water depletion, soil health, and greenhouse gas emissions.

Modern developments

Contemporary farming increasingly uses precision agriculture technologies such as GPS-guided machinery, drones, soil sensors, and satellite imagery, as well as genetically modified crops in some countries. Agroecology, regenerative agriculture, and vertical farming are promoted as more sustainable alternatives.
