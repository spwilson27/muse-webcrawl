Iridium Communications Inc. is an American company that operates the Iridium satellite constellation, a system of 66 active low-Earth-orbit satellites (plus spares) providing truly global voice and data coverage, including the polar regions and open ocean. It is the only satellite communications network covering the entire Earth.

History

The original Iridium system, conceived at Motorola in the late 1980s, launched commercial service in November 1998 after an investment of roughly $5 billion. Handsets were expensive and bulky, service was costly, and terrestrial mobile networks had expanded faster than forecast; the original company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in August 1999 — one of the largest corporate failures of its era. A new investor group bought the assets for a small fraction of their cost (about $25 million) and relaunched service in 2001, focusing on maritime, aviation, defence, mining, and remote-industry customers rather than competing with consumer mobile phones.

Iridium NEXT

Between 2017 and 2019, the company replaced the entire constellation with Iridium NEXT satellites, launched by SpaceX Falcon 9 rockets from Vandenberg. The new satellites support Iridium Certus broadband and carry hosted payloads for Aireon aircraft tracking and maritime monitoring. In 2023, Iridium announced a partnership to bring satellite connectivity standards for ordinary smartphones, after an earlier collaboration with Qualcomm ended. The constellation is also famous for "Iridium flares" — bright reflections from the first-generation satellites' antennas, a spectacle that ended as the old satellites were deorbited.
