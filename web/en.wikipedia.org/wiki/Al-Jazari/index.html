Badīʿ az-Zaman Abū l-ʿIzz ibn Ismāʿīl ibn ar-Razāz al-Jazarī (1136–1206) was a Muslim polymath, engineer and inventor from Jazira in Mesopotamia who served as chief engineer at the Artuqid court in Diyarbakır. He is best known for his 1206 treatise, The Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices, which describes fifty mechanical inventions — water clocks, automata, pumps and fountains — in unprecedented practical detail, and he is sometimes called the father of robotics and of modern engineering.

Life and work

Al-Jazari was born in 1136 in Cizre (al-Jazira). Like his father before him, he entered the service of the Artuqid sultans of Diyarbakır as an engineer. At the request of the sultan Nasir al-Din Mahmud, he compiled his great book in 1206, the year of his death, describing devices he had built over some twenty-five years at court. The book survives in several illustrated manuscripts and is remarkable for giving dimensions, construction methods and operating principles rather than mere marvels.

Inventions

Among the devices described are monumental water clocks, including the famous elephant clock, in which figures, a phoenix and an elephant form an integrated timing mechanism celebrating multicultural design; candle clocks; and the castle clock of Diyarbakır. His automata include a waitress automaton that serves drinks, a hand-washing automaton with a flushing mechanism anticipating the modern flush toilet, peacock fountains, and a celebrated musical "robot band" of four automated musicians on a boat.

Al-Jazari's practical machinery includes a double-acting reciprocating pump with suction pipes — an early form of the suction pump — a chain pump, and water-raising machines. Historians of technology credit him with early descriptions of mechanisms such as the crankshaft combined with a connecting rod (in some reconstructions debated), conical valves, segmental gears and the use of camshafts, paper models for design, and static balancing of wheels.

Legacy

Writing in a tradition including the Banu Musa brothers and Hellenistic engineers such as Hero of Alexandria, al-Jazari's treatise was copied for centuries and influenced later Islamic and Ottoman engineering. Modern reconstructions of his clocks, notably by science museums in Turkey and elsewhere, have renewed his reputation as one of the outstanding engineers of the medieval world.
