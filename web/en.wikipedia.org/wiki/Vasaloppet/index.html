Vasaloppet

LocationDalarna, Sweden (Sälen to Mora)
Distance90 km (56 mi)
TechniqueClassic
First held1922
Participants~15,800 (main race)

Vasaloppet is an annual long-distance cross-country ski race held in Dalarna, Sweden, over a 90 km course between the village of Sälen and the town of Mora. It is the oldest and one of the largest cross-country ski races in the world, first held in 1922. The race is held on the first Sunday of March and forms the centrepiece of Vasaloppet Week, which features several shorter races and draws tens of thousands of participants.

History and legend

The race commemorates the journey of Gustav Vasa, the future king of Sweden, in the winter of 1520–21. Fleeing Danish troops after the Stockholm Bloodbath and having failed to persuade the men of Mora to rise in rebellion, Gustav skied eastward toward Norway; two skilled skiers from Mora, Lars and Engelbrekt, were sent after him and overtook him near Sälen, bringing him back to lead the uprising that eventually freed Sweden from the Danish-dominated Kalmar Union. The modern race retraces this route in reverse.

The first race in 1922 was won by Ernst Alm, aged 22, the youngest winner in the event's history. The race's motto, I fäders spår för framtids segrar ("In the tracks of our fathers, for the victories of tomorrow"), is displayed on the finish banner in Mora.

The race

The classic-style race begins with a mass start of some 15,800 skiers. Winning times are around three and a half to four hours. Participants who finish within 50 percent of the winner's time receive a medal, and all finishers are served the traditional blueberry soup at checkpoints along the route. The event is part of the Ski Classics series and the Worldloppet federation.
