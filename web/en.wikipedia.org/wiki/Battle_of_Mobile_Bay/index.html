Battle of Mobile Bay

Date5 August 1864
LocationMobile Bay, Alabama
ResultUnion victory; Mobile Bay closed to blockade runners
BelligerentsUnited States (Union) vs. Confederate States
CommandersDavid Farragut vs. Franklin Buchanan

The Battle of Mobile Bay was a naval engagement of the American Civil War fought on 5 August 1864, in which a Union fleet commanded by Rear Admiral David Farragut attacked a Confederate fleet and three forts guarding the entrance to Mobile Bay, Alabama. The victory closed one of the Confederacy's last major Gulf ports to blockade runners.

Background

Mobile was one of the few remaining ports through which the Confederacy imported arms and supplies. The bay's entrance was guarded by Forts Morgan and Gaines and a field of naval mines ("torpedoes"), and defended afloat by the ironclad ram CSS Tennessee and three gunboats under Admiral Franklin Buchanan.

The battle

Farragut attacked in two columns, with four monitors leading. When the monitor USS Tecumseh struck a mine and sank almost instantly, the lead ships hesitated in the minefield under the guns of Fort Morgan. Farragut, lashed in the rigging of his flagship Hartford, gave the order immortalised as "Damn the torpedoes, full speed ahead!" The fleet passed the forts, defeated Buchanan's gunboats, and then surrounded the Tennessee, which fought single-handed until its steering was shot away and Buchanan wounded; it surrendered later that morning.

Aftermath

The forts were isolated and surrendered within weeks; Fort Morgan held out until 23 August. The city of Mobile itself was not captured until April 1865, but the bay was useless to the Confederacy thereafter. Coming alongside the Atlanta campaign, the victory materially boosted Northern morale and President Lincoln's re-election prospects in 1864.
