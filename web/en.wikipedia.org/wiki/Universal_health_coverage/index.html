Universal health coverage

Universal health coverage (UHC) is a health care system in which all residents of a particular country or region are assured access to health care. According to the World Health Organization, UHC means that all people have access to the full range of quality health services they need, when and where they need them, without financial hardship.

Models

Countries achieve UHC through different financing and delivery models. In the Beveridge model (as in the United Kingdom's National Health Service), care is provided by government and funded through taxation. In the Bismarck model (as in Germany and Japan), care is delivered largely by private providers and funded through regulated social health insurance funds. Other countries use mixed systems or national health insurance administered by a single public payer, as in Canada and Taiwan.

Global policy

Universal health coverage is a target of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3.8). The WHO and World Bank track progress using a service coverage index and measures of catastrophic out-of-pocket health spending; global reports estimate that several billion people still lack full coverage of essential services.
