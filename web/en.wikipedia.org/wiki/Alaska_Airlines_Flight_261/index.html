Alaska Airlines Flight 261

Alaska Airlines Flight 261
Date: 31 January 2000
Aircraft: McDonnell Douglas MD-83 (N963AS)
Route: Puerto Vallarta – San Francisco – Seattle
Site: Pacific Ocean near Anacapa Island, California
Fatalities: 88 (all aboard)

Alaska Airlines Flight 261 was a scheduled international passenger flight from Puerto Vallarta, Mexico, to Seattle, Washington, via San Francisco, operated by a McDonnell Douglas MD-83. On 31 January 2000 the aircraft crashed into the Pacific Ocean about 2.7 miles (4.3 km) north of Anacapa Island, California, killing all 88 people aboard.

Accident

During the flight the horizontal stabilizer jammed, and the pilots struggled for over an hour to control pitch while troubleshooting with airline maintenance and attempting to divert to Los Angeles. The stabilizer's jackscrew assembly then failed catastrophically: the worn acme nut threads stripped, releasing the jackscrew and driving the stabilizer to its full nose-down stop. After briefly recovering from a first dive — even flying inverted — the aircraft plunged into the sea at high speed.

Investigation

The US National Transportation Safety Board determined that the probable cause was loss of pitch control from the in-flight failure of the horizontal stabilizer trim system jackscrew assembly, caused by insufficient lubrication of the jackscrew and acme nut by Alaska Airlines. Extended lubrication and inspection intervals, approved by the Federal Aviation Administration, contributed to the wear. The accident led to sweeping changes in MD-80 maintenance oversight; in 2004 the flight crew was posthumously awarded the Air Line Pilots Association Gold Medal for Airmanship.
