Nunchaku

Nunchaku (ヌンチャク)
Type: Flail weapon
Place of origin: Okinawa, Japan
Used in: Okinawan kobudō, karate

The nunchaku (Japanese: ヌンチャク, often called "nunchucks" in English) is a traditional Okinawan martial arts weapon consisting of two sticks, usually made of wood, connected at one end by a short metal chain or a rope. It was popularised worldwide by Bruce Lee in his films of the 1970s, such as Fist of Fury and Enter the Dragon.

Construction

The sticks (kon) are typically about 30 cm (12 inches) long, often octagonal in cross-section, joined by a cord or chain roughly the width of the user's palm. Traditional Okinawan nunchaku use rope tied through holes in the sticks; modern versions often use ball-bearing swivels. Training versions are made of foam or lightweight materials, and freestyle practitioners use glow or fire nunchaku for performance.

Use

In Okinawan kobudō the weapon is wielded with gripping, striking, blocking, trapping and twirling techniques, and is practised through kata such as those of the Ryūkyū kobudō tradition. Although popular accounts link the nunchaku to rice flails, the historical evidence for that origin is weak; similar two-section flails exist in Chinese and Filipino martial arts. The related three-section weapon is the sansetsukon.

Legality

Possession is restricted in several countries and some US states and cities because of its association with street violence in the 1970s; New York's long-standing ban was struck down as unconstitutional in 2018. In the United Kingdom nunchaku are legal to own but offensive-weapon laws restrict carrying them in public, and scenes showing them were cut from films distributed in Britain for many years.
