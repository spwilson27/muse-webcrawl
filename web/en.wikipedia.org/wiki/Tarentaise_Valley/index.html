Tarentaise Valley

The Tarentaise Valley (French: Vallée de la Tarentaise) is a valley of the Isère River in the French Alps, in the department of Savoie. It stretches from Albertville through Moûtiers to the river's source near Val d'Isère, and is world-famous for its concentration of major ski resorts.

Geography

The valley is bounded to the south by the Vanoise massif, home to France's first national park, and to the north by the Beaufortain. Side valleys lead to the resorts of the Trois Vallées (Courchevel, Méribel, Val Thorens), Paradiski (Les Arcs, La Plagne) and Espace Killy (Tignes, Val d'Isère), making the Tarentaise the largest concentration of linked ski areas in the world.

History

The valley takes its name from the Ceutrones, a Celtic tribe, and its Roman centre Darantasia — modern Moûtiers. It belonged to the Kingdom of Sardinia until Savoy was annexed by France in 1860. The region hosted events of the 1992 Winter Olympics centred on Albertville. Agriculture, notably the production of Beaufort cheese from Tarentaise cattle grazing alpine pastures, remains emblematic of the valley.
