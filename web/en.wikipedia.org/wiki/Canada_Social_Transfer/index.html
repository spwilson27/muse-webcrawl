Canada Social Transfer

The Canada Social Transfer (CST) is a federal block transfer payment programme in Canada that supports provincial and territorial governments in funding post-secondary education, social assistance and social services, including early childhood development, early learning and child care. It is one of the major federal transfers to the provinces, alongside the Canada Health Transfer and equalization payments.

History

The CST was created in the 2004 federal budget, when the former Canada Health and Social Transfer (CHST) was split into two separate transfers: the Canada Health Transfer, devoted to health care, and the Canada Social Transfer, devoted to social programmes and education. The split, effective 1 April 2004, was intended to improve the transparency and accountability of federal health funding.

Before the CHST, federal contributions in these areas had flowed through the Canada Assistance Plan (1966) and Established Programs Financing (1977), which were merged into the CHST in 1996 amid federal deficit-reduction measures that substantially reduced transfer levels.

Structure

The CST is a block fund: provinces and territories may spend it according to their own priorities within the broad areas it supports, and it is allocated on an equal per capita basis. Legislation provided for the transfer to grow at an annual escalator of three per cent for a period following the 2007 budget, which also moved the CST to the equal per capita cash basis. The only statutory condition attached to the CST is that provinces maintain the prohibition on minimum residency requirements for social assistance, a condition carried over from the Canada Assistance Plan.

Debate

Because post-secondary education has no separately identified share within the block, student and university groups have periodically called for a dedicated education transfer or for greater transparency about how much of the CST reaches colleges and universities.
