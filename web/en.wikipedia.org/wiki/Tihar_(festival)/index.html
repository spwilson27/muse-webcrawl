Tihar (festival)

Tihar, also known as Deepawali and Yamapanchak, is a five-day Hindu festival celebrated in Nepal and by the Nepali diaspora. It is the second-biggest Nepali festival after Dashain, and parallels the Indian festival of Diwali, but is distinctive for honouring not only gods and humans but also animals — crows, dogs and cows — that share close relationships with people.

The five days

The festival falls in the month of Kartik (October–November). The first day, Kaag Tihar, honours crows, regarded as messengers of Yama, the god of death. The second day, Kukur Tihar, thanks dogs with garlands of marigolds, a red tika on the forehead and special foods, for their loyalty and service. The third day, Gai Tihar, worships cows — symbols of prosperity — in the morning, and in the evening honours Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, with oil lamps, candles and colourful rangoli-like designs called mandalas; this is the main day of the festival, similar to Diwali night. The fourth day sees worship of the ox (Goru Tihar) and, among the Newar community, Mha Puja, the worship of one's own body and soul, which also marks the start of the Nepal Sambat new year. The fifth and final day, Bhai Tika (Kija Puja among Newars), celebrates the bond between brothers and sisters: sisters place a multi-coloured tika on their brothers' foreheads and pray for their long life.

Customs

Homes and streets are lit with butter lamps and electric lights, children go door to door singing deusi and bhailo songs in exchange for money and treats, and families prepare feasts including sel roti, a ring-shaped rice bread. The festival generally occurs around the same time as Diwali in India but follows the Nepali calendar and traditions of its own.
