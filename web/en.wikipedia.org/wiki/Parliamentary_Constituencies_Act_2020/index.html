The Parliamentary Constituencies Act 2020 (c. 25) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom relating to the number and size of parliamentary constituencies. It received royal assent on 14 December 2020.

The Act reversed the previous proposal, set out in the Parliamentary Voting System and Constituencies Act 2011, to reduce the number of MPs from 650 to 600; instead it fixed the size of the House of Commons at 650 constituencies. It also changed the rules and timetable for periodic reviews of constituency boundaries by the four Boundary Commissions of the United Kingdom.

Provisions

  • The number of constituencies is fixed at 650.
  • Each constituency (with limited exceptions, such as island constituencies) must have an electorate within 5% of the UK electoral quota.
  • Boundary reviews are to be held every eight years rather than every five.
  • The requirement for parliamentary approval of boundary changes was replaced by automatic implementation: the commissions' final reports are given effect by Order in Council without a vote in Parliament.
  • The date of the electoral register used for the review was set at 1 December 2020 for the review launched under the Act.

The 2023 boundary review

The Act triggered a fresh boundary review, conducted during 2021–2023 using the December 2020 registers. The Boundary Commissions submitted their final reports in June 2023, and the new boundaries were used at the 2024 general election. Although the total of 650 seats was retained, significant redistribution occurred: England gained seats while Scotland and Wales lost seats, reflecting population changes.
