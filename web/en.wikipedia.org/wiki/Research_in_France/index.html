Science and research in France

Research in France is carried out by a mixture of universities, specialized public research organizations, grandes écoles, and private industry, overseen by the Ministry of Higher Education and Research. France spends roughly 2.2–2.4% of GDP on research and development and ranks among the leading countries by numbers of researchers, scientific publications, and Nobel laureates.

Public research organizations

  • CNRS (National Centre for Scientific Research), founded 1939 — the largest fundamental research organization in France, covering all fields.
  • INSERM — biomedical and health research.
  • INRAE — agriculture, food, and the environment.
  • INRIA — computer science and applied mathematics.
  • CEA — atomic and alternative energies, including defense research.
  • CNES — the French space agency.
  • Ifremer — marine research; IRD — research for development.

Funding

Project funding is channelled substantially through the Agence Nationale de la Recherche (ANR), created in 2005. Much basic research is performed in joint research units (unités mixtes de recherche) shared between universities and the national organizations.
