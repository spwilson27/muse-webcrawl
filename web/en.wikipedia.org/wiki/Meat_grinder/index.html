Meat grinder

A meat grinder (also called a meat mincer in British English) is a kitchen appliance for fine chopping ("mincing") of raw or cooked meat, fish, vegetables, or similar food. It replaces tools such as the mincing knife and is used to produce ground (minced) meat for products such as hamburgers, sausages, meatballs, kibbeh, and pâtés.

History

The first meat grinder was invented in the 19th century by the German revolutionary and food writer Karl Drais (Karl Friedrich Christian Ludwig Freiherr Drais von Sauerbronn), the inventor of the draisine (an early bicycle forerunner). Hand-cranked models that clamp to a table became common household items, and in the later 19th century manufacturers such as the Enterprise Manufacturing Company of Philadelphia mass-produced cast-iron grinders. Powered versions followed for butcher shops and industrial food processing.

Operation

Food is placed in a funnel above a horizontal screw conveyor (auger) driven by a hand crank or electric motor. The screw forces the food forward against a rotating cutting blade and a stationary perforated metal plate (die). The ground product exits through the plate's holes; the fineness of the grind is set by the hole size of the plate, commonly ranging from coarse (about 10–12 mm) to fine (about 3–4 mm). Attachments allow the same machine to stuff sausage casings or form kubbe.

Types

  • Manual grinders – clamp-on cast iron or plastic models for home use.
  • Stand-mixer attachments – grinder heads powered by a stand mixer motor.
  • Standalone electric grinders – countertop appliances for heavier home use.
  • Commercial grinders – large floor or counter machines for butcher shops and processing plants, often with refrigerated hoppers and mincer-mixer combinations.

Safety and sanitation

Because grinding mixes surface bacteria throughout the product, ground meat must be handled and cooked carefully. Grinders should be disassembled and washed thoroughly after use; in commercial settings they are subject to food-safety regulations. Push tools (stompers) keep fingers away from the screw.
