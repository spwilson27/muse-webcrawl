Non-combatant

A non-combatant is a person who is not engaged in fighting during armed conflict. In international humanitarian law the term covers civilians as well as members of armed forces who do not take part in hostilities, such as medical personnel and chaplains, and combatants who are hors de combat (wounded, sick, shipwrecked or surrendered).

Legal status

Under the Geneva Conventions of 1949 and their Additional Protocols, non-combatants must not be made the object of attack and must be treated humanely. Military medical staff and chaplains are protected persons entitled to retain their status when captured, rather than becoming prisoners of war.

Distinction

The principle of distinction requires parties to a conflict to distinguish between combatants and the civilian population. Some categories are debated, including civilians directly participating in hostilities (who lose protection "for such time" as they participate) and unprivileged belligerents.
