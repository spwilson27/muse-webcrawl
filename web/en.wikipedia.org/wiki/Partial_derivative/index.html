Partial derivative

In mathematics, a partial derivative of a function of several variables is its derivative with respect to one of those variables, with the others held constant (as opposed to the total derivative, in which all variables are allowed to vary). Partial derivatives are used in vector calculus and differential geometry.

Notation and definition

The partial derivative of a function f(x, y, …) with respect to x is written ∂f/∂x, fx or ∂xf. The symbol ∂, a stylised letter d, was introduced by Legendre and popularised by Jacobi. Formally it is the limit of the difference quotient as the increment in x alone tends to zero, all other variables fixed.

Examples and applications

The partial derivative ∂V/∂r of the volume of a cone with respect to its radius, holding height constant, gives the rate at which the volume grows as the base widens. Partial derivatives assemble into the gradient vector, the Jacobian matrix and the Hessian, and they underpin the partial differential equations that govern heat flow, wave propagation, fluid dynamics and quantum mechanics — including the heat equation and the Schrödinger equation.

Higher-order derivatives

Partial derivatives can be iterated, giving second-order and higher derivatives. For sufficiently smooth functions, Clairaut's theorem (Schwarz's theorem) states that mixed partial derivatives are equal: ∂²f/∂x∂y = ∂²f/∂y∂x.
