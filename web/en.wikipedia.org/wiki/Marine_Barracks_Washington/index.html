Marine Barracks Washington

Marine Barracks Washington, D.C., also known as 8th and I, is the oldest post in the United States Marine Corps, established in 1801. Located in the Capitol Hill neighbourhood of Washington, D.C., it is the official residence of the Commandant of the Marine Corps and the ceremonial home of units including the Silent Drill Platoon and “The President’s Own” United States Marine Band.

History

President Thomas Jefferson and Lieutenant Colonel William Ward Burrows, the second Commandant, selected the site in 1801 for its proximity to the Navy Yard and the Capitol. The barracks largely survived the burning of Washington in 1814. The Commandant’s House, completed in 1806, is the oldest public building in continuous use in Washington and reputedly the only original building in the city spared by the British in 1814 out of respect for the Marines’ gallantry at Bladensburg.

Ceremonies

The barracks is best known for the Evening Parade, held on Friday nights in summer since 1957, featuring the Marine Band, the Marine Drum and Bugle Corps, and the Silent Drill Platoon’s precision rifle drill. Its Marines also support funerals at Arlington National Cemetery and official ceremonies at the White House.
