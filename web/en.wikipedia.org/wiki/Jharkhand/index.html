Jharkhand (Hindi: झारखण्ड, lit. "the land of forests") is a state in eastern India. It was carved out of the southern part of Bihar on 15 November 2000, becoming the 28th state of the Indian Union. Its capital is Ranchi, and its largest city and industrial hub is Jamshedpur.

The state is bordered by Bihar to the north, West Bengal to the east, Odisha to the south, Chhattisgarh to the west, and Uttar Pradesh to the northwest. Jharkhand accounts for a large share of India's mineral wealth, including major reserves of coal, iron ore, copper, and uranium.

History

The region of the Chota Nagpur Plateau has been home to numerous indigenous (Adivasi) peoples, including the Santhal, Munda, Oraon, and Ho. Demands for a separate tribal state date to the early 20th century, led by figures such as Jaipal Singh Munda and later the Jharkhand Mukti Morcha. The movement culminated in the Bihar Reorganisation Act of 2000.

Geography

Most of Jharkhand lies on the Chota Nagpur Plateau, a heavily forested upland drained by rivers including the Damodar, Subarnarekha, and Koel. Forest cover is around 29% of the state's area, and the state contains important wildlife sanctuaries such as Betla National Park.

Economy

Mining and heavy industry dominate the economy: the steel cities of Jamshedpur (Tata Steel) and Bokaro, coal fields around Dhanbad, and copper and uranium mines at Singhbhum anchor the industrial base, though agriculture employs the majority of the population.

Demographics

Scheduled Tribes make up about 26% of the population. Hindi is the official language; regional and tribal languages include Santali, Mundari, Ho, Kharia, Nagpuri, and Khortha.
