Mikhailovsky Castle

Mikhailovsky Castle
Location: Saint Petersburg, Russia
Built: 1796–1801
Architects: Vincenzo Brenna, Vasily Bazhenov
Also called: Engineers' Castle

Mikhailovsky Castle (Russian: Михай́ловский за́мок), also known as the Engineers' Castle, is a former imperial palace in the historic centre of Saint Petersburg, Russia. It was built between 1796 and 1801 as a residence for Emperor Paul I, who, fearing assassination, designed it as a moated fortress — yet was murdered in his own bedroom there only weeks after moving in.

History

Paul I laid the castle's foundation stone in 1797 on the site of the demolished Summer Palace of Empress Elizabeth. Designed by Vincenzo Brenna and Vasily Bazhenov, the square fortress-palace was surrounded by the Moika and Fontanka rivers and newly dug canals, accessible only by drawbridges. Legend holds that the palace's unusual pink-orange colour came from the shade of a glove dropped by one of the emperor's favourites. Paul moved in on 1 February 1801; on the night of 23–24 March 1801 he was strangled by a group of officers in his bedroom.

Later use

The imperial family abandoned the castle, and in 1823 it was given to the Main Engineering School of the Russian army, from which it takes its alternative name; the writer Fyodor Dostoevsky studied there as a cadet. After decades of Soviet institutional use, the castle was transferred to the State Russian Museum in the 1990s and restored, recovering Paul's state apartments. It now hosts exhibitions of Russian portraiture and sculpture.
