Tumen River

The Tumen River (Chinese: 图们江; Korean: 두만강) is a 521-kilometre (324 mi) river that forms part of the boundary between China, North Korea, and Russia. It rises on the slopes of Mount Paektu on the China–North Korea border and flows northeast and then southeast into the Sea of Japan.

Geography

For most of its course the Tumen forms the border between China's Jilin Province and North Korea's North Hamgyong and Rason regions. In its final 17 kilometres it separates North Korea from Russia's Primorsky Krai. The river is shallow and freezes in winter. Its estuary lies near the tripoint of the three countries, where the Sino-Russian Friendship Bridge and the Korea–Russia Friendship Bridge cross it.

Significance

The Tumen is one of the principal crossing routes used by North Korean defectors travelling into China. The river basin is also the focus of the Greater Tumen Initiative, a regional development programme involving China, Russia, Mongolia, and the Koreas, concerned with trade access to the Sea of Japan. Flooding of the river, as in 2016, has caused severe damage in North Hamgyong Province.
