BALUBAKAT (Association des Baluba du Katanga, "Association of the Baluba of Katanga") was a political association and party in the late Belgian Congo and the independent Republic of the Congo, founded in 1957 by Jason Sendwe to represent the Luba (Baluba) people of northern Katanga Province.

Background

Katanga, rich in copper and other minerals, was ethnically and politically divided. In the south, Moïse Tshombe's CONAKAT party drew support from peoples such as the Lunda and Yeke and favoured close ties with the Belgian mining interests of the Union Minière. BALUBAKAT, representing the Baluba of the north, became CONAKAT's principal rival, positioning itself instead within the wider Congolese nationalist movement and in alliance with Patrice Lumumba's Mouvement National Congolais.

Congo Crisis

When Tshombe declared Katanga's secession days after Congolese independence in July 1960, BALUBAKAT refused to recognise the secessionist state. Sendwe served as a deputy prime minister in Lumumba's national government, and BALUBAKAT organised resistance in northern Katanga, where fighting between Baluba forces and the Katangese gendarmerie became one of the bloodiest threads of the Congo Crisis. The party's youth wing, the Jeunesse Balubakat, operated as a militia in the north. Sendwe was murdered in June 1964, amid the turmoil of the Simba rebellion, a blow from which the association never fully recovered.

Legacy

BALUBAKAT is remembered as the principal vehicle of Baluba political identity in Katanga and as a key opponent, from within the province itself, of the Katangese secession of 1960–1963.
