United Airlines Flight 328 was a scheduled domestic passenger flight from Denver International Airport to Honolulu, Hawaii, which on 20 February 2021 suffered an uncontained failure of its right Pratt & Whitney PW4077 engine shortly after departure. Parts of the engine inlet and fan blades fell over the suburb of Broomfield, Colorado; the crew declared an emergency and returned to Denver, landing safely about 23 minutes after takeoff. None of the 241 people on board, and no one on the ground, was injured.

Incident

Minutes after departure, passengers heard a loud bang and saw the right engine on fire with its cowling gone. The pilots ran checklists, dumped no fuel (the 777 can land overweight), and made a single-engine ILS approach to runway 26. A large section of the inlet cowl landed in front of a house in Broomfield, an image that was widely shared; other debris scattered across neighbourhoods and a park, damaging vehicles and property but hurting no one.

Investigation and aftermath

The National Transportation Safety Board found that a fan blade had fractured from metal fatigue, in an area where cracks could be missed between inspections; a second blade then broke and the failure tore away the inlet and cowl structures. United removed its PW4000-powered 777s from service, the FAA ordered enhanced inspections using thermal acoustic imaging, and Boeing recommended suspending operations of the type worldwide until inspections were complete. The NTSB's final report (2023) also criticised the design of the cowling and the adequacy of fan-blade inspection intervals. The aircraft, N772UA — the same jet involved in a similar uncontained failure as United Flight 1175 in 2018 over the Pacific — was repaired and returned to service.
