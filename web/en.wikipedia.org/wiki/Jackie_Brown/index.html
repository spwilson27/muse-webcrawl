Jackie Brown is a 1997 American crime film written and directed by Quentin Tarantino, adapted from Elmore Leonard's 1992 novel Rum Punch. It stars Pam Grier as a middle-aged flight attendant who smuggles cash for an arms dealer (Samuel L. Jackson) and, caught between federal agents and the dealer, schemes with a bail bondsman (Robert Forster) to steal a half-million dollars. The film pays homage to 1970s blaxploitation cinema, particularly Grier's own films Coffy (1973) and Foxy Brown (1974).

Plot

Jackie Brown, a flight attendant for a low-rent Mexican airline, supplements her income by smuggling money from Mexico for Los Angeles arms dealer Ordell Robbie. When ATF agent Ray Nicolette and LAPD detective Mark Dargus catch her with cash and cocaine, she faces prison unless she cooperates. Ordell arranges bail through bondsman Max Cherry, who falls for Jackie. Fearing Ordell will have her killed, Jackie proposes to help the police in a staged money transfer while secretly conspiring with Max to divert the full $500,000. The climactic exchange unfolds at the Del Amo Fashion Center, shown from multiple characters' viewpoints, ending with Jackie escaping with the money and Ordell dead at the agents' hands.

Production

Tarantino changed the novel's white protagonist, Jackie Burke, into Jackie Brown, writing the role specifically for Grier, and set the story in Los Angeles's South Bay, where he grew up. The soundtrack draws heavily on 1970s soul and funk, opening with Bobby Womack's "Across 110th Street." Robert Forster's career was revived by his performance; he was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor.

Reception

The film received positive reviews, with praise for its performances, dialogue, and comparatively restrained, character-driven tone, though some critics expecting a repeat of Pulp Fiction found it slow. It grossed $74.7 million against a $15 million budget. Its reputation has grown substantially since release, and it is frequently cited among Tarantino's finest and most mature works. Grier and Jackson received Golden Globe nominations.
