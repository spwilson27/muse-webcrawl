Treaty of Stralsund (1370)

The Treaty of Stralsund was concluded on 24 May 1370 between the Hanseatic League and King Valdemar IV of Denmark. It ended the Second Danish–Hanseatic War and marked the high point of the League's power in northern Europe.

Background

Valdemar IV's conquests, including the seizure of Gotland and the sack of Visby in 1361, threatened Hanseatic trade in the Baltic. The cities of the Cologne Confederation (1367) allied with Sweden and Mecklenburg and defeated Denmark in the ensuing war.

Terms

Denmark ceded to the League the fortresses of Helsingborg, Malmö, Skanör, and Falsterbo for fifteen years, guaranteeing the Hanseatic towns two-thirds of the revenues of Scania for that period. The League secured free trade throughout Denmark, control of the vital Scanian herring markets, and even a voice in the succession to the Danish throne: no new king could be crowned without the League's consent until the terms expired.

Significance

The Treaty of Stralsund confirmed the Hanseatic League as the dominant commercial and political power in the Baltic Sea region, a position it would hold until the rise of stronger territorial states in the following century.
