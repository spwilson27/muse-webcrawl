The River Nith is a river in south-west Scotland. At about 112 kilometres (70 mi), it is the seventh-longest river in Scotland. It rises on Prickeny Hill near Dalmellington in East Ayrshire and flows generally south-east through Nithsdale, passing New Cumnock, Kirkconnel, Sanquhar, Thornhill, and Dumfries, before emptying into the Solway Firth south of Dumfries near Glencaple.

Course and features

The upper Nith runs through former coal-mining country in Ayrshire and upper Nithsdale. In its middle reaches it passes Drumlanrig Castle, seat of the Duke of Buccleuch, and the scenic gorge at Drumlanrig. At Dumfries the river is crossed by several bridges, including the 15th-century Devorgilla Bridge, one of Scotland's oldest surviving bridges. Below Dumfries the river becomes tidal, with wide mudflats forming part of the Caerlaverock wetlands.

Fishing and wildlife

The Nith is one of Scotland's best-known salmon and sea trout rivers, with a long season and historically large catches; it is also noted for grayling fishing in winter. The estuary and adjacent saltmarshes support internationally important populations of wintering wildfowl, including barnacle geese, protected within the Caerlaverock National Nature Reserve and the Solway Firth designations.

Flooding

Dumfries has a long history of flooding from the Nith, with the Whitesands area inundated regularly; flood defence schemes for the town have been debated and developed over many years.
