Kondō Isami

Born: 1834, Kami-Ishihara, Musashi Province

Died: 1868, Itabashi, Edo

Role: Commander of the Shinsengumi

Kondō Isami (近藤 勇, November 9, 1834 – May 17, 1868) was a Japanese swordsman and the commander of the Shinsengumi, the special police force organised by the Tokugawa shogunate in Kyoto during the Bakumatsu period.

Life

Born to a farming family in Musashi, Kondō became head master of the Tennen Rishin-ryū school of swordsmanship. In 1863 he travelled to Kyoto with his students, forming the core of the Shinsengumi under the command of the Aizu domain. He led the corps through events such as the Ikedaya incident of 1864.

Death and legacy

After the shogunate's defeat at the Battle of Toba–Fushimi in 1868, Kondō surrendered to imperial forces and was executed by beheading at Itabashi. He remains a popular figure in Japanese historical fiction about the Shinsengumi.
