Clifford Jordan

Clifford Laconia Jordan (September 2, 1931 – March 27, 1993) was an American jazz tenor saxophonist known for his warm tone, fluent bebop vocabulary, and long career as both sideman and bandleader.

Early life

Jordan was born in Chicago and grew up playing with fellow Chicagoans including Johnny Griffin and John Gilmore. He studied with Walter Dyett at DuSable High School, a noted training ground for jazz musicians.

Career

After moving to New York in 1957, Jordan recorded three albums for Blue Note, including Blowing In from Chicago with John Gilmore. He played with Horace Silver, J. J. Johnson, Kenny Dorham, and Max Roach, and joined Charles Mingus's band in 1964, appearing on recordings alongside Eric Dolphy. In the late 1960s he toured Africa for the U.S. State Department and spent time in Europe. In the 1970s he recorded for the artist-run Strata-East label, including the well-regarded Glass Bead Games (1973). Later he worked with Art Farmer, led his own big band, and recorded a series of albums for Mapleshade Records.

Style

Jordan's playing combined a rich, dark tenor sound with melodic inventiveness that kept him in demand across hard bop and more open-ended settings, and he was also an accomplished composer.
