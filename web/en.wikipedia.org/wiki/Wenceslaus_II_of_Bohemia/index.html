Wenceslaus II of Bohemia

Wenceslaus II (Czech: Václav II; 27 September 1271 – 21 June 1305) was King of Bohemia from 1278 to 1305, King of Poland from 1300 to 1305, and briefly (1301–1304) King of Hungary as Ladislaus V. A member of the Přemyslid dynasty, he presided over the apex of Bohemian power in Central Europe, his reign marked by economic prosperity, territorial expansion and a flourishing of culture.

Life

Wenceslaus succeeded his father Ottokar II, who was killed at the Battle on the Marchfeld in 1278 when Wenceslaus was six. After a regency, he assumed personal rule in 1283. His reign coincided with the discovery of rich silver deposits at Kutná Hora, which made Bohemia one of the wealthiest kingdoms in Europe and funded his coinage reform, the Prague groschen (1300), which became a widely used currency in Central Europe. He founded the city of Plzeň in 1295.

Following the extinction of the Árpád dynasty, Wenceslaus's son was elected king of Hungary, and Wenceslaus himself was crowned King of Poland at Gniezno in 1300. He died in Prague in 1305, aged 33, and was succeeded by his son Wenceslaus III, whose murder in 1306 ended the male line of the Přemyslid dynasty.
