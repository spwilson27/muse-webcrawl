Baltic Fleet


Baltic Fleet ships during a naval review.

The Baltic Fleet (Russian: Балтийский флот, Baltiyskiy flot) is the fleet of the Russian Navy in the Baltic Sea. Founded by Peter the Great in 1703 during the Great Northern War, it is the oldest of Russia's fleets and is twice decorated with the Order of the Red Banner. Its headquarters are in Kaliningrad, with its main base at Baltiysk and facilities at Kronstadt near Saint Petersburg.

History

Peter the Great ordered the first ships built for the Baltic in 1702–03, and on 18 May 1703 Russian boats captured two Swedish vessels at the mouth of the Neva — a date still celebrated as the fleet's birthday. The fortress of Kronstadt was founded in 1704 to guard the approaches to the new capital, Saint Petersburg. The fleet grew to defeat Sweden's navy in the Great Northern War, winning at Gangut (1714) and Grengam (1720).

In the nineteenth century the fleet adopted steam and armoured ships and defended the Gulf of Finland during the Crimean War. During the Russo-Japanese War, much of the Baltic Fleet was re-formed as the Second Pacific Squadron and sailed around the world under Admiral Zinovy Rozhestvensky, only to be annihilated at the Battle of Tsushima in May 1905.

Twentieth century

Baltic sailors played a central role in the revolutions of 1917; the cruiser Aurora's blank shot signalled the assault on the Winter Palace. In 1918 the fleet made the "Ice Cruise" from Helsinki to Kronstadt to escape capture by German forces. In the Second World War the fleet endured the siege of Leningrad, providing artillery support, while its submarines and aircraft campaigned in the Baltic despite heavy losses to mines.

Present day

After 1991 the fleet declined in size, and its assets were divided between the Kaliningrad exclave and the Saint Petersburg area. Modern units include Steregushchiy-class corvettes, Buyan-M missile corvettes armed with Kalibr cruise missiles, Karakurt-class corvettes, and Kilo-class (Project 636/877) diesel-electric submarines, together with naval aviation and coastal missile troops. The fleet's strategic importance rose again amid tensions with NATO, whose Baltic members now surround the sea following the accession of Finland (2023) and Sweden (2024).
