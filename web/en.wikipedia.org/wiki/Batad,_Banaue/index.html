Batad is a barangay (village) in the municipality of Banaue, Ifugao province, in the Cordillera mountains of northern Luzon, Philippines. It is world-famous for its amphitheatre-like rice terraces, carved into the mountainside by the Ifugao people over some two thousand years and often called an "amphitheatre of rice terraces".

The terraces

The Batad terraces form a near-semicircular bowl above the village, irrigated by mountain springs through an ancient system of channels. They are one of the five terrace clusters inscribed in 1995 on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, the first property in the "cultural landscape" category. The nearby Tappiya Falls reward hikers who descend into the village, which has no road access and is reached on foot.

Culture

Residents maintain traditional Ifugao wooden houses, granaries and rituals tied to the rice cycle. Tourism, while important, coexists with continuing terrace farming; abandonment and erosion of some terraces remain conservation concerns.
