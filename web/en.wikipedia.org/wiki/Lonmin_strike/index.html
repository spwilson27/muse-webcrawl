Lonmin strike

Marikana strike (2012)
Date: 10 August – 20 September 2012
Location: Marikana, North West, South Africa
Company: Lonmin platinum mine
Deaths: 44 (incl. 34 shot by police on 16 August)

The Lonmin strike was a wildcat strike at the Marikana platinum mine operated by Lonmin in South Africa's North West province in August and September 2012. It is best known for the Marikana massacre of 16 August 2012, when members of the South African Police Service shot and killed 34 striking miners — the most lethal use of force by South African security forces against civilians since the end of apartheid.

Background and events

Rock-drill operators, many of them migrant workers living in informal settlements, struck from 10 August demanding a monthly wage of R12,500, bypassing the established National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) in favour of the rival Association of Mineworkers and Construction Union (AMCU). Violence in the days before the massacre killed ten people, including two police officers and two security guards. On 16 August police opened fire on strikers gathered at a koppie near the mine; 34 miners were killed and 78 wounded. The strike ended on 18–20 September with a settlement granting wage increases of up to 22 per cent.

Aftermath

The massacre provoked national outrage and comparisons with Sharpeville and Soweto. President Jacob Zuma appointed the Farlam Commission of Inquiry, whose 2015 report criticised the police operation and Lonmin's failure to address workers' housing and social obligations. The strike helped reshape South African labour politics, weakened the NUM, and became a defining event in debates about inequality, policing and the post-apartheid social contract.
