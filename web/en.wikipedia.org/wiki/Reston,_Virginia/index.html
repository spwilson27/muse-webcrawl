Reston, Virginia

Reston is a census-designated place in Fairfax County, Virginia, United States, within the Washington metropolitan area. Founded in 1964 by real estate developer Robert E. Simon Jr., Reston was one of the first modern post–World War II planned communities in the United States and a landmark of the new town movement. Simon named the community using his initials (Robert E. Simon → "Reston").

History

Simon purchased more than 6,700 acres of Fairfax County land in 1961 with proceeds from the sale of New York's Carnegie Hall, which his family had owned. His vision drew on ideas from garden city planning: a community of distinct villages, each with its own center, schools, and amenities, where residents could live, work, and play without leaving the community. Reston was also notable for being racially integrated from its founding, at a time when segregation remained common in Virginia housing.

Financial difficulties led Simon to sell Reston to Gulf Oil in 1967, though the community's master plan continued to guide development. Reston Town Center, a high-density mixed-use downtown, opened in 1990 and has since expanded substantially. The Washington Metro's Silver Line reached Reston in 2014 with the Wiehle–Reston East station, followed by the Reston Town Center station in 2022.

Geography and demographics

Reston covers roughly 15.7 square miles in northwestern Fairfax County, about 18 miles west of downtown Washington, D.C. Its population was about 63,000 at the 2020 census. The community is organized into village clusters including Lake Anne (the original village), Hunters Woods, South Lakes, North Point, and Tall Oaks. Lake Anne Village Center, with its modernist plaza on a man-made lake, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Economy

Reston sits within the Dulles Technology Corridor and is home to numerous technology and government-contracting firms. Major employers have included Leidos (headquartered in Reston), Google, Microsoft, Oracle, and Rolls-Royce North America. The United States Geological Survey is headquartered in Reston.
