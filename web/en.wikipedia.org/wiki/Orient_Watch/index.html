Orient Watch

Orient Watch Co., Ltd. (オリエント時計株式会社) is a Japanese watch manufacturer founded in 1950 in Tokyo. Since 2017 it has been a wholly owned subsidiary of Seiko Epson Corporation. Orient is one of Japan's major watch brands and is unusual among large manufacturers in producing its mechanical movements entirely in-house; roughly, mechanical watches form the core of its catalogue, sold in more than 70 countries.

History

The company's origins lie in a wholesale watch shop founded by Shogoro Yoshida in Ueno, Tokyo, in 1901. The Orient Watch Company was established on 13 July 1950, and its first watch, the Orient Star, appeared in 1951, followed by popular models such as the Dynamic (1956) and the Grand Prix 100 (1964). Orient developed calibres such as the 46 series (introduced 1971), which remained in production for decades and underpinned the brand's reputation for affordable, robust automatic watches. Epson took a controlling stake in 2001, and Orient was fully integrated as a subsidiary in 2017; its watches are produced at facilities in Akita Prefecture, Japan.

Products

Orient's best-known lines include the Orient Star premium range, the Mako and Ray dive watches, the dressy Bambino, and the versatile Kamasu. Signature design features include in-house automatic movements with hacking and hand-winding in later calibres (such as the F69 family), power-reserve indicators on higher models, and the brand's lion crest logo.
