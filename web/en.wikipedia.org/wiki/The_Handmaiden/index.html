The Handmaiden

The Handmaiden (Korean: 아가씨, Agassi, lit. "The Lady") is a 2016 South Korean erotic psychological thriller film directed by Park Chan-wook, starring Kim Min-hee, Kim Tae-ri, Ha Jung-woo and Cho Jin-woong. Inspired by Sarah Waters's 2002 novel Fingersmith, it transposes the story from Victorian London to Korea and Manchuria under Japanese colonial rule in the 1930s.

Plot

In three parts retelling events from shifting perspectives, the film follows Sook-hee, a pickpocket hired by a con man posing as "Count Fujiwara" to serve as handmaiden to the Japanese heiress Hideko. The count plans to seduce Hideko, marry her, commit her to an asylum and steal her fortune. Hideko, meanwhile, is dominated by her uncle Kouzuki, a Korean collaborator and collector of erotic literature who forces her to give readings to prospective buyers. Unexpectedly, Sook-hee and Hideko fall in love, and each woman's concealed plan is progressively overturned; together they engineer an escape that destroys both of their tormentors.

Reception

The film premiered in competition at the 2016 Cannes Film Festival, where it won praise for its direction, production design and performances; production designer Ryu Seong-hee won the Vulcan Award of the Technical Artist. At the 71st British Academy Film Awards it won Best Film Not in the English Language, the first Korean film to do so. It holds a near-unanimous positive critical consensus and was a box-office success internationally.
