The Finnish People's Delegation (Finnish: Suomen kansanvaltuuskunta) was the government of the Finnish Socialist Workers' Republic, established by a section of the Social Democratic Party of Finland on 28 January 1918 during the Finnish Civil War. It governed the "Red Finland" in the south of the country from Helsinki, while the rival Senate of the "Whites" sat in Vaasa.

History

The Delegation was formed as Finnish socialists seized power in Helsinki at the start of the civil war. Chaired by Kullervo Manner, it included figures such as Otto Wille Kuusinen and Yrjö Sirola. It proclaimed a socialist republic, signed a treaty of friendship with Soviet Russia in March 1918, and proposed a democratic constitution drafted by Kuusinen, but its reforms were overtaken by the war. After the Whites, aided by German troops, captured Helsinki in April 1918, the Delegation fled to Vyborg and then to Soviet Russia, where it dissolved; many of its members went on to found the Communist Party of Finland in Moscow in August 1918.
