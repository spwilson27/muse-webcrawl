Disney Parks

Disney Parks is one of the three major business segments of The Walt Disney Company, and the part of the company's Disney Experiences division responsible for its theme parks and resorts worldwide. The first Disney park, Disneyland in Anaheim, California, opened on 17 July 1955, personally overseen by Walt Disney.

The Tokyo resort is owned and operated by the Oriental Land Company under licence; the others are wholly or partly owned by Disney. Beyond theme parks, the division's wider portfolio has included Disney Cruise Line, Disney Vacation Club, Adventures by Disney guided tours, and the design and engineering arm Walt Disney Imagineering.

History

Walt Disney conceived Disneyland after visiting amusement parks with his daughters and finding them dirty and unappealing; he wanted a clean, family-oriented park where parents and children could ride attractions together. Its success led to the much larger Florida Project, announced days before his death in 1966, which opened as Walt Disney World in 1971. International expansion began with Tokyo Disneyland in 1983 and Euro Disneyland (now Disneyland Paris) in 1992.

The parks division was long known as Walt Disney Parks and Resorts, renamed Disney Parks, Experiences and Products in 2018 and then folded into Disney Experiences in 2023. Disney parks collectively draw well over 100 million visitors per year, making the company the world's most visited theme-park operator.
