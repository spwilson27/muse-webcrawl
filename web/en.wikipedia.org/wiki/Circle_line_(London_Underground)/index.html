The Circle line is a line of the London Underground, coloured yellow on the Tube map. It runs in a spiral-shaped route — a loop around central London with a western arm — serving 36 stations over 27 kilometres of mostly sub-surface track, which it shares with the District, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan lines.

History

The "inner circle" was completed in 1884 by the Metropolitan and District railways, rival companies whose trains ran the same ring in opposite directions. For over a century trains circulated continuously around the loop. On 13 December 2009 the line was restructured: services were extended over the former Hammersmith & City route to Hammersmith, and the closed loop became a "teacup" spiral in which trains terminate at Edgware Road, improving reliability.

Services and stock

Trains run about every ten minutes on each section. The line is operated by seven-car S7 Stock trains, introduced in the 2010s as part of the sub-surface fleet renewal, maintained at Hammersmith and Neasden depots.
