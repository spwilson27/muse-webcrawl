High-definition optical disc format war

The high-definition optical disc format war was a competition between the Blu-ray Disc and HD DVD formats to become the standard for high-definition home video distribution, lasting from 2006 until 2008. It is frequently compared to the earlier VHS versus Betamax videotape war.

The formats

Blu-ray, backed by Sony and the Blu-ray Disc Association, offered greater capacity (25 GB per layer versus 15 GB) and stronger studio support, and was built into every PlayStation 3. HD DVD, developed by Toshiba and the DVD Forum, was cheaper to manufacture and enjoyed support from Microsoft, which sold an HD DVD add-on drive for the Xbox 360. Studios initially split their allegiances: Warner Bros., Paramount and Universal supported HD DVD at various points, while Disney, Fox and Sony Pictures backed Blu-ray exclusively.

Resolution

The war ended swiftly in early 2008. On 4 January 2008, Warner Bros. announced it would release exclusively on Blu-ray; retailer and rental companies (Blockbuster, Netflix, Walmart, Best Buy) followed by dropping HD DVD. On 19 February 2008, Toshiba announced it would discontinue HD DVD players and recorders, conceding the market to Blu-ray.
