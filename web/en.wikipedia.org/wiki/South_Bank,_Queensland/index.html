South Bank, Queensland

South Bank is a cultural, social, educational and recreational precinct in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia. The precinct is located in the suburb of South Brisbane, on the southern bank of the Brisbane River, directly opposite the Brisbane central business district. It stretches from the Queensland University of Technology at Gardens Point through to the Maritime Museum.

History

South Bank was originally a meeting place for the traditional landowners, the Turrbal and Jagera people. In the 1840s it became the central point of early European settlement, but after the floods of 1893 the central business district moved to the northern bank of the river. The area later became a mixture of wharves, dry docks, and industry.

The precinct was transformed for World Expo 88, a major event celebrating Australia's bicentenary. After the Expo, the people of Brisbane voted to keep the parklands, and the South Bank Parklands opened in 1992. Development proceeded in stages, adding streets of restaurants and cafes, and eventually a major cultural district.

Attractions

  • South Bank Parklands — 17.5 hectares of rainforest, grassed areas, and the Streets Beach, a man-made swimming beach with a lagoon, in the middle of the city.
  • Queensland Cultural Centre — home to the Queensland Performing Arts Centre (QPAC), the Queensland Museum and Sciencentre, the State Library of Queensland, and the Queensland Art Gallery and Gallery of Modern Art (QAGOMA).
  • The Wheel of Brisbane — a 60-metre observation wheel.
  • Nepal Peace Pagoda — a legacy structure from World Expo 88.
  • South Bank Piazza and the Arbour, a kilometre-long walkway covered in bougainvillea.

The precinct also hosts the Brisbane Convention & Exhibition Centre, Griffith University's Queensland Conservatorium and College of Art, and markets on weekends. It was affected by the 2010–11 Queensland floods, after which damaged sections were restored and redeveloped.
