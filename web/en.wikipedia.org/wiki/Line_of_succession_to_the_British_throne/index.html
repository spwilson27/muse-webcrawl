Line of succession to the British throne

The line of succession to the British throne is the sequence of individuals entitled to inherit the throne of the United Kingdom. It is determined by descent, legitimacy, religion and sex, under rules set by the Bill of Rights 1689, the Act of Settlement 1701 and the Succession to the Crown Act 2013. Succession is restricted to the Protestant descendants of Sophia, Electress of Hanover (1630–1714), the nearest Protestant relative of Queen Anne.

Rules

Under the 2013 Act — agreed by all Commonwealth realms at Perth in 2011 — male-preference primogeniture was replaced by absolute primogeniture for persons born after 28 October 2011, so birth order alone determines the place of children regardless of sex. The bar on a person married to a Roman Catholic was removed, though the sovereign must still be in communion with the Church of England and cannot be a Roman Catholic. Six persons nearest the throne require the sovereign's consent to marry (replacing the Royal Marriages Act 1772, which had covered all descendants of George II).

The full line extends through thousands of eligible descendants of Electress Sophia worldwide.

Commonwealth realms

The same person is sovereign of the other Commonwealth realms, and changes to succession law require their agreement under the Statute of Westminster convention; the 2013 reforms were legislated across the realms in 2013–2015.
