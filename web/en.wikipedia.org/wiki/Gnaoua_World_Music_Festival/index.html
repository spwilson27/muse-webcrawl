Gnaoua World Music Festival


A maâlem performing at the festival in Essaouira.

The Gnaoua World Music Festival (Festival Gnaoua et Musiques du Monde) is an annual music festival held in Essaouira, Morocco, celebrating the music and culture of the Gnaoua (Gnawa) people and promoting fusions between Gnaoua maâlems (master musicians) and performers from around the world. First held in 1998, it takes place each year in early summer and is one of Morocco's most prominent cultural events.

Background

Gnaoua music derives from the traditions of West African communities brought to Morocco, blending sub-Saharan African rhythms, Islamic spirituality and ritual healing practices. Its instruments include the deep-toned three-stringed bass lute called the guembri (or hajhuj), iron double castanets known as qraqeb, and large drums. Night-long ritual ceremonies called lilas use music, incense, colour and dance to invoke spirits (mluk). The festival was founded to preserve and promote this heritage, which UNESCO recognised in 2019 by inscribing Gnaoua culture on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

The festival

Across several days, the festival stages free open-air concerts on Essaouira's main stages — including the Moulay Hassan Square and the beach stage — alongside intimate acoustic performances, fusions and late-night lilas in traditional venues. Gnaoua masters such as Mustapha Baqbou, Hassan Hakmoun (long resident in the United States) and Hamid El Kasri have performed alongside jazz, reggae, rock and world-music artists including Randy Weston, Pat Metheny, Marcus Miller and Tinariwen. The programme also features the Human Rights Forum (Forum des droits de l'Homme), a parallel debating event. Attendance is typically estimated in the hundreds of thousands, transforming the small Atlantic port town for the festival weekend.
