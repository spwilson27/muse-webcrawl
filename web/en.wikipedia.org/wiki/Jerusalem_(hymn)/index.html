Jerusalem (hymn)

"Jerusalem" is a famous English hymn whose words come from a poem by William Blake, written in 1804 as part of the preface to his epic Milton: A Poem, and set to music by Sir Hubert Parry in 1916. It is popularly known by its opening words, "And did those feet in ancient time".

Text and origins

Blake's poem draws on the legend that Jesus, as a young man, visited England with Joseph of Arimathea. It asks whether heaven was present in ancient England and contrasts the "dark Satanic Mills" of the Industrial Revolution with a vision of building Jerusalem — a just and holy society — "in England's green and pleasant land."

Parry's setting and popularity

Parry composed the music in 1916 at the request of the Fight for Right campaign; Edward Elgar later orchestrated it. The hymn was adopted by the suffragettes, the Women's Institute (with which it remains closely associated), and the Labour Party. It is regularly sung at the Last Night of the Proms, at English sporting events including cricket and rugby league, and is frequently proposed as a national anthem for England.
