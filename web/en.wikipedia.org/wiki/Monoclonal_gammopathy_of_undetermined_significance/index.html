Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance

Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) is a plasma cell dyscrasia in which an abnormal monoclonal immunoglobulin (M protein) is present in the blood or urine without signs of the malignancies it can precede, such as multiple myeloma. It is a premalignant, asymptomatic condition, most often discovered incidentally during laboratory testing for other reasons.

Diagnostic criteria

MGUS is defined by three criteria:

  • Serum monoclonal protein less than 3 g/dL;
  • Clonal bone marrow plasma cells less than 10%; and
  • Absence of end-organ damage attributable to the plasma cell disorder (no myeloma-defining events such as hypercalcaemia, renal failure, anaemia, or bone lesions — the "CRAB" features).

Subtypes include non-IgM MGUS (most common), IgM MGUS (which may progress to Waldenström macroglobulinemia), and light-chain MGUS, in which only abnormal free light chains are produced.

Epidemiology and progression

MGUS affects about 3% of people over the age of 50 and becomes more common with age; it is more frequent in men and in people of African ancestry. It progresses to multiple myeloma or a related malignancy at a rate of roughly 1% per year, and this risk persists indefinitely. Risk stratification uses the size and type of the M protein and the serum free light-chain ratio to identify patients needing closer follow-up.

Management

No treatment is given for MGUS itself. Management consists of periodic monitoring of the M protein and blood counts to detect progression, with the frequency determined by risk category. MGUS is also associated with some non-malignant conditions, including peripheral neuropathy and an increased risk of fractures and thrombosis.
