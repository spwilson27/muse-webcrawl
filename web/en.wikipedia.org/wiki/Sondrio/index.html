Sondrio

Sondrio (Italian: [ˈsondrjo]; Romansh: Sunder; Lombard: Sùndri) is an Italian city and comune in Lombardy, located in the heart of the Valtellina valley. As of 2023, Sondrio counted approximately 21,359 inhabitants, and it is the capital and administrative centre of the province of Sondrio.

Geography

The city lies at about 307 metres above sea level at the confluence of the Mallero torrent with the Adda river, surrounded by the Bergamo Alps to the south and the Rhaetian Alps to the north. The territory is known for its terraced vineyards on the south-facing slopes, producing the Valtellina wines.

History

In ancient times the area was inhabited by Camunni and other Alpine peoples before Roman conquest. In the Middle Ages Sondrio was contested by the bishops of Como and local lords; the castle of Masegra, which still overlooks the town, dates in part from this period. The Valtellina passed under the rule of the Three Leagues (Graubünden) from 1512 until 1797, a period marked by religious conflict including the Sacro Macello massacre of Protestants in 1620. Sondrio then followed the fortunes of Lombardy, joining the Kingdom of Italy in 1859.

Main sights

Notable sites include the collegiate church of Santi Gervasio e Protasio, the Renaissance Palazzo Pretorio, the Castel Masegra with its museum of the Valtellina, and the sanctuary of the Madonna della Sassella on a rocky spur above the city. The terraced vineyards of the surrounding slopes are part of a landscape recognized for its cultural significance.

Economy

Sondrio is a service and administrative centre for the valley; local products include Valtellina wines (such as Sforzato and Inferno), apples, bresaola, and Bitto cheese from the surrounding province.
