Jack Daniel's is a brand of Tennessee whiskey produced in Lynchburg, Tennessee, by the Jack Daniel Distillery, founded by Jasper Newton "Jack" Daniel in the 19th century. It is the best-selling American whiskey in the world and has been owned by the Brown-Forman Corporation since 1956.

Production

The whiskey is made from a mash of about 80% corn, 12% rye, and 8% malted barley, distilled in copper stills and then filtered through ten feet of sugar maple charcoal — the "Lincoln County Process" that distinguishes Tennessee whiskey from bourbon. It is aged in handcrafted barrels at the distillery, which sits in Moore County, a dry county where the sale of alcohol is restricted even though the whiskey is produced there.

History

Jack Daniel learned distilling as a boy from preacher and enslaved man Nathan "Nearest" Green, who is now recognized as the distillery's first master distiller. Daniel registered his distillery with the U.S. government in 1866, making it among the oldest registered distilleries in the country. The flagship product is the black-labelled Old No. 7; other expressions include Gentleman Jack, Single Barrel, and Tennessee Honey.
