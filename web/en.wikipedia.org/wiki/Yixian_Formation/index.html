Yixian Formation

Location: Liaoning, China
Age: Early Cretaceous, c. 124–122 million years ago (Barremian–Aptian)
Named for: Yi County, Liaoning
Known for: Feathered dinosaur fossils of the Jehol Biota

The Yixian Formation is a geological formation in Liaoning province, northeastern China, famous for its exceptionally preserved fossils of the Jehol Biota. Deposited about 124 to 122 million years ago during the Early Cretaceous, its lake sediments and volcanic ash beds preserved soft tissues — feathers, fur, and skin impressions — providing transformative evidence about the evolution of birds and feathered dinosaurs.

Geology

The formation consists mainly of lacustrine mudstones and shales interbedded with basaltic and andesitic volcanic rocks. Repeated volcanic eruptions ash-buried lake ecosystems, and fine-grained sediment entombed organisms before decay, producing a Konservat-Lagerstätte. Argon–argon dating of the volcanic layers places the main fossil beds at roughly 124.6 million years old.

Famous fossils

The Yixian yielded Sinosauropteryx (1996), the first non-avian dinosaur found with filamentous feathers; the four-winged Microraptor; the early bird Confuciusornis, known from thousands of specimens; and the large tyrannosauroid Yutyrannus, which showed that even giant theropods bore feathers. Mammals such as the gliding Volaticotherium and the badger-sized Repenomamus — one preserved with a baby dinosaur in its stomach — also come from these beds, together with early flowering plants like Archaefructus.

Significance

Together with the overlying Jiufotang Formation, the Yixian records a complete freshwater ecosystem of the Early Cretaceous and has reshaped understanding of dinosaur–bird evolution, the origins of feathers and flight, and early angiosperm diversification. Its fossil fields are centred around Beipiao, Chaoyang, and Yi County, and specimens are exhibited in museums worldwide.
