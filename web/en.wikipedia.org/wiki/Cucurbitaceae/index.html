Cucurbitaceae

Cucurbitaceae
Order: Cucurbitales
Genera: ~95
Species: ~965
Common name: Gourd family

Cucurbitaceae, the gourd family, is a plant family of about 95 genera and nearly 1,000 species, including some of the world's most important fruit and vegetable crops: cucumber, melon, watermelon, squash, pumpkin and luffa.

Description

Most are fast-growing, trailing or climbing annual vines with tendrils, palmately lobed leaves and unisexual flowers. The fruit is a modified berry called a pepo, with a hard rind in many species. Bitter compounds called cucurbitacins defend wild plants.

Distribution and uses

The family is concentrated in the tropics of both hemispheres. Cucurbita pepo and other New World squashes, African watermelon, and Asian cucumber and melons were domesticated independently. Bottle gourds serve as containers; luffa provides sponges; some species are ornamental or medicinal.

Classification

Major tribes include Benincaseae (melons, gourds), Cucurbiteae (squashes) and Sicyoeae. Molecular phylogenetics has repeatedly revised genus boundaries.
