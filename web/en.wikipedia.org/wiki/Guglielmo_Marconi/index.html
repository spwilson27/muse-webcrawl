Guglielmo Marconi

Guglielmo Giovanni Maria Marconi (25 April 1874 – 20 July 1937) was an Italian inventor and electrical engineer known for his creation of a practical radio wave-based wireless telegraph system, for which he shared the 1909 Nobel Prize in Physics with Karl Ferdinand Braun.

Born25 April 1874, Bologna, Italy
Died20 July 1937, Rome
Known forWireless telegraphy, radio
AwardNobel Prize in Physics (1909)

Work

Building on Hertz's experiments, Marconi in 1895 transmitted signals over 1.5 miles at his family estate, then moved to England, patented his system (1896) and founded the Wireless Telegraph & Signal Company. He sent signals across the English Channel (1899) and, in December 1901, claimed the first transatlantic reception at St. John's, Newfoundland, from Poldhu, Cornwall.

Legacy

Marconi's companies equipped ships — wireless distress calls famously aided Titanic survivors in 1912 — and pioneered shortwave and broadcasting. Later life included Italian politics and controversial support for Mussolini's Fascist party. See Radio and Wireless telegraphy.
