Pythonidae

The Pythonidae, commonly known as pythons, are a family of nonvenomous constricting snakes found in Africa, Asia and Australia. The family includes some of the largest snakes in the world, such as the reticulated python and the Burmese python.

Characteristics

Pythons kill prey by constriction, coiling around the animal and squeezing until it suffocates or suffers cardiac arrest. Unlike boas, their close relatives, pythons lay eggs (they are oviparous), and females of many species incubate their eggs, sometimes generating heat by muscular contractions. Pythons possess heat-sensing pits along their lips that detect warm-blooded prey. Like other snakes they retain vestiges of hind limbs in the form of small spurs.

Distribution and habitat

Pythons inhabit rainforests, savannas, wetlands and deserts. The family contains roughly 40 species in about ten genera, including the true pythons (Python), the amethystine and scrub pythons of Australasia, and the small ant-hill python, among the world's smallest pythons.

Relationship with humans

Pythons are hunted for their skin and meat and are popular in the pet trade; escaped or released Burmese pythons have become a damaging invasive species in Florida's Everglades.
