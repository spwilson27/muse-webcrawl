Appalachian Mountains

The Appalachian Mountains, often called the Appalachians, are a mountain range in eastern to northeastern North America. They run about 3,200 kilometres from Newfoundland in Canada southwest to central Alabama in the United States. The range is among the oldest on Earth, formed by collisions that began about 480 million years ago during the assembly of the supercontinent Pangaea.

The highest peak is Mount Mitchell in North Carolina at 2,037 metres. The range includes the Great Smoky Mountains, the Blue Ridge, the Alleghenies and the White Mountains. The Appalachian Trail, roughly 3,500 kilometres long, follows the crest from Georgia to Maine and is one of the world's most famous hiking routes.

Regions and resources

Appalachia, the cultural region associated with the central and southern mountains, is historically known for coal mining, timber and a distinctive folk culture and music. The mountains' rugged terrain slowed westward settlement in colonial times and still shapes the settlement pattern of the eastern United States.
