Bushmills (from the Irish Muileann na Buaise, "mill of the River Bush") is a village on the north coast of County Antrim, Northern Ireland. It lies near the Giant's Causeway and is best known as the home of the Old Bushmills Distillery, which produces Bushmills Irish whiskey.

Whiskey

The area has a distilling tradition claimed to date to 1608, when Sir Thomas Phillips received a royal licence to distill in County Antrim — a date the distillery still cites. The Old Bushmills Distillery, formally established in 1784, makes single malt and blended Irish whiskeys, triple-distilled in copper pot stills. It is one of the major visitor attractions of the north coast.

Geography and tourism

Bushmills stands on the River Bush, a short distance inland from the Atlantic coast. As the gateway village to the Giant's Causeway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and to Dunluce Castle and the Carrick-a-Rede rope bridge, it receives substantial tourist traffic. The Giant's Causeway and Bushmills Railway, a heritage narrow-gauge line, connects the village to the Causeway.
