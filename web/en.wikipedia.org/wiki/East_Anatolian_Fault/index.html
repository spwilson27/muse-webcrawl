East Anatolian Fault

East Anatolian Fault
Country: Turkey
Length: ~700 km
Type: Left-lateral strike-slip fault
Plates: Anatolian and Arabian

The East Anatolian Fault (Turkish: Doğu Anadolu Fay Hattı) is a major strike-slip fault zone in eastern Turkey, running for about 700 kilometres from the Karlıova triple junction in the northeast to the Gulf of İskenderun in the southwest. It forms the boundary between the Anatolian Plate to the northwest and the Arabian Plate to the southeast.

The East Anatolian Fault forms the south-eastern boundary of the Anatolian Plate.

Tectonic setting

The fault accommodates the westward extrusion of Anatolia as the Arabian Plate pushes northwards into Eurasia. Movement is predominantly left-lateral (sinistral) strike-slip, at a rate of roughly 6–10 mm per year, and the fault consists of several segments separated by step-overs and basins.

Earthquakes

Although historically less active than the North Anatolian Fault, the East Anatolian Fault has produced damaging earthquakes, including events in 1789, 1866, 1872, 1893, 1905, 1971 (Bingöl), 2003, 2010 and 2020 (Elazığ). On 6 February 2023 a magnitude 7.8 earthquake ruptured the southern part of the fault near Pazarcık, followed nine hours later by a magnitude 7.5 event on the neighbouring Sürgü–Çardak fault; the disaster killed more than 50,000 people in Turkey and Syria and is the fault's largest known event.
