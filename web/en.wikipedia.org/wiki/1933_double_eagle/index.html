1933 double eagle

The 1933 double eagle is a United States twenty-dollar gold coin struck in 1933, designed by Augustus Saint-Gaudens. Because President Franklin D. Roosevelt removed the United States from the gold standard and ordered gold coins melted before the issue was released, the 1933 double eagle never circulated, and nearly all of the 445,500 struck were destroyed. The few survivors are among the most valuable coins in the world.

History

Executive Order 6102 in April 1933 prohibited the hoarding of gold coin, and the Mint's 1933 double eagles were held in vaults; all but two — set aside for the national collection — were melted in 1937. A small number had been stolen, however, and surfaced over the following decades. One coin was exported with a licence to King Farouk of Egypt; after decades of litigation, that specimen was sold at auction in 2002 for $7.59 million and again in 2021 for $18.9 million, making it the most valuable coin ever sold at auction.

Legal status

The United States government maintains that all 1933 double eagles except the Farouk specimen — legalised under a settlement — remain government property. Ten coins surrendered by the family of Philadelphia jeweller Israel Switt were ruled forfeit to the government after litigation concluded in 2016–2017.
