Cesare Borgia

Cesare Borgia (13 September 1475 – 12 March 1507) was an Italian politician, condottiero and cardinal, whose fight for power was a major inspiration for Niccolò Machiavelli's The Prince. He was the son of Pope Alexander VI and Vannozza dei Cattanei.

Cesare Borgia
Born: 13 September 1475, Rome
Died: 12 March 1507, Viana, Navarre
Father: Pope Alexander VI
Sister: Lucrezia Borgia
Titles: Duke of Valentinois, Duke of Romagna

Rise and fall

Made a cardinal at 18, Cesare became the first person to resign the cardinalate (1498) to pursue a military career. Backed by France and his father's papal armies, he conquered much of the Romagna and central Italy, ruling as Duke of Romagna and employing Leonardo da Vinci as military engineer. After Alexander VI died in 1503, Cesare was arrested under Pope Julius II, escaped to Spain, and died fighting at Viana in Navarre.

Legacy

Machiavelli, who observed him as a Florentine envoy, praised his energy and ruthlessness while noting his dependence on fortune. His motto was "Aut Caesar aut nihil" — either Caesar or nothing.
