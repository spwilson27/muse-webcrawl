Korea Train Express (Korean: 한국고속철도), widely known by its acronym KTX, is South Korea's high-speed rail system, operated by Korail. Construction began on the Gyeongbu high-speed line in 1992, and KTX services launched on 1 April 2004, making South Korea one of the few countries in the world operating trains at speeds above 300 km/h.

History

The Seoul–Busan corridor carried more than two-thirds of South Korea's population and a large share of its freight and passenger traffic. By the 1980s the existing Gyeongbu Line and the parallel expressway were saturated, prompting the government to plan a dedicated high-speed railway based on French TGV technology transfer agreements signed with Alstom.

The first KTX trains, the KTX-I, were derived from the TGV Réseau. The first phase opened in 2004 between Seoul and Daegu, with trains continuing to Busan on upgraded conventional track; the fully dedicated line to Busan was completed in 2010. The Honam high-speed line to Mokpo opened in 2015, and services to Gangneung for the 2018 Winter Olympics began in December 2017.

Rolling stock

The fleet includes the KTX-I (46 trainsets, based on TGV technology), the domestically developed KTX-Sancheon (KTX-II), and the newer KTX-Eum, which entered service in 2021 on the Jungang and Gangneung lines with a distributed-traction design and a maximum service speed of 260 km/h. The experimental HEMU-430X prototype reached 421.4 km/h in testing in 2013.

Services and impact

KTX trains cut Seoul–Busan travel time from over four hours to about two hours and fifteen minutes. The service reshaped domestic travel, sharply reducing domestic air traffic on the Seoul–Busan route and enabling same-day business travel between the capital and major provincial cities. A competing operator, SR Corporation, launched SRT services from Suseo station in 2016.
