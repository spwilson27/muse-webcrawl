Impeachment process against Richard Nixon
Accused: Richard Nixon, 37th President
Period: October 1973 – August 1974
Outcome: Resignation, 9 August 1974
Articles adopted by committee: 3 of 5

The impeachment process against Richard Nixon began in the United States House of Representatives on 31 October 1973 as the Watergate scandal unfolded, following the 'Saturday Night Massacre' firing of special prosecutor Archibald Cox. The House Judiciary Committee, chaired by Peter Rodino, opened a formal inquiry in February 1974.

Proceedings

Chief counsel John Doar and minority counsel Albert Jenner assembled evidence on obstruction, abuse of power and contempt. Meanwhile the Supreme Court ruled unanimously in United States v. Nixon (24 July 1974) that the President had to surrender subpoenaed tapes. The committee adopted three articles between 27 and 30 July 1974: obstruction of justice (27–11), abuse of power (28–10) and contempt of Congress (21–17), rejecting articles on the secret bombing of Cambodia and on tax matters.

Resignation

Release of the 'smoking gun' tape on 5 August 1974 — showing Nixon approving a cover-up days after the break-in — collapsed his congressional support. Senators Goldwater, Rhodes and Scott informed him he could not survive a Senate trial. Nixon resigned on 9 August 1974, the only US president to do so, before the full House voted. Gerald Ford succeeded him and pardoned him a month later.
