The Ministry of State Security (Korean: 국가안전보위성), commonly known by its former name, the State Security Department, is the secret police and state security agency of North Korea. An autonomous agency of the North Korean government reporting directly to the Supreme Leader, it is responsible for internal security, counterintelligence, surveillance of the population for political disloyalty, border security functions, and the operation of the country's political prison camps (kwalliso). It is sometimes described in English by the colloquial nickname "Bowibu".

The ministry reports directly to the Supreme Leader of North Korea.

History

The agency's origins lie in the security organs created under Kim Il Sung after 1945. In 1973 the political security functions were separated from the Ministry of Public Security to form the State Security Department, which Kim Jong Il used as a key power base while consolidating his succession. In 2016, at the 7th Congress of the Workers' Party of Korea, it was upgraded in name to a ministry. The agency is distinct from the Ministry of Social Security (the regular police) and from military intelligence organs.

Functions

According to defector testimony and reports by the UN Commission of Inquiry on Human Rights in the DPRK (2014), the ministry maintains an extensive informant network, monitors communications and movement, investigates "anti-state" offences connected with the songbun classification system, interrogates suspects at detention facilities, and administers political prison camps where prisoners — sometimes including three generations of a family — are held under conditions the UN described as crimes against humanity. It also guards the person of the leader and conducts counterintelligence against foreign services.

Leadership

For much of its history the agency had no formally announced minister, being effectively controlled by Kim Jong Il and then Kim Jong Un through first vice-directors such as U Dong Chuk. Kim Won Hong served as minister from 2012 until his reported purge in 2017; Jong Kyong Thaek was appointed thereafter. Little reliable information about the agency's internal structure is available publicly.
