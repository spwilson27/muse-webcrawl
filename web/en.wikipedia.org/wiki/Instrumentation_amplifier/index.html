Instrumentation amplifier
A differential amplifier with input buffers and a single gain-setting resistor.

An instrumentation amplifier (sometimes spelled in-amp) is a type of differential amplifier that has been outfitted with input buffer amplifiers, which eliminate the need for input impedance matching and make the amplifier suitable for use with sensors and in measurement and test equipment. Its characteristics include very low DC offset, low drift, low noise, very high open-loop gain, a very high common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR), and very high input impedances.

Three-op-amp topology

The classic circuit uses three operational amplifiers. Two non-inverting buffer amplifiers amplify the differential signal while passing the common-mode signal at unity gain; a single resistor Rgain connected between the buffers sets the first-stage gain, G = 1 + 2R/Rgain. A third op-amp wired as a differential (subtractor) stage removes the common-mode component and provides additional gain. Because gain is set by one external resistor and no matched resistor pairs are needed in the first stage, precision monolithic in-amps (such as the AD620 and INA128 families) achieve CMRR values exceeding 100 dB.

Applications

Instrumentation amplifiers are used wherever small differential signals ride on large common-mode voltages and must be extracted accurately: bridge transducers such as load cells and strain gauges, thermocouples, pressure sensors, and biomedical front ends for ECG and EEG. Variants include two-op-amp designs for lower cost, current-feedback in-amps, and fully integrated programmable-gain and digital-output devices.
