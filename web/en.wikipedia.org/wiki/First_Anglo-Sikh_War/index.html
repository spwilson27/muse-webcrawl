First Anglo-Sikh War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The First Anglo-Sikh War was fought between the Sikh Empire and the British East India Company in 1845 and 1846 around the Ferozepur district of Punjab. It resulted in a partial subjugation of the Sikh kingdom and the cession of the Jullundur Doab and Kashmir.

Background

After the death of Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1839, the Sikh Empire fell into disorder, with successive rulers assassinated and power increasingly held by the Sikh Khalsa Army, whose regimental committees (panchayats) acted as a state within the state. The court at Lahore, dominated by the child Maharaja Duleep Singh, his mother Maharani Jind Kaur, and rival courtiers, feared and sought to weaken the army. In December 1845 the Khalsa crossed the Sutlej River into territory the British treated as protected, providing the Governor-General, Lord Hardinge, with a casus belli; the British commander-in-chief was Sir Hugh Gough.

Course of the war

The war's major battles were Mudki (18 December 1845), Ferozeshah (21–22 December), a hard-fought two-day engagement the British nearly lost; Aliwal (28 January 1846), where Sir Harry Smith decisively defeated a Sikh force; and Sobraon (10 February 1846), where the British stormed the Sikh entrenchments on the Sutlej at heavy cost. Historians have long debated the conduct of the Sikh commanders Lal Singh and Tej Singh, who were widely accused of treachery in withholding support at critical moments, in collusion with the Lahore court faction that wanted the army humbled.

Aftermath

Under the Treaty of Lahore (March 1846), the Sikhs ceded the Jullundur Doab, accepted a British resident at Lahore, reduced their army, and paid a large indemnity; unable to pay in full, they ceded Kashmir, which the British sold to Gulab Singh of Jammu under the Treaty of Amritsar, creating the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. A subsequent treaty in December 1846 increased British control. Resentment led to the Second Anglo-Sikh War in 1848–1849 and the annexation of Punjab.
