Half-Life 2 is a 2004 first-person shooter game developed and published by Valve. It is the sequel to Half-Life (1998) and was created using Valve's Source engine. Players control Gordon Freeman, who, after years in stasis, awakens in City 17, a dystopian Eastern European city ruled by the interdimensional Combine empire through the collaborationist administrator Dr Wallace Breen.

Gameplay

Like its predecessor, the game is a continuous first-person narrative without cutscenes or level breaks, mixing combat, exploration and physics-based puzzles. It introduced the Gravity Gun, which lets the player pick up, carry and hurl heavy objects, and became a showcase for the Source engine's realistic physics (implemented using Havok), facial animation and materials simulation. Sequences alternate on foot with driving segments in a scout car and an airboat, and in the later chapters the player leads squads of resistance fighters and turns the Combine's own "antlion" creatures against them.

Plot

Some twenty years after the Black Mesa incident, the G-Man releases Freeman from stasis onto a train bound for City 17, where humanity lives under Combine occupation following the brief Seven Hour War. Freeman joins the resistance, reuniting with Eli and Alyx Vance and Dr Judith Mossman, and becomes a symbol of revolt after his flight through the canals and Ravenholm. A teleport accident scatters the group and triggers open uprising; Freeman storms the prison at Nova Prospekt and later leads the street war in City 17 itself. In the Citadel, the Combine's towering headquarters, he confronts Breen, destroys the dark-fusion reactor at its summit, and is returned to stasis by the G-Man as the reactor explodes around him and Alyx.

Development and release

Development took about five years and cost an estimated $40 million. The game was the first major title distributed through Valve's Steam platform, a controversial requirement at the time, and its source code was famously stolen in a 2003 hack that led to an FBI investigation and arrests in Germany. Half-Life 2 was released on 16 November 2004 to universal acclaim, winning more than 35 game-of-the-year awards; reviewers praised its physics, animation, art direction and pacing. It has since sold well over ten million copies at retail, with additional millions through Steam, and has been ported to consoles and mobile platforms. It was followed by the episodic sequels Episode One (2006) and Episode Two (2007).
