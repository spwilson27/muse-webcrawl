Signalling block system

Block signalling
Application: Railway signalling
Purpose: Prevent train collisions by spacing
Related: Absolute block, token block, track circuit

A signalling block system is a railway signalling method that divides the track into sections, or blocks, and permits only one train to occupy a block at a time. By ensuring trains are separated by at least one clear block, the system prevents rear-end and head-on collisions and forms the basis of nearly all railway signalling.

Principles

Each block has defined entry points controlled by signals. A train may enter a block only when the signal permits and the block ahead is known to be clear. Historically, occupancy was confirmed by signallers observing trains pass; modern systems detect trains automatically using track circuits or axle counters.

Methods of working

Under absolute block working, used traditionally on British railways, signallers at adjacent signal boxes exchange bell codes and operate block instruments to grant permission for each train. On single lines, token systems give the driver a physical or electronic token unique to the section, so possession of the token is the authority to occupy it. Permissive block working, used in some freight contexts, allows a second train to enter an occupied block at low speed under strict rules.

Fixed and moving blocks

Conventional systems use fixed blocks tied to lineside signals. Modern communications-based train control (CBTC) and ETCS Level 3 use moving blocks, in which safe separation is computed continuously from each train's exact position and speed, allowing shorter headways and higher capacity on metros and main lines.
