Faras (anciently Pachoras) was a town in Lower Nubia, in what is now northern Sudan near the Egyptian border. A bishopric and important centre of the Christian Nubian kingdom of Nobadia (later part of Makuria), it is best known for its cathedral, whose extraordinary medieval wall paintings were rescued before the site was flooded by Lake Nasser in the 1960s.

History

The site was occupied from the Meroitic period and became a fortified town on the Nile between the First and Second Cataracts. After the conversion of Nobadia to Christianity in the 6th century, Pachoras became a bishopric; its great cathedral was built and rebuilt over several centuries and decorated with successive layers of frescoes depicting bishops, kings, saints and donors in a distinctive Nubian style.

Excavation and salvage

In 1961–1964, as part of the UNESCO Nubian Salvage Campaign ahead of the Aswan High Dam, a Polish expedition directed by Kazimierz Michałowski excavated Faras. Its most celebrated achievement was the preservation of the cathedral's paintings: more than 120 frescoes were detached from the walls and divided between the National Museum in Warsaw — which displays over sixty in its Faras Gallery — and the Sudan National Museum in Khartoum. The town's remains were subsequently submerged beneath Lake Nasser.
