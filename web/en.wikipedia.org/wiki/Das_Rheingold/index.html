Das Rheingold (The Rhinegold), WWV 86A, is the first of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen. Wagner called it a Vorabend — a "preliminary evening" or prelude to the trilogy proper. It is performed in a single act of four continuous scenes and tells how the ring of world power was forged, stolen and cursed, setting the entire tragedy in motion.

Composition and premiere

Wagner wrote the libretto working backwards from Siegfrieds Tod and composed the music of Das Rheingold between 1852 and 1854. Against his wishes, it was premiered separately in Munich on 22 September 1869 under Franz Wüllner, at the insistence of his patron Ludwig II of Bavaria; the first complete performance within the cycle came at the Bayreuth Festival in August 1876 under Hans Richter.

Synopsis

In the depths of the Rhine, the dwarf Alberich renounces love, steals the Rhinegold guarded by the Rhinemaidens, and forges the ring that gives mastery of the world. Meanwhile Wotan, chief of the gods, has promised the goddess Freia to the giants Fafner and Fasolt as payment for building Valhalla; to redeem her he steals Alberich's hoard and the ring, but Alberich lays a curse on it. Wotan surrenders the ring to the giants, and Fafner promptly murders Fasolt over the gold. As the gods cross a rainbow bridge into Valhalla, the Rhinemaidens lament their lost gold.

Music

The opera opens with one of the most famous passages in music: 136 bars built on a single E-flat major chord, arpeggiated to suggest the primal flow of the Rhine and the origin of the world. The score introduces many of the cycle's leitmotifs and calls for unusual instruments, including tuned anvils for the scene in Nibelheim.
