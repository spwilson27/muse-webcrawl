AdaBoost

AdaBoost (short for Adaptive Boosting) is a statistical classification meta-algorithm formulated by Yoav Freund and Robert Schapire in 1995, who won the 2003 Gödel Prize for their work. It can be used in conjunction with many other types of learning algorithms to improve performance. The output of the other learning algorithms (weak learners) is combined into a weighted sum that represents the final output of the boosted classifier.

Algorithm

AdaBoost is adaptive in the sense that subsequent weak learners are tweaked in favor of those instances misclassified by previous classifiers. Each training example carries a weight; after each round, the weights of incorrectly classified examples are increased so that later learners focus on hard cases, while the learner's own contribution to the final vote is weighted by a coefficient derived from its error rate. AdaBoost is sensitive to noisy data and outliers, but in some problems it is less susceptible to overfitting than most learning algorithms.

Applications

AdaBoost was famously applied to face detection in the Viola–Jones framework, combining Haar-like features with cascaded boosted classifiers to detect faces in real time.
