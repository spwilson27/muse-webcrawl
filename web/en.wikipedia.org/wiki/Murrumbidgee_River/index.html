Murrumbidgee River

The Murrumbidgee River is a major tributary of the Murray River within the Murray–Darling basin, and its second-longest tributary after the Darling. Rising in the Snowy Mountains of New South Wales, it flows about 1,488 kilometres through the Australian Capital Territory — passing near Canberra — and across the Riverina to join the Murray near Boundary Bend. Its name derives from the Wiradjuri word murrumbidjigee, "big water".

Course and towns

Major towns on the river include Cooma (near its headwaters), Wagga Wagga, Narrandera, Hay and Balranald. The river flows through Canberra's rural fringe, where it is a popular recreation corridor with swimming holes such as Kambah Pool and Casuarina Sands.

Regulation

Flows are controlled by Burrinjuck Dam (completed 1928) and Blowering Dam, part of the Snowy Mountains Scheme. The river supplies the Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area around Griffith and Leeton, one of Australia's most productive agricultural districts, growing rice, citrus, wine grapes and vegetables. Regulation and extraction have greatly reduced natural flows, affecting wetlands such as the Lowbidgee floodplain, and environmental flows are now delivered to sustain river red gum forests and waterbird breeding.
