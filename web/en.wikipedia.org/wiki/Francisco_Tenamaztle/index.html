Francisco Tenamaztle

Francisco Tenamaztle (died c. 1560s) was a Caxcan indigenous leader in 16th-century New Spain, best known as the principal leader of the Mixtón War (1540–1542), the first major indigenous uprising against Spanish rule in western Mexico.

Life and the Mixtón War

Tenamaztle was a lord of Nochistlán in what is now Zacatecas. Brutal treatment by the conquistador Nuño de Guzmán and his successors — enslavement, tribute demands, and abuses by encomenderos — drove the Caxcanes and their allies to revolt in 1540. The rebels fortified hilltop strongholds (peñoles) such as Mixtón and Nochistlán, defeated Spanish expeditions, and killed the veteran conquistador Pedro de Alvarado, who died of wounds after his horse fell on him during a retreat in 1541. Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza eventually suppressed the rebellion with a large force of Spaniards and indigenous allies.

Later life and legacy

Tenamaztle escaped capture and remained a fugitive for about nine years before surrendering peacefully in 1550, reportedly influenced by Bishop Vasco de Quiroga. Baptized as Francisco, he was sent to Spain in 1552, where, with support from Bartolomé de las Casas, he petitioned the Council of the Indies for justice for his people, arguing the war had been a just defence against tyranny. His petitions went unanswered and he died in Spain around 1560. He is remembered in Mexico as an early defender of indigenous rights, with statues and commemorations in Zacatecas and Nochistlán.
