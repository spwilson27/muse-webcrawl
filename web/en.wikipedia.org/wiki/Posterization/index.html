Posterization

Posterization or posterisation of an image is the conversion of a continuous gradation of tone to several regions of fewer tones, causing abrupt changes from one tone to another. This was originally done with photographic processes to create poster effects. It can now be done photographically or digitally, and may be deliberate or an unintended artifact of color quantization.

Process

The effect is produced by reducing the number of distinct colors or gray levels in an image — for example, mapping each channel of an RGB image from 256 possible values to a handful of levels. Smooth gradients then break into visible steps or bands, an artifact known as banding or contouring. In the darkroom, posterization was achieved by making a series of high-contrast lithographic negatives from selected tonal ranges and printing them in register, a technique related to the Sabattier effect (solarization), with which posterization is sometimes combined.

Uses

Deliberate posterization is a common artistic device in graphic design, screen printing and poster art, where flat areas of color suit screen-based printing; the iconic 2008 "Hope" poster of Barack Obama by Shepard Fairey is based on a posterized source photograph. Unintentional posterization occurs when an image with subtle gradients is converted to a format with a limited color palette, such as GIF with 256 colors, or displayed at low bit depth, and is a common defect in compressed video of skies, shadows and underwater scenes. Dithering is used to conceal the effect by distributing quantization error.
