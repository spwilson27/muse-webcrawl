Octoechos (Greek: Ὀκτώηχος, "eight sounds/tones") is the system of eight modes, or echoi, used to organise the chant of the Byzantine rite and other Eastern Christian traditions, and by extension the liturgical book containing the cycle of hymns that rotate through the eight tones week by week. The system structures most of the variable hymnody of the daily and weekly services in the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic churches of Byzantine tradition.

Origins

The eight-mode system developed in the monasteries of Palestine, especially the Great Lavra of St Sabbas near Jerusalem, between the 5th and 8th centuries, drawing on older Jerusalem chant practice. Tradition credits John of Damascus (c. 676–749) with organising the resurrectional hymns and canons according to the eight tones. Comparable eight-mode organisations appear in Syriac and Armenian chant, and the Latin West developed its own eight church modes, possibly influenced by the Byzantine system.

The eight echoi

The echoi comprise four authentic modes (first, second, third, fourth) and their four plagal counterparts. Each echos has characteristic melodic formulas, opening intonations and cadences rather than being a simple scale; chanters learn to improvise within a mode using model phrases. In Byzantine chant theory the modes are further described by their final notes and by systems of modal signatures in the notation.

The liturgical book

The book called the Octoechos (or Parakletike) contains the hymns for each weekday — stichera, canons, sessional hymns and others — arranged by tone, providing a complete eight-week cycle for the periods of the year when no festal Menaion material displaces it. A shorter "Great Octoechos" includes fuller material for Sundays.

The weekly cycle

Each week is sung in one tone, beginning with vespers on Saturday evening; the tone advances weekly in sequence from the first to the eighth and repeats, with the sequence restarting after Pascha following the Pentecostarion period. The tone of the week governs the resurrectional hymns of Sunday and the ordinary hymns of the weekdays, giving the liturgical year a repeating melodic rhythm.

Related systems

The West Syrian tradition employs its own eight-mode weekly system (also called Octoechos), and the early Slavic printed Octoechos (Oktoikh) was among the first books printed in Cyrillic (Kraków, 1491).
