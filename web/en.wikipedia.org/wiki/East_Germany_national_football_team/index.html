The East Germany national football team represented the German Democratic Republic (GDR) in international association football from 1952 until 1990, when the team was dissolved following German reunification. It was governed by the Deutscher Fußball-Verband der DDR (DFV).

History

East Germany played its first international in 1952, a 3–0 defeat to Poland in Warsaw. Overshadowed by the far more successful West Germany team, the GDR qualified for the FIFA World Cup only once, in 1974 — a tournament hosted by West Germany. Drawn together in the first group stage, the two German states met in Hamburg on 22 June 1974, and East Germany achieved its most famous result, a 1–0 victory secured by a goal from Jürgen Sparwasser. The East advanced to the second group stage but did not progress further; the result remains the only competitive meeting between the two teams.

Olympic success

Because East German "amateur" players were effectively state-supported full-time athletes, the GDR was a major force in Olympic football, in which Western professionals were long excluded. East Germany won the bronze medal in 1964 (competing within the United Team of Germany in earlier Games) and 1972, the gold medal at Montreal in 1976, defeating Poland 3–1 in the final, and silver at Moscow in 1980.

Final years

The team's last match was a 2–0 friendly victory over Belgium in Brussels on 12 September 1990, weeks before reunification on 3 October 1990 ended the GDR. East German players became eligible for the unified Germany team; Matthias Sammer and Ulf Kirsten were among the former internationals who went on to represent Germany. In total East Germany played 293 internationals.
