Karlskrona

Karlskrona is a city in Blekinge County in southern Sweden, built on a cluster of islands in the Baltic Sea archipelago. Founded in 1680 by King Charles XI as Sweden's principal naval base, it remains the home port of the Swedish Navy's main fleet. Its exceptionally preserved baroque naval town was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998 as the "Naval Port of Karlskrona".

History

After Sweden gained Blekinge from Denmark in 1658, Charles XI chose the sheltered, ice-free harbour of Trossö island for a new naval headquarters to replace Stockholm's vulnerable base. The city — "Charles's crown" — was laid out on a grand baroque grid plan by leading architects including Erik Dahlbergh and Nicodemus Tessin the Younger. The naval shipyard, ropewalk, dockyards and fortifications made it one of Europe's most complete naval towns; the great dry dock in the bedrock, begun in the 18th century, is still in use.

Sights

Landmarks include the Admiralty Church (Amiralitetskyrkan), Sweden's largest wooden church (1685), with the wooden figure of Rosenbom outside; the Fredrik Church and Holy Trinity Church on the main square, Stortorget; the Karlskrona Naval Museum (Marinmuseum) on Stumholmen island; and the offshore Kungsholm Fortress, which has guarded the harbour entrance since the 1680s. The archipelago of some thirty islands is connected by bridges and ferries.

Modern Karlskrona

Today the city hosts the Swedish Coast Guard headquarters, the Blekinge Institute of Technology, and a cluster of telecom and IT companies. The name of the mobile phone technology Bluetooth, coined by an Ericsson engineer, is often linked to the region's Viking heritage; Ericsson has long had a presence in the city.
