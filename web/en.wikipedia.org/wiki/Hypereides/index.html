Hypereides

Hypereides or Hyperides (Greek: Ὑπερείδης; c. 390 – 322 BC) was an Athenian logographer (speech writer) and statesman, ranked among the ten canonical Attic orators. He was a leading opponent of Macedonian domination of Greece and, alongside Demosthenes, led Athens in the Lamian War against Antipater after the death of Alexander the Great.

Bornc. 390 BC, Athens
Died322 BC, Aegina
OccupationOrator, statesman, logographer

Life

Hypereides was born into a wealthy Athenian family and studied rhetoric, possibly under Isocrates. He became a successful writer of speeches for others — famously prosecuting the general Autocles and defending the courtesan Phryne, whose trial gave rise to the anecdote that he unveiled her before the judges to win acquittal. In politics he supported Demosthenes against Philip II of Macedon, took part in the diplomacy following the Battle of Chaeronea (338 BC), and prosecuted the pro-Macedonian Demades. After Alexander's death in 323 BC he persuaded Athens to lead the Greek revolt known as the Lamian War. Following its failure, he fled to Aegina, where he was captured and executed on Antipater's orders in 322 BC; ancient sources report that his tongue was cut out before his death.

Works

Seventy-seven speeches were attributed to Hypereides in antiquity, of which 55 were considered genuine. They were largely lost until papyrus discoveries in nineteenth-century Egypt recovered substantial texts, including Against Demosthenes, For Lycophron, For Euxenippus, Against Athenogenes, and his celebrated Funeral Oration for the general Leosthenes and the fallen of the Lamian War's first campaign. In 2002 the Archimedes Palimpsest yielded a further portion of a speech, Against Diondas. Ancient critics praised his wit, charm, and subtlety, comparing his style favorably with that of Lysias.
