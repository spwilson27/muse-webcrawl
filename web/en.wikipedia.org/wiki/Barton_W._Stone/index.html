Barton W. Stone

Barton Warren Stone (December 24, 1772 – November 9, 1844) was an American evangelist during the Second Great Awakening in the United States. He was a leader of the western Restoration Movement, also known as the Stone-Campbell Movement, whose followers called themselves simply Christians and sought to restore the church of the New Testament without creeds or denominational structures.

Early life and Cane Ridge

Born in Maryland and raised in Virginia, Stone was licensed as a Presbyterian minister and settled in Kentucky, preaching at Cane Ridge and Concord. In August 1801 he hosted the Cane Ridge Revival, one of the largest camp meetings of the Second Great Awakening, attended by as many as 20,000–25,000 people, marked by intense religious exercises and unprecedented cooperation among Presbyterians, Methodists and Baptists.

The Stone Movement

Questioning Calvinist doctrines such as predestination, Stone and colleagues issued the Last Will and Testament of the Springfield Presbytery (1804), dissolving their presbytery and resolving to be "Christians only". Stone edited the Christian Messenger from 1826. In 1832 his followers united with the "Disciples" of Alexander Campbell at Lexington, Kentucky, forming the movement from which the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), the Churches of Christ and the Christian churches descend.

Later life

Stone moved to Illinois in 1834, preaching and publishing until his death in 1844. His remains were reinterred at Cane Ridge in 1847, where a memorial shrine preserves the original meeting house.
