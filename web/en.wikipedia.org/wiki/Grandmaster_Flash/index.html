Grandmaster Flash (born Joseph Robert Saddler) is an American hip hop DJ and record producer, widely credited with inventing many of the turntable techniques that define DJing. Raised in the Bronx, he developed the Quick Mix Theory — using headphones to cue records and duplicate break sections with two copies of the same disc — transforming the turntable into a musical instrument.

Innovations

Flash systematized backspinning, punch-phrasing and cutting: he marked records with crayon to find exact cue points, used a headphone mix to preview the incoming record, and manipulated discs with what he called "rubbing" — foundational moves of hip hop DJing. His 1981 single "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" was the first record to showcase turntable scratching and live mixing as the main attraction.

The Furious Five and "The Message"

With his group Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five (Melle Mel, Kidd Creole, Scorpio, Rahiem and Cowboy), Flash recorded for Sugar Hill Records. Their 1982 single "The Message" — with its refrain "Don't push me 'cause I'm close to the edge" — brought social realism to rap and became one of the most influential hip hop songs ever, later inducted into the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry. In 2007 the group became the first hip hop act inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. Flash also won the Polar Music Prize in 2019.
