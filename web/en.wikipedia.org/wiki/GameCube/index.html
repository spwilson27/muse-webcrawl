GameCube


The GameCube is a home video game console released by Nintendo in 2001.

The GameCube is a home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. It was released in Japan on September 14, 2001, in North America on November 18, 2001, and in Europe and Australia in 2002. As a sixth-generation console, it competed with Sony's PlayStation 2, Microsoft's Xbox, and Sega's Dreamcast. It is the successor to the Nintendo 64.

Hardware

The GameCube is a compact cube-shaped unit with a carrying handle, powered by an IBM PowerPC-based "Gekko" CPU and an ATI "Flipper" graphics processor. It was Nintendo's first console to use optical discs — proprietary 1.5 GB miniDVD-based GameCube Optical Discs — chosen partly to combat piracy and avoid licensing fees, though this limited storage compared to rivals. Games saved to proprietary memory cards. Its controller introduced dual-stage analog triggers; the wireless WaveBird controller was an early popular wireless gamepad. An add-on, the Game Boy Player, allowed playing Game Boy cartridges on a television.

Games and legacy

Best-selling titles included Super Smash Bros. Melee, Mario Kart: Double Dash, Super Mario Sunshine, The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, Metroid Prime, and Resident Evil 4 (initially exclusive). The console sold about 21.7 million units worldwide — commercially behind the PlayStation 2 and Xbox — but is remembered fondly for its software library. It was discontinued in 2007 and succeeded by the Wii, which was backward-compatible with GameCube games.
