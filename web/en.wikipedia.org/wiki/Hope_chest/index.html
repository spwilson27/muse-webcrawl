Hope chest

A hope chest, also called a dowry chest, cedar chest, bottom drawer or glory box, is a piece of furniture traditionally used by unmarried young women to collect items in anticipation of married life.

Custom

From adolescence, a young woman would fill the chest with household goods she made or received as gifts — typically linens, quilts, tablecloths, clothing and sometimes silverware or china — which would form part of her trousseau and equip her future home. The custom was widespread in Europe and North America, and cedar chests were popular because cedar repels moths and protects stored textiles.

Variations

Related traditions include the Italian cassone and the Amish and Mennonite hope chests of Pennsylvania. While the custom has declined, hope chests remain popular as decorative storage furniture and graduation gifts.
