Marx's theory of alienation

Marx's theory of alienation
AuthorKarl Marx
SourceEconomic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844
Key conceptEntfremdung (estrangement)

Marx's theory of alienation describes the estrangement (German: Entfremdung) of people from aspects of their human nature that Karl Marx argued results from living in a society of stratified social classes. The theory, developed in the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, holds that workers under capitalism are alienated from the products of their labour, the labour process, their fellow workers, and their own 'species-being'.

Four forms of alienation

Marx identified alienation from the product of labour, which belongs to the capitalist rather than the worker; alienation from the act of production itself, experienced as forced activity; alienation from other people, replaced by competition; and alienation from species-being, humanity's creative essence.

Influence

Although the 1844 manuscripts were not published until 1932, the theory of alienation became central to Western Marxism and influenced existentialism, critical theory, and twentieth-century sociology.
