Necessity

Necessity is a modal property of propositions and states of affairs: a proposition is necessary if it could not have been false — if it is true in every possible world. Necessity contrasts with contingency, the property of propositions that are true in the actual world but might have been false, and with impossibility, the property of propositions true in no possible world. The concept is central to metaphysics, logic, and the philosophy of language.

Types of necessity

Philosophers distinguish several kinds of necessity. Logical necessity attaches to propositions whose denial is self-contradictory, such as "all bachelors are unmarried" or the truths of mathematics on some views. Metaphysical necessity is necessity grounded in the nature of things themselves, such as the claim that water is necessarily H2O. Nomological (or physical) necessity is necessity relative to the laws of nature: given the actual laws, some outcomes cannot fail to occur. Deontic necessity concerns obligation — what must be done — rather than what must be the case.

History

Aristotle discussed necessity extensively in the Prior Analytics and Metaphysics, distinguishing things that are necessary in themselves from things necessary only on a hypothesis. Medieval scholastics developed elaborate theories of modal distinctions. Leibniz grounded necessity in truth in all possible worlds, a picture later formalised in the 20th century through the possible-worlds semantics of modal logic associated with Saul Kripke and others.

Kripke and a posteriori necessity

In Naming and Necessity (1972), Saul Kripke argued that necessity is a metaphysical rather than epistemic notion: some necessary truths, such as "water is H2O" or "Hesperus is Phosphorus," are knowable only empirically (a posteriori), because the terms involved are rigid designators that pick out the same object in every possible world. This separated the necessary/contingent distinction from the a priori/a posteriori distinction, against the earlier assumption that the two pairs coincide.
