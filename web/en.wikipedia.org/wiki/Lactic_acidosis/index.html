Lactic acidosis


Lactate production and clearance

Lactic acidosis is a medical condition characterised by a build-up of lactate in the body, with formation of an excessively low pH in the bloodstream (a form of metabolic acidosis). It is conventionally defined as a blood lactate concentration above 4–5 mmol/L together with an arterial pH below 7.35. Lactate accumulates when production from anaerobic glycolysis exceeds the capacity of the liver and kidneys to clear it.

Causes

The Cohen and Woods classification divides lactic acidosis into type A, caused by tissue hypoxia — as in shock, severe sepsis, cardiac arrest, mesenteric ischaemia or extreme exertion — and type B, occurring without overt hypoxia, for example with certain drugs (notably metformin in renal failure), liver failure, malignancy, thiamine deficiency or inherited metabolic disorders such as MELAS.

Signs and management

Symptoms are often those of the underlying illness, with deep rapid breathing (Kussmaul respiration), nausea, abdominal pain and altered consciousness in severe cases. Treatment is directed primarily at the cause — restoring perfusion and oxygen delivery — since attempts to correct the pH directly, for example with bicarbonate, remain controversial. Elevated lactate is an important prognostic marker in critical illness, guiding resuscitation in sepsis.
