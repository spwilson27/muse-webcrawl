History of Syria

The history of Syria covers one of the world's oldest civilised regions, from the ancient city of Ebla through Aramaean, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Islamic caliphates, Ottoman rule, French mandate, independence, Ba'athist rule under the Assad family, and civil war from 2011.

Syria
Ancient centres: Ebla, Aleppo, Damascus, Palmyra
Independence: 1946 (from France)
Ba'ath rule: 1963–2024
Capital: Damascus

Ancient Syria

Ebla (3rd millennium BC) left vast cuneiform archives. Amorites, Aramaeans (whose language became the Near East's lingua franca), Phoenicians, Assyrians, Persians, Alexander the Great and the Seleucids followed. Under Rome, Syria was a rich province — Palmyra under Queen Zenobia briefly ruled an eastern empire (270–272) — and Antioch was a major early Christian centre.

Islamic and Ottoman eras

Muslim armies took Damascus in 636; it became capital of the Umayyad Caliphate (661–750), building the Umayyad Mosque. Abbasids, Fatimids, Crusader-era Zengids and Ayyubids (Saladin), Mamluks, and finally Ottomans after 1516 ruled Syria for four centuries.

Mandate and independence

France received the League of Nations mandate after World War I, suppressing the Great Syrian Revolt (1925–27). Independence came in 1946, followed by coups, the United Arab Republic with Egypt (1958–61), and the Ba'athist seizure of power in 1963. Syria lost the Golan Heights to Israel in 1967.

Assad era and civil war

Hafez al-Assad ruled 1970–2000, crushing the Muslim Brotherhood uprising at Hama (1982); his son Bashar succeeded him. Protests in 2011 escalated into a devastating multi-sided civil war involving opposition forces, Kurdish-led forces, Islamic State, Russia, Iran, Turkey and the US-led coalition, displacing over half the population. In December 2024 a rebel offensive led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham toppled the Assad regime, beginning a transitional government period.
