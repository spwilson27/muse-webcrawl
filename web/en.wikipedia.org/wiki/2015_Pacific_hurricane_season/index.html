The 2015 Pacific hurricane season was one of the most active and record-breaking Pacific hurricane seasons on record, fuelled by a strong El Niño event and exceptionally warm ocean temperatures. Across the eastern and central Pacific basins it produced 31 named storms, 25 hurricanes and a record number of major hurricanes.

Records

The season set numerous records: it saw three simultaneous Category 4 hurricanes in the Pacific in August (Kilo, Ignacio and Jimena) — a first for the basin — and the central Pacific had its busiest season on record. Above all, Hurricane Patricia in October became the strongest tropical cyclone ever measured in the Western Hemisphere, with maximum sustained winds of 215 mph (345 km/h) and a minimum pressure of 872 mbar, before making landfall in Jalisco, Mexico, as a Category 4 hurricane; remarkably, deaths and damage were limited thanks to evacuations and its compact size and rapid weakening over the Sierra Madre.

Season summary

Activity began early with Hurricane Andres in late May. Other notable storms included Blanca, which reached Category 4 before striking Baja California as a tropical storm; Dolores, which brought flooding rains to Southern California; and late-season Sandra, among the latest major hurricanes recorded in the eastern Pacific. Accumulated cyclone energy for the basin ranked among the highest ever observed.
