Vermicompost

Vermicompost
ProcessVermicomposting (worm composting)
Key organismsEisenia fetida (red wiggler), Eisenia andrei, Eudrilus eugeniae
InputsFood scraps, paper, manure, yard waste
ProductsWorm castings, worm tea (leachate)

Vermicompost (also vermicast) is the product of composting organic material using various species of earthworms, usually red wigglers (Eisenia fetida), which eat the material and excrete it as nutrient-rich castings. The process, called vermicomposting, converts kitchen scraps, paper, manure and other organic waste into a dark, crumbly soil amendment prized by gardeners. It can be done indoors in small bins, making it popular in apartments, or at large commercial scale.

Properties and benefits

Vermicompost contains nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium and micronutrients in plant-available forms, together with humic acids, beneficial microbes and plant-growth regulators. Compared with ordinary compost it is often richer in immediately available nutrients. Applied to soil, it improves structure, water retention and drainage, suppresses some plant diseases, and generally promotes seed germination and growth. The liquid draining from a worm bin ("worm tea," properly a leachate) is sometimes used as a liquid fertilizer, though true worm tea is brewed by steeping finished castings in aerated water.

Practice

A typical home system is a ventilated bin layered with moist shredded paper or coconut coir as bedding, to which food scraps are added under the bedding. Worms prefer temperatures of roughly 15–25 °C and cannot tolerate extremes. Meat, dairy, oily foods and citrus are usually avoided because they attract pests and acidity. Worms consume roughly half their body weight per day under good conditions, and finished compost can be harvested in three to six months by separating the worms from the castings.

Large-scale use

Municipal and industrial vermicomposting processes food waste, biosolids and livestock manure; windrow and continuous-flow reactor systems handle tonnes per day. Vermicomposting reduces the volume of organic waste sent to landfill, where it would otherwise generate methane, though it requires careful management of moisture, temperature and feedstock.
