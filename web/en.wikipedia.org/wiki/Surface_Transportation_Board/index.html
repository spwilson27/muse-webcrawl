Surface Transportation Board

The Surface Transportation Board (STB) is an independent federal agency of the United States government charged with the economic regulation of various modes of surface transportation, principally freight railroads. It was established by the ICC Termination Act of 1995 as the successor to the Interstate Commerce Commission, taking over its remaining functions on January 1, 1996.

Responsibilities

The Board adjudicates railroad rate and service disputes, reviews rail mergers, line sales, construction, and abandonments, and has certain oversight roles over Amtrak's on-time performance, pipeline carriage by rail-related modes, and household-goods moving services. Its decisions are made by a board of up to five members appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

Independence

Although administratively housed within the Department of Transportation in its early years, the STB was made a fully independent agency by the Surface Transportation Board Reauthorization Act of 2015.
