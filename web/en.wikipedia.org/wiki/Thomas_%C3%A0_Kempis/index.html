Thomas à Kempis

Bornc. 1380, Kempen, Duchy of Guelders
Died1471, near Zwolle, Netherlands
OccupationCanon regular, writer
Known forThe Imitation of Christ

Thomas à Kempis (c. 1380 – 1471) was a German-Dutch canon regular of the late medieval period and the author of The Imitation of Christ, one of the best-known Christian devotional books ever written. He was a member of the Brethren of the Common Life movement and a follower of Gerard Groote's Modern Devotion (Devotio Moderna).

Life

Thomas was born at Kempen in the Rhineland, the son of a blacksmith. He travelled to Deventer in the Netherlands to study under the Brethren of the Common Life, and later entered the Augustinian priory of Mount St Agnes near Zwolle, where he was ordained priest and spent most of his long life copying manuscripts, preaching, and writing devotional works.

The Imitation of Christ

De imitatione Christi, composed in Latin around 1418–1427, consists of four books of spiritual counsel on detachment from the world, humility, and inner life. It has been translated into more languages than almost any book except the Bible and remains a classic of Catholic spirituality, admired by readers across confessional lines.
