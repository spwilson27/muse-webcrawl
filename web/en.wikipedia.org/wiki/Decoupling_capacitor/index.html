Decoupling capacitor

A decoupling capacitor (or bypass capacitor) is a capacitor used to decouple one part of an electronic circuit from another, shunting noise on the power supply to ground.

Placed close to an integrated circuit's power pins, it acts as a local energy reservoir, supplying transient current demands and suppressing voltage spikes and high-frequency interference. Typical values are 100 nF ceramic capacitors, often combined with larger bulk capacitors. Without adequate decoupling, circuits may suffer glitches, oscillation or unreliable logic behaviour.
