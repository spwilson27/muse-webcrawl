The Barber of Seville

The Barber of Seville, or The Useless Precaution (Italian: Il barbiere di Siviglia, ossia L'inutile precauzione) is an opera buffa in two acts composed by Gioachino Rossini with an Italian libretto by Cesare Sterbini. The libretto was based on Pierre Beaumarchais's French comedy of the same name. The première took place on 20 February 1816 at the Teatro Argentina in Rome.

Synopsis

Count Almaviva, disguised as a poor student named Lindoro, serenades Rosina beneath the window of Dr Bartolo's house in Seville. Figaro, the town barber and factotum, agrees to help him. Bartolo, Rosina's elderly guardian, plans to marry her himself and keeps her closely watched. Through a series of disguises — a drunken soldier, then a music teacher substituting for the supposedly indisposed Don Basilio — the Count gains access to the house. Despite Bartolo's schemes, a notarised marriage contract is signed before he can intervene, and the lovers are united. Bartolo is reconciled to the outcome when he is allowed to keep Rosina's dowry.

Music

The opera contains some of Rossini's most celebrated numbers, including the overture (adapted from earlier works), Figaro's entrance aria "Largo al factotum", Rosina's cavatina "Una voce poco fa", and Don Basilio's "La calunnia". The ensembles are noted for Rossini's trademark crescendo and rapid-fire patter.

Reception

The first performance was a failure, disrupted by supporters of Giovanni Paisiello, who had set the same play, and by on-stage mishaps. The second performance was a triumph, and the opera quickly became one of the most popular in the repertoire; it remains among the most frequently performed operas worldwide and is considered the exemplar of the opera buffa style.
