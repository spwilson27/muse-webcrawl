Crown of thorns

According to the New Testament, a woven crown of thorns was placed on the head of Jesus during the events leading up to his crucifixion. It was one of the Instruments of the Passion, employed by Jesus's captors both to cause him pain and to mock his claim of authority. It is mentioned in the gospels of Matthew, Mark and John, and is often alluded to by the early Church Fathers.

Biblical account

The Gospels relate that Roman soldiers, after scourging Jesus, twisted together a crown of thorns and set it on his head, placing a staff in his hand and mocking him as "King of the Jews". The crown remained on his head during the crucifixion.

Relic

A relic venerated as the crown of thorns was housed in Jerusalem and later Constantinople before being acquired by King Louis IX of France in 1239. It was kept in the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris, built to house it, and later transferred to Notre-Dame Cathedral. The relic was rescued from the 2019 Notre-Dame fire. Individual thorns said to come from the crown were distributed widely as relics throughout the Christian world.

Plant identification

Various thorny plants have been proposed as the species used, including the Jerusalem thorn (Paliurus spina-christi) and Ziziphus spina-christi, both bearing flexible branches and sharp thorns. The name has also been given to unrelated organisms, such as the crown-of-thorns starfish (Acanthaster planci) and the plant Euphorbia milii.
