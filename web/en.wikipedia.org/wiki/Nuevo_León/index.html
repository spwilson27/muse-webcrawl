Nuevo León

Nuevo León (Spanish: [ˈnweβo leˈon]) is a state in northeastern Mexico, bordered by Tamaulipas to the north and east, Coahuila to the west, and San Luis Potosí and Zacatecas to the south; it also shares a short border with the US state of Texas. Its capital and largest city is Monterrey, one of Mexico's most important industrial and commercial centres. With an area of about 64,220 square kilometres and a population of over five million, Nuevo León is among Mexico's most prosperous and industrialised states.

History

The region was inhabited by nomadic indigenous groups whom Spanish colonisers called Chichimecas. Monterrey was founded in 1596 by Diego de Montemayor, and the area was organised as the New Kingdom of León (Nuevo Reino de León) within New Spain. After Mexican independence, Nuevo León became a state in 1824. In the 19th century it briefly formed part of separatist movements and was occupied during the Mexican–American War and the French intervention. Under leaders such as Santiago Vidaurri it exercised considerable autonomy.

Economy and geography

From the late 19th century, Monterrey grew into a steel, brewing, glass and cement centre, home to firms such as FEMSA, Cemex and the former Fundidora steelworks; today the state is a hub of manufacturing, nearshoring investment and higher education, hosting institutions such as the Monterrey Institute of Technology (ITESM) and the Autonomous University of Nuevo León. The terrain is mostly arid plains rising to the Sierra Madre Oriental in the west and south, crowned by peaks such as Cerro el Potosí and the Cerro de la Silla, the symbol of Monterrey. The climate is semi-arid and prone to extremes of heat.
