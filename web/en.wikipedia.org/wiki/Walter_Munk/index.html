Walter Munk

Born: 19 October 1917, Vienna, Austria
Died: 8 February 2019 (aged 101), La Jolla, California
Fields: Physical oceanography, geophysics
Institutions: Scripps Institution of Oceanography

Walter Heinrich Munk (19 October 1917 – 8 February 2019) was an Austrian-American physical oceanographer, often called the "Einstein of the oceans". Based for most of his career at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography in La Jolla, California, he made fundamental contributions to the understanding of wind-driven ocean circulation, waves, tides, ocean acoustics, and the Earth's rotation.

Early life and wartime work

Born in Vienna, Munk was sent by his family to a preparatory school in New York and later worked in banking before turning to science, earning his doctorate at Scripps in 1947. During the Second World War, working with Harald Sverdrup, he developed methods for predicting surf and wave conditions for Allied amphibious landings, including the Normandy landings — work that helped establish oceanography as a critical wartime science.

Research

Munk's theory of wind-driven circulation incorporated wind-stress curl and helped explain the great subtropical gyres and western boundary currents. He studied swell propagation across the Pacific, measured tides in the deep ocean, and in the 1990s led the Heard Island Feasibility Test, transmitting low-frequency sound across ocean basins to demonstrate acoustic thermometry of ocean climate. His work with Gordon MacDonald on the Earth's rotation and wobble linked geophysics with oceanography and astronomy. He also co-founded the Institute of Geophysics and Planetary Physics at Scripps and received honours including the National Medal of Science and the Kyoto Prize.

Later life

Munk remained scientifically active past the age of 100, publishing and lecturing until shortly before his death at 101 in 2019.
