Merrill Lynch

Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith Incorporated, styled as Merrill, is an American investment management and wealth management division of Bank of America. Along with BofA Securities, the investment banking arm, both firms engage in prime brokerage and broker-dealer activities. Founded as Charles E. Merrill & Co. in 1914, it became one of the world's largest brokerage firms, famous for its 'thundering herd' of financial advisors and its bull logo.

FoundedJanuary 6, 1914
FoundersCharles E. Merrill; Edmund C. Lynch
ParentBank of America (since 2009)
HeadquartersNew York City

History

Charles Merrill pioneered bringing Wall Street investing to middle-class Americans, building a retail brokerage network and taking the firm public in 1971. Merrill Lynch grew into a diversified financial giant, rivalling firms descended from J. P. Morgan's.

Financial crisis and acquisition

Heavy losses on mortgage-related securities in 2007–2008, under CEO Stan O'Neal and successor John Thain, forced Merrill to accept acquisition by Bank of America in September 2008, closing January 1, 2009 for about $50 billion. The Merrill brand survives for wealth management within Bank of America.
