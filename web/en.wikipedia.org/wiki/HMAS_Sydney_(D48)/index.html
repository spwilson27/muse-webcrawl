HMAS Sydney (D48)

HMAS Sydney (D48) was a Modified Leander-class light cruiser of the Royal Australian Navy (RAN), the third ship to bear the name. She was laid down by Swan Hunter in England, launched in 1934, commissioned into the Royal Navy as HMS Phaeton in 1935, and purchased by the Australian government and renamed Sydney before entering RAN service.

Second World War service and loss

Sydney served in the Mediterranean in 1940, sinking the Italian destroyer Espero in June and playing a leading role in the Battle of Cape Spada in July, where she sank the cruiser Bartolomeo Colleoni. After refitting in Australia she conducted convoy escort duties in home and Indian Ocean waters.

On 19 November 1941, off Western Australia, Sydney encountered the German auxiliary cruiser Kormoran, disguised as a Dutch merchantman. In the ensuing battle both ships were fatally damaged: Kormoran was scuttled by her crew, most of whom survived, while Sydney sank with the loss of all 645 aboard — the largest loss of life on an Australian warship and Australia's worst naval disaster. The wrecks of both ships were located in March 2008, and a formal inquiry confirmed the sequence of events reconstructed from German survivors' accounts.
