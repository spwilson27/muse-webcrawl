Bridge (music)

In music, especially Western popular music, a bridge is a contrasting section that prepares for the return of the original material, such as the verse or chorus. It provides variety and a change of perspective before the final repetitions, and is typically used once in a song, often after the second chorus.

Function and characteristics

The bridge contrasts with the rest of the song in melody, harmony, rhythm or lyrics. It frequently modulates to a related key, uses a different chord progression — a common pattern moves to the subdominant or the relative minor — or strips back the arrangement before building into a final chorus. Lyrically, the bridge often offers a new insight, a climax of emotion, or a turn in the narrative.

Related terms

In jazz and musical theatre, the bridge of a 32-bar AABA standard is the B section, also called the middle eight or release. In classical music, related contrasting linking passages appear in sonata form as transitions, and in fugues as episodes. The bridge should not be confused with the pre-chorus, which builds tension immediately before each chorus, or with an instrumental interlude or solo.
