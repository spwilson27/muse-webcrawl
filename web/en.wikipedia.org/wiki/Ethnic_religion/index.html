Ethnic religion

An ethnic religion is a religion or belief associated with a particular ethnic group. Ethnic religions are often distinguished from universal religions, such as Christianity, Islam or Buddhism, which seek converts across ethnic boundaries. Membership in an ethnic religion is typically by birth into the community rather than by conversion, and its practices are bound up with the group's culture, language, calendar and ancestral traditions.

Examples

Frequently cited examples include Judaism, traditionally the religion of the Jewish people; Hinduism in its historical association with the peoples of India; Shinto in Japan; the traditional religions of many Indigenous peoples in Africa, the Americas and Oceania; Chinese folk religion; and historical traditions such as ancient Greek and Norse paganism. In the modern period, revived or reconstructed ethnic religions — sometimes grouped under modern paganism — have appeared in Europe and elsewhere.

Characteristics

Ethnic religions tend to emphasise communal and ancestral observance over individual doctrinal assent. Sacred narratives concern the origins and fortunes of the particular people, and rituals mark its agricultural seasons, rites of passage and historical commemorations. Because identity is inherited, ethnic religions usually do not proselytise, although many accept converts through formal procedures, as with conversion to Judaism.

Scholars caution that the boundary between "ethnic" and "universal" religion is porous: universal religions become embedded in particular cultures, and ethnic religions may spread through migration, forming diaspora communities worldwide.
