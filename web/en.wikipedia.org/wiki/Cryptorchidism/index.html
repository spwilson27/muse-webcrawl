Cryptorchidism, also known as undescended testis, is the failure of one or both testes to descend from the abdomen into the scrotum before birth. It is the most common congenital abnormality of the male genitalia.

Signs and causes

The condition is detected on physical examination when a testis cannot be palpated in the scrotum. Descent normally occurs in the third trimester and is guided by the gubernaculum and hormonal signals including androgens and insulin-like factor 3 (INSL3). Risk factors include prematurity, low birth weight, and certain genetic and endocrine conditions. Many undescended testes descend spontaneously in the first months of life.

Complications

Untreated cryptorchidism is associated with reduced fertility, an increased risk of testicular cancer (particularly seminoma), inguinal hernia, and testicular torsion.

Treatment

Current guidelines recommend surgical correction by orchiopexy between 6 and 18 months of age if the testis has not descended spontaneously. Hormonal treatment with hCG or GnRH analogues is less effective and less commonly recommended. Early correction improves fertility prospects and allows the testis to be monitored, though it does not eliminate the elevated cancer risk.

In animals

Cryptorchidism also occurs in dogs, cats, horses and other mammals; affected breeding animals are usually excluded from stud because the trait is heritable.
