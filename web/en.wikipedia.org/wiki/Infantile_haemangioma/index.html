Infantile haemangioma

Also called: Strawberry mark, infantile hemangioma

Specialty: Dermatology, paediatrics

Treatment: Observation; propranolol for problematic lesions

An infantile haemangioma (hemangioma) is a common benign vascular tumour of infancy, appearing as a raised red ("strawberry") mark on the skin. It affects roughly 4–5 percent of infants, more often girls and premature babies.

Course

Lesions typically appear in the first weeks of life, grow rapidly during the first few months (the proliferative phase), and then slowly shrink over years (involution), often leaving little or no trace.

Treatment

Most require no treatment beyond observation. Lesions that threaten vision, breathing, or ulcerate are commonly treated with oral propranolol, which has become first-line therapy since its serendipitous discovery in 2008.
