Three Parallel Rivers

RiversJinsha (Yangtze), Lancang (Mekong), Nu (Salween)

The Three Parallel Rivers region of Yunnan, China, is where the upper courses of three of Asia's great rivers — the Jinsha (upper Yangtze), the Lancang (upper Mekong), and the Nu (upper Salween) — run roughly parallel to each other, north to south, through deep gorges in the Hengduan Mountains, in places little more than 60 kilometres apart yet flowing to different seas.

Ecology

The steep terrain, spanning from high Tibetan-plateau peaks above 6,000 metres to river valleys near 760 metres, supports an extraordinary range of climates and habitats. The area is one of the world's centres of plant diversity and a refuge for species such as the snow leopard and the Yunnan snub-nosed monkey.

Protection

In 2003 UNESCO inscribed the Three Parallel Rivers of Yunnan Protected Areas as a World Heritage Site, covering a network of national parks and nature reserves. The region is also home to many ethnic minority peoples, including the Lisu, Nu, Yi, Tibetan and Naxi communities.
