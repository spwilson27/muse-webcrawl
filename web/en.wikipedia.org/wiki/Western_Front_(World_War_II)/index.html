Western Front (World War II)

Western Front
Date:1939–1945
Location:Western and northern Europe
Phases:Phoney War & Fall of France (1939–40); Atlantic Wall defence; Normandy to the Elbe (1944–45)
Key commanders:Eisenhower, Montgomery, Patton (Allied); Rundstedt, Rommel, Model (German)
Result:Allied victory; end of Nazi Germany

The Western Front was a theatre of World War II encompassing combat in western Europe from 1939 to 1945. It opened with the "Phoney War" and Germany's conquest of France in 1940, was relatively quiet until the Allied invasions of 1944, and ended with the Western Allies driving into Germany and meeting Soviet forces at the Elbe.

1939–1940: defeat of France

After the invasion of Poland, Britain and France declared war but undertook little offensive action (the Saar Offensive) during the "Phoney War". In May 1940 Germany attacked through the Ardennes, encircling the Allied armies; the British evacuated at Dunkirk, and France signed an armistice on 22 June 1940.

1941–1943: the Channel front

Germany fortified the coast with the Atlantic Wall while Britain and the United States built up forces; the disastrous Dieppe Raid (1942) gave hard lessons in amphibious warfare. The main Anglo-American effort went first to North Africa and Italy, while the bombing offensive over Germany intensified.

1944: Normandy and the liberation of France

Operation Overlord landed some 156,000 troops in Normandy on 6 June 1944 (D-Day). After brutal fighting in the bocage, the breakout (Operation Cobra) and the Falaise pocket destroyed much of the German army in France; Paris was liberated in August. A second landing, Operation Dragoon, opened southern France. In September the airborne Operation Market Garden failed to cross the Rhine at Arnhem.

1944–1945: into Germany

Germany's last offensive, the Battle of the Bulge (December 1944), was defeated, after which the Allies crossed the Rhine (Remagen, March 1945), encircled the Ruhr and advanced to the Elbe, meeting the Red Army at Torgau on 25 April 1945. Germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 May 1945.
