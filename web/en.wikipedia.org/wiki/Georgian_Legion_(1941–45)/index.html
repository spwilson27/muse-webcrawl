Georgian Legion (1941–45)

Active 1941–1945
Country Nazi Germany
Type Infantry (Wehrmacht foreign legion)
Size ~30,000 (all Georgians in German service)

The Georgian Legion (German: Georgische Legion) was a military formation of Nazi Germany during World War II, composed of ethnic Georgians. It was formed by émigrés living in Western Europe after the 1921 Soviet invasion of Georgia, together with Soviet prisoners of war who chose, often under duress in the camps, to fight against the Soviet Union in the hope of restoring Georgian independence.

Formation

The legion was established in December 1941 near Kruszyna in occupied Poland. It was organized as a series of infantry battalions (the 795th, 796th, 797th, 798th, 799th, 822nd, 823rd, and 824th), trained at camps in Poland, Ukraine, and later France. Georgian émigré organizations such as the Tetri Giorgi association supported recruitment, and the legion's political leadership envisioned a Georgian state freed from Soviet rule. Most legion battalions served on occupation and anti-partisan duties; some fought at the front, including in the North Caucasus campaign and in Normandy, where Georgian battalions were stationed in 1944.

Georgian uprising on Texel

The best-known episode of the legion's history is the Georgian uprising on Texel in the Netherlands. On the night of 5–6 April 1945, Georgian soldiers of the 822nd Battalion rose against their German officers, killing hundreds, in what has been called "Europe's last battlefield." German reinforcements retook most of the island after weeks of fighting; about 565 Georgians, 120 Dutch islanders, and some 800 Germans died. The surviving Georgians are commemorated at the Georgian war cemetery "Loladze" on Texel.

Aftermath

Under the Yalta agreements, surviving Georgian legionnaires in Western hands — many of them captured Soviet citizens — were forcibly repatriated to the Soviet Union, where many were shot or sent to the Gulag. Émigré Georgians who had held non-Soviet citizenship were generally exempted from repatriation.
