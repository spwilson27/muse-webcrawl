Real-time payments

Real-time payments (RTP) are payment systems specifically designed to transfer and receive money in real time, at any time of the day and on any day of the year. Unlike traditional payment methods that experience a lag time between the initiation and final settlement of funds, real-time payments complete the transfer within seconds, providing immediate access to funds for both the sender and the receiver.

Characteristics

Real-time payment systems generally offer continuous availability (24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year), immediate or near-immediate interbank settlement, confirmation of payment to the sender, and rich data messaging often based on the ISO 20022 standard. Payments are typically irrevocable once made.

Systems worldwide

Examples of real-time payment schemes include the RTP network operated by The Clearing House and the Federal Reserve's FedNow service in the United States; Faster Payments in the United Kingdom; SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) in Europe; PromptPay in Thailand; Pix in Brazil; and the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) in India, which processes billions of transactions monthly.

Adoption and effects

Real-time payments are used for person-to-person transfers, bill payments, gig economy payouts, and business disbursements. Supporters argue they improve cash-flow management and financial inclusion, while regulators have raised concerns about fraud, since instant irrevocable transfers leave little time to intercept erroneous or fraudulent payments.
