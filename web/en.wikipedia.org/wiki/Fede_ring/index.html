Fede ring

A fede ring is a ring in which two clasped hands form the bezel, symbolising faith, loyalty and friendship. The name derives from the Italian mani in fede, "hands in faith" (or from Latin fede, faith). The design descends from the Roman dextrarum iunctio motif — the joining of right hands that signified a marriage contract — and fede rings were used in Roman times and became especially popular in medieval and early modern Europe.

History and use

Fede rings were commonly exchanged as betrothal and wedding rings, as tokens between friends, and as memorial gifts. Surviving examples range from simple base-metal rings to elaborate gold and enamelled pieces; some incorporate hearts, inscriptions or posies engraved inside the band. In the later development of the design, the clasped hands were combined with a crowned heart to produce the Irish Claddagh ring. A related form, the gimmel ring, consists of two or three interlocking hoops that separate and reunite, with the fede hands often forming its bezel.
