Koichi Tanaka (田中 耕一, Tanaka Kōichi, born August 3, 1959) is a Japanese electrical engineer who shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2002 for developing soft laser desorption, a method of mass spectrometry analysis of biological macromolecules.

Biography

Tanaka was born and raised in Toyama, Japan. He graduated from Tohoku University in 1983 with a degree in electrical engineering and joined Shimadzu Corporation, where he worked in the Central Research Laboratory on mass spectrometry. Unusually for a Nobel laureate, Tanaka held only a bachelor's degree and had spent his career as a company engineer rather than as an academic.

Soft laser desorption

In 1985 Tanaka discovered that by mixing a sample with an ultrafine cobalt powder suspended in glycerol and irradiating it with a low-energy laser, large molecules such as proteins could be ionized intact for mass spectrometric analysis. Shimadzu patented the method, known as soft laser desorption, and applied it in its LAMS-50K laser desorption mass spectrometer. Tanaka shared one half of the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Bennett Fenn, who developed electrospray ionization; the other half went to Kurt Wüthrich.

Later work

Tanaka continued working at Shimadzu, later directing a research laboratory bearing his name, and has worked on applying mass spectrometry to medical diagnostics, including early detection of Alzheimer's disease from blood samples.
