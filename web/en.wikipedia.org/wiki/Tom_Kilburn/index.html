Tom Kilburn (11 August 1921 – 17 January 2001) was an English mathematician and computer scientist. Working with Freddie Williams at the University of Manchester, he designed the Williams–Kilburn tube, the first practical random-access computer memory, and led the team that built the Manchester Baby — the world's first stored-program computer to run a program, on 21 June 1948.

Early life and wartime work

Kilburn was born in Dewsbury and studied mathematics at Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge. During the Second World War he was seconded to the Telecommunications Research Establishment at Malvern to work on radar, where he met Freddie Williams.

Manchester computers

Following Williams to Manchester in 1946, Kilburn co-invented a cathode-ray-tube memory — the Williams–Kilburn tube — which stored bits as charges on a CRT screen. With it they built the small experimental Manchester Baby to prove the concept. Its successors, the Manchester Mark 1 and the commercial Ferranti Mark 1, followed. Kilburn later led the MARS and Atlas projects; Atlas (1962), developed with Ferranti, was one of the world's first supercomputers and pioneered virtual memory and paging.

Legacy

As Manchester's first Professor of Computer Science (from 1964), Kilburn founded one of the earliest university computer science departments. The Kilburn Building at the University of Manchester is named in his honour.
