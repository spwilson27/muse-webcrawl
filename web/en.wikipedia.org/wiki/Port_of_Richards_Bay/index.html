Port of Richards Bay

The Port of Richards Bay is a deep-water harbour on the Indian Ocean coast of KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, situated at the town of Richards Bay about 160 kilometres north-east of Durban. It is South Africa's largest port by cargo volume, built primarily as a bulk export harbour for coal and other dry bulk commodities from the interior.

History

The port was developed in the 1970s on the Mhlatuze lagoon after surveys identified the site as suitable for a deep-water harbour. It opened in 1976, initially with a coal terminal designed to export coal from the Mpumalanga coalfields via a dedicated electrified railway line. The first ship to load coal was in April 1976. Subsequent phases added dry bulk, breakbulk, and liquid bulk facilities, along with a small craft harbour and repair facilities.

Facilities and cargo

The port covers a land area of some 2,157 hectares with a water area of about 1,495 hectares. Its principal facility is the Richards Bay Coal Terminal (RBCT), one of the largest coal export terminals in the world, with a design capacity of about 91 million tonnes per year. Other terminals handle dry bulk commodities such as chrome ore, ferrochrome, alumina, phosphoric acid, wood chips, and steel, as well as liquid bulk. The port is operated by Transnet National Ports Authority, and terminal operations are largely run by Transnet Port Terminals and private operators such as RBCT.

Significance

Richards Bay handles the majority of South Africa's bulk exports and is a key outlet for the mining industry of the interior. Its growth transformed the surrounding area into an industrial zone with aluminium smelters, fertiliser plants, and pulp and paper mills, although coal exports have been constrained in recent years by capacity problems on the rail line serving the port.
