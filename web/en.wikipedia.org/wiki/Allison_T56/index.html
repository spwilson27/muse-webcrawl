The Allison T56 is a military single-shaft, modular-design turboprop engine with a 14-stage axial-flow compressor driven by a four-stage turbine. Originally developed by the Allison Engine Company of Indianapolis for the Lockheed C-130 Hercules transport, it entered service in 1954 and remains in production under Rolls-Royce, which acquired Allison in 1995.

Design and variants

The T56 combines a fixed single spool with a reduction gearbox driving a constant-speed propeller. Variants have steadily increased power and durability: the early T56-A-1 produced about 3,460 shaft horsepower, while the Series III T56-A-15 of the C-130H delivers 4,591 shp, and the Series IV T56-A-427 powers late-model E-2 Hawkeyes. A marinised derivative, the 501K, drives generators in US Navy service. The Rolls-Royce T56 Series 3.5 enhancement package improved fuel efficiency and reliability for ageing C-130 fleets.

Applications and service

More than 18,000 T56 engines have been delivered. The engine powers the C-130 Hercules, the P-3 Orion maritime patrol aircraft, the E-2 Hawkeye airborne early-warning aircraft and the C-2 Greyhound carrier transport, as well as the Lockheed Electra airliner (as the 501-D civil version). Its derivative, the AE2100, powers the C-130J Super Hercules. With C-130s expected to fly for decades more, the T56's production and support span will approach a century.
