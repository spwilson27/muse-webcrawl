Sigma baryon

The sigma baryons are a family of subatomic hadron particles, baryons composed of one up or down quark and a pair of quarks in a symmetric flavour combination — specifically one strange, charm, or bottom quark together with a symmetric pair of up/down quarks. They are symbolised by the Greek letter Σ. Like all baryons, they contain three quarks and have half-integer spin.

Compositionuus, uds, dds (light sigmas); analogous charm and bottom states
StatisticsFermionic
InteractionsStrong, weak, electromagnetic, gravity
Spin1/2 (ground state); 3/2 (Σ* resonances)
Isospin1

The light sigmas

The three light sigma baryons form an isospin triplet:

  • Σ⁺ (uus), mass about 1189 MeV/c²
  • Σ⁰ (uds), mass about 1193 MeV/c²
  • Σ⁻ (dds), mass about 1197 MeV/c²

The neutral Σ⁰ decays almost instantaneously by electromagnetic decay to the lighter lambda baryon and a photon (Σ⁰ → Λ + γ), with a lifetime of only about 7 × 10⁻²⁰ seconds. The charged sigmas live much longer by particle standards, decaying weakly: Σ⁺ mainly to a proton and a neutral pion or a neutron and a positive pion, and Σ⁻ essentially always to a neutron and a negative pion, with lifetimes around 10⁻¹⁰ seconds.

Excited and heavy sigmas

The spin-3/2 partners, the Σ* baryons, are resonances decaying strongly to a lambda or sigma plus a pion. Heavier families replace the strange quark with a charm quark (Σc states, around 2455 MeV/c²) or a bottom quark (Σb states, around 5810 MeV/c²), discovered at particle colliders. In the quark model, the sigma baryons partner the lambda baryons: the difference lies in the isospin and spin coupling of the light-quark pair, which makes the Σ slightly heavier than the Λ.
