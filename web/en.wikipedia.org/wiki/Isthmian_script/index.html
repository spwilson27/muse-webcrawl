Isthmian script

The Isthmian script, also called the Epi-Olmec script, is a Mesoamerican writing system used in the Isthmus of Tehuantepec region of what is now Mexico from roughly 500 BCE to 500 CE. It is one of the earliest scripts of Mesoamerica and remains incompletely understood.

Corpus

The best-known texts in the script are those on La Mojarra Stela 1, the Tuxtla Statuette, the Tres Zapotes Stela C, and several ceramic and mask inscriptions. The Tuxtla Statuette and Tres Zapotes Stela C carry Long Count dates corresponding to 162 CE and 32 BCE respectively, among the earliest such dates known. The script appears to descend from, or share ancestry with, the earlier Olmec writing tradition, and it was used in the region between the Olmec heartland and the area of the later Maya civilisation.

Proposed decipherment

In 1993 John Justeson and Terrence Kaufman announced a decipherment arguing that the language of the texts was an early form of Zoquean (pre-proto-Zoque), based partly on a vocabulary list found in a colonial-era document. Their readings remain controversial: some epigraphers find them plausible, while others consider the language identification and many sign values unproven. The script is generally thought to be logosyllabic, like other Mesoamerican scripts including Maya writing.
