William Pynchon


Born 26 October 1590, Springfield, Essex, England
Died 29 October 1662 (aged 72), Wraysbury, England
Known for Founder of Springfield, Massachusetts

William Pynchon (26 October 1590 – 29 October 1662) was an English colonist, fur trader, and magistrate in the Massachusetts Bay Colony, and the founder of Springfield, Massachusetts.

Colonist and founder

Pynchon sailed to New England with the Winthrop Fleet in 1630 as one of the colony's patentees and served as its treasurer. He founded Roxbury's settlement and, in 1636, established a trading post on the Connecticut River at Agawam, which developed into the town of Springfield, named for his birthplace in England. His fur-trading and provisioning business made him one of the wealthiest men in early New England, and he controlled the Connecticut Valley trade for years.

The Meritorious Price of Our Redemption

In 1650 Pynchon published in London a theological treatise, The Meritorious Price of Our Redemption, which questioned the orthodox Calvinist doctrine of the atonement. The Massachusetts General Court condemned the book as heretical and ordered it publicly burned in Boston — often described as the first book banned and burned in New England — and Pynchon was cited to answer charges.

Return to England

Rather than face trial, Pynchon transferred his property to his son John and returned to England in 1652, settling at Wraysbury, Buckinghamshire, where he wrote further theological works and died in 1662. His descendants remained prominent in the Connecticut Valley.
