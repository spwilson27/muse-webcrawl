Carol II (15 October 1893 – 4 April 1953) was King of Romania from 8 June 1930 until his abdication on 6 September 1940. The eldest son of Ferdinand I, his reign was marked by scandal over his private life, political manipulation, and ultimately a royal dictatorship, ending amid the territorial losses and crisis of 1940.

Early life and renunciations

As crown prince, Carol twice renounced his succession rights: first after his morganatic marriage to Zizi Lambrino in 1918 (annulled), and again in 1925 after beginning his relationship with Magda Lupescu. He went into exile, and on Ferdinand's death in 1927 the throne passed to Carol's young son Michael, under a regency.

Reign

Carol returned unexpectedly in June 1930 and was proclaimed king. He undermined the party system, and in February 1938 he abolished the constitution, banned parties and proclaimed a royal dictatorship, ruling through the National Renaissance Front. In 1940 Romania was forced to cede Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina to the USSR, northern Transylvania to Hungary, and Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria. Carol abdicated in favour of Michael and fled the country with Lupescu, whom he later married. He died in exile in Estoril, Portugal; his remains were returned to Romania in 2003.
