Peace park

A peace park is a transboundary protected area spanning the borders of two or more countries, managed cooperatively to conserve ecosystems and promote peace and cooperation between the neighbouring states.

Examples

The first international peace park is generally considered to be the Waterton-Glacier International Peace Park, established in 1932 between Canada and the United States. Other examples include the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park in southern Africa (linking Kruger, Limpopo and Gonarezhou national parks), the Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park between South Africa and Botswana, and La Amistad International Park between Costa Rica and Panama.

Purpose

Beyond conservation, peace parks are intended to allow wildlife migration across borders, encourage eco-tourism and build trust between countries with a history of conflict; the concept is promoted by the IUCN and the Peace Parks Foundation, founded in 1997.
