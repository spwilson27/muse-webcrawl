The Chicago Marathon is an annual marathon held in Chicago, Illinois, each October. It is one of the six World Marathon Majors and among the largest marathons in the world by number of finishers, with a field of roughly 50,000. Its flat, fast loop course through 29 neighbourhoods has produced numerous world records.

History

The modern race began in 1977, reviving a Chicago marathon tradition from the early 1900s. Growing under long-time race director Carey Pinkowski, it attracted elite fields with large prize purses. The course, with few turns and minimal elevation change, is considered one of the fastest in the world.

Records

World records set in Chicago include those by Steve Jones (1984), Khalid Khannouchi, Catherine Ndereba, and Paula Radcliffe. In 2019 Brigid Kosgei broke the women's world record with 2:14:04. In 2023 Kelvin Kiptum ran 2:00:35, a men's world record, on the Chicago course; Ruth Chepngetich set a women's world record of 2:09:56 in 2024.

Course and participation

The race starts and finishes in Grant Park, passing landmarks and neighbourhoods including Lincoln Park, Chinatown, Pilsen, and Bronzeville, supported by over a million spectators. Entry for amateurs is by lottery or guaranteed-entry programmes.
