Privatisation

Privatisation (also spelled privatization) is the transfer of ownership, property or business from the government to the private sector. It includes the sale of state-owned enterprises, contracting out of public services, voucher schemes, and deregulation that allows private competition in formerly public monopolies.

Methods and rationale

Governments privatise by public share offerings, trade sales to strategic investors, management buyouts, or voucher distributions to citizens. Supporters argue private ownership improves efficiency, investment and service quality, reduces fiscal burdens, and broadens share ownership. Critics argue privatisation can create unregulated private monopolies, reduce access for low-income users, worsen labour conditions, and sacrifice long-term public value for short-term revenue.

History

Large-scale privatisation began in the United Kingdom under Margaret Thatcher in the 1980s, with sales of British Telecom, British Gas, British Airways, and water and electricity utilities, alongside the sale of council housing. The model spread to Latin America, and after 1989 to Central and Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union, where mass (voucher) privatisation transferred thousands of enterprises — in Russia controversially producing a class of oligarchs through loans-for-shares deals. China and Vietnam adopted gradual reforms mixing state ownership with market mechanisms. Some sectors have since seen renationalisation or remunicipalisation, notably water services and railways in several countries.
