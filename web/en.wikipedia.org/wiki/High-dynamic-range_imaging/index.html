High-dynamic-range imaging

High-dynamic-range imaging (HDRI) is a technique used in digital photography and imaging to reproduce a greater dynamic range of luminosity than is possible with standard techniques. The aim is to present a similar range of luminance to that experienced by the human visual system, which can adapt to scenes spanning many orders of magnitude of brightness.

Technique

In photography, HDR is usually achieved by capturing multiple exposures of the same scene — underexposed, normally exposed and overexposed frames — and merging them into a single radiance map, which is then tone mapped for display on conventional (low-dynamic-range) devices. Specialised file formats such as Radiance HDR (.hdr) and OpenEXR store the full floating-point range. Modern smartphones perform computational HDR automatically, merging bursts of frames in milliseconds.

Applications

Beyond photography, HDRI is fundamental in computer graphics, where HDRI environment maps provide realistic image-based lighting for rendering, and in HDR video and television standards such as HDR10 and Dolby Vision, which extend the brightness and colour range of displays.
