Bleu de France (colour)

Bleu de France ("Blue of France") is a colour closely associated with France and French identity. A vivid, slightly azure blue, it derives historically from the blue field of the royal arms of France, which bore gold fleurs-de-lis on azure. In hexadecimal terms the shade is commonly rendered as #318CE7.

Motor racing

The colour is best known internationally as the traditional racing colour of France. Under the racing colours assigned in the early days of Grand Prix motor racing by the Gordon Bennett Cup, French entries were painted blue. Bugatti made the colour famous in the 1920s and 1930s, and it was later carried by Talbot-Lago, Delahaye, Matra, Ligier and Alpine. French racing cars in Bleu de France became one of the most recognisable liveries in motorsport, contrasting with British racing green, Italian Rosso Corsa and German silver.

Other uses

Bleu de France is also associated with French national sports teams, although the blue worn by the French football and rugby teams is generally darker. Variants of the name are used for fabrics, porcelain and paint colours, echoing the blue of Sèvres porcelain produced under royal patronage in the eighteenth century.
