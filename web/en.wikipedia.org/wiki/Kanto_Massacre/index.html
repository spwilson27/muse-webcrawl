Kantō Massacre

The Kantō Massacre was a mass murder of Korean residents of Japan's Kantō region that took place in the chaotic aftermath of the Great Kantō earthquake of 1 September 1923. Japanese civilians, police and military, acting on false rumours that Koreans were committing arson and robbery, poisoning wells, and plotting revolt, killed thousands of Koreans. Chinese residents, Japanese socialists, anarchists and labour activists were also murdered in related incidents.

Background

The magnitude 7.9 earthquake devastated Tokyo and Yokohama, killing over 100,000 people and leaving the capital in ruins. The Home Ministry declared martial law. In the days after the quake, rumours spread that Koreans — colonial subjects of the Japanese Empire since Japan's annexation of Korea in 1910 — were exploiting the disaster. Vigilante groups (jikeidan) formed across the region, setting up checkpoints where suspected Koreans were identified by pronunciation tests using words such as "jūgo-en gojissen" (fifteen yen, fifty sen), whose sounds are difficult for native Korean speakers.

Killings

Mobs armed with bamboo spears, clubs, swords and firearms attacked Koreans in Tokyo, Yokohama and surrounding prefectures. In some cases army and police units participated in or organised killings. In the Kameido Incident, police murdered detained labour activists, and in the Amakasu Incident military police officer Masahiko Amakasu murdered the anarchist Ōsugi Sakae, Itō Noe, and Ōsugi's six-year-old nephew. Hundreds of Chinese residents were also killed.

Aftermath and historical memory

The government suppressed reporting and minimised the death toll; official figures were far below later estimates, which commonly range from several hundred to roughly 6,000 Korean victims. For decades the massacre received little official acknowledgment in Japan, while it has remained an important part of Korean historical memory. Memorial services have been held annually in Tokyo since the 1970s by citizens' groups and Korean residents, and the events continue to be a sensitive point in Japan–South Korea relations.
