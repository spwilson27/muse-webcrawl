Revolt of the Three Feudatories

The Revolt of the Three Feudatories (Chinese: 三藩之亂) was a rebellion lasting from 1673 to 1681 in the early Qing dynasty of China. The revolt was led by the three lords of the fiefs granted by the Ming-turned-Qing dynasty to the Chinese generals who submitted to the Qing: Wu Sangui, Shang Zhixin, and Geng Jingzhong, the "Three Feudatories" (Sanfan), who controlled large semi-autonomous fiefdoms in Yunnan and Guizhou, Guangdong, and Fujian respectively.

Background

The early Qing emperors, short of troops to control the south, had enfeoffed the defecting Ming generals who had helped conquer it. By the 1670s the fiefs consumed a large share of imperial revenue, and the young Kangxi Emperor determined to abolish them. When the court moved to force Wu Sangui's retirement in 1673, Wu rebelled, proclaimed himself emperor of a new Zhou dynasty, and was joined by Geng Jingzhong and later Shang Zhixin, along with other rebels including Wang Fuchen and the Zheng regime on Taiwan.

Course and outcome

The rebels initially overran much of southern and southwestern China, but the revolt stalled: Wu failed to win broad support, his allies quarrelled and defected, and Wu died in 1678, soon after proclaiming himself emperor in Hengzhou. Qing forces under generals such as Cai Yurong gradually recovered the lost provinces; Shang Zhixin was forced to commit suicide in 1680, Geng Jingzhong surrendered and was executed in 1682, and Wu's grandson Wu Shifan killed himself as Kunming fell in 1681, ending the revolt. Its defeat consolidated Qing rule and ended the feudatory system.
