Prüfer domain

In commutative algebra, a Prüfer domain is a type of integral domain that generalises Dedekind domains to non-Noetherian settings. It is named after the German mathematician Heinz Prüfer. Equivalently, an integral domain is a Prüfer domain if every localization at a prime (or maximal) ideal is a valuation domain, or if every nonzero finitely generated ideal is invertible.

Properties

In a Prüfer domain every finitely generated ideal is cancellative, the lattice of ideals is distributive, and every overring (intermediate ring between the domain and its field of fractions) is again a Prüfer domain. Dedekind domains and valuation domains are examples; the ring of all algebraic integers and rings of entire functions are non-Noetherian examples. Bézout domains — in which every finitely generated ideal is principal — form an important subclass.

Characterizations

Numerous equivalent definitions exist: a domain is Prüfer if and only if the set of ideals forms a lattice under addition and multiplication (arithmetic rings in the sense of Claude Chabauty and later Wolfgang Krull), equivalently if for all elements a, b there exist elements s, t with (a,b) properties captured by the identity (a+b)(a∩b) = (a)(b). Prüfer domains are precisely the semihereditary integral domains, meaning every finitely generated ideal is projective.
