Individual fishing quota

Individual fishing quotas (IFQs), also known as individual transferable quotas (ITQs) when they may be bought and sold, are a type of catch share: a fishery management tool that allocates a portion of the total allowable catch (TAC) to individual fishers, vessels or companies.

Purpose

IFQs were introduced to end the "race to fish" that occurs when a fleet competes for a shared catch cap, which tended to produce dangerously short seasons, gluts, waste and overcapitalisation. By guaranteeing each participant a defined share, quotas give fishers an incentive to fish more carefully and to support conservation of the stock.

Implementation

New Zealand pioneered a comprehensive quota management system in 1986; Iceland, Canada, the United States (for example the Alaska halibut and sablefish programmes of 1995) and other countries have adopted versions of the approach. Where quotas are transferable they can become valuable assets, which has prompted debate about concentration of ownership, effects on small fishing communities and leasing ("armchair fishermen").
