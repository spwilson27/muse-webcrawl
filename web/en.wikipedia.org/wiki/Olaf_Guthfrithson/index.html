Olaf Guthfrithson (Old Norse: Óláfr Guðrøðarson; Old Irish: Amlaíb mac Gofraid; died 941) was a Norse-Gael king of the Uí Ímair dynasty who ruled Dublin from 934 and seized the kingship of Northumbria in 939, briefly restoring Viking rule at York.

Reign

Olaf succeeded his father Gofraid ua Ímair as king of Dublin in 934. His early reign in Ireland was marked by warfare against rival Norse and Irish kings, including the destruction of the rival Viking base at Limerick's sphere of influence and conflict with the high king Donnchad Donn. In 937 Olaf allied with Constantine II of Scotland and Owain of Strathclyde against Æthelstan of England, but the coalition was crushed at the Battle of Brunanburh, one of the most celebrated battles of the age, after which Olaf escaped back to Ireland.

Following Æthelstan's death in 939, Olaf returned to England and was accepted as king of Northumbria at York; he subsequently raided into the English Midlands, and by a treaty with King Edmund I he was confirmed in control of the north and parts of the Five Boroughs region, reportedly being baptised with Edmund as sponsor. His coins, struck at York, include the famous raven type associated with the Uí Ímair. Olaf Guthfrithson died in 941 — the Irish annals imply violence or illness while campaigning in Scotland — and Northumbria passed briefly to his cousin Olaf Sigtryggsson before being contested between English and Norse kings for another decade.
