Aaron Temkin Beck (July 18, 1921 – November 1, 2021) was an American psychiatrist and a professor in the department of psychiatry at the University of Pennsylvania. He is regarded as the father of cognitive therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), among the most widely practised forms of psychotherapy in the world.

Development of cognitive therapy

Trained as a psychoanalyst, Beck set out in the late 1950s to validate Freud's theory that depression results from anger turned inward. His studies instead showed that depressed patients' dreams and free associations were dominated by themes of loss and failure, leading him to focus on patients' conscious, automatic negative thoughts. From this work he developed cognitive therapy, first set out in Depression: Causes and Treatment (1967), built around identifying and testing distorted cognitions — the "cognitive triad" of negative views of the self, the world and the future.

Instruments and institutes

Beck devised several widely used self-report instruments, including the Beck Depression Inventory (1961, revised as the BDI-II), the Beck Anxiety Inventory and the Beck Hopelessness Scale. In 1994 he co-founded the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy in Philadelphia with his daughter, psychologist Judith S. Beck.

Later work and honours

Beck extended cognitive therapy to anxiety disorders, personality disorders, substance use, couples therapy and, late in his career, recovery-oriented cognitive therapy for schizophrenia. He received the Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award in 2006 and was among the most cited psychiatrists of the twentieth century. He died at home in Philadelphia in 2021, aged 100.
