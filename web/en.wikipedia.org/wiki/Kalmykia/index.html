Kalmykia

Kalmykia, officially the Republic of Kalmykia (Russian: Республика Калмыкия; Kalmyk: Хальмг Таңһч), is a republic of Russia located in the Lower Volga region of Southern Russia. Its capital and largest city is Elista. Kalmykia is the only region in Europe where Buddhism is the predominant religion, practised by the Kalmyks, a Mongolic people who migrated from Dzungaria in the early 17th century.

History

The Kalmyk Khanate was established in the Volga steppe in the 17th century and was absorbed into the Russian Empire in 1771, when part of the population returned to Dzungaria. A Kalmyk autonomous oblast was created in 1920 and raised to an autonomous republic in 1935. In 1943 the entire Kalmyk population was deported to Siberia by Stalin's regime and the republic abolished; it was restored in 1957 after the survivors were allowed to return.

Geography

Kalmykia covers about 76,100 km² of steppe and semi-desert between the Volga and the Caspian Sea. Its economy is based on agriculture, sheep and cattle breeding, and oil and gas extraction. The republic is also noted for chess: long-serving president Kirsan Ilyumzhinov, who headed the world chess federation FIDE, built Chess City in Elista and hosted the 1998 Chess Olympiad.
