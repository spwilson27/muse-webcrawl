Ringwork

A ringwork is a form of fortified settlement or early castle consisting of a circular or oval area defended by a bank and external ditch, sometimes with a palisade. Ringworks are characteristic of the 11th and 12th centuries in Britain, Ireland, and Normandy, and are often considered predecessors of, or alternatives to, the motte-and-bailey castle.

Characteristics

Unlike a motte, a ringwork has no artificial mound: the interior is roughly level with the surrounding defences, and buildings — a hall, chapel, and service structures — stood against the inside of the bank. Ringworks range from simple enclosures under 0.1 hectares to large defended settlements of several hectares, and some earlier examples are Iron Age or early medieval in origin, re-used by Norman lords.

Hundreds of examples are recorded in England and Wales, and they are particularly numerous in Ireland, where they must be distinguished from the broadly similar native ringforts (ráths).
