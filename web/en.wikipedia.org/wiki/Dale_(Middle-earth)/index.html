Dale (Middle-earth)

Dale is a fictional city in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. A city of Men (the Bardings) situated in a valley between the arms of the Lonely Mountain (Erebor), on the River Running, it was a prosperous trading partner of the Dwarf-kingdom of Erebor until its destruction by the dragon Smaug, and was later refounded after Smaug's death.

History

Dale grew rich on trade with Erebor and the lands down the River Running to the Long Lake and Dorwinion. In 2770 Smaug descended on the Mountain, destroyed Dale, and its lord Girion was killed; the survivors fled to Lake-town (Esgaroth).

After Bilbo Baggins's companions provoked Smaug's attack on Lake-town in 2941, Bard of Dale — a descendant of Girion — slew the dragon. Bard then led the rebuilding of Dale and became its king. Dale flourished again in alliance with the Dwarves of Erebor under Dáin II Ironfoot. During the War of the Ring, Dale was overrun by Easterlings in the Battle of Dale (3019), in which King Brand fell alongside Dáin, but after Sauron's defeat the Easterlings were driven out and Brand's son Bard II became king.
