Times Square Ball

LocationOne Times Square, New York City
First dropDecember 31, 1907
Current ballGeodesic sphere, 12 feet (3.7 m) diameter, Waterford Crystal triangles, LED lighting

The Times Square Ball is a time ball located in New York City's Times Square. It is lowered down a specially designed flagpole atop the One Times Square building, commencing at 11:59:00 p.m. ET and reaching the bottom at midnight to signal the start of the new year. The ball drop has been held since December 31, 1907, and the phrase "ball drop" has become closely associated with New Year's Eve celebrations in the United States.

History

The first ball, made of iron and wood and lit by one hundred 25-watt incandescent bulbs, was built by sign manufacturer Artkraft Strauss and lowered for the 1908 celebration organized by New York Times owner Adolph Ochs. Successive balls grew lighter and more elaborate; the drop was suspended only in 1942 and 1943 because of wartime blackout restrictions, when revellers observed a minute of silence instead.

The current ball

The present sphere, introduced for the 2008 celebration and used year-round as a permanent display thereafter, is twelve feet in diameter, weighs nearly 12,000 pounds, and is covered with 2,688 Waterford Crystal triangles illuminated by tens of thousands of LEDs capable of displaying billions of colour patterns. The numerals for each new year are displayed in lights beside the ball.
