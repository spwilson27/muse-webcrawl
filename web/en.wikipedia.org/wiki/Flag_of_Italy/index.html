Flag of Italy

The flag of Italy (Italian: bandiera d'Italia), often referred to as il Tricolore, is a tricolour featuring three equally sized vertical bands of green, white, and red. It was first adopted on 7 January 1797, when the Cispadane Republic in Reggio Emilia chose the three colours for its banner, inspired by the ideals of the French Revolution and its own tricolour. The current form has been in use since 18 June 1946, when the arms of the House of Savoy were removed following the birth of the Italian Republic; the flag was formally defined by the Constitution of 1948.

History

The green, white, and red cockade and banners circulated among Italian patriots during the Napoleonic era. During the Risorgimento, the tricolour became the symbol of national unification; it was adopted by the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1848 with the Savoy shield at its centre, and became the flag of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861. Flag Day, the Festa del Tricolore, is celebrated on 7 January.

Interpretation

Popular interpretations link the colours to the Italian landscape: green for the plains and hills, white for the snow-capped Alps, and red for the blood of those who fought for independence. Religious interpretations associate them with the theological virtues: faith (green), hope (white), and charity (red).
