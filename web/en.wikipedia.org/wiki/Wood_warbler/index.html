Wood warbler

The wood warbler (Phylloscopus sibilatrix) is a leaf warbler that breeds across northern and temperate Europe and western Asia and winters in tropical Africa. It is one of the larger Phylloscopus warblers, with bright green upperparts and clean white underparts.

Description

The wood warbler is 11–12.5 cm long, with a prominent yellow supercilium, yellow throat and upper breast, white belly, and green wings with contrasting dark flight feathers. The sexes are similar; juveniles resemble adults.

Distribution and habitat

It breeds in mature deciduous woodland, especially oak and beech, with a closed canopy and sparse undergrowth. The breeding range stretches from Britain and France east to Siberia. It is a long-distance migrant, wintering in West and Central Africa.

Behaviour

The song is distinctive: a series of accelerating metallic notes ending in a shivering trill, often delivered from a branch or in a short song flight. The bird feeds on insects and spiders gleaned from foliage. The nest is a domed structure of grass and leaves built on the ground, usually among leaf litter.

Status

Although still widespread, wood warbler populations have declined significantly in parts of western Europe, including Britain, leading to conservation concern and targeted woodland research.
