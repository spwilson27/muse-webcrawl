Single (music)

In music, a single is a type of release, typically a recording of fewer tracks than an album or EP. A single can be released for sale to the public in a variety of formats, including 7-inch and 12-inch vinyl records, cassette, CD, and digital download or streaming. Historically, a single featured one main song (the A-side) backed by another track (the B-side) and served both as a product in its own right and as promotion for radio airplay and for a forthcoming album.

History

The origins of the single lie in early gramophone records. The 45 rpm 7-inch disc, introduced by RCA Victor in 1949, became the standard pop format in the 1950s, and singles drove the charts of the rock and roll era. In the 1960s and 1970s, the single was the principal vehicle of pop music, while albums grew in artistic importance. The 12-inch single emerged in the disco era, offering extended mixes for DJs, and became central to hip hop and dance music. Singles later appeared on cassette ("cassingles") and CD, before digital downloads — led by the iTunes Store in the 2000s — and then streaming reshaped the market in the 2010s, making individual tracks rather than albums the dominant unit of consumption.

Types

A lead single is released ahead of an album to build anticipation. Promotional singles are distributed to radio and media rather than sold. Double A-side singles give equal billing to two songs, famously including the Beatles' "Strawberry Fields Forever" / "Penny Lane" (1967). Split singles feature different artists on each side, common in punk and indie scenes.

In the streaming era

Chart compilers now count streams toward singles charts: in the UK and US, a set number of streams (or a formula blending ad-supported and premium plays) is treated as equivalent to one sale. The economics of streaming have encouraged artists to release singles frequently, sometimes weekly, and album track listings increasingly behave like bundles of singles.
