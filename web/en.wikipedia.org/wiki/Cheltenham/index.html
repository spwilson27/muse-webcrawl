Cheltenham

Cheltenham is a spa town and borough on the edge of the Cotswolds in Gloucestershire, England. It is known for its Regency architecture, its mineral springs, and the Cheltenham Festival of horse racing, held annually in March at Cheltenham Racecourse. The town had a population of roughly 115,000 at the 2021 census.

History

Cheltenham appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Celteham. Its modern fame began in 1716 with the discovery of mineral springs; a visit by King George III in 1788 established it as a fashionable spa resort. Grand Regency terraces, crescents and the Pittville Pump Room followed, built around the Promenade and Montpellier districts.

Culture and education

The town hosts the Cheltenham Literature Festival, the world's oldest festival of its kind, as well as festivals of jazz, science and music. Educational institutions include Cheltenham Ladies' College, Cheltenham College and the University of Gloucestershire. The Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) is based at Benhall on the edge of the town.

Sport

National Hunt racing at Prestbury Park culminates in the Cheltenham Gold Cup. Cheltenham Town F.C. play at Whaddon Road, and the town has a strong tradition of hockey, rugby and Olympic swimming.
