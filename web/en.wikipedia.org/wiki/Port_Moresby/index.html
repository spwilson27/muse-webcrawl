Port Moresby

Port Moresby (Pom / Moresby)
Country: Papua New Guinea
District: National Capital District
Population: approx. 400,000+
Elevation: 35 m

Port Moresby (Tok Pisin: Pot Mosbi) is the capital and largest city of Papua New Guinea. It lies on the shores of the Gulf of Papua, on the south-eastern coast of the Papuan Peninsula of the island of New Guinea. The city is at the centre of the National Capital District, surrounded by the Central Province, and is one of the largest cities in the South Pacific outside Australia and New Zealand.

The area was home to the Motu-Koitabu people for centuries. The harbour was named by Captain John Moresby in 1873 in honour of his father, Admiral Fairfax Moresby. Port Moresby became a strategic Allied base during the Second World War, following the Battle of the Coral Sea and the Kokoda Track campaign, which halted a Japanese overland advance toward the city in 1942.

Geography and climate

Port Moresby has a tropical savanna climate that is notably drier than the rest of Papua New Guinea, being in the rain shadow of the Owen Stanley Range, with a wet season from December to April. The city is not connected by road to most of the rest of the country; travel to other major centres such as Lae is by air or sea, and Jacksons International Airport is the nation's largest airport.
