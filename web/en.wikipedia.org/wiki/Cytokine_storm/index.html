Cytokine storm

A cytokine storm, also known as hypercytokinemia, is a severe, potentially fatal immune reaction consisting of a positive feedback loop between cytokines and immune cells, with highly elevated levels of circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines. It is a form of cytokine release syndrome. Cytokine storms can occur in a number of infectious and non-infectious diseases including influenza, COVID-19, Ebola, sepsis, graft-versus-host disease and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.

Mechanism

When the immune system encounters a pathogen, cytokines signal immune cells to travel to the site of infection and activate them, stimulating production of still more cytokines. In a cytokine storm this feedback escapes control: massive simultaneous activation of T cells and macrophages floods the body with cytokines such as IL-6, IL-1, TNF-α and interferons. The results include widespread endothelial damage, capillary leak, disseminated intravascular coagulation, acute respiratory distress syndrome and multi-organ failure. The damage is done largely by the host response rather than the pathogen itself.

Causes and historical episodes

Cytokine storms are believed to have contributed to the high mortality among healthy young adults in the 1918 influenza pandemic, and to deaths in H5N1 avian influenza and SARS. During the COVID-19 pandemic, a hyperinflammatory state with high IL-6 was identified in some severely ill patients, and immunosuppressive treatment with corticosteroids (notably dexamethasone) and IL-6 blockade improved outcomes in those requiring oxygen or ventilation. Non-infectious triggers include immunotherapy (see cytokine release syndrome), pancreatitis and certain genetic disorders of immune regulation.

Treatment

Treatment combines management of the underlying cause, intensive supportive care, and immunomodulation: corticosteroids, IL-6 receptor antagonists (tocilizumab), IL-1 blockade (anakinra), JAK inhibitors, and in some settings intravenous immunoglobulin or plasma exchange. Timing and patient selection are crucial, since suppressing immunity too early can impair pathogen clearance.
