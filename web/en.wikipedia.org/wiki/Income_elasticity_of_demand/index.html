Income elasticity of demand

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image placeholder: Engel curves]

In economics, income elasticity of demand (YED) measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded of a good to a change in consumer income. It is calculated as:

YED = (% change in quantity demanded) ÷ (% change in income)

Interpretation

  • Negative YED — inferior goods: demand falls as income rises (e.g. cheap staple foods, budget brands), as consumers switch to preferred alternatives.
  • YED between 0 and 1 — normal necessities: demand rises with income but more slowly (e.g. food in general, clothing, utilities).
  • YED greater than 1 — luxuries / superior goods: demand rises more than proportionally (e.g. restaurant meals, foreign holidays, premium cars).
  • Zero YED: demand is unresponsive to income (e.g. salt).

Uses

Income elasticity underlies Engel's law — the observation that the share of income spent on food falls as income grows. Firms use YED to forecast demand over the business cycle (luxury sellers suffer most in recessions), and governments use it in tax and welfare analysis; estimates vary by country and period, generally being higher for services and lower for basic goods in rich economies.
