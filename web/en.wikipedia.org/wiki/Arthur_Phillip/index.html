Admiral Arthur Phillip (1738–1814)

Admiral Arthur Phillip (11 October 1738 – 31 August 1814) was a British Royal Navy officer who served as the first governor of the Colony of New South Wales in what is now Australia. Phillip was appointed in 1786 as the commander of the First Fleet, a fleet of 11 ships which he led to New South Wales, where in January 1788 he established a penal colony, in disregard of the claims of the continent's Indigenous peoples to their ancestral lands.

Governor of New South Wales

After landing at Botany Bay and judging it unsuitable, Phillip moved the fleet north to Port Jackson, founding the settlement of Sydney at Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788. He led the colony through its precarious first years, contending with poor soils, food shortages, disease, and tensions with the Eora people, the Aboriginal inhabitants of the region.

Phillip retired in 1792 due to ill health and returned to England. He resumed naval service during the wars with revolutionary France and rose to the rank of Admiral of the Blue. He died at Bath in 1814.

Legacy

Numerous places in Australia bear his name, including Port Phillip Bay in Victoria, Phillip Island, the suburb of Phillip in Canberra, and several schools and statues. His role is commemorated each year around Australia Day, 26 January, the anniversary of the 1788 landing.
