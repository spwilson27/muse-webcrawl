The AFC Champions League Elite (ACLE) is an annual continental club football competition organised by the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) and contested by the top-division clubs of Asia. It is the highest tier of the AFC's restructured three-tier club competition system, above the AFC Champions League Two and the AFC Challenge League. The tournament is the successor to the AFC Champions League, which itself descended from the Asian Champion Club Tournament founded in 1967.

History

In December 2022 the AFC announced a major revamp of its club competitions. From the 2024–25 season the top tier was rebranded the AFC Champions League Elite, reduced to 24 teams, and given a greatly increased prize fund, with the winners receiving at least US$12 million. Saudi Arabia's Al-Ahli won the inaugural Elite edition in 2025, defeating Japan's Kawasaki Frontale in the final in Jeddah.

Format

Twenty-four clubs are split into two regional league-phase groups of twelve (West and East). Each team plays eight matches against eight different opponents in a "Swiss-style" league format. The top eight in each region advance to the round of 16, played over two legs; from the quarter-finals onward the tournament is staged as a single-leg centralised finals event in a host country (Saudi Arabia for the first editions).

The winners qualify for the FIFA Intercontinental Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup, and also enter the following season's competition as title holders.

Most successful clubs

Including predecessor tournaments, Saudi Arabia's Al-Hilal is the most successful club in Asian history with four titles, followed by South Korea's Pohang Steelers with three.
