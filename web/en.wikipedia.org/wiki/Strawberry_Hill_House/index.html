Strawberry Hill House, often called simply Strawberry Hill, is a Gothic Revival villa in Twickenham, London, built by the writer and antiquarian Horace Walpole from 1749 onward. It is the type specimen of the "Strawberry Hill Gothic" style and a foundational monument of the Gothic Revival, influencing architecture and taste across Europe. Walpole's novel The Castle of Otranto (1764), written there, is credited with launching Gothic fiction.

Creation

Walpole, youngest son of Prime Minister Robert Walpole, leased the small house in 1747 and purchased it in 1749. Over three decades he and his "Committee of Taste" — including John Chute and Richard Bentley — enlarged it in picturesque, asymmetric stages, adding battlements, pinnacles, cloisters, a round tower, and interiors modeled on medieval sources: the Gallery's fan vault echoes Henry VII's Chapel at Westminster Abbey; the Library's bookcases were copied from old St Paul's Cathedral; chimney pieces derived from medieval tombs. Interiors were richly colored, with the Holbein Chamber and the crimson-and-gold Gallery among the most celebrated rooms.

Collections and the press

The house held Walpole's celebrated collection of antiquities, curiosities, paintings, and miniatures, catalogued in his own Description of the Villa (1774, expanded 1784), printed at his private Strawberry Hill Press, which he founded in 1757 — the first private press in England. The press published Walpole's works, Thomas Gray's odes, and other limited editions prized by collectors.

Later history

After Walpole's death in 1797 the house passed to the sculptor Anne Seymour Damer and then to the Waldegrave family. The contents were dispersed in the notorious 24-day "Great Sale" of 1842. The house later served as part of St Mary's University, which restored it in partnership with the Strawberry Hill Trust; a major restoration costing about £9 million, supported by the Heritage Lottery Fund, reopened the house to the public in 2010. Many lost objects were temporarily reunited in the 2018–19 exhibition "Lost Treasures of Strawberry Hill."

Influence

Strawberry Hill popularized the application of Gothic forms to domestic architecture, freed designers from strict classical rules through its asymmetry, and — together with The Castle of Otranto — seeded the Gothic imagination in literature, art, and design.
