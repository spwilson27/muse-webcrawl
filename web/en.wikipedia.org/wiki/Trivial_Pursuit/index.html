Trivial Pursuit

Trivial Pursuit is a board game in which winning is determined by a player's ability to answer general knowledge and popular culture questions. Players move their pieces around a circular board and collect coloured wedges by answering questions correctly at designated spaces; the first to fill their piece with six wedges and answer a final question in the centre wins.

History

The game was created on 15 December 1979 in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, by Canadian Chris Haney, a photo editor for Montreal's The Gazette, and Scott Abbott, a sports editor for The Canadian Press. Legend has it that an argument over who was the better Scrabble player — and the discovery of missing pieces from Haney's Scrabble set — inspired them to invent their own game. Released commercially in 1981, Trivial Pursuit became a phenomenon in 1984, when more than 20 million games were sold in North America alone. The rights were acquired by Parker Brothers in 1988 and later passed to Hasbro.

Gameplay

The board has 72 squares arranged in a wheel with six spokes. Six categories, each with a colour, structure the question cards: Geography (blue), Entertainment (pink), History (yellow), Arts & Literature (brown or purple), Science & Nature (green), and Sports & Leisure (orange). Landing on a "headquarters" square and answering correctly earns a wedge of that colour.

Editions and adaptations

Hundreds of editions have appeared, including the Genus editions, junior and family editions, and themed versions based on films, television series and decades. The game has been adapted into television game shows in several countries and into numerous video game versions.

Controversies

In 1984, Fred L. Worth, author of trivia encyclopedias, sued the game's distributors, alleging that a substantial fraction of the questions were copied from his books; the suit was ultimately unsuccessful after the defendants argued that facts themselves are not copyrightable. Separately, early player David Wall won publicity by spotting an error in the first edition's cards.
