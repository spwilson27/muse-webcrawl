Genrō

The genrō (元老, "elder statesmen") were a small group of unofficial advisers to the Emperor of Japan in the Meiji, Taishō and early Shōwa periods. Drawn from the leaders of the Meiji Restoration — including Itō Hirobumi, Yamagata Aritomo, Matsukata Masayoshi, Inoue Kaoru, Ōyama Iwao, Saigō Tsugumichi, Katsura Tarō and Saionji Kinmochi — they wielded great behind-the-scenes influence.

Role

The genrō's most important function was recommending candidates for prime minister to the emperor, effectively choosing Japan's governments; they also influenced foreign and military policy. As they died off, the institution faded: Saionji, the last surviving genrō, died in 1940.
