Jones–Shafroth Act

The Jones–Shafroth Act (Pub. L. 64–368, 39 Stat. 951), signed on 2 March 1917, reformed the government of Puerto Rico and granted United States citizenship to Puerto Ricans. Named for Representative William Atkinson Jones and Senator John Franklin Shafroth, it replaced much of the earlier Foraker Act.

The Act created a bill of rights, an elected Senate, and separated executive, judicial and legislative branches, while the governor and other key officials remained presidential appointees and Puerto Ricans gained no voting representation in Congress. It also exempted Puerto Rican bonds from taxation. The citizenship grant came as the United States entered the First World War, making Puerto Ricans eligible for the draft.
