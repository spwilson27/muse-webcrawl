Peter Morgan

Born: Peter Julian Robin Morgan, 10 April 1963, London, England

Occupation: Screenwriter, playwright, producer

Known for: The Queen; Frost/Nixon; The Crown

Peter Julian Robin Morgan (born 10 April 1963) is a British screenwriter and playwright, best known for dramatising recent British history and the private lives of public figures. He is the creator of the Netflix series The Crown (2016–2023).

Career

Morgan first attracted attention with the television film The Deal (2003), about the rise of Tony Blair and Gordon Brown. His screenplay for The Queen (2006), depicting the Royal Family in the week after the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, won him wide acclaim and an Academy Award nomination; Helen Mirren won the Oscar for Best Actress as Elizabeth II. The same period produced The Last King of Scotland (2006), co-written by Morgan, and his stage play Frost/Nixon (2006), about David Frost's interviews with Richard Nixon, which he adapted into Ron Howard's 2008 film.

Other credits include The Damned United (2009), Rush (2013), about the Formula One rivalry of James Hunt and Niki Lauda, and the play The Audience (2013), again built around Queen Elizabeth II's weekly meetings with her prime ministers. The Crown, which Morgan created, wrote and executive-produced, dramatised the Queen's reign across six seasons and won numerous Emmy and Golden Globe awards.

Personal life

Morgan was born in Wimbledon, London, to a German Jewish refugee father and a Polish Catholic mother. He read fine art at the University of Leeds. He was married to Anna Carolina Schwarzenberg, with whom he has five children, and later had a relationship with actress Gillian Anderson.
