Victoria Memorial, London

The Victoria Memorial is a monument to Queen Victoria, located at the end of The Mall in London, directly in front of Buckingham Palace. It was designed by sculptor Sir Thomas Brock and architect Sir Aston Webb, built between 1901 and 1924, and formally unveiled in 1911. At 25 metres (82 ft) tall it remains the tallest monument to a monarch in the United Kingdom.

Design

At the centre, on a large pylon, sits a 4.3-metre seated statue of Queen Victoria facing down The Mall. Around the base are figures of Constancy, Courage, the Motherhood of the Empire, Justice, Science and Art, Industry and Agriculture, and Peace and Progress. At the pinnacle stands a gilded Winged Victory. Corner groups in bronze represent lions and allegorical figures, and surrounding pools, gates and the Memorial Gardens complete the scheme, funded by subscriptions across the Empire.

History

Proposed after Victoria's death in 1901, the memorial anchored Webb's refronting of Buckingham Palace and the ceremonial Mall. King George V unveiled it in 1911; finishing work, including the bronze groups, continued into the 1920s. It is Grade I listed and a focal point for royal ceremonies and public gatherings.
