Banach–Tarski paradox

Banach–Tarski paradox
FieldSet-theoretic geometry
AuthorsStefan Banach, Alfred Tarski (1924)
Depends onAxiom of choice
ResultA ball can be decomposed and reassembled into two balls of the same size

The Banach–Tarski paradox is a theorem in set-theoretic geometry which states that a solid ball in 3-dimensional space can be split into a finite number of disjoint subsets, which can then be reassembled in a different way to yield two identical copies of the original ball. This "duplication" is possible only by using the axiom of choice, and the pieces are non-measurable sets — they cannot be assigned a volume — so no physical process can perform it.

Statement and sketch

Formally, a set E in Euclidean space is called paradoxical if it can be partitioned into two disjoint subsets each of which is equidecomposable with E using rotations and translations. Banach and Tarski proved in 1924 that the unit ball (minus its centre, handled separately) is paradoxical; five pieces suffice in a suitable formulation. The proof exploits the fact that the free group on two generators is paradoxical, and that the rotation group SO(3) contains such a free group.

Interpretation

The result conflicts with intuition about volume because the pieces are so irregular that volume is undefined for them. It is often cited in discussions of the consequences of the axiom of choice and of non-measurable sets, which had earlier been exhibited by Vitali (1905) and Hausdorff (1914).
