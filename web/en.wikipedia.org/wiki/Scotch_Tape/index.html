Scotch Tape

Scotch Tape is a brand name for pressure-sensitive adhesive tapes manufactured by 3M, first sold in 1930. The name has become a genericised trademark in many countries for transparent cellulose or plastic film tape used for sealing, mending, and gift-wrapping. The Scotch brand now covers hundreds of tape products, from Magic Tape to packaging and masking tapes.

History

In 1925, 3M engineer Richard Guggenheim Drew invented masking tape for auto painters. Drew went on to develop a transparent tape using cellophane — a new moisture-proof film from DuPont — coated with a rubber-based adhesive. The first Scotch Cellulose Tape was shipped to customers in September 1930, during the Great Depression, when an inexpensive way to mend books, papers, and household items proved commercially successful. A popular story, repeated by 3M, links the name to a painter who, frustrated with an early two-tone masking tape's sparse adhesive, told Drew to "take this tape back to your Scotch bosses and tell them to put more adhesive on it" — "Scotch" then being slang for stingy. In 1939 3M introduced the now-familiar snail-shaped handheld dispenser.

Products

Magic Tape, introduced in 1961, is a matte-finish tape that becomes virtually invisible on paper and can be written on; it resists yellowing and is widely used for document repair. Other lines include double-sided tape, removable tape, gift-wrap tape, heavy-duty packaging tape, and archival products. The backing of modern transparent tapes is typically cellulose acetate or polypropylene rather than the original cellophane.

Cultural notes

Scotch Tape is notable in physics for the triboluminescence and X-rays emitted when tape is peeled in a vacuum — demonstrated in 2008 by UCLA researchers, who produced enough X-radiation from peeling tape to image a finger. The brand's plaid (tartan) packaging motif is a long-standing trademark.
