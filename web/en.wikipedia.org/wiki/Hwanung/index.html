Hwanung

Hwanung (Korean: 환웅; Hanja: 桓雄) is a central figure in Korean mythology and in the Dangun creation myth, the founding legend of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom. He is the son of Hwanin, the "Lord of Heaven" (identified in some traditions with the Buddhist Indra), and the father of Dangun Wanggeom, the legendary founder of Korea.

The myth

According to the Samguk Yusa (13th century) and the Jewang Un-gi, Hwanung repeatedly longed to descend from heaven to live in the human world. His father, perceiving his son's wish, surveyed the three great mountains and chose Mount Taebaek as a place that would benefit humankind, giving Hwanung the three heavenly seals (cheonbuin) and permission to rule the earthly realm.

Hwanung descended with 3,000 followers to the summit of Mount Taebaek, beneath a sacred sandalwood tree, and established the "Divine City" (Sinsi). With his ministers of wind, rain, and clouds, he promulgated laws and taught humans some 360 arts, including agriculture, medicine, and moral conduct.

In the best-known episode, a bear and a tiger prayed to Hwanung to be made human. He gave them sacred mugwort and garlic and told them to avoid sunlight for one hundred days. The tiger gave up, but the bear endured, was transformed into a woman named Ungnyeo ("bear woman"), and later married Hwanung. Their son was Dangun Wanggeom, who in 2333 BCE — by traditional reckoning — founded the kingdom of Gojoseon.

Legacy

The Dangun myth, with Hwanung as the culture-bringing intermediary between heaven and earth, became a cornerstone of Korean national identity, especially during and after the Japanese colonial period, and inspired the new religion of Daejonggyo, which venerates Hwanin, Hwanung, and Dangun as a divine triad. Hwanung's descent is commemorated on Gaecheonjeol (National Foundation Day, 3 October), which marks the founding of Gojoseon by his son.
