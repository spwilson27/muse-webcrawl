Ada Deer

Ada Deer (August 7, 1935 – August 15, 2023) was an American social worker, educator, and Native American rights activist of the Menominee Nation of Wisconsin. She led the movement that reversed the termination of the Menominee tribe, and in 1993 became the first Native American woman to serve as Assistant Secretary of the Interior for Indian Affairs, heading the Bureau of Indian Affairs.

Early life and education

Deer was born on the Menominee reservation in Keshena, Wisconsin. She earned a bachelor's degree from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1957 and a master's degree in social work from Columbia University, and later completed coursework toward a doctorate.

Restoration of the Menominee

Under the federal termination policy, the Menominee Termination Act of 1954 ended the tribe's federal recognition and trust status, with devastating economic results for the community. Deer became a leader of DRUMS (Determination of Rights and Unity for Menominee Shareholders), the grassroots organization campaigning to restore federal recognition. Her lobbying culminated in the Menominee Restoration Act, signed by President Richard Nixon on December 22, 1973 — the first reversal of a tribal termination and a landmark in modern federal Indian policy.

Later career

Deer ran for office several times, including campaigns for Wisconsin Secretary of State and Congress, and taught at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where she helped build the American Indian Studies program. Appointed by President Bill Clinton, she led the Bureau of Indian Affairs from 1993 to 1997, overseeing the federal recognition of several tribes. She remained a prominent voice for Indigenous rights and education until her death in 2023.
