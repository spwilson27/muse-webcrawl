Claymation

Claymation
Type: Stop-motion animation
Medium: Plasticine / oil-based clay
Term coined by: Will Vinton
Notable studios: Aardman Animations, Laika

Claymation is a form of stop-motion animation in which characters and environments are built from malleable materials, typically plasticine clay. Each frame is recorded as the animator slightly repositions the figures, producing the illusion of movement when the frames are played back in sequence. The word is a portmanteau of "clay" and "animation".

History

Clay figures appeared in animation from the earliest decades of cinema. The technique was popularised in the United States by Art Clokey's Gumby (from 1955) and by Will Vinton's studio, whose work on the California Raisins commercials of the 1980s made "Claymation" a household term — Vinton trademarked the word in the late 1970s.

Technique

Unlike drawing-based animation, claymation figures are sculpted in three dimensions, often over wire armatures. Because clay sags and softens under hot studio lights, animators may use replacement animation — swapping in a series of pre-sculpted heads or mouths — for dialogue and expressions. Feature films typically shoot at 24 frames per second, requiring thousands of individual poses.

Notable works

Aardman Animations' Wallace and Gromit films, including The Wrong Trousers (1993) and Chicken Run (2000), are among the most celebrated examples. Other notable clay-animated features include The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993, predominantly puppet-based), Coraline (2009), Mary and Max (2009) and Shaun the Sheep Movie (2015).
