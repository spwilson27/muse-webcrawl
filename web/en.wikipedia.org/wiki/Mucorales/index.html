Mucorales

KingdomFungi
PhylumMucoromycota
OrderMucorales
Notable generaMucor, Rhizopus, Lichtheimia

The Mucorales are an order of fungi in the phylum Mucoromycota. They are fast-growing moulds with coenocytic (largely aseptate) hyphae, commonly found in soil, decaying plant material, and stored food. Many species are saprotrophs, while some are used in food fermentations and a few are opportunistic pathogens of humans.

Description

Mucorales reproduce asexually by sporangiospores formed inside sporangia borne on sporangiophores, and sexually by the fusion of gametangia to produce thick-walled zygospores. Their rapid growth and cottony colonies are familiar as common "pin moulds" on bread and fruit.

Human disease

Certain Mucorales — notably Rhizopus, Mucor, and Lichtheimia species — cause mucormycosis, an invasive infection that primarily affects immunocompromised people, such as those with uncontrolled diabetes. The fungi invade blood vessels, causing tissue necrosis, and the disease requires urgent antifungal therapy and surgery.

Uses

Species such as Rhizopus oligosporus are used to make tempeh, and other Mucorales are used in producing fermented bean curd and some traditional cheeses and sausages.
