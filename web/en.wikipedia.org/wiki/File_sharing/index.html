File sharing

File sharing is the practice of distributing or providing access to digital media, such as computer programs, multimedia (audio, images and video), documents, or electronic books. Common methods of storage, transmission, and dispersion include removable media, centralised servers on computer networks, Internet-based hyperlinked documents, and the use of distributed peer-to-peer networking.

History

Files were first exchanged on removable media such as floppy disks and, in early networked culture, through bulletin board systems (BBS) and Usenet. The advent of the World Wide Web and broadband internet made sharing practical at scale. In 1999, Napster, created by Shawn Fanning, popularised peer-to-peer music sharing; at its peak it had tens of millions of users before being shut down in 2001 after litigation by the recording industry. Successors included Gnutella, Kazaa, eDonkey, and BitTorrent (2001), whose swarming protocol splits files into pieces downloaded from many peers simultaneously. Centralised cyberlockers and streaming services later reshaped the landscape.

Types and technologies

  • Peer-to-peer (P2P) networks, where each participant both downloads and uploads — including BitTorrent, eMule, and Gnutella.
  • File hosting services and cloud storage (such as Dropbox, Google Drive, and earlier RapidShare and MegaUpload) for direct downloads and link sharing.
  • Local network sharing using protocols like SMB and NFS, and operating-system features such as AirDrop.
  • Removable media and "sneakernet" transfer.

Legal and social issues

File sharing is a neutral technology with many lawful uses — distributing open-source software, public-domain works, and one's own creations. Most controversy concerns the unauthorised sharing of copyrighted material. Landmark litigation includes A&M Records v. Napster (2001) and MGM Studios v. Grokster (2005), in which the US Supreme Court held that distributors of file-sharing software could be liable for inducing infringement. Studies of file sharing's effect on media sales have produced mixed results. Sharing copyrighted works without permission is illegal in most jurisdictions, though enforcement and exceptions such as fair use vary.

Impact

File sharing influenced the business models of the music and film industries, accelerating the shift from ownership of copies toward licensed downloads and subscription streaming. It also underpins legitimate distribution technologies, from Linux ISO torrents to content delivery in games and scientific datasets.
