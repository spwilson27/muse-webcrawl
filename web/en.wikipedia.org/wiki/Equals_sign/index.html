Equals sign

The equals sign (British English) or equal sign (American English), formerly known as the equality sign, is the mathematical symbol =, which is used to indicate equality in some well-defined sense. In an equation, it is placed between two expressions that have the same value, or for which one studies the conditions under which they have the same value.

History

The = symbol was invented by Welsh mathematician Robert Recorde in his book The Whetstone of Witte (1557). Recorde chose two parallel lines "because no two things can be more equal". Before Recorde, equality was expressed verbally (e.g. aequalis, often abbreviated æq.) or with various symbols; Recorde's sign, originally much longer, gradually prevailed over rivals such as Descartes's ∝ during the 17th and 18th centuries.

Usage

Beyond mathematics, the equals sign is used in chemistry to separate reactants and products from totals, in programming languages — where a single = is often an assignment operator while == tests equality — and in logic, linguistics, and informal writing. Related symbols include ≠ (not equal), ≈ (approximately equal), ≡ (identical/defined as), and := (defined as). In Unicode, the equals sign is U+003D.
