Birdman

Birdman, marketed in full as Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance), is a 2014 American black comedy-drama film directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu. It stars Michael Keaton as Riggan Thomson, a washed-up actor famous for playing a superhero, who attempts to stage a Broadway adaptation of Raymond Carver's short story "What We Talk About When We Talk About Love" to regain relevance and critical respect.

Plot

Riggan, haunted by the mocking voice of his Birdman persona, struggles through disastrous previews: a lead actor is injured, and his replacement, the brilliant but volatile method actor Mike Shiner (Edward Norton), upstages him. Riggan's daughter Sam (Emma Stone), fresh from rehab, berates his vanity. A scathing critic threatens a hostile review, yet the opening performance triumphs after Riggan, locked out of the theatre, walks through Times Square in his underwear and, in the final scene, uses a real gun on stage. The film ends ambiguously, with Sam smiling at an empty hospital window.

Production

The film is famous for appearing to be shot in a single continuous take, achieved through long takes stitched together and choreographed by cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki. Antonio Sánchez provided a largely improvised jazz-drum score. Keaton, who had played Batman, drew on parallels widely noted by critics.

Reception

Birdman premiered at the Venice Film Festival, was a critical and commercial success, and won four Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Director, from nine nominations.
