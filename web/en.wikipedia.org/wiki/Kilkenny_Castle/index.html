Kilkenny Castle (Irish: Caisleán Chill Chainnigh) is a castle in Kilkenny, Ireland, built in 1195 to control a fording-point of the River Nore and the junction of several routeways. It was a symbol of Norman occupation and in its original 13th-century condition would have formed an important element of the defences of the town with four large circular corner towers and a massive ditch, part of which can still be seen today.

History

The first stone castle on the site was built by William Marshal, 1st Earl of Pembroke, who had acquired the lordship through marriage to Isabel de Clare, heiress of Strongbow. In 1391 the castle was bought by James Butler, 3rd Earl of Ormond, and it remained the principal seat of the Butler family—Earls, Marquesses and Dukes of Ormonde—for nearly six hundred years. The Confederate Parliament met in the castle during the 1640s, when Kilkenny was the capital of Confederate Ireland.

The castle was substantially remodelled in the Victorian era, acquiring its present character as a comfortable country house with a long picture gallery wing. In 1967 Arthur Butler, 6th Marquess of Ormonde, sold the castle and its grounds to the people of Kilkenny for the nominal sum of £50, and it passed to the State.

Today

Managed by the Office of Public Works, Kilkenny Castle has been extensively restored and is one of Ireland's most popular heritage attractions. The surrounding parkland of about 21 hectares (52 acres) is open to the public, and the former stable yard opposite houses the Design & Crafts Council of Ireland and the National Craft Gallery.
