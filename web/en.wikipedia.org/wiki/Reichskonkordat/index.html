Reichskonkordat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Reichskonkordat ("Reich Concordat") is a concordat signed on 20 July 1933 between the Holy See and Nazi Germany, governing the relations of the Catholic Church in Germany with the state. It was negotiated by Cardinal Secretary of State Eugenio Pacelli (the future Pope Pius XII) and Vice-Chancellor Franz von Papen, and ratified in September 1933. The treaty remains in force in Germany today, having been upheld by the Federal Constitutional Court in 1957.

Terms

The concordat guaranteed freedom of religion for Catholics, the Church's right to regulate its own affairs, the protection of Catholic organisations, schools and religious orders, and the continued existence of theological faculties. Clergy were barred from membership of political parties and political activity. In return, bishops were required to swear an oath of loyalty to the state, and the Vatican accepted the dissolution of the Catholic Centre Party's political role in Germany.

Controversy

The agreement was controversial from the start. Critics argued that it gave Adolf Hitler's regime international legitimacy only months after taking power, while defenders maintained that it sought to protect German Catholics in a hostile state. The Nazi regime violated the terms repeatedly through the 1930s, prompting Pope Pius XI's 1937 encyclical Mit brennender Sorge. The concordat's long-term significance and Pacelli's motivations remain subjects of historical debate.
