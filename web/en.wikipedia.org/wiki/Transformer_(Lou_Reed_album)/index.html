Transformer (Lou Reed album)

Transformer
Studio album by Lou Reed
Released: November 8, 1972
Recorded: August 1972, Trident Studios, London
Genre: Glam rock
Label: RCA
Producers: David Bowie, Mick Ronson

The album cover, photographed by Mick Rock.

Transformer is the second solo studio album by American rock musician Lou Reed, released on November 8, 1972, by RCA Records. Produced by David Bowie and arranged by Mick Ronson, it is widely regarded as one of the defining albums of glam rock and as Reed's finest solo work.

Content

The album contains Reed's best-known songs: "Walk on the Wild Side", a portrait of figures from Andy Warhol's Factory scene, which became a surprise hit single despite its transgressive lyrics; "Perfect Day", later widely covered and featured in the film Trainspotting; "Satellite of Love"; and "Vicious", written at Warhol's suggestion. Songs such as "Andy's Chest" and "New York Telephone Conversation" drew on material from Reed's years with the Velvet Underground.

Reception and legacy

Transformer reached the top 20 in both the US and the UK. It has repeatedly appeared on lists of the greatest albums of all time, including Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums list, and is credited with bringing the subjects and style of the New York underground into mainstream rock.

Track listing

All tracks written by Lou Reed: "Vicious", "Andy's Chest", "Perfect Day", "Hangin' 'Round", "Walk on the Wild Side", "Make Up", "Satellite of Love", "Wagon Wheel", "New York Telephone Conversation", "I'm So Free", "Goodnight Ladies".
