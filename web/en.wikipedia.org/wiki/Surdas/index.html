Surdas

Surdas (c. 1478 – c. 1581) was a Hindu devotional poet and singer, traditionally described as blind, who composed lyrics in praise of Krishna in Braj Bhasha. His principal work, the Sursagar ("Ocean of Sur"), is one of the great classics of Hindi literature, narrating the childhood exploits of Krishna with tenderness and wit. Surdas is regarded as a saint and is counted among the eight principal disciples (ashtachhap) of the Vallabha tradition of Krishna devotion.

Born: c. 1478
Died: c. 1581
Occupation: Poet, singer, saint
Major work: Sursagar

Life and legend

Details of Surdas's life come largely from hagiographies such as the Bhaktamal and the Vallabhite varta literature, which describe him as blind from birth, a gifted musician who sang at the temple of Shrinathji at Govardhan, and a devotee instructed by the theologian Vallabha to sing the episodes of the Bhagavata Purana. Later tradition places him at the court of the Mughal emperor Akbar, though this is uncertain.

Works

The Sursagar exists in many recensions, growing over centuries from a few hundred poems to over five thousand attributed lyrics; scholarly consensus holds that many were added by later poets writing in his voice. His other attributed works include the Sursaravali and the Sahitya Lahari. Surdas's poems remain central to Hindustani devotional music, especially dhrupad and bhajan singing, and to the worship of Krishna in Braj.
