Wah-wah pedal

A wah-wah pedal, or simply wah pedal, is a type of electric guitar effects pedal that alters the tone and frequencies of the guitar signal to create a distinctive sound, mimicking the human voice saying the onomatopoeic name "wah-wah". The pedal sweeps a peak in the frequency response of the signal up and down, controlled by the player's foot on a rocking treadle.

History

The wah pedal was invented in 1966 by engineers at the Thomas Organ Company, which owned the Vox brand; Brad Plunkett created the circuit while working on a solid-state amplifier project, adapting a midrange boost circuit into a foot-controlled filter. It was marketed first to wind instrument players — trumpeter Clyde McCoy endorsed the original Vox model — but was rapidly adopted by guitarists. The Jen Cry Baby, built in Italy, and the later Dunlop Cry Baby became the most famous models.

Use

The effect became a signature of funk and psychedelic rock. Jimi Hendrix used the wah extensively, as on "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)"; Eric Clapton used it with Cream on "White Room"; and funk guitarist Wah Wah Watson made the rhythmic "waka-waka" guitar style famous. The pedal is also used in a fixed position as a tone filter, and variants include the auto-wah (envelope filter), which sweeps the filter in response to playing dynamics rather than a treadle.
