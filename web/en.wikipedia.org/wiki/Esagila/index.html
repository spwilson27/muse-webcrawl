Esagila

Esagila (Sumerian: É-SAǦ-ÍL.LA, "temple whose top is lofty") was a temple dedicated to Marduk, the chief god of Babylon, and his consort Zarpanitu. Located south of the ziggurat Etemenanki in Babylon (in modern Iraq), it was the religious heart of the city and the destination of the New Year (Akitu) procession along the Processional Way.

Description

The temple complex covered roughly 170 × 120 metres around courtyards, with the main shrine housing a gold statue of Marduk, enthroned, which the king ritually "took by the hand" at New Year. Herodotus described its treasures, including a large golden statue and table. The Esagila tablet, copies of which survive, gives the temple's dimensions and those of Etemenanki.

History

Esagila was rebuilt over centuries; it was restored by Esarhaddon after Sennacherib's sack of Babylon (689 BC), embellished by Nebuchadnezzar II, and still functioning under Seleucid and Parthian rule. Alexander the Great ordered its debris cleared for rebuilding. Its priesthood, astronomy and library traditions fed Babylonian scholarship into the Hellenistic world.
