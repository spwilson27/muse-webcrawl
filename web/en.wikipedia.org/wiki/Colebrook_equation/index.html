Colebrook equation

The Colebrook equation or Colebrook–White equation is a semi-empirical formula used in fluid mechanics to calculate the Darcy–Weisbach friction factor for flow in a pipe, accounting for both pipe roughness and the Reynolds number. It was developed from experiments by C. F. Colebrook and C. M. White in the 1930s.

Form

The equation expresses the friction factor implicitly as a function of the relative roughness of the pipe wall and the Reynolds number, valid for turbulent flow. Because it is implicit in the friction factor, it is solved iteratively or by explicit approximations such as the Swamee–Jain and Haaland equations. Its results are plotted on the Moody chart, widely used in hydraulic engineering.
