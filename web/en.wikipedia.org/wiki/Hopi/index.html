The Hopi are a Native American ethnic group who primarily live on the Hopi Reservation in northeastern Arizona, United States. They are one of the Pueblo peoples, descended in part from the Ancestral Puebloans, and speak the Hopi language, one of the Uto-Aztecan languages. The Hopi call themselves Hopisinom, often rendered "the peaceful people" or "peaceful, well-mannered people".

Homeland and villages

The Hopi homeland, called Hopituskwa, centres on three sandstone mesas — First Mesa, Second Mesa, and Third Mesa — rising from the desert of the Colorado Plateau. The village of Old Oraibi on Third Mesa, founded around the 12th century, is among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the United States. The Hopi Reservation (established 1882) is entirely surrounded by the much larger Navajo Nation, a geography that produced a long-running land dispute largely settled by legislation and partitioning in the 1970s–1990s; the village of Moenkopi near Tuba City forms a separate western district.

Culture and religion

Hopi society is traditionally matrilineal, organised in clans, with women owning the houses and much farmland. Farming — especially dry-farming of maize in sandy desert soils without irrigation — is central to Hopi life and spirituality. The religious calendar is organised around ceremonies conducted in underground ceremonial rooms (kivas), including dances by masked katsinam (spiritual messengers) such as the winter Soyal ceremony and the summer Niman "going home" ceremony. Intricate traditions of pottery (revived by Nampeyo in the early 20th century), basketry, weaving, and carved katsina figures (tihu) are renowned.

History

Spanish missionaries arrived in 1629 and established missions, but the Hopi joined the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, destroyed the missions, and thereafter successfully kept Spanish (and later Mexican) colonisation at arm's length — the Hopi mesas were never permanently re-missionised. Relations with the United States began in the mid-19th century; periods of coercive assimilation (boarding schools, the imprisonment of resisters at Alcatraz in 1895) contrast with the Hopi's persistent cultural continuity. A tribal government under the Indian Reorganization Act was adopted in 1936, though some traditional villages have maintained degrees of opposition to it.

The Hopi language is noted in linguistics and philosophy for its treatment of time and events, which Benjamin Lee Whorf used (in claims later much debated) as a prime example of linguistic relativity.
