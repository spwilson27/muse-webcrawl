Elasmoid scale

Elasmoid scales are the thin, flexible, overlapping scales found on most modern bony fishes (teleosts). They are composed of two layers: a superficial bony layer and a deeper fibrous layer of collagen. Unlike the rigid, enamel-covered ganoid scales of more primitive fishes, elasmoid scales are light and flexible, allowing efficient swimming while still protecting the body.

Types

Elasmoid scales occur in two main forms. Cycloid scales have a smooth outer edge and are typical of softer-rayed fishes such as salmon, carp and herring. Ctenoid scales bear tiny spines (ctenii) along their exposed rear edge, giving a rough texture; they are characteristic of spiny-rayed fishes such as perch and bass.

Growth

Elasmoid scales grow with the fish by the addition of concentric rings (circuli) around the focus. Closely spaced rings formed during slow winter growth produce annuli, which can be counted to estimate a fish's age — a technique widely used in fisheries science. Uniquely, the embedded front portion of the scale differs from the exposed rear portion, which carries the circuli and, in ctenoid scales, the spines.

Structure

The scale sits in a pocket in the dermis, with roughly one third exposed. The bony upper layer contains hydroxyapatite crystals, while the lower elasmodine layer is built of collagen fibres arranged in a plywood-like pattern that resists tearing.
