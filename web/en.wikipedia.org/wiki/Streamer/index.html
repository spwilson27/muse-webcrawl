An online streamer or live streamer is a type of social media influencer who broadcasts themselves online through a live stream to an audience. Streamers produce a wide range of content, most commonly playing video games, but also including "in real life" (IRL) broadcasts of daily activities, music, creative work, commentary and talk formats.

History

Live video streaming developed from webcam culture and early platforms such as Justin.tv (founded 2007), whose gaming section was spun off as Twitch in 2011. Twitch's rapid growth—leading to its acquisition by Amazon in 2014 for about $970 million—established streaming as a major entertainment medium. YouTube Gaming, Facebook Gaming and later Kick and TikTok Live followed, and the COVID-19 pandemic sharply accelerated audiences and the professionalisation of streaming.

Monetisation

Streamers earn income through channel subscriptions, viewer donations and "bits", advertising revenue, brand sponsorships, merchandise, and platform exclusivity contracts. A small top tier command audiences comparable to television programmes; figures such as Ninja (Tyler Blevins), Pokimane (Imane Anys) and Spanish-speaking streamers like Ibai Llanos became mainstream celebrities, and "VTubers" performing through animated avatars became a major subculture originating in Japan.

Culture

Streaming culture centres on real-time chat interaction, emotes, channel-specific slang and community rituals. It has also faced recurring controversies over gambling streams, copyright enforcement (notably DMCA takedowns for music), harassment, and working conditions, including very long broadcast hours and burnout among full-time creators.
