Peter Lawrence Buck (born 6 December 1956) is an American musician and songwriter, best known as the lead guitarist and a co-founder of the alternative rock band R.E.M.. His jangling arpeggiated guitar style — often played on a Rickenbacker — was a defining sound of 1980s college rock and a major influence on later alternative and indie music.

R.E.M.

Buck met vocalist Michael Stipe while working at Wuxtry Records in Athens, Georgia; with Mike Mills and Bill Berry they formed R.E.M. in 1980. Buck co-wrote much of the band's catalogue and played on all fifteen studio albums, from Murmur (1983) through Collapse into Now (2011), when the band amicably disbanded. He also played mandolin on some of the band's biggest hits, including "Losing My Religion".

Other projects

A famously prolific collaborator and record collector, Buck has been a member or contributor to numerous side projects, including The Minus 5 (with Scott McCaughey), Tuatara, The Baseball Project, Filthy Friends (with Corin Tucker), and work with Robyn Hitchcock. He released solo albums beginning with Peter Buck (2012) and has produced records for bands such as Uncle Tupelo. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with R.E.M. in 2007.
