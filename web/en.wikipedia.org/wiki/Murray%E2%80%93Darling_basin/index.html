Murray–Darling basin

The Murray–Darling basin is a large geographical area in the interior of southeastern Australia, draining through the Murray and Darling river systems. Encompassing about one million square kilometres — roughly one seventh of Australia's land area — across New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the Australian Capital Territory, it is the country's most important agricultural region, often called Australia's "food bowl".

Intensive irrigation, river regulation, prolonged drought (notably the Millennium Drought) and salinity have caused severe environmental stress, including mass fish kills and degradation of wetlands such as the Coorong and the Macquarie Marshes. Management is coordinated by the Murray–Darling Basin Authority under the Basin Plan of 2012, which set sustainable diversion limits amid continuing political controversy between states, irrigators and environmental groups.
