East India Company

East India Company (EIC)
Founded: 31 December 1600
Dissolved: 1 June 1874
HQ: Leadenhall Street, London
Charter: Granted by Elizabeth I

The East India Company (EIC) was an English (later British) joint-stock company chartered in 1600 to trade in the Indian Ocean, especially India and China. It grew to rule large parts of India with its own armies, accounting at its height for roughly half of world trade in commodities such as cotton, silk, tea, saltpetre and opium.

History

From trading posts at Surat, Madras, Bombay and Calcutta, the Company expanded after the Battle of Plassey (1757) under Robert Clive and victory at Buxar (1764), gaining revenue rights (diwani) in Bengal. Successive acts of Parliament curbed its autonomy; after the Indian Rebellion of 1857 the Crown took direct control (Government of India Act 1858), beginning the British Raj. The Company was formally dissolved in 1874.
