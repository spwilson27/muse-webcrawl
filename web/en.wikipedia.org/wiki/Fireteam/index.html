Fireteam

A fireteam or fire team is a small military sub-subunit of infantry designed to optimize bounding overwatch and fire and movement tactical doctrine in combat. Depending on the mission requirements, a typical fireteam consists of four or fewer members: an automatic rifleman, a grenadier, a rifleman, and a designated team leader; the role of each fireteam member is determined by the weaponry they carry, such as a machine gun for the automatic rifleman.

Fireteams are organized as the smallest military organization in armed forces such as the United States Army, the United States Marine Corps, and many other militaries. The concept of the fireteam is based on the need for tactical flexibility in infantry operations. A fireteam is capable of autonomous movement and combat operations, though it is typically a component of a larger squad.

History

The modern fireteam concept emerged during the Second World War and was refined during the Korean and Vietnam Wars. The idea built on earlier small-unit tactics, including the German Sturmtruppen of the First World War and the fire-and-movement tactics developed by various armies in response to the increasing firepower of automatic weapons on the battlefield.

RiflemanCarries a rifle; provides security and may carry anti-armor weapons

In the United States Army, a fireteam is typically composed of four soldiers. The team leader is usually a sergeant or corporal. Two fireteams, together with a squad leader, form a rifle squad of nine soldiers. The United States Marine Corps organizes its rifle squads into three fireteams of four Marines each, plus a squad leader, for a total of thirteen.

Tactics

Fireteams employ techniques such as bounding overwatch, in which one element moves while the other provides covering fire, and buddy-team rushes. The small size of the fireteam allows it to maneuver in terrain where larger units cannot, and to maintain control under the noise and confusion of combat.
