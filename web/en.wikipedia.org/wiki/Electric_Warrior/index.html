Electric Warrior is the second studio album by English rock band T. Rex, and the sixth including their work as Tyrannosaurus Rex. Released on 24 September 1971, it is widely regarded as a landmark glam rock album and the record that launched "T. Rextasy" in Britain, reaching number one on the UK Albums Chart.

Background and recording

Fronted by Marc Bolan, T. Rex had recently expanded from an acoustic duo into an electric four-piece. Producer Tony Visconti recorded the album in London and at Château d'Hérouville in France and during sessions in Los Angeles. Background vocals by former Turtles singers Flo & Eddie (Mark Volman and Howard Kaylan), string arrangements by Visconti, and saxophone by Ian McDonald of King Crimson enriched the band's boogie-rock sound.

Music and singles

The album blends rock and roll revivalism, boogie and surreal, sensual lyrics. It contains the UK number-two single "Get It On" (renamed "Bang a Gong (Get It On)" in the United States, where it reached the top ten) and "Jeepster", which reached number two in the UK despite being released as a single without Bolan's approval. Other tracks include "Mambo Sun", "Cosmic Dancer", "Monolith" and "Life's a Gas".

Reception and legacy

Electric Warrior is routinely listed among the greatest albums of all time; it placed highly in critics' polls by publications such as Pitchfork and Rolling Stone, whose 2020 list of the 500 greatest albums ranked it in the top 200. Its fusion of raw rock guitar, camp glamour and pop concision influenced punk, Britpop and indie rock, and "Cosmic Dancer" gained renewed fame through its use in the film Billy Elliot.
