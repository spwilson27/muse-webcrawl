Charles de Batz de Castelmore (c. 1611 – 25 June 1673), known as d'Artagnan, was a French musketeer who served Louis XIV and Cardinal Mazarin as a soldier, courier, and trusted agent. He is immortalized as the hero of Alexandre Dumas's novels The Three Musketeers (1844), Twenty Years After, and The Vicomte of Bragelonne, though the fictional character differs considerably from the historical man.

d'Artagnan, the real musketeer behind the legend

Early life

D'Artagnan was born at the Château de Castelmore near Lupiac in Gascony, the son of Bertrand de Batz, lord of Castelmore. Like many younger sons of the Gascon gentry, he traveled to Paris in the 1630s to seek his fortune, taking his mother's more distinguished family name, d'Artagnan. He entered the French Guards and later the King's Musketeers, serving in campaigns of the Franco-Spanish War.

Service to Mazarin and Louis XIV

During the Fronde, d'Artagnan became one of Cardinal Mazarin's most trusted agents, carrying secret dispatches and performing delicate missions. Louis XIV continued to employ him after Mazarin's death: in 1661 d'Artagnan personally arrested the disgraced finance minister Nicolas Fouquet at Nantes and guarded him during his trial and imprisonment. He later served as acting governor of Lille. In 1667 he became captain-lieutenant of the first company of Musketeers — effectively its commander and one of the most prestigious posts in the French army.

Death at Maastricht

During the Franco-Dutch War, d'Artagnan was killed by a musket ball to the throat on 25 June 1673 at the Siege of Maastricht, leading a charge on an outwork. Louis XIV, who had relied on him for decades, is said to have remarked that he had lost the one man he could trust.

The legend

D'Artagnan's posthumous fame rests on the semi-fictional Mémoires de M. d'Artagnan (1700) by Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras, Dumas's source. Dumas's swashbuckling hero — companion of Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, themselves based on real musketeers — fixed d'Artagnan in world literature, with dozens of film adaptations. A statue of d'Artagnan stands in Lupiac, where a museum honors him.
