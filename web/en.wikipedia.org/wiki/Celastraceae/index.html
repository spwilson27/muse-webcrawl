Celastraceae

The Celastraceae (known as the staff-tree or bittersweet family) are a family of 97 genera and some 1,350 species of herbs, vines, shrubs and small trees, belonging to the order Celastrales. The great majority of the genera are tropical, with only Celastrus, Euonymus and Paxistima widespread in temperate climates.

Description

Members of the family typically have small, regular flowers with a conspicuous nectar disc, usually four or five petals, and opposite or alternate simple leaves. The fruit is often a capsule, berry, drupe or samara, and the seeds are frequently surrounded by a brightly coloured fleshy aril that attracts birds, which disperse them.

Genera and uses

Notable genera include Euonymus (spindles), widely grown as ornamentals; Celastrus, including the American and Oriental bittersweets; Maytenus; Catha, whose leaves are the stimulant khat chewed in the Horn of Africa and Arabia; and Celastrus species used in traditional medicine. Khat's active alkaloid, cathinone, is a controlled substance in many countries.

Classification

In the APG IV system the family is placed in the order Celastrales within the COM clade of the rosids, and now includes the former families Hippocrateaceae and Stackhousiaceae, as well as the grass-like genus Parnassia, formerly treated separately.
