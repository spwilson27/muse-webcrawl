Almost complex manifold

In mathematics, an almost complex manifold is a smooth manifold equipped with a smooth linear complex structure on each tangent space. This structure is called an almost complex structure: it is a tensor field J of type (1,1) such that J2 = −Id at every point. Every complex manifold has an almost complex structure, but the converse is not true in general: an almost complex structure need not come from holomorphic coordinate charts.


An almost complex structure J on a tangent space, with J² = −Id

Definition

An almost complex structure on a manifold M is a linear map Jp : TpM → TpM on each tangent space, varying smoothly with p, such that Jp2 = −Idp. Because a real vector space admits such a map only in even dimension, an almost complex manifold is necessarily even-dimensional. The map J allows one to treat each tangent space as a complex vector space, with multiplication by i defined by J.

An almost complex manifold is a pair (M, J) where M is a smooth manifold and J is an almost complex structure on its tangent bundle. A map between almost complex manifolds is called (pseudo)holomorphic if its differential commutes with the almost complex structures.

Integrability

An almost complex structure is called integrable if it arises from the complex structure of a complex manifold, that is, if there is an atlas of charts whose transition maps are holomorphic and whose natural almost complex structure equals J. The obstruction to integrability is measured by the Nijenhuis tensor NJ, defined for vector fields X, Y by

NJ(X, Y) = [JX, JY] − J[JX, Y] − J[X, JY] + J²[X, Y].

The celebrated Newlander–Nirenberg theorem (1957) states that an almost complex structure is integrable if and only if the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes identically. On real two-dimensional manifolds every almost complex structure is integrable, so almost complex surfaces are Riemann surfaces; in higher dimensions this fails.

Examples

  • Spheres. Among the spheres, only S2 and S6 admit almost complex structures. The standard structure on S6, induced from the octonions, is not integrable, and whether S6 admits any integrable complex structure is a famous open problem.
  • Calabi–Eckmann manifolds are non-Kähler complex manifolds built from products of odd-dimensional spheres.
  • Every symplectic manifold admits a compatible almost complex structure, unique up to homotopy; this fact is central to Gromov's theory of pseudoholomorphic curves.

Types of forms and cohomology

On an almost complex manifold, differential forms decompose into types (p, q) according to their behaviour under J. The exterior derivative splits into components, and although the clean Dolbeault cohomology of complex manifolds is only available in the integrable case, analogues such as almost complex Dolbeault cohomology can still be defined.
