The International Football Association Board (IFAB) is the body that determines the Laws of the Game of association football. It was founded in 1886 by the four British football associations — the Football Association (England), the Scottish Football Association, the Football Association of Wales and the Irish Football Association — to agree a single standardised set of laws for international matches. FIFA, founded in 1904, joined the Board in 1913.

Voting

Decisions require a three-quarters majority. Each British association has one vote, while FIFA has four votes, representing its member associations; a change to the Laws therefore needs six of the eight votes. This structure means the British associations together can block a proposal, and FIFA cannot pass one alone.

Role and meetings

The IFAB meets annually: an Annual Business Meeting in the autumn considers proposed changes, and the Annual General Meeting, held in February or March, approves amendments, which normally take effect on 1 June. The Board has sanctioned major changes including the back-pass rule (1992), the introduction of goal-line technology (2012), the video assistant referee (VAR, 2018), and temporary experiments such as sin-bins for grassroots football. The Laws are published in a book of 17 laws, covering the field of play, the ball, the players, equipment, the referee, offside, fouls and misconduct, free kicks, the penalty kick and restarts.

History

Before 1886, each British association played to its own rules. The IFAB's first meeting, in Manchester on 2 June 1886, adopted a common code. When FIFA was formed and adopted the IFAB laws in 1904, the board's authority became global. The 1913 settlement admitted FIFA to the board with the voting split still in force today. A major rewrite of the Laws in simplified language was approved in 2016.
