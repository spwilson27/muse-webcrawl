Elan Valley

The Elan Valley (Welsh: Cwm Elan) is a river valley in the Cambrian Mountains of mid-Wales, in the county of Powys, lying to the west of the town of Rhayader. It is famous for the chain of reservoirs and dams built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries to supply water to the city of Birmingham in England, a major feat of Victorian engineering. The estate, about 180 square kilometres, is managed by Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water and is largely open to the public as the Elan Valley Estate.

The reservoirs

Following an Act of Parliament in 1892, Birmingham Corporation acquired the Elan and Claerwen watersheds and built four dams — Caban Coch, Garreg Ddu, Pen y Garreg and Craig Goch — along with an aqueduct carrying water by gravity some 118 kilometres (73 miles) to Frankley reservoir in Birmingham. Work began in 1893 and the scheme was opened by King Edward VII in 1904. A model village, Elan Village, was built for the workforce. A fifth, much larger dam, Claerwen, was completed in 1952, doubling the storage capacity.

Landscape and wildlife

The estate comprises moorland, oak woodland and blanket bog of high conservation value, much of it designated Sites of Special Scientific Interest and Special Areas of Conservation. Species include the red kite, which has become emblematic of the area, along with merlin, peregrine falcon and rare upland plants. The Elan Valley is also celebrated for its dark skies and has International Dark Sky Park status.

Visitor attractions

A visitor centre near Rhayader tells the story of the dams and the valley, and waymarked trails, cycle routes and the Elan Valley Trail follow the course of the former Birmingham Corporation Water Department railway. The drowned valley's history — including the farmsteads submerged beneath the reservoirs and the poet Percy Bysshe Shelley's links to the area through the Nantgwyllt estate — is a notable part of local heritage.
