Ossuary

An ossuary is a chest, box, building, well, or site made to serve as the final resting place of human skeletal remains. They are frequently used where burial space is scarce: a body is first buried in a temporary grave, and after decomposition the bones are removed to an ossuary, where they can be stored in a much smaller space.

The Sedlec Ossuary in the Czech Republic

History and examples

Secondary burial in ossuaries is ancient: in Second Temple Judaism, limestone ossuaries held the bones of the dead a year after burial, and Zoroastrian practice placed remains in ossuaries after exposure. In medieval and early modern Europe, overcrowded churchyards led to charnel houses; famous examples include the Sedlec Ossuary in the Czech Republic, decorated with the bones of tens of thousands, the Capela dos Ossos in Évora, Portugal, and the Paris Catacombs, created in the late 18th century to relieve overflowing cemeteries. The Douaumont ossuary at Verdun contains the remains of unidentified soldiers of the First World War.

Modern use

Ossuaries remain in use in parts of southern Europe and Latin America, and the term is also applied archaeologically to collective bone deposits in Neolithic and other prehistoric contexts.
