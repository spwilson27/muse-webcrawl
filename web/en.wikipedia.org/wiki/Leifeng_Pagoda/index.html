Leifeng Pagoda is a five-storey tower on Sunset Hill (Xizhao Shan) south of West Lake in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China. Originally built in 977 CE by Qian Hongchu, the last king of Wuyue, to celebrate the birth of a son by his favourite concubine Huang, it is famous throughout China as the prison of the White Snake in the beloved folk tale, the Legend of the White Snake. The sunset view of the pagoda — "Leifeng Pagoda in the Evening Glow" — is one of the traditional Ten Scenes of West Lake.

History

The original pagoda was a brick-and-wood structure: a seven-storey (later five visible storeys) brick core with wooden eaves and galleries. Japanese pirates burned the wooden exterior during a raid in the Ming dynasty, leaving the bare brick skeleton that stood, romantically ruined, for the following centuries and appears in countless paintings. In 1924 the weakened tower finally collapsed — an event that prompted Lu Xun's famous essay "On the Collapse of Leifeng Pagoda," in which he welcomed the fall of the symbol of feudal oppression that had crushed the White Snake.

For decades the site was a heap of rubble from which local people had carted away bricks (believed to ward off evil and help silkworms — a superstition Lu Xun noted with irony). Archaeological excavation of the ruins in 2000–2001 uncovered the pagoda's underground palace (a relic chamber), yielding treasures including a gilt silver Ashoka pagoda, bronze mirrors, coins and Buddhist scriptures.

The modern pagoda

The rebuilt Leifeng Pagoda, completed in 2002, was the first Chinese pagoda reconstructed with modern construction — a steel and copper-clad structure erected over the protected original brick foundations, which are displayed in situ in the basement levels. It has five storeys, stands about 71.7 m tall including its base, and is fitted with lifts and escalators; each floor displays artworks retelling the White Snake legend, including celebrated wood-carving panels. From its galleries there are panoramic views over West Lake toward the city.

The legend

In the folk tale, the monk Fahai, having defeated the white snake spirit Bai Suzhen, imprisoned her beneath Leifeng Pagoda, declaring she could not emerge until the lake dried and the pagoda fell — a curse undone in later versions by the filial devotion of her son. The pagoda's real collapse in 1924 was widely read as the legend fulfilled.
