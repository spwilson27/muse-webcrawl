Kitchen garden

A kitchen garden is a garden or part of a garden in which vegetables, herbs and fruits are grown for use in the kitchen, as distinct from an ornamental garden. The French term potager is often used for a garden that combines utility with ornamental layout. Historically, great houses maintained large walled kitchen gardens staffed by teams of gardeners to supply the household year-round.

History

Kitchen gardens descend from the medieval herb and monastery gardens and the Roman hortus. In the 17th century, Jean-Baptiste de La Quintinie created the Potager du roi at Versailles for Louis XIV, a landmark of intensive vegetable growing. In Britain, Victorian walled gardens reached a peak of sophistication, with heated glasshouses, pineapple pits and trained fruit trees on brick walls that stored the sun's warmth.

Design and practice

A kitchen garden is usually laid out in beds or rows for crop rotation, with paths for access and composting. Companion planting, succession sowing and the use of cloches, cold frames and polytunnels extend the season. Typical crops include leafy greens, root vegetables, legumes, alliums, culinary herbs and soft fruit. Home kitchen gardens range from a few containers to a full allotment; their popularity surged during wartime "Dig for Victory" and "victory garden" campaigns and again with modern interest in local, seasonal food.
