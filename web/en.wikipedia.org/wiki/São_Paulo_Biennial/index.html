São Paulo Biennial

Founded1951, by Ciccillo Matarazzo
LocationCiccillo Matarazzo Pavilion, Ibirapuera Park, São Paulo, Brazil
FrequencyEvery two years

The São Paulo Biennial (Portuguese: Bienal de São Paulo) is an international contemporary art exhibition held every two years in São Paulo, Brazil. Founded in 1951 by the industrialist Ciccillo Matarazzo and modelled on the Venice Biennale, it is the second-oldest art biennial in the world and the largest in the Southern Hemisphere.

History

The first editions, organised by the São Paulo Museum of Modern Art, introduced Brazilian audiences to artists such as Picasso, Mondrian and Max Bill, whose prize-winning work in 1951 catalysed the Brazilian concrete art movement. Since 1957 the exhibition has been held in the Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion in Ibirapuera Park, a building designed by Oscar Niemeyer. From 1962 the Fundação Bienal de São Paulo has organised the show; the 1985 edition pioneered the "Grande Tela" open-plan hang. The event weathered censorship under the military dictatorship and periodic financial crises, and remains Brazil's flagship contemporary art exhibition, typically drawing several hundred thousand visitors with free admission.

Format

Early editions were organised by national representations with prizes, but from the 1980s onward thematic curatorship by an invited chief curator became dominant, following the model of documenta. Recent editions have emphasised Afro-Brazilian and indigenous art and global South perspectives.
