Districts of the Czech Republic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Districts of the Czech Republic are territorial units known in Czech as okresy (singular okres). The country is divided into 76 districts, which sit below the 13 regions (kraje) and the capital city of Prague, and above the municipalities. Prague itself is not divided into districts in this system; it is treated as both a region and, for some statistical purposes, as a single district.

History and role

Districts were introduced in the nineteenth century under Austrian administration and were retained by Czechoslovakia. From 1960 the communist-era system had around 75 districts with their own district national committees. In the Czech Republic, district offices (okresní úřady) were abolished on 1 January 2003 as part of public administration reform, and most of their functions passed either to the regions or to about 205 designated municipalities with extended powers. Districts survive as territorial and statistical units used by courts, police, and state administration, and their boundaries generally follow those of the former district offices.
