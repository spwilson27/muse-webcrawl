Madurese language

Madurese is a language of the Madurese people of Madura Island and eastern Java, Indonesia, belonging to the Austronesian language family. Despite the official promotion of Indonesian, it remains widely spoken, with roughly 13–15 million speakers, making it one of the most spoken regional languages in the country.

Characteristics

Madurese has a system of speech levels reflecting social hierarchy, analogous to Javanese, its larger neighbour. It is notable phonologically for aspirated and voiced-aspirated consonant contrasts and for harmony effects in its vowels.

Writing

The language has historically been written in the Javanese-derived Carakan script and in Pegon (Arabic-based) script; today the Latin alphabet predominates.
