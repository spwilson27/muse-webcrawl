Independence, California

County: Inyo County, California

Area: approx. 4.9 sq mi (12.6 km²)

Elevation: 3,930 ft (1,198 m)

Population (2020): 593

Independence is a census-designated place and the county seat of Inyo County, California, United States. It lies in the Owens Valley at the foot of the Sierra Nevada, along U.S. Route 395, about 41 miles (66 km) south of Bishop. The population was 593 at the 2020 census.

History

The settlement traces its origin to Camp Independence, a U.S. Army post established on 4 July 1862 during the Owens Valley Indian War; the name commemorated the date of its founding. When Inyo County was created in 1866, Independence became its county seat. The town grew as a supply centre for mining districts in the surrounding mountains, and the Carson and Colorado Railroad reached the area in the 1880s (serving nearby Kearsarge/Independence station).

Geography and sights

Independence sits at an elevation of nearly 3,930 feet (1,198 m). Nearby attractions include the Eastern California Museum, the home of writer Mary Austin (author of The Land of Little Rain), and Mount Williamson, one of California's highest peaks, visible to the west. The Manzanar National Historic Site, a World War II Japanese-American incarceration camp, lies about 5 miles south of town.

Demographics

At recent censuses the community has numbered in the high hundreds; most residents are employed in county government, services, and tourism connected with recreation in the Sierra Nevada and Owens Valley.
