Globular protein

In biochemistry, globular proteins or spheroproteins are spherical ("globe-like") proteins and are one of the common protein types. Globular proteins are somewhat water-soluble (forming colloids in water), unlike the fibrous or membrane proteins. There are multiple fold classes of globular proteins, since there are many different architectures that can fold into a roughly spherical shape.

The term globin can refer more specifically to proteins including the globin fold. Globular proteins often have multiple roles: many enzymes are globular, as are most antibodies, some hormones, and transport proteins such as serum albumin and haemoglobin. Their solubility arises because hydrophobic amino acid residues are buried in the protein core while hydrophilic residues are exposed to solvent.

Examples of globular proteins include myoglobin, the first protein to have its structure solved by X-ray crystallography, and the enzyme triosephosphate isomerase. Globular proteins play a central role in metabolism because of their catalytic and regulatory functions.
