Dark fantasy

Dark fantasy is a subgenre of fantasy literary, artistic, and cinematic works that incorporates disturbing and frightening themes. The term can be used broadly to refer to fantastical works that have a dark, gloomy atmosphere or a sense of horror and dread.

Definition

Dark fantasy combines elements of fantasy with elements of horror. Definitions vary: some critics apply the term to fantasy works with supernatural horror elements, while others use it for fantasy set in bleak, morally ambiguous worlds where protagonists are anti-heroes and happy endings are not guaranteed. The related term "grimdark," drawn from the tagline of the Warhammer 40,000 universe, is often used for the most nihilistic examples of the mode.

History

Early examples include the weird fiction of writers such as Clark Ashton Smith and the sword-and-sorcery tales of Robert E. Howard, whose Conan stories mixed adventure with cosmic horror. Karl Edward Wagner's Kane stories and Michael Moorcock's Elric saga — whose doomed albino sorcerer-emperor became an archetype — were central to the genre's development in the 1960s and 1970s. Later landmarks include Glen Cook's The Black Company, Stephen R. Donaldson's Thomas Covenant novels, Clive Barker's Weaveworld, and George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire.

In other media

In games, dark fantasy is exemplified by the Dark Souls series, Bloodborne, Diablo, and the tabletop worlds of Warhammer and Ravenloft. In anime and manga, Kentaro Miura's Berserk is widely considered the defining dark fantasy work.
