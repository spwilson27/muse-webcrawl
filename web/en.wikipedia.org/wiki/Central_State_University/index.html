Central State University (CSU) is a public, historically Black land-grant university in Wilberforce, Ohio, United States. It is the only public historically Black university (HBCU) in Ohio and one of the oldest institutions of higher education for African Americans in the country.

History

The university originated in 1887, when the Ohio legislature established a department for the education of Black students at Wilberforce University, a private institution founded by the African Methodist Episcopal Church. The department grew into a separate state-supported normal and industrial school, and in 1941 it was formally separated as Wilberforce State College. It became Central State College in 1947 and gained university status in 1965. In 2014, Central State was designated a land-grant institution under the Second Morrill Act of 1890, making it an 1890 land-grant university.

Academics

Central State offers undergraduate and graduate programs through colleges including Arts and Sciences, Business, Education, and Engineering, Science, Technology and Agriculture. Its jazz studies program and its marching band, the Invincible Marching Marauders, are nationally recognised. The university's athletics teams compete in NCAA Division II as the Marauders, primarily in the Southern Intercollegiate Athletic Conference (SIAC); its football team won NAIA national championships in the 1990s.
