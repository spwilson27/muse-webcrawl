Enix

Enix Corporation was a Japanese video game developer and publisher, established in September 1975 by Yasuhiro Fukushima, that produced the smash-hit Dragon Quest series of role-playing video games. Enix is also known for its manga magazines and anime productions through its publishing imprint Gangan Comics, which serialised series such as Fullmetal Alchemist. On 1 April 2003, Enix merged with video game developer Square to form Square Enix.

History

The company began in 1975 as Eidansha Boshu Service Center, a publisher of real-estate advertising tabloids, and moved into software in the early 1980s, holding contests to recruit game programmers. Taking its name from a combination of "ENIAC" (the early computer) and "phoenix", Enix became one of the pioneering Japanese game publishers, though — unusually — it had no in-house development teams and instead commissioned outside developers.

Enix's breakthrough came with Dragon Quest (1986), designed by Yuji Horii with art by Akira Toriyama and music by Koichi Sugiyama, which established the template for the Japanese console role-playing game and became one of Japan's best-selling franchises. Other notable releases included the ActRaiser, Star Ocean, Valkyrie Profile and Grandia series (the latter published by Enix in Japan). In North America, Enix's subsidiary localised Dragon Quest titles beginning in the Nintendo Entertainment System era.

Merger

Amid rising development costs and Square's financial difficulties after the failure of Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within, Enix and Square announced a merger in November 2002. The merger took effect on 1 April 2003, with Enix the surviving legal entity renamed Square Enix; because Enix shareholders received more shares in the new company, the transaction was widely reported as Enix acquiring Square.
