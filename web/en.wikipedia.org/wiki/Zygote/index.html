Zygote

A zygote (from Greek ζυγωτός, "joined, yoked") is a eukaryotic cell formed by a fertilization event between two gametes. The zygote's genome is a combination of the DNA in each gamete, and contains all of the genetic information necessary to form a new individual organism. In multicellular organisms, the zygote is the earliest developmental stage. In humans the zygote is formed when a sperm cell fuses with an ovum (egg cell); it is a single cell with a complete, diploid set of 46 chromosomes.

Formation

During fertilisation, the sperm penetrates the egg's outer layers and its nucleus (the male pronucleus) approaches the egg nucleus (female pronucleus). The membranes of the pronuclei break down and the chromosomes combine (syngamy), restoring the diploid number. The resulting zygote then begins a series of mitotic divisions called cleavage, forming first a two-cell stage, then four, eight, and a solid ball of cells called a morula, followed by the blastocyst stage in mammals. In humans, the zygote travels down the fallopian tube for about a week while dividing, before implanting in the uterus as a blastocyst.

In other organisms

In fungi, sexual reproduction produces a zygote (often within a thick-walled, resistant zygospore in zygomycetes) that may remain dormant before undergoing meiosis. In plants, the zygote formed in the archegonium or embryo sac develops into the embryo of the sporophyte generation. Algae and protists show wide variety, including zygotic meiosis, in which the zygote is the only diploid stage. Identical (monozygotic) twins arise when a single zygote splits into two embryos, whereas fraternal (dizygotic) twins come from two separate zygotes.

Development and totipotency

The zygote and its earliest daughter cells are totipotent, able in principle to give rise to all cell types of the body and the extraembryonic tissues. Epigenetic reprogramming in the zygote resets most parental imprints (with the exception of imprinted genes) and activates the embryonic genome.
