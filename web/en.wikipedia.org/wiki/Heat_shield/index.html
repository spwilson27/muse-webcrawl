Heat shield

A heat shield is designed to protect an object from overheating by absorbing, reflecting or dissipating excess heat. In aerospace engineering, heat shields protect spacecraft during atmospheric entry, when friction and compression of air heat the vehicle's surface to temperatures that can exceed 1,500 °C.

Types

Ablative heat shields are made of materials that char, melt and erode away, carrying heat with them; examples include AVCOAT on Apollo capsules and PICA (phenolic-impregnated carbon ablator) used on SpaceX's Dragon. Passive/reusable shields use insulating ceramic tiles or blankets, as on the Space Shuttle orbiter, or metallic structures that re-radiate heat.

Principles

Blunt body shapes create a detached shock wave that keeps much of the heated gas away from the surface. Heat shields are also used in automotive, industrial and building applications to protect components from exhausts, furnaces and radiation.
