National Wild and Scenic Rivers System

The National Wild and Scenic Rivers System was created by the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act of 1968 to preserve certain rivers with outstanding natural, cultural and recreational values in a free-flowing condition for present and future generations.

Classification

Designated river segments are classified as wild (essentially primitive and unpolluted), scenic (largely undeveloped but accessible in places by road), or recreational (readily accessible, with some development). Designation generally prohibits new dams and federally assisted water projects that would harm the river's values.

Extent

The system protects more than 13,000 miles of over 200 rivers in 40 states and Puerto Rico — a small fraction of the nation's river miles — administered by federal land agencies and states.
