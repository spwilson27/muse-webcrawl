The Very Rev. Edward Frederick Sorin, CSC (6 February 1814 – 31 October 1893) was a French Catholic priest and missionary who founded the University of Notre Dame in Indiana in 1842 and served as its first president. He was a leading figure of the Congregation of Holy Cross in the United States, serving as its American provincial and later as superior general of the whole congregation.

Founding Notre Dame

Sorin arrived in Indiana in November 1842 with seven brothers of Holy Cross, taking possession of land offered by Bishop Célestin Hacquet de la Hailandière of Vincennes on condition that a college be built within two years. He named the site Notre Dame du Lac after Our Lady and the lakes on the property. Despite poverty, a cholera epidemic and a devastating fire in 1879 that destroyed the Main Building, Sorin rebuilt the university — the present Main Building with its Golden Dome dates from that reconstruction — and also founded schools, parishes and the journal Ave Maria. He is commemorated by a statue on campus and is regarded as the founder of the institution, whose president traditionally resides in Sorin Hall's lineage of leadership.
