Dr. Jeremiah Arkham, psychiatrist-director of Arkham Asylum.

Dr. Jeremiah Arkham is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, commonly in association with the superhero Batman. The nephew of Arkham Asylum founder Amadeus Arkham, he is the psychiatrist who runs the asylum for Gotham City's criminally insane, and is later depicted as descending into madness himself and becoming the second person to take up the identity of the villain Black Mask.

Fictional character biography

Jeremiah Arkham was introduced during the "Last Arkham" storyline in Shadow of the Bat (1992), written by Alan Grant and drawn by Norm Breyfogle. He inherited the directorship of Arkham Asylum from his uncle Amadeus, whose own descent into insanity founded the family's dark legacy. A rigid, severe clinician, Jeremiah rebuilt the asylum after its destruction and treated the inmates — among them the Joker, Victor Zsasz, and Mr. Freeze — with controversial and authoritarian methods. He features prominently in storylines such as Knightfall, in which Bane frees the asylum's inmates, and Dan Slott and Ryan Sook's miniseries Arkham Asylum: Living Hell (2003).

Descent into villainy

In the aftermath of the death of the original Black Mask, Roman Sionis, in the Battle for the Cowl era, Jeremiah — his sanity finally shattered by years among his patients — was revealed to have assumed the Black Mask identity himself, with a mask carved from Sionis's coffin lid, and to have used drugs and manipulation to run Gotham's underworld. He was eventually captured and confined as an inmate of his own asylum, a status explored in subsequent Batman titles and tie-in books.

In other media

Elements of the character influenced the director Dr. Jeremiah Arkham portrayed in some adaptations of the asylum setting, and he appears in reference works and episode guides for animated Batman productions, as well as in video-game lore connected to the Batman: Arkham series' depiction of the asylum and its staff.
