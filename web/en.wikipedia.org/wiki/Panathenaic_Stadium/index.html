The Panathenaic Stadium (Greek: Παναθηναϊκό Στάδιο), also known as Kallimarmaro, is a multi-purpose stadium in Athens, Greece. Built entirely of white Pentelic marble, it is one of the oldest stadiums in the world and the only major stadium built entirely of marble. It hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of the first modern Olympic Games in 1896.

Ancient history

The stadium occupies a natural ravine between the hills of Ardettos and Agra, southeast of the Acropolis. A simple racecourse was laid out here under the statesman Lycurgus around 330 BC for the athletic contests of the Panathenaic Games. Around AD 144 the wealthy benefactor Herodes Atticus rebuilt it in marble on a grand scale, with a capacity said to approach 50,000–80,000 spectators.

Revival

Excavated in the 19th century, the stadium was restored with funding from the benefactor Evangelos Zappas and later George Averoff for the 1896 Athens Olympics, where it hosted athletics and the finish of the marathon, won by the Greek runner Spyridon Louis. It again hosted events at the 2004 Athens Olympics — archery and the marathon finish — and remains the finishing point of the annual Athens Classic Marathon and the site of the ceremony at which the Olympic flame is handed over to each host city.
