Dizziness

Dizziness is an imprecise term that can refer to a sense of disorientation, a disturbed sense of a person's relationship to space, or a range of sensations including feeling faint, woozy, weak, unsteady, or off-balance. When the sensation is of spinning or the environment rotating, it is specifically called vertigo. Dizziness is one of the most common reasons adults visit a doctor.

The vestibular apparatus of the inner ear helps maintain balance.

Classification

Dizziness is commonly divided into four subtypes: vertigo (a spinning or whirling sensation), presyncope (a feeling of light-headedness or about to faint), disequilibrium (unsteadiness or imbalance), and nonspecific light-headedness, which may be psychiatric in origin.

Causes

Causes include benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), Ménière's disease, vestibular neuritis, dehydration, low blood pressure, anaemia, hypoglycaemia, anxiety disorders, and, less commonly, stroke or other serious neurological conditions. Many medications list dizziness as a side effect.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause. BPPV is often treated with repositioning manoeuvres such as the Epley manoeuvre; other cases may be managed with medication, hydration, vestibular rehabilitation, or treatment of the causal condition.
