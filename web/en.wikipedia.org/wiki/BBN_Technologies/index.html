BBN Technologies, founded in 1948 as the acoustical consulting firm Bolt Beranek and Newman, is an American research and development company based in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Long one of the most influential contract research organisations in computing, it played a central role in the creation of the ARPANET and, through it, the modern Internet.

Acoustics origins

Richard Bolt and Leo Beranek, professors at MIT, founded the firm to consult on acoustics; early commissions included the acoustics of the United Nations headquarters in New York and some of America's best-known concert halls. Architect Robert Newman joined soon afterwards, giving the firm its original name.

Computing and the ARPANET

From the 1960s BBN moved into computing, buying one of the first PDP-1 computers and doing influential work on time-sharing, artificial intelligence, and the educational programming language Logo. In 1968–69 BBN won the contract to build the Interface Message Processors (IMPs) — the first packet switches and, in effect, the first routers — that formed the ARPANET, the forerunner of the Internet. In 1971 BBN engineer Ray Tomlinson implemented the first networked email and chose the "@" sign to separate user name from host. The firm also pioneered packet radio and speech-recognition research, and produced systems such as the TENEX operating system.

Later history

BBN passed through ownership by GTE and Verizon before being acquired in 2009 by Raytheon, under which it operates as Raytheon BBN Technologies (now RTX BBN Technologies), continuing research in networking, quantum information, speech and language processing, and defence systems.
