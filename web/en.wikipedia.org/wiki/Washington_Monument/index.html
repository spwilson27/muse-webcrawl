The Washington Monument is an obelisk on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., built to commemorate George Washington, the first president of the United States. Standing 555 feet 5⅛ inches (169.294 m) tall, it is both the world's tallest predominantly stone structure and the world's tallest obelisk, and was the tallest structure in the world from its completion in 1884 until the Eiffel Tower surpassed it in 1889.

Construction

The monument was designed by Robert Mills in 1836 for the private Washington National Monument Society; his original, grander scheme envisioned the obelisk rising from a circular Greek temple colonnade. The cornerstone was laid on 4 July 1848, but funding failures, political turmoil — including the theft of a commemorative stone donated by Pope Pius IX in 1854 — and the Civil War halted work for over two decades at about 152 feet. Congress assumed responsibility in 1876, and the US Army Corps of Engineers under Lt. Col. Thomas Lincoln Casey completed the shaft in a simplified form between 1879 and 1884; because the marble came from a different quarry layer, a visible colour change marks the old stopping point about one-third of the way up. The aluminium pyramidion capstone was set on 6 December 1884.

Description and visiting

The hollow shaft contains an iron staircase of 897 steps and an elevator, and its interior walls are lined with 193 carved commemorative stones donated by states, cities, foreign nations and societies. Visitors can ride the elevator to the 500-foot observation level, with windows on all four sides. The monument stands at the centre of the Mall's cross-axis, ringed by fifty US flags. It was damaged by the 2011 Virginia earthquake, closed for repairs until 2014, and again for elevator modernisation, reopening in 2019.
