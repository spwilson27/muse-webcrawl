Fehling's solution

Fehling's solution is a chemical reagent used to differentiate between water-soluble carbohydrate and ketone functional groups, and as a test for reducing sugars and non-reducing sugars, supplementary to the Tollens' reagent test. The test was developed by the German chemist Hermann von Fehling in 1849.

Preparation and use

The reagent is prepared by mixing two solutions stored separately: Fehling's A, a deep-blue aqueous solution of copper(II) sulfate, and Fehling's B, a colourless solution of potassium sodium tartrate (Rochelle salt) in strong alkali. The tartrate complexes the Cu2+ ions and keeps them dissolved in the alkaline medium. Equal volumes are mixed immediately before use.

The test

A sample is added to the reagent and the mixture heated. Reducing sugars and most aldehydes reduce the copper(II) complex to copper(I), which precipitates as brick-red copper(I) oxide (Cu2O), turning the solution from deep blue through greenish and yellow stages to a red precipitate. Ketones (other than alpha-hydroxy ketones) and aromatic aldehydes do not react. The test was historically important for detecting glucose in urine in the diagnosis of diabetes.
