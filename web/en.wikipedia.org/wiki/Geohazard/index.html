Geohazard

A geohazard is a geological state or process that may lead to widespread damage or risk to people, property, or the natural environment. Geohazards are of the general category of natural hazards arising from the Earth's geological processes, including seismic, volcanic, mass-movement, and hydrological events. The study and mitigation of geohazards is a central concern of engineering geology, geotechnical engineering, and disaster risk reduction.

Types of geohazards

  • Earthquakes — sudden ground shaking and its secondary effects: surface fault rupture, soil liquefaction, and tsunamis.
  • Volcanic hazards — lava flows, pyroclastic flows, ashfall, lahars, and volcanic gases.
  • Landslides and slope failure — rockfalls, debris flows, avalanches, and slow-moving slope creep, on land and under the sea.
  • Ground subsidence and collapse — sinking of the ground due to groundwater or hydrocarbon extraction, mining, or the dissolution of soluble rocks forming sinkholes.
  • Flooding and erosion — riverine and coastal flooding, channel migration, and shoreline retreat.
  • Expansive and collapsible soils — clays that swell and shrink with moisture changes, damaging foundations.
  • Gas-related hazards — methane release from sediments, gas hydrate dissociation, and naturally occurring radioactive radon gas.

Assessment and mitigation

Geohazard assessment combines geological mapping, remote sensing, geophysical surveys, borehole investigation, and monitoring instruments such as seismometers, GPS stations, and inclinometers. Risk is commonly expressed as a function of hazard (the probability of an event of a given magnitude), exposure (people and assets in harm's way), and vulnerability (how badly they are affected). Mitigation strategies include land-use zoning away from hazardous ground, engineered defences such as retaining structures and drainage, building codes for earthquake resistance, and early-warning systems.

Notable events

Major geohazard disasters include the 1556 Shaanxi earthquake (the deadliest earthquake in recorded history), the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, the 1963 Vajont Dam landslide in Italy, the 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami, and the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami in Japan. Offshore, geohazard surveys are a routine part of siting oil and gas platforms, pipelines, and wind farms.
