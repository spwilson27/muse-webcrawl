Rugby union in Australia

The Wallabies in action

Rugby union in Australia has a history dating to the 1860s and is governed by Rugby Australia. The national team, the Wallabies, has won the Rugby World Cup twice, in 1991 and 1999, and Australia has hosted the tournament twice, in 1987 (with New Zealand) and 2003.

History

The first rugby club, Sydney University Football Club, was founded in 1863, and the Southern Rugby Union (later the New South Wales Rugby Union) in 1874. Queensland followed, and interstate matches began in 1882. The first Australian team toured Britain in 1908, winning Olympic gold at the London Games. Rugby league's breakaway in 1908–09 drew many players away, particularly in Queensland and New South Wales, where league became the dominant code.

Competitions

Australian professional teams compete in Super Rugby Pacific against sides from New Zealand, Fiji and the Pacific. At state level the traditional powers are New South Wales (the Waratahs) and Queensland (the Reds), with the ACT Brumbies the most successful Australian franchise. Club competitions such as Sydney's Shute Shield sustain the grassroots game.

Popularity

Rugby union competes with rugby league, Australian rules football and soccer for players and audiences, with its strength concentrated in New South Wales, Queensland and the ACT, and in private schools. The women's game has grown rapidly, with the Wallaroos contesting the Women's Rugby World Cup and sevens winning Olympic gold in 2016.
