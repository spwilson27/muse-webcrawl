Automated guideway transit

Automated guideway transit (AGT) is a fully automated driverless transit system in which vehicles — usually of light capacity and rubber-tyred — are guided by a fixed guideway and operate on exclusive rights of way. AGT includes most people mover systems and smaller automated metros. The term is used by the United States Federal Transit Administration (FTA) to describe these systems; in Europe similar systems fall under the broader umbrella of automated metro designations.

Characteristics

AGT systems are fully grade-separated, use small driverless vehicles or short trains running at high frequency, and are computer-controlled from a central facility. Vehicles are typically electric, often rubber-tyred on concrete guideways, which permits steep gradients and tight curves. Capacity ranges from a few thousand passengers per hour per direction for airport people movers to metro-scale systems such as Vancouver's SkyTrain or Singapore's Light Rail Transit lines.

History

Development began with the U.S. Urban Mass Transportation Administration's research in the 1960s, leading to airport systems such as Tampa International Airport's people mover (1971). Morgantown Personal Rapid Transit (opened 1975) is the largest and longest-running personal rapid transit (PRT) implementation. From the 1980s onward, France's VAL system (Lille, 1983) popularised AGT as city metro technology, and the concept spread widely in Japan, where AGT (shin-kōtsū shisutemu, "new transportation system") lines operate in many cities.

Applications

  • Airport people movers — connecting terminals at airports such as Atlanta, Denver, Singapore Changi and London Heathrow.
  • Urban feeder lines — e.g. Kobe's Port Island Line, Tokyo's Yurikamome, and the Miami Metromover.
  • Personal rapid transit — Morgantown PRT and Heathrow's ULTra system.
  • Automated metros — larger AGT-derived systems such as Vancouver SkyTrain (Bombardier Innovia Metro) and Kuala Lumpur's Kelana Jaya Line.
