America's Most Wanted is an American television program that profiles fugitives and missing persons, asking viewers to phone in tips. Hosted for most of its run by John Walsh, whose son Adam was murdered in 1981, the show is credited with helping capture more than 1,100 fugitives and reuniting dozens of missing children with their families.

Format

Each episode presents re-enactments of crimes, photographs and descriptions of wanted suspects, and a toll-free hotline. Tips from viewers led to captures sometimes within hours of broadcast. The program also campaigned for victims' rights legislation and child-safety measures.

History

The show premiered on the Fox network in February 1988 and became one of the network's longest-running programs. A brief cancellation in 1996 was reversed after a public and law-enforcement campaign. Fox ended the series as a regular program in 2011, after which specials aired until 2013; Lifetime aired additional episodes. Fox revived the series in 2021 hosted by Elizabeth Vargas, and again in 2024 with John Walsh and his son Callahan Walsh.

Impact

The program is regarded as a pioneer of reality television and of interactive crime-fighting media; its success inspired imitators in many countries.
