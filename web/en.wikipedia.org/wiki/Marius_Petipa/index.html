Marius Petipa

Born11 March 1818, Marseille, France
Died14 July 1910, Gurzuf, Crimea
OccupationBallet dancer, choreographer, ballet master
Known forThe Sleeping Beauty, Swan Lake (1895 revival), The Nutcracker

Marius Ivanovich Petipa (born Victor Marius Alphonse Petipa; 11 March 1818 – 14 July 1910) was a French and Russian ballet dancer, pedagogue and choreographer. He is considered the most influential ballet master and choreographer in history, and his productions form the foundation of the classical ballet repertoire.

Career in Russia

Born in Marseille into a theatrical family, Petipa danced in France, Belgium, Spain and the United States before accepting a post as premier danseur at the Imperial Theatres of St. Petersburg in 1847. He became chief choreographer in 1869 and remained the dominant creative force of the Mariinsky Theatre for more than three decades.

Major works

Petipa created or revived more than fifty ballets. His greatest successes came in collaboration with Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky: The Sleeping Beauty (1890), The Nutcracker (1892, with Lev Ivanov) and the 1895 revival of Swan Lake. Other landmarks include Don Quixote, La Bayadère, Giselle and Le Corsaire. His choreography is characterised by structural clarity, grand ensemble scenes, virtuoso variations and the codified vocabulary of academic classicism.

Legacy

Petipa's works were recorded in Stepanov notation and are still staged worldwide in versions derived from his originals. He retired in 1903 and died at Gurzuf in the Crimea in 1910.
