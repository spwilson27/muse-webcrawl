Messerschmitt KR200

The Messerschmitt KR200, or Kabinenroller ("cabin scooter"), is a three-wheeled bubble car designed by aircraft engineer Fritz Fend and produced by Messerschmitt from 1955 to 1964. Roughly 40,000 were built, making it the most successful of the Fend designs built by the German aircraft company while it was barred from producing aircraft after the Second World War.

Design

The KR200 seats two in tandem under a transparent bubble canopy, accessed by lifting the canopy and part of the body side. Steering is by a tiller-like handlebar. Power comes from a 191 cc Fichtel & Sachs single-cylinder two-stroke engine mounted over the single rear wheel, giving a top speed of around 105 km/h (65 mph) — brisk for a microcar of the time. To reverse, the engine is stopped and restarted running backwards, providing four reverse gears as well.

History

The KR200 succeeded the KR175 in 1955 with a larger engine and improved suspension. Variants included the KR201 Roadster and the four-wheeled Tiger (Tg500), a sports model. Production ended in 1964 as prosperity shifted buyers to full-size small cars such as the Mini.
