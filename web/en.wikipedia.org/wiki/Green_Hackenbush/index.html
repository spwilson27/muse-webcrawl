Green Hackenbush

Green Hackenbush is a two-player impartial game invented by mathematician John Horton Conway. It was originally known simply as Hackenbush. It can be played on a paper and pencil by two players.

Gameplay

Green Hackenbush is played with a diagram consisting of line segments called "stalks" or edges, connected at endpoints, with one distinguished horizontal line called the "ground". A move consists of deleting a single edge. Any edges that are no longer connected to the ground (directly or indirectly) are also deleted. The player who cannot move — because the diagram has been completely erased — loses. In the standard (normal play) version, the last player to move wins.

Analysis

Green Hackenbush is a partizan-free impartial game notable for the richness of its values. A single stalk with n edges stacked vertically is equivalent to a nim-heap of size n, and general positions correspond to nimbers obtained by the "colon principle" and the "fusion principle" developed by Conway. Arbitrary Green Hackenbush positions can take any nimber value, making the game a natural realization of the Sprague–Grundy theory.

In the original game of Hackenbush, edges can be colored red, blue, or green. Blue edges may be removed by either player (Left), red edges by the other (Right), and green edges by either. Green Hackenbush restricts all edges to green, making it impartial.

Variants

Related variants include Red-Blue Hackenbush (a partizan game whose values include dyadic rational numbers and some surreal numbers) and Red-Blue-Green Hackenbush. These games feature prominently in Conway's book On Numbers and Games (1976) and in Winning Ways for your Mathematical Plays by Berlekamp, Conway, and Guy.
