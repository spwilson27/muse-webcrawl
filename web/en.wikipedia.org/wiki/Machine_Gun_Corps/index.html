Machine Gun Corps

The Machine Gun Corps (MGC) was a corps of the British Army formed in October 1915 in response to the need for more effective use of machine guns on the Western Front in the First World War. The Heavy Branch of the MGC was the first to use tanks in combat, in 1916, and subsequently became the Tank Corps (later the Royal Tank Regiment).

Formation and organization

Before 1915, each infantry battalion and cavalry regiment operated its own small machine-gun section. The corps consolidated machine gunners into dedicated companies and squadrons: infantry companies armed chiefly with the Vickers gun for sustained indirect and barrage fire, a Motor Machine Gun Service using guns mounted on motorcycle combinations and armoured cars, a Cavalry branch, and the Heavy Section (later Heavy Branch) that crewed the first tanks at the Battle of the Somme in September 1916. Its training centre was at Belton Park, Grantham.

Service

The MGC served on all fronts. Its guns fired millions of rounds in planned barrage programmes — famously, at High Wood in August 1916, ten guns of the 100th Company fired nearly one million rounds in twelve hours. Machine gunners suffered heavy casualties — the corps earned the grim nickname "the Suicide Club". Around 170,500 officers and men served in the corps, of whom some 62,000 became casualties.

Disbandment

With the post-war reduction of the army, the Machine Gun Corps was disbanded in 1922, and machine-gun duties returned to infantry battalions (later including dedicated regiments such as the Royal Northumberland Fusiliers with machine-gun battalions in the Second World War).
