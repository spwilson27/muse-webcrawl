Antioch

Antioch on the Orontes (Greek: Ἀντιόχεια) was a Hellenistic Greek city founded near the end of the 4th century BC by Seleucus I Nicator, located on the eastern side of the Orontes River near modern Antakya in Turkey. It was the capital of the Seleucid Empire and later one of the largest cities of the Roman Empire.

History

Founded around 300 BC, Antioch grew into a commercial and cultural metropolis rivalling Alexandria and, later, Constantinople. It was a major early centre of Christianity: the disciples were first called Christians there (Acts 11:26), and it became one of the five patriarchates of the early Church. The city suffered repeated earthquakes, Persian invasions (notably in 540 AD), and Arab conquest in 637. Captured by Crusaders in 1098, it became the capital of the Principality of Antioch until Mamluk forces under Baibars destroyed it in 1268, after which it declined into a small town.

Archaeology

Excavations in the 1930s uncovered a celebrated corpus of Roman mosaics, many now in the Hatay Archaeology Museum. Ancient Antioch's street plan featured the colonnaded avenue described by Libanius, its most famous native rhetorician.
