Kengo Kuma (Japanese: 隈 研吾, born August 8, 1954) is a Japanese architect and professor at the University of Tokyo. One of the most prominent contemporary Japanese architects, he is known for an approach he calls "weak architecture" — dissolving buildings into their surroundings through natural materials, especially wood and stone, and fine partitions of light and air. He designed the Japan National Stadium, the main venue of the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games.

Career

Kuma studied architecture at the University of Tokyo and at Columbia University as a visiting scholar. He founded Kengo Kuma and Associates in 1990. His early works, such as the Kiro-san Observatory (1994) and the Water/Glass villa in Atami (1995), explored transparency and landscape. Major commissions in Japan followed, including the Stone Museum in Nasu (2000), the Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center in Tokyo (2012) with its stacked wooden house forms, and the Yusuhara Wooden Bridge Museum.

International work and the Olympic stadium

After Zaha Hadid's winning design for the Tokyo Olympic stadium was scrapped in 2015 amid cost concerns, Kuma's replacement design — a low-profile oval wrapped in wooden lattice echoing traditional Japanese temple eaves — was selected and completed in 2019. Internationally, his works include the V&A Dundee museum in Scotland (2018), clad in layered precast concrete inspired by cliff faces; the Hans Christian Andersen museum in Odense; and numerous projects in China, France, and the United States. In 2021 Time named him among the 100 most influential people in the world.
