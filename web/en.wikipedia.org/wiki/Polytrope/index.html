Polytrope

In astrophysics, a polytrope is a self-gravitating body in hydrostatic equilibrium whose pressure P and density ρ obey a polytropic equation of state:

P = K ρ1 + 1/n

where K is a constant and n is the polytropic index. Combined with the equations of hydrostatic equilibrium and mass continuity, the relation yields the Lane–Emden equation, whose solutions describe the internal structure of the body as a function of radius.

Applications

  • n = 0: constant-density body (incompressible limit).
  • n = 1: analytic solution; used for some planetary and neutron-star approximations.
  • n = 1.5: a fully convective star or gas giant with an ideal-gas equation of state, and a model for non-relativistic white dwarfs.
  • n = 3: the Eddington standard model of stellar structure for a radiative star; also describes relativistic white dwarfs. At n = 5 the solution extends to infinite radius.

Polytropes remain useful first approximations for stars, planets, and stellar remnants, and the polytropic process P·Vγ = constant appears generally in thermodynamics (e.g., in atmospheric science and engineering cycles).
