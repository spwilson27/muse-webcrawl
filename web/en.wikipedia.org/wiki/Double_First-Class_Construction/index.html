Double First-Class Construction

In graph theory and extremal combinatorics, double first-class construction refers to a constructive method in which a candidate extremal structure is built in two successive "first-class" stages: first the ground set is constructed to satisfy a primary extremal property, and then a second, compatible construction is layered on the same ground set to achieve a strengthened or refined property. The technique is used in the study of extremal graphs, hypergraph Turán-type problems and Ramsey–Turán theory, where direct one-step constructions rarely achieve the required density or independence conditions.

Overview

A construction is informally called first-class when it is optimal for its primary parameter — for example a graph with the maximum number of edges that avoids a given forbidden configuration — and can be described explicitly rather than merely shown to exist probabilistically. In a double first-class construction, the output of the first construction is not merely counted but used as a scaffold: the second stage adds, removes or recolours elements while provably preserving the first-stage property, thereby yielding examples that simultaneously satisfy two extremal conditions.

Applications

The method has been applied to lower bounds for Ramsey–Turán numbers, to constructions of dense graphs with small independence number, and to extremal problems for hypergraphs where the extremal examples are highly structured. Because both stages are explicit, double first-class constructions often produce infinite families of extremal examples whose parameters can be computed exactly, in contrast to random constructions which give only asymptotic bounds.
