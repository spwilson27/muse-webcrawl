Close helm

TypeKnightly helmet
PeriodLate 15th – 17th century

The close helm was a type of helmet worn by knights and heavy cavalry from the late 15th century into the 17th century. Unlike the armet, to which it is closely related, the close helm pivots its visor and bevor on the same points and opens differently.

Design

The close helm fully enclosed the head, with a skull piece, a pivoting visor with vision slits and breaths, and a bevor protecting the chin and neck, all rotating around common pivot points on each side. It was often worn with a gorget and used in battle, tournaments and, in highly decorated form, for parade armour.

Use

Close helms were standard equipment for men-at-arms and later for cuirassiers in the 16th and early 17th centuries, before lighter open helmets became dominant.
