Atlantic Recording Corporation (simply known as Atlantic Records) is an American record label founded in October 1947 by Ahmet Ertegun and Herb Abramson. Over the course of two decades, starting from the release of the first recordings in January 1948, Atlantic earned a reputation as one of the most important American labels, specialising first in jazz, R&B, and soul by artists including Ray Charles, Aretha Franklin, Otis Redding, and Wilson Pickett.

In 1967, Atlantic became a wholly owned subsidiary of Warner Bros.-Seven Arts, later part of Warner Music Group. The label expanded dramatically into rock and pop in the late 1960s and 1970s with signings such as Led Zeppelin, Yes, Genesis, Crosby, Stills & Nash, and ABBA (in North America).

History

Ahmet Ertegun, son of Turkey's ambassador to the United States, founded Atlantic with $10,000 borrowed from his family dentist. With producer Jerry Wexler joining in 1953, the label coined the term "rhythm and blues" marketing and built a house style at its New York studios, where engineer Tom Dowd pioneered multitrack recording techniques. Ruth Brown's hits earned Atlantic the nickname "the house that Ruth built."

In the 1960s, Atlantic distributed Stax Records releases in a partnership that produced classic Southern soul before ending acrimoniously in 1967. Aretha Franklin's signing that year launched her reign as the "Queen of Soul" with songs such as "Respect." Led Zeppelin's 1968 signing, reportedly one of the largest deals of its era, anchored Atlantic's rock era.

Later years

Atlantic remained a major force through the hip hop and pop eras, with artists including T.I., Bruno Mars, Ed Sheeran, Cardi B, Charli XCX, and Lizzo. In 2004, Atlantic Records Group was formed as a Warner Music Group umbrella, and the label has continued as one of the "big three" recording groups' flagship imprints. In 2024 Warner Music restructured Atlantic under new leadership as part of a broader reorganisation.
