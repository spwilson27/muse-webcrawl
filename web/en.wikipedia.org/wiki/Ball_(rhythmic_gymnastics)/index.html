Ball (rhythmic gymnastics)

A gymnast performing a ball routine

The ball is one of the five apparatus used in rhythmic gymnastics, alongside the hoop, ribbon, clubs and rope. It is made of rubber or a synthetic material and, for senior gymnasts, must be 18 to 20 centimetres in diameter and weigh at least 400 grams.

Use in routines

Unlike other apparatus, the ball may not be gripped; it must rest in the palm or roll continuously over the body, which produces the flowing, dance-like quality characteristic of ball routines. Typical elements include rolls over the arms, back and legs, bounces, throws and catches, figure-eight movements, and balances in which the ball is held against the body without the use of the hands' grip. Routines are performed to music on a 13 × 13 metre carpet and are judged under the Code of Points of the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) on difficulty, artistry and execution. Dropping the ball or letting it come to an unintended stop results in penalties.
