Reinhart Koselleck

Born: 23 April 1923, Görlitz, Germany
Died: 3 February 2006, Bad Oeynhausen
Field: History, historiography
Known for: Conceptual history (Begriffsgeschichte)

Reinhart Koselleck (23 April 1923 – 3 February 2006) was a German historian, regarded as one of the most influential historians of the twentieth century. He was the leading figure of conceptual history (Begriffsgeschichte), the study of how basic political and social concepts change their meaning over time.

Life

Koselleck served as a soldier in the Second World War and spent time as a Soviet prisoner of war, experiences that shaped his thinking about historical time and crisis. After studying at Heidelberg and Bristol, he taught at the universities of Heidelberg, Bochum and Bielefeld, where he became a central figure of the Bielefeld school of social history.

Work

His dissertation, published as Critique and Crisis (1959), examined the political consequences of Enlightenment thought. As editor of the monumental lexicon Geschichtliche Grundbegriffe (Basic Concepts in History, 1972–1997), he developed conceptual history into a major method. In works such as Futures Past (1979), he analysed the "space of experience" and "horizon of expectation" and identified a Sattelzeit, a "saddle period" around 1750–1850 in which the modern meanings of key concepts crystallised.
