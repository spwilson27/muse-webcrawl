Hadrian's Wall

LocationNorthern England, Wallsend to Bowness-on-Solway

Hadrian's Wall (Latin: Vallum Hadriani) was a defensive fortification of the Roman province of Britannia, begun in AD 122 under the emperor Hadrian. It ran from the banks of the River Tyne near the North Sea to the Solway Firth on the Irish Sea, marking the northern frontier of the Roman Empire in Britain.

Construction

The wall was built of stone in the east and initially turf in the west, with a ditch to the north, a military road to the south, milecastles every Roman mile, two turrets between each pair, and sixteen large forts. It was manned by auxiliary troops, many recruited from elsewhere in the Empire.

Later history

After Roman rule ended the wall was quarried for building stone, but substantial stretches survive, notably around Housesteads and Vindolanda. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, and the Hadrian's Wall Path national trail follows its length.
