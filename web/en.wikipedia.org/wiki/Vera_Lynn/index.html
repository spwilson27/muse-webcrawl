Vera Lynn

Dame Vera Margaret Lynn (née Welch; 20 March 1917 – 18 June 2020) was an English singer and entertainer whose recordings and performances were enormously popular during the Second World War, earning her the title "the Forces' Sweetheart".

Career

Lynn performed from childhood and was singing with dance bands, including Joe Loss and Ambrose, in the 1930s. Her BBC radio programme Sincerely Yours and songs such as "We'll Meet Again" (1939), "The White Cliffs of Dover" and "There'll Always Be an England" became emblems of wartime morale; she toured Egypt, India and Burma entertaining troops. After the war she continued recording — "Auf Wiederseh'n, Sweetheart" topped the early American charts in 1952 — and remained a fixture of remembrance events for the rest of her long life.

She was appointed DBE in 1975 and a Member of the Order of the Companions of Honour in 2016. In 2009, aged 92, she became the oldest living artist to top the UK Albums Chart, and her centenary album repeated the feat in 2017. She died aged 103 in 2020.
