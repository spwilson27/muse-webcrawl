Bear

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bears are carnivoran mammals of the family Ursidae: large, stocky animals with powerful limbs, plantigrade feet, shaggy coats and an excellent sense of smell. Eight species survive — brown bear, polar bear, American and Asiatic black bears, sloth bear, sun bear, spectacled bear and giant panda — mostly omnivorous despite their order's name, with diets from bamboo (panda) to almost pure meat (polar bear).

Bears are solitary except for mothers with cubs, communicate by scent and scratch marks, and northern species pass winter in torpor (hibernation) — a state in which they neither eat, drink, urinate nor defecate for months, recycling urea and sparing muscle, a physiology studied for human medicine from Diabetes to spaceflight.

Polar bears and climate

The polar bear depends on sea ice to hunt seals; Arctic warming is its central threat, and it has become climate change's most famous animal symbol. Southern populations are already stressed by lengthening ice-free seasons.

Conflict and conservation

Bear–human conflict (crops, livestock, garbage, encounters on trails) is the main management issue for recovering populations in Europe and North America; the spectacled, sloth, sun and Asiatic black bears face habitat loss and poaching for the bile and gallbladder trade.
