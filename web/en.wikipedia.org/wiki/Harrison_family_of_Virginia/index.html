The Harrison family of Virginia is a prominent American political family descended from English settlers in the Colony of Virginia. It produced two presidents of the United States — William Henry Harrison and his grandson Benjamin Harrison — a feat shared only with the Adams and Bush families — as well as a signer of the Declaration of Independence and numerous governors, congressmen and judges.

History

The family traces its American line to Benjamin Harrison I (c. 1594–1648), who emigrated to Jamestown and became clerk of the Virginia council. His descendants built Berkeley Plantation on the James River, which remained the family seat. Benjamin Harrison V (1726–1791) signed the Declaration of Independence and served as Governor of Virginia; his son William Henry Harrison became a hero of the War of 1812, a US senator and the ninth president, dying in office in 1841 after only a month — the shortest presidency in US history.

Later generations

William Henry's grandson Benjamin Harrison, a Civil War general and senator from Indiana, won the presidency in 1888. The family's Virginia branch continued in state politics into the 20th century. The Harrison family is related by descent and marriage to other First Families of Virginia, including the Carters, and to presidents John Tyler and (through the Indiana line) others of the Virginia dynasty era.
