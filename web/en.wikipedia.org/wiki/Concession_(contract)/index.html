Concession (contract)

A concession or concession agreement is a grant of rights, land or property by a government, local authority, corporation, individual or other legal entity. In the public sector, concessions most often involve a private company (the concessionaire) financing, building and operating infrastructure — such as a toll road, bridge, airport, port, water system or railway — for a fixed period, after which the asset reverts to the public authority.

Toll roads are frequently operated by private companies under government concessions.

Types

Concession arrangements include build–operate–transfer (BOT) schemes, in which the concessionaire builds and operates a facility before transferring it to the state; long-term leases of existing public assets; and natural-resource concessions granting rights to extract timber, minerals or hydrocarbons from public land in exchange for royalties and fees. Historically, concessions were also granted by colonial powers and by states such as the Ottoman Empire and Imperial China to foreign companies, sometimes under unequal terms that generated lasting political controversy.

Terms and regulation

Concession contracts specify duration, tariff or toll-setting rules, performance standards, risk allocation and conditions for early termination or compensation. Because concessions often create private monopolies over essential services, they are typically awarded by competitive tender and regulated by sector-specific authorities.
