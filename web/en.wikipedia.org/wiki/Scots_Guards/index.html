Scots Guards

Active: 1642–present
Country: United Kingdom
Branch: British Army
Type: Foot Guards
Role: Mechanised infantry / public duties
Motto: Nemo me impune lacessit ("No one provokes me with impunity")

The Scots Guards (SG) is one of the five regiments of Foot Guards in the British Army, and the third regiment in order of precedence. Its origins lie in the Royal Regiment raised in Scotland in 1642 by Archibald Campbell, 1st Marquess of Argyll, for service in Ireland; it became a Guards regiment after the Restoration and was known successively as the Scots Foot Guards and Scots Fusilier Guards before receiving its present name in 1877.

Role

The regiment consists of a single regular battalion, which serves as mechanised infantry (equipped with Mastiff protected patrol vehicles) and rotates through public duties in London and Edinburgh, plus an incremental company maintained for ceremonial duties. Its soldiers wear the bearskin cap with a red-over-white hackle worn on the left side, and buttons arranged in threes on the tunic, reflecting its precedence. The regimental badge is the thistle within the Order of the Thistle, and its motto is Scotland's: Nemo me impune lacessit.

History

The Scots Guards have fought in most major British campaigns since the 17th century, including the Napoleonic Wars (at Waterloo as the Scots Fusilier Guards), the Crimean War, both World Wars — serving at Mons, the Somme, El Alamein, and in Normandy — and, in 1982, the Falklands War, where the 2nd Battalion famously captured Mount Tumbledown. In recent decades the regiment has deployed to Northern Ireland, the Gulf War, Iraq, and Afghanistan.

Pipes and Drums

The regiment maintains a celebrated Pipes and Drums, whose pipers wear Royal Stewart tartan. The Scots Guards' affiliated marches include "Hielan' Laddie" (quick march) and "The Garb of Old Gaul" (slow march).
