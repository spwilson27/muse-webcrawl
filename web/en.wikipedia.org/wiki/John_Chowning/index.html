John Chowning

John M. Chowning
Born: August 22, 1934, Salem, New Jersey, U.S.
Occupation: Composer, researcher
Known for: Inventing FM synthesis; founding CCRMA

John M. Chowning (born August 22, 1934) is an American composer and researcher best known for inventing frequency modulation (FM) synthesis and for founding the Center for Computer Research in Music and Acoustics (CCRMA) at Stanford University.

Career

Chowning studied music in Paris with Nadia Boulanger and later joined Stanford University, where he worked with the early computer music community around Max Mathews's MUSIC program tradition. In 1967, experimenting with vibrato at audio rates, he discovered that modulating one waveform with another produced rich, complex spectra — the basis of FM synthesis, which he patented and Stanford licensed to Yamaha. The technique powered Yamaha's hugely successful DX7 synthesizer (1983) and much of 1980s digital sound.

Chowning also made pioneering contributions to the simulation of moving sound sources and spatialisation in computer music. In 1975 he founded CCRMA, which became one of the world's leading centres for research in computer music and audio signal processing, and directed it for many years. His compositions, including Stria (1977) and Turenas (1972), are landmarks of early computer music, exploring inharmonic spectra and perceptual illusions of space and motion.

Recognition

Chowning has received numerous honours for his work at the intersection of music and technology, and FM synthesis remains one of the most widely used sound synthesis methods in music history.
