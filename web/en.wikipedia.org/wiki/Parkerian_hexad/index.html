Parkerian hexad

Parkerian hexad
Proposed by: Donn B. Parker (1998)
Elements: Confidentiality, Possession, Integrity, Authenticity, Availability, Utility
Field: Information security
Extends: CIA triad

The Parkerian hexad is a set of six elements of information security proposed by Donn B. Parker in his 1998 book Fighting Computer Crime. It extends the classic CIA triad (confidentiality, integrity, availability) with three further elements, and is intended as a more complete model for analysing threats to information.

The six elements

  • Confidentiality — information is accessible only to authorised persons.
  • Possession (or control) — the owner retains physical or logical control of the information; a stolen encrypted tape breaches possession even if confidentiality survives.
  • Integrity — information is complete, accurate, and unmodified in unauthorised ways.
  • Authenticity — information or its attribution is genuine; it correctly represents what it claims to represent.
  • Availability — information is accessible and usable when needed by authorised users.
  • Utility — information is in a form useful to its owner; data encrypted with a lost key has lost utility even though every other element may be intact.

Analysis

Parker designed the elements to be non-overlapping (atomic), so that every information-security incident can be characterised by which elements it violates. The model is frequently cited alongside the CIA triad in security education, but critics note that its extra categories complicate risk assessments, and it has been adopted less widely in practice than the simpler triad.
