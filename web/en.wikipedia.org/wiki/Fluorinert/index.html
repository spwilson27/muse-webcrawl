Fluorinert

Fluorinert is the trademark name for a family of perfluorinated liquids manufactured by 3M, based on perfluorocarbons (such as perfluorohexane and perfluorotributylamine, depending on the grade). These clear, colorless, odorless liquids are electrically insulating, chemically inert, and nonflammable, and have been widely used as coolants in electronics and as test fluids.

Manufacturer3M
TypePerfluorinated liquid
UsesElectronics cooling, thermal testing

Properties and applications

Fluorinert grades (such as FC-72, FC-77, FC-40, and FC-70) differ mainly in boiling point, which ranges from about 56 °C to over 200 °C, allowing engineers to select a fluid that boils at a desired temperature for two-phase cooling. Because the liquid is a dielectric, live electronics can be immersed in it directly; Fluorinert was famously used to cool the Cray-2 supercomputer (1985), whose modules were submerged in flowing Fluorinert, and it has been used in thermal shock and gross leak testing of sealed components, including in semiconductor manufacturing.

Discontinuation

Perfluorocarbons are potent, long-lived greenhouse gases, and Fluorinert belongs to the wider PFAS family of "forever chemicals." In December 2022, 3M announced it would exit all PFAS manufacturing by the end of 2025, discontinuing Fluorinert and related products such as Novec engineered fluids, prompting electronics and semiconductor industries to qualify alternative coolants.
