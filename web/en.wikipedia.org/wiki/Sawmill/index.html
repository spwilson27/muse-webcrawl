Sawmill

A sawmill or lumber mill is a facility where logs are cut into lumber. Modern sawmills use motorised machinery to cut logs into rough planks, boards, and beams, which may then be kiln-dried, planed, and graded for sale.

Process

Logs arriving at a mill are debarked and scanned to determine the most efficient cutting pattern. Primary breakdown is performed by large band saws or circular saws, after which the resulting cants are re-sawn into boards, edged to width, and trimmed to length. Sawdust, bark, and offcuts are collected for use as fuel, mulch, or feedstock for pulp and particleboard, so that little of the log is wasted.

History

Water-powered sawmills appeared in late antiquity and spread widely in medieval Europe, multiplying through wind and water power in the Netherlands and Scandinavia. Steam power in the 19th century, and later electric drive and computerised optimisation, transformed milling into a high-throughput industrial process. Portable sawmills allow small-scale operators to mill timber on site.

By-products

Beyond structural lumber, sawmill residues supply pulpwood chips, hog fuel for boilers, and raw material for engineered wood products.
