World Wide Web Consortium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The W3C logo, bold letters W3C in a rounded mark]

The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) is the main international standards organisation for the World Wide Web. Founded by Tim Berners-Lee in 1994 at MIT (after he left CERN), it develops open standards — Recommendations — intended to ensure the long-term growth of the web as a single, interoperable platform ('Leading the Web to Its Full Potential').

W3C standardised HTML (through HTML5, before stewardship passed to the WHATWG in a 2019 agreement), XML, CSS (jointly, ongoing), SVG, WebAssembly's web integration, accessibility guidelines (WCAG), and hundreds of other specifications, developed by member organisations in working groups aiming for royalty-free licensing.

Governance and evolution

Berners-Lee served as Director throughout; in 2023 W3C reincorporated as a public-interest non-profit (W3C Inc.) after decades hosted by MIT, ERCIM, Keio, and Beihang. Decision-making by consensus and director review has been criticised as slow — browser vendors' WHATWG effectively took HTML and DOM — while W3C remains central for accessibility, payments, identity, and internationalisation standards.

The consortium's defence of an open, royalty-free, accessible web is a recurring theme — from the DRM-in-the-browser debate (EME, 2017) to privacy standards work.
