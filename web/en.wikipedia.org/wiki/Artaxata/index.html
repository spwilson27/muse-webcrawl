Artaxata (Armenian: Զրտա֋ատ, Artashat) was an ancient city and the capital of the Kingdom of Armenia from its founding around 176 BC until the 2nd century AD. It was founded by King Artaxias I — reportedly on the advice of the Carthaginian general Hannibal, who had taken refuge at the Armenian court — on a site of hills in the Ararat plain near the confluence of the Araxes and Metsamor rivers, southwest of modern Yerevan.

History

Artaxata grew into a major Hellenistic metropolis and commercial hub on trade routes linking the Black Sea, Mesopotamia and Persia; Greek writers called it the "Armenian Carthage." It was burned by the Roman general Corbulo in 58 AD during the Roman–Parthian War, rebuilt with the support of the emperor Nero under King Tiridates I, and briefly renamed Neronia. The city was destroyed again in later invasions, and the Armenian capital moved to Vagharshapat and then Dvin. The modern town of Artashat, founded in 1945, lies near the ancient site.

Archaeology

Excavations begun in the 1970s on the hills of Pokr Vedi uncovered traces of the city's fortifications, pottery workshops, coins, and a Latin inscription associated with Tiridates I and the Roman rebuilding. The Khor Virap monastery, where Gregory the Illuminator was imprisoned, stands on one of the hills of the ancient city.
