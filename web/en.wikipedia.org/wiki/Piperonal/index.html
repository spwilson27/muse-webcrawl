Piperonal

Piperonal, also known as heliotropin, is an organic compound — an aromatic aldehyde bearing a methylenedioxy group — found in small amounts in a number of essential oils, including those of sassafras, dill and violets. It has a sweet, floral, vanilla- and cherry-like scent and is widely used in perfumery and flavouring. Because it is also a precursor in the illicit manufacture of MDMA (ecstasy), it is a regulated chemical in many jurisdictions.

Occurrence and production

Piperonal occurs naturally in plants such as sassafras, black pepper relatives, dill and violet flowers, usually at low concentrations. Industrially it has been prepared by oxidation of isosafrole (itself derived from safrole in sassafras oil), and by synthetic routes from catechol via the methylenedioxy intermediate.

Uses

Its heliotrope-like floral odour made piperonal a popular ingredient in perfumes, soaps and cosmetics from the late nineteenth century; the scent was named "heliotropin" after the heliotrope flower it resembles. It is also used as a flavouring (vanilla/cherry notes) and as an intermediate in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals and fragrances.

Regulation

Because piperonal can be converted into MDP2P and thence MDMA, it is listed in Table I of the 1988 UN Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs, and is a List I chemical under US federal law, subject to reporting and record-keeping requirements on manufacture and trade.
