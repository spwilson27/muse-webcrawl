Martín Melitón Pablo de Sarasate y Navascués (10 March 1844 – 20 September 1908), commonly known as Pablo de Sarasate, was a Spanish violin virtuoso, composer and conductor of the Romantic period. One of the most celebrated violinists of the nineteenth century, he was noted for the purity and sweetness of his tone, the ease and elegance of his technique, and the Spanish character of the showpieces he composed for his own performance.

Life

A child prodigy, Sarasate gave his first public concert at the age of eight in La Coruña. His talent attracted the support of Queen Isabella II, and he entered the Paris Conservatoire in 1856, winning its first prize for violin the following year while studying with Delphin Alard. After touring serviceable early ventures, he launched a sustained international career from 1874, performing across Europe, North and South America to enormous acclaim, and debuting in London under the auspices of the Philharmonic Society. He was a favorite in Germany as well as in France and Britain, and gave annual concerts in his native Pamplona, whose citizens honored him repeatedly; the city conservatory and a principal avenue bear his name. He died at his villa in Biarritz in 1908.

Works and dedications

Sarasate's own compositions — 54 works with opus numbers — were written to display his singing tone and effortless virtuosity: the best known are Zigeunerweisen ("Gypsy Airs", Op. 20, 1878), the Spanish Dances (Opp. 21–23, 26), the Carmen Fantasy (Op. 25, 1883) on themes from Bizet's opera, Introduction and Tarantella (Op. 43) and the Navarra for two violins (Op. 33). Many major composers dedicated celebrated works to him, including Saint-Saëns (Violin Concertos Nos. 1 and 3, and the Introduction and Rondo Capriccioso), Lalo (Symphonie espagnole and Violin Concerto in F), Wieniawski (Violin Concerto No. 2), Bruch (Scottish Fantasy and Violin Concerto No. 2) and Dvořák (Mazurek). He made a small number of gramophone recordings in 1904, among the earliest documents of a great nineteenth-century virtuoso's playing.

Instruments

His principal violins were two Stradivari: the 1713 instrument (the "Boissier"), which he bequeathed to the Madrid Conservatory, and the 1724 "da Vinci", later owned by the French state museum in Paris.
