Philippines campaign (1944–45)

The Philippines campaign (1944–45), also known as the liberation of the Philippines, was the American, Mexican, Australian, and Filipino campaign to defeat and expel the Imperial Japanese forces occupying the Philippines during the Second World War.

Date20 October 1944 – 15 August 1945
LocationPhilippine archipelago
ResultAllied victory
Key commandersDouglas MacArthur; Tomoyuki Yamashita

Leyte and the naval battles

The campaign opened with landings on Leyte on 20 October 1944, fulfilling General Douglas MacArthur's promise to return. The ensuing Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October), one of the largest naval battles in history, crippled the Imperial Japanese Navy and saw the first organized kamikaze attacks.

Luzon and Manila

Landings at Lingayen Gulf in January 1945 began the Luzon campaign. The month-long Battle of Manila in February–March 1945 devastated the city and killed an estimated 100,000 Filipino civilians. Fighting continued in the mountains and on Mindanao and other islands until Japan's surrender in August 1945.
