Targowica Confederation

The Targowica Confederation (Polish: konfederacja targowicka) was a confederation of Polish and Lithuanian magnates established on 27 April 1792 in Saint Petersburg by the Russian Empress Catherine II, and formally proclaimed on 14 May 1792 in the small town of Targowica (now Torhovytsia, Ukraine). Its stated aim was to overthrow the reforming Constitution of 3 May 1791; in Polish usage, "Targowica" has since become a synonym for treason.

Background

The Four-Year Sejm had enacted sweeping reforms crowned by the Constitution of 3 May 1791, which strengthened the central government and abolished the liberum veto. Magnates who stood to lose influence, led by Stanisław Szczęsny Potocki, Franciszek Ksawery Branicki and Seweryn Rzewuski, sought Russian help to restore the old order of "Golden Liberty".

War and aftermath

Backed by about 97,000 Russian troops crossing the border in May 1792, the confederates triggered the War in Defence of the Constitution. Despite victories such as Zieleńce, the Polish cause collapsed when King Stanisław August Poniatowski himself joined the Confederation in July 1792. The reform government fell, and in 1793 Russia and Prussia carried out the Second Partition of Poland, ratified by the coerced Grodno Sejm, which abrogated the May Constitution.

Legacy

The Confederation's leaders were condemned as traitors in Polish national memory; several were hanged in effigy, and some in person, during the Kościuszko Uprising of 1794.
