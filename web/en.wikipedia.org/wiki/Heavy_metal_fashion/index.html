Heavy metal fashion

Heavy metal fashion is the clothing, hairstyles and accessories associated with heavy metal music and its subcultures, worn by both musicians and fans since the genre's emergence around 1970. Its enduring core elements are black band T-shirts, denim and leather, long hair, studs and spikes, and military-inspired gear — a uniform that signals allegiance to particular bands and subgenres as much as personal style.

Origins and classic look

The style drew from biker culture, working-class denim and leather, and the stage dress of bands such as Black Sabbath, Judas Priest and Motörhead; Rob Halford's adoption of leather-and-studs imagery from biker and S&M subcultures in the late 1970s codified much of the look. Fans adopted ripped jeans, high-top trainers or boots, and T-shirts printed with band logos and album art.

The battle jacket

The most distinctive garment is the battle jacket (or "kutte", from German Kutte): a denim or leather vest or jacket — sleeves often removed — densely covered with sewn patches of favoured bands, pins and studs. A large "backpatch" displays a principal band. The jacket functions as a curated record of musical identity and festival attendance; in thrash metal scenes the patched denim vest became near-obligatory.

Subgenre variations

Thrash favoured jeans, white high-tops and bullet belts; death metal added camouflage shorts and illegible-logo shirts; black metal developed corpse paint, spikes and medieval weaponry; glam metal embraced spandex and teased hair; and doom and stoner scenes adopted retro 1970s dress. Elements of metal fashion have repeatedly been absorbed by mainstream and high fashion, sometimes controversially among fans.

Significance

Sociologists of subculture read metal dress as a durable identity marker: unlike many youth styles it persists across the lifespan, and the band shirt remains both merchandise economy and badge of belonging at festivals worldwide.
