Sevillanas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sevillanas (Spanish pronunciation: [seβiˈʎanas]) are a type of folk music and dance, originally from the city of Seville and its province in Andalusia, Spain, and now popular throughout the region. They derive from the Castilian seguidilla and are closely related to, but distinct from, flamenco. Sevillanas are danced by couples or groups during festivals such as the Seville Fair (Feria de Abril), the pilgrimage of El Rocío, and other local ferias.

Music

Sevillanas music is in 3/4 (or 6/8) time and is played on guitar, often accompanied by singing, hand-clapping (palmas) and castanets. A complete sevillana consists of four sections called coplas, each with its own choreography, separated by short interludes. The lyrics traditionally treat themes of love, the countryside, pilgrimage and local life, though modern compositions cover many subjects.

Dance

The dance is performed facing a partner, with elaborate arm movements, turns and passes (paseíllos). Each of the four coplas follows a fixed sequence of steps, making sevillanas one of the most widely learned traditional dances in Andalusia; they are taught to children from an early age and danced socially by people of all ages. Women traditionally wear long, ruffled flamenco dresses, and men may wear short jackets and trousers.

Sevillanas rocieras

A popular subgenre is the sevillanas rocieras, associated with the pilgrimage to the shrine of Our Lady of El Rocío in the province of Huelva. Groups such as Los Romeros de la Puebla and Los Marismeños became famous for this style.
