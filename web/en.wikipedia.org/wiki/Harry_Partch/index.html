Harry Partch (24 June 1901 – 3 September 1974) was an American composer, music theorist and creator of unique musical instruments. Rejecting Western equal temperament, he built a system based on just intonation dividing the octave into 43 unequal tones, designed and built an orchestra of sculptural instruments to play it, and composed ritualistic music-theatre works he called "corporeal" — integrating dance, speech and spectacle. He is regarded as a founding figure of microtonal music and of the American experimental tradition.

Life

Partch was born in Oakland to Presbyterian missionaries who had fled the Boxer Rebellion in China; he grew up in Arizona and New Mexico, absorbing Mexican, Chinese and Yaqui musical influences. As a young pianist and composer he became convinced that the twelve-tone tempered scale was a historical accident, burned his early works in a stove in 1930, and adopted just intonation. During the Depression he lived for a time as a transient worker, riding freight trains; his experiences became the basis of The Wayward (including Barstow: Eight Hitchhiker Inscriptions, 1941), settings of graffiti he had copied from highway railings.

Instruments and theory

To realise his tuning Partch built more than two dozen instruments: the Chromelodeon (a retuned reed organ), the Diamond Marimba, the Quadrangularis Reversum, the Spoils of War, glass "Cloud-Chamber Bowls", giant bamboo marimbas (the Marimba Eroica), and the Kithara, a 72-string upright lyre. His theoretical testament, Genesis of a Music (1949; enlarged 1974), sets out his tuning, his concept of "tonality diamond", and a polemic history of Western tuning. His large stage works — Oedipus (1951), The Bewitched (1955), Revelation in the Courtyard Park (1960) and Delusion of the Fury (1966) — were produced mostly at universities, and his own Gate 5 record label issued his music to a growing cult audience. The instrument collection is now held at the University of Washington, with working replicas in use by the ensemble PARTCH.

Legacy

Partch influenced composers from Lou Harrison and Ben Johnston to Tom Waits, and is routinely cited as a pioneer of instrument building, microtonality, and total theatre. His 43-tone scale remains a landmark in tuning theory.
