Adolph Weinman

Adolph Alexander Weinman (December 11, 1870 – August 8, 1952) was a German-born American sculptor and architectural sculptor. He is best known as the designer of the Mercury dime and the Walking Liberty half dollar, two of the most admired designs in American coinage.

Early life and training

Weinman was born in Karlsruhe, Germany, and immigrated to the United States as a child. He studied at Cooper Union and the Art Students League of New York, and worked in the studios of Augustus Saint-Gaudens and Daniel Chester French, two of the leading American sculptors of the era.

Coin designs

In 1916 the United States Mint selected Weinman's designs for the new dime and half dollar. The dime's winged Liberty head was popularly mistaken for the Roman god Mercury, giving the coin its enduring nickname. The Walking Liberty half dollar obverse was later reused for the American Silver Eagle bullion coin, first issued in 1986.

Architectural sculpture

Weinman's monumental works include the pediment sculpture and frieze of the Jefferson Memorial's interior panels, figures for the Pennsylvania Railroad Station in New York, and works for the National Academy of Design, of which he was a member. He died in Port Chester, New York, in 1952.
