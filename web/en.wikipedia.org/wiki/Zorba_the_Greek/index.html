Zorba the Greek

Zorba the Greek (Greek: Βίος και Πολιτεία του Αλέξη Ζορμπά, Life and Times of Alexis Zorbas) is a novel written by the Cretan author Nikos Kazantzakis, first published in Greek in 1946. It is the tale of a young Greek intellectual who ventures to escape his bookish life with the aid of the mysterious and passionate Alexis Zorba.

Plot summary

The unnamed narrator, a reserved scholar, meets the exuberant older workman Alexis Zorba in Piraeus and hires him to help reopen a lignite mine on Crete. On the island they lodge with Madame Hortense, encounter a young widow, monks, villagers, and a series of triumphs and disasters. Zorba teaches the narrator to embrace dance, music on the santouri, wine, and lived experience over abstract thought, while his own schemes — notably a grand cable system for transporting timber — end in spectacular collapse, answered with a dance on the beach.

Film and other adaptations

The 1964 film directed by Michael Cacoyannis, starring Anthony Quinn as Zorba and Alan Bates as the narrator (renamed Basil), won three Academy Awards and popularised the “Zorba dance” — the sirtaki, choreographed for the film with music by Mikis Theodorakis. A Broadway musical, Zorba (1968, revived 1983), and a radio adaptation followed.

Themes

The novel contrasts intellect and instinct, Apollonian restraint and Dionysian freedom, and explores Greek identity, freedom, death, and the search for meaning through action.
