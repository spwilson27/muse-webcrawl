Lieutenant Governor of Tennessee

The Lieutenant Governor of Tennessee is a constitutional officer in the government of the U.S. state of Tennessee. Unusually among American lieutenant governors, the officeholder is not elected separately by the voters or appointed by the governor; instead, the Tennessee Senate elects one of its own members to serve as its Speaker, and that Speaker holds the title of Lieutenant Governor by virtue of the office.

IncumbentRandy McNally (since 2017)
SelectionElected by the Tennessee Senate as its Speaker
TermTwo years, concurrent with the Senate term
SuccessionFirst in line to succeed the Governor of Tennessee

Duties

The Lieutenant Governor presides over the Tennessee Senate, appoints its committees and their chairs, and is first in the line of succession to the Governor of Tennessee. The officeholder also serves on various state boards and commissions.

Tennessee is one of only a handful of states in which the lieutenant governor is chosen by the state senate rather than in a statewide election. The arrangement dates to the Tennessee Constitution of 1870, which provided that the Senate elect a Speaker who would bear the title of lieutenant governor for succession purposes.

History

Long-serving holders of the office include John Wilder, who served from 1971 to 2007 and became one of the most influential figures in Tennessee politics, and Ron Ramsey, who served from 2007 to 2017 and was the first Republican to hold the post in 140 years. Randy McNally of Oak Ridge succeeded Ramsey in January 2017.
