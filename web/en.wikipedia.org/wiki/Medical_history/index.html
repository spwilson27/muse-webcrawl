Medical history

The medical history, case history, or anamnesis (from Greek ἀνά, aná, "open", and μνήσις, mnesis, "memory") of a patient is information gained by a physician from asking specific questions, either of the patient or of other people who know the person and can give suitable information, with the aim of obtaining information useful in formulating a diagnosis and providing medical care to the patient.

The medically relevant complaints reported by the patient or others familiar with the patient are referred to as symptoms, in contrast with clinical signs, which are ascertained by direct examination on the part of medical personnel. Most clinical encounters will involve a history of some sort. Medical history taking may be idiosyncratic and vary within specialties; for example, a psychiatrist's history differs from that of a general surgeon.

Process

A clinician typically asks questions to obtain the following information about the patient:

  • Identifying data and the chief complaint
  • History of the present illness (HPI)
  • Past medical history, including previous illnesses, operations, and injuries
  • Medications currently taken, including over-the-counter preparations
  • Allergies
  • Review of systems — a systematic enquiry about symptoms in each body system
  • Family history of disease
  • Social history, including occupation, smoking, alcohol and drug use, and living circumstances
  • Sexual history, where relevant

History-taking may be comprehensive (undertaken on hospital admission) or focused (limited to the presenting problem). The history is usually recorded in the patient's notes and may be structured using frameworks such as SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan).

History of the present illness

The history of the present illness explores the chief complaint in detail. A common mnemonic for characterising pain and other symptoms is SOCRATES: Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associations, Time course, Exacerbating/relieving factors, and Severity. Another mnemonic, OPQRST, covers Onset, Provocation/palliation, Quality, Region/Radiation, Severity, and Timing.

Review of systems

The review of systems is a checklist of questions covering each major body system — constitutional, eyes, ears/nose/throat, cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, neurological, psychiatric, endocrine, haematological, and allergic/immunological — intended to uncover symptoms the patient may not have mentioned spontaneously.

Computer-assisted history taking

Since the 1960s, computer-assisted history taking systems have been developed, allowing patients to answer structured questionnaires before or during consultation. Studies suggest patients may disclose sensitive information more readily to a computer, and the data can integrate with electronic health records.
