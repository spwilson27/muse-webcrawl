The United States Coast Guard Academy (USCGA) is a service academy of the United States Coast Guard in New London, Connecticut. Founded in 1876, it is the smallest of the five federal service academies and provides a four-year Bachelor of Science degree programme with a full scholarship in exchange for a commitment of five years of active-duty service upon graduation.

Admissions and academics

Unlike the other service academies, admission to the Coast Guard Academy does not require a congressional nomination; cadets are selected through a nationwide competitive process. The corps of cadets numbers about 1,000. Academic majors include naval architecture and marine engineering, marine and environmental sciences, electrical and computer engineering, cyber systems, mechanical engineering, civil engineering, operations research and data analytics, management, and government. Cadets train each summer, including cruises aboard the barque Eagle and assignments to operational cutters and stations.

History

The academy originated in 1876 as the Revenue Cutter School of Instruction, training aboard the schooner Dobbin and later at Fort Trumbull in New London. It took its present name in 1915 when the Revenue Cutter Service merged with the Life-Saving Service to form the Coast Guard, and moved to its current 103-acre campus overlooking the Thames River in 1932. Women were admitted beginning in 1976, among the first classes at any U.S. service academy.

Graduates are commissioned as ensigns in the Coast Guard. Alumni include astronauts, flag officers, and Admiral Linda Fagan, who became the service's first female commandant in 2022.
