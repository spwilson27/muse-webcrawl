Lake Alaotra

Lake Alaotra (French: Lac Alaotra) is the largest lake in Madagascar, located in the Alaotra-Mangoro Region in the north-east of the island. It lies in a fertile rice-growing basin at an elevation of around 750 metres above sea level and covers about 900 square kilometres including surrounding marshes, of which the open water is roughly 450 km².

The lake is shallow, with a maximum depth of only a few metres, and is surrounded by extensive reed beds and marshland. Its basin is Madagascar's most important rice-producing area, sometimes called the country's rice granary. The lake drains via the Maningory River to the Indian Ocean.

Lake Alaotra is a Ramsar wetland of international importance. It is the only habitat of the Alaotra gentle lemur (Hapalemur alaotrensis), a critically endangered primate that lives in the reed beds. The lake's endemic fish and waterbirds have declined due to habitat conversion, introduced species and overfishing; the Alaotra grebe was declared extinct in 2010.
