Bosra (Arabic: بُصْرَىٰ) is a city in southern Syria, in the Daraa Governorate, historically part of the Hauran region. It is renowned for its remarkably preserved Roman theatre and other remains from the Roman, Byzantine, and early Islamic periods. The old city of Bosra was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980.

History

Bosra was the capital of the Nabataean kingdom under Rabbel II before its annexation by Rome in 106 AD, when it became capital of the province of Arabia Petraea under the name Nova Trajana Bostra. It flourished as a caravan city on trade routes to the Red Sea. In the 3rd century, Philip the Arab, a native of the region, raised its status. Bosra became an important early Christian center and later passed under Islamic rule in 634; the al-Omari mosque, traditionally dated to the caliphate of Umar, is among the oldest mosques in Syria.

Roman theatre

The Roman theatre, built in the 2nd century AD from black basalt, could seat some 15,000 spectators. Unusually, it was later incorporated into a Muslim fortress, which protected its structure; today it is one of the largest and best-preserved Roman theatres in the world and hosts cultural festivals.

Modern era

The modern town surrounds the ancient ruins. During the Syrian civil war, Bosra changed hands and its heritage suffered damage and looting, leading UNESCO to place the site on its list of World Heritage in Danger in 2013.
