Lattice (order)

Hasse diagram of the lattice of divisors of 60.
FieldOrder theory, abstract algebra
OperationsMeet (∧) and join (∨)

A lattice is an abstract structure studied in order theory and abstract algebra. As a partially ordered set, a lattice is a poset in which every pair of elements has a unique supremum (the join) and a unique infimum (the meet). Equivalently, a lattice can be defined algebraically as a set with two commutative, associative, idempotent operations satisfying absorption laws.

Examples

The subsets of a set, ordered by inclusion, form a lattice in which the join is the union and the meet the intersection. The positive integers ordered by divisibility form a lattice where the meet is the greatest common divisor and the join is the least common multiple. Totally ordered sets, such as the real numbers, are lattices where meet and join are simply min and max.

Important classes

Distributive lattices (including Boolean algebras and Heyting algebras), modular lattices, complete lattices — in which arbitrary joins and meets exist — and bounded lattices with greatest and least elements are central to logic, topology, universal algebra and theoretical computer science, for example in domain theory and formal concept analysis.
