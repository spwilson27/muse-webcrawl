Horus name

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Horus name is the oldest known title of, and form of name used by, the pharaohs of ancient Egypt. It presents the king as the earthly incarnation of the falcon god Horus and forms one part of the royal fivefold titulary.

Form

The Horus name was written inside a serekh, a stylised depiction of a palace façade, surmounted by a falcon representing Horus. The serekh device appears in the Predynastic Period and was used from the earliest dynasties, for kings such as Narmer and Djer. The name within the serekh was usually a short programme-like statement — for example, "Horus the Strong", or a name asserting the king's might in battle.

Development

Over time the Horus name came to be accompanied by additional royal names: the Nebty ("Two Ladies") name, the Golden Horus name, the prenomen (throne name, written in a cartouche), and the nomen (birth name, also in a cartouche). By the Middle Kingdom this fivefold titulary was standard. The Horus name remained in use throughout pharaonic history, although in later periods it was sometimes written in a cartouche-like frame.

Golden Horus name

A related but distinct element is the Golden Horus name, in which the falcon is shown perched on the hieroglyph for gold (nebw). Its precise meaning is debated; gold was associated with eternity and with the flesh of the gods, and the name may express the king's eternal triumph over chaos, personified by Seth.
