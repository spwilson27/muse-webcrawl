Corbett (hill)

A Corbett is a Scottish mountain between 2,500 and 3,000 feet (762.0 and 914.4 m) high with a prominence of at least 500 feet (152.4 m). The list was compiled in the 1920s by John Rooke Corbett, a Bristol-based climber and founder member of the Scottish Mountaineering Club, and was published posthumously by the club after his death in 1949.

The list

There are 222 Corbetts in Scotland, spread across the Highlands and Islands; because the threshold is imperial, the list is defined in feet. Corbetts are the next category below the Munros (peaks over 3,000 feet) and above the Grahams (2,000–2,500 feet). Hillwalkers who climb all the Corbetts are known as "Corbetteers"; the first was the list's compiler himself, John Corbett, who completed them in 1930 — he was also the first person recorded to have climbed all the Munros and Corbetts.

Popular Corbetts include Ben Vrackie, Goat Fell on Arran, The Cobbler (Ben Arthur), and Ben Ledi. As with the Munros, periodic resurveying with modern GPS can promote or demote peaks near the boundaries of height or prominence, and the list is periodically revised by the Scottish Mountaineering Club.
