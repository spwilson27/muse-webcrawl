Khabib Abdulmanapovich Nurmagomedov (born September 20, 1988) is a Russian former professional mixed martial artist. He competed in the lightweight division of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), where he was the longest-reigning UFC Lightweight Champion, holding the title from April 2018 to March 2021. With 29 wins and no losses, he retired undefeated and is widely regarded as one of the greatest mixed martial artists of all time.

Background

Nurmagomedov was born in the village of Sildi in Dagestan, then part of the Soviet Union, to an Avar family. His father, Abdulmanap Nurmagomedov, a decorated wrestler and coach, converted the ground floor of their home into a gym and trained his son in wrestling from an early age — famously including grappling with a bear cub as a child. Khabib later studied judo and combat sambo, becoming a two-time world champion in combat sambo before turning professional in MMA in 2008.

UFC career

Nurmagomedov signed with the UFC in late 2011 and built a dominant record with his relentless grappling, defeating opponents including Rafael dos Anjos and Michael Johnson. On April 7, 2018, at UFC 223, he defeated Al Iaquinta to win the vacant UFC Lightweight Championship. His first defence, against Conor McGregor at UFC 229 in October 2018, was the biggest event in UFC history; Nurmagomedov won by submission in the fourth round, after which a post-fight brawl led to fines and suspensions for both camps. He went on to defend the title against Dustin Poirier (2019) and Justin Gaethje (2020), both by submission.

Retirement

Immediately after defeating Gaethje at UFC 254 in October 2020, Nurmagomedov announced his retirement, citing a promise to his mother following the death of his father from COVID-19 complications earlier that year. He was inducted into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2022 and has since worked as a coach and promoter, founding the Eagle Fighting Championship.
