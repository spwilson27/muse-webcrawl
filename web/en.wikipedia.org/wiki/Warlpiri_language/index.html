Warlpiri is an Australian Aboriginal language spoken by the Warlpiri people of the Tanami Desert region of the Northern Territory of Australia, in communities including Yuendumu, Lajamanu, Nyirripi, and Willowra. With a few thousand speakers, it is one of the most widely spoken Indigenous Australian languages and one of the few still being learned by children as a first language.

Bilingual signage in Warlpiri and English

Features

Warlpiri belongs to the Ngumpin–Yapa subgroup of the Pama–Nyungan family. It is famous in linguistic theory for its extremely free word order and discontinuous expressions, which Kenneth Hale used in arguing for "non-configurational" languages. Nouns carry ergative–absolutive case marking, and a system of second-position auxiliary clitics encodes tense and the person and number of subject and object. Warlpiri also has a well-developed sign language, used especially by women during mourning periods when speech is taboo.

Education and media

Warlpiri has been taught in bilingual education programmes since the 1970s, notably at Yuendumu school, and is used in local media, including broadcasting by the Warlpiri Media Association (PAW Media). A standard orthography and dictionaries have been produced with the community.
