Federal Information Processing Standards

Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) are publicly announced standards developed by the United States federal government for use in computer systems by government agencies and contractors. They are issued by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) following approval by the Secretary of Commerce.

Notable standards

  • FIPS 140-3 — security requirements for cryptographic modules, the basis of the Cryptographic Module Validation Program.
  • FIPS 197 — the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES).
  • FIPS 186 — the Digital Signature Standard (DSS).
  • FIPS 180-4 — the Secure Hash Standard (SHA-1 and SHA-2 families).
  • FIPS 202 — SHA-3.
  • FIPS 46-3 — the former Data Encryption Standard (DES), withdrawn in 2005.

FIPS also historically published geographic codes (such as the FIPS place and county codes, largely superseded by GNIS and ANSI codes).

Process

Under the Federal Information Security Management Act, NIST develops FIPS for categories including computer security and interoperability; proposed standards are published for public comment in the Federal Register before final approval. Federal agencies must comply with FIPS unless a waiver is granted.
