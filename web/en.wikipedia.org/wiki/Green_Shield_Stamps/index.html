Green Shield Stamps

Green Shield Stamps were a British sales promotion and loyalty scheme introduced in 1958 by entrepreneur Richard Tompkins, modelled on the S&H Green Stamps trading stamp programme in the United States. Participating retailers gave customers stamps in proportion to the value of their purchases; customers pasted the stamps into savings books and exchanged filled books for goods from the Green Shield catalogue or its showrooms.

Operation

Retailers bought the stamps from the Green Shield company and earned customer loyalty by issuing them; shoppers accumulated stamps — famously including "quadruple stamp" offers — and redeemed books of them for household goods, toys, and consumer electronics at Green Shield redemption centres on high streets across Britain.

From stamps to Argos

In 1973 Tompkins converted the redemption business into a catalogue retail chain, Argos, where customers could buy the catalogue goods directly, initially also accepting trading stamps. As supermarkets withdrew from the scheme amid the price wars of the 1970s — Tesco famously dropped Green Shield in 1977 to fund its "Checkout" price-cut campaign — the stamp business declined, though filled books continued to be honoured for some years.

Legacy

Green Shield Stamps are remembered as a defining feature of British shopping in the 1960s and 1970s and as a precursor of modern loyalty programmes such as supermarket points schemes. The successor business, Argos, became one of Britain's largest general merchandise retailers.
