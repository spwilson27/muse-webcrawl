The axiom of determinacy (AD) is a proposed axiom of set theory, introduced by Jan Mycielski and Hugo Steinhaus in 1962. It concerns certain two-player infinite games of perfect information: AD states that every such game in which the players alternately pick natural numbers, producing an infinite sequence, is determined — that is, one of the two players has a winning strategy, where the winner is decided by whether the resulting sequence belongs to a payoff set fixed in advance.

Statement

Formally: for every set A of infinite sequences of natural numbers, the game in which players I and II alternately choose natural numbers n0, n1, n2, … is determined, with player I winning exactly when the completed sequence lies in A. AD asserts that for every A, either player I or player II has a strategy guaranteeing victory regardless of the opponent's play.

Relation to the axiom of choice

AD contradicts the full axiom of choice: using choice, one can construct (via a transfinite diagonal argument exploiting well-orderings of strategies) a payoff set for which neither player has a winning strategy, and choice also yields non-measurable sets of reals, whereas AD implies that every set of real numbers is Lebesgue measurable, has the property of Baire, and has the perfect set property. AD is, however, compatible with ZF set theory together with dependent choice, and it agrees with choice on well-behaved (e.g. Borel and analytic) sets, for which determinacy is provable in ZFC — Borel determinacy was proved by Donald A. Martin in 1975.

Consequences and consistency

Under AD, the theory of the real line is strikingly regular: there are no maximal almost-disjoint families of the kind produced by choice, the club filter on ℵ1 is an ultrafilter, and many cardinal invariants take definite values. AD is not provable in ZF, but its consistency relative to large cardinal axioms was established through the work of Martin, John R. Steel, and W. Hugh Woodin: the existence of infinitely many Woodin cardinals with a measurable cardinal above them implies the consistency of AD in L(ℝ), the smallest model containing all reals and ordinals. Conversely, AD in L(ℝ) implies the existence of inner models with Woodin cardinals.

Variants

Weaker and stronger variants include the axiom of projective determinacy (PD), which restricts AD to projective payoff sets and follows from large cardinals while remaining compatible with choice, and AD+, an extension studied by Woodin that strengthens AD's consequences for the theory of L(ℝ).
