Treaty of Xanten

The Treaty of Xanten was signed on 12 November 1614 in the town of Xanten, ending the War of the Jülich Succession (1609–1614) over the inheritance of the United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg in northwestern Germany.

Terms

The rival claimants were John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg, and Wolfgang Wilhelm, Count Palatine of Neuburg. Under the treaty the duchies were partitioned: Brandenburg received Cleves, Mark, and Ravensberg in the north, while Neuburg received Jülich and Berg in the south.

Significance

The settlement gave Brandenburg its first territories in the Rhineland and Westphalia, a major step in the rise of Brandenburg-Prussia, and the partition it established largely endured until the Napoleonic era. Mediated by France and England, the treaty also averted a wider European war between the Protestant Union and Catholic powers in the tense years before the Thirty Years' War.
