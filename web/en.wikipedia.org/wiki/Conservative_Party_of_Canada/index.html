The Conservative Party of Canada (CPC; French: Parti conservateur du Canada), sometimes referred to as the Tories, is a federal political party in Canada. It was formed in 2003 by the merger of the two main right-leaning parties, the Progressive Conservative Party and the Canadian Alliance. The party sits at the centre-right to right of the Canadian political spectrum and is one of the two parties that have governed Canada for most of its history, the other being the Liberal Party.

History

The party's roots lie in the conservative parties of John A. Macdonald, who became Canada's first prime minister in 1867. After the Progressive Conservatives' collapse in the 1993 election and a decade of a divided right, the Canadian Alliance under Stephen Harper and the Progressive Conservatives under Peter MacKay agreed to merge in October 2003. Harper became leader of the new party in March 2004.

In government

The Conservatives won a minority government in 2006 and governed until 2015, winning a majority in 2011. The Harper government cut taxes, including the Goods and Services Tax, pursued free-trade agreements, and took a more assertive foreign policy. The party lost the 2015 election to Justin Trudeau's Liberals and returned to opposition.

Leaders

Party leaders since the merger have included Stephen Harper (2004–2015), interim leader Rona Ambrose, Andrew Scheer (2017–2020), Erin O'Toole (2020–2022), interim leader Candice Bergen, and Pierre Poilievre (from 2022).

Ideology and base

The party combines fiscal conservatives favouring lower taxes and balanced budgets, social conservatives, and supporters of a strong military and law-and-order policies. Its strongest support has historically been in Western Canada, particularly Alberta and Saskatchewan, and in rural Ontario.
