TypeSailing and rowing warship / cargo vessel
Used byNorse and Scandinavian peoples; Vikings
Periodc. 8th–11th centuries (Viking Age), with earlier antecedents

A longship (from Old Norse langskip) was a type of specialised Scandinavian warship with a long, narrow, light hull, a shallow draft, and symmetrical ends, developed over several centuries and reaching its peak form in the Viking Age. Longships were clinker-built of overlapping wooden planks, propelled by oars and a single large square sail, and were fast, flexible, and able to land directly on beaches and navigate shallow rivers.

Types and characteristics

Longships were classified by size and number of rowing positions: types included the karvi (13 pairs of oars, as in the Gokstad ship), the snekkja (20 pairs), and the large skeid warships with 30 or more pairs. Their shallow draught — as little as one metre — allowed them to penetrate far inland along rivers, a decisive advantage in Viking raids. The largest known example, found at Roskilde, was about 37 metres long.

Archaeological finds

Exceptionally preserved examples were excavated from ship burials at Oseberg and Gokstad in Norway and from the scuttled Skuldelev ships in Denmark, several of which have been replicated and sailed in modern times.
