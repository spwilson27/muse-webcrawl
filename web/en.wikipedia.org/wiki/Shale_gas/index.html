Shale gas is natural gas that is found trapped within shale formations. Shale gas has become an increasingly important source of natural gas in the United States since the start of this century, and interest has spread to potential gas shales in the rest of the world. In 2000 shale gas provided only 1% of U.S. natural gas production; by 2010 it was over 20%, and the U.S. government's Energy Information Administration predicted that by 2035, 46% of the United States' natural gas supply would come from shale gas.

Geology

Because shales ordinarily have insufficient permeability to allow significant fluid flow to a well bore, most shales are not commercial sources of natural gas. Shale gas is one of several forms of unconventional gas, alongside coal-bed methane, tight gas in low-permeability sandstones, and methane hydrates.

Extraction

Commercial production became feasible through the combination of horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing ("fracking"), in which large volumes of water, sand, and chemicals are pumped underground at high pressure to crack the rock and release the gas. The technique was pioneered in the Barnett Shale of Texas in the 1990s by Mitchell Energy under George P. Mitchell.

Economics and environmental issues

The shale gas boom transformed the United States into the world's largest natural gas producer and a net exporter of liquefied natural gas. Environmental debate centres on groundwater contamination, methane leakage, wastewater disposal, and induced seismicity, while supporters note that gas generation emits roughly half the carbon dioxide of coal per unit of electricity.
