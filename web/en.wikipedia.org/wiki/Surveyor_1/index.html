Surveyor 1 was the first lunar soft-lander in the uncrewed Surveyor program of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA, United States). It was launched on 30 May 1966 and landed successfully in the Oceanus Procellarum on 2 June 1966, becoming the first U.S. spacecraft to make a soft landing on the Moon, following the Soviet Union's Luna 9 earlier that year.

Mission

Surveyor 1 was designed to demonstrate the technology necessary to achieve a soft landing on the Moon and to take photographs of the lunar terrain to determine whether the surface was suitable for the crewed landings of the Apollo program. The spacecraft was launched directly toward the Moon on an impact trajectory and made a flawless three-axis-stabilized midcourse correction five hours after launch.

Landing and operations

The landing was televised live and was an unqualified success: the spacecraft came down within about 14 km of its target point, in the Flamsteed crater region. During its first lunar day, Surveyor 1 transmitted 11,237 television pictures of the lunar surface back to Earth, along with data on surface strength, temperatures, and radar reflectivity. It survived the frigid lunar night in a dormant state and resumed transmitting during a second lunar day before contact finally ended on 7 January 1967.

Legacy

The success of Surveyor 1 and its successors dispelled fears that Apollo landers might sink into deep lunar dust, and provided essential ground-truth for the selection of Apollo landing sites.
