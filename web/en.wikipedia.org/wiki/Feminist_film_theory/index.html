Feminist film theory

Feminist film theory examines how cinema represents gender and structures spectatorship.

Feminist film theory is a theoretical film criticism derived from feminist politics and theory. It emerged in the late 1960s and 1970s alongside second-wave feminism, analysing how women are represented on screen, how films are structured by patriarchal ways of seeing, and how women spectators engage with cinema.

Early criticism

Early works such as Molly Haskell's From Reverence to Rape (1974) and Marjorie Rosen's Popcorn Venus (1973) catalogued stereotypes of women in Hollywood film — the vamp, the girl next door, the long-suffering mother — and argued that screen images reflected and reinforced women's social subordination.

The male gaze

The field's most famous essay is Laura Mulvey's "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975), which drew on psychoanalysis to argue that classical Hollywood cinema organises looking around a male gaze: the camera and narrative align the spectator with an active male protagonist, while women appear as passive objects "to-be-looked-at". Mulvey called for an avant-garde cinema that would destroy this pleasure. Her later "Afterthoughts" (1981) addressed the female spectator, who may adopt a "masculinised" viewing position or oscillate between positions.

Developments

Subsequent theorists complicated the model. Mary Ann Doane studied the female spectator and genres addressed to women, such as melodrama; Teresa de Lauretis reframed cinema as a "technology of gender"; bell hooks developed the concept of the "oppositional gaze" of Black women spectators; and scholars such as Jackie Stacey used audience research to show how real viewers negotiate images. Feminist film theory also engaged with women filmmakers — from Germaine Dulac and Maya Deren to Chantal Akerman and Claire Denis — and with queer and intersectional approaches that examine sexuality, race and class together with gender.

Influence

Concepts from feminist film theory, above all the male gaze, have entered general cultural criticism, influencing analysis of advertising, video games and social media, as well as debates about representation and casting in the contemporary film industry.
