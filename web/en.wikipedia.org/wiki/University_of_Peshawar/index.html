The University of Peshawar (Pashto: د پېښور پوهنتون; Urdu: جامعۂ پشاور) is a public research university located in Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. Founded in 1950, shortly after the independence of Pakistan, it is one of the oldest and largest universities in the province and a major centre of higher education in northwestern Pakistan.

History

The university was established in 1950 with Muhammad Ali Jinnah's vision of spreading higher education in the then North-West Frontier Province. It grew out of the historic Islamia College Peshawar (founded 1913), which became its constituent college. Founding figures included Sahibzada Abdul Qayyum, and the university's first vice-chancellor was Khan Bahadur Muhammad Ibrahim Khan. The campus, laid out over roughly a thousand acres along Jamrud Road, is known for the Gandhara Museum and for centres such as the Area Study Centre (Russia, China & Central Asia), the Pakistan Study Centre, and the Shaykh Zayed Islamic Centre.

Academics

The university has eight faculties covering arts and humanities, life and environmental sciences, management and information sciences, numerical and physical sciences, social sciences, and Islamic and Oriental studies, among others. It enrolls tens of thousands of students, including many from the merged tribal districts and Afghanistan, and maintains hundreds of affiliated colleges across the province.
