Peter Andreas Thiel (born 11 October 1967) is a German-American billionaire entrepreneur, venture capitalist and political activist. A co-founder of PayPal, Palantir Technologies and Founders Fund, he was the first outside investor in Facebook. He is a partner at Founders Fund and has been an influential and controversial figure in Silicon Valley and American politics.

Early life and education

Thiel was born in Frankfurt and raised in the United States and South West Africa (Namibia). He studied philosophy at Stanford University, where he founded The Stanford Review, and earned a law degree from Stanford Law School. He briefly worked as a securities lawyer and derivatives trader before turning to technology ventures.

Business career

In 1998 Thiel co-founded Confinity, which merged with X.com to form PayPal; he served as CEO until its sale to eBay in 2002. He co-founded the data-analytics firm Palantir in 2003 and the venture capital firm Founders Fund in 2005. His 2004 investment of $500,000 bought 10.2% of Facebook. His book Zero to One (2014), based on lectures on startups, became a bestseller. He is associated with the "PayPal Mafia" of founders and early employees who went on to start many prominent technology companies.

Politics and philanthropy

Thiel describes himself as a libertarian and has supported Republican candidates, most notably Donald Trump in 2016, as well as funding legal cases and causes including the lawsuit that bankrupted Gawker Media. Through the Thiel Foundation he funds initiatives such as the Thiel Fellowship, which pays young people to leave college and start companies, and research into life extension and seasteading.
