The Doliolida are an order of small, pelagic tunicates in the class Thaliacea. Doliolids are free-floating marine animals, usually less than a few centimetres long, with transparent, barrel-shaped bodies encircled by bands of muscle. They move by jet propulsion, contracting their muscle bands to pump water through the body, which simultaneously serves filter feeding.

Life cycle

Doliolids have an exceptionally complex life cycle with an alternation of generations. The solitary asexual phase (the oozooid or "nurse") develops a long ventral stolon on which buds are produced in successive generations, maturing into feeding phorozooids and eventually sexual gonozooids that are released to reproduce sexually. Under favourable conditions this rapid asexual budding allows populations to bloom explosively, with doubling times among the fastest of any multicellular animal.

Ecology

Doliolids feed on phytoplankton and other small particles, capturing food on a mucous net. They are important grazers in oligotrophic oceans and their dense faecal pellets and discarded bodies contribute significantly to the export of carbon to the deep sea. The order includes the families Doliolidae and Doliopsidae, with the genus Doliolum the best known.
