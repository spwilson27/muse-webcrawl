Wood flooring

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wood flooring is any product manufactured from timber that is designed for use as flooring, either structural or aesthetic. Wood is a common choice as a flooring material and can come in various styles, colours, cuts, and species. Bamboo flooring is often considered a form of wood flooring, although it is made from a grass (bamboo) rather than a timber.

Types

Solid wood

Solid wood flooring is made of planks milled from a single piece of timber. It is typically 18–20 mm thick and is usually installed by nail-down fixing to a subfloor. Because it is a single piece of wood, it can be sanded and refinished many times over its service life. However, solid wood expands and contracts with changes in humidity and is generally not recommended below grade or in rooms with high moisture.

Engineered wood

Engineered wood flooring consists of two or more layers of wood in the form of a plank. The top layer (lamella) is the wood that is visible when the flooring is installed and is adhered to a core, typically made of plywood, high-density fibreboard, or softwood battens. Engineered boards are more dimensionally stable than solid wood and may be installed above, on, or below grade. The thickness of the wear layer determines how many times the floor can be refinished.

Wood laminate and acrylic-impregnated wood

Laminate flooring uses a photographic image of wood bonded to a fibreboard core under a clear wear layer; it contains no real surface wood. Acrylic-impregnated wood, developed in the 1960s, injects acrylic monomers into the cell structure of the timber and polymerises them, substantially increasing hardness and wear resistance.

Species and grading

Common species include oak (red and white), maple, American cherry, hickory, walnut, ash, and imported tropical species such as Brazilian cherry (jatoba), teak, and merbau. Hardness is measured by the Janka hardness test. Grading terms such as "clear", "select", "common", and "rustic" describe the amount of colour variation, knots, and mineral streaks present.

Installation and patterns

Traditional installations lay boards parallel to the longest wall, but decorative patterns include herringbone, chevron, basket weave, and parquet (mosaic panels of small wood blocks, popularised at Versailles in the 17th century). Floating installation, in which boards are glued or clicked together but not fixed to the subfloor, is common for engineered products.

Finishing and maintenance

Finishes include site-applied polyurethane, factory-applied aluminium oxide coatings, hard-wax oils, and traditional shellac or lacquer. Wood floors should be protected from standing water, swept or vacuumed regularly, and refinished when the coating wears through.

Comparison with other flooring

Wood flooring is warmer underfoot than stone or ceramic tile and generally increases residential resale value, but it is more expensive and moisture-sensitive than vinyl, laminate, or tile. See also flooring and parquet.
