Modica

Modica (Sicilian: Muòrica) is a city and comune in the province of Ragusa, Sicily, southern Italy, with a population of about 54,000. The city is situated in the Hyblaean Mountains, in a deep gorge carved by rivers that have since been covered over, and is divided into Modica Alta (upper) and Modica Bassa (lower). Together with seven other towns of the Val di Noto, it is listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site for its late Baroque architecture.

History

The site was inhabited from prehistoric times — the necropolis of Quartiriccio dates to about 2200 BC — and the town, possibly the Sicel Motyka, passed through Greek, Roman, Arab (from 845, as Mudiqah) and Norman hands. Under Spanish rule it was the seat of a large semi-independent county. The catastrophic earthquake of 1693 destroyed much of the city; it was rebuilt in the Sicilian Baroque style, producing landmarks such as the Cathedral of San Giorgio, reached by a monumental flight of about 250 steps, and the church of San Pietro.

Chocolate

Modica is famous for its chocolate (cioccolato modicano), made by a cold process said to descend from Aztec techniques introduced under Spanish rule: cacao mass is mixed with sugar at low temperature so the sugar crystals never dissolve, giving the chocolate — often flavoured with chilli, vanilla, cinnamon or citrus — a distinctive grainy, crumbly texture. In 2018 Modica chocolate received EU Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) status, the first chocolate in Europe to do so.

Culture

The poet Salvatore Quasimodo, Nobel laureate in Literature (1959), was born in Modica in 1901; his birthplace is a museum. The town's Easter celebrations, centred on the Madonna "Vasa Vasa," and its cave dwellings and rock churches of the surrounding gorges are notable sights.
