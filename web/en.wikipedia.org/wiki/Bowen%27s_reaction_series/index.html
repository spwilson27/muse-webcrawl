Bowen's reaction series

Bowen's reaction series
Formulated by: Norman L. Bowen, 1915–1928
Field: Petrology / igneous geology

Bowen's reaction series is the work of Canadian petrologist Norman L. Bowen describing the order in which common silicate minerals crystallise from cooling magma, based on his experiments (1915, expanded in The Evolution of the Igneous Rocks, 1928).

The series

The discontinuous branch (ferromagnesian minerals) proceeds olivine → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite as temperature falls, each reacting with the melt to form the next. The continuous branch shows plagioclase feldspar evolving from calcium-rich to sodium-rich. The branches merge into alkali feldspar, muscovite, and finally quartz — the last to crystallise.

Significance

The series explains igneous rock composition, fractional crystallisation and magma differentiation, and predicts mineral stability/weathering (reverse order in Goldich's series). See Magma, Igneous rock, Norman L. Bowen.
