Shirley Temple (drink)

A Shirley Temple is a non-alcoholic mixed drink traditionally made with ginger ale and a splash of grenadine, garnished with a maraschino cherry. Modern recipes often substitute lemon-lime soda or combine it with ginger ale, and sometimes add orange juice. It is commonly served to children dining with adults, as a celebratory alcohol-free alternative to a cocktail.

Origin

The drink is named after the child actress Shirley Temple. The most popular account credits a bartender at Chasen's restaurant in West Hollywood in the 1930s, who is said to have created it for Temple when she dined there; other claimants include the Royal Hawaiian Hotel in Honolulu and the Brown Derby. Temple herself later said she disliked the drink, finding it too sweet, and she successfully defended her name against bottled versions marketed in the 1980s.

Variations

Adding vodka produces a "Dirty Shirley". A version made with grenadine and lemon-lime soda alone is sometimes distinguished from the ginger-ale original. The drink's male counterpart, made similarly for the child actor and dancer, is the Roy Rogers, typically cola-based.

Recipe

Pour about 150–180 ml ginger ale over ice in a highball glass, add roughly 15 ml grenadine, stir gently, and garnish with a maraschino cherry.
