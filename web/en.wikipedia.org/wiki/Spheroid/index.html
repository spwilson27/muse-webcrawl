Spheroid

A spheroid, also known as an ellipsoid of revolution, is a quadric surface obtained by rotating an ellipse about one of its principal axes. In other words, it is an ellipsoid with two equal semi-diameters.

IMAGE: Diagram comparing prolate (rugby-ball-shaped) and oblate (flattened) spheroids generated by rotating an ellipse

Types

  • Prolate spheroid — the polar axis is longer than the equatorial diameter (like a rugby ball); generated by rotating an ellipse about its major axis.
  • Oblate spheroid — the equatorial diameter exceeds the polar axis (a flattened sphere); generated by rotating an ellipse about its minor axis.

Applications

The Earth is well approximated by an oblate spheroid, bulging at the equator due to rotation — a fact central to geodesy and to historic measurements such as the Schiehallion experiment. Spheroids also model rotating fluid bodies (planets and stars), rugby and American footballs, and many atomic nuclei.

Properties

The volume of a spheroid with equatorial radius a and polar radius c is (4/3)πa²c. Surface-area formulae differ between the prolate and oblate cases and involve inverse trigonometric or hyperbolic functions.
