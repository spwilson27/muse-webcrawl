Ephedra (plant)

Ephedra — genus of gymnosperm shrubs, family Ephedraceae

Ephedra is a genus of gymnosperm shrubs in the family Ephedraceae. The various species of Ephedra are widespread around the arid regions of the world, including North and South America, Europe, Asia, and northern Africa. Together with Gnetum and Welwitschia, it forms the division Gnetophyta, an unusual group of seed plants whose reproductive structures resemble those of flowering plants.

Description

Ephedra plants are much-branched shrubs (occasionally small trees or climbers) with slender, jointed, green photosynthetic stems and tiny scale-like leaves reduced to sheaths at the nodes. They are mostly dioecious: male cones produce pollen, while female cones bear ovules and often become fleshy and red at maturity, resembling berries. Notable species include Ephedra sinica (Chinese ma huang), E. distachya, E. nevadensis (Mormon tea), and E. trifurca.

Uses

Species of Ephedra are the natural source of the alkaloids ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, stimulants that raise heart rate and blood pressure and open the airways. E. sinica has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for thousands of years, chiefly for asthma and congestion. Ephedra-containing dietary supplements were widely marketed for weight loss and athletic performance until safety concerns — including cardiovascular events — led the US Food and Drug Administration to ban their sale in 2004, the first such ban under the 1994 dietary supplement law. Because ephedrine can be used to manufacture methamphetamine, sales of ephedra products are controlled in many jurisdictions.
