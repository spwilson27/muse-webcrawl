The Republic of North Ossetia-Alania is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe, on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus range. Its capital and largest city is Vladikavkaz. The republic takes its name from the Ossetians, an Iranian-speaking people descended in part from the medieval Alans.

Geography

North Ossetia borders Georgia to the south — including the disputed territory of South Ossetia, which Ossetians regard as the southern part of a divided homeland — and the Russian republics of Ingushetia, Chechnya, Kabardino-Balkaria and Stavropol Krai. The Terek River flows through the republic, and Mount Kazbek rises on its southern border.

History

The region came under Russian control in the late 18th and 19th centuries. Vladikavkaz was founded as a Russian fortress in 1784. In Soviet times the North Ossetian Autonomous Oblast was created in 1924 and raised to an autonomous republic in 1936. In September 2004 the republic was the site of the Beslan school siege, in which more than 330 people, many of them children, were killed during a hostage crisis.

Demographics

Ossetians form the majority of the population, with ethnic Russians the largest minority; most Ossetians are Orthodox Christians, with a significant Muslim minority. The Ossetian language belongs to the Iranian branch of the Indo-European family and is written in the Cyrillic alphabet.
