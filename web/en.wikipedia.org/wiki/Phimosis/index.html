Phimosis

Phimosis is the inability to retract the foreskin over the glans.

Phimosis is a condition in which the foreskin of the penis cannot be stretched to retract it past the glans. A balloon-like swelling under the foreskin may occur with urination. In young children, a non-retractile foreskin is normal physiological development and typically resolves by adolescence; in adults, persistent phimosis is pathological and usually results from scarring, infection, or skin conditions such as lichen sclerosus.

Causes and complications

Pathological phimosis in adults is most often caused by repeated inflammation (balanoposthitis), diabetes-related infection, forceful retraction causing micro-tears and scar tissue, or lichen sclerosus, which produces a whitish scarred ring at the foreskin tip. Complications can include painful erections, recurrent infection, urinary difficulties, and paraphimosis, in which a retracted tight foreskin becomes trapped behind the glans and requires urgent treatment.

Treatment

Treatment options range from topical corticosteroid creams combined with gentle stretching exercises, which succeed in most cases, to manual stretching devices and surgical procedures such as preputioplasty or circumcision for resistant or scarred cases. Physiological non-retraction in children generally requires no treatment other than hygiene and time.
