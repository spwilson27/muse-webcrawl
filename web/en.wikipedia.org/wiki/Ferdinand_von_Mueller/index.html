Baron Sir Ferdinand Jacob Heinrich von Mueller (1825–1896) was a German-born Australian botanist and explorer, the dominant figure in Australian plant science in the 19th century. He described thousands of species, named much of Australia's flora, and built one of the world's great herbaria.

Career

Mueller trained as a pharmacist and studied botany at Kiel before emigrating to South Australia in 1847 for his health. In 1853 he became the first Government Botanist of the young colony of Victoria, and in 1857 director of the Melbourne Botanic Gardens, a post he held until 1873. He travelled widely on collecting expeditions, including Augustus Gregory's North Australian expedition of 1855–56, and distributed seeds and plants worldwide — notably promoting eucalypts, which he regarded as a boon for other dry countries.

Legacy

Mueller founded the National Herbarium of Victoria, helped establish the Royal Society of Victoria, and was made a hereditary baron by the King of Württemberg in 1871. The botanical author abbreviation "F.Muell." follows his name on some 2,000 species. The Victorian town of Mueller's name survives in Mount Mueller, the Mueller River and many plant names, including Eucalyptus muelleriana.
