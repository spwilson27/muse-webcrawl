The Fulbright Program, including the Fulbright–Hays Program, is one of several United States cultural exchange programs whose goal is to improve intercultural relations, cultural diplomacy, and intercultural competence between the United States and other countries through the mutual exchange of students, scholars, and teachers. It was founded in 1946 under legislation introduced by Senator J. William Fulbright of Arkansas, initially funded by the sale of surplus war property.

Structure

The program operates in more than 160 countries, coordinated by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs and, in about 50 countries, by binational Fulbright commissions. Grants support U.S. students, scholars, teachers, and professionals to study, teach, or conduct research abroad, and equivalent exchanges for citizens of other countries to the United States. Approximately 8,000 grants are awarded annually. Distinctive components include the Fulbright U.S. Student Program, the Fulbright U.S. Scholar Program, and the Fulbright Foreign Language Teaching Assistant Program.

Alumni

Fulbright alumni include dozens of heads of state or government, Nobel Prize laureates, MacArthur Foundation fellows, and Pulitzer Prize winners. The program is widely regarded as among the most prestigious academic exchange schemes in the world, and a flagship instrument of American cultural diplomacy.
