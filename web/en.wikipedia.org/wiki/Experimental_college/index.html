Experimental college

An experimental college is an educational institution or program that departs from conventional structures of curriculum, grading, and governance in order to test alternative approaches to teaching and learning. The term is associated particularly with two phenomena: small residential liberal-arts colleges founded on progressive principles, and student-run course collectives within larger universities.

Residential experimental colleges

Examples include Black Mountain College (1933–1957), known for its influence on the arts; Deep Springs College, a two-year school on a working cattle ranch; St. John's College with its Great Books curriculum; and colleges such as Hampshire, Evergreen State, and Marlboro, which adopted narrative evaluations instead of grades, interdisciplinary study, and self-designed majors. The movement flourished during the educational reform ferment of the 1960s and 1970s, when many such colleges were founded in the United States.

Student-run programs

On many campuses "Experimental College" (or ExCo) names a programme, largely student-taught, offering not-for-credit or low-credit courses in subjects outside the regular curriculum — a model popularized by the San Francisco State and Oberlin experimental colleges in the 1960s.
