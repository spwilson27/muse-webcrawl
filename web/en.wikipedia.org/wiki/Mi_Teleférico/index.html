Mi Teleférico

Location: La Paz and El Alto, Bolivia

Opened: 30 May 2014

System length: approx. 33 km (10 lines)

Operator: Empresa Estatal de Transporte por Cable "Mi Teleférico"

Mi Teleférico (Spanish for "My Cable Car"), also known as Teleférico La Paz–El Alto, is an aerial cable car urban transit system serving the La Paz–El Alto metropolitan area in Bolivia. It is the longest and highest urban cable-car network in the world, built to connect the city of La Paz, which sits in a deep canyon at about 3,600 metres above sea level, with the rapidly growing city of El Alto on the Altiplano plateau above it, at about 4,100 metres.

History

The project was announced by President Evo Morales in 2012 and built by the Austrian company Doppelmayr. The first three lines — Red, Yellow, and Green — opened in 2014, with the Red Line inaugurated on 30 May 2014. A second phase added further lines (Blue, Orange, White, Sky Blue, Purple, Brown, and Silver), bringing the network to ten lines and more than 30 kilometres, with over 1,300 cabins. The system was built at a cost of roughly US$740 million across both phases.

Operation and impact

Cabins carry ten passengers and depart stations every 12 seconds or so, giving each line a capacity of up to about 3,000 passengers per hour per direction. A trip that could take an hour or more by road through the steep, congested streets between El Alto and La Paz takes only minutes by cable car. The system carries well over 100,000 passengers daily and has become a major tourist attraction as well as public transport, noted for its panoramic views of La Paz and the Illimani mountain.

Mi Teleférico is frequently cited internationally as a model of cable-propelled transit for cities with difficult topography, following the earlier example of Medellín's Metrocable in Colombia.
