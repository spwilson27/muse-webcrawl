Historia Calamitatum

The Historia Calamitatum (The Story of My Misfortunes) is an autobiographical letter written by the medieval philosopher and theologian Peter Abelard around 1132. Addressed to an unnamed friend, it recounts Abelard's academic triumphs and the disasters that befell him, and it famously prompted the exchange of letters between Abelard and Héloïse that became one of the most celebrated correspondences of the Middle Ages.

Contents

Abelard narrates his rise as a dialectician: his studies under Roscellinus and William of Champeaux, his victories over his masters in debate, and his fame as a teacher in Paris. He then describes his affair with Héloïse, his brilliant pupil, their secret marriage, and the revenge of her uncle Fulbert, whose men castrated Abelard. Both entered religious life — Abelard as a monk at Saint-Denis, Héloïse as a nun at Argenteuil. Abelard goes on to describe the condemnation and burning of his book on the Trinity at the Council of Soissons (1121), his unhappy abbacy at St. Gildas in Brittany, and the founding of the Paraclete oratory, which he later gave to Héloïse and her nuns.

Context and the Letters

The Historia was written as a consolatio — a letter of consolation arguing that the author's sufferings exceeded the addressee's. Héloïse obtained a copy, and her passionate reply began the famous correspondence, in which the two debated love, marriage, vocation and Abelard's theological advice to her community.

Significance

The work is regarded as one of the first true autobiographies of the Latin Middle Ages and a key document for understanding twelfth-century intellectual life, the schools of Paris, and the early development of scholasticism. Its frank self-examination anticipates later autobiographical writing in the West.
