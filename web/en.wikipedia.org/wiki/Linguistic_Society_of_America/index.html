Linguistic Society of America

The Linguistic Society of America (LSA) is a learned society for the scientific study of language, founded in 1924 in New York City. It is the largest linguistic society in the world and serves linguists and others interested in the study of language through publications, meetings, and advocacy.

Activities

The LSA publishes the flagship journal Language, along with other journals and proceedings. It holds an annual meeting each January, co-located with sister societies, and runs the biennial Linguistic Institute, a summer school in linguistics held since 1928. The Society also engages in public advocacy on language-related policy, including language rights and education.

History

Among its founders were prominent early American linguists such as Leonard Bloomfield, George Melville Bolling, and Edgar Sturtevant. Membership grew over the twentieth century as linguistics developed as an academic discipline in the United States.
