Battle of Mantinea (418 BC)

The Battle of Mantinea was fought in 418 BC during the Peloponnesian War, near the city of Mantinea in Arcadia. A Spartan-led army under King Agis II defeated the combined forces of Argos, Athens, Mantinea and Elis. It was the largest land battle of the war and restored Spartan prestige, badly damaged by the surrender at Sphacteria in 425 BC. Thucydides gives a famous detailed account.

Background

After the Peace of Nicias (421 BC), Argos built an anti-Spartan coalition with Mantinea, Elis and Athens. In 418 Agis marched into the Argolid, but a truce (the controversial "Four Months' Truce") let the Argives slip away; discontent forced Agis to seek battle again. Manoeuvring around Mantinea — including an attempt to divert a stream to flood the city — ended with the armies meeting on the plain.

The battle

As the armies advanced, the Spartan line drifted right, as hoplite lines do, outflanking the enemy left. Agis attempted a complicated redeployment of the Skiritai and his left-hand divisions to plug a gap, but his subordinate polemarchs refused the order, leaving a hole in the Spartan left. The Mantineans and Argive picked troops broke through there and killed the Athenian generals Laches and Nicostratus. But the Spartan centre and right smashed the Argive and Athenian lines opposite them, then wheeled to rescue their own left and complete the victory.

Aftermath

Sparta regained dominance in the Peloponnese: the Argive coalition collapsed, Argos made peace and later an alliance with Sparta, and Mantinea came to terms. Spartan confidence, shaken since Pylos, was restored.
