EOTech, Inc. is an American company that designs, manufactures and markets electro-optic products and systems, best known for its holographic weapon sights (HWS) for small arms. Headquartered in Ann Arbor, Michigan, the company grew out of research at the Environmental Research Institute of Michigan (ERIM), where holographic sighting technology originally developed for fighter aircraft head-up displays was adapted for firearms.

Products

EOTech's holographic sights project a laser-generated hologram of a reticle — typically a 68-MOA circle with a 1-MOA center dot — onto a viewing window. Unlike a conventional red dot sight, the reticle is embedded in a holographic grating, so it remains visible across the whole window and appears focused at the target distance. Popular models include the 512, XPS2 and EXPS3 series. The company also produces magnifiers, thermal and night-vision devices, and laser aiming modules.

Military use and controversy

EOTech sights were widely adopted by U.S. and allied military and law-enforcement units, including U.S. special operations forces. In 2015, the U.S. government sued EOTech's then-parent L-3 Communications, alleging the company had known of defects causing thermal drift (point-of-aim shift with temperature) and reticle fading in cold weather and humidity without disclosing them. L-3 settled the case for $25.6 million, and EOTech offered refunds to owners. In 2020 L3Harris sold EOTech to an affiliate of the private equity firm American Holtzkamp.
