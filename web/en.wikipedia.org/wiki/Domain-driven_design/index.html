Domain-driven design

Domain-driven design (DDD) is an approach to software development that centres the design on a rich conceptual model of the business domain, developed collaboratively by developers and domain experts. The term was coined by Eric Evans in his 2003 book Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software.

Key concepts

DDD emphasises a ubiquitous language shared by team and experts, and modelled building blocks: entities, value objects, aggregates with aggregate roots enforcing invariants, repositories, domain services and domain events. Bounded contexts delimit where a model applies, with context maps describing relationships between models — ideas later influential in microservices architecture.

Strategic and tactical design

Strategic design addresses decomposing large systems into bounded contexts; tactical design supplies the patterns for modelling within one. The approach is recommended chiefly for complex domains where business rules, rather than technology, dominate the difficulty.
