Elephantine is an island in the Nile at Aswan in southern Egypt, forming part of the city. In ancient times it marked Egypt's southern frontier with Nubia and was a major trading post for ivory — hence its name (Egyptian Abu, Greek Elephantine).

History

The island hosted temples of the ram-headed god Khnum and the goddess Satet, repeatedly rebuilt from the Old Kingdom to Roman times, and a famous Nilometer used to measure flood levels. A fortress guarded the First Cataract frontier. In the Persian period a Jewish military colony lived here with its own temple of YHW; their Aramaic Elephantine papyri (5th century BC) are a crucial source for early Jewish history.

Archaeology

Excavations by German and Swiss institutes have uncovered stratified settlement layers spanning 5,000 years. The Aswan Museum stands on the island's southern end; today the island also contains villages of Nubian residents and a major hotel.
