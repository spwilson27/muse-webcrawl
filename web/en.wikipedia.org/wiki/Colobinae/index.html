Colobinae

The Colobinae are a subfamily of Old World monkeys comprising about 60 species in 10 genera, including the black-and-white colobus, red colobus, langurs, lutungs, proboscis monkey and the odd-nosed snub-nosed monkeys. They are often called leaf-eating monkeys.

Characteristics

Colobines possess a large, multi-chambered stomach where bacteria ferment cellulose, letting them digest mature leaves that few other primates can use — an adaptation convergent with ruminants. Most have reduced or absent thumbs (the name colobus derives from Greek for 'mutilated'), long tails, and ischial callosities. Unlike their cercopithecine cousins they lack cheek pouches.

Distribution and conservation

African colobines include the colobus and red colobus monkeys; Asian ones the langurs, lutungs, surilis, doucs, snub-nosed monkeys and the proboscis monkey of Borneo. Many species are threatened by hunting and deforestation; several red colobus taxa rank among the world's most endangered primates.
