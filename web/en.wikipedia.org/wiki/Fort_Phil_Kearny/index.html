Fort Phil Kearny

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Phil Kearny was a U.S. Army outpost established in 1866 in present-day Johnson County, Wyoming, to protect travellers on the Bozeman Trail. Named for Major General Philip Kearny, it was the largest of three forts built along the trail and became a focal point of Red Cloud's War, the conflict with the Lakota, Cheyenne, and Arapaho over control of the Powder River country.

History

The fort, garrisoned under Colonel Henry B. Carrington, was under near-constant harassment. On 21 December 1866, a detachment of 81 men under Captain William J. Fetterman was lured into an ambush and annihilated — the Army's worst defeat on the northern plains until Little Bighorn. In August 1867, soldiers near the fort repelled attacks in the Hayfield Fight and the Wagon Box Fight. Under the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868), the Army abandoned the Bozeman Trail forts, and Fort Phil Kearny was burned by the Cheyenne shortly after its evacuation.

Today

The site is a Wyoming state historic site with an interpretive centre; the Fetterman and Wagon Box battlefields are preserved nearby.
