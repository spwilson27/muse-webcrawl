Monastery of the Incarnation

The Monastery of the Incarnation (Spanish: Monasterio de la Encarnación) is a Carmelite convent in Ávila, Spain, founded in the late 15th and early 16th centuries. It is best known as the convent where Saint Teresa of Ávila spent much of her religious life: she entered the Incarnation in 1535, lived there for nearly thirty years, and served as its prioress from 1571 to 1574. Her cell and many objects associated with her are preserved in the museum attached to the convent.

Originally founded in 1478 as the Monastery of Saint Mary of Grace, the community moved to the site of a former Jewish cemetery in 1513 and took the name of the Incarnation. Unlike the discalced (reformed) convents Teresa later founded, the Incarnation followed the mitigated Carmelite rule, and its large, socially mixed community and relatively relaxed observance were among the circumstances that prompted Teresa's reform of the order. The church has a sober Herrerian-style façade, and the convent remains home to a community of Discalced Carmelite nuns.
