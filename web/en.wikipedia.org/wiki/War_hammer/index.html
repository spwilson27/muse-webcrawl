War hammer

A war hammer is a weapon used by both foot soldiers and cavalry in the late medieval period. It is a close-combat weapon intended to be wielded against armoured opponents, developed as plate armour became resistant to the cutting edge of swords.

Design

The war hammer typically consists of a handle and a head with a hammer face on one side and a beak-like spike, or pick, on the other. The hammer face could deliver blunt-force trauma through armour, while the spike could pierce armour or hook and pull at an opponent's shield or limbs. Handles varied from short one-handed versions to long two-handed polearms, such as the bec de corbin and the Lucerne hammer.

History

War hammers became popular in the 14th and 15th centuries as full plate harness spread across Europe. They were effective because armour designed to deflect blades could still be dented or penetrated by concentrated force. The weapon is frequently seen in the hands of knights in period artwork, including the famous depictions of fully armoured combat in martial arts treatises such as Hans Talhoffer's fechtbuch.

Related weapons

The war hammer is related to the mace, horseman's pick, and the pollaxe, all anti-armour impact weapons. A ceremonial or symbolic descendant persists in the judge's gavel-like mauls of some traditions, though its martial use faded with the decline of heavy armour in the gunpowder age.
