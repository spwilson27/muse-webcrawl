Anastomosis

An anastomosis connects two tubular structures, such as blood vessels or loops of intestine.

An anastomosis (plural anastomoses) is a connection or opening between two things (especially cavities or passages) that are normally diverging or branching, such as between blood vessels, leaf veins, or streams. The term is used in medicine, biology, mycology, geology, and other fields.

Medicine

An anastomosis is the connection of two normally divergent structures. It refers to connections between blood vessels or between loops of the intestine. A circulatory anastomosis is a connection between two blood vessels, such as between arteries (arterio-arterial anastomosis), between veins (veno-venous anastomosis) or between an artery and a vein (arterio-venous anastomosis). Anastomoses between arteries and between veins result in a multitude of arteries and veins, respectively, serving the same volume of tissue. Such anastomoses occur normally in the body in the circulatory system, serving as back-up routes in a collateral circulation that allows blood to flow if one link is blocked or otherwise compromised, but may also occur pathologically.

Surgery

A surgical anastomosis is a surgical technique used to make a new connection between two body structures. When an organ such as a segment of intestine is severed for surgical removal (resection), the ends may be reconnected by an anastomosis, restoring continuity. Examples include intestinal anastomosis after bowel resection, vascular anastomosis in bypass surgery, and the connections made during organ transplantation. Failure of a surgical anastomosis to heal properly can lead to leakage, a serious complication.

Biology

In evolution, anastomosis describes a pattern of branching in which lineages reconnect, as in the reticulate evolution seen in some plants, fungi, and symbiogenesis. In mycology, anastomosis is the fusion between branches of the same or different hyphae. In botany, the term describes veins in leaves that form a net-like pattern of connections.
