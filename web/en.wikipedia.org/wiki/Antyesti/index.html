Antyesti

Antyesti (Sanskrit: अन्त्येष्टि, literally "last sacrifice"), sometimes referred to as Antima Sanskar, is the funeral rites for the dead in Hinduism, which usually involve cremation of the body.

Antyesti, sometimes referred to as Antima Sanskar, is the Hindu funeral rite, the "last sacrifice", in which the deceased's body is typically cremated. The rite is one of the sixteen major samskaras (life-cycle sacraments) in Hindu tradition and is described in texts such as the Garuda Purana and the funeral hymns of the Rigveda.

The rite

The body is usually cremated within a day of death, washed, wrapped in cloth, and carried in procession to the cremation ground, traditionally on the bank of a river. The chief mourner — typically the eldest son — circumambulates the pyre and lights it, a rite known as mukhagni. After cremation, the ashes are collected and, in the rite of asthi visarjana, immersed in a sacred river such as the Ganges.

Beliefs

Cremation is believed to release the soul (atman) from the body and aid its passage to the ancestors, after which, through the shraddha offerings made by descendants, it joins the realm of the fathers (Pitrloka) before eventual rebirth. The period of mourning and the associated rituals extend over a number of days, commonly concluding with a ceremony on the tenth to thirteenth day.

Variations

Practice varies by region, caste, and sect. Infants, ascetics, and in some traditions those who die of particular diseases may be buried rather than cremated, and burial is customary in a number of Hindu communities.
