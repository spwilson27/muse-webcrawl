Netherlands Marine Corps

The Netherlands Marine Corps (Dutch: Korps Mariniers) is the elite naval infantry service branch of the Royal Netherlands Navy. Founded on 10 December 1665 by Michiel de Ruyter and Johan de Witt, it is one of the oldest marine corps in the world. Its motto is Qua Patet Orbis ("As far as the world extends"). The corps specializes in amphibious, arctic, and mountain warfare, as well as special operations support.

Dutch marines conducting amphibious training.

History

The corps was raised during the Second Anglo-Dutch War. Dutch marines fought in the raid on the Medway in 1667 and distinguished themselves in numerous colonial and overseas campaigns. In the Second World War, marines defended Rotterdam in May 1940, earning the nickname "Black Devils" from their German opponents, and fought in the Dutch East Indies. The corps later served in Korea, Cambodia, Iraq, Afghanistan, and in counter-piracy operations off Somalia.

Organization

The corps is headquartered in Rotterdam and organized within the Netherlands Maritime Force. Its principal components include marine battalions grouped in the Marine Combat Groups, the Maritime Special Operations Forces (MARSOF), including the Unit Interventie Mariniers (UIM) for domestic counter-terrorism, and surface assault and training elements. Since 1972 the corps has trained and operated closely with the British Royal Marines as part of the UK/NL Amphibious Force.
