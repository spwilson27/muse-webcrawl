Sécurité (French for "security" or "safety") most commonly refers in an institutional context to the Sécurité civile, the French civil protection service responsible for disaster relief, firefighting support, search and rescue, and emergency management throughout France and in international assistance operations.

Organisation

The Sécurité civile is administered by the Directorate General for Civil Security and Crisis Management (DGSCGC) within the Ministry of the Interior. Its operational resources include:

  • The military civil-security formations (formations militaires de la sécurité civile), army units specialised in disaster response, including the UIISC regiments based at Nogent-le-Rotrou, Brignoles and Corte.
  • A fleet of firefighting aircraft and helicopters based at Nîmes–Garons, including Canadair CL-415 water bombers, Dash 8 airtankers and EC145 helicopters.
  • Bomb disposal (déminage) services, which neutralise unexploded ordnance, much of it dating from the two World Wars.
  • The BRGM-supported rescue and intervention units deployed after natural disasters in France and abroad.

Role

Day-to-day emergency response in France is largely the responsibility of departmental fire and rescue services (SDIS) staffed by sapeurs-pompiers. The Sécurité civile coordinates major incidents, provides specialised national reinforcements — particularly during the summer wildfire season in the Mediterranean south — and represents France in the European Union Civil Protection Mechanism, deploying rescue teams after earthquakes, floods and industrial accidents worldwide.

History

Organised civil defence in France grew out of the "passive defence" measures of the 1930s and the Second World War. A national civil protection service was created in 1951; it took the name Sécurité civile in 1975. The modern directorate was formed by successive reorganisations, most recently in 2011 and 2013, integrating crisis management functions.
