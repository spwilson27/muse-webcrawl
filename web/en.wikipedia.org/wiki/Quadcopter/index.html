Quadcopter

A quadcopter, or quadrotor, is a multirotor aircraft lifted and propelled by four rotors. Two rotors spin clockwise and two counter-clockwise, cancelling torque so that no anti-torque tail rotor is needed. Flight is controlled by varying the speed of individual rotors to produce pitch, roll, yaw and thrust.

History

Full-scale quadcopters were among the earliest heavier-than-air craft attempted: the Breguet-Richet Gyroplane No. 1 lifted a pilot briefly in 1907, and the de Bothezat helicopter was tested by the US Army in the 1920s. The configuration was abandoned for crewed aircraft because of complexity, but revived in the 2000s for small unmanned aerial vehicles thanks to cheap sensors, brushless motors and lithium batteries.

Uses

Quadcopters dominate the consumer drone market and are used for aerial photography, racing, inspection, agriculture, research and military reconnaissance. Their mechanical simplicity and agility come at the cost of lower aerodynamic efficiency than fixed-wing aircraft.
