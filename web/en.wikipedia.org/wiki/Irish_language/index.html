Irish language

Irish

Native name: Gaeilge
Family: Indo-European > Celtic > Goidelic
Status: First official language of Ireland; EU official
Daily speakers: ~70,000 (plus many learners)

Irish (Gaeilge), also known as Irish Gaelic, is a Goidelic language of the Celtic family, indigenous to Ireland, and historically the island's dominant language.

History

Irish is attested from Primitive Irish ogham inscriptions, through Old and Middle Irish literary wealth, to Classical Irish. English rule, and especially the Great Famine of the 1840s, drove a catastrophic shift to English. The Gaelic League (founded 1893) led a revival; after independence in 1922 Irish became the first official language, compulsory in schools, though native communities — the Gaeltacht — continued to shrink.

Present day

About 1.7 million people in the Republic report some ability in Irish, with tens of thousands of daily speakers, concentrated in western Gaeltacht districts in Donegal, Galway, Mayo and Kerry. Irish-language media include RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta and TG4, and Irish became a full EU official language in 2022. Its three main dialects are Ulster, Connacht and Munster.
