Rampage killer

A rampage killer, or spree killer, is a person who commits multiple homicides in a single continuous episode, or in a series of attacks over a short period without the "cooling-off" interval that characterises serial murder. The term is commonly applied to perpetrators of mass shootings and other mass killings carried out in public places.

Definitions

Criminologists distinguish rampage killings from serial killing (multiple victims over an extended period with cooling-off intervals) and from mass murder in a single location. Definitions vary between agencies: the FBI has used the term "spree killer" for two or more murders committed without a cooling-off period, while some modern frameworks fold rampage attacks into active-shooter and mass-killing categories.

Characteristics

Research profiles typically note grievance, social isolation, and suicidal intent as common features; many rampage attacks end with the perpetrator's suicide or death at the hands of police. The phenomenon, documented across many countries, has driven policy debates about firearms access, media coverage of perpetrators, and threat-assessment practices in schools and workplaces.
