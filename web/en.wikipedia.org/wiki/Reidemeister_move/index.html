Reidemeister move

Field: Knot theory
Introduced by: Kurt Reidemeister (1927); independently J. W. Alexander and G. B. Briggs
Number of types: Three

In knot theory, a Reidemeister move is one of three local manipulations of a knot diagram that does not change the knot represented. A theorem of Kurt Reidemeister (1927), also proved independently by James Waddell Alexander and Garland Baird Briggs, states that two diagrams represent the same knot or link if and only if they are related by a finite sequence of these moves together with planar deformations.

The three moves

Type I adds or removes a single twist in a strand. Type II slides one strand over another, creating or cancelling a pair of crossings. Type III slides a strand across a crossing without changing the crossings themselves. Moves are understood up to mirror versions and rotations of the pictured configurations.

Significance

The theorem reduces the study of knots to the study of diagrams modulo the three moves. A quantity defined on diagrams is a knot invariant if it is unchanged by all three moves; this is the standard method for proving that quantities such as the bracket polynomial and many knot polynomials are genuine invariants.
