Baji Rao II

Baji Rao II (1775 – 28 January 1851) was the 13th and the last Peshwa of the Maratha Confederacy. He was installed as a puppet ruler by the nobles whose father was Raghunathrao. He ruled from 1795 to 1818, when he was defeated by the British East India Company in the Third Anglo-Maratha War and the Peshwa's office was abolished.

Early life and accession

Baji Rao was the son of the former Peshwa Raghunathrao and Anandibai. Suspected of involvement in the murder of Peshwa Narayan Rao, Raghunathrao's family was kept under close watch. After the death of Peshwa Madhavrao II in 1795, Baji Rao II was installed as Peshwa with the support of the powerful minister Nana Fadnavis, whose influence he resented and later escaped.

Reign

His reign was marked by intrigue and the decline of Maratha power. In 1802, defeated by Yashwantrao Holkar at the Battle of Poona, Baji Rao fled to the British and signed the Treaty of Bassein, accepting a subsidiary alliance — a step that led to the Second Anglo-Maratha War. In 1817 he rose against the British in the Third Anglo-Maratha War but was defeated at the battles of Khadki, Koregaon, and Ashti.

Exile and death

Baji Rao surrendered in June 1818 and was pensioned off to Bithoor, near Kanpur, where he lived for more than three decades on an annual pension of eight lakh rupees. He died in 1851. His adopted son, Nana Saheb, denied the pension after Baji Rao's death, became a leading figure in the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
