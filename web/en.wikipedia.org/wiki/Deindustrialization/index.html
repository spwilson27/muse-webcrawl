Deindustrialization

Deindustrialization is a process of social and economic change caused by the removal or reduction of industrial capacity or activity in a country or region, especially of heavy industry or manufacturing industry. It is the opposite of industrialization. The term came into wide use in the late twentieth century to describe the decline of manufacturing in the advanced economies of North America and Western Europe, exemplified by the "Rust Belt" of the United States.

Causes

Several mechanisms are proposed. A mature economy's demand shifts from goods towards services, so employment naturally migrates to the service sector. Productivity growth in manufacturing means output can rise even as factory employment falls. Globalisation and trade liberalisation moved labour-intensive production to lower-wage countries, while automation reduced the number of workers needed per unit of output. Changes in exchange rates, transport costs, and investment patterns reinforced these trends.

Consequences

Regions dependent on a single industry experienced plant closures, job losses, population decline, and deterioration of urban infrastructure. Former industrial workers often faced long-term unemployment or lower-paid service work, and deindustrialization has been linked to declining union membership, widening regional inequality, and political realignment. In the United States, manufacturing employment peaked in 1979 at about 19.5 million jobs and fell sharply during the 1980s and 2000s, even where manufacturing output continued to grow.

Interpretations

Economists debate whether deindustrialization is a natural and largely benign stage of development or a policy failure. "Positive" deindustrialization, driven by productivity growth in a full-employment economy, contrasts with "premature" deindustrialization in developing countries, where manufacturing shrinks before incomes have risen, and with decline driven by trade shocks. Recent policy responses have included reshoring incentives, industrial strategy, and place-based regeneration programmes for former industrial towns.
