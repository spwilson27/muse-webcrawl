Foresight (futures studies)

Foresight, in futures studies, is the systematic exploration of possible, probable and preferable futures to support present-day decision-making. Unlike prediction, which claims to state what will happen, foresight examines multiple plausible futures and their implications, and is a core activity of governments, corporations and research organisations planning under uncertainty.

Methods

Common foresight methods include horizon scanning for weak signals of change, the Delphi method, trend extrapolation, scenario planning, backcasting from desired futures, futures workshops, and roadmapping. The "futures cone" diagram, distinguishing possible, plausible, probable and preferable futures, is a widely used framing device in the field.

Strategic foresight

Strategic foresight applies these methods to policy and strategy. National programmes such as Singapore's Centre for Strategic Futures and Finland's parliamentary Committee for the Future institutionalise foresight in government, while organisations such as the OECD and the European Commission run foresight units. Foresight exercises typically span horizons of ten to fifty years.
