Port Lincoln

Port Lincoln is a town on the Lower Eyre Peninsula in the Australian state of South Australia, situated on the shore of Boston Bay, one of the largest protected natural harbours in the world. With a population of about 16,000, it is the largest town on the Eyre Peninsula and styles itself the "Seafood Capital of Australia".

History

The area was the home of the Barngarla (Parnkalla) Aboriginal people, who called it Galinyala. Matthew Flinders charted the bay in 1802 and named it in honour of his home county of Lincolnshire. Although considered as a site for the South Australian capital, a shortage of fresh water led to Adelaide being chosen instead; Port Lincoln was settled from 1839 and grew as a port for the peninsula's grain farms.

Economy

Port Lincoln's wealth is built on fishing and aquaculture: its southern bluefin tuna fleet and ranching industry, pioneered by families such as the Lukins and Stehrs, made the town famous — at one point it was said to have more millionaires per capita than anywhere else in Australia. Kingfish, mussels, abalone, and oysters are also farmed, and tourism includes cage diving with great white sharks off the Neptune Islands.
