The Cotton Bowl is an outdoor stadium in Dallas, Texas, United States. Opened in 1930 as Fair Park Stadium, it stands in Fair Park, the grounds of the State Fair of Texas. The stadium gave its name to the Cotton Bowl Classic college football bowl game, which it hosted from 1937 until the game moved to AT&T Stadium after the 2009 edition.

[Image: Cotton Bowl stadium, Dallas]

History

Built in 1930 with a capacity of about 46,000, the stadium was enlarged repeatedly, reaching over 75,000 seats by the late 1940s and its present capacity of 92,967 after renovations in 2008–2009. It was the original home of the Dallas Cowboys of the NFL (1960–1971), the Dallas Texans of the AFL, and the SMU Mustangs, whose 1948 season, led by Doak Walker, drove one of the expansions and earned the venue the nickname "The House That Doak Built."

Events

The stadium's signature annual event is the Red River Rivalry between the Oklahoma Sooners and Texas Longhorns, played there since 1932 during the State Fair of Texas. It also hosted matches of the 1994 FIFA World Cup and has staged the Dallas Cup soccer tournament and numerous concerts. From 2021 the First Responder Bowl has been played at the stadium.
