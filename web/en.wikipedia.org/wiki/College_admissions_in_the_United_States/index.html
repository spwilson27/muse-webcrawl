College admissions in the United States is the process by which students apply to and are selected by colleges and universities for undergraduate study. Unlike many countries with centralized national systems, admissions in the United States are decentralized: each institution sets its own criteria, deadlines, and selection methods, and students commonly apply to several institutions at once.

Application components

A typical application includes a secondary school transcript showing courses and grades, a school report, letters of recommendation from teachers and a counselor, a personal essay and institution-specific supplements, a record of extracurricular activities, and — where required or submitted — standardized test scores on the SAT or ACT. Many colleges use the Common Application, which allows a student to apply to multiple member institutions with one core form. Portfolios or auditions are required for some arts programs, and interviews are offered by some selective colleges.

Admissions plans

Colleges offer several application plans. Regular Decision deadlines usually fall in December or January, with decisions released by early spring. Early Action is non-binding with autumn deadlines, while Early Decision binds the applicant to attend if admitted. Some institutions use rolling admissions, evaluating applications as they arrive. Admitted students generally must reply by May 1, National Candidates Reply Date.

Holistic review and selectivity

Highly selective institutions practice holistic review, weighing academic achievement alongside essays, recommendations, activities, and context such as family background and school resources. A small number of colleges admit fewer than 10 percent of applicants, while most four-year colleges admit a majority. Following the Supreme Court's 2023 decisions in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard and the companion UNC case, race-conscious affirmative action in admissions was ended nationwide.

Financial aid

Cost and aid are integral to the process. Applicants for aid file the FAFSA (and, at some private colleges, the CSS Profile); institutions respond with packages combining grants, loans, and work-study. Some colleges are need-blind and meet full demonstrated need; others are need-aware. Transfer admission, by which students move between institutions, and admission of international students follow parallel but distinct procedures.
