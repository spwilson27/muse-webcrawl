The Rolls-Royce Merlin is a British liquid-cooled V-12 piston aero engine of 27 litres' capacity. Designed by Rolls-Royce and first run in 1933 as the PV-12, it became one of the most successful aircraft engines of the Second World War, powering the Hawker Hurricane, Supermarine Spitfire, Avro Lancaster and de Havilland Mosquito. License-built in the United States by Packard as the V-1650, it also powered the North American P-51 Mustang, transforming it into one of the finest fighters of the war.

Design and development

The Merlin used a single-stage, later two-stage, supercharger to maintain power at altitude. Output grew from about 890 hp in early versions to over 2,000 hp in late-war variants through improved supercharging, stronger components and higher-octane fuels. Development chief Stanley Hooker's supercharger work was central to these gains.

Applications and legacy

Merlin-powered aircraft played decisive roles in the Battle of Britain and the bomber offensive. After the war Merlins served in airliners, transports and racing aircraft, and remain prized in warbird restorations. It is widely considered one of the finest piston aero engines ever built and was succeeded in Rolls-Royce service by the larger Griffon.
