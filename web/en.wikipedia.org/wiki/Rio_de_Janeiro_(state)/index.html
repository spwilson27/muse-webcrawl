Rio de Janeiro is one of the 26 states of Brazil, located in the Southeast Region. It has the country's second-largest state economy and third-largest population. Its capital, the city of Rio de Janeiro, served as Brazil's capital from 1763 to 1960 and hosted the 2016 Summer Olympics.

History

Guanabara Bay was sighted by the Portuguese in January 1502—hence "River of January." The city of São Sebastião do Rio de Janeiro was founded in 1565 after expelling French colonists. The Portuguese court transferred to Rio in 1808, fleeing Napoleon, and the city became the capital of the Empire and then the Republic until Brasília's inauguration in 1960. The state in its current form dates from the 1975 merger of Guanabara and the old Rio de Janeiro state.

Geography

The state is dominated by the Serra do Mar escarpment, coastal lagoons, and resort regions: the Costa Verde (Angra dos Reis, Paraty) to the south and the Região dos Lagos (Búzios, Cabo Frio) to the north. Petrópolis, the imperial summer residence city, lies in the mountains. Offshore oil fields of the Santos and Campos basins make Rio a center of Brazil's petroleum industry.

Culture and economy

Rio is the birthplace of samba and bossa nova; its carnival, centered on the Sambadrome, is world-famous. Tourism, oil and gas, media (Globo), and finance anchor the economy. Like Bahia to the north, Rio state is central to Brazil's national identity and carnival traditions.
