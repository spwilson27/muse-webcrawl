Tumshuqese language

Tumshuqese is an extinct Eastern Iranian language of the Saka group, once spoken in the oasis kingdom of Tumshuq in the northwestern Tarim Basin (present-day Xinjiang, China). It is closely related to Khotanese, the Saka language of the neighbouring kingdom of Khotan, and like it is known chiefly from Buddhist manuscripts written in a form of the Brahmi script, dating mainly from the 7th to 10th centuries CE.

Texts

The surviving corpus is small and consists largely of Buddhist sutras and monastic documents recovered from sites around Tumshuq by expeditions in the early 20th century. Tumshuqese differs from Khotanese in a number of phonological and morphological details, showing that the two oasis kingdoms maintained distinct literary languages despite their kinship. The language died out as the region was gradually Turkicised following the expansion of the Uyghurs.
