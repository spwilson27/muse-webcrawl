Data protection

Data protection is the process of safeguarding important data from corruption, compromise or loss, and providing for its restoration following an adverse event. In legal usage — the sense common in European law — it refers to the body of rules governing the processing of personal data and the privacy rights of individuals; the two senses are distinguished in this article.

Technical data protection

Organisations protect data through backup and replication, access control and encryption, and disaster-recovery planning. Key measures include the 3-2-1 backup rule (three copies, on two different media, one off-site), regular restore testing, and defined recovery point and recovery time objectives. Threats include hardware failure, ransomware, accidental deletion, and insider misuse.

Legal data protection

The legal discipline developed from the 1970s, beginning with the German state of Hesse's data protection act of 1970 and Sweden's Data Act of 1973. The Council of Europe's Convention 108 (1981) and the EU Data Protection Directive (1995) established principles of lawful, purpose-limited processing and rights for data subjects. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), applicable from 2018, unified and strengthened these rules across the European Union, introducing substantial fines and requirements such as data protection impact assessments and, for many organisations, a designated data protection officer. Other jurisdictions have enacted comparable laws, including California's CCPA/CPRA and Brazil's LGPD.
