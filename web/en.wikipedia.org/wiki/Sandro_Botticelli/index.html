Sandro Botticelli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Birth of Venus (c. 1485): the goddess standing on a great shell at the shore, blown by Zephyr, greeted by a Hora with a floral cloak]

Alessandro di Mariano di Vanni Filipepi (c. 1445 – 17 May 1510), better known as Sandro Botticelli, was an Italian painter of the Early Renaissance. He belonged to the Florentine School under the patronage of the Medici and is best known for his mythological paintings, which revived classical subjects for Christian humanist patrons.

His masterpieces include Primavera (c. 1482) and The Birth of Venus (c. 1485) — the first full-length female nude painted since antiquity — as well as frescoes in the Sistine Chapel (1481–82) and illustrations to Dante's Divine Comedy. His graceful linear style fell from fashion after 1500 and he was largely forgotten until the 19th-century Pre-Raphaelites revived interest in his work.
