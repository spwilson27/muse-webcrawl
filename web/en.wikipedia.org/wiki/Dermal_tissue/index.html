Dermal tissue

The dermal tissue system of plants is the outer protective covering of the plant body. It forms the first line of defence against physical damage and pathogens, and in leaves it regulates gas exchange and water loss.

Components

The primary dermal tissue is the epidermis, a single layer of cells covering young plant organs. Epidermal specialisations include stomata flanked by guard cells, trichomes (hairs), and the waxy cuticle. In woody plants the epidermis is replaced by the periderm, which includes cork cells forming bark.

Function

Beyond protection, dermal tissue controls transpiration, aids in absorption in roots via root hairs, and can deter herbivory.
