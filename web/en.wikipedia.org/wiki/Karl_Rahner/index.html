Karl Rahner

Karl Rahner SJ (5 March 1904 – 30 March 1984) was a German Jesuit priest and Catholic theologian, widely regarded as one of the most influential Catholic theologians of the 20th century. He served as a peritus (expert adviser) at the Second Vatican Council.

Life

Born in Freiburg im Breisgau, Rahner entered the Society of Jesus in 1922. His doctoral work under the influence of Joseph Maréchal and in dialogue with Martin Heidegger developed a transcendental interpretation of Thomas Aquinas, published in Spirit in the World (1939). He taught dogmatic theology at Innsbruck, Munich, and Münster.

Theology

Rahner's theology centred on human experience as openness to God's self-communication in grace. Key concepts include the "supernatural existential" — the idea that every human being is constitutively oriented toward divine revelation — and the controversial notion of "anonymous Christians", those who implicitly accept grace without explicit Christian faith. His multi-volume Theological Investigations explored topics from Christology and the Trinity to pastoral and ecumenical questions. His economic/Immanent Trinity axiom — "the economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity and vice versa" — remains a standard reference point in Trinitarian theology.

Legacy

Rahner's work shaped the documents of Vatican II and post-conciliar Catholic theology, though elements of his thought attracted criticism from both traditionalist and liberal quarters.
