Mireille (opera)

A performance of Mireille

Mireille is an opera in five acts by Charles Gounod to a French libretto by Michel Carré, based on Frédéric Mistral's 1859 Occitan poem Mirèio. It was first performed at the Théâtre Lyrique in Paris on 19 March 1864 in a three-act version with spoken dialogue.

Synopsis

In Provence, the basket-weaver's daughter Mireille loves the poor basket-maker Vincent, but her father, Ramon, favours the wealthy bull-herder Ourrias. After Ourrias is drowned crossing the Rhône, Mireille makes a pilgrimage across the scorching Crau plain to the shrine of the Saintes-Maries to pray for the wounded Vincent; she dies of sunstroke in Vincent's arms.

Music

The opera is noted for its Provençal colour, the Farandole-like Act 2 chorus, Mireille's waltz song "O légère hirondelle", and the pastoral sheep-shearing scene.
