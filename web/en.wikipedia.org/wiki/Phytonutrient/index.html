Phytonutrient

Colourful plant foods are rich sources of phytonutrients such as carotenoids and flavonoids.

Phytonutrients, also called phytochemicals, are chemical compounds produced by plants, generally to help them resist fungi, bacteria and plant virus infections, and to deter consumption by insects and other animals. The name derives from the Greek phyton, meaning "plant". Thousands of phytonutrients have been identified in fruits, vegetables, grains, legumes, nuts, tea and other plant foods.

Classes

Major classes of phytonutrients include:

  • Carotenoids — pigments such as beta-carotene, lycopene and lutein.
  • Flavonoids — a large family including anthocyanins, flavones, isoflavones and catechins.
  • Glucosinolates — sulphur compounds in cruciferous vegetables.
  • Phenolic acids and stilbenes, such as resveratrol.
  • Phytoestrogens, such as the isoflavones of soy.
  • Organosulfur compounds, such as allicin in garlic.

Health research

Unlike vitamins and minerals, phytonutrients are not considered essential nutrients, and no dietary reference intakes have been established for them. Epidemiological studies associate diets rich in fruits and vegetables with lower risks of some chronic diseases, and individual phytonutrients are studied for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, but health authorities note that evidence for specific health claims of isolated phytonutrient supplements remains limited and inconclusive. Whole plant foods also provide fibre and essential nutrients alongside phytonutrients.
