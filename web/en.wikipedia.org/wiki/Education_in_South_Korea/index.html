Education in South Korea

Education in South Korea is provided by public and private schools under the supervision of the Ministry of Education. The system follows a 6-3-3-4 ladder: six years of elementary school, three of middle school, three of high school and four of university. Education is compulsory and free through middle school, and South Korea consistently ranks near the top of international assessments such as PISA.

Competition and private tutoring

Admission to university is dominated by the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT, or Suneung), a national examination held each November for which the country famously adjusts transport and even flight schedules. Intense competition for places at prestigious universities — above all the "SKY" trio of Seoul National University, Korea University and Yonsei University — has fuelled a vast private tutoring industry of hagwons (cram schools), on which households spend record sums despite government attempts to curb costs and hours. South Korea has one of the highest tertiary-education attainment rates in the world among young adults.

History

Mass education expanded rapidly after 1945 and during the country's industrialisation, and education is widely credited as a foundation of South Korea's economic development. Traditional Confucian respect for scholarship reinforces family investment in schooling.

Criticism

The system's rigour is blamed for high levels of student stress and unhappiness, long study hours, and pressure linked to youth mental-health problems. Successive reforms have tried to reduce reliance on the CSAT, diversify admissions and lighten curricula, while falling birth rates are now forcing school and university closures.
