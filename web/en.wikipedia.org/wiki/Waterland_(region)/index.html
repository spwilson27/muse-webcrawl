Waterland (region)

CountryNetherlands
ProvinceNorth Holland
Principal placesBroek in Waterland, Monnickendam, Marken, Volendam, Edam, Ilpendam, Landsmeer
LandscapeLow-lying peat polderland, lakes and wetlands, largely below sea level

Waterland is a region in the Dutch province of North Holland, situated directly north of Amsterdam. It is a low-lying area of peat meadows, ditches, lakes and wetlands, much of it below sea level, bounded by the IJsselmeer (Markermeer) to the east and the Zaanstreek to the west. The region takes its name from its long history of flooding and land reclamation: medieval drainage of the peat bogs caused the land to subside, leaving a landscape that had to be defended by dikes and managed by water boards.

History

The area was reclaimed from the tenth to the thirteenth centuries. Subsidence of the drained peat repeatedly caused lakes to expand during storm floods, most catastrophically in the St. Lucia's flood of 1287, which created the Zuiderzee. Several of the resulting lakes — including the Beemster, Purmer and Schermer — were later pumped dry as polders in the seventeenth century. Towns such as Monnickendam, Edam and Volendam developed as fishing and trading ports on the Zuiderzee, while the island village of Marken remained a fishing community until it was connected to the mainland by a causeway in 1957.

Geography and character

Today Waterland is a protected rural landscape of green polder meadows divided by water, noted for picturesque villages such as Broek in Waterland — famous for its painted wooden houses of wealthy seventeenth-century merchants and shipowners — and for the cheese town of Edam and the fishing port of Volendam. Since 2006 the municipalities of Waterland (comprising Monnickendam, Marken, Broek in Waterland and several smaller villages) and Landsmeer have covered much of the historic region. Its proximity to Amsterdam makes it a popular area for cycling and recreation, while large parts are designated as protected nature and landscape areas, including the Ilperveld and Varkensland peat-meadow reserves.
