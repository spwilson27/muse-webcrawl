Picton (Māori: Waitohi) is a town in the Marlborough Region of New Zealand's South Island. The town is located near the head of Queen Charlotte Sound / Tōtaranui, 25 km north of Blenheim and 65 km west of Wellington. The name honours Sir Thomas Picton, a Welsh general killed at the Battle of Waterloo; the Māori name Waitohi refers to the stream by the original settlement.


Picton's waterfront and marina, the gateway to the Marlborough Sounds.

Transport

Picton is the South Island terminal of the Interislander and StraitNZ (Bluebridge) ferry services across Cook Strait to Wellington, linking State Highway 1 and the Main North Line railway between the two islands; roll-on/roll-off freight and passenger traffic make the port one of New Zealand's busiest. The railway station is a notable heritage building, and the Edwin Fox Maritime Centre preserves the 1853 ship Edwin Fox, among the world's oldest surviving merchant vessels.

History

The area was settled by Te Āti Awa, and Europeans founded the town in 1848 as part of the New Zealand Company's Wairau purchase; it became the capital of the Marlborough Province from 1860 until 1876, when Blenheim took over. The discovery of gold at Havelock in 1864 briefly boosted the port. The arrival of the railway from Christchurch (completed to Picton in 1945) and car ferries from 1962 cemented its transport role.

Tourism

Picton is the gateway to the Marlborough Sounds and the Queen Charlotte Track, a 71 km walking and mountain-biking trail to Ship Cove, where Captain James Cook made landfall five times in the 1770s. Dolphin watching, kayaking, fishing charters and cruises operate from the town's marina and foreshore, and it serves as a base for visiting the Marlborough wine region centred on Blenheim.
