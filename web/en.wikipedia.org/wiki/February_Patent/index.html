The February Patent (German: Februarpatent) was a constitution of the Austrian Empire promulgated by Emperor Franz Joseph I on 26 February 1861. Drafted under the minister-president Anton von Schmerling, it replaced the October Diploma of 1860 and established a more centralised constitutional order with a bicameral imperial parliament, the Reichsrat, for the whole empire.

Provisions

The Patent created a Reichsrat of two houses: an upper House of Lords (Herrenhaus) of princes, nobles, clergy and appointees, and a lower House of Deputies (Abgeordnetenhaus) elected indirectly by the provincial diets (Landtage) from restricted curiae. Imperial competence covered foreign affairs, war, finance and commerce; diets retained limited local matters. The Patent framed the empire as a unitary state, reversing the October Diploma's more federal concessions.

Reception and failure

The centralism of the Patent alienated non-German nationalities. The Hungarians, led by Ferenc Deák, refused to send deputies to the Reichsrat and pursued passive resistance, demanding restoration of Hungary's 1848 constitution; Czech and Croat deputies also boycotted. Schmerling's system — mocked as a "theatre" constitution — collapsed after Austria's diplomatic failures, and Schmerling resigned in 1865. The Patent's constitutional experiment gave way to the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867; its Reichsrat provisions, revised, lived on in Cisleithania's December Constitution.
