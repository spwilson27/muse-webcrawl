Saint Denis of Paris

Saint Denis of Paris (French: Saint Denis; also Denis, Dionysius) was a third-century Christian martyr and saint. According to tradition he was the first bishop of Paris and, with his companions Rusticus and Eleutherius, was martyred during a persecution of Christians, most likely under the emperor Decius around 250 AD. He is the patron saint of Paris and of France.

Life and martyrdom

Gregory of Tours recounts that Denis was one of seven apostles of Gaul sent by St Peter to Christianise the country. As bishop of Paris he converted many inhabitants until, in the persecution of Decius, he was arrested with the priest Rusticus and the deacon Eleutherius and beheaded on the hill of Montmartre — whose name is explained in later legend as the "Mount of Martyrs".

The cephalophore legend

Famous medieval legend, elaborated in the ninth century, holds that after his beheading Denis picked up his own head and walked several miles north, preaching a sermon of repentance all the way, before finally collapsing at the place where the Basilica of Saint-Denis would later stand. Denis is thus the most famous of the cephalophores, saints depicted carrying their heads. The abbey built over his tomb became the necropolis of the kings of France and a centre of French royal devotion; "Montjoie Saint Denis!" was the battle cry of French armies.
