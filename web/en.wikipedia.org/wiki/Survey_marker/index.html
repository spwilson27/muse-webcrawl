Survey marker

A survey marker, also called a survey mark, survey monument or geodetic mark, is an object placed to mark key survey points on the Earth's surface. Markers include brass or bronze disks (benchmarks), iron rods, concrete monuments, drill holes in rock, and marks on structures, and they are used in geodesy, boundary demarcation and construction.

Types

Benchmarks record a precisely known elevation above a datum. Triangulation stations (trig points) mark points of known horizontal position, often on hilltops. Boundary markers delimit property or political borders, and reference marks and azimuth marks help surveyors re-locate a principal station.

Agencies and records

In the United States, the National Geodetic Survey maintains a database of marks originally set by the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey and other agencies; datasheets give coordinates, elevations and recovery notes. Deliberately disturbing or destroying survey markers is an offence in many jurisdictions because re-establishing them is costly.

Mark recovery

Surveyors and hobbyists "recover" marks by visiting them, verifying their condition and reporting updates. Activities such as benchmark hunting (a precursor and offshoot of geocaching) involve locating marks listed in official databases.
