Muon

The muon (symbol μ−) is an elementary particle in the Standard Model of particle physics. It is a lepton, similar to the electron but with a much greater mass — about 105.7 MeV/c2, roughly 207 times that of the electron.

Muons were discovered in cosmic rays in 1936 by Carl D. Anderson and Seth Neddermeyer. They have a mean lifetime of 2.2 microseconds, decaying mainly into an electron, an electron antineutrino and a muon neutrino.

Properties

Like the electron, the muon has electric charge −1 e and spin 1/2. Because muons are produced in the upper atmosphere by cosmic-ray interactions and travel near the speed of light, time dilation allows many to reach the Earth's surface, where they form a major component of cosmic radiation at sea level.

Applications

Muons are used in muon tomography to image dense structures, in studies of materials, and in experiments measuring the muon's anomalous magnetic moment (g−2), which test the Standard Model.
