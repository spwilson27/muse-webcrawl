The Soviet–Japanese border conflicts, sometimes known as the Soviet–Japanese border war, were a series of undeclared clashes between 1932 and 1939 between the Soviet Union and Mongolia on one side and the Empire of Japan and its puppet state of Manchukuo on the other, along the disputed borders of Manchuria.

Course of the conflicts

After Japan's seizure of Manchuria in 1931–32, incidents multiplied along the ill-defined borders. In 1938 the fighting escalated at Lake Khasan (the Changkufeng incident), where Soviet forces repelled a Japanese incursion. The largest clash came in 1939 on the Khalkhin Gol river near Nomonhan, where a Soviet-Mongolian counteroffensive under Georgy Zhukov in August 1939 encircled and badly defeated the Japanese Sixth Army, with tens of thousands of casualties on both sides.

Consequences

The defeat at Khalkhin Gol pushed Japanese strategy away from a northern war against the USSR and toward expansion into Southeast Asia and the Pacific. The two countries signed the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact in April 1941, which held until the Soviet invasion of Manchuria in August 1945.
