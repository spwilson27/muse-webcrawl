German occupation of the Channel Islands

The German occupation of the Channel Islands lasted from 30 June 1940 to 9 May 1945. The islands — Jersey, Guernsey, Alderney, Sark and Herm — were the only part of the British Isles occupied by Nazi Germany during the Second World War.

Evacuation and occupation

Before the invasion, around 25,000 islanders, including most schoolchildren, were evacuated to Britain; Alderney was emptied almost entirely. The British government demilitarised the islands, and German forces landed unopposed after bombing raids on St Helier and St Peter Port killed dozens of civilians. The islands were administered by a German civil administration working through the local island governments, which remained in place.

Life under occupation

Islanders endured rationing, curfews, censorship and, from 1942, the deportation of over 2,000 UK-born residents to internment camps in Germany. The Organisation Todt built massive fortifications as part of the Atlantic Wall using forced labour; on Alderney, concentration camps including Lager Sylt held prisoners in brutal conditions, and hundreds died. By the winter of 1944–45, after the Normandy landings cut supply lines, civilians and soldiers alike faced near-starvation, relieved partly by Red Cross parcels carried by the SS Vega.

Liberation

The islands were liberated on 9 May 1945, the day after VE Day, without a battle. Liberation Day remains a public holiday in Jersey and Guernsey.
