T-duality

T-duality is an equivalence of two physical theories in string theory. In the simplest example, a string propagating on a circle of radius R is physically equivalent to a string propagating on a circle of radius α′/R, where α′ is the string tension parameter. The duality exchanges momentum modes of the string with its winding modes — the number of times the string wraps around the circle.

Overview

T-duality has the striking consequence that physics is insensitive to whether a compact dimension is very large or very small: there is a minimum observable length scale in string theory, of the order of the Planck length. Unlike point-particle theories, a string cannot probe distances below this scale because it cannot distinguish a tiny circle from a huge one.

T-duality relates type IIA string theory to type IIB string theory, and the two heterotic string theories to one another. It is one of a web of dualities, together with S-duality, that led to the conjecture of M-theory as a unifying framework. Applied to spaces with fluxes and branes, T-duality gives rise to mirror symmetry and to non-geometric backgrounds.

Mathematical formulation

For toroidal compactifications the duality group is the orthogonal group O(d, d; ℤ). The Buscher rules describe how the metric and B-field of a background transform under T-duality along an isometry direction.
