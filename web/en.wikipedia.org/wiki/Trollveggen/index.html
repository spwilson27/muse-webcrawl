The Troll Wall (Norwegian: Trollveggen) is part of the Trolltindene mountain range in the Romsdalen valley in Rauma Municipality, Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. Its near-vertical rock face, rising about 1,100 metres (3,600 ft) from the valley floor to the summit ridge, is the tallest vertical rock face in Europe, and its overhanging upper section leans out roughly 50 metres from vertical.

Climbing history

The wall was long considered unclimbable because of its loose, fractured rock and severe weather. A Norwegian team made the first ascent in 1958 by a route on the left side of the face. In 1965 a Norwegian party and a British party — the latter including John Amatt and Tony Howard — raced each other up different lines, both succeeding within days of each other; the rivalry drew wide attention in Norway. New routes added in the following decades progressively tackled the steepest central sections of the wall, and the Troll Wall became one of the classic big-wall test pieces of Europe, though rockfall, including a major fall in the 1990s that altered parts of the face, continues to change its routes.

BASE jumping

From the late 1970s the wall became a celebrated site for BASE jumping. The Finnish jumper Jorma Öster made early exits from the summit in 1980, and in 1984 Carl Boenish, widely regarded as the father of modern BASE jumping, died after a jump from the wall. Following this and other accidents, Norway prohibited BASE jumping from Trollveggen in 1986; the ban remains in force, and several jumpers have died attempting illegal jumps since.

Geography

The Trolltindene range forms the western wall of Romsdalen, opposite the famous Trollstigen mountain road, which climbs the neighbouring valley side and gives tourists a view of the wall. Below the face runs the Rauma river and the Rauma railway line. The highest point on the rim, Store Trolltinden, reaches 1,788 metres above sea level.
