Maheshwar is a town in Khargone district of Madhya Pradesh state, central India. It is located on the north bank of the Narmada River, 91 kilometres south-west of Indore. An ancient religious centre identified by tradition with the Mahishmati of the epics, it became famous in the 18th century as the capital of the Holkar queen Ahilyabai.

History

Maheshwar is associated in Hindu tradition with Shiva (Mahesh, "great lord") and with the thousand-armed king Kartavirya Arjuna, whose capital Mahishmati is often placed here. In 1767 Ahilyabai Holkar made Maheshwar her capital and transformed it over three decades: she built the Rajwada palace, the Ahilya Fort on the riverbank, numerous temples and the broad, stepped ghats that remain the town's core, while supporting temples and charities across India.

Textiles

Ahilyabai revived the town's handloom weaving; Maheshwari saris and fabrics — silk-cotton cloth with reversible borders and traditional motifs inspired by the fort and river — remain a celebrated craft, supported in the modern era by the Rehwa Society, founded in 1978 to sustain weavers.

Tourism and religion

The Sahastradhara waterfalls, where the Narmada splits into many streams, the temple of Rajrajeshwara, and boat rides along the ghats draw pilgrims and visitors. The town has also served as a filming location and is linked by road to Omkareshwar, another major Narmada pilgrimage town.
