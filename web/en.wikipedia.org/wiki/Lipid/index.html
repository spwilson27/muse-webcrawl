Lipid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lipids are a broad group of naturally occurring molecules that are insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents. They include fats and oils (triglycerides), phospholipids, waxes and steroids such as Cholesterol. Lipids store energy, form the membranes of cells, insulate and protect organs, carry fat-soluble vitamins, and serve as signalling molecules and hormones.

Main classes

Triglycerides — a glycerol backbone with three fatty acids — are the main storage form in fat tissue and the main fat in food; fatty acids may be saturated, monounsaturated or polyunsaturated (including the essential omega-3 and omega-6 families), and industrial partial hydrogenation created trans fats, now widely restricted. Phospholipids, with a water-loving head and two fatty tails, build the bilayer of every cell membrane. Steroids share a four-ring structure; cholesterol is their precursor for steroid hormones, bile acids and vitamin D.

Health

Blood lipids — cholesterol carried in LDL and HDL particles, and triglycerides — are central to cardiovascular risk: high LDL cholesterol causes atherosclerosis, and lipid-lowering drugs (statins and others) are mainstays of prevention. Dietary guidance generally favours replacing saturated and trans fats with unsaturated fats.
