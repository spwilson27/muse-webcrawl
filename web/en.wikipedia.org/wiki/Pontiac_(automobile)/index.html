Pontiac (automobile)

Founded: 1926

Defunct: 2010

Parent: General Motors

Pontiac was an American automobile brand owned and manufactured by General Motors (GM). Introduced in 1926 as a companion marque to GM's Oakland Motor Car Company, Pontiac quickly outsold its parent, which was discontinued in 1931. The brand was named after the Ottawa chief Pontiac and long used Native American imagery in its badging.

Performance era

Positioned between Chevrolet and Oldsmobile in GM's hierarchy, Pontiac reinvented itself in the late 1950s and 1960s under executives Semon "Bunkie" Knudsen and John DeLorean as GM's performance division. The 1964 Pontiac GTO, an option package on the Tempest created by DeLorean, is widely credited with starting the muscle car era. Iconic models followed: the Firebird (1967), the Trans Am, the Grand Prix and the Bonneville.

Later years and discontinuation

From the 1980s Pontiac's identity blurred amid GM's badge engineering, though models such as the Fiero mid-engine sports car and the imported Australian-built GTO revival and G8 sedan kept a performance flavour. As part of its government-backed restructuring during the 2009 bankruptcy, GM announced Pontiac's elimination; the last Pontiacs were built in late 2009 and the franchise agreements ended on 31 October 2010.
