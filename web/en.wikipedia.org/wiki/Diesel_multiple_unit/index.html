Diesel multiple unit

A diesel multiple unit or DMU is a multiple-unit train powered by on-board diesel engines. A DMU requires no separate locomotive, as the engines are incorporated into one or more of the carriages. Diesel-powered single-unit railcars are also generally classed as DMUs. A diesel-powered train composed of carriages operated as a single unit, together with a control car, may be called a push-pull train; coaches powered by diesel engines for railways that are not multiple units are called railcars.

Design

The diesel engine may be located above the frame in an engine compartment, or under the floor, and driving can be by mechanical, hydraulic or diesel-electric transmission. Multiple units allow trains to be lengthened or shortened by coupling units together, and a driver can control several units from the leading cab. DMUs are widely used on non-electrified branch and secondary routes where traffic does not justify electrification.

Use

DMUs operate regional and commuter services in many countries, including the extensive British Pacer and Sprinter families, German railcars, Japanese KiHa series, and North American Budd Rail Diesel Cars. Modern designs emphasise fuel efficiency, low floors for accessibility, and reduced emissions; hybrid variants combine diesel engines with batteries.
