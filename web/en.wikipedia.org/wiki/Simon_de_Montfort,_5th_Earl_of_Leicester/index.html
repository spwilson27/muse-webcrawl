Simon IV (or V) de Montfort (c. 1175 – 25 June 1218), known as Simon de Montfort the Elder, was a French nobleman and the 5th Earl of Leicester in England. He is best known as the military leader of the Albigensian Crusade against the Cathars of southern France.

Early life and the Fourth Crusade

Montfort inherited the lordship of Montfort-l'Amaury and, through his mother Amicia de Beaumont, a claim to the earldom of Leicester, which he was granted in 1206–07. He took part in the Fourth Crusade but refused to participate in the attack on the Christian city of Zara, leaving the expedition and travelling to the Holy Land separately — an episode that enhanced his reputation for piety.

Albigensian Crusade

In 1209 Montfort was chosen to lead the crusade against the Cathar heresy in Languedoc. A gifted and ruthless commander, he won the decisive Battle of Muret in 1213, where King Peter II of Aragon was killed, and was granted the conquered county of Toulouse. His harsh treatment of conquered populations and the Statutes of Pamiers (1212) made him widely hated in the south. He was killed by a stone from a catapult — reportedly operated by women of the city — while besieging Toulouse on 25 June 1218.

Family

His younger son, also Simon de Montfort (the 6th Earl), later led the baronial opposition to Henry III of England and is remembered for summoning the 1265 parliament.
