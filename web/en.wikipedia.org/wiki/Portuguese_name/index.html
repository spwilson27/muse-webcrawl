Portuguese name

A Portuguese name is typically composed of one or two given names (nomes próprios) followed by a sequence of family names (apelidos or sobrenomes), which may combine the surnames of both parents. By law in Portugal, a person's full name may contain at most six simple names: usually up to two given names and up to four surnames. This structure contrasts with the Spanish naming system, in which two surnames (paternal first, then maternal) are standard and rarely exceeded.

Order of surnames

Traditionally, Portuguese children received the mother's surname first and the father's surname last, with the final surname being the one used most prominently in everyday reference and alphabetisation. Since the late 20th century, parents have had greater freedom to choose the order or to include surnames from grandparents. Married people may add a spouse's surname to their own, though this practice has become less common.

Common names

Very common given names include Maria, João, Manuel, António, Ana, Francisco, José, and Pedro. Extremely frequent surnames include Silva, Santos, Ferreira, Pereira, Oliveira, Costa, Rodrigues, Martins, Jesus, Sousa, Fernandes, and Gonçalves. Compound names such as Maria João or João Pedro are widespread, and religious naming traditions historically favoured names of saints.

Naming in Brazil

Brazil follows the Portuguese tradition, with long multi-surname names common, though in practice people are often known by a single given name or nickname, especially footballers. Brazilian names may also incorporate the particles da, de, do, das, dos, and e ("and") between surnames, which are not themselves considered part of the surname proper.
