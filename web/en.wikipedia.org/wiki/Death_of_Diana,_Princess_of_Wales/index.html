In the early hours of 31 August 1997, Diana, Princess of Wales, died from injuries sustained in a car crash in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris. Her partner Dodi Fayed and the driver, Henri Paul, were found dead at the scene; bodyguard Trevor Rees-Jones survived. The crash followed pursuit by paparazzi photographers after the party left the Ritz Hotel.

Events

French investigators concluded in 1999 that Paul, driving at high speed and with alcohol in his system, lost control of the Mercedes-Benz; the chasing paparazzi were not held responsible. The British Metropolitan Police investigation, Operation Paget (2006), reached similar conclusions, and the 2007–2008 London inquest returned a verdict of unlawful killing through the grossly negligent driving of Paul and of the following vehicles. Diana was 36 years old.

Reaction and funeral

The death triggered an extraordinary outpouring of public grief in the United Kingdom and worldwide, with mountains of flowers outside Kensington Palace. Her funeral at Westminster Abbey on 6 September 1997 was watched by an estimated 2.5 billion people; Elton John performed a rewritten "Candle in the Wind", which became the best-selling single of all time. The royal family's initial reserve prompted public criticism, answered by the Queen's televised tribute.

Legacy

Diana's death reshaped the monarchy's public relations, intensified debate over media intrusion (contributing to stronger press regulation), and spawned enduring — officially rejected — conspiracy theories promoted in part by Dodi's father Mohamed Al-Fayed.
