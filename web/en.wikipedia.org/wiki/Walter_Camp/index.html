Walter Camp

Walter Chauncey Camp (April 7, 1859 – March 14, 1925) was an American college football player and coach, sportswriter, and athletic administrator. Known as the "Father of American Football", he is credited with many of the rule changes that transformed rugby-style football into the distinct American game.

Rule innovations

Camp played at Yale and, after graduating, spent decades on the intercollegiate rules committees. Innovations associated with him include the line of scrimmage, the snap from centre, the system of downs and yardage (requiring a team to advance the ball a set distance in a fixed number of plays), the eleven-man team, the quarterback position, and the standardised offensive formation. He also introduced numerical scoring values resembling the modern system.

Coaching and writing

Camp coached at Yale (1888–1892) and Stanford (1892–1895), compiling an outstanding win record. He selected an annual All-American team, a tradition begun in 1889 that shaped college football honours. Outside sport he worked as an executive for the New Haven Clock Company. The Walter Camp Football Foundation and the Walter Camp Award, given to the top college player of the year, commemorate him.
