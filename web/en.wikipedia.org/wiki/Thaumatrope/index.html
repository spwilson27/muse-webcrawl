Thaumatrope

A thaumatrope is an optical toy that was popular in the 19th century. A disk with a picture on each side is attached to two pieces of string. When the strings are twirled quickly, spinning the disk, the two pictures appear to blend into one due to the persistence of vision — the same perceptual effect that underlies animation and cinema.

Examples

The classic design shows a bird on one side and an empty cage on the other; when spun, the bird appears inside the cage. Other pairs include a horse and rider, a flower and vase, or a man bowing to a woman.

History

Thaumatrope-like devices were described by John Ayrton Paris, who is credited with popularising the toy in 1824; commercial sets were sold in London from 1825. Related philosophical toys that exploit persistence of vision include the phenakistiscope and zoetrope.
