LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design) is a green building certification program developed by the U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC). It is the most widely used green building rating system in the world, providing a framework for healthy, efficient, and cost-saving green buildings across categories including energy use, water, materials, indoor environmental quality, and site selection.

Rating system

Projects earn points across credit categories such as Sustainable Sites, Water Efficiency, Energy and Atmosphere, Materials and Resources, and Indoor Environmental Quality. Depending on the total points earned, a project is certified at one of four levels: Certified (40–49 points), Silver (50–59), Gold (60–79), and Platinum (80 or more). Separate rating systems cover building design and construction, interior design, operations and maintenance, neighbourhood development, and homes. Certification is administered by the Green Business Certification Inc. (GBCI).

History

The USGBC launched the LEED pilot in 1998, followed by LEED 2.0 in 2000, LEED 2009 (v3), and LEED v4 in 2013, with a further major revision, LEED v5, released in 2025 with stronger emphasis on decarbonisation, equity, and resilience. LEED-credentialed professionals include the LEED Green Associate and LEED AP (Accredited Professional).

Criticism

Critics have argued that LEED certification is based on design intentions rather than measured performance, that some credits can be earned with minimal environmental benefit, and that certified buildings do not always outperform conventional ones in measured energy use. Supporters respond that the system has transformed the building market and driven demand for green products.
