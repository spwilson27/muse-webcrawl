José Alfredo Jiménez Sandoval (19 January 1926 – 23 November 1973) was a Mexican singer-songwriter of rancheras, one of the most prolific and beloved composers in the history of Mexican music. He wrote more than a thousand songs and is often ranked alongside Agustín Lara and other giants of the Mexican songbook.

Life and career

Jiménez was born in Dolores Hidalgo, Guanajuato, the town where Mexico's independence movement began. His father died when he was young, and the family moved to Mexico City, where he worked various jobs, including as a waiter and a football goalkeeper, while writing songs. He had little formal musical training; famously, he could not read music or play an instrument well, instead whistling melodies to arrangers such as Rubén Fuentes, who set many of his songs for mariachi.

His breakthrough came around 1950 with the Trio Los Rebeldes and radio broadcasts on XEW. Songs such as "El Rey", "El Jinete", "Si Nos Dejan", "Ella", "La Media Vuelta", "Paloma Querida" and "Camino de Guanajuato" became standards, recorded by artists including Pedro Infante, Jorge Negrete, Javier Solís, Vicente Fernández and Luis Miguel.

Style and legacy

Jiménez's rancheras distilled heartbreak, pride, drinking and fate into short, quotable lines; "El Rey" opens with the declaration that though he has no throne or queen, he remains a king. He died in Mexico City in 1973 at the age of 47, of complications related to alcoholism, and is buried in Dolores Hidalgo, where his tomb is a pilgrimage site for fans. His songs remain staples of mariachi performance and Mexican popular culture worldwide.
