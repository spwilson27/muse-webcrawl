Shirak International Airport (IATA: LWN, ICAO: UDSG) is an international airport serving Gyumri, the second-largest city of Armenia, and the surrounding Shirak Province. It is located about 5 kilometres from the centre of Gyumri, at an elevation of roughly 1,524 metres above sea level, making it one of the highest airports in the country.

History

The airport was commissioned in 1961 during the Soviet period. Its current terminal building dates to 1982, designed in a style intended to echo the dark tuff stone architecture characteristic of Gyumri. In 2007 the airport was granted to the concessionaire Armenia International Airports CJSC, the same operator that runs Zvartnots International Airport in Yerevan, under a long-term concession agreement. The runway was subsequently resurfaced and modern navigation equipment installed, and in 2021 the airport received a renovated arrivals hall.

Facilities

Shirak has a single asphalt runway, 3,220 metres long, capable of handling aircraft up to the size of the Boeing 747. Its high elevation and cold winters mean operations can be affected by fog and snow, though the airport also serves as an alternate for Yerevan.

Airlines and destinations

Services have historically focused on routes to Russia, reflecting the large Armenian diaspora and the presence of the Russian 102nd Military Base in Gyumri. Low-cost and charter services have periodically linked Gyumri with Moscow and other Russian cities.
