Rysy

Elevation2,499 m (north-western peak, on the Polish border)
LocationPoland–Slovakia border, High Tatras
RangeCarpathians, Tatra Mountains
First ascent1840 (Eduard Blasy, guided by Ján Ruman-Driečny)

Rysy is a mountain in the crest of the High Tatras, lying on the border between Poland and Slovakia. Its highest point on Polish territory, the north-western summit at 2,499 metres, is the highest point of Poland, making Rysy one of the most popular peaks in the country. The main (middle) summit, at about 2,503 metres, lies in Slovakia.

Geography

The massif has three peaks: the middle (main) peak at 2,503 m, the north-western peak at 2,499 m — the highest point in Poland — and the lower south-eastern peak at 2,473 m. Below the Polish side lies the lake Czarny Staw pod Rysami and, further down, Morskie Oko, one of the most visited lakes in the Tatras. The Slovak side is protected within Tatra National Park, as is the Polish side.

History and climbing

The first recorded ascent was made in 1840. In the late 19th century a trail with chains was established on the Polish side, and Rysy became a symbol of Polish mountaineering. Vladimir Lenin is said to have climbed the mountain in 1913 while living in the region, an event commemorated in the socialist era. Today the Polish route, though equipped with chains, is a demanding and often crowded ascent; the Slovak route from Štrbské Pleso is somewhat gentler. A mountain hut, Chata pod Rysmi (2,250 m), on the Slovak side is among the highest in the Tatras and is supplied by porters.
