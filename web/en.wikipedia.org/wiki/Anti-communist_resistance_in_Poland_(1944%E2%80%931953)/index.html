Anti-communist resistance in Poland (1944–1953)

Period1944–1953 (isolated cases later)
OpponentsCommunist authorities, Soviet NKVD

The anti-communist resistance in Poland was an armed struggle by Polish underground organisations against the Soviet-backed communist authorities between 1944 and the early 1950s. Its fighters are known in Poland as the "cursed soldiers" (żołnierze wyklęci).

Background

As the Red Army advanced into Poland in 1944, the Soviet NKVD arrested members of the Home Army, the main wartime resistance loyal to the Polish government-in-exile. Rather than accept communist rule imposed from Moscow, many units refused to disband and continued fighting.

Organisations and decline

Groups included Freedom and Independence (WiN), the National Armed Forces (NSZ) and units of the former Home Army. At its height in 1945 the resistance numbered tens of thousands. Amnesties in 1945 and 1947, infiltration and brutal repression by the security services steadily destroyed the movement; the last known partisan, Józef Franczak, was killed in 1963.
