Very light jet

The Embraer Phenom 100 is among the most successful very light jets.

A very light jet (VLJ), also known as a personal jet or microjet in its earliest conception, is a category of small business jet seating four to eight people. VLJs are considered the lightest business jets and are approved for single-pilot operation. Maximum takeoff weights are generally under 10,000 lb (4,540 kg).

History

The category emerged in the early 2000s, driven by advances in small turbofan engines such as the Williams FJ33 and Pratt & Whitney Canada PW600 series, and by NASA's Small Aircraft Transportation System programme. Eclipse Aviation's Eclipse 500, certified in 2006, pioneered the class and an ambitious "air taxi" business model before the company entered bankruptcy in 2008. The concept helped popularise low-cost, owner-flown jet travel.

Examples

Production VLJs include the Embraer Phenom 100, the Honda HA-420 HondaJet, the Cessna Citation Mustang and Citation M2, the Eclipse 550, and the single-engine Cirrus Vision SF50, which is often described as a "personal jet". Typical range is around 1,000–1,300 nautical miles with cruising speeds near 380 knots.

Market

Although early forecasts predicted sales of thousands of VLJs per year, actual deliveries were far lower, constrained by the 2008 financial crisis, insurance and training requirements, and competition from turboprops and larger light jets. The segment nevertheless endures, particularly with owner-pilots stepping up from high-performance piston and turboprop aircraft.
