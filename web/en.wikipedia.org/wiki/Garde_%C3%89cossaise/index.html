Garde Écossaise

The Garde Écossaise (Scottish Guard) was an elite Scottish military unit founded in 1418 by Charles VII of France to serve as the personal bodyguard of the French monarchy. Recruited from Scotland under the Auld Alliance between France and Scotland against England, it was among the oldest and most prestigious units of the French royal household guard, surviving in ceremonial form until its disbandment in 1830.

History

Scottish soldiers had served French kings since the Hundred Years' War; the guard was formalized after the arrival of a large Scottish expeditionary force under the Earl of Buchan, victors at the Battle of Baugé in 1421. The Garde Écossaise formed the senior company of the Gardes du Corps, mounted close guard around the king, and held ceremonial privileges such as carrying the royal coffin. Its ranks were opened to Frenchmen in later centuries, but Scottish officers remained prominent for generations.

Disbandment and legacy

The unit was dissolved during the July Revolution of 1830 along with the rest of the royal bodyguard. The tradition is remembered in the close historical ties of the Auld Alliance and in literary works such as Sir Walter Scott's Quentin Durward, whose hero is an archer of the Scottish Guard at the court of Louis XI.
