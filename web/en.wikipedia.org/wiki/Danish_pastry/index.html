Danish pastry
Danish: wienerbrød (“Viennese bread”)
Type: laminated yeast pastry
Fillings: remonce, custard, fruit, nuts

A Danish pastry — in Denmark called wienerbrød, “Viennese bread” — is a multilayered, laminated sweet pastry in the viennoiserie tradition, made from a yeast-leavened dough layered with butter like puff pastry but enriched with egg, milk, and sugar.

History

The pastry came to Denmark in the mid-19th century when striking Danish bakers were replaced by Austrian bakers who brought Viennese laminated-dough techniques, which Danish bakers then adapted and sweetened.

Varieties

Common forms include the spandauer (a folded “snail” or square with custard or jam in the centre), the cinnamon kringle, and tebirkes topped with poppy seeds. Fillings often include remonce, a butter-sugar-almond paste.

Around the world

Danish emigrants and bakers such as Lauritz Klitteng popularised “danishes” in the United States in the early 20th century, where the term covers a wide range of filled breakfast pastries.
