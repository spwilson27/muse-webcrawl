Prince Shōtoku (574 – 8 April 622), also known as Prince Umayado, was a semi-legendary regent and politician of the Asuka period in Japan who served under Empress Suiko. He is credited with sweeping reforms that laid the foundations of the Japanese imperial state and with championing Buddhism at court.

Reforms

Tradition attributes to Shōtoku the Twelve Level Cap and Rank System (603), which appointed officials by merit rather than heredity, and the Seventeen-article constitution (604), a moral code for officials drawing on Buddhist and Confucian principles. He sent embassies to Sui China, beginning with Ono no Imoko in 607, to import Chinese institutions, learning, and religion.

Buddhism and legacy

Shōtoku founded temples including Shitennō-ji in Osaka and Hōryū-ji in Nara, among the oldest wooden buildings in the world, and wrote commentaries on Buddhist sutras. Revered after death as a sage and even an incarnation of a bodhisattva, his portrait appeared on Japanese banknotes for much of the 20th century. Modern historians debate how much of the attributed record is later legend.
