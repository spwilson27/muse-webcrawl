A fifth-generation fighter is a jet fighter classification used around the world that encompasses the most advanced aircraft designs to emerge in the early 21st century. Defining characteristics include all-aspect stealth incorporating internal weapons carriage, advanced integrated avionics with sensor fusion, network-centric data sharing, and in some designs the ability to supercruise — sustained supersonic flight without afterburners.

Characteristics

  • Stealth: airframe shaping, radar-absorbent materials and internal stores reduce radar cross-section in all aspects.
  • Sensor fusion: radar, infrared search and track, electro-optical and electronic-warfare sensors are fused into a single tactical picture for the pilot.
  • Networking: secure data links share sensor data between aircraft and other forces.
  • Performance: high manoeuvrability, in some types thrust vectoring and supercruise.

Aircraft

The Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor entered US service in 2005 as the first fifth-generation fighter. It was followed by the multinational F-35 Lightning II family, China's Chengdu J-20 Mighty Dragon, and Russia's Sukhoi Su-57. Several other states have development programmes under way, and upgraded "4.5-generation" aircraft incorporate some of the same avionics and signature-reduction features.
