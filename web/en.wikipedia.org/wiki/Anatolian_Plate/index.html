The Anatolian Plate is a continental tectonic plate comprising most of the Anatolian peninsula (and hence most of Turkey). It is bounded to the north by the Eurasian Plate along the North Anatolian Fault, to the east by the Arabian Plate along the East Anatolian Fault, to the south by the African Plate, and to the southwest by the Aegean Sea Plate (often treated as part of the same deforming region).

Tectonics

The ongoing collision of the Arabian Plate with Eurasia squeezes the Anatolian block westward — often compared to a watermelon seed squeezed between fingers. The motion is accommodated mainly by two great strike-slip faults: the right-lateral North Anatolian Fault, a 1,200 km structure running across northern Turkey south of the Black Sea, and the left-lateral East Anatolian Fault in the southeast. The western part of the plate is also affected by north–south extension that has formed the graben systems of western Anatolia and the Aegean.

Earthquakes

The faults bounding the plate are among the most seismically active in the world. Major events include the 1939 Erzincan earthquake, the sequence of westward-migrating ruptures along the North Anatolian Fault through the twentieth century, the 1999 İzmit earthquake, and the devastating February 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes on the East Anatolian Fault system, which killed tens of thousands in Turkey and Syria.
