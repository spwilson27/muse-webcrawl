Teiji Takagi (高木 貞治, 21 April 1875 – 28 February 1960) was a Japanese mathematician, best known for developing class field theory through his work on the Takagi existence theorem. He is regarded as the founder of modern Japanese mathematics.

Life and work

Takagi studied at the Imperial University of Tokyo and then in Germany, where he worked with David Hilbert at Göttingen. Returning to Tokyo, he became a professor and spent years working largely in isolation on algebraic number theory. His 1920 paper on class field theory, presented at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Strasbourg, established the existence theorem describing abelian extensions of number fields — though its significance was fully recognised only after Emil Artin's reciprocity law completed the picture.

Other contributions

Takagi also described the Blancmange curve (Takagi function), a continuous but nowhere-differentiable function, in 1901. During the Second World War he worked on Japanese cipher systems. His textbooks, including works on elementary number theory and the history of mathematics in Japan, influenced generations of Japanese mathematicians; his students included Shokichi Iyanaga.
