Jean Bugatti (15 January 1909 – 11 August 1939) was a French automotive designer and test driver, the eldest son of Ettore Bugatti, founder of the Bugatti automobile company. He is credited with designing many of the marque's most celebrated bodies, including the Type 57 Atlantic.

Career

Jean joined the family firm at Molsheim in the late 1920s. His designs — the Type 55 roadster, the Type 57 range, and the streamlined Atlantic coupé with its distinctive dorsal seam — defined Bugatti's interwar aesthetic. He also managed much of the company's day-to-day engineering as his father's interests drifted toward railcars and aircraft.

Death

On 11 August 1939, while road-testing the Type 57C tank-bodied racer that had won Le Mans weeks earlier, Jean swerved to avoid a cyclist near Duppigheim and was killed when the car left the road. He was 30. His death marked the beginning of the decline of the original Bugatti company.
