An iceboat (or ice yacht) is a recreational or racing sailboat supported on metal runners (skates) designed to glide over the surface of frozen lakes and rivers. Because friction on ice is extremely low, iceboats can sail several times faster than the wind that propels them, with large "stern-steerer" yachts historically exceeding 100 km/h and modern craft going faster still.

Design

A typical iceboat has a light hull with a cockpit, a cross-plank mounted near the stern carrying two runners, and a third, steerable runner at the bow (or, in traditional stern-steerers, at the stern). The sailor hikes out to windward to keep the boat balanced as apparent wind builds with speed.

History and classes

Ice yachting developed on the frozen Hudson River and in the Netherlands in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when large stern-steerers were among the fastest vehicles on Earth. The most popular modern class is the International DN, a small one-design boat designed in 1936–37 (its name honouring a Detroit News competition) and now raced widely in North America and Europe, with world championships held annually when ice conditions allow.
