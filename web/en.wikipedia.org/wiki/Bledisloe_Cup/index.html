The Bledisloe Cup is an annual rugby union competition between the national teams of Australia and New Zealand. It is one of the most famous trophies in world rugby, contested since 1931, and is named after Lord Bledisloe, the Governor-General of New Zealand, who donated the large silver cup.

History

The cup was first played for in 1931, when New Zealand defeated Australia at Eden Park, Auckland. Matches were initially sporadic, fitted around tours, but from the 1980s the contest became an annual fixture, and since 1996 it has been incorporated into the Tri Nations, later The Rugby Championship. A series is usually decided over two or three matches, and the holder retains the cup if the series is drawn.

Dominance

New Zealand has dominated the trophy for long stretches. Australia held it from 1998 to 2003, winning five consecutive series, but New Zealand regained it in 2003 and has held it continuously since, Australia's long drought becoming a recurring storyline in trans-Tasman sport. The All Blacks have won the cup far more times overall than the Wallabies.

The trophy

The Bledisloe Cup is reputedly the largest trophy in world rugby, a substantial silver cup made in 1921. It is traditionally presented to the winning captain after the deciding match.
