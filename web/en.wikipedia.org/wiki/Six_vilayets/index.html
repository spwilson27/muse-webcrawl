Six vilayets

RegionEastern Anatolia
EmpireOttoman Empire
ProvincesErzurum, Van, Bitlis, Diyarbekir, Kharput, Sivas

The Six Vilayets were the six Ottoman vilayets (provinces) of western Armenia in eastern Anatolia: Erzurum, Van, Bitlis, Diyarbekir, Kharput (Mamuretulaziz) and Sivas. The term gained currency in European diplomacy in the late 19th century because these provinces contained large Armenian populations whose protection became an international question.

Diplomatic history

Article 61 of the Treaty of Berlin (1878) obliged the Ottoman Empire to carry out reforms in the Armenian-inhabited provinces under European supervision. The Six Vilayets were the focus of reform schemes in 1895 and of the Armenian reform agreement of 1914, which provided for European inspector-generals.

Demographics

Armenians formed a large share, in some districts a majority, of the population alongside Turks, Kurds and others, though Ottoman and Armenian statistics differed sharply. The region was the heartland of historic Armenia, containing centres such as Van, Erzurum and the monastery districts around Lake Van.

Aftermath

During the First World War the Armenian population of the Six Vilayets was largely destroyed or deported in the Armenian genocide. After the war the provinces became part of the Republic of Turkey.
