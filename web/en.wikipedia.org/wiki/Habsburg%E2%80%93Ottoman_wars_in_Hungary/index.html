Habsburg–Ottoman wars in Hungary
Date1530–1718
LocationKingdom of Hungary, Central Europe
ResultHabsburg reconquest of Hungary; Treaties of Karlowitz (1699) and Passarowitz (1718)

The Habsburg–Ottoman wars in Hungary were a series of conflicts between the Habsburg Monarchy and the Ottoman Empire fought over the territory of the Kingdom of Hungary between 1530 and 1718. After the Ottoman victory at the Battle of Mohács in 1526 and the fall of Buda in 1541, Hungary was divided into three parts: Royal Hungary under Habsburg rule in the west and north, Ottoman Hungary in the central plains, and the Principality of Transylvania in the east.

Background

The Ottoman advance into Central Europe after Mohács brought Suleiman the Magnificent to the gates of Vienna in 1529. A fortified Military Frontier of captaincies and border fortresses developed along the contested zone, and warfare consisted largely of sieges, raids and seasonal campaigns.

Major conflicts

The Long Turkish War (1593–1606) ended inconclusively with the Peace of Zsitvatorok. The tide turned after the Ottoman defeat at the second Siege of Vienna in 1683: the Holy League reconquered Buda in 1686, and Prince Eugene of Savoy's victory at Zenta in 1697 led to the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), which confirmed Habsburg control of most of Hungary.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Passarowitz (1718) extended Habsburg rule to the Banat and northern Serbia. The long wars left much of central Hungary depopulated, reshaping its ethnic and religious landscape.
