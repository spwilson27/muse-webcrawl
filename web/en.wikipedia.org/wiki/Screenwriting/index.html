Screenwriting

Screenwriting is the art and craft of writing scripts for mass media such as feature films, television productions and video games. The practitioner is a screenwriter. Screenwriting involves creating characters, narrative structure, dialogue and visual action that can be translated into moving images, and it is generally a collaborative, heavily revised process shaped by producers, directors and studios.

The work

Screenwriters may originate ideas and sell them as spec scripts, pitch projects, write on commission, adapt existing material such as novels, or perform rewrites of scripts by others. Development typically proceeds through outlines, treatments and successive drafts, with notes from executives and collaborators at each stage. In television, writers work in a writers' room under a showrunner and produce episodes to a series format.

Craft and structure

The craft emphasises economy of dialogue, visual storytelling ("show, don't tell"), and clear structure — most famously the three-act structure popularised by Syd Field, and later systems such as the hero's journey drawn from Joseph Campbell and the "Save the Cat" beat sheet. Screenwriting is taught in film schools and through an extensive literature of manuals and courses.

Profession

In the United States, screenwriters are represented by the Writers Guild of America, which negotiates minimum compensation, credit rules and residuals; credit disputes are arbitrated by the Guild. Screenwriting competitions, fellowships and the Academy Nicholl Fellowship are common entry routes for new writers.
