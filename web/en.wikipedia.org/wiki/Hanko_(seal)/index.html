Hanko (seal)

A hanko (判子), also called inkan (印鑑), is a carved stamp used in Japan and other East Asian countries in place of, or alongside, a handwritten signature. Hanko are used on official documents, bank accounts, contracts, and receipts, and remain a routine part of Japanese administrative and business life.

Types

A jitsuin is a personal seal officially registered with a local authority and used for important legal acts; a ginkōin is registered with a bank; and a mitomein is an informal everyday seal used for common paperwork and deliveries. Company seals include the representative's seal and the square company seal.

Materials and carving

Seals are carved in wood, horn, ivory, stone, or synthetic materials, usually with the owner's name in kanji in a seal script style. They are stamped with a red paste called shuniku. Ready-made seals for common surnames are sold cheaply, while registered seals are individually made.

Modern debate

Digitalisation efforts have prompted debate over reducing reliance on hanko for paperwork, though the seals remain deeply embedded in practice and are also appreciated as a traditional craft.
