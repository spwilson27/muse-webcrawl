Te Matatini

Te Matatini is New Zealand's national festival and competition of kapa haka (Māori performing arts), widely regarded as the pinnacle event of Māori culture and sometimes described as the "Olympics of kapa haka". Held biennially over several days, it brings together dozens of qualifying regional groups (rōpū) who perform bracket programmes of song, dance, and haka before large live audiences and national television broadcasts. The festival's name — "the many faces" — was gifted by Professor Wharehuia Milroy.

History

The festival began in 1972 at Rotorua as the New Zealand Polynesian Festival, part of a broader Māori cultural renaissance. It grew steadily in scale and prestige, was renamed Te Matatini in 2004, and now rotates among host regions (rohe), each of which builds significant infrastructure for the event. The 2023 festival in Auckland and the 2025 festival in New Plymouth (Taranaki) drew tens of thousands of spectators, with millions more watching online and on television. The event alternates with Te Matatini o Te Kāhui Maunga regional competitions that select the qualifying groups.

Competition format

Groups qualify through regional competitions and perform a roughly 25–30 minute bracket judged across compulsory and aggregate items: choral singing (waiata tira), entrance (whakaeke), traditional chant (mōteatea), action song (waiata-ā-ringa), poi, haka, and exit (whakawātea), plus language (te reo Māori) quality. The highest-scoring teams advance to the finals day (Te Matangirua), and the overall champion is awarded the title Toa Whakaihuwaka. Renowned champion groups include Te Waka Huia, Te Kapahaka o Te Whānau a Apanui, Ngā Tūmanako, and Whāngārā Mai Tawhiti.

Cultural significance

Te Matatini is credited with driving excellence in te reo Māori composition, revitalising traditional performance, and showcasing contemporary Māori identity. Compositions frequently address current social and political issues, and the festival is a major vehicle for intergenerational transmission of language and custom.
