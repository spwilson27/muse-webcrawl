Ten Great Buildings

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ten Great Buildings (Chinese: 十大建筑) are ten monumental public buildings constructed in Beijing in 1959 to commemorate the tenth anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China. They were completed in an extraordinary ten months as part of the Great Leap Forward-era transformation of the capital, built with mass mobilized labour.

The buildings

  1. The Great Hall of the People
  2. The National Museum of China (originally the Museum of Chinese Revolution and Chinese History)
  3. The Cultural Palace of Nationalities
  4. Beijing railway station
  5. The Workers' Stadium
  6. The National Agriculture Exhibition Hall
  7. The Diaoyutai State Guesthouse
  8. The Minzu Hotel
  9. The Overseas Chinese Hotel (later demolished)
  10. The Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution

Architecture

The buildings combined Soviet-influenced monumental neoclassicism with elements of traditional Chinese architecture, such as tiled roofs and decorative motifs. Centred on Tiananmen Square, the Great Hall of the People and the museum face each other across the square, reshaping the ceremonial heart of Beijing. Most of the ten remain in use; later lists of "ten great buildings" were compiled for subsequent decades.
