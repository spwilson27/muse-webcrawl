Morpheus (Ancient Greek: Μορφεύς, "fashioner", derived from μορφή meaning "form, shape") is a god associated with sleep and dreams. In Ovid's Metamorphoses he is the son of Somnus (Sleep) and appears in dreams in human form. From the medieval period onward, the name began to stand almost exclusively for the god of dreams, and it survives in the name of the drug morphine and in idioms such as "in the arms of Morpheus."

Mythology

Morpheus is one of the Oneiroi, the thousand sons of Somnus, the god of sleep. His brothers Phobetor and Phantasos appear in dreams in the forms of animals and inanimate objects respectively, while Morpheus himself specializes in human shapes. In Book 11 of Ovid's Metamorphoses, the goddess Iris visits the cave of Somnus and rouses Morpheus to imitate the drowned king Ceyx and appear to his widow Alcyone in a dream, informing her of her husband's death. Morpheus dwells in a dim, poppy-strewn cave in the land of the Cimmerians, through which flows the river of forgetfulness, Lethe.

Cultural influence

The name has been taken up repeatedly in later literature and popular culture: for Dream of the Endless, the protagonist of Neil Gaiman's comic series The Sandman, and for the character Morpheus in The Matrix films, who offers the hero the choice between the red pill and the blue pill. The opiate morphine, isolated in the early 19th century by Friedrich Sertürner, was likewise named for the god of dreams because of its sleep-inducing properties.
