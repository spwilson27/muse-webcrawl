Core city (Japan)

Japanese: 中核市 (chūkakushi)

Legal basis: Local Autonomy Law, Article 252

Population requirement: 200,000 or more

Number: 62 (as of the mid-2020s)

A core city (中核市, chūkakushi) is a class of Japanese city designated by the national government under the Local Autonomy Law. Core cities are delegated many of the administrative functions normally performed by prefectural governments — particularly in health and welfare, environmental administration, urban planning, and education — but to a lesser extent than the largest category, the government-ordinance designated cities (seirei shitei toshi).

History

The system was created by a 1994 amendment to the Local Autonomy Law and took effect in April 1996, when the first twelve core cities were designated, among them Utsunomiya, Matsuyama, and Kagoshima. The original population threshold was 300,000, together with minimum area criteria that were later dropped; in 2014 the threshold was lowered to 200,000, which prompted a further wave of designations and the abolition of the separate "special city" (tokureishi) category, whose functions were folded into the core city system in 2015 and whose member cities transitioned by 2020.

Designation and powers

Designation is made by cabinet order following an application from the city, the consent of its assembly, and consultation with the prefecture and the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications. A core city operates its own public health centre (a power previously reserved to prefectures and designated cities), issues permits in fields such as waste disposal and pollution control, administers welfare services including the training and supervision of social welfare corporations, and manages certain aspects of teacher training for municipal elementary and junior high schools.

Examples of core cities include Toyama, Kanazawa, Nagano, Gifu, Toyohashi, Okazaki, Nara, Himeji, Kurashiki, Fukuyama, Takamatsu, Kochi, Miyazaki, and Naha. The largest core cities by population — such as Funabashi, Hachioji, and Kawaguchi — exceed 600,000 inhabitants without (yet) having shifted to designated-city status.
