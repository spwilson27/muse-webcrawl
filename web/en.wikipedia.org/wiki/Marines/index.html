Marines

Marines, or naval infantry, are soldiers who generally operate in the littorals (coastal areas) in support of naval operations, and also serve as a rapid-reaction force. Historically, tasks undertaken by marines have included raiding ashore in support of naval objectives, and providing security detachments aboard warships. Marines also help maintain discipline on board naval ships.

History

The earliest naval infantry were embarked soldiers in classical antiquity; Roman fleets carried legionaries trained to fight from ships. In the early modern period, Spain raised the first permanent naval infantry formations in 1537. The Netherlands followed in 1665 with its Marine Corps, and Britain raised the Royal Marines in 1664. The United States Marine Corps traces its origins to the Continental Marines of 1775.

During the age of sail, marines manned ships' guns, provided sharpshooters in the fighting tops, and formed boarding and landing parties. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the role shifted towards amphibious warfare, reaching its largest scale in the Pacific island-hopping campaigns of the Second World War and the landing at Inchon in the Korean War.

Roles

Modern marine forces specialise in amphibious assault, expeditionary operations, and rapid deployment from the sea, using landing craft, amphibious vehicles and helicopters launched from amphibious warfare ships. Depending on the country, they may also provide ceremonial guards, special forces detachments, and security for naval installations and embassies.

Marine forces by country

Large marine forces include the United States Marine Corps, the People's Liberation Army Navy Marine Corps of China, the Russian Naval Infantry, the Republic of Korea Marine Corps, and the Royal Marines of the United Kingdom. Smaller corps are maintained by many coastal nations, often as part of the navy or as specialist brigades.

The operations of marine forces depend heavily on marine propulsion and amphibious shipping, and their senior officers historically held ranks such as Marshal of France in combined commands. Compare the maritime republics, whose fleets carried similar expeditionary forces.
