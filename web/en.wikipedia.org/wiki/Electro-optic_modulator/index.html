Electro-optic modulator

An electro-optic modulator (EOM) is an optical device in which a signal-controlled element exhibiting the electro-optic effect is used to modulate a beam of light. The modulation may be imposed on the phase, frequency, amplitude or polarisation of the beam. Electro-optic modulators are essential components of fibre-optic communication systems and laser instrumentation.

Principles

Most EOMs exploit the Pockels effect, in which an applied voltage changes the refractive index of a crystal such as lithium niobate. A phase modulator simply retards the light's phase in proportion to the voltage. An intensity modulator is often built as a Mach–Zehnder interferometer, in which the beam is split between two arms, at least one of which is phase-modulated; recombination converts the phase difference into amplitude modulation. Polarisation modulation uses a Pockels cell between polarisers.

Characteristics

Key specifications include the half-wave voltage (Vπ) required for a π phase shift, bandwidth — which in travelling-wave integrated designs exceeds 40 GHz — insertion loss and extinction ratio. Bulk modulators are used with free-space laser beams, while waveguide modulators in lithium niobate, indium phosphide and silicon photonics serve telecommunications.

Applications

EOMs encode data onto optical carriers in telecommunications, act as Q-switches and pulse pickers in lasers, generate frequency sidebands for spectroscopy and optical frequency combs, and stabilise lasers by feedback.
