Okakura Kakuzō

Okakura Kakuzō (1863–1913), also known as Okakura Tenshin, was a Japanese scholar, art critic, and curator who contributed to the development of modern Japanese art and introduced Asian art and philosophy to the Western world. He is best known as the author of The Book of Tea (1906).

Life and career

Educated at Tokyo Imperial University, Okakura worked with Ernest Fenollosa to survey and preserve Japanese traditional art during the Meiji period. He co-founded the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in 1889 and later the Japan Art Institute. From 1904 he headed the Asian art division of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.

Works

Writing in English, Okakura presented pan-Asian ideals in The Ideals of the East (1903), which opens with the famous line "Asia is one." The Book of Tea explores the philosophy of tea culture (teaism) and its connections to Taoism and Zen.
