Saucy Jacky postcard

The "Saucy Jacky" postcard is a message received on 1 October 1888, postmarked that day, which claimed to be from the serial killer known as Jack the Ripper. Sent to the Central News Agency in London, it arrived the day after the "double event" — the murders of Elizabeth Stride and Catherine Eddowes in the early hours of 30 September 1888.

Text

Written in red ink, the postcard read, in part: "I was not codding dear old Boss when I gave you the tip, you'll hear about Saucy Jacky's work tomorrow double event this time number one squealed a bit couldn't finish straight off. Had not got time to get ears for police thanks for keeping last letter back till I got to work again." It referred to the earlier "Dear Boss" letter, which had promised to clip a victim's ears; Eddowes's ear had in fact been partially severed, a detail that lent the correspondence an air of authenticity at the time.

Authenticity

Police published facsimiles of the postcard and the "Dear Boss" letter hoping someone would recognise the handwriting. Most modern Ripperologists consider both items likely hoaxes, possibly written by journalists — the name "Jack the Ripper" itself is generally believed to have been invented in the "Dear Boss" letter. The original postcard was lost from police files; only photographs survive. Handwriting comparisons and the sheer volume of hoax mail received during the autumn of terror leave its authorship unresolved.
