Jackson State University (Jackson State or JSU) is a public historically black research university in Jackson, Mississippi. It is one of the largest HBCUs in the United States and the fourth-largest university in Mississippi by enrollment. The university is classified among "R2: Doctoral Universities – High research activity."

History

The institution was founded on October 23, 1877, in Natchez, Mississippi, as Natchez Seminary by the American Baptist Home Mission Society to educate newly freed African Americans. It moved to Jackson in 1882 and was renamed Jackson College. The state of Mississippi assumed control in 1940, and the school became Jackson State College, then Jackson State University in 1974. On May 15, 1970, Jackson police opened fire on students during civil-rights-era protests at the university, killing two young men — Phillip Gibbs and James Earl Green — in the Gibbs–Green shooting, an event less widely remembered than the Kent State shootings eleven days earlier.

Academics

Jackson State offers bachelor's, master's, specialist, and doctoral degrees through colleges of business, education and human development, health sciences, liberal arts, and science, engineering and technology. It is known for its meteorology program, its marching band, and growing research activity in environmental science and health disparities.

Athletics and band

The Jackson State Tigers compete in the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC). The football program, long a powerhouse of Black college football, drew national attention under head coach Deion Sanders (2020–2022). The university's marching band, the Sonic Boom of the South, founded in the 1940s, is one of the most celebrated HBCU bands, famous for its high-stepping style and "Get Ready" theme.
