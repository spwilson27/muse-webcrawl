Isengard is a fictional fortress in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy world of Middle-earth. It was built in the Second Age by the Númenóreans of Gondor at the valley of Nan Curunír ("Wizard's Vale"), at the southern end of the Misty Mountains, to guard the Gap of Rohan against the Dunlendings. Its Sindarin name is Angrenost, "Iron Fortress"; its centrepiece is the indestructible tower of Orthanc, which housed one of the seven seeing-stones, the palantíri.

Description

Isengard consists of a great circular stone ring about a mile in diameter, built into the mountainside, with a single gated entrance tunnelled through the south wall. At its centre stands Orthanc, a tower 500 feet high of polished black rock, fashioned by the Númenóreans into four fused pillars crowned by a platform between great spikes. In its years of neglect the valley within the ring was pleasant and wooded, with avenues of trees and a lake.

Role in the War of the Ring

In the Third Age the fortress decayed until the Stewards of Gondor granted the keys of Orthanc to the wizard Saruman in 2759, expecting him to hold it against Gondor's enemies. Instead Saruman fortified Isengard for himself: he felled the trees, filled the valley with furnaces, forges, wolf-pits and armouries, bred the Uruk-hai, and raised an army to seize the One Ring and overthrow Rohan. During the War of the Ring his armies were destroyed at the Battle of Helm's Deep, and Isengard itself was flooded and wrecked by the Ents of Fangorn Forest, enraged by Saruman's destruction of their trees. Saruman remained a prisoner in Orthanc until he was released by Treebeard after the war.

After the war

The Ents replanted the valley as the Treegarth of Orthanc, Orthanc was re-keyed by King Elessar (Aragorn), and the recovered palantír was used by the restored kingdom. Critics have read Tolkien's depiction of industrialised Isengard — smoke, machinery and defiled nature — as a lament for the destruction of the rural England of his youth by industrialisation.
