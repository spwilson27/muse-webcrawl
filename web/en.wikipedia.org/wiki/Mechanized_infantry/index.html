Mechanized infantry


Mechanised infantry dismounting from an infantry fighting vehicle

Mechanized infantry are infantry units equipped with armored personnel carriers (APCs) or infantry fighting vehicles (IFVs) for transport and combat. In the US Army, the distinction between mechanised infantry, equipped with the M2 Bradley IFV, and "Stryker infantry," mounted in the lighter Stryker vehicle, is organisational as much as doctrinal.

History

Mechanised infantry emerged during the Second World War, when Germany fielded Panzergrenadier units in half-tracks to accompany tank formations, and the Allies used armoured half-tracks and "Kangaroo" carriers. The post-war development of fully tracked APCs — such as the American M113 and the Soviet BMP series, the first true IFVs — allowed infantry to keep pace with tanks, fight mounted, and operate in contaminated environments.

Role and equipment

Mechanised infantry combine mobility, protection, and firepower. IFVs such as the Bradley, Warrior, Marder, and BMP-2 carry cannon, machine guns, and anti-tank missiles, enabling the infantry squad to fight from the vehicle or dismount for close combat. Compared with motorised infantry, which uses unarmoured trucks or light vehicles, mechanised units are better suited to high-intensity combined-arms operations but require much greater maintenance and logistics support.

Distinctions

The boundary between APCs and IFVs, and between mechanised and armoured infantry, varies by country and period. Heavy mechanised formations typically operate alongside main battle tanks within armoured brigades and divisions.
