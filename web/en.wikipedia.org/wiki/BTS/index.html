BTS (Korean: 방탄소년단; RR: Bangtan Sonyeondan), also known as the Bangtan Boys, is a South Korean boy band formed in 2010 and debuting in 2013 under Big Hit Entertainment. The septet — RM, Jin, Suga, J-Hope, Jimin, V and Jung Kook — is widely credited with popularising K-pop globally and is the best-selling musical act in South Korean history, with tens of millions of albums sold.

History

BTS debuted in June 2013 with the single album 2 Cool 4 Skool. Early work leaned toward hip-hop and addressed school-age anxiety and social pressure. Breakthrough success came with The Most Beautiful Moment in Life series (2015–2016) and the album Wings (2016). International stardom followed: Love Yourself: Tear (2018) became the first K-pop album to top the US Billboard 200, and "Dynamite" (2020), their first English-language single, debuted at number one on the Billboard Hot 100. Subsequent singles "Butter" and "Permission to Dance" (2021) also topped the chart. The anthology album Proof was released in June 2022, after which the group announced a hiatus from group activities so members could complete mandatory South Korean military service, with a reunion planned for 2025.

Artistry and impact

BTS co-writes and co-produces much of its catalogue, addressing mental health, self-love, and the pressures on youth — themes central to the "Love Yourself" campaign run with UNICEF and to leader RM's 2018 address to the United Nations General Assembly. Their fanbase, ARMY, is one of the largest and most organised online fandoms. Economists have estimated BTS's annual contribution to South Korea's economy in the billions of dollars, spanning tourism, merchandise and exports, and the group has received numerous honours, including the Order of Cultural Merit from the President of South Korea and multiple Billboard, American Music and Grammy nominations (with a Grammy win eluding them as of their hiatus).
