The American Association of Variable Star Observers (AAVSO) is an international nonprofit organisation of amateur and professional astronomers who study variable stars. Founded in 1911, it coordinates observing campaigns and maintains one of the world's largest databases of variable star observations.

History

The AAVSO was founded by amateur astronomer William Tyler Olcott, inspired by Harvard College Observatory director Edward Charles Pickering's call for systematic amateur monitoring of variable stars. Long-time Harvard astronomer Leon Campbell served as its first Recorder, standardising observation reports. The organisation was based at Harvard for decades before moving to its own headquarters in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Activities

Members submit brightness estimates and CCD photometry of variable stars, novae, supernovae and other transient phenomena. The AAVSO International Database holds tens of millions of observations dating back more than a century, and its light-curve generators and comparison-star charts are standard tools of variable-star astronomy. The AAVSO also runs the Variable Star Index (VSX), publishes the Journal of the AAVSO, and issues alerts coordinating observers with professional observatories and space missions.
