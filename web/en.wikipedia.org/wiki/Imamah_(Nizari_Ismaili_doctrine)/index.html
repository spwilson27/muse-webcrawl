Imamah (Nizari Ismaili doctrine)

In Nizari Ismaili doctrine, the Imamah (leadership) is a divinely ordained institution: the Imam is the spiritual and religious leader of the community, designated by explicit appointment (nass) from his predecessor, in a line traced through Ali ibn Abi Talib and Fatimah, the daughter of the Prophet Muhammad, and then through their descendants.

Doctrine

Nizari teaching holds that the Imam is the bearer of the divine light (nur) and possesses authoritative knowledge ('ilm) enabling him to interpret the faith — including its esoteric dimension (ta'wil) — for each age. Unlike the Twelver Shia doctrine of a hidden (occulted) Imam, Nizaris affirm a living, present (hazir) Imam.

Succession and history

The Nizari line separated from the Musta'li branch after the death of the Fatimid caliph-Imam al-Mustansir in 1094, when Nizar's claim was championed by Hasan-i Sabbah from the fortress of Alamut in Persia. The Alamut period ended with the Mongol destruction of the fortress in 1256, after which the Imams lived for centuries in concealment in Persia before emerging publicly; in the 19th century the Imam Hasan Ali Shah received the title Aga Khan.

The modern Imamate

The 49th Imam, Aga Khan IV (Shah Karim al-Hussaini), led the community from 1957 until his death in 2025, building a global development network and a network of jamatkhanas (prayer halls). He was succeeded by his son Rahim al-Hussaini as Aga Khan V. Nizari Ismailis today live in more than 25 countries across Central and South Asia, East Africa, the Middle East, Europe, and North America.
