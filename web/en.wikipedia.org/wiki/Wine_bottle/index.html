Wine bottle

A wine bottle is a bottle, generally of glass, used for holding wine. Some wines are fermented in the bottle, while others are bottled only after fermentation. The standard size of a wine bottle is 750 millilitres (mL), though many other sizes are produced, from the 187.5 mL piccolo to large-format bottles of many litres, often named after biblical figures such as the Jeroboam, Methuselah, and Nebuchadnezzar.

Shapes

Traditional regional shapes include the Bordeaux bottle, with straight sides and high shoulders, used for red Bordeaux and many other wines; the Burgundy bottle, with sloping shoulders; and the tall, slender Alsace or German bottle used for Riesling and similar wines. Champagne bottles are thicker and stronger to withstand the pressure of the sparkling wine inside, with a deep punt in the base. The colour of the glass—typically dark green, amber, or clear—protects the wine from light, which can degrade its quality.

Closures and capsules

Wine bottles are traditionally sealed with natural cork, though screw caps, synthetic corks, and glass stoppers are now common. A capsule of foil or plastic covers the top of the bottle and cork. The punt, the indentation in the base of many bottles, is variously explained as aiding stability, strength, sediment collection, or efficient stacking.
