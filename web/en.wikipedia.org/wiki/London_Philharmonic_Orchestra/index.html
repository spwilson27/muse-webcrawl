The London Philharmonic Orchestra (LPO) is one of five permanent symphony orchestras based in London. It was founded in 1932 by the conductor Sir Thomas Beecham together with Malcolm Sargent. The orchestra has been resident at the Royal Festival Hall on the South Bank since the hall opened in 1951, and since 1964 it has also been the resident orchestra at Glyndebourne Festival Opera.

History

Beecham assembled the LPO from the finest players in London for the 1932–33 season, and it quickly established a reputation for virtuosity. After Beecham's departure in 1939 the orchestra became self-governing, one of the first British orchestras to be run by its own players. During the Second World War the orchestra toured extensively to maintain morale. Post-war principal conductors included Eduard van Beinum, Adrian Boult, Bernard Haitink, Georg Solti, Klaus Tennstedt, Franz Welser-Möst, Kurt Masur, Vladimir Jurowski (2007–2021) and Edward Gardner.

Work

Besides its concert and opera work, the LPO has recorded many film scores, including The Lord of the Rings trilogy (music by Howard Shore), and has appeared on video game and popular music recordings. It runs an extensive education and community programme and its own record label, founded in 2005.
