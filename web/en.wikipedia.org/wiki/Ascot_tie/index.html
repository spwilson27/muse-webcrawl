Ascot tie

An ascot tie worn with morning dress

An ascot tie, or ascot, is a neckband with wide pointed wings, traditionally made of pale grey patterned silk. It is worn with formal morning dress for men, fastened around the neck and secured with a tie pin or stickpin. The ascot takes its name from Ascot Racecourse in England, where the Royal Ascot meeting required formal morning dress.

Description and wear

The formal ascot is worn above a wing-collared shirt, its wings folded over a knot or loop and pinned at the front. A more casual variant, the day cravat, is a simpler neckband tucked inside an open-collared shirt. The ascot evolved in the late 19th century from the earlier cravat and became standard daytime formal wear in the Edwardian era, before being largely displaced by the long necktie.

Today ascots are seen mainly at weddings and at events with a strict morning-dress code, and remain part of the livery of some grooms and horsemen. In the United States the term "ascot" also denotes the casual day cravat worn inside the shirt.
