Ottoman–Habsburg wars

The Ottoman–Habsburg wars were a series of conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and the Habsburg monarchy — including the Austrian Habsburgs and Habsburg Spain — fought from the 16th to the 18th century over Hungary, the Balkans, and the Mediterranean.

Course of the conflicts

The struggle began in earnest after the Ottoman victory at Mohács in 1526 destroyed the Kingdom of Hungary, leaving the Habsburgs and Ottomans to partition its lands. The first Siege of Vienna in 1529 marked the high point of Ottoman advance into Central Europe. Fighting continued intermittently: the Long Turkish War (1593–1606), naval wars in the Mediterranean including the Battle of Lepanto (1571) against a Spanish-led Holy League, and renewed campaigns in the 17th century.

Turning point

The failed second Siege of Vienna in 1683, relieved by a coalition including Polish forces under John III Sobieski, began a sustained Habsburg counteroffensive. Under commanders such as Prince Eugene of Savoy, Austria recovered Hungary and Transylvania, gains confirmed by the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) and the Treaty of Passarowitz (1718).

Later wars and legacy

Further wars in 1737–39 and 1788–91 produced mixed results, but the balance had permanently shifted. The centuries of conflict shaped the Military Frontier in Croatia, left deep marks on Balkan demography and memory, and helped define the Habsburg monarchy as a great power of Central Europe.
