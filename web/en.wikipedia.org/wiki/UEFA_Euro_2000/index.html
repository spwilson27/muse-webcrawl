The 2000 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly known as Euro 2000, was the 11th UEFA European Championship, held from 10 June to 2 July 2000. It was the first tournament co-hosted by two countries, Belgium and the Netherlands. France, the reigning world champions, won the tournament, defeating Italy 2–1 in the final in Rotterdam thanks to a golden goal by David Trezeguet.

Tournament summary

Sixteen teams competed. The Netherlands thrilled home crowds with attacking football, including a 6–1 quarter-final rout of Yugoslavia, but were eliminated by Italy in a semi-final remembered for Dutch missed penalties — one in normal time and two in the shoot-out. In the other semi-final, France beat Portugal 2–1 with a golden-goal penalty by Zinedine Zidane.

Final

Italy led the final through Marco Delvecchio's goal and were seconds from victory when Sylvain Wiltord equalised in stoppage time. In extra time, Trezeguet volleyed in the golden goal, making France the first team to hold the World Cup and European Championship simultaneously since West Germany in 1974, and the only one to do so as reigning world champions winning the Euro afterwards.
