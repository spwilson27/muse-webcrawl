Hachikō (ハチ公, 10 November 1923 – 8 March 1935) was a Japanese Akita dog remembered for his remarkable loyalty to his owner, Hidesaburō Ueno, for whom he continued to wait for over nine years following Ueno's death.

Life

Hachikō was brought to Tokyo by Hidesaburō Ueno, a professor of agriculture at the Tokyo Imperial University. Each day Ueno commuted to work by train, and Hachikō would meet him at Shibuya Station when he returned. On 21 May 1925 Ueno died of a cerebral hemorrhage while at work and never returned. From then until his own death on 8 March 1935, Hachikō returned to Shibuya Station every day to await Ueno's return.

Legacy

A bronze statue of Hachikō was erected at Shibuya Station in 1934, with Hachikō himself present at its unveiling; it remains a famous meeting point in Tokyo. His story has been told in books and films, including the Japanese film Hachikō Monogatari (1987) and the American film Hachi: A Dog's Tale (2009). Hachikō's remains are preserved at the National Museum of Nature and Science in Ueno, Tokyo.
