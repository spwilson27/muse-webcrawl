Aesthetics is the branch of Philosophy that studies beauty, art, taste and aesthetic experience and judgement. The word derives from the Greek aisthetikos (of sense perception); the German philosopher Alexander Baumgarten gave it its modern philosophical use in the 18th century. Key questions include what makes something beautiful or sublime, whether aesthetic judgements are objective or subjective, and how art should be defined.

History

Plato distrusted art as imitation at two removes from truth, while Aristotle defended poetry and tragedy in the Poetics. Immanuel Kant, in the Critique of Judgment (1790), analysed judgements of beauty as 'disinterested' pleasure claiming universal assent, and distinguished the beautiful from the sublime. Later thinkers — from Arthur Schopenhauer to the pragmatists and analytic philosophers — debated expression, representation and the institutional definition of art.

Theories of art

Major approaches define art by representation (mimesis), by expression of emotion, by formal properties, or by the conventions of the 'artworld' (Arthur Danto, George Dickie). Aesthetics also covers everyday aesthetic experience, nature, design and the ethics of art.
