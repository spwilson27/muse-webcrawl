Green Bay

[Image: Green Bay]

Green Bay is an arm of Lake Michigan, located along the south coast of Michigan's Upper Peninsula and the east coast of Wisconsin. It is about 120 miles (193 km) long and 10 to 20 miles wide, separated from the rest of the lake by the Door Peninsula and the Garden Peninsula.

Geography and history

The bay receives the Fox River at its southern end, where the city of Green Bay, Wisconsin, stands. French explorer Jean Nicolet reached the bay in 1634, making contact with the Ho-Chunk people. The bay's estuary is one of the largest freshwater estuaries in the world and supports major fisheries, though the lower Fox River became heavily contaminated with PCBs from paper mills, leading to a large remediation programme.

Navigation

Important ports include Green Bay, Marinette, Sturgeon Bay, and Menominee. The bay freezes in winter; icebreaking keeps shipping routes open as long as possible.
