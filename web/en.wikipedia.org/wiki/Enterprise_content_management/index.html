Enterprise content management

Enterprise content management (ECM) extends the concept of content management by adding a time line for each content item and possibly enforcing processes for its creation, approval and distribution. ECM is defined by the Association for Intelligent Information Management (AIIM) as the strategies, methods and tools used to capture, manage, store, preserve and deliver content and documents related to organisational processes.

Formal concept and term first used by AIIM in 2000, ECM combines components traditionally classed as document management, records management, document imaging, workflow and web content management into an integrated discipline concerned with unstructured information — documents, scanned images, email, video and web pages — as opposed to the structured data handled by database systems.

Components

AIIM groups ECM technologies into five components:

  • Capture — entering content into the system, including scanning, OCR, forms recognition and direct authoring.
  • Manage — document management, collaboration, records management, workflow and business process management, and web content management.
  • Store — repositories, library services (check-in/check-out, versioning), and storage infrastructure.
  • Preserve — long-term archiving and digital preservation consistent with retention policies.
  • Deliver — presentation of content through output management, portals, and distribution channels.

Later AIIM definitions reframed ECM around "intelligent information management", and analysts have increasingly used terms such as content services platforms for cloud-based, API-driven successors to the traditional monolithic ECM suites sold by vendors such as OpenText, IBM (FileNet), Hyland (OnBase), Microsoft (SharePoint) and Alfresco.

Uses

Organisations use ECM to control large volumes of documents, meet regulatory and audit requirements (Sarbanes–Oxley, GDPR), automate processes such as invoice handling and case management, and support knowledge work by making information searchable and retrievable across the enterprise.
