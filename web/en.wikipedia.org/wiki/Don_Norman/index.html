Donald Arthur Norman (born December 25, 1935) is an American researcher, professor, and author in the fields of design, usability engineering, and cognitive science. He is best known for his book The Design of Everyday Things (originally published in 1988 as The Psychology of Everyday Things), a foundational text of user-centered design. Norman co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group usability consultancy with Jakob Nielsen and served as a vice president at Apple, where he popularised the job title "user experience architect."

Norman's best-known book introduced concepts such as affordances, signifiers, and the "Norman door."

Career

Norman earned his bachelor's degree from MIT and his master's and PhD in psychology from the University of Pennsylvania. He spent much of his academic career at the University of California, San Diego, where he helped build its cognitive science program, and later taught at Northwestern University, co-directing the Segal Design Institute. At Apple in the 1990s he was a vice president of advanced technology. His industry roles have also included positions at HP and executive education work.

Ideas

Norman argues that design failures — such as doors that give no clue whether to push or pull, often called "Norman doors" — are faults of design, not of users. He developed the stages of the "action cycle" (forming goals, executing actions, evaluating results) and popularised the concepts of affordance, constraints, mapping, feedback, and conceptual models in design. In later work, including Emotional Design (2004) and Design for a Better World (2023), he extended his arguments to emotion, sustainability, and what he calls humanity-centered design.
