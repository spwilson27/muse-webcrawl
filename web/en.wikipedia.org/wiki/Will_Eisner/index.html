William Erwin Eisner (1917–2005) was an American cartoonist, writer and entrepreneur, regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of comics. He was a pioneer of the graphic novel and of the study of comics as an art form.

Career

Eisner co-founded the Eisner & Iger studio in the late 1930s, a packaging studio that employed Jack Kirby and Bob Kane. From 1940 to 1952 he produced The Spirit, a 16-page Sunday newspaper supplement whose masked detective stories used cinematic layouts, splash pages integrating the title into the artwork, and mature, literary themes. After war service producing instructional comics (a field he pursued for decades for the US Army with PS Magazine), he returned to long-form work.

Graphic novels and theory

A Contract with God (1978), stories of Jewish tenement life in the Bronx, popularised the term "graphic novel". Works such as A Life Force, The Dreamer and To the Heart of the Storm followed, as did the theoretical texts Comics and Sequential Art (1985) and Graphic Storytelling and Visual Narrative. The Eisner Awards, presented annually at San Diego Comic-Con, are the comics industry's leading honours — fittingly, since Eisner helped inspire the convention culture that also celebrates creations like Wonder Woman.
