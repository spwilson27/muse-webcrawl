Margery Louise Allingham (May 20, 1904 – June 30, 1966) was an English novelist from the "Golden Age of Detective Fiction," and considered one of its four "Queens of Crime," alongside Dorothy L. Sayers, Agatha Christie, and Ngaio Marsh. She created the gentleman detective Albert Campion, who starred in eighteen novels and numerous short stories.

Life and career

Allingham was born in Ealing, London, into a family of writers; her father Herbert Allingham was a pulp magazine editor and novelist. She sold her first story at age eight. Campion first appeared as a minor character in The Crime at Black Dudley (1929) but proved so popular that he became her series hero — a mild-mannered, bespectacled aristocrat working with his ex-burglar manservant Magersfontein Lugg.

Early Campion novels were light-hearted thrillers, but the series darkened and deepened with Death of a Ghost (1934) and Flowers for the Judge (1936). Tiger in the Smoke (1952), a pursuit of a psychopathic killer through fog-bound post-war London, is widely regarded as her finest novel. Allingham died of breast cancer in 1966. Her husband, Philip Youngman Carter, completed her last novel, Cargo of Eagles (1968).
