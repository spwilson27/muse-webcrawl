Militia organizations in the United States

Militia organizations in the United States are private paramilitary groups, distinct from the official militia (the National Guard and state defence forces), whose members train with firearms and organise in military-style units. The modern movement grew in the 1990s and generally espouses a belief that citizens must be prepared to resist perceived government tyranny.

Background

The groups draw rhetorical support from the Second Amendment and colonial militia traditions. Membership surged after federal law-enforcement actions at Ruby Ridge (1992) and Waco (1993), declined after the Oklahoma City bombing of 1995, and revived in later decades. Prominent networks have included the Michigan Militia, the Oath Keepers and the Three Percenters.

Legal status and scrutiny

Most states have laws restricting private paramilitary activity, though enforcement is rare absent criminal conduct. Civil-rights monitors such as the Southern Poverty Law Center track these groups as part of the antigovernment movement; some members have been prosecuted for weapons offences and plots against officials.
