Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
FieldStochastic processes, statistical physics, finance
Introduced byLeonard Ornstein & George Uhlenbeck (1930)
TypeGaussian, Markov, mean-reverting diffusion

In mathematics, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is a stochastic process with applications in financial mathematics and the physical sciences. Its original application in physics was as a model for the velocity of a massive Brownian particle under the influence of friction. It is named after Leonard Ornstein and George Uhlenbeck. The process is a stationary Gaussian process that is also Markovian, and it is the only nontrivial process satisfying all three properties of being Gaussian, Markov, and time-homogeneous; it can be regarded as the continuous-time analogue of the discrete-time AR(1) process.

Definition

The process Xt is defined by the stochastic differential equation

dXt = θ(μ − Xt) dt + σ dWt,

where θ > 0 is the rate of mean reversion, μ is the long-term mean, σ > 0 is the volatility, and Wt is a Wiener process. Unlike the Wiener process, whose variance grows without bound, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is pulled back towards μ, giving a stationary distribution: Xt is normally distributed with mean μ and variance σ²/(2θ) in the long run. An explicit solution exists in terms of an Itô integral, and the transition density is Gaussian.

Applications

In physics, the process models the velocity of a Brownian particle subject to viscous drag, improving on Einstein's theory by incorporating friction. In mathematical finance, it underlies the Vasicek model (1977) of interest rates, in which the short rate mean-reverts, and it is used to model commodity prices, volatility, and pairs-trading spreads that revert to a historical mean. In neuroscience, simplified neuron models use it to describe membrane potential fluctuations driven by noisy synaptic input (the leaky integrate-and-fire model).
