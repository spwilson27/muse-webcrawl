Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben

Friedrich Wilhelm August Heinrich Ferdinand von Steuben (17 September 1730 – 28 November 1794), also known as Baron von Steuben, was a Prussian military officer who became a major general in the Continental Army and is credited with transforming the American forces into a disciplined fighting army during the Revolutionary War.

Early career

Born in Magdeburg, Steuben served in the Prussian Army during the Seven Years' War, rising to captain and serving on the staff of Frederick the Great. After leaving Prussian service he worked for smaller German courts before being recruited in Paris by Benjamin Franklin and Silas Deane.

American service

Steuben arrived at Valley Forge in February 1778, where George Washington appointed him acting inspector general. Despite speaking little English, he retrained the troops in European drill, bayonet use, camp sanitation and manoeuvre, working through interpreters and a model company of soldiers. His system was codified in the 1779 Regulations for the Order and Discipline of the Troops of the United States, the "Blue Book", which remained in use for decades.

Later life

Steuben later commanded troops in the Yorktown campaign, became an American citizen, and settled in New York, where land grants were made to him in recognition of his service. He died in 1794 near Remsen, New York. His contributions are commemorated by Steuben Day parades and numerous statues.
