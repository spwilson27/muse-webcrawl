OpenAI Five

OpenAI Five is a system of five artificial neural networks developed by the research organization OpenAI that learns to play the video game Dota 2 at a professional level. It is notable as the first AI system to defeat world champions at an esports game on a public stage.

Development

The project grew out of OpenAI's earlier 1v1 bot that defeated top players in the mid-lane duel format in 2017. For the full 5v5 game, five identical neural networks — one controlling each hero — were trained using large-scale reinforcement learning (a scaled version of Proximal Policy Optimization) through self-play, accumulating the equivalent of many thousands of years of gameplay experience. The networks receive observations via the game's API and act only through learned behavior, without search or human data.

Matches

OpenAI Five defeated amateur and semi-professional teams in 2018 but lost two games to professional teams at The International 2018 under restrictions on the hero pool and game rules. In April 2019, in San Francisco, it defeated OG — the reigning world champion team — two games to zero in a public exhibition, becoming the first AI to beat world champions at an esports title. A subsequent public arena let human teams challenge the system online; over several days it won the overwhelming majority of games before some teams found strategies to beat it.

Significance

The project demonstrated that large-scale reinforcement learning with self-play could master a complex, partially observable team game requiring long-horizon coordination. The techniques and infrastructure developed for OpenAI Five influenced later work in multi-agent learning and robotics.
