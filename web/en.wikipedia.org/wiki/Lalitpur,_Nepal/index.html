Lalitpur (Nepali: ललितपुर), also known historically as Patan, is a metropolitan city in the south-central part of the Kathmandu Valley in Nepal. It lies immediately south of Kathmandu, from which it is separated by the Bagmati River. Lalitpur is renowned as a city of fine arts, famous for its rich cultural heritage, particularly its tradition of arts and crafts produced by the Newar community.

The city is home to Patan Durbar Square, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and is noted for its temples, courtyards, water spouts and festivals, including the Rato Machhindranath Jatra, one of the longest-running chariot festivals in Nepal.

History

Lalitpur is believed to be among the oldest known cities in Nepal, founded in the 3rd century BCE, traditionally attributed to the Kirata dynasty. The city flourished under the Licchavi and later the Malla kings, under whom many of its temples and palaces were built. In 1768 the city was annexed into the expanding kingdom of Prithvi Narayan Shah during the unification of Nepal.

The old city centre, Patan, became a centre of both Hindu and Buddhist culture; four stupas at the city's corners are traditionally attributed to the Mauryan emperor Ashoka. The city's metalworkers and woodcarvers supplied temples and palaces throughout the valley.

Geography

Lalitpur sits on the elevated south bank of the Bagmati River at an elevation of roughly 1,400 metres. Its metropolitan area covers about 37 square kilometres and adjoins Kathmandu to the north and Bhaktapur to the east.

Culture

The city preserves a dense ensemble of Hindu temples and Buddhist monasteries (bahals). The Patan Museum, housed in a restored Malla palace, exhibits traditional sacred art. Major festivals include Yenya Punhi customs, the Bisket Jatra of the valley, and the month-long Rato Machhindranath chariot festival.

Economy

Handicrafts — metal statuary, wood carving, thangka painting and weaving — remain important livelihoods, alongside services, education and a growing suburban residential sector.
