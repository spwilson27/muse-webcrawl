M. V. Lomonosov Moscow State University (MSU; Russian: Московский государственный университет имени М. В. Ломоносова) is a public research university in Moscow, Russia. Founded in 1755 by the decree of Elizabeth of Russia, on the initiative of the polymath Mikhail Lomonosov and the statesman Ivan Shuvalov, it is the oldest and largest university in Russia and is generally regarded as the country's most prestigious institution of higher education.

History

The university opened on January 25 (Tatiana Day), 1755, with faculties of philosophy, jurisprudence, and medicine. Its main building on Mokhovaya Street stood near Red Square. In the 19th century, Moscow University became a centre of Russian science and liberal thought. After the 1917 Revolution it was renamed and expanded massively in the Soviet period.

In 1953 the university moved to a vast new campus on the Lenin (now Sparrow) Hills, dominated by one of the "Seven Sisters" Stalinist skyscrapers. At 240 m, designed by Lev Rudnev, it was the tallest educational building in the world and remains one of the tallest. The campus hosts most of the university's 40-plus faculties and institutes, its botanical garden, and a scientific library.

Academics

MSU comprises faculties in mathematics and mechanics, computational mathematics and cybernetics, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, geology, geography, philology, history, economics, law, journalism, and many others, together with research institutes such as the Skobeltsyn Institute of Nuclear Physics. Thirteen Nobel laureates and numerous Fields medalists have been affiliated with the university. Its alumni include Anton Chekhov, Mikhail Gorbachev, Boris Pasternak, and Andrei Sakharov.
