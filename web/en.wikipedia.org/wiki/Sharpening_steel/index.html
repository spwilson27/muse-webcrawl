Sharpening steel

A sharpening steel, also called a honing steel, butcher's steel, or chef's steel, is a rod of steel, ceramic, or diamond-coated material used to maintain the edge of a knife. A typical steel is about 30 centimetres (12 inches) long, round or oval in cross-section, with a handle and sometimes fine longitudinal ridges.

Despite its name, a plain steel does not truly sharpen a dull blade by removing significant material; instead it hones it — realigning the microscopic edge, which bends and folds with use, back into a straight, true edge. Diamond and ceramic steels do remove a small amount of metal and can restore a moderately dull edge. Actual sharpening of a worn edge is done with a whetstone or other abrasive sharpener.

Use

The knife is drawn across the steel at a consistent angle of roughly 15–20 degrees, alternating sides, either with the steel held point-down on a board or in the air, as commonly demonstrated by chefs and butchers. Steels are used frequently — traditionally before each use of the knife — while stoning is needed only occasionally.
