American International Pictures

American International Pictures (AIP) was an American film production company founded in 1954 by James H. Nicholson and Samuel Z. Arkoff. It specialised in low-budget films aimed at the teenage drive-in market — horror, science fiction, beach party, biker, and blaxploitation pictures — and became one of the most successful independent studios in Hollywood history.

History

Originally named the American Releasing Corporation, the company pioneered techniques such as releasing two films as a double bill and deciding productions by title and poster before a script existed. Roger Corman directed many of its early successes, including the Edgar Allan Poe adaptations with Vincent Price beginning with House of Usher (1960). AIP hits included I Was a Teenage Werewolf (1957), the Beach Party films with Frankie Avalon and Annette Funicello, The Wild Angels (1966), and Blacula (1972). The studio also gave early work to Francis Ford Coppola, Martin Scorsese, Jack Nicholson, and Robert De Niro.

Decline and legacy

Following Nicholson's departure in 1972 and rising production costs, AIP merged with Filmways in 1979; the combined company's library eventually passed to Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. In 2020, MGM announced a revival of the AIP label for new productions. The studio's catalogue remains a touchstone of cult and exploitation cinema.
