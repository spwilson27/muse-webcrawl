Castro Theatre
Location429 Castro Street, San Francisco, California
Opened1922
ArchitectTimothy L. Pflueger
DesignationSan Francisco Designated Landmark No. 100

The Castro Theatre is a historic movie palace in the Castro District of San Francisco, California, and one of the city's best-known LGBTQ landmarks. Its Spanish Colonial Revival facade, designed by Timothy L. Pflueger, features a great central arched window surmounted by a Spanish bell tower, and a neon marquee and vertical sign added in 1937.

History

The theatre opened on 22 June 1922 and was built by the Nasser brothers, who ran a chain of San Francisco cinemas. The 1,400-seat auditorium combines Spanish, Asian, and Italian motifs, with a rare leatherette ceiling "tent" and a Mighty Wurlitzer organ played before screenings. It was designated a San Francisco landmark in 1977.

Programming

The Castro is renowned for repertory cinema, sing-along screenings, and film festivals including Frameline, the San Francisco International LGBTQ+ Film Festival. It has hosted sing-alongs of films such as The Sound of Music, drag performances by house icon Peaches Christ, and the citywide mourning after the assassination of Harvey Milk in 1978. In 2022 its centenary year, a renovation by Another Planet Entertainment converted it into a multi-use live venue while preserving its film capability and historic features.
