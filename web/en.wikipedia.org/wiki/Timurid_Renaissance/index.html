Timurid Renaissance

The Timurid Renaissance was a historic period of cultural, artistic, and scientific revival during the late 14th and 15th centuries under the Timurid Empire, centered on the cities of Samarkand and Herat in Central Asia.

Origins

Amir Timur (Tamerlane), founder of the empire, brought artisans and scholars from conquered lands to his capital at Samarkand. His descendants, notably Shah Rukh and Ulugh Beg, became great patrons of learning and the arts.

Art and architecture

Timurid patronage produced monumental architecture sheathed in turquoise tilework — the Shah-i-Zinda necropolis, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque, and the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum — and a golden age of Persian miniature painting in the Herat school under artists such as Kamāl ud-Dīn Behzād. Chagatai Turkish literature flourished under Mir Ali Shir Nava'i.

Science

Ulugh Beg built a great observatory at Samarkand and compiled star catalogues of remarkable accuracy. Mathematicians and astronomers such as Jamshīd al-Kāshī computed π to unprecedented precision.

Legacy

The Timurid cultural synthesis deeply influenced the later Safavid, Ottoman, and Mughal worlds; Babur, founder of Mughal India, was a Timurid prince.
