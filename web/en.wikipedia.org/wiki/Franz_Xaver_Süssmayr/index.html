Franz Xaver Süssmayr (1766 – 17 September 1803) was an Austrian composer and conductor best known as the musician who completed Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart's Requiem in D minor after Mozart's death in 1791. Although his own operas were popular in Vienna during his lifetime, his reputation rests almost entirely on his association with Mozart's final work.

Life

Süssmayr was born in Schwanenstadt in Upper Austria and was educated at the Benedictine abbey school at Kremsmünster. He moved to Vienna around 1787, where he studied with Antonio Salieri and, in 1790–91, became a pupil and assistant of Mozart, copying scores and helping with rehearsals. Composers such as Joseph Eybler and Süssmayr were drawn into the task of finishing the Requiem, which had been commissioned anonymously by Count Franz von Walsegg.

The Requiem completion

Mozart died on 5 December 1791 leaving the Requiem unfinished: the Introitus was complete, other sections existed in various stages of draft, and the Lacrymosa broke off after eight bars. Mozart's widow Constanze, needing the completion fee, first asked Eybler and then Süssmayr to finish the work. Süssmayr's completion — which includes his own composition of the Sanctus, Benedictus, and Agnus Dei, drawing on Mozart's sketches and repeating the opening music for the Lux aeterna — is the version most often performed, though several modern scholars have produced alternative completions. The extent and authenticity of his contribution have been debated ever since.

Other works and death

Süssmayr wrote some twenty-seven stage works, including the successful opera Der Spiegel von Arkadien (1794), as well as sacred music and instrumental pieces. From 1794 he was deputy Kapellmeister, and later Kapellmeister, at Vienna's National Theatre. He died of tuberculosis in 1803 and was buried in an unmarked grave in St. Marx Cemetery — the same cemetery where Mozart had been buried twelve years earlier.
