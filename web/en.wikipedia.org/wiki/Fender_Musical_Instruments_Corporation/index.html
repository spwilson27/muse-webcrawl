The Fender Musical Instruments Corporation is an American manufacturer of stringed instruments and amplifiers. Founded in Fullerton, California, in 1946 by Leo Fender, the company produced the first mass-produced solid-body electric guitar, the Telecaster (1951); the first mass-produced electric bass, the Precision Bass (1951); and the Stratocaster (1954), one of the most iconic and widely copied electric guitar designs in history. Fender instruments and amplifiers were foundational to rock and roll, blues, country, and popular music generally.

History

Leo Fender began in radio repair, founding Fender's Radio Service in 1938 and, with Clayton "Doc" Kauffman, the K&F Manufacturing Corporation in 1945 to build lap steel guitars and amplifiers. After Kauffman's departure, Fender founded the Fender Electric Instrument Company in 1946. The single-pickup Esquire and dual-pickup Broadcaster/Telecaster of 1950–51 introduced a bolt-on neck, slab body construction designed for cheap manufacture and easy repair — a radical departure from traditional luthiery. The Precision Bass revolutionised popular music by giving bassists a portable, amplified, fretted instrument; the Stratocaster added a contoured body, three pickups, and a synchronised tremolo bridge.

Fender sold the company to CBS in 1965. Instruments from the pre-CBS period are especially prized, and quality concerns during the CBS years damaged the brand. In 1985 a group of employees and investors led by Bill Schultz bought the company back, forming the modern Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. Production centres on Corona, California, and Ensenada, Mexico, with budget instruments sold under the Squier brand. Fender has since acquired or licensed several other brands, including Gretsch, Jackson, and Charvel.

Notable players

Fender guitars have been played by many of the most influential guitarists of the past century, including Buddy Holly, Jimi Hendrix (whose upside-down right-handed Stratocaster became an emblem of the late 1960s), Eric Clapton, David Gilmour, Stevie Ray Vaughan, Keith Richards, Bruce Springsteen, and John Mayer. The Bass VI, Jazzmaster, and Jaguar models found later favour in surf, punk, and alternative rock.
