Hieroglyph

A hieroglyph (from Greek hieroglyphikos, "sacred carving") is a character of the ancient Egyptian writing system, Egyptian hieroglyphs. By extension, the term is applied to the characters of other pictographic or logographic scripts, including Anatolian hieroglyphs, Cretan hieroglyphs, Maya glyphs, and — in older usage — Chinese characters and the symbols of Ojibwe writing.

Egyptian hieroglyphs

The Egyptian system combined logographic, syllabic, and alphabetic elements, with a total of some 1,000 distinct characters in its classical form. Individual signs could function as logograms standing for a whole word, as phonograms representing one, two, or three consonants, or as determinatives silently indicating a word's semantic class. Hieroglyphs were used from around 3200 BC until the end of the fourth century AD, chiefly for monumental and religious inscriptions, while cursive forms — hieratic and later Demotic — served everyday writing.

Decipherment

Knowledge of how to read hieroglyphs was lost in late antiquity, and Renaissance and early modern scholars such as Athanasius Kircher produced fanciful allegorical interpretations. The discovery of the Rosetta Stone in 1799, inscribed with the same decree in hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Greek scripts, provided the key. Building on the work of Thomas Young, Jean-François Champollion announced his decipherment in 1822, demonstrating that the script was largely phonetic.

Other scripts called hieroglyphs

Anatolian hieroglyphs were used in Anatolia and northern Syria to write the Luwian language in the second and early first millennia BC. Cretan hieroglyphs, found on seals and clay documents of Minoan Crete, remain undeciphered. The term is also loosely applied to the glyphs of Maya script, a fully developed logosyllabic writing system of pre-Columbian Mesoamerica, deciphered in the later twentieth century.

Modern use of the word

In modern usage "hieroglyph" and "hieroglyphic" are used metaphorically for anything hard to decipher, and also for graphic symbols generally; in Unicode, the Egyptian Hieroglyphs block encodes over a thousand signs for digital use.
