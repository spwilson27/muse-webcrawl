Provinces of China

Number: 23 (22 administered by the PRC, plus Taiwan claimed)
Largest by population: Guangdong
Largest by area: (autonomous region) Xinjiang; largest province proper: Qinghai

Provinces (Chinese: 省; pinyin: shěng) are the most common type of province-level administrative division in the People's Republic of China. There are 23 units formally designated as provinces, of which the PRC administers 22; the 23rd, Taiwan Province, is claimed by the PRC but administered by the Republic of China.

Government

Each province is led by a provincial government headed by a governor, with real power shared with the provincial secretary of the Chinese Communist Party. Provinces sit below the central government and above prefecture-level cities and counties.

List and history

The provincial system descends from the Yuan dynasty's branch secretariats. Today's provinces include huge and populous units such as Guangdong (over 120 million people), Shandong, Henan, and Sichuan, as well as island Hainan, established as a province and special economic zone in 1988. Province-level units also include four municipalities, five autonomous regions, and two special administrative regions.
