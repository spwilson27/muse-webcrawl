Morphea

Morphea
Other names: Localized scleroderma
Field: Dermatology

Morphea is a form of scleroderma that involves isolated patches of hardened skin on the face, hands, and feet, or anywhere else on the body, with no internal organ involvement. It is also known as localised scleroderma.

Signs and symptoms

Morphea most often presents as macules or plaques a few centimetres in diameter, but may also occur as bands or in other distributions. The skin becomes thickened and bound down due to excess collagen deposition.

Cause and treatment

The cause is unknown, though it is thought to be autoimmune in nature. Mild cases may resolve spontaneously over several years; treatments include topical corticosteroids, phototherapy, and immunosuppressive drugs for severe cases.
