The Grasberg mine (Indonesian: Tambang Grasberg) is the largest gold mine and one of the largest copper mines in the world. It is located in Mimika Regency, Central Papua, Indonesia, near Puncak Jaya, the highest mountain in the country. It employs some 20,000 workers and is operated by PT Freeport Indonesia, a joint venture between Freeport-McMoRan and the Indonesian government.

History

The original Ertsberg ("Ore Mountain") deposit was discovered in 1936 by Dutch geologist Jean Jacques Dozy, and the mine was developed by Freeport from the 1960s, opening in 1972. The far richer Grasberg deposit was found in 1988 and became the main operation. Open-pit mining at Grasberg concluded in 2019–2020, after which production moved to large underground block-cave mines.

Controversy

The mine has long been controversial for its environmental impact—riverine disposal of tailings into the lowland river system—its payments to Indonesian security forces, and its role in grievances among the indigenous Amungme and Kamoro peoples and the Papuan independence movement. In 2018, after protracted negotiations, Indonesia's state-owned holding company Inalum acquired a majority stake in PT Freeport Indonesia.
