Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church

The Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church (German: Kaiser-Wilhelm-Gedächtniskirche) is a Protestant church on Breitscheidplatz in Charlottenburg, Berlin. The original church, built 1891–1895 in the Romanesque Revival style to plans by Franz Schwechten, was commissioned by Wilhelm II in honour of his grandfather Wilhelm I, the first German Kaiser.

War damage and reconstruction

The church was badly damaged in a bombing raid on the night of 23 November 1943. After the war its ruined spire — the "hollow tooth", as Berliners call it — was preserved as a memorial to the horrors of war rather than demolished or fully restored. A modern ensemble designed by Egon Eiermann was built around it between 1959 and 1961, comprising an octagonal concrete-and-glass church, a hexagonal bell tower, a chapel and a foyer building.

The memorial hall

The ground floor of the preserved tower serves as a memorial hall containing mosaics and relics from the old church, including the damaged Christ figure from the altar, and a cross made of nails from the ruins of Coventry Cathedral, itself destroyed by bombing and rebuilt as a symbol of reconciliation.
