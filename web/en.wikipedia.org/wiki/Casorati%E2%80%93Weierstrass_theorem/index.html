Casorati–Weierstrass theorem

The Casorati–Weierstrass theorem in complex analysis describes the behaviour of a holomorphic function near an essential singularity. It states that the image of any punctured neighbourhood of an essential singularity under the function is dense in the complex plane.

FieldComplex analysis
Named afterFelice Casorati, Karl Weierstrass
First stated1868 / 1876

Statement

If f is holomorphic on a punctured neighbourhood of a point z0 and z0 is an essential singularity of f, then for every neighbourhood U of z0 (with z0 removed), the set f(U) is dense in the complex numbers: its closure is the whole plane.

History

The result was published by Felice Casorati in 1868 and independently treated by Karl Weierstrass in 1876. Yulian Sokhotski published related work in 1868, so the theorem is sometimes called the Casorati–Sokhotski–Weierstrass theorem.

Consequences and strengthening

The theorem implies that near an essential singularity a function comes arbitrarily close to every complex value, infinitely often in a suitable sense. It is greatly strengthened by Picard's great theorem, which says the function actually attains every complex value with the possible exception of one. A classic example is exp(1/z) near z = 0. Compare Cauchy's integral formula.
