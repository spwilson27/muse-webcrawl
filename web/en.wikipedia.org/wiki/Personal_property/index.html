Personal property

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the law of property, personal property (also called personalty or moveable property) is property that is not land or attached to land: goods and chattels, money, securities, intellectual property and other intangible rights (“choses in action”). It contrasts with real property (immovables). Personal property may be owned absolutely or in divided interests, transferred by delivery, sale or gift, and used as security (pledges, liens, and in the United States the Article 9 system of the Uniform Commercial Code).

Because personalty is movable and often fungible, its law emphasises commerce: title passes by sale (with rules protecting good-faith purchasers), documents of title circulate in trade, and bankruptcy law ranks creditors' claims over personal assets. Digital assets — cryptocurrencies, in-game items, domain names — have revived classical questions about whether intangibles are property and against whom they are enforceable; courts in several jurisdictions have treated cryptoassets as property for purposes of injunctions and trusts.
