Piazza Navona

Piazza Navona is a public square in Rome, Italy. It is built on the site of the Stadium of Domitian, built in the 1st century AD, and follows the form of the open space of the stadium, an elongated oval. The ancient Romans went there to watch the agones ("games"), and hence it was known as Circus Agonalis ("competition arena"). Over time the name corrupted through in agone and innagone to navona.

The square with the Fountain of the Four Rivers and Sant'Agnese in Agone.

Features

The square is famous for its Baroque art and architecture. At its centre stands Gian Lorenzo Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers (1651), surmounted by an Egyptian obelisk, representing the Danube, Nile, Ganges and Rio de la Plata. At the southern end is the Fountain of the Moor, and at the northern end the Fountain of Neptune. Facing the central fountain is the church of Sant'Agnese in Agone, designed by Francesco Borromini and Girolamo Rainaldi. The Palazzo Pamphilj, also on the square, houses the Brazilian Embassy.

History

After the stadium fell out of use, houses and churches rose over its seating tiers, preserving its outline. From the fifteenth century the square hosted a market (transferred from the Campidoglio), and in the seventeenth century Pope Innocent X Pamphilj rebuilt it as a showcase of his family's palazzo. For centuries in August the square was flooded to stage mock naval spectacles. Today it is filled with cafés, street performers and artists, and hosts a popular Christmas market.
