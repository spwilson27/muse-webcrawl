Type-I superconductor

A type-I superconductor is a superconductor that shows a complete Meissner effect — the total expulsion of magnetic flux from its interior — below a single critical magnetic field, above which superconductivity is abruptly destroyed.

Properties

Type-I behaviour is typical of pure elemental superconductors such as lead, mercury, tin and aluminium. In Ginzburg–Landau theory they are characterised by a Ginzburg–Landau parameter κ < 1/√2, meaning the magnetic penetration depth is smaller than the coherence length and the surface energy between normal and superconducting phases is positive. Their critical fields are generally too low for magnet applications, but they are important in fundamental studies and in devices such as superconducting shields and some SQUID and detector components.
