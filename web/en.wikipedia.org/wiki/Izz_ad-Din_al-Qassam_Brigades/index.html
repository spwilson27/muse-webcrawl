Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades

Emblem of the al-Qassam Brigades, the military wing of Hamas.

The Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades (Arabic: كتائب الشهيد عز الدين القسام, "Battalions of the Martyr Izz ad-Din al-Qassam"), often shortened to the al-Qassam Brigades, is the military wing of the Palestinian Islamist organisation Hamas. It is named after Izz ad-Din al-Qassam, the Syrian-born cleric killed by British forces in 1935.

History

The Brigades were formally created in 1991, growing out of earlier Hamas armed cells active during the First Intifada. During the 1990s and the Second Intifada they carried out numerous attacks on Israeli military and civilian targets, including suicide bombings, and developed the short-range Qassam rocket, named after the Brigades' namesake. After Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and took control of the Gaza Strip in 2007, the Brigades evolved into a more structured, quasi-conventional force with battalions, specialised units, an arsenal of rockets and drones, and an extensive tunnel network.

Organisation and leadership

The Brigades are led by a military council; their best-known commander, Mohammed Deif, headed the organisation for decades and survived repeated Israeli assassination attempts before Israel announced his death in a 2024 airstrike. Figures such as Yahya Ayyash, the bomb-maker killed in 1996, and Ahmed Jabari, killed in 2012, have been prominent in the group's history. The Brigades led the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel that opened the ensuing Gaza war.

Designation

The al-Qassam Brigades are designated a terrorist organisation by several countries; Hamas as a whole is similarly designated by the United States, the European Union, Israel, and others.
