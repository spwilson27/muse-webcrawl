Marshall Harvey Stone (April 8, 1903 – January 9, 1989) was an American mathematician who contributed to real analysis, functional analysis, topology, and the study of Boolean algebras. He is best known for the Stone–Weierstrass theorem and the Stone representation theorem for Boolean algebras.

Career

Stone earned his PhD from Harvard University in 1926 under George David Birkhoff. He taught at Yale and Harvard, and later became the first chairman of the mathematics department at the University of Chicago, which he built into a leading research center.

Contributions

His 1937 paper 'Applications of the theory of Boolean rings to general topology' introduced Stone spaces, in which a Boolean algebra is represented as a ring of sets. The Stone–von Neumann theorem on the uniqueness of representations of the canonical commutation relations underlies quantum mechanics.
