Palmanova

Palmanova is a town in the province of Udine in northeastern Italy, famous as a Renaissance ideal city built as a fortress by the Republic of Venice. Founded on 7 October 1593, it was laid out as a nine-pointed star, with concentric rings of fortifications designed according to the trace italienne principles of bastioned defence.

History

Venice founded Palmanova to defend its eastern frontier against the Ottoman Empire and the Habsburgs after the lessons of the Battle of Lepanto era and growing pressure on Friuli. The fortress was designed under the direction of Vincenzo Scamozzi and the military engineer Giulio Savorgnan. Three monumental gates — Udine, Cividale, and Aquileia — lead into the town, whose streets radiate from a hexagonal central piazza, the Piazza Grande.

Despite its ideal plan, Palmanova was slow to attract civilian settlers, and Venice resorted to pardoning criminals who agreed to live there. Napoleon later strengthened the outer ring of fortifications. In 2017 the fortress was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the transnational site "Venetian Works of Defence between the 16th and 17th centuries".

Layout

The star fort has nine bastions and a surrounding moat and ravelins; a second ring of ramparts and a third Napoleonic ring extend the defences. The town's radial-concentric plan remains almost perfectly preserved and is a celebrated example of Renaissance urban planning theory made real.
