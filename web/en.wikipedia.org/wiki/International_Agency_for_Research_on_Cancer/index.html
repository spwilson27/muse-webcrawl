International Agency for Research on Cancer

The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC; French: Centre International de Recherche sur le Cancer, CIRC) is an intergovernmental agency forming part of the World Health Organization of the United Nations. Its role is to conduct and coordinate research into the causes of cancer. It is headquartered in Lyon, France, and was founded in 1965.

Monographs

IARC is best known for its Monographs on the Identification of Carcinogenic Hazards to Humans, which classify agents, mixtures, and exposures into groups: Group 1 (carcinogenic to humans), Group 2A (probably carcinogenic), Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic), and Group 3 (not classifiable). Classifications such as tobacco smoke, asbestos, processed meat (Group 1, 2015), and glyphosate (Group 2A, 2015) have attracted wide public attention.

Other work

IARC maintains global cancer statistics (GLOBOCAN), publishes the WHO Classification of Tumours ("Blue Books"), and coordinates collaborative cancer research and training, especially in low- and middle-income countries.
