Rahm Israel Emanuel (born November 29, 1959) is an American politician and diplomat who served as the 55th mayor of Chicago from 2011 to 2019. A member of the Democratic Party, he previously served as White House chief of staff to President Barack Obama (2009–2010), as a senior advisor to President Bill Clinton, and in the U.S. House of Representatives, where he chaired the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and the House Democratic Caucus.

Early career

The son of an Israeli immigrant pediatrician, Emanuel studied ballet before earning degrees from Sarah Lawrence College and Northwestern University. He worked in consumer advocacy and Democratic fundraising, and during the 1991 Gulf War volunteered as a civilian mechanic with the Israel Defense Forces. As Clinton's chief fundraiser and later senior advisor, he earned a reputation for intensity summed up in the nickname "Rahmbo."

Congress and the White House

Elected to Congress from Illinois in 2002, Emanuel led the DCCC's successful 2006 campaign that returned the House to Democratic control. As Obama's first chief of staff he helped steer the stimulus act and the Affordable Care Act, and is known for the maxim "You never want a serious crisis to go to waste."

Mayor of Chicago and diplomacy

Emanuel won the 2011 Chicago mayoral election and a 2015 runoff, focusing on finances, education, and infrastructure, but his administration was damaged by the police shooting of Laquan McDonald and the handling of its video. He did not seek a third term. President Joe Biden appointed him ambassador to Japan, a post he held from 2022 to 2025, where he was noted for blunt public criticism of China.
