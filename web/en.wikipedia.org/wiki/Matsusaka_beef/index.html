Matsusaka beef

Matsusaka beef (松阪牛, Matsusaka-gyū) is a premium brand of wagyu beef from black Japanese cattle raised in and around Matsusaka, Mie Prefecture, Japan. It is regarded, together with Kobe beef and Ōmi beef, as one of the three great "sandai wagyu" (三大和牛) brands of Japan, and is known for its intense marbling, tenderness and low melting point of fat.

Production

Matsusaka beef comes from virgin female Tajima-line Kuroge Washu cattle, typically heifers that have never calved. Calves from famous producing regions (often Hyōgo Prefecture) are bought young and raised for an unusually long fattening period — about three years, longer than for most wagyu — on a diet of grains. Traditional accounts hold that the cattle were fed beer to stimulate appetite and massaged, although producers today describe such practices as exceptional or historical.

Strict traceability applies: each animal is individually registered and its meat can be verified against a database by a certificate and nose print. The highest grades command very high prices at auction; the annual Matsusaka beef competition in Mie awards prizes to the finest animals, with champion carcasses selling for record sums.

Characteristics

The meat has a high ratio of unsaturated to saturated fat, with fine "shimofuri" (frost-like) marbling. It is typically served in small portions as sukiyaki, shabu-shabu, or steak, and its fat melts at a temperature below that of the human body.
