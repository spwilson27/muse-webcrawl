Seaworthiness

Seaworthiness is a concept in maritime practice and law describing whether a vessel is fit to withstand the conditions it will encounter at sea and to complete its intended voyage safely. A seaworthy vessel has a sound hull and equipment, is properly crewed, adequately fuelled and provisioned, and is not overloaded.

Factors

Seaworthiness encompasses the vessel's structural condition, stability and watertight integrity; the condition of engines, navigation and safety equipment; the competence and sufficiency of the crew; correct stowage and loading; and suitability for the particular cargo and route. The concept is relative: a vessel may be seaworthy for one voyage but not another.

Legal significance

In marine insurance and the carriage of goods by sea, seaworthiness is a central obligation. Under the Hague-Visby Rules, carriers must exercise due diligence to make a vessel seaworthy before and at the beginning of a voyage. In insurance law, a warranty of seaworthiness historically applied at the commencement of the insured voyage. Unseaworthiness may defeat claims or establish liability following a casualty.

Certification

Flag states and classification societies survey vessels and issue certificates under conventions such as SOLAS and MARPOL attesting to their condition and equipment.
