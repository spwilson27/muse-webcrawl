Overview

Doctor Who is a British science fiction television series broadcast by the BBC since 1963. The programme depicts the adventures of the Doctor, an alien Time Lord from the planet Gallifrey who travels through space and time in the TARDIS — a ship disguised, by a broken camouflage circuit, as a blue British police box — exploring the universe with (usually human) companions and fighting enemies such as the Daleks, Cybermen and the Master.

Regeneration and history

When the first Doctor, William Hartnell, left for health reasons in 1966, the writers invented regeneration — the Doctor's ability to change body and personality — which has allowed the role to be recast ever since and underpins the show's longevity. The original run ceased in 1989; after a 1996 TV-movie attempt, Russell T Davies revived the series in 2005 to great success ("New Who"), with later showrunners Steven Moffat and Chris Chibnall; Davies returned in 2023 for the 60th anniversary and Disney+ era. Doctor Who holds the Guinness record for the longest-running science-fiction TV series and is a cornerstone of British popular culture — Daleks, the sonic screwdriver and the TARDIS are national icons.
