Karst Plateau

The Karst Plateau (Slovene: Kras; Italian: Carso; German: Karst) is a limestone plateau in southwestern Slovenia and northeastern Italy, extending from the Vipava Valley and the Gulf of Trieste inland toward the Dinaric Alps. It is the original region after which karst topography — landscapes formed by the dissolution of soluble rocks — is named in many languages.

Geography

The plateau covers about 550 square kilometres across the border region. It is bounded by the Vipava Valley to the north, the hills around Trieste to the west, and the Brkini Hills and the Čičarija ridge to the south. The surface is dotted with dolines (sinkholes), dry valleys, caves and disappearing streams; there are almost no permanent surface watercourses, as rainwater sinks rapidly into the fractured limestone.

Notable caves in the Slovenian Karst include the Škocjan Caves, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and Postojna Cave; on the Italian side lies the Grotta Gigante, one of the largest show caves in the world. The plateau is also the homeland of the Lipizzaner horse, bred at the Lipica stud farm since 1580.

Climate and culture

The Karst is known for the bora (burja), a cold, violent northeasterly wind that descends from the plateau toward the Adriatic. The region's dry stone architecture, teran wine (made from Refošk grapes grown in the red terra rossa soil), and Karst prosciutto (kraški pršut) are characteristic products of the area.

History

The Karst was heavily contested in the First World War, when the plateau formed part of the Isonzo Front between Italy and Austria-Hungary. After 1945 the region was divided between Italy and Yugoslavia, and today it is shared between Italy and Slovenia.
