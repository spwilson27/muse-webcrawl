The National Science Foundation (NSF) is an independent agency of the United States federal government that supports fundamental research and education in all the non-medical fields of science and engineering. Its medical counterpart is the National Institutes of Health.

Mission and budget

With an annual budget of roughly nine to eleven billion dollars in recent years, the NSF funds approximately a quarter of all federally supported basic research conducted by America's colleges and universities, in fields such as mathematics, computer science, biology, geology, economics and the social sciences.

History

The NSF was created by the National Science Foundation Act of 1950, growing out of wartime experience with the Office of Scientific Research and Development and Vannevar Bush's report Science, the Endless Frontier. Its remit expanded after Sputnik to include science education and major facilities.

Organization

The agency is led by a director and a 24-member National Science Board appointed by the President. It is organized into directorates for each major field, plus offices overseeing polar programmes — including the U.S. Antarctic Program — major telescopes, oceanographic vessels and supercomputing centres.
