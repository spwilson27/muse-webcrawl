London Zoo is the world's oldest scientific zoo. It was opened in Regent's Park on 27 April 1828 by the Zoological Society of London (ZSL), founded by Sir Stamford Raffles, initially as a collection for scientific study; it was opened to the public in 1847.

History and attractions

The zoo pioneered many features later standard elsewhere: it opened the first reptile house in 1849, the first public aquarium in 1853 (which popularised the word "aquarium"), the first insect house in 1881, and the first children's zoo in 1938. Famous residents have included Jumbo the elephant, Guy the gorilla, and Winnie, a black bear whose visits by A. A. Milne and his son inspired Winnie-the-Pooh.

Notable modern exhibits include Gorilla Kingdom, Penguin Beach, the Land of the Lions, and the Blackburn Pavilion. The zoo's architecture is notable in its own right, with Grade I listed structures such as Berthold Lubetkin's Penguin Pool (1934). ZSL also runs Whipsnade Zoo and carries out conservation work worldwide.
