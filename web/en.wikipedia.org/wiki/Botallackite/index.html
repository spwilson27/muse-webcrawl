Botallackite
Copper chloride hydroxide mineral
Formula: Cu2Cl(OH)3
Type locality: Botallack mine, Cornwall

Botallackite is a rare copper mineral with formula Cu2Cl(OH)3, a copper hydroxide chloride. It is a polymorph of atacamite, paratacamite, and clinoatacamite, sharing their composition but differing in crystal structure. Botallackite crystallises in the monoclinic system, forming pale green crusts and small crystals.

Occurrence

The mineral was first described in 1865 from specimens collected at the Botallack mine near St Just in Cornwall, England, from which it takes its name. Like its polymorphs it is a secondary mineral formed by the alteration of copper ores, and is notable for forming in submarine settings and through the corrosion of copper and bronze exposed to seawater — it appears, for example, in the alteration crusts of shipwreck artefacts.

Properties

Botallackite is the least stable of the four Cu2Cl(OH)3 polymorphs under ordinary conditions; on exposure to air and moisture it commonly recrystallises as atacamite or paratacamite, which makes well-preserved specimens uncommon.
