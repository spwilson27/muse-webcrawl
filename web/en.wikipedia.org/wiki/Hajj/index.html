The Hajj is an annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, Saudi Arabia, the holiest city for Muslims, mandatory once in a lifetime for every physically and financially capable Muslim. It is one of the Five Pillars of Islam and takes place from the 8th to the 13th of Dhu al-Hijjah, the last month of the Islamic calendar, drawing some two to three million pilgrims in recent normal years (numbers vary).

Rites commemorate Abraham (Ibrahim), Hagar and Ishmael: circling the Kaaba (tawaf), walking between Safa and Marwah hills (sa'i, recalling Hagar's search for water), the Day of Arafat (standing in prayer on the plain, the Hajj's climax), stoning pillars at Mina (ramy al-jamarat), animal sacrifice at Eid al-Adha, and shaving/cutting the hair. Pilgrims wear simple white ihram garments, symbolising equality and purity. Managing the world's largest annual gathering is a huge logistical undertaking; crowd disasters (notably 1990 and 2015) led to major redesigns.
