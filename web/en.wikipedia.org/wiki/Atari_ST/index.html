The Atari ST is a line of personal computers from Atari Corporation and the successor to the company's 8-bit home computers. The initial model, the Atari 520ST, had limited release in April–June 1985, and was widely available in July. It was the first personal computer with a bitmapped colour graphical user interface, using a version of Digital Research's GEM environment from February 1985 in ROM. The "ST" officially stands for "Sixteen/Thirty-two", referring to the Motorola 68000's 16-bit external bus and 32-bit internals.

The ST was sold with either Atari's colour monitor or a less expensive monochrome monitor. The ST's colour palette and high-resolution monochrome mode made it a strong platform for desktop publishing and CAD, while its built-in MIDI ports made it a favourite of musicians and recording studios — a niche it held for years. It competed directly with Commodore's Amiga and, in some markets, with Apple's Macintosh and IBM PC compatibles.

History

After Jack Tramiel left Commodore and acquired Atari's consumer division in 1984, his team developed the ST in about five months, previewing it at the Consumer Electronics Show in January 1985. Later models included the 1040ST (1986), the Mega ST, the STE series (1989) with enhanced graphics and sound, and finally the TT030 and Falcon030 machines of the early 1990s.

Music use

Because every ST shipped with MIDI-in and MIDI-out ports, sequencing software such as Steinberg's Cubase and C-Lab's Notator made the ST a standard tool in professional and home studios; many musicians continued using STs long after the platform's commercial decline.
