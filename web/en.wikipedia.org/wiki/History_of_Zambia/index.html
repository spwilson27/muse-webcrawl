History of Zambia

The history of Zambia spans from early human settlement — the region holds some of Africa's oldest archaeological sites — through Bantu migrations, pre-colonial kingdoms, British rule as Northern Rhodesia, and independence in 1964 under Kenneth Kaunda.

Key dates1890s British South Africa Company rule; 1924 Crown colony of Northern Rhodesia; 1953–63 Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland; 24 Oct 1964 independence
First presidentKenneth Kaunda (1964–1991)
CapitalLusaka (from 1935)

Pre-colonial and colonial eras

Bantu-speaking peoples established states such as the Lunda and Lozi (Barotse) kingdoms. Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company administered the territory from the 1890s; copper mining on the Copperbelt transformed the economy after the 1920s. Africans resisted the white-minority Central African Federation (1953–63).

Independence

Kaunda's UNIP won elections and Zambia became independent on 24 October 1964. Kaunda's 'Zambian humanism' and one-party state (from 1972) governed until economic decline and protest brought multiparty elections in 1991, won by Frederick Chiluba's MMD. Zambia has since held regular, mostly peaceful transfers of power.

Modern era

Copper dependence, debt, and the HIV/AIDS epidemic shaped recent decades; presidents have included Levy Mwanawasa, Rupiah Banda, Michael Sata, Edgar Lungu, and Hakainde Hichilema (from 2021).
