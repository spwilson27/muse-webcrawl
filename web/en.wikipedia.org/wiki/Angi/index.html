Anji County (Chinese: 安吉县; pinyin: Ānjí Xiàn) is a county in the prefecture-level city of Huzhou, in northwestern Zhejiang province, China. The name, bestowed in 185 AD, derives from a line in the Book of Songs meaning "peaceful and auspicious". Anji is famed across China for its vast bamboo groves and its production of Anji white tea.

Geography and environment

Covering about 1,886 square kilometres of hilly terrain in the Tianmu mountain region, Anji is one of the most forested counties in the Yangtze River Delta, with bamboo forests occupying a large share of its land. The county's "China Bamboo Sea" was a filming location for the Oscar-winning film Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000). Anji has been promoted as a model of ecological development under the slogan "lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets", a phrase associated with a 2005 visit by then-provincial leader Xi Jinping to Yucun village in the county.

Economy

Local industries include bamboo products, eco-tourism, and the celebrated Anji white tea (a green tea whose young leaves are pale and low in chlorophyll). The county has received national and UN recognition for rural revitalization and sustainable development.
