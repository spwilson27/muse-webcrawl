Nikolai Bukharin

Nikolai Ivanovich Bukharin (9 October 1888 – 15 March 1938) was a Russian revolutionary, Soviet politician, Marxist philosopher and economist, and the author of numerous theoretical works. A leading Bolshevik, he edited the party newspaper Pravda, led the Communist International (Comintern), and was the chief architect and defender of the New Economic Policy (NEP). After opposing Joseph Stalin's forced collectivisation, he was removed from power, arrested, and executed after the most famous of the Moscow show trials.

Early life and revolution

Bukharin was born in Moscow to schoolteacher parents. He joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1906 while a student at Moscow University and became a leading Bolshevik organiser, spending years in exile in Europe and the United States. In exile he wrote Imperialism and World Economy, which influenced Lenin. Returning to Russia in 1917, he played a prominent role in the October Revolution and edited Pravda.

Theorist of the party

With Yevgeni Preobrazhensky he co-authored The ABC of Communism (1919), the most popular exposition of Bolshevik doctrine. His Economics of the Transformation Period (1920) defended War Communism, but he then became the leading advocate of Lenin's market-oriented New Economic Policy, urging peasants to "enrich themselves" and arguing for gradual, balanced growth — "socialism at a snail's pace", as his critics mocked.

Power struggle and fall

After Lenin's death in 1924, Bukharin allied with Stalin against Trotsky and then against the Left Opposition of Zinoviev and Kamenev. When Stalin broke with the NEP in 1928 and launched forced collectivisation and crash industrialisation, Bukharin led the so-called Right Opposition. Defeated, he was stripped of his posts by 1929–1930, though he later edited the newspaper Izvestia and helped draft the 1936 Soviet Constitution.

Trial and execution

Arrested in February 1937 during the Great Purge, Bukharin was the star defendant of the Trial of the Twenty-One in March 1938, accused of leading a "Bloc of Rightists and Trotskyites" conspiring with foreign powers. He confessed to general political responsibility while denying many specific charges of espionage and murder — a performance some observers read as a coded repudiation. He was shot on 15 March 1938. His final plea to future generations of party leaders, memorised by his wife Anna Larina, asked them to clear his name. Bukharin was posthumously rehabilitated by the Soviet Supreme Court in 1988 during Mikhail Gorbachev's glasnost.
