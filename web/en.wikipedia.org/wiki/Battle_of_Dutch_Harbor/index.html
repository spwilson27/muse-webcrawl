The Battle of Dutch Harbor was a Japanese carrier air attack on the American naval and army base at Dutch Harbor on Unalaska Island in the Aleutians on 3–4 June 1942, at the opening of the Aleutian Islands Campaign of the Second World War. It was one of only a few attacks on the continental United States during the war.

Background

Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto's plan for the Midway operation included a northern thrust: Rear Admiral Kakuji Kakuta's Second Carrier Division, with the carriers Ryūjō and Jun'yō, was to strike Dutch Harbor and cover landings on Kiska and Attu, partly to extend Japan's defensive perimeter and partly as a diversion.

The attacks

On the morning of 3 June, aircraft from Ryūjō attacked through poor weather, bombing radio stations, fuel tanks and barracks and killing about 25 servicemen; a second strike from Jun'yō was largely turned back by weather. On 4 June a heavier combined strike hit the base again, destroying oil storage tanks, a hospital and the barracks ship Northwestern, and killing about 18 more. American anti-aircraft fire and fighters from Umnak shot down several Japanese aircraft; one Zero fighter crash-landed on Akutan Island and was recovered almost intact — a major intelligence prize that informed the design of tactics and the F6F Hellcat.

While Dutch Harbor burned, the decisive Battle of Midway was unfolding far to the south. The Japanese occupied Attu and Kiska unopposed on 6–7 June, beginning an occupation of American soil that lasted until 1943.
