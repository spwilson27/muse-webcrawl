Demiurge

The Demiurge (Greek: δημιουργός, dēmiourgós, "craftsman" or "artisan") is a figure in Platonic and Gnostic philosophy: an artisan-like deity responsible for fashioning and maintaining the physical universe. In Plato's dialogue Timaeus, the Demiurge is a benevolent craftsman who shapes pre-existing chaotic matter according to the eternal Forms, producing the best possible cosmos.

In Platonism

Plato presents the Demiurge as good and ungrudging, desiring that all things should be as like himself as possible. Unlike the creator of later monotheism, he does not create matter from nothing but orders what already exists, working against the constraints of "Necessity". Later Platonists and Neoplatonists debated how literally to take the account.

In Gnosticism

In many Gnostic systems the Demiurge is a very different figure: an ignorant or malevolent lesser god, sometimes named Yaldabaoth, who creates the material world in error or malice and mistakenly believes himself to be the only god, while the true, transcendent God remains beyond his creation. This dualistic view explained the presence of evil and suffering in the world.
