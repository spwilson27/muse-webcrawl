Hair (musical)

Hair: The American Tribal Love-Rock Musical is a rock musical with a book and lyrics by Gerome Ragni and James Rado and music by Galt MacDermot. A product of the hippie counterculture and sexual revolution of the late 1960s, it premiered Off-Broadway in 1967 and on Broadway in 1968, becoming a landmark of musical theatre and a defining statement of anti–Vietnam War protest.

Plot

The loosely structured show follows a "Tribe" of young hippies in New York City. Its central figure, Claude, a young man from Oklahoma, is torn between his pacifist beliefs and the pressure to comply with his draft notice for the Vietnam War. Around him, Berger, Sheila, and the other Tribe members sing about race, sexuality, drugs, and peace, culminating in Claude's death in Vietnam and the finale "Let the Sunshine In."

Songs

Several songs became major pop hits, including "Aquarius" and "Let the Sunshine In" (combined by The 5th Dimension into a number-one single in 1969), "Hair," "Easy to Be Hard," and "Good Morning Starshine."

Impact and controversy

The show's nudity (a brief nude scene at the end of Act I), profanity, drug references, and treatment of the American flag provoked censorship battles and court cases, including a Supreme Court case over a production in Chattanooga. Hair ran 1,750 performances on Broadway, spawned productions worldwide, and was adapted into a 1979 film directed by Miloš Forman. A 2009 Broadway revival won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Musical.
