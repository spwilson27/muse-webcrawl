Deep Learning Super Sampling

Deep Learning Super Sampling (DLSS) is a family of real-time image upscaling and anti-aliasing technologies developed by Nvidia for use in video games. DLSS renders the game at a lower resolution and then uses a deep learning neural network, running on the Tensor Cores of Nvidia's RTX graphics cards, to reconstruct a higher-resolution image. The aim is to substantially increase frame rates while producing image quality comparable to, or in some respects better than, native rendering.

History and versions

DLSS was announced with the GeForce RTX 20 series (Turing) in 2018 and launched in early 2019. The first version used a per-game-trained network and produced mixed results. DLSS 2.0, released in March 2020, replaced the per-game model with a single general network and combined upscaling with temporal anti-aliasing, using motion vectors and previous frames; it offered quality modes (Quality, Balanced, Performance, Ultra Performance) trading internal resolution against speed, and was widely praised.

DLSS 3 (2022), for the RTX 40 series, added AI Frame Generation, which inserts entirely generated frames between rendered ones, together with the Reflex low-latency system. DLSS 3.5 (2023) added Ray Reconstruction, a neural denoiser for ray-traced lighting. DLSS 4, announced with the RTX 50 series in 2025, moved the AI models to a transformer architecture and introduced Multi Frame Generation on the new hardware. A related feature, Deep Learning Anti-Aliasing (DLAA), applies the same network at native resolution without upscaling.

Technology

DLSS takes as inputs the low-resolution rendered frame, depth and motion vector data, and temporally accumulated history. The neural network is trained on Nvidia supercomputers using very high resolution reference renders. On supported hardware the network runs in a few milliseconds per frame. Because the reconstruction is temporal, fast motion, disoccluded objects, and fine detail such as fences, hair, and text can show artefacts, though successive versions have reduced these.

Reception and competition

DLSS helped establish AI upscaling as a standard feature of PC and console gaming. Competing technologies include AMD's FidelityFX Super Resolution (FSR) and Intel's XeSS, and Sony's PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution. Support for DLSS has been added to hundreds of games, as well as to game engines such as Unreal Engine and Unity through official plug-ins.
