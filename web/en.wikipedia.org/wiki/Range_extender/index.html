Range extender

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A range extender is an auxiliary power unit in an electric vehicle that extends the vehicle's range beyond what its battery alone provides. Most commonly it is a small internal combustion engine that drives a generator to recharge the battery or supply power to the electric motor once the battery is depleted; the wheels are still driven electrically, making such vehicles a type of series hybrid electric vehicle.

Operation

In a range-extended electric vehicle (REEV or EREV), the engine does not mechanically drive the wheels. The vehicle runs purely on battery power until the charge reaches a threshold, after which the engine-generator starts and maintains the battery at a minimum level, allowing continued driving and refuelling at ordinary petrol stations. This permits a smaller, cheaper battery than a pure battery-electric vehicle of comparable total range, while most daily journeys can still be completed electrically, often from grid charging as a plug-in hybrid.

Examples

Well-known examples include the BMW i3 with Range Extender, which used a small two-cylinder scooter engine, early versions of the Chevrolet Volt concept (marketed as a range-extended EV, though its drivetrain can also engage the engine mechanically), the Fisker Karma, and Mazda's MX-30 R-EV, which uses a small rotary engine as a generator. Range extenders have also been explored using fuel cells and micro-turbines. In wireless networking, the term "range extender" also refers to a wireless repeater that rebroadcasts a Wi-Fi signal to cover a larger area.
