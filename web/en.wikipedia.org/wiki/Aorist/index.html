The aorist (from Ancient Greek ἀόριστος, "undefined") is a grammatical form found in certain languages, most famously Ancient Greek and Sanskrit. It typically expresses an action as a simple, complete, undifferentiated whole — a perfective viewpoint — without reference to its internal duration or structure. In Ancient Greek, the aorist functions both as an aspect and, in the indicative mood, as the usual narrative past tense.

In Ancient Greek

The Greek aorist is built on a dedicated verb stem, often with its own endings and, in the indicative, an augment. Outside the indicative it is purely aspectual: the aorist infinitive or imperative presents the act as a single event. Types include the sigmatic ("first") aorist, the thematic ("second") aorist, and root aorists. Greek grammarians debate distinctions such as the constative, ingressive and effective uses of the form.

In other languages

In Sanskrit the aorist is one of the past-tense systems of the verb, largely supplanted in later stages by other forms. In Slavic linguistics, the aorist survives as a simple past tense in Bulgarian and Macedonian, and archaically in Serbian and Croatian. In Turkish grammar the term is sometimes applied to the habitual "geniş zaman" form, though its function — expressing general truths and habits — differs from the classical perfective sense.
