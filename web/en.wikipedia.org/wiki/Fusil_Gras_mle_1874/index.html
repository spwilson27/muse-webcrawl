Fusil Gras mle 1874

The Fusil Modèle 1874, commonly known as the Gras rifle, was the standard infantry rifle of the French Army from 1874 until 1886, when it was replaced by the Lebel Model 1886. Designed by Colonel Basile Gras, it was a single-shot bolt-action rifle chambered for the 11×59mmR metallic cartridge, a significant advance over the paper-cartridge Chassepot rifle it replaced.

History

The Gras was adapted from the Chassepot design, modified to fire centerfire brass cartridges. Some 400,000 Chassepot rifles were converted to the Gras standard (Modèle 1866/74). The rifle saw service in the French colonial campaigns and was exported widely; Greece adopted it as a standard arm, and Gras rifles were used extensively in the First World War by second-line and colonial troops, and by Russian forces after emergency purchases.

Design

The rifle fired a 11 mm black-powder cartridge with a lead bullet of about 25 grams at a muzzle velocity of roughly 455 m/s. It remained the basis for later developments, and its cartridge case served as the parent for the 8mm Lebel round.
