Toyotama-hime (Japanese: 豊玉姫, "Lady Bountiful Jewel") is a goddess in Japanese mythology, a princess of the sea and daughter of the sea god Watatsumi. She appears in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki in the myth of the hunter Hoori, also known as Hohodemi or Yamasachihiko.

Myth

Hoori travelled to the undersea palace of Watatsumi (Ryūgū) searching for a fish-hook he had lost and borrowed from his brother Hoderi. There he met Toyotama-hime and married her, living in the palace for three years. When Hoori longed to return to the land, Watatsumi gave him the tide jewels — the jewel of the ebbing tide and the jewel of the flowing tide — with which he could subdue his brother.

Toyotama-hime followed Hoori to the shore to give birth, asking him not to watch her during labour, for she would take her true form. Hoori broke the taboo and saw that she had become a great wani (sea creature, often rendered as a shark or dragon). Ashamed, she returned to the sea, but sent her younger sister Tamayori-hime to help raise the child, Ugayafukiaezu, who became the father of Emperor Jimmu, Japan's legendary first emperor. The story is a classic example of the supernatural-wife tale type found throughout East and Southeast Asian folklore.

Worship

Toyotama-hime is enshrined at several shrines, including Watatsumi Shrine on Tsushima and sites associated with the Ryūgū legend in Kagoshima Prefecture. Under the name Otohime she is a familiar figure in the folktale of Urashima Tarō, though the two traditions are distinct in the early texts.
