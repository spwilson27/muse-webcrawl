Na-Dené languages

The Na-Dené languages are a family of Indigenous languages of North America, spoken in Alaska, western Canada and the southwestern United States. The family comprises the Athabaskan languages, Eyak, and Tlingit; Haida was formerly included but is now generally excluded.

Classification

The Athabaskan branch includes Northern Athabaskan languages such as Dene Suline and Gwich'in, Pacific Coast languages, and the Southern Athabaskan (Apachean) languages, including Navajo — the most widely spoken Indigenous language north of Mexico.

Characteristics

Na-Dené languages are known for their complex tonal and consonant systems and highly intricate verb morphology, with long prefixes encoding subject, object, mode and aspect.
