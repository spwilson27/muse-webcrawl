Ormen Lange (gas field)

The Nyhamna terminal, which processes gas from Ormen Lange.

Ormen Lange is a natural gas field on the Norwegian continental shelf in the Norwegian Sea, about 120 kilometres northwest of Kristiansund. Discovered in 1997, it was Norway's second-largest gas field, with initial recoverable reserves of around 400 billion cubic metres of natural gas.

Development

Because the field lies in water 800–1,100 metres deep, on a seabed scarred by the ancient Storegga Slide, no surface platform was built: wells are produced through subsea templates tied back by pipeline to an onshore processing plant at Nyhamna, near Molde. From Nyhamna, gas is exported through the Langeled pipeline — at about 1,166 kilometres, for a time the world's longest subsea pipeline — to Easington in England, supplying a significant share (at peak around 20 per cent) of the United Kingdom's gas demand. Production began in October 2007. The field was initially operated by Norsk Hydro, with operatorship passing to Shell in December 2007; partners include Petoro, Equinor, DONG Energy (later Ørsted/Ineos) and ExxonMobil.

Name

The field is named after Ormen Lange ("the Long Serpent"), the famous longship of the Norwegian king Olaf Tryggvason.
