Vassar College is a private liberal arts college in Poughkeepsie, New York, United States. Founded in 1861 by the brewer and philanthropist Matthew Vassar, it was the first degree-granting institution of higher education for women in the United States and became a founding member of the Seven Sisters group of elite women's colleges. It has been coeducational since 1969.

History

Matthew Vassar, an English-born brewer who had prospered in Poughkeepsie, endowed the college with about $408,000 and 200 acres, guided by his niece Lydia Booth and the educator Milo Parker Jewett, who became its first president. Vassar Female College opened in September 1865 with 353 students; the word "Female" was dropped in 1867. Maria Mitchell, the noted astronomer, was among the founding faculty, and her observatory made the college a centre of early women's science education.

Academics and campus

Vassar offers the Bachelor of Arts in more than 50 majors under an open curriculum with no core distribution requirements beyond a first-year writing seminar, quantitative reasoning and foreign-language proficiency. The college is known for small classes, a low student–faculty ratio, and strong programmes in the arts and humanities. Its 1,000-acre campus includes an arboretum, the Frances Lehman Loeb Art Center, and the Main Building (1865), a National Historic Landmark that once housed the entire college under one roof.

Student life and notable alumni

Nearly all students live in the college's residence houses, and the college fields Division III teams (the Brewers). Alumnae and alumni include the poet Edna St. Vincent Millay, the astronomer Maria Mitchell's successors in science, First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis (who attended for two years), actress Meryl Streep, and writer and activist Grace Hopper, the pioneering computer scientist.
