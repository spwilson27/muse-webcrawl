European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System

Image: European higher education (placeholder)

The European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) is a standard means for comparing academic credits in higher education across Europe. Developed by the European Commission from 1989, initially for the Erasmus exchange programme, it became a central element of the Bologna Process and the European Higher Education Area.

How it works

ECTS credits express the volume of learning based on defined learning outcomes and their associated workload. Sixty credits represent the workload of one full-time academic year, so one credit typically corresponds to 25–30 hours of student work, including lectures, seminars, and private study. A bachelor's degree usually carries 180–240 credits and a master's degree 60–120 credits.

Purpose

The system is designed to make study programmes transparent, to facilitate the recognition of qualifications, and to support student mobility between institutions and countries. It is accompanied by a grading scale and standard documents such as the Learning Agreement and the Transcript of Records.
