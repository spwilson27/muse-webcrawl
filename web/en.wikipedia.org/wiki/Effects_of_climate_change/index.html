Effects of climate change

The effects of climate change span the physical environment, ecosystems and human societies. They include the warming of the atmosphere and oceans, shrinking ice sheets and glaciers, rising sea levels, ocean acidification, and changes in precipitation and extreme weather, driven primarily by human emissions of greenhouse gases since the Industrial Revolution. The global surface temperature has risen by roughly 1.2 °C since the late 19th century, with the rate of warming fastest in the Arctic.

Physical changes

Observed changes include the retreat of most mountain glaciers worldwide, loss of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets, declining Arctic sea ice, and global mean sea-level rise of about 20 cm since 1900, accelerating in recent decades. The oceans have absorbed most of the excess heat and about a quarter of anthropogenic carbon dioxide, making surface waters measurably more acidic.

Weather and extremes

Warming increases the frequency and intensity of heatwaves and heavy precipitation in many regions, intensifies drought in others, and raises the proportion of the most intense tropical cyclones. Attribution science now routinely quantifies how much climate change has altered the likelihood of specific events.

Ecosystems and food

Species are shifting their ranges poleward and to higher elevations; coral reefs have suffered repeated mass bleaching events. Climate change affects agriculture through heat stress, shifting rainfall and pests, with yields of major staples already affected in many low-latitude regions, threatening food security for vulnerable populations.

Human health and society

Effects on people include heat-related illness and death, spread of some vector-borne diseases, flooding and displacement, water scarcity, and economic losses. The IPCC concludes that risks escalate with every increment of warming, and that limiting warming to 1.5 °C — the aspirational goal of the Paris Agreement — would substantially reduce harm compared with 2 °C or higher. Responses divide into mitigation (reducing emissions) and adaptation (adjusting to impacts).
