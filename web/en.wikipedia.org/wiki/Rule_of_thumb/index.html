Rule of thumb

A rule of thumb is a principle or guideline that is broadly accurate but not intended to be strictly correct in every situation. Rules of thumb are typically derived from experience and practical observation rather than from scientific measurement or formal proof, and they serve as easily remembered approximations for making everyday decisions.

Origin of the term

The phrase is generally understood to refer to approximations made using the thumb as a rough unit of measurement, since the width of a thumb approximates an inch in some traditional systems. A popular but unsupported folk etymology claims the phrase comes from an English law supposedly permitting a man to beat his wife with a stick no thicker than his thumb; historians have found no basis for this claim, and it is regarded as a myth.

Characteristics

Rules of thumb are valued for their simplicity and speed. They trade precision for convenience, and they are common in cooking, carpentry, finance, computing, and medicine. A well-known example is the guideline that a cook can estimate whether water is deep enough by comparing it to the first joint of a finger. In finance, the "rule of 72" estimates how long an investment takes to double by dividing 72 by the annual rate of return.
