Rome Prize

The Rome Prize is an American award made annually by the American Academy in Rome through a national competition. It supports advanced independent work and research in the arts and humanities by providing winners with a stipend, room and board, and a studio or study space at the Academy's campus on the Janiculum Hill in Rome, Italy, for periods typically of six months to two years (most commonly eleven months).

Fields

Roughly thirty fellowships are awarded each year across two broad areas. In the arts: architecture, design, historic preservation and conservation, landscape architecture, literature, musical composition and visual arts. In the humanities: ancient studies, medieval studies, Renaissance and early modern studies, and modern Italian studies. Applicants must be United States citizens (with some categories open to long-term residents), and selection is made by independent juries of peers.

History

The American Academy in Rome was founded in 1894 as a centre for American artists and scholars, modelled on the French Academy's Prix de Rome tradition; its first fellowship competition followed in the mid-1890s. The Academy merged the American School of Classical Studies with the School of Fine Arts, uniting humanities and arts under one institution. The McKim, Mead & White Building, completed in 1914, houses the community, whose shared meals and interdisciplinary life are considered a defining feature of the fellowship.

Notable fellows

Rome Prize winners have included composer Aaron Copland, architect Louis Kahn, writer Ralph Ellison, classicist Lily Ross Taylor, composer Samuel Barber and artist Philip Guston. The prize is regarded as one of the most prestigious American fellowships for artists and humanities scholars.
