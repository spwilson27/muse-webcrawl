LocationMaria-Theresien-Platz, Vienna, Austria
Opened1891
CollectionHabsburg imperial collections

The Kunsthistorisches Museum Wien (Museum of Art History) in Vienna is one of the world's foremost fine-art museums, housing the Habsburg dynasty's collections.

Building and collections

Commissioned by Emperor Franz Joseph I for the Ringstrasse, the palatial building by Gottfried Semper and Karl von Hasenauer opened in 1891, facing its twin, the Natural History Museum. Highlights include the world's largest Bruegel collection, works by Vermeer (The Art of Painting), Raphael, Rembrandt, Caravaggio and Arcimboldo, the Kunstkammer, Egyptian and Near Eastern antiquities, Greek and Roman art, and a major coin cabinet. The Picture Gallery's opulent interior features a café under the central dome.

Satellites

Associated collections include the Imperial Treasury, the Ephesos Museum, and collections at Schloss Ambras in Innsbruck and the Theseus Temple.
