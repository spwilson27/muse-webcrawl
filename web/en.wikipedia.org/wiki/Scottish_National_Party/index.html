The Scottish National Party (SNP; Scottish Gaelic: Pàrtaidh Nàiseanta na h-Alba) is a Scottish nationalist and social democratic political party. The party holds that Scotland should become an independent country within the European Union. Formed in 1934 by the merger of the National Party of Scotland and the Scottish Party, the SNP has been the governing party of the devolved Scottish Parliament since 2007.

History

The SNP's breakthrough came when Winnie Ewing won the 1967 Hamilton by-election. The party surged in the 1970s on the "It's Scotland's Oil" campaign, winning 11 Westminster seats in October 1974. After the failed 1979 devolution referendum, the party regrouped under Alex Salmond in the 1990s. In 2007 the SNP formed a minority government at Holyrood; in 2011 it won an outright majority, enabling the 2014 independence referendum, in which 55% voted No to independence.

In the 2015 UK general election, the SNP won 56 of Scotland's 59 Westminster seats. Under Nicola Sturgeon (2014–2023) the party continued in government in Edinburgh, though its Westminster representation collapsed in the 2024 general election, when it fell to single figures while Labour resurged in Scotland. John Swinney became leader and First Minister in May 2024.

Policies

The party's central aim is Scottish independence. Domestically it has pursued social democratic policies in devolved areas, including free university tuition for Scottish students, free prescriptions, the Scottish Child Payment, and opposition to the UK nuclear deterrent (Trident) based at Faslane. The SNP supports Scotland rejoining the European Union as an independent state.
