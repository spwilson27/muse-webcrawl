Satakunta

Country: Finland
Capital: Pori
Area: approx. 8,400 km²
Population: approx. 210,000

River landscape in Satakunta.

Satakunta (Swedish: Satakunda) is a region of western Finland, on the coast of the Gulf of Bothnia. Its capital and largest city is Pori. Satakunta is one of Finland's historical provinces and a modern administrative region, and its name derives from the Finnish for "hundred" (sata), referring to an old administrative division.

Geography

The region is characterised by the Kokemäenjoki river, one of Finland's largest river systems, which flows through the region to the sea at Pori. The coastline is marked by rapid land uplift following the last ice age, a phenomenon especially visible in the delta of the Kokemäenjoki and in the Rauma archipelago. The port town of Rauma, with its wooden old town, Vanha Rauma, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lies in the south of the region.

History and culture

Satakunta was settled early and contains Bronze Age burial sites such as the Sammallahdenmäki cairns, also a World Heritage Site. The region is known in Finland for its distinctive dialect, its food culture — including the lamprey delicacies of the Kokemäenjoki — and the annual Pori Jazz festival, one of Europe's best-known jazz events.

Municipalities

The region comprises municipalities including Pori, Rauma, Ulvila, Kankaanpää, Huittinen, Kokemäki, Harjavalta, Eura, Eurajoki, Nakkila, Pomarkku, Merikarvia, Karvia, Jämijärvi, Luvia and Säkylä.
