Pont-Aven School

The Pont-Aven School (French: École de Pont-Aven) was a group of artists who worked in and around the village of Pont-Aven in Brittany, France, in the late 19th century. The term is most closely associated with Paul Gauguin and his followers, who developed the Synthetist style there in the 1880s and 1890s.

History

Artists had visited Pont-Aven from the 1860s, attracted by Brittany's low cost of living, picturesque scenery, and traditional culture. In 1888 Gauguin worked with Émile Bernard, whose cloisonnism — flat areas of bold colour separated by dark outlines — influenced Gauguin's landmark painting Vision of the Sermon (1888).

Style

The Pont-Aven artists rejected naturalism and Impressionist detail in favour of symbolic content, simplified forms, vivid non-naturalistic colour, and expressive outlines, an approach Gauguin called Synthetism. Members and associates included Paul Sérusier, whose painting The Talisman (1888) inspired the Nabis, as well as Meyer de Haan, Jan Verkade, and others.

Legacy

The Pont-Aven School is regarded as a bridge between Impressionism and modern art, and its ideas spread through the Nabis and Symbolist movements across Europe.
