F-1 visa

In the United States, the F visas are a type of non-immigrant student visa that allows foreigners to pursue education (academic studies and/or language training programs) in the country. F-1 students must maintain a full course of study. F-1 visas are issued by the U.S. Department of State through consulates abroad, and status is administered in the United States through the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) of Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

Requirements

To qualify, an applicant must be accepted by an SEVP-certified school, demonstrate sufficient funds, show ties to a home country (non-immigrant intent), and receive a Form I-20 from the school. Students are admitted for "duration of status" (D/S) and must report to their Designated School Official (DSO), maintain a full course load, and obtain authorization before working.

Employment

F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours per week during term. Off-campus employment is restricted: Curricular Practical Training (CPT) is available for work integral to the program of study, and Optional Practical Training (OPT) permits up to 12 months of employment related to the field of study, with a 24-month extension available for STEM graduates.

Dependents and statistics

Spouses and children of F-1 students enter on F-2 visas; F-2 spouses are not permitted to work. The F-1 is one of the largest U.S. non-immigrant categories, with over a million international students enrolled in peak years, chiefly from China and India.
