Peter Parler

Born: c. 1330, probably Schwäbisch Gmünd, Holy Roman Empire

Died: 13 July 1399, Prague, Bohemia

Occupation: Architect, sculptor, stonemason

Known for: St. Vitus Cathedral, Prague; Charles Bridge

Peter Parler (Latin: Petrus de Gemunden in Suevia; c. 1330 – 1399) was a German-Bohemian architect and sculptor, the most famous member of the Parler family of master masons and a leading figure of late Gothic architecture in Central Europe.

Works

Trained in the lodge of his father, Heinrich Parler, at Schwäbisch Gmünd and Cologne, Peter was summoned to Prague by Emperor Charles IV in 1356, aged about 23, to take over construction of St. Vitus Cathedral after the death of Matthias of Arras. He redesigned the chancel with one of the earliest net vaults (completed 1385), created the St. Wenceslaus Chapel, and carved the realistic portrait busts of the cathedral triforium — including a celebrated self-portrait.

From 1357 he oversaw the construction of the Charles Bridge across the Vltava, whose Old Town bridge tower, richly sculpted, is among the masterpieces of Gothic civic architecture. Other works attributed to Parler and his lodge include the Church of St. Bartholomew in Kolín, the choir of the Church of St. Barbara in Kutná Hora, and Prague's Church of Our Lady of the Snows.

Influence

The "Parler style" — flowing tracery, net and star vaults, naturalistic sculpture — spread across late-medieval Germany, Austria and Bohemia, shaping the Sondergotik (special Gothic) of the following century. Peter Parler died in Prague in 1399 and is commemorated by the bust he carved of himself in St. Vitus.
