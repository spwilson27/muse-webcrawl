Göktürks

EmpireGöktürk Khaganate (552–744)
FoundersBumin Qaghan, Istämi
ReligionTengrism
ScriptOld Turkic (Orkhon) script

The Göktürks, or Celestial Turks, were a Turkic people of the early Middle Ages who founded the Göktürk Khaganate, the first empire to use the name "Turk" politically. Under Bumin Qaghan and his sons, the khaganate, established in 552 after overthrowing the Rouran Khaganate, stretched at its height from Manchuria in the east to the Black Sea in the west, becoming the first transcontinental steppe empire of Turkic peoples.

History

After Bumin's death the empire was divided between his sons and his brother Istämi, who ruled the west and opened diplomatic and trade relations with the Byzantine Empire and Sasanian Persia along the Silk Road. Civil wars split the khaganate into eastern and western halves, both eventually subdued by Tang China. An eastern khaganate was restored by Ilterish Qaghan in 682 (the Second Turkic Khaganate) before falling to the Uyghurs in 744.

Legacy

The Göktürks left the earliest substantial texts in a Turkic language, the Orkhon inscriptions, written in the Old Turkic runiform script and erected in the 8th century in the Orkhon Valley of Mongolia.
