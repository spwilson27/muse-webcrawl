Archdiocese of Milan

Milan Cathedral, the metropolitan cathedral of the Archdiocese of Milan.

The Archdiocese of Milan (Latin: Archidioecesis Mediolanensis; Italian: Arcidiocesi di Milano) is a Latin Church archdiocese of the Catholic Church in Italy. It covers the provinces of Milan, Monza, Lecco and Varese and parts of other Lombard provinces, serving roughly five million Catholics, which makes it one of the largest dioceses in Europe. Its cathedral is the Duomo di Milano, and its archbishop is customarily created a cardinal.

History

Tradition attributes the evangelisation of Milan to the apostle Barnabas, though the historically secure succession of bishops begins in the third century. The see rose to prominence under Saint Ambrose (bishop 374–397), whose liturgical and theological legacy endures in the Ambrosian Rite, a distinct Latin liturgy still celebrated in most of the archdiocese. Saint Charles Borromeo (archbishop 1564–1584) made Milan a model of Counter-Reformation reform, founding seminaries and conducting extensive pastoral visitations.

Organisation

The archdiocese is the metropolitan see of an ecclesiastical province comprising several suffragan dioceses in Lombardy, including Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Crema, Cremona, Lodi, Mantua, Pavia and Vigevano. It is divided into pastoral zones corresponding broadly to its territory. In the twentieth century the see was held by figures such as Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster, Giovanni Battista Montini (later Pope Paul VI), and Carlo Maria Martini; Mario Delpini became archbishop in 2017.
