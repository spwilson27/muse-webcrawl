Military unit

A military unit or formation is a component of an armed force organised under a commander with a defined size, composition and role. Units are arranged in a hierarchy that allows armies to coordinate operations at every scale, from a handful of soldiers to hundreds of thousands.

Hierarchy

Typical land-force echelons, from smallest to largest, are the squad (section), platoon, company (battery or squadron), battalion, regiment, brigade, division, corps, field army and army group. Naval and air forces use parallel structures such as flotillas, squadrons, wings and fleets. Sizes vary between countries and eras; a modern division typically contains 10,000–25,000 personnel.

Organisation

Units combine combat arms (infantry, armour, artillery) with combat support and logistics. Permanent units such as regiments often carry battle honours and traditions, while higher formations may be assembled for particular operations as task forces.
