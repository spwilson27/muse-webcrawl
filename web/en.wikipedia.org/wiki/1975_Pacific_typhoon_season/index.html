The 1975 Pacific typhoon season had no official start or end dates but ran year-round, with most tropical cyclones in the northwestern Pacific forming between May and November. The season is remembered above all for Typhoon Nina, whose torrential rains in August 1975 caused the catastrophic Banqiao Dam failure in China — one of the deadliest disasters of the twentieth century — and for Typhoon June, at the time the most intense tropical cyclone ever recorded.

Season summary

The Joint Typhoon Warning Center (JTWC) monitored the basin and assigned names from its lists. Twenty-one tropical storms formed, fourteen reaching typhoon strength. Activity was concentrated in mid-summer and autumn. In addition to Nina and June, notable storms included Typhoon Elsie, which struck Taiwan and Fujian in September, and Typhoon Rita late in the season. Several systems affected Japan, the Philippines, Vietnam and southern China.

Typhoon Nina

Nina formed east of Taiwan at the end of July 1975, crossed Taiwan and made landfall in Fujian on 3 August as a weakening typhoon. Its remnants stalled inland over Henan, interacting with a cold front to dump record-breaking rainfall (over 1,000 mm in three days) on the Ru and Hong river basins. The resulting floods destroyed the Banqiao and Shimantan dams and 60 others on 8 August, killing on the order of 171,000 people when deaths from the following famine and epidemics are included. Nina is the deadliest tropical cyclone in the basin's recorded history and among the deadliest worldwide.

Typhoon June

Typhoon June, which developed in November 1975, reached a minimum central pressure of 875 mbar (25.8 inHg) with one-minute sustained winds estimated at 295 km/h (185 mph), then the lowest pressure ever measured in a tropical cyclone — a record later surpassed by Typhoon Tip in 1979. June executed a rare triple eyewall structure observed by reconnaissance aircraft. It recurved over open water and caused no significant land damage.

Other systems

Other named storms of the season included Tropical Storms Alice, Betty, Cora, Dottie, Faye, Grace, Helen, Ida, Lola, Mamie, Ora, Phyllis, Rita, Susan, Tess, Violet and Wilda (JTWC naming of the period). Collectively the secondary storms caused comparatively minor damage next to Nina, though Elsie and others brought destructive floods to Taiwan and coastal China.
