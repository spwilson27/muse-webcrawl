Useless eater


Nazi propaganda used cost calculations to devalue the lives of people with disabilities.

"Useless eaters" (German: Nutzlose Esser) is a pejorative term used in Nazi Germany for people deemed unable to contribute productively to society — in particular people with serious disabilities, the chronically or mentally ill, and the elderly in institutional care. The phrase is closely associated with the regime's eugenics ideology and with the Aktion T4 "euthanasia" program, in which such people were murdered.

Terminology and origins

The phrase circulated in German eugenic and economic writing before and after the First World War, sometimes alongside Ballastexistenzen ("ballast existences"). It gained lethal significance in the 1920 tract Permitting the Destruction of Life Unworthy of Life (Die Freigabe der Vernichtung lebensunwerten Lebens), by jurist Karl Binding and psychiatrist Alfred Hoche, which argued for killing people whose lives were considered burdensome and without value — the related concept of "life unworthy of life" (lebensunwertes Leben).

Use under Nazism

After 1933 the term entered official and propaganda discourse. School mathematics textbooks posed problems calculating the cost of caring for "hereditarily ill" people; propaganda films and posters emphasized what could be bought instead with the money spent on asylums. Franz Gürtner and other officials applied such reasoning to institutionalized patients. The framing prepared the ground for Aktion T4 (1939–1941), the centralized killing of institutional patients in gas chambers at six killing centers, which murdered around 70,000 people in its official phase and continued more covertly until 1945. In occupied Poland and the Soviet Union, and later in concentration camps under the code name 14f13, similar classifications were used to select victims.

The epithet was also applied in food policy: people classified as "useless eaters" — including psychiatric patients, prisoners, and Jewish ghetto inhabitants — were assigned starvation rations, making hunger itself an instrument of murder.
