Isobaric labeling

Isobaric labeling is a mass spectrometry strategy for the relative and absolute quantification of proteins in proteomics. Samples are chemically labelled with tags that have the same total mass (isobaric) but fragment in tandem mass spectrometry to release reporter ions of different masses, allowing several samples to be analysed together and compared.

Methods

The two main commercial implementations are TMT (tandem mass tags) and iTRAQ (isobaric tags for relative and absolute quantitation). Each tag has three parts: a reporter group, a mass-balancer group that keeps the total mass identical across channels, and a reactive group that attaches to peptide N-termini and lysine side chains. Multiplexing of up to 16–18 samples in one run is possible with modern TMT reagents.

Workflow and limitations

Proteins from each sample are digested, labelled with a different channel, pooled, and analysed by LC–MS/MS. Quantification is read from reporter-ion intensities in MS2 (or MS3) spectra. A known limitation is ratio compression caused by co-isolation of interfering peptides, which is mitigated by MS3 methods such as SPS-MS3 and by real-time database searching.

References: Thompson et al. (2003), invention of tandem mass tags; Ross et al. (2004), iTRAQ methodology.
