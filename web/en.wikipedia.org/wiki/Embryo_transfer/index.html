Embryo transfer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An embryo in a transfer catheter

Embryo transfer refers to a step in the process of assisted reproduction in which embryos are placed into the uterus of a female with the intent to establish a pregnancy. This technique — which is often used in connection with in vitro fertilization (IVF) — may be used in humans or in other animals, in which situations and goals may vary.

Embryo transfer can be done at day two or day three, or later in the blastocyst stage, which was first performed in 1984.

Procedure

The embryo transfer procedure is usually carried out under ultrasound guidance using a thin catheter passed through the cervix into the uterus, where the embryo or embryos are gently deposited. It is generally a brief procedure that does not require anaesthesia. Before transfer, the endometrium is prepared either in a natural menstrual cycle or with hormone replacement therapy, and progesterone support is given to support implantation.

Fresh versus frozen transfers

Embryos may be transferred in the same cycle in which the eggs were retrieved ("fresh" transfer) or cryopreserved by vitrification and transferred in a later cycle ("frozen" embryo transfer, FET). Frozen transfers allow time for preimplantation genetic testing and may reduce the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome. Pregnancy rates for good-quality blastocyst transfers are broadly comparable between the two approaches in many patient groups.

Number of embryos

Guidelines in many countries recommend elective single embryo transfer (eSET) for patients with a good prognosis, in order to reduce the risk of multiple pregnancy, which is associated with higher maternal and neonatal complications. The appropriate number depends on the patient's age, embryo quality, and previous treatment history.

In animals

Embryo transfer is widely used in cattle and horse breeding to obtain multiple offspring from genetically valuable females; embryos are collected from a donor and transferred to surrogate recipients.
