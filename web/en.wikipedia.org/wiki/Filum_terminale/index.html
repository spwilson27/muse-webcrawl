Filum terminale

The filum terminale ("terminal thread") is a delicate strand of fibrous tissue, about 20 cm in length, proceeding downward from the apex of the conus medullaris of the spinal cord.

Anatomy

The filum terminale consists of an upper part (filum terminale internum) surrounded by the meninges within the dural sac, and a lower part (filum terminale externum) that attaches to the coccyx. It is composed largely of pia mater and serves to anchor the spinal cord longitudinally.

Clinical significance

An abnormally short or thickened filum terminale can contribute to tethered cord syndrome, in which traction on the spinal cord causes neurological symptoms.
