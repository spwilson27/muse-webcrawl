Block design

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In combinatorial mathematics, a block design is an incidence structure consisting of a set of points together with a family of subsets of that set, called blocks, chosen so that the frequency of the elements satisfies certain regularity conditions. Block designs have applications in the design of experiments, finite geometry, and error-correcting codes.

Balanced incomplete block designs

A balanced incomplete block design (BIBD) with parameters (v, b, r, k, λ) has v points arranged in b blocks of size k, each point appearing in r blocks, and each pair of distinct points appearing together in exactly λ blocks. The Fano plane is a symmetric BIBD with parameters (7, 7, 3, 3, 1).

History

The theory grew out of R. A. Fisher and Frank Yates's work on agricultural experiments in the 1930s, and Kirkman's schoolgirl problem of 1850, an early example of a resolvable design. See also Combinatorial design and Steiner system.
