Cofferdam

A cofferdam is a temporary enclosure built in or around a body of water to allow the enclosed area to be pumped dry, so that construction or repair work can be carried out in the dry. Cofferdams are used for building bridges, dams, piers and locks, and for repairing ship hulls.

Types

Common types include earthen embankment cofferdams for shallow water, rockfill and crib cofferdams, and double-walled sheet-pile cofferdams filled with earth or gravel for deeper water. Cellular cofferdams, made of interlocking steel sheet-pile cells filled with soil, are used for major works such as dam construction.

Construction

Once the enclosure is complete, pumps remove the water and must keep pace with seepage. The design must resist hydrostatic pressure, currents and ice, and failure can flood the works catastrophically, so cofferdams are carefully engineered and monitored.
