Huzhou

Huzhou is a prefecture-level city in northern Zhejiang province, China, on the southern shore of Lake Tai. It borders Jiaxing to the east, Hangzhou to the south, and the provinces of Anhui and Jiangsu. The city has a population of over three million.

History

Huzhou has a history of more than 2,300 years and is famed in Chinese tradition for silk, tea, and the Huzhou writing brush, one of the celebrated Four Treasures-related crafts of the region. The nearby town of Nanxun preserves canal-town architecture from the Ming and Qing dynasties, when local silk merchants prospered.

Geography and economy

The prefecture administers districts including Wuxing and Nanxun and counties such as Deqing, Changxing and Anji — Anji being known for its bamboo forests and white tea. Modern industries include textiles, machinery, and tourism around Lake Tai and Mount Mogan (Moganshan).
