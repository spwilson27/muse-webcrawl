CODOFIL

The Council for the Development of French in Louisiana (CODOFIL; French: Conseil pour le développement du français en Louisiane) is a state agency of Louisiana charged with promoting the use of the French language in Louisiana's culture, education and economy. Founded in 1968 by state legislator and lawyer James Domengeaux, CODOFIL was created to stem the decline of French — especially Louisiana French and Louisiana Creole — after decades in which children were punished for speaking French in public schools.

History

CODOFIL was established by Act 409 of the Louisiana legislature in 1968. Domengeaux, its first chairman, recruited French teachers from France, Belgium, Quebec and other Francophone regions to staff French immersion and second-language programmes in Louisiana schools, after the state constitution's earlier suppression of French in classrooms (the 1921 constitution mandated English-only public schooling) had driven sharp declines in transmission of the language.

Programmes

The agency supports French immersion schools, scholarships (including the long-running exchange scholarships for Louisiana students and teachers), teacher recruitment and certification arrangements with Francophone governments, and cultural promotion within the 22-parish Acadiana region. It also cooperates with groups such as the Louisiana Folklife Program and community radio on Louisiana French revitalisation, including documentation of the distinctive local dialects rather than only standard French.

Criticism

CODOFIL has at times been criticised by Louisiana French speakers for emphasising international standard French and imported teachers over the local vernacular varieties, a tension that later programmes sought to address by incorporating Louisiana French materials and speakers.
