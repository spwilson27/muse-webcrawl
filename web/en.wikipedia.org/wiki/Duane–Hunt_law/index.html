Duane–Hunt law

Bremsstrahlung spectra terminate at a minimum wavelength set by the tube voltage

The Duane–Hunt law, named after the American physicists William Duane and Franklin L. Hunt, relates the minimum wavelength (equivalently, the maximum frequency) of X-rays produced by bremsstrahlung in an X-ray tube to the accelerating voltage applied to the tube.

Statement

When electrons accelerated through a voltage V strike a metal target, the maximum photon energy that can be produced occurs when an electron loses all of its kinetic energy in a single collision. Conservation of energy then gives:

eV = hc/λmin

so that λmin ≈ 1239.8 / V nanometres, with V in volts. The spectrum of emitted X-rays rises from zero intensity at this sharp cutoff wavelength and peaks at longer wavelengths.

Historical significance

Duane and Hunt published the law in 1915. Because the relation provided a precise way to measure the ratio h/e from a straightforward voltage and wavelength measurement, it gave one of the most accurate early determinations of Planck's constant and supplied strong early confirmation of the photon picture of radiation and of energy conservation in individual quantum events.
