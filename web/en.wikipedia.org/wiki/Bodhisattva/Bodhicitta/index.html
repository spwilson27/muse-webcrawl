Bodhicitta

Bodhicitta (Sanskrit: बोधिचित्त, "awakening-mind") is, in Mahayana Buddhism, the mind that strives toward awakening and enlightenment for the benefit of all sentient beings. Generating bodhicitta is considered the defining act of a bodhisattva and the entry point to the Mahayana path. The classic text on its cultivation is Śāntideva's Bodhicaryāvatāra (8th century), which praises bodhicitta as a wish-fulfilling jewel that transforms the practitioner and ultimately the world.

Two aspects

Commentators distinguish relative (or conventional) bodhicitta — the compassionate intention and active engagement in practice for others' sake — from ultimate bodhicitta, the direct realisation of emptiness (śūnyatā), the true nature of phenomena. Relative bodhicitta is in turn divided into aspirational bodhicitta, the wish to attain Buddhahood for all beings, and practical or "engaged" bodhicitta, the actual undertaking of the six perfections (pāramitās): generosity, ethics, patience, effort, meditation and wisdom.

Cultivation

Traditional methods for generating bodhicitta include the "sevenfold cause and effect" instruction, traced to Atiśa, and the practice of "equalising and exchanging self and others", in which the practitioner reflects that all beings have been one's kind parents through beginningless rebirths and resolves to repay their kindness. In Tibetan Buddhism, bodhicitta is cultivated through lojong (mind training) slogans and the practice of tonglen, giving and taking, and is formally generated in the ceremony of taking the bodhisattva vow.
