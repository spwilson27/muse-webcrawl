Bodhisattva

In Buddhism, a bodhisattva (Sanskrit: बोधिसत्त्व, bodhisattva; Pali: bodhisatta) is a being who, having generated bodhicitta — the resolve to attain awakening for the sake of all sentient beings — is on the path to Buddhahood. In Mahayana Buddhism the bodhisattva ideal is the central model of the religious life: the bodhisattva vows to postpone final liberation in order to work for the salvation of others, practising the perfections (pāramitās) over countless lifetimes. In Theravada Buddhism, the term chiefly denotes the Buddha in his previous lives before his awakening.

In early Buddhism and Theravada

In the Pali Canon, bodhisatta refers to Siddhartha Gautama before his enlightenment, and the Jataka tales recount his previous lives, in which he perfects qualities such as generosity and patience. Theravada tradition recognises the bodhisattva path as a rare and exceptionally long vocation, distinct from the paths of the disciple (sāvaka) and the solitary buddha (paccekabuddha). The future Buddha Maitreya (Metteyya) is awaited as a bodhisattva currently residing in Tusita heaven.

In Mahayana Buddhism

Mahayana sutras, emerging around the 1st century BCE–1st century CE, universalised the bodhisattva calling: all beings are urged to aspire to full Buddhahood rather than arhatship alone. The bodhisattva's career is mapped onto stages (bhūmis), classically ten, and structured by the six perfections: generosity (dāna), morality (śīla), patience (kṣānti), vigour (vīrya), meditation (dhyāna), and wisdom (prajñā). The doctrine of skilful means (upāya) and the transfer of merit underpin the ideal, expressed in the bodhisattva vows and in Shantideva's Bodhicaryāvatāra (8th century).

Celestial bodhisattvas

Great bodhisattvas became objects of devotion across Asia. Avalokiteshvara embodies compassion and is known as Guanyin in China and Kannon in Japan; Manjushri embodies wisdom and carries a sword and the Prajnaparamita text; Samantabhadra represents practice; Ksitigarbha vows to save beings in the hell realms; and Maitreya awaits his future birth as the next Buddha. In Vajrayana Buddhism, bodhisattvas are also central figures of tantric practice and visualisation.

The bodhisattva in the modern world

The ideal informs modern "engaged Buddhism": figures such as Thich Nhat Hanh and the 14th Dalai Lama — regarded by Tibetan Buddhists as an emanation of Avalokiteshvara — frame social and humanitarian work as bodhisattva activity. The concept has influenced Buddhist ethics, environmental activism, and hospice and chaplaincy movements.
