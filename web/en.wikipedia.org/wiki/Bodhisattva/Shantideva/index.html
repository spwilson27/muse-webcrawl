Shantideva

Śāntideva (Sanskrit: शान्तिदेव; c. 685 – c. 763 CE) was an 8th-century CE Indian philosopher, Buddhist monk, poet and scholar of the Mahayana tradition. He was a monk at the monastic university of Nalanda and a proponent of the Madhyamaka school of philosophy. Śāntideva is best known as the author of the Bodhicaryāvatāra ("A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"), one of the most influential texts of Mahayana Buddhism, and of the Śikṣāsamuccaya ("Compendium of Training").

Life

Traditional biographies, regarded as partly legendary, recount that Śāntideva was born a prince in Saurastra who renounced the throne after a vision of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, and went to Nalanda, where he was given the name Śāntideva ("god of peace"). Despised by fellow monks as lazy — nicknamed "Bhusuku", one who only eats, sleeps and defecates — he was challenged to recite scripture before the assembly, whereupon he delivered the Bodhicaryāvatāra and, legend says, rose into the air as he recited the ninth chapter on wisdom.

Works

The Bodhicaryāvatāra, in ten chapters and about 1,000 verses, describes the path of the bodhisattva from the awakening mind (bodhicitta) through the six perfections (pāramitās): generosity, ethics, patience, diligence, meditation and wisdom. Its meditations on exchanging self and other and on the equality of self and others are central to Tibetan Buddhist lojong (mind training) teachings, and the 14th Dalai Lama has frequently taught the text. The Śikṣāsamuccaya is a prose compendium of Mahayana sutra passages arranged as a manual of practice.
