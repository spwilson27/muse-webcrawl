Pratītyasamutpāda

Pratītyasamutpāda (Sanskrit: प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद; Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda), commonly translated as dependent origination or dependent arising, is a key doctrine of Buddhism shared by all schools. It states that all phenomena (dharmas) arise in dependence upon other phenomena: "If this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also ceases to exist." The basic principle is that all things arise in dependence upon multiple causes and conditions; nothing is independent. The doctrine underlies the Buddhist analysis of suffering and of emptiness, and is applied in the classic twelvefold chain of causation (the twelve nidānas) that describes the cycle of rebirth in saṁsāra.

The twelve nidānas depicted on the outer rim of the Wheel of Life (bhavacakra)

The general formula

The abstract principle of conditionality (idappaccayatā) is stated in the early texts: "When this is, that is. From the arising of this comes the arising of that. When this isn't, that isn't. From the cessation of this comes the cessation of that." The Buddha is reported to have said that whoever sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma, and whoever sees the Dhamma sees dependent origination.

The twelve nidānas

The most famous application is the twelvefold chain explaining the arising of suffering: (1) ignorance (avidyā), (2) volitional formations (saṁskāra), (3) consciousness (vijñāna), (4) name-and-form (nāmarūpa), (5) the six sense bases (ṣaḍāyatana), (6) contact (sparśa), (7) feeling (vedanā), (8) craving (tṛṣṇā), (9) clinging (upādāna), (10) becoming (bhava), (11) birth (jāti), and (12) aging-and-death (jarāmaraṇa). Read in reverse, the chain shows how suffering ceases when its conditions cease — the basis of the path to liberation.

Interpretations

Theravāda commentaries traditionally distribute the twelve links across three lifetimes, while modern teachers often interpret them psychologically, as moment-to-moment processes. In Madhyamaka, dependent origination is identified with emptiness itself: Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā argues that because things arise dependently they lack inherent existence, and this dependent designation is the middle way. In East Asian Huayan Buddhism, the doctrine developed into the vision of total mutual interpenetration, symbolized by Indra's net.
