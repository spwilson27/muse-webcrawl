Guanyin (Chinese: 觀音, pinyin: Guānyīn) is a bodhisattva associated with compassion as venerated by Mahayana Buddhists. She is the East Asian form of the Indian bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara; her full Chinese name, Guanshiyin, means "[The One Who] Perceives the Sounds of the World". In Japan she is known as Kannon, in Korea as Gwan-eum, and in Vietnam as Quan Âm.

Origins and gender

The popularity of Guanyin in China was established by the Lotus Sutra, whose 25th chapter describes the bodhisattva's saving powers and many forms. In India Avalokiteśvara was male, but in China the figure gradually took on female form from around the Song dynasty, aided by legends such as that of Princess Miaoshan, and Guanyin is now most commonly depicted as a compassionate woman in white robes.

Iconography

Guanyin is often shown holding a willow branch and a vase of pure water, seated on a lotus, or in the eleven-headed, thousand-armed form symbolising all-seeing compassion. The bodhisattva is honoured as patron of seafarers, women and children, and as a hearer of cries for help. Mount Putuo in Zhejiang is her sacred island and a major pilgrimage centre.
