Avalokiteśvara is a bodhisattva of Mahayana Buddhism who embodies the compassion (karuṇā) of all Buddhas. The name means "the lord who looks down (on the world)", or "the one who hears the cries of the world". Avalokiteśvara is among the most widely revered bodhisattvas in Asia.

Forms across Asia

In China, Avalokiteśvara became Guanyin, generally depicted as female, a transformation that occurred over the Tang and Song dynasties; devotion to Guanyin spread to Korea (Gwan-eum), Japan (Kannon) and Vietnam (Quan Âm). In Tibet, as Chenrezig, the bodhisattva is regarded as the patron deity of Tibet, with the Dalai Lamas considered his emanations. Iconography varies widely, including the thousand-armed, eleven-headed form symbolising compassion reaching everywhere, and the four-armed form associated with the mantra Oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ.

Scriptures

The Lotus Sutra's "Universal Gateway" chapter describes how Avalokiteśvara rescues beings who call his name from fire, flood, demons and other perils, taking whatever form is needed to teach them. He also appears at the heart of the Heart Sutra, whose opening lines describe Avalokiteśvara perceiving the emptiness of the five aggregates. According to tradition he made a great vow to postpone his own Buddhahood until every being is freed from suffering.
