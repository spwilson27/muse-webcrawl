Avalokiteśvara

Avalokiteśvara (Sanskrit: अवलोकितेश्वर, "the Lord who looks down [with compassion]") is a bodhisattva who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas and is one of the most widely revered figures in Mahayana Buddhism. Avalokiteśvara appears in many forms across Asia: as Guanyin in China, Kannon in Japan, Chenrezig in Tibet and Quán Thế Âm in Vietnam. The bodhisattva is the focus of the Heart Sutra's teaching on emptiness and of the Lotus Sutra's "Universal Gateway" chapter, and is associated with the mantra Oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ.

Origins and scriptures

The earliest depictions of Avalokiteśvara date to roughly the 1st–2nd centuries CE in north-western India. Chapter 25 of the Lotus Sutra, often circulated independently as the Avalokiteśvara Sutra, describes how the bodhisattva hears the cries of suffering beings and rescues them from shipwreck, fire, demons and executioners. The Heart Sutra is framed as Avalokiteśvara's teaching to the disciple Śāriputra on the emptiness of the five aggregates.

Iconography

Avalokiteśvara is commonly shown holding a lotus, a vase of nectar or a wish-fulfilling jewel, and wearing a crown bearing the Buddha Amitābha, of whose Pure Land tradition he is an attendant. Esoteric forms include the eleven-headed and thousand-armed manifestations, whose many eyes and hands symbolise seeing and aiding all beings. In East Asia the bodhisattva gradually took predominantly female forms as Guanyin, while in Tibet Chenrezig is male and regarded as the patron deity of the Tibetan people; the Dalai Lamas are considered his emanations.

Devotion

Reciting Avalokiteśvara's name or mantra is one of the most widespread Buddhist devotional practices. Major pilgrimage sites include Mount Putuo in China, dedicated to Guanyin, and the Potala Palace in Lhasa, named after the bodhisattva's mythical abode, Mount Potalaka.
