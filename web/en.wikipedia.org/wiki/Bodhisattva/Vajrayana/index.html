Vajrayana

Vajrayāna (Sanskrit: वज्रयान, "Thunderbolt Vehicle" or "Diamond Vehicle"), also known as Mantrayāna, Tantrayāna and esoteric or tantric Buddhism, is a complex and multifaceted system of Buddhist thought and practice which evolved over several centuries. It spread from India, where it flourished roughly from the 7th to the 12th century CE, to Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan, Mongolia and East Asia, where related traditions survive in Japanese Shingon and Tendai.

Vajrayāna builds on Mahayana philosophy — emptiness, compassion and the bodhisattva ideal — but adds esoteric methods intended to achieve Buddhahood swiftly, often "in this very life": empowerment (abhiṣeka) by a guru, mantra recitation, mudrā, maṇḍala visualisation and deity yoga, in which the practitioner visualises themselves as a meditational deity (yi dam). Its scriptures are the tantras, and its practice traditionally requires initiation and oral transmission from a qualified teacher (lama).

History

Tantric Buddhism developed in early medieval India, influenced by and in dialogue with Śaiva tantra, at centres such as Nālandā and Vikramaśīla. Between the 8th and 13th centuries it was transmitted to Tibet in two waves, giving rise to the Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya and Gelug schools of Tibetan Buddhism. After the decline of Buddhism in India, the Himalayan regions preserved the tradition most completely; in the 20th century, Tibetan exile communities brought Vajrayāna teachers and practices to the West.
