The Diamond Sutra (Sanskrit: Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, "The Perfection of Wisdom Text that Cuts Like a Thunderbolt") is a Mahayana sutra from the genre of Prajñāpāramitā ("Perfection of Wisdom") sutras. Structured as a dialogue between the Buddha and the elder Subhuti, it teaches the emptiness of all phenomena and the paradox that a bodhisattva saves beings precisely by perceiving that no beings inherently exist.

Content

The Buddha instructs Subhuti that a bodhisattva should give without attachment to signs or notions of giver, gift or recipient, and that all conditioned phenomena are "like a dream, an illusion, a bubble, a shadow, like dew or a flash of lightning". Its famous four-line summary of impermanence closes the text.

The Dunhuang copy

A copy found at Dunhuang, printed in 868 CE and now in the British Library, is the world's earliest dated printed book; its colophon records that Wang Jie commissioned it for free distribution. The manuscript was recovered by Aurel Stein in 1907 from the Mogao Caves.

Influence

The sutra is especially revered in East Asian Buddhism and Chan/Zen, where tradition holds that reading it awakened the Sixth Patriarch Huineng.
