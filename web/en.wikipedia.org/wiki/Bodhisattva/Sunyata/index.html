Śūnyatā

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sanskritशून्यता (Śūnyatā)
Literal meaningEmptiness, voidness
Related termsPratītyasamutpāda, anattā, svabhāva

Śūnyatā (Sanskrit: शून्यता; usually translated as "emptiness" or "voidness") is a central concept in Mahayana Buddhism, denoting that all things are empty (śūnya) of inherent existence and self-nature (svabhāva). Things do not exist independently or by their own essence; they arise dependently, through pratītyasamutpāda (dependent origination).

Development

The concept develops from early Buddhist teachings on impermanence and non-self (anattā) and is elaborated in the Prajñāpāramitā sutras, which extend emptiness to all dharmas, including nirvana itself. The philosopher Nāgārjuna (c. 2nd century CE), founder of the Madhyamaka school, gave the doctrine its classic formulation in the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, arguing that emptiness and dependent origination are the same thing viewed from different angles, and that emptiness too is empty — a teaching meant to prevent reifying emptiness into a metaphysical absolute.

Interpretation

Śūnyatā is not nihilism: it denies intrinsic, independent existence, not existence altogether. In Nagarjuna's two-truths framework, things function conventionally while being ultimately empty. Later schools debated the concept extensively — Yogācāra emphasised emptiness of the subject–object duality, and Tibetan traditions developed influential distinctions between "self-emptiness" and "other-emptiness". A bodhisattva's realisation of emptiness, united with compassion, is said to be what motivates and enables the path to Buddhahood.
