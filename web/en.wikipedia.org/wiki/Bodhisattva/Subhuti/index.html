Subhūti

Subhūti (Sanskrit: सुभूति; Pali: Subhūti; Chinese: 須菩提, Xūpútí) was one of the ten principal disciples of the Buddha. In the Pali canon he is declared foremost among those who dwell in remote and peaceful abodes (araṇavihārī) and foremost among those worthy of gifts (dakkhiṇeyya). In Mahāyāna Buddhism, Subhūti becomes the Buddha's principal interlocutor in the Prajñāpāramitā (Perfection of Wisdom) sūtras, where he expounds the doctrine of emptiness.

Life in the early texts

According to the Pali commentaries, Subhūti was a nephew of the wealthy merchant Anāthapiṇḍika, the Buddha's great patron. Hearing the Buddha teach at the dedication of the Jetavana monastery, he resolved to become a monk. After ordination he mastered the teachings and, practicing loving-kindness (mettā) meditation, attained arahantship. It is said that whenever he received alms he would first enter a meditative attainment of loving-kindness, so that the donor's gift would bear the greatest fruit — one reason he was called foremost among those worthy of gifts.

In the Prajñāpāramitā sūtras

Subhūti is the main speaker in much of the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā (Perfection of Wisdom in 8,000 Lines) and the Buddha's dialogue partner throughout the Diamond Sūtra, in which he asks how one who has set out on the bodhisattva path should stand, subdue his mind, and practice generosity without attachment to signs. His bewilderment-turned-insight models the student of emptiness: in the larger Prajñāpāramitā texts, Subhūti famously teaches that he finds no dharma called "bodhisattva", no dharma called "perfection of wisdom" — and that this very teaching is the perfection of wisdom. In the Lotus Sūtra, he receives a prediction of future Buddhahood under the name Śaśiketu.

In popular culture

Subhūti appears in the Chinese classic Journey to the West as Patriarch Subhūti (須菩提祖師), the Daoist-Buddhist sage who teaches the monkey Sun Wukong the Way, magic transformations, and somersault flight, before dismissing him from his school. In Chan/Zen Buddhism, Subhūti is also remembered for the story of the god Śakra scattering flowers on him while he sat in meditation on emptiness — a parable about the emptiness of both the meditator and the act of praise.
