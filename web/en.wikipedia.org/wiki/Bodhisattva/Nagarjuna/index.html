Nagarjuna (c. 150 – c. 250 CE) was an Indian Mahayana Buddhist thinker, scholar-saint and philosopher, founder of the Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") school. He is widely considered one of the most important Buddhist philosophers. Tradition credits him with retrieving the Perfection of Wisdom sutras from the realm of the nagas, and he is venerated in East Asian and Tibetan Buddhism as a patriarch.

Philosophy

In his masterpiece, the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, Nagarjuna argues that all phenomena are empty (śūnya) of inherent existence (svabhāva) because they arise dependently; emptiness is itself the middle way between eternalism and nihilism, and is ultimately to be understood as identical with dependent origination. His dialectical method of reductio arguments, and the doctrine of the two truths (conventional and ultimate), shaped all later Mahayana thought. Other works attributed to him include the Vigrahavyāvartanī, the Ratnāvalī and the Suhṛllekha.

Life and legend

Historical details are sparse; biographies were written centuries later and mingle legend with history, associating him with the court of a Satavahana king and with the monastic university of Nalanda.

Influence

Madhyamaka became foundational for Tibetan Buddhism and strongly influenced Chan/Zen and the East Asian schools; Nagarjuna is counted among the Six Ornaments of Jambudvipa in Tibetan tradition.
