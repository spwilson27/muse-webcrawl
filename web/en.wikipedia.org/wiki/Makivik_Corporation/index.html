Makivik Corporation

Makivik Corporation (Inuktitut: ᒪᑭᕝᕕᒃ ᓇᖕᒥᓂᖃᖃᑎᒌᑦ) is the Inuit-owned development corporation and political organisation representing the Inuit of Nunavik, the northern region of Quebec, Canada. Its name derives from the Inuktitut word for "advancement" or "to rise up." It was established in 1978 following the signing of the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement (JBNQA) of 1975.

Role

Under the JBNQA — the first major modern land-claims settlement in Canada — the Inuit of Nunavik received compensation funds, land rights, and new institutions. Makivik was created to receive and manage the Inuit share of the compensation (initially about $90 million), to administer it on behalf of Inuit beneficiaries, and to promote the economic, social, and cultural development of Nunavik's fourteen communities. Its head office is in Kuujjuaq, with an office in Montreal.

Business activities

Makivik invests settlement funds through wholly owned and joint-venture subsidiaries. Its best-known holdings have included Air Inuit, the regional airline serving Nunavik; First Air (later part of Canadian North) through a holding structure; construction, shipping, fuel distribution, fisheries, and hotel ventures in the North. Profits support social and cultural programmes, including initiatives for elders, housing advocacy, and the preservation of Inuktitut.

Political role

Makivik represents Nunavik Inuit in negotiations with the governments of Quebec and Canada, including long-running discussions on Nunavik self-government, and works alongside the Kativik Regional Government and other institutions created under the JBNQA. All Inuit beneficiaries of the agreement are members of the corporation, and its executives are elected by the region's communities.
