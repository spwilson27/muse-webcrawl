Sinn Féin (Irish for "we ourselves" or "ourselves alone") is an Irish republican and democratic socialist political party active in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. Founded in 1905 by Arthur Griffith, it is the oldest political party on the island of Ireland still in existence.

History

The party was founded on 28 November 1905 by Arthur Griffith, who advocateded Irish self-reliance and the withdrawal of Irish MPs from Westminster. After the Easter Rising of 1916, Sinn Féin won a landslide in the 1918 general election, declared an Irish Republic, and established Dáil Éireann. The party later split over the Anglo-Irish Treaty, giving rise to Cumann na nGaedheal (later Fine Gael) and Fianna Fáil.

During the Troubles, Sinn Féin was closely associated with the Provisional IRA. Following the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, the party became a major force in Northern Ireland politics, and under Gerry Adams and later Mary Lou McDonald it grew into the largest party in the Republic of Ireland by popular vote in the 2020 general election.

Policies

Sinn Féin's central aim is the reunification of Ireland. It advocates increased public spending on housing, healthcare and childcare, and describes itself as a democratic socialist party. It abstains from taking seats in the UK Parliament at Westminster.

Electoral performance

The party won the most first-preference votes in the 2020 Irish general election and became the largest party in the Northern Ireland Assembly in 2022, with Michelle O'Neill becoming First Minister in 2024.
