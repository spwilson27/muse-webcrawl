National Intelligence Service (South Korea)

The National Intelligence Service (NIS; Korean: 국가정보원) is the principal intelligence agency of South Korea. It was founded in 1961 as the Korean Central Intelligence Agency (KCIA), renamed the Agency for National Security Planning (ANSP) in 1981, and given its current name in 1999.

Role

The NIS collects and analyses foreign and domestic intelligence, with a particular focus on North Korea, and handles counter-espionage and counter-terrorism. Reforms in 2020 transferred its domestic investigative powers over anti-state activities to the police, aiming to make the agency a purely intelligence-gathering body.

Controversies

The agency's predecessors were involved in political repression under authoritarian governments, and the NIS has faced scandals including alleged interference in the 2012 presidential election.
