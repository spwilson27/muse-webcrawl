Tank classification

SubjectMilitary armoured vehicles
Historical classesLight, medium, heavy, cruiser, infantry tanks
Modern classMain battle tank (MBT)

Tank classification is the practice of categorizing tanks by weight, role, or a combination of both. Classification systems varied widely between nations and eras, reflecting differing doctrines for the use of armour in battle.

By weight

During the Second World War, tanks were commonly classed as light (reconnaissance and airborne roles), medium (general-purpose battle tanks), and heavy (breakthrough and anti-tank duties). Post-war designs converged on the universal main battle tank, combining the firepower and protection of heavies with the mobility of mediums.

By role

The British Army long distinguished fast cruiser tanks, intended for exploitation, from slow, heavily armoured infantry tanks that supported foot soldiers. Other role-based types have included tank destroyers, flame tanks, airborne tanks such as the Tetrarch, and super-heavy experimental designs.
