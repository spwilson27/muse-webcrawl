The César Award for Best Actor (French: César du meilleur acteur) is one of the César Awards, presented annually by the French Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma to recognise the outstanding performance of a leading actor in a French film of the preceding year. The award has been given since the first César ceremony in 1976, when Philippe Noiret won for Le Vieux Fusil (The Old Gun).

History

Nominees and winners are chosen by the members of the Académie, made up of professionals from all branches of the French film industry. The category is the male counterpart of the César Award for Best Actress and is among the most closely watched of the ceremony.

Notable winners

Gérard Depardieu and Daniel Auteuil are among the most honoured performers in the category, each winning twice, with Depardieu receiving a record number of nominations. Other celebrated winners include Michel Serrault (three wins, a record for the category), Jean Gabin-era successors such as Jean Rochefort, Philippe Noiret, Michel Piccoli, Mathieu Amalric, Omar Sy — the first Black winner, for Intouchables (2011) — and Tahar Rahim. Michel Serrault's three victories, for La Cage aux folles (1978), Garde à vue (1981) and Nelly et Monsieur Arnaud (1995), stand as the record for the award.
