Century Series

The Century Series is a popular name for a group of US fighter aircraft designs of the 1950s and early 1960s, designated with numbers in the 100s (F-100 to F-110 in the pre-1962 system). They represented the first generation of supersonic combat aircraft in regular United States Air Force service and incorporated the lessons of the Korean War and rapid advances in aerodynamics and jet propulsion.

The aircraft

The series comprises the North American F-100 Super Sabre, the first USAF fighter capable of supersonic speed in level flight; the McDonnell F-101 Voodoo, designed as a long-range escort and reconnaissance aircraft; the Convair F-102 Delta Dagger and its development the F-106 Delta Dart, all-weather interceptors shaped by the area rule; the Lockheed F-104 Starfighter, a Mach 2 day fighter widely exported and nicknamed "the missile with a man in it"; the Republic F-105 Thunderchief, a supersonic fighter-bomber that bore the brunt of bombing over North Vietnam; and the Convair F-107 and others that did not enter service. The designation grouping also popularly includes the F-110 Spectre, later redesignated F-4 Phantom II.

Characteristics and legacy

Century Series aircraft introduced thin swept or delta wings exploiting the area rule, afterburning turbojets, radar-guided missiles, and — controversially — a design philosophy that sometimes omitted internal guns in favour of missiles alone, an assumption the Vietnam War would challenge. Several types served for decades with allied air forces; the F-104 and F-105 became symbols of Cold War air power, and the series set the pattern for the teen-series fighters that followed.
