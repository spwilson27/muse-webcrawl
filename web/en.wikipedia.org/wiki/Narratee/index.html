Narratee

In narratology, the narratee is the person or audience to whom a narrator addresses a narrative. The term was developed by critic Gerald Prince to distinguish the receiver inscribed within the text from the actual reader.

Concepts

The narratee may be a fully characterised listener inside the story (an intradiegetic narratee), as with the wedding guest in Coleridge's Rime of the Ancient Mariner, or an uncharacterised, zero-degree addressee. The narratee is distinct from the implied reader, a concept describing the reader presupposed by the text as a whole.

Use in analysis

Identifying the narratee helps critics analyse unreliable narration, apostrophe, and embedded storytelling, as in frame tales such as Heart of Darkness.
