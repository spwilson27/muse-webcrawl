Rose Hall, Montego Bay

Rose Hall is a Georgian Palladian great house in Montego Bay, Jamaica, famous for the legend of the "White Witch of Rose Hall". The house stands on a hilltop overlooking the Caribbean Sea, east of Montego Bay, within the Rose Hall estate.

History

The mansion was built in the 1770s and was the home of John Palmer, Custos of St James, and his wife Annie Palmer. According to popular legend, Annie Palmer murdered several of her husbands and enslaved workers and was herself murdered, and her ghost is said to haunt the house. The legend was popularised by Herbert G. de Lisser's 1929 novel The White Witch of Rosehall and later by the Johnny Cash song "The Ballad of Annie Palmer"; historians regard most of the story as fiction.

Today

Rose Hall was restored in the 1960s and now operates as a museum and tourist attraction, offering guided day tours and evening "haunted" night tours. The surrounding estate includes golf courses and resort hotels.
