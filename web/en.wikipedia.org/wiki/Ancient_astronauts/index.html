Ancient astronauts

ClaimExtraterrestrials visited Earth in antiquity and influenced human culture
Key proponentsErich von Däniken; Zecharia Sitchin; Giorgio A. Tsoukalos
StatusPseudoscience / pseudohistory

Ancient astronauts (or ancient aliens) refers to a pseudoscientific hypothesis that intelligent extraterrestrial beings visited Earth in ancient or prehistoric times and made contact with humans, influencing early cultures, technologies and religions. Proponents reinterpret myths, religious texts and archaeological monuments — such as the Egyptian pyramids, the Nazca Lines and Stonehenge — as evidence of such contact.

History of the idea

Ideas of cosmic visitors appear in early science fiction and in the writings of figures such as Charles Fort, but the hypothesis was popularised by Erich von Däniken's 1968 bestseller Chariots of the Gods?, which argued that ancient structures were built with alien help. Zecharia Sitchin later claimed, from his own translations of Sumerian texts, that the Anunnaki were extraterrestrials who created humanity. The television series Ancient Aliens (from 2010) brought the idea to a wide audience.

Criticism

Historians, archaeologists and scientists reject the hypothesis as pseudoscience: the cited artefacts are adequately explained by known human capabilities, the textual readings are inaccurate, and no verified physical evidence of extraterrestrial visitation exists. Critics also note that the argument often underestimates or dismisses the achievements of non-European cultures.
