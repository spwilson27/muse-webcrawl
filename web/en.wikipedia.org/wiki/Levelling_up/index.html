Levelling up

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Levelling up is a political policy agenda first outlined prominently in the United Kingdom by the Conservative government of Boris Johnson following the 2019 general election. It aims to reduce economic and social disparities between different regions of the country — in particular between London and the South East and the rest of the UK — by investing in infrastructure, education, skills, transport and local services in areas that feel "left behind".

Background

The phrase gained prominence during the 2019 general election campaign, in which the Conservatives won many seats in traditionally Labour-voting areas of northern England, the Midlands and Wales — the so-called "Red Wall". The agenda was presented as a response to long-standing regional inequalities in productivity, income, health and life expectancy.

Policy

In February 2022 the government published the Levelling Up the United Kingdom white paper, which set out twelve "missions" to be achieved by 2030, covering areas such as pay and employment, research and development spending outside the South East, public transport, digital connectivity, education, skills, health, housing, pride in place and local leadership. Funding mechanisms included the Levelling Up Fund, the Towns Fund and the UK Shared Prosperity Fund, distributed partly through competitive bidding by local authorities.

Criticism

Critics, including opposition parties, local government leaders and bodies such as the Institute for Fiscal Studies, argued that the funding available was small relative to cuts in local government budgets during the 2010s, that competitive bidding wasted resources, and that the definition of levelling up remained vague. Following the change of prime minister in 2024, the succeeding Labour government renamed the responsible department and dropped the phrase from its title, while retaining some regional development programmes.
