Stephen King

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen King at a book signing, glasses and grey hair, fountain pen in hand
Stephen King at a book signing, glasses and grey hair, fountain pen in hand

Stephen Edwin King (born 21 September 1947) is an American author of horror, supernatural fiction, suspense, crime, science fiction and fantasy novels — 'the King of Horror'. His books have sold more than 400 million copies and have been adapted into numerous films, television series and comics; they include Carrie (1974), The Shining, It, Misery, The Green Mile and the Dark Tower series. He has also written under the pen name Richard Bachman.

Career

King, a former schoolteacher from Maine, sold Carrie in 1973 (rescuing the manuscript from the trash, by Tabitha King's account); its paperback sale freed him to write full-time. The 1970s–80s run — 'Salem's Lot, The Shining, The Stand, Cujo, Pet Sematary, It — made him popular fiction's dominant brand; his craftsman memoir On Writing (2000) is a standard text. His collaborations (with son Joe Hill and Owen King) and constant output (some 65 novels and 200 short stories) continue into his late seventies.

Themes and standing

Set largely in fictional Maine towns (Castle Rock, Derry, Jerusalem's Lot), King's work fuses small-town realism with supernatural dread, children in peril, abusive authority and addiction — the latter fought in his own life (cocaine and alcohol in the 1980s, an intervention, and a 1999 van accident that nearly killed him and entered his fiction). A National Medal of Arts recipient (2014), he anchors the argument that popular storytelling is literature.
