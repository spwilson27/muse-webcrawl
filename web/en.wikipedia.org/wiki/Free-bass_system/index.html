Free-bass system

A free-bass system is a system of left-hand bass buttons on an accordion in which each button sounds a single note, giving the left hand a wide chromatic range of individual pitches, rather than the fixed chords of the standard Stradella bass system. It allows the accordion to perform serious classical and contrapuntal repertoire.

Description

In a free-bass instrument the bass side typically mirrors the range of several octaves, arranged in rows of thirds, minor thirds, or as a chromatic button layout related to the right-hand keyboard on chromatic button accordions. Many modern instruments are "converter" accordions that can switch between Stradella and free-bass modes.

History

Free-bass designs appeared in the nineteenth century, notably in the Russian bayan tradition, and were refined in the twentieth century by makers and performers in Scandinavia, Italy, and the Soviet Union. The system became a standard requirement for advanced accordion study and for performing transcriptions of organ and piano works.
