Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh (Arabic: عبد الحميد الدبيبة; born 1958) is a Libyan politician and businessman serving as prime minister of Libya's Government of National Unity since 2021. English-language sources render his family name both as Dbeibeh and as Dbeibah; this article uses the Dbeibeh spelling, and a parallel article exists under the Dbeibah spelling.

Background

Dbeibeh hails from Misrata, Libya's third-largest city and a commercial hub. During the era of Muammar Gaddafi he directed the Libyan Investment and Development Company, the state conglomerate responsible for large public construction programmes, accumulating a substantial fortune and close ties to the former regime's business establishment. He studied engineering abroad, commonly reported as at the University of Toronto.

Rise to the premiership

After the 2011 revolution and the second Libyan civil war, Dbeibeh remained a power broker in Misrata. In February 2021 the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum, convened under UN auspices in Geneva, selected his unity ticket over rival lists; with Mohamed al-Menfi heading the Presidential Council, Dbeibeh became prime minister-designate of a Government of National Unity charged with merging the rival administrations in Tripoli and the east and holding national elections on 24 December 2021.

In office

The unity government took office in March 2021. When the December 2021 elections collapsed over disputes about electoral laws and candidate eligibility — Dbeibeh himself registered as a presidential candidate despite a pledge not to — the eastern House of Representatives declared his mandate expired and in 2022 installed a rival administration under Fathi Bashagha, later succeeded by Osama Hamada. Dbeibeh retained Tripoli and international recognition as head of the GNU, insisting he would hand over power only to an elected government. His tenure saw increased oil-revenue spending, subsidies and public-sector salaries, alongside persistent criticism over corruption, the role of armed groups, and stalled elections.
