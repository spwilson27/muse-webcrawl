Prayer

Practice: Invocation or act of supplication addressed to a deity or object of worship

Forms: Petition, intercession, thanksgiving, adoration, confession

Prayer is an invocation or act that seeks to activate a rapport with an object of worship through deliberate communication. In the narrow sense, the term refers to an act of supplication or intercession directed towards a deity or a deified ancestor. More generally, prayer can also have the purpose of thanksgiving or praise, and in comparative religion is closely associated with more abstract forms of meditation and with charms or spells.

Forms and practices

Prayer can take a variety of forms: it can be part of a set liturgy or ritual, and it can be performed alone or in groups. Prayer may take the form of a hymn, incantation, formal creedal statement, or a spontaneous utterance in the praying person. Postures associated with prayer include standing, sitting, kneeling, bowing, and prostration; objects such as prayer beads, wheels, and mats support the practice in different traditions.

In world religions

In Christianity, set prayers such as the Lord's Prayer are combined with private devotion. In Islam, ṣalāt — the ritual prayer performed five times daily facing Mecca — is one of the Five Pillars. Jewish prayer includes the thrice-daily services and the Amidah. In Hinduism, prayer is entwined with puja, mantra recitation such as the Gayatri mantra, and devotional song. Buddhist practices include prostrations, chanting, and recitation before images and stupas.

Research

Scientific studies have examined the effects of prayer on health and the efficacy of intercessory prayer for third parties, with largely inconclusive or negative results, while research on private prayer associates it with psychological benefits such as reduced anxiety for practitioners.
