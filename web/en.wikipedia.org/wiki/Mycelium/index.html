Mycelium

Mycelium (plural mycelia) is a root-like structure of a fungus consisting of a mass of branching, thread-like hyphae. Its normal form is that of branched, slender, entangled threads. Fungal colonies composed of mycelium are found in and on soil and many other substrates.

A typical single spore germinates into a monokaryotic mycelium, which cannot reproduce sexually; when two compatible monokaryotic mycelia join and form a dikaryotic mycelium, that mycelium may form fruiting bodies such as mushrooms.

Uses

Mycelium is used in agriculture, mycoremediation, and as a sustainable material for packaging, leather substitutes and building products. Through the mycelial network, fungi transfer nutrients, water and chemical signals across ecosystems, including via mycorrhizal networks linking plant roots.
