Objectivism is a philosophical system developed by Russian-American writer Ayn Rand (1905–1982). Rand described Objectivism as "the concept of man as a heroic being, with his own happiness as the moral purpose of his life, with productive achievement as his noblest activity, and reason as his only absolute."

Rand first expressed Objectivism in her fiction, most notably The Fountainhead (1943) and Atlas Shrugged (1957), and later in non-fiction essays and books. Leonard Peikoff, a professional philosopher and Rand's designated intellectual heir, later gave it a more systematic structure.

Philosophy

Objectivism's central tenets are: reality exists independently of consciousness; human beings have direct contact with reality through sense perception; knowledge is gained through reason, the process of forming concepts from perceptual data; the proper moral purpose of one's life is the pursuit of one's own happiness; and the only social system consistent with this morality is one that displays full respect for individual rights — embodied in pure laissez-faire capitalism. In art, Objectivism advocates romantic realism.

Metaphysics and epistemology

Rand's metaphysics rests on three axioms: existence, identity, and consciousness — summarized in the dictum "existence exists." Her epistemology holds that reason is man's only means of knowledge and rejects faith and mysticism. She held that concepts are formed by a process of measurement-omission from percepts.

Ethics

Objectivist ethics is a form of rational egoism: it holds that each person should act in their own rational self-interest, and that values such as honesty, integrity, independence, productiveness, justice, and pride are virtues. Rand rejected altruism as she defined it — the doctrine that self-sacrifice is the highest moral duty.

Politics

Politically, Objectivism supports individual rights, including property rights, and holds that the sole proper function of government is the protection of rights through the police, the military, and the courts. It opposes taxation beyond what is needed for those functions (and regards even that as ideally voluntary) and rejects any form of collectivism or statism.

Criticism

Academic philosophers have generally dismissed Objectivism, arguing that Rand's defense of egoism and her derivations from the axioms are flawed. Defenders respond that mainstream criticism often misrepresents her positions. The philosophy has nonetheless been influential in libertarian and conservative political movements in the United States.
