Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley

Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley
LocationNear Rotorua, North Island, New Zealand
Formed10 June 1886, by the eruption of Mount Tarawera
FeaturesFrying Pan Lake, Inferno Crater Lake, Lake Rotomahana
ActivityWaimangu Geyser erupted 1900–1904 (extinct)

The Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley is the hydrothermal system created on 10 June 1886 by the volcanic eruption of Mount Tarawera, on the North Island of New Zealand. It encompasses Lake Rotomahana, the site of the formerly famous Pink and White Terraces, as well as the location of the Waimangu Geyser, which was active from 1900 to 1904 and for a time was the largest geyser in the world.

The valley runs roughly 4 km south-west from Mount Tarawera along the line of the 1886 eruption fissure. It is the only hydrothermal system in the world known to have been wholly created by a volcanic eruption, and its developing ecosystem has been studied from the moment of its formation.


Frying Pan Lake, Waimangu

Features

Frying Pan Lake, formed in 1917 after an eruption in the Echo Crater, is one of the largest hot springs in the world, covering about 38,000 square metres with an average temperature around 50–60 °C. Inferno Crater Lake occupies a crater blasted out in 1886; its striking blue water rises and falls in a rhythmic cycle linked to the deep hydrothermal system. Warbrick Terrace and a series of colourful sinter terraces, silica formations, steaming fumaroles and acidic streams line the valley floor.

Waimangu Geyser

The Waimangu Geyser began erupting in 1900, throwing water and debris up to 460 metres high — the tallest geyser eruptions ever recorded. In 1903 four tourists were killed by a sudden eruption. The geyser ceased playing in 1904, apparently after changes in the local water table.
