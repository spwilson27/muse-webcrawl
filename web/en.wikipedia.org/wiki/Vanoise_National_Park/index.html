Vanoise National Park (French: Parc national de la Vanoise) is a national park in the French Alps, located in the Tarentaise and Maurienne valleys of the department of Savoie. Founded on 6 July 1963, it was France's first national park. It is famous for its population of Alpine ibex and, together with Italy's adjoining Gran Paradiso National Park, forms one of the largest protected areas in Western Europe.

Geography

The park covers the Vanoise massif, a heavily glaciated range between the valleys of the Isère (Tarentaise) to the north and the Arc (Maurienne) to the south. Its highest summit is the Grande Casse at 3,855 metres (12,648 ft). The core protection zone covers about 535 square kilometres, surrounded by a peripheral zone in which 28 communes lie. Major ski resorts — including Tignes, Val d'Isère, Les Trois Vallées and Val Thorens — stand at its edges, a proximity that has generated recurring debate about development pressure on the park.

Wildlife

The park was created partly to protect the Alpine ibex, which had survived in the neighbouring Gran Paradiso but had been hunted almost to extinction in France; the population has since recovered to several thousand animals. Other notable fauna include chamois, alpine marmots, golden eagles, bearded vultures (reintroduced from 1986) and, since the 1990s, dispersing wolves from Italy. The flora includes alpine specialities such as the glacier buttercup and the blue thistle of the Alps (Eryngium alpinum).

Tourism

The park is a major destination for hiking, with a network of refuges and long-distance routes including sections of the GR5 and GR55 trails, as well as for ski touring and mountaineering in season.
