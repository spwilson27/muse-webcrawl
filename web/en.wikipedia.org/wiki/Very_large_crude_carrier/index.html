Very large crude carrier

A very large crude carrier (VLCC) is a class of oil tanker with a deadweight tonnage of between 200,000 and 320,000 tonnes (DWT). VLCCs carry around two million barrels of crude oil and are the workhorses of long-haul crude trades, such as from the Persian Gulf to East Asia, Europe, and North America. Tankers above 320,000 DWT are classed as ultra large crude carriers (ULCCs).

Characteristics

A typical VLCC is about 330 metres (1,080 ft) long with a beam of about 60 metres and a laden draft of over 20 metres, which restricts the ports and straits it can use fully loaded; the largest size able to transit the Strait of Malacca fully laden is termed "Malaccamax". VLCCs carry their cargo in a series of segregated tanks protected by double hulls, required for tankers built since the 1990s under MARPOL regulations.

History

The VLCC class emerged in the late 1960s as the closure of the Suez Canal and growing oil demand made ever-larger tankers economical on the long Cape of Good Hope route. Hundreds were built during the tanker boom of the 1970s; the largest ship ever built, the ULCC Seawise Giant (later Knock Nevis), exceeded 550,000 DWT. Modern VLCCs are built mainly in South Korea and China and are frequently used as floating storage when oil markets are in contango.
