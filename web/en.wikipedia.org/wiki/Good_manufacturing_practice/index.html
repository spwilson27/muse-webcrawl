Good manufacturing practice

Good manufacturing practice (GMP) is a system of principles and procedures ensuring that products are consistently produced and controlled according to quality standards appropriate to their intended use. It covers all aspects of production — from starting materials, premises and equipment to staff training, hygiene, documentation and quality control — and is a legal requirement in most countries for manufacturers of pharmaceuticals, medical devices, food and cosmetics. "Current" GMP (cGMP) emphasises that practices must evolve with technology and knowledge.

Principles

GMP regimes generally require that manufacturing processes are clearly defined and validated; that critical steps are controlled and recorded; that facilities and equipment are designed, qualified and maintained to minimise contamination and error; that personnel are trained and follow written procedures (standard operating procedures); that complete batch records allow full traceability; and that systems exist for quality review, handling deviations, complaints and product recalls.

Regulatory frameworks

In the United States, GMP regulations for drugs are set out in 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211, enforced by the Food and Drug Administration. In the European Union, GMP is governed by EudraLex Volume 4 and enforced by national inspectorates under the coordination of the European Medicines Agency. The World Health Organization publishes its own GMP guidelines used by many countries, and the PIC/S (Pharmaceutical Inspection Co-operation Scheme) works to harmonise inspection standards internationally. Related quality systems include Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) and Good Clinical Practice (GCP), collectively known as GxP.

Enforcement

Regulators inspect manufacturing sites, and failures can lead to warning letters, import alerts, product seizures, consent decrees and criminal penalties. Notable GMP failures — such as contamination incidents leading to large recalls — have historically driven tightening of the rules.
