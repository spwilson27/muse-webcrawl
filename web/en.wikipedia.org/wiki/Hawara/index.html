Hawara

Hawara is an archaeological site of Ancient Egypt, in the Faiyum Governorate, at the entrance to the depression of Faiyum, south of the site of Crocodilopolis. Population (2006): approximately 7,300.

Hawara (Arabic: هوارة‎) is a town and archaeological site in the Faiyum region of Egypt. It is best known as the location of the pyramid of the Twelfth Dynasty pharaoh Amenemhat III, the second pyramid built by that king after the so-called Black Pyramid at Dahshur.

The pyramid complex

The pyramid at Hawara, sometimes called the "White Pyramid", was built of mudbrick with a limestone casing, now mostly stripped away. Its internal structure is unusually complex, with corridors and chambers filled with quartzite slabs, a burial chamber carved from a single enormous block of quartzite, and a system of sliding portcullis traps intended to defeat tomb robbers. The complex attached to the pyramid was vast — the mortuary temple to its south was so large and labyrinthine that Greek visitors such as Herodotus and Strabo identified it with the legendary Labyrinth of Egypt, describing some 3,000 rooms.

Excavations

The site was excavated by Karl Richard Lepsius in 1843 and extensively by Flinders Petrie in 1888–1889 and 1910–1911. Petrie discovered the famous Faiyum mummy portraits in the Roman-period cemetery north of the pyramid — naturalistic painted panels attached to mummies, among the finest surviving examples of ancient panel painting. He also recovered papyri, including fragments of the Iliad.
