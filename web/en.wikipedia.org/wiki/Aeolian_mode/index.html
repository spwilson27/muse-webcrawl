Aeolian mode

The Aeolian mode is a musical mode or diatonic scale. It is the sixth mode of the major scale, and in modern usage it is identical to the natural minor scale. On the white keys of a piano, it is the scale running from A to A: A, B, C, D, E, F, G.

Structure

The Aeolian mode follows the pattern of whole steps and half steps: whole, half, whole, whole, half, whole, whole. Its interval formula relative to the tonic is: root, major second, minor third, perfect fourth, perfect fifth, minor sixth, minor seventh. The lowered third, sixth, and seventh degrees distinguish it from the major (Ionian) scale and give it its characteristically sombre quality.

History

The name derives from the ancient Greek Aiolos (Aeolian) tribe and its associated harmonia, though the medieval and modern mode bearing the name does not correspond exactly to the Greek scale. Heinrich Glarean assigned the name "Aeolian" to this mode in his Dodecachordon of 1547. In modern practice, the Aeolian mode is the basis of the natural minor scale in classical music and is ubiquitous in folk, rock, and popular music.

Examples

Well-known melodies in the Aeolian mode include the traditional song "Scarborough Fair" and Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean" (in F♯ Aeolian for much of its harmony).
