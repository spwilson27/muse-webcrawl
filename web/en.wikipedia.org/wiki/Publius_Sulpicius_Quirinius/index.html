Publius Sulpicius Quirinius (c. 51 BC – AD 21) was a Roman aristocrat and administrator under Augustus and Tiberius. He served as consul in 12 BC and later as governor of Syria.

Census of Quirinius

Quirinius is best known for the census he conducted in Judaea in AD 6, after the province came under direct Roman rule. The tax census provoked the revolt of Judas the Galilean, described by Josephus. The census is mentioned in the Gospel of Luke in connection with the nativity of Jesus, which has generated long-standing scholarly debate about chronology, since Herod the Great had died in 4 BC.

Later career

Earlier, Quirinius campaigned successfully against the Homonadenses in Cilicia and served as tutor to Augustus's grandson Gaius Caesar in the East. He died in AD 21.
