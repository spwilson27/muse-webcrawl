Tracy-Widom distribution

The Tracy-Widom distribution (often written with an en dash as the Tracy–Widom distribution) is the limiting probability distribution of the rescaled largest eigenvalue in ensembles of large random matrices. It was derived by Craig Tracy and Harold Widom in 1993–1994 and has become a central object in modern probability, mathematical physics and statistics.

Overview

When the entries of a large Hermitian matrix are random, its eigenvalues repel one another and spread over an interval. The largest eigenvalue fluctuates about the edge of that interval on a characteristic scale, and after rescaling its distribution tends to the Tracy-Widom law. Different versions exist for real symmetric (β = 1), complex Hermitian (β = 2) and quaternionic (β = 4) matrices, matching the symmetry classes of the Gaussian orthogonal, unitary and symplectic ensembles.

Universality

The same distribution governs problems with no obvious matrix structure: the length of the longest increasing subsequence in a random permutation, fluctuations in one-dimensional growth models of the KPZ class, and certain interacting particle systems such as the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process. This universality makes the Tracy-Widom distribution a hallmark of a large class of "edge" fluctuation phenomena.

Statistical use

In multivariate statistics, the Tracy-Widom approximation for the largest eigenvalue of a sample covariance matrix is used, for example, in tests for the number of factors or principal components in high-dimensional data, and in signal detection problems.
