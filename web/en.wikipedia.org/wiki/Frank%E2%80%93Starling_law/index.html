Frank–Starling law

The Frank–Starling law of the heart represents the relationship between stroke volume and end diastolic volume. It states that the stroke volume of the left ventricle increases as the volume of blood in the ventricle increases before contraction, when all other factors remain constant.

Physiology

Greater filling stretches the ventricular wall, increasing preload. At the cellular level, stretching of cardiac myocytes improves the overlap of actin and myosin filaments and increases the sensitivity of troponin C to calcium, so the ensuing contraction is more forceful. This mechanism matches the output of the two ventricles and adjusts cardiac output to venous return.

History

The law is named for physiologists Otto Frank, who described the pressure–volume relationship in the frog heart in 1895, and Ernest Starling, whose work with the heart–lung preparation a generation later established its importance in mammals.
