Coherence length

In physics, coherence length is the propagation distance over which a coherent wave (such as an electromagnetic wave) maintains a specified degree of coherence — that is, a predictable phase relationship. Interference is strong when path differences are shorter than the coherence length and washes out beyond it.

Formulation

Coherence length Lc is approximately λ²/Δλ, where λ is the central wavelength and Δλ the spectral width of the source; equivalently Lc = c·τc with τc the coherence time. Lasers may have coherence lengths of metres to kilometres, while white light's is only a few micrometres, which is why thin-film interference appears in everyday white light but long-baseline interference needs lasers.

Other uses

In superconductivity, the coherence length ξ is the characteristic size of Cooper pairs and sets the scale over which the order parameter can vary — one of the two fundamental lengths (with penetration depth) classifying type-I and type-II superconductors. Similar correlation-length concepts appear in particle physics and critical phenomena.
