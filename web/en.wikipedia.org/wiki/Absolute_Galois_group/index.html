Absolute Galois group

Absolute Galois group
FieldAlgebraic number theory, Galois theory
ObjectProfinite group Gal(Ksep/K)

In mathematics, the absolute Galois group GK of a field K is the Galois group of Ksep over K, where Ksep is a separable closure of K. Alternatively, it is the group of all automorphisms of the algebraic closure of K that fix K. The absolute Galois group is well-defined up to isomorphism, independent of the choice of separable closure.

Definition and structure

The absolute Galois group is the inverse limit, over all finite Galois extensions L/K, of the finite Galois groups Gal(L/K). Equipped with the Krull topology, it is a profinite group. The fundamental theorem of infinite Galois theory gives a bijection between closed subgroups of GK and intermediate extensions of Ksep/K.

Examples

  • The absolute Galois group of a finite field is isomorphic to the profinite completion of the integers, generated by the Frobenius automorphism.
  • The absolute Galois group of the real numbers has order 2, generated by complex conjugation, by the Artin–Schreier theorem.
  • The absolute Galois group of a local field is understood in considerable detail through local class field theory.

Problems

For the field of rational numbers, no algorithm is known for computing GQ; its structure is a central open problem. The inverse Galois problem asks which finite groups occur as quotients of it. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's Cohomology of Number Fields treats GQ in depth.
