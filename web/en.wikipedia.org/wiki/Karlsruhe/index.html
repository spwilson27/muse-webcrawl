Karlsruhe is a city in the German state of Baden-Württemberg, on the Rhine plain near the French border. Founded in 1715 by Margrave Charles III William of Baden-Durlach, it is famous for its fan-shaped Baroque street plan radiating from the palace tower, and it is the seat of Germany's Federal Constitutional Court and Federal Court of Justice, as well as the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT).

History

Legend holds that Charles III William fell asleep while hunting and dreamed of a city laid out like a fan; on 17 June 1715 he laid the foundation stone of Karlsruhe Palace, with 32 streets radiating from its tower. Unusually for its time, the margrave invited settlers with promises of religious tolerance and tax privileges. Karlsruhe became the capital of the Grand Duchy of Baden. In the 19th century it developed into a centre of liberalism and industry; Karl Benz, inventor of the automobile, was born there, and Karl Drais invented the draisine (an early bicycle) in the city.

The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt. In 1951 it became the seat of the Federal Constitutional Court (Bundesverfassungsgericht), earning it the nickname "Residence of the Law" (Residenz des Rechts).

Science and culture

KIT, formed from the University of Karlsruhe and a national research centre, is one of Germany's leading technical universities. The ZKM Center for Art and Media is an internationally known institution for new media art. Karlsruhe's tram-train system, the "Karlsruhe model", pioneered light rail running on mainline tracks.
