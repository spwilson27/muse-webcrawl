The Volksmarine (VM, "People's Navy") was the naval force of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1956 until the country's reunification with West Germany in 1990. It formed the naval branch of the National People's Army (Nationale Volksarmee).

History

The service grew out of the maritime police formations created in 1950 and was formally named Volksmarine in 1960. Its headquarters were at Rostock, with main bases at Peenemünde, Warnemünde and Sassnitz.

Role and fleet

Within Warsaw Pact planning, the Volksmarine was tasked with keeping Baltic sea lanes open, supporting amphibious landings alongside the Soviet Baltic Fleet and Polish Navy, and defending the East German coast. Its fleet emphasised small, fast vessels: missile boats, torpedo boats, minesweepers and coastal anti-submarine ships, plus the larger Koni-class frigates of the Rostock class acquired in the 1970s.

Dissolution

On 3 October 1990 the Volksmarine ceased to exist; a portion of its personnel and some vessels were temporarily taken over by the Bundesmarine, though most ships were sold or scrapped.
