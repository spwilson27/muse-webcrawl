Wilhelm Röntgen

Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen
Born: 27 March 1845, Lennep, Prussia
Died: 10 February 1923, Munich, Germany
Known for: Discovery of X-rays
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1901)

Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen (1845–1923) was a German physicist who discovered electromagnetic radiation in the wavelength range known as X-rays. On 8 November 1895, while experimenting with cathode rays at the University of Würzburg, he noticed a fluorescent screen glowing across the room and identified a new, penetrating kind of ray, which he called "X" for its unknown nature.

Discovery and impact

Within weeks Röntgen had produced the first X-ray image — of his wife's hand, showing her bones and wedding ring — and published his paper "On a New Kind of Rays." The discovery transformed medicine and physics almost immediately. In 1901 Röntgen received the first Nobel Prize in Physics. He refused to patent his discovery, believing it belonged to humanity.

Legacy

The unit of radiation exposure, the roentgen, was named after him, as was the element roentgenium (Rg, atomic number 111).
