Intraperitoneal

Intraperitoneal means within or administered through the peritoneum, the serous membrane that lines the abdominal cavity and covers most of the intra-abdominal organs. Organs described as intraperitoneal are almost completely surrounded by visceral peritoneum and are suspended by mesenteries, in contrast to retroperitoneal organs, which lie behind the peritoneum and are covered by it only on their anterior surface.

Intraperitoneal organs

Structures generally classified as intraperitoneal include the stomach, the first part of the duodenum, the jejunum, ileum, caecum and appendix, the transverse and sigmoid colon, the liver, spleen, and the tail of the pancreas. In females, the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries are also intraperitoneal.

Clinical uses

The intraperitoneal route is used in peritoneal dialysis, in which dialysate fluid is instilled into the peritoneal cavity to remove waste products in kidney failure. Intraperitoneal chemotherapy delivers anticancer drugs directly into the cavity, notably in ovarian cancer, and intraperitoneal injection is a common route of administration in laboratory animals. Complications of intraperitoneal procedures include infection (peritonitis) and bleeding.
