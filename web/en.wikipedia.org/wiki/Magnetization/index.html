Magnetization

In classical electromagnetism, magnetization is the vector field that expresses the density of permanent or induced magnetic dipole moments in a magnetic material. It is commonly denoted M and is defined as the magnetic moment per unit volume of the material.

Definition and units

Magnetization is defined by M = dm/dV, where dm is the magnetic moment within an infinitesimal volume dV. In SI units it is measured in amperes per metre (A/m), the same units as the magnetic field strength H, with which it is related through the magnetic susceptibility χ: M = χH for linear materials. The magnetic flux density B is then given by B = μ0(H + M).

Sources

At the microscopic level, magnetization arises from the magnetic moments of electrons — both their spin and their orbital motion about the nucleus — and, to a much smaller degree, from nuclear moments. In most materials these moments are randomly oriented or paired off, giving no net magnetization; in ferromagnets, the exchange interaction aligns them within magnetic domains.

Hysteresis and remanence

In ferromagnetic materials, magnetization depends on the history of the applied field, tracing a hysteresis loop. When the field is removed, a residual remanent magnetization remains — the basis of permanent magnets and magnetic recording. The field needed to drive the magnetization back to zero is the coercivity.

Demagnetization

Processes that destroy net magnetization include heating above the Curie temperature, hammering or mechanical shock, and applying a decaying alternating field, which walks the material around shrinking hysteresis loops toward zero.
