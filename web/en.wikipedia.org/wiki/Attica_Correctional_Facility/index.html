Attica Correctional Facility

Attica Correctional Facility is a maximum security campus state prison in the town of Attica, New York, United States, operated by the New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision. It was constructed in the 1930s in response to earlier riots within New York prisons.

Location: Attica, Wyoming County, New York

Opened: 1931

Security level: Maximum

Managed by: New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision

History

The prison opened in 1931 and for decades housed many of New York's most serious offenders. A state-of-the-art facility when built, it later became a symbol of the tensions in the American prison system.

1971 uprising

The prison is famous for the Attica Prison riot of September 1971, when about 1,300 inmates seized control of part of the facility and took hostages, demanding better living conditions and political rights. After four days of negotiations, Governor Nelson Rockefeller ordered state police and National Guard troops to retake the prison by force. In total, 43 people died, including 10 correctional officers and civilian employees and 33 inmates, most killed during the retaking. The uprising and its aftermath had a lasting influence on prison reform debates and litigation in the United States.

Present day

Attica remains one of New York's largest and best-known maximum-security prisons. It has housed numerous notorious offenders over the years and continues to feature in public discussion of incarceration and criminal justice reform.
