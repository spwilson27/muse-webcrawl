The Carlist Wars (Spanish: Guerras Carlistas) were a series of civil wars in 19th-century Spain between supporters of the Infante Carlos, Count of Molina — who claimed the throne after his brother Ferdinand VII altered the succession in favour of his daughter Isabella II — and the liberal government of Isabella and her successors. Carlism, rallying under the motto "God, Fatherland, King", defended traditional monarchy, Catholicism and the regional privileges (fueros) of the Basque provinces and Navarre against liberal centralism.

First Carlist War (1833–1840)

The longest and bloodiest of the wars, fought mainly in the north under Carlist generals such as Tomás de Zumalacárregui. Britain, France and Portugal supported the Isabeline government under the Quadruple Alliance, and British Auxiliary Legion volunteers fought in Spain. The war ended with the Convention of Vergara (1839), arranged by the liberal general Baldomero Espartero, and the final Carlist defeat in 1840.

Second Carlist War (1846–1849)

Also known in Catalonia as the War of the Matiners, this was a smaller, largely Catalan uprising in support of Carlos VI, Count of Montemolín, partly triggered by the failure of plans to marry him to Isabella II.

Third Carlist War (1872–1876)

Fought under Carlos VII during the upheavals that followed the 1868 revolution and the First Spanish Republic. The restoration of Alfonso XII and internal Carlist divisions brought defeat in 1876, after which the Basque fueros were abolished. Carlism nevertheless survived as a political movement and Carlist requetés fought for Franco in the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939).
