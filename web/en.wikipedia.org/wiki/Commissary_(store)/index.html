Commissary (store)

A commissary is a store for selling provisions, originally connected with an army or military establishment. In the United States armed forces, commissaries are supermarkets that sell groceries and household goods to military personnel, retirees and their families at reduced prices; they are operated by the Defense Commissary Agency (DeCA).

History

Commissary privileges date to 1825, when the United States Army began selling goods at cost from its stores; the modern commissary system developed after the American Civil War. Sales are made at cost plus a surcharge, funding construction and maintenance of the stores.

Other uses

The term also refers to shops in prisons and remote work camps, and to company stores serving employees of factories, mines and film studios.
