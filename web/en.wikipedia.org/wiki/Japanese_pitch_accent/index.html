Japanese pitch accent

[Image: Pitch accent patterns in Japanese]

Japanese pitch accent (高低アクセント, kōtei akusento) is a feature of spoken Japanese. In Japan, it is a distinctive, regionally varying phonological property in which the pitch of successive morae in a word may differ from one another. In pitch-accent languages, the location of a fall in pitch can distinguish otherwise identical words.

Standard (Tokyo) accent

In Tokyo Japanese, words are described by the position of the "accent nucleus", the mora after which pitch drops from high to low. Words fall into patterns such as heiban (flat, no drop), atamadaka (drop after the first mora), nakadaka (drop in the middle), and odaka (drop after the final mora, heard on a following particle). For example, はし hashi may mean "chopsticks", "bridge", or "edge" depending on its accent pattern.

Regional variation

Pitch accent varies considerably across Japan. The Kansai (Kyoto-Osaka) system differs from Tokyo's, for example in the initial pitch of words. Some regions, such as parts of Tohoku and Kyushu, have systems quite distinct from both Tokyo and Kansai, while a few dialects lack lexical pitch accent entirely.

Learning pitch accent

Japanese dictionaries often mark accent numbers for headwords, and broadcasters such as NHK publish accent dictionaries for announcers. Pitch accent is a common difficulty for second-language learners, since textbooks rarely teach it systematically even though misplaced accents can sound foreign or change meaning.
