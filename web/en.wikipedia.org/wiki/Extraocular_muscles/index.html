Extraocular muscles

The extraocular muscles are the seven extrinsic muscles of the human eye. Six of them control the movement of the eyeball, and the seventh, the levator palpebrae superioris, controls the movement of the eyelid. The actions of the six muscles responsible for eye movement depend on the position of the eye at the time of muscle contraction.

The four rectus muscles arise from the annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex and insert on the front of the eyeball; the superior oblique's tendon passes through a fibrous pulley, the trochlea, giving the muscle its name and its unusual action. A common mnemonic for the innervation is "LR6 SO4" — lateral rectus by nerve VI, superior oblique by nerve IV, and the rest by nerve III.

Coordination and clinical relevance

Eye movements are yoked between the two eyes by Hering's law of equal innervation, so that both eyes move together in versions and vergences. Palsy of one of the supplying cranial nerves produces strabismus (misalignment) and diplopia (double vision) in characteristic patterns, making extraocular muscle testing a core part of the neurological examination. Disorders such as Graves' ophthalmopathy can thicken and restrict the muscles themselves.
