Howell Davis

Howell Davis
Born: c. 1690, Milford Haven, Wales
Died: 19 June 1719, Príncipe, Portuguese Guinea
Type: Pirate
Years active: 1718–1719
Base of operations: Caribbean, West African coast

Howell Davis (c. 1690 – 19 June 1719) was a Welsh pirate who operated in the Caribbean and on the coast of West Africa during the Golden Age of Piracy. His career was short but exceptionally successful, and he preferred cunning and deception to violence when taking prizes.

Career

Davis was chief mate aboard the slave ship Cadogan when it was captured by the pirate Edward England; joining the pirates, he was given command of the captured vessel. After a mutiny and various adventures — including being imprisoned in Barbados on suspicion of piracy and escaping — he worked his way up the West African coast, taking ships off the Gambia, Sierra Leone and the Gold Coast, often by flying false colours or posing as a legitimate trader or pirate hunter.

Bartholomew Roberts

Davis took the young Bartholomew Roberts under his wing after capturing the slave ship on which Roberts was serving as third mate in 1719; Roberts would succeed Davis and become one of the most famous pirates of the era.

Death

On 19 June 1719 Davis was ambushed and killed by Portuguese forces on the island of Príncipe, where he had planned to kidnap the governor while posing as a Royal Navy pirate-hunting captain. His crew elected Roberts captain in his place.
