Kyocera

Kyocera Corporation (京セラ株式会社, Kyōsera Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese multinational ceramics and electronics manufacturer headquartered in Kyoto. It was founded in 1959 by Kazuo Inamori as Kyoto Ceramic Company, initially producing fine ceramic insulators for television picture tubes.

Business

Kyocera grew into a diversified conglomerate whose products include ceramic components and semiconductor packages, electronic devices, solar cells, cutting tools, printers and copiers (through Kyocera Document Solutions), mobile phones, and medical and automotive components. It acquired the American manufacturer AVX in 1990 and is the originator of the "amoeba management" system promoted by Inamori.

Philosophy and sponsorship

The company is known for its corporate philosophy, the "Kyocera Philosophy", which emphasises ethical management. Kyocera has sponsored Kyoto Sanga F.C. and held naming rights to the Kyocera Dome Osaka and the former Kyocera Stadion in the Netherlands.
