Non-player character

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A non-player character shopkeeper, a staple of role-playing games.

A non-player character (NPC) is a character in a game that is not controlled by a player. The term originated in tabletop role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, where it refers to characters controlled by the gamemaster (or referee) rather than by the players. In video games, NPCs are controlled by the computer, typically through scripted behaviour or artificial intelligence.

Tabletop role-playing games

In traditional tabletop role-playing games, the gamemaster portrays all NPCs: innkeepers, villains, monsters, quest-givers and bystanders. NPCs provide the world with inhabitants for the player characters to interact with, oppose, befriend or rescue. Gamemasters often prepare statistics and personality notes for important NPCs in advance, while minor characters may be improvised on the spot. Non-player characters controlled by a player on behalf of the group, or by the gamemaster as a party member, are sometimes called "GMPCs" or henchmen and hirelings.

Video games

In video games, NPCs range from simple background figures with a single line of dialogue to complex companions with branching conversation trees, schedules and simulated needs. Common roles include merchants, quest-givers, trainers, guards, enemies and ambient townsfolk. Their behaviour is usually driven by scripts, finite-state machines, behaviour trees or, in more recent games, machine-learning systems. The quality of NPC behaviour is often cited as a major contributor to a game's sense of immersion; the term "NPC" has also entered popular slang, where it is used (often pejoratively) to describe a person perceived as lacking independent thought.

LARP

In live action role-playing games (LARPs), NPCs are played by members of the organising crew rather than by paying participants, and are used to drive plot, portray antagonists and staff in-game locations.
