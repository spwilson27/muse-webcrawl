Cardiac pacemaker
TypeImplantable medical device
FunctionRegulates heartbeat with electrical impulses
First human implant1958 (Åke Senning and Rune Elmqvist, Sweden)

A cardiac pacemaker is an implanted medical device that generates electrical impulses delivered by electrodes to one or more chambers of the heart, causing the targeted chamber to contract and pump blood. By doing so, the pacemaker regulates the function of the heart's electrical conduction system when it is too slow, irregular, or blocked.

History

The first fully implantable pacemaker was implanted in 1958 in Stockholm by surgeon Åke Senning, using a device designed by Rune Elmqvist; the patient, Arne Larsson, survived to 2001 after receiving many replacement devices. In the United States, Wilson Greatbatch's implantable design of 1960 transformed the field. Modern devices are small, titanium-cased units powered by lithium-iodine batteries lasting 7–15 years.

Types

Single-chamber pacemakers pace one chamber (usually the right ventricle); dual-chamber devices pace atrium and ventricle; biventricular pacemakers (cardiac resynchronisation therapy) pace both ventricles in heart failure. Leadless pacemakers, implanted directly in the right ventricle via catheter, avoid transvenous leads altogether.

Indications

Common indications include symptomatic bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, and high-grade atrioventricular block.
