Belle-Île-en-Mer, usually called Belle Île, is a French island in the Bay of Biscay off the coast of Brittany in the department of Morbihan. It is the largest of Brittany's islands, measuring about 17 by 9 kilometres, with a rugged southern "wild coast" facing the Atlantic and gentler beaches on its sheltered northern side.

History

The island was occupied in prehistoric times and fortified over the centuries; its citadel at Le Palais was strengthened under Vauban. During the Seven Years' War, British forces captured Belle Île in 1761 and held it until 1763, when it was returned to France in exchange for Menorca. In the 19th century the island became a fashionable retreat for artists and writers; Claude Monet painted its rock formations in 1886, and Sarah Bernhardt kept a home there.

Geography and tourism

The island's four communes are Le Palais, Sauzon, Bangor and Locmaria. Ferries connect Le Palais with Quiberon on the mainland. Coastal paths (the GR 340) circle the island past landmarks such as the Aiguilles de Port-Coton sea stacks, the Pointe des Poulains lighthouse and the sheltered Donnant beach. Tourism dominates the modern economy, and the population swells greatly in summer.
