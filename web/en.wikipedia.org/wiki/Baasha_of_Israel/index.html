Baasha of Israel

Baasha (Hebrew: בַּעְשָׁא, Baʿšāʾ) was the third king of the northern Kingdom of Israel. He reigned for twenty-four years in the late 10th century BCE (conventionally dated to around 909–886 BCE). Baasha, the son of Ahijah of the tribe of Issachar, seized the throne by assassinating Nadab, the son of Jeroboam I, while he besieged the Philistine city of Gibbethon.

Reign

After taking power, Baasha destroyed the entire house of Jeroboam, fulfilling — according to the biblical narrative — the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite. He made Tirzah his capital and spent much of his reign at war with Asa, king of Judah. When Baasha fortified Ramah to blockade Judah, Asa bribed Ben-Hadad I of Aram-Damascus to attack Israel from the north, forcing Baasha to withdraw; Asa then dismantled Ramah's fortifications and used the materials to fortify Geba and Mizpah.

The prophet Jehu son of Hanani pronounced judgment on Baasha's house for its idolatry. Baasha was succeeded by his son Elah, who was soon assassinated by Zimri, ending the dynasty after only two reigns.
