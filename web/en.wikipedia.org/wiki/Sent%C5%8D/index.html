Sentō

A sentō (銭湯) is a type of Japanese communal bathhouse where customers pay for entrance. Bathers wash thoroughly outside the tubs before soaking in large communal hot-water baths, with facilities strictly separated by gender.

History

Public baths trace their roots to Buddhist temple baths of the Nara period. The modern sentō developed in the Edo period, when most homes lacked baths; Tokyo once had more than 2,600 sentō, though numbers have fallen to only a few hundred as private bathing became universal.

Etiquette and features

Customers undress in a changing room, wash at rows of taps and stools, and only then enter the baths. Many sentō feature a painted mural, traditionally of Mount Fuji, a custom begun in 1912 at Tokyo's Kikai-yu bathhouse.

Decline and revival

Despite decline, some sentō have been repurposed as cultural venues, cafés and "super sentō" spa complexes, keeping the tradition alive.
