Impulse purchase

An impulse purchase or impulse buy is an unplanned decision by a consumer to buy a product or service, made just before a purchase. Such purchases tend to be emotional rather than the result of considered comparison shopping, and they form a significant share of all consumer purchases in many retail categories.

History and research

One of the earliest definitions came from the DuPont Consumer Buying Habits studies (1948–1965), which classified purchases by the degree of planning involved. Later researchers, notably Dennis Rook (1987), described impulse buying as a sudden, powerful, and persistent urge to buy something immediately, often accompanied by emotional conflict and diminished regard for consequences.

Retail strategies

Retailers encourage impulse purchases through product placement near checkouts, point-of-sale displays, limited-time offers, and prominent pricing. Goods commonly bought on impulse include sweets, magazines, batteries, and small accessories. Online retail uses similar techniques, such as one-click purchasing, countdown timers, and personalised recommendations.

Psychology

Impulse buying is associated with personality traits such as impulsivity and susceptibility to influence, as well as mood states. It overlaps with the study of impulse control, and in extreme, repeated forms can shade into compulsive buying disorder. Researchers distinguish pure impulse buying, reminder impulse buying, suggestion impulse buying, and planned impulse buying.
