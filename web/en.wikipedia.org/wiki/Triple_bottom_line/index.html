Triple bottom line

The triple bottom line (TBL or 3BL) is an accounting and business framework that evaluates an organization's performance in three dimensions: social, environmental (or ecological), and economic — often summarized as people, planet, and profit. The phrase was coined by John Elkington in 1994 and popularized in his 1997 book Cannibals with Forks.

The three Ps

  • People — fair and beneficial business practices toward labour, the community, and the region in which the organization operates.
  • Planet — sustainable environmental practices: reducing the ecological footprint, managing resource consumption, and minimizing harm across the product life cycle.
  • Profit — the economic value created, calculated in a way that accounts for the costs of natural and social capital as well as ordinary financial results.

Criticism and development

Critics note that the three dimensions are difficult to measure in a common unit and can be used superficially as a public-relations device. Elkington himself issued a partial "product recall" of the concept in 2018, arguing it had been diluted. Related frameworks include ESG reporting, social enterprise measurement, and the Global Reporting Initiative standards.
