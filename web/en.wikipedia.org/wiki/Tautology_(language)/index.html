Tautology (language)

In language and rhetoric, a tautology is the needless repetition of an idea, statement or word — saying the same thing twice in different words. Examples include "free gift", "past history", "PIN number" and "they arrived one after the other in succession".

Usage

Tautologies are often condemned as redundant in style guides, yet they serve legitimate purposes: emphasis ("safe and sound"), clarity for non-native speakers, legal precision, and fixed idiomatic expressions. Some arise from combining words from different languages, as in "chai tea" or "Sahara desert", where the borrowed word already contains the meaning of its companion.

Related concepts

Tautology contrasts with the pleonasm (superfluous words generally) and with contradiction. In logic, by contrast, a tautology is a statement true in every possible interpretation, a different technical sense of the term.
