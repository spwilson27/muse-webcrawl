Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland

The Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland (RCSI) is a not-for-profit medical professional and educational institution, which is also known as RCSI University of Medicine and Health Sciences, Ireland's first private university. It was established in 1784 as the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland with a charter from George III "to promote surgical education and training in Ireland". Its main campus is located on St Stephen's Green and York Street in central Dublin, Ireland.

History

Before 1784, surgery in Ireland was regulated by the Dublin Society of Surgeons and its origins lay in the barber-surgeons' guild, the Guild of St Mary Magdalene. The College was founded to provide a proper system of surgical education, independent of the physicians' college (the Royal College of Physicians of Ireland). Its landmark building at 123 St Stephen's Green was constructed in the early 19th century and was famously occupied by rebels during the 1916 Easter Rising, commanded by Countess Markievicz; bullet marks remain visible on the facade.

In 2019 the institution was granted university status, becoming RCSI University of Medicine and Health Sciences, the first private university in Ireland, while retaining its role as a postgraduate surgical training body.

Education

RCSI offers undergraduate degrees in medicine, pharmacy and physiotherapy, and an extensive range of postgraduate programmes in surgery, medicine, nursing, radiology and other health sciences. It is one of Ireland's largest medical schools, with a notably international student body drawn from more than 60 countries. RCSI also operates campuses and programmes overseas, including RCSI Bahrain, RCSI Dubai, and a long-standing partnership in Penang, Malaysia (RCSI & UCD Malaysia Campus).

Training and professional role

As a royal college, RCSI is responsible for the training and examination of surgeons in Ireland, awarding the Fellowship (FRCSI) and Membership (MRCSI) qualifications, and setting standards for surgical practice. It also hosts national training programmes in specialties such as emergency medicine, ophthalmology, dentistry through its Faculty of Dentistry, and nursing and midwifery.
