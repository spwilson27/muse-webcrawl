Semantic search

Semantic search denotes search with meaning, as distinguished from lexical search where a search engine finds literal matches of query words. Semantic search seeks to improve accuracy by understanding the searcher's intent and the contextual meaning of terms, whether on the World Wide Web or within a closed system.

Approaches

Techniques include query expansion with synonyms, concept-based indexing, natural-language question answering, and the use of ontologies and knowledge graphs—most prominently Google's Knowledge Graph, introduced in 2012 under the slogan "things, not strings." Modern systems embed queries and documents into dense vector spaces using neural language models, retrieving items by semantic similarity rather than keyword overlap, often combined with traditional ranking in hybrid retrieval architectures.

Applications

Semantic search powers web search engines, enterprise search, e-commerce product discovery, legal and medical document retrieval, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that supply large language models with relevant source material.
