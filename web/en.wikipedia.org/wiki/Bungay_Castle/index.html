Bungay Castle

Bungay Castle
Location: Bungay, Suffolk, England
Built: ~1100; keep c. 1140s; gatehouse 13th century
Builder: Roger Bigod; Hugh Bigod, 1st Earl of Norfolk
Condition: Ruined

Bungay Castle is a ruined castle in the town of Bungay in Suffolk, England, overlooking a loop of the River Waveney. It was founded around 1100 by the Norman lord Roger Bigod and became a principal stronghold of the Bigod earls of Norfolk.

History

Hugh Bigod, 1st Earl of Norfolk, rebuilt the castle with a large square Norman keep during the 1140s amid the civil war of the Anarchy. Hugh rebelled against Henry II in the revolt of 1173–74, after which the castle was besieged and its keep undermined and slighted. The Bigods recovered the site and added a twin-towered gatehouse in the 13th century, but the castle declined after Roger Bigod, 5th Earl of Norfolk, surrendered his estates to Edward I in 1302. It later passed through several owners, decayed into a picturesque ruin, and came into the care of the Bungay Castle Trust in the 20th century.

Legend

Bungay Castle is associated with the legend of Black Shuck, the spectral black dog said to have attacked St Mary's Church in Bungay in 1577, and Hugh Bigod is reputed in local rhyme to have taunted the king from its walls: "Were I in my castle of Bungay, upon the river of Waveney, I would ne care for the King of Cockney."
