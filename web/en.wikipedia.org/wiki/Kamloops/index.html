Kamloops is a city in south-central British Columbia, Canada, at the confluence of the North and South Thompson rivers near Kamloops Lake. Its name derives from the Shuswap (Secwepemc) word Tk'emlúps, "meeting of the waters". The city is the seat of the Thompson-Nicola Regional District and a transportation hub where the Trans-Canada Highway, the Yellowhead Highway and two national railways converge.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Secwepemc people, whose Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc band's reserve adjoins the city. Fur traders established posts here from 1811 onward; the Hudson's Bay Company's Fort Kamloops became an important post. A gold rush, ranching, and the arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1885 spurred growth, and Kamloops was incorporated in 1893.

Economy and culture

Forestry, mining (including the nearby Highland Valley Copper mine), ranching, health care, Thompson Rivers University and tourism anchor the economy. Kamloops styles itself the "Tournament Capital of Canada" for its many sports facilities. In 2021 the Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc First Nation announced findings of potential unmarked graves at the site of the former Kamloops Indian Residential School, drawing national and international attention.
