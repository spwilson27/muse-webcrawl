Melodic minor scale

In music theory, the melodic minor scale is a musical scale derived from the natural minor scale by raising the sixth and seventh degrees when ascending, while (in traditional classical practice) reverting to the natural minor form when descending. It arose to create smoother melodic lines toward the tonic and to provide a major dominant chord in minor keys, avoiding the awkward augmented second interval of the harmonic minor scale.

Structure

In A, the ascending melodic minor scale runs A–B–C–D–E–F♯–G♯–A: compared with A natural minor, the F and G are sharpened. Descending in the classical convention, it returns through G♮ and F♮. The interval pattern ascending is whole–half–whole–whole–whole–whole–half.

Jazz melodic minor

In jazz theory, the melodic minor scale is generally used in the same form both ascending and descending, and is sometimes called the "jazz minor scale." It is a major source of melodic and harmonic material: its modes include the Dorian ♭2 (Phrygian ♮6), Lydian augmented, Lydian dominant, Mixolydian ♭6, Locrian ♮2 (half-diminished), and the altered scale (Super Locrian), the last being widely used over dominant seventh chords resolving to a tonic.

Modes

  1. Melodic minor (jazz minor)
  2. Dorian ♭2
  3. Lydian augmented
  4. Lydian dominant (overtone scale)
  5. Mixolydian ♭6
  6. Locrian ♮2
  7. Altered scale
