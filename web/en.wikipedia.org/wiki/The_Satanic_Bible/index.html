The Satanic Bible

Author: Anton Szandor LaVey
Published: 1969, Avon Books

The Satanic Bible is a book by Anton Szandor LaVey, founder of the Church of Satan, published in 1969. It sets out the philosophy of LaVeyan Satanism — an atheistic worldview in which "Satan" symbolizes self-indulgence, individualism, and earthly values rather than a literal deity — and describes rituals for its adherents.

Contents

The book is divided into four sections named for the four crown princes of Hell in its system: The Book of Satan, The Book of Lucifer, The Book of Belial, and The Book of Leviathan. The opening "Book of Satan" draws heavily (with edits) on Ragnar Redbeard's 1896 social-Darwinist tract Might Is Right. The volume includes the Nine Satanic Statements, the Eleven Satanic Rules of the Earth, and ritual material drawing on the Enochian Keys of John Dee.

Reception and influence

Though born of a marketing assignment from Avon Books, the work has remained continuously in print, has sold over a million copies, and is the central scripture of the Church of Satan and a foundational text for modern religious Satanism. Critics have debated its originality and LaVey's self-mythologizing, but its cultural influence — on metal music, occult subcultures, and later Satanic organizations — is widely acknowledged.
