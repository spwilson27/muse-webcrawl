The flexbone formation is an offensive formation in American football that is a variation of the wishbone formation. It features a fullback lined up directly behind the quarterback, two slotbacks (or "A-backs") aligned just outside and behind the offensive tackles, and two wide receivers split wide.

Description

The flexbone is the base formation of the triple option offence run by coaches such as Paul Johnson at Georgia Tech and by the service academies, notably Navy and Army. On a standard play, the quarterback reads the defence while deciding whether to hand off to the fullback on the dive, keep the ball around the end, or pitch to a trailing slotback, giving the offence three options on a single play. The slotbacks frequently go in motion before the snap, which also makes them threats as receivers and runners on counters and sweeps.

History

The formation evolved from Emory Bellard's wishbone at Texas in the late 1960s; it took its modern shape when coaches moved the halfbacks up toward the line as slotbacks, allowing a more credible passing game. It remains associated with option football and with teams that seek to control the clock and compensate for size disadvantages.
