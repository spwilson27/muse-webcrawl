Max Roach

Maxwell Lemuel Roach (January 10, 1924 – August 16, 2007) was an American jazz drummer and composer. A pioneer of bebop, he is regarded as one of the most important drummers in history, known for his virtuoso technique, melodic approach to the drums, and later work in civil-rights-themed music and percussion ensembles.

Early life and bebop

Roach was born in Newland, North Carolina, and raised in Brooklyn, New York. He studied piano as a child and drums at the Manhattan School of Music. By his late teens he was playing in the clubs of 52nd Street, and with Kenny Clarke he revolutionised jazz drumming: moving the timekeeping role to the ride cymbal, punctuating with bass-drum "bombs", and freeing the drums for interactive, conversational playing. He recorded with Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, Coleman Hawkins and Miles Davis, including the sessions that became Birth of the Cool.

Hard bop and later work

In 1954 Roach co-founded a quintet with trumpeter Clifford Brown, one of the defining hard bop groups; it ended tragically with Brown's death in a car accident in 1956. Roach's subsequent recordings include Money Jungle (1962) with Duke Ellington and Charles Mingus, and the politically charged We Insist! — Freedom Now Suite (1960), recorded with his wife, singer Abbey Lincoln, addressing the civil rights movement. He founded the all-percussion ensemble M'Boom in 1970, experimented with solo drum composition, and taught at the University of Massachusetts Amherst.

Legacy

Roach received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 1988 and numerous other honours. He died in New York City in 2007. His melodic, compositional approach — treating the drum kit as a complete instrument capable of theme and development — permanently changed jazz drumming.
