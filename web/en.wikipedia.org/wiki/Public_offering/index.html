Public offering

A public offering is the offering of securities — shares, bonds or other instruments — to the general public, as opposed to a private placement to a limited group of investors. The best-known form is the initial public offering (IPO), in which a company first sells its shares to the public and lists them on a stock exchange.

Regulation

Public offerings are heavily regulated to protect investors: issuers must typically register the offering with a securities regulator, publish a prospectus disclosing financial and risk information, and comply with continuing reporting obligations afterwards.

Subsequent offerings

Companies already listed may raise further capital through follow-on or secondary public offerings, and governments and companies issue bonds to the public in the same way.
