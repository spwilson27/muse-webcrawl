Written on Skin

Composer: George Benjamin
Librettist: Martin Crimp
Premiere: 7 July 2012, Aix-en-Provence Festival
Acts: 3, in 15 scenes

Written on Skin is an opera in three parts by the British composer George Benjamin, to a libretto by Martin Crimp. It premiered at the Aix-en-Provence Festival on 7 July 2012, conducted by the composer, and quickly became one of the most acclaimed and widely performed new operas of the 21st century.

Story

The libretto adapts a 12th-century troubadour legend from Provence, the tale of Guillem de Cabestaing, retold in Boccaccio's Decameron. A wealthy landowner, the Protector, commissions an illuminator — the Boy — to create a book celebrating his property and his obedient wife, Agnès. The Boy and Agnès begin an affair, which they record in the book itself. When the Protector discovers the truth, he kills the Boy and feeds his heart to Agnès, who, on learning what she has eaten, leaps to her death. A chorus of Angels frames and comments on the action.

Music and reception

Benjamin's score, for a large orchestra including glass harmonica and viola da gamba, was praised for its luminous, sensuous orchestration and dramatic precision. Critics called the work a modern masterpiece; it has been staged by the Royal Opera, the Opéra national de Paris, the Bolshoi, and many other houses, and was later released commercially on record and DVD.
