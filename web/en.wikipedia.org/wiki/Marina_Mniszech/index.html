Marina Mniszech (Polish: Maryna Mniszchówna; Russian: Марина Мнишек; c. 1588 – 24 December 1614) was a Polish noblewoman who became Tsaritsa of Russia for a brief period in 1606 as the wife of False Dmitry I, the pretender to the Russian throne during the Time of Troubles. She was the first woman to be crowned Tsaritsa in Russia in her own ceremony and remains one of the most controversial figures of the period, remembered in Russian tradition often under the name "Marinka the Witch".

Background

Marina was the daughter of Jerzy Mniszech, voivode of Sandomierz, a magnate of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. When the man claiming to be Tsarevich Dmitry Ivanovich, the youngest son of Ivan the Terrible, sought Polish support for his claim, the Mniszech family backed him; the alliance was sealed by Dmitry's promise to marry Marina and to grant her family vast estates and wealth, together with undertakings favourable to Catholic interests.

Marriage and coronation

Marina married Dmitry by proxy in Kraków in November 1605, and travelled to Moscow in the spring of 1606. On 8 May 1606 she was crowned Tsaritsa in the Kremlin's Dormition Cathedral — the first coronation of a tsar's wife in Russian history — and the wedding was celebrated the same day. Her refusal to convert from Catholicism to Orthodoxy, and the behaviour of her large Polish retinue, provoked hostility among Muscovites.

The Time of Troubles

Only days after the wedding, on 17 May 1606, a popular uprising led by Vasily Shuisky overthrew Dmitry, who was killed. Marina survived the massacre of Poles in Moscow and was eventually released and returned to her father in 1608. She then joined the camp of the second False Dmitry at Tushino, where she publicly "recognised" him as her miraculously saved husband and secretly married him. She bore a son, Ivan Dmitrievich, in January 1611.

After the death of the second pretender, Marina fled with the Cossack leader Ivan Zarutsky, who proclaimed her son tsar. They were captured in 1614; Zarutsky and the three-year-old Ivan were executed, and Marina died in captivity at Kolomna, according to tradition of grief — or, in darker legends, by curse or drowning.

Legacy

Marina's life was dramatised in Pushkin's play Boris Godunov and in Mussorgsky's opera of the same name, where the "Polish act" centres on her ambition and her manipulation of Dmitry.
