Erythroblastosis fetalis

Maternal antibodies crossing the placenta can destroy fetal red blood cells.

Erythroblastosis fetalis
Other namesHaemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN)
SpecialtyObstetrics, neonatology, haematology
CausesMaternal alloimmunisation against fetal red-cell antigens, esp. RhD

Erythroblastosis fetalis, more generally termed haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN), is an alloimmune condition that develops when maternal IgG antibodies directed against fetal red blood cell antigens cross the placenta and destroy fetal erythrocytes. The name derives from the appearance of large numbers of immature nucleated red cells (erythroblasts) in the fetal circulation as the marrow responds to anaemia.

Causes

The classic cause is RhD incompatibility: an Rh-negative mother sensitised to the D antigen—usually during delivery of an Rh-positive first child—produces anti-D antibodies that attack the red cells of an Rh-positive fetus in a subsequent pregnancy. ABO incompatibility and other antigens such as Kell (K1), c, E and Fya can also cause the disease; anti-Kell disease additionally suppresses fetal erythropoiesis.

Pathophysiology

Progressive haemolysis produces fetal anaemia, extramedullary haematopoiesis, hepatosplenomegaly and, in severe cases, high-output cardiac failure with generalised oedema—hydrops fetalis. After birth, continued destruction of red cells raises bilirubin, risking kernicterus if untreated.

Diagnosis

Antenatal screening identifies maternal antibodies; titres and middle cerebral artery Doppler velocimetry estimate fetal anaemia. The direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test on cord blood is typically positive in the newborn.

Prevention and treatment

Routine antenatal and postnatal administration of anti-D immunoglobulin to Rh-negative mothers has dramatically reduced RhD disease since the late 1960s. Affected fetuses may receive intrauterine transfusions; newborns are treated with phototherapy, intravenous immunoglobulin and, when necessary, exchange transfusion.
