The Padma (Bengali: পদ্মা, Padmā) is a major river in Bangladesh and the main distributary of the Ganges in the country. It flows generally southeast for about 356 kilometres (221 miles) to its confluence with the Meghna River near Chandpur. The city of Rajshahi is situated on the banks of the river.

Course

Though the name Padma is used for the stretch of the lower Ganges from the point where it is joined by the Jamuna (the lower course of the Brahmaputra) in some local usages, geographically it refers to the section from the Ganges' bifurcation point at the India–Bangladesh border near Shibganj down to the confluence with the Meghna. From there, the combined stream flows to the Bay of Bengal under the name Meghna.

Ferry crossings and bridges

The Padma is extremely wide and difficult to bridge. Historically, ferry crossings at Goalundo and Mawa were major bottlenecks on routes between Dhaka and southwestern Bangladesh. The Padma Bridge, a multipurpose road-rail bridge across the river between Louhajong and Zajira, was inaugurated on 25 June 2022, providing the first fixed crossing of the river. The bridge is the longest in Bangladesh and across the Ganges.

Culture

The river features prominently in Bengali literature and music. It is the setting of Manik Bandopadhyay's classic 1936 novel Padma Nadir Majhi ("The Boatman of the Padma"), which depicts the lives of fishermen and boatmen along the river. Hilsa fishing on the Padma is economically and culturally important in Bangladesh.

Erosion

The Padma is one of the most erosion-prone rivers in the world; its channels shift continuously, and bank erosion regularly displaces riverside communities and destroys farmland, particularly in the monsoon season.
