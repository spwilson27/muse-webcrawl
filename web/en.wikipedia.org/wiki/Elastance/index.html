Elastance

Electrical elastance is the inverse of capacitance. The SI unit of elastance is the inverse farad (F−1). The concept is not widely used by electrical and electronic engineers, as the value of capacitors is usually specified in units of capacitance; however, elastance is useful in theoretical work and when capacitors are combined in series.

Definition

Elastance S is defined as

S = V / Q = 1 / C,

where V is the voltage across a capacitor, Q the charge stored, and C the capacitance. Oliver Heaviside coined the term in the 1880s as part of his systematic naming of electromagnetic quantities and their reciprocals — resistance/reactance, conductance/susceptance, inductance, and elastance among them.

Usage

Elastances, like resistances, add when components are connected in series, which occasionally simplifies calculations. The term also survives in mechanical and physiological analogies: in respiratory physiology, elastance of the lungs or chest wall is the reciprocal of compliance, expressing the change in pressure per unit change in volume. Heaviside also proposed the unit name "daraf" (farad spelled backwards) for elastance, which was never officially adopted; the inverse farad remains the standard unit.
