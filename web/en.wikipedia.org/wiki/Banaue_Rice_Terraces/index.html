The Banaue Rice Terraces (Filipino: Hagdan-hagdang Palayan ng Banawe) are terraces carved into the mountains of Banaue, Ifugao, in the Philippines by the ancestors of the Igorot people. They are frequently described in the Philippines as the "Eighth Wonder of the World" and appear on the reverse of the Philippine twenty-peso note. Unlike the nearby terrace clusters of Batad and Bangaan, the Banaue terraces are not inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, which instead covers five other Ifugao terrace clusters under "Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras".

Description

The terraces rise some 1,500 metres above sea level and form part of a vast system fed by an ancient irrigation network drawing water from the rainforests above. They were built largely by hand with minimal equipment, using stone and mud retaining walls. Popular accounts long dated them to about 2,000 years ago, though more recent scholarship has suggested that large-scale terrace construction may be considerably younger, expanding after the arrival of the Spanish in the 16th century.

Culture and maintenance

The terraces are maintained through cooperative labour and traditional rituals led by the Ifugao, who grow heirloom rice varieties such as tinawon. Maintenance has been threatened by erosion, giant earthworms, drought caused by El Niño, and the migration of young Ifugao away from farming, which led to stretches of abandoned paddies; restoration programmes by government and private groups aim to preserve them.
