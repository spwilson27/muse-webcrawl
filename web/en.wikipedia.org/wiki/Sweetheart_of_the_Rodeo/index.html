Sweetheart of the Rodeo is the sixth studio album by the American rock band the Byrds, released in August 1968. Recorded after the arrival of singer and songwriter Gram Parsons, it is widely considered the first major country rock album and one of the most influential records of its era.

Background and recording

Parsons, hired as a sideman, pushed the band toward his vision of "Cosmic American Music" — a fusion of country, soul, gospel and rock and roll. Sessions began in Nashville in March 1968 with veteran session players including pedal steel guitarist Lloyd Green, followed by further recording in Los Angeles. Parsons' lead vocals were partly re-recorded by Roger McGuinn amid a contract dispute over Parsons' status with another label.

Track listing highlights

The album leans heavily on covers: Dylan's "You Ain't Goin' Nowhere" and "Nothing Was Delivered", the Louvin Brothers' "The Christian Life", Woody Guthrie's "Pretty Boy Floyd", Merle Haggard's "Life in Prison", and two Parsons originals, "Hickory Wind" and "One Hundred Years from Now". Though a poor seller at the time — peaking outside the US top 70 — it became a cornerstone of the country rock and Americana movements and is routinely ranked among the greatest albums of all time.
