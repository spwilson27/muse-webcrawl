Drugstore


A modern drugstore interior.

A drugstore, in American English, is a retail store that sells prescription and over-the-counter medicines alongside a wide range of general merchandise — cosmetics, toiletries, household goods, snacks, greeting cards, photo services and seasonal items. The equivalent store in British English is usually called a pharmacy or chemist's, though large British chains such as Boots resemble the American drugstore model.

History

The American drugstore evolved from apothecaries and patent-medicine shops. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the soda fountain became its social centre, and pharmacists invented or popularised drinks such as Coca-Cola (first sold at a drugstore counter in Atlanta in 1886). Lunch counters in chains like Woolworth's and Walgreens became fixtures of town life — and, in 1960, sites of civil-rights sit-ins. Self-service layouts, drive-through pharmacies and in-store clinics followed in the late twentieth century.

Modern format

Major United States chains include CVS Pharmacy, Walgreens and Rite Aid; CVS's acquisition of the health insurer Aetna in 2018 pushed the model toward integrated health care. The pharmacy counter is staffed by licensed pharmacists who dispense medication, counsel patients and administer vaccinations, while the "front end" operates as a convenience store. Front-end sales have drawn controversy for including tobacco (dropped by CVS in 2014) and for locked cabinets introduced against shoplifting.
