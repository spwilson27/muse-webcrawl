Dual (category theory)

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, duality is the correspondence between properties of a category C and the dual properties of its opposite category Cop, obtained by reversing the direction of all morphisms (arrows). Given any statement true of C, interchanging the source and target of every morphism and reversing the order of composition of morphisms yields a corresponding true statement about Cop.

The duality principle

The duality principle asserts that, since categories are defined purely in terms of objects and arrows satisfying certain axioms, every theorem proved from those axioms has a dual theorem obtained by the above reversal, valid in equal generality. Thus, for example, monomorphisms in C correspond to epimorphisms in Cop; products correspond to coproducts; initial objects to terminal objects; limits in general to colimits.

Examples

  • A limit in C is a colimit in Cop, and vice versa.
  • The category of sets and functions is dual to itself only in trivial respects, but the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces is equivalent to its own opposite via the dual space functor.
  • In order theory, a partially ordered set's dual reverses the ordering, exchanging meets and joins, least and greatest elements.
