Revolving restaurant

A revolving restaurant or rotating restaurant is usually a tower restaurant eating space designed to rest atop a disc platform that rotates as a turntable, so that diners can enjoy a panoramic view without leaving their seats. The rotation is usually slow — often one revolution per hour — and imperceptible while dining.

History

The first revolving restaurant is generally credited to the Florianturm in Dortmund, Germany, opened in 1959. The concept spread worldwide in the 1960s and 1970s, most famously with the restaurant at the top of the Space Needle in Seattle (1962) and the CN Tower's 360 Restaurant in Toronto. John H. Lautner is credited with an early American design, and revolving restaurants became signature features of observation towers and landmark hotels.

Design

Typically only the diners' floor section rotates, carried on a ring of wheels over a stationary core containing kitchens, lifts, and services. Safety and comfort require very low rotational speeds and precise tolerances in the rotating platform.
