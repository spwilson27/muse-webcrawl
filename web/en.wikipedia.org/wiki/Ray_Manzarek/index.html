Raymond Daniel Manzarek Jr. (12 February 1939 – 20 May 2013) was an American musician and singer, best known as the keyboardist and co-founder of the rock band the Doors. His swirling Vox Continental organ and Fender Rhodes piano bass — played simultaneously, providing the band's bass lines in the absence of a bassist — defined the Doors' sound on songs such as "Light My Fire", "Riders on the Storm" and "Break On Through (To the Other Side)".

Early life and formation of the Doors

Manzarek grew up in a Polish-American family on Chicago's South Side, trained as a classical pianist, and studied economics at DePaul University before moving to Los Angeles to study film at UCLA. There he met Jim Morrison; after a chance reunion on Venice Beach in the summer of 1965, where Morrison sang him the lyrics of "Moonlight Drive", the two formed the Doors with drummer John Densmore and guitarist Robby Krieger.

Career

The Doors released six studio albums between 1967 and 1971 and became one of the era's most controversial and influential bands. After Morrison's death in 1971, Manzarek sang lead on the band's final two albums and pursued solo records including The Golden Scarab (1974). He produced the first four albums of the Los Angeles punk band X, and in the 2000s toured with Krieger as Manzarek–Krieger, performing the Doors' catalogue. His memoir Light My Fire: My Life with The Doors appeared in 1998. Manzarek died in 2013 of bile duct cancer in Rosenheim, Germany. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Doors in 1993.
