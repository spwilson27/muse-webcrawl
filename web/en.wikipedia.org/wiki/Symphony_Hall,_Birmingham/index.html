Symphony Hall, Birmingham

Symphony Hall is a 2,262-seat concert venue in Birmingham, England. It was officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 12 June 1991, as part of the International Convention Centre (ICC) complex on Broad Street in the city centre. It is the home of the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (CBSO) and hosts around 270 events a year, ranging from classical concerts to jazz, folk, rock and pop performances, as well as conferences and community events.

Design and acoustics

The hall was designed by the Percy Thomas Partnership as part of the ICC, with acoustic design by Russell Johnson of Artec Consultants Inc. It follows the traditional "shoebox" form of celebrated 19th-century halls such as the Vienna Musikverein and the Amsterdam Concertgebouw, a shape widely considered favourable for orchestral sound. Adjustable acoustic features include a large movable canopy above the stage, reverberation chambers whose doors can be opened or closed to vary the acoustic, and U-shaped sound-reflecting panels. The hall is widely regarded as one of the finest concert halls in the United Kingdom acoustically.

The building sits within a massive concrete box decoupled from the structure of the surrounding convention centre, protecting performances from vibration and noise from nearby rail lines and roads.

The organ

Symphony Hall houses a 6,000-pipe symphonic organ built by the German firm Johannes Klais Orgelbau of Bonn. It was installed in 2001, a decade after the hall opened, following a fundraising campaign, and its pipes form a prominent visual feature at the rear of the stage.

History

Before Symphony Hall, Birmingham's main orchestral venue was Birmingham Town Hall, opened in 1834. The construction of the ICC and Symphony Hall in the late 1980s formed a centrepiece of the city's regeneration of its canalside and Broad Street area. In 2016 the hall and the neighbouring Town Hall came under the management of the charity Performances Birmingham Ltd, later renamed B:Music. In 2021 the foyer was redeveloped to create new public spaces, bars and performance areas facing Centenary Square.
