Tropical geometry
FieldAlgebraic geometry, combinatorics

Name originCoined in honour of Brazilian computer scientist Imre Simon; no deeper link to the tropics

Tropical geometry is a relatively recent area of mathematics studying algebraic geometry through the tropical semiring, in which the operations of addition and multiplication are replaced by minimum (or maximum) and addition respectively. Under this substitution, polynomials become piecewise-linear convex functions, and algebraic varieties become combinatorial objects called tropical varieties—polyhedral complexes that retain much of the classical geometry in a simplified form.

Basic ideas

A tropical polynomial such as min(x+1, y, 0) defines a tropical curve: the locus where the minimum is attained at least twice, a planar graph with edges of rational slope. Counting problems in classical enumerative geometry—such as counting rational curves through given points—can be transferred to counting such graphs, a technique used to great effect by Grigory Mikhalkin in the 2000s.

Applications

Tropical methods apply to mirror symmetry, the geometry of phylogenetic tree spaces, auction design and optimisation, and the study of sandpile models and chip-firing games. The word "tropical" was chosen by French computer scientists in honour of their Brazilian colleague Imre Simon, a pioneer of the semiring.
