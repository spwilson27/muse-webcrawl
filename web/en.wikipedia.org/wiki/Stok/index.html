Stok

Stok is a village in the Leh district of Ladakh, India, lying south of Leh on the far side of the Indus River. It is best known for the Stok Palace, the summer residence of the Namgyal dynasty of Ladakh, and as the usual starting point for climbs of Stok Kangri, one of the most popular trekking peaks in the Indian Himalaya.

Stok Palace

Stok Palace was built in 1820 by King Tsepal Thondup Namgyal. After the Dogra conquest of Ladakh in the 1830s and 1840s, the royal family was obliged to leave the Leh Palace and took up residence at Stok, where descendants of the dynasty still live. Part of the palace functions as a museum housing royal artefacts, including thangkas, jewellery, crowns, coins, and ritual objects; the palace complex includes a Buddhist temple (gompa) with notable murals. A large statue of the Buddha, consecrated in 2016, stands near the palace.

Stok Kangri

Stok Kangri (6,153 metres) rises above the village in the Stok Range of the Zanskar mountains. Long promoted as a non-technical "trekking peak" suitable for fit first-time high-altitude climbers, it drew heavy traffic, and in 2020 the peak was closed to climbing for several years to allow environmental recovery — a notable example of overtourism pressure in Ladakh. The trek to base camp passes through the Hemis National Park, habitat of the snow leopard.

Geography

Stok village is irrigated by meltwater from the Stok glacier, supporting fields of barley and apricot orchards in an otherwise cold-desert landscape at an altitude of about 3,500 metres. The village has become a centre for homestays catering to trekkers and visitors to the palace museum.
