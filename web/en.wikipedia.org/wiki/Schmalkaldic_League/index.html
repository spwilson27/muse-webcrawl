Schmalkaldic League

Schmalkalden, where the league was founded in 1531

The Schmalkaldic League (German: Schmalkaldischer Bund) was a military alliance of Lutheran princes and cities within the Holy Roman Empire, founded in February 1531 at the town of Schmalkalden in Thuringia. It was formed to defend its members against any attempt by the Emperor Charles V to suppress the Protestant Reformation by force.

Formation and members

The leading members were Philip I, Landgrave of Hesse, and John Frederick I, Elector of Saxony. Other members included Brunswick-Lüneburg, Anhalt, Mansfeld, and imperial cities such as Strasbourg, Ulm, Constance, Lübeck, Bremen, and Magdeburg. The league's theologians produced the Schmalkaldic Articles (1537), drafted by Martin Luther as a statement of Lutheran doctrine.

Activities

For a decade the league's military strength deterred imperial action and allowed Protestantism to consolidate. It intervened in Württemberg (1534), restoring Duke Ulrich, and its existence shaped imperial politics throughout the 1530s and early 1540s.

Schmalkaldic War and dissolution

When Charles V turned against the league in 1546, the resulting Schmalkaldic War ended in decisive imperial victory at the Battle of Mühlberg (24 April 1547), where John Frederick of Saxony was captured and Philip of Hesse imprisoned. The league was dissolved, although Protestant resistance continued and the religious settlement was eventually renegotiated in the Peace of Augsburg (1555).
