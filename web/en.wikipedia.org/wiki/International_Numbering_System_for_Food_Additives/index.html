International Numbering System for Food Additives

The International Numbering System for Food Additives (INS) is an international system for naming food additives, maintained by the Codex Alimentarius Commission, a body established by the Food and Agriculture Organization and the World Health Organization of the United Nations. The system is defined in the Codex Class Names and the International Numbering System for Food Additives (CAC/GL 36-1989).

Overview

The INS assigns an identifying number to each food additive, generally following the same numbering as the European Union's system of E numbers, but without the initial "E". Inclusion of an additive in the INS does not in itself imply that its use is permitted; rather, the system provides a harmonized means of identifying additives in food labelling and in the Codex General Standard for Food Additives.

Numbering ranges

  • 100–199: Colours
  • 200–299: Preservatives
  • 300–399: Antioxidants, acidity regulators
  • 400–499: Thickeners, stabilizers, emulsifiers
  • 500–599: Acidity regulators, anti-caking agents
  • 600–699: Flavour enhancers
  • 700–799: Antibiotics
  • 900–999: Sweeteners, glazing agents and other additives
  • 1000–1599: Additional chemicals

Letters appended to a number distinguish closely related variants of an additive, such as different salts or forms of the same basic substance.
