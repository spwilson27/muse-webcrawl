The Football League is a league competition featuring professional association football clubs from England and Wales. Founded in 1888 as the Football League, it was the top-level football league in England from its foundation until the formation of the Premier League in 1992, and remains the oldest such competition in world football.

History

The league was founded at a meeting in Manchester on 17 April 1888, largely at the initiative of Aston Villa director William McGregor, who sought a regular fixture list to replace the haphazard scheduling of friendly and cup matches following the legalisation of professionalism. The twelve founding members were Accrington, Aston Villa, Blackburn Rovers, Bolton Wanderers, Burnley, Derby County, Everton, Notts County, Preston North End, Stoke, West Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers. Preston North End won the inaugural championship in 1888–89 without losing a match.

The League expanded steadily, reaching four divisions by the 1920s and 92 member clubs, a structure that endured for most of the twentieth century. A Second Division was added in 1892 (absorbing much of the rival Football Alliance), followed by the Third Division in 1920 and a regional Third Division North, reorganised into national Third and Fourth Divisions in 1958.

The Premier League split and the EFL

In 1992 the clubs of the First Division resigned en masse to form the FA Premier League, ending the Football League's 104 years as the top tier. The League continued with three divisions and seventy-two clubs. Since the 2016–17 season it has been branded the English Football League (EFL), with its divisions known as the Championship, League One and League Two. Promotion and relegation links it to the Premier League above and the National League below.

Competitions

Besides its league championship, the organisation runs the EFL Cup (League Cup) and the EFL Trophy, and operates the play-off system at the end of each season to decide the final promotion places from each division.
