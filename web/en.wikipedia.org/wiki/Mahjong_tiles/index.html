Mahjong tiles

Mahjong tiles are the playing pieces used in the game of mahjong, a tile-based game that originated in China in the nineteenth century. A standard set contains 144 tiles, although some regional variants use more or fewer. The tiles are traditionally made of bone, bamboo, or ivory, and are today most often moulded from plastic or resin. Each tile bears a suit marking or character engraved or printed on its face.

Composition of a set

Suited tiles

There are three suits, each numbered 1 through 9 with four identical copies of each tile, for 108 suited tiles:

  • Dots (also called circles or coins): patterns of coloured circles.
  • Bamboo (also called sticks or bams): patterns of bamboo sticks; the 1 of bamboo is usually a large bird, often a peacock or sparrow.
  • Characters (also called wan or cracks): the Chinese character for ten thousand (萬) above the character for the number.

Honour tiles

Honours comprise four copies each of the three dragons and the four winds, 28 tiles:

  • Dragons: red, green, and white (the white dragon is often a blank or framed tile).
  • Winds: East, South, West, and North.

Bonus tiles

Four flowers and four seasons, eight tiles in total, are used as bonus tiles in many variants. Japanese and American sets add jokers and red fives (aka dora tiles), and some sets include blank spares.

Materials and manufacture

Traditional tiles were hand-carved from bone or ivory faces dovetailed to bamboo backs. Export sets of the 1920s mahjong craze in the West were made by firms using early plastics such as Bakelite and Catalin. Modern tiles are injection-moulded, then engraved or printed and paint-filled. Tile dimensions vary by region: Chinese tiles are large, Japanese tiles smaller to suit the faster riichi game, and American tiles include racks and pushers.

Unicode

Mahjong tiles are encoded in Unicode in the block "Mahjong Tiles" (U+1F000–U+1F02F), added in Unicode 5.1, allowing each tile of the standard set to be represented as a character.
