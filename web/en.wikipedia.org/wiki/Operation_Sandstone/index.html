Operation Sandstone was the third series of American nuclear weapons tests, conducted in the spring of 1948 at Enewetak Atoll in the Pacific Proving Grounds. It consisted of three tower shots — X-Ray, Yoke, and Zebra.

Purpose and results

Sandstone tested new, more efficient fission core designs developed at Los Alamos since the wartime Fat Man device, using levitated pits and composite uranium–plutonium cores. The results were striking: yields of 37, 49, and 18 kilotons respectively, achieved with far less fissile material than earlier designs, effectively multiplying the size of the potential US stockpile.

Significance

The success of Sandstone validated the post-war weapon designs that went into production as the Mark 4 bomb and confirmed the feasibility of efficient use of plutonium and uranium — a key step in the build-up of the American nuclear arsenal at the start of the Cold War.
