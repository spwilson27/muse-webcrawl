Becket (1964 film)

Richard Burton as Thomas Becket and Peter O'Toole as Henry II.

Becket is a 1964 British-American historical drama film directed by Peter Glenville and based on the 1959 play Becket or The Honour of God by Jean Anouilh. It stars Richard Burton as Thomas Becket and Peter O'Toole as King Henry II of England, and dramatises the conflict between the two men after Becket, the king's friend and chancellor, is appointed Archbishop of Canterbury and begins defending the Church against royal authority, a struggle that ends in Becket's murder in Canterbury Cathedral in 1170.

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success. It was nominated for twelve Academy Awards, including Best Picture and three acting nominations (Burton and O'Toole both received Best Actor nominations), and won the Oscar for Best Adapted Screenplay, written by Edward Anhalt. O'Toole reprised the role of Henry II four years later in The Lion in Winter (1968).

Restoration

For many years the film was difficult to see in a satisfactory version; a major restoration, overseen by the Academy Film Archive with support from Martin Scorsese's Film Foundation, was premiered in 2007 and released on DVD and Blu-ray.
