Thali

Thali (Hindi: थाली, meaning "plate") is a round platter used to serve food in South Asia, and also the name for an Indian-style meal made up of a selection of dishes served together on that platter. The small bowls arranged on the thali are called katoris, and the meal is traditionally designed to balance the six tastes of Ayurvedic tradition: sweet, salty, bitter, sour, astringent and spicy.

A North Indian thali

Contents

A typical thali includes rice and/or flatbreads such as roti, chapati or puri; dal (lentils); one or more vegetable curries (sabzi); a raita or curd; chutneys and pickles; papad; and a sweet such as gulab jamun or kheer. Regional versions vary widely: the Gujarati thali is known for its sweetness and variety of breads and snacks; the Rajasthani thali features hearty dishes such as dal baati churma; and the South Indian meals served on banana leaves follow a similar principle of many small portions.

Custom

In many restaurants thalis are offered as an all-you-can-eat meal, with servers continuously refilling the katoris until the diner signals they are finished. Thalis are also served at festivals, weddings and religious occasions, where they embody hospitality and abundance.
