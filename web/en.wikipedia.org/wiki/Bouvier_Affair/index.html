Bouvier Affair

The Bouvier Affair is a legal dispute and art-world scandal that erupted in 2015 between Swiss art dealer and freeport magnate Yves Bouvier and Russian billionaire collector Dmitry Rybolovlev. Rybolovlev accused Bouvier of fraudulently overcharging him by about $1 billion on 38 works of art purchased over a decade.

Bouvier Affair
Began: 2015
Parties: Yves Bouvier; Dmitry Rybolovlev
Sum in dispute: ~$1 billion
Venues: Monaco, Geneva, Singapore, New York

Background

Bouvier, owner of art-shipping firm Natural Le Coultre and developer of art freeports in Geneva, Singapore, and Luxembourg, acted from 2003 as Rybolovlev's agent in assembling one of the world's great private collections, including Leonardo da Vinci's Salvator Mundi, bought for $127.5 million. Rybolovlev claimed Bouvier presented himself as a negotiator while actually buying the works and reselling them at large markups.

Litigation

Bouvier was arrested in Monaco in February 2015. Claims and counterclaims multiplied across jurisdictions: Rybolovlev accused Bouvier of fraud and money laundering; Bouvier maintained he was an independent dealer free to set his prices. Related litigation involved Sotheby's, which Rybolovlev accused of aiding the alleged scheme; a New York jury found for Sotheby's in 2024. In December 2023, Geneva prosecutors closed the main criminal case, and the parties reached agreements ending most proceedings.

Significance

The affair exposed the opacity of the high-end art market — undisclosed markups, offshore dealing, and freeport storage — and prompted calls for regulation of art intermediaries. The Salvator Mundi at the centre of the dispute was resold at Christie's in 2017 for $450.3 million, the highest price ever paid for a painting.
