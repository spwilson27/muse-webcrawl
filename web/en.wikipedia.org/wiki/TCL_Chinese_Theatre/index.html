TCL Chinese Theatre

The TCL Chinese Theatre, commonly referred to as Grauman's Chinese Theatre, is a movie palace on the historic Hollywood Walk of Fame at 6925 Hollywood Boulevard in Hollywood, Los Angeles. It opened on May 18, 1927, with the premiere of Cecil B. DeMille's The King of Kings.

History

The theatre was commissioned by showman Sid Grauman, who had already built the Egyptian Theatre nearby. Designed in an Exotic Revival style evoking a giant Chinese pagoda, with a dragon carved from stone and imported temple bells, it became the most famous movie theatre in the world and hosted the Academy Awards ceremonies of 1944, 1945 and 1946.

In 2013 the Chinese electronics company TCL Corporation purchased the naming rights, and the theatre was converted to IMAX, briefly becoming the largest IMAX auditorium in the world by seating capacity at the time of its reopening.

Forecourt of the Stars

The theatre's concrete forecourt bears the signatures, footprints and handprints of popular motion picture personalities from the 1920s to the present day. The tradition is said to have begun accidentally when silent film star Norma Talmadge stepped in wet cement, though Mary Pickford and Douglas Fairbanks were the first to leave prints deliberately, in April 1927. Nearly 200 prints are now set in the forecourt.
