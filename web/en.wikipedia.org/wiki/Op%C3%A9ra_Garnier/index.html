The Palais Garnier (pronounced [palɛ ɡaʁnje]), also known as the Opéra Garnier, is a 1,979-seat opera house in Paris, France, inaugurated in 1875. Commissioned by Emperor Napoleon III as part of the Haussmann renovation of Paris, it was designed by architect Charles Garnier in an eclectic, opulent style that became emblematic of the Second Empire.

Architecture

The building is famous for its marble grand staircase, multi-coloured friezes, gilded statuary, and the Grand Foyer modelled on a classical chateau gallery. The auditorium is horseshoe-shaped in the Italian style, with a ceiling painted by Marc Chagall in 1964 around the original chandelier — the seven-tonne bronze and crystal chandelier that figures in Gaston Leroux's novel The Phantom of the Opera, whose fictional phantom haunts the opera's underground lake (in reality a cistern beneath the building).

Use today

Since the opening of the Opéra Bastille in 1989, the Palais Garnier is used primarily for ballet by the Paris Opera Ballet, alongside some opera performances, concerts, and exhibitions. It is one of the most visited monuments in Paris.
