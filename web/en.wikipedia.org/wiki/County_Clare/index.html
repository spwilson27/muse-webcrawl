County Clare

County Clare (Irish: Contae an Chláir) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Munster and the Mid-West Region. It is named after the town of Clarecastle, itself named for a castle built beside a bridge (clár meaning a plank bridge) over the River Fergus. The county town and largest settlement is Ennis.

Geography

Clare is bounded by County Galway to the north, County Tipperary to the east, County Limerick to the south and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. The River Shannon and Lough Derg form much of its eastern border, while the Shannon Estuary lies to the south. The county is famous for the Burren, a vast karst limestone landscape in the north-west with rare flora, and for the Cliffs of Moher, which rise over 200 metres above the Atlantic.

History

Clare was historically part of the kingdom of Thomond, ruled by the O'Brien dynasty, descendants of Brian Boru. It became a county in the late 16th century. In 1828 the county made history when Daniel O'Connell won a by-election there, an event that forced the British government to pass Catholic Emancipation the following year.

Culture

Clare is renowned as a heartland of Irish traditional music, hosting festivals such as the Willie Clancy Summer School in Milltown Malbay. Its nickname, "the Banner County", comes from the banners carried by Clare regiments. In sport, the Clare hurling team won the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship in 1914, 1995, 1997, 2013 and 2024.
