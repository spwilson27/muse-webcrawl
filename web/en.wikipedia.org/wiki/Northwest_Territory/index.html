The Territory Northwest of the River Ohio, more commonly known as the Northwest Territory, was an organized incorporated territory of the United States that existed from July 13, 1787, until March 1, 1803, when the southeastern portion was admitted to the Union as the state of Ohio.

History

The territory was created by the Northwest Ordinance of 1787, enacted by the Congress of the Confederation. It encompassed lands ceded by Britain in the Treaty of Paris (1783) and by individual states, lying west of Pennsylvania, north of the Ohio River, east of the Mississippi River and south of the Great Lakes. The Ordinance established a framework for governance and for the admission of new states, and notably prohibited slavery in the territory.

Settlement and conflict

The first permanent settlement was founded at Marietta in 1788. Expansion led to conflict with Native American nations of the Western Confederacy in the Northwest Indian War, concluded by the Treaty of Greenville in 1795 after the Battle of Fallen Timbers.

Legacy

Portions of the territory eventually became the states of Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin and part of Minnesota. Arthur St. Clair served as its first and only long-term governor.
