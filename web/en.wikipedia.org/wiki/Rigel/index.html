Rigel is a blue supergiant star in the constellation of Orion. It has the Bayer designation β Orionis, though it is usually brighter than α Orionis (Betelgeuse). Rigel is the brightest star of Orion and typically the sixth-brightest star in the night sky, shining with an apparent magnitude of about 0.13. Its name derives from the Arabic rijl, "foot", referring to its position at the foot of the hunter Orion.

Characteristics

Rigel is a class B8 blue supergiant roughly 860 light-years away. With a mass of about 21 solar masses and a radius around 79 times that of the Sun, it radiates on the order of 120,000 times the Sun's total luminosity, making it one of the most luminous stars known within a thousand light-years. It is a slightly variable star, an Alpha Cygni variable whose brightness fluctuates irregularly as its atmosphere pulsates. Although only about 8 million years old, Rigel has exhausted the hydrogen in its core and is expected to end its life in a Type II supernova.

System

Rigel is a multiple-star system. Its companion, Rigel B, visible in small telescopes, is itself a spectroscopic binary, and a fourth, fainter component is also associated with the system. The primary illuminates the nearby Witch Head Nebula (IC 2118), a faint reflection nebula visible in photographs.

Observation

Rigel marks the lower right (southwestern) corner of Orion's hourglass figure for northern observers and culminates at midnight around mid-December. Its blue-white colour contrasts strikingly with the orange-red of Betelgeuse at the opposite corner of the constellation.
