Holi

Holi (Hindi: होली) is a major Hindu festival celebrated as the Festival of Colours, Love, and Spring. It celebrates the eternal and divine love of the deities Radha and Krishna, and also signifies the triumph of good over evil, commemorating the victory of Vishnu as Narasimha over the demon king Hiranyakashipu. Holi marks the arrival of spring in India, the end of winter, and the blossoming of love. It is also an invocation for a good spring harvest season.

The festival is celebrated on the evening and day of Phalguna Purnima (the full moon of the Hindu month of Phalguna), typically falling in March. It is observed primarily in India and Nepal, and by the Hindu diaspora worldwide. Regional names and customs include Phagua in Bihar, Dol Purnima in West Bengal and Odisha, and the famed Lathmar Holi of Barsana and Nandgaon in Uttar Pradesh, where women playfully beat men with sticks, re-enacting a legend of Krishna and Radha.

History and rituals

Holi finds mention in ancient Sanskrit literature, including the Ratnavali of the 7th century and the works of the poet Kalidasa. The night before Holi, bonfires known as Holika Dahan (or Chhoti Holi) are lit to symbolize the burning of the demoness Holika, who tried to kill the devotee Prahlada, and the triumph of devotion and righteousness. On the main day, Rangwali Holi, people gather in open streets, parks, and temple grounds to throw colored powder (gulal) and water at one another, accompanied by music, dancing, and festive foods such as gujiya, mathri, and the cannabis-infused drink thandai in some regions.

Significance

Beyond its mythological roots, Holi is a social festival that dissolves hierarchy: young and old, rich and poor, friends and strangers all become equal under a layer of color. It is a time to repair broken relationships, forgive grievances, and renew social bonds at the turning of the agricultural year.

Celebrations elsewhere

Large public Holi celebrations are held in cities around the world with significant South Asian communities, including London, New York, Toronto, and Sydney, and color-throwing events inspired by Holi have become popular in many other countries.
