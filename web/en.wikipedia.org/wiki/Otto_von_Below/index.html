Otto Ernst Vinzent Leo von Below (18 January 1857 – 15 March 1944) was a German general of the First World War. He is best remembered for commanding the Austro-German 14th Army at the Battle of Caporetto in October–November 1917, one of the most complete breakthrough victories of the war.

Career

Commissioned into the Prussian infantry in the 1870s, Below rose through staff and regimental appointments and was a corps commander when war began. On the Eastern Front he led the 8th Army in East Prussia and fought at the Second Battle of the Masurian Lakes in February 1915. Transferred to the Balkans, he commanded German forces in the Macedonian campaign, defending against the Allied Salonika army. In 1917 he was sent to the Italian Front to command the combined 14th Army.

Caporetto

At Caporetto, Below's army, spearheaded by infiltration tactics and a short, intense gas and artillery bombardment, shattered the Italian Second Army, advanced more than 100 kilometres to the Piave River and took hundreds of thousands of prisoners. The victory nearly knocked Italy out of the war. In 1918 Below returned to the Western Front, leading the 17th Army and later briefly the 1st Army, before commanding the homeland defence forces during the collapse of November 1918.

Later life

Below retired after the war and died in Danzig in 1944. His cousin Fritz von Below was likewise a prominent German army commander of the war.
