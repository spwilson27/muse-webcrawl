The Villa Hügel is a grand mansion in Bredeney, now a district of Essen, Germany. Built between 1870 and 1873 for the industrialist Alfred Krupp, founder of the modern Krupp steel empire, it overlooks the Ruhr valley and Lake Baldeney from an estate of about 28 hectares.

History

Alfred Krupp supervised the design himself, intending a modern, fireproof residence and business headquarters combined. The main house contains 269 rooms across some 8,100 square metres; a guest house (the "Kleines Haus") and extensive parkland complete the complex. The villa remained the Krupp family residence until 1945, receiving guests including Kaiser Wilhelm II. After the Second World War it was used by the Allied Coal Control Commission.

Today

Since 1953 the Villa Hügel has been owned by the Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation. It is open to the public as a museum and cultural venue, housing the Krupp historical exhibition, concerts by the Folkwang Kammerorchester and others, and temporary art exhibitions in the main hall.
