AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) is an open, royalty-free video coding format developed by the Alliance for Open Media, a consortium founded in 2015 by companies including Google, Mozilla, Cisco, Microsoft, Netflix, Amazon and Intel. It was designed as a successor to VP9 and a royalty-free alternative to HEVC, targeting real-time and streaming applications.

Development

AV1 merged three experimental codebases: Google's VP10, Mozilla's Daala, and Cisco's Thor. The bitstream specification was frozen on 28 March 2018, alongside the reference encoder/decoder libaom. Early AV1 encoding was extremely slow, but encoders such as Intel/Netflix's SVT-AV1 and Mozilla's rav1e, plus faster libaom presets, made production use practical.

Features

AV1 supports resolutions up to 8K and beyond, 10- and 12-bit colour depth, film grain synthesis, screen content coding tools, and improved intra/inter prediction that typically yields roughly 20–30% better compression than VP9 and HEVC at comparable quality. The related still-image format AVIF uses AV1 intra coding.

Adoption

YouTube and Netflix began large-scale AV1 deployment around 2020; Netflix brought AV1 to televisions and later mobile devices, citing bandwidth savings. Hardware decoding arrived in GPUs and SoCs from 2020 onward (NVIDIA RTX 30-series, AMD RDNA 2, Intel Xe, Apple A17 Pro for decode), and browsers including Chrome, Firefox and Edge support software or hardware playback. A successor, AV2, was under development by the Alliance in the mid-2020s.
