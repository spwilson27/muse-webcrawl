Alphecca, also designated Alpha Coronae Borealis (α Coronae Borealis), is an eclipsing binary star in the constellation of Corona Borealis, the Northern Crown. It is the brightest star in the constellation and one of the most prominent members of the Ursa Major moving group. The star is located about 75 light-years from the Sun.

Nomenclature

The traditional name Alphecca derives from the Arabic nayyir al-fakka, "the bright (star) of the broken (ring of stars)". Another traditional name is Gemma, Latin for "jewel", referring to its position as the gem in the crown. In 2016 the International Astronomical Union's Working Group on Star Names approved the name Alphecca for the primary component of this system.

System

Alphecca is a detached eclipsing binary with an orbital period of about 17.36 days. The primary is a white main-sequence star of spectral type A0V, roughly 2.6 times the mass of the Sun and about 60 times as luminous. The secondary is a yellow main-sequence star similar to the Sun, of spectral type G5V, with about 0.92 solar masses. Eclipses cause the combined apparent magnitude to vary between about 2.21 and 2.32.

Properties

The primary shows excess infrared emission suggesting a debris disk of dust around it, similar to that of Vega. The system is a kinematical member of the Ursa Major moving group, giving it an estimated age of a few hundred million years.

In culture

As the jewel of the Northern Crown, Alphecca featured in classical mythology associated with the crown of Ariadne. It has also been identified with the star Gnosia mentioned by early Greek writers.
