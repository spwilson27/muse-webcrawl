Zbigniew Kazimierz Brzeziński (March 28, 1928 – May 26, 2017) was a Polish-American diplomat and political scientist. He served as National Security Advisor to President Jimmy Carter from 1977 to 1981, playing a central role in normalising relations with China, the Camp David Accords, the response to the Iranian Revolution, and the decision to arm the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan.

Early life and academia

Born in Warsaw to a diplomat father, Brzezinski spent his childhood in France and Germany before the family settled in Canada in 1938. He studied at McGill University and earned his PhD from Harvard in 1953, becoming a noted Sovietologist and co-author (with Carl J. Friedrich) of an influential theory of totalitarianism. He taught at Harvard and Columbia University and co-founded the Trilateral Commission with David Rockefeller in 1973.

Later career

After the Carter administration, Brzezinski remained a prominent strategic commentator, teaching at Johns Hopkins University's School of Advanced International Studies. His 1997 book The Grand Chessboard argued that American primacy depended on preventing any rival power from dominating Eurasia. He died in Virginia in 2017.
