The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter

The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter (Japanese: 竹取物語, Taketori Monogatari) is a Japanese monogatari (fictional prose narrative) containing elements of fantasy, thought to date from the late 9th or early 10th century during the Heian period. It is considered the oldest extant work of Japanese prose fiction and is widely known as the story of Princess Kaguya.

Story

An old bamboo cutter finds a tiny, glowing girl inside a bamboo stalk and raises her with his wife. Named Kaguya-hime, she grows into a woman of unearthly beauty. Five noble suitors seek her hand; she sets each an impossible task — bringing her the Buddha's begging bowl, a branch of the jeweled tree of Penglai, fire-rat skin, a dragon's jewel, a cowrie shell born of a swallow — and all fail or cheat and are exposed. Even the Emperor of Japan is refused. At last Kaguya reveals she is a being from the Moon, sent to Earth as punishment or exile. On the night of the full moon, a celestial procession descends despite the Emperor's guards, and she ascends home, leaving behind a letter and the elixir of immortality. The Emperor, grieving, has them burned atop Japan's highest mountain — Mount Fuji, whose smoke is said to rise from the elixir still.

Legacy

The tale stands at the head of the Japanese literary tradition; its motifs recur across a millennium of art, theatre and manga. Studio Ghibli's The Tale of the Princess Kaguya (2013), directed by Isao Takahata, was acclaimed internationally. The story has also been invoked in science — the lunar orbiter Kaguya, the Apollo-era parallels drawn by commentators, and a popular claim that it is an early example of science fiction with its lunar civilisation.
