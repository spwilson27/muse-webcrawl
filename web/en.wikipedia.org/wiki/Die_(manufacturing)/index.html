Die (manufacturing)

A die is a specialised machine tool used in manufacturing industry to cut or form material, mostly using a press. Like molds, dies are generally customised to the item they are used to create. Products made with dies range from simple paper clips to complex pieces used in advanced technology.

Die forming

Forming dies are typically made by tool and die makers and put into production after mounting into a press. The die is a metal block placed in a press; the workpiece — usually sheet metal or plastic — is placed across or into it, and a mating punch presses the material into or through the die, cutting or shaping it. Processes employing dies include blanking, piercing, drawing, bending, coining and extrusion. In wire drawing and extrusion, a die is a plate or ring with a tapered hole through which material is pulled or pushed to reduce its cross-section.

Die cutting

Die cutting of flat material (paper, cardboard, fabric, foam and thin metal) uses sharp steel rules or blades set in a backing board (a "steel-rule die") to cut out shapes, as in the manufacture of packaging, gaskets and labels. Rotary die cutting uses cylindrical dies for high-volume production.

Thread dies

A die is also a tool that cuts external threads on rods and bolts — the counterpart of a tap, which cuts internal threads — used in a die stock or in thread-cutting machines.
