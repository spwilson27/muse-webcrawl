Vienna Offensive

Date16 March – 15 April 1945
LocationHungary and Austria
ResultSoviet victory
Soviet commandFyodor Tolbukhin; Rodion Malinovsky

The Vienna Offensive was a strategic offensive operation conducted by the Soviet Red Army's 2nd and 3rd Ukrainian Fronts between 16 March and 15 April 1945 in the final weeks of the Second World War in Europe. It culminated in the capture of Vienna, the capital of Austria, then part of Nazi Germany.

Following the fall of Budapest in February 1945 and the failure of Germany's last major offensive, Operation Spring Awakening around Lake Balaton, Soviet forces under Marshal Fyodor Tolbukhin advanced through western Hungary toward the Austrian border. Vienna itself was defended by German forces under orders to hold the city as a fortress.

Battle for Vienna

Soviet troops reached Vienna's outskirts in early April 1945 and encircled the city by 8 April. Fierce street fighting followed; by 13 April the city center had been taken, and organized resistance in Vienna ended. Austrian resistance groups and the provisional Austrian government of Karl Renner, formed later that month, sought to position Austria as a victim of Nazi aggression in line with the 1943 Moscow Declaration. The offensive cost the Red Army tens of thousands of casualties and brought Soviet forces to the gates of the Alps and into contact with Western Allied armies advancing from Italy and Germany. Soviet forces withdrew from Austria in 1955 after the signing of the Austrian State Treaty.
