Internal communications

Internal communications (IC) is the function responsible for effective communication among participants within an organisation. It encompasses the flow of information between leadership and employees, between departments, and among staff themselves, and it is closely related to employee engagement, organisational culture, and change management.

Scope

Internal communications professionals inform employees about strategy, results, policies, and organisational change; they also create channels through which employees can feed views back to management. Unlike external public relations, which addresses customers, investors, and the media, IC is concerned with the workforce as its primary public. In large organisations the function may sit within human resources, corporate communications, or the chief executive's office.

Channels

Traditional channels include printed staff magazines, noticeboards, team briefings, and cascade briefings passed down through line managers. Digital channels have largely displaced them: intranets, internal social networks and enterprise social platforms, email newsletters, video messages, podcasts, instant-messaging tools such as Slack and Microsoft Teams, and all-hands or town-hall meetings (increasingly held by video link for distributed workforces). The choice of channel depends on urgency, sensitivity, and whether dialogue as well as dissemination is required.

Purpose and effects

Research in organisational communication links effective internal communication with higher employee engagement, lower turnover, better alignment with strategy, and safer working practices. Poor internal communication, by contrast, is associated with rumour, resistance to change, and disengagement. During crises — restructurings, redundancies, accidents, or corporate scandals — internal audiences are often reached before or alongside the press, since employees act as ambassadors for the organisation.

Professional practice

Internal communication emerged as a distinct specialism in the twentieth century, with staff magazines appearing in industrial firms from the late 1800s. Professional bodies such as the Institute of Internal Communication (UK) and the International Association of Business Communicators promote standards, training, and measurement. Modern practice emphasises two-way communication, segmentation of audiences, evaluation against business outcomes, and the enabling role of line managers, who remain the channel employees most often say they trust.
