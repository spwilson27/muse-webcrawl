Western Wei

Capital: Chang'an
Period: 535–557
Preceded by: Northern Wei
Succeeded by: Northern Zhou

Western Wei (Chinese: 西魏; pinyin: Xī Wèi) was a dynasty of northern China that ruled from 535 to 557 during the Northern and Southern dynasties period. It arose from the division of the Northern Wei empire of the Tuoba (Xianbei) clan after the civil war of the 530s: the warlord Gao Huan controlled the east, enthroning an emperor at Ye as Eastern Wei, while Yuwen Tai controlled the west and set up Emperor Wen at Chang'an.

Government and legacy

Real power lay with Yuwen Tai, who reorganised the army under the fubing militia system and governed with the help of Su Chuo's administrative reforms. Western Wei conquered Liang dynasty territory in Sichuan and the middle Yangtze. In 557 Yuwen Tai's son Yuwen Jue deposed the last Wei emperor and founded the Northern Zhou, which went on to reunify northern China — laying the institutional groundwork later inherited by the Sui dynasty.
