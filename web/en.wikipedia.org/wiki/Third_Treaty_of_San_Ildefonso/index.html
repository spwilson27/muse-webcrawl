Third Treaty of San Ildefonso

The Third Treaty of San Ildefonso was a secret agreement signed on 1 October 1800 between the French Republic and the Kingdom of Spain at the Royal Palace of La Granja de San Ildefonso. By its terms, Spain agreed to retrocede the vast North American territory of Louisiana to France.

Terms

Spain ceded Louisiana — which it had held since the Treaty of Paris of 1763 — back to France. In exchange, Napoleon Bonaparte promised that the Duke of Parma, Ferdinand, brother-in-law of King Charles IV of Spain, would receive an enlarged kingdom in Italy, eventually realised as the Kingdom of Etruria, carved out of Tuscany. Spain was also to transfer several ships of the line to the French navy.

Context

The treaty formed part of the Franco-Spanish alliance against Britain established by the Second Treaty of San Ildefonso (1796). Louisiana had become an expensive and sparsely settled colony for Spain, while Napoleon sought to rebuild a French colonial empire in the Americas, with Louisiana serving as a breadbasket for the sugar colonies of the Caribbean.

Consequences

The retrocession was confirmed by the Treaty of Aranjuez in 1801. French failure to restore its position in Saint-Domingue, the renewal of war with Britain, and Napoleon's financial needs led him to sell Louisiana to the United States in the Louisiana Purchase of 1803, doubling the size of the young republic. Spanish officials formally transferred Louisiana to France in November 1803, and France transferred it to the United States only weeks later.
