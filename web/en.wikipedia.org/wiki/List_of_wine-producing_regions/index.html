Wine is produced in dozens of countries on every continent except Antarctica. This list of wine-producing regions summarises the major regions of the world's leading wine-producing countries.

France

  • Bordeaux — Médoc, Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Graves, Sauternes; Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot blends.
  • Burgundy — Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune, Chablis, Mâconnais; Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.
  • Champagne — the original sparkling wine region.
  • Loire Valley — Sancerre, Vouvray, Muscadet, Chinon.
  • Rhône Valley — Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, Châteauneuf-du-Pape.
  • Alsace — aromatic whites: Riesling, Gewürztraminer.
  • Languedoc-Roussillon — France's largest wine area by production.
  • Provence — famed for dry rosé.

Italy

  • Piedmont — Barolo and Barbaresco (Nebbiolo).
  • Tuscany — Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, "Super Tuscans".
  • Veneto — Prosecco, Amarone della Valpolicella, Soave.
  • Sicily — Etna wines, Marsala.
  • Friuli-Venezia Giulia — noted white wines.

Spain

  • Rioja — Spain's flagship red-wine region (Tempranillo).
  • Ribera del Duero — powerful Tempranillo reds.
  • Penedès — home of Cava sparkling wine.
  • Jerez — Sherry.
  • Rías Baixas — Albariño whites.
  • Priorat — intense old-vine Garnacha and Cariñena.

Germany

  • Moselle — steep-slope Rieslings.
  • Rheingau and Rheinhessen — Riesling heartlands.
  • Pfalz, Nahe, Franken (Silvaner), Baden (Spätburgunder).

United States

  • California — Napa Valley, Sonoma, Paso Robles, Santa Barbara; about 90% of U.S. wine.
  • Oregon — Willamette Valley Pinot Noir.
  • Washington — Columbia Valley.
  • New York — Finger Lakes Riesling, Long Island.

Other major producing countries

  • Australia — Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale, Margaret River, Hunter Valley, Yarra Valley.
  • New Zealand — Marlborough (Sauvignon Blanc), Central Otago (Pinot Noir), Hawke's Bay.
  • Chile — Maipo, Colchagua and Casablanca valleys.
  • Argentina — Mendoza (Malbec), Salta.
  • South Africa — Stellenbosch, Paarl, Franschhoek, Swartland.
  • Portugal — Douro Valley (Port), Vinho Verde, Alentejo, Madeira.
  • Austria — Wachau, Kamptal, Burgenland (Grüner Veltliner).
  • Greece — Santorini (Assyrtiko), Naoussa, Nemea.
  • China — Ningxia, Shandong and Xinjiang; among the world's largest vineyard areas.
