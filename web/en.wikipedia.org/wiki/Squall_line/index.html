Squall line

A squall line approaching, with a prominent shelf cloud along its gust front.

A squall line, or more specifically a quasi-linear convective system (QLCS), is a line of thunderstorms forming along or ahead of a cold front. In the early 20th century, the term was used as a synonym for a cold front. It contains heavy precipitation, hail, frequent lightning, strong straight-line winds, and possibly tornadoes and waterspouts. Strong straight-line winds may occur where the squall line bows out, becoming a bow echo. Tornadoes can occur along waves within a line echo wave pattern (LEWP), where mesoscale low-pressure systems form. Some bow echoes can grow to become derechos as they move swiftly across large areas.

Characteristics

Squall lines typically form in unstable atmospheric conditions ahead of a cold front or dry line. A mature squall line consists of a convective region of intense updrafts along the leading edge and a trailing stratiform region of lighter, steady rain produced by older cells. The boundary between the two is marked by a gust front, often visible as a shelf cloud or roll cloud. Ahead of the line, winds are typically warm and moist; behind it, a cold pool of rain-cooled air surges outward, which can undercut and weaken the system or spawn new cells at its edge.

Formation

The formation of a squall line is driven by the convergence of moist, unstable air at a frontal boundary. As individual thunderstorms develop, their cold outflows merge into a continuous cold pool that lifts the warm air ahead of it, generating new cells in a self-sustaining cycle. A strong low-level jet and favorable wind shear help organize the cells into a line that can persist for many hours and travel hundreds of kilometres.

Severe weather

Squall lines are among the most common producers of severe straight-line wind damage in mid-latitudes. Embedded supercell structures and mesovortices along the line can produce brief but intense tornadoes. Heavy rainfall along slow-moving lines can cause flash flooding. See also bow echo and derecho.
