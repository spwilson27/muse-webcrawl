Creatio ex nihilo

Image: Gustave Doré's engraving of the creation of light (placeholder)

Creatio ex nihilo (Latin for "creation out of nothing") is the doctrine that matter is not eternal but was created by God. It is the position held by mainstream Judaism, Christianity and Islam, in contrast to creatio ex materia, in which God shapes pre-existing material, and to emanationist or pantheist views in which the world flows from or is part of God.

Origins and development

Whether the opening of Genesis teaches creation from nothing has long been debated: Genesis 1:1 can be read as an absolute beginning ("In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth") or as God ordering pre-existing chaos. The earliest explicit statement of the doctrine is usually traced to 2 Maccabees 7:28 (c. 2nd century BC), where a mother urges her martyred son to recognise that "God made [heaven and earth] out of things that did not exist".

In the 2nd century AD, Christian writers such as Theophilus of Antioch and Irenaeus articulated the doctrine against Gnostic and Platonic views of eternal matter; Tertullian defended it against Hermogenes. Augustine and later scholastics, notably Thomas Aquinas, developed its philosophical basis: Aquinas held that creation ex nihilo could be known by faith, that reason could show the world depends wholly on God for its existence, and that an eternal yet created world was not logically impossible. The Fourth Lateran Council (1215) defined creation from nothing as Catholic dogma.

Philosophical discussion

The doctrine raises questions about the principle ex nihilo nihil fit ("nothing comes from nothing") associated with Parmenides and Lucretius: theists reply that the principle applies to natural causes, whereas creation is the free act of an omnipotent being who is not a material cause. Modern discussions also connect the doctrine with cosmology, including the origin of the universe in the Big Bang and proposals in physics that invoke quantum fluctuations; scholars caution that physical "nothing" (a quantum vacuum) is not the metaphysical nothing of the doctrine.
