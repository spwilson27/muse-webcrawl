Villanovan culture

The Villanovan culture (c. 900–700 BC) was the earliest Iron Age culture of central and northern Italy, named after the cemetery at Villanova near Bologna excavated in the 1850s. It is generally regarded as the early phase of Etruscan civilisation.

Characteristics

Villanovan communities practised cremation, burying ashes in distinctive biconical urns, sometimes covered by a bronze helmet or bowl, in pit graves. Settlements of round huts stood on defensible heights; a hut-shaped urn type preserves their form. Metalwork included bronze razors, fibulae and helmets, alongside iron tools and weapons.

Development

Emerging from the late Bronze Age Proto-Villanovan horizon, the culture urbanised through contact with Greek and Phoenician traders, evolving into the Orientalising-period Etruscan cities by the 7th century BC.
