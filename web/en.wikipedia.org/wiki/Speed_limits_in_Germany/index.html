Speed limits in Germany

Speed limits in Germany are set by the federal government and enforced locally. The default limit within built-up areas is 50 km/h and outside built-up areas 100 km/h. Germany is famous for its autobahns, large sections of which have no mandatory speed limit for cars and motorcycles — only an advisory limit (Richtgeschwindigkeit) of 130 km/h.

Autobahn limits

Roughly half to two-thirds of the autobahn network is unrestricted for cars, though temporary or permanent limits apply near cities, at interchanges, in construction zones and on dangerous stretches. Vehicles towing trailers, trucks and buses are subject to lower limits, generally 80 or 100 km/h. Exceeding the advisory speed can increase a driver's liability and insurance consequences after a crash, even where no limit applies.

Debate

The absence of a general autobahn limit is a recurring political controversy in Germany. Proponents of a blanket 130 km/h limit cite safety and carbon emissions; opponents cite personal freedom and the quality of German road engineering and driver training. Polls regularly show majority support for a limit, but attempts to introduce one have repeatedly failed in federal politics.
