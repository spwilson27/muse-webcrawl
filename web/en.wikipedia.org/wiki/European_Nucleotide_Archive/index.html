European Nucleotide Archive

The European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) is a comprehensive public repository of nucleotide sequence data, maintained by the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) at the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI) in Hinxton, Cambridgeshire, United Kingdom. It archives raw sequencing reads, sequence assemblies, functional annotation, and associated sample and study metadata submitted by researchers worldwide.

History

The ENA grew out of the EMBL Data Library, established in 1982 as one of the first public databases of nucleotide sequences. It is a founding member of the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC), together with the DNA Data Bank of Japan (DDBJ) and GenBank at the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) in the United States. The three partners exchange data daily so that the same core archive is available from each site.

The launch of high-throughput sequencing technologies in the mid-2000s led to the creation of the Sequence Read Archive component, which stores raw reads from instruments such as those produced by Illumina, Oxford Nanopore, and Pacific Biosciences. The ENA also operates the European Genome-phenome Archive (EGA) for controlled-access human data.

Contents and access

Data in the ENA are organised around studies, samples, experiments, runs, assemblies, and analyses, each identified by a stable accession number. Submitters deposit data through interactive web tools or programmatic submission pipelines, and users can search, browse, and download records through the ENA Browser, a RESTful API, and bulk FTP services. Most data are released openly under terms that place them in the public domain once any submitter-imposed embargo has expired.
