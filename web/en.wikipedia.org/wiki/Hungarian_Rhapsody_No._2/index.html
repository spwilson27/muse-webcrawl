Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2

Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 in C-sharp minor, S.244/2
Composer: Franz Liszt
Composed: 1847
Published: 1851
Dedication: Count László Teleki

The Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 in C-sharp minor, S.244/2, is the second in a set of 19 Hungarian Rhapsodies by composer Franz Liszt, and is by far the most famous. Few other piano solos have achieved such widespread popularity, offering the pianist both a quasi-improvisational, brooding introduction and a dazzling display of virtuosity in its second half.

Structure

Like Liszt's other Hungarian rhapsodies, the work follows the traditional lassan–friss pattern taken from Hungarian folk dance. The slow, sombre lassan opens with a powerful C-sharp minor theme and proceeds through improvisatory, cadenza-like passages; the lively friss that follows — beginning quietly in F-sharp major and building through accelerating folk-like themes — rises to a brilliant, technically demanding climax. Many pianists have added their own cadenzas to the friss, a practice Liszt himself invited; a famous one was recorded by Sergei Rachmaninoff.

Arrangements and popular culture

Liszt orchestrated the rhapsody (as part of a set of six) with the help of Franz Doppler, and it remains a concert staple in both versions. In popular culture it is indelibly associated with cartoons: it features in The Cat Concerto (Tom and Jerry, 1947), Bugs Bunny's Rhapsody Rabbit (1946), and Daffy Duck's performance in Yankee Doodle Daffy, among many other film and television uses.
