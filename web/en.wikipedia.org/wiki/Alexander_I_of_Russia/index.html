Alexander I of Russia

Alexander I (23 December 1777 – 1 December 1825) was Emperor of Russia from 1801 until his death in 1825. Raised by his grandmother Catherine the Great amid Enlightenment ideas, he came to the throne after the assassination of his father Paul I — a shadow that hung over his reign. He was the central figure of the coalition that defeated Napoleon: humbled at the Battle of Austerlitz and forced into the Treaties of Tilsit (1807), he endured the French invasion of 1812, then led his armies across Europe to enter Paris in 1814, and became a dominant voice at the Congress of Vienna.

Early reign

Alexander's first years promised liberal reform: the Unofficial Committee of young friends discussed constitutions, ministries replaced Peter's colleges (1802), universities were founded, and censorship eased — though serfdom, the great question, was barely touched. War with France from 1805 ended at Austerlitz and Friedland; the Tilsit meeting on a raft in the Niemen (1807) made Russia Napoleon's ally against Britain, a partnership that frayed over the Continental System and Poland.

1812 and triumph

Napoleon's invasion of June 1812 forced a long Russian retreat, the battle of Borodino and the abandonment and burning of Moscow. Alexander refused all negotiation — the decision that doomed the Grande Armée in its winter retreat. In 1813–14 he was the coalition's political anchor, insisting on carrying the war into France. Russian troops entered Paris on 31 March 1814; Alexander was fêted as Europe's liberator, and at Vienna he proposed the Holy Alliance of Christian monarchs (1815).

Later years

After 1815 Alexander turned mystical and conservative, working through the reactionary minister Arakcheyev and the hated military colonies. He died suddenly at Taganrog in December 1825 — legend long insisted he had faked his death to become the Siberian hermit Fyodor Kuzmich. His death without a clear public succession arrangement triggered the Decembrist revolt at his brother Nicholas's accession.
