Charles Pierre de Frédy, Baron de Coubertin (1 January 1863 – 2 September 1937) was a French educator and historian, and the founder of the modern Olympic Games. Inspired by the role of sport in English public schools and by the ancient games at Olympia, he devoted his life to reviving the Olympics as an international festival of sport and peace, founding the International Olympic Committee (IOC) in 1894 and serving as its president from 1896 to 1925.

Life

Coubertin was born into an aristocratic Parisian family. Rejecting the military career expected of him, he studied law and public affairs and became an educational reformer, campaigning to introduce physical education and organised sport into French schools, which he believed would restore French vigour after the defeat of 1870–71. His visits to England, where he admired the sporting ethos of Rugby School under Thomas Arnold, and to the United States convinced him that sport built character and international understanding.

Reviving the Games

At a congress at the Sorbonne in June 1894, Coubertin proposed reviving the Olympic Games; the delegates agreed, and the first modern Games were held in Athens in 1896. He designed the Olympic rings symbol (1913) and wrote the Olympic oath; the Olympic motto Citius, Altius, Fortius ("Faster, Higher, Stronger") was adopted from his friend Henri Didon. Coubertin also competed under a pseudonym, winning the gold medal for literature at the 1912 Stockholm Games for his poem "Ode to Sport". His most famous dictum is:

"The important thing in life is not the triumph but the struggle; the essential thing is not to have conquered but to have fought well."

Views and legacy

Coubertin's legacy is mixed. He championed amateurism and opposed women's participation in the Games, views now rejected by the Olympic movement. He also controversially supported holding the 1936 Games in Berlin. He died in Geneva in 1937 and is buried in Lausanne, seat of the IOC, while his heart was interred at Olympia in Greece, as he had wished. The Pierre de Coubertin medal is awarded by the IOC for exceptional sportsmanship.
