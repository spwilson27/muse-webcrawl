Sir Alfred Ernest Ramsey (22 January 1920 – 28 April 1999) was an English football player and manager. As manager of the England national team from 1963 to 1974, he guided England to victory in the 1966 FIFA World Cup, still the country's only World Cup title.

Playing career

Ramsey played as a right-back for Southampton and Tottenham Hotspur, winning the First Division title with Spurs in 1951, and earned 32 caps for England.

Managerial career

After leading Ipswich Town from the Third Division to the First Division championship in 1962, Ramsey took charge of England in 1963. Rejecting conventional wingers, he built his famous "Wingless Wonders" around a narrow 4-3-3/4-4-2 formation, and England defeated West Germany 4–2 in the 1966 World Cup final at Wembley. He was knighted in 1967. England reached the quarter-finals of the 1970 World Cup, and Ramsey was dismissed in 1974 after failing to qualify for the 1974 tournament.
