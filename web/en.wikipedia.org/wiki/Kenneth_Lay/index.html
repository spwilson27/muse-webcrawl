Kenneth Lay

Died5 July 2006, Snowmass, Colorado, U.S.

Kenneth Lee Lay (15 April 1942 – 5 July 2006) was an American businessman who founded Enron in 1985 through a merger of Houston Natural Gas and InterNorth. As Enron's chairman and chief executive, Lay presided over the company's rise into one of the largest energy corporations in the world and over the accounting scandal that destroyed it in 2001, then one of the largest bankruptcies in American history.

Early life and career

Lay was born in Tyrone, Missouri, into a poor family; his father was a Baptist preacher. He studied economics at the University of Missouri and the University of Houston, earning a Ph.D. He worked for Humble Oil, served in the U.S. Navy, and held positions in the federal government, including Deputy Undersecretary for Energy in the Department of the Interior, before returning to the energy industry.

Enron

Lay built Enron from a pipeline company into an energy-trading giant that was named "America's Most Innovative Company" by Fortune for six consecutive years. He briefly stepped down as CEO in 2001 in favour of Jeffrey Skilling, resuming the role when Skilling resigned that August. As Enron's stock collapsed amid revelations of off-balance-sheet partnerships hiding debt, Lay sold shares while publicly reassuring employees and investors. Enron filed for bankruptcy in December 2001.

Trial and death

In 2006, Lay was tried alongside Skilling in Houston and convicted on multiple counts of securities fraud, wire fraud, and making false statements. Before sentencing, Lay died of a heart attack on 5 July 2006 while vacationing in Colorado. Because he died before sentencing and appeal, his conviction was vacated under the doctrine of abatement ab initio.
