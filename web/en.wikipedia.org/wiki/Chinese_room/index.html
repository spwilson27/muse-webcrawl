Chinese room

The Chinese room argument is a thought experiment proposed by philosopher John Searle in 1980, intended to show that a computer program manipulating symbols according to rules cannot be said to understand a language or have a mind, however convincingly it simulates conversation.

[Image placeholder: Diagram of the Chinese room thought experiment — hero image]

The argument

Searle imagines himself locked in a room with a rulebook (in English) for responding to Chinese characters passed under the door. Following the rules, he produces appropriate Chinese replies, so that outsiders judge the room to "speak Chinese" — yet Searle himself understands no Chinese. By analogy, he argues, a digital computer running a program likewise manipulates syntax without semantics: strong AI — the claim that the right program with the right inputs and outputs constitutes a mind — is false.

Replies and influence

Standard responses include the systems reply (the whole system understands, not the person), the robot reply (embodiment would ground meaning), and the brain simulator reply. The argument has been one of the most discussed in the philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence, and remains relevant to debates about whether large language models understand language.
