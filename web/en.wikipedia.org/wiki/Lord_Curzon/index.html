George Nathaniel Curzon, 1st Marquess Curzon of Kedleston (11 January 1859 – 20 March 1925), known as Lord Curzon, was a British statesman and Conservative politician who served as Viceroy of India from 1899 to 1905 and as Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs from 1919 to 1924. An ambitious and imperious figure, he was one of the most prominent imperial statesmen of his generation and was widely expected — but ultimately passed over — to become prime minister in 1923.

Early life and travels

Curzon was educated at Eton and Balliol College, Oxford, where he was president of the Oxford Union. Elected to Parliament for Southport in 1886, he travelled extensively in Central Asia, Persia, India, China, Japan and Korea, writing authoritative books including Russia in Central Asia (1889) and Persia and the Persian Question (1892). His travels and writings made him the Conservative Party's leading expert on Asian affairs, feeding his lifelong concern with the defence of British India — the "Great Game" against Russian expansion.

Viceroy of India

As Viceroy, Curzon pursued vigorous administrative reform: he tackled famine relief, police and education reform, railway expansion, and the conservation of India's monuments, passing the Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 1904 and restoring the Taj Mahal. He created the North-West Frontier Province and staged the lavish Delhi Durbar of 1903. His partition of Bengal in 1905, officially administrative, provoked furious opposition and the swadeshi movement, and was reversed in 1911. He resigned in 1905 after a dispute with the commander-in-chief, Lord Kitchener.

Later career

Curzon served in the wartime coalition governments and, as Foreign Secretary under Lloyd George and Bonar Law, negotiated the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) and gave his name to the Curzon Line, proposed in 1919 as Poland's eastern frontier. When Bonar Law resigned in 1923, George V chose Stanley Baldwin rather than Curzon as prime minister, partly because Curzon sat in the House of Lords. He died in 1925, having served briefly as Lord President of the Council.
