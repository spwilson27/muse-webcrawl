A five-star rank is the highest military rank in many countries, corresponding to NATO rank code OF-10, and is typically held only by the most senior commanders in wartime. The term originates with the United States, which created five-star ranks in December 1944 during the Second World War so that its senior commanders would not be outranked by Allied field marshals: General of the Army for the Army and Fleet Admiral for the Navy, and later General of the Air Force (1949).

United States

Only nine Americans have held five-star rank. Fleet Admirals William D. Leahy, Ernest J. King, and Chester W. Nimitz were appointed in December 1944, followed by William Halsey Jr. in 1945. Generals of the Army were George C. Marshall, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight D. Eisenhower, and Henry H. "Hap" Arnold (all December 1944), and Omar Bradley (1950). Arnold additionally became the only General of the Air Force in 1949. The rank is lifelong, carries pay for life, and has not been awarded since 1950, though it remains legally available. Six-star rank (General of the Armies) has been held only by John J. Pershing and, posthumously, George Washington and Ulysses S. Grant.

Other countries

Equivalent OF-10 ranks elsewhere include the British field marshal, admiral of the fleet, and marshal of the Royal Air Force; the marshal of France (a dignity rather than a rank); the Russian marshal of the Russian Federation; and ranks such as China's former da yuan shuai. In India, Field Marshal Sam Manekshaw and K. M. Cariappa, Marshal of the Indian Air Force Arjan Singh, and Admiral of the Fleet (honorary) have held five-star-equivalent rank. Several countries (e.g., the United Kingdom after 1995) have ceased routine appointments, reserving the rank for exceptional wartime or honorary cases.
