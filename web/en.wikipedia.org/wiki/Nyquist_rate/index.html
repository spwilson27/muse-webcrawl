Nyquist rate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Sampling below the Nyquist rate produces aliasing.

In signal processing, the Nyquist rate, named after the Swedish-American engineer Harry Nyquist, is the minimum sampling rate required to capture all the information in a continuous signal without loss. It is equal to twice the highest frequency component present in the signal:

fNyquist = 2 · B

where B is the bandwidth (maximum frequency) of the signal. A sampling rate higher than the Nyquist rate is a necessary (and, for bandlimited signals, sufficient) condition to avoid aliasing and to permit perfect reconstruction of the original signal from its samples, as established by the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem.

Nyquist frequency

The Nyquist rate should not be confused with the Nyquist frequency, which is half of a given sampling rate — the highest frequency that a system sampling at that rate can represent without aliasing. For example, the compact disc's sampling rate of 44,100 Hz has a Nyquist frequency of 22,050 Hz, chosen to exceed the Nyquist rate for the roughly 20 kHz upper limit of human hearing.

Aliasing

When a signal is sampled below its Nyquist rate, higher frequencies "fold" into lower ones, producing false components that cannot be removed afterwards. Practical converters therefore place an anti-aliasing low-pass filter before sampling, and typically sample somewhat above the theoretical minimum.
