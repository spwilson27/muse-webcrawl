Corrugated fiberboard

Cross-section of single-wall corrugated fiberboard: fluted medium between two linerboards.

Corrugated fiberboard or corrugated cardboard is a material consisting of a fluted corrugated sheet (the "medium") glued between one or two flat linerboards. It is widely used in the manufacture of shipping containers and corrugated boxes, and is the dominant packaging material for transporting goods worldwide.

Structure and grades

In single-wall board, one layer of fluted medium is sandwiched between two liners; double-wall and triple-wall boards add further fluting layers for strength. The flutes are classified by size — common profiles include A, B, C, E and F flutes, differing in height and number of flutes per metre. C-flute is the most common for shipping boxes. The linerboard and medium are usually made from kraft paper or recycled testliner. Strength is measured by tests such as the edge crush test (ECT) and the Mullen burst test.

History

Corrugated paper was patented in England in 1856 for use as a liner for tall hats. Albert Jones of New York received a US patent in 1871 for single-sided corrugated board used for wrapping bottles, and in 1874 Oliver Long patented double-faced board with liners on both sides. The first machine for continuous production was built in the 1890s, and corrugated boxes displaced wooden crates for many goods in the early 20th century.

Uses and recycling

Beyond shipping boxes, corrugated fiberboard is used for retail packaging, point-of-sale displays, pizza boxes and protective packaging inserts. It is one of the most widely recycled materials, with recovery rates exceeding 90% in some countries; recycled board is repulped to make new containerboard.
