RealD 3D is a digital stereoscopic projection technology developed by the American company RealD Inc. It is the most widely used system for showing 3D films in cinemas. Unlike older dual-projector systems, RealD uses a single digital projector that alternates left- and right-eye images at 144 frames per second (72 frames per eye per second, with each frame flashed three times — "triple flash").

Technology

A device called the ZScreen, mounted in front of the projector lens, electronically switches the circular polarisation of the light between clockwise for one eye and counter-clockwise for the other. Viewers wear inexpensive passive glasses with oppositely circularly polarised lenses, so each eye sees only its intended image. Because polarisation is circular, viewers can tilt their heads without losing the 3D effect, unlike linear polarisation systems. A silver screen is required to preserve polarisation and compensate for the light loss inherent in polarised projection.

History and adoption

RealD launched commercially with Disney's Chicken Little in 2005. The technology expanded rapidly with the success of films such as Avatar (2009). RealD installations were installed on tens of thousands of screens worldwide at the format's peak, and the company also offered a premium large-format brand, "RealD Premium" auditoriums, and the brighter XL Cinema System. Key patents drew on the work of Lenny Lipton and StereoGraphics Corporation, which RealD acquired.
