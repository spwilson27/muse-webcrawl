Victoria (commonly abbreviated as Vic) is a state in southeastern Australia. It is the second-smallest state (after Tasmania), with a land area of 227,594 km², and the second most populous state (after New South Wales), with a population of over 6.8 million. Mostly located in the south-eastern corner of the Australian mainland, it is bordered by the Bass Strait and Tasmania to the south, New South Wales to the north, the Tasman Sea (a marginal sea of the South Pacific Ocean) to the east, and South Australia and the Great Australian Bight to the west. The state encompasses a range of climates and geographical features from its temperate coastal and central regions to the Victorian Alps in the northeast and the semi-arid north-west.

Victoria, in south-eastern Australia

The capital and largest city is Melbourne, Australia's second-largest city, with over a third of Victorians living in the Greater Melbourne area. The city is the state capital and the commercial, cultural and sporting centre of the state. Other major population centres include Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, Shepparton, Mildura, Warrnambool, Wodonga and the Latrobe Valley.

History

Aboriginal Victorians have occupied the area for at least 40,000 years. The first European settlers arrived in the mid-1830s, and the area was part of the colony of New South Wales until the Colony of Victoria was proclaimed in 1851. The Victorian gold rush of the 1850s and 1860s dramatically increased the population and wealth of the colony; by the time of Australian Federation in 1901, Melbourne had become the largest city and leading financial centre in Australasia, and served as the seat of the federal government until the opening of Parliament House in Canberra in 1927.

Geography

Victoria's northern border follows a straight line from Cape Howe to the start of the Murray River and then along the Murray River as the remainder of the northern border. On the Murray River, the border is the southern bank of the river. West of this longitude, Victoria's border is the South Australian border, which it shares to the west. The Great Dividing Range runs east–west through the centre of the state. In the far northwest is part of the semi-arid Mallee country. The state has many significant rivers, including the Murray, the Goulburn, the Campaspe, the Loddon, the Wimmera and the Yarra.

Economy

Victoria has a diverse economy with service sectors as the main sources of employment. Historically, Victoria was a major centre of manufacturing; it is the country's financial and property hub, and Melbourne hosts the headquarters of many major Australian companies. The state is also a significant producer of dairy products, and its agricultural sector contributes substantially to exports.
