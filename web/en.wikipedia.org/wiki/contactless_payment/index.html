Contactless payment

Contactless payment systems are credit cards, debit cards, key fobs, smart cards, or other devices — including smartphones and other mobile devices — that use radio-frequency identification (RFID) or near-field communication (NFC) to make secure payments. The embedded chip and antenna enable consumers to wave or tap the device over a reader at the point of sale.

Technology

Contactless cards communicate with the terminal over a short-range radio link (13.56 MHz) using standards based on ISO/IEC 14443 and the EMV contactless specifications maintained by the card schemes (Visa payWave, Mastercard Contactless, American Express ExpressPay, and others). Transactions are protected by dynamic cryptograms, and card payments above a country-specific limit require additional verification such as a PIN or device biometrics. Mobile wallets such as Apple Pay, Google Pay and Samsung Pay emulate contactless cards using NFC and tokenisation.

Adoption

Contactless payment spread from the 2000s onward, with rapid adoption in Europe, Australia and Canada, and major deployments in public transport systems such as London's. Use expanded sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic as transaction limits were raised and consumers preferred touch-free payment. By the early 2020s contactless had become the dominant mode of card payment in many countries.
