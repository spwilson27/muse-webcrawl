Ziguinchor (French pronunciation: [ziɡɛ̃ʃɔʁ]) is the capital of the Ziguinchor Region in southwestern Senegal and the chief city of the Casamance region. It lies on the Casamance River, about 70 km from the Atlantic Ocean and south of The Gambia, which separates Casamance from the rest of Senegal. Its port is an important outlet for the agricultural produce of the region, especially groundnuts, cashews and rice.

History

The Portuguese established a trading post at Ziguinchor in 1645, making it one of the oldest European settlements in the region. Portugal ceded the town to France in 1886, and under French rule it developed as a colonial administrative centre and groundnut port. After Senegalese independence in 1960, Ziguinchor became the capital of Casamance. Since the early 1980s, the region has been affected by a low-level separatist conflict led by the Movement of Democratic Forces of Casamance (MFDC), though peace agreements have greatly reduced violence. In 2002, the ferry MV Le Joola, travelling between Ziguinchor and Dakar, capsized with the loss of more than 1,800 lives — one of the worst maritime disasters in history.

Geography and climate

Ziguinchor has a tropical savanna climate with a rainy season from June to October, receiving far more rainfall than northern Senegal. The surrounding area is characterised by rice paddies, mangroves and palm groves, in contrast to the drier Sahel to the north.

Economy

The economy is based on trade and processing of agricultural products — groundnuts, cashew nuts, mangoes, rice and fish — and on its river port. The city is also a gateway for tourists travelling to the coastal resorts of Cap Skirring. Ziguinchor is served by an airport and by road and ferry links to Dakar.
