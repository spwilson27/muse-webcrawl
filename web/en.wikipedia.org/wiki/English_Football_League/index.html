The English Football League (EFL) is a league competition featuring professional association football clubs from England and Wales. Founded in 1888 as the Football League, it is the oldest such competition in the world and was the top-level league in England until the breakaway formation of the Premier League in 1992. Since then it has comprised the second, third, and fourth tiers of the English football league system.

Structure

The EFL consists of 72 clubs divided into three divisions of 24: the Championship (second tier), League One (third tier), and League Two (fourth tier). Each club plays the others in its division twice over a season running from August to May. Promotion and relegation link the divisions: the top Championship clubs are promoted to the Premier League, with the final place decided by the play-offs, while the bottom clubs in League Two are relegated to the National League.

History

The Football League was founded at a meeting in Manchester in April 1888, driven by Aston Villa director William McGregor, and its first season began in September 1888 with twelve founder members from the Midlands and North. Preston North End won the inaugural title and the FA Cup "Double" without losing a match. The league expanded over the following decades, introducing a Second Division in 1892, regional Third Divisions in 1920–21, and a national Fourth Division in 1958. In 1992 the First Division clubs resigned en masse to form the FA Premier League, leaving the Football League with three divisions. It rebranded as the English Football League in 2016.

Competitions

Besides the league, the EFL organizes the EFL Cup (League Cup, first held in 1960–61) and the EFL Trophy for League One and League Two clubs (with invited academy teams since 2016). EFL clubs also compete in the FA Cup.
