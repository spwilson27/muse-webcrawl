Alexander Baron (born Joseph Alexander Bernstein; 4 December 1917 – 6 December 1999) was a British author and screenwriter. He is best known for his war novel From the City, From the Plough (1948), drawn from his own experience as an infantryman in the Second World War, and for The Lowlife (1963), a portrait of Jewish life in post-war Hackney.

Early life and war service

Baron was born in Maidenhead to Jewish parents of Russian descent and grew up in Hackney, east London. Before the war he was politically active on the left and worked as a journalist. He served as a pioneer and infantryman, landing in Normandy on D-Day and fighting through France and into Germany; the experience formed the basis of his first and most celebrated novel, and thereafter he wrote pseudonymously under the name Alexander Baron, later adopting it by deed poll.

Writing career

From the City, From the Plough (1948) was a major critical and popular success and is often ranked among the finest British novels of the Second World War. It formed the first part of an informal trilogy continued in There's No Home (1950), set in Sicily, and The Human Kind (1953). Baron wrote many further novels, among them Rosie Hogarth (1951), The Lowlife (1963) — later reissued to acclaim — and King Dido (1969). He also had a substantial career as a screenwriter for British television, contributing to series such as The Adventures of Robin Hood.

Legacy

Long comparatively neglected, Baron's fiction was revived in the 2000s and 2010s with reissues of The Lowlife and From the City, From the Plough, restoring his reputation as one of the most distinctive London novelists of the post-war period.
