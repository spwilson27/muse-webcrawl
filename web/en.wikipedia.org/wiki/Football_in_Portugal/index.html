Football in Portugal

Football is the most popular sport in Portugal. The game was introduced in the late nineteenth century by Portuguese students returning from England and by British expatriates, and the first organized match in the country was played in 1875 in Funchal, Madeira. The sport is governed by the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF), founded in 1914.

Club football

The top professional league is the Primeira Liga, founded in 1934. Portuguese club football is dominated by the "Big Three" — SL Benfica, FC Porto, and Sporting CP — which between them have won nearly every league title; only Belenenses (1946) and Boavista (2001) have broken the monopoly. FC Porto has been the most successful Portuguese club in Europe, winning the European Cup/Champions League in 1987 and 2004 and the UEFA Cup/Europa League in 2003 and 2011. The main cup competition is the Taça de Portugal.

National team

The Portugal national team made its first major impact finishing third at the 1966 World Cup, led by Eusébio. In the modern era, captained by Cristiano Ronaldo — widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time — Portugal won its first major trophy at UEFA Euro 2016, beating hosts France in the final, and also won the inaugural UEFA Nations League in 2019.

Notable players

Portugal has produced world-class players including Eusébio, Luís Figo, Rui Costa, Deco, Cristiano Ronaldo, and Bernardo Silva, and renowned coaches such as José Mourinho.
