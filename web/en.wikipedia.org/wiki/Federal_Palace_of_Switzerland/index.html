Federal Palace of Switzerland

The Federal Palace (German: Bundeshaus; French: Palais fédéral; Italian: Palazzo federale) is the building in Bern housing the Swiss Federal Assembly (the parliament) and the Federal Council (the executive). It sits on the edge of the terrace above the Aare river, south of the medieval old town, and is crowned by a dome 64 metres high.

History and description

The palace is the third parliament building on the site. The present Bundeshaus was designed by the architect Hans Wilhelm Auer and built between 1894 and 1902 using some 30 types of Swiss stone and marble — a deliberate showcase of the country's materials and craftsmanship. The two chambers sit in the wings: the National Council to the west and the Council of States to the east, beneath the central dome hall. The interior is decorated with paintings, mosaics, stained glass and statuary illustrating Swiss history and the virtues of the state, including the monumental painting of the Rütli meadow in the Council of States chamber.

Surroundings

In front of the palace lies the Bundesplatz, the Federal Square, paved with natural stone and fitted with a fountain of 26 water jets representing the cantons; it hosts markets and political demonstrations. The building was extensively renovated between 2006 and 2008.
