Christophorus Henricus Diedericus Buys Ballot (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈbœys bɑˈlɔt]; 10 October 1817 – 3 February 1890) was a Dutch chemist and meteorologist, remembered for Buys Ballot's law of winds and as the founder of the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute (KNMI).

Life and career

Buys Ballot was born in Kloetinge, the son of a minister. He studied at Utrecht University, earning his doctorate in 1844, and became professor of mathematics and later of physics at Utrecht. Although trained as a chemist — he worked on topics including the study of the Doppler effect, which he verified experimentally in 1845 using musicians on a moving train between Utrecht and Maarssen — his lasting influence lay in meteorology.

Meteorology

In 1854 Buys Ballot founded the KNMI at the Sonnenborgh observatory in Utrecht and served as its director until his death. He was a pioneer of international cooperation in weather observation, advocating for standardized measurements and telegraphic exchange of data, and he helped found the International Meteorological Organization, predecessor of the World Meteorological Organization, serving as its first president from 1873 to 1879.

Buys Ballot's law

In 1857 he published the empirical rule now called Buys Ballot's law: in the Northern Hemisphere, if an observer stands with their back to the wind, atmospheric pressure is lower to their left than to their right; the reverse holds in the Southern Hemisphere. The law, related to the geostrophic wind and Coriolis effect, became a practical rule for mariners and forecasters.

Honours

The lunar crater Buys-Ballot is named after him, as are streets and institutions in the Netherlands. A commemorative medal for meteorology bears his name.
