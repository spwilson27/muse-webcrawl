Trijet

Trijet
ConfigurationThree jet engines: two wing- or rear-mounted, one in the tail
ExamplesBoeing 727, Tupolev Tu-154, McDonnell Douglas DC-10, Lockheed L-1011 TriStar
StatusNo longer in production for airliners; still used in some business jets

A trijet is a jet aircraft powered by three jet engines. In general, passenger airline trijets are considered second-generation jet airliners. Because of their efficiency and a historically favourable regulatory environment, trijets once dominated wide-body and medium-range fleets.

Design

Most trijets mount two engines either on the wings or at the rear of the fuselage, with the third engine located in the tail. The tail engine may be fed through an S-shaped duct, as on the Boeing 727 and Tupolev Tu-154, or through a straight duct, as on the McDonnell Douglas DC-10 and Lockheed L-1011 TriStar.

History

Trijets were favoured in the 1960s–1980s because ETOPS rules restricted twin-engine aircraft from flying far from diversion airports, while four-engine types were costlier to operate. As twin-engine reliability improved and ETOPS limits were extended, trijets were displaced by more economical twins. The configuration survives in business jets such as the Dassault Falcon 7X and 8X, where the third engine improves short-field and overwater performance.
