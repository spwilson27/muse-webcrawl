Ruck (rugby union)

Ruck
SportRugby union
Governing lawWorld Rugby Law 15
Related phaseMaul (ball off the ground), tackle

A ruck is a phase of play in rugby union in which one or more players from each team, who are on their feet and in physical contact, close around the ball on the ground. It is formed after a tackle when the ball carrier has gone to ground and contesting players drive over the ball to secure possession for their side.

Laws

Under Law 15 of World Rugby's laws, a ruck requires at least one player from each team on their feet, bound, over the ball on the ground. Players must join from behind the hindmost foot (the offside line at the ruck) and through the "gate" — joining from the side is penalised. Once the ruck is formed, players may not handle the ball in the ruck (except the scrum-half or acting half-back who may dig the ball out when it is clearly available); they contest by pushing ("rucking" with the feet in a backward motion is permitted only to hook the ball back, and never to contact opponents' bodies, especially heads). Players must not collapse the ruck, jump on it, or return the ball into it. When the ball is available, the referee calls "use it", and the side in possession has five seconds to play it.

Tactics and safety

Fast, clean ruck ball is the foundation of attacking rugby: forwards "clear out" defenders while backs realign. The breakdown is also the most refereed and debated area of the game, with regular law trials addressing jackalling (stealing the ball over a tackled player), entry angles and player welfare, particularly head and neck safety in clean-outs.
