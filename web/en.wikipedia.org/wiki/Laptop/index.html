Laptop

Laptop computer

Type: Personal computer
Form factor: Clamshell (typically)
Power: Rechargeable battery / AC adapter
Input: Keyboard, touchpad, webcam, microphone

A laptop computer or notebook computer is a small, portable personal computer with a screen and alphanumeric keyboard, typically in a clamshell form factor: the screen folds down over the keyboard for transport. Laptops combine the display, processor, memory, storage, battery, and input devices of a desktop computer into a single unit that can run on rechargeable batteries. They are widely used for work, education, communication, and entertainment.

History

The idea of a portable notebook-like computer was articulated in Alan Kay's Dynabook concept of 1968. Early portable machines of the late 1970s and early 1980s, such as the Osborne 1 (1981), were "luggable" rather than truly lap-sized. The Grid Compass (1982) pioneered the clamshell design with a flat-panel display, and machines such as the Toshiba T1100 (1985) helped establish the standard laptop format. Through the 1990s and 2000s, colour screens, lithium-ion batteries, Wi-Fi, and falling prices made laptops mainstream; by around 2008 laptop sales surpassed desktop sales worldwide.

Categories

  • Ultraportables / ultrabooks — thin, light designs emphasizing battery life.
  • Gaming laptops — powerful discrete graphics and cooling, at the cost of weight.
  • 2-in-1 convertibles — touchscreens that fold back or detach for tablet use.
  • Chromebooks — low-cost machines running ChromeOS, common in education.
  • Desktop replacements — large screens and high performance.
  • Rugged laptops — reinforced for field, military, and industrial use.

Components and design

A laptop integrates a CPU (often with integrated graphics), RAM, solid-state storage, a display (commonly 13–17 inches), a lithium-ion or lithium-polymer battery, wireless networking, and ports such as USB-C, HDMI, and audio jacks. Because everything must fit in a thin chassis with limited cooling, laptop components are designed for power efficiency, and upgradability is usually more limited than in desktops.
