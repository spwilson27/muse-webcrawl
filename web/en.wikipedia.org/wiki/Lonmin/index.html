Lonmin

Lonmin plc, formerly Lonrho plc, was a British producer of platinum group metals operating mainly in the Bushveld Complex of South Africa. It was listed on the London Stock Exchange and was a constituent of the FTSE 250 Index at one time. The company was incorporated in the United Kingdom on 13 May 1909 as the London and Rhodesian Mining and Land Company Limited. In 2019 it was acquired by Sibanye-Stillwater (now Sibanye Stillwater) for approximately $286 million.

History

Under Tiny Rowland, who led the company from 1962, Lonrho became a vast conglomerate with interests across Africa — mining, agriculture, hotels, newspapers (The Observer) — and was famously described by Prime Minister Edward Heath in 1973 as "the unacceptable face of capitalism" during a dispute over director payments. Rowland was ousted in the 1990s, and the group progressively sold its non-mining assets, renaming itself Lonmin in 1999 (from "London Mining") to focus on platinum.

Marikana massacre

In August 2012, a wildcat strike at Lonmin's Marikana platinum mine over pay demands escalated into violence; on 16 August 2012 South African police shot dead 34 striking miners, the deadliest use of force by South African security forces since the end of apartheid. The Farlam Commission of Inquiry criticised both the police operation and Lonmin's handling of the dispute and its unmet obligations to build housing for workers.

Acquisition

Weakened by low platinum prices, labour unrest, and debt, Lonmin agreed in December 2017 to be acquired by gold and PGM producer Sibanye-Stillwater; the all-share deal completed in June 2019 after competition approvals, making the combined group the world's largest platinum producer.
