Battle of Edson's Ridge

The Battle of Edson's Ridge, also known as the Battle of the Bloody Ridge or the Battle of Bloody Ridge, was fought from 12 to 14 September 1942 during the Guadalcanal campaign of the Second World War. United States Marine Corps Raiders and Paramarines, commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Merritt A. "Red Mike" Edson, defended a grassy ridge south of Henderson Field against repeated night attacks by Japanese forces under Major General Kiyotake Kawaguchi.

Background

After the defeat of the Ichiki detachment at the Tenaru in August, the Japanese landed Kawaguchi's brigade — some 6,000 men — to retake the airfield at Henderson Field. Kawaguchi planned a three-pronged night assault, with his main body attacking from the south across a long, bare ridge that ran toward the airfield perimeter. Edson, warned by intelligence and coastwatchers, rested his roughly 840 Raiders and Paramarines on the ridge on 11 September.

The battle

Japanese aircraft bombed the ridge on 11 and 12 September, and on the night of 12–13 September Kawaguchi's troops attacked, driving in the Marine left flank and forcing Edson to contract his line around the ridge's central knoll. On the following night, repeated frontal assaults struck the shortened perimeter; fighting was often hand-to-hand, and Marine artillery fired at point-blank ranges over open sights. Edson moved constantly along the line encouraging his men. By dawn on 14 September the attacks had collapsed, and Japanese survivors retreated into the jungle.

Aftermath

The Marines lost about 104 killed; Japanese dead numbered around 700–850. The failure convinced Japanese commanders that stronger forces would be needed, leading to the larger October battles for Henderson Field. Edson received the Medal of Honor for his leadership, and the ridge has carried his name ever since.
