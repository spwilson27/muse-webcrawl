Somali Region

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Somali Region (Somali: Dawlad Deegaanka Soomaalida), officially the Somali Regional State, is the easternmost of the regions (kililoch) of Ethiopia. It is the second-largest region by area and is inhabited predominantly by ethnic Somalis. Its capital and largest city is Jijiga. Much of the region corresponds to the Ogaden, the arid lowland plateau between the Ethiopian highlands and Somalia.

Geography and economy

The region is largely hot, dry lowland, descending from the Harar plateau in the west toward the Shebelle River and the Somali border. Pastoralism — herding camels, sheep, and goats — is the dominant livelihood, supplemented by agro-pastoral farming along rivers and by cross-border trade. The region borders Djibouti and Somaliland to the north, Somalia to the east and south, and Kenya to the southwest, as well as the Ethiopian regions of Oromia, Afar, and Harari.

History

The Ogaden was incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire during the conquests of Menelik II in the late 19th century. In 1977–78 Somalia invaded in an attempt to annex the region, precipitating the Ogaden War, which Ethiopia won with Soviet and Cuban support. Under Ethiopia's ethnic-federal constitution of 1995, the area became the Somali Regional State. From 1994 the Ogaden National Liberation Front waged a low-level insurgency until a peace agreement in 2018. In August 2018 the region's president Abdi Mohamoud Omar was removed amid unrest, and Mustafa Muhumed Omar became acting president. The region has repeatedly suffered drought and humanitarian crises, including the severe Horn of Africa drought of the early 2020s.
