Ernst & Young, doing business as EY, is a multinational professional services partnership headquartered in London, England. It is one of the largest professional services networks in the world and, alongside Deloitte, PwC and KPMG, one of the "Big Four" accounting firms. EY member firms provide assurance (audit), tax, consulting and strategy-and-transactions services to clients in more than 150 countries.

History

The firm traces its origins to 19th-century accountants including Alwin C. Ernst in Cleveland (Ernst & Ernst, founded 1903) and Arthur Young in Chicago (Arthur Young & Co., founded 1906). In 1989 Ernst & Whinney merged with Arthur Young to form Ernst & Young. The firm rebranded globally as EY in 2013.

Notable controversies

EY was the auditor of Lehman Brothers and settled litigation over Repo 105 accounting after the bank's 2008 collapse. It was also Wirecard's auditor for a decade before that company's 2020 collapse amid a €1.9 billion accounting scandal, leading to regulatory sanctions in Germany. A planned global split of its audit and consulting arms ("Project Everest") was abandoned in 2023.
