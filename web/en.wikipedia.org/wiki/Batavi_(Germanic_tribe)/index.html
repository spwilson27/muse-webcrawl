Batavi (Germanic tribe)

The Batavi (also Batavians) were an ancient Germanic tribe who lived in the marshy island region between the Rhine and Waal rivers in what is now the Netherlands — the area the Romans called the Insula Batavorum ("Island of the Batavi"). By Roman accounts they were an offshoot of the Chatti who had migrated to the lower Rhine.

Relations with Rome

The Batavi formed a special alliance with Rome: exempt from tribute, they instead supplied soldiers, becoming renowned as elite troops — especially cavalry and swimmers who could cross rivers in full armour. Batavian cohorts served across the empire, including in Britain, and Batavian troops formed part of the emperor's mounted bodyguard in Rome.

The Batavian revolt

In 69–70 AD, during Rome's Year of the Four Emperors, the Batavi under Julius Civilis rose in the Revolt of the Batavi, one of the most serious uprisings on the Rhine frontier. Although eventually suppressed by Quintus Petillius Cerialis, the revolt secured negotiated terms, and the Batavi resumed their privileged military role.

Legacy

The Batavi were later celebrated in Dutch national mythology as ancestral heroes of the Netherlands; the name survives in the region of Betuwe and in Jakarta's colonial name, Batavia.
