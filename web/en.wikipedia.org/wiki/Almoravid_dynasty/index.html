Almoravid dynasty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Qubba al Barudiyyin in Marrakesh, an Almoravid ablutions pavilion with carved dome]

The Almoravid dynasty (Arabic al-Murābiṭūn, from whose name 'marabout' also derives) was a Berber Muslim dynasty of the Sanhaja tribes of the western Sahara, which between the 1040s and 1147 built an empire spanning Morocco, Mauritania, western Algeria and Muslim Spain (al-Andalus). Born as a puritan reform movement under the scholar Abdullah ibn Yasin — its veiled warriors living as monk-soldiers in a ribat (fortified monastery) — the Almoravids conquered Morocco (founding Marrakesh, c. 1070), intervened in Spain to defeat Alfonso VI at Zallaqa (1086) and annex the taifa kingdoms, and briefly reunited the western Islamic world before falling to the yet more puritanical Almohads.

From ribat to empire

Ibn Yasin's preaching among the Lamtuna and Gudala Sanhaja hardened into a jihad state under Yusuf ibn Tashufin (r. 1061–1106), who took Fez, founded Marrakesh as his capital, and crossed to Spain at the taifas' desperate invitation after Toledo's fall (1085). At Zallaqa (Sagrajas) he shattered Alfonso VI's army; within a decade he had deposed the taifa kings he came to save, uniting al-Andalus under a stern Maliki Islam — poets and philosophers, and the minorities, felt the change.

Decline and Almohad succession

Under Ali ibn Yusuf the empire overextended: war in Spain against a resurgent Christian coalition (Zaragoza fell 1118), revolts in the Maghreb, and the rise of Ibn Tumart's Almohad movement in the Atlas, preaching unitarianism against Almoravid 'anthropomorphism'. Almohad armies took Marrakesh in 1147 after an eleven-month siege; the last Almoravid emir died in the fall of the city. The Almoravids left the Koutoubia's first mosque, the Qubba's exquisite plasterwork in Marrakesh, and a name still carried by Mauritania's marabouts.
