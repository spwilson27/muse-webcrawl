Unit record equipment
Era1890s-1960s
MediumPunched cards
Principal makerIBM

Unit record equipment was a class of electromechanical data processing machines that operated on punched cards, in which each card ("unit record") carried one record of information. Before the widespread adoption of electronic computers, these machines performed the world's business data processing, including billing, payroll, inventory, and census tabulation.

The equipment included keypunches for entering data onto cards, sorters that arranged cards by column values, collators that merged decks, reproducers, and accounting machines (tabulators) that read cards and printed reports and totals.

History

The technology originated with Herman Hollerith's tabulating machines used for the 1890 United States census. His company eventually became part of IBM, which dominated the unit record market. Machines were "programmed" by removable plugboard control panels wired for each application. Unit record installations remained common into the 1960s and 1970s before being displaced by stored-program computers.
