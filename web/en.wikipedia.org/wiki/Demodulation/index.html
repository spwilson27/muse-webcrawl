Demodulation is the process of extracting the original information-bearing signal from a modulated carrier wave. A demodulator (sometimes called a detector) is an electronic circuit — or, in modern systems, a software algorithm — that recovers the content from the modulated carrier. Demodulation is the inverse of modulation and is performed by every radio, television, telephone modem and wireless network receiver.

Overview

Because audio, video and data signals occupy low frequencies that cannot be transmitted efficiently through space or long cables, they are impressed onto a high-frequency carrier by varying its amplitude, frequency, phase, or combinations of these. At the receiver, demodulation reverses the process: the carrier is removed and the original baseband signal is recovered, then amplified, filtered, or converted back into digital data. The pair of operations is combined in the word "modem" (modulator–demodulator).

Techniques

Amplitude modulation

For AM signals, the simplest demodulator is the envelope detector — a diode rectifier followed by a low-pass filter that follows the outline of the carrier's amplitude. Synchronous (coherent) detection, in which the received signal is multiplied by a locally generated carrier, gives better performance and is used in more sophisticated receivers. Single-sideband (SSB) signals must be demodulated coherently, for instance with a product detector and beat-frequency oscillator.

Frequency and phase modulation

FM can be demodulated with a frequency discriminator, a Foster–Seeley discriminator, a ratio detector, or a phase-locked loop, all of which convert frequency variations into voltage variations. The quadrature detector is common in integrated-circuit FM radios. Phase modulation is closely related to FM and is demodulated by similar coherent methods.

Digital modulation

Digital schemes such as ASK, FSK, PSK and QAM are demodulated by correlating the received waveform with reference signals, often after down-conversion to in-phase and quadrature (I/Q) components in a software-defined radio. Error-correction decoding follows demodulation to reconstruct the transmitted bit stream. Historically, specialised demodulators such as the coherer and magnetic detector enabled the first wireless telegraphy receivers before the advent of thermionic valves.
