New York Comic Con (NYCC) is an annual fan convention held in New York City dedicated to comics, graphic novels, anime, manga, video games, toys, movies, and television. Held each fall at the Jacob K. Javits Convention Center in Manhattan, it is produced by ReedPop and has grown into one of the largest pop-culture conventions in North America, rivalling San Diego Comic-Con in attendance.

History

The first New York Comic Con was held February 24–26, 2006, at the Javits Center. Unexpectedly large crowds on the Saturday overwhelmed the smaller exhibit space and fire marshals temporarily limited entry, prompting the show to expand in subsequent years. By the early 2010s NYCC occupied the entire Javits Center and added satellite venues, and its four-day attendance passed 150,000. The 2020 edition was held online as part of a virtual "Metaverse" event because of the COVID-19 pandemic, returning to an in-person format in 2021 with capacity limits. Recent editions have drawn more than 200,000 attendees across four days.

Programming

NYCC features publisher and studio panels — Marvel, DC, and major film and television studios regularly preview upcoming releases — an Artist Alley hosting hundreds of comics creators, autograph and photo-op sessions, screenings, gaming areas, and a large cosplay presence, including the NYCC Championships of Cosplay. From 2011 the convention also absorbed the New York Anime Festival. The show is a major venue for industry announcements and exclusive merchandise.
