Flash point

The flash point of a material is the lowest liquid temperature at which, under certain standardized conditions, a liquid gives off vapours in a quantity such as to be capable of forming an ignitable vapour/air mixture.

Measurement

There are two basic types of flash point measurement: open cup and closed cup. In open-cup devices the sample is contained in an open cup heated from below; in closed-cup testing the vapours are confined and the measured flash point is lower. Standard methods include the Pensky–Martens closed-cup and the Cleveland open-cup tests.

Examples

Gasoline has a flash point of about −23 °C (−10 °F), diesel fuel ranges from 52 to 96 °C, and ethanol is about 13 °C. Liquids with a flash point below about 60 °C are generally classified as flammable, while those above are combustible under many regulatory schemes.

Relation to fire point and autoignition

The flash point is distinct from the fire point, the temperature at which vapours continue to burn after ignition, and from the autoignition temperature, at which the material ignites spontaneously without an ignition source.
