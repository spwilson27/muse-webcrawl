Jozef Gašpar Tiso (13 October 1887 – 18 April 1947) was a Slovak politician and Roman Catholic priest who headed the Slovak Republic, a client state of Nazi Germany during World War II, from 1939 to 1945. Under his leadership Slovakia deported the great majority of its Jewish population to German extermination and concentration camps.

Early career

Ordained in 1910, Tiso became a leading figure in Andrej Hlinka's Slovak People's Party, which campaigned for Slovak autonomy within Czechoslovakia. After Hlinka's death in 1938 Tiso led the party and became prime minister of the autonomous Slovak region created after the Munich Agreement.

The Slovak state

On 14 March 1939, under direct pressure from Adolf Hitler, the Slovak Diet declared independence. Tiso became prime minister and, in October 1939, president. His regime was authoritarian and aligned with Germany. In 1942 the government organised the deportation of some 58,000 Slovak Jews, most of whom were murdered; deportations resumed in 1944 after Germany occupied Slovakia in response to the Slovak National Uprising. Tiso fled in 1945, was captured by American forces in Austria, and was extradited to Czechoslovakia.

Trial and execution

Tiso was tried by the National Court in Bratislava, convicted of treason, crimes against humanity and responsibility for the persecution and deportation of Jews, and hanged on 18 April 1947. His legacy remains deeply contested: commemorated by some Slovak nationalists, he is condemned by historians for his regime's collaboration and its central role in the Holocaust in Slovakia.
