Apostolic Tradition

The Apostolic Tradition is an early Christian church order traditionally attributed to Hippolytus of Rome (c. 215 AD), though modern scholarship views it as a composite, layered document from multiple regions and periods.

Content

It describes ordination of bishops, presbyters and deacons, the catechumenate, baptism, Eucharist — including one of the earliest recorded eucharistic prayers (anaphora) — fasting, daily prayer, and burial.

Influence

Preserved in Latin, Ethiopic, Coptic and Arabic versions, it heavily influenced modern liturgical reform, including the Roman Missal's Eucharistic Prayer II. See Apostolic Fathers.
