Tanya Tagaq

Tanya Tagaq (born 5 May 1975), also credited as Tagaq, is a Canadian Inuk throat singer, composer, writer and visual artist from Cambridge Bay (Ikaluktuuttiaq), Nunavut. She is acclaimed internationally for reinventing the traditional Inuit practice of katajjaq — normally performed by two women facing each other — as a solo, improvisatory art form fused with punk, metal and avant-garde influences.

Career

Tagaq came to attention through collaborations with Björk, including on the 2004 album Medúlla and the film Drawing Restraint 9, and with the Kronos Quartet. Her album Animism won the 2014 Polaris Music Prize and a Juno Award, bringing her ferocious, politically charged performances to a wide audience. Subsequent recordings include Retribution (2016) and Tongues (2022).

Writing and advocacy

Her debut book, Split Tooth (2018), a genre-blending work of fiction and memoir, was longlisted for the Scotiabank Giller Prize. Tagaq is a prominent advocate for Indigenous rights, food sovereignty and action on missing and murdered Indigenous women, and was appointed a Member of the Order of Canada.
