Obsessive–compulsive disorder

Obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD) is a mental disorder characterised by intrusive, unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviours or mental acts (compulsions) performed in response to them. Common obsessions concern contamination, harm, symmetry and forbidden thoughts; common compulsions include washing, checking, counting and ordering. The symptoms are time-consuming and cause significant distress or impairment, and are not simply excessive versions of ordinary worries.

Causes

The causes are incompletely understood. Genetics contributes substantially, and neuroimaging implicates dysfunction in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits and serotonergic signalling. Psychological models emphasise inflated responsibility beliefs and intolerance of uncertainty.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical, based on criteria such as those in the DSM-5, which since 2013 places OCD in its own chapter rather than among the anxiety disorders. Severity is often measured with the Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS).

Treatment

First-line treatments are cognitive behavioural therapy centred on exposure and response prevention (ERP), and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), often at higher doses than for depression. Many patients respond to a combination of the two.
