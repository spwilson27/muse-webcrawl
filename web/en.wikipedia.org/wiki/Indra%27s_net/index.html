Indra's net

Traditions: Hinduism; Huayan and Mahayana Buddhism

Key text: Avatamsaka Sutra

Indra's net is a metaphor used in Indian religions to illustrate interdependence and interpenetration. In the myth, the god Indra's palace is covered by an infinite net, with a jewel at every knot; each jewel reflects every other jewel, so that all things contain and are contained by all others.

In Buddhism

The metaphor was developed extensively in the Avatamsaka Sutra and by the Chinese Huayan school, whose masters such as Fazang used it (and the image of a golden lion) to explain how phenomena arise interdependently while remaining distinct.

Modern use

The image is frequently invoked in discussions of systems theory, ecology, and network philosophy as a symbol of mutual interconnection.
