Sierra Leone, officially the Republic of Sierra Leone, is a country on the south-west coast of West Africa, bordered by Guinea to the north and east, Liberia to the south-east, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west and south-west. It has a tropical climate with a diverse environment ranging from savanna to rainforest, a total area of about 71,740 km² and a population of roughly eight and a half million. The capital and largest city is Freetown, founded as a settlement for freed slaves.

History

The coastal region was visited by Portuguese explorers in the 1460s; Pedro de Sintra named the hills around the harbour Serra Leoa ("Lion Mountains"). The region became a significant centre of the transatlantic slave trade. In 1787 British abolitionists and the Sierra Leone Company established a settlement of freed slaves—the "Province of Freedom"—at what became Freetown; in 1808 the coastal colony became a British crown colony, and a protectorate over the hinterland was declared in 1896. The Krio people, descendants of the settlers and liberated Africans, developed a distinctive Creole culture and language.

Sierra Leone became independent on 27 April 1961 under Prime Minister Milton Margai. A republic was declared in 1971 under Siaka Stevens, whose All People's Congress ruled, increasingly authoritarianly, until 1985. Military coups followed in 1992. In 1991 the Revolutionary United Front (RUF), backed by Charles Taylor's Liberia, launched a brutal civil war notorious for atrocities and the use of child soldiers and "blood diamonds". The war ended in 2002 after British and UN intervention (Operation Palliser, 2000), followed by disarmament and reconstruction. In 2014–16 Sierra Leone was one of the countries worst hit by the West African Ebola epidemic, which killed nearly 4,000 Sierra Leoneans.

Geography and climate

The country has four main regions: coastal mangrove swamps, the Sierra Leone Peninsula hills near Freetown, an inland plain, and eastern mountains and plateaus, including Mount Bintumani (Loma Mansa, 1,948 m), the highest peak in West Africa west of Cameroon. The climate is tropical monsoon, with a rainy season from May to November. The capital region sits around one of the world's largest natural harbours.

Politics and economy

Sierra Leone is a constitutional republic with an elected president and unicameral parliament; politics has been dominated by the Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP) and the All People's Congress (APC). Julius Maada Bio of the SLPP won the presidency in 2018 and was re-elected in 2023. The economy depends on agriculture (employing most of the workforce), fishing and mining—diamonds, rutile, bauxite, gold and iron ore. Despite rich resources, Sierra Leone remains one of the world's poorer countries, with high rates of maternal mortality and limited infrastructure, though it has sustained long periods of post-war growth.

Demographics and culture

The population comprises about sixteen ethnic groups, the largest being the Temne and Mende, each roughly a third of the population; Krio is spoken by nearly everyone as a lingua franca. Islam is the majority religion (about three-quarters of the population), with a substantial Christian minority and strong inter-religious tolerance traditions. The country is known for its secret societies (such as the Bondo and Poro), its music and storytelling traditions, and for Freetown's landmark 500-year-old cotton tree, which fell in a storm in 2023.
