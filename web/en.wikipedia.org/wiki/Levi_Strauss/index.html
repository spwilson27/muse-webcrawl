Levi Strauss (born Löb Strauß; 26 February 1829 – 26 September 1902) was a German-born American businessman who founded Levi Strauss & Co., the first company to manufacture blue jeans. His firm, established in San Francisco in 1853 during the California Gold Rush, grew from a wholesale dry goods business into one of the world's most recognizable clothing companies.

Early life and emigration

Strauss was born into a Jewish family in Buttenheim, Bavaria, in the Kingdom of Bavaria. In 1847 he emigrated to the United States to join his brothers in New York, where they ran a dry goods wholesale business. After becoming an American citizen, he travelled west to San Francisco in 1853 to open a West Coast branch, carrying goods around Cape Horn. The business, Levi Strauss & Co., supplied miners and merchants with clothing, bedding, combs, and other dry goods, and Strauss became a prominent and philanthropic figure in San Francisco's business and Jewish communities.

Blue jeans

In 1872 Strauss received a letter from Jacob Davis, a tailor in Reno, Nevada, who had devised a method of strengthening work trousers by riveting the stress points of the pockets with copper rivets, but lacked the money to patent the idea. Strauss agreed to finance the patent, and on 20 May 1873 the two men received U.S. Patent No. 139,121 for an "Improvement in Fastening Pocket-Openings." Their riveted "waist overalls" made of denim — with the famous two-horse leather patch and, from 1886, the arcuate stitching on the back pockets — became the foundation product of the company and eventually the world's most popular garment: blue jeans.

Later life and legacy

Strauss remained head of the company until his death in San Francisco in 1902, leaving the business to his four nephews. Never married, he supported numerous charities and funded scholarships at the University of California, Berkeley. The company's headquarters remain in San Francisco, where Levi's Plaza and the Levi Strauss & Co. archives commemorate his role in creating an enduring symbol of American culture.
