The 1953 Flint–Beecher tornado was a devastating tornado that struck the communities of Beecher and Flint in Genesee County, Michigan, on the evening of 8 June 1953. Rated F5 on the Fujita scale, it killed 116 people and injured 844, making it the deadliest tornado in Michigan history and one of the ten deadliest in United States history.

The storm

The tornado touched down near a drive-in theatre north of Flint and moved eastward along Coldwater Road through the suburb of Beecher, where it reached maximum intensity. Whole rows of homes were swept clean from their foundations, cars were thrown hundreds of yards, and debris was carried for miles. Some twenty families lost multiple members, and entire households were killed. The tornado dissipated after a path of roughly 27 miles.

Context and aftermath

The Flint–Beecher tornado occurred during a remarkable and deadly sequence of tornadoes in the spring of 1953, which also included the Waco tornado of 11 May (114 deaths) and the Worcester tornado of 9 June — the very next day — which killed 94 in Massachusetts. The 1953 disasters prompted public and congressional pressure for improved storm research and warnings, contributing to the development of organised severe-storm forecasting and radar networks in the United States. Beecher rebuilt, and a memorial to the victims stands in the community.
