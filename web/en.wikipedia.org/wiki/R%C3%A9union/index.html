Réunion is an island in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar and southwest of Mauritius, administered as an overseas department and region of France. Its capital is Saint-Denis. The island is about 63 km long and 45 km wide, with a population of roughly 880,000.

Geography

Réunion is volcanic in origin. The dormant Piton des Neiges is the island's highest peak, while the Piton de la Fournaise, on the southeastern coast, is one of the world's most active volcanoes and erupts frequently. Three great natural amphitheatres, the cirques of Salazie, Cilaos and Mafate, cut into the island's mountainous interior; much of Réunion is protected as a national park and UNESCO World Heritage site.

History

Uninhabited when Portuguese explorers sighted it in the early 16th century, the island was settled by the French from the 17th century as Île Bourbon. Slavery underpinned its sugar economy until abolition in 1848, after which indentured labourers arrived from India and elsewhere. Réunion became an overseas department in 1946.

Economy and culture

Sugarcane, rum and tourism are mainstays of the economy, alongside French public-sector employment. The population is ethnically mixed — of African, European, Indian and Chinese descent — and Réunionese Creole is widely spoken alongside French.
