Gatun Lake (Spanish: Lago Gatún) is a large artificial lake in Panama, created between 1907 and 1913 by the building of the Gatun Dam across the Chagres River during construction of the Panama Canal. At the time of its creation, it was the largest man-made lake in the world, and the Gatun Dam was the largest earth dam.

Role in the canal

The lake forms the summit level of the Panama Canal: ships are lifted about 26 metres (85 ft) by the Gatun Locks on the Atlantic side (and by the Pedro Miguel and Miraflores locks on the Pacific side) and then transit some 33 km across the lake and through the Gaillard (Culebra) Cut. Because the lake sits above sea level, the locks are supplied by gravity from its water; each full transit consumes roughly 200 million litres of fresh water, discharged to the sea.

Geography and ecology

Flooding the Chagres valley submerged rainforest hills, whose tops became islands. The most famous is Barro Colorado Island, administered by the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute and one of the most intensively studied tropical forests in the world. The lake teems with introduced peacock bass, a popular sport fish, and its watershed forests are protected to limit siltation and maintain the water supply the canal depends on.

Water supply pressures

Gatun Lake also supplies drinking water to Panama City and Colón. In recent drought years — notably 2019 and 2023–24, associated with El Niño — low lake levels forced the Panama Canal Authority to impose draft restrictions and cut daily transits, making the lake's level a matter of global shipping significance.
