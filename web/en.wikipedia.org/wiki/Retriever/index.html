Retriever

A retriever is a type of gun dog that retrieves game for a hunter. Generally gun dogs are divided into three major classifications: retrievers, flushing spaniels, and pointing breeds. Retrievers were bred primarily to retrieve birds or other prey and return them to the hunter without damage; although some pointing breeds can also retrieve, and many retrievers are skilled in finding game, marking, and even flushing it, retrievers are distinguished in that non-slip retrieval is their primary function.

As a result, retriever breeds are bred for "soft mouths" — they carry game gently — and a strong instinctive desire to please, learn, and obey. A soft mouth is a highly desirable trait, as a dog that damages the bird makes it unfit for the table. Retrievers' willingness to please, patient nature, and trainability have also made breeds such as the Labrador Retriever and Golden Retriever popular as guide dogs for the blind, therapy dogs and general family pets.

Skills and temperament

A well-trained retriever sits quietly by the hunter's side (or in a blind or boat — "non-slip" work) until commanded to retrieve after the shot, watches ("marks") where birds fall, swims readily in cold water, and searches dense cover for downed game by scent. Retrievers are known for their intelligence, gentle disposition, and eagerness. Their water-resistant double coats and webbed feet suit them for waterfowl work in cold conditions.

Breeds

Recognised retriever breeds include:

  • Labrador Retriever
  • Golden Retriever
  • Flat-coated Retriever
  • Curly-coated Retriever
  • Chesapeake Bay Retriever
  • Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever

The Labrador Retriever has for decades been among the most popular dog breeds in the United States, the United Kingdom and many other countries, both as a working gun dog and as a companion.

History

Retrievers developed in the 19th century in Britain, as improvements in guns made shooting birds in flight common and created a need for dogs that would find and return fallen game. The St. John's water dog of Newfoundland was an ancestor of several retriever breeds, crossed with British hunting dogs to produce the modern types. Field trials and shows subsequently split many breeds into working and show strains.
