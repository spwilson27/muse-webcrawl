Dowth (Irish: Dubhadh, "darkness") is a Neolithic passage tomb in the Boyne Valley, County Meath, Ireland. One of the three principal monuments of the Brú na Bóinne complex — alongside Newgrange and Knowth — it was built around 3200 BC and is among the oldest surviving roofed structures in the world. The complex was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

Description

The mound is roughly 85 metres (279 ft) in diameter and 15 metres (49 ft) high, ringed by a kerb of ornately carved stones. Unusually, Dowth contains two burial chambers, both entered from the west side. The northern chamber (Dowth North) has a cruciform layout with decorated stones; the southern chamber (Dowth South) is smaller, with a circular chamber and a single recess.

Solar alignment

Dowth South is aligned on the setting sun at the winter solstice. Around 21 December, the rays of the setting sun travel along the passage and illuminate the chamber's back stone, which is richly decorated with sun-like motifs — an astronomical function complementary to the winter-solstice sunrise alignment at neighbouring Newgrange.

Excavation and access

The mound was crudely excavated in 1847 by the Royal Irish Academy, leaving a crater in its summit, and later investigations clarified the plan of its passages. Unlike Newgrange and Knowth, Dowth's chambers are not open to regular public tours, though the site itself is accessible.
