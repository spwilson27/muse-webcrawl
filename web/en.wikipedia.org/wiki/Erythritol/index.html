Erythritol is an organic compound, a sugar alcohol (polyol) with the formula C4H10O4, used as a food additive and sugar substitute. It occurs naturally in some fruits and fermented foods and is produced industrially by fermenting glucose with yeast such as Moniliella pollinis. Erythritol is about 60–70% as sweet as sucrose but provides only about 0.24 kilocalories per gram — roughly 6% of sugar's energy — because most of it is absorbed and excreted unchanged in urine rather than metabolised.

Properties and uses

Unlike other sugar alcohols, erythritol does not cause a glycaemic response and does not promote tooth decay, since oral bacteria cannot metabolise it. It produces a cooling sensation when it dissolves in the mouth, and it is frequently blended with high-intensity sweeteners such as stevia to mask aftertastes and provide bulk. It is approved as a food additive in many jurisdictions (E number E968 in the European Union).

Digestive tolerance and safety

Erythritol is generally better tolerated than other polyols because most is absorbed before reaching the colon, though large doses can still cause digestive symptoms in some people. A 2023 study reported an association between elevated blood erythritol levels and cardiovascular events, prompting debate and calls for further research; regulators have continued to regard dietary erythritol as safe at approved levels.
