Multiplicative group of integers modulo n

In modular arithmetic, the integers coprime to n form a group under multiplication modulo n, called the multiplicative group of integers modulo n. It is written (ℤ/nℤ)×, or (ℤ/n)*. Its elements are the residue classes a mod n with gcd(a, n) = 1, and the order of the group is Euler's totient φ(n).

Structure

The group is cyclic if and only if n is 1, 2, 4, pk, or 2pk for an odd prime p; in those cases a generator is called a primitive root modulo n. In general, by the Chinese remainder theorem, the group decomposes into a direct product of the groups for the prime powers dividing n; for odd prime powers the group is cyclic, while for 2k (k ≥ 3) it is the product of a cyclic group of order 2 and one of order 2k−2, generated by −1 and 5 respectively.

Examples

Modulo 8, the units are {1, 3, 5, 7}, each of order 2, forming the Klein four-group. Modulo 7, the group {1,…,6} is cyclic of order 6, generated by 3 (and by 5). Euler's theorem — that aφ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n) for a coprime to n — is the statement that the group has order φ(n); Fermat's little theorem is the case of prime modulus.

Applications

The group underlies the RSA cryptosystem and the Diffie–Hellman key exchange, whose security rests on the difficulty of the discrete logarithm problem in such groups.
