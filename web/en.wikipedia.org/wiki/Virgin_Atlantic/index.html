Virgin Atlantic

Virgin Atlantic, a trading name of Virgin Atlantic Airways Limited, is a British airline with its head office in Crawley, West Sussex, England. It was established in 1984 as British Atlantic Airways and originally planned to fly between London and the Falkland Islands. Renamed Virgin Atlantic Airways, it began operations on 22 June 1984 with a flight from London Gatwick to Newark, using the slogan-driven challenger branding of founder Richard Branson's Virgin Group.

History

Virgin Atlantic was co-founded by Randolph Fields and Richard Branson, who bought out Fields' stake early on. Positioning itself as a service-led challenger to British Airways on transatlantic routes, it pioneered amenities such as individual seat-back entertainment and a premium economy cabin (1992). The rivalry with BA produced the celebrated "dirty tricks" legal case of the early 1990s, which Virgin won. Singapore Airlines bought a 49 per cent stake in 1999; Delta Air Lines acquired that stake in 2012–2013 and the two airlines formed a transatlantic joint venture. Virgin Atlantic joined the SkyTeam alliance in March 2023, its first global alliance membership.

Destinations and fleet

The airline operates long-haul services from London Heathrow and Manchester to North America, the Caribbean, Africa, the Middle East and Asia, including routes to New York, Los Angeles, Orlando, Barbados, Johannesburg, Mumbai, Delhi, Tel Aviv and Shanghai. Its fleet is all wide-body, built around the Boeing 787-9, Airbus A330-300 and A330-900, and Airbus A350-1000; it previously operated the Boeing 747-400 and Airbus A340. Cabins comprise Upper Class (business), Premium and Economy (Delight, Classic and Light).

Subsidiaries and ventures

Virgin Atlantic is closely associated with Virgin Holidays. The group's wider aviation ventures have included Virgin Australia and the short-lived Virgin America and Virgin Nigeria. A short-haul sister, Virgin Connect, was planned from the Flybe acquisition but was abandoned when Flybe collapsed in 2020.
