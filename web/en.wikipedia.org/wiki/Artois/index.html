Artois

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Grand-Place of Arras, capital of Artois

Artois (French pronunciation: [aʁ.twa]; Picard: Artoé; Dutch: Artesië; Latin: Artesium) is a region of northern France. Its territory covers most of the modern department of Pas-de-Calais, the western part of the department of Nord, and a small part of the Somme. Its capital is Arras.

History

Artois was originally a Carolingian county, later a county of the Kingdom of France. In the Middle Ages it passed between French and Flemish/Burgundian control; it was held by the Dukes of Burgundy and then by the Spanish Habsburgs as part of the Spanish Netherlands. France recovered it under the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) and the Treaty of Nijmegen (1678).

The county gave its name to the title comte d'Artois, borne by the younger brother of Louis XVI who later reigned as Charles X. During the First World War, Artois was the site of three major battles (1914, 1915, and 1917), and its coal-mining basin was a major industrial region. The region is also known for the Artesian wells named after it, where water rises under natural pressure from a confined aquifer.
