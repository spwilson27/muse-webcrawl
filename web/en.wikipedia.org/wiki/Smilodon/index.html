Smilodon

Smilodon is a genus of extinct felids belonging to the subfamily Machairodontinae. It is one of the best known saber-toothed cats and one of the most famous prehistoric mammals. Smilodon lived in the Americas during the Pleistocene epoch, from 2.5 million years ago to about 10,000 years ago. The genus was named in 1842 based on fossils from Brazil; the type species is S. populator.

Species

Three species are recognised: S. gracilis, the smallest and earliest, from North and South America; S. fatalis, the familiar North American species abundantly preserved at the La Brea Tar Pits; and S. populator of eastern South America, among the largest cats ever to live, with males possibly exceeding 400 kg.

Description

Smilodon was more robustly built than any extant cat, with particularly well-developed forelimbs, a short tail, and extremely long, flattened upper canines — up to 28 cm (11 in) in S. populator — that were serrated and surprisingly fragile. It had a reduced bite force compared with modern big cats, and probably used its powerful forelimbs to wrestle and immobilise large prey — ground sloths, bison, young mammoths and camels — before delivering a precise shearing bite to the throat with its sabers.

Behaviour

Healed injuries in many fossils have suggested to some researchers that Smilodon may have been a social animal that lived in prides, allowing injured members to feed from kills made by others. This remains debated.

Extinction

Smilodon disappeared around 10,000 years ago as part of the end-Pleistocene extinction event, in which most large American mammals vanished, probably through a combination of climate change, the disappearance of prey, and pressure from human hunters.
