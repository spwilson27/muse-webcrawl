Pink fairy armadillo

Chlamyphorus truncatus, the smallest armadillo

The pink fairy armadillo (Chlamyphorus truncatus) is the smallest species of armadillo, endemic to the sandy plains, dunes and scrublands of central Argentina. First described by Richard Harlan in 1825, it is a nocturnal, solitary, burrowing mammal rarely seen above ground.

Description

The species measures only about 90–115 mm in head-body length and weighs around 120 g. Its shell is a pale rose or pink colour, the tint coming from blood vessels beneath the thin dorsal shield; uniquely among armadillos, the shell is attached to the body mainly along the spine and is soft and flexible. Soft white fur covers its underside. Large front claws and a vertical bony plate at the rear of the shell are adaptations for swift burrowing — the animal can bury itself completely within seconds when threatened, using the rear plate to plug the burrow.

Behaviour and diet

The pink fairy armadillo feeds mainly on ants and ant larvae, which it digs out near its burrows, supplemented by worms, snails and plant material. It spends most of its life underground in dry, sandy soils and emerges mainly at night.

Conservation

The species is listed as Data Deficient by the IUCN; it is believed to be declining because of habitat conversion to agriculture, predation by domestic dogs and cats, and its extreme sensitivity to stress and temperature change, which makes it almost impossible to keep in captivity.
