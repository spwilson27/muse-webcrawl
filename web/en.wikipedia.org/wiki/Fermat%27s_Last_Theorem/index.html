Fermat's Last Theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fermat's Last Theorem
StatementNo positive integers a, b, c satisfy an + bn = cn for n > 2
Conjectured byPierre de Fermat, c. 1637
Proved byAndrew Wiles (1994), with Richard Taylor

Fermat's Last Theorem states that no three positive integers a, b and c satisfy the equation an + bn = cn for any integer value of n greater than 2. For n = 2 the equation has infinitely many solutions, the Pythagorean triples.

History

Around 1637, Pierre de Fermat wrote in the margin of his copy of Diophantus's Arithmetica that he had discovered a "truly marvelous proof" that the margin was too narrow to contain. For more than three centuries the claim resisted all attempts, while partial results for specific exponents (by Euler, Sophie Germain, Dirichlet, Lamé and Kummer, among others) drove the development of algebraic number theory, including the theory of ideals.

Proof

The eventual proof came indirectly: work by Gerhard Frey, Jean-Pierre Serre and Ken Ribet linked a hypothetical counterexample to the modularity theorem for elliptic curves. Andrew Wiles, working in secrecy for seven years, proved enough of the modularity theorem to establish Fermat's Last Theorem, announcing the result in 1993; after a gap was found, he completed the proof with Richard Taylor in 1994, and it was published in 1995. Whether Fermat himself ever had a valid proof is doubted by most historians, since the required mathematics did not exist in his time.
