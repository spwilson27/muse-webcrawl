Battle of Portland

The Battle of Portland was a naval engagement fought from 18 to 20 February 1653 (Old Style; 28 February – 2 March New Style) during the First Anglo-Dutch War, off the Isle of Portland in the English Channel. An English fleet under Robert Blake attacked a large Dutch convoy escorted by Maarten Tromp. Over three days of running fighting up the Channel the Dutch lost numerous merchantmen and several warships, but Tromp succeeded in saving most of his convoy. In Dutch historiography the battle is known as the Driedaagse Zeeslag (Three Days' Battle).

Course of the battle

Blake, with part of the English fleet, engaged Tromp's escort on the first day before the rest of his squadrons came up, and was wounded. Fighting continued eastward past the Isle of Wight toward Beachy Head over the following two days. The Dutch lost perhaps a dozen warships and 20–40 merchantmen captured or sunk.

Aftermath

Although the English held the field and claimed victory, Tromp's conduct of the retreat preserved the bulk of the convoy, demonstrating the difficulty of destroying a well-handled fleet. The battle contributed to English efforts to assert control of the Channel and fed reforms of fleet tactics, including the line-ahead instructions issued later in 1653.
