Sega Corporation is a Japanese video game company and a subsidiary of Sega Sammy Holdings. Founded in 1960, Sega produced coin-operated amusement machines before entering the home console market in the 1980s. Its most successful console, the Genesis (Mega Drive outside North America), made it Nintendo's chief rival in the early 1990s, helped by its mascot, Sonic the Hedgehog.

History

The company traces its origins to Service Games, founded in Hawaii in 1940 to supply coin-operated games to U.S. military bases, which later merged with Rosen Enterprises to form Sega Enterprises. Sega scored arcade hits such as Periscope and Zaxxon, then launched the SG-1000, Master System, and Genesis consoles. The 32-bit Saturn and the underperforming Dreamcast (1999) led to heavy losses; in 2001 Sega exited the console hardware business and became a third-party software developer. In 2004 it merged with the pachinko manufacturer Sammy Corporation to form Sega Sammy Holdings.

Studios and franchises

Sega's development studios include Sonic Team, Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio, and the UK-based Creative Assembly; it also owns Atlus, developer of the Persona and Shin Megami Tensei series. Major franchises include Sonic the Hedgehog, Like a Dragon, Total War, Football Manager, and Virtua Fighter. Sega remains a major operator and manufacturer in the Japanese arcade market.
