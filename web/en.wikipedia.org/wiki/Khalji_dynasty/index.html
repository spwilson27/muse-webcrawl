The Khalji dynasty (also spelled Khilji) was a dynasty of Turco-Afghan origin that ruled the Delhi Sultanate in northern India from 1290 to 1320. It was the second of the five dynasties of the Sultanate, succeeding the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. Under its most famous ruler, Alauddin Khalji, the Sultanate reached its greatest territorial extent to that date, repelled repeated Mongol invasions and extended Muslim rule deep into southern India.

History

The dynasty was founded by Jalaluddin Firuz Khalji, an elderly commander of the Khalji tribe, who seized the throne in 1290 in what contemporaries called the "Khalji revolution", ending the monopoly of Turkish slave-officers and opening high office to Indian Muslims and others. Jalaluddin was murdered in 1296 by his nephew and son-in-law Alauddin, who proclaimed himself sultan.

Alauddin Khalji (r. 1296–1316) conquered Gujarat, Ranthambore, Chittor, Malwa and Jalore, while his general Malik Kafur led expeditions into the Deccan and far south against the Yadavas, Kakatiyas, Hoysalas and Pandyas, bringing back enormous plunder. Alauddin is remembered for his sweeping administrative and economic reforms — a large standing army paid in cash, market price controls, land revenue reforms — and for successfully defending India against Mongol invasions. Within Delhi his reign saw major building, including the Alai Darwaza and the Siri fort. After his death in 1316 the dynasty declined rapidly amid succession struggles; the last Khalji sultan, Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah, was murdered in 1320 by Khusrau Khan, and within months Ghazi Malik (Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq) founded the Tughlaq dynasty.
