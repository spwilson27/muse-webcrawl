Highland (council area)

Highland
Scottish Gaelic: Gàidhealtachd
Country: Scotland
Administrative centre: Inverness
Area: about 25,657 km²
Population: about 235,000

Highland (Scottish Gaelic: Gàidhealtachd) is a council area in the Scottish Highlands and is the largest local government area in both Scotland and the United Kingdom as a whole. It covers much of the historic counties of Inverness-shire, Ross and Cromarty, Sutherland, Caithness, and parts of Argyll and Moray.

Geography

The area includes Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles, Loch Ness, and large areas of mountainous and coastal terrain. Its administrative centre is the city of Inverness. Other significant towns include Fort William, Nairn, Wick, Thurso, Dingwall and Portree on the Isle of Skye, which lies within the council area.

History

The Highland region was created in 1975 as a two-tier region, and became a single-tier council area in 1996 under the Local Government etc. (Scotland) Act 1994.

Governance

Highland Council is based at Glenurquhart Road in Inverness. The area is divided into wards electing councillors by single transferable vote.
