Tawaraya Sōtatsu

Bornc. 1570, Kyoto
Diedc. 1640
NationalityJapanese
MovementRinpa school

Tawaraya Sōtatsu (c. 1570 – c. 1640) was a Japanese painter of the early Edo period and, together with Ogata Kōrin, a founder of the Rinpa school of painting. He ran an atelier in Kyoto that sold fans and decorated papers.

Works

Sōtatsu is celebrated for lavish gold-ground folding screens such as the Wind God and Thunder God screens, later famously copied by Kōrin, and screens depicting scenes from The Tale of Genji and the Tales of Ise. His style features bold abstracted shapes, pooled ink effects (tarashikomi) and daring asymmetric composition against gold leaf.

Legacy

Long overshadowed, Sōtatsu was reassessed in the 20th century as one of Japan's greatest painters. Major works are held by the Freer Gallery in Washington, D.C., and Kennin-ji temple in Kyoto.
