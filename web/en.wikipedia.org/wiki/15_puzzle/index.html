15 puzzle

The 15 puzzle (also called the Gem Puzzle, Boss Puzzle, Game of Fifteen or Mystic Square) is a sliding puzzle with 15 square tiles numbered 1–15 in a 4×4 frame, leaving one unoccupied space. Tiles adjacent to the empty space can slide into it; the object is to restore the tiles to numerical order from a scrambled starting position.

History

The puzzle was invented by Noyes Chapman, a postmaster in Canastota, New York, around 1874 and became a craze in the United States and Europe in 1880 after reaching Boston. Chess master Henry Dudeney and puzzle author Sam Loyd popularised it; Loyd's claim to have invented it is widely repeated but inaccurate.

Solvability

Exactly half of all starting positions are solvable. A configuration is solvable if and only if the parity of the tile permutation plus the taxicab distance of the blank from its home row is invariant; swapping only tiles 14 and 15 — a famous challenge configuration — is provably impossible to solve. The result was established by Johnson and Story in 1879 using permutation parity.
