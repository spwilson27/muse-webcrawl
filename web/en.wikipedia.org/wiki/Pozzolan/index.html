Pozzolan

Pozzolan
TypeSiliceous / aluminous material
Named afterPozzuoli, Italy
UseCement and concrete

Pozzolans are a broad class of siliceous or siliceous-and-aluminous materials which, in themselves, possess little or no cementitious value but which, in finely divided form and in the presence of water, react chemically with calcium hydroxide at ordinary temperatures to form compounds with cementitious properties.

History

The name derives from Pozzuoli, near Naples, where volcanic ash with these properties was used by the Romans. Roman concrete made with pozzolanic ash was exceptionally durable, including in marine structures such as harbour moles.

Modern use

Modern pozzolans include natural volcanic ashes and industrial by-products such as fly ash, silica fume, and calcined clays. Blending them into Portland cement reduces cost and carbon emissions and can improve the long-term strength and durability of concrete.
