Nominalism

Nominalism is the metaphysical view that universals and abstract objects do not exist independently: only particular things exist, and general terms ('redness', 'humanity') are names (nomina) or concepts grouping resembling particulars. Opposed by realism (Plato's Forms, medieval moderate realism), nominalism was championed by William of Ockham, whose razor ('entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity') is its methodological emblem.

Stakes

The medieval problem of universals bore on theology (the Trinity, transubstantiation) and science alike. Modern nominalism, revived by Goodman and Quine, tries to do mathematics and science without abstract objects — a programme whose difficulty is measured by the apparent indispensability of numbers.
