Theotokos

Theotokos (Greek: Θεοτόκος, "God-bearer") is a title of Mary, mother of Jesus, used especially in Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic Christianity. In Latin it is rendered Dei Genitrix or Deipara, and in English "Mother of God". The title affirms that the one born of Mary is, from conception, fully God as well as fully human.

History of the title

The term was in devotional use by the third or fourth century. It became a flashpoint in the Nestorian controversy: Patriarch Nestorius of Constantinople objected to Theotokos, preferring Christotokos ("Christ-bearer"), fearing it confused Christ's natures. Cyril of Alexandria defended the title, and the Council of Ephesus in 431 affirmed it, deposing Nestorius; the Council of Chalcedon (451) confirmed the usage. Affirming Theotokos thus became a touchstone of orthodox Christology: Mary is mother not of a merely human person joined to God, but of the single divine Person of the Son.

In devotion and art

The Theotokos is central to Byzantine and Orthodox devotion; the Akathist hymn and countless icons — classified into types such as Hodegetria ("she who shows the Way"), Eleusa (Virgin of Tenderness), and Orans — depict her with the Christ child. In Orthodox liturgy she is honoured with the hymn "It is Truly Meet" and commemorated at every service. Roman Catholicism shares the doctrine under the title Mother of God, solemnly celebrated on 1 January, though the Greek term itself is used mainly in the East.
