Fastnet Race

First held1925
OrganizerRoyal Ocean Racing Club
CourseCowes – Fastnet Rock – finish
DistanceAbout 695 nautical miles (current course)

The Fastnet Race is a biennial offshore yacht race organized by the Royal Ocean Racing Club (RORC). One of the world's classic ocean races, it takes fleets of several hundred boats from Cowes on the Isle of Wight, west along the English Channel, across the Celtic Sea to round Fastnet Rock off the south-west coast of Ireland, and back to the finish.

History

The race was first held in 1925 and won by the cutter Jolie Brise; the event led directly to the founding of the RORC. For most of its history the race finished at Plymouth; since 2021 the finish has been at Cherbourg, France, lengthening the course to about 695 nautical miles. The race forms the centrepiece of the Admiral's Cup tradition and attracts both professional crews and Corinthian amateurs.

The 1979 race

The Fastnet Race is remembered for the disaster of August 1979, when an unexpectedly severe storm struck the 303-boat fleet. Fifteen sailors died, 24 yachts were abandoned and five sank; only 86 boats finished. It remains the largest rescue operation in peacetime British history and led to sweeping changes in offshore safety rules, yacht design stability requirements, and race weather monitoring.

Records

Modern racing machines have cut the course record to well under two days; the outright record is held by large multihulls and foiling designs, while the monohull record was set by the supermaxi Comanche-class yachts of the 2020s era.
