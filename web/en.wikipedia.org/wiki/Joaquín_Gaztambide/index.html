Joaquín Romualdo Gaztambide y Garbayo (7 February 1822 – 18 March 1870) was a Spanish composer and conductor, one of the leading figures in the nineteenth-century revival of the zarzuela, the Spanish lyric-dramatic genre combining opera, spoken dialogue and popular song.

Life and career

Born in Tudela, Navarre, Gaztambide studied at the Madrid Conservatory and worked as a double bassist and pianist in theatre orchestras before turning to conducting and composition. In 1849 his first zarzuela, La mensajera, was staged, and together with composers such as Francisco Asenjo Barbieri, Cristóbal Oudrid and José Inzenga he founded the Sociedad Artística in 1851 to promote the genre. Their production Jugar con fuego (1851), to a libretto by Ventura de la Vega, marked a turning point for the modern zarzuela.

Works

Gaztambide composed dozens of zarzuelas, among them El valle de Andorra (1852), Catalina (1854), Los magiares (1857), El juramento (1858), Una vieja (1860) and El cocinero. El juramento is often considered his masterpiece, admired for its polished orchestration and melodic invention. He also worked extensively as an impresario and conductor, organising seasons at Madrid's Teatro de la Zarzuela after its opening in 1856.

Death

In 1869 Gaztambide embarked on a tour of Cuba with a zarzuela company; falling ill there, he returned to Spain and died in Madrid in March 1870. A street in Madrid's Chamberí district bears his name, and a theatre in his hometown of Tudela commemorates him.
