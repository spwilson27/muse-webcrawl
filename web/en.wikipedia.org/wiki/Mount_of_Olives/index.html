Mount of Olives

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mount of Olives, with the Jewish cemetery in the foreground and Jerusalem beyond.

The Mount of Olives (Hebrew: הַר הַזֵּיתִים, Har ha-Zeitim; Arabic: جبل الزيتون, Jabal az-Zaytūn) is a mountain ridge east of and adjacent to the Old City of Jerusalem. It is named for the olive groves that once covered its slopes. The ridge has three summits extending from Mount Scopus in the north to the south, the highest of which, at-Tur, reaches 818 metres (2,684 ft).

Religious significance

The mount is sacred in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. It has been used as a Jewish burial ground for over 3,000 years and holds an estimated 70,000 to 150,000 graves, including those of prophets Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi and many prominent rabbis and Israeli leaders. In Jewish tradition the resurrection of the dead will begin here with the coming of the Messiah. For Christians, the mount is the site of Gethsemane, where Jesus prayed before the crucifixion, and of the Ascension; the gospels describe Jesus' entry into Jerusalem, his weeping over the city, and the Olivet Discourse as taking place here. Churches on the mount include the Church of All Nations, Dominus Flevit, the Russian Orthodox Church of Mary Magdalene, and the Chapel of the Ascension.

Modern history

Between 1948 and 1967 the mount lay in Jordanian-controlled East Jerusalem; thousands of gravestones were damaged during that period, and the road to the cemetery and the Intercontinental Hotel were built over graves. After 1967 the cemetery was partially restored. The Hebrew University's Mount Scopus campus overlooks the ridge from the north.
