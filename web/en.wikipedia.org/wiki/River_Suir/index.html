The River Suir (Irish: An tSiúr, pronounced [ənˠ ˈtʲuːɾˠ]) is a river in Ireland that flows into the Atlantic Ocean through Waterford after a distance of 185 kilometres (115 mi). It is one of the "Three Sisters" rivers, together with the Barrow and the Nore, which share the estuary of Waterford Harbour. The Suir rises on the slopes of Devil's Bit mountain, north of Templemore in County Tipperary, and flows south and east through Thurles, Cahir, Clonmel, and Carrick-on-Suir before reaching the city of Waterford.

Course and history

The upper Suir winds through the rich farmland of the Golden Vale. At Cahir it passes the medieval Cahir Castle on an island in the river, and at Clonmel—the largest inland town in Ireland—it is joined by tributaries from the Comeragh and Knockmealdown mountains. The river is tidal below Carrick-on-Suir. Historically the Suir was navigable to Clonmel and carried commercial traffic in coal, grain, and butter; the port of Waterford, founded by the Vikings, stands near its mouth.

Fishing and ecology

The Suir is renowned as a brown trout and salmon river and is considered one of Ireland's premier game fisheries, with notable spring salmon runs. Its catchment supports freshwater pearl mussel populations and extensive floodplain wetlands. Flooding in Clonmel and Carrick-on-Suir has prompted major flood relief works.
