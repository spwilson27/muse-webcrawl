Pretty Woman is a 1990 American romantic comedy film directed by Garry Marshall and written by J. F. Lawton. It stars Richard Gere as Edward Lewis, a wealthy corporate raider, and Julia Roberts as Vivian Ward, a Hollywood sex worker whom Edward hires to accompany him for a week of business and social functions. The supporting cast includes Héctor Elizondo, Ralph Bellamy, Jason Alexander, and Laura San Giacomo.

Plot

Lost while driving in Hollywood, Edward Lewis stops to ask directions from Vivian Ward and ends up hiring her for the night; impressed, he extends the arrangement to a week for $3,000, installing her in his penthouse at the Regent Beverly Wilshire hotel. As Vivian navigates Beverly Hills boutiques, an opera performance of La traviata, and Edward's business world, the two fall in love, and Edward reconsiders his plans to dismantle the shipbuilding company of James Morse. The film ends with Edward climbing the fire escape of Vivian's building with flowers, asking what happens after the prince rescues the princess — "she rescues him right back."

Production and reception

Lawton's original screenplay, titled 3,000, was a much darker drama about addiction and prostitution; under Marshall and producer Laura Ziskin it was reshaped into a modern fairy tale. The film was an enormous commercial success, grossing over $463 million worldwide on a $14 million budget, and it made Roberts a superstar — she won the Golden Globe for Best Actress and received an Academy Award nomination. The soundtrack features Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman," from which the film takes its title, and Roxette's "It Must Have Been Love." A stage musical adaptation premiered on Broadway in 2018. The film remains one of the most financially successful romantic comedies of all time, though it has also drawn criticism for romanticizing sex work.
