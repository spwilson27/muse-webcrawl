Comancheria was the region of the Southern Great Plains of North America historically inhabited and controlled by the Comanche people. Extending from the Arkansas River in the north to the Rio Grande in the south, and from present-day New Mexico eastward into Oklahoma and Texas, it formed the heartland of the Comanche sphere of influence during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Geography

The Comancheria encompassed the Llano Estacado, the Edwards Plateau, and large portions of the southern plains. Its river valleys — the Red, Brazos, Colorado, and Canadian Rivers — provided water, grazing, and shelter in winter camps.

History

Having acquired horses in the late seventeenth century, the Comanche moved south from the Wyoming basin and displaced Apache peoples from the southern plains by the mid-1700s. From this base they raided deep into Spanish, Mexican, and later American settlements, and traded horses, captives, and bison products at trade fairs such as Taos.

Decline

Following the American Civil War, disease, military campaigns, and the destruction of the bison herds undermined Comanche power. The Red River War of 1874–75 ended with the surrender of the last free bands and their removal to reservation in Indian Territory.
