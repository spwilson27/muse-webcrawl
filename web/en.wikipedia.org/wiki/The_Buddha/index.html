The Buddha (also known as Siddhartha Gautama, Gautama Buddha, or Shakyamuni Buddha) was a philosopher, mendicant, meditator, and religious teacher who lived in ancient India (c. 5th to 4th century BCE). He is revered as the founder of Buddhism and is regarded by Buddhists as an awakened (enlightened) teacher who attained full Buddhahood and shared his insights to help sentient beings end rebirth and suffering.

The word "Buddha" means "the awakened one" or "the enlightened one". According to Buddhist tradition, Siddhartha Gautama was born in Lumbini, in what is now Nepal, into the Shakya clan. After leading a sheltered life in his father's palace, he encountered old age, sickness, death, and a wandering ascetic — the "four sights" — which prompted him to renounce his princely life at the age of 29 in search of liberation from suffering.

Enlightenment and teaching

After years of ascetic practice and meditation, Gautama attained enlightenment (bodhi) while meditating beneath a pipal tree at Bodh Gaya, thereafter known as the Bodhi Tree. He delivered his first sermon at Sarnath, near Varanasi, setting in motion the "Wheel of Dharma". In this sermon he expounded the Four Noble Truths — that life involves suffering (dukkha), that suffering arises from craving, that suffering can cease, and that the Noble Eightfold Path leads to its cessation.

For the remaining 45 years of his life, the Buddha traveled throughout the Ganges plain of northern India, teaching a diverse range of people and founding the sangha, a community of monks and nuns who carried on his teachings. He attracted royal patrons such as King Bimbisara of Magadha and taught a path — the Middle Way — between the extremes of self-indulgence and self-mortification.

Death and legacy

The Buddha died at Kushinagar at the age of 80, attaining parinirvana, the final passing beyond the cycle of rebirth. His teachings were preserved in the Pali Canon and other early Buddhist texts, compiled by his followers. Buddhism spread across Asia and is today followed by roughly 500 million people worldwide.
