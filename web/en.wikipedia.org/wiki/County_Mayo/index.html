County Mayo (Irish: Contae Mhaigh Eo, meaning "plain of the yew trees") is a county in the province of Connacht in western Ireland, with a long Atlantic coastline. It is the third-largest county in Ireland by area. Its county town is Castlebar; other towns include Ballina, Westport, Claremorris and Ballinrobe.

Geography

Mayo's landscape ranges from the fertile limestone plains of the south-east to the wild blanket bogs of Erris in the north-west and the quartzite cone of Croagh Patrick (764 m), Ireland's holy mountain, overlooking Clew Bay near Westport. Nephin Beg and the Mullet Peninsula, Achill Island (Ireland's largest island, connected by bridge), Lough Mask and Lough Conn are other landmarks. North Mayo contains the Céide Fields, among the oldest known field systems in the world, preserved beneath blanket bog.

History

Mayo suffered terribly in the Great Famine of the 1840s, which emptied whole districts through death and emigration; the county's population fell from over 388,000 in 1841 to a fraction of that figure. The Land War of the late 1870s began in Mayo, where Michael Davitt founded the Irish National Land League at Castlebar in 1879, and the word "boycott" entered the language from events at Lough Mask in 1880. The village of Knock became a major Catholic pilgrimage site after a reported apparition in 1879.

Sport

Mayo's Gaelic football team, in green and red, is famous both for passionate support and for its long wait for an All-Ireland title: champions in 1936, 1950 and 1951, Mayo have lost many finals since, a drought popularly attributed to the "Curse of 1951".
