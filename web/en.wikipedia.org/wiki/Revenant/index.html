Revenant

A revenant is a legendary creature, a corpse that is believed to have been revived to haunt the living. The word revenant is derived from the Old French word revenant, "returning" (from the verb revenir, to come back). Revenants appear throughout European folklore, particularly in medieval chronicles from England and northern France, and share many traits with the later vampire legends of Eastern Europe.

Folklore

Medieval accounts, such as those collected by William of Newburgh in the 12th century, describe the restless dead returning because of a violent death, an improper burial, excommunication or unfinished business. Revenants were said to spread disease, attack relatives and wander at night; remedies included exhuming and beheading or burning the corpse, or driving a stake through the heart — practices also documented in archaeological "deviant burials". In Norse tradition the related figure is the draugr, an undead inhabitant of a burial mound, and Icelandic sagas describe hauntings laid by exhumation and reburial at a crossroads.

In popular culture

The revenant is a staple antagonist in fantasy and horror fiction, role-playing games and films. The term gained renewed prominence with the 2015 film The Revenant, which uses it in its older sense of one who has returned (from presumed death) rather than as a supernatural creature.
