Lake George is a broad and shallow lake on the St. Johns River in the U.S. state of Florida. It is the second-largest lake in Florida after Lake Okeechobee, covering about 46,000 acres, and lies on the borders of Volusia, Putnam, Lake, and Marion counties.

Description

The St. Johns River flows northward through the lake, entering at the south end and leaving at the north. Despite its size, Lake George is remarkably shallow, averaging only about eight feet deep. The lake is unusual in having elevated levels of salt: springs on its western side, including Salt Springs, discharge slightly saline water, and the lake supports some brackish and even marine species, such as Atlantic stingrays, mullet, and blue crabs, far inland.

History and use

The lake was named for King George III during Florida's British period. In the nineteenth century it was an important link in the steamboat route up the St. Johns River. Much of the eastern shore lies within the Ocala National Forest. The U.S. Navy formerly operated the Pinecastle bombing range nearby, and ordnance clearance in the lake has been an occasional issue. The lake is popular for bass and crappie fishing and is fringed by wetlands and hydric hammock.
