Fuel efficiency

Fuel efficiency (or fuel economy) is a measure of how effectively a vehicle or machine converts the energy in fuel into useful work or distance travelled. For road vehicles it is commonly expressed as fuel consumption—litres per 100 kilometres (L/100 km) in most of the world—or as miles per gallon (mpg) in the United States and United Kingdom.

Measurement

Consumption and distance-per-volume are reciprocals, and the relationship is nonlinear: replacing a low-mpg vehicle saves more fuel than a similar mpg improvement on an efficient one. Official figures come from standardized test cycles such as the European WLTP and the US EPA cycles, which historically understated real-world consumption. Fuel efficiency depends on engine thermodynamics, vehicle mass, aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, driving style and speed; consumption rises steeply at high speeds because drag grows with the square of velocity.

Regulation

Governments regulate fuel economy through standards such as the US Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) rules, introduced after the 1973 oil crisis, and EU fleet CO2 targets, which function as efficiency standards. Technologies improving efficiency include direct injection, turbocharging downsizing, variable valve timing, lightweight materials, low-rolling-resistance tyres, hybridization, and improved aerodynamics.
