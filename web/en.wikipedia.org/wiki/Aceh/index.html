Aceh (Acehnese: Acèh) is a province of Indonesia at the northern tip of the island of Sumatra. Its capital and largest city is Banda Aceh. Aceh is known for its long history of resistance to outside control, its strong Islamic identity, and the special autonomy it holds within Indonesia, under which it is the only Indonesian province permitted to implement Sharia law officially.

The Baiturrahman Grand Mosque in Banda Aceh, a symbol of Aceh.

History

Aceh was historically a powerful trading region. The Aceh Sultanate, founded in the early sixteenth century under Ali Mughayat Syah, became a major regional power controlling much of the pepper trade and resisting Portuguese, Dutch and British encroachment. Islam reached Aceh early — it is often called the "Veranda of Mecca" (Serambi Mekkah) because pilgrims departing for the Hajj often stopped there.

The Aceh War (1873–1904) against the Dutch was one of the longest and costliest colonial wars in the Dutch East Indies. In the twentieth century, Aceh joined the Republic of Indonesia, but grievances over autonomy and resource revenues led to the Darul Islam rebellion in the 1950s and, from 1976, the separatist insurgency of the Free Aceh Movement (GAM) under Hasan di Tiro.

On 26 December 2004, Aceh was devastated by the Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami, which killed well over 100,000 people in the province. The disaster helped spur a peace process: the Helsinki Memorandum of Understanding of August 2005 ended the GAM insurgency, and Aceh received special autonomy, including the right to local political parties and control of a large share of natural-resource revenues.

Geography

Aceh occupies the northwestern end of Sumatra, bordered by the Andaman Sea and the Strait of Malacca to the north and east, and by North Sumatra province to the southeast. The Barisan Mountains run through the interior; Mount Leuser, with its national park, is part of the Tropical Rainforest Heritage of Sumatra UNESCO site. The province includes outlying islands such as Weh (Sabang) and Simeulue.

Government and law

Under the 2006 Law on the Governing of Aceh, the province elects its own governor and legislature (DPRA) and may field local parties. Aceh's Qanun Jinayat (Islamic criminal bylaw) applies Sharia-based offences, enforced by the Wilayatul Hisbah religious police, a system unique within Indonesia.

Economy

Oil and natural gas (notably the Arun field near Lhokseumawe) long dominated the economy, alongside agriculture — coffee from the Gayo highlands, palm oil, rice and rubber — and fisheries.
