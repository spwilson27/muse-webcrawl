War in Abkhazia (1992–1993)

Date: 14 August 1992 – 27 September 1993
Location: Abkhazia, Georgia
Result: Abkhaz victory; de facto independence of Abkhazia; mass displacement of ethnic Georgians

The War in Abkhazia of 1992–1993 was fought between the government of Georgia and Abkhaz separatists, supported by North Caucasian volunteers and, at key moments, Russian forces, over the status of Abkhazia, a region on Georgia's Black Sea coast. The war ended with the loss of Georgian control over Abkhazia and the flight or expulsion of the majority of the region's ethnic Georgian population.

Background

Abkhazia had been an autonomous republic within Soviet Georgia, its ethnic Abkhaz population a minority in the region. As the Soviet Union collapsed, tensions rose: the Abkhaz feared absorption in an independent, nationalising Georgia, while Georgians viewed Abkhaz separatism as encouraged by Moscow. In July 1992 the Abkhaz Supreme Soviet moved to restore the 1925 constitution, effectively declaring separation; Tbilisi annulled the decision.

Course of the war

On 14 August 1992, Georgian National Guard forces under Defence Minister Tengiz Kitovani entered Abkhazia — officially to secure the railway and free hostages taken by supporters of the ousted president Zviad Gamsakhurdia — and reached the capital, Sukhumi. Abkhaz forces under Vladislav Ardzinba withdrew to Gudauta, where Russian military facilities were located, and reorganised. Volunteers from the Confederation of Mountain Peoples of the Caucasus, including the Chechen commander Shamil Basayev, joined the Abkhaz side. After an October 1992 Abkhaz victory at Gagra, the front stabilised along the Gumista River; Russian mediation produced ceasefires that repeatedly broke down amid mutual accusations, and Russian aircraft bombed Georgian positions.

In September 1993 the Abkhaz broke a Russian-brokered ceasefire and launched a final offensive, capturing Sukhumi on 27 September 1993. A massacre of Georgian civilians and officials in the city followed, and some 250,000 ethnic Georgians fled Abkhazia, many crossing the mountains at Svaneti in deadly conditions — events Georgia and international observers have characterised as ethnic cleansing.

Aftermath

A 1994 Moscow ceasefire agreement placed Commonwealth of Independent States (mostly Russian) peacekeepers along the Inguri River, and the UN Observer Mission in Georgia monitored the line. Abkhazia functioned as a de facto independent but unrecognised state; most displaced Georgians have never returned. The conflict reignited briefly in the Kodori Valley in 1998 and 2001, and Russia formally recognised Abkhazia's independence after the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, a move rejected by Georgia and most of the international community.
