Rail transport in Canada

A freight train in the Canadian Rockies.

Rail transport in Canada comprises a network of more than 40,000 kilometres of standard-gauge track, dominated by two transcontinental freight railways, Canadian National (CN) and Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC), with passenger services provided mainly by the Crown corporation Via Rail.

History

Canada's first railway, the Champlain and St. Lawrence Railroad, opened in 1836. Railways were central to Canadian Confederation: the promise of a transcontinental line brought British Columbia into the union, and the Canadian Pacific Railway was completed in 1885 with the driving of the Last Spike at Craigellachie. Canadian National was formed in 1919 from several bankrupt railways and was privatised in 1995.

Freight and passenger services

Canadian railways move bulk commodities — grain, potash, coal, petroleum, and forest products — as well as intermodal containers, much of it destined for export or trade with the United States. Passenger rail is limited compared to other developed countries; Via Rail operates intercity services including the transcontinental Canadian, while commuter networks serve Toronto (GO Transit), Montreal (Exo), and Vancouver (West Coast Express). Urban transit systems operate in several cities, including the Toronto subway and Vancouver's SkyTrain.

Regulation

Rail safety and economic regulation fall to Transport Canada and the Canadian Transportation Agency. See also Canadian National Railway and Via Rail.
