Seam carving

Low-energy seams (highlighted) ready for removal in content-aware image resizing.

Seam carving (also known as image retargeting or liquid resizing) is an algorithm for content-aware image resizing, developed by Shai Avidan of Mitsubishi Electric Research Labs and Ariel Shamir. It works by removing or inserting paths of pixels ("seams") that pass through the least important parts of an image, so that resizing preserves the most salient content better than uniform scaling or cropping.

Type: Image processing algorithm

Introduced: 2007 (SIGGRAPH paper by Avidan & Shamir)

Purpose: Content-aware image resizing

Key concepts: Energy function, dynamic programming, seams

Algorithm

An energy function assigns each pixel an importance value, commonly based on the gradient magnitude so that edges and textured regions score highly. A seam is a connected path of pixels running from one side of the image to the other (top to bottom, or left to right), with one pixel per row or column. Dynamic programming finds the seam of minimum total energy, which is then removed (to shrink the image) or duplicated with interpolated values (to enlarge it). The process repeats, one seam at a time.

Applications and reception

Seam carving was adopted in Adobe Photoshop as "Content-Aware Scaling" and is available in GIMP via a plugin and in libraries such as OpenCV and ImageMagick (as liquid rescale). It became famous through a widely viewed SIGGRAPH demonstration video. Limitations include distortion of straight lines, faces and high-entropy scenes, mitigated by user-painted protection masks and improved energy functions incorporating face detection and forward-energy criteria.
