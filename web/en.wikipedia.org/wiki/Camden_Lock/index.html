Camden Lock

Camden Lock is a small part of Camden Town, London Borough of Camden, England, which was formerly a wharf with stables on the Regent's Canal. It is the site of the twin locks on the canal, properly known as the Hampstead Road Locks, and lends its name to the surrounding Camden Lock Market and to the wider Camden Market complex.

History

The Regent's Canal through Camden was built in the 1810s and opened in 1820, serving wharves and warehouses including the gin warehouses of W. & A. Gilbey. The area remained a working industrial and transport interchange until the 1970s, when craft workshops and a weekend market began in 1974 and grew into one of London's most visited attractions.

The market

Camden Lock Market, beside the canal, and neighbouring markets such as the Stables Market occupy former industrial buildings. A major fire in 2008 damaged part of the Lock Market; it reopened after redevelopment. The area is known for alternative fashion, crafts, food stalls and street food, drawing millions of visitors annually.
