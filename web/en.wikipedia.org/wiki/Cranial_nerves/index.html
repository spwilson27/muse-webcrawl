Cranial nerves

The cranial nerves are the twelve paired nerves that emerge directly from the brain — including the brainstem — rather than from the spinal cord. They relay information between the brain and the head and neck, with the vagus nerve (CN X) extending additionally into the thorax and abdomen. They are numbered with Roman numerals I–XII in order from front to back.

Clinical significance

Testing each cranial nerve's function forms a standard part of the neurological examination, since characteristic deficits localise lesions. Disorders include Bell's palsy (CN VII), trigeminal neuralgia (CN V), and anosmia from olfactory nerve damage. A common mnemonic for the names is "On old Olympus's towering top, a Finn and German viewed some hops."
