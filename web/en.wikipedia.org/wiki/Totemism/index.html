Totemism is a system of belief in which humans are said to have a kinship or mystical relationship with a spirit-being, such as an animal or plant, called a totem. The totem is commonly associated with a social group — such as a clan or lineage — which regards the totem as an emblem or ancestor, observes prohibitions (taboos) concerning it, and often is required to marry outside the group (exogamy). The word derives from the Ojibwe word ototeman (roughly, "he is a relative of mine").

Overview

Totemic beliefs have been reported among Indigenous peoples of Australia, North America, Africa, and elsewhere. In Aboriginal Australian cultures, totemic beings are linked with the Dreaming and with ancestral tracks across the landscape; clan members may be forbidden to kill or eat their totem species except on ritual occasions. On the Northwest Coast of North America, carved totem poles display clan crests (totems) recording lineage histories, myths and rights.

In anthropological theory

Totemism was a central problem of early anthropology. James George Frazer compiled its worldwide forms in Totemism and Exogamy (1910). Émile Durkheim, in The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life (1912), treated Australian totemism as the most primitive religion and argued that the totem is the material symbol of both the god and society itself — so that in worshipping the totem, society worships itself. Sigmund Freud's Totem and Taboo (1913) offered a speculative psychoanalytic account linking totemism to the Oedipus complex and a primal act of patricide.

Twentieth-century anthropologists increasingly questioned whether "totemism" names a single institution at all. Claude Lévi-Strauss, in Totemism (1962), argued that totemic classifications are a way of thinking: natural species are chosen not because they are "good to eat" but because they are "good to think" — systems of differences among animals map onto differences among social groups.

Forms

  • Clan totemism – a totem shared by a descent group, usually with exogamy.
  • Individual totemism – a personal guardian spirit or animal acquired by an individual.
  • Sex totemism – separate totems for men and women (reported in parts of Australia).
  • Conception totemism – a child's totem determined by the place of conception.
