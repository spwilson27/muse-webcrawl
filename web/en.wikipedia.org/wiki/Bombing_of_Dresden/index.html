Bombing of Dresden

The bombing of Dresden was a joint British and American aerial bombing attack on the city of Dresden, the capital of Saxony, during the Second World War. In four raids between 13 and 15 February 1945, 772 heavy bombers of the Royal Air Force and 527 of the United States Army Air Forces dropped more than 3,900 tons of high-explosive bombs and incendiary devices on the city, creating a firestorm that destroyed much of the historic centre.

The raids

On the night of 13–14 February, RAF Bomber Command attacked in two waves, and American B-17s followed at midday on the 14th and again on the 15th. The great heat of the firestorm suffocated and burned thousands who had sheltered in cellars. Dresden was a major rail junction and contained over a hundred factories and workshops contributing to the German war effort, though it was also crowded with refugees fleeing the advancing Red Army.

Casualties and controversy

A 2010 study by a historical commission appointed by the city concluded that between 22,700 and 25,000 people were killed. Nazi propaganda inflated the toll enormously, and exaggerated figures were later popularised by writers including David Irving and in Kurt Vonnegut's novel Slaughterhouse-Five. The raid has remained one of the most controversial Allied actions of the war, debated by historians and ethicists as an example of the moral questions raised by area bombing of cities; the destroyed Frauenkirche, left as a ruin memorial for decades, was rebuilt and reconsecrated in 2005.
