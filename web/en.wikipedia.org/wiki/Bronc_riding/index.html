Bronc riding

SportRodeo
VariantsBareback bronc riding, saddle bronc riding
Ride duration8 seconds
Governing bodiesPRCA, PBR

Bronc riding, either bareback bronc or saddle bronc competition, is a rodeo event that involves a rodeo participant riding a horse (sometimes called a bronc or bronco) that attempts to throw or buck off the rider. Originally based on the necessary horse-breaking skills of a working cowboy, the event is now a highly stylized competition.

Rules and scoring

The rider must stay on for eight seconds, holding a rein with one hand only; touching the horse or himself with the free hand disqualifies the ride. Two judges score the ride out of 100 points: up to 50 for the rider's control, spurring technique and style, and up to 50 for the horse's bucking power and difficulty. In saddle bronc riding the rider uses a specialized saddle and must begin with spurs held above the horse's shoulders — the "mark out" — on the first jump.

Equipment and horses

Bareback riders use a leather rigging with a handle similar to a suitcase grip. Bucking horses are bred for the sport, and a flank strap around the horse's flank encourages bucking action. Famous bucking horses become stars in their own right, and stock contractors are judged partly on their animals' performance.
