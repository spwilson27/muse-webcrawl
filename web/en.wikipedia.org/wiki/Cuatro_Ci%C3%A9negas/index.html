Cuatro Ciénegas

Cuatro Ciénegas is a municipality and town in the Mexican state of Coahuila, in the state's central desert region. The name ("Four Marshes") refers to the springs and wetlands of the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, an unusual desert valley whose pools harbour an exceptional concentration of endemic species.

The basin

The valley's spring-fed pools (pozas) support stromatolites, endemic fish such as the Cuatro Ciénegas pupfish, and the only aquatic box turtle, the Coahuilan box turtle. Because its microbial communities resemble ancient ecosystems, the basin has drawn comparisons to early Earth environments. It was declared a federal Flora and Fauna Protection Area in 1994 and is a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Town

The town of Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza is the birthplace of Venustiano Carranza, leader of the Mexican Revolution and president of Mexico, whose home is now a museum. Water extraction for agriculture outside the reserve has threatened the wetlands, making the basin a focus of Mexican conservation debate.
