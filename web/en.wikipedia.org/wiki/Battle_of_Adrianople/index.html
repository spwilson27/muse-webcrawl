The Battle of Adrianople took place on 9 August 378 AD between an Eastern Roman army led by Emperor Valens and Gothic rebels (largely Thervingi, Greuthungi and Alans) led by Fritigern. It ended in an overwhelming victory for the Goths and the death of Valens on the field; roughly two-thirds of the eastern army was destroyed, according to Ammianus Marcellinus, our main source.

Background

In 376 the Thervingi, fleeing the Huns, were admitted across the Danube into the Empire but, mistreated by Roman officials, revolted. Valens marched from the east to deal with them personally, declining to wait for his nephew Gratian's western reinforcements.

The battle

The Roman army, tired and thirsty after a long march, attacked the Gothic wagon laager before its full deployment was complete. Gothic and Alan cavalry returning from a foraging expedition then struck the Roman flank, compressing the infantry so tightly that many men could not use their weapons. Valens was killed — accounts differ as to whether he died on the field or in a farmhouse burned by the Goths.

Significance

Adrianople is often treated as a landmark in the decline of the Roman Empire and a milestone in the rise of heavy cavalry over infantry. Theodosius I eventually settled the Goths within the Empire as foederati in 382.
