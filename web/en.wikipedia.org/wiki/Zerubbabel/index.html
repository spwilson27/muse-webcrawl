Zerubbabel (Hebrew: זְרֻבָּבֶל, likely from Akkadian "seed/offspring of Babylon") was a governor of the Persian province of Yehud and the grandson of Jehoiachin, penultimate king of Judah. He led the first group of Jewish exiles returning from the Babylonian captivity and is credited with laying the foundation of the Second Temple in Jerusalem.

Return from exile

After Cyrus the Great of Persia conquered Babylon in 539 BCE and authorised the exiles to return, Zerubbabel appears in the books of Ezra, Nehemiah, Haggai, and Zechariah as the leader of the returnees and a descendant of the Davidic line. Together with the high priest Jeshua (Joshua), he rebuilt the altar and, after a long interruption caused by opposition from neighbouring peoples, resumed construction of the Temple, encouraged by the prophets Haggai and Zechariah. The Second Temple was completed and dedicated in 515 BCE, in the reign of Darius I.

Messianic expectations

The prophet Haggai addresses Zerubbabel with an oracle promising that God will make him "like a signet ring", language reversing the curse on his grandfather Jehoiachin and raising hopes that the Davidic monarchy would be restored through him. In the event, the monarchy was not restored, and Zerubbabel disappears from the historical record; he nevertheless appears in the New Testament genealogies of Jesus (Matthew 1 and Luke 3) as an ancestor in the line of David.
