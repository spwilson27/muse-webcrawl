Magna Graecia

Magna Graecia (Latin for 'Greater Greece'; Greek: Μεγάλη Ἑλλάς) was the Roman name for the coastal areas of southern Italy colonised by Greek settlers from the 8th century BC, in modern Campania, Puglia, Basilicata, Calabria and Sicily.

Colonies

Major cities included Tarentum (Taras), Syracuse, Neapolis (Naples), Rhegium, Croton, Sybaris and Paestum (Poseidonia). The region was home to Pythagoras and his school at Croton, the Eleatic philosophers Parmenides and Zeno, and Archimedes of Syracuse. Its temples at Paestum and Agrigento are among the best-preserved Greek temples anywhere.

Decline

From the 4th century BC the cities, weakened by wars among themselves and with Italic peoples and Carthage, fell under Roman control after the Pyrrhic War (280–275 BC). Greek language and culture persisted in parts of Calabria and Puglia, where Griko is still spoken by a small minority.
