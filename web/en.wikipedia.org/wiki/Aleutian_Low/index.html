Aleutian Low

The Aleutian Low as a winter centre of low pressure in the North Pacific
TypeSemi-permanent low-pressure system
LocationNorth Pacific Ocean, near the Aleutian Islands
SeasonStrongest in winter; weak/absent in summer
EffectsStorm track, weather of Alaska and the Pacific Northwest

The Aleutian Low is a semi-permanent low-pressure system centred over the Aleutian Islands and the Gulf of Alaska in the North Pacific Ocean. It is one of the principal centres of action of Northern Hemisphere atmospheric circulation, the Pacific counterpart of the Icelandic Low in the North Atlantic.

Formation and seasonal behaviour

The low deepens in autumn and reaches its greatest intensity in mid-winter, when the sharp temperature contrast between the cold Siberian and North American continents and the relatively warm ocean fuels frequent cyclogenesis along the Aleutian chain. In summer the system weakens dramatically and retreats, replaced by the North Pacific High. On any given winter day, the "low" is not a single storm but the statistical average of the many cyclones that pass through the region.

Influence on climate

The position and strength of the Aleutian Low steer the Pacific storm track and thereby control winter weather across western North America. A deep, eastward-displaced low tends to bring warm, wet conditions to Alaska and British Columbia and drier conditions further south, while a weak low favours cold air outbreaks over Alaska. Variations in the low are linked to the Pacific Decadal Oscillation and to shifts in North Pacific marine ecosystems, including salmon productivity regimes. Together with the Icelandic Low, its fluctuations form part of the pattern measured by Arctic and North Pacific oscillation indices.
