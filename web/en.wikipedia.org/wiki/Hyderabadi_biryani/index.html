Hyderabadi biryani, also known as Hyderabadi dum biryani, is a style of biryani from Hyderabad, India made with basmati rice and meat (mostly chicken or mutton/goat). Originating in the kitchens of the Nizam of Hyderabad, it combines elements of Hyderabadi and Mughlai cuisines. The dish is a signature of Hyderabadi cuisine and is typically served with mirchi ka salan and raita.

History

Hyderabadi biryani developed in the court of the Nizams, who ruled Hyderabad State from the 18th century. Legend holds that the dish was perfected by the Nizam's chefs, blending Persian-influenced Mughlai techniques with the spicier tastes of the Deccan. The Asaf Jahi court employed khansamas (royal chefs) from many regions, and the resulting cuisine became famous across India.

Preparation

Two main forms are recognised. In kacchi biryani, raw marinated meat is layered with partly cooked rice and the sealed pot (handi) is cooked on dum — slow steam — often with the lid sealed by dough. In pakki biryani, the meat is cooked separately before layering. Key ingredients include basmati rice, meat marinated in yogurt and spices, fried onions (birista), mint and coriander leaves, lemon juice, and saffron or food colouring that gives the rice its characteristic patchwork of white and orange grains.

Accompaniments

The biryani is traditionally served with mirchi ka salan, a spicy peanut-and-chilli curry, and raita or dahi chutney. Baghara baingan (aubergine curry) is another classic accompaniment. The dish is the centrepiece of Hyderabadi wedding feasts and is sold by restaurants across the city, some of which have become landmarks in their own right.
