Disabled American Veterans (DAV) is an organization created in 1920 by World War I veterans for disabled military veterans of the United States Armed Forces that helps them and their families through various means. It is a congressionally chartered veterans service organization and currently has over one million members.

History

The DAV grew out of the hardships faced by disabled veterans returning from the First World War, when federal support systems were fragmented and inadequate. Judge Robert S. Marx of Cincinnati, a wounded veteran, led the founding of the Disabled American Veterans of the World War in 1920, and the organization received its federal charter from Congress in 1932. During and after the Second World War its membership and service network expanded enormously.

Services

DAV provides free assistance to veterans through a corps of trained National Service Officers, most of them disabled veterans themselves, who help veterans and their families file claims with the Department of Veterans Affairs and appeal adverse decisions. Other programs include:

  • A nationwide volunteer transportation network that drives veterans to VA medical appointments.
  • Disaster relief grants to veterans affected by natural disasters.
  • Employment resources and job fairs for veterans and their spouses.
  • Local chapter-level outreach, scholarships, and advocacy on veterans' legislation in Washington.
