William H. McNeill (historian)

William Hardy McNeill (October 31, 1917 – July 8, 2016) was a Canadian-American historian and author, known for his work in world history and global history.

William Hardy McNeill (31 October 1917 – 8 July 2016) was a Canadian-American historian and author. A professor at the University of Chicago from 1947 until his retirement in 1987, he is best known for The Rise of the West: A History of the Human Community (1963), which won the U.S. National Book Award in History and Biography in 1964.

Early life and education

McNeill was born in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. He earned his bachelor's degree from the University of Toronto in 1938 and his master's degree there in 1939. He completed his doctorate at Cornell University in 1947, after wartime service in the United States Army.

Career

McNeill joined the faculty of the University of Chicago in 1947, where he spent his entire academic career. His 1963 book The Rise of the West was one of the first major works of world history written from a deliberately global perspective, tracing how contact between civilizations drove historical change across Eurasia. It influenced generations of historians and helped launch world history as a recognized scholarly field.

His later works included Plagues and Peoples (1976), a pioneering study of the role of infectious disease in human history; Venice: The Hinge of Europe, 1081–1797 (1974); The Pursuit of Power: Technology, Armed Force, and Society since A.D. 1000 (1982); and A World History (1967, with later revisions).

The Rise of the West

Rejecting earlier civilizational models that treated each culture in isolation, McNeill argued that the principal driver of historical change was contact with "strangers" possessing new skills — that civilizations borrowed, adapted, and were transformed by ideas, technologies, and diseases from beyond their borders. The book covered human history from the Paleolithic to the modern era.

Death and legacy

McNeill died in Torrington, Connecticut, on 8 July 2016, aged 98. His son John Robert McNeill is also a historian (of environmental history), and father and son co-authored The Human Web: A Bird's-Eye View of World History (2003).
