Terma (religion)

Terma (Tibetan: གཏེར་མ་, Wylie: gter ma, "treasure") is a concept in Tibetan Buddhism referring to esoteric teachings and sacred objects believed to have been hidden by Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) and his consort Yeshe Tsogyal during the 8th century, to be rediscovered at the appropriate time by destined revealers known as tertöns (treasure revealers). Terma are a central feature of the Nyingma school and are also found in Bon.

Types of terma

Terma are traditionally divided into earth terma (sa gter), physical objects such as texts, statues, ritual implements and relics discovered in specific locations such as temples, rocks, lakes and caves; and mind terma (dgongs gter), teachings discovered within the mindstream of the tertön. A further category, pure vision (dag snang), involves teachings received in visionary experience.

Tertöns

The revealers of terma, tertöns, are considered emanations of Padmasambhava's original disciples. Great tertöns include Nyangrel Nyima Özer (12th century), Guru Chöwang, Longchenpa's contemporary revealers, and Jigme Lingpa (18th century), whose Longchen Nyingthig cycle became one of the most influential terma collections. The "five king tertöns" hold a special place in Nyingma history.

Famous terma

Perhaps the best-known terma text in the West is the Bardo Thödol, commonly called the Tibetan Book of the Dead, revealed by Karma Lingpa in the 14th century. Terma cycles typically include sadhanas, tantric commentaries, dzogchen instructions and prophecies.
