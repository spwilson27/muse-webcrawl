Overview

Benghazi is Libya's second-largest city and the principal city of the eastern region of Cyrenaica, situated on the Gulf of Sidra. It stands near the site of the Greek colony of Euesperides and the later Ptolemaic city of Berenice.

History

Benghazi was an Ottoman and then Italian colonial city before becoming, with Tripoli, a co-capital of the Kingdom of Libya after independence in 1951. In 2011 it was the birthplace of the uprising against Muammar Gaddafi and seat of the National Transitional Council. The 2012 attack on the US diplomatic compound in the city killed Ambassador J. Christopher Stevens and three other Americans and became a major political controversy in the United States. From 2014 the city saw heavy fighting during Libya's second civil war.
