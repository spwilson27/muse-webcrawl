Jerome John Garcia (August 1, 1942 – August 9, 1995) was an American guitarist, singer and songwriter known principally as the lead guitarist and a vocalist of the rock band the Grateful Dead, which he co-founded in 1965 and with which he performed for the band's entire thirty-year career. Garcia is regarded as one of the most influential guitarists of his generation.

Early life

Garcia was born in San Francisco. His father, a Spanish immigrant and musician, drowned when Garcia was four; at about the same age Garcia lost the middle finger of his right hand in a wood-chopping accident — a feature visible throughout his career. As a teenager in the Bay Area folk and bluegrass scene he played banjo and guitar, and in 1964 formed the jug band Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions with Bob Weir and Ron "Pigpen" McKernan.

Grateful Dead

The jug band electrified to become the Warlocks and then, in 1965, the Grateful Dead, house band of Ken Kesey's Acid Tests and a cornerstone of the San Francisco psychedelic scene. With lyricist Robert Hunter, Garcia wrote much of the band's signature repertoire, including "Dark Star", "Scarlet Begonias", "Ripple", "Touch of Grey" and "Casey Jones". The band's improvisational concerts built an enormous touring following of "Deadheads."

Other projects

Alongside the Dead, Garcia led the Jerry Garcia Band, played pedal steel with the New Riders of the Purple Sage, and explored bluegrass with Old & In the Way (1973) and acoustic music with David Grisman. His solo albums include Garcia (1972) and Reflections (1976).

Death and legacy

Garcia struggled for years with diabetes, obesity and drug addiction. He died of a heart attack on August 9, 1995, at a rehabilitation clinic in Forest Knolls, California, eight days after his 53rd birthday. The Grateful Dead disbanded following his death. Garcia was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of the band in 1994. Cultural tributes range from the Ben & Jerry's ice cream flavour Cherry Garcia to the asteroids and species named in his honour.
