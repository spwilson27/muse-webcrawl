Hong Kong identity card

The Hong Kong identity card (officially the Registration of Persons Card, known as the HKID) is an official identity document issued to Hong Kong residents by the Immigration Department. All residents aged 11 or over who stay in Hong Kong for more than 180 days must hold one. The card records the holder's name, photograph, date of birth and a unique identity card number.

History

Paper identity cards were first issued in 1949 to control immigration from mainland China. Computerised cards followed in the 1980s. A chip-based smart identity card was introduced in 2003, and a territory-wide replacement for the newer generation of smart cards began in 2018.

Types

The two main types are the Hong Kong permanent identity card — held by residents with the right of abode — and the standard Hong Kong identity card for other residents. Symbols on the card denote eligibility for a re-entry permit and other statuses; the asterisk symbols indicate, for example, eligibility for a Hong Kong Special Administrative Region passport.

Number format

The HKID number consists of one or two letters followed by six digits and a check digit in parentheses, the check character being computed from the rest of the number.
