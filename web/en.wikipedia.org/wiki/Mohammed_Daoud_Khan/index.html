Mohammed Daoud Khan

Sardar Mohammed Daoud Khan (18 July 1909 – 28 April 1978) was an Afghan politician who served as prime minister of Afghanistan from 1953 to 1963 and, after overthrowing the monarchy of his cousin King Zahir Shah, as the first president of the Republic of Afghanistan from 1973 until his assassination in the Saur Revolution of 1978.

Early life

Daoud Khan was born in Kabul into the Musahiban branch of the Durrani royal family. Educated in Kabul and France, he pursued a military career, rising to command the central forces. He served as minister of defence and minister of the interior before becoming prime minister in 1953.

Prime minister (1953–1963)

As prime minister, Daoud pursued modernisation programmes with aid from both the United States and the Soviet Union during the Cold War. He promoted women's rights, including the voluntary removal of the veil in 1959, expanded education, and launched five-year economic plans. His vigorous support for Pashtunistan — the irredentist claim on Pashtun areas of Pakistan — damaged relations with Pakistan and contributed to his resignation in 1963 after King Zahir Shah sought to reduce tensions.

President (1973–1978)

On 17 July 1973, while Zahir Shah was abroad, Daoud seized power in a bloodless coup and proclaimed a republic, becoming its president. His government initially included leftists of the People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA), but he later purged them and moved toward one-man rule under his National Revolutionary Party. Relations with the Soviet Union deteriorated. On 27–28 April 1978, communist military officers launched the Saur Revolution; Daoud and most of his family were killed when the presidential palace in Kabul was stormed.
