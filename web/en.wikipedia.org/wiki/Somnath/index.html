Somnath

Somnath is a city and pilgrimage centre in the Gir Somnath district of Gujarat, India, on the Arabian Sea coast. It is chiefly famous for the Somnath temple, dedicated to Shiva as "Lord of the Moon" (Somanātha), which is traditionally regarded as the first of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva. The temple was destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly over the centuries; the present structure, consecrated in 1951, stands as a symbol of reconstruction after independence.

History

The site, ancient Prabhas Patan, is associated in tradition with the moon god Chandra's worship of Shiva and with the death of Krishna. Historical records show a rich temple by the early medieval period. In 1025–26 CE, Mahmud of Ghazni raided Somnath, looting its celebrated wealth and destroying the shrine — an event recorded in Persian chronicles and long remembered in Hindu tradition. The temple was rebuilt by later rulers, including the Chaulukya king Kumarapala in the 12th century, and was attacked again in subsequent centuries, including under Alauddin Khalji's generals (1299) and during the reign of Aurangzeb (1706 demolition order).

Reconstruction

After Indian independence, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel vowed to rebuild the temple; the project was carried forward after his death under K. M. Munshi and the Somnath Trust. The new temple was completed in the Māru-Gurjara style of medieval Gujarat temple architecture, and its Jyotirlinga was consecrated on 11 May 1951 by Rajendra Prasad, the first President of India. A light-and-sound show, museum, and the nearby Triveni Sangam (confluence of the Hiran, Kapila, and legendary Sarasvati rivers) and Bhalka Tirth draw millions of pilgrims annually. In 1990, L. K. Advani began his Ram Rath Yatra from Somnath to Ayodhya, a pivotal event in modern Indian politics.
