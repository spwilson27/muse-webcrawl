Jasper Tudor, Duke of Bedford (c. November 1431 – December 1495) was the uncle of King Henry VII of England and a leading Lancastrian commander during the Wars of the Roses. The younger half-brother of King Henry VI, he was the guardian and mentor of his nephew Henry Tudor, the future Henry VII, whom he protected through fourteen years of exile in Brittany and France and brought to victory at the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485.

Born the second son of Owen Tudor and the dowager queen Catherine of Valois (widow of Henry V), Jasper and his elder brother Edmund were raised after their mother's death under the care of Katherine de la Pole, Abbess of Barking. Henry VI recognised them as his half-brothers, ennobling Edmund as Earl of Richmond and Jasper as Earl of Pembroke in 1452–53.

Early life

Jasper Tudor was born at the Bishop of Ely's manor of Hatfield in Hertfordshire around November 1431. In 1449 he was knighted, and in November 1452 Henry VI raised him to the peerage as Earl of Pembroke. He was granted extensive lands and offices in Wales, including the lordships and castles that made Pembroke the base of his power, and he acted as a mediator between the Lancastrian court and the Yorkist opposition during the political crises of the 1450s.

When his brother Edmund died in captivity at Carmarthen Castle in November 1456, Jasper took responsibility for Edmund's pregnant widow, Margaret Beaufort; her son Henry was born at Pembroke Castle on 28 January 1457, with Jasper as one of his godparents and guardians.

Wars of the Roses

Jasper remained loyal to Henry VI throughout the Wars of the Roses. In 1461 he was defeated by the future Edward IV at the Battle of Mortimer's Cross and after the Lancastrian defeat at Towton he fled to Scotland and France, conducting Lancastrian resistance from Harlech Castle, which held out until 1468 — the longest siege of the wars. He returned with Henry VI's readeption in 1470 but, after the Yorkist victories at Barnet and Tewkesbury in 1471 ended the Lancastrian royal line, he fled with his nephew Henry to Brittany.

Exile and guardianship of Henry Tudor

From 1471 to 1484 Jasper and Henry lived as guests and virtual prisoners of Duke Francis II of Brittany, principally at the Château de Suscinio and Vannes. Jasper negotiated repeatedly with English envoys and resisted attempts by Edward IV and Richard III to secure the fugitives' extradition. In 1484, learning that the Duke's treasurer Pierre Landais had agreed to surrender them, Jasper escaped with Henry into France, where they were supported by the regent Anne of Beaujeu and financed the invasion force that sailed from Harfleur in August 1485.

The expedition landed at Mill Bay, near Milford Haven, on 7 August 1485 — with Jasper, a native Welsh speaker, central to recruiting Welsh support on the march through Wales. At Bosworth on 22 August, Jasper commanded part of the Tudor army that defeated and killed Richard III.

Bosworth and later years

Henry VII rewarded his uncle lavishly: Jasper was created Duke of Bedford in October 1485, restored as Earl of Pembroke, made Justiciar of South Wales, and granted extensive estates including Thornbury Castle in Gloucestershire, which he rebuilt. He remained a trusted councillor and hosted the King and Queen at Thornbury in 1488. He helped suppress the Lovell–Stafford rising of 1486 and remained a mainstay of the early Tudor regime. Jasper died at Thornbury in December 1495 and was buried at Keynsham Abbey in Somerset.

Marriage and family

On 7 November 1485 Jasper married Catherine Woodville, sister of Edward IV's queen Elizabeth Woodville and widow of the Duke of Buckingham. They had no children. Jasper had at least two illegitimate daughters, Helen and Joan, born before his marriage; Joan was the grandmother of the poet and antiquary noted in Welsh genealogies.
