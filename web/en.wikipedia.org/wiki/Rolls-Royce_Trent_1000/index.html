The Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 is a three-shaft high-bypass turbofan engine developed for the Boeing 787 Dreamliner, where it competes with the General Electric GEnx. A member of the Trent family, it first ran on 14 February 2006 and was certified in 2007. The engine powered the 787's first flight on 15 December 2009 and entered service with All Nippon Airways in 2011.

Design

The Trent 1000 uses Rolls-Royce's characteristic three-spool architecture, a 2.85 m fan, and bleedless engine systems suited to the 787's more-electric architecture — it was the launch engine variant to provide all engine bleed functions electrically through shaft-driven generators. Variants include the original Trent 1000-A through later packages, and the improved Trent 1000 TEN (Thrust, Efficiency and New technology), which borrows compressor technology from the Trent XWB and entered service in 2017.

Service issues

From 2016, premature cracking and corrosion of intermediate-pressure turbine blades caused significant fleet disruption, groundings and inspections for 787 operators; Rolls-Royce redesigned the affected blades and the issue cost the company well over £2 billion in charges and compensation.
