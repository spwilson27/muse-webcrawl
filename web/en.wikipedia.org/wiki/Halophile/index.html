Halophile

Halophiles (from the Greek for "salt-loving") are organisms that thrive in environments with high salt concentrations — in some cases far exceeding that of seawater. They are a type of extremophile, found in all three domains of life, and include archaeal genera such as Halobacterium, bacteria such as Salinibacter, and eukaryotes such as the alga Dunaliella salina.

Classification

Halophiles are classed as slight, moderate or extreme according to the salt concentration they prefer: extreme halophiles grow best at 15–30% NaCl. They colour hypersaline waters such as the Great Salt Lake, salt evaporation ponds and the Dead Sea shores with red-orange pigments.

Adaptations

Organisms balance the osmotic pressure of their surroundings either by accumulating high concentrations of potassium chloride inside the cell (the "salt-in" strategy of haloarchaea, whose enzymes are adapted to function in molar salt) or by synthesising compatible solutes such as glycerol. Light-driven proton pumps like bacteriorhodopsin help many haloarchaea harvest energy.
