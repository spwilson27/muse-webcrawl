The Maronite Church is an Eastern Catholic particular church in full communion with the Pope, tracing its origins to Saint Maron, a 4th-century Syriac monk, and centred historically on Lebanon, where Maronites form one of the largest Christian communities.

History

Followers of Maron gathered around his monastery on the Orontes in Syria. From the 7th century, under pressure of war and doctrinal disputes, the community consolidated in the mountains of Lebanon, electing its own patriarch — John Maron is traditionally regarded as the first. Contacts with Rome were renewed during the Crusades, and communion was affirmed at the Council of Florence.

Structure

The church is headed by the Patriarch of Antioch of the Maronites, resident at Bkerké. Eparchies serve Lebanon, Syria, Cyprus, the Holy Land and a large diaspora in the Americas, Australia, Africa and Europe.

Liturgy

The Maronites celebrate the West Syriac liturgy of Saint James, with prayers in Syriac (Aramaic) alongside Arabic and local languages. Maronite saints include Charbel Makhlouf and Rafqa Pietra Choboq Ar-Rayès.
