The National League is the highest division of the National League System and the fifth tier of English association football, sitting below the English Football League. For most of its modern history it was known as the Football Conference, and before that the Alliance Premier League.

The National League is the top tier of non-League football in England.

Structure

The division has 24 clubs. The champions are promoted to EFL League Two, along with the winners of the play-offs contested by the clubs finishing second to seventh. The bottom four clubs are relegated to the National League North or National League South, the sixth tier, with promotion and relegation linking the division to the fully professional leagues above and the semi-professional game below.

History

The division was created in 1979 as the Alliance Premier League, the first single national division below the Football League. Automatic promotion to the Football League was introduced in 1987, ending the old re-election system, and play-offs followed in 2003. It adopted the name National League in 2015. Almost all of its clubs are now fully professional.
