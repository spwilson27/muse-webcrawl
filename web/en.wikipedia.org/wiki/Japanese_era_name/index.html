Japanese era name

The Japanese era name (Japanese: 元号, gengō), also known as nengō, is a traditional calendar era system used in Japan, in which years are counted from the beginning of a named era, traditionally marking the reign of an emperor or another significant event. The system, adopted from China in the 7th century, remains in official use alongside the Gregorian calendar.

The first era name was Taika (645), proclaimed in connection with the Taika Reforms. Between the 7th and 19th centuries era names changed frequently — on imperial succession, at auspicious points in the sexagenary cycle, or to dispel misfortune after disasters. Since the Meiji era, by the Imperial House Law, each emperor has had exactly one era name, which also becomes his posthumous name.

Use

Era years appear on official documents, driver's licences, newspapers and coins. Conversion adds a constant to the era year: for example, Reiwa 1 = 2019, so Reiwa year n corresponds to Gregorian year 2018 + n. Era names are chosen for auspicious meaning from classical texts; "Reiwa" ("beautiful harmony") was the first drawn from a Japanese source, the Man'yōshū, rather than a Chinese classic.
