Linear discriminant analysis

Linear discriminant analysis (LDA), also called Fisher's linear discriminant in its original form, is a method used in statistics and machine learning to find a linear combination of features that separates two or more classes of objects or events. The resulting combination may be used as a classifier, or for dimensionality reduction before classification.

Approach

LDA models the data of each class as a Gaussian distribution with a shared covariance matrix, and assigns new observations to the class with the highest posterior probability, which yields decision boundaries that are linear in the features. In Fisher's geometric formulation, the projection direction is chosen to maximise the ratio of between-class scatter to within-class scatter — pulling class means apart while keeping each class compact.

Relation to other methods

With two classes, LDA is closely related to logistic regression and to analysis of variance, though LDA is generally more stable when its Gaussian assumptions roughly hold and performs well with small samples. Quadratic discriminant analysis (QDA) relaxes the shared-covariance assumption and produces curved boundaries. As a supervised dimensionality-reduction technique, LDA contrasts with principal component analysis, which ignores class labels; a multiclass LDA can yield at most classes − 1 useful discriminant directions.

Applications

Applications have included face recognition (the "Fisherfaces" method), medical diagnosis, credit scoring, marketing and bankruptcy prediction — historically one of the earliest uses of discriminant analysis in business was predicting corporate failure.
