Aston Webb

Sir Aston Webb (22 May 1849 – 21 August 1930) was a British architect who designed the principal façade of Buckingham Palace and the main building of the Victoria and Albert Museum in London. He was President of the Royal Academy from 1919 to 1924, and was among the most prominent official architects of late Victorian and Edwardian Britain.

Career

Webb was born in Clapham, London, the son of the watercolourist Edward Webb. After training as an architect, he won the competition in 1891 to design the main building of the Victoria and Albert Museum in South Kensington (completed in 1909). His London masterwork was the Queen Victoria Memorial scheme: he remodelled the approach to Buckingham Palace, designing The Mall as a ceremonial route, the Admiralty Arch (1910), and in 1913 the new Portland-stone east front of the palace itself, completed in only thirteen weeks of round-the-clock work.

Other works

Elsewhere he designed the University of Birmingham's main buildings, the Royal Naval College at Dartmouth, buildings for Christ's Hospital school, and numerous banks, offices, and country houses. He received the Royal Gold Medal of the RIBA in 1905 and was knighted the same year.
