Statistical population

In statistics, a population is a complete set of items or events sharing at least one property in common that is the subject of a statistical analysis. A population need not consist of people: it may be the set of all manufactured items from a factory, all measurements of a physical quantity, or all possible outcomes of an experiment.

Sub-populations

A subset of a population that shares additional characteristics is called a sub-population. For example, if the population is all adults in a country, women over 65 form a sub-population. Statistical analysis may be performed separately on sub-populations, and results pooled or compared.

Population and sample

Because collecting data from an entire population (a census) is often impractical or impossible, statisticians typically study a sample — a subset of the population — and use statistical inference to draw conclusions about the whole. Properties of the population, such as its mean or variance, are called parameters, whereas corresponding quantities computed from a sample are statistics.

Infinite populations

Some populations are conceptual or infinite, such as the set of all future tosses of a coin. In such cases the population is modeled by a probability distribution.
