Spin structure

In differential geometry, a spin structure on an orientable Riemannian manifold (M, g) is a topological structure that allows one to consistently define spinor fields on the manifold. Spin structures are the manifold analogue of the double cover of the special orthogonal group by the spin group, and they play a central role in mathematical physics, where spinors describe fermionic matter.

Definition

Let P be the principal bundle of oriented orthonormal frames over M, with structure group SO(n). A spin structure is a principal Spin(n)-bundle Q over M together with a bundle map from Q to P which, on each fibre, is the standard two-to-one covering map Spin(n) → SO(n). A manifold admitting a spin structure is called a spin manifold.

Existence and classification

An orientable manifold admits a spin structure if and only if its second Stiefel–Whitney class w2(M) vanishes. When spin structures exist, they are not unique: the set of equivalence classes forms a torsor for the cohomology group H1(M, Z2), so a simply connected spin manifold has a unique spin structure, while a manifold with non-trivial fundamental group can have many.

Spinors and the Dirac operator

Given a spin structure, one can form the associated spinor bundle, whose sections are spinor fields, and define the Dirac operator acting on them. Spin structures thus underpin the Atiyah–Singer index theorem for the Dirac operator, Lichnerowicz's vanishing theorem for scalar curvature, and Edward Witten's proof of the positive energy theorem in general relativity. Generalisations include Spinc structures, which exist on almost every manifold of interest and are fundamental in Seiberg–Witten theory.
