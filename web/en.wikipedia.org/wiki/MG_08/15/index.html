MG 08/15

The MG 08/15 was a German light machine gun of the First World War. Developed in 1915 as a lighter, more portable derivative of the standard heavy Maxim-derived MG 08, its designation — model 08, pattern of 1915 — became proverbial in German: the expression null-acht-fünfzehn ("zero-eight-fifteen") entered everyday language meaning something utterly standard, mediocre, or "run of the mill".

Design

Like the MG 08, the 08/15 is a water-cooled, recoil-operated Maxim-action gun chambered for 7.92×57mm Mauser. To save weight the water jacket was reduced in diameter and capacity, the receiver was slimmed with thinner side plates, and the heavy sled mount was replaced by a bipod and a rifle-style wooden buttstock and pistol grip, allowing a two-man crew to move the gun forward with the infantry. Even so, it weighed roughly 18 kg (about 40 lb) with water and bipod — heavy by later light machine gun standards. It was fed from 100- or 250-round fabric belts, often carried in a distinctive drum-like belt box.

Service history

The MG 08/15 entered service in 1916 and was produced in very large numbers — well over 100,000 guns — making it by far the most common German machine gun of the war. It was issued at platoon level and used by stormtrooper units for mobile fire support. Variants included the LMG 08/15 aircraft gun (air-cooled, synchronized) and the further-lightened MG 08/18 air-cooled version of 1918, which arrived too late for wide use. The gun remained in limited use into the early Second World War with reserve and training units.
