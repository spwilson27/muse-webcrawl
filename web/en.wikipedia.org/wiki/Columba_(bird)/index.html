Columba is a genus of medium to large pigeons. The terms "dove" and "pigeon" are used inconsistently for the smaller and larger Columbidae respectively, but Columba meaning Latin for "dove" or "pigeon" has come to be applied generally to the larger species. The genus includes the familiar rock dove (Columba livia), ancestor of the domestic and feral pigeons found worldwide.

Taxonomy

The genus Columba was introduced by the Swedish naturalist Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in the tenth edition of his Systema Naturae. The name is the Latin word for a dove or pigeon. The American species formerly placed in Columba are now generally assigned to the genus Patagioenas on the basis of molecular studies.

Species

The genus contains roughly 35 recognised species, including:

  • Rock dove, Columba livia
  • Stock dove, Columba oenas
  • Common wood pigeon, Columba palumbus
  • Trocaz pigeon, Columba trocaz
  • Bolle's pigeon, Columba bollii
  • Laurel pigeon, Columba junoniae
  • Afep pigeon, Columba unicincta
  • Snow pigeon, Columba leuconota
  • Speckled pigeon, Columba guinea
  • Metallic pigeon, Columba vitiensis
  • The extinct Liverpool pigeon, Columba caledonica-like spotted green pigeon, is sometimes placed in its own genus Caloenas complex of relatives.

Distribution

Species in the genus are found across Europe, Africa, Asia, and Oceania, occupying habitats from coastal cliffs and farmland to montane forest. Several island species are threatened by habitat loss and introduced predators.
