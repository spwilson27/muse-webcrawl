Arthur Lyman (1932–2002)

Arthur Lyman (2 February 1932 – 24 February 2002) was an American jazz vibraphonist and marimba player from Hawaii, best known as a leading figure of the exotica music genre of the 1950s and 1960s.

Career

Lyman grew up in Honolulu and learned marimba as a teenager. At 21 he joined Martin Denny's combo, playing in the Shell Bar of the Hawaiian Village Hotel, where the group's blend of jazz, Latin rhythms, and imitated bird calls defined the exotica sound. In 1957 Lyman left to form his own group, the Arthur Lyman Group, which recorded a string of albums for HiFi Records, beginning with Taboo (1958).

His 1959 recording of "Yellow Bird" reached number 4 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1961. Lyman's albums, including Taboo, Bahia and Isle of Enchantment, were noted for their wide dynamic range and were used by hi-fi shops to demonstrate stereo equipment. He performed for decades at the New Otani Kaimana Beach Hotel in Waikiki.
