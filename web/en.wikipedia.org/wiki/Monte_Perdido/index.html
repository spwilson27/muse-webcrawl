Monte Perdido (in French: Mont Perdu; in Aragonese: Mont Perdito; all meaning "lost mountain") is the third highest mountain in the Pyrenees. Its summit, at 3,355 metres (11,007 ft), is situated in Spain, in the province of Huesca, Aragon, close to the border with France. The mountain forms part of the Monte Perdido massif, the largest and highest limestone massif in Europe, and lies at the heart of the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park.

Geology and geography

The massif is composed of limestone, and includes the peaks of Monte Perdido, Cilindro de Marboré, and Soum de Ramond (Añisclo). Its glaciers, including the Monte Perdido glacier on the north face, are among the southernmost in Europe and have shrunk markedly over the past century. Deep glacial canyons radiate from the massif — Ordesa, Añisclo, Escuaín, and Pineta — famous for their cliffs, waterfalls, and beech and pine forests.

History and protection

The naturalist Louis Ramond de Carbonnières promoted the mountain's exploration; the first known ascent of the summit was made in 1802 by guides he had sent ahead. The Ordesa Valley was declared a national park in 1918, and the park was extended in 1982 to cover the whole massif as the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. In 1997 the area, together with the adjoining French Pyrenees park, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the transboundary site "Pyrénées – Mont Perdu".
