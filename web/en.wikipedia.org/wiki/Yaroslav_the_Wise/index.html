Yaroslav I Vladimirovich (c. 978 – 20 February 1054), called Yaroslav the Wise, was Grand Prince of Kiev from 1019 until his death, and earlier prince of Novgorod and Rostov. His reign is regarded as the zenith of Kievan Rus', marked by legal codification, church building, diplomacy and learning.

Rise to power

A son of Vladimir the Great, Yaroslav was placed over Novgorod, the second city of Rus'. After Vladimir's death in 1015, his son Sviatopolk seized Kiev and murdered several brothers. Yaroslav, backed by Novgorod and Varangian mercenaries, fought a bitter civil war, finally defeating Sviatopolk at the Alta River in 1019. He then had to share Rus' with his brother Mstislav of Chernigov until Mstislav's death in 1036, when Yaroslav became sole ruler.

Reign

In 1037 Yaroslav began building Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv, modelling his capital on Constantinople; he founded schools and a library, and sponsored translations of Greek books. He promulgated the earliest East Slavic law code, the Russkaya Pravda. Militarily he defeated the Pechenegs decisively in 1036, campaigned against Baltic and Polish neighbours, and founded the city of Yuryev (modern Tartu). His last war against Byzantium, in 1043, ended in failure but was followed by a peace treaty in 1046.

Marriage diplomacy

Yaroslav's epithet as "the father-in-law of Europe" reflects his dynastic marriages: he wed Ingegerd, daughter of King Olof of Sweden; his daughters married kings of France (Anne of Kiev to Henry I), Hungary and Norway, and his sons married into the Byzantine and German imperial families.

Death and legacy

Yaroslav died in 1054 and was buried in Saint Sophia Cathedral. His will divided Rus' among his sons, a partition often blamed for the fragmentation that followed. He is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church, and his image appears on Ukraine's 2-hryvnia banknote and Russia's 1000-ruble banknote.
