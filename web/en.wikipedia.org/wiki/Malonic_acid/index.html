Malonic acid (IUPAC systematic name: propanedioic acid) is a dicarboxylic acid with the structure CH2(COOH)2. Its ionised form and its salts are known as malonates. The name derives from the Greek word μᾶλον (malon), meaning apple, since the acid can be prepared by oxidising malic acid.

Properties and preparation

Malonic acid is a white crystalline solid that is soluble in water. It is a diprotic acid, with pKa values of about 2.83 and 5.69. On heating it readily decarboxylates, yielding acetic acid and carbon dioxide. Industrially it is prepared from chloroacetic acid, which is converted to cyanoacetic acid and then hydrolysed.

Reactions and uses

The methylene group between the two carboxyl groups is acidic, making malonic acid and especially its ester, diethyl malonate, valuable building blocks in organic synthesis. The malonic ester synthesis uses this reactivity to prepare substituted acetic acids, and malonic acid undergoes Knoevenagel condensations with aldehydes. Condensation with urea gives barbituric acid, the parent of the barbiturate drugs.

Biological role

Malonate is a classic competitive inhibitor of the enzyme succinate dehydrogenase in the citric acid cycle, resembling the substrate succinate but lacking the reactive methylene structure needed for oxidation. It occurs naturally in some plants and is an intermediate in the biosynthesis of fatty acids in the form of malonyl-CoA.
