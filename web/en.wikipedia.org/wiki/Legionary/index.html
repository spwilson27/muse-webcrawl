Legionary

The Roman legionary (Latin: legionarius) was a professional heavy infantryman of the Roman army after the Marian reforms. Legionaries were Roman citizens who signed up for a term of service — 25 years under Augustus — and were equipped with armour (lorica segmentata or mail), a curved rectangular shield (scutum), a helmet (galea), a heavy javelin (pilum) and a short sword (gladius). They formed the core fighting force of the Roman legions.

Recruitment and service

Recruits were required to be Roman citizens at least 5 feet 8 inches tall in some periods' stated ideals, and underwent four months of rigorous training in marching, weapons drill, swimming and camp construction. On enlistment a legionary received a signing bonus and regular pay, from which deductions were made for food and equipment. Service culminated in an honourable discharge (honesta missio) with a grant of money or land; auxiliary troops, by contrast, received citizenship on discharge.

Equipment and tactics

On campaign each legionary carried his weapons and armour plus entrenching tools, cooking gear and several days' rations — a load nicknamed the "Marian mule". In battle legionaries typically threw their pila in volleys to disrupt the enemy and weigh down their shields, then closed with the gladius in tight formation behind overlapping shields, using formations such as the tortoise (testudo) under missile fire. Every night on campaign the legion built a fortified marching camp.

Organisation

Legionaries were organised in centuries of 80 men commanded by a centurion, grouped into cohorts of six centuries; a legion comprised ten cohorts, roughly 5,000 legionaries, supported by specialist immunes, officers, and attached cavalry and artillery.
