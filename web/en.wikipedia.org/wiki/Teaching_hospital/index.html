A teaching hospital is a hospital or medical centre that provides medical education and training to future and current health professionals, in addition to delivering patient care. Teaching hospitals are almost always affiliated with one or more universities or medical schools, and are often co-located with them, forming an academic medical centre.

Functions

Teaching hospitals serve a triple mission: clinical care, education and research. They train medical students during clinical rotations (clerkships), and host graduate medical education — residency and fellowship programs — in which qualified doctors specialise under the supervision of attending physicians. Because trainees ask questions and cases are discussed on ward rounds and at case conferences, teaching hospitals are sometimes said to offer particularly thorough diagnostic care, though outcomes vary by condition and specialty.

Care profile

Major teaching hospitals typically provide tertiary and quaternary care: trauma centres, transplant programs, burns units and advanced intensive care attracting complex referrals from wide regions. They conduct much of the clinical research in their countries and are centres for clinical trials. In the United States, teaching hospitals are represented by the Association of American Medical Colleges; in the United Kingdom, teaching hospitals are typically large NHS trusts attached to medical schools in cities such as London, Oxford and Edinburgh. Their higher staffing levels and research activity generally make care more expensive per case than at non-teaching hospitals, a difference partly offset by specific funding streams for training and research.
