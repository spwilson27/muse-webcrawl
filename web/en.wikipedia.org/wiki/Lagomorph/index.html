Lagomorph

The lagomorphs are the members of the taxonomic order Lagomorpha, of which there are two living families: the Leporidae (rabbits and hares) and the Ochotonidae (pikas). There are 110 recent species of lagomorph, across 12 genera in the two families, according to most taxonomic schemes.

Characteristics

Lagomorphs are distinguished from rodents, to whom they were long classified as a superfamily, by having a second pair of incisors behind the first pair in the upper jaw. They also differ in that their maxilla is fenestrated, and in having their testes located in front of the penis.

Distribution

Lagomorphs are native to every continent except Antarctica, and occur in a variety of habitats including grasslands, rocky mountains, and tundra. Many species, particularly rabbits, have been widely introduced by humans.

Evolution

The earliest known lagomorph fossils date to the late Eocene of Asia. The group diversified through the Oligocene and Miocene, and several extinct families, such as the Prolagidae, are recognised.
