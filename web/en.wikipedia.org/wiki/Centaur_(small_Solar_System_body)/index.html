Centaur (small Solar System body)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In planetary astronomy, a centaur is a small Solar System body that makes an unstable orbit between Jupiter and Neptune and crosses the orbits of one or more of the giant planets. Centaurs generally have characteristics of both asteroids and comets. They are named after the mythological centaurs, which were a mixture of horse and human, reflecting their dual nature. It has been estimated that there are around 44,000 centaurs in the Solar System with diameters larger than 1 kilometre.

The first centaur to be discovered was 944 Hidalgo in 1920. However, they were not recognized as a distinct population until the discovery of 2060 Chiron in 1977. The largest confirmed centaur is 10199 Chariklo, which at 260 kilometres in diameter is as big as a mid-sized main-belt asteroid, and is known to possess a ring system.

Orbits

Centaurs orbit the Sun at distances between the orbits of Jupiter (5.5 AU) and Neptune (30 AU). Their orbits are dynamically unstable, with lifetimes of a few million years, because of perturbations by the giant planets. A centaur may eventually be ejected from the Solar System, collide with a planet or the Sun, or be perturbed inward to become a short-period comet of the Jupiter family. No centaur's orbit is known to be stable over the long term.

Physical characteristics

Centaurs range in colour from neutral-grey to very red, reflecting differing surface compositions and histories. Some, such as Chiron, show cometary activity with a coma, despite never approaching the Sun closely; Chiron is therefore classified as both a centaur and a comet. Spectroscopic observations have detected water ice on several centaurs. Chariklo was found in 2013 to have two narrow rings, the first ring system discovered around a small Solar System body.

Notable centaurs

  • 2060 Chiron — the first recognized centaur; exhibits a coma
  • 5145 Pholus — extremely red surface
  • 8405 Asbolus
  • 10199 Chariklo — largest known; has rings
  • 60558 Echeclus — experienced a large cometary outburst
