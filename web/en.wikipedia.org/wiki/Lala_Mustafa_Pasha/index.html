Lala Mustafa Pasha

Lala Mustafa Pasha (c. 1500 – 7 August 1580) was an Ottoman Bosnian general and statesman who served as Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire for a few months in 1580. The honorific Lala means "tutor", as he had served as tutor to the sons of Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. He is best remembered as the commander of the Ottoman conquest of Cyprus in 1570–71.

Career

Of Bosnian origin, Mustafa rose through the palace service and held a succession of high posts, including beylerbey (governor-general) of Damascus and of Yemen, where he campaigned in the 1560s, and fifth vizier. A bitter rivalry with the Grand Vizier Sokollu Mehmed Pasha marked much of his later career.

Conquest of Cyprus

In 1570 Lala Mustafa commanded the land forces sent to conquer Venetian Cyprus, the expedition that provoked the formation of the Holy League and culminated in the Battle of Lepanto. Nicosia fell in September 1570, but Famagusta, under Marcantonio Bragadin, held out for eleven months until August 1571. Despite having agreed terms of surrender, Mustafa had Bragadin flayed alive — reportedly after accusing the Venetians of murdering Muslim prisoners — an act notorious throughout Europe. Famagusta's Gothic cathedral of St Nicholas was converted into the mosque that still bears his name.

Later life and death

In 1578 he commanded Ottoman forces in the Caucasus campaign against Safavid Persia, conquering much of Georgia and Shirvan. On the death of Sokollu in 1579 his position strengthened, and he became Grand Vizier on 28 April 1580, but died only a few months later, on 7 August 1580. He was buried in the courtyard of the Eyüp Sultan Mosque in Istanbul; the architect Sinan designed several works commissioned by him, including mosques in Damascus and Konya Ereğlisi.
