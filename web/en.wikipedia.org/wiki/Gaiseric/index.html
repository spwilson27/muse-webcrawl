Gaiseric (c. 389 – 25 January 477), also known as Genseric, was king of the Vandals and Alans from 428 to 477. One of the most formidable opponents of the late Roman Empire, he led his people from Iberia across to North Africa, conquered Carthage in 439, built a powerful fleet, and famously sacked Rome in 455.

Rise to power

Gaiseric succeeded his half-brother Gunderic in 428. In 429 he ferried some 80,000 people — the entire Vandal and Alan nation — across the Strait of Gibraltar into Roman Africa, whether by invitation from the rebel governor Boniface or on his own initiative remains disputed. Over the following decade he defeated Roman and allied forces and seized Hippo Regius and, in 439, Carthage itself, which became his capital.

King of Carthage

From Carthage, Gaiseric built the Vandals into the dominant naval power of the western Mediterranean, raiding Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica and the Italian coast. In June 455, after the assassination of the emperor Valentinian III, his forces occupied Rome for two weeks and plundered it systematically — an event that gave the word "vandalism" its modern sense, though contemporaries noted the sack was less bloody than that of 410.

Gaiseric defeated two great Roman attempts to reconquer Africa: the expedition of Majorian in 460 and the massive joint armada of 468, destroyed at the Battle of Cape Bon. An Arian Christian, he persecuted the Nicene clergy of Africa. He died peacefully in 477, having secured Vandal independence by treaty in 442 and again in 474.
