Turf house

A turf house is a traditional dwelling built with walls and roofs of turf (sod) laid over a timber or stone frame. Turf construction was widespread across the North Atlantic — in Iceland, Norway, the Faroe Islands, Ireland, and Scotland — where wood was scarce but grass and peat were abundant.

Construction

Thick blocks of turf were stacked to form insulating walls, often a metre thick, around a wooden frame made from driftwood or imported timber; roofs were built of birch bark and layered sod, which continues to grow and must be mown. Interiors were commonly panelled with wood. Turf provides excellent thermal insulation but biodegrades, so walls and roofs require periodic renewal — typically every 20 to 70 years.

History

In Iceland, turf houses were the standard dwelling from the settlement era in the 9th century until well into the 20th century, evolving from Viking longhouses into clusters of gabled farm buildings. Preserved examples such as Glaumbær and Árbær are now museums, and the turf house tradition has been nominated for UNESCO recognition.
