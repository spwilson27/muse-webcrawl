The Harvey Awards are among the most prestigious honours in the American comics industry, given for achievements in comic books and sequential art. Named after writer-artist Harvey Kurtzman (1924–1993), the co-founder of Mad magazine, the awards were first presented in 1988, succeeding the Kirby Awards, which had been discontinued amid disputes the previous year.

History

The Harvey Awards were established by publisher Gary Groth of Fantagraphics Books in the wake of the collapse of the Kirby Awards in 1987. The first ceremony was held at the Chicago Comicon in 1988. Over the following decades the awards moved between conventions, including long associations with Pittsburgh Comicon, the Museum of Comic and Cartoon Art festival in New York, and Baltimore Comic-Con. In 2017, ReedPop acquired the awards and moved the ceremony to New York Comic Con, also restructuring the voting process.

Voting and categories

Unlike the Eisner Awards, which use a judging panel to select nominees, the Harvey Awards are notable for being voted on entirely by comics professionals — creators, publishers, retailers, and other industry workers. Categories have included Best Writer, Best Artist, Best Cartoonist, Best New Series, Best Continuing or Limited Series, Best Graphic Album, Best Single Issue or Story, and special awards for humour and excellence in presentation. Since the 2018 restructuring, the awards have focused on a smaller set of book-of-the-year categories, including Book of the Year, Digital Book of the Year, and Best Adaptation.

Hall of Fame

The Harvey Kurtzman Hall of Fame Award honours lifetime achievement. Inductees include Will Eisner, Jack Kirby, Harvey Kurtzman himself, Carl Barks, and many other foundational figures of the medium.
