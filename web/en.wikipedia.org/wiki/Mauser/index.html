Mauser

Founded1874 (origins 1811), Oberndorf am Neckar, Germany
FoundersPaul Mauser and Wilhelm Mauser
IndustryFirearms
Notable productsGewehr 98, Karabiner 98k, C96 pistol

Mauser, originally Königliche Waffen Schmieden and later Mauser-Werke Oberndorf Waffensysteme GmbH, was a German arms manufacturer founded by the brothers Paul and Wilhelm Mauser. Its bolt-action rifle designs became among the most widely copied and exported military firearms in history.

History

The Mauser brothers developed a rotating-bolt rifle adopted by Prussia as the Model 1871, and the company was formally established at Oberndorf in 1874. Successive improvements produced the Gewehr 98 of 1898, whose controlled-feed bolt action, three-lug bolt, and five-round stripper-clip magazine set the pattern for military rifles worldwide; its shortened successor, the Karabiner 98k, was Germany's standard service rifle in World War II. Mauser also produced the C96 "Broomhandle" self-loading pistol and the 7.92×57mm Mauser cartridge, one of the most influential rifle rounds ever made.

Legacy

Mauser-action rifles were manufactured under licence or copied in dozens of countries — including the American Springfield M1903 and British Pattern 1914 — and remain prized for sporting rifles, with the Mauser M 98 still in production. After World War II the Oberndorf works passed through several owners, including Rheinmetall, and the Mauser name continues as a brand of hunting rifles.
