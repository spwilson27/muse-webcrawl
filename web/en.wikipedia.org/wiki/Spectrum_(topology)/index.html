Spectrum (topology)

In algebraic topology, a branch of mathematics, a spectrum is an object representing a generalized cohomology theory. A spectrum consists of a sequence of topological spaces (or pointed simplicial sets) En together with structure maps relating each space to the loop space of the next, ΣEn → En+1. Homology and cohomology theories then arise as homotopy groups of mapping spaces into or out of the spectrum.

A spectrum: a sequence of spaces with suspension (structure) maps between them.

Motivation

Eilenberg–Steenrod cohomology theories on spaces extend to spectra: the homotopy groups πn(E) = colim πn+k(Ek) define a graded abelian group-valued theory in all integer degrees, positive and negative. The sphere spectrum 𝕊, whose spaces are spheres Sn, represents stable homotopy groups of spheres, the central and notoriously difficult invariants of stable homotopy theory.

Examples

  • Eilenberg–MacLane spectra HA for an abelian group A, representing ordinary cohomology.
  • K-theory spectra KU and KO, representing complex and real topological K-theory, with Bott periodicity built into their structure.
  • Cobordism spectra such as MU (complex cobordism) and MSO, central to stable homotopy and index theory.
  • Thom spectra, and chromatic spectra such as Morava K-theories K(n) and Morava E-theories.

Modern foundations

Rigorous categories of spectra — Boardman's stable homotopy category, and later symmetric spectra, orthogonal spectra, and the ∞-category of spectra via model categories and higher algebra — provide commutative ring spectra ("brave new rings"), smash products, and the machinery of derived algebraic geometry. Spectra are the stabilisation of the category of spaces: in the ∞-categorical setting, Sp is obtained by inverting the suspension functor, and it is the natural home for generalised cohomology, algebraic K-theory, and topological Hochschild homology.
