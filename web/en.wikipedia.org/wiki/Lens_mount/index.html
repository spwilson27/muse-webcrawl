Lens mount

A lens mount is an interface — mechanical and often also electrical — between a photographic camera body and a lens. It is used on cameras with interchangeable lenses, allowing the user to combine bodies and lenses from compatible systems. Mount designs differ in flange focal distance, throat diameter, locking mechanism (bayonet, breech-lock or screw thread) and the electrical contacts that carry aperture, focus and image-stabilisation data.

Types

Early interchangeable lenses used screw mounts such as the Leica thread mount (M39) and the M42 mount. Bayonet mounts became standard for their speed and precise alignment: examples include Nikon F, Canon EF and EF-S, Pentax K, Sony A and E, Micro Four Thirds, and the mirrorless Canon RF, Nikon Z and L-Mount Alliance systems. Larger throats and short flange distances on mirrorless mounts permit smaller bodies and faster lens designs.

Flange focal distance

The flange focal distance — the distance from the mounting flange to the film or sensor plane — determines whether lenses from one system can be adapted to another with a simple mechanical adapter: this is possible only when the donor system's distance is longer, unless the adapter contains optics. Electronic communication may also be lost or require active adapters.
