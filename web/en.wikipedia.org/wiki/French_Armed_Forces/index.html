The French Armed Forces (French: Forces armées françaises) are the military forces of France, comprising the French Army, the French Navy, the French Air and Space Force, and the National Gendarmerie, together with joint services for health, logistics, and cyber defence. Under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic, the President of the Republic is commander-in-chief, and the Prime Minister and Minister of the Armed Forces are responsible for implementing defence policy. With around 200,000 active personnel and one of the largest defence budgets in Europe, France maintains the most capable military in the European Union, including an independent nuclear deterrent.

Structure and roles

The Army (Armée de terre) fields armoured, mechanised, airborne, and mountain forces, including the French Foreign Legion, and is equipped with Leclerc tanks and Caesar howitzers. The Navy (Marine nationale) operates the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle, ballistic-missile submarines carrying the sea-based deterrent, and frigates and patrol forces protecting France's vast overseas territories and exclusive economic zone. The Air and Space Force (Armée de l'air et de l'espace), renamed in 2020 to reflect its space role, operates Rafale multirole fighters and the airborne leg of the nuclear deterrent. The Gendarmerie, a military force with police duties, polices rural France and provides specialised units such as the GIGN.

Nuclear deterrent

France's force de frappe, developed from the 1960s under President Charles de Gaulle, rests on submarine-launched ballistic missiles (Triomphant-class SSBNs armed with M51 missiles) and air-launched ASMP-A cruise missiles carried by Rafales. France maintains a stockpile of roughly 290 nuclear warheads under a doctrine of strict sufficiency, aimed at deterring any state-level threat to its vital interests.

Recent operations

Conscription was suspended in 2001 under President Jacques Chirac, completing the transition to an all-volunteer professional force. French forces have been heavily engaged abroad: in Afghanistan, the Balkans, Libya (2011), Mali and the Sahel (Operation Serval, 2013, and Operation Barkhane, 2014–2022), and against Islamic State in Iraq and Syria (Operation Chammal). Domestically, Operation Sentinelle has deployed soldiers to guard sensitive sites since the January 2015 Paris attacks. France is a founding member of NATO but left its integrated military command in 1966 and rejoined fully only in 2009, preserving a tradition of strategic autonomy.
