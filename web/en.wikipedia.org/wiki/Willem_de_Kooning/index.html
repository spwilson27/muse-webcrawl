Willem de Kooning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Woman I (1950–52): a fierce, grinning female figure built from slashing strokes of pink, yellow and green]

Willem de Kooning (24 April 1904 – 19 March 1997) was a Dutch-American abstract expressionist artist. Born in Rotterdam, he stowed away to the United States in 1926 and became a central figure of the New York School, known for violent, gestural brushwork that oscillated between abstraction and the figure.

His reputation was made with the black-and-white abstractions culminating in Excavation (1950) and the controversial Woman series (1950–53), whose aggressive female figures seemed to many a betrayal of pure abstraction. Later work turned to landscape abstraction and, in the 1980s, spare ribbon-like paintings made as Alzheimer's disease advanced. His Interchange (1955) sold privately in 2015 for a reported $300 million.
