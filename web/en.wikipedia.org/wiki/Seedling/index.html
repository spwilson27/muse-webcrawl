Seedling

A seedling is a young sporophyte plant that has recently emerged from a seed. The seedling stage begins with germination and lasts until the plant is fully established and photosynthetically self-sufficient; it is the most vulnerable phase of a plant's life, and the great majority of seedlings die before maturity.

Development

Germination starts when the seed imbibes water and the embryo's root (the radicle) breaks through the seed coat, anchoring the plant and absorbing water. The shoot then grows toward light. In epigeal germination (e.g. beans), the cotyledons are carried above ground and may photosynthesise; in hypogeal germination (e.g. peas, oaks), they remain below ground and the seedling draws on their stored food. Monocot seedlings bear a single cotyledon and dicots two. Initially seedlings live off seed reserves (endosperm or cotyledons); once the first true leaves expand, they switch to photosynthesis. A seedling grown in darkness becomes etiolated — pale and elongated — as it searches for light.

Ecology and cultivation

Seedling survival depends on moisture, light, temperature, and escaping herbivores, fungal "damping-off" diseases, and competition. Gardeners and foresters exploit the stage by raising seedlings in nurseries, greenhouses, or seed trays under controlled conditions before transplanting them out — hardier and more predictable than direct sowing — while "volunteer" seedlings arise spontaneously from self-sown seed.
