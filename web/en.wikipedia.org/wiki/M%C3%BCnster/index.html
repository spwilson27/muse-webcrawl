Münster is an independent city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It is in the northern part of the state and is considered the cultural centre of the Westphalia region. It is also the capital of the local government region Münsterland.

History

The city was founded around 793 when Charlemagne sent the missionary Ludger to evangelize the region; its name derives from the monastery (monasterium) he established. In the 1530s the city fell under the radical Anabaptist regime of the Münster Rebellion, whose leaders were executed in 1536 and whose cages still hang from the tower of St Lambert's Church.

In 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was negotiated simultaneously in Münster and Osnabrück, ending the Thirty Years' War and the Eighty Years' War and reshaping the political order of Europe.

Modern city

Heavily bombed during the Second World War, Münster's old town was rebuilt in its historic style. The University of Münster, founded in 1780, is one of Germany's largest universities. The city is famous for its bicycle culture: bicycles outnumber residents, and the Promenade ring road is reserved for cyclists and pedestrians.
