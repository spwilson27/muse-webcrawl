The Pennsylvania State Capitol is the seat of government for the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, located in downtown Harrisburg. President Theodore Roosevelt called it "the handsomest building I ever saw" at its dedication in 1906.

Architecture

Designed by Philadelphia architect Joseph Miller Huston, the Beaux-Arts building is crowned by a dome modelled on St Peter's Basilica in Rome, topped by the gilded statue Commonwealth. The interior is richly decorated with art celebrating Pennsylvania history, including murals by Violet Oakley — the first woman to receive a large American mural commission — Edwin Austin Abbey's paintings in the House chamber, and a floor of Moravian tiles by Henry Chapman Mercer.

History

The capitol replaced an earlier building destroyed by fire in 1897. Although its construction was marred by a graft scandal that sent the architect and others to prison, the building is now a National Historic Landmark and houses the General Assembly, the governor's offices and the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania.
