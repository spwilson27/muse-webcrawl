Catherine of Valois

Catherine of Valois (27 October 1401 – 3 January 1437) was Queen of England from 1420 until 1422 as the wife of King Henry V. The youngest daughter of King Charles VI of France and Isabeau of Bavaria, she married Henry as part of the Treaty of Troyes during the Hundred Years' War.

Catherine of Valois
Queen consort of England: 1420–1422
Born: 27 October 1401, Paris
Died: 3 January 1437, London
Spouses: Henry V of England; Owen Tudor
Issue: Henry VI; Edmund Tudor; Jasper Tudor

Life

Catherine bore Henry a son, the future Henry VI, before Henry V died in 1422. Widowed at 21, she later formed a relationship — probably a marriage — with Welsh courtier Owen Tudor. Their sons Edmund and Jasper Tudor made her the grandmother of Henry VII, founder of the Tudor dynasty: Edmund Tudor was father of Henry VII by Margaret Beaufort.

Legacy

Her secret union with Owen Tudor had lasting consequences for English history, linking the Valois, Lancastrian and Tudor lines. She is a character in Shakespeare's Henry V.
