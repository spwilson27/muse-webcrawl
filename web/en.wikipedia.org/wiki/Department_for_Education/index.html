The Department for Education (DfE) is a department of the Government of the United Kingdom responsible for child protection, education, training, apprenticeships, and skills in England. It was formed in May 2010, succeeding the Department for Children, Schools and Families, and its remit has at various times included higher and further education and aspects of children's social care.

Responsibilities

The department oversees the state-funded school system in England, including academies and free schools, the national curriculum, teacher training and recruitment, school funding and standards, and children's services policy. It sponsors executive agencies and works alongside the inspectorate Ofsted and the examinations regulator Ofqual. Following machinery-of-government changes, responsibility for higher education and skills has moved between the DfE and other departments; since 2023 the DfE has again held the full education and skills portfolio for England.

History

The department traces its origins to the Committee of the Privy Council on Education of 1839 and the Board of Education established in 1899. It became the Ministry of Education in 1944, the Department of Education and Science in 1964, and passed through several names — including the Department for Education and Skills and the Department for Children, Schools and Families — before taking its present form in 2010.

Location

The department's headquarters is at Sanctuary Buildings in Westminster, London, with major offices including sites in Sheffield, Manchester, Coventry, and Darlington, where it forms part of the Darlington Economic Campus.
