The PlayStation Camera is a motion sensor and camera accessory for the PlayStation 4, developed by Sony Interactive Entertainment. It is the successor to the PlayStation Eye for the PlayStation 3 and was released alongside the PS4 in November 2013, sold separately from the console. Unlike Microsoft's bundled Kinect for the rival Xbox One, the camera was optional — a decision widely credited with helping the PS4 launch at a lower price.

Hardware

The rectangular bar-shaped device contains two cameras with wide-angle lenses and a resolution of 1280×800 each, allowing it to capture depth through stereoscopic comparison and to track the position of the DualShock 4's light bar and of PlayStation Move controllers. A four-channel microphone array provides voice commands and audio for streaming. The cameras can record RAW and YUV video at up to 60 frames per second at full resolution, and up to 240 frames per second at reduced resolution for motion tracking.

Uses

The camera enables facial-recognition login, voice control of the console, picture-in-picture broadcasting of the player during livestreams, and augmented-reality play in titles such as The Playroom, which was pre-installed on every PS4. Its most substantial role came in 2016: the PlayStation Camera is required for the PlayStation VR headset, tracking the headset's LED lights to determine the player's head position. A subtly redesigned, rounder version of the camera was released alongside the PS4 Slim and PS4 Pro. The PlayStation 5 is compatible with the PS4 camera for VR games through a free adapter, though the PS5's own HD Camera is a separate product that does not support VR tracking.

Reception

Sales were modest before the launch of PlayStation VR, with attachment rates reported at roughly one in seven PS4 owners by 2016; demand rose sharply once the VR headset made the camera mandatory, and the bundle of headset, camera, and controllers became the standard way to buy PlayStation VR.
