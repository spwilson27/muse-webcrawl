Scone (Scots: Scone; Scottish Gaelic: Sgàin) is a village in Perth and Kinross, Scotland. The medieval village of Scone, which grew up around a monastery and a royal residence, was abandoned in the early 19th century when the residents were removed and a new palace was built on the site by the Earl of Mansfield. Hence the modern village of New Scone, which developed further east. Today "Scone" generally denotes the modern village.

Scone was the historic capital of the Kingdom of Scotland and the coronation place of Scottish monarchs, on the Moot Hill and over the Stone of Scone (the Stone of Destiny). It was the seat of Scone Abbey, founded in the 12th century.

History

Scone's importance dates from the early medieval period, when it was a major centre of the Pictish kingdom and then of the emerging Kingdom of Alba. Kenneth MacAlpin is traditionally said to have brought the Stone of Scone here in the 9th century. Robert the Bruce was crowned at Scone in 1306, and Charles II was crowned there in 1651, the last coronation held at Scone.

In 1296 Edward I of England removed the Stone of Scone to Westminster Abbey; it was officially returned to Scotland in 1996 and is now displayed in Perth Museum, near Scone. Scone Abbey was destroyed by a mob during the Scottish Reformation in 1559.

Scone Palace

Scone Palace, a Category A listed Georgian Gothic house built of red sandstone (1803–1812), stands on the site of the old palace and abbey precinct and is the seat of the Earl of Mansfield. Its grounds include the Moot Hill, topped by a small chapel, where Scottish kings were crowned.

Geography

The village lies just north-east of Perth, close to the River Tay. The A94 road passes through it, linking Perth with Coupar Angus and Forfar.
