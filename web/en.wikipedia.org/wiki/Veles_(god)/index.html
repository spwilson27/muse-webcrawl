Veles (god)

Veles, also known as Volos, is a major god of Slavic mythology. His earthly duties include the patronage of cattle and wealth, and he is also associated with the waters, the earth, the underworld, magic, music, and trickery. In the reconstructed Slavic pantheon he is the principal adversary of the thunder god Perun.

Sources and worship

Veles is attested in East Slavic sources, where he is named in the treaties of the Rus' with Byzantium as a god by whom oaths were sworn, and in the Primary Chronicle, which records that Prince Vladimir placed idols of the gods in Kyiv in 980 but that the idol of Volos stood separately, down by the market. Hypatios Chronicle and later folklore preserve his association with cattle — hence his title skotii bog, "cattle god". Place names and the South Slavic and Polish records also attest to his cult.

Mythology

Comparative reconstruction, notably by Roman Jakobson and by Vyacheslav Ivanov and Vladimir Toporov, portrays Veles as a serpentine or bear-like chthonic deity dwelling at the roots of the world tree, in the damp realm of the dead. His recurring theft of Perun's cattle, wife, or son provokes Perun to pursue and strike him down with lightning, releasing the waters or cattle — an annual cycle myth compared with the combat myths of other Indo-European traditions.

Legacy

After Christianisation, aspects of Veles's cult were absorbed by Saint Nicholas and by Saint Blaise (Vlasii), protector of cattle. Veles is a prominent deity in modern Slavic neopaganism, and his name survives in place names across Slavic lands.
