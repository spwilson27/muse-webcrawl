Golden Ring (Russia)

The Golden Ring (Russian: Золотое кольцо, Zolotoye koltso) is a popular tourist route in Russia that passes through a ring of ancient historic towns northeast of Moscow. The towns preserve outstanding monuments of medieval Russian architecture — white-stone churches, kremlins, monasteries, and frescoes — from the period of Kievan Rus' and the early Russian principalities.

The towns

The classical ring includes Sergiyev Posad, Pereslavl-Zalessky, Rostov Veliky, Yaroslavl, Kostroma, Ivanovo, Suzdal, and Vladimir, though lists vary and often add smaller towns such as Uglich, Plyos, and Yuryev-Polsky. The name "Golden Ring" was coined in 1967 by the art historian and writer Yuri Bychkov in a series of essays in the newspaper Sovetskaya Kultura.

Highlights

Sergiyev Posad is home to the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius, the spiritual centre of the Russian Orthodox Church and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Vladimir preserves the twelfth-century Assumption and St. Demetrius cathedrals, masterpieces of white-stone carving; nearby Suzdal is an open-air museum of churches and wooden architecture. Yaroslavl's historic centre, also UNESCO-listed, contains richly frescoed seventeenth-century churches along the Volga.
