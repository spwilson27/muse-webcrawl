Tenebrism

Tenebrism, from the Italian tenebroso ("dark, gloomy, mysterious"), also called dramatic illumination, is a style of painting using especially pronounced chiaroscuro, where violent contrasts of light and dark dominate and darkness becomes a significant feature of the image.

Characteristics and artists

The technique was developed most famously by Caravaggio (1571–1610), whose spotlit figures emerge from black backgrounds, and spread across Europe by his followers, the Caravaggisti — including Artemisia Gentileschi, Jusepe de Ribera, and Georges de La Tour. Unlike general chiaroscuro, tenebrism uses darkness as an active compositional element, heightening drama and focusing attention on the lit subject.

Legacy

Tenebrism influenced Baroque painting throughout Italy, Spain, France, and the Netherlands, and its lighting approach was later echoed in film noir and dramatic photography.
