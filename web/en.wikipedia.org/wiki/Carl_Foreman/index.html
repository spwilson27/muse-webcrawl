Carl Foreman (23 July 1914 – 26 June 1984) was an American screenwriter and film producer who wrote or produced some of the most notable films of the 1950s and 1960s, including High Noon (1952), The Bridge on the River Kwai (1957) and The Guns of Navarone (1961).

Career and blacklist

Foreman came to prominence writing for producer Stanley Kramer, earning acclaim for Champion (1949), Home of the Brave (1949) and The Men (1950). During production of High Noon he was subpoenaed by the House Un-American Activities Committee; having briefly been a member of the Communist Party in the 1930s, he was blacklisted in Hollywood after declining to name names. He moved to Britain, where he wrote scripts uncredited or under pseudonyms, and shared the Academy Award for The Bridge on the River Kwai — an award credited for decades to Pierre Boulle and only restored to Foreman and Michael Wilson posthumously in 1984.

Later work

Based in London, Foreman produced and wrote major international productions including The Guns of Navarone and Born Free (1966). He briefly headed Columbia Pictures' British operation and later returned to the United States.
