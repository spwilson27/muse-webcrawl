Nome, Alaska

Nome (Inupiaq: Sitŋasuaq) is a city in the Nome Census Area in the Unorganized Borough of the U.S. state of Alaska, located on the southern Seward Peninsula coast on Norton Sound of the Bering Sea. Its population is about 3,700. Nome is famous for its 1899 gold rush and as the finish line of the Iditarod sled dog race.

History

Gold was discovered on Anvil Creek in 1898 by the "Three Lucky Swedes", triggering a rush that drew some 20,000 prospectors to Nome's beaches by 1900 — one of the largest gold rushes in Alaska's history. In 1925, a diphtheria epidemic threatened the isolated town; antitoxin was rushed from Nenana by dog sled relay over nearly 700 miles — the "Great Race of Mercy" — completed in about five and a half days by teams including the lead dogs Togo and Balto. The event is commemorated by the annual Iditarod race, which finishes under the burled arch on Nome's Front Street.

Geography and life

Nome has no road connection to the rest of Alaska; access is by air and sea during the ice-free months. The population is roughly half Alaska Native, principally Iñupiat. The economy centres on government services, mining, fishing and tourism.
