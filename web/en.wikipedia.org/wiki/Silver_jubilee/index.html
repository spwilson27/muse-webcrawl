A silver jubilee is a celebration held to mark a 25th anniversary. The term derives from the biblical Jubilee — a year of emancipation and restoration proclaimed every fifty years — and is applied to the reigns of monarchs, marriages, institutions, and other long-running endeavours. Silver is the traditional gift and symbolic material for twenty-fifth anniversaries.

Street parties and decorations are a traditional feature of royal silver jubilee celebrations in the United Kingdom.

Royal silver jubilees

Among the most famous silver jubilees was that of Queen Victoria in 1862 — largely a private observance — and, in the modern era, the Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II in 1977, marking 25 years since her accession in 1952. The 1977 celebrations included nationwide street parties, a service of thanksgiving at St Paul's Cathedral, tours of the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth, and the naming of London Underground's Jubilee line in honour of the occasion. Other notable royal silver jubilees include those of Emperor Hirohito of Japan (1951), Haile Selassie of Ethiopia (1955), and Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi of Iran (1966).

Other uses

In the Roman Catholic Church, priests and religious celebrate a silver jubilee after 25 years of ordination or profession. Universities, schools, companies, and married couples likewise mark twenty-five years with silver jubilee celebrations; in wedding-anniversary tradition, the 25th anniversary is the "silver anniversary," for which silver gifts are customary.
