Sorbonne

Sorbonne
Location: Latin Quarter, 5th arrondissement, Paris, France
Founded: 1253 (Collège de Sorbonne)
Founder: Robert de Sorbon
Present occupants: Several Paris universities and the Paris rectorate

The Sorbonne is a historic building in the Latin Quarter of Paris that gave its name to one of the world's most famous universities. The name derives from the Collège de Sorbonne, founded in 1253 by Robert de Sorbon, chaplain and confessor to King Louis IX, as a theological college for poor students at the University of Paris.

History

The college quickly became the centre of the university's faculty of theology, and "the Sorbonne" became synonymous with the University of Paris itself. Cardinal Richelieu, a proviseur of the college, rebuilt it in the 17th century and is buried in its domed chapel. Closed during the Revolution, the institution was revived under Napoleon and reconstructed in grand style in the late 19th century under the Third Republic.

The modern Sorbonne

After the student unrest of May 1968, the University of Paris was split into thirteen autonomous universities (1970), several of which retain "Sorbonne" in their names, including Sorbonne University, Panthéon-Sorbonne (Paris 1) and Sorbonne Nouvelle (Paris 3). The historic building on the Rue des Écoles houses university administration, lecture halls including the Grand Amphithéâtre, and the Paris academy's rectorate, remaining a symbol of French intellectual life.
