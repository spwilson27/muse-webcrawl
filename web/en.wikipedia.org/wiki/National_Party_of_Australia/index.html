The National Party of Australia, commonly known as The Nationals or The Nats, is an Australian political party traditionally representing graziers, farmers, and rural voters. Founded in 1920 as the Australian Country Party, it adopted the name National Country Party in 1975 and National Party in 1982. Since the 1940s it has generally governed in a durable coalition with the Liberal Party of Australia; by convention the Nationals' leader serves as Deputy Prime Minister when the Coalition is in government.

History

The party emerged from farmers' movements angered by the policies of the major parties, winning the balance of power at the 1919 federal election and entering government with the Nationalists in 1923. Its longest-serving and most influential leader, John McEwen (1958–1971), used the trade portfolio to protect Australian agriculture and manufacturing. In Queensland and Western Australia the party was at times the senior coalition partner; Queensland premier Joh Bjelke-Petersen dominated that state from 1968 to 1987.

Profile and policies

The Nationals draw their vote almost entirely from regional and rural electorates outside the major cities. They advocate decentralisation, infrastructure spending in the regions, support for agriculture and mining, and socially conservative positions. Although their share of the vote has declined with Australia's urbanisation, careful preference management and the coalition arrangement have kept them a persistent presence in federal parliaments.
