Home computer

Home computers were a class of microcomputers that entered the market in 1977 and became common during the 1980s. They were marketed to consumers as affordable, useful machines for home productivity, education and video games, typically connecting to a television as display.

History

The "1977 trinity" — Apple II, Commodore PET and TRS-80 — launched the category. In the 1980s machines such as the ZX Spectrum, Commodore 64, BBC Micro, Amstrad CPC and later the Amiga and Atari ST spread computing into homes, with software distributed on cassette and floppy disk.

Decline

By the early 1990s standardised IBM PC compatibles and games consoles displaced the distinct home computer category, merging it into the general personal computer market.
