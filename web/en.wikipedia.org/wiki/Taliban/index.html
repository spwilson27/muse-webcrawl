Taliban

The Taliban (Pashto: طالبان, ṭālibān, "students" or "seekers") is a militant Islamic fundamentalist movement that governs Afghanistan under the name of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan. The group emerged in 1994 from the Afghan mujahideen who had fought the Soviet–Afghan War, taking control of most of the country by 1996 and ruling until the United States-led invasion in 2001. After a twenty-year insurgency, the Taliban retook Kabul on 15 August 2021 following the withdrawal of US forces, and re-established their government.

History

The Taliban were founded in Kandahar in 1994 by Mullah Mohammed Omar, drawing students from Afghan refugee madrassas in Pakistan. With Pakistani support they captured Kabul in September 1996 and imposed a strict interpretation of Sharia, severely restricting women's education and public life. Only Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE recognized their Islamic Emirate.

After the September 11 attacks, the Taliban's refusal to hand over Osama bin Laden led to the US-led invasion of October 2001, which drove them from power. The Taliban regrouped as an insurgency; the February 2020 Doha Agreement set the US withdrawal, and in summer 2021 the movement seized the country in a rapid offensive.

Government and policies

Since 2021 the Taliban have reimposed many earlier restrictions, including bans on girls' secondary education and most women's employment, drawing international condemnation; no UN member state formally recognizes the Islamic Emirate, though several countries maintain working relations. The regime faces resistance from the Islamic State – Khorasan Province and inherited a severe humanitarian and economic crisis.
