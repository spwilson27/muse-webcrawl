Rensis Likert

BornAugust 5, 1903, Cheyenne, Wyoming, U.S.
DiedSeptember 3, 1981 (aged 78), Ann Arbor, Michigan, U.S.
Alma materUniversity of Michigan; Columbia University
Known forLikert scale, participative management
FieldsSocial psychology, organizational psychology

Rensis Likert (August 5, 1903 – September 3, 1981) was an American social and organizational psychologist best known for developing the Likert scale, a widely used approach to measuring attitudes in survey research. He founded the Institute for Social Research at the University of Michigan and directed its Survey Research Center.

Career

Likert earned his PhD in psychology from Columbia University in 1932. He worked on survey research for the US Department of Agriculture during the Second World War and moved to the University of Michigan in 1946, where he remained until retiring in 1970.

Contributions

His 1932 paper introduced the Likert scale of agree–disagree response options. In management research, his books New Patterns of Management (1961) and The Human Organization (1967) described four systems of management, from exploitative-authoritative (System 1) to participative (System 4), arguing that participative management produced better performance and satisfaction.
