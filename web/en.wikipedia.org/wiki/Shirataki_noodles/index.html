Shirataki noodles

Shirataki (Japanese: しらたき or 白滝, "white waterfall") are translucent, gelatinous Japanese noodles made from the corm of the konjac plant (Amorphophallus konjac). Composed mostly of water and the soluble fibre glucomannan, with almost no digestible carbohydrate or calories, they are popular in low-carbohydrate and weight-loss diets and are sometimes marketed in the West as "miracle noodles."

Production and forms

Konjac flour is mixed with water and limewater to form a gel (konnyaku), which is extruded into noodle strands. Traditional shirataki are thin and white-ish, while ito konnyaku are thinner and darker. Modern products are sold packed in liquid and benefit from rinsing and dry-frying to remove their characteristic odour and improve texture.

Culinary use

In Japan, shirataki are used in hot-pot dishes such as sukiyaki and oden, where they absorb the flavour of the broth. Because they are nearly flavourless and chewy, they take on the taste of accompanying sauces and ingredients. A related product, tofu shirataki, blends in tofu for a softer texture and slightly higher protein.

Nutrition and regulation

Glucomannan fibre can promote satiety and moderate post-meal blood-sugar rises; the European Food Safety Authority has approved certain health claims for glucomannan in the context of energy-restricted diets. Whole konjac jelly confectionery has been restricted in some countries because of choking risk, a concern distinct from the noodles.
