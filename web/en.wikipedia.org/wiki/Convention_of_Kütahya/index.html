Convention of Kütahya

The Ottoman–Egyptian settlement of 1833

The Convention of Kütahya, also known as the Peace of Kütahya, was a convention signed in May 1833 between the Ottoman Empire and Muhammad Ali of Egypt, ending the First Egyptian–Ottoman War (1831–1833). It was concluded at Kütahya in Anatolia, where the Egyptian army under Ibrahim Pasha had halted its advance after defeating Ottoman forces at the Battle of Konya in December 1832.

Terms

Under the convention, Muhammad Ali was confirmed as governor of Egypt and additionally granted control of Syria, including Damascus and Aleppo, while his son Ibrahim Pasha received the governorship of Jeddah and the district of Adana in Anatolia as a tax farm. In return, Egyptian forces withdrew from deeper Anatolia and Muhammad Ali nominally acknowledged the suzerainty of Sultan Mahmud II.

Aftermath

Neither side regarded the settlement as final. The Ottoman Empire sought to recover Syria, while Muhammad Ali pressed for hereditary rule and eventual independence. When the Ottomans invaded Syria in 1839 they were defeated at the Battle of Nezib, precipitating the Second Egyptian–Ottoman War and a crisis among the European powers resolved by the Convention of London (1840), which stripped Muhammad Ali of Syria but confirmed hereditary rule in Egypt for his family.
