Fort Worth, Texas

Fort Worth is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the seat of Tarrant County, covering nearly 350 square miles (910 km²) into Denton, Johnson, Parker, and Wise counties in the U.S. state of Texas. According to the 2020 census, Fort Worth's population was 918,915, and it has since approached or surpassed one million residents, making it among the most populous cities in the United States and one of the fastest-growing large cities in the country. Fort Worth is the second-largest city in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, after Dallas, and part of the fourth-largest metropolitan statistical area in the United States.

Fort Worth is known for its western heritage — nicknamed "Cowtown" and "Panther City" — and for its Stockyards National Historic District, as well as major cultural institutions in its Cultural District, including the Kimbell Art Museum, the Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth and the Amon Carter Museum of American Art.

History

The city began in June 1849 as an army outpost on a bluff overlooking the Trinity River, one of a line of forts established after the Mexican–American War to protect settlers; it was named for Major General William Jenkins Worth. The fort was abandoned in 1853, but settlers occupied the site and the town became the Tarrant County seat in 1860. Fort Worth boomed as a stop on the Chisholm Trail cattle drives and, after the Texas and Pacific Railway arrived in 1876, as a major cattle-shipping and later meatpacking centre — the Stockyards hosted Armour and Swift plants from 1902. Oil, aviation and defence industries (notably the plant that built B-24 bombers and today builds F-35 fighters) drove twentieth-century growth.

Geography and climate

Fort Worth sits in North Texas at the eastern edge of the Cross Timbers, where the rolling prairie meets woodland. Its climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers, mild winters and a spring severe-storm season. The Trinity River flows through the city, with an extensive trail and park system along its forks.

Economy and culture

Major employers include American Airlines (headquartered in Fort Worth), Lockheed Martin Aeronautics, Bell Textron, BNSF Railway and Texas Health Resources. The Cultural District, west of downtown, holds the Kimbell Art Museum (whose building by Louis Kahn is an architectural landmark), the Modern Art Museum (Tadao Ando), the Amon Carter Museum, the Fort Worth Museum of Science and History and the National Cowgirl Museum. The Stockyards preserve the cattle heritage with twice-daily longhorn cattle drives; the city also hosts the Southwestern Exposition and Livestock Show (the Fort Worth Stock Show and Rodeo) and Texas Christian University (TCU).
