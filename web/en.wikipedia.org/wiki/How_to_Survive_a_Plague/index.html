How to Survive a Plague is a 2012 American documentary film directed by journalist David France. The film chronicles the early years of the AIDS epidemic in the United States and the work of the activist groups ACT UP and TAG (Treatment Action Group), whose members — many of them HIV-positive and facing death — taught themselves immunology and pharmacology, pressured government agencies and pharmaceutical companies, and helped force the development and approval of effective antiretroviral drugs.

Content

Built largely from archival videotape shot by activists themselves, the film follows figures including Peter Staley, Mark Harrington, Spencer Cox and Larry Kramer from the founding of ACT UP in New York in 1987 through protests at the Food and Drug Administration and the National Institutes of Health, the internal split that produced TAG, and the mid-1990s arrival of protease inhibitors, which transformed HIV infection from a death sentence into a manageable condition for those with access to treatment.

Reception

The film premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in 2012 and was widely praised by critics. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature and won awards including the New York Film Critics Circle award for best first film. France later expanded the story into a 2016 book of the same name.
