Boris Andreevich Uspensky (born 1 March 1937) is a Russian semiotician, linguist, and cultural historian. He is one of the leading figures of the Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School, alongside Yuri Lotman, with whom he collaborated extensively on the semiotics of Russian culture.

Career

Uspensky graduated from Moscow State University, where he later taught in the Faculty of Philology. He worked closely with the semiotics circle centered at the University of Tartu in Estonia, contributing to the journal Sign Systems Studies. After the collapse of the Soviet Union he held positions at several universities abroad, including in Italy and Austria.

Work

Uspensky's research spans the structural analysis of art, the semiotics of icons, and the cultural history of Russia. His book The Semiotics of the Russian Icon examined how medieval icon painting encodes meaning through compositional structures, and his A Poetics of Composition (1970) applied structuralist methods to narrative point of view. With Lotman he wrote influential essays on the role of dual models in the dynamics of Russian culture and on the binary structures underlying Russian historical self-understanding.

Themes

A recurring theme in Uspensky's work is the concept of point of view — in literature, painting, and historical writing — and the idea that cultures can be studied as sign systems whose members model the world in characteristic ways.
