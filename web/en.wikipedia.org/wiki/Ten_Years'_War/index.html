Ten Years' War
Date10 October 1868 – 1878
LocationCuba
ResultSpanish victory; Pact of Zanjón

The Ten Years' War (Spanish: Guerra de los Diez Años), also known in Cuba as the Great War (Guerra Grande), was the first of three wars that Cuba fought against Spain for its independence. It began on 10 October 1868 with the Grito de Yara, when sugar mill owner Carlos Manuel de Céspedes freed his slaves and declared Cuban independence, and ended a decade later with the Pact of Zanjón.

Cuban independence fighters (mambises)

Course of the war

The uprising began in eastern Cuba (Oriente), where Céspedes and fellow landowners raised an army of creoles, free people of colour and freed slaves. Leadership passed to generals such as Máximo Gómez and Antonio Maceo, who perfected guerrilla warfare and the machete charge in the island's countryside. The rebels proclaimed a republic at Guáimaro in 1869 and abolished slavery in their territories, but internal divisions, regionalism and a lack of foreign support weakened the cause. Spain deployed some 200,000 troops over the course of the war and waged a brutal counterinsurgency.

Pact of Zanjón

Exhausted, most rebel forces accepted the Pact of Zanjón on 10 February 1878, which promised reforms, amnesty and the freeing of slaves who had fought, but not independence. Antonio Maceo rejected the settlement in the Protest of Baraguá, though resistance soon collapsed. The war devastated Cuba's economy and cost, by some estimates, more than 200,000 lives, yet it forged the independence movement that resumed in the Little War (1879–80) and the Cuban War of Independence (1895).
