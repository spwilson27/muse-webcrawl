2016 Indian banknote demonetisation

On 8 November 2016, the Government of India announced the demonetisation of all ₹500 and ₹1,000 banknotes of the Mahatma Gandhi Series. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the move in a televised address, stating that the notes would cease to be legal tender from midnight, in an effort to combat black money, corruption, counterfeit currency and terror financing. The affected notes represented about 86% of India's cash in circulation by value.

Implementation

Holders could deposit or exchange the old notes at banks until 30 December 2016, subject to limits and identification requirements, while strict caps on ATM and counter withdrawals created long queues across the country. New ₹500 and ₹2,000 notes were introduced, with the Reserve Bank of India managing a vast remonetisation exercise. Digital payment platforms reported sharp growth during the cash shortage.

Reception and impact

The move was praised by supporters as a decisive strike against unaccounted wealth, but criticised by opposition parties and many economists, including former prime minister Manmohan Singh, who called it "organised loot and legalised plunder" in Parliament. Cash-dependent sectors such as agriculture, small retail and construction were severely disrupted, and India's GDP growth slowed in the following quarters. The RBI eventually reported that 99.3% of the demonetised currency had been returned to the banking system, prompting debate over whether the measure had achieved its stated goal of extinguishing black money; the government pointed instead to a widened tax base and increased digital transactions as longer-term gains.
