Musical work
Related conceptsComposition, performance, recording, score

In music philosophy and copyright law, a musical work is an abstract musical composition — a piece of music considered as a type that can be instantiated in multiple performances, scores, and recordings. The concept distinguishes the work itself from any single physical copy or performance of it.

Ontology

Philosophers debate whether musical works are discovered or created, whether they are eternal abstract objects (Platonism) or historically situated constructs, and how much variation in performance is compatible with a performance being "of" a given work.

Legal treatment

Copyright law typically protects the musical work (melody, harmony, and lyrics as composed) separately from sound recordings of it, giving rise to distinct publishing and master rights administered by collecting societies such as ASCAP and PRS.
