Fife

Fife (Scottish Gaelic: Fìobha) is a council area and historic county of Scotland, situated between the Firth of Tay and the Firth of Forth, with inland boundaries with Perth and Kinross and Clackmannanshire. It is known as the "Kingdom of Fife" from its history as one of the major Pictish kingdoms and its later status as a royal heartland. The population is around 370,000, making it Scotland's third-most-populous council area.

Geography and towns

The largest towns include Dunfermline (a former royal burgh and Scotland's newest city), Kirkcaldy, Glenrothes (the administrative centre) and St Andrews, home of the University of St Andrews — Scotland's oldest university — and of the Royal and Ancient Golf Club, the historic home of golf. The coastal path and fishing villages of the East Neuk, such as Anstruther, Crail and Pittenweem, are notable for tourism.

History

Fife was a centre of royal power in medieval Scotland; Dunfermline was a royal residence and burial place of monarchs including Robert the Bruce. In the industrial era, coal mining, linen and later electronics shaped the region. Today the economy mixes public services, education, tourism, food and drink, and manufacturing.
