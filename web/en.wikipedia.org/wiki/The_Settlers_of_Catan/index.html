The Settlers of Catan

[Hero image placeholder: the hexagonal board of Catan with settlements, roads and resource cards.]

The Settlers of Catan, now marketed simply as Catan, is a multiplayer board game designed by Klaus Teuber. It was first published in 1995 in Germany by Kosmos as Die Siedler von Catan. It is considered one of the first German-style board games to achieve popularity outside Europe and is credited with popularising the modern board game renaissance.

Gameplay

Players take on the roles of settlers establishing settlements on the island of Catan. The island is made of hexagonal tiles representing resources: wood, brick, wheat, sheep and ore. On each turn, dice are rolled to determine which tiles produce resources; players collect those resources if they have settlements adjacent to the producing tiles. Resources are spent to build roads, settlements and cities, buy development cards, and trade with other players or the bank. The first player to reach ten victory points wins.

Reception

The game won the Spiel des Jahres in 1995 and has sold tens of millions of copies worldwide, being translated into dozens of languages. Numerous expansions and extensions exist, including Seafarers, Cities & Knights, Traders & Barbarians and Explorers & Pirates, as well as digital adaptations.
