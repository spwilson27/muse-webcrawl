Mostar is a city in southern Bosnia and Herzegovina, the most populous city of the Herzegovina region and the administrative centre of the Herzegovina-Neretva Canton. Straddling the deep valley of the Neretva River, it is celebrated for the Stari Most (Old Bridge), a reconstructed 16th-century Ottoman arch bridge whose name — from mostari, the bridge's keepers — the city bears. Mostar is the country's fifth-largest city and one of its principal tourist destinations.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times, and the medieval settlement grew around a crossing of the Neretva. Mostar developed under Ottoman rule after the 15th century into an important administrative and trading town. Its emblem, the Stari Most, was commissioned by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and completed in 1566–1567 to a design of the architect Mimar Hayruddin, a student of the great Sinan. The single-span stone arch, 4 metres wide and 30 metres long, spanned the river 24 metres above it and was regarded as a masterpiece of Islamic architecture in the Balkans. Around it grew the old bazaar quarter, Kujundžiluk, with its mosques, hammams, and craftsmen's shops.

Bosnian War and reconstruction

During the Bosnian War (1992–1995), Mostar was devastated by two conflicts: the siege by Bosnian Serb forces in 1992, and the Croat–Bosniak war of 1993–1994, during which the city was effectively divided between Croat-held west and Bosniak-held east. On November 9, 1993, Croat forces shelled the Stari Most until it collapsed into the Neretva. After the war the city remained divided along ethnic lines, administered for a decade under an EU envoy and a special statute, and unified administratively only in 2004. That same year, the reconstructed Old Bridge — rebuilt with original techniques and stone from the same quarry, with blocks recovered from the river — was ceremonially reopened. In 2005 the Old Bridge area was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a symbol of reconciliation and coexistence; the traditional diving competition from the bridge was added to UNESCO's intangible heritage list in 2021-era recognitions of the practice. Divers have leapt from the bridge for centuries, and the annual diving competition remains a hallmark of the city.

Geography and climate

Mostar lies in a broad valley of the Neretva surrounded by karst mountains. It has a Mediterranean-influenced climate with very hot summers — it is among the hottest cities in Europe, with temperatures regularly exceeding 40 °C — and mild, wet winters.

Economy and transport

Historically the city hosted the SOKO aircraft factory and the Aluminij aluminium works, one of Bosnia's largest exporters. Today tourism centred on the Old Town is a major economic driver. Mostar is served by Mostar International Airport, a railway line from Sarajevo to the Adriatic coast, and the A1 motorway under development along Corridor Vc.
