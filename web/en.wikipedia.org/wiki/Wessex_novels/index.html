Wessex novels

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Wessex novels are a group of novels by Thomas Hardy (1840-1928) set in a fictional region of south-west England that Hardy named "Wessex", after the medieval Anglo-Saxon kingdom. Hardy gave real places fictional names — Dorchester became "Casterbridge", Salisbury "Melchester", and so on — and in later editions he revised the novels to make their geography more consistent, publishing maps of the region.

The novels

The principal Wessex novels include Under the Greenwood Tree (1872), Far from the Madding Crowd (1874), The Return of the Native (1878), The Mayor of Casterbridge (1886), The Woodlanders (1887), Tess of the d'Urbervilles (1891) and Jude the Obscure (1895). Hardy himself classified them as "Novels of Character and Environment", "Romances and Fantasies", and "Novels of Ingenuity".

The setting

Wessex corresponds roughly to Dorset (Hardy's "South Wessex") together with parts of Wiltshire, Somerset, Devon, Hampshire and Oxfordshire. The landscape — especially the heathland of Egdon Heath and the chalk downlands — functions almost as a character in the novels, embodying the indifference of nature and the pressure of a vanishing rural world against encroaching modernity.

Legacy

"Hardy Country" tourism in Dorset trades on the association, and Hardy's use of a consistent fictional county anticipated later literary worlds such as Faulkner's Yoknapatawpha County.
