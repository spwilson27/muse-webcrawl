The Red Violin (French: Le Violon rouge) is a 1998 drama film directed by François Girard and starring Samuel L. Jackson, Carlo Cecchi and Sylvia Chang. It spans four centuries and five countries as it tells the story of a mysterious scarlet-coloured violin and its many owners. The film's score, composed by John Corigliano and performed by violinist Joshua Bell, won the Academy Award for Best Original Score.

Plot

In Cremona, Italy, in 1681, master violin maker Nicolò Bussotti creates a perfect violin for his unborn child. His wife Anna, pregnant, receives a tarot reading foretelling a long journey; she dies in childbirth, and Bussotti, grief-stricken, varnishes the violin with her blood — giving it its distinctive red colour.

The instrument passes through an orphanage in 18th-century Vienna, where the prodigy Kaspar Weiss performs with it before dying of a heart condition; to the English virtuoso Frederick Pope in Oxford; to China, where during the Cultural Revolution the violin is nearly destroyed as a symbol of Western decadence; and finally to Montreal, where appraiser Charles Morritz (Samuel L. Jackson) identifies it at auction and secretly substitutes a copy, keeping the original for his daughter.

Production

The film was shot in Canada, Italy, Austria, England and China, with dialogue in five languages. The tarot readings that open each segment frame the violin's journey and mirror Anna's prophesied life. The instrument itself was inspired by the Red Mendelssohn Stradivarius.

Reception

The Red Violin was a critical and modest commercial success, praised for its cinematography, structure and Corigliano's score. It won eight Genie Awards, including Best Motion Picture.
