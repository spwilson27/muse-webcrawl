The HiSET (High School Equivalency Test) is a high school equivalency examination in the United States. Passing the HiSET earns a candidate a state-issued high school equivalency credential, recognised by employers and colleges as equivalent to a high school diploma. It is one of three tests U.S. states use for this purpose, alongside the GED and the TASC (the latter discontinued in 2021).

History

The HiSET was introduced in 2014 by the Educational Testing Service (ETS) in partnership with the University of Iowa's Iowa Testing Programs, after several states sought alternatives following changes to the GED test that year, including its move to computer-only delivery and a price increase. ETS later transferred the programme to PSI Services. The test is offered in English and Spanish, in both computer-based and paper-based formats.

Structure

The exam comprises five subtests: Language Arts – Reading, Language Arts – Writing (including an essay), Mathematics, Science, and Social Studies. Each subtest is scored on a scale, with a minimum score required on each and a minimum combined total to pass; higher scores can earn "College and Career Ready" or honours designations. Eligibility rules, minimum ages, and residency requirements vary by state.
