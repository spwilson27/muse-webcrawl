Olivia Newton-John

Born: 26 September 1948, Cambridge, England

Died: 8 August 2022 (aged 73), Santa Ynez, California, US

Occupations: Singer, songwriter, actress, activist

Years active: 1963–2022

Dame Olivia Newton-John AC DBE (26 September 1948 – 8 August 2022) was a British-Australian singer, songwriter, actress, entrepreneur and activist. With global sales of more than 100 million records, she was one of the best-selling music artists of all time.

Early life

Newton-John was born in Cambridge, England, to Brinley "Bryn" Newton-John, a Welsh master at King's College School, and Irene Born, daughter of the German Nobel laureate physicist Max Born. The family emigrated to Melbourne, Australia, in 1954, where her father became master of Ormond College at the University of Melbourne. She formed a girl group, Sol Four, as a teenager and won a talent contest that took her back to Britain.

Music career

Her early 1970s hits, including "If Not for You" and "Let Me Be There", brought her four Grammy Awards and major success on the American country and adult contemporary charts. "I Honestly Love You" reached number one in 1974, and "Have You Never Been Mellow" repeated the feat.

Grease and Physical

In 1978 she starred opposite John Travolta in the film musical Grease, one of the highest-grossing musical films ever; its soundtrack produced the hits "You're the One That I Want", "Hopelessly Devoted to You" and "Summer Nights". Her 1981 album Physical, whose title track spent ten weeks atop the Billboard Hot 100, marked a provocative new image and was her biggest-selling studio record.

Activism and later life

First diagnosed with breast cancer in 1992, Newton-John became a prominent advocate for cancer research and founded the Olivia Newton-John Cancer Wellness & Research Centre in Melbourne. She also campaigned for animal rights and environmental causes. She died at her ranch in California in 2022 after the cancer recurred.

Legacy

Newton-John was appointed a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2020 and a Companion of the Order of Australia in 2006.
