Clarence Leonard "Kelly" Johnson (February 27, 1910 – December 21, 1990) was an American aeronautical and systems engineer. He is credited with founding Lockheed's Advanced Development Projects division, universally known as the Skunk Works, and with leading or contributing to the design of more than forty aircraft, including the P-38 Lightning, the Constellation airliner, the F-104 Starfighter, the U-2 spyplane and the SR-71 Blackbird.

Early life and career

Johnson was born in Ishpeming, Michigan, to Swedish immigrant parents. He studied aeronautical engineering at the University of Michigan. Joining Lockheed in 1933, he quickly rose to chief research engineer, famously correcting the tail design of the Model 10 Electra in the university wind tunnel. His twin-boom P-38 Lightning of 1939 was one of the most successful American fighters of the Second World War.

The Skunk Works

In 1943 Johnson's small, semi-independent team built the XP-80, America's first operational jet fighter, in only 143 days, working in a makeshift facility beside a plastics factory whose smell inspired the "Skunk Works" nickname, borrowed from the Li'l Abner comic strip. The unit went on to produce the U-2 (1955), designed for high-altitude reconnaissance, and the SR-71 Blackbird (1964), still the fastest air-breathing manned aircraft ever flown. Johnson codified his management philosophy in "14 rules" emphasising small teams, trust, simplicity and minimal bureaucracy.

Honours

Johnson received numerous awards, including two Collier Trophies, the National Medal of Science and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (1964). He was inducted into the National Aviation Hall of Fame in 1974. He retired from Lockheed in 1975, having served as a senior vice president.
