Teshub

Teshub (also Teshup, Teššub) was the Hurrian god of sky and storm, king of the Hurrian pantheon. Adopted into Hittite religion, he headed the state cult of the Hittite Empire, where his Hattic counterpart was Taru and his name written with the Sumerogram dU / dIM.

Mythology

Teshub's consort was Hepat, and his sons included Sharruma. His sacred animal was the bull; two bulls, Seri and Hurri ("Day" and "Night"), drew his chariot. He rides in a chariot wielding a triple lightning bolt and a mace or axe. In the Hurro-Hittite Kumarbi cycle (Kingship in Heaven), Teshub succeeds Kumarbi as king of the gods and battles Kumarbi's monstrous champions — the stone giant Ullikummi among them (Song of Ullikummi) — to keep his throne.

Cult and parallels

His main sanctuary was at Kumme; he was worshipped widely across Anatolia and north Syria, notably at Aleppo and Sam'al. Teshub belongs to the family of ancient Near Eastern storm gods including Hadad/Baal, Adad, and (in later Anatolia) Zeus Dolichenus.
