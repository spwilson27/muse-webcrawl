Beren is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, a hero of the First Age of Middle-earth and, with the elf-maiden Lúthien, the central figure of the "Tale of Beren and Lúthien", which Tolkien regarded as the heart of his mythology. Their story — the quest for a Silmaril — is told in The Silmarillion and published in full form in the 2017 volume Beren and Lúthien, edited by Christopher Tolkien.

The tale

Beren was the son of Barahir of the House of Bëor, who aided the elf-king Finrod Felagund at the Battle of Sudden Flame. When the outlaw band of Barahir was betrayed and slain, Beren survived and waged a lone guerrilla war until, driven into Doriath, he beheld Lúthien, daughter of King Thingol and the Maia Melian, dancing in the woods. Thingol, scornful of the mortal, demanded an impossible bride-price: one of the Silmarils from the Iron Crown of Morgoth. Beren accepted, and with Finrod's aid entered Sauron's isle, where Finrod died killing the werewolf sent to devour him; Lúthien, coming in disguise, defeated Sauron with the great hound Huan and freed Beren.

Disguised, they entered Angband itself; Lúthien sang Morgoth to sleep and Beren cut a Silmaril from the crown, but fleeing, the wolf Carcharoth bit off Beren's hand with the jewel. Both later died — Beren of the wolf's wound after killing Carcharoth in the hunt — and Lúthien sang before Mandos so movingly that the two were restored to life, Lúthien choosing mortality to share Beren's fate. They dwelt on Tol Galen and were ancestors, through their son Dior and granddaughter Elwing, of Elrond and of the kings of Númenor and Gondor.

Significance

Tolkien modelled the tale on his own romance with Edith Bratt; the names "Beren" and "Lúthien" are carved on their shared gravestone in Oxford. The story is the principal link between the Silmarillion tradition and The Lord of the Rings, where Aragorn sings the Lay of Beren and Lúthien and his union with Arwen re-enacts it.
