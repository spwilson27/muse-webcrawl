R. Edward Freeman

R. Edward Freeman
Born: 18 December 1951, Columbus, Georgia, U.S.
Fields: Business ethics, strategic management
Known for: Stakeholder theory
Institution: Darden School of Business, University of Virginia

Robert Edward Freeman (born 18 December 1951) is an American philosopher and professor of business administration at the Darden School of Business at the University of Virginia. He is best known for his work on stakeholder theory and business ethics, most fully developed in his 1984 book Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach.

Career

Freeman studied mathematics and philosophy at Duke University and earned his PhD in philosophy from Washington University in St. Louis in 1978, with a dissertation on the idea of a social responsibility of business. Before joining Darden, he taught at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, where the Stakeholder Project developed, and at the University of Minnesota. At Virginia he has served as Elis and Signe Olsson Professor of Business Administration and as an academic director of the Business Roundtable Institute for Corporate Ethics.

Stakeholder theory

Freeman's central claim is that a business should create value for all its stakeholders — groups and individuals who can affect or are affected by the organisation, including employees, customers, suppliers, communities, financiers, and shareholders — rather than maximising returns to shareholders alone. The 1984 book gave the idea an academic foundation in strategic management, and later works, including Stakeholder Theory: The State of the Art (2010), extended it into ethics, finance, marketing, and public policy. The approach contrasts with the shareholder-primacy doctrine associated with Milton Friedman, and Freeman's work is frequently cited in debates over corporate purpose, including the Business Roundtable's 2019 statement on the purpose of a corporation.
