Luxembourg Gardens

Location6th arrondissement, Paris, France
Area23 hectares (56.8 acres)
Created1612
PatronMarie de' Medici
StatusOpen to the public; grounds of the French Senate

The Luxembourg Gardens (French: Jardin du Luxembourg) are a public park in the 6th arrondissement of Paris, created in 1612 by Marie de' Medici, widow of King Henry IV, as the grounds of the Luxembourg Palace. Covering some 23 hectares, the gardens are among the most beloved green spaces in Paris, known for their lawns, tree-lined promenades, flowerbeds, model sailboats on the Grand Bassin pond, and the Medici Fountain.

History

Marie de' Medici, nostalgic for the Boboli Gardens of her native Florence, commissioned gardens in the Italian style around her new palace. The park was enlarged in later centuries, and its present layout owes much to 19th-century work under the architect Jean-François Chalgrin. During the French Revolution the palace became a prison; in the Second World War the gardens were occupied by German forces. The palace now houses the French Senate, which also administers the gardens.

Features

The gardens contain more than a hundred statues, including a cycle of queens and illustrious women of France, and an orchard of heritage apple and pear varieties. A small-scale model of the Statue of Liberty stands in the park. Visitors rent toy sailboats to push across the octagonal Grand Bassin with wooden sticks, a tradition dating to the 1920s. The park also hosts tennis courts, pétanque, beehives tended by a beekeeping school, and the Musée du Luxembourg exhibition space.
