Étienne Tshisekedi

Étienne Tshisekedi wa Mulumba (14 December 1932 – 1 February 2017) was a Congolese politician and the leader of the Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS), the main opposition party in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). He served as Prime Minister of Zaire (the former name of the DRC) three times—briefly in 1991, again from 1992 to 1993, and finally for a few days in 1997.

Career

Tshisekedi was one of the first Congolese to earn a law degree, graduating from Lovanium University in 1961. He held ministerial posts under President Mobutu Sese Seko in the 1960s but broke with the regime, and in 1980 he was one of thirteen parliamentarians who wrote an open letter to Mobutu demanding democratic reform; they were arrested for it. In 1982 he co-founded the UDPS, which, despite being banned, became the principal domestic opposition movement to Mobutu's one-party state.

The Sovereign National Conference made him prime minister in 1992, but Mobutu undermined his government, and he was removed the following year. After Mobutu's fall, Tshisekedi remained the veteran leader of the opposition under Laurent-Désiré Kabila and Joseph Kabila. He boycotted the 2006 elections but ran for president in 2011, officially finishing second to Joseph Kabila in a vote widely criticised by observers. He died in Brussels in 2017 while receiving medical treatment; his body was repatriated in 2019 and given a state funeral. His son, Félix Tshisekedi, became President of the DRC in January 2019.
