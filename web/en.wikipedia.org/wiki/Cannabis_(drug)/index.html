Cannabis (drug)

Cannabis, also known as marijuana among other names, is a psychoactive drug from the cannabis plant. Native to South or Central Asia, the plant has been used as a drug for both recreational and entheogenic purposes, and in various traditional medicines, for centuries. Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the main psychoactive component of cannabis.

Cannabis can be used by smoking, vaporising, within food, or as an extract. Its mental effects include altered senses, altered sense of time, euphoria, changes in mood and body movement, difficulty with problem solving and memory, and increased appetite. Onset of effects is felt within minutes when smoked, and after about 30 to 60 minutes when cooked and eaten.

History

Cannabis has been used since at least the 3rd millennium BCE in Central Asia. It spread along trade routes and was widely criminalised during the 20th century under international treaties such as the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961. Beginning in the 2010s, several jurisdictions — including Canada, Uruguay, and a number of U.S. states — legalised recreational use, while many more permit medical use.

Health effects

Short-term adverse effects may include anxiety, panic, impaired memory, and psychomotor impairment; the drug impairs driving ability. Between 9% and 17% of users may develop cannabis use disorder with dependence. Long-term heavy use, particularly beginning in adolescence, is associated with adverse cognitive and mental-health outcomes, and cannabis use is a risk factor for psychosis in susceptible individuals. Medicinal preparations are used for conditions such as chemotherapy-induced nausea and certain forms of epilepsy; cannabidiol (CBD) is the active ingredient of the approved drug Epidiolex.

Forms

The drug is consumed as dried flower (marijuana), as hashish (compressed resin), as hash oil, and in edible products. THC content in modern cultivated cannabis is considerably higher than in the past.
