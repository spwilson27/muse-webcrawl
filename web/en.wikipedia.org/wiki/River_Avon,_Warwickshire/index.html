The River Avon in central England flows generally southwestwards from its source in Northamptonshire to its confluence with the River Severn at Tewkesbury in Gloucestershire. It is 85 miles (137 km) long. It is also known as the Warwickshire Avon or Shakespeare's Avon, to distinguish it from several other English rivers of the same name. "Avon" derives from the Welsh afon, simply meaning "river".

Course

The Avon rises near Naseby in Northamptonshire, close to the sources of several other rivers. It flows west through Welford and past Stanford Hall, skirts the town of Rugby, and continues southwest past Coventry's outskirts at Wolston. It passes the ruins of Kenilworth Castle's mere, runs through Warwick—beneath the walls of Warwick Castle—then charms its way through Stratford-upon-Avon, birthplace of William Shakespeare, where it is crossed by the medieval Clopton Bridge and lined by the Royal Shakespeare Theatre and Holy Trinity Church. Downstream it passes Bidford-on-Avon, Evesham, and Pershore in the Vale of Evesham, a fruit-growing district famous for its plum orchards and market gardens, before joining the Severn at Tewkesbury.

Tributaries

Major tributaries include the River Leam (joining near Warwick), the River Sowe (near Stoneleigh), the River Arrow, the River Isbourne, and the River Swift. The River Stour of Warwickshire joins the Avon south of Stratford.

Navigation

The Avon is navigable from Tewkesbury to Stratford-upon-Avon, a distance of about 43 miles. The navigation was created in the 17th century, with major works by William Sandys in the 1630s and later improvements. It divides informally into the Lower Avon (Tewkesbury to Evesham), managed by the Avon Navigation Trust, and the Upper Avon (Evesham to Stratford). A long-standing ambition has been to extend navigation above Stratford to link with the Grand Union Canal near Warwick or Leamington Spa, which would create a cruising ring — the so-called Avon Ring extension — though this remains unbuilt.

History and flooding

The river powered mills along its length, including those at Warwick and Evesham Abbey. Its wide, flat valley makes it prone to flooding; severe floods affected Evesham and the Vale in July 2007, and flood-defence schemes have since been built at several settlements. In July Shakespeare's home town marks the river with rowing and the annual Stratford River Festival.
