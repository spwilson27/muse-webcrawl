François de La Rocque

Born6 October 1885, Lorient, France
Died28 April 1946, Paris, France
Known forLeader of the Croix-de-Feu and French Social Party

François de La Rocque (6 October 1885 – 28 April 1946) was a French soldier and political figure who led the Croix-de-Feu, a league of veterans that became one of the largest nationalist movements of the interwar Third Republic.

Political career

Taking over the Croix-de-Feu in 1931, La Rocque transformed it into a mass movement. After the riots of 6 February 1934, his refusal to march on parliament drew criticism from more radical nationalists. When the Popular Front dissolved the leagues in 1936, he founded the French Social Party (PSF), which attracted perhaps a million members before the war.

War and death

During the Second World War La Rocque first supported Marshal Pétain but later passed intelligence to the Allies; he was deported to Germany in 1943. He returned to France in 1945 under house arrest and died the following year, his reputation the subject of lasting historiographical debate.
