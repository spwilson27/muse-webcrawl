Satellite altimeter

A satellite altimeter is a radar instrument carried aboard an Earth-observing satellite that measures the height of the satellite above the surface below by timing how long a microwave pulse takes to travel to the surface and back. Over the oceans, combined with precise orbit determination, this yields sea surface height with an accuracy of a few centimetres.

Principle

The altimeter transmits short radar pulses (typically in the Ku band) straight down and measures the return time and the shape of the reflected waveform. Corrections are applied for atmospheric water vapour (using a microwave radiometer), ionospheric delay (using dual-frequency measurements), tides, and the sea state. Subtracting the measured range from the satellite's altitude above a reference ellipsoid gives sea surface height; variations relative to the marine geoid reveal ocean currents, eddies, and sea level change.

Missions and applications

Landmark missions include Seasat (1978), Geosat, ERS-1 and ERS-2, TOPEX/Poseidon (1992), the Jason series, Envisat, CryoSat-2 (optimised for ice), and Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich. Altimetry provides the primary record of global mean sea level rise — about 3.7 mm per year in recent decades — and data for oceanography, geodesy, ice-sheet monitoring, and measurement of lake and river levels.
