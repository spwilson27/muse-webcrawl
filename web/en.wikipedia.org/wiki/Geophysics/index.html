Geophysics

Geophysics is a subject of natural science concerned with the physical processes and physical properties of the Earth and its surrounding space environment, and the use of quantitative methods for their analysis. Geophysicists study phenomena including the Earth's gravitational and magnetic fields, seismic waves, interior structure, plate tectonics, volcanism, the dynamics of the oceans and atmosphere, and the exploration of subsurface resources.

Branches

Major branches include seismology (the study of earthquakes and elastic waves, which reveals the layered structure of the Earth's interior), geodesy (the Earth's shape, gravity field and rotation), geomagnetism and paleomagnetism, geodynamics, mineral physics, and exploration geophysics. Related fields apply the same methods to other planets (planetary geophysics) and to the near-surface for engineering and environmental purposes.

Methods

Geophysical methods are largely remote and non-invasive: seismometers record natural and artificial seismic waves; gravimeters and magnetometers map anomalies in gravity and magnetic fields; electrical resistivity, ground-penetrating radar and induced polarisation probe the shallow subsurface; and satellite missions such as GRACE and GOCE measure the gravity field from orbit. In resource industries, seismic reflection surveys are the principal tool for locating petroleum, gas and mineral deposits.

History

Although compasses and earthquake observations are ancient, geophysics emerged as a distinct discipline in the 19th century with the systematic measurement of geomagnetism (associated with Alexander von Humboldt and Carl Friedrich Gauss) and took modern form in the 20th century, when seismic evidence of the Earth's core, and paleomagnetic support for continental drift, helped establish plate tectonics in the 1960s. Major professional organisations include the American Geophysical Union and the European Geosciences Union.
