The Saane (French: Sarine) is a river in Switzerland, about 126 kilometres long. It rises near the Sanetsch Pass in the Bernese Alps and flows north through the cantons of Valais, Bern and Fribourg before joining the Aare below Lake Wohlen.

The Saane winding through the old town of Fribourg.

Course

The river's upper valley, the Saanenland, includes the resort towns of Gstaad and Saanen. Further downstream the Saane is dammed to form Lake Gruyère, and it then passes through deep limestone gorges before reaching the city of Fribourg, whose medieval old town occupies a rocky promontory in a meander of the river. North of Fribourg the river is again impounded at Lake Schiffenen before joining the Aare.

History and significance

The Saane/Sarine marks part of the so-called Röstigraben, the language border between French-speaking and German-speaking Switzerland: the river's German name is used upstream and in the canton of Bern, while the French name Sarine predominates in Fribourg. The bridges of Fribourg, from the medieval wooden Saint-Jean bridge to the modern Poya Bridge (opened 2014), are landmarks of the river's course.
