Murano glass

Murano glass is glassware made on the Venetian island of Murano, which has been a centre of luxury glassmaking for centuries. In 1291 the Venetian Republic ordered glassmakers to move their furnaces to Murano, ostensibly to reduce fire risk in Venice, and the craft became tightly guarded.

Techniques

Murano masters developed techniques including cristallo (clear glass), lattimo (milk glass), millefiori (thousand flowers), enamelled glass, gold-flecked aventurine glass, and intricate lampworking and chandeliers. Families and workshops passed secrets between generations.

Modern era

Today Murano glass remains a major export and tourist attraction, with factories, showrooms and the Museo del Vetro (Glass Museum) founded in 1861. Authentic products are protected by the Vetro Artistico Murano trademark.
