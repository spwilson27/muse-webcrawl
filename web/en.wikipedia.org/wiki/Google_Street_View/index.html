Google Street View is a technology featured in Google Maps and Google Earth that provides interactive panoramas from positions along many streets in the world. It was launched in 2007 in several cities in the United States, and has since expanded to include cities and rural areas worldwide. Streets with Street View imagery available are shown as blue lines on Google Maps.

History

Street View was first introduced in the United States on May 25, 2007, initially covering San Francisco, Las Vegas, Denver, Miami, and New York City. The project grew out of Google's interest in building a photographic record of the world's roads; early prototypes used vans loaded with cameras, lasers, and GPS devices. Over the following years coverage expanded across North America, Europe, Asia, Australia, and parts of Africa and South America.

In addition to cars, Google has used trikes, snowmobiles, boats, backpacks (the "Trekker"), and even camels to capture imagery in locations inaccessible to vehicles, including hiking trails, museums, university campuses, and the interiors of landmarks through the Google Arts & Culture program.

Implementation

Imagery is captured by camera systems that record 360-degree panoramic photographs at regular intervals along a route. The images are stitched together and positioned using GPS data so users can virtually "walk" along a street, rotate their view, and zoom. Faces and license plates are automatically blurred to protect privacy.

Users can also contribute their own imagery through connected photospheres and, later, through the Street View app and Photo Path features, extending coverage to roads and paths the official fleet has not visited.

Privacy concerns

Street View has attracted controversy over privacy. Google has faced legal challenges in several countries, and in 2010 it was revealed that Street View vehicles had collected payload data from unencrypted Wi-Fi networks, leading to investigations and fines in multiple jurisdictions. Google subsequently apologized, deleted the data, and discontinued Wi-Fi collection by the fleet.

Coverage

By the 2020s, Street View had captured more than 10 million miles of road imagery across over 100 countries and territories, comprising hundreds of billions of images. Coverage is densest in North America, Europe, Japan, and Australia.
