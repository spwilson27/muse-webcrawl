Edinburgh Festivals

The Edinburgh Festivals are a collection of festivals that take place each year in Edinburgh, Scotland, most of them concentrated in August. Together they form the largest annual celebration of the arts in the world, drawing audiences comparable to those of the FIFA World Cup or the Olympic Games. The city is often described as the world's leading festival city.

The August festivals

The original event is the Edinburgh International Festival, founded in 1947 in the aftermath of the Second World War to promote cultural reconciliation; it presents classical music, opera, theatre and dance by invitation. Alongside it, unofficially and from the same year, independent companies performed on the margins, giving rise to the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, now the world's largest performing-arts festival, with thousands of shows across hundreds of venues.

Other August festivals include the Edinburgh International Book Festival, the Edinburgh Art Festival, the Edinburgh International Film Festival (founded 1947 and long held in August), the Edinburgh International Television Festival, and the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo, performed on the esplanade of Edinburgh Castle. Festivals at other times of year include the Edinburgh Science Festival, the Edinburgh International Children's Festival, and Edinburgh's Hogmanay celebrations.

Impact

The festivals attract millions of visits annually and generate hundreds of millions of pounds for the Scottish economy. They also raise recurring debates about accommodation costs, overtourism and the availability of venues and housing for residents. The festivals were cancelled in their live form in 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, returning in reduced and then full form in following years.
