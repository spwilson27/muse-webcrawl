Omnipotence

Omnipotence is the quality of having unlimited power. Monotheistic religions generally attribute omnipotence only to the deity of their faith. In the monotheistic philosophies of Abrahamic religions, omnipotence is often listed as one of God's characteristics among many, including omniscience, omnipresence, and omnibenevolence.

Definitions

Philosophers distinguish several senses of the term. On the strongest reading, an omnipotent being can do absolutely anything, including the logically impossible. A weaker and widely defended reading, associated with Thomas Aquinas, holds that a being is omnipotent if it can bring about any state of affairs that is logically possible; on this view, "God cannot make a contradiction true" is no limitation on power, because a contradiction describes no possible state of affairs at all.

The omnipotence paradox

The best-known problem is the paradox of the stone: "Could God create a stone so heavy that even He could not lift it?" If yes, there is something He cannot do (lift it); if no, there is something He cannot do (create it). Standard responses argue that the paradox describes a logically impossible task and therefore falls outside omnipotence properly understood, or that the paradox rests on a misunderstanding of what "unlimited power" means. The question of whether an omnipotent God can act contrary to His own nature — for example, to do evil or to lie — has likewise been treated differently by scholastic theologians, Descartes, and modern analytic philosophers.
