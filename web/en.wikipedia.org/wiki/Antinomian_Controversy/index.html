Antinomian Controversy

The Antinomian Controversy was a religious and political conflict in the Massachusetts Bay Colony from 1636 to 1638. It pitted most of the colony's ministers and magistrates against the followers of Anne Hutchinson and her brother-in-law, the minister John Wheelwright, over the nature of grace, sanctification, and the authority of clergy.

Background

Hutchinson, who hosted popular religious meetings in her Boston home, accused most of the colony's ministers of preaching a "covenant of works" rather than a "covenant of grace," emphasizing the indwelling of the Holy Spirit and downplaying moral law (antinomianism, "against law") and outward conduct as evidence of salvation. Her views attracted prominent supporters, including the young governor Henry Vane and the minister John Cotton, whose position was ambiguous.

Outcome

After Vane lost the governorship to John Winthrop in 1637, the authorities moved against the dissenters: Wheelwright was banished for sedition, and Hutchinson was tried by the General Court in November 1637 and excommunicated by the Boston church in March 1638. She and her family left for Rhode Island, and many followers were disarmed or departed. The controversy strengthened orthodox clerical authority in Massachusetts and became a defining episode in American religious history and in the history of women's religious leadership.
