The Agaricales are an order of fungi in the class Agaricomycetes. The group includes the familiar gilled mushrooms, but molecular studies have expanded it to encompass many forms once placed elsewhere, such as puffballs, earthstars, and some club and shelf fungi. The order contains over 13,000 described species in more than 400 genera — roughly a third of all known basidiomycetes.

Characteristics

Most Agaricales produce fruiting bodies (basidiocarps) bearing basidia on gills (lamellae), although the order also includes poroid, toothed, and gasteroid (enclosed) forms. Spores are typically ballistochoric — forcibly discharged — in gilled members. Many species form ectomycorrhizal associations with trees, while others are saprotrophic wood or litter decomposers; a few are parasites or lichen-formers.

Notable members

The order includes economically and culturally important fungi such as the cultivated button mushroom Agaricus bisporus, the poisonous death cap Amanita phalloides, the fly agaric Amanita muscaria, shiitake (Lentinula edodes), oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus), and the bioluminescent jack-o'-lantern (Omphalotus).

Classification

Modern classifications based on DNA phylogenetics, notably the work of Matheny and colleagues (2006), recognize several major clades within the order and around 30–38 families, including Agaricaceae, Amanitaceae, Marasmiaceae, Psathyrellaceae, and Tricholomataceae in a reduced sense.
