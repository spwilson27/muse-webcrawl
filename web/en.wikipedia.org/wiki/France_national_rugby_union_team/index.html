France national rugby union team

The France national rugby union team (French: Équipe de France de rugby à XV) represents the French Rugby Federation (FFR) in men's international rugby union matches. Colloquially known as Le XV de France ("The fifteen of France"), the team has represented France in various test matches, tournaments and championships since 1906, and it is administered by the Fédération Française de Rugby.

France competes annually in the Six Nations Championship (including its predecessor, the Five Nations Championship) with England, Ireland, Italy, Scotland and Wales. France have won the championship on 27 occasions (including 8 shared victories), winning the Grand Slam 10 times. The French team has participated in every Rugby World Cup since the competition began in 1987, reaching the final three times (1987, 1999, 2011) and finishing as runners-up on each occasion.

History

Rugby was introduced to France in 1872 by the British. France's first Test match was played on 1 January 1906 against New Zealand. They joined the Five Nations in 1910, and although they were expelled in 1932 over allegations of professionalism and on-field violence, they were re-admitted in 1947. France won its first Grand Slam in 1968 and has since been one of the leading European rugby nations, noted for the flair of its running game.

Stadium

France's home matches are mostly played at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis, Paris, which seats over 81,000 spectators.
