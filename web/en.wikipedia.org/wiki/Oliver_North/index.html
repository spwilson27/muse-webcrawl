Oliver North

Oliver Laurence North (born October 7, 1943) is an American political commentator, television host, military historian, author, and retired United States Marine Corps lieutenant colonel. A veteran of the Vietnam War, he became a central figure in the Iran–Contra affair of the late 1980s while serving on the staff of the National Security Council.

Iran–Contra affair

As a National Security Council staff member, North helped coordinate the secret sale of arms to Iran, intended to secure the release of American hostages, and the diversion of proceeds to support the Contra rebels in Nicaragua, in violation of the Boland Amendment's restrictions. His televised testimony before Congress in 1987 made him a national figure. He was convicted in 1989 on three felony counts, but the convictions were vacated on appeal because of the use of his immunized congressional testimony.

Later career

North ran unsuccessfully for the U.S. Senate from Virginia in 1994. He subsequently became a syndicated columnist, radio and television host, and author of several books of fiction and military history. In 2018 he was named president of the National Rifle Association; he resigned the following year amid an internal dispute.
