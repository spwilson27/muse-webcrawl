CTV Television Network

Type: Television network
Country: Canada
Launched: 1 October 1961
Owner: Bell Media

The CTV Television Network, commonly known as CTV, is a Canadian English-language terrestrial television network. It launched on 1 October 1961 and is owned by Bell Media, a subsidiary of BCE Inc. CTV is Canada's largest privately owned television network and one of its two national English-language networks, alongside the CBC Television network.

History

CTV began as a cooperative of privately owned stations formed to provide a second television service in Canada, with John Bassett's Toronto station CFTO as its flagship. The network was initially owned collectively by its affiliated stations before being restructured. In 2000, BCE acquired CTV Inc.; the network later became part of Bell Media following BCE's 2011 acquisition of the remaining shares of CTVglobemedia.

The network is known for CTV National News, long anchored by Lloyd Robertson and later Lisa LaFlamme and Omar Sachedina, and for programming such as Canada AM, W-FIVE, and Canadian dramas including Corner Gas (originally on CTV) and Flashpoint. CTV has broadcast major events including the Olympic Games (as part of Canada's Olympic Broadcast Media Consortium) and the Super Bowl, whose Canadian broadcast became controversial for its treatment of simultaneous substitution of advertisements.
