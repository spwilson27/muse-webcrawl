Kurt Josef Waldheim (21 December 1918 – 14 June 2007) was an Austrian politician and diplomat. He served as Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1972 to 1981, and as President of Austria from 1986 to 1992. His presidency was overshadowed by the "Waldheim affair," revelations about his service as a Wehrmacht intelligence officer in the Balkans during the Second World War.

Early life and war service

Waldheim was born near Vienna, studied law at the University of Vienna, and joined the Austrian diplomatic service in 1945. During the war he served as an intelligence and liaison officer (Ordonnanzoffizier) with German Army Group E in Yugoslavia and Greece. His postwar accounts downplayed this service; documents surfacing in 1986 showed he had been attached to units involved in reprisals and deportations, though an international historians' commission in 1988 concluded he had known of war crimes but found no evidence he personally committed them.

Diplomatic career

Waldheim represented Austria at the United Nations, served as ambassador to Canada, and was Austria's foreign minister (1968–1970). In 1971 he ran unsuccessfully for the Austrian presidency but was elected UN Secretary-General, succeeding U Thant. His two terms (1972–1981) saw the 1973 Arab-Israeli war diplomacy, the opening of the UN to the People's Republic of China era, and a 1977 message recorded aboard the Voyager Golden Record. A third term was blocked by a Chinese veto in 1981, and he was succeeded by Javier Pérez de Cuéllar.

Presidency and the Waldheim affair

Elected President of Austria in 1986 amid the controversy over his wartime record, Waldheim was placed on the United States' "Watchlist" of persons ineligible to enter the country in 1987, and he was largely isolated diplomatically, though he completed his term and declined to seek re-election in 1992. The affair prompted a broader Austrian reckoning with the country's role in the Nazi era, challenging the postwar "first victim" narrative.
