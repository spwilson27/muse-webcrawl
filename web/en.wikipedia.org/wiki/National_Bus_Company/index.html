The National Bus Company (NBC) was a nationalised bus company in England and Wales that operated from 1969 until the privatisation of its subsidiaries in the 1980s. Formed under the Transport Act 1968, it brought together the bus operations of the state-owned Transport Holding Company (the former BET and Tilling groups) into a single organisation with around 60,000 employees and some 20,000 buses and coaches.

Operations

NBC operated through regional subsidiary companies — names such as Midland Red, Ribble, Bristol Omnibus, Eastern National, United Counties, Crosville and Southdown — each retaining a local identity within a corporate framework. From 1972 the subsidiaries progressively adopted a standard corporate livery of either "poppy red" or "leaf green" with white relief and the distinctive NBC double-N logo.

The company's express coach services were marketed as National Express, and its engineering and vehicle strategy promoted standard types, most famously the Leyland National single-decker developed jointly with British Leyland, and the Bristol VR double-decker.

Deregulation and privatisation

The Transport Act 1985 deregulated local bus services outside London and required NBC to be broken up and sold. Its more than 70 subsidiaries were privatised individually — many through management buyouts — between 1986 and 1988, and the National Bus Company itself was wound up in 1988. Several successor companies became constituents of today's large groups such as FirstGroup, Stagecoach and Arriva.
