Socialist Republic of Romania

The Socialist Republic of Romania (Romanian: Republica Socialistă România) was the official name of the Romanian state from 1965 until the revolution of December 1989. It was a one-party socialist republic ruled by the Romanian Communist Party, succeeding the Romanian People's Republic proclaimed in 1947 after King Michael I was forced to abdicate. It was a member of the Warsaw Pact and Comecon, though it pursued a notably independent foreign policy within the Eastern Bloc.

Ceaușescu era

Nicolae Ceaușescu became party leader in 1965 on the death of Gheorghe Gheorghiu-Dej. An initial liberalisation and his condemnation of the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia won him Western favour, but after his 1971 July Theses he imposed a neo-Stalinist personality cult and tightened cultural control. His wife Elena rose to the top of the regime, and the Securitate secret police maintained pervasive surveillance.

Economy and society

Ceaușescu industrialised rapidly using Western loans, then in the 1980s imposed severe austerity to repay the entire foreign debt — achieved in 1989 — causing shortages of food, heat and electricity. Decree 770 (1966) banned abortion and contraception to raise the birth rate, producing overcrowded orphanages. The "systematisation" programme demolished villages and historic districts of Bucharest, where the colossal Palace of the Parliament was built.

Fall

Protests beginning in Timișoara on 16 December 1989 spread to Bucharest; on 22 December the Ceaușescus fled by helicopter, were captured, tried by a special military tribunal and executed on 25 December. The National Salvation Front took power, ending 42 years of communist rule.
