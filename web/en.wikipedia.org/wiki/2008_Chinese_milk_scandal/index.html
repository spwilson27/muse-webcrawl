2008 Chinese milk scandal

Date2008 (publicly revealed September 2008)
LocationChina, with recalls worldwide
CauseMilk and infant formula adulterated with melamine
AffectedAn estimated 300,000 infants in China; at least 6 deaths
Key companySanlu Group

The 2008 Chinese milk scandal was a major food safety incident in China in which milk and infant formula produced by the Sanlu Group and other manufacturers were adulterated with melamine, an industrial chemical used in plastics and fertilisers. Melamine was added to diluted raw milk to make its protein content appear higher in standard tests. The contaminated products caused kidney stones and kidney failure in infants; Chinese authorities estimated some 300,000 babies were affected, tens of thousands were hospitalised, and at least six infants died.

Discovery and cover-up

Complaints about Sanlu formula reached the company months before the scandal became public in September 2008, shortly after the Beijing Olympics. Local officials in Shijiazhuang were accused of suppressing the reports. New Zealand dairy cooperative Fonterra, which held a stake in Sanlu, escalated the matter to the Chinese central government through diplomatic channels after failing to obtain a public recall, prompting a nationwide investigation.

Scope

Testing found melamine in products from 22 Chinese dairy companies, and contaminated Chinese-made ingredients triggered recalls of biscuits, candies, chocolate and other products in dozens of countries. Many states banned Chinese dairy imports. Sanlu, once one of China's largest formula makers, collapsed and was declared bankrupt in December 2008.

Legal consequences and reform

Twenty-one people were tried; two men judged central to the melamine supply chain were executed in November 2009, and Sanlu's chairwoman Tian Wenhua received a life sentence. The scandal badly damaged confidence in Chinese food products at home and abroad, spurred enduring demand for imported infant formula, and led to China's 2009 Food Safety Law and the creation of stronger national food safety oversight.
