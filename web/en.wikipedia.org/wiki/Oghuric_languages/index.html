The Oghuric, Onoguric or Oguric languages (also known as Lir-Turkic) are a branch of the Turkic language family. The only extant member of the branch is the Chuvash language. The Oghuric languages are sometimes considered the earliest divergent branch of Turkic, having separated from Common Turkic at an early stage.

Historically, Oghuric-speaking peoples included the Bulgars, the Khazars, and possibly the Pannonian Avars. The Bulgar language, spoken in Volga Bulgaria and in the First Bulgarian Empire on the Danube, is known mainly from epitaphs, loanwords and inscriptions; Danube Bulgar died out as its speakers were Slavicised, while Volga Bulgar is regarded as the ancestor of modern Chuvash.

Features

The branch is named for a characteristic sound correspondence: Oghuric languages show r where Common Turkic shows z, and l where Common Turkic shows š. Thus Chuvash tăhar "nine" corresponds to Common Turkic toquz, and Chuvash çul "stone" to Common Turkic taš. Because of these two correspondences the branch is also called r- or Lir- Turkic. Whether Oghuric and Common Turkic descend from a single Proto-Turkic ancestor, or represent two closely related early branches, remains a subject of scholarly debate.

Historical peoples

The ethnonym is connected with the Oghur (Onogur) tribal confederations of the Pontic steppe. Bulgar inscriptions in Greek letters from the Balkans and Arabic-script epitaphs from the Volga provide the main direct evidence for the extinct languages of the branch. Khazar is poorly attested, surviving mostly in names and a few words recorded by Arabic and Persian geographers, so its classification as Oghuric, though widely accepted, is not certain.
