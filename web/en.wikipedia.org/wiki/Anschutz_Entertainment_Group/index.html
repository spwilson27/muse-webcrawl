The Anschutz Entertainment Group (AEG) is an American worldwide sporting and music entertainment presenter, a subsidiary of The Anschutz Corporation owned by Philip Anschutz. It is one of the world's largest owners of sports teams and venues and the world's second-largest concert promoter.

Venues and events

AEG owns, operates or consults for more than 300 venues worldwide, including Crypto.com Arena (formerly Staples Center) in Los Angeles, The O2 in London, the Mercedes-Benz Arena in Berlin, and T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas. Its concert division, AEG Presents, promotes tours and festivals including the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival (through Goldenvoice), Stagecoach, BST Hyde Park and Rock on the Range. Its ticketing arm, AXS, competes with Ticketmaster.

Sports

AEG Sports' holdings have included the LA Galaxy of Major League Soccer — a league Anschutz helped found and finance in its early years — and a stake in the Los Angeles Kings of the NHL. AEG was instrumental in developing downtown Los Angeles's L.A. Live entertainment district adjacent to its arena.

History

Founded in 1994 as Anschutz began investing in sports teams, AEG expanded aggressively into arena development and concert promotion during the 2000s. In 2012 Anschutz explored selling the company, but took it off the market in 2013 and replaced its longtime chief executive Tim Leiweke with Dan Beckerman.
