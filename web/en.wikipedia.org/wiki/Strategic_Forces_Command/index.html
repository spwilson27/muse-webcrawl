Strategic Forces Command

The Strategic Forces Command (SFC) is the operational command of India's Nuclear Command Authority responsible for the management and administration of the country's tactical and strategic nuclear weapons stockpile. Formed in 2003, it forms part of India's nuclear triad alongside naval and air delivery elements, and executes the directives of the Political Council headed by the Prime Minister.

Strategic Forces Command
Country: India
Formed: 2003
Role: Custody and employment of strategic nuclear forces
Parent: Nuclear Command Authority

Role and organisation

India announced the SFC in January 2003, together with the Nuclear Command Authority, following its 1998 nuclear tests and the adoption of a draft nuclear doctrine emphasising credible minimum deterrence and no first use. The SFC, headed by a Commander-in-Chief drawn in rotation from the three services at senior rank, controls India's land-based nuclear-capable ballistic missiles, including the Prithvi and Agni series, and coordinates with the Air Force's nuclear-strike aircraft and the Navy's ballistic-missile submarines. Its commander works under the Executive Council chaired by the National Security Adviser, which conveys the Political Council's authorisation.

Operations

The SFC conducts user trials and training launches of Agni and Prithvi missiles from the Integrated Test Range at Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island off Odisha. It maintains the weapons in a de-mated state in peacetime — warheads, missiles and launchers held under separate arrangements — in line with India's recessed deterrence posture.
