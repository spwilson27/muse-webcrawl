Occupational segregation

Occupational segregation is the distribution of workers across and within occupations, based upon demographic characteristics, most often gender, race or ethnicity. It is usually measured by indices such as the Duncan dissimilarity index.

Forms

Horizontal segregation is concentration in different occupations (e.g. women over-represented in care and education, men in construction and engineering). Vertical segregation is concentration at different levels within occupations — the glass ceiling. Segregation contributes substantially to the gender pay gap.

Causes

Explanations include social norms and socialisation, educational choices, employer discrimination, occupational crowding, and workplace structures around caregiving. Segregation has declined for gender in many countries since the 1970s but remains substantial, and racial segregation interacts with it.
