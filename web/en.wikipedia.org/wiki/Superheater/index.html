Superheater

A superheater is a device found in steam boilers that converts saturated ("wet") steam into superheated ("dry") steam by raising its temperature above the boiling point at the given pressure, without significant change in pressure. Superheated steam has higher enthalpy per unit mass, reduces condensation losses in cylinders and turbines, and improves the overall thermal efficiency of steam engines and power plants.

Principle

Saturated steam leaving the boiler drum is passed through tubes exposed to hot flue gases. Because no liquid water remains to be evaporated, the added heat raises the steam's temperature. In modern power-station boilers, superheaters bring steam to temperatures of 540–600 °C, and a reheater returns partially expanded steam from the turbine to the boiler to restore its temperature. Materials of superheater tubes must withstand high temperature and oxidation, commonly chromium-molybdenum alloy steels.

Steam locomotives

Superheating transformed locomotive performance in the early 20th century. Wilhelm Schmidt's designs, widely adopted after about 1905, placed superheater elements inside enlarged flue tubes in the boiler. Superheated locomotives used roughly 20–25% less coal and water than saturated equivalents, produced more power, and became standard for mainline engines worldwide. Their introduction required changes in lubrication (high-temperature oils) and valve design (piston valves in place of slide valves).

Types

Superheaters are classified by heat transfer mode: radiant superheaters line the furnace walls and absorb radiation; convection superheaters sit in the flue-gas path; and combined designs use both. Pendant and platen arrangements differ in tube geometry and spacing, chosen to resist ash fouling and slagging in coal-fired plant.

Advantages and challenges

Beyond efficiency, dry steam reduces erosion of turbine blades by water droplets and avoids condensation in long steam lines. Challenges include controlling steam temperature over varying loads (using attemperators or spray desuperheaters), tube overheating during startup, and creep of alloys under sustained high temperature and pressure.
