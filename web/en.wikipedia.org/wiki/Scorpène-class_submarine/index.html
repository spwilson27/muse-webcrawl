The Scorpène class (Spanish: clase Scorpène) is a class of diesel-electric attack submarines jointly developed by the French company Naval Group (formerly DCNS) and the Spanish company Navantia, and now marketed by Naval Group. Designed for the export market, the class has been sold to Chile, Malaysia, India and Brazil, with several boats built under licence in the customer's country.

Design

The Scorpène is a conventionally powered submarine with a hydrodynamic hull form derived from the French Agosta 90B and incorporating technology from the French nuclear submarine programme. Variants include the basic CM-2000, the AM-2000 fitted with the MESMA air-independent propulsion (AIP) system, and the enlarged CA-2000. Later derivatives offer fuel-cell AIP or lithium-ion batteries, and the evolved "Scorpène Evolved" uses lithium-ion propulsion throughout. Armament typically comprises six 533 mm bow tubes for heavyweight torpedoes and Exocet anti-ship missiles.

Operators

  • Chile — two boats (O'Higgins class), delivered 2005–2006, the class's first customer.
  • Malaysia — two boats (Tunku Abdul Rahman class), delivered 2009.
  • India — six boats (Kalvari class) built by Mazagon Dock in Mumbai under Project 75, commissioned from 2017.
  • Brazil — four boats (Riachuelo class) built by Itaguaí Construções Navais under the PROSUB programme, the first commissioned in 2022, adapted as a stepping stone to Brazil's planned nuclear submarine.

Data leak

In August 2016, The Australian newspaper published extracts from more than 22,000 pages of leaked documents concerning the Scorpène design and its combat capabilities, an incident that caused diplomatic friction and security reviews among operator navies, particularly India, at the time Australia was selecting a design for its own future submarine programme.
