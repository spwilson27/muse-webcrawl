Dalkeith

Dalkeith (Scots: Dawkeith; Scottish Gaelic: Dail Cheith) is a town in Midlothian, Scotland, on the River Esk. It is situated about 11 kilometres (7 mi) southeast of Edinburgh city centre and is the administrative centre of the Midlothian council area, with a population of around 12,000. The name is generally derived from Brittonic words meaning "wood meadow". Dalkeith developed as a market town on the route south from Edinburgh and was long associated with the earls and dukes of Buccleuch.

History

Dalkeith is recorded from the 12th century as a barony of the Graham family, passing to the Douglas earls of Morton in the 14th century. Dalkeith Castle, rebuilt in the early 18th century as Dalkeith Palace, was the seat of the Douglas family and later of the dukes of Buccleuch, who acquired the estate in 1642; it was one of the earliest great classical houses in Scotland. The town flourished as a grain market — its Corn Exchange of 1853 was among the largest in Scotland — and later as a coal-mining community, with collieries in the surrounding Midlothian coalfield closing in the later 20th century. George IV and Queen Victoria both stayed at the palace during royal visits to Scotland.

Landmarks

Dalkeith Country Park, centred on the palace and the restored 18th-century stables, surrounds the confluence of the North and South Esk rivers and includes the Montagu Bridge and an adventure playground (Fort Douglas). In the town, St Nicholas Buccleuch Church, founded in 1406 as a collegiate church, retains its medieval choir, which houses the effigies of James Douglas, 1st Earl of Morton, and his wife Joan, daughter of James I of Scotland. The tolbooth, mercat cross, and the 19th-century Corn Exchange are other notable buildings.
