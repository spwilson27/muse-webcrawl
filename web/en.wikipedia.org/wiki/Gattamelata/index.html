Gattamelata

BornErasmo da Narni, c. 1370, Narni
Died16 January 1443, Padua
OccupationCondottiero
AllegianceRepublic of Venice

Erasmo da Narni (c. 1370 – 16 January 1443), better known by his nickname Gattamelata (meaning "the Honeyed Cat"), was an Italian condottiero of the Renaissance. He was born in Narni and rose from humble origins to become one of the most celebrated mercenary captains of his age, serving the Republic of Venice.

Career

Erasmo began his military career in the service of the condottiero Braccio da Montone and fought in the wars between the Italian city-states. He later served the Papal States and then Venice, becoming captain-general of Venetian land forces in 1434. His campaigns in Lombardy against the Duchy of Milan, conducted alongside Francesco Sforza, secured Venetian territory on the Italian mainland.

The equestrian monument

Gattamelata is best remembered today for the monumental bronze equestrian statue created by Donatello between 1447 and 1453, erected in the Piazza del Santo in Padua in front of the Basilica of Saint Anthony. It was the first full-scale equestrian bronze cast since antiquity, and one of the great masterpieces of Renaissance sculpture, modelled on the ancient Roman statue of Marcus Aurelius.
