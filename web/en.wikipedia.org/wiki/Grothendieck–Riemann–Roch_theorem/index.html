Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem

FieldAlgebraic geometry
Proved byAlexander Grothendieck
Announced1957 (letter to Jean-Pierre Serre)
GeneralisesRiemann–Roch theorem, Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem

The Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalisation in algebraic geometry of the classical Riemann–Roch theorem on Riemann surfaces. Alexander Grothendieck announced the result in a letter to Jean-Pierre Serre in 1957; the proof, developed with Armand Borel and Serre, appeared in 1958.

Statement

For a proper morphism f : X → Y of smooth varieties and a coherent sheaf (or K-theory class) on X, the theorem relates the Chern character of the pushforward of the class along f to the pushforward of its Chern character multiplied by the Todd class of the relative tangent bundle. In symbols: ch(f!x) · td(Y) = f*(ch(x) · td(X)).

Special cases

When Y is a point, the formula reduces to the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem, computing the Euler characteristic of a sheaf from characteristic classes. For a curve mapped to a point, it gives the classical Riemann–Roch theorem for line bundles.

Significance

The theorem motivated the creation of algebraic K-theory, which Grothendieck developed partly for its proof, and it inspired the Atiyah–Singer index theorem in differential geometry. It remains a cornerstone of intersection theory and modern algebraic geometry.
