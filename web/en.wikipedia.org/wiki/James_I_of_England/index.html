James VI and I (19 June 1566 – 27 March 1625) was King of Scotland as James VI from 1567 and King of England and Ireland as James I from 1603 until his death in 1625. The son of Mary, Queen of Scots, he succeeded to the Scottish throne as an infant after his mother's forced abdication, and in 1603 inherited the English crown from the childless Elizabeth I, uniting the crowns of Scotland and England in his person — the Union of the Crowns.

Reign in Scotland

Raised by regents amid civil war and tutored by the humanist George Buchanan, James survived kidnapping attempts and noble factionalism, gradually asserting personal rule in the 1580s–90s. He wrote on kingship in The True Law of Free Monarchies and Basilikon Doron, survived the Gowrie conspiracy of 1600, and in 1590 married Anne of Denmark.

Reign in England

In England, James pursued peace with Spain (Treaty of London, 1604), survived the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, and promoted the translation of the Bible known as the King James Version (1611). He styled himself King of Great Britain and promoted a closer union, though the English and Scottish parliaments remained separate. His reign saw the founding of the first permanent English colonies in America (Jamestown, 1607; Plymouth, 1620) and the Plantation of Ulster. Relations with Parliament were strained by disputes over finance, impositions, and his reliance on favourites, notably Robert Carr and George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham. James supported his son-in-law Frederick V, Elector Palatine, only weakly during the early Thirty Years' War, and sought a Spanish marriage for his son Charles, a policy that collapsed amid public hostility.

Legacy

Scholarly, peaceable, and devoted to the theory of the divine right of kings, James was called "the wisest fool in Christendom" in a gibe attributed to the French court. The Jacobean era named for him was a high point of English literature, with Shakespeare, Jonson, and Donne writing under his rule, though the fiscal and constitutional tensions of his reign deepened under his son Charles I.
