Fort Collins is a home rule municipality in Larimer County, Colorado, United States, and the county seat. Located on the Cache la Poudre River along the Northern Front Range, about 56 miles (90 km) north of Denver, it is the fourth most populous city in Colorado, with a population of roughly 170,000 at the 2020 census. It is home to Colorado State University and is known for its craft brewing industry, historic Old Town district, and outdoor recreation at the edge of the Rocky Mountain foothills.

History

Fort Collins began as a US Army post, Camp Collins, established in 1864 to protect the Overland Trail mail route along the Cache la Poudre River; it was named after Lieutenant Colonel William O. Collins. After the original camp was destroyed by a flood, the fort was rebuilt at the present city site. The military post was abandoned in 1867, but settlers remained, and the town was platted in 1872 and incorporated in 1883. Colorado Agricultural College (now Colorado State University) was founded in 1870 and opened to students in 1879, shaping the town's growth. Lamb feeding, sugar beets, and agriculture supported the early economy.

Geography and climate

The city sits at about 5,003 feet (1,525 m) elevation where the plains meet the foothills. Horsetooth Reservoir, west of the city, is a major recreation area. Fort Collins has a semi-arid climate with four seasons, abundant sunshine, low humidity, and winters moderated by downslope chinook winds.

Economy and culture

Major employers include Colorado State University, technology and manufacturing firms, and a nationally prominent craft beer sector — New Belgium Brewing Company and Odell Brewing Company were both founded in Fort Collins, and the city is sometimes called the "Napa Valley of beer". The pedestrianised Old Town, said to be an inspiration for Disneyland's Main Street, U.S.A., anchors dining and nightlife. The city is frequently cited in "best places to live" rankings for its bike infrastructure, parks, and proximity to Rocky Mountain National Park.
