Sliding filament theory

The sliding filament theory explains the mechanism of muscle contraction in which actin (thin) and myosin (thick) filaments within the sarcomere slide past one another, shortening the sarcomere without the filaments themselves shortening.

Sliding filament theory
Proposed1954
ProposersHugh Huxley & Jean Hanson; Andrew Huxley & Rolf Niedergerke

History

Two papers published independently in Nature in 1954 – by Hugh Huxley and Jean Hanson, and by Andrew F. Huxley and Rolf Niedergerke – showed from microscopy that the A-band length stays constant while the I-band and H-zone shorten during contraction.

Mechanism

Calcium ions released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind troponin, moving tropomyosin to expose myosin-binding sites on actin. Myosin heads, energised by ATP hydrolysis, form cross-bridges, perform a power stroke pulling actin toward the centre, then detach on binding new ATP (its absence causes rigor mortis). Repeated cycles shorten the muscle; the cycle is regulated by troponin–tropomyosin and ATP-driven calcium reuptake.
