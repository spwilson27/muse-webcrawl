Japanese whisky

Japanese whisky is a style of whisky developed and produced in Japan. Commercial production began in the 1920s with the founding of the Yamazaki distillery by Shinjiro Torii of Suntory, with Masataka Taketsuru — who had studied distilling in Scotland — as its first distiller; Taketsuru later founded Nikka and built the Yoichi distillery in Hokkaido.

Style

Japanese whisky was modelled on Scotch whisky, with single malts and blends produced using malted barley, pot stills and oak-cask maturation. Japanese distillers developed a distinctive emphasis on clarity of fermentation, varied still shapes within one distillery to create blending components, and use of mizunara oak for some expressions.

Industry

The two largest producers are Suntory and Nikka (Asahi). After a domestic decline in the 1980s–2000s, Japanese whisky won major international awards in the 2000s–2010s, causing a global boom and shortages of aged stock. A voluntary industry standard defining "Japanese whisky" was adopted in 2021.
