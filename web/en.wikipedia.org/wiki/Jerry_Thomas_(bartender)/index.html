Jerry Thomas (bartender)

Jerry Thomas
Born: 30 October 1830, Sackets Harbor, New York, US
Died: 15 December 1885 (aged 55), New York City
Known for: Author of the first bartenders' guide (1862)

Jerry Thomas (30 October 1830 – 15 December 1885) was an American bartender, saloon keeper and author regarded as the father of American mixology. His flamboyant showmanship — he was nicknamed "the Professor" — and his pioneering cocktail book made him one of the most famous bartenders of the 19th century.

Career

Thomas worked in bars from New Haven to California, where he prospected during the Gold Rush, and became principal bartender at the Metropolitan Hotel in New York and later at the Planter's House in St. Louis. He ran several celebrated New York saloons, toured Europe demonstrating American drinks, and was famous for showy technique and jewellery, as well as for signature drinks such as the Blue Blazer, a flaming whisky concoction tossed between mugs.

The Bar-Tender's Guide

In 1862 Thomas published How to Mix Drinks, or The Bon-Vivant's Companion, the first bartenders' guide published in the United States. It collected some 236 recipes — punches, cocktails, flips, sours and more — and codified the emerging American art of mixed drinks. An expanded edition, The Bar-Tender's Guide, appeared posthumously in 1887. The book remains a foundational text of the modern cocktail revival.
