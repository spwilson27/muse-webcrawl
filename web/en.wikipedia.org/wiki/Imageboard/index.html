Imageboard

An imageboard (or image board) is a type of Internet forum that focuses on the posting of images, often alongside text and discussion. The first imageboards were created in Japan, where they were based on the concept of the textboard. Some of the most well-known English-language imageboards include 4chan, 8kun (formerly 8chan), and various smaller boards devoted to particular interests.

Imageboards are distinct from other forum software in their anonymity and ephemerality: most do not require registration, many posts are made under a shared default name such as "Anonymous", and threads are automatically deleted after they expire or fall off the board's last page.

Characteristics

Imageboards, similar to bulletin board systems, are used for discussions of a variety of topics. The primary focus of imageboards, however, is directed away from text posts and instead is placed on picture posts. The two formats share a structural similarity: threads are started with an initial post that may contain both text and an image, and replies accumulate beneath it.

Tripcodes

Most imageboards and 2channel-style discussion boards allow (and encourage) anonymous posting and use a system of tripcodes instead of registration. A tripcode is a hashed password used to generate a visually distinct identifier, allowing a user to prove authorship of a post without an account, though tripcodes offer only weak security.

Ephemerality

On many boards, threads receive a limited number of replies (or bumps) before they stop being promoted, and old threads are pruned to make room for new ones. Nothing is archived by default, which encourages a fast-moving culture and discourages long-term identity-building.

History

The concept derives from Japanese textboards such as 2channel (2ch), founded in 1999 by Hiroyuki Nishimura. Futaba Channel (2chan), launched in 2001, added image posting and became the direct model for 4chan, founded in 2003 by Christopher "moot" Poole, who imported Futaba's software and layout. English-language imageboards went on to have an outsized influence on Internet culture, popularizing memes, copypasta, and formats such as the rage comic and greentext stories.

Software

Widely used imageboard software packages have included Futaba, Wakaba, Kusaba X, Tinyboard and its successor vichan, and LynxChan. Each implements boards divided by topic, catalog views, moderation tools, and varying degrees of support for file types beyond images, such as WebM video.

Culture and influence

Imageboards have been associated with the hacktivist collective Anonymous, with the creation of numerous memes (including LOLcats and Rickrolling in its early spread), and with controversies over extremist content and harassment campaigns organized on some boards. Several boards have been shut down, deplatformed, or split into rival communities as a result.
