Pointer (user interface)

In computing, a pointer or mouse cursor is a graphical image used to activate or control certain elements in a graphical user interface. The pointer moves in response to a pointing device such as a mouse, trackball, touchpad, or stylus, and indicates the position at which clicking, dragging, or other gestures will take effect. The active pixel of the pointer image is called the hot spot.

History

The pointer emerged with the first graphical user interfaces developed at Xerox PARC in the 1970s and was popularised by the Apple Macintosh in 1984. The familiar arrow shape, angled up and to the left, was chosen to remain legible on low-resolution displays; Douglas Engelbart's earlier pointing experiments used different indicators, and Engelbart is credited with coining the term "cursor" for the on-screen position marker.

Pointer shapes

Operating systems change the pointer shape to communicate state and affordances:

  • Arrow — the default pointer for selecting and clicking.
  • I-beam — over editable text, indicating where typed characters will be inserted.
  • Pointing hand — over hyperlinks and other clickable items on the web.
  • Hourglass / spinning wheel — the application is busy.
  • Resize arrows — at window edges and corners.
  • Crosshair — for precise positioning in drawing applications and games.

Pointer trails and accessibility

Systems offer options to enlarge the pointer, display trails, or highlight its position with a key press, assisting users who have difficulty tracking it. On touchscreens there is no persistent pointer; instead, touch targets respond directly, although styluses may show a hover indicator.
