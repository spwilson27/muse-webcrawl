Mahavidya

The Mahavidyas are a group of ten Hindu Tantric goddesses, considered forms of the Divine Mother, Adi Shakti. The word derives from the Sanskrit mahā (great) and vidyā (wisdom, knowledge). The ten goddesses are commonly given as Kali, Tara, Tripura Sundari (Lalita), Bhuvaneshvari, Bhairavi, Chhinnamasta, Dhumavati, Bagalamukhi, Matangi, and Kamala.

Significance

The Mahavidyas occupy a central place in Shaktism and Tantra. Each goddess embodies a distinct aspect of divine consciousness and a particular form of wisdom, ranging from the fierce and terrifying forms such as Kali, Chhinnamasta, and Dhumavati to the gentle and auspicious forms such as Tripura Sundari and Kamala. Together they are said to represent the full cycle of existence, from creation to destruction.

Origins

The grouping of the ten Mahavidyas is described in texts such as the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the Guhyatiguhya Tantra, and later Tantric works. A well-known legend relates that Sati, in order to overcome Shiva's objection to her attending her father's sacrifice, assumed ten terrifying forms surrounding him in every direction; these forms became the Mahavidyas.

Worship

The Mahavidyas are worshipped for both worldly attainment (bhukti) and liberation (mukti). Their worship is especially associated with Tantric practice at centers such as Kamakhya in Assam and at various Shakti Pithas across the Indian subcontinent.
