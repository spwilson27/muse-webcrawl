Populist Party

The Populist Party, officially the People's Party, was an agrarian populist political party in the United States that flourished in the 1890s. Growing out of the Farmers' Alliances of the South and Midwest, it championed farmers and labourers against railroads, banks, and the gold standard.

Platform

The party's Omaha Platform of 1892 demanded the free and unlimited coinage of silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of the railroads and telegraph, postal savings banks, an eight-hour working day, and the subtreasury plan of crop loans. Its presidential candidate that year, James B. Weaver, won over 8 percent of the popular vote and 22 electoral votes — an exceptional showing for a third party.

Fusion and decline

In 1896 the Populists fused with the Democratic Party behind William Jennings Bryan and his "Cross of Gold" campaign. Bryan's defeat, together with rising farm prices after 1897, drained the party's momentum; it lingered as a minor organisation until 1908. Many Populist demands were later enacted by Progressive and New Deal reforms, and the party remains a landmark in the history of American third-party movements.
