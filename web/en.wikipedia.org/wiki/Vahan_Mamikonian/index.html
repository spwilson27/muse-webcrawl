Vahan Mamikonian

Vahan Mamikonian (Armenian: Վահան Մամիկոնյան; c. 440/445 – 503/510) was an Armenian nobleman of the Mamikonian family who led the successful Armenian revolt of 481–484 against Sasanian Persian rule. The resulting Treaty of Nvarsak (484) secured religious freedom and a measure of autonomy for Christian Armenia within the Persian Empire, and Vahan was later appointed marzpan (governor) of Persian Armenia, a post he held from 485 until his death.

Background

Armenia had been partitioned between Persia and Rome in 387, and the Persian part lost its Arsacid monarchy in 428. Under King Peroz I, Persian attempts to impose Zoroastrianism provoked growing resistance. Vahan — whose father had died after the earlier, failed revolt of 450–451 led by Vardan Mamikonian (Vahan's uncle) — initially pursued compromise, but after hostages and Armenian nobles were detained in Persia, he emerged as leader of a new uprising in 481.

The revolt

The rebels won victories including at the Battle of Nersehapat (482), and although Armenian forces were defeated in some engagements, Peroz's death in war against the Hephthalites in 484 changed the strategic picture. His successor Balash agreed to negotiate; the Treaty of Nvarsak guaranteed that churches would not be destroyed, no one would be forced to convert, and Persian administration would deal fairly with the Armenian nobility.

Governorship and legacy

Appointed marzpan in 485, Vahan governed Armenia for roughly two decades, rebuilding churches and supporting the activities that produced a flourishing of Armenian Christian culture — the era traditionally called the Armenian "Golden Age" of the fifth century's close. He is remembered alongside Vardan Mamikonian as a national hero of Armenian resistance; his life is described in the history written by his contemporary Ghazar Parpetsi.
