The Selous Scouts was a special forces regiment of the Rhodesian Army that operated during the Rhodesian Bush War (1964–1979). Named after explorer Frederick Courteney Selous, it was founded in 1973 under Lieutenant Colonel Ron Reid-Daly.

Role

The regiment specialized in pseudo-operations: soldiers — the majority of its rank and file were Black Rhodesians, including turned insurgents — posed as ZANLA or ZIPRA guerrillas to gather intelligence and direct strikes. It also conducted tracking, reconnaissance, and external raids, notably the controversial 1976 Nyadzonya raid in Mozambique. Official Rhodesian claims credited the Scouts with involvement in a large share of insurgent casualties, figures historians treat cautiously.

Disbandment and reputation

The unit was disbanded in 1980 after Zimbabwean independence, amid allegations of atrocities and chemical/biological warfare associations that remain disputed. Its brutal selection course and bushcraft influenced later counter-insurgency doctrine. Compare the irregular warfare of the region's earlier wars.
