Thomas Tallis

Thomas Tallis (c. 1505 – 23 November 1585) was an English Renaissance composer of sacred music and one of the greatest figures in English musical history. His long career spanned the reigns of four monarchs — Henry VIII, Edward VI, Mary I and Elizabeth I — and required him to adapt his style to the repeated religious reversals of the English Reformation.

Career

Tallis was organist of Dover Priory and then of Waltham Abbey, whose dissolution in 1540 left him in London, where he joined the Chapel Royal as a gentleman by 1543. He remained a member for the rest of his life, serving as organist and composer, and taught William Byrd, with whom in 1575 he received a royal patent to print music and published the joint collection Cantiones sacrae.

Works

His surviving music includes Latin masses and motets, English anthems and services, and keyboard and consort music. The forty-part motet Spem in alium is among the most celebrated works of Renaissance polyphony. Other famous pieces include the lamentations, the motets O nata lux and If ye love me, and the tune "Third Mode Melody", used by Ralph Vaughan Williams in his Fantasia on a Theme by Thomas Tallis (1910).

Legacy

Tallis died at Greenwich in 1585. His adaptability and craftsmanship made him the father figure of English church music, honoured as the mentor of Byrd and a bridge between the medieval and Elizabethan traditions.
