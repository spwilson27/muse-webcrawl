Theodore Komnenos Doukas (Greek: Θεόδωρος Κομνηνὸς Δούκας, c. 1180/85 – c. 1253) was a Byzantine Greek nobleman who became ruler of Epirus in 1215 and, after conquering Thessalonica in 1224, proclaimed himself emperor, rivalling the Empire of Nicaea in its claim to restore the Byzantine Empire after the Fourth Crusade. His ambitions collapsed at the Battle of Klokotnitsa in 1230, when he was defeated and captured by Ivan Asen II of Bulgaria.

Background

Theodore was a son of the sebastokrator John Doukas and a half-brother of Michael I Komnenos Doukas, founder of the Despotate of Epirus. In his youth he spent time at the court of Theodore I Laskaris in Nicaea, where he swore allegiance to the Nicaean emperor before returning west. On Michael's assassination in 1215, Theodore succeeded him as ruler of Epirus, though he would later repudiate his oath to Nicaea.

Expansion

An energetic and ruthless campaigner, Theodore rapidly expanded his territory at the expense of the Latin Empire, the Kingdom of Thessalonica, and his Greek neighbours. He captured Peter of Courtenay, the Latin emperor-elect, in 1217 as Peter crossed his territory, and Peter died in captivity. After a long siege, Theodore took Thessalonica in 1224, ending the Latin kingdom there. Soon afterwards he was crowned emperor — probably in 1225 or 1227 — by the archbishop of Ohrid, Demetrios Chomatenos, asserting a claim to the imperial title equal to that of John III Doukas Vatatzes of Nicaea.

His dominions at their height stretched from the Adriatic to the Aegean and deep into Thessaly and Macedonia, and contemporaries spoke of an "Empire of Thessalonica". He disputed Nicaean influence in the southern Balkans and pressed toward Constantinople itself.

Downfall

In 1230, breaking a recent treaty and marriage alliance, Theodore invaded Bulgaria and met Ivan Asen II at the Battle of Klokotnitsa on 9 March 1230. His army was annihilated, and Theodore was captured and later blinded after allegedly plotting against his captor. His empire fragmented: his brother Manuel took Thessalonica as a Bulgarian client, while Epirus proper passed under Michael II Komnenos Doukas.

Released around 1237, Theodore returned to Thessalonica, expelled Manuel with Bulgarian help, and installed his son John as emperor while ruling in fact himself. Unable to resist Nicaean pressure, he was eventually compelled to accept Vatatzes' suzerainty; in 1252 he was arrested on suspicion of conspiracy and exiled to Nicaea, where he died about 1253.
