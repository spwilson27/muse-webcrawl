Curb weight
Also calledKerb weight (British English)
ExcludesPassengers and cargo
IncludesStandard equipment, fluids, (usually) fuel

Curb weight (American English) or kerb weight (British English) is the total mass of a vehicle with all standard equipment and all necessary operating consumables — such as engine oil, coolant, and (in most definitions) fuel — but without passengers or cargo. It is a standard specification quoted by manufacturers and used in performance and regulatory calculations.

Definitions

Definitions vary by jurisdiction. In the United States, curb weight includes a full tank of fuel and standard equipment. European Union rules define the "mass in running order" with a 90% full fuel tank, a 75 kg allowance for the driver, and fluids. Curb weight should be distinguished from dry weight (without any consumables), gross vehicle weight rating (the maximum operating mass including payload), and tare weight in freight contexts. A vehicle's payload capacity is the difference between its gross weight rating and its curb weight.
