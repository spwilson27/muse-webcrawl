Justus von Liebig

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Justus von Liebig
Born: 12 May 1803, Darmstadt
Field: Chemistry
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Justus Freiherr von Liebig (1803–1873) was a German chemist who founded the first great teaching laboratory (at Giessen) and helped create both organic chemistry and agricultural chemistry. His students — including Kekulé and Hofmann — carried his model of laboratory-based training worldwide.

Liebig improved organic analysis (the Kaliapparat), identified the benzoyl radical with Wöhler, showed plants need mineral nutrients (his 'law of the minimum'), and campaigned for chemical fertilisers. Later ventures included meat extract (Liebig's Extract of Meat Company — OXO) and a best-selling Chemical Letters for the public.

[Image: Liebig in his Giessen laboratory with students at benches]

Fertiliser and food

Field trials convinced Liebig that replacing harvested minerals was essential; his early patent manure was flawed (insoluble), but the principle drove the fertiliser industry. He also developed an infant food formula and championed baking powder.

Silvered mirrors

His silvering process for mirrors (reducing silver salts onto glass, 1835) replaced toxic mercury amalgam mirrors and remained in use for a century.
