Bharatiya Janata Party

The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP; lit. "Indian People's Party") is a major political party in India and, since 2014, the country's ruling party under Prime Minister Narendra Modi. It is the world's largest political party by membership.

BJP
Founded: 6 April 1980
Predecessor: Bharatiya Jana Sangh
Ideology: Hindutva, Hindu nationalism, conservatism, economic liberalism
Symbol: Lotus
Headquarters: New Delhi
Alliance: National Democratic Alliance (NDA)

History

The BJP grew out of the Bharatiya Jana Sangh (founded 1951 by Syama Prasad Mookerjee), closely linked to the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS). After the Janata Party experiment of 1977–79, the BJP was founded in 1980 under Atal Bihari Vajpayee. The Ram Janmabhoomi movement and L. K. Advani's Rath Yatra propelled its rise; Vajpayee led coalition governments in 1996 and 1998–2004.

In government

Under Modi the BJP won outright majorities in 2014 and 2019 and led the NDA to a third term in 2024. Policies include the revocation of Jammu and Kashmir's special status (2019), a new Ram temple at Ayodhya (consecrated 2024), economic and welfare programmes, and promotion of Hindu cultural priorities; critics raise concerns about minority rights and democratic institutions.
