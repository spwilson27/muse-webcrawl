Polish–Russian War of 1792

The Polish–Russian War of 1792 (also known as the War in Defence of the Constitution) was fought between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Russian Empire over the Constitution of 3 May 1791, which sought to reform the Commonwealth's dysfunctional political system. Russia, invited to intervene by the conservative Targowica Confederation of Polish magnates, invaded in May 1792.

Course of the war

Polish forces under Prince Józef Poniatowski and Tadeusz Kościuszko fought a delaying campaign in Ukraine, winning the Battle of Zieleńce on 18 June 1792 — after which King Stanisław August Poniatowski created the Virtuti Militari order of merit — but were heavily outnumbered. In Lithuania, the Commonwealth army retreated before superior Russian forces. Hoping to negotiate, the king acceded to the Targowica Confederation in July and ordered the army to cease fighting, ending the war in a Russian victory despite the Polish army remaining largely intact in the field.

Consequences

The war led directly to the Second Partition of Poland in 1793, in which Russia and Prussia annexed vast territories and the rump Commonwealth was forced to annul the constitution. The humiliation of 1792–93 set the stage for the Kościuszko Uprising of 1794 and the final Third Partition that erased Poland from the map in 1795.
