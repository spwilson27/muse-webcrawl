Absorbance

Absorbance
SymbolA
DefinitionA = log10(Φei / Φet)
DimensionDimensionless

Absorbance (also called optical density, though that term is used differently in some fields) is the logarithm of the ratio of incident to transmitted radiant power through a material. In spectroscopy, absorbance is defined as A = log10(Φei/Φet), where Φei is the radiant flux received by the material and Φet is the radiant flux transmitted by it.

Beer–Lambert law

For a solution of an absorbing substance, absorbance is proportional to the concentration and the path length, as expressed by the Beer–Lambert law: A = εlc, where ε is the molar attenuation coefficient, l is the optical path length, and c is the concentration. This relationship underlies quantitative spectrophotometry in analytical chemistry.

Measurement

Absorbance is measured with a spectrophotometer, usually relative to a blank sample. Because measurements are logarithmic, an absorbance of 1 corresponds to 10% transmission, and an absorbance of 2 to 1%. Scattering and reflection losses can contribute to measured attenuation, a distinction relevant in fields such as ocean optics and microbiology, where "optical density" readings (e.g. OD600) are dominated by scattering.
