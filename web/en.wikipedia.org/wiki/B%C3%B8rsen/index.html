Børsen

Børsen ("the Exchange"), also known as Børsbygningen ("the Stock Exchange building"), is a 17th-century stock exchange and landmark in central Copenhagen, Denmark. Built under Christian IV between 1619 and 1640, it is one of the finest examples of Dutch Renaissance architecture in Denmark and is famous for its distinctive spire, formed by the intertwined tails of four dragons.

History

Christian IV commissioned the building as part of his plan to strengthen Copenhagen's role as a trading centre in northern Europe. Designed by the brothers Lorenz and Hans van Steenwinckel the Younger, its ground floor held stalls and warehouses for merchants, with the exchange hall above. The dragon spire was completed in 1625 under the direction of Ludvig Heidtrider. The building served as Denmark's stock exchange until 1974 and later housed the Danish Chamber of Commerce.

Fire of 2024

On 16 April 2024, during renovation work, a major fire broke out at Børsen. The dragon spire collapsed, and much of the roof and interior were destroyed, though firefighters and staff rescued many historic artworks and artifacts, including Peder Severin Krøyer's large painting of the exchange's merchants. The fire was widely compared in Danish reaction to the 2019 Notre-Dame fire in Paris, and rebuilding the spire and hall was pledged by the building's owners and Danish business leaders.

Architecture

The long, red-brick building is richly decorated with sandstone ornament, gabled dormers, and a copper-clad spire rising about 56 metres. Børsen stands on Slotsholmen beside Christiansborg Palace and the more recent Danish National Bank building.
