Victor Houteff

Born2 March 1885, Raicovo, Bulgaria
Died5 February 1955, Waco, Texas, United States

Victor Tasho Houteff (2 March 1885 – 5 February 1955) was a Bulgarian immigrant to the United States who founded the Shepherd's Rod movement, a reform movement within Seventh-day Adventism whose followers became known as Davidian Seventh-day Adventists.

Houteff emigrated from Bulgaria to the United States in 1907 and joined the Seventh-day Adventist Church in 1919. While teaching a Sabbath school class in Los Angeles in the 1920s, he developed teachings he believed constituted a message of reform and impending judgment for the church, published beginning in 1930 in the first volume of The Shepherd's Rod, named after the rod of Micah 6:9. His central claims concerned the purification of the church, the sealing of the 144,000 of Revelation, and the establishment of a Davidic kingdom in Palestine.

Mount Carmel Center

After being disfellowshipped from the Adventist Church in 1930, Houteff organized his followers and in 1935 purchased land near Waco, Texas, establishing the Mount Carmel Center as the movement's headquarters. The community operated a school, a farm, and a publishing operation, producing the multi-volume Shepherd's Rod series and tracts mailed to Adventist members worldwide.

Houteff died in 1955, shortly after setting — according to his associates' interpretations — an expectation of prophetic events around 1959. Disappointments and disputes over succession split the movement; the larger body dissolved in the early 1960s, while a breakaway group under Benjamin Roden, and later Roden's successors including David Koresh, occupied a new Mount Carmel site east of Waco. That offshoot, the Branch Davidians, became internationally known during the 1993 Waco siege.
