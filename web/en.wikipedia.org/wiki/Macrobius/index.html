Macrobius

Macrobius Ambrosius Theodosius, usually referred to as Macrobius (fl. early 5th century CE), was a Roman author and philosopher who lived during the reigns of Honorius and Theodosius II. Little is known of his life; he may have been a provincial governor (vicarius) and later praetorian prefect of Italy in 430.

Works

His best-known work is the Commentarii in Somnium Scipionis, a commentary on the "Dream of Scipio" from Cicero's De re publica, which preserved that portion of Cicero's text and became a major channel for Neoplatonic cosmology in the medieval West; its discussion of the music of the spheres and its world map (the "Macrobian map") were influential for over a thousand years. He also wrote the Saturnalia, a dialogue in seven books set at the December festival, preserving a wealth of antiquarian learning on Roman religion, literature, and customs, including discussion of Virgil.

Influence

Macrobius was a standard school authority through the Middle Ages, and the lunar crater Macrobius is named after him.
