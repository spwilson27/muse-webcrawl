FMR1

FMR1 (fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein 1, formerly fragile X mental retardation 1) is a human gene that codes for the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein (FMRP), an RNA-binding protein most commonly found in the brain and testes, essential for normal cognitive development and female reproductive function.

Function

FMRP binds to messenger RNAs and regulates their transport and translation at synapses, acting largely as a translational repressor. It is involved in synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory. The gene is located on the long arm of the X chromosome at Xq27.3.

Trinucleotide repeat expansion

The FMR1 gene contains a CGG trinucleotide repeat in its 5′ untranslated region. In most people the repeat length is between about 5 and 44. Expansions into the premutation range (55–200 repeats) are associated with fragile X-associated tremor/ataxia syndrome (FXTAS) and primary ovarian insufficiency. Full mutations of more than 200 repeats cause hypermethylation and silencing of the gene, so little or no FMRP is produced.

Fragile X syndrome

Loss of FMRP causes fragile X syndrome, the most common inherited cause of intellectual disability and the most common monogenic cause of autism. The syndrome takes its name from the fragile site visible on the X chromosome in affected cells cultured in folate-deficient medium, described following work by Herbert Lubs and later researchers in the 1970s–1990s; the gene and its repeat expansion were identified in 1991.

Clinical testing

Diagnosis is by DNA testing measuring CGG repeat number and methylation status, typically by PCR and Southern blot. Carriers of premutations may be identified through cascade testing in families.
