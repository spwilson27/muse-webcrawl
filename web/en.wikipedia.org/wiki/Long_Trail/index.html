Long Trail

The Long Trail is a hiking trail located in Vermont, running the length of the state. It is the oldest long-distance trail in the United States, constructed between 1910 and 1930 by the Green Mountain Club. The trail runs 273 miles (439 km) through the Green Mountains, from the Massachusetts–Vermont border at Clarksburg, Massachusetts, to the Canada–United States border at Highwater, Québec, crossing Vermont's principal peaks including Camel's Hump, Mount Mansfield, Killington Peak, and Jay Peak.

History

The trail was conceived by James P. Taylor, a teacher at Vermont Academy in Saxtons River, who founded the Green Mountain Club in 1910 to build it. Construction was completed in 1930, with the final section near Jay Peak finished that year. The Long Trail's success inspired Benton MacKaye's proposal for the Appalachian Trail in 1921; the two trails coincide for about 100 miles in southern Vermont.

Route and use

Thru-hikers typically need 20–30 days. The trail is generally open year-round but mud season closures apply to fragile alpine areas in spring. About 70 shelters and lodges, maintained by the Green Mountain Club and its caretakers, line the route. "End-to-enders" who complete the entire trail are recognised by the club.
