Silver carp

The silver carp (Hypophthalmichthys molitrix) is a species of freshwater cyprinid fish native to China and eastern Siberia, from the Amur River in the north to the Xi Jiang River in the south. Although a threatened species in its natural habitat, it has long been cultivated in China as one of the "Four Famous Domestic Fishes" of polyculture. By weight, more silver carp are produced worldwide in aquaculture than any other species of fish except grass carp.

Description and feeding

Silver carp are deep-bodied, laterally compressed fish with a silvery coloration. Their eyes are set unusually low on the head. They are specialised filter feeders: the gill rakers are fused into a sponge-like structure, allowing them to consume phytoplankton and fine particles. Adults typically reach 60–100 cm (24–39 in) in length and can exceed 45 kg (100 lb) in exceptional cases.

Invasive species

Silver carp were imported into North America in the 1970s to control algae in aquaculture and municipal wastewater facilities. They escaped into the Mississippi River system, where they have become a notorious invasive species, outcompeting native fish. They are famous for leaping several metres out of the water when startled by boat motors, posing a hazard to boaters and water-skiers. Efforts to prevent their entry into the Great Lakes include electric barriers in the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal and commercial harvest programmes.
