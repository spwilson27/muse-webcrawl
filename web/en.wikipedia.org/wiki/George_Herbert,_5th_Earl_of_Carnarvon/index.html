George Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon

George Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon (26 June 1866 – 5 April 1923) was an English aristocrat and financial backer of the excavation of Tutankhamun's tomb.

Born: Highclere Castle, Hampshire
Died: Cairo, Egypt
Seat: Highclere Castle

George Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon, styled Lord Porchester until 1890, was an English peer best known as the financial backer of the search for and excavation of Tutankhamun's tomb in the Valley of the Kings.

Life

Carnarvon inherited the earldom and the Highclere Castle estate in Hampshire (now famous as the filming location of Downton Abbey) in 1890. After a serious motoring accident in Germany in 1903 damaged his health, he wintered in Egypt, where he became an enthusiastic amateur Egyptologist and racehorse owner — his stud bred several classic winners.

Tutankhamun

From 1907 Carnarvon employed the archaeologist Howard Carter to supervise excavations in Thebes. After years of unproductive seasons in the Valley of the Kings, Carnarvon nearly withdrew funding in 1922; Carter persuaded him to finance one final season. In November 1922 Carter discovered the steps leading to the tomb of Tutankhamun, and Carnarvon was present when the burial chamber was opened in February 1923.

Death and the "curse"

Just weeks later, Carnarvon died in Cairo on 5 April 1923 after an infected mosquito bite and a cut sustained while shaving led to blood poisoning and pneumonia. His sudden death fuelled newspaper legends of a "curse of the pharaohs" — popularly the "curse of Tutankhamun" — which attached itself to several subsequent deaths of people connected with the tomb.
