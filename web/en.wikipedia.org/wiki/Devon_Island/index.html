Devon Island

Devon Island (Inuktitut: Tatlurutit) is an island in Nunavut, Canada. It is located in Baffin Bay, off the east coast of Ellesmere Island's Grinnell Peninsula. It is the largest uninhabited island (no permanent residents) in the world, covering about 55,247 km², and the 27th-largest island in the world overall.

The island's harsh polar desert environment and the 23 km-wide Haughton impact crater — formed about 39 million years ago — make it a valuable analogue for Mars. Since 1997 the Haughton–Mars Project research station has supported studies of Mars-like terrain and field training for exploration technologies. A short-lived RCMP outpost at Dundas Harbour operated in the 1920s–1950s, and the island was named by William Edward Parry after Devon, England.

Wildlife includes muskoxen and small bird colonies; the island is classified as a polar desert with very low precipitation.
