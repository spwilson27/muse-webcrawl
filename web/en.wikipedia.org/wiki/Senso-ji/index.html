Sensō-ji

Sensō-ji (also known as Asakusa Kannon Temple) is an ancient Buddhist temple in Asakusa, Taitō, Tokyo. It is Tokyo's oldest temple and one of its most significant, dedicated to Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. The temple draws some 30 million visitors a year, making it among the most visited spiritual sites in the world.

Legend and history

According to legend, in the year 628 two fishermen brothers, Hinokuma Hamanari and Takenari, found a small statue of Kannon in their net in the Sumida River. Their village chief, Hajino Nakatomo, recognized its sanctity, converted to Buddhism, and remade his house into a small temple so villagers could worship the statue. The temple was formally founded in 645.

During the Edo period the temple flourished under the patronage of the Tokugawa shogunate. Much of the complex was destroyed in the air raids of the Second World War and later rebuilt, becoming a symbol of Tokyo's rebirth.

Grounds

Visitors approach through the Kaminarimon ("Thunder Gate"), famous for its enormous red paper lantern and statues of the guardian gods Fujin and Raijin. Beyond it runs Nakamise-dōri, a shopping street of stalls selling traditional crafts and snacks that has served pilgrims for centuries. A second gate, the Hōzōmon, leads to the main hall and a five-story pagoda. The temple's largest festival, Sanja Matsuri, is held each May.
