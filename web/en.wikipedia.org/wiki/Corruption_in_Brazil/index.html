Corruption in Brazil

Corruption in Brazil is a long-standing problem affecting politics, business and public administration. Brazil consistently ranks in the middle tiers of Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index, and a series of major scandals since the return to democracy in 1985 have toppled ministers, legislators and presidents.

Major scandals

Mensalão

The mensalão ("big monthly allowance") scandal, exposed in 2005, revealed that the governing Workers' Party (PT) had paid monthly bribes to deputies in Congress in exchange for votes. In the 2012 trial (AP 470), the Supreme Federal Court convicted senior figures including former presidential chief of staff José Dirceu.

Operation Car Wash

Operation Car Wash (Operação Lava Jato), begun in 2014, uncovered a vast scheme in which executives of the state oil company Petrobras took bribes from construction firms such as Odebrecht (now Novonor) in exchange for inflated contracts, channelling money to politicians and parties. The investigation, led for a time by judge Sergio Moro, produced hundreds of convictions and billions of dollars in recovered funds, and implicated politicians across Latin America through Odebrecht's dedicated bribery division. It also generated controversy: leaked messages published in 2019 raised questions about collusion between prosecutors and Moro, and the Supreme Court later annulled former president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's convictions on jurisdictional grounds and declared Moro biased.

Institutions and law

Brazilian anti-corruption institutions include the Federal Public Ministry (prosecution service), the Federal Police, the Comptroller General (CGU) and the Federal Court of Accounts (TCU). The Clean Records Act (Ficha Limpa, 2010) bars convicted politicians from office, and the Clean Company Act (2014) introduced corporate liability for corrupt acts.
