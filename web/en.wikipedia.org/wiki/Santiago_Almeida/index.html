Santiago Almeida (25 July 1911 – 8 July 1999) was a Mexican-American musician celebrated as the pioneering bajo sexto player of Texas-Mexican conjunto music. His rhythmic twelve-string bass-guitar accompaniment to accordionist Narciso Martínez on recordings made from 1935 onwards created the template for the conjunto sound that would dominate Tejano music for the rest of the century.

Life and career

Almeida was born in Skidmore, Texas, into a family of musicians; by his teens he was playing with his family's group, Los Hermanos Almeida, around South Texas. In the mid-1930s he teamed up with accordion player Narciso Martínez, and the duo began recording for the Bluebird label in 1935–36. Their records — polkas, waltzes, schottisches, redovas and mazurkas with Spanish-language titles such as "La Chicharronera" — were among the first commercially successful conjunto recordings.

Martínez's light, fast accordion treble was anchored by Almeida's innovative bajo sexto work: rather than merely strumming chords, he played bass runs and chordal figures in a driving, rhythmic style that gave the duo a full-band sound. The Martínez–Almeida partnership became the model every conjunto followed: accordion plus bajo sexto as the core, later joined by tololoche (upright bass) and drums.

Later years and legacy

In the 1940s Almeida moved with his family to the Pacific Northwest as a migrant agricultural worker, eventually settling in Sunnyside, Washington, where he continued to play locally for decades while working in the fields and canneries. Long overlooked outside the conjunto tradition, he received the National Endowment for the Arts' National Heritage Fellowship in 1993, the United States government's highest honour in folk and traditional arts, and was inducted into the Conjunto Music Hall of Fame in 2005 (posthumously).

Almeida died in Sunnyside in 1999. His partnership with Martínez is documented on reissue collections of early conjunto recordings, and his bajo sexto technique remains foundational for conjunto, norteño and Tejano musicians.
