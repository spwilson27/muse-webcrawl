Drawing (manufacturing)

Drawing is a metalworking process that uses tensile forces to stretch (elongate) metal, glass, or plastic. As the material is drawn (pulled), it stretches, thins, and takes the shape defined by a die. Drawing is classified into two types: sheet metal drawing and wire, bar, and tube drawing.

Process

In wire drawing, wire is pulled through a series of dies of decreasing diameter, often with lubrication to reduce friction. Drawing is usually performed at room temperature and is therefore a cold-working process, although it may be done at elevated temperatures for large workpieces. Deep drawing is a sheet-metal variant in which a flat blank is formed into a hollow shape, such as a cup or can, by a punch pressing it into a die.

Applications

Drawn products include wire and cable, tubes, cans, kitchen sinks, and automotive body panels. Glass drawing is used to produce optical fibres.
