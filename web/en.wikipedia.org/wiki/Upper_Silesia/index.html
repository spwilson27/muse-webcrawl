Upper Silesia (Polish: Górny Śląsk; German: Oberschlesien; Silesian: Gōrny Ślōnsk) is the southeastern part of the historical and geographical region of Silesia, situated mostly in modern Poland with a smaller part in the Czech Republic. It lies along the upper Oder River, centered on the Upper Silesian Industrial Region around Katowice — one of Europe's largest conurbations and historically a heartland of coal mining and steel production.

History

In the early Middle Ages the region was settled by Slavic tribes and contested between Poland and Bohemia. It fragmented into the Silesian Piast duchies (Opole, Racibórz, Teschen) and passed with the Bohemian crown to the Habsburgs in 1526. Prussia seized most of Silesia from Austria in the Silesian Wars of the 1740s, leaving only Austrian Silesia (around Troppau/Opava and Teschen/Cieszyn) under Vienna. Nineteenth-century industrialization transformed Upper Silesia into the German Empire's second great industrial district after the Ruhr, drawing a mixed Polish-, German-, and Czech-speaking workforce.

After the First World War, the region was claimed by both Germany and the reborn Poland. The 1921 Upper Silesia plebiscite and the three Silesian Uprisings led the League of Nations to partition the territory in 1922: the eastern, industrial part went to Poland as the autonomous Silesian Voivodeship, while the west remained German. After the Second World War, nearly all of Upper Silesia was transferred to Poland at Potsdam, and most of the German population was expelled westward. A substantial population today identifies as Silesian, and the Silesian language/dialect remains widely spoken.

Geography and cities

Upper Silesia comprises most of Poland's Silesian and Opole voivodeships and the eastern Moravian-Silesian Region of Czechia. Its principal cities include Katowice, Opole, Bytom, Gliwice, Zabrze, Tychy, Rybnik, and — on the Czech side — Ostrava (partly) and Opava. The Upper Silesian metropolitan area is Poland's largest urban agglomeration.

Economy

The region was built on hard coal, zinc, lead, and steel. Since 1989, mine closures and restructured heavy industry have driven a shift toward automotive manufacturing (Opel/Stellantis in Gliwice, Fiat/Stellantis in Tychy), logistics, and services, though mining around the Jastrzębie and Rybnik coal basins continues, and energy transition remains the region's central economic debate.
