Pannonian Basin

The Pannonian Basin, or Carpathian Basin, is a large sedimentary basin in Central and Eastern Europe, enclosed by the Carpathian Mountains to the north and east, the Alps to the west, and the Dinaric Alps to the south and southwest. The Danube River flows through it, dividing the plain roughly in two. The basin underlies most of Hungary as well as parts of Slovakia, Austria, Slovenia, Croatia, Serbia, Romania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Ukraine.

Geology

The basin formed during the Miocene by extension and subsidence as the Carpathian arc migrated and the Paratethys Sea retreated. For a time much of the basin was filled by the brackish Pannonian Sea (Lake Pannon), whose sediments — locally several kilometres thick — are the source of the region's oil and natural gas deposits and its deep aquifers of thermal water. The present landscape features the Great Hungarian Plain (Alföld) in the east and the Little Hungarian Plain (Kisalföld) in the northwest, separated by the low Transdanubian Mountains.

Climate and settlement

The basin has a continental climate with hot summers and cold winters, and its fertile loess and alluvial soils support major grain and wine production. It takes its name from the Roman province of Pannonia. Budapest, Vienna (at its edge), Bratislava, Zagreb, Belgrade and Novi Sad are among its major cities.
