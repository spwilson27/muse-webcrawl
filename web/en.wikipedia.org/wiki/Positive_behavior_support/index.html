Positive behavior support

Positive behavior support (PBS) is an approach to managing challenging behavior based on applied behavior analysis, emphasizing prevention, teaching of replacement skills, and environmental redesign rather than punishment. It grew out of work in the 1980s and 1990s on alternatives to aversive interventions for people with developmental disabilities.

Principles and methods

PBS begins with a functional behavior assessment to determine what purpose a problem behavior serves for the individual. Intervention plans then modify antecedents and settings, teach functionally equivalent replacement behaviors and broader skills, and rearrange consequences so that appropriate behavior is reinforced. Person-centered values — self-determination, inclusion, and quality of life — are central to the approach.

School-wide positive behavioral interventions and supports

In education, the related framework of school-wide positive behavioral interventions and supports (SWPBIS or PBIS) applies PBS at a whole-school level through a multi-tiered system: universal expectations and reinforcement for all students (tier 1), targeted supports for at-risk groups (tier 2), and individualized plans (tier 3). It has been widely adopted in United States schools and is recognized in federal education law.
