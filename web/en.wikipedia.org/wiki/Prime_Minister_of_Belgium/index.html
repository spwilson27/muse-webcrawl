Prime Minister of Belgium

Style: His/Her Excellency

Residence: 16 Rue de la Loi, Brussels

Appointer: The King of the Belgians

Formation: 1831

The prime minister of Belgium (Dutch: Eerste minister van België; French: Premier ministre de Belgique; German: Premierminister von Belgien) or premier of Belgium is the head of the federal government of Belgium, and the most powerful person in Belgian politics.

Although leaders of government (chiefs of the cabinet) had been appointed since the independence of the country, until 1918 the King of the Belgians often presided over the Council of Ministers, so the modern era of the "premiership" started after World War I with Léon Delacroix. The political importance of the Crown has declined over time, whereas the position of prime minister has gradually become more important.

History

Since the independence of Belgium in 1830, governments have been named after the minister who formed the government. In 1830, the title used was "head of cabinet" (chef de cabinet). After the First World War, the title "prime minister" came into common use. The office is appointed by the monarch, usually after the formation of a coalition government following federal elections.

Role

The prime minister heads the Council of Ministers, coordinates the policies of the government departments, and represents the federal government in Belgium and abroad. The official residence and office is at 16 Rue de la Loi ("Wetstraat 16") in Brussels, close to the Belgian Parliament.

Because Belgium is a federal state with strong linguistic divisions, the prime minister often acts as a mediator between the Dutch-speaking Flemish and French-speaking Walloon communities. By constitutional practice, the Council of Ministers is composed of an equal number of Dutch-speaking and French-speaking ministers, with the prime minister counted as linguistically neutral.
