Ludwig Müller (23 June 1883 – 31 July 1945) was a German theologian and Nazi supporter who became Reich Bishop (Reichsbischof) of the German Evangelical Church in 1933. As head of the pro-Nazi "German Christians" movement, he attempted to unify Germany's Protestant churches under National Socialist control.

Early life and career

Müller was born in Gütersloh in Westphalia, studied theology, and served as a naval chaplain during the First World War. He later became a military district pastor in Königsberg, East Prussia, where he met and supported Adolf Hitler and joined the Nazi movement early.

Reich Bishop

After Hitler came to power in 1933, Müller — with the regime's backing — was elected Reich Bishop of the newly unified German Evangelical Church in September 1933. His programme sought to align the church with Nazi ideology, including introducing the "Aryan paragraph" into church law and removing Jewish elements from Christianity. His authoritarian measures provoked fierce opposition: pastors led by Martin Niemöller formed the Pastors' Emergency League, and in 1934 the Confessing Church declared itself the legitimate Protestant church at the Barmen Synod.

Decline and death

Müller's incompetence and scandals steadily eroded his authority; from 1935 the regime governed church affairs largely through the Reich Ministry for Church Affairs under Hanns Kerrl, leaving Müller a figurehead. He died in Berlin on 31 July 1945, shortly after the end of the Second World War, reportedly by suicide.
