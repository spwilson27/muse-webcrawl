Kansas City-style barbecue

Kansas City-style barbecue is a regional style of barbecue from the Kansas City metropolitan area (in both Kansas and Missouri), characterised by slow-smoking a wide variety of meats over hickory and oak and serving them with a thick, sweet, tomato-and-molasses-based sauce. Kansas City calls itself the "barbecue capital of the world", with more than 100 barbecue restaurants in the metro area.

Characteristics

Unlike styles built around a single meat, Kansas City barbecue embraces pork ribs, beef brisket, pulled pork, chicken, turkey, sausage, ham and mutton. Meats are rubbed with spices, smoked low and slow, and usually finished with sauce. The style's signature dish is burnt ends — caramelised cubes cut from the point of a smoked brisket, re-seasoned and cooked further until crisp and tender. The classic sauce is rich and sweet, based on tomatoes and molasses, though many restaurants offer spicier variants.

History

The style traces to Henry Perry, who began selling slow-smoked meats from a street cart around 1908 and opened a restaurant in the Garment District; his protégés included Charlie Bryant and George Gates, founders of Arthur Bryant's and Gates Bar-B-Q, two enduring institutions. Ollie Gates, Calvin Trillin's 1970s magazine praise, and the founding of the Kansas City Barbeque Society (1985) helped spread the style; the American Royal World Series of Barbecue in Kansas City is among the world's largest barbecue competitions.

Notable restaurants

Historic names include Arthur Bryant's, Gates Bar-B-Q, Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que (opened in a gas station in 1996), LC's, Jack Stack and Q39. U.S. presidents and visiting celebrities have famously been photographed at Arthur Bryant's.
