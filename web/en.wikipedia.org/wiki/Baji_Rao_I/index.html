Baji Rao I (born Bajirao Bhat; 18 August 1700 – 28 April 1740) was the seventh Peshwa (prime minister) of the Maratha Empire, serving Chhatrapati Shahu I from 1720 until his death. Regarded as one of the greatest cavalry generals of Indian history, he is credited with expanding the Maratha polity from a Deccan kingdom into an empire spanning much of the Indian subcontinent, and is said never to have lost a battle in a twenty-year career.

Early life and appointment

Baji Rao was the eldest son of Balaji Vishwanath, the Peshwa who had consolidated Shahu's rule. On his father's death in 1720, the twenty-year-old Baji Rao was appointed Peshwa despite opposition from senior courtiers. He persuaded Shahu that the decaying Mughal Empire should be struck "at the trunk of the withering tree", and that Maratha power should expand northward beyond the Narmada.

Campaigns

Baji Rao's generalship relied on fast-moving cavalry, living off the land, and avoiding pitched battle against superior forces until he chose the ground. His triumphs included:

  • Palkhed (1728) — a masterly campaign of manoeuvre that trapped and defeated the Nizam of Hyderabad, Asaf Jah I, forcing the Treaty of Mungi Shegaon and securing Maratha revenue rights in the Deccan.
  • Malwa and Bundelkhand (1728–29) — he relieved the Bundela raja Chhatrasal against Mughal attack; in gratitude Chhatrasal gave him territory and his daughter Mastani entered Baji Rao's household.
  • Delhi (1737) — dashing past Mughal forces, his cavalry raided the outskirts of the imperial capital, humiliating the Mughal court.
  • Bhopal (1737–38) — he defeated a combined Mughal–Nizam army, winning the Treaty of Doraha Sarai and Maratha control of Malwa.

He also secured the Konkan coast against the Portuguese and the Siddis, culminating in the capture of Vasai (Bassein) by his brother Chimaji Appa in 1739. At home he shifted the Peshwa's seat to Pune, building the Shaniwar Wada palace in 1730.

Personal life and death

Baji Rao's relationship with Mastani — daughter of Chhatrasal, celebrated for her beauty and martial skill — caused lasting controversy with his family and the orthodox Pune court. In April 1740, while campaigning in the north, he fell ill with fever and died suddenly at Raverkhedi on the banks of the Narmada, aged 39. He was succeeded as Peshwa by his son Balaji Baji Rao (Nanasaheb).

Legacy

Military historians rank his campaigns alongside those of the great captains of the age; British field marshal Bernard Montgomery later praised his Palkhed campaign as a masterpiece of strategic mobility. His life has been retold in novels, television and the 2015 Hindi film Bajirao Mastani.
