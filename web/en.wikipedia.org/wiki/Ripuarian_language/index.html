Ripuarian (German: Ripuarisch) is a group of Central Franconian dialects of the German language spoken in the Rhineland, roughly between the Benrath line in the north and the Moselle in the south, centred on Cologne, Bonn, Aachen and Düren, and extending into small areas of Belgium and the Netherlands. The name refers to the Ripuarian Franks who settled along the Middle Rhine in late antiquity. The best-known variety is Kölsch, the dialect of Cologne.

Features

Ripuarian dialects participated only partly in the High German consonant shift, which gives them a transitional character between Low Franconian and the High German dialects to the south. They are famous among German dialects for their use of tone — a pitch accent distinguishing word meanings — shared with neighbouring Limburgish and Moselle Franconian. Characteristic features include distinctive vowel systems, the palatalisation of certain consonants in Kölsch, and a lexicon rich in words of Latin and French origin absorbed over two millennia of Rhineland history.

Varieties

Almost every town and village traditionally had its own Ripuarian variety, differing in pronunciation and vocabulary over short distances. Notable varieties include Kölsch (Cologne), the Bönnsch of Bonn, the Öcher Platt of Aachen, and the dialects of the Eifel and Bergisches Land margins. The westernmost varieties shade into Limburgish in the Dutch province of Limburg and around Kerkrade, where the local dialect, Kirchröadsj Plat, is closely related.

Status and use

Ripuarian is not officially recognised as a separate language in Germany and has no standard written form, though Kölsch has a codified spelling developed by the Akademie för uns Kölsche Sproch. The dialects remain vigorous in carnival culture — Cologne's Karneval songs are performed in Kölsch by bands such as Bläck Fööss and Brings — in dialect theatre, and in everyday speech, even as Standard German dominates education and media and younger speakers increasingly shift toward a regional colloquial German.
