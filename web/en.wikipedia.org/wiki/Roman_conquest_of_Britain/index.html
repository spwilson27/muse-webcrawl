Roman conquest of Britain


Date: AD 43 – 84 (main phase)
Commander: Aulus Plautius (initial invasion)
Result: Roman province of Britannia established

The Roman conquest of Britain began in AD 43 under Emperor Claudius and continued as a process of expansion and consolidation for decades thereafter. Julius Caesar had raided Britain in 55 and 54 BC, but the permanent conquest came nearly a century later, when Claudius — a new emperor in need of military prestige — ordered the invasion under the general Aulus Plautius.

The invasion of AD 43

An army of some 40,000 men, including the legions II Augusta, IX Hispana, XIV Gemina, and XX Valeria Victrix, landed in Kent, probably at Richborough. After defeating the Catuvellauni under Togodumnus and Caratacus at the battles of the Medway and the Thames, the Romans paused for Claudius himself to arrive with elephants and reinforcements to lead the final advance on Camulodunum (Colchester), the tribal capital. Claudius returned to Rome for a triumph; Plautius became the first governor of the new province.

Expansion and resistance

Subsequent governors pushed the frontier west and north. Ostorius Scapula campaigned against the tribes of Wales and the Brigantes; Caratacus was finally captured in 51 and paraded in Rome. In AD 60–61 the province nearly collapsed in the revolt of Boudica of the Iceni, whose forces burned Colchester, London, and St Albans before Suetonius Paulinus defeated her. Under governors such as Petillius Cerialis, Julius Frontinus, and Gnaeus Julius Agricola, Roman armies subdued Wales (by 78) and advanced deep into Scotland. Agricola's victory at Mons Graupius (83/84) over the Caledonians under Calgacus marked the furthest extent of the conquest, but Scotland was never permanently held.

Consolidation

Emperor Hadrian ordered the construction of Hadrian's Wall (from 122) across northern England to mark and defend the frontier; the Antonine Wall further north was occupied only briefly. Southern Britain became a prosperous, Romanised province of towns, villas, roads, and baths. Roman rule lasted until the early fifth century, when imperial authority in Britain ended around 410.
