Energy condition

In relativistic classical field theories of gravitation, particularly general relativity, an energy condition is a criterion that places restrictions on the allowable "reasonable" matter content of the theory. Energy conditions are not physical constraints imposed by the theory itself, but rather mathematically convenient a priori assumptions about the stress–energy tensor that allow many important results — such as the singularity theorems and the laws of black hole thermodynamics — to be proven in broad generality.

Motivation

In general relativity, the Einstein field equations relate spacetime geometry to the stress–energy tensor of matter. Many matter models are idealizations, and the known exact solutions include some with unphysical features. Energy conditions capture widely accepted properties of ordinary matter — for example, that energy density is non-negative and that energy does not flow faster than light — in a form that can be applied to any stress–energy tensor, whether or not it comes from a specific Lagrangian model.

The standard conditions

Null energy condition

The null energy condition (NEC) states that for every future-pointing null vector field, the stress–energy tensor satisfies Tabkakb ≥ 0. For a perfect fluid, this amounts to the requirement that ρ + p ≥ 0 for each principal pressure. The NEC is the weakest of the standard conditions and is a prerequisite for the others.

Weak energy condition

The weak energy condition (WEC) requires that the energy density measured by any observer be non-negative: Tabtatb ≥ 0 for every timelike vector t. For a perfect fluid, this means ρ ≥ 0 and ρ + p ≥ 0.

Dominant energy condition

The dominant energy condition (DEC) strengthens the WEC by requiring that, for every observer, the local energy flow vector be non-spacelike — that is, energy cannot flow faster than light. For a perfect fluid it requires ρ ≥ |p|.

Strong energy condition

The strong energy condition (SEC) requires that (Tab − ½T gab)tatb ≥ 0 for every timelike vector; through the Einstein equations this expresses the attractive character of gravity for ordinary matter. For a perfect fluid it requires ρ + p ≥ 0 and ρ + 3p ≥ 0.

Violations and applications

Several physically interesting situations violate one or more energy conditions: a positive cosmological constant violates the SEC; inflationary scalar fields violate the SEC; Casimir-type quantum effects can violate even the NEC locally; and hypothetical traversable wormholes and warp drives require NEC violation ("exotic matter"). The conditions underpin Hawking and Penrose's singularity theorems, the Penrose inequality, and the classical area theorem for black holes. Quantum energy inequalities provide weaker, averaged replacements in semiclassical gravity.
