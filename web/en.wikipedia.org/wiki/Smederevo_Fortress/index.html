Smederevo Fortress

Smederevo Fortress (Serbian: Смедеревска тврђава, Smederevska tvrđava) is a medieval fortified city in Smederevo, Serbia, on the right bank of the Danube. Built between 1428 and 1430 by Despot Đurađ Branković, it served as the temporary capital of the Serbian Despotate and was one of the largest lowland fortresses in southeastern Europe.

Construction

After Belgrade was returned to Hungary under treaty obligations, Đurađ Branković built a new capital at Smederevo, near the confluence of the Jezava and the Danube. The fortress was raised with remarkable speed in the Byzantine style, in the shape of an irregular triangle. It comprises the Small Town (the inner citadel, once containing the despot's court) and the Large Town, enclosed by walls roughly 1.5 km in total length with 25 towers over 20 m high. The construction effort was immense and unpopular; the despot's consort Jerina, a Byzantine noblewoman, was blamed for the burden and entered folk tradition as "the damned Jerina."

History

The fortress withstood Ottoman sieges in 1439 (falling after three months, then being restored to the despot in 1444) and again in 1456 and 1459. Its final fall on 20 June 1459 marked the end of the medieval Serbian state, after which it became an Ottoman border stronghold — a role it kept for centuries, passing between Ottoman and Austrian hands several times. During the Second World War, German forces used the fortress as an ammunition depot; a massive explosion on 5 June 1941 devastated both the fortress and the town, killing thousands. Allied bombing in 1944 caused further damage.

Present day

Today the fortress is a protected cultural monument of exceptional importance and one of Serbia's best-known historic sites. The Small Town hosts concerts, festivals, and theatre performances, and the grounds serve as a public park and venue for events such as the Smederevo Autumn festival.
