Charleston Farmhouse

Charleston Farmhouse

Charleston, in the parish of Firle in East Sussex, England, is a farmhouse associated with the Bloomsbury Group. It was the home of the artists Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant from 1916 until their deaths, and a gathering place for writers, painters and intellectuals including Virginia Woolf, John Maynard Keynes, E. M. Forster and Lytton Strachey.

History

Vanessa Bell took the lease in 1916 so that Duncan Grant and David Garnett, both conscientious objectors, could work on the land as an alternative to military service during the First World War. Over the following decades Bell and Grant decorated virtually every surface of the house — walls, doors, furniture, fireplaces — in their distinctive Post-Impressionist style, and the garden was designed by Roger Fry with a mosaic pavement, ponds and sculptures.

Clive Bell, Maynard Keynes (who wrote much of The Economic Consequences of the Peace there), and Vanessa's children Julian, Quentin and Angelica Bell all lived at Charleston for periods. After Grant's death in 1978 the house passed to the Charleston Trust, which restored it and opened it to the public in 1986.

The house today

Charleston operates as a museum, gallery and festival venue, preserving the painted interiors as they were left, and hosts the Charleston Festival of literature and ideas each spring. A major extension with new galleries and a restaurant opened in 2018.
