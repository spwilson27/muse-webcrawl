Royal Lytham & St Annes Golf Club is a links golf course in Lytham St Annes, Lancashire, England, and one of the courses on the rota for The Open Championship. Founded in 1886, it received its "Royal" designation in 1926.

History

The course, hemmed in by suburban housing and a railway line rather than open dunes, is famous for its 200-plus bunkers and for a finish that includes the celebrated final holes beside the red-brick clubhouse. Bobby Jones won the Open here in 1926, famously playing a recovery from the sand at the 17th; a plaque marks the spot.

Major championships

Royal Lytham has hosted The Open Championship eleven times, with winners including Bobby Jones, Peter Thomson, Bobby Locke, Tony Jacklin, Gary Player, Seve Ballesteros (twice, in 1979 and 1988), Tom Lehman, David Duval and Ernie Els (2012). It has also staged the Women's British Open, the Ryder Cup (1961 and 1977) and the Walker Cup.
