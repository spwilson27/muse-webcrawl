Highway Trust Fund

The Highway Trust Fund is a transportation fund in the United States which receives money from a federal fuel tax of 18.3 cents per gallon on gasoline and 24.3 cents per gallon of diesel fuel, and other related excise taxes. It was established by the Highway Revenue Act of 1956 to finance the Interstate Highway System.

Accounts

The fund has two accounts: the Highway Account, which funds road and bridge construction, and the Mass Transit Account, established in 1982, which receives a portion of the fuel tax to support public transportation.

Solvency

Since 2001, spending from the fund has exceeded its revenue, as the federal fuel tax has not been raised since 1993 and vehicle fuel efficiency has improved. Congress has repeatedly transferred money from the general fund to keep the Highway Trust Fund solvent.
