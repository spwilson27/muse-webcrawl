Vision mixer


A broadcast vision mixing console

A vision mixer (American: video switcher) is a device used to select between several different live video sources and, in some cases, compositing them together to create visual effects. In North America, the person operating the device is called a technical director, while in British and Commonwealth usage the operator is the vision mixer and the device itself is also called a production switcher.

Operation

The operator works in a production control room (the gallery), following the director's commands to cut or transition between cameras, graphics and recorded sources as a programme is broadcast or recorded. Basic operations include cuts, mixes (dissolves), wipes, and keying, in which one image is inserted into another using chroma key or luminance key.

Equipment

Professional vision mixers are divided into mix/effects (M/E) banks with rows of crosspoint buttons for program and preview buses. Modern systems add digital video effects, still and clip stores, and control of graphics and replay devices.

History

Vision mixing emerged with multi-camera television in the 1930s–1950s; early switchers were mechanical or relay-based, and pioneering designs came from broadcast manufacturers such as Grass Valley, Sony and Ross Video.
