Tactical role-playing game

A tactical role-playing game (abbreviated TRPG or SRPG, for strategy role-playing game) is a video game genre that combines core elements of role-playing games with those of tactical strategy games. Battles take place on a map, often divided into a grid, where the player moves individual units and orders them to attack, defend, use abilities, or cast spells, with positioning, terrain, and unit statistics determining the outcome.

Gameplay

Unlike conventional RPGs, where combat may be menu-driven in a separate arena, tactical RPGs emphasize spatial manoeuvre: height advantage, flanking, cover, movement ranges, and area-of-effect attacks. Between battles, players typically manage a roster of characters whose classes, equipment, and experience develop over the course of a story-driven campaign.

History

The genre crystallized in Japan with Nintendo's Fire Emblem series (1990) and titles such as Tactics Ogre and Final Fantasy Tactics (1997), the latter widely regarded as a landmark. In the West, the X-COM series applied similar structure to a science-fiction setting, and its 2012 revival XCOM: Enemy Unknown renewed the genre's popularity.
