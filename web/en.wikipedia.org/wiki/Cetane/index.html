Cetane is an alkane hydrocarbon with the chemical formula C16H34, systematically named hexadecane. The name "cetane" derives from cetus (Latin for whale), because the compound was first obtained from spermaceti and sperm whale oil. It is a colourless liquid that is a standard component of diesel fuel research.

Cetane number

Cetane is the reference compound for the cetane number, the standard measure of the ignition quality of diesel fuel: it ignites very readily under compression and is assigned a cetane number of 100 on the original scale. The other reference fuel, 1-methylnaphthalene (later replaced by heptamethylnonane, cetane number 15), defines the low end. Diesel fuels typically have cetane numbers of 40–55; higher values mean a shorter ignition delay, easier cold starting, and smoother, quieter combustion in compression-ignition engines. This role is the inverse of the octane rating used for petrol, where resistance to autoignition is desirable.

Properties and uses

n-Hexadecane is a straight-chain saturated hydrocarbon, insoluble in water and miscible with organic solvents. It occurs in petroleum fractions in the diesel range and can be produced by hydrogenation of palmitic acid derivatives. Beyond its use as a calibration standard for engine tests (ASTM D613), it serves as a solvent and as a model compound in studies of fuel combustion and phase-change materials.
