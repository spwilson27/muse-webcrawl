Augustinian nuns

Augustinian nuns are women religious who follow the Rule of St. Augustine. The Augustinian order is one of the oldest continuous forms of religious life for women in the Roman Catholic Church. Communities of women attached to the friars of the Order of St. Augustine (the "second order") exist alongside numerous independent congregations of Augustinian sisters devoted to teaching, nursing, and other apostolic works.

History

Communities of women living under the Rule of Augustine existed from the time of Augustine of Hippo himself, whose letter 211 addressed to a community of religious women in Hippo Regius became one of the foundational texts of the Rule. During the Middle Ages many monasteries of Augustinian canonesses and nuns were founded across Europe.

When Pope Alexander IV united various groups of hermits into the Order of Hermits of St. Augustine in 1256, communities of women were aggregated to the order as its second order, placed under the direction of the prior general. Enclosed Augustinian nuns spread particularly in Italy, Spain, and the Low Countries.

Among the most famous Augustinian nuns is Saint Rita of Cascia (1381–1457), an Italian widow who entered the Augustinian monastery of Saint Mary Magdalene in Cascia and is venerated as a patron saint of impossible causes. Saint Clare of Montefalco (1268–1308) was another notable Augustinian abbess and mystic.

Second order and congregations

The strictly enclosed nuns of the second order follow the Rule and constitutions of the Augustinian order, wearing a black habit with a leather cincture. Since the 16th century, many active congregations of Augustinian sisters have been founded for work in education and healthcare, including the Augustinian Sisters of Mercy and numerous teaching congregations in Europe, the Americas, Africa, and Asia.

The Augustinian Recollect nuns emerged from the Spanish Recollection movement of the late 16th century, associated with Mother Mariana de San José, emphasising a more austere observance. The Discalced Augustinian nuns were founded in Naples in the 17th century.

Spirituality

Augustinian spirituality emphasises life in community—"one heart and one soul on the way to God," in the words of the Rule—together with the search for God through interiority, study, and contemplation. Augustinian monasteries traditionally support themselves through prayer, work, and in some cases the production of liturgical items, altar breads, and crafts.
