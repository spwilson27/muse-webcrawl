Crooks fluctuation theorem

The Crooks relation links the work distributions of a forward process and its time reverse.

The Crooks fluctuation theorem (also known as the Crooks relation) is an equation in statistical mechanics that relates the probability distribution of the work done on a system in a forward process to the work distribution of the reverse process. It was proposed by Gavin E. Crooks in 1998 and published in 1999.

Statement

For a system driven away from equilibrium by changing a control parameter from A to B, and for the reverse protocol from B to A, the theorem states that the ratio of the probability of observing work W in the forward process to the probability of observing work −W in the reverse process equals exp[β(W − ΔF)], where β = 1/(kBT) is the inverse temperature and ΔF is the free-energy difference between the equilibrium states at B and A.

Implications

The theorem implies the Jarzynski equality, from which free-energy differences can be computed from non-equilibrium work measurements, and it is consistent with the second law of thermodynamics: on average, work must be dissipated when a system is driven, although small systems can transiently show the opposite behaviour. The Crooks relation has been tested experimentally, notably in the mechanical unfolding and refolding of single RNA molecules, where the crossing point of the forward and reverse work distributions gives the free-energy difference directly.
