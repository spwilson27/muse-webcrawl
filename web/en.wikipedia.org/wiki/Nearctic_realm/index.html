Nearctic realm
Area~22.9 million km²
ExtentNorth America and Greenland, south to the highlands of Mexico
Adjacent realmsPalearctic, Neotropical

The Nearctic realm is one of the eight terrestrial biogeographic realms recognised in biogeography. It covers most of North America, including Greenland, Canada, the United States, and the mountainous regions of Mexico; its southern boundary is usually drawn through central Mexico, south of which lies the Neotropical realm. Together with the Palearctic realm of Eurasia, it forms the Holarctic region.

Geography and climate

The realm spans environments from Arctic tundra and boreal coniferous forest (taiga) in the north, through temperate deciduous forest in the east, grasslands in the continental interior, and deserts and Mediterranean scrub in the west and south-west. The great mountain chains of the Rockies and the Appalachians shape strong climatic gradients.

Fauna and flora

Characteristic Nearctic animals include bison, pronghorn (an endemic family), beavers, raccoons, and rattlesnakes; many groups, such as deer, bears, and wolves, are shared with the Palearctic through the former Bering land bridge. The realm's flora includes rich temperate forests of oaks, pines, and maples. The World Wildlife Fund divides the Nearctic into numerous terrestrial ecoregions within biomes such as tundra, boreal forest, temperate forest, grassland, and desert.
