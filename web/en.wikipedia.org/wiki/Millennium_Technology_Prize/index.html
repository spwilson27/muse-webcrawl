Millennium Technology Prize

Awarded for: Technological innovations that improve the quality of human life
Country: Finland
Presented by: Technology Academy Finland
Prize: €1 million
First awarded: 2004

The Millennium Technology Prize is one of the world's largest technology prizes, awarded by the Technology Academy Finland to innovators whose technological innovations significantly improve the quality of human life and promote sustainable development. Awarded generally every two years, the prize is worth one million euros and is often described as the "Nobel Prize of technology".

History and laureates

The prize was first awarded in 2004 to Tim Berners-Lee, inventor of the World Wide Web. Subsequent laureates have included Shuji Nakamura, for energy-efficient LED lighting; Robert Langer, for biomaterials and controlled drug delivery; Linus Torvalds, for the Linux kernel; Michael Grätzel, for dye-sensitized solar cells; Frances Arnold, for directed evolution of enzymes; and Tuomo Suntola, for atomic layer deposition. The 2020 prize went to Shankar Balasubramanian and David Klenerman for next-generation DNA sequencing, and the 2022 prize to Martin Green for high-efficiency silicon solar cells.

Selection

Nominations are invited from universities, research institutes, and academies worldwide. An international selection committee evaluates candidates, and the board of Technology Academy Finland makes the final decision. The prize is traditionally presented by the President of Finland at a ceremony in Helsinki.
