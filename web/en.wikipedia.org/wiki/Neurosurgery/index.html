Neurosurgery or neurological surgery, known in common parlance as brain surgery, is the medical specialty concerned with the surgical treatment of disorders which affect any portion of the nervous system including the brain, spinal cord and peripheral nerves. Practitioners are called neurosurgeons.

Subspecialties

General areas of neurosurgical practice include cerebrovascular surgery (aneurysms and arteriovenous malformations), neuro-oncology (brain and spinal cord tumors), functional neurosurgery (epilepsy, movement disorders, and pain), neurotrauma, spinal surgery, skull base surgery, peripheral nerve surgery, and pediatric neurosurgery.

History

Neurosurgery emerged as a distinct specialty in the early 20th century, pioneered by figures such as Harvey Cushing, Walter Dandy, and Wilder Penfield. Key advances included the development of cerebral angiography, the operating microscope, computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging, microsurgical technique, and stereotactic radiosurgery. Modern practice increasingly combines open microsurgery with endovascular techniques, endoscopy, neuronavigation, and intraoperative monitoring.

Training of neurosurgeons is among the longest of any specialty, typically involving a residency of seven years after medical school in the United States.
