Fathers' rights movement

The fathers' rights movement campaigns for shared parenting and reform of family law.

The fathers' rights movement is a social movement made up of fathers and others interested in issues affecting fathers and children, especially in relation to family law after divorce or separation. Its members contend that fathers are deprived of their children and discriminated against by family courts, and they advocate for shared parenting as the default legal arrangement following separation.

History

The movement grew out of the divorce reforms of the 1960s and 1970s, when increasing divorce rates and maternal-preference custody doctrines (such as the "tender years" doctrine) led many fathers to organise. Groups such as Families Need Fathers in the United Kingdom (founded 1974) and various organisations in the United States, Canada, and Australia campaigned for changes in custody law. In the 2000s, the British group Fathers 4 Justice gained publicity through high-profile stunts by protesters dressed as superheroes.

Positions

Common demands of the movement include a legal presumption of equal shared parenting time, stronger enforcement of contact and visitation orders, reform of child support systems, penalties for false allegations, and greater openness in family court proceedings.

Criticism

Critics, including some feminist scholars and domestic-violence advocates, argue that blanket shared-parenting presumptions can endanger women and children in cases involving abuse, and that court outcomes broadly reflect caregiving patterns during the relationship. Disputes over the movement's claims about bias in custody statistics remain a feature of family-law debate.
