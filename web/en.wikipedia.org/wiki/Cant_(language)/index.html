Cant (language)

Cant
TypeArgot / cryptolect
ExamplesThieves' cant, Shelta, Polari
PurposeConcealment from outsiders
An early glossary of thieves' cant

A cant is the jargon or language of a group, often used to exclude or mislead people outside the group. It may also be called a cryptolect, argot, or anti-language. Cants differ from ordinary slang in that they are deliberately engineered for secrecy: familiar words are given new meanings, borrowed from other languages, or transformed by processes such as backslang and rhyming slang.

Thieves' cant

The most famous historical example is thieves' cant (also called rogues' cant or peddler's French), spoken by criminals, beggars, and vagabonds in Britain from at least the 16th century. Pamphleteers such as Thomas Harman published glossaries of it for curious and fearful readers. Many cant words passed into general English slang.

Other examples

Shelta, the traditional language of Irish Travellers, is a cant based largely on Irish with systematic distortions. Polari was used by actors, sailors, fairground workers, and gay subcultures in Britain. Occupational groups — from carnival workers (carny) to medieval stonemasons — have developed their own cants.
