Epoch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An epoch is an instant in time chosen as the origin of a particular calendar era or system of time measurement. In its original sense, from the Greek epochē (ἐποχή), meaning "pause" or "fixed point", an epoch is the reference moment from which elapsed time is counted — the "zero point" of a chronology. The word also commonly denotes a distinctive period of history characterized by particular events or conditions, though strictly speaking an epoch is a point, not a span.

An epoch is the fixed reference instant from which a time system counts.

Uses

Calendars and chronology

Every calendar era begins at an epoch. The epoch of the Christian (Gregorian and Julian) era is the traditional year of the birth of Jesus, 1 January 1 AD. The Islamic calendar counts from the Hijra of Muhammad in 622 CE; the Hebrew calendar from a traditional date of creation; the Japanese era system from the accession of each emperor. Historians and astronomers use a common epoch in the Julian day system, which counts days from noon on 24 November 4714 BC (proleptic Gregorian) in the Julian period.

Astronomy

In astronomy, an epoch specifies the exact date for which the coordinates and orbital elements of celestial objects are given, because quantities such as star positions change slowly through precession, nutation, and proper motion. Standard epochs have included B1950.0 and the current standard J2000.0 (1 January 2000, 12h Terrestrial Time). An ephemeris computed for one epoch must be transformed to another for observations made at different times.

Computing

Computer systems represent time as a count of seconds (or smaller units) from a system epoch. The Unix epoch is 00:00:00 UTC on 1 January 1970; the classic Mac OS epoch was 1 January 1904; GPS time began on 6 January 1980; and Microsoft Windows counts from 1 January 1601 in some APIs. The fixed 32-bit signed count of Unix seconds will overflow on 19 January 2038 — the "Year 2038 problem".

Geology

In the geologic time scale, an epoch is also a formal unit of time — a subdivision of a period and itself divided into ages — such as the Holocene and Pleistocene epochs of the Quaternary Period. This usage, in which "epoch" is a duration rather than an instant, is a long-established exception to the chronological sense.
