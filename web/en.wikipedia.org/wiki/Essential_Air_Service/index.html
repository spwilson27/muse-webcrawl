Essential Air Service

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Essential Air Service (EAS) is a United States federal program that subsidises scheduled commercial air service to small and rural communities that would otherwise lose airline service. It was created by the Airline Dereulation Act of 1978, which guaranteed that communities receiving certificated air service at the time of deregulation would continue to receive a minimal level of scheduled service, supported by federal subsidies administered by the Department of Transportation.

Operation

Under the program, the Department of Transportation contracts with regional carriers — selected through a competitive bidding process — to provide a specified minimum number of daily round trips from an eligible community to a hub airport, typically with aircraft of at least a minimum size. Subsidies are paid per departure and are intended to cover the carrier's operating losses on the route plus a margin of profit. Roughly 160 communities in the contiguous United States, Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico have participated in the program in recent years, with annual appropriations in the hundreds of millions of dollars.

Eligibility and reform

Eligibility rules have been tightened over time. Later legislation capped per-passenger subsidy levels — generally at $200 per passenger for communities within 210 miles of a medium or large hub — limited the number of communities that could join after certain dates, and encouraged community cost-sharing and alternative transport experiments. A related pilot, the Small Community Air Service Development Program, provides grants rather than ongoing operating subsidies. Critics argue that some subsidised routes carry very few passengers at high public cost; supporters counter that air links are essential infrastructure for remote areas, supporting medical access, business and tourism.

Alaska

In Alaska, where many communities are inaccessible by road, subsidised service and the related bypass-mail system are particularly significant, and mileage and subsidy caps applied in the contiguous states do not apply in the same way.
