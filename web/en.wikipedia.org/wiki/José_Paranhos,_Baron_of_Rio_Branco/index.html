José Paranhos, Baron of Rio Branco

José Maria da Silva Paranhos, Baron of Rio Branco (20 April 1845 – 10 February 1912) was a Brazilian diplomat, geographer, historian, and politician. As Minister of Foreign Affairs (1902–1912) under four presidents, he peacefully settled Brazil's borders with nearly all its neighbours through negotiation and arbitration, greatly expanding and consolidating Brazilian territory, and is regarded as the patron of Brazilian diplomacy.

Life

Son of the Viscount of Rio Branco, statesman of the Empire, Paranhos studied law in São Paulo and Recife, worked as journalist and consul, and served as Brazil's envoy in Berlin. Appointed foreign minister by President Rodrigues Alves in 1902, he remained almost a decade — unusual in Brazilian politics.

Boundary settlements

His achievements included the Treaty of Petrópolis (1903) acquiring Acre from Bolivia, arbitration victories over French Guiana (Amapá, 1900, while envoy) and British Guiana, and treaties fixing borders with Argentina, Colombia, Ecuador, the Netherlands (Suriname), Peru, Uruguay, and Venezuela. He favoured rapprochement with the United States and a Monroe Doctrine alignment, appointed Brazil's first cardinal-era ambassador Joaquim Nabuco to Washington, and supported Pan-American conferences.

Legacy

He was a founding member of the Brazilian Academy of Letters and president of the Brazilian Historic and Geographic Institute. The Rio Branco Institute trains Brazilian diplomats; his image appeared on currency, and the capital of Acre is named Rio Branco.
