Sports betting

Sports betting
ActivityWagering on the outcome of sporting events
IntermediariesBookmakers, betting exchanges, pari-mutuel pools, online sportsbooks
Common bet typesMoneyline, point spread, totals (over/under), parlays, props, futures

Sports betting is the activity of predicting sports results and placing a wager on the outcome. Bets are placed with a bookmaker (in person, by phone, or via an online sportsbook), through a betting exchange that matches bettors against each other, or in pari-mutuel pools in which winnings are shared from the pool after deductions.

Types of bets

  • Moneyline – a straight bet on which team or competitor wins.
  • Point spread – the favourite must win by more than a set margin; designed to balance wagering on both sides.
  • Totals (over/under) – a bet on whether the combined score exceeds a set number.
  • Parlay – several selections combined; all must win, at multiplied odds.
  • Proposition bets – wagers on specific occurrences within a game, from first scorer to novelty markets.
  • Futures – long-term bets such as the winner of a championship, placed weeks or months ahead.
  • In-play betting – wagering during the event with continuously updated odds.

Odds and the bookmaker's margin

Odds express both the implied probability of an outcome and the payout. They are quoted in fractional (e.g. 5/1), decimal (6.00), or American (+500) form. Bookmakers set prices so that the implied probabilities of all outcomes sum to more than 100 percent; the excess (the overround or vigorish) is the built-in profit margin.

Legality

Regulation varies widely. The United Kingdom has licensed bookmaking shops since the 1960s and a large regulated online market. In the United States, the Supreme Court's 2018 decision in Murphy v. NCAA struck down the federal Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act, after which most states legalised and regulated sports wagering. Many jurisdictions address problem gambling through self-exclusion schemes, advertising limits, and helplines.

History and integrity

Wagering on contests is ancient, from Greek athletics to Roman chariot racing; organised bookmaking grew with horse racing in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century Britain. Betting has periodically collided with sporting integrity, from the 1919 Black Sox Scandal in baseball to cricket's spot-fixing cases, prompting monitoring of betting patterns by leagues and regulators.
