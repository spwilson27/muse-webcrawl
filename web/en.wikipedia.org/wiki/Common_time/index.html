Common time

Common time may be written as 4/4 or with the C-shaped "common time" symbol.

Common time is a time signature in music with four quarter-note beats per measure — that is, a 4/4 metre. It is the most frequently used time signature in Western popular music and much classical music. The term refers both to the metre itself and to the symbol used to denote it: a large letter C, which historically derives not from the word "common" but from the broken circle used in mensuration signs of medieval and Renaissance notation, where the full circle indicated "perfect" triple time and the incomplete circle indicated imperfect (duple) time.

Notation

Common time may be written either as the fraction-like 4/4 or as the C symbol placed after the clef and key signature. Its half-time counterpart, 2/2, is called cut time (or alla breve) and is written as a C with a vertical stroke through it. In 4/4, a measure typically contains four quarter notes, two half notes, or one whole note, and the beats are normally grouped into two pairs, with a primary accent on the first beat and a secondary accent on the third.

Usage

Because of its prevalence in rock, pop, country, and much orchestral and chamber music, common time is often the first metre taught to beginning musicians. In marching, the standard tempo associated with common time underlies the typical march beat. Many national anthems and hymns are in common time, although a significant number are in triple metres such as 3/4.
