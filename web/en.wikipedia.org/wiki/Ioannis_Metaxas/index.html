Ioannis Metaxas

Ioannis Metaxas (12 April 1871 – 29 January 1941) was a Greek military officer and politician who served as Prime Minister of Greece from 1936 until his death, ruling as dictator under the authoritarian 4th of August Regime.

Early life and career

Born in Ithaca, Metaxas trained as an engineer officer and studied in Germany. A royalist, he opposed Eleftherios Venizelos and Greek entry into the First World War on the Allied side, and played a central role in the National Schism. After years in politics as leader of the small Freethinkers' Party, he was appointed prime minister in April 1936 amid political instability.

The 4th of August Regime

On 4 August 1936, with the backing of King George II, Metaxas suspended parliament and ruled by decree, suppressing communists, censorship and opposition parties while promoting a nationalist ideology of the "Third Hellenic Civilization."

"Ohi" and death

On 28 October 1940 Metaxas rejected Italy's ultimatum demanding occupation rights, plunging Greece into the Greco-Italian War; Greek forces repelled the invasion. His refusal is commemorated as Ohi Day. Metaxas died in Athens in January 1941, weeks before the German invasion.
