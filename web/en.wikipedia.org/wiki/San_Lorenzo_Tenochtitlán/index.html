San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán

San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán (or San Lorenzo) is an archaeological site in the Mexican state of Veracruz, the earliest known major Olmec centre. It flourished roughly 1200–900 BCE during Mesoamerica's Early Formative period, and is famed for its colossal stone heads.

San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán
Location: Veracruz, Mexico
Culture: Olmec
Period: Early Formative, c. 1200–900 BCE
Known for: Colossal heads

Site

The site sits on a plateau above the Coatzacoalcos River system, giving control over river trade in obsidian, basalt and other goods. It covers several hundred hectares, with elite residences, workshops and monumental art.

Colossal heads

Ten colossal heads, carved from basalt boulders transported from the Tuxtla Mountains, have been found at San Lorenzo. Each depicts an individual, probably a ruler, wearing a helmet-like headdress, and weighs many tonnes.

Decline

Around 900 BCE many monuments were defaced or buried and the centre declined, with La Venta rising to prominence thereafter.

Excavations

Matthew Stirling excavated in the 1940s; later projects by Michael Coe and Ann Cyphers clarified the chronology and layout.
