Edward Bouverie Pusey (22 August 1800 – 16 September 1882) was an English Anglican clergyman, for more than fifty years Regius Professor of Hebrew at the University of Oxford. He was one of the leading figures in the Oxford Movement, a group of Anglican clergymen and dons who sought to renew the Church of England through the revival of catholic doctrine and ritual; the movement's followers came to be known as "Puseyites" after him.

Early life and education

Pusey was born at Pusey House in Berkshire, the second son of Philip Bouverie, a younger son of Jacob Bouverie, 1st Viscount Folkestone. He was educated at Eton College and Christ Church, Oxford, where he took a first-class degree in 1822 and was elected a fellow of Oriel College. Studying in Germany in the 1820s, he acquired a deep knowledge of German theology and oriental languages, then unusual in England.

The Oxford Movement

In 1828 Pusey was appointed Regius Professor of Hebrew and a canon of Christ Church. He joined John Henry Newman, John Keble, and Richard Hurrell Froude in the movement begun in 1833 to defend the apostolic character of the English Church. Pusey contributed substantial theological tracts to the Tracts for the Times, notably on baptism (1835) and the Eucharist, and his learning and wealth gave the movement intellectual weight and financial backing. His 1843 sermon on the Eucharist led to his suspension from preaching for two years, a sentence that made him a national figure.

After Newman's conversion to Roman Catholicism in 1845, Pusey became the acknowledged leader of the Anglo-Catholic party. He promoted the revival of religious communities for women, supported the restoration of confession and Eucharistic devotion, and campaigned for comprehension with other churches while firmly opposing doctrinal liberalism. His Eirenicon (1865) explored the possibility of reunion with Rome.

Legacy

Pusey died on 16 September 1882. His vast library formed the nucleus of Pusey House, Oxford, founded in his memory in 1884 as a house of theological learning within the Anglo-Catholic tradition. He is commemorated in the calendar of saints of the Church of England on 18 September.
