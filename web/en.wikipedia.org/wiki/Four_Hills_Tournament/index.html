The Four Hills Tournament (German: Vierschanzentournee) is a ski jumping event composed of four World Cup events held annually in Germany and Austria since 1953. It is one of the most prestigious competitions in ski jumping, second only to the Winter Olympics and the World Championships in tradition and public attention.

The tournament takes place over the New Year period, beginning in late December and concluding on Epiphany, 6 January. The overall winner is determined by the aggregate score across all four events rather than by World Cup points.

Venues and schedule

The four hills, in the order in which they are jumped, are:

  • Oberstdorf, Germany (Schattenbergschanze) — opening event, usually 29 or 30 December
  • Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Germany (Große Olympiaschanze) — the traditional New Year's Day competition, 1 January
  • Innsbruck, Austria (Bergiselschanze) — usually 3 or 4 January
  • Bischofshofen, Austria (Paul-Ausserleitner-Schanze) — final event on 6 January

Format

Since the 1996–97 edition, the first round of each event has used a knockout format: 50 jumpers compete in 25 head-to-head duels, with the 25 winners and the five best losers ("lucky losers") advancing to the final round of 30. The tournament ranking is decided by the total points accumulated over all eight jumps.

History

The tournament was founded in 1953 by jumpers and officials from Innsbruck and Partenkirchen. Winning all four events in a single edition — the "Grand Slam" of ski jumping — was first achieved by Sven Hannawald in 2001–02, and subsequently by Kamil Stoch (2017–18) and Ryōyū Kobayashi (2018–19). Janne Ahonen of Finland holds the record for most overall tournament victories, with five.
