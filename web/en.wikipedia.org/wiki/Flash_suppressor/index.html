Flash suppressor

A flash suppressor, also known as a flash guard, flash eliminator, flash hider, or flash cone, is a muzzle device fitted to the barrel of a rifle or other firearm to reduce the visible muzzle flash produced when the weapon is fired. It works by cooling or dispersing the burning gases that exit the muzzle, or by shielding the flash from the shooter's line of sight.

Purpose

Muzzle flash can reveal the shooter's position, damage night vision, and ignite nearby flammable material. Flash suppressors mitigate these effects but do not silence the shot or eliminate the flash entirely. Common designs include the slotted "birdcage" hider of the M16 family and pronged designs derived from early conical flash guards.

Distinction and regulation

Flash suppressors differ from muzzle brakes, which redirect gases to reduce recoil, though some devices combine both functions. Because of their association with military-style rifles, flash suppressors have been named as regulated features in some assault-weapon legislation.
