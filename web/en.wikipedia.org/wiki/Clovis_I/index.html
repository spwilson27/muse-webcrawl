Clovis I (c. 466 – 27 November 511) was the first king of the Franks to unite all of the Frankish tribes under one ruler, changing the form of leadership from a group of petty kings to rule by a single king and ensuring that the kingship was passed down to his heirs. He is considered to have founded the Merovingian dynasty, which ruled the Frankish kingdoms for the next two centuries.

Reign

Clovis succeeded his father Childeric I as king of the Salian Franks in 481/482. He defeated Syagrius, the last Roman commander in Gaul, at the Battle of Soissons (486), annexed the Alemanni after the Battle of Tolbiac, and crushed the Visigoths under Alaric II at the Battle of Vouillé (507), driving them from most of Gaul. He made Paris his capital.

Conversion to Christianity

Influenced by his wife Clotilde, a Catholic princess of Burgundy, Clovis converted from Germanic paganism to Catholic Christianity and was baptised at Reims by Saint Remigius (traditionally dated to 496). His conversion won the support of the Gallo-Roman Church and set the Franks apart from their Arian Christian neighbours.

Death and legacy

Clovis died in Paris in 511 and was buried in the Church of the Holy Apostles, which he had founded. Following Frankish custom, his kingdom was divided among his four sons, yet his conquests laid the foundations of medieval France — whose name derives from the Franks — and he is regarded as a national founder by both France and Germany.
