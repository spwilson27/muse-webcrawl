Tanenbaum–Torvalds debate

ParticipantsAndrew S. Tanenbaum and Linus Torvalds
VenueUsenet newsgroup comp.os.minix
BeganJanuary 29, 1992
SubjectMicrokernel vs. monolithic kernel design

The Tanenbaum–Torvalds debate is a famous exchange on the Usenet newsgroup comp.os.minix in early 1992 between Andrew S. Tanenbaum, creator of the MINIX operating system, and Linus Torvalds, creator of the Linux kernel, over the merits of microkernel versus monolithic kernel architectures.

The debate

Tanenbaum opened with a post titled "LINUX is obsolete", arguing that Linux's monolithic design tied to the Intel 386 was a step backward compared with the portable, modular microkernel design of MINIX. Torvalds defended his pragmatic design choices, and the thread grew to involve other figures, including Ken Thompson. The exchange is remembered for both its technical arguments about portability, performance, and design elegance and its occasionally heated tone.

Legacy

The debate became a classic of computing history, frequently cited in discussions of operating-system design. Tanenbaum later wrote that the disagreement had been a "storm in a teacup" and that he bore Torvalds no ill will, while Linux went on to become the most widely used kernel in the world.
