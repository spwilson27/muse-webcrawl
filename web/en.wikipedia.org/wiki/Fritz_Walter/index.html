Fritz Walter

Friedrich "Fritz" Walter (31 October 1920 – 17 June 2002) was a German footballer who spent his entire club career with 1. FC Kaiserslautern. An attacking midfielder and playmaker, he captained West Germany to victory at the 1954 FIFA World Cup — the "Miracle of Bern" — and is regarded as one of the greatest German footballers of all time.

Career

Walter was born in Kaiserslautern and joined his hometown club as a boy, making his first-team debut aged 17. Conscripted during the Second World War, he served as a paratrooper and was taken prisoner by Soviet forces; his footballing skill helped secure his release, and he returned home in 1945. With Kaiserslautern he won the German championship in 1951 and 1953.

For West Germany he won 61 caps and scored 33 goals between 1940 and 1958. In the 1954 World Cup final in Bern, his team came back from 2–0 down to defeat the heavily favoured Hungary 3–2; the rainy conditions became legendary as "Fritz Walter weather", since Walter — who had contracted malaria during the war — was said to play better in the rain. He also played at the 1958 World Cup in Sweden, where injury in the semi-final effectively ended his international career.

Legacy

Kaiserslautern's stadium is named the Fritz-Walter-Stadion in his honour. Walter was voted Germany's Golden Player for the UEFA Jubilee in 2003, and a widely used German idiom for heavy rain at football matches preserves his name. He died in 2002 during the World Cup in Korea and Japan, which Germany reached the final of.
