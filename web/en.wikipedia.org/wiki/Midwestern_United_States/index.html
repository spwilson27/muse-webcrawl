The Midwestern United States, also referred to as the Midwest, the Middle West, or the American Midwest, is one of four census regions of the United States. As defined by the United States Census Bureau, it comprises twelve states: Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, and Wisconsin (the East North Central division), and Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota, and South Dakota (the West North Central division). The region lies mainly in the great interior plains between the Appalachian Mountains and the Rocky Mountains, drained by the Mississippi, Missouri, and Ohio river systems and bordering the Great Lakes in the northeast.

Geography and economy

Much of the Midwest consists of flat or gently rolling terrain, including some of the most productive farmland in the world. The eastern portion forms the Corn Belt, dominated by maize and soybean production, while the drier western plains form part of the Wheat Belt and cattle country. Historically the region was also a centre of heavy industry and manufacturing around the Great Lakes — the area later nicknamed the Rust Belt — with major industrial cities including Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, Milwaukee, St. Louis, and Minneapolis–Saint Paul. Chicago, the region's largest city and a global transportation and financial hub, has long been considered its unofficial capital.

History

The region was inhabited for millennia by Indigenous peoples, including the mound-building cultures of the Ohio and Mississippi valleys. French explorers and fur traders arrived in the 17th century, and the territory passed to Britain after the French and Indian War and to the United States after the American Revolution. The Northwest Ordinance of 1787 organised the territory northwest of the Ohio River and prohibited slavery there. In the 19th century the Midwest was settled rapidly by migrants from the eastern states and by European immigrants, especially Germans and Scandinavians; the Homestead Act of 1862 opened the plains to farm settlement. The Midwest became the nation's agricultural heartland and an industrial powerhouse in the 20th century, and its states often play a pivotal role as swing states in presidential elections.

Culture

The Midwest is associated in American culture with small towns, farming communities, and a reputation for plain-spoken friendliness ("Midwestern nice"). It has a distinctive regional accent (the Inland North and North-Central dialects), major research universities organised in the Big Ten Conference, and significant contributions to American literature, architecture (the Prairie School of Frank Lloyd Wright), and music.
