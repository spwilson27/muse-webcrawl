Universal Music Group

Universal Music Group N.V. (often abbreviated as UMG and known simply as Universal Music) is a Dutch–American multinational music corporation. It is generally considered the largest music company in the world, one of the "Big Three" record labels alongside Sony Music Entertainment and Warner Music Group. UMG's corporate headquarters are located in Hilversum, Netherlands, while its operational headquarters are in Santa Monica, California.

Universal Music Group is the world's largest recorded-music company.

History

The company's origins trace to the American branch of Decca Records, founded in 1934, which later became part of MCA Inc. Through a series of mergers and acquisitions — including MCA's purchase of PolyGram in 1998, which created the modern Universal Music Group — the company assembled an unmatched catalogue of labels and recordings. The French media conglomerate Vivendi acquired full control in the early 2000s. In September 2021 Vivendi spun off 60% of UMG in an initial public offering on Euronext Amsterdam, valuing the company at roughly €45 billion; Tencent had earlier acquired a significant minority stake.

Labels and divisions

UMG operates dozens of frontline labels and imprints, including Interscope Geffen A&M, Republic Records, Capitol Records, Def Jam Recordings, Island Records, Motown, Geffen Records, Deutsche Grammophon, Decca Records, and Verve. Its music publishing arm, Universal Music Publishing Group, administers millions of copyrights. Other divisions include Bravado (merchandising), Eagle Rock Entertainment, and Universal Music Enterprises (catalogue).

Artists

UMG's roster has included many of the best-selling recording artists in history, among them Taylor Swift, The Beatles (through Capitol/Apple catalogue interests), Drake, Billie Eilish, The Weeknd, Ariana Grande, Eminem, ABBA, Queen, Elton John, Bob Marley, and the Rolling Stones' recorded catalogue for much of their career.

Market position and criticism

UMG has consistently held the largest share of the global recorded-music market, typically around 30 percent or more. The company has been criticised over royalty practices, catalogue acquisitions, and its 2008 warehouse fire — revealed publicly in 2019 — which destroyed a large archive of master recordings; a class-action lawsuit by artists followed and was later settled in part.
