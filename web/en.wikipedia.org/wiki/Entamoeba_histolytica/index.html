Entamoeba histolytica

Entamoeba histolytica
Domain: Eukaryota
Phylum: Amoebozoa
Family: Entamoebidae
Genus: Entamoeba
Disease: Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery, amoebic liver abscess)

Entamoeba histolytica is an anaerobic parasitic amoebozoan that infects humans and other primates, causing amoebiasis. It is estimated to infect 30–50 million people worldwide each year, with tens of thousands of deaths, chiefly in regions with poor sanitation. The species name histolytica ("tissue-dissolving") refers to its ability to invade and destroy host tissues.

Life cycle

Infection begins with ingestion of mature cysts in food or water contaminated with faeces. In the small intestine the cysts excyst, releasing trophozoites that migrate to the large intestine, where they feed on bacteria and multiply by binary fission. Trophozoites may invade the colonic mucosa, causing flask-shaped ulcers and bloody diarrhoea (amoebic dysentery), or enter the portal circulation and form abscesses — most often in the liver. Other trophozoites encyst as they pass down the colon, and the hardy four-nucleate cysts are excreted to continue the cycle.

Pathogenicity and diagnosis

Virulence factors include the galactose/N-acetylgalactosamine lectin mediating attachment to host cells, amoebapores that perforate cell membranes, and cysteine proteases that degrade extracellular matrix and immune proteins. Many infections are asymptomatic, and microscopy alone cannot distinguish E. histolytica from the morphologically identical, non-pathogenic E. dispar; stool antigen tests and PCR are used for definitive diagnosis.

Treatment

Invasive disease is treated with a tissue-active agent such as metronidazole or tinidazole, followed by a luminal agent (paromomycin or diloxanide furoate) to eradicate cysts. Prevention rests on clean water, sanitation and food hygiene. The organism's ~20-megabase genome, unusual in its high A+T content and abundant repetitive elements, was sequenced in 2005.
