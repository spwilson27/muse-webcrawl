Centaurus A

Centaurus A (also known as NGC 5128 or Caldwell 77) is a galaxy in the constellation of Centaurus. It was discovered in 1826 by Scottish astronomer James Dunlop from his home in Parramatta, in New South Wales, Australia. There is considerable debate in the literature regarding the galaxy's fundamental properties such as its Hubble type (lenticular galaxy or giant elliptical galaxy) and distance (10–16 million light-years).

Properties

Centaurus A is one of the closest radio galaxies to Earth, at a distance of roughly 12 million light-years, and is the fifth-brightest galaxy in the sky, making it an ideal target for amateur astronomers in the southern hemisphere. Its peculiar appearance — a bright elliptical body bisected by a warped dark dust lane — is thought to result from a merger with a smaller spiral galaxy. The centre contains a supermassive black hole with a mass estimated at about 55 million solar masses, which ejects a relativistic jet detected in X-ray and radio wavelengths; enormous radio lobes extend over a million light-years.

Supernovae and observations

Two supernovae have been observed in Centaurus A: SN 1986G, a Type Ia supernova discovered in the dust lane, and SN 2016adj. The galaxy has been studied across the electromagnetic spectrum by observatories including Chandra, Hubble, and the Very Large Telescope, and it is a target of the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration's studies of active galactic nuclei. Starburst activity in the disc produces clusters of young blue stars visible along the dust lane.
