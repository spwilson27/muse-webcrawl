Nashville is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Tennessee and the seat of Davidson County. Located on the Cumberland River in north-central Tennessee, it is widely known as "Music City" for its central role in the American music industry, particularly country music.

History

The area was settled in 1779 when James Robertson and John Donelson founded Fort Nashborough, named for Revolutionary War general Francis Nash. Nashville was incorporated as a city in 1806 and became the state capital in 1843. During the American Civil War it was the first Confederate state capital to fall to Union forces (1862) and site of the Battle of Nashville (December 1864). The city grew after the war as a rail and river trade hub.

Nashville earned the nickname "Athens of the South" for its many colleges and universities, including Vanderbilt University and Fisk University, and for the full-scale Parthenon replica built in Centennial Park for the 1897 Tennessee Centennial Exposition.

Music

The Grand Ole Opry, founded in 1925 as a radio barn dance on WSM, made Nashville the center of country music. The Ryman Auditorium, the Country Music Hall of Fame, and Music Row's recording studios followed. Artists from Elvis Presley to Dolly Parton recorded there, and the city remains a hub for songwriters across genres.

Geography and climate

Nashville lies in the Nashville Basin, surrounded by the Highland Rim. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and mild winters. The Cumberland River runs through the city; the May 2010 floods caused extensive damage.

Economy

Major sectors include music, healthcare (HCA Healthcare and many hospital companies), education, publishing, and tourism. Nissan North America is headquartered in nearby Franklin.
