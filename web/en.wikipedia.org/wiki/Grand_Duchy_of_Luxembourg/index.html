The Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, commonly known as Luxembourg, is a landlocked country in Western Europe bordered by Belgium, France and Germany. It is the world's only remaining sovereign grand duchy — a state whose head of state is a grand duke or grand duchess.

History

Luxembourg's history began with the construction of Luxembourg Castle in 963. The country became a grand duchy in 1815 under the Congress of Vienna, in personal union with the Netherlands until 1890, when the Nassau-Weilburg line inherited the throne. It was occupied by Germany in both World Wars and became a founding member of the European Communities after 1945.

Economy and society

Luxembourg has one of the world's highest GDPs per capita and is a major centre for banking and finance, as well as a seat of several European Union institutions, including the Court of Justice of the European Union. The country is trilingual: Luxembourgish, French and German are all official or administrative languages.
