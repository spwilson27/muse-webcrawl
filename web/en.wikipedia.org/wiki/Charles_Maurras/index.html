Charles-Marie-Photius Maurras (20 April 1868 – 16 November 1952) was a French author, politician, poet and critic. He was the principal theorist of integral nationalism and the organising mind of Action Française, the monarchist movement that sought the restoration of the French monarchy and exerted wide influence on the French right in the first half of the 20th century.

Charles Maurras (1868–1952)

Early life

Maurras was born in Martigues, Provence, into a bourgeois family. A childhood illness left him progressively deaf. He moved to Paris as a young journalist and became associated with the Félibrige movement for Provençal culture and with classical, order-loving literary ideals opposed to romanticism. His thought combined positivism, admiration for classical antiquity and Catholic social order — though he himself was an agnostic for much of his life, valuing the Church chiefly as an institution of order.

Action Française

During the Dreyfus Affair Maurras rose to prominence as an anti-Dreyfusard, arguing that the interests of the state and army outweighed the innocence of an individual — "politics first" (politique d'abord). In 1899 he helped transform Action Française into a monarchist movement, and from 1908 its newspaper, L'Action française, edited with Léon Daudet, became the daily voice of his doctrine: nationalism, monarchism, decentralisation and hostility to democracy, liberalism and the legacy of 1789. His slogan was that the restoration of the king was the precondition of national renewal. The movement's ideas influenced authoritarian movements across Europe, and Maurras was elected to the Académie française in 1938.

Vichy, trial and death

Maurras welcomed the defeat of 1940 as a "divine surprise" and supported Marshal Pétain's Vichy regime, though he remained hostile to Germany. Arrested after the Liberation, he was convicted in 1945 of collaboration and sentenced to life imprisonment, famously crying "C'est la revanche de Dreyfus!" ("It is Dreyfus's revenge!"). Released on health grounds in 1952, he died later that year in Tours.
