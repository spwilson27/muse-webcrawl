Hybrid log–gamma

Hybrid log–gamma (HLG) is a high-dynamic-range (HDR) transfer function developed jointly by the BBC and NHK for broadcasting high-dynamic-range television. It was standardized as ARIB STD-B67 and is included in the ITU-R BT.2100 recommendation alongside the perceptual quantizer (PQ).

Design

HLG was designed to be compatible with existing broadcast infrastructure and standard-dynamic-range (SDR) displays. The lower portion of its opto-electrical transfer function follows a conventional gamma curve, while the upper portion follows a logarithmic curve, allowing peak brightness up to 1,000 cd/m² to be encoded without metadata. Because it is scene-referred and carries no dynamic metadata, a single HLG signal can be broadcast to both HDR and SDR receivers; SDR sets simply display the signal as if it were ordinary gamma-encoded video, with a slightly altered but acceptable tone reproduction.

Adoption

HLG was the first HDR format used in live broadcast trials, including BBC iPlayer streams of sporting events and natural-history programming. It is supported by major television manufacturers and is used by broadcasters in the United Kingdom, Japan, and elsewhere for live HDR production, where its metadata-free workflow suits real-time transmission.
