Madison Hemings, born James Madison Hemings (January 19, 1805 – November 28, 1877), was an American carpenter and farmer. Enslaved from birth at Monticello, he was the son of Sally Hemings; in an 1873 memoir published in an Ohio newspaper he identified Thomas Jefferson as his father. His account, long dismissed, became central to the historical reassessment of the Jefferson–Hemings relationship.

Life

Hemings was named by Dolley Madison, according to his memoir, after a promise made to his mother. At Monticello he was trained as a carpenter under his uncle John Hemings. Freed under the terms of Jefferson's will in 1826, along with his brother Eston, he moved to Charlottesville and later to Pike County, Ohio, where he farmed, married Mary Hughes McCoy, and raised a family. Some of his children served in the Union Army during the Civil War.

Memoir and legacy

In 1873 the Pike County Republican published Hemings's memoir, in which he described his mother, her time in Paris with Jefferson, and the understanding by which her children were freed. In 1998 DNA testing showed a match between the Jefferson male line and descendants of his brother Eston Hemings; combined with documentary evidence, most historians concluded Jefferson fathered Sally Hemings's children. Hemings's descendants were recognised by the Monticello Association in the 21st century.
