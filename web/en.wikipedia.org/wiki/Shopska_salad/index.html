Shopska salad

Shopska salad (Bulgarian: шопска салата) is a cold salad of diced tomatoes, cucumbers, onions and peppers, topped with grated sirene white brined cheese. It is popular throughout the Balkans and is regarded as a national dish of Bulgaria, where its colours — white cheese over green cucumber and red tomato — evoke the Bulgarian flag.

History

The salad takes its name from the Shopi, the inhabitants of the Shopluk region around Sofia. Although similar chopped salads are old in the region, the modern Shopska salad in its standard form is often credited to chefs of the state tourist organisation Balkantourist in the 1950s, who codified it as part of a repertoire of dishes promoted to foreign visitors. It subsequently spread across Bulgaria and neighbouring countries and became a fixture of restaurant menus.

Preparation

Ripe tomatoes, cucumbers, raw or roasted green peppers, and onion are chopped and seasoned with salt and a light sunflower-oil (or olive-oil) dressing. The salad is mounded on the plate and generously covered with finely grated sirene, and is often garnished with parsley and a hot pepper or olives. In Bulgaria it is traditionally served as a starter, frequently accompanied by rakia.

Variations

Versions exist across the Balkans: in North Macedonia, Serbia, Croatia, Romania and elsewhere the same combination appears, sometimes with feta-type cheese, vinegar in the dressing, or roasted peppers. A related Bulgarian salad, Ovcharska ("shepherd's salad"), adds ham, eggs and mushrooms.
