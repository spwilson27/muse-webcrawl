Gustav Leonhardt

Gustav Leonhardt (1928–2012)
Born: 's-Graveland, Netherlands
Occupations: Harpsichordist, organist, conductor
Known for: Early music performance

Gustav Maria Leonhardt (30 May 1928 – 16 January 2012) was a Dutch keyboard player, conductor, musicologist, teacher, and editor. He was a leading figure in the historically informed performance movement, influential in restoring period instruments and performance practices to the performance of Baroque music, especially that of Johann Sebastian Bach.

Career

Leonhardt studied organ and harpsichord at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis in Basel (1947–1950) and musicology in Vienna. He made his debut as a harpsichordist in Vienna in 1950 with a performance of Bach's The Art of Fugue. In 1955 he founded the Leonhardt Baroque Ensemble, with which he made pioneering recordings of Bach cantatas. From 1971 to 1990, together with Nikolaus Harnoncourt, he recorded the complete Bach church cantatas for Teldec — a landmark of the early music movement. He also famously portrayed Bach himself in Jean-Marie Straub's 1968 film Chronicle of Anna Magdalena Bach.

Teaching and legacy

Leonhardt taught harpsichord at the Amsterdam Conservatory from 1954, and generations of leading early-music performers — including Ton Koopman, Bob van Asperen, and Christophe Rousset — studied with him. His catalogue of recordings, numbering in the hundreds, spans Frescobaldi, Froberger, Couperin, Rameau, and Bach; his recording of the Goldberg Variations (made as early as 1953) remains celebrated. He gave his final public performance in Paris in December 2011 and died in Amsterdam the following month.
