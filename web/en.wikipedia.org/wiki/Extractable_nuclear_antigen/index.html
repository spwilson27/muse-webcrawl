Extractable nuclear antigen

Extractable nuclear antigen (ENA)
TypeGroup of autoantigens
TestENA panel (ELISA, immunoblot)
Associated diseasesLupus, Sjögren syndrome, scleroderma, mixed connective tissue disease

Extractable nuclear antigens (ENAs) are a group of more than 100 different soluble cytoplasmic and nuclear antigens. They are so named because they can be extracted from the nuclei of cells with saline. Autoantibodies against ENAs are tested in the investigation of suspected systemic autoimmune (connective tissue) diseases, typically as a follow-up when an antinuclear antibody (ANA) test is positive.

Testing

ENA panels are performed by enzyme immunoassay or line immunoblot. Results are interpreted alongside the ANA titre and pattern, clinical features and other serology, as individual antibodies may occur in several diseases and, at low titre, in healthy people.
