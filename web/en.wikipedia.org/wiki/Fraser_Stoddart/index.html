Sir James Fraser Stoddart (24 May 1942 – 30 December 2024) was a British-American chemist who was Board of Trustees Professor of Chemistry and head of the Stoddart Mechanostereochemistry Group in the Department of Chemistry at Northwestern University in the United States. He worked in the area of supramolecular chemistry and nanotechnology, and shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2016 with Ben Feringa and Jean-Pierre Sauvage "for the design and synthesis of molecular machines."

Education and career

Stoddart was educated at the University of Edinburgh, receiving his BSc in 1964 and PhD in 1966. After postdoctoral work at Queen's University in Canada and the University of Sheffield, he held posts at Sheffield and the University of Birmingham before moving to the University of California, Los Angeles in 1997, and then to Northwestern University in 2008.

Research

Stoddart developed the efficient synthesis of mechanically interlocked molecular architectures such as catenanes, rotaxanes and molecular Borromean rings, exploiting donor-acceptor interactions. His group created molecular switches and shuttles that can be driven chemically, electrically or by light, laying the groundwork for artificial molecular machines, nanoelectronic devices and drug delivery systems. He was knighted in 2007 for services to chemistry and molecular nanotechnology.
