Laurence Stephen Lowry (1887–1976) was an English artist famous for paintings of industrial districts of North West England in the mid-20th century, populated by stylised human figures nicknamed "matchstick men".

Life and work

Lowry worked for nearly four decades as a rent collector for the Pall Mall Property Company in Manchester and Salford, painting in his spare time; he studied art part-time under the French impressionist Adolphe Valette. His characteristic style — a white background, limited palette of five colours, factory chimneys, terraced houses, mills and crowds — was developed after the 1920s. Works such as Coming from the Mill (1930) and Going to the Match (1953) became icons of Northern working-class life.

Recognition

He received wide recognition late in life, declined various honours including a knighthood, and holds the record for the most declined British honours. The Lowry arts centre in Salford Quays holds the world's largest public collection of his work.
