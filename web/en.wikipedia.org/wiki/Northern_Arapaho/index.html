Northern Arapaho

Northern Arapaho dancers at Wind River

The Northern Arapaho are one of the two federally recognised Arapaho tribes in the United States, the other being the Southern Arapaho of Oklahoma, with whom they form part of the Arapaho people (Hinono'eino). They are enrolled in the federally recognised Northern Arapaho Tribe and reside principally on the Wind River Indian Reservation in west-central Wyoming, which they share with the Eastern Shoshone.

History

The Arapaho, an Algonquian-speaking people, migrated onto the Great Plains, where by the early 19th century they ranged from Montana to Colorado. During the 19th century the tribe divided into northern and southern divisions. After the Medicine Lodge Treaty of 1867, the Southern Arapaho were removed to Oklahoma, while the Northern Arapaho, who had not been assigned a reservation of their own, were eventually placed in 1878 on the Shoshone reservation at Wind River — an arrangement initially intended to be temporary but made permanent.

Life at Wind River

The reservation covers more than 2.2 million acres around the Wind River Range. The tribe operates enterprises including the Wind River Casino and pursues water rights, land recovery and language revitalisation; the Arapaho language is highly endangered, and immersion programmes for children have been established. In 2020 the tribe also received a founding herd of buffalo as part of the restoration of bison to tribal lands.

Culture

Traditional Arapaho culture centred on the Sun Dance, the sacred Flat Pipe, and age-graded societies. The Northern Arapaho hold annual powwows and ceremonies, and their beadwork and quillwork traditions continue.
