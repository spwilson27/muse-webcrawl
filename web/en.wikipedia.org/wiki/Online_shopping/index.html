Online shopping

Online shopping is a form of electronic commerce in which consumers buy goods or services directly from a seller over the internet. Shoppers browse online catalogs, place items in a virtual shopping cart, and pay electronically, after which goods are delivered by post, courier, or digital download, or collected in store.

History

Early forms of electronic shopping appeared with videotex systems in the late 1970s and 1980s. The rise of the World Wide Web in the mid-1990s enabled secure consumer transactions; companies such as Amazon (founded 1994) and eBay (founded 1995) became early large-scale online retailers. Online shopping grew steadily and expanded sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic, when lockdowns pushed many consumers and businesses online.

Process and models

Retail models include business-to-consumer (B2C) stores, online marketplaces hosting third-party sellers, auction sites, and direct-to-consumer brand shops. Features that shape the experience include product search and recommendation, customer reviews, price comparison, personalized offers, and subscription replenishment services. Payment methods include credit and debit cards, digital wallets, and buy-now-pay-later services.

Advantages and disadvantages

Online shopping offers convenience, wider selection, and easy price comparison. Its disadvantages include the inability to inspect goods physically, delivery delays and costs, returns logistics, and exposure to fraud, counterfeit goods, and data breaches. The sector's growth has had wide effects on traditional retail, warehousing, and delivery employment, as well as environmental impacts from packaging and transport.

Statistics

E-commerce accounts for a substantial and growing share of retail sales worldwide, with particularly high penetration in China, South Korea, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Mobile commerce — purchases made on smartphones — now makes up the majority of online orders in many markets.
