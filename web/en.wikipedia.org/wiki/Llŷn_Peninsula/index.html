Llŷn Peninsula
Welsh: Penrhyn Llŷn
Country: Wales
Principal area: Gwynedd
Length: about 30 miles (48 km)

The Llŷn Peninsula (Welsh: Penrhyn Llŷn) extends some 30 miles (48 km) south-west from the coast of north-west Wales into the Irish Sea, largely within the county of Gwynedd. It is one of the most strongly Welsh-speaking areas of Wales and is noted for its rural, unspoilt coastline, much of which is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Geography

The peninsula is bounded by Cardigan Bay to the south and Caernarfon Bay to the north. Its landscape is a patchwork of small fields, low hills and volcanic outcrops, among them Yr Eifl (Tre'r Ceiri) and Garn Fadrun. The main town is Pwllheli, a market town and sailing centre; other settlements include Nefyn, Abersoch, Criccieth (at the peninsula's base) and the village of Aberdaron at the western tip, the traditional embarkation point for pilgrims crossing to Bardsey Island (Ynys Enlli), famed in legend as the burial place of 20,000 saints.

Culture and tourism

The Wales Coast Path traverses the peninsula, and its beaches — including Porthor (the "Whistling Sands") and Hell's Mouth (Porth Neigwl) — draw walkers and surfers. The Welsh Language and Heritage Centre at Nant Gwrtheyrn, a former quarrying village, is a well-known landmark of the north coast.
