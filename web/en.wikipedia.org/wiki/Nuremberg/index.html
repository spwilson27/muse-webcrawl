Nuremberg (German: Nürnberg; in the local East Franconian dialect: Nämberch) is the second-largest city of Bavaria after Munich, with a population of roughly 526,000. It lies on the Pegnitz River and the Rhine–Main–Danube Canal, about 170 km north of Munich, and forms a metropolitan region with Fürth and Erlangen.

History

First documented in 1050, Nuremberg grew around its Imperial Castle into one of the most important free imperial cities of the Holy Roman Empire; the Golden Bull of 1356 directed that every new emperor hold his first Imperial Diet there. In the 15th and 16th centuries the city was a centre of the German Renaissance — home to Albrecht Dürer, the poet Hans Sachs and the astronomers Regiomontanus and Martin Behaim — and of crafts, printing and instrument-making. Germany's first railway, the Bavarian Ludwig Railway to Fürth, opened in 1835.

In the 20th century the Nazi Party staged its huge annual rallies in Nuremberg (immortalised in Leni Riefenstahl's Triumph of the Will), and the anti-Semitic Nuremberg Laws were promulgated there in 1935. The old town was largely destroyed by Allied bombing in January 1945 and was painstakingly rebuilt after the war. From November 1945 to October 1946 the city hosted the Nuremberg trials of the major Nazi war criminals before an International Military Tribunal in the Palace of Justice — chosen because the building had survived largely intact and the city symbolised the party's power — giving rise to the Nuremberg Code in the subsequent Doctors' Trial and to enduring principles of international criminal law.

Economy and culture

Modern Nuremberg is a centre for engineering, electronics, market research (GfK is headquartered here), exhibitions (NürnbergMesse, including the world's largest toy fair) and printing. It is famous for its Christmas market (Christkindlesmarkt), gingerbread (Lebkuchen), small grilled sausages (Nürnberger Rostbratwürste, an EU-protected speciality) and the Germanisches Nationalmuseum, Germany's largest museum of cultural history. The Nazi Party Rally Grounds and the Memorium Nuremberg Trials serve as documentation centres.
