Treaty of Kraków

The Treaty of Kraków was signed on 8 April 1525 between the Kingdom of Poland, under Sigismund I the Old, and Albrecht of Hohenzollern, Grand Master of the Teutonic Order. It ended the Polish–Teutonic War of 1519–1521.

Terms

The treaty secularized the Teutonic Order's Prussian territory: Albrecht, having adopted Lutheranism on Martin Luther's advice, converted the monastic state into the hereditary, secular Duchy of Prussia, held as a fief of the Polish Crown. On 10 April 1525, in Kraków's market square, Albrecht performed the Prussian Homage, kneeling before Sigismund and receiving the ducal banner—an event immortalized in Jan Matejko's 1882 painting. The remaining Order territory in Livonia persisted separately, while the German branch of the Order continued within the Holy Roman Empire.

Significance

The settlement created the first officially Protestant state in Europe and bound Prussia to Poland for over a century; the duchy passed to the Brandenburg Hohenzollerns in 1618, and the fief relationship was dissolved by the Treaty of Wehlau in 1657.
