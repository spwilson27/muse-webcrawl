Eärendil the Mariner and his wife Elwing are fictional characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. Half-elven figures of the First Age, they are the parents of Elrond and Elros and the means by which the Valar are moved to intervene in the War of Wrath, ending the tyranny of Morgoth in Middle-earth. Eärendil's voyage is the subject of some of Tolkien's earliest writings on the legendarium, beginning with the 1914 poem "The Voyage of Éarendel the Evening Star".

Eärendil

Eärendil was the son of the Man Tuor and the Elf Idril of Gondolin. Born in the hidden city, he escaped its fall as a child and grew up at the Havens of Sirion. Troubled by the suffering of Elves and Men under Morgoth, he built the ship Vingilot ("Foam-flower") and sailed west again and again, seeking Valinor to plead for mercy on behalf of the Two Kindreds.

Elwing

Elwing was the daughter of Dior, son of Beren and Lúthien, and inherited the Silmaril that Beren had won from Morgoth's crown. After the sons of Fëanor destroyed the Havens of Sirion in their demand for the jewel, Elwing cast herself into the sea with the Silmaril; Ulmo bore her up in the form of a great white bird, and she flew to Eärendil's ship.

The voyage to Valinor

Guided by the light of the Silmaril, Eärendil and Elwing passed the Enchanted Isles and reached Aman, where Eärendil spoke as ambassador for Elves and Men. The Valar granted his plea and prepared the War of Wrath. Because the couple had used the Silmaril's light to reach the Blessed Realm, they were not permitted to return to mortal lands; Eärendil chose to be counted among the Elves, and Elwing with him. Vingilot was hallowed and set to sail the heavens, the Silmaril bound on Eärendil's brow, so that he became the brightest star in the sky — the Evening Star, a sign of hope to the peoples of Middle-earth.

Legacy within the legendarium

Their sons, Elrond and Elros, were given the choice of the Half-elven: Elrond chose the Elves and became a great lord of the Second and Third Ages, while Elros chose mortality and became the first King of Númenor. The light of Eärendil's star, captured in the Phial of Galadriel, later guides Frodo Baggins and Sam Gamgee in The Lord of the Rings.
