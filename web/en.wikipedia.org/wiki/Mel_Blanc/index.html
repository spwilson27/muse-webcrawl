Melvin Jerome Blanc (born Blank; 30 May 1908 – 10 July 1989) was an American voice actor and radio personality whose career spanned over 60 years. Dubbed "The Man of a Thousand Voices", he is best known as the original voice of virtually all the major Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies characters for Warner Bros. — Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Tweety, Sylvester, Porky Pig, Elmer Fudd (after 1940 sharing duties), Yosemite Sam, Foghorn Leghorn, Marvin the Martian, Pepé Le Pew, Speedy Gonzales and the Tasmanian Devil — and later voiced Barney Rubble on The Flintstones and Cosmo Spacely on The Jetsons for Hanna-Barbera.

Early life

Blanc was born in San Francisco to Jewish parents and grew up in Portland, Oregon, where he began performing on radio while still at school. He changed the spelling of his surname from "Blank" to "Blanc" as a teenager. After working in radio in Portland, San Francisco and Los Angeles, he joined the cast of Jack Benny's radio programme, becoming a beloved regular with characters including Sy the Mexican and the sounds of Benny's Maxwell automobile.

Warner Bros.

Blanc joined Warner Bros.' cartoon studio in 1937, first voicing a drunken bull in Picador Porky. He took over Porky Pig that year and in 1940 originated Bugs Bunny in A Wild Hare, giving the rabbit his famous Bronx-meets-Brooklyn accent and the catchphrase "Eh... what's up, doc?" His contracts evolved from flat fees to his unique demand of on-screen credit — "Voice characterization by Mel Blanc" — making him one of the first voice actors to be credited.

Later work

In 1960 Blanc became the voice of Barney Rubble in The Flintstones. A near-fatal car crash in January 1961 left him in a coma; he famously responded to doctors while in character as Bugs Bunny, and recorded Flintstones episodes from his hospital bed. He continued voicing his Warner characters in new productions into the 1980s, including Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), in which several of his classic characters appear.

Death and legacy

Blanc died of heart disease and emphysema in Los Angeles on 10 July 1989. His gravestone at Hollywood Forever Cemetery is inscribed with Porky Pig's signature sign-off: "That's all Folks!" He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and his son Noel Blanc succeeded him in several of the Warner roles.
