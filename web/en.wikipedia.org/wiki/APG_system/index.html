The APG system (Angiosperm Phylogeny Group system) is a modern system of flowering plant taxonomy produced by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group. It classifies angiosperms into orders and families according to monophyletic groups inferred from molecular phylogenetic evidence, chiefly chloroplast DNA sequences.

Development

The first APG paper appeared in 1998 in the Annals of the Missouri Botanical Garden, recognising 40 orders and 462 families. Unlike earlier morphology-based systems (such as Cronquist's), the APG classification avoids formal ranks above order and organises families into clades such as the basal angiosperms, magnoliids, monocots, and the eudicots (with core eudicots split into rosids and asterids). Revisions followed as evidence accumulated: APG II (2003) offered "bracketed" optional family circumscriptions; APG III (2009) recognised 59 orders and 415 families; APG IV (2016) recognised 64 orders and 416 families, adding orders such as Boraginales and Icacinales.

Adoption

The APG system is now the standard reference for angiosperm classification, used by major databases (Plants of the World Online), herbaria, floras and textbooks. Among its well-known consequences were the dissolution of the old order Scrophulariales, the transfer of many genera between families (e.g. Digitalis to Plantaginaceae), and recognition that groups such as the former "dicots" are paraphyletic, replaced by the eudicots.
