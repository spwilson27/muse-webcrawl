Z3 (computer)

The Z3 was a German electromechanical computer designed by Konrad Zuse in 1938 and completed in 1941. It is considered to be the world's first working programmable, fully automatic digital computer. The Z3 was built with 2,600 relays, implementing a 22-bit word length that operated at a clock frequency of about 5–10 Hz. Program code was stored on punched film.

Design

The Z3 operated on binary floating-point numbers and included addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root operations. It had a memory of 64 words. Unlike Zuse's earlier Z1, the Z3 was built entirely from telephone relays. A replica is on display at the Deutsches Museum in Munich.

History

The original Z3 was destroyed on 21 December 1943 during an Allied bombardment of Berlin. In 1998 it was shown that the Z3 was, in principle, Turing complete, although this required exploiting the machine in ways its designer may not have intended. Zuse built the machine with funding from the German Aerodynamics Research Establishment, which used it for statistical analyses of wing flutter.
