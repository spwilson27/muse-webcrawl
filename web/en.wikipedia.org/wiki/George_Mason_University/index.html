George Mason University (GMU or Mason) is a public research university in Fairfax County, Virginia, near Washington, D.C. Named for the Founding Father George Mason, it is the largest public research university in Virginia by enrolment.

History

The institution began in 1949 as a branch of the University of Virginia and became George Mason College in 1960. It was established as an independent university in 1972 and grew rapidly, adding a law school (now the Antonin Scalia Law School), the Volgenau School of Engineering, and the Schar School of Policy and Government. Campuses are located in Fairfax, Arlington, and Prince William County, with a campus in Incheon, South Korea.

Academics and athletics

Mason is classified as an R1 research university. Two of its economics professors, James M. Buchanan and Vernon L. Smith, won the Nobel Memorial Prize. Its men's basketball team, the Patriots, gained national fame by reaching the NCAA Final Four in 2006 as a number 11 seed.
