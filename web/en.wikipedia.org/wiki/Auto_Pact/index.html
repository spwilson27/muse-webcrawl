Auto Pact
Full title: Canada–United States Automotive Products Agreement
Signed: 16 January 1965, Johnson City, Texas
Parties: Canada, United States
Superseded by: NAFTA / WTO rulings (2001)

The Auto Pact, formally the Canada–United States Automotive Products Agreement, was a trade agreement signed in 1965 that removed tariffs on cars, trucks, buses and original-equipment auto parts traded between Canada and the United States, while guaranteeing Canadian production safeguards.

Terms and effects

Under the pact, the major manufacturers could sell vehicles across the border duty-free provided they maintained production-to-sales ratios and Canadian value-added commitments agreed with the Canadian government. The agreement rationalised the North American industry: Canadian plants specialised in particular models for the whole continental market, greatly increasing Canadian auto production and exports and integrating the sector across the border. The pact's conditions were effectively ended after a WTO ruling against them, and the remaining provisions were repealed in 2001, the continental trade by then governed by NAFTA.
