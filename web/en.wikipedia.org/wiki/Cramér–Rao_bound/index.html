Cramér–Rao bound

In estimation theory and statistics, the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB), also known as the Cramér–Rao lower bound, expresses a lower bound on the variance of any unbiased estimator of a parameter: the precision with which a parameter can be estimated cannot exceed the reciprocal of the Fisher information contained in the data. The bound is named for Harald Cramér and C. R. Rao, who derived it independently in the 1940s; related results were obtained earlier by Maurice Fréchet, and the bound is sometimes called the Fréchet–Rao–Cramér bound.

Statement

Let θ̂ be an unbiased estimator of a scalar parameter θ based on observations whose probability density (or mass) function is f(x; θ). Under regularity conditions permitting differentiation under the integral sign, the variance of θ̂ satisfies

Var(θ̂) ≥ 1 / I(θ),

where I(θ) is the Fisher information, I(θ) = E[ (∂/∂θ ln f(X; θ))² ]. For vector parameters the bound takes the form of a matrix inequality: the covariance matrix of any unbiased estimator is bounded below, in the positive-semidefinite order, by the inverse of the Fisher information matrix.

Efficiency

An unbiased estimator whose variance attains the bound is called efficient. Maximum likelihood estimators are asymptotically efficient under standard conditions, achieving the Cramér–Rao bound as the sample size grows, even when no exactly efficient estimator exists for finite samples. The bound is used throughout statistics and signal processing as a benchmark: no unbiased method for estimating a quantity such as a signal's delay, frequency, or direction of arrival can do better than the bound allows.

Generalisations

Extensions include bounds for biased estimators, which involve the derivative of the bias; the Barankin bound and other tighter bounds for problems where the Cramér–Rao bound is loose; Bayesian analogues such as the Van Trees inequality; and a quantum version, the quantum Cramér–Rao bound based on quantum Fisher information, which limits the precision of quantum metrology.
