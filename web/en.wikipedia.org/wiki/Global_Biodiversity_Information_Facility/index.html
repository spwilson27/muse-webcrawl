The Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) is an international organisation that focuses on making scientific data on biodiversity available via the Internet using web services. The data are provided by many institutions from around the world; GBIF's information architecture makes these data accessible and searchable through a single portal. It is funded by governments worldwide and headquartered at the Natural History Museum of Denmark in Copenhagen.

Data and services

GBIF aggregates species occurrence records — what was observed or collected, where, and when — along with species checklists and taxonomic data. Its index holds billions of occurrence records published by museums, herbaria, research institutes, citizen-science platforms and other publishers, using the Darwin Core standard. Data are freely available under open licences and citable with DOIs.

Uses

Researchers use GBIF-mediated data to model species distributions, study climate change impacts, monitor invasive species, and support conservation assessments such as the IUCN Red List. GBIF also supports global policy through bodies such as IPBES and the Convention on Biological Diversity.

History

GBIF was established in 2001 following a 1999 recommendation of the OECD's Megascience Forum. It operates through a network of national and organisational participant nodes that coordinate data publishing in their communities.
