The Hill Forts of Rajasthan are a series of sites located on rocky outcrops of the Aravalli mountain range in Rajasthan, India. They were inscribed collectively as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013, comprising six majestic forts: Chittorgarh Fort, Kumbhalgarh Fort, Ranthambore Fort, Gagron Fort, Amber Fort, and Jaisalmer Fort.

Built between the 8th and 18th centuries by Rajput clans, the forts exemplify the military defensive architecture and courtly culture of medieval Rajput kingdoms.

Component sites

  • Chittorgarh Fort — among the largest forts in India, associated with the Sisodia rulers of Mewar and famous sieges by Alauddin Khalji and Akbar.
  • Kumbhalgarh Fort — built by Rana Kumbha in the 15th century; its perimeter wall extends some 36 km, among the longest continuous walls in the world.
  • Jaisalmer Fort — a "living fort" founded in 1156 CE by Rawal Jaisal, still home to a population within its golden sandstone walls.
  • Amber Fort — the palace-fortress of the Kachhwaha rulers near Jaipur, known for the Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace).
  • Ranthambore Fort — set within a forested plateau, now within Ranthambore National Park.
  • Gagron Fort — a rare water fort surrounded by rivers on three sides.

Architecture

The forts enclose palaces, temples, water reservoirs, bazaars, and residential quarters within massive perimeter walls adapted to hilltop terrain. Their architecture combines defensive engineering with refined Rajput palace design, including carved sandstone facades, frescoes, and stepwells.
