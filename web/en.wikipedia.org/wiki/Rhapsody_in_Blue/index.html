Rhapsody in Blue

Rhapsody in Blue is a 1924 musical composition written by George Gershwin for solo piano and jazz band, which combines elements of classical music with jazz-influenced effects. Commissioned by bandleader Paul Whiteman, the work premiered on February 12, 1924, at the Aeolian Hall in New York City, as part of Whiteman's concert "An Experiment in Modern Music." Gershwin himself played the solo piano part.

Composition and premiere

Whiteman had previously worked with Gershwin on the Scandals of 1922 and asked him in early January 1924 to write a "jazz concerto" for an upcoming concert. Gershwin reportedly began sketching the piece on a train journey to Boston, inspired by the rhythmic sounds of the train. Because Gershwin did not yet have the technical skill to orchestrate the work, Ferde Grofé, Whiteman's arranger, scored it for the band.

Music

The rhapsody opens with one of the most famous gestures in American music: a clarinet trill followed by a long glissando rising to a high note. Gershwin wrote the opening as a two-octave scale, but clarinettist Ross Gorman played it as a glissando at rehearsal, and Gershwin liked it so much that he kept it. The work moves through several contrasting themes, including a train-inspired rhythmic section and a broad, lyrical Andantino theme, before a virtuosic coda.

Legacy

Rhapsody in Blue established Gershwin's reputation as a serious composer and became one of the most popular concert works in the American repertoire. It has been widely used in film and advertising, notably as the theme of United Airlines and in the opening sequence of Woody Allen's Manhattan (1979).
