Norepinephrine transporter

The norepinephrine transporter (NET), also known as noradrenaline transporter or solute carrier family 6 member 2 (SLC6A2), is a monoamine transporter protein that moves the neurotransmitter norepinephrine from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron, terminating its signal. It also transports dopamine, especially in regions such as the prefrontal cortex where the dopamine transporter is sparse.

NET / SLC6A2
Gene: SLC6A2 (chromosome 16)
Substrates: Norepinephrine, dopamine
Family: Sodium:neurotransmitter symporter

Function

NET is a sodium- and chloride-dependent symporter embedded in the presynaptic membrane. Reuptake through NET is the principal means of clearing norepinephrine from synapses in the central and sympathetic nervous systems, regulating mood, arousal, attention, and cardiovascular tone.

Clinical significance

NET is the target of norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (NRIs) such as atomoxetine, used for ADHD, and of serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) used for depression and pain. Cocaine and tricyclic antidepressants also block NET. Mutations in SLC6A2 can cause orthostatic intolerance, and altered NET availability has been studied in depression and PTSD.
