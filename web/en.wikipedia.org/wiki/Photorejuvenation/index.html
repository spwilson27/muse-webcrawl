Photorejuvenation

Photorejuvenation uses light to reduce signs of sun damage and ageing of the skin.

Photorejuvenation is a skin treatment that uses lasers, intense pulsed light (IPL), or photodynamic therapy to treat skin conditions and remove effects of photoaging such as wrinkles, spots, and uneven textures. The process induces controlled wounds on the skin, which prompts the skin to heal itself by creating new cells and stimulating collagen production.

Conditions treated

Photorejuvenation is commonly offered for sun damage and age spots (solar lentigines), freckles, broken capillaries and facial redness, rosacea, port-wine stains, fine lines, and uneven pigmentation. IPL devices emit a broad spectrum of light filtered to target melanin and haemoglobin, while ablative and non-ablative lasers work at a single wavelength chosen for water or pigment absorption.

Procedure and risks

Treatments are performed in clinics in sessions of roughly 20–30 minutes, usually with little recovery time, though temporary redness, darkening of spots, or swelling are common, and multiple sessions are typically needed. Risks include burns, blistering, and pigment changes — particularly in darker skin, which is more prone to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation — and eye protection is required during light-based treatment.
