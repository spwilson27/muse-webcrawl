Fred Manville Taylor (1855–1932) was an American economist and professor at the University of Michigan, best known for a celebrated thought experiment on the feasibility of market socialism, presented in his 1928 presidential address to the American Economic Association, "The Guidance of Production in a Socialist State."

Life and career

Taylor was born in Northville, Michigan. He studied at Northwestern University and the University of Michigan, receiving his PhD from Michigan in 1888 with a dissertation on the history of the right of property. Apart from a brief period teaching at Albion College, he spent his entire academic career at Michigan, where he became one of its most beloved teachers of economic theory.

The socialist calculation argument

In his 1928 address (published in the American Economic Review in 1929), Taylor argued that a socialist state could in principle achieve rational resource allocation by fixing accounting prices for goods and factors of production and adjusting them by trial and error — raising prices where shortages appeared and lowering them where surpluses accumulated, while instructing plant managers to produce at minimum cost and expand output until price equaled marginal cost. The paper became a foundational text in the socialist calculation debate, anticipating the market-socialist models of Oskar Lange and Abba Lerner, and was repeatedly cited by critics such as Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek as the position to be answered.

Other work

Taylor's textbook Principles of Economics went through many editions and was widely used in American colleges. He also wrote on monetary theory and the history of economic thought.
