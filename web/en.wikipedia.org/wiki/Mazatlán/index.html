Mazatlán is a Mexican city and the seat of the Mazatlán Municipality in the state of Sinaloa. It stands on the Pacific coast, across from the southernmost tip of the Baja California peninsula. The city is a major port, a commercial fishing centre, and a popular beach resort, sometimes called the "Pearl of the Pacific".

The name Mazatlán is of Nahuatl origin, from mazatl ("deer") and tlān ("place"), meaning "place of deer". The metropolitan area, including nearby localities, is among the largest in Sinaloa.

History

Until the early 19th century, Mazatlán was a small fishing and trading settlement. Spanish settlement in the area dates to 1531, when Nuño de Guzmán's forces reached the region. In the 1820s a permanent port was established, and by the mid-19th century Mazatlán had become the principal Pacific port of northwestern Mexico, trading in goods from as far away as China and exporting gold and silver from the mines of the Sierra Madre.

German immigrants played an important role in the city's development in the 19th century, contributing to its commerce, brewing industry, and musical traditions — the Sinaloan banda music, with its brass and wind instrumentation, reflects this German influence. Mazatlán was occupied by American forces during the Mexican–American War and was bombarded by French forces in 1864 during the French intervention.

Geography and climate

Mazatlán lies at the mouth of the Gulf of California, just below the Tropic of Cancer. It has a tropical savanna climate with a marked dry season in winter and heavy rains in summer. The city fronts a long bay with several offshore islands, of which Isla de la Piedra ("Stone Island") is effectively a peninsula south of the harbour.

Economy

Tourism and fishing are the backbone of the economy. Mazatlán hosts one of the largest shrimp fleets in Mexico and important tuna and sardine fisheries, along with shrimp farming. The historic centre (Centro Histórico) has been restored since the 1990s, and the city's malecón — roughly 21 km long — is among the longest in the world. The Mazatlán International Carnival, held since 1898, is one of the largest carnivals in Mexico.

Culture

The city is known for its historic Ángela Peralta Theatre, named for the celebrated Mexican soprano who died of yellow fever in Mazatlán in 1883. Local cuisine centres on seafood, including aguachile and shrimp dishes. Mazatlán is also a noted sportfishing destination and home to the Venados de Mazatlán baseball team of the Mexican Pacific League.
