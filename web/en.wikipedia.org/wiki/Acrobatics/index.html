Acrobatics (from Greek akrobateō, "to walk on tiptoe") is the performance of extraordinary human feats of balance, agility and motor coordination. It is found in many performing arts and sports, and is most closely associated with the circus.

Acrobats performing a partner balancing act.

History

Acrobatic feats are depicted in art from ancient Egypt, Minoan Crete (bull-leaping), China and Greece. Acrobats entertained courts in Han-dynasty China, and troupes of tumblers and jugglers performed across medieval Europe. In the modern circus, acrobatics expanded with aerial disciplines such as the trapeze, invented by Jules Léotard in the nineteenth century, and with apparatus including the tightrope, Spanish web, aerial silks and teeterboard.

Disciplines

Acrobatics includes ground disciplines — tumbling, hand balancing, contortion, partner acrobatics (acro) and human pyramids — and aerial disciplines — trapeze, aerial hoop (lyra), silks, rope and straps. As acrobatic gymnastics, it is a competitive sport governed by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG), with pairs and groups performing balance, dynamic and combined routines to music. Related modern forms include parkour, tricking, and cheerleading stunts.
