Schwarz–Christoffel mapping

In complex analysis, a Schwarz–Christoffel mapping is a conformal map of the upper half-plane onto the interior of a simple polygon. The formula, developed independently by Elwin Christoffel (1867) and Hermann Schwarz (1869), expresses the mapping as an integral whose integrand is a product of powers determined by the polygon's interior angles and the pre-images of its vertices on the real axis.

Formula

For a polygon with vertices w1, ..., wn with interior angles α1π, ..., αnπ, the map from the upper half-plane is f(z) = A + C ∫ (ζ − x1)α1−1 ... (ζ − xn)αn−1 dζ, where the xk are real parameters (prevertices) and A, C are complex constants fixing position and scale.

Parameter problem

For more than three vertices, the prevertices cannot be freely chosen: determining them from the polygon's side lengths is the classic Schwarz–Christoffel "parameter problem," usually solved numerically. The mapping is widely used in fluid dynamics (flow around polygonal obstacles), electrostatics, and mesh generation; software such as the SC Toolbox implements it.

Extensions

Variants map from a disk or strip instead of the half-plane, and generalizations handle polygons with curved sides or slits.
