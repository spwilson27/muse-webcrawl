Hearings of the Commission, 1981

The Commission on Wartime Relocation and Internment of Civilians (CWRIC) was a United States government commission created by Congress in 1980 to review the facts and circumstances surrounding Executive Order 9066 and the internment of Japanese Americans during the Second World War, and to recommend appropriate remedies.

Work

Chaired by Joan Z. Bernstein, the nine-member commission held hearings in cities across the United States in 1981, hearing emotional testimony from more than 750 witnesses, many of them former internees speaking publicly about their experiences for the first time. It reviewed government records, including documents showing that military necessity had not in fact justified mass exclusion, and that contrary intelligence assessments had been suppressed.

Findings and recommendations

The commission's report, Personal Justice Denied, issued in December 1982 (with recommendations following in 1983), concluded that Executive Order 9066 "was not justified by military necessity, and the decisions which followed from it — detention, ending detention, and ending exclusion — were not driven by analysis of military conditions. The broad historical causes which shaped these decisions were race prejudice, war hysteria and a failure of political leadership." It recommended a formal government apology and redress payments of $20,000 to each surviving internee.

Legacy

The commission's findings laid the groundwork for the Civil Liberties Act of 1988, signed by President Ronald Reagan, which apologised on behalf of the United States and authorised the payments, ultimately delivered to more than 82,000 people.
