Nasu (那須町, Nasu-machi) is a town in Nasu District, Tochigi Prefecture, Japan. It lies in the northern part of the prefecture at the foot of the Nasu volcanic group, whose highest peak, Mount Chausu (1,915 m), is an active volcano. The town is a well-known resort area famous for its hot springs (onsen), highland farms, and ski areas, easily reached from Tokyo by the Tōhoku Shinkansen to Nasushiobara Station.

Geography

Nasu occupies a broad fan-shaped area descending from the Nasu mountains, which form part of the Ōu Mountains on the border with Fukushima Prefecture. The Naka River has its headwaters in the town. The Imperial Family maintains the Nasu Imperial Villa in the area, first built in 1926, and members of the family regularly vacation there.

History

The Nasu area is known in Japanese folklore for the legend of Tamamo-no-Mae, the nine-tailed fox spirit, said to have been killed here and transformed into the Sessho-seki ("Killing Stone"), a volcanic boulder that was a famous local landmark; the stone split in two in March 2022, attracting wide media attention. During the Edo period, the area lay along the Ōshū Kaidō highway. The modern town of Nasu was formed in 1954 through the merger of several villages.

Tourism

Attractions include the Nasu Onsen hot-spring district (including the sulfurous Shika-no-yu baths), Nasu Animal Kingdom, the Nasu Highland Park amusement area, dairy and ranch tourism on the highland plateau, and skiing and hiking in the Nasu mountains, part of Nikkō National Park.
