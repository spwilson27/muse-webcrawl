Dithematic name

Dithematic names combine two meaningful elements, or "themes".

A dithematic name is a personal name formed from two elements, or "themes", each carrying its own meaning. Dithematic naming was characteristic of the early Germanic peoples, and similar systems existed in other Indo-European cultures, including Celtic, Slavic, Greek and Indo-Iranian traditions. The two elements were drawn from a traditional stock of name themes and combined, often so that children shared one element with a parent or relative, signalling kinship.

Germanic examples

Many familiar names of Germanic origin are dithematic. Æthelred combines æthel ("noble") and ræd ("counsel"); Alfred combines ælf ("elf") and ræd; Wolfgang combines "wolf" and "path". Elements referring to battle, fame, protection, animals and gods recur throughout the stock. Hypocoristic (short) forms and monothematic names also existed alongside the dithematic system.

Use and decline

In the early Middle Ages, dithematic names predominated among the Germanic-speaking peoples of Europe. From around the eleventh and twelfth centuries, the system gradually broke down as Christian saints' names spread and as hereditary surnames developed; many dithematic names survived, however, fossilised in modern given names and place names.
