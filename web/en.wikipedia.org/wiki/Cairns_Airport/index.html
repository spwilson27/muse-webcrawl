Cairns Airport (IATA: CNS, ICAO: YBCS) is an international airport serving Cairns and the surrounding region of Far North Queensland, Australia. Located about 7 kilometres north of the Cairns central business district in the suburb of Aeroglen, it is one of Australia's busiest regional airports and the main aviation gateway to the Great Barrier Reef and the Wet Tropics of Queensland.

History

The airport began as a landing ground in the 1920s and was developed as a military base during the Second World War, when it was used by Royal Australian Air Force and United States forces. Post-war it grew into a significant regional airport; an international terminal opened in 1984, allowing direct overseas services, and the airport was privatised in 2008 when it was acquired by a consortium, North Queensland Airports.

Facilities and airlines

Cairns Airport has two passenger terminals: Terminal 1 for international flights and Terminal 2 for domestic flights, connected by a covered walkway. The main runway, 15/33, is 3,156 metres long. Airlines serving Cairns include Qantas, Virgin Australia, Jetstar and Air New Zealand, with domestic routes to Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane and international services that have included Tokyo, Singapore, Port Moresby and Denpasar. The airport is also an important base for scenic flights, helicopters and fixed-wing services to Cape York and Torres Strait communities, and for the Royal Flying Doctor Service.
