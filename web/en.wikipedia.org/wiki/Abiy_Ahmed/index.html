Abiy Ahmed Ali (Amharic: ዐቢይ አሕመድ ዐሊ; born 15 August 1976) is an Ethiopian politician who has served as Prime Minister of Ethiopia since April 2018. A former army intelligence officer and founder of Ethiopia's cyber-security agency INSA, he came to power amid mass protests and launched sweeping political and economic reforms. He received the 2019 Nobel Peace Prize for ending the 20-year border conflict with Eritrea.

Rise to power

Born in Beshasha in southwestern Ethiopia to a Muslim Oromo father and an Orthodox Christian Amhara mother, Abiy fought as a teenager against the Derg regime, later serving as a UN peacekeeper in Rwanda and in the Ethio-Eritrean War. He entered parliament in 2010 and rose through the Oromo People's Democratic Organization. When Prime Minister Hailemariam Desalegn resigned in February 2018 after years of unrest, the ruling EPRDF coalition chose Abiy as chairman, and he became prime minister on 2 April 2018.

Premiership

Abiy freed political prisoners, lifted bans on opposition parties and media, appointed a gender-balanced cabinet, and in July 2018 signed a peace declaration with Eritrean President Isaias Afwerki. He merged the EPRDF's ethnic parties into the new Prosperity Party in 2019. From November 2020 his government fought a devastating civil war against the Tigray People's Liberation Front, marked by mass atrocities and famine conditions, ended by a peace agreement signed in Pretoria in November 2022. His later tenure has faced further insurgencies in the Amhara and Oromia regions and tension over Ethiopia's pursuit of sea access.
