Hayreddin Barbarossa

Hayreddin Barbarossa (c. 1478 – 4 July 1546), born Khizr Reis, was an Ottoman corsair and later admiral of the Ottoman Navy. Beylerbey of Algiers; Kapudan Pasha (grand admiral) 1533–1546.

Hayreddin Barbarossa (Arabic: خير الدين بربروس‎, romanized: Khayr al-Dīn Barbarūs; Turkish: Barbaros Hayrettin Paşa), also known as Hızır Reis, was an Ottoman corsair and later admiral who dominated the Mediterranean Sea during the first half of the 16th century. Born on the island of Lesbos, he began his career as a privateer alongside his elder brother Oruç Reis, from whose red beard the name "Barbarossa" (Italian for "red beard") derives.

Rise in Algiers

The brothers established themselves on the North African coast, capturing Algiers in 1516. After Oruç was killed by the Spanish in 1518, Hayreddin offered Algiers to the Ottoman Sultan Selim I, securing Ottoman protection and the title of beylerbey. Over the following years he consolidated Ottoman control over much of the Barbary Coast and raided Christian shipping and coastal towns throughout the western Mediterranean.

Kapudan Pasha

In 1533 Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent summoned Hayreddin to Istanbul and appointed him Kapudan Pasha, grand admiral of the Ottoman fleet. His greatest victory came at the Battle of Preveza in 1538, where his fleet defeated a Holy League armada assembled by Pope Paul III, securing Ottoman dominance of the eastern Mediterranean for decades. In 1543 he wintered his fleet at Toulon at the invitation of Francis I of France, in accordance with the Franco-Ottoman alliance.

Death and legacy

Hayreddin retired to Istanbul in 1545 and died the following year. His tomb stands in the Beşiktaş district of Istanbul, near the modern Turkish Naval Museum. His memoirs, the Gazavât-ı Hayrettin Paşa, remain an important source for 16th-century Mediterranean warfare.
