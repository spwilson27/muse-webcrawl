Tanami Desert

The Tanami Desert is a desert in the Northern Territory and Western Australia of Australia. It is an Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia (IBRA) region covering approximately 259,972 square kilometres (100,376 sq mi), making it one of Australia's largest deserts and among the most remote and least explored regions of the country. It is considered the most isolated and arid-drought bioregion in Australia.

Geography and climate

The Tanami consists largely of undulating sandplains covered with spinifex hummock grassland, with rocky ranges — including the Tanami Range and the Granites — rising in places. Rainfall averages around 400 mm per year, much of it falling during summer storms associated with the northern monsoon, but is highly erratic. The desert is crossed by the Tanami Road (Tanami Track), an unsealed route linking Alice Springs with Halls Creek in the Kimberley.

People and ecology

The region is home to Aboriginal peoples including the Warlpiri, Pintupi and Kukatja, and contains significant Aboriginal communities and outstations. It was among the last parts of Australia contacted by Europeans; gold was mined at the Granites from the 1930s and large-scale gold mining continues today. The Tanami supports vulnerable species such as the bilby, great desert skink and brush-tailed mulgara, and is bounded to the south by the Great Sandy Desert.
