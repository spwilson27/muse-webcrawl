Hallucination

A hallucination is a perception in the absence of an external stimulus that has the compelling sense of reality of a true perception. Hallucinations are vivid, substantial, and appear to be located in external objective space; they are distinguishable from illusions, which involve misperception of a real stimulus, and from imagery, which is under voluntary control. They can occur in any sensory modality — most commonly auditory (hearing voices) and visual, but also olfactory, gustatory, tactile, and somatic.

Causes

Hallucinations are associated with psychotic disorders such as schizophrenia (where auditory verbal hallucinations are a core symptom), with neurological conditions including Parkinson's disease and Charles Bonnet syndrome (visual hallucinations following sight loss), with psychedelic and other drugs, with delirium, fever, and with states at the edges of sleep — the hypnagogic and hypnopompic hallucinations experienced by many healthy people. Brief hallucinations also occur in a minority of the general population without any disorder.

Treatment

Management depends on the cause: antipsychotic medication for psychosis, treatment of underlying medical conditions, and psychological approaches such as coping-strategy enhancement and avatar therapy for distressing voices.
