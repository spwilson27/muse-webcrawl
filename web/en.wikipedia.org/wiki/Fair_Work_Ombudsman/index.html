Fair Work Ombudsman

The Fair Work Ombudsman is Australia's workplace regulator

The Fair Work Ombudsman (FWO) is an independent statutory agency of the Australian Government, created by the Fair Work Act 2009. It promotes harmonious, productive and cooperative workplace relations and ensures compliance with Australian workplace laws. The Ombudsman is the head of the agency and is appointed for a term of up to five years.

Functions

The Ombudsman's functions include:

  • Providing free information and advice to employers and employees about pay, conditions, and rights and obligations under awards and agreements.
  • Investigating complaints and suspected breaches of workplace laws, including underpayment of wages.
  • Enforcing compliance through tools ranging from compliance notices and enforceable undertakings to litigation in the courts.
  • Educating the community through campaigns, calculators and translated resources.

The FWO is distinct from the Fair Work Commission, which is the national workplace relations tribunal responsible for awards, agreement approval and dispute resolution. High-profile FWO enforcement work has included major underpayment cases involving franchise networks, universities, supermarkets and hospitality businesses, and a sustained focus on protecting migrant and other vulnerable workers.
