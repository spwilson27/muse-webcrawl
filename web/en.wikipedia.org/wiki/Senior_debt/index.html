Senior debt

In finance, senior debt is debt that takes priority over other unsecured or subordinated debt owed by the same issuer. If the borrower becomes insolvent or is liquidated, senior debt must be repaid in full before holders of junior, or subordinated, debt receive anything, and before any distribution to shareholders.

Seniority and security

Seniority describes the ranking of claims in a borrower's capital structure. It is distinct from, though often combined with, being secured: a secured loan is backed by specific collateral, and its holder has first claim on that collateral. Senior unsecured debt ranks behind secured debt with respect to the collateral, but ahead of subordinated debt and equity among unsecured claims. Creditors can agree to reorder these priorities through subordination and intercreditor agreements.

Capital structure position

A typical priority order in liquidation is:

  1. Secured creditors (to the value of their collateral)
  2. Senior unsecured debt
  3. Subordinated (junior) debt, including mezzanine debt
  4. Preference shares
  5. Common equity

Pricing and use

Because senior lenders bear lower loss risk, senior debt carries a lower interest rate than junior instruments from the same issuer. Senior loans are a common tool in corporate finance and leveraged buyouts, where a syndicated senior term loan and revolving credit facility sit atop a stack that may include high-yield bonds and mezzanine finance. In sovereign and bank resolution contexts, the seniority of bondholders determines who absorbs losses first in a "bail-in".
