Orbicularis oris muscle

Orbicularis oris
Origin: Maxilla and mandible
Insertion: Skin and mucous membrane of the lips
Nerve: Facial nerve (CN VII), buccal and marginal mandibular branches
Action: Closes and protrudes the lips

The orbicularis oris muscle is a complex of muscles in the lips that encircles the mouth. It is not a single sphincter but a layered arrangement of fibres — some intrinsic to the lips and many derived from other facial muscles that insert into the lips, especially at the modiolus, the fibrous node at each corner of the mouth. It is sometimes called the "kissing muscle".

Structure

The muscle comprises marginal and peripheral parts in each lip. Fibres from the buccinator, levator and depressor muscles of the mouth angles, and the zygomatic muscles, interweave with its own fibres, allowing the fine, varied movements of the lips. It is supplied by the buccal and marginal mandibular branches of the facial nerve and by branches of the facial artery.

Function

Orbicularis oris closes the lips, protrudes them (as in whistling and kissing), and shapes them for speech, sucking, feeding and playing wind instruments. Competence of the muscle is essential for oral continence and articulation of labial sounds such as /p/, /b/ and /m/.

Clinical significance

Damage to the facial nerve weakens the muscle, causing drooling and distorted speech. In infants, the sucking reflex depends on it; cleft lip involves disruption of its continuity and requires surgical reconstitution of the muscle ring.
