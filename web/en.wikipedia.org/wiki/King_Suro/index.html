King Suro (수로왕), posthumously known as Sureung, was the legendary founder and first king of Geumgwan Gaya (42–199), one of the Gaya polities of southern Korea, and the progenitor of the Gimhae Kim clan, one of the largest Korean clans. His story is recorded in the Samguk Yusa and the Garakgukgi.

Legend of founding

According to legend, in the year 42 CE the chiefs of nine villages in the Gimhae region, guided by a heavenly voice, climbed Gujibong peak and discovered a golden bowl wrapped in red cloth containing six golden eggs. From the first egg hatched a boy, Suro, who grew rapidly and was proclaimed king; the other eggs became the kings of the other Gaya states. Because he was the first to appear, he was named Suro ("first revealed").

Queen Heo Hwang-ok

In 48 CE, Suro married Heo Hwang-ok, a princess said to have sailed from Ayuta (often identified with Ayodhya, India) on a ship with red sails, guided by a dream of her parents. She is credited with bringing tea and other cultural elements to Korea, and her descendants include both the Gimhae Kim and Gimhae Heo clans; the couple is said to have had ten sons. The story is often cited as evidence of ancient maritime links between Korea and South or Southeast Asia, though its historicity is debated.

Legacy

The Tomb of King Suro in Gimhae, South Gyeongsang Province, is a large burial mound with a shrine complex at which descendants still hold memorial rites. King Suro's legendary reign is given as lasting until 199 CE, and he was succeeded by his son Geodeung. The foundation myth remains central to the identity of the Gimhae Kim clan, which claims millions of members in modern South Korea.
