Type of service

Type of Service (ToS)
LocationIPv4 header, second byte (bits 8–15)
Defined byRFC 791 (1981); refined by RFC 1349
Superseded byDifferentiated Services field (RFC 2474, 1998)

The Type of Service (ToS) field is the second byte of the IPv4 header, originally defined in RFC 791 (1981) to let hosts specify how their datagrams should be handled — trading off delay, throughput, and reliability — as packets traversed the network. In practice the field was little used end-to-end, and in 1998 it was redefined as the Differentiated Services (DS) field by RFC 2474; together with the ECN bits, this new interpretation covers the same byte in modern IPv4 (and corresponds to the Traffic Class field in IPv6).

Original layout

As originally specified, the byte's high-order bits gave a 3-bit Precedence value (0–7, from "routine" to "network control") indicating the datagram's priority; three following bits flagged desired qualities: low Delay, high Throughput, and high Reliability; one bit remained reserved, and RFC 1349 (1992) added a fourth flag for low monetary Cost. Applications such as Telnet would request low delay while FTP requested high throughput. Routers were permitted — but not required — to honour these hints, and inconsistent implementations across vendors and providers meant the markings rarely had predictable end-to-end effects.

Replacement

The Differentiated Services architecture reinterpreted the top six bits as the DS Code Point (DSCP), a label used for per-hop behaviours (such as Expedited Forwarding and Assured Forwarding) within a provider's network, and the low two bits became Explicit Congestion Notification (ECN). The terms "ToS byte" and "ToS field" persist informally: operating systems and packet-capture tools still display the byte as "Type of Service", and utilities like ping retain a -Q option to set it, even though its semantics are now those of DiffServ.
