Catherine of Alexandria

Saint Catherine of Alexandria
Bornc. 287, Alexandria, Roman Egypt

Catherine of Alexandria, also spelled Katherine, is, according to tradition, a Christian saint and virgin who was martyred in the early 4th century at the hands of the emperor Maxentius. She is a popular saint in both the Eastern and Western churches, numbered among the Fourteen Holy Helpers, and regarded as the patroness of students, scholars and philosophers. There is no contemporary evidence for her life, and many historians consider her legend to be a composite or an entirely legendary figure.

Legend

According to her hagiography, Catherine was the highly educated daughter of the governor of Alexandria. Converting to Christianity as a teenager, she confronted the emperor Maxentius over his persecution of Christians and, in a public disputation, confuted fifty pagan philosophers, who were converted and then executed. The emperor ordered her to be tortured on a spiked wheel, which miraculously shattered at her touch — hence her attribute, the Catherine wheel, and the firework named after it. She was finally beheaded. Angels were said to have carried her body to Mount Sinai, where the monastery of Saint Catherine was later built at the mountain's foot, and where her relics are venerated.

Veneration

Catherine's cult spread widely in the Middle Ages; she was one of the saints whose voices Joan of Arc reported hearing. Hundreds of churches were dedicated to her, and her feast on 25 November was a major holy day. In 1969 the Roman Catholic Church removed her feast from the General Roman Calendar owing to doubts about her historicity, but it was restored as an optional memorial in 2002.
