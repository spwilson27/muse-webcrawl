Ubangi River

The Ubangi River (also spelled Oubangui) is the largest right-bank tributary of the Congo River in Central Africa. Formed by the confluence of the Mbomou and Uele rivers, it flows west and then south for about 1,060 kilometres (660 miles), forming much of the border between the Central African Republic and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and then between the Republic of the Congo and the DR Congo, before joining the Congo.

Geography and use

Together with its headstreams, the Ubangi system reaches about 2,270 kilometres in length. The river is a vital transport artery: barges carry goods between Bangui, capital of the Central African Republic, and Brazzaville and Kinshasa downstream, although navigation is interrupted by rapids near Bangui and varies seasonally with water levels.
