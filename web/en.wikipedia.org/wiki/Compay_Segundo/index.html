Compay Segundo (born Máximo Francisco Repilado Muñoz Telles; 18 November 1907 – 13 July 2003) was a Cuban trova guitarist, singer, and composer. He achieved worldwide fame in his nineties as a leading figure of the Buena Vista Social Club project.

Early life and career

Born in Siboney near Santiago de Cuba, Repilado began playing guitar, tres, and clarinet as a boy, performing with municipal bands and the Estudiantina Oriental. In the 1930s he invented the armónico, a seven-string hybrid of guitar and tres. His nickname came from singing second voice (segunda voz) as the companion (compay, from compadre) in the duo Los Compadres, which he formed with Lorenzo Hierrezuelo in 1942.

Buena Vista Social Club

After decades of relative obscurity, including work as a cigar roller, Segundo was brought back to international attention by Ry Cooder's Buena Vista Social Club album (1997) and Wim Wenders' 1999 film. His best-known composition, "Chan Chan", opens the album and became a standard of Cuban music worldwide.

He continued touring and recording into his nineties, performing for audiences across Europe and the Americas before his death in Havana in 2003.
