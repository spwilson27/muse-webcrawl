Good King Wenceslas

"Good King Wenceslas" is a Christmas carol that tells the story of a Bohemian king (in reality Duke Wenceslaus I of Bohemia) who, on the Feast of Stephen (26 December, Boxing Day), goes out in harsh winter weather to give alms to a poor peasant, accompanied by his page, who nearly gives up in the snow but follows in the king's footprints. The lyrics were written by the English hymnwriter John Mason Neale and published in 1853, set to the melody of "Tempus adest floridum", a 13th-century spring carol from the Finnish collection Piae Cantiones (1582).

Lyrics and story

The carol's five verses narrate the episode in dialogue between the king and his page. Seeing a poor man gathering firewood, the king has food and wine brought to the peasant's dwelling; as the night grows colder the page falters, and the king tells him to tread in his footprints, whereupon the cold is less keen — the song's moral being that those who bless the poor "shall themselves find blessing." The story rests on legends of Saint Wenceslaus (c. 907–935), patron saint of Bohemia, famed for his almsgiving and murdered by his brother Boleslaus.

Origins and reception

Neale, a leading figure of the Oxford Movement and translator of many medieval hymns, wrote the carol for children; it appeared in Carols for Christmas-tide (1853). Although folklorists have sometimes dismissed it as a Victorian fabrication, it has remained a staple of carol services and recordings, and its tune's earlier life as a spring song is a classic example of contrafactum.
