Martín Alonso Pinzón (c. 1441 – c. 1493) was a Spanish mariner from Palos de la Frontera, in Huelva. He was the eldest of the Pinzón brothers and captained the Pinta on Christopher Columbus's first voyage of discovery in 1492.

The first voyage

Pinzón, an experienced shipowner and navigator, helped recruit crews and prepare the expedition's ships. His local influence made the voyage possible after Columbus had struggled to find sailors willing to sail west into the unknown Atlantic.

Separation and return

In November 1492 Pinzón separated from the fleet with the Pinta and explored independently, reaching Hispaniola on his own. He rejoined Columbus in January 1493 and returned to Spain, dying shortly after the fleet's arrival amid accusations of rivalry with Columbus.
