Sol Plaatje

Solomon Tshekisho Plaatje (9 October 1876 – 19 June 1932) was a South African intellectual, journalist, linguist, translator, writer, and politician. He was a founding member and the first General Secretary of the South African Native National Congress (SANNC), which later became the African National Congress (ANC).

Life and work

Born near Boshof in the Orange Free State, Plaatje was educated at a Lutheran mission station at Pniel and became a gifted polyglot, fluent in at least seven languages. He worked as a court interpreter in Kimberley and Mafeking; during the Siege of Mafeking in the Second Boer War he kept a remarkable diary, published posthumously as The Boer War Diary of Sol T. Plaatje.

As an author, Plaatje wrote Native Life in South Africa (1916), a landmark protest against the Natives Land Act of 1913, and Mhudi (1930), one of the first novels in English by a black South African. He translated Shakespeare's plays into his mother tongue, Setswana, and devoted himself to preserving Tswana language and oral tradition.

Political career

Plaatje travelled to Britain and North America to campaign against discriminatory legislation, meeting figures including W. E. B. Du Bois. Despite his efforts, segregationist laws deepened during his lifetime. He died in Kimberley in 1932. The Sol Plaatje Local Municipality, which includes Kimberley, is named after him, as is Sol Plaatje University, opened in 2014.
