The Jagiellonian dynasty (Polish: Dynastia Jagiellonów) was a royal dynasty of Lithuanian origin, a branch of the Gediminids, that reigned in several Central and Eastern European states between the 14th and 16th centuries. Members of the dynasty were Grand Dukes of Lithuania (1377–1392 and 1440–1572), Kings of Poland (1386–1572), Kings of Hungary (1440–1444 and 1490–1526) and Kings of Bohemia (1471–1526). At its height around 1500, the family's realms stretched from the Baltic to the Black and Adriatic Seas, and their era is remembered as a golden age of Polish-Lithuanian power and culture.

Origins

The dynasty takes its name from Jogaila, Grand Duke of Lithuania, who in the Union of Krewo (1385) agreed to accept Roman Catholic baptism, marry Queen Jadwiga of Poland and unite his lands with the Polish Crown. Crowned as Władysław II Jagiełło in 1386, he and his cousin Vytautas the Great led the Polish-Lithuanian alliance that crushed the Teutonic Knights at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410, one of the largest battles of medieval Europe.

Expansion and golden age

Jagiełło's sons and grandsons consolidated the succession: Casimir IV (r. 1447–1492) subdued Prussia and placed his son Vladislaus on the thrones of Bohemia (1471) and Hungary (1490). His grandsons John I Albert, Alexander and Sigismund I the Old reigned in Poland in turn. The dynasty fostered Renaissance culture — Kraków's Jagiellonian University, founded by the earlier Casimir III and refounded under Jadwiga and Jagiełło, educated Nicolaus Copernicus. In 1515 the Congress of Vienna arranged Jagiellonian–Habsburg marriages that would eventually bring Bohemia and Hungary to the Habsburgs after the Hungarian king Louis II died childless at the Battle of Mohács in 1526.

End of the dynasty

The last Jagiellonian king, Sigismund II Augustus (r. 1548–1572), presided over the Union of Lublin (1569), which merged Poland and Lithuania into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. His death without an heir in 1572 ended the male line, and the Commonwealth became an elective monarchy; his sister Anna Jagiellon later reigned as an elected king alongside her husband Stephen Báthory. The dynasty's remains rest largely in the Sigismund Chapel of Wawel Cathedral, a masterpiece of Renaissance art.
