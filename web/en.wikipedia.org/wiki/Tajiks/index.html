Tajiks (Persian: تاجیکان, Tājīkān; Tajik: Тоҷикон, Tojikon) are a Persian-speaking Iranian ethnic group native to Central Asia and Afghanistan. They form the majority in Tajikistan, a major minority in Afghanistan (where they are usually termed Tajiks or, for Persian speakers generally, Farsiwan), and a large minority in Uzbekistan, concentrated in the historic cities of Samarkand and Bukhara. Tajiks speak varieties of Persian—called Tajik in the former Soviet sphere and Dari in Afghanistan—and are predominantly Sunni Muslims, with Ismaili Shia communities in the Pamir mountains (Badakhshan).

History

The Tajiks descend in large part from the Eastern Iranian peoples of ancient Transoxiana—Sogdians, Bactrians, Khwarezmians—who adopted New Persian language and culture during the Islamic era, especially under the Persianate Samanid dynasty (819–999), when Bukhara was a centre of Persian letters. Over the following millennium, Turkic migration and rule reshaped Central Asia; the Persian-speaking sedentary population of the oases and cities became known as Tajiks (or Sarts), distinguished from nomadic Turks. The name derives from Middle Persian tāzīk, originally a term for Arabs, extended to Persianised Muslims.

In the 20th century, Soviet national delimitation created a Tajik republic (1924 as an autonomous republic; full union republic from 1929), though the Tajik cultural centres of Samarkand and Bukhara were assigned to Uzbekistan—a grievance still felt by many Tajiks. Afghanistan's Tajiks, concentrated in the north-east, Kabul and Herat, have played central roles in the country's politics and in resistance movements, including Ahmad Shah Massoud's Northern Alliance.

Culture

Tajik culture is part of the wider Persianate world: the poetry of Rudaki (born near Panjakent) is claimed as a national foundation, and Nowruz, the Persian New Year, is the principal festival. Traditional arts include miniature painting, wood carving, suzani embroidery, and the classical Shashmaqam music shared with Uzbekistan. Cuisine features plov (osh), qurutob and green tea taken in chaikhanas.
