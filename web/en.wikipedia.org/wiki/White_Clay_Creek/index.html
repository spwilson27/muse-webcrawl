White Clay Creek is a tributary of the Christina River in the U.S. states of Pennsylvania and Delaware. Rising in southeastern Chester County, Pennsylvania, the creek flows generally southeastward through the rolling Piedmont, past the communities of Avondale, Landenberg and Newark, Delaware, before joining the Christina River near Churchman's Marsh. The creek takes its name from the pale clay deposits along its banks, and its watershed drains some 107 square miles (277 km²).

Wild and Scenic designation

In 2000 the United States Congress designated White Clay Creek under the National Wild and Scenic Rivers System — the first watershed in Delaware to receive the designation, and notable for protecting essentially the entire creek system rather than a single segment. The designation, expanded in 2014 to include additional tributary miles, is managed through a cooperative, locally driven model involving dozens of municipalities, the states of Pennsylvania and Delaware, and the White Clay Watershed Association. Tributaries protected under the programme include the East, Middle and West Branches, Pike Creek, Mill Creek and others.

Ecology and recreation

The creek corridor is protected in part by White Clay Creek State Park in Delaware and White Clay Creek Preserve in Pennsylvania, together preserving thousands of acres of forest along the Mason–Dixon Line. The valley is known for trout fishing — the creek is stocked and also supports wild brown trout in its upper reaches — as well as hiking, horseback riding and birding. Historically, the creek powered mills, and the area retains structures from the region's colonial and early industrial past, including sites associated with the Lenape and with 18th-century milling communities.
