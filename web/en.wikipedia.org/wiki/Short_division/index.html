Short division

Short division is a formal written method of dividing numbers, a simplified form of long division suitable when the divisor is a (usually single-digit) number. It is sometimes called the bus stop method in British schools because the division bracket resembles a bus shelter. Unlike long division, the intermediate multiplication and subtraction steps are performed mentally and only the quotient digits and carried remainders are written down.

Method

The dividend is placed inside the bracket and the divisor outside to the left. Working from the most significant digit, each digit is divided by the divisor; any remainder is carried (often written as a small superscript) to the next digit, forming a two-digit number to be divided in turn. For example, 1234 ÷ 6: 1 is not divisible by 6, so consider 12 → 2, remainder 0; 3 → 0, remainder 3, carried to make 34; 34 ÷ 6 = 5 remainder 4, giving 205 r 4, or 205.666….

Comparison with long division

Short division requires holding more intermediate results in memory but takes up less space and is faster for small divisors. Long division is preferred for two-digit or larger divisors and when a decimal quotient to several places is required.
