Greek government-debt crisis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greek government-debt crisis (2010–2018) was a sovereign debt crisis faced by Greece in the aftermath of the global financial crisis of 2007–2008. Widely known in Greece as "the Crisis" (Η Κρίση), it reached the population through successive austerity measures, unemployment reaching above 27 percent, and a contraction of the economy by roughly a quarter.

The crisis began in late 2009 when the newly elected government revised the budget deficit sharply upward — to over 15 percent of GDP — undermining confidence in Greek statistics and sending borrowing costs to unsustainable levels.

Bailouts

Greece received three rescue packages, in 2010, 2012, and 2015, totalling around €290 billion, from the "troika" of the European Commission, the European Central Bank, and the International Monetary Fund, conditional on austerity and structural reforms. A 2012 private-sector involvement (PSI) deal wrote down about €107 billion of privately held debt — the largest sovereign restructuring in history.

2015 referendum and aftermath

In July 2015, Greek voters rejected creditor terms in a referendum, yet the government of Alexis Tsipras accepted a third bailout with further conditions; capital controls were imposed and banks temporarily closed. Greece exited the bailout programmes in August 2018, though its public debt remained around 180 percent of GDP, the highest in the eurozone.
