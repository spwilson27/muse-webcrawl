The Bank of North Dakota (BND) is a state-owned bank in the U.S. state of North Dakota, headquartered in Bismarck. Established in 1919 by the state legislature under the Nonpartisan League administration of Governor Lynn Frazier, it is the only general bank in the United States owned by a state government, and most revenues of the state must be deposited in it by law.

History

The bank was created as part of the Nonpartisan League's programme of state-owned enterprises, alongside the state mill and elevator, to free North Dakota farmers from the control of out-of-state banks and grain companies. Early opposition mounted a legal challenge that the U.S. Supreme Court rejected in Green v. Frazier (1920). The bank began operations modestly and for much of the twentieth century worked quietly as a "bankers' bank".

Operations

Rather than competing with local banks, BND partners with them: it participates in loans, provides student and agricultural lending programmes, and supports economic development. The bank's profits are transferred to the state's general fund or retained as capital, and its stability has frequently been cited in debates about public banking in other states. It is governed by the North Dakota Industrial Commission, consisting of the governor, attorney general and agriculture commissioner.
