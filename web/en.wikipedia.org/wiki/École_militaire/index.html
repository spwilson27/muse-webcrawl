École militaire

École militaire Founded: 1750
Location: Place Joffre, 7th arrondissement, Paris, France
Architect: Ange-Jacques Gabriel
Function: Military training complex
The École Militaire facing the Champ de Mars.

The École militaire (Military School) is a vast complex of buildings in Paris housing various military training facilities. It stands in the 7th arrondissement, at the south-eastern end of the Champ de Mars, opposite the Eiffel Tower.

The school was founded in 1750 by King Louis XV, on the proposal of the financier Joseph Paris Duverney and with the support of Madame de Pompadour, to provide an education in military science for cadets from poor noble families. The buildings were designed by the royal architect Ange-Jacques Gabriel and constructed from 1751 onwards in an elegant neoclassical style, centred on a domed central pavilion.

Notable history

The school's most famous graduate was Napoleon Bonaparte, who was admitted in 1784 and completed the two-year course in a single year, passing out in 1785 as a second lieutenant of artillery; the examiners noted that he would "go far if circumstances favour him." The complex was the scene of episodes of the French Revolution and later gave its name to a Paris Métro station. Today it houses the École de guerre and other staff and training institutions of the French armed forces. A building of the complex briefly hosted UNESCO before the organisation built its own headquarters.
