The Assyrian people (also known as Syriacs, Arameans, or Chaldeans in some communities) are an ethnic group indigenous to northern Mesopotamia — present-day northern Iraq, south-eastern Turkey, north-western Iran and north-eastern Syria — who trace their origins to the ancient Assyrians of the Neo-Assyrian Empire. They are distinguished by their Christian faith, their use of Aramaic languages descended from the tongue of ancient Mesopotamia and the early church, and a continuous cultural identity reaching back to antiquity.

History

After the fall of the Assyrian Empire in 609 BC, the population of the region persisted under Persian, Greek, Roman and Parthian rule, and the area became an early centre of Christianity; tradition holds that the apostles Thomas, Thaddeus and Mari evangelised Mesopotamia in the first century AD. The Church of the East spread Assyrian Christianity as far as India and China in the medieval period. Under successive empires — Sassanian, Arab, Mongol and Ottoman — Assyrians remained a distinct, largely self-governing religious community.

Genocide and diaspora

During the First World War, the Ottoman Empire and allied forces carried out the Assyrian genocide (Seyfo, "the sword"), alongside the Armenian and Greek genocides, killing a large fraction of the Assyrian population and driving most survivors from their homelands in Hakkari and Tur Abdin. Further persecution — including the Simele massacre in Iraq in 1933, the exodus after the 2003 Iraq War, and the 2014 ISIS invasion of the Nineveh Plains — has produced a global diaspora larger than the population remaining in the homeland, with major communities in the United States, Sweden, Australia, Germany and Canada.

Language and culture

Assyrians speak Northeastern Neo-Aramaic dialects (often called Sureth) and write in the Syriac script; Classical Syriac remains the liturgical language of their churches. Cultural life centres on the church, family, traditional dress, music and dance, and festivals such as Kha b-Nisan, the spring new year celebration. Naming and identity among subgroups — Assyrian, Chaldean, Aramean — remain matters of community debate.
