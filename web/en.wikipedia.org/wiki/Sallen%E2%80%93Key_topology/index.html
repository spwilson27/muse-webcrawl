Sallen–Key topology

The Sallen–Key topology is an electronic filter topology used to implement second-order active filters such as low-pass, high-pass and band-pass filters. It is one of the most widely known and used filter topologies for its simplicity, requiring a single operational amplifier.

History

The topology was first described in 1955 by R. P. Sallen and E. L. Key of MIT Lincoln Laboratory. It uses positive feedback around a non-inverting amplifier together with a resistor–capacitor network to form a two-pole filter section.

Design

Component values are chosen from the desired cutoff frequency and quality factor Q. In the common unity-gain configuration with equal resistors and equal capacitors, Q is fixed at 1/2; other choices of component ratios and amplifier gain allow Bessel, Butterworth and Chebyshev responses.

Applications

Sallen–Key stages are cascaded to build higher-order filters and are ubiquitous in audio processing, anti-aliasing stages before analogue-to-digital converters, and instrumentation.
