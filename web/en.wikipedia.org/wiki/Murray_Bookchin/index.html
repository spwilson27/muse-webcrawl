Murray Bookchin

Murray Bookchin (January 14, 1921 – July 30, 2006) was an American social theorist, author, orator and political philosopher. He founded the school of thought known as social ecology and developed the political ideology of libertarian municipalism.

Life and work

Born in New York City to Russian Jewish immigrant parents, Bookchin was active in left-wing movements from his youth, moving from communism and Trotskyism toward anarchism. His early book Our Synthetic Environment (1962), published under the pen name Lewis Herber, was one of the first popular works on the ecological crisis. Later books include Post-Scarcity Anarchism (1971) and The Ecology of Freedom (1982).

Ideas

Bookchin argued that social hierarchy and domination of people by people underlie humanity's domination of nature, and that ecological problems cannot be solved without transforming society. He proposed confederated, directly democratic municipalities as an alternative to the nation-state, an idea that influenced the Kurdish freedom movement's adoption of democratic confederalism. In the 1990s he broke with anarchism and called his mature philosophy "communalism".
