Frederick Jay Rubin (born 10 March 1963) is an American record producer and author. He co-founded Def Jam Recordings with Russell Simmons in 1984 and later founded American Recordings. One of the most influential producers in modern music, Rubin is known for a minimalist production philosophy and for landmark work across hip hop, heavy metal, alternative rock and country, including the career-reviving American Recordings series with Johnny Cash.

Def Jam and early career

Rubin grew up on Long Island and, while studying at New York University, began producing records in his dorm room at Weinstein Hall. From that base he and Russell Simmons built Def Jam into the defining label of hip hop's first commercial era, producing the Beastie Boys' Licensed to Ill (1986), LL Cool J's Radio (1985) and Run-DMC's collaboration with Aerosmith on "Walk This Way" (1986), which bridged rap and rock. His stripped-down, drum-machine-and-guitar sound defined mid-1980s hip hop. Rubin left Def Jam in 1988 after disputes over its direction and moved to Los Angeles to found Def American (later American Recordings).

Producer

Rubin's production credits span genres: thrash metal (Slayer's Reign in Blood, 1986), hard rock (The Cult's Electric, 1987), alternative and funk rock (Red Hot Chili Peppers' Blood Sugar Sex Magik, 1991, and a long collaboration with the band; Tom Petty's Wildflowers, 1994), rap-metal (Rage Against the Machine's demos; System of a Down), and pop and R&B (Adele's 21, 2011; Ed Sheeran; Eminem; Kanye West's Yeezus, 2013). His most celebrated late-career work was with Johnny Cash: beginning with American Recordings (1994), Rubin recorded Cash largely alone with guitar, then with spare arrangements of contemporary songs — including Nine Inch Nails' "Hurt" — producing a series of albums that restored Cash's reputation before his death in 2003. Rubin served as co-head of Columbia Records from 2007 to 2012.

Philosophy and The Creative Act

Rubin describes his method as reducing a work to its essence and creating conditions in which artists do their best work; he is as much a guru-like presence as a technical engineer, often working from his Shangri-La studio in Malibu. His 2023 book The Creative Act: A Way of Being, a meditation on creativity and attention, became a bestseller. He is a practitioner of Transcendental Meditation and a vegan.
