Liliʻuokalani (born Lydia Liliʻu Loloku Walania Kamakaʻeha; 2 September 1838 – 11 November 1917) was the last sovereign monarch of the Hawaiian Kingdom, reigning from 29 January 1891 until the overthrow of the kingdom on 17 January 1893.

Early life

Liliʻuokalani was born in Honolulu to high chiefs Analea Keohokālole and Caesar Kapaʻakea, and was raised in the hānai tradition by Chiefess Laura Kōnia and Chief Abner Pākī. She was educated at the Royal School with the other royal children and in 1862 married John Owen Dominis, who would become governor of Oʻahu. Named heir apparent by her brother King Kalākaua in 1877, she served as regent during his travels.

Reign and overthrow

Ascending the throne on Kalākaua's death in 1891, Liliʻuokalani sought to restore the powers of the monarchy reduced by the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution," and in January 1893 attempted to promulgate a new constitution. A committee of American and European businessmen, backed by the United States minister John L. Stevens and the landing of US forces, proclaimed a Provisional Government. The queen yielded under protest to the "superior force" of the United States. After an unsuccessful royalist rebellion in 1895 she was arrested, tried for misprison of treason, imprisoned for eight months in an upstairs bedroom of ʻIolani Palace, and formally abdicated.

Later life and legacy

The queen spent her remaining years pursuing claims against the United States and championing Hawaiian causes; Hawaii was annexed by the US in 1898 under the Newlands Resolution. An accomplished composer trained in Western and Hawaiian music, she wrote more than 160 songs, including the beloved hymn-like "Aloha ʻOe" ("Farewell to Thee"). She died in 1917 at Washington Place, her Honolulu residence. In 1993 the US Congress issued a formal apology for the American role in the overthrow.
