Chebyshev filter

Chebyshev filters are a type of electronic filter used in signal processing, named after the Russian mathematician Pafnuty Chebyshev because their mathematical characteristics derive from Chebyshev polynomials. They achieve a steeper roll-off between passband and stopband than Butterworth filters, at the cost of ripple in the passband (Type I) or the stopband (Type II).

Type I Chebyshev filters

Type I filters have an equiripple response in the passband and a monotonic response in the stopband. The gain oscillates between unity and a minimum set by the ripple factor ε, which is related to the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind. For a given filter order, a Chebyshev filter rolls off faster than a Butterworth filter of the same order, making them useful where a sharp transition is needed and passband ripple can be tolerated.

Type II Chebyshev filters

Type II ("inverse Chebyshev") filters are flat in the passband and equiripple in the stopband. They do not roll off as fast as Type I filters but avoid passband ripple and have better phase behaviour; they are used less commonly.

Properties and drawbacks

Compared with Butterworth and elliptic filters, Chebyshev filters offer a compromise: sharper cut-off than Butterworth, but without the stopband ripple and extra complexity of elliptic designs. Their drawbacks include poorer transient response and group delay, and, for Type I designs, the passband ripple. Analogue Chebyshev filters can be realised with standard LC ladder networks, and digital versions are obtained via the bilinear transform.
