Reionization

In the Big Bang theory, reionization is the process by which luminous sources — the first stars, galaxies, and quasars — once again ionized the primarily neutral intergalactic medium that existed after recombination. Recombination had occurred around 378,000 years after the Big Bang, leaving hydrogen mostly neutral; as the first structures formed several hundred million years later, their ultraviolet radiation gradually re-ionized the universe.

Evidence

Observations of the cosmic microwave background, notably from the Planck satellite, constrain the midpoint of reionization to a redshift of about z ≈ 7–8. Absorption in the spectra of distant quasars (the Gunn–Peterson trough) shows that reionization was essentially complete by redshift z ≈ 6, roughly 900 million years after the Big Bang. The epoch of reionization is a major target for the James Webb Space Telescope and for future 21-cm cosmology experiments such as HERA and the Square Kilometre Array.
