Giant squid

The giant squid (Architeuthis dux) is a species of deep-ocean dwelling squid in the family Architeuthidae. It can grow to a tremendous size, offering an example of abyssal gigantism: recent estimates put the maximum size at around 12–13 m (39–43 ft) for females and 10 m (33 ft) for males, from the posterior fins to the tip of the two long feeding tentacles.

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Mollusca
Order: Oegopsida
Family: Architeuthidae
Genus: Architeuthis

The giant squid is widespread, occurring in all of the world's oceans. It is usually found near continental and island slopes from the North Atlantic — particularly Newfoundland, Norway, and the northern British Isles — to the tropical and temperate waters of the Pacific. Despite its size, it remained almost mythical until the 21st century: the first images of a live adult in its natural habitat were taken in 2004 by Japanese researchers, and the first video footage followed in 2012.

Anatomy

Like all squid, a giant squid has a mantle (torso), eight arms, and two longer tentacles. The arms and tentacles account for much of the squid's great length. Its eyes are the largest in the animal kingdom, up to 27 cm (11 in) in diameter, an adaptation for detecting bioluminescence and moving predators such as sperm whales in the deep sea.

Feeding and predators

Giant squid feed on deep-sea fish and other squid, catching prey with the suckered clubs of their feeding tentacles. They are in turn a major food source for sperm whales, and beaks recovered from whale stomachs long provided the main evidence of their existence, along with specimens washed ashore or caught in trawls.

In culture

The giant squid is thought to be a source of the kraken legend of Norse sailors and appears in literature including Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas and Herman Melville's Moby-Dick.
