European Spallation Source

Image: European Spallation Source, Lund (placeholder)

The European Spallation Source (ESS) is a multi-disciplinary research facility under construction in Lund, Sweden, based on what is planned to be the world's most powerful pulsed neutron source. It is organised as a European Research Infrastructure Consortium (ERIC) with 13 member countries, and its data management and software centre is located in Copenhagen, Denmark.

Design

The facility uses a two-gigaelectronvolt linear proton accelerator, nearly 600 metres long, to drive protons into a rotating tungsten target wheel, producing intense pulses of neutrons by spallation. Fifteen scientific instruments are planned initially, supporting research in materials science, chemistry, biology, energy technology, and fundamental physics. The target station's neutron brightness is designed to exceed that of existing sources such as ISIS in the UK and the SNS in the United States.

Status

Construction began in 2014, and the project has proceeded in stages toward first neutrons and a user programme, with completion of the initial scope expected in the late 2020s.
