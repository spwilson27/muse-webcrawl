MacGillycuddy's Reeks

MacGillycuddy's Reeks (Irish: Na Cruacha Dubha, meaning "the black stacks") is a mountain range in County Kerry, Ireland. The range includes Carrauntoohil, at 1,038.6 metres (3,407 ft) the highest peak in Ireland, along with the country's only other summits above 1,000 metres, Beenkeragh and Caher.

Geology

The mountains are composed of Devonian Old Red Sandstone that was strongly folded during the Variscan orogeny and later sculpted by glaciers, leaving sharp arêtes, corries and U-shaped valleys such as the Black Valley and the Gap of Dunloe.

Name

The name derives from the Mac Giolla Mo-Chuda clan, long-standing landowners in the area; "reeks" is a local word for stacks or peaks.
