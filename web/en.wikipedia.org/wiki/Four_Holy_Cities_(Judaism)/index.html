Four Holy Cities (Judaism)

The Four Holy Cities of Judaism (Hebrew: ארבע ערי הקודש) are Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed and Tiberias. The concept dates from the 16th century, when these were the main centres of Jewish life in Ottoman Palestine and the destinations of the halukka system of charitable donations collected in the diaspora to support their Jewish communities.

The cities

  • Jerusalem — the holiest city, site of the Temple, associated with the element of fire.
  • Hebron — burial place of the patriarchs at the Cave of Machpelah, associated with earth.
  • Safed (Tzfat) — centre of Kabbalah, home of Joseph Karo and Isaac Luria, associated with air.
  • Tiberias — where the Jerusalem Talmud was compiled and a seat of the Sanhedrin, associated with water.

Halukka

Emissaries (meshulachim) travelled to diaspora communities collecting funds for the four cities, each of which maintained its own collection organisation. The system sustained the Old Yishuv for centuries.
