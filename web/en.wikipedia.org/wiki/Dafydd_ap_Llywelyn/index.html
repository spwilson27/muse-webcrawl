Dafydd ap Llywelyn (c. 1212 – 25 February 1246) was Prince of Gwynedd from 1240, and the first to claim the title Prince of Wales. He was the only legitimate son of Llywelyn the Great (Llywelyn ab Iorwerth) by Joan, daughter of King John of England, and his father's designated heir, in preference to his elder half-brother Gruffudd.

Reign

On succeeding in 1240 Dafydd faced immediate pressure from Henry III of England, who forced him by the Treaty of Gwerneigron (1241) to surrender territory in north-east Wales and to hand over his half-brother Gruffudd as a hostage; Gruffudd died in 1244 falling from the Tower of London while trying to escape. Dafydd then allied with other Welsh rulers and appealed to Pope Innocent IV to recognise his inheritance, while war resumed with England in 1244–45. Welsh forces won notable successes, but Dafydd died suddenly on 25 February 1246 without issue, leaving Gwynedd to be divided between his nephews, the sons of Gruffudd.
