Fall of Antwerp

The Fall of Antwerp (1584–1585) was the capture of the city of Antwerp, then the richest commercial centre of northern Europe, by Spanish forces under Alexander Farnese, Duke of Parma, during the Eighty Years' War. The long siege ended with the surrender of the Dutch rebels in August 1585.

Background

Antwerp had joined the Dutch Revolt against Philip II of Spain, surviving the Spanish Fury sack of 1576, but Parma's reconquest of the southern Netherlands progressively isolated the city.

Siege

Parma built a remarkable fortified bridge — the Farnese Bridge — across the Scheldt to blockade the city by water, defeating attempts by Antwerp's defenders, including fireships ("hellburners") devised by Federigo Giambelli, to break it. Starvation forced negotiations.

Aftermath

Protestant inhabitants were given time to leave; roughly half the population emigrated, many to Amsterdam, carrying skills and capital that fuelled the Dutch Golden Age. Antwerp remained under Spanish rule and its trade declined as the Dutch blockaded the Scheldt.
