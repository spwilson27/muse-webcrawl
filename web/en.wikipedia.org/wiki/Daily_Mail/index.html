The Daily Mail is a British daily middle-market tabloid newspaper founded in 1896 by Lord Northcliffe, published by DMG Media (Associated Newspapers). Famous for its campaigns, its Femail pages and its instinct for its largely female readership, it became — through MailOnline, launched in 2003 — one of the most-visited English-language news websites in the world.

Character and controversies

The Mail's populist editorial voice ('the voice of Middle England') and campaigning style make it one of Britain's most influential and most criticised papers; its historical support for 1930s fascist movements under Lord Rothermere is a documented stain the paper's defenders contextualise differently. MailOnline's picture-led, high-volume model reshaped popular news online.
