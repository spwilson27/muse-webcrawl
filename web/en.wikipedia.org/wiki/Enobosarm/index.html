Enobosarm, also known as ostarine or MK-2866, is an investigational selective androgen receptor modulator (SARM) developed originally by GTx, Inc. (later Oncternal) and subsequently by Veru, for conditions including muscle wasting, cachexia associated with cancer, and breast cancer. SARMs were designed to produce anabolic effects in muscle and bone with reduced androgenic activity in tissues such as the prostate compared with testosterone.

Clinical development

In clinical trials in patients with non-small cell lung cancer, enobosarm improved lean body mass but failed to meet a co-primary endpoint of physical function, and it was not approved for cancer cachexia. Veru later pursued development in androgen-receptor-positive, oestrogen-receptor-positive metastatic breast cancer, and in combination approaches for obesity to preserve lean mass alongside GLP-1 therapies.

Use in sport and regulation

Enobosarm is prohibited by the World Anti-Doping Agency as an anabolic agent, and numerous athletes have been sanctioned after testing positive for it, sometimes attributing results to contaminated supplements. It has never been approved as a medicine by the FDA, which has warned consumers that SARMs sold online as dietary supplements are unapproved and associated with risks including liver injury and cardiovascular events.
