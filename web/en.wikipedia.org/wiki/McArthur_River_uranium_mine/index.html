The McArthur River uranium mine, in northern Saskatchewan, Canada, is the world's largest high-grade uranium deposit and mine. Discovered in 1988, it is operated by Cameco, which owns about 70% of the operation, with Orano Canada holding the remainder. Production began in 1999.

Geology and mining

The deposit lies about 500–600 metres underground at the unconformity between Athabasca sandstone and basement rock. Ore grades average well over 10% U3O8 — more than 100 times the world average — and in some zones exceed 20%. Because of the high radioactivity and weak, water-bearing ground, mining uses non-entry methods: raise boring from tunnels in the basement below the ore, ground freezing to control water, and remote handling of ore, which is crushed underground, ground into a slurry and pumped to surface.

Processing

Ore slurry is trucked roughly 80 km to the Key Lake mill, where it is blended with lower-grade feed and processed into uranium concentrate.

Suspension and restart

Cameco suspended production in July 2018 in response to sustained low uranium prices, placing the mine in care and maintenance. With prices recovering, the company announced a restart in February 2022, and production resumed in November 2022, ramping toward the licensed capacity of 18 million pounds of U3O8 per year.
