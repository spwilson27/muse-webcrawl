Yokkaichi (四日市市, Yokkaichi-shi) is a city in Mie Prefecture, Japan, on the shore of Ise Bay. Its name, literally "fourth-day market," derives from a market held on the fourth day of each month in the medieval period. It is the largest city in Mie Prefecture and a major industrial port.

History

During the Edo period Yokkaichi prospered as a post station (Yokkaichi-juku) on the Tōkaidō highway between Edo and Kyoto, and as a port. It is also a centre of Banko ware, a heat-resistant pottery tradition begun in the 18th century. In the late 1950s and 1960s, large petrochemical complexes were built on reclaimed land along the bay.

Yokkaichi asthma

The city became notorious for Yokkaichi asthma, an epidemic of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, chronic bronchitis, and bronchial asthma caused by sulfur oxide emissions from the petrochemical complexes, first recognized around 1960–61. Residents sued the companies in 1967 and won a landmark 1972 court ruling that established principles of corporate liability for pollution damage in Japan. The case, one of Japan's "four big pollution diseases" litigations alongside Minamata disease, helped prompt national pollution-control legislation. Air quality in the city has since improved dramatically, and the Yokkaichi Pollution and Environmental Museum preserves the history of the episode.

Economy

Yokkaichi remains a centre for petrochemicals, semiconductor materials, and automotive components, and its port handles substantial container and LNG traffic. The city centre has been redeveloped around Kintetsu Yokkaichi Station, and the port area is noted for its nightscape of refineries, a popular subject for industrial photography.
