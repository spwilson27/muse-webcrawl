Achsah

Achsah (Hebrew: עַכְסָה, ʿAḵsā, "anklet") is a biblical figure, the daughter of Caleb, son of Jephunneh, one of the spies sent by Moses into Canaan. Her story is told in the Book of Joshua (15:16–19) and repeated in the Book of Judges (1:12–15).

Biblical account

Caleb offered his daughter in marriage to whoever would conquer Kiriath-Sepher (Debir). The city was taken by Othniel, son of Caleb's younger brother Kenaz, who thus became Achsah's husband and later the first judge of Israel. Achsah then persuaded Othniel to ask Caleb for a field; dismounting from her donkey, she asked her father directly for a blessing — springs of water to match the dry Negev land she had been given. Caleb granted her the "upper springs and the lower springs".

Interpretation

Commentators have often noted Achsah's initiative: she is one of several women in the Hebrew Bible who successfully petition for land and inheritance rights, comparable to the daughters of Zelophehad. The springs she obtained are sometimes identified with sites near Hebron or Debir in the northern Negev.
