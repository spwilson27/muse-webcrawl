Plan S is an initiative for open-access science publishing launched in September 2018 by cOAlition S, a consortium of national research funders, initially organised around Science Europe and supported by the European Commission. Its "S" stands for "science, speed, solution, shock". The plan requires that, from 2021 onward, scientific publications resulting from research funded by public grants from participating funders must be published in compliant open-access journals or platforms, or made immediately available in open-access repositories, without embargo.

Principles

The plan's ten principles include full open access with authors retaining copyright under open licences (default CC BY), no publication in "hybrid" journals except under transformative arrangements, transparent and capped article processing charges where applicable, and the development of robust criteria for open-access venues. Funders committed to monitoring compliance and sanctioning non-compliant grant holders.

Implementation

Following pushback and consultation, implementation guidance was revised in 2019: the start date moved to 1 January 2021, transformative agreements were recognised as a compliant route for a transition period, and a "Rights Retention Strategy" was developed so that authors could deposit the accepted manuscript immediately under CC BY regardless of publisher policy. Major funders in cOAlition S include UK Research and Innovation, the French ANR, the Dutch NWO, and — beyond Europe — the Gates Foundation and others.

Reception

Supporters hailed Plan S as a decisive step toward publicly funded research being publicly available, while critics — including many learned societies and commercial publishers, and some researchers fearing restrictions on where they could publish — warned of reduced academic freedom, costs to early-career researchers, and risks to society journals funded by subscriptions. It has been credited with accelerating the growth of open-access publishing and transformative ("read and publish") agreements.
