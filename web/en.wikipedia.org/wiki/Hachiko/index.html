Hachikō (ハチ公, 10 November 1923 – 8 March 1935) was a Japanese Akita dog remembered for his remarkable loyalty to his owner, Hidesaburō Ueno, for whom he continued to wait for over nine years following Ueno's death. In Japan he is known as chūken Hachikō ("faithful dog Hachikō").

Life

Hachikō was adopted in 1924 by Hidesaburō Ueno, a professor of agriculture at Tokyo Imperial University, and lived with him in Shibuya. Each day the dog accompanied Ueno to Shibuya Station and returned in the evening to meet the train. On 21 May 1925 Ueno suffered a cerebral haemorrhage at work and died. From then until his own death in March 1935, Hachikō appeared at the station each evening exactly when the train was due, waiting in vain. Station staff and commuters cared for him; the story was reported nationally in 1932, and in 1934 a bronze statue of Hachikō was erected at Shibuya Station, with Hachikō himself present at its unveiling.

Death and legacy

Hachikō died on 8 March 1935 and was found in a street in Shibuya; an autopsy later revealed terminal cancer and a filarial infection. He was buried beside Professor Ueno at Aoyama Cemetery. His statue was melted down during the Second World War; a replacement was erected in 1948 and remains one of Tokyo's most popular meeting points. His mounted remains are exhibited at the National Museum of Nature and Science in Ueno. A second statue, showing Hachikō reunited with Professor Ueno, was unveiled at the University of Tokyo in 2015. The story inspired the Japanese film Hachikō Monogatari (1987) and the American film Hachi: A Dog's Tale (2009), starring Richard Gere.
