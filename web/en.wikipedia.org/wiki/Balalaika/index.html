Balalaika

ClassificationString instrument (plucked lute)
DevelopedRussia, 18th–19th centuries
Related instrumentsDomra, prima balalaika family

The balalaika (Russian: балала́йка) is a Russian stringed musical instrument with a characteristic triangular wooden body, a long neck with three strings, and a short sustain. It is the most recognisable member of a family of instruments of different sizes, from the high-pitched piccolo to the enormous contrabass, that make up the modern Russian folk orchestra.

History

Instruments resembling the balalaika appear in Russian documents from the late 17th century, and the name is recorded in the early 18th century; it likely developed from the Central Asian dombra brought into Russia, with the triangular body being a peasant simplification of construction. In the 1880s the musician Vasily Andreyev, working with violin maker Vladimir Ivanov and later the luthiers Semyon Nalimov and Franz Paserbsky, standardised the instrument's design and tuning and created the family of sizes modelled on the string quartet. Andreyev's Great Russian Orchestra toured internationally and made the balalaika famous worldwide.

Types and tuning

The standard or prima balalaika has three strings tuned E4, A4, A4; two strings are traditionally of gut or nylon and one of steel. Larger members include the sekunda, alto, bass, and contrabass balalaikas, the largest being over 1.7 metres long and played standing on end, often with a wooden peg resting on the floor. The triangular body typically has three, five, or seven ribs of maple or other hardwood.

Playing technique

The prima is played with the index finger striking and strumming across the strings near the sound hole, producing a rapid tremolo used to sustain melody notes. Virtuoso technique, codified by Andreyev and his successors, allows the balalaika a repertoire ranging from folk dances to transcriptions of classical violin music.

In popular culture

The balalaika appears as an emblem of Russian folk culture in films and music, from the score of Doctor Zhivago ("Lara's Theme") to popular ensemble performances. Balalaika orchestras exist in many countries, particularly in North America, where the Balalaika and Domra Association of America was founded in 1978.
