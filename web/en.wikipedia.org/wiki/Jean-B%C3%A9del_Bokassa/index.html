Jean-Bédel Bokassa

Jean-Bédel Bokassa (22 February 1921 – 3 November 1996), also known as Bokassa I, was a Central African political and military leader who ruled the Central African Republic from 1966 and proclaimed himself Emperor of the Central African Empire in 1976, reigning until his overthrow in 1979.

Born22 February 1921, Bobangui
Died3 November 1996, Bangui
Reign1966–1979 (president); 1976–1979 (emperor)

Rise to power

A career soldier in the French colonial army, Bokassa seized power in a coup on 1 January 1966, overthrowing President David Dacko. His rule became increasingly authoritarian and extravagant.

Empire and fall

In 1976 he renamed the country the Central African Empire and crowned himself in a lavish 1977 coronation modelled on Napoleon's. Accusations of atrocities, including the killing of schoolchildren in 1979, led France to support his overthrow in Operation Barracuda in September 1979. He later returned, was tried, imprisoned, and released in 1993.
