Anheuser-Busch Companies, LLC is an American brewing company headquartered in St. Louis, Missouri. Founded in 1852, it grew out of a brewery purchased by soap manufacturer Eberhard Anheuser, whose son-in-law Adolphus Busch joined the business and gave the company its name in 1879. It became the largest brewer in the United States and is famous for Budweiser, launched in 1876, and for its Budweiser Clydesdale horses.

History

Adolphus Busch pioneered the use of pasteurization, refrigerated rail cars, and artificial refrigeration to ship beer nationwide, building Budweiser into the first national beer brand in the United States. The company survived Prohibition (1920–1933) by selling near beer, soda, and yeast products. In the post-war decades it expanded aggressively, passing competitors to become the world's largest brewing company.

Acquisition by InBev

In 2008, after a hostile takeover bid, Anheuser-Busch agreed to be acquired by the Belgian-Brazilian brewer InBev for approximately $52 billion, forming Anheuser-Busch InBev, the world's largest brewer. Anheuser-Busch continues to operate as its North American subsidiary.
