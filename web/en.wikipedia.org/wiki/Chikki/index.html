Chikki is a traditional Indian sweet candy, a type of nut brittle generally made from roasted nuts, seeds, or puffed grains set in a hardened syrup of jaggery. The most common variety is peanut chikki, but chikki is also made with cashews, almonds, pistachios, sesame seeds, puffed rice, roasted Bengal gram, coconut, or combinations of these. It is a popular snack throughout India, valued for its long shelf life and nutritional energy, and is frequently consumed during winter months and festivals such as Makar Sankranti and Lohri.

Preparation

Jaggery is melted and boiled to the hard-crack stage, tested traditionally by dropping a little syrup into cold water. The roasted nuts or seeds are stirred in, and the hot mixture is spread on a greased surface, rolled flat, and cut into bars or squares before it cools and hardens into a crisp brittle. Modern commercial versions may substitute sugar syrup for some or all of the jaggery and are produced as individually wrapped bars.

Lonavala chikki

The hill station of Lonavala in Maharashtra is especially famous for chikki, where it developed in the late 19th century as a wholesome, portable snack sold to railway passengers on the Mumbai–Pune line. Shops in Lonavala sell numerous varieties, and the town's name is strongly associated with the sweet across India.
