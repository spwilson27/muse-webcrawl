Anthony Downs

James Anthony Downs (born 21 November 1930) is an American economist specialising in public policy and public administration. He is known for his work on voting theory, bureaucracy, and urban and transportation policy, and was a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution.

Contributions

His first book, An Economic Theory of Democracy (1957), applied rational-choice methods to politics, arguing that voters are rationally ignorant and that, in a two-party system with single-peaked preferences, parties converge toward the position of the median voter. In Inside Bureaucracy (1967) he modelled bureaucratic behaviour.

Transportation work

Downs formulated the "law of peak-hour expressway congestion" (1962): on urban commuter expressways, peak-hour traffic rises to fill new capacity (triple convergence), a form of induced demand. His books Stuck in Traffic (1992) and Still Stuck in Traffic (2004) developed these ideas; the Downs–Thomson paradox in transport planning is partly named for him.
