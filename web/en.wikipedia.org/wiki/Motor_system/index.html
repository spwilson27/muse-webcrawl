Motor system

Principal motor pathways of the human nervous system

The motor system is the part of the central nervous system involved in movement. It consists of the pyramidal and extrapyramidal systems, which together control voluntary and involuntary motion through networks of upper and lower motor neurons.

Pyramidal system

The pyramidal system, including the corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts, originates in the motor cortex and provides direct cortical control of spinal and cranial motor neurons. It is principally responsible for skilled voluntary movements, especially of the distal limbs. Damage to this system produces weakness, spasticity and the Babinski sign.

Extrapyramidal system

The extrapyramidal system comprises the basal ganglia, cerebellum and brainstem motor nuclei and tracts, including the reticulospinal, vestibulospinal, rubrospinal and tectospinal tracts. It modulates movement, regulating posture, muscle tone and the initiation and smoothness of motion. Disorders of the extrapyramidal system include Parkinson's disease and Huntington's disease, which cause tremor, rigidity and involuntary movements.

Control hierarchy

Movement is organised hierarchically: the association cortex and basal ganglia contribute to planning, the motor cortex to execution commands, the cerebellum to error correction and coordination, and spinal interneuron circuits to reflexes and rhythmic patterns such as walking.
