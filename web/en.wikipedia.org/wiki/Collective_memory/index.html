Collective memory

Key thinker: Maurice Halbwachs
Fields: Sociology, history, psychology

Collective memory is the shared pool of memories, knowledge and information of a social group that is significantly associated with the group's identity. The term was developed by French philosopher and sociologist Maurice Halbwachs in his work on social frameworks of memory in the 1920s and in the posthumously published La mémoire collective (1950).

Halbwachs

Halbwachs argued that individual memory is always shaped by social frameworks — family, class, religion, nation — and that a group sustains a common image of its past that members draw upon. Collective memory is thus reconstructed in the present rather than simply preserved.

Related concepts

Historians such as Pierre Nora distinguished memory from history and described lieux de mémoire (sites of memory) — monuments, museums and commemorations. Jan Assmann developed the idea of cultural memory. The concept is used in studies of nationalism, trauma, commemoration, transitional justice and how societies remember wars, atrocities and founding events.

Criticism

Critics note the risk of reifying a group mind, and ask who selects what is remembered or forgotten, and how power shapes official memory.
