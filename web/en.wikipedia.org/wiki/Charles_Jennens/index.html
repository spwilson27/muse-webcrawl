Charles Jennens (1700 – 20 November 1773) was an English landowner, patron of the arts and librettist. He is best remembered as the compiler of the scriptural libretto for George Frideric Handel's oratorio Messiah (1741), drawn from the King James Bible and the Book of Common Prayer, and as the librettist or collaborator on several other Handel oratorios, including Saul, Israel in Egypt, L'Allegro, il Penseroso ed il Moderato and Belshazzar.

Charles Jennens (1700–1773), librettist of Messiah

Life

Jennens was born into a wealthy family of ironmasters and inherited the estate of Gopsall Hall in Leicestershire, which he rebuilt in grand Palladian style. He studied at Balliol College, Oxford, but as a nonjuror — one who refused the oath of allegiance to the Hanoverian succession — he could not take a degree or pursue a public career. He devoted his life instead to religion, scholarship and music.

Work with Handel

A devoted admirer of Handel, Jennens supplied the composer with texts in the late 1730s and 1740s. His libretto for Saul (1738) began the collaboration; for Messiah he assembled a meditation on Christ's nativity, passion and resurrection entirely from scripture. Jennens's letters reveal both his theological intentions for the work and his occasional frustrations with Handel's speed of composition. The collaboration cooled after Belshazzar (1745) but Jennens remained one of Handel's most important literary partners.

Other interests

Jennens also prepared editions of Shakespeare's plays — among the earliest attempts to edit the texts play by play with collation of the early editions — and maintained a large musical and literary collection at Gopsall. He died unmarried in 1773.
