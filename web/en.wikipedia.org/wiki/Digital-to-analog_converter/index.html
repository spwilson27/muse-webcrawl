Digital-to-analog converter

In electronics, a digital-to-analog converter (DAC, D/A, D2A, or D-to-A) is a system that converts a digital signal into an analog signal. An analog-to-digital converter (ADC) performs the reverse function.

Architectures

Common DAC architectures include the binary-weighted resistor network, the R-2R ladder, segmented and current-steering DACs, and oversampling delta-sigma converters, which use noise shaping to achieve high resolution from a low-resolution internal quantiser. The output is typically a voltage or current proportional to the digital input code.

Performance measures

Key specifications are resolution (in bits), maximum sampling rate, monotonicity, differential and integral nonlinearity, glitch energy, and dynamic measures such as signal-to-noise ratio and spurious-free dynamic range. Most signals from physical systems are inherently analog, so DACs are essential anywhere a digital system must drive the real world.

Applications

DACs are used in audio players, video displays, motor and industrial control, telecommunications, function generators and digital attenuators. Most modern audio signals are stored digitally (for example, as MP3s and CDs), and in order for them to be heard through speakers they must be converted into an analog signal.
