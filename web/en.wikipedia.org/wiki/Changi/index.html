Changi

Changi is a planning area located in the geographical region of Tampines in the East Region of Singapore, at the easternmost tip of the main island. It is best known internationally as the location of Singapore Changi Airport, consistently ranked among the world's best airports. The name Changi is also historically associated with the prisoner-of-war camps of the Japanese occupation (1942–1945), centred on Changi Prison, where Allied servicemen and civilians were interned after the fall of Singapore.

History

Before development, Changi was a rural area of villages, coconut plantations and jungle. In the 1920s and 1930s the British built a major military base there — barracks, a hospital and coastal defences — as part of the Singapore strategy. After the surrender of Singapore in February 1942, the Japanese used the barracks complex and Changi Prison as the principal POW and civilian internment camp in Singapore; conditions, the Selarang Barracks incident of 1942, and the prisoners' murals and chapel became enduring symbols of wartime endurance. The nearby Changi Beach was the site of one of the Sook Ching massacres of Chinese civilians in early 1942.

Modern Changi

Changi Airport opened in 1981 on reclaimed land and has expanded to four terminals plus the Jewel mixed-use complex, which opened in 2019. The surrounding area hosts the Singapore Expo, the Changi Business Park, the Singapore University of Technology and Design, and the Changi Air Base and Changi Naval Base of the Singapore Armed Forces. Changi Village, at the northern edge, retains a hawker centre, ferry terminal for boats to Pulau Ubin and destinations in Malaysia, and the popular Changi Beach Park. A fifth airport terminal and the "Changi East" development are under construction, and the area will be served by the Cross Island MRT line.

Changi Prison and museum

The original Changi Prison, built in 1936, was largely demolished in the 2000s and replaced by a modern correctional complex, though the entrance gatehouse, tower and a section of wall were preserved as a national monument. The Changi Chapel and Museum, first established in 1988 and rebuilt nearby, commemorates the prisoners of war and internees of the occupation.
