Clarendon Press

The Clarendon Press is an imprint of Oxford University Press (OUP). Historically it was the name used for the University of Oxford's printing operation when publishing academic and scholarly books, while books for general and educational markets appeared under the Oxford University Press name.

History

Oxford's university printing began in the 15th century and was put on a firm footing by Archbishop William Laud in the 1630s. The name "Clarendon" comes from Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon, whose posthumously published History of the Rebellion (1702–04) earned profits that funded the Clarendon Building in Broad Street, opened in 1715 as the Press's home. From 1830 the Bible business and learned publishing were run as distinct sides of the Press; "Clarendon Press" came to denote the learned list — scholarly editions, monographs and reference works such as the Oxford Classical Texts.

In the later 20th century the distinction faded, and today Clarendon survives mainly as an imprint for academic books, including law titles published under Clarendon Law. OUP's printing works moved from Oxford to other sites, ending centuries of printing in the city.
