Maronites

The Maronites are an ethnoreligious Christian group native to the Levant, principally Lebanon, whose members belong to the Maronite Church, an Eastern Catholic Church in full communion with the Pope. Their tradition traces its origins to Saint Maron, a 4th–5th century Syriac hermit, and to the monastery founded by his followers.

History

Maronite communities took shape in the mountains of Lebanon, where they preserved a distinct Syriac liturgy and identity through Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule. Relations with Rome were formalised from the Crusader period onward, and the Maronite Church is notable among Eastern churches for its longstanding communion with the Latin Church.

Distribution

Today roughly a third of Lebanon's population is Maronite, and large diaspora communities exist in the Americas, Australia, Africa and Europe as a result of emigration since the 19th century. Under Lebanon's confessional system, the President of the Republic is by convention a Maronite Christian.
