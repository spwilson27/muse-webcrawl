Hypomania

Hypomania (literally "under mania" or "lesser mania") is a mental and physiological state characterised by elevated or irritable mood, increased energy and activity, and other symptoms similar to but less severe than those of mania. Hypomanic episodes are a defining feature of bipolar II disorder and may also occur in cyclothymia and bipolar I disorder (where full manic episodes also occur).

Signs and symptoms

During a hypomanic episode a person may feel euphoric, energetic, and unusually productive, with a decreased need for sleep, racing thoughts, pressured speech, increased talkativeness and sociability, heightened self-confidence, and distractibility. Unlike mania, hypomania does not involve psychotic features, does not cause marked impairment in social or occupational functioning, and does not require hospitalisation. Under DSM-5 criteria, a hypomanic episode lasts at least four consecutive days and represents an unequivocal change from the person's usual state that is observable by others.

Because hypomania can feel pleasant and productive, individuals may not regard it as a problem and may not report it, which contributes to bipolar II disorder being misdiagnosed as recurrent major depression. Hypomanic episodes are often followed by depressive episodes, and risky behaviour during the episode — spending sprees, sexual indiscretions, ill-judged investments — can have serious consequences despite the absence of gross impairment.

Causes and treatment

Hypomania shares the genetic and neurobiological underpinnings of bipolar disorder, involving dysregulation of neurotransmitter systems including dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine. Episodes can be triggered by sleep deprivation, stress, and antidepressant medication. Treatment typically involves mood stabilisers such as lithium or certain anticonvulsants, sometimes atypical antipsychotics, together with psychotherapy and regular sleep–wake routines.
