Clog (shoe)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Clogs are shoes with thick, usually wooden, soles.

Clogs are a type of footwear made in part or completely from wood. Traditional clogs were often worn in heavy labor, but today they are also used in some professions for their protective and ergonomic value and as part of national or local traditional dress.

Because wooden clogs stay solid underfoot, they protect the foot from sharp objects, impacts, and wet or muddy ground. They have been a common form of peasant and workers' footwear in Europe for centuries and remain emblematic of the Netherlands, where the wooden shoe known as the klomp is a national symbol.

Types

Whole wooden clogs

Whole wooden upper clogs are made from a single piece of wood, roughly hewn and hollowed to fit the foot. They were traditionally made from willow, poplar, alder, birch, lime, or elm. Such shoes, like the Dutch klompen, enclose the whole foot.

Wooden-soled shoes

Wooden-soled shoes have a carved wooden sole with a leather or fabric upper nailed or stapled to it. English clogs and the Japanese geta fall into this broad category, although geta use raised wooden platforms rather than a shaped sole.

Partial wooden shoes

Some footwear, such as the Dutch tripklomp and the Spanish albarca, combine a wooden sole or partial wooden structure with straps or uppers.

History

The origins of wooden footwear are ancient. The oldest known wooden shoes, found in the Netherlands, date to roughly 1230 and 1280. Clogs were cheap, durable, and repairable, making them ideal work shoes for farmers, miners, factory workers, and mill hands across northwestern Europe during the Industrial Revolution.

In Lancashire and Yorkshire, clog dancing developed among mill workers who tapped and stamped their wooden soles rhythmically, a tradition that influenced later forms of step dancing.

Modern use

Today clogs are worn by nurses, chefs, surgeons, and other professionals who stand for long hours, typically as molded leather or synthetic shoes with thick cushioned soles inspired by the original wooden form. Brands such as Dansko, Crocs, and Birkenstock popularized modern clog styles, and backless clogs became fashion items in the 1970s and again in the 2020s.

In Sweden, clogs were famously revived by the fashion industry in the 1970s, and the slip-on wooden-soled design is sometimes called the "Swedish clog".
