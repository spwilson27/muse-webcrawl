Malocclusion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A malocclusion is a misalignment or incorrect relation between the teeth of the upper and lower dental arches when they approach each other as the jaws close. The English-language term dates from 1864; Edward Angle, the "father of modern orthodontics", popularised it. The word derives from Latin mal- (bad) and occlusion.

Angle's classification

Edward Angle classified malocclusions based on the relationship of the mesiobuccal cusp of the upper first molar to the buccal groove of the lower first molar:

  • Class I: normal molar relationship but teeth crowded, rotated, or misaligned.
  • Class II: the lower molars are positioned distally relative to the upper molars (including division 1, proclined upper incisors, and division 2, retroclined upper central incisors).
  • Class III: the lower molars are positioned mesially, often producing an underbite.

Signs and symptoms

Malocclusion may cause difficulties with biting, chewing and speech, increased risk of tooth decay and periodontal disease, and temporomandibular joint problems. Crowding of teeth is among the most common features.

Treatment

Treatment is provided by orthodontics, using fixed or removable appliances, and in severe skeletal cases by orthognathic surgery. The evidence base for the timing of treatment of some malocclusions remains debated.
