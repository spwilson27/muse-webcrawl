Jason Fried is an American software entrepreneur and author, best known as the co-founder of the software company 37signals (now Basecamp), which created the project management tool Basecamp and, more recently, the email service HEY. Together with partner David Heinemeier Hansson, he has advocated a calmer, bootstrapped approach to building businesses, in contrast to the venture-funded growth model typical of Silicon Valley.

Career

Fried founded the web design firm 37signals in Chicago in 1999. Frustrated by the tools available for managing client projects, the company built Basecamp in 2004, which became its flagship product. In 2014 the company renamed itself Basecamp after its main product, before reverting to 37signals in 2022. The company also launched the Ruby on Rails web framework, created by Hansson while working on Basecamp.

Writing

Fried has co-authored several bestselling business books with Hansson, including Rework (2010), Remote: Office Not Required (2013), and It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work (2018). He was a columnist for Inc. magazine for many years, writing the "Signal v. Noise" column, which shares its name with the company's long-running blog.

Views

Fried is known for criticising long work hours, excessive meetings, and open-plan offices, and for championing remote work long before it became widespread. Basecamp implemented a four-day work week in summer and a policy of paying salaries in the top percentiles of the San Francisco market regardless of employee location.
