Southern University

Southern University and A&M College
Established: 1880
Type: Public HBCU, land-grant
Location: Baton Rouge, Louisiana, U.S.
System: Southern University System

Southern University and A&M College is a public historically black university in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. It is the largest historically black college or university (HBCU) in Louisiana, a member-school of the Thurgood Marshall College Fund, and the flagship institution of the Southern University System — the only HBCU university system in the United States.

History

Southern was chartered in 1880 in New Orleans by a group of black political leaders including former governor P. B. S. Pinchback. It moved to a bluff overlooking the Mississippi River in Scotlandville, Baton Rouge, in 1914. Under president Felton Grandison Clark the campus and enrolment expanded greatly in the mid-20th century.

Academics and culture

The university offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees, including programmes in engineering, business, law (through the Southern University Law Center), nursing, and agriculture. It is famous for its marching band, the Human Jukebox, and for the Bayou Classic football game against Grambling State.
