Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta

The Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta is an annual festival of hot air balloons held in Albuquerque, New Mexico, over nine days in early October. It is the largest ballooning event in the world, drawing hundreds of balloons and pilots and hundreds of thousands of spectators to Balloon Fiesta Park.

History

The fiesta began in 1972 as a highlight of the 50th birthday celebration of local radio station KOB, when 13 balloons launched from a shopping mall parking lot. It grew rapidly: the first World Hot Air Balloon Championship was held at the event in 1975, and the launch site moved to its permanent 365-acre Balloon Fiesta Park home in the city's north valley in the 1990s.

Events

The signature event is the Mass Ascension, when all participating balloons launch in coordinated waves at dawn. Other fixtures include the Special Shape Rodeo (balloons shaped as animals, cartoon characters, and objects), evening Balloon Glows in which tethered balloons light their burners in unison after dark, fireworks, and flying competitions. The predictable local wind pattern known as the "Albuquerque box" — different wind directions at different altitudes — lets pilots steer back toward the launch field. The fiesta is often described as the most photographed event in the world.
