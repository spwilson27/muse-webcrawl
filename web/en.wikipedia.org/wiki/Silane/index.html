Silane

FormulaSiH₄
AppearanceColourless gas
Boiling point−112 °C

Silane (systematically named silicane, chemical formula SiH₄) is an inorganic compound and the simplest silicon hydride, analogous to methane. It is a colourless, pyrophoric gas with a repulsive odour; it ignites spontaneously on contact with air.

Properties and structure

Silane is tetrahedral, like methane, but the Si–H bond is weaker and longer than the C–H bond, making silane far more reactive. The term "silanes" also denotes the family of related compounds SinH2n+2 and a large class of organosilicon compounds bearing Si–H bonds.

Uses

Silane is used in the semiconductor industry, where its thermal decomposition deposits polycrystalline and amorphous silicon in chemical vapour deposition. It is also a precursor to silicones, silicon nitride and silicon carbide. Because it is pyrophoric and toxic, it is handled with great care in industry.
