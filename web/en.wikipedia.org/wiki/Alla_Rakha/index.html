Ustad Alla Rakha Qureshi (29 April 1919 – 3 February 2000), popularly known as Alla Rakha, was an Indian tabla player and a leading exponent of the Punjab gharana. He was a frequent accompanist of sitar virtuoso Ravi Shankar and played a central role in popularizing the tabla in the West.

Early life and training

Born in Ghagwal village (now in Jammu and Kashmir), Alla Rakha began playing tabla at age 12 and studied under Mian Qadir Baksh of the Punjab gharana. He also trained in Hindustani vocal music and raga under Ashiq Ali Khan.

Career

He worked at All India Radio in Lahore and later in Bombay (Mumbai), composing music for Hindi films under the name A. R. Qureshi. From the 1960s, his partnership with Ravi Shankar brought him worldwide fame; the two performed at the Monterey Pop Festival (1967), Woodstock (1969), and the Concert for Bangladesh (1971). He also collaborated with Western musicians including Buddy Rich on the album Rich à la Rakha (1968).

Legacy and family

His sons Zakir Hussain, Fazal Qureshi, and Taufiq Qureshi are all noted tabla players. He received the Padma Shri in 1977 and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award in 1982.
