Flying wing

A flying wing is a tailless fixed-wing aircraft design in which the fuselage is merged into the wing, with no distinct horizontal stabiliser or separate fuselage, so that the entire aircraft contributes to lift.

History

Pioneers included the Horten brothers in Germany and Jack Northrop in the United States, whose YB-35 and YB-49 bombers explored the concept in the 1940s.

The design culminated operationally in stealth aircraft such as the Northrop B-2 Spirit and the later B-21 Raider, where the shape reduces radar cross-section.

Characteristics

Flying wings offer low drag but pose stability and control challenges historically solved with split drag rudders and, later, fly-by-wire computers.
