Corpus striatum

The corpus striatum, or simply the striatum, is a cluster of interconnected nuclei that make up a major part of the basal ganglia in the forebrain. It is composed principally of the caudate nucleus and the putamen, divided by the white matter tract of the internal capsule; the putamen together with the globus pallidus forms the lentiform nucleus. The striatum is the largest component of the basal ganglia and the main input structure of the system.

Function

The corpus striatum receives excitatory input from the cerebral cortex and thalamus and modulatory dopaminergic input from the substantia nigra pars compacta. It is involved in motor control, procedural learning, habit formation, and the processing of reward through the mesolimbic pathways, whose inclusion of the nucleus accumbens (ventral striatum) links motivation and reinforcement.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the corpus striatum is implicated in Parkinson's disease (loss of dopaminergic input), Huntington's disease (degeneration of striatal medium spiny neurons), obsessive–compulsive disorder, Tourette syndrome and addiction.
