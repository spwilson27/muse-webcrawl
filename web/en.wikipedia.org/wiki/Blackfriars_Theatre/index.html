The Blackfriars Theatre was the name given to two separate indoor playhouses in the precinct of the former Dominican (Blackfriars) friary in the City of London. The second Blackfriars became the principal winter home of Shakespeare's company, the King's Men, from 1608 until the theatres closed in 1642.

History

The first Blackfriars playhouse was created in 1576 within the old friary buildings for the Children of the Chapel Royal. A larger second theatre was fitted out by James Burbage in 1596 in the former parliament hall of the friary, but local residents' petitions initially blocked its use by an adult company; it was instead used by boy players. In 1608 the Burbage syndicate took it over for the King's Men, who thereafter alternated between the open-air Globe in summer and the intimate, candlelit Blackfriars in winter.

Influence on drama

The indoor venue charged higher prices, seated perhaps 600–700 spectators, and allowed music between acts and elaborate stage effects. Its conditions are often linked to the character of Shakespeare's late plays — the romances such as Cymbeline and The Winter's Tale — and to the development of Jacobean indoor drama generally. Parliament closed all theatres in 1642, and the building was demolished in 1655. A modern replica, the Sam Wanamaker Playhouse at Shakespeare's Globe, opened in 2014.
