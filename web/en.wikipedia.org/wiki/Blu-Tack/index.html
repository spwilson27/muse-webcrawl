Blu Tack is a reusable putty-like pressure-sensitive adhesive produced by the Australian and British divisions of the adhesive manufacturer Bostik. It is used to attach lightweight objects — most famously posters — to walls, doors and other dry surfaces without leaving holes, and to hold small items in place. The original product is blue, although versions are sold in white, pink and other colours.

History

Blu Tack was developed around 1970 at Bostik's Leicester works, reportedly as an accidental by-product of research into a new sealant. It was initially produced in white; the blue colouring was added amid concerns that children might mistake the putty for chewing gum or sweets. It has become a genericised trademark in Britain and Australia, where "blu-tack" is used for adhesive putties generally.

Composition and properties

The exact formulation is a trade secret; it is a synthetic rubber compound that behaves as a viscoelastic solid. It adheres best to non-porous surfaces and can be re-used many times, though over long periods it may leave an oily mark on absorbent surfaces such as paper and painted walls. About 180 million packs were reported sold in its first decades, and in 2015 the brand was celebrated with anniversary packaging.

Other uses

Beyond posters, Blu Tack is used by modellers to mask camouflage patterns when airbrushing, by sculptors and stop-motion animators, by audio enthusiasts to damp loudspeaker stands, and as an improvised earplug or keyboard cleaner.
