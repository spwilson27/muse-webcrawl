Solar chimney

Solar chimney (solar updraft tower)
TypeSolar thermal power / passive ventilation
PrototypeManzanares, Spain (1982–1989): 195 m tower, 50 kW
PrincipleGreenhouse heating + stack-effect convection + wind turbines

A solar chimney — often referred to as a thermal chimney — is a way of improving the natural ventilation of buildings by using convection of air heated by passive solar energy. In its power-generating form, the solar updraft tower, air is heated by the sun under a vast circular greenhouse collector, and the rising hot air drives turbines at the base of a tall chimney tower.

Power generation

The concept was described by Colonel Isidoro Cabanyes in 1903 and developed by German engineer Jörg Schlaich. A prototype built at Manzanares, Spain, in 1982 had a 195-metre tower and a collector 244 metres in diameter, producing up to 50 kW; it operated for seven years before the tower's guy wires rusted and it collapsed in a storm in 1989. Efficiency is low — around 1–2% of incident sunlight — because the physics depends on the temperature difference and tower height; proposed commercial plants call for towers up to 1,000 metres tall. A 200 MW design was long proposed for Buronga, Australia, but never built; a smaller plant began operating at Jinshawan, Inner Mongolia, in 2010.

Ventilation use

At building scale, a solar chimney is a vertical shaft, often glazed and painted black to absorb heat, whose buoyant warm air draws fresh air through the building — a passive cooling technique used since antiquity in Persian windcatcher (badgir) architecture and in modern sustainable design.
