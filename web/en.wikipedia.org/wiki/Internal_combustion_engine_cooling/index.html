Internal combustion engine cooling

In automotive engineering, an internal combustion engine's cooling system maintains the engine at its most efficient temperature, and no higher, for all operating conditions. Only about a quarter to a third of the energy released by burning fuel is converted into useful work; much of the rest must be removed as heat to prevent damage to engine components.

Types

Liquid cooling

Most modern engines are liquid-cooled: a coolant (typically a mixture of water and ethylene glycol antifreeze) is circulated by a water pump through channels in the engine block and cylinder head, absorbing heat and carrying it to a radiator, where it is transferred to the air. A thermostat regulates coolant flow to maintain operating temperature.

Air cooling

Air-cooled engines dissipate heat directly from finned cylinders and heads, as used historically by Volkswagen, Porsche, and many motorcycle engines. Air cooling is simpler and lighter but gives less precise temperature control.

Components

  • Radiator and cooling fan
  • Water pump
  • Thermostat
  • Expansion (overflow) tank
  • Hoses and coolant passages
