Pearl farming

Pearl farming is the cultivation of pearl oysters or freshwater mussels to produce cultured pearls. A small nucleus bead and a piece of mantle tissue are surgically inserted into the mollusc, which coats the nucleus with nacre over a period of months to years.

Process

Oysters are typically farmed in sheltered bays, suspended from rafts or lines in baskets that are regularly cleaned. After a growing period of roughly 18 months to several years, the pearls are harvested, graded by size, shape, lustre and surface quality, and matched for jewellery.

History

The modern cultured-pearl industry was developed in Japan in the early 20th century by Kokichi Mikimoto and others, and later spread to Australia, French Polynesia (Tahitian pearls), Indonesia, the Philippines and China, which dominates freshwater pearl production.
