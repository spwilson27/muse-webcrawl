Sensation novel
Period1860s–1870s, Britain
Key authorsWilkie Collins, Mary Elizabeth Braddon, Ellen Wood
Key worksThe Woman in White, Lady Audley's Secret, East Lynne

The sensation novel was a literary genre of fiction, mainly in Britain in the 1860s and 1870s, whose plots revolved around crime, bigamy, mistaken identity, secrets, and shocking revelations within respectable domestic settings.

Characteristics and reception

Wilkie Collins's The Woman in White (1860) and The Moonstone (1868), Mary Elizabeth Braddon's Lady Audley's Secret (1862), and Ellen Wood's East Lynne (1861) were bestsellers, serialized for a growing middle-class readership. Critics attacked the genre as lurid and morally corrupting — Henry Mansel's 1863 review coined much of the hostile framing — while its assault on domestic respectability, strong female characters, and narrative suspense proved enormously popular.

Influence

The sensation novel drew on Gothic fiction, melodrama, and newspaper crime reporting, and fed into detective fiction and the modern thriller. Its publishing phenomenon anticipated later mass-culture sensations such as Seinfeld in a different medium.
