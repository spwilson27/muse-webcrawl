Book value

In accounting, book value (also carrying value or carrying amount) is the value of an asset as recorded on a company's balance sheet. For most assets it equals the original cost minus accumulated depreciation, amortisation and impairment. For a company as a whole, book value means its net asset value: total assets minus total liabilities — equivalently, shareholders' equity.


Book value of a firm = total assets − total liabilities

Book value of an asset

An asset's book value starts at its acquisition cost and is written down over its useful life. A machine bought for $100,000 and depreciated by $60,000 has a book value of $40,000. Because depreciation schedules and historical cost rarely track changing market prices, book value often diverges substantially from market value; land, for example, may sit on the books at a decades-old purchase price far below its current worth. If an asset's recoverable amount falls below its carrying value, an impairment charge reduces the book value.

Book value of a company

For investors, the book value of equity divided by the number of shares gives book value per share, and the ratio of share price to book value per share is the price-to-book (P/B) ratio. Value investors, following Benjamin Graham, historically looked for shares trading below book value as potentially undervalued, on the logic that the firm's net assets alone justified the price. Tangible book value excludes intangible assets such as goodwill and patents, giving a more conservative measure.

Limitations

Book value is a backward-looking accounting figure. It omits internally generated intangibles — brands, know-how, customer relationships — that dominate the worth of modern service and technology companies, which therefore often trade at many times book value. Conversely, firms in declining industries may trade below book value because the market doubts that their assets are worth their carrying amounts. Book value remains most informative for asset-heavy businesses such as banks, insurers and manufacturers.
