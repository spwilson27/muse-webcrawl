Star Trek: Voyager

Star Trek: Voyager is an American science fiction television series created by Rick Berman, Michael Piller, and Jeri Taylor. It aired on UPN from 16 January 1995 to 23 May 2001, running for seven seasons and 172 episodes. It is the fifth series in the Star Trek franchise and was the flagship show of the UPN network at its launch.

Premise

The series follows the starship USS Voyager, commanded by Captain Kathryn Janeway (Kate Mulgrew) — the first woman to lead a Star Trek series. While pursuing a Maquis vessel in the Badlands, both ships are pulled 70,000 light-years across the galaxy into the Delta Quadrant by the alien Caretaker. With their crews forced to merge, Janeway sets a course for home — a journey that would take some 75 years at maximum warp.

Cast and characters

The ensemble includes Commander Chakotay (Robert Beltran), the half-Klingon engineer B'Elanna Torres (Roxann Dawson), the Vulcan security officer Tuvok (Tim Russ), helmsman Tom Paris (Robert Duncan McNeill), Ensign Harry Kim (Garrett Wang), the holographic Emergency Medical Hologram known as the Doctor (Robert Picardo), and the Talaxian Neelix (Ethan Phillips) and Ocampan Kes (Jennifer Lien). In the fourth season, Jeri Ryan joined as Seven of Nine, a liberated Borg drone whose struggle to regain her humanity became a central theme.

Reception and legacy

Voyager explored themes of survival, ethics far from Federation oversight, and the conflict between Starfleet principles and the necessities of the journey home. The series concluded with the two-part finale "Endgame," in which Voyager returns to Earth. It won several Emmy Awards, particularly for its visual effects and for the Doctor's episodes, and its characters have reappeared in later franchise series, including Janeway and Seven of Nine in Star Trek: Prodigy and Star Trek: Picard.
