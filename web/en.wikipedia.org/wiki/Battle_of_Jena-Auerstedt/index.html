Battle of Jena–Auerstedt

Date14 October 1806
LocationJena and Auerstedt, present-day Thuringia, Germany
ResultDecisive French victory
BelligerentsFrench Empire vs. Kingdom of Prussia and Electorate of Saxony
Strength~96,000 French (Jena); ~27,000 under Davout at Auerstedt. Prussians ~53,000 at Jena; ~63,000 at Auerstedt

The Battle of Jena–Auerstedt was a pair of simultaneous battles fought on 14 October 1806 between the forces of Napoleon Bonaparte's First French Empire and those of Frederick William III of Prussia. The twin victories destroyed Prussia's military power in the War of the Fourth Coalition and opened the road to Berlin.

Background

Prussia, confident in the army inherited from Frederick the Great, issued an ultimatum demanding that French forces withdraw east of the Rhine. Napoleon instead invaded, advancing rapidly through Thuringia. The Prussian army under the Duke of Brunswick and Prince Hohenlohe was caught strung out and outmanoeuvred.

The battles

At Jena, Napoleon believed he faced the main Prussian army and crushed Hohenlohe's force with the bulk of the Grande Armée. In fact, the main Prussian army was at Auerstedt, where Marshal Louis-Nicolas Davout's III Corps — outnumbered roughly two to one — held and then routed it. The Duke of Brunswick was mortally wounded at Auerstedt, contributing to the collapse of Prussian command.

Aftermath

The Prussian army disintegrated in the pursuit. Berlin fell on 27 October, Prussian fortresses surrendered with little resistance, and the king fled to East Prussia. The following year the Treaties of Tilsit stripped Prussia of half its territory and population, and the humiliation spurred sweeping Prussian military and administrative reforms.
