Basava (1131–1167), also known as Basavanna and Basaveshwara, was a twelfth-century Indian statesman, philosopher, poet and social reformer in the kingdom of the Kalachuri king Bijjala II in present-day Karnataka. He is revered as the founding saint of the Lingayat tradition within Shaivism and is remembered for a religious and social movement that challenged caste hierarchy, ritualism and gender inequality.

Life

Basava was born into a Brahmin family at Basavana Bagewadi. Tradition recounts that he rejected the sacred-thread ceremony and left home for Kudalasangama, a pilgrimage centre at the confluence of the Krishna and Malaprabha rivers, where he studied and had his formative spiritual experiences. He rose to become a minister — ultimately treasurer or prime minister — at the court of Bijjala in Kalyana (modern Basavakalyan).

Teachings and movement

At Kalyana, Basava established the Anubhava Mantapa, a "hall of spiritual experience" where devotees of all castes and both sexes — including the poets Akka Mahadevi, Allama Prabhu and Siddharama — debated religion and society. His followers, the sharanas, composed vachanas: short, direct devotional poems in Kannada, addressed to Shiva as "Lord of the Meeting Rivers" (Kudalasangamanatha). Basava taught personal devotion through the ishtalinga worn on the body, the dignity of labour (kayaka), sharing one's earnings (dasoha), and rejected caste distinctions, animal sacrifice and temple ritual mediated by priests. A controversial inter-caste marriage among his followers provoked a backlash at court, and the movement dispersed after 1167; Basava died at Kudalasangama.

Legacy

The Lingayat community that traces its origins to Basava is a major presence in Karnataka today. Vachana literature is a cornerstone of Kannada letters, and Basava is celebrated with statues across Karnataka, including a prominent equestrian-era statue in Bengaluru and the Basava statue unveiled in London in 2015 — said to be the first statue of an Indian philosopher installed in that city. His birth anniversary, Basava Jayanti, is a public holiday in Karnataka.
