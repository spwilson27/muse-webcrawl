The Trienio Liberal (Spanish for "Liberal Triennium" or "Three Liberal Years") was a period of liberal government in Spain from 1820 to 1823. It began when a military revolt led by Rafael del Riego forced King Ferdinand VII to restore the liberal Spanish Constitution of 1812, which the king had abolished in 1814.

Background

After the Peninsular War, Ferdinand VII had annulled the Constitution of Cádiz and restored absolutism, persecuting liberals. Discontent in the army—particularly among troops assembled at Cádiz for service in the rebellious American colonies—erupted on 1 January 1820 when Riego proclaimed the constitution at Las Cabezas de San Juan.

The liberal government

The revolutionary government freed political prisoners, abolished the Inquisition (again), restored freedom of the press, and pursued reforms of the church, the military and local government. However, the liberals were divided between moderates (moderados) and radicals (exaltados), and Ferdinand conspired constantly against the regime he nominally headed.

Collapse

At the Congress of Verona in 1822, the Holy Alliance authorised France to intervene. In 1823 a French army, the "Hundred Thousand Sons of Saint Louis", invaded Spain, defeated the constitutional forces, and restored Ferdinand to absolute power. Riego was executed, and the ensuing repression opened the period known as the Ominous Decade.
