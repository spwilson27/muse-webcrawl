Mantle (vesture)

A mantle is a type of loose garment, usually worn over indoor clothing, serving the same purpose as an overcoat. Technically, the term describes a long, flowing, typically sleeveless cloak or shawl-like garment, usually fastened at the neck or chest, and it has been worn by both men and women in many periods and cultures — from the ancient Greek himation and the Roman pallium to medieval and early-modern ceremonial dress.

Uses

Mantles have served as warm outdoor garments, as insignia of rank (royal and noble mantles of ermine or velvet, as in coronation robes), and as religious vesture. In Catholic tradition, the cope is a long mantle-like liturgical vestment; the Virgin Mary is often depicted sheltering supplicants under her mantle (the Schutzmantelmadonna or Madonna of Mercy motif). In heraldry, a mantle (or pavilion) is the flowing drapery shown behind a shield in a coat of arms, especially for royalty and high nobility.

Etymology and metaphor

The word comes from Old French mantel, from Latin mantellum, a cloak. Because a mantle covers and is passed from one wearer to another, the word acquired figurative senses: one may "assume the mantle" of a predecessor (an allusion to Elijah's mantle falling on Elisha in the Hebrew Bible), and the term is extended to other covering layers, such as the Earth's mantle and the soft body wall of molluscs.
