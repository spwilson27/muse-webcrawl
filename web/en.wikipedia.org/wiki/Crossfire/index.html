Crossfire

Crossfire is a situation in which weapons fire from two or more directions strikes an area, so that the target is caught between converging lines of fire. The term is also used figuratively for exposure to attacks or criticism from opposing sides, and appears as the title of numerous works of film, television, music and games.

Military usage

In military tactics, crossfire greatly increases the difficulty of finding cover, because a position protected from fire in one direction may be exposed to fire from another. Defenders aim to construct positions with interlocking fields of fire so that attackers advancing into a kill zone are engaged from multiple sides simultaneously. The phrase "caught in the crossfire" describes combatants — or bystanders — struck by fire exchanged between opposing forces.

In popular culture

The word has been used as the title of the 1947 film noir Crossfire, the CNN debate programme Crossfire (1982–2005, briefly revived in 2013), songs by artists including Stevie Ray Vaughan and Brandon Flowers, and the board game Crossfire, in which players shoot ball bearings at targets. AMD's multi-GPU technology was also marketed under the name CrossFire.
