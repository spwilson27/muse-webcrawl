NORC, formerly the National Opinion Research Center, is an independent, nonpartisan research organization affiliated with the University of Chicago. Founded in 1941, it is one of the largest social science research organizations in the United States and conducts research in economics, health, education, public opinion and society.

History

NORC was established in 1941 at the University of Denver and relocated to the University of Chicago in 1947. Over the following decades it became known for rigorous survey methodology and for large-scale longitudinal studies. In 2010 the organization formally shortened its name from the National Opinion Research Center to NORC, reflecting the breadth of its work beyond opinion polling.

Major projects

NORC is best known for the General Social Survey (GSS), conducted since 1972, which tracks American social attitudes and trends and is widely used by social scientists. The organization also operates the AmeriSpeak Panel, a probability-based panel used for survey research, and has conducted major studies for federal agencies, foundations and academic institutions, including health interview surveys and evaluations of social programs.
