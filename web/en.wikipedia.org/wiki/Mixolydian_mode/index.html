Mixolydian mode

The Mixolydian mode is the fifth mode of the major scale. It can be formed by playing all the notes of a major scale starting from its fifth degree, or equivalently by taking a major scale and lowering its seventh degree by a semitone. A G Mixolydian scale, for example, contains the notes G–A–B–C–D–E–F, using the key signature of C major.

Characteristics

Because its third degree is major but its seventh is minor, the Mixolydian mode sounds like a major key with a bluesy, relaxed pull, and its tonic chord is a dominant seventh chord. The flattened seventh weakens the leading-tone resolution of the major scale, giving the mode a circular quality suited to modal vamps rather than functional cadences.

Usage

Mixolydian is widespread in folk music, blues, rock, and jazz. It underlies the harmony of many rock and pop songs built on the I–♭VII–IV progression, and appears in traditional music of the British Isles and in the modes of Indian classical music, where it corresponds approximately to the raga Khamaj thaat. It should not be confused with the ancient Greek mixolydian harmonia, which described a different scale arrangement.
