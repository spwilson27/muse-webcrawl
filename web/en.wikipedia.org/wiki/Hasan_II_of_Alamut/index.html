Hasan II of Alamut

Hasan II of Alamut (1142 or 1143 – 9 January 1166), known in Nizari tradition by the epithet 'ala dhikrihi al-salam ("on his mention be peace"), was the fourth ruler of the Nizari Isma'ili state at Alamut. He is famous for proclaiming the qiyama (resurrection) in 1164, an event that radically reinterpreted Nizari doctrine by declaring the abrogation of the outward sharia law for those who had attained spiritual resurrection.

Background

Hasan was the son of Muhammad ibn Buzurg-Ummid, the third lord of Alamut. Nizari tradition maintained that he was in fact a descendant of the imam Nizar ibn al-Mustansir, secretly brought to Alamut as an infant, and Hasan himself encouraged the belief that he was the imam's hidden descendant rather than merely the da'i ruling in his name.

The qiyama proclamation

On 17 Ramadan 559 AH (8 August 1164), Hasan assembled the Nizaris of Persia at Alamut and, in a carefully staged ceremony, announced a message in the name of the hidden imam: the resurrection (qiyama) had come, the obligations of the religious law were lifted for the community of the resurrected, and Hasan was declared the imam's caliph and proof (hujja). A similar proclamation was made in Quhistan. The qiyama doctrine held that outward ritual concealed an inner spiritual reality, and that paradise and hell were spiritual states.

Death

Hasan was assassinated on 9 January 1166 at Lambsar Castle by his brother-in-law, a Buyid prince named Hasan ibn Namawar, who opposed his revolutionary doctrine. He was succeeded by his son, Nur al-Din Muhammad II.
