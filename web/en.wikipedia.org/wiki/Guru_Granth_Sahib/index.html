The Guru Granth Sahib is the central scripture of Sikhism, treated by Sikhs not merely as a book but as the eternal living Guru: it is enthroned in every gurdwara, fanned and attended, and put to rest at night. Compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604 and given final form by Guru Gobind Singh, its 1,430 pages contain hymns by six Sikh Gurus and by Hindu and Muslim saints including Kabir and Sheikh Farid, set to ragas and arranged by musical mode.

Status and use

Guru Gobind Singh ended the line of human Gurus in 1708, declaring the Granth and the community (Panth) his successors. A random opening reading (hukamnama) provides daily guidance, and continuous readings (akhand path) take about 48 hours. Its theology centres on remembrance of God's name (nam simran), the unity of God and the rejection of caste — radical inclusions for its time, since low-caste and Muslim voices share its sanctity.
