Maasvlakte

The Maasvlakte is a massive port and industrial area forming the westernmost part of the Port of Rotterdam in the Netherlands. It is built on land reclaimed from the North Sea in the 1960s and takes its name from the river Maas (Meuse), whose estuary it adjoins.

Development

The original Maasvlakte was created in the 1960s by dredging and reclaiming sand from the sea, extending the Europoort area seaward to accommodate the largest ships then in service. It is dominated by container terminals, bulk handling facilities for coal and iron ore, a large power station, and tank storage and chemical industry.

Maasvlakte 2

To preserve Rotterdam's position as Europe's largest port, construction of Maasvlakte 2 began in 2008 on a further reclamation of about 2,000 hectares immediately west of the original area, roughly half of it usable port land. The first container terminals — Rotterdam World Gateway and APM Terminals Maasvlakte II — opened in 2014–2015 and feature remotely operated ship-to-shore cranes and automated stacking cranes. The new port area created additional beach and recreation space along its seaward edge.

Transport links

The Maasvlakte is served by the Betuweroute freight railway to Germany, motorway access via the A15, and inland shipping through the port's basins. A wind farm and nature compensation areas were built as part of the environmental conditions for the expansion.
