Pseudonocardia is a genus of Gram-positive, aerobic bacteria in the family Pseudonocardiaceae, within the actinobacteria. The genus was described by Aino Henssen in 1957; its name, "false Nocardia", reflects a resemblance to the genus Nocardia, from which it is distinguished by cell-wall chemistry and the absence of mycolic acids.

Description

Species form branching substrate mycelium that fragments or bears chains of spores on aerial hyphae. They are widespread in soils, composts, plant material and indoor environments. Some species are thermophilic and grow in self-heating compost and fodder.

Ecology and significance

Pseudonocardia species are notable symbionts of fungus-growing (attine) ants: bacteria cultivated on the ants' cuticle produce antibiotics that suppress the parasitic fungus Escovopsis in the ants' gardens. Other species produce antibiotics and industrially useful enzymes, and some degrade recalcitrant compounds — P. dioxanivorans, for example, can grow on the solvent pollutant 1,4-dioxane. A few species have been reported as rare opportunistic pathogens in humans.
