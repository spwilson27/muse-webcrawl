Queercore (also called homocore) is a cultural and social movement that began in the mid-1980s as an offshoot of punk subculture. Distinct from mainstream gay culture — which its founders criticised as commercialised and conformist — and from a punk scene they saw as homophobic and macho, queercore expressed queer identity through punk music, zines, film and art, embracing do-it-yourself methods of production and distribution.

Origins

The movement coalesced around the Toronto zine J.D.s, founded in 1985 by Bruce LaBruce and G. B. Jones, which coined the milieu's culture of queer punk provocation, and the San Francisco zine Homocore (1988) by Tom Jennings and others. In the early 1990s, labels such as Donna Dresch's Chainsaw Records and Outpunk, and the pop-punk band Pansy Division, brought queercore wider attention; bands including Tribe 8, Team Dresch, God Is My Co-Pilot and Limp Wrist defined its range from riotous hardcore to experimental rock.

Relationship to riot grrrl

Queercore developed in parallel with, and partly overlapped, the riot grrrl movement; the two shared zine networks, all-ages shows and feminist, anti-assimilationist politics, and artists such as Kaia Wilson and Donna Dresch moved between both scenes.

Legacy

Queercore festivals (such as Homo-a-Go-Go), films including The Yo-Yo Gang (1992), and later retrospectives have documented the scene; contemporary queer punk and labels such as Get Better Records continue its DIY ethos.
