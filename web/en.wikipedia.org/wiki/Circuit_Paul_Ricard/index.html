The Circuit Paul Ricard is a French motor racing circuit at Le Castellet, near Marseille, built in 1969 by drinks magnate Paul Ricard and opened in 1970. It is famous for its very long Mistral straight and its distinctive blue and red abrasive run-off areas.

History

The circuit hosted the French Grand Prix of Formula One fourteen times between 1971 and 1990, and the Bol d'Or motorcycle endurance race for many years. In 1999 it was bought and rebuilt as the Paul Ricard High Tech Test Track, an advanced testing facility with over 160 possible layouts and an automatic sprinkler system for wet-weather testing. Racing returned progressively, and Formula One came back for the French Grand Prix from 2018 to 2022.

Characteristics

The full Grand Prix layout is 5.842 km with 15 corners, including the fast Signes curve at the end of the Mistral straight (now interrupted by a chicane for Formula One). The coloured run-off zones — blue paint being less abrasive than the red "high-grip" strips — replace gravel traps and are widely imitated. The circuit adjoins Le Castellet Airport and several research facilities.
