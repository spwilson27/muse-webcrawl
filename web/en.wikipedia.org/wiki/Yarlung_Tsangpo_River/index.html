Yarlung Tsangpo River

Yarlung Tsangpo
SourceAngsi Glacier, Tibet
Length~1,125 km (699 mi) (as Yarlung Tsangpo)
Basin size~241,700 km²

The Yarlung Tsangpo, also called Yarlung Zangbo (Tibetan: ཡར་ཀླུངས་གཙང་པོ་; Chinese: 雅鲁藏布江), is the upper stream of the Brahmaputra River located in the Tibet Autonomous Region, China. It is one of the highest major rivers in the world, flowing eastward across the southern Tibetan Plateau at elevations above 4,000 metres before turning sharply south through the Himalayas.

Course

The river originates from the Angsi Glacier in western Tibet, near Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar. It flows east through the wide, arid Yarlung Valley of south Tibet — the cradle of Tibetan civilisation, from which the river takes its name — passing near Lhasa's region and receiving tributaries such as the Lhasa River (Kyi Chu), Nyang, and Parlung.

Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon

Near the eastern end of the Himalayas, the river makes a great bend around the peak of Namcha Barwa and cuts the Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon, one of the deepest canyons in the world, with depths exceeding 5,000 metres in places and a length of about 504 km. After descending through the gorge, the river enters Arunachal Pradesh, India, where it is called the Siang (or Dihang), and later becomes the Brahmaputra in Assam, eventually joining the Ganges delta in Bangladesh.

Development

China has built several hydropower dams on the river's middle reaches, including Zangmu, and much larger projects in the lower gorge have been studied and approved, raising discussion in downstream India and Bangladesh about water flows and seismic risk.
