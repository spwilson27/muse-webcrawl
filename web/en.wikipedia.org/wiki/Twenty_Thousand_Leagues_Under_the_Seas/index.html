Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas

Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (French: Vingt mille lieues sous les mers) is a classic science fiction adventure novel by French writer Jules Verne, first published in 1870. It recounts the voyage of the submarine Nautilus, commanded by the enigmatic Captain Nemo, as narrated by Professor Pierre Aronnax, who is held aboard after being rescued from the sea. The title refers to the distance travelled under the seas, not to depth (20,000 leagues being roughly 80,000 km).

Plot summary

In 1866 a mysterious sea monster is reported attacking ships. Aronnax, his servant Conseil and the harpooner Ned Land join an American expedition aboard the frigate Abraham Lincoln. Thrown overboard in an encounter with the "monster," they discover it is an advanced electric submarine, the Nautilus, and are taken aboard as permanent guests of its captain, Nemo. Over a journey of some 20,000 leagues they tour the oceans: pearl banks off Ceylon, the lost continent of Atlantis, an underwater funeral, the South Pole, a battle with giant squid, and the wreck-strewn scene of Nemo's vengeance. The three finally escape in the maelstrom off Norway, Nemo's fate unresolved in this volume.

Captain Nemo and themes

Nemo — whose backstory Verne developed further in The Mysterious Island — is a scientific genius and embittered exile who has renounced the world to live free beneath the seas, waging war on oppression as he defines it. The novel is notable for its detailed, plausible treatment of submarine technology, much of which anticipated later developments, and for long descriptive catalogues of marine life.

Legacy

The book has never been out of print and is among the most translated works of fiction. Adaptations include Disney's 1954 film, and the Nautilus and Captain Nemo have become enduring fixtures of popular culture.
