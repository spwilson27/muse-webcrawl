Mare Imbrium

Mare Imbrium (Latin imbrium, “the Sea of Showers” or “Sea of Rains”) is a vast lava plain within the Imbrium Basin on the Moon. It is one of the larger craters in the Solar System, formed when a proto-planet roughly the size of New Jersey struck the Moon during the Late Heavy Bombardment about 3.9 billion years ago. The basin later flooded with basaltic lava, producing the smooth plain visible today.

Characteristics

The mare has a diameter of about 1,145 km (711 mi). It is surrounded by three concentric rings of mountains raised by the impact: the outer ring includes the Montes Jura and Montes Alpes, the middle ring the Montes Apenninus and Montes Caucasus, and the innermost ring has largely been submerged by lava. The crater Plato lies on its north-eastern shore, and Sinus Iridum, the Bay of Rainbows, juts into its north-western edge.

Exploration

The Soviet rover Lunokhod 1 landed in Mare Imbrium in November 1970, and Apollo 15 landed near its eastern edge in 1971, by the Hadley Rille at the foothills of the Apennines. In December 2013 China’s Chang’e 3 lander and Yutu rover touched down in the northern part of the mare.
