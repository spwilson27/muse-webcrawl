Music of Korea

Korean music encompasses court music, folk music, and modern popular music of the Korean Peninsula. Traditional Korean music is generally divided into aak (ritual court music), dangak (court music of Chinese origin), and hyangak (native court music) on the one hand, and the folk traditions of the common people on the other.

Traditional music

Court music includes the Confucian shrine music jongmyo jeryeak, performed with dance at the Jongmyo ancestral shrine in Seoul, recognized by UNESCO in 2001. Characteristic instruments include the geomungo and gayageum zithers, the daegeum bamboo flute, the haegeum fiddle, the piri oboe, and percussion such as the janggu hourglass drum and bak clappers.

Folk music

Folk genres include pansori, an epic sung storytelling form performed by a singer and drummer; sanjo, virtuosic instrumental solo music; nongak (farmers' dance), a boisterous percussion band tradition; and folk songs (minyo) such as "Arirang", Korea's best-known song. Pansori and nongak are UNESCO-listed intangible heritage.

Modern music

During the 20th century Western music entered Korea, producing genres such as trot and ballad. Since the 1990s, K-pop—idol pop music blending song, rap, and choreography—has become a global phenomenon through groups such as BTS, Blackpink, EXO, Twice, and solo artist Psy, whose "Gangnam Style" (2012) went viral worldwide. South Korea has also developed active hip-hop, indie rock, and electronic scenes.
