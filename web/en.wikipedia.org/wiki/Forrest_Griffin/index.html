Forrest Griffin

Forrest Griffin
Born: 1 July 1979, Columbus, Ohio, U.S.
Height: 6 ft 3 in (191 cm)
Division: Light Heavyweight
MMA record: 19 wins, 7 losses

Forrest Griffin (born 1 July 1979) is an American retired mixed martial artist and former UFC Light Heavyweight Champion. He won the first season of The Ultimate Fighter reality series in 2005, and his finale bout against Stephan Bonnar — a three-round war that Griffin won by unanimous decision — is widely credited with driving the mainstream breakthrough of the UFC and modern MMA. Griffin and Bonnar were inducted together into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2013.

Early life

Griffin grew up in Ohio and Georgia, played American football, studied political science at the University of Georgia, and worked as a law enforcement officer in Georgia while beginning his amateur and professional fighting career.

Mixed martial arts career

After building a record on the regional circuit, Griffin joined the cast of The Ultimate Fighter 1 (2005). His fight with Bonnar at the finale, broadcast live on Spike TV, is routinely described as the most important bout in UFC history. Griffin went on to defeat Mauricio "Shogun" Rua by submission at UFC 76 in 2007, and at UFC 86 in July 2008 he beat Quinton "Rampage" Jackson by unanimous decision to win the UFC Light Heavyweight Championship. He lost the title to Rashad Evans at UFC 92 in December 2008.

In later years Griffin split a trilogy with Tito Ortiz, was knocked out by Anderson Silva at UFC 101, and defeated Rich Franklin at UFC 126. Plagued by injuries, he retired in 2013 with a professional record of 19 wins and 7 losses, and took a role with the UFC working at the UFC Performance Institute in Las Vegas.

Author and media work

Griffin is the co-author of two humorous books on fighting and masculinity, Got Fight? The 50 Zen Principles of Hand-to-Face Combat (2009) and Be Ready When the Sh*t Goes Down (2010).
