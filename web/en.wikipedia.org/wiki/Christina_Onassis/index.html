Christina Onassis


Placeholder image: Christina Onassis

Christina Onassis (Greek: Χριστίνα Ωνάση; 11 December 1950 – 19 November 1988) was a Greek-Argentine businesswoman and socialite, the only daughter of the shipping magnate Aristotle Onassis and his first wife, Athina Livanos. She inherited a large share of her father's fortune and the management of the Onassis shipping empire after his death in 1975.

Early life

Christina was born in New York City and raised between New York, London and the family's private island of Skorpios in the Ionian Sea. Her parents divorced in 1960 after her father's affair with the opera singer Maria Callas; her mother later married Stavros Niarchos, her father's great shipping rival. Christina's brother Alexander died in a plane crash in Athens in 1973, a loss from which the family never fully recovered.

Career

After Aristotle Onassis died in Paris in March 1975, Christina took control of the Onassis Group, restructuring its tanker fleet and companies, and reached a settlement with her stepmother, Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis, over the estate. She was widely regarded as a shrewd negotiator who preserved the empire through the shipping crisis of the early 1980s.

Personal life and death

Christina married four times: to Joseph Bolker (1971), to Alexander Andreadis (1975), to the Russian shipping agent Sergei Kauzov (1978), and to the French businessman Thierry Roussel (1984), with whom she had her only child, Athina Onassis, born in 1985. Plagued by unhappiness, weight problems and dependence on prescription drugs, she was often described by the press as "the richest girl in the world" and a tragic heiress. She died suddenly of a heart attack, attributed to acute pulmonary oedema, at a country club near Buenos Aires on 19 November 1988, aged 37. She is buried in the Onassis family plot on Skorpios beside her father and brother.
