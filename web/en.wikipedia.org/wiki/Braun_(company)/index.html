Braun (company)

Braun GmbH (German pronunciation: [bʁaʊn], "brown") is a German consumer products company founded in 1921 in Frankfurt am Main by engineer Max Braun. It is best known for its electric shavers, epilators, oral-care products (marketed under Oral-B in partnership), hair-care appliances, and small kitchen appliances. Braun is celebrated in design history for the functionalist industrial design developed under Dieter Rams, which influenced generations of product designers, including those at Apple. Since 1967 the company has been owned by Gillette and, following Gillette's acquisition in 2005, by Procter & Gamble.

History

Max Braun established his workshop in Frankfurt in 1921, initially making drive belts and radio components. The company moved into complete radio receivers in 1929 and combined radio-phonograph sets soon after. After the Second World War Braun rebuilt and launched its first electric shaver, the S 50, in 1950, beginning a line of foil shavers that became the company's signature product. Kitchen machines such as the KM 3 food processor (1957) and the Multimix followed, and Braun became a byword for high-quality German consumer engineering.

In 1967 the Gillette Company of Boston acquired a majority stake in Braun as part of its expansion into electrical grooming appliances, and Braun later became wholly Gillette-owned. Procter & Gamble acquired Braun with Gillette in 2005. In 2012 P&G licensed the Braun brand for small household appliances such as irons, blenders, and coffee makers to Italy's De'Longhi Group, while retaining personal-care categories.

Design

Braun's design legacy stems from the 1950s, when the founder's sons Artur and Erwin Braun, advised by the Ulm School of Design (Hochschule für Gestaltung Ulm) and designer Hans Gugelot, adopted a rational, minimalist approach. Dieter Rams, who joined Braun in 1955 and led its design department from 1961 to 1995, produced classics such as the SK 4 record player (with Gugelot, 1956), the T 3 pocket radio, the ET 66 calculator, and the Sixtant shaver. Rams summarised his philosophy in "ten principles of good design," emphasising that good design is as little design as possible. Braun products are held in major museum collections, including the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Products

Braun's core range comprises Series shavers (including the Series 9), Silk-épil epilators, styling tools, thermometers, and clocks and watches. Oral care is marketed as Oral-B electric toothbrushes, developed with Braun technology. Kitchen appliances bearing the Braun name are produced under licence by De'Longhi.
