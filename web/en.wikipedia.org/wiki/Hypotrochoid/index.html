Hypotrochoid

A hypotrochoid is a roulette traced by a point attached to a circle of radius r rolling around the inside of a fixed circle of radius R, where the point is a distance d from the centre of the interior circle. The curve is the internal counterpart of the epitrochoid, and the family includes the hypocycloid (when d = r) and the ellipse as special cases.

Parametric equations

The parametric equations of the hypotrochoid are:

x(θ) = (R − r) cos θ + d cos( ((R − r)/r) θ )

y(θ) = (R − r) sin θ − d sin( ((R − r)/r) θ )

where θ is the angle swept by the centre of the rolling circle. The shape depends on the ratio R/r: if it is an integer n, the curve has n cusps or lobes in its hypocycloid form; if it is a rational number p/q, the curve closes after q revolutions; if irrational, the curve never closes and is dense in the annulus.

Special cases

When d = r, the point lies on the rolling circle and the hypotrochoid is a hypocycloid. When R = 2r, the curve degenerates to a line segment — the principle of the Tusi couple. When R = 2r and the point is inside the rolling circle (d < r), the traced curve is an ellipse; this is the mechanism of the ancient "trammel of Archimedes".

Applications

Hypotrochoid patterns are produced by the Spirograph drawing toy. The curve also appears in engineering: the housing of the Wankel rotary engine is approximately an epitrochoid, while hypotrochoid (hypocycloid) gear profiles are used in some gear pumps and in the design of certain internal combustion and compressor mechanisms because of their favourable contact properties.
