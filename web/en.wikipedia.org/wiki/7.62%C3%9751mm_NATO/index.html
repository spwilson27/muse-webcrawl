The 7.62×51mm NATO is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge developed in the 1950s as a standard for small arms among NATO countries. It was introduced in U.S. service in the M14 rifle and M60 machine gun, and later used in the M240 machine gun, M24 sniper weapon system, and other firearms.

History

Work on the cartridge began shortly after World War II, as the United States sought a shorter cartridge that could match the performance of the .30-06 Springfield while allowing lighter automatic weapons. The commercial .308 Winchester, released in 1952, is derived from the same development program and is dimensionally similar, though the two are not identical in chamber specifications.

Performance

The cartridge typically fires a bullet of about 147 grains at muzzle velocities around 850 m/s, delivering effective ranges of 800 metres and beyond in designated marksman and machine-gun roles. It remains in widespread military use worldwide despite the adoption of intermediate cartridges such as the 5.56×45mm NATO for standard infantry rifles.
