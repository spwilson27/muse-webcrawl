James Earle Fraser (November 4, 1876 – October 11, 1953) was an American sculptor during the first half of the 20th century. Among his best-known works are the sculpture The End of the Trail and the design of the Buffalo nickel, minted from 1913 to 1938.

Early life and training

Fraser was born in Winona, Minnesota, and spent part of his childhood on the prairie in South Dakota, where his father, a railroad engineer, worked during the period of westward expansion — experiences that later informed his depictions of Native American subjects and pioneers. He studied at the Art Institute of Chicago and then in Paris at the École des Beaux-Arts and the Académie Julian, where he worked as an assistant to Augustus Saint-Gaudens.

Career

Fraser's End of the Trail, depicting a weary Native American on a drooping horse, was modeled in 1894, won the Wanamaker Prize in Paris in 1898, and became one of the most widely reproduced American sculptures after a monumental version was shown at the Panama–Pacific International Exposition in San Francisco in 1915.

In 1913 Fraser designed the Buffalo nickel (Indian Head nickel), featuring a Native American portrait on the obverse and an American bison on the reverse; it is regarded as one of the finest American coin designs. His other major works include the statues of Alexander Hamilton and Albert Gallatin at the U.S. Treasury Building, the Benjamin Franklin statue at the Franklin Institute, the Second Division Memorial in Washington, D.C., and elements of the National Archives Building pediment.

Personal life and legacy

Fraser married fellow sculptor Laura Gardin in 1913. He was a leading figure in the American sculptural establishment, serving as president of the National Sculpture Society. He died in Westport, Connecticut, in 1953.
