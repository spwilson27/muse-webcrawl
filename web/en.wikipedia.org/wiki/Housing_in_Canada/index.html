Housing in Canada

Housing in Canada is delivered mainly through private markets, with federal, provincial, territorial and municipal governments sharing responsibility for housing policy, mortgage insurance, social housing and building standards. Roughly two-thirds of Canadian households own their homes. Since the early 2000s, and especially after 2015, house prices in metropolitan areas such as Toronto and Vancouver rose to among the highest relative to incomes in the developed world, making affordability a dominant political issue.

History

Federal involvement began with the Dominion Housing Act of 1935 and expanded after the Second World War under the National Housing Act, with the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC, founded 1946) insuring mortgages and financing veterans' and social housing. Postwar suburbs of detached houses defined the Canadian urban form. From the 1990s the federal government largely withdrew from funding new social housing, downloading responsibility to provinces, before re-engaging with the National Housing Strategy of 2017.

Affordability crisis

The Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation attributes the affordability gap principally to a shortage of supply relative to population growth, estimating that millions of additional homes are needed by 2030 to restore affordability. Contributing factors include restrictive municipal zoning favouring detached houses, construction costs and labour shortages, immigration-driven demand, and investor purchases. Responses have included the Housing Accelerator Fund, restrictions on foreign buyers, taxes on vacant homes (such as Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax), and measures to encourage purpose-built rental construction.

Social and Indigenous housing

Social and community housing accounts for a small share of the stock (around 4–5%), provided by municipal and non-profit providers. On-reserve and northern housing, much of it overcrowded and in poor condition, is a longstanding concern for Indigenous communities and a subject of federal commitments.
