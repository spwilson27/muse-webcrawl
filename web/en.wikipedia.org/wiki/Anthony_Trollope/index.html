Anthony Trollope

Anthony Trollope (24 April 1815 – 6 December 1882) was an English novelist and civil servant of the Victorian era. Among his best-known works are two series set in the imaginary English county of Barsetshire — the Chronicles of Barsetshire — and the political Palliser novels.

Life and career

The son of the writer Frances Trollope, he endured an unhappy, impoverished childhood before joining the General Post Office in 1834. Posted to Ireland in 1841, he prospered, began writing, and later, back in England, introduced the pillar box to Britain. He wrote with factory-like discipline — 250 words every 15 minutes before his working day — producing 47 novels plus travel books and short stories.

Works

The Barsetshire sequence begins with The Warden (1855) and Barchester Towers (1857); the Palliser novels follow the politician Plantagenet Palliser. The Way We Live Now (1875), a satire of financial corruption centred on the swindler Melmotte, is now often considered his masterpiece. His posthumous Autobiography (1883), with its frank account of writing for money, damaged his Victorian reputation, which recovered strongly in the twentieth century.
