Central, Hong Kong

Central is Hong Kong's central business district, on the north shore of Hong Kong Island.

Central (Chinese: 中環) is the central business district of Hong Kong. It is located in the Central and Western District, on the north shore of Hong Kong Island, across Victoria Harbour from Tsim Sha Tsui in Kowloon. As the financial heart of the territory, it contains the headquarters of many banks and multinational financial corporations, as well as the Hong Kong Government's offices, the Court of Final Appeal, and the Legislative Council Complex.

History

After Hong Kong Island was ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Nanking in 1842, the colonial city of Victoria was laid out along the northern shore, and Central became its administrative and commercial core. Extensive land reclamation has repeatedly pushed the shoreline northward: landmarks such as the Hong Kong Club and City Hall that once stood on the waterfront are now well inland, and the district's modern skyline rises largely on reclaimed land.

Landmarks and institutions

Central's skyline is dominated by skyscrapers including Two International Finance Centre (the tallest building on Hong Kong Island), the Bank of China Tower designed by I. M. Pei, and the HSBC Main Building by Foster + Partners. Statue Square, the Cenotaph, the Court of Final Appeal Building (the former Legislative Council building) and St. John's Cathedral — among the oldest Western ecclesiastical buildings in the Far East — anchor its historic core. The Hong Kong Stock Exchange, Government Hill, and the official residences on The Peak above the district reinforce its role as the territory's political and financial centre.

Transport

The district is served by Central and Hong Kong stations of the MTR (metro), the Star Ferry and Hong Kong–Macau ferry piers, an extensive network of elevated pedestrian walkways, and the Central–Mid-Levels escalators, the longest outdoor covered escalator system in the world.
