Investiture of the Gods

Investiture of the Gods, also known by its Chinese names Fengshen Yanyi and Fengshen Bang, is a 16th-century Chinese novel and one of the major vernacular Chinese works in the gods-and-demons genre. Written during the Ming dynasty, it is traditionally attributed to Xu Zhonglin.

The novel weaves together history, folklore, mythology, legends, and Taoist and Buddhist elements into a grand narrative of the fall of the Shang dynasty and the rise of the Zhou dynasty.

Plot

The story is set in the twilight years of the Shang dynasty. King Zhou of Shang, once a capable ruler, falls under the spell of the fox spirit Daji, whom he takes as a consort after offending the goddess Nüwa. Under Daji's influence the king becomes a tyrant, and the realm descends into cruelty and misrule.

The sage Jiang Ziya, a disciple of the primordial deity Yuanshi Tianzun, is charged with aiding the virtuous Ji Fa (King Wu of Zhou) in overthrowing the Shang. Armies of mortals clash alongside immortals, spirits, and demons wielding magical weapons and treasures. At the story's end, the fallen heroes and villains alike are invested as gods — enrolled in the celestial bureaucracy from the Register of Gods (the Fengshen Bang) — giving the novel its title.

Principal characters

  • Jiang Ziya — the aged strategist and commander of the Zhou forces.
  • King Zhou of Shang — the last Shang king, corrupted by Daji.
  • Daji — a thousand-year-old fox spirit in human form.
  • Nezha — the child warrior deity armed with the Wind Fire Wheels and the Universe Ring.
  • Yang Jian (Erlang Shen) — the three-eyed god and master of transformations.
  • King Wen and King Wu of Zhou — founders of the succeeding dynasty.

Influence

The novel greatly shaped the Chinese popular pantheon, fixing the identities and stories of many deities worshipped in folk religion. Its characters and episodes have been adapted countless times in opera, television, film, comics, and video games across East Asia.
