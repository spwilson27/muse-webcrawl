Thal Ghat

Location: Maharashtra, India

Range: Western Ghats

Summit elevation: about 585 m

Thal Ghat (also written Thull Ghat or Thul Ghat) is a mountain pass in the Western Ghats in the Indian state of Maharashtra, carrying the Mumbai–Nashik road (National Highway 3) and the Central Railway line from Mumbai to northern and eastern India over the Sahyadri range near Kasara and Igatpuri.

Railway ghat section

The Thal Ghat railway section, between Kasara and Igatpuri, is one of the steepest on Indian Railways, with a ruling gradient of about 1 in 37. It was constructed by the Great Indian Peninsula Railway in the 1860s using reversing stations and later realigned with tunnels to ease the climb. Trains ascending the ghat require additional banker locomotives attached at Kasara to push them up to Igatpuri. The section is notable for its series of tunnels, viaducts and catch sidings built into the hillside to arrest runaway vehicles on the descent.

The ghat is one of three principal railway passes by which lines climb the Western Ghats from the Mumbai region, the others being the Bhor Ghat (towards Pune) and the routes further south. The Thal Ghat line carries heavy passenger and freight traffic between Mumbai and the Deccan plateau, and electrified operation replaced the original steam and later DC electric workings.
