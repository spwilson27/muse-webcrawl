E-boat

E-boat
German: Schnellboot (S-Boot)
Type: Torpedo boat
Used by: Kriegsmarine, World War II

E-boat was the Western Allies' designation for the fast attack craft of the German Kriegsmarine during the Second World War; the German name was Schnellboot ("fast boat"), abbreviated S-Boot.

Design

E-boats were larger and more seaworthy than contemporary American PT boats and British motor torpedo boats. Built mainly by Lürssen, they had round-bilge displacement hulls of wood over metal framing, were about 35 metres long, and could exceed 40 knots powered by three Daimler-Benz diesel engines. Diesel power gave them greater range and reduced fire risk compared with petrol-engined rivals.

Service

Operating mainly in the English Channel, North Sea and Baltic, E-boats attacked coastal convoys, laid mines and rescued downed airmen. A notorious success came in April 1944, when E-boats attacked a convoy rehearsing for D-Day at Lyme Bay (Exercise Tiger), killing 749 American servicemen. Allied countermeasures included radar-equipped escorts and motor gun boats.

Survivors

Only one wartime S-Boot, S130, is known to survive; it has been the subject of a long restoration project in Britain.
