Pertussis vaccine

The pertussis vaccine is a vaccine that protects against whooping cough (pertussis), a highly contagious respiratory disease caused by the bacterium Bordetella pertussis. It is almost always administered in combination with diphtheria and tetanus vaccines, as the DTaP vaccine in young children and Tdap or Td formulations in older children and adults.

Types and effectiveness

Two types exist: whole-cell vaccines, made from inactivated whole bacteria and first developed in the 1940s (notably through the work of Pearl Kendrick and Grace Eldering), and acellular vaccines, introduced in the 1980s–1990s, which contain only purified components of the bacterium and cause fewer side effects. The World Health Organization estimates that pertussis vaccination prevents millions of cases and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually, although the protection from acellular vaccines wanes faster than was initially appreciated, contributing to periodic resurgences of the disease.

Recommendations

Most national schedules recommend a primary series in infancy with boosters in childhood; Tdap is also recommended during each pregnancy to protect newborn infants, who face the highest risk of severe disease, through transplacental antibody transfer.
