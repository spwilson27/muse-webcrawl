Texas A&M University (Texas A&M, A&M, or TAMU) is a public, land-grant, research university in College Station, Texas, United States. Founded in 1876 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas under the Morrill Land-Grant Acts, it was the first public institution of higher education in Texas.

History

The university opened on 4 October 1876 with six students and six faculty members. It was established as a military college, and membership in the Corps of Cadets was compulsory for most of its early history; the school provided more officers to the United States military in the two World Wars than any other non-service-academy institution. Under president James Earl Rudder, the university was desegregated, admitted women on a full basis, and made Corps membership voluntary in the 1960s.

Academics and research

Texas A&M is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is a member of the Association of American Universities. It is one of the largest universities in the United States by enrollment, with more than 70,000 students at the College Station campus. The university is a land-grant, sea-grant, and space-grant institution, and it operates the George Bush Presidential Library and Museum on its West Campus. Its engineering, agriculture, veterinary medicine, and business programs are consistently ranked among the best in the nation.

Campus and student life

The main campus covers more than 5,500 acres. Texas A&M is known for its strong school spirit and traditions, including the Aggie Ring, Midnight Yell Practice, the 12th Man, and the Bonfire memorial. Students and alumni are called Aggies, and the school colors are maroon and white.

Athletics

The Texas A&M Aggies compete in the Southeastern Conference (SEC), which the university joined in 2012. Kyle Field, the football stadium, is among the largest stadiums in the world, with a capacity exceeding 100,000.
