Cléo from 5 to 7 (French: Cléo de 5 à 7) is a 1962 French New Wave drama film written and directed by Agnès Varda. Shot in near real time and divided into chapters marked by the clock, it follows the pop singer Cléo Victoire across Paris between 5 and 7 p.m. as she awaits the results of a biopsy that may confirm cancer.

Plot

The film opens with a tarot reading that foretells death. Cléo visits a hatter, rehearses with her songwriters (one played by composer Michel Legrand), meets a friend, wanders through the Parc Montsouris with a friendly soldier on leave from the Algerian War, and finally receives her results at the hospital: two months of treatment, but she will recover. Structured around Cléo's transformation from a superficial object of others' gazes into a seeing subject in her own right, the film is a landmark of feminist cinema — "Cléo opens her eyes" was Varda's own summary.

Reception

Premiered in competition at Cannes in 1962, Cléo is now regarded as one of the essential French New Wave films and one of the great films about Paris, time, and mortality. Its real-time structure and subjective camerawork — mirrors, shop windows, and the city observed through a woman's anxiety — anticipated later experiments in duration, including the strict real-time rules echoed by Dogme 95 decades later.
