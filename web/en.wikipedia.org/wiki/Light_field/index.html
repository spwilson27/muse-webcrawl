Light field

Light field
Concept introducedMichael Faraday (1846); term by A. Gershun (1936)
Formalised asPlenoptic function (Adelson & Bergen, 1991)
ApplicationsPlenoptic cameras, VR, rendering

A light field is a vector function that describes the amount of light flowing in every direction through every point in space — the radiance along all rays in a scene. The concept was proposed by Michael Faraday and named by Andrey Gershun in 1936; it was formalised in computer vision as the plenoptic function by Adelson and Bergen in 1991.

The plenoptic function

In full generality, radiance is a function of position (three coordinates), direction (two angles), wavelength and time — a seven-dimensional function. In free space, where radiance is constant along a ray, the light field reduces to four dimensions, allowing it to be parameterised by the intersection of rays with two parallel planes (the "light slab" representation of Levoy and Hanrahan, 1996).

Applications

Capturing a light field — with a camera array or a microlens-based plenoptic camera such as the Lytro — permits post-capture refocusing, synthetic aperture effects and novel-view synthesis. Light-field rendering underlies some virtual- and augmented-reality displays and microscopy techniques that record volumetric data in a single exposure.
