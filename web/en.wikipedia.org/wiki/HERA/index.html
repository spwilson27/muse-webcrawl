HERA

HERA (Hadron-Electron Ring Accelerator, German: Hadronen-Elektronen-Ring-Anlage) was a particle accelerator at the DESY laboratory in Hamburg, Germany. In operation from 1992 to 2007, it was the first — and remains the only — collider of electrons or positrons with protons, and Germany's largest research instrument of its era.

Design and experiments

HERA consisted of two storage rings in an underground tunnel 6.3 kilometres in circumference: one accelerated protons to energies up to 920 GeV, the other electrons or positrons to 27.5 GeV, and the beams were brought into collision at interaction points. Two large collider experiments, H1 and ZEUS, studied deep inelastic electron–proton scattering, measuring the proton's internal structure functions, the strong coupling and aspects of the Standard Model, while HERMES used the electron beam on a polarised gas target and HERA-B operated at a fixed target in the proton ring.

HERA data reduced uncertainties on parton distribution functions that are essential to interpreting results at the Large Hadron Collider. The machine was shut down on 30 June 2007, and parts of the tunnel were later reused for other accelerator projects at DESY.
