Municipal governance in India

Municipal governance in India refers to the system of urban local bodies that administer cities and towns. Modern municipal institutions date to the Madras Municipal Corporation, founded in 1688, and the system took its present constitutional form with the 74th Amendment Act of 1992, which gave urban local bodies a defined role in the Constitution of India.

Types of urban local bodies

  • Municipal corporations for large metropolitan cities.
  • Municipal councils (municipalities) for smaller urban areas.
  • Nagar panchayats for areas in transition from rural to urban.
  • Cantonment boards for civilian populations in military cantonments, and notified area committees for special cases.

Structure and functions

Municipal bodies consist of elected councils headed by a mayor or chairperson, with executive functions typically vested in a municipal commissioner appointed by the state government. The Twelfth Schedule of the Constitution lists 18 functions that may be devolved to municipalities, including urban planning, water supply, public health and sanitation, solid waste management, roads, and slum improvement.

Finance

Municipalities raise revenue from property taxes, user charges, and fees, supplemented by grants and devolution recommended by State Finance Commissions and the central Finance Commission. Limited own revenue and dependence on higher levels of government remain persistent challenges, as do capacity constraints and overlapping authority with state-level development agencies.
