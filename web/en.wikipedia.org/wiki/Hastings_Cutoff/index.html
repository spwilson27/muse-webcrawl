The Hastings Cutoff was an alternative route for westward emigrants to California, proposed by Lansford Hastings in his 1845 Emigrants' Guide to Oregon and California. The cutoff branched from the California Trail at Fort Bridger, Wyoming, passed through the Wasatch Range and across the Great Salt Lake Desert in present-day Utah, and rejoined the main trail along the Humboldt River in Nevada.

History

Hastings promoted the route as a shortcut saving some 350 miles, but he had not travelled much of it with wagons. In 1846 several parties attempted the cutoff. Emigrants had to cut a new wagon road through Weber Canyon and the Wasatch, then endure an approximately 80-mile waterless crossing of the salt desert, far longer than Hastings had described. The Harlan–Young party and the Donner Party were among those who used it.

The Donner Party

The delays caused by the cutoff proved catastrophic for the Donner Party, which lost weeks and much livestock and arrived late at the Sierra Nevada, where the wagon train was snowbound for the winter of 1846–47 with terrible loss of life. The route was largely abandoned by later emigrants in favour of the established trail, though parts of it influenced later road and rail routes around the Great Salt Lake.
