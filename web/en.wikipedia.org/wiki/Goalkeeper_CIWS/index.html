Goalkeeper CIWS

The Goalkeeper is a Dutch close-in weapon system (CIWS) developed by Hollandse Signaalapparaten (now Thales Nederland) to defend warships against incoming anti-ship missiles and aircraft at short range. Introduced into service with the Royal Netherlands Navy in 1980, it is built around the seven-barrel 30 mm GAU-8 Avenger rotary cannon — the same gun fitted to the A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft — and is widely regarded as one of the most powerful gun-based CIWS in service.

Design

Unlike the American Phalanx, whose radar and gun share one mount, Goalkeeper uses separate search and tracking radars, both housed in its characteristic radome, which lets it hand targets smoothly from search to tracking and engage a second target quickly. The system operates autonomously: its I-band search radar detects and prioritises threats, a dual-frequency tracking radar follows the target, and the gun fires bursts of armour-piercing or high-explosive 30 mm ammunition at up to 4,200 rounds per minute. A magazine below or within the mount holds 1,190 rounds. The heavy subcalibre APDS and later FMPDS rounds were designed so that a missile struck head-on would be destroyed by the sheer kinetic energy and density of the stream of fire.

Service

Goalkeeper equips most major Royal Netherlands Navy surface combatants and has been exported to, among others, the British Royal Navy (fitted to ships including the Type 22 frigates and HMS Illustrious), Belgium, Chile, South Korea, Portugal and Qatar. In British service it was sometimes nicknamed "the flying dustbin" after the shape of its mount. The system has been progressively upgraded with improved radars, electro-optical tracking and new ammunition to counter faster, manoeuvring and low-observable missiles.
