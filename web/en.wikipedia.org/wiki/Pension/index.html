Pension

TypesDefined benefit, defined contribution, state pension
PurposeRetirement income

A pension is a fund into which amounts are paid during an individual's working life and from which periodic payments are made in retirement, intended to provide income when earnings from employment cease. The term is also used for the payments themselves and, historically, for regular payments made by states to individuals for past service or as stipends.

Types

Defined benefit (DB) plans promise a specified retirement income, typically calculated from salary and years of service (for example, final-salary or career-average schemes); the employer or plan sponsor bears the investment and longevity risk. Defined contribution (DC) plans, such as the American 401(k) or individual retirement accounts, accumulate contributions and investment returns in an individual's account, so the retirement income depends on market performance and the member bears the risk.

Most developed countries combine state pensions (public, often pay-as-you-go systems such as Social Security in the United States or the State Pension in the United Kingdom), occupational schemes, and personal saving — a framework the World Bank describes as a multi-pillar model.

History

Early examples include military pensions in ancient Rome (the aerarium militare funded veterans' discharge payments), civil list pensions in early modern Europe, and the first modern public-employee schemes of the 19th century. Bismarck's Germany introduced old-age social insurance in 1889, a model widely copied. Private employer pension plans expanded rapidly in the United States after the Second World War, then shifted decisively from defined benefit to defined contribution from the 1980s onward.

Funding and challenges

Population ageing, low interest rates, and rising life expectancy have placed funding pressure on pension systems worldwide, prompting reforms such as higher statutory retirement ages, automatic enrolment into workplace pensions (introduced in the UK in 2012), and the use of collective defined contribution designs. Pension funds are among the world's largest institutional investors, and their regulation — covering solvency, member protection schemes, and disclosure — is a major field of financial law.
