High Sheriff of Nottinghamshire


Nottingham Castle

The High Sheriff of Nottinghamshire is an ancient office in the English county of Nottinghamshire. High Sheriffs are the monarch's judicial representative in the county; the office is appointed annually and is now largely ceremonial, involving support for the judiciary, law enforcement, and voluntary and charitable organizations.

History

The shrievalty of Nottinghamshire dates to the Anglo-Saxon period. For centuries the counties of Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire shared a single sheriff — the office famously associated, in legend, with the Sheriff of Nottingham, the antagonist of Robin Hood. From the Norman period the Sheriff of Nottingham was based at Nottingham Castle and was a powerful royal officer responsible for law and order, tax collection, and military levies. The joint sheriffdom of Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire was not separated until 1568.

Famous medieval sheriffs included William Peverel the Younger and Philip Marc, sheriff from 1208 to 1222 and a possible model for the legendary Sheriff. Under the Sheriffs Act 1887 and later reforms, the office lost most of its executive powers; today the High Sheriff is nominated at a ceremony in the county and formally appointed by the sovereign "pricking" a name on the sheriff roll.
