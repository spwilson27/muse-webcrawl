David Koresh (born Vernon Wayne Howell; 17 August 1959 – 19 April 1993) was an American cult leader who played a central role in the Waco siege of 1993. As self-proclaimed final prophet and leader of the Branch Davidians, a sect that had split from the Seventh-day Adventist tradition, he gathered followers at the Mount Carmel Center near Waco, Texas, taught an apocalyptic interpretation of the Book of Revelation, and claimed the right to take multiple "spiritual wives", including underage girls. He died, along with 75 followers, in the fire that ended the federal siege of the compound.

Early life and rise

Howell had a troubled childhood marked by dyslexia and academic failure, but an extraordinary memory for scripture. Expelled from the Seventh-day Adventist Church, he joined the Branch Davidians at Mount Carmel in 1981 and, after a power struggle with the previous leader's son George Roden — which included an armed confrontation in 1987 — gained control of the community by 1990, legally changing his name to David Koresh (for King David and Cyrus, Koresh in Hebrew).

The Waco siege

Suspecting the group of stockpiling illegal weapons, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms raided Mount Carmel on 28 February 1993; the gun battle killed four agents and six Davidians. The FBI then besieged the compound for 51 days while Koresh preached to negotiators and released some children. On 19 April 1993 the FBI launched a tear-gas assault; a fire engulfed the building, killing Koresh and 75 others, including many children. The disaster, with the earlier Ruby Ridge incident, became a lasting source of controversy over federal law-enforcement tactics and a rallying grievance for the American militia movement.
