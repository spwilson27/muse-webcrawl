The Maxwell Award has honoured college football's best player since 1937.

The Maxwell Award is presented annually to the best all-around player in college football in the United States, as judged by a panel of sportscasters, sportswriters and NCAA head coaches, and by the membership of the Maxwell Football Club. It was first awarded in 1937.

History

The award is named in honour of Robert "Tiny" Maxwell, a Swarthmore College football player, coach and sportswriter who died in 1922 from injuries sustained in a car accident after officiating a game. The first recipient was Clint Frank of Yale in 1937. The Maxwell Award is often compared with the Heisman Trophy; on many occasions the two awards have gone to the same player, though the Maxwell is voted on by coaches and club members as well as media. Winners are announced at the Home Depot College Football Awards show each December, and the trophy is formally presented at the Maxwell Football Club's annual awards gala in the spring.

Notable winners

Two-time winners include Johnny Lattner (1952, 1953) and Tim Tebow (2007, 2008). Other celebrated recipients include Doak Walker, Roger Staubach, Tony Dorsett, Barry Sanders, Peyton Manning, Drew Brees, Cam Newton and Lamar Jackson.
