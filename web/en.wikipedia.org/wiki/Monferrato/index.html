Monferrato

Monferrato (Italian: Monferrato, Piedmontese: Monfrà) is a historical and geographical region of Piedmont in north-western Italy, extending roughly between the Po river to the north and the Ligurian Apennines to the south, across the modern provinces of Alessandria and Asti. Its main centres are Casale Monferrato and Acqui Terme; the region is famous for its rolling vine-covered hills, castles and wine production.

The hills of Monferrato, part of the UNESCO-listed Vineyard Landscape of Piedmont.

History

In the Middle Ages Monferrato was a march (border province) of the Kingdom of Italy within the Holy Roman Empire, granted in the 10th century to Aleramo, legendary founder of the Aleramici dynasty. His descendants, the Marquises of Montferrat, became prominent in the Crusades: William V, Conrad of Montferrat (a leader of the Third Crusade and king-elect of Jerusalem) and Boniface I, leader of the Fourth Crusade and founder of the Kingdom of Thessalonica, were all of the house. In 1305 the marquisate passed to the Byzantine imperial family of the Palaiologoi, and in 1536 to the Gonzaga dukes of Mantua. After the War of the Mantuan Succession, Monferrato was partitioned between Savoy and Mantua; the Savoy share grew by the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), and the remaining Gonzaga portion passed to Savoy in 1708 and was confirmed in the 18th century, making the whole region part of the future Italian state of Piedmont.

Wine and culture

Monferrato is one of Italy's great wine regions, producing Barbera d'Asti and Barbera del Monferrato, Grignolino, Dolcetto, the sparkling Asti Spumante and Moscato d'Asti, and the aromatic white wines around Gavi. The region is famous for its infernot — hand-carved underground cellars dug into the sandstone — and for white truffles, hazelnuts and the bagna càuda tradition.

In 2014 the "Vineyard Landscape of Piedmont: Langhe-Roero and Monferrato" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognising the cultural landscape shaped by centuries of viticulture. The region is divided into the Alto Monferrato (upper, towards the Apennines) and Basso Monferrato (lower, towards the Po), with Casale Monferrato, once a formidable fortress city, as its historic capital.
