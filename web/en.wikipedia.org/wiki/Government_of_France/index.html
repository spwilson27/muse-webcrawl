Government of France

The Government of France, officially the Government of the French Republic (Gouvernement de la République française), exercises executive power in France together with the President of the Republic under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic (1958).

Government of France
Formation4 October 1958 (Fifth Republic)
Head of statePresident of the Republic
Head of governmentPrime Minister
SeatHôtel Matignon, Paris
LegislatureParliament (National Assembly & Senate)

Structure

France is a semi-presidential system. The President, elected directly for five years, appoints the Prime Minister, who directs the government, controls the civil service and armed forces administration, and is responsible to Parliament. Ministers are appointed on the Prime Minister's recommendation.

Powers

The government determines and conducts national policy, may issue decrees and ordinances, introduces bills, and can be overturned by a motion of censure in the National Assembly. Periods when President and parliamentary majority differ are called cohabitation.

Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers meets weekly, usually at the Élysée Palace, chaired by the President. See also France, Prime Minister of France and Constitution of France.
