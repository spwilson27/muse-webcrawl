Bowhill House

Bowhill House is a country house in the Scottish Borders, near Selkirk, and the principal seat of the Duke of Buccleuch. Set in extensive parkland at the confluence of the Ettrick Water and the Yarrow Water, the house is home to part of the Buccleuch art collection, one of the finest private collections in Britain, and is open to the public during the summer season.

History

The Bowhill estate was acquired by the Scott family in the sixteenth century. The present house began in 1812 as a modest villa designed for the 4th Duke of Buccleuch, and was greatly enlarged in the nineteenth century to designs by William Burn and David Bryce for the 5th Duke, producing the present large classical mansion with its long south front. Bowhill remains the home of the 10th Duke of Buccleuch and his family, alongside the family's other seats at Drumlanrig Castle and Boughton House.

Collection

The house contains notable paintings, including works by Canaletto, Gainsborough, Reynolds and Raeburn, an important collection of French furniture, porcelain and silver, and relics of the 1st Duke of Monmouth, the family's ancestor, as well as items associated with Sir Walter Scott, a kinsman and frequent visitor to the area. The grounds include gardens, a country park, an adventure playground and a restored Victorian kitchen garden, together with the Bowhill theatre.
