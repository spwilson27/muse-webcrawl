Kutaisi (Georgian: ქუთაისი) is a city in western Georgia, the capital of the Imereti region and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. Situated on both banks of the Rioni River, it is Georgia's third most populous city after Tbilisi and Batumi. It served as the capital of the ancient kingdom of Colchis and, later, of the united Kingdom of Georgia.

History

Archaeological evidence points to settlement from the 2nd millennium BC; in Greek tradition, Kutaisi (Aea) was the destination of Jason and the Argonauts in their quest for the Golden Fleece in Colchis. It was the capital of Colchis and then of the Kingdom of Egrisi (Lazica). In 975 Kutaisi became the capital of the newly united Kingdom of Georgia; King David IV "the Builder" was crowned there, and Gelati Monastery, founded by him in 1106, became a great centre of learning.

After the Mongol invasions and the fragmentation of the kingdom, Kutaisi was capital of the Kingdom of Imereti. It was captured by the Ottoman Empire and later, in 1810, annexed by the Russian Empire. During the Soviet period it grew into a major industrial centre. From 2012 to 2018 the Parliament of Georgia sat in a purpose-built glass-domed building in Kutaisi.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the ruined Bagrati Cathedral (built 1003), a masterpiece of medieval Georgian architecture and a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with Gelati Monastery, the hilltop Ukimerioni fortress, the White Bridge over the Rioni, the Colchis Fountain in the central square, and the Sataplia and Prometheus caves nearby. Kutaisi International Airport (David the Builder) serves low-cost flights to Europe.
