Easter Bilby

CountryAustralia
OriginConcepts from 1968; popularised from 1991
PurposeNative alternative to the Easter Bunny; conservation fundraising

The Easter Bilby is an Australian alternative to the Easter Bunny, in which the native greater bilby — a long-eared desert marsupial — replaces the rabbit as the bringer of Easter eggs. Chocolate bilbies are sold across Australia at Easter, with a portion of proceeds from many brands funding bilby conservation.

Background

Rabbits are a devastating introduced pest in Australia, damaging agriculture and native ecosystems, which has made the Easter Bunny an uneasy symbol for some Australians. The bilby, by contrast, is a threatened native species whose numbers have collapsed under pressure from introduced predators and competition with rabbits. Promoting the bilby at Easter was intended both to celebrate a native animal and to highlight the harm caused by rabbits.

History

The idea can be traced to 1968, when Rose-Marie Dusting wrote a story about "Billy the Aussie Easter Bilby" under the name R. M. Dusting. In 1991 the Foundation for Rabbit-Free Australia began promoting the Easter Bilby more widely and licensed the concept to chocolate makers; Darrell Lea was a long-standing producer of chocolate bilbies, and other manufacturers such as Haigh's Chocolates (which stopped making chocolate rabbits in favour of bilbies) have supported the campaign. Conservation groups including the Save the Bilby Fund receive royalties or donations from bilby chocolate sales.

Reception

The Easter Bilby has become a familiar part of Australian Easter retail, though the Easter Bunny remains dominant. Bilby adoption programmes, school activities and Easter bilby appearances at wildlife parks accompany the chocolate campaign each year.
