Urinary retention

Urinary retention is an inability to completely empty the bladder. Onset may be sudden (acute retention) or gradual (chronic retention). Acute urinary retention is a medical emergency typically accompanied by suprapubic pain and an urgent inability to pass urine, while chronic retention may develop insidiously with a weak stream, hesitancy, and incomplete emptying.

Causes

Causes are commonly grouped into obstructive, infectious and inflammatory, pharmacologic, and neurologic categories. In older men the most common cause is benign prostatic hyperplasia. Other causes include urethral stricture, prostate cancer, severe constipation, urinary tract infection, neurological conditions such as multiple sclerosis or spinal cord injury, and medications with anticholinergic or sympathomimetic effects.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves physical examination, which may reveal a palpably distended bladder, measurement of post-void residual urine by bladder scan or catheterization, urinalysis, and blood tests including renal function. Imaging or urodynamic studies may be used to identify the underlying cause in chronic cases.

Treatment

Acute retention is relieved by catheterization, either urethral or suprapubic. Subsequent treatment targets the cause: alpha blockers or surgery for prostatic obstruction, treatment of infection, review of causative medications, and intermittent self-catheterization for chronic cases. Untreated retention can lead to bladder damage, urinary tract infection, and kidney failure.
