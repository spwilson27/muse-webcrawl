Vacuum flask

InventorJames Dewar
Year1892
UsesKeeping liquids hot or cold; cryogenic storage

A vacuum flask, also known as a Dewar flask or by the brand name Thermos, is an insulating storage vessel that keeps its contents at a stable temperature far longer than an ordinary container. It consists of two flasks, one inside the other, joined at the neck, with the gap between them partially evacuated of air.

Principle

Heat moves by conduction, convection, and radiation. The vacuum between the walls nearly eliminates conduction and convection, while silvered (mirror-coated) inner surfaces reflect thermal radiation. A stopper of low-conductivity material further reduces losses, so hot drinks stay hot and cold drinks stay cold for many hours.

History

The Scottish physicist and chemist Sir James Dewar invented the flask in 1892 during his work on cryogenics, where he needed to store liquefied gases such as liquid oxygen. Dewar never patented the design; German glassblowers Reinhold Burger and Albert Aschenbrenner recognised its household potential and founded the Thermos company in 1904 to market it commercially.

Uses

Beyond food and drink containers, large Dewar vessels store liquid nitrogen, oxygen, and helium in laboratories, hospitals, and industry, and vacuum insulation panels based on the same principle are used in refrigeration and construction.
