Midlothian
Country: Scotland
Admin HQ: Dalkeith
Area: 354 km²
Population: approx. 96,000

Midlothian (Scottish Gaelic: Meadhan Lodainn) is a historic county, registration county, lieutenancy area and one of 32 council areas of Scotland, used for local government. It borders Edinburgh to the north, East Lothian to the east, and the Scottish Borders to the south. The modern council area is much smaller than the historic county of Midlothian, which included Edinburgh itself and was formerly known as Edinburghshire.

History

The historic county of Midlothian took its name from its position in the middle of the Lothians. The county town was Edinburgh, although after the city became a county of itself the county administration moved to Dalkeith. In 1975 Midlothian became a district of the Lothian region, and in 1996 it was reconstituted as a unitary council area, excluding the city of Edinburgh and some border settlements.

Geography and towns

Midlothian is largely rural in its southern parts, rising to the Pentland Hills in the west and the Moorfoot Hills in the south, while the north is suburban in character, forming part of the Edinburgh commuter belt. Principal towns include Dalkeith, Penicuik, Bonnyrigg, Loanhead, Gorebridge and Newtongrange. The area has a strong coal-mining heritage, commemorated at the National Mining Museum Scotland in Newtongrange.

Landmarks

Notable sites include Rosslyn Chapel, famed for its ornate 15th-century stone carvings; Dalkeith Palace; Roslin Glen; and the castles of Crichton and Newbattle Abbey. Walter Scott's novel The Heart of Mid-Lothian (1818) took its name from the old Tolbooth prison of Edinburgh, nicknamed the "Heart of Midlothian".
