Hexaplex trunculus (previously known as Murex trunculus), commonly called the banded dye-murex, is a medium-sized predatory sea snail, a marine gastropod mollusk in the family Muricidae, the murex shells or rock snails. It is native to the Mediterranean Sea and the adjacent Atlantic coast of Europe and Africa.

Description and habitat

The shell reaches about 4–10 cm in length, with a raised spire, a large body whorl bearing varices with short spines, and variable bands of brown, grey or yellowish colour. The snail lives on rocky and muddy bottoms in shallow water, where it preys on bivalves and other mollusks by boring through their shells.

Tyrian purple

Hexaplex trunculus is famous as one of the principal sources of Tyrian purple, the enormously costly dye of Phoenician and Roman antiquity. The hypobranchial gland of the snail secretes a mucus containing precursors that, on exposure to sunlight and air, develop into the pigment 6,6′-dibromoindigo, yielding purple to blue-purple shades. Thousands of snails were needed to dye a single garment, and huge shell middens survive at ancient dye-works such as Tyre and Sidon. Many scholars identify the dye from this species with the biblical tekhelet, the blue dye used for ritual fringes, though this identification is debated.

Imposex

Females of the species develop imposex — male sexual characteristics — in response to tributyltin (TBT) antifouling paint pollution, making the snail a widely used bioindicator of TBT contamination in the Mediterranean.
