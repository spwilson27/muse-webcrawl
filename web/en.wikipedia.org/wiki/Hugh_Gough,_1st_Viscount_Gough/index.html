Hugh Gough, 1st Viscount Gough


Field Marshal Hugh Gough, 1st Viscount Gough.

Field Marshal Hugh Gough, 1st Viscount Gough, KP, GCB, GCSI, PC, FRS (3 November 1779 – 2 March 1869) was a senior British Army officer. After serving as a junior officer at the seizure of the Cape of Good Hope during the French Revolutionary Wars, Gough commanded the 2nd Battalion of the 87th (Royal Irish Fusiliers) Regiment of Foot during the Peninsular War.

Military career

Gough served as Commander-in-Chief in China, commanding the British forces which secured victory over the Qing dynasty at the conclusion of the First Opium War in 1842. He then became Commander-in-Chief in India and led the British forces in action against the Marathas at the Battle of Maharajpur in the Gwalior campaign of 1843, against the Sikhs at the Battle of Mudki, the Battle of Ferozeshah, and the Battle of Sobraon during the First Anglo-Sikh War, and at the Battle of Chillianwala and the Battle of Gujrat during the Second Anglo-Sikh War.

Later life

Gough was promoted to the local rank of general in India in 1854, recalled to England to sit on courts of inquiry, and promoted to field marshal in 1862. He died at his home, St Helen's, Booterstown, in 1869.
