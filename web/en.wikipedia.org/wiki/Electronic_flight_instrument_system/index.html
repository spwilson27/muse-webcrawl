Electronic flight instrument system

[Hero image placeholder: airliner glass cockpit EFIS displays]

An electronic flight instrument system (EFIS) is a flight deck instrument display system in an aircraft that displays flight data electronically rather than electromechanically. An EFIS normally consists of a primary flight display (PFD), a multi-function display (MFD), and an engine-indicating and crew-alerting system (EICAS) display. Early EFIS models used cathode-ray tube (CRT) displays; liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) are now universal. EFIS is the technological core of the so-called "glass cockpit".

Components

The primary flight display consolidates the attitude indicator, airspeed, altitude, vertical speed, and heading into a single integrated display with flight-mode annunciations. The navigation display (ND) presents a moving-map view of the route, weather radar, traffic (TCAS), and terrain data. Engine and systems information, checklists, and alerts are shown on EICAS or ECAM (Airbus) displays. Symbol generators (display processors) receive data from air data computers, inertial reference systems, GPS, and flight management systems over avionics data buses such as ARINC 429 and ARINC 664.

History

EFIS entered airline service in the early 1980s on the Boeing 757 and 767 and the Airbus A310, replacing dozens of individual electromechanical gauges. The technology spread to business jets and, by the 2000s, to general aviation aircraft through integrated systems such as the Garmin G1000, which brought glass-cockpit displays to light aircraft and training fleets.

Advantages and considerations

Advantages include reduced panel clutter, greater flexibility in presenting information, lower weight and maintenance burden, built-in monitoring and self-testing, and the ability to show synthetic vision and integrated warnings. Human-factors research emphasizes mode awareness and consistent design, since poorly understood automation and display modes have contributed to accidents. Standby instruments are retained to guard against total display failure.
