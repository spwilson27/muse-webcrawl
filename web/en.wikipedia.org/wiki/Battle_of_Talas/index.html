Battle of Talas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Talas valley near Taraz with the Karatau mountains beyond]

The Battle of Talas (751 AD) was fought between the Abbasid Caliphate (with Tibetan and Karluk allies) and Tang China near the Talas River in present-day Kazakhstan/Kyrgyzstan, the only recorded battle between Arab and Chinese imperial armies. The Tang expedition under Gao Xianzhi — a general of Goguryeo descent who had conquered the Pamir kingdoms — was defeated when its Karluk Turkic allies defected mid-battle. The battle is famous in popular history as the channel by which Chinese papermaking reached the Islamic world (through captured artisans, by the traditional account), and it marks the high-water line of Tang power in Central Asia — though the Tang withdrawal from the region owed far more to the catastrophic An Lushan Rebellion of 755 than to Talas itself.

The campaign

Gao Xianzhi, governor of Anxi, had stormed 'Little Bolü' (Gilgit) in 747 and in 750–751 executed the king of Tashkent after his surrender — treachery that drove the western Turks and the Sogdian cities to call in the Abbasids, newly victorious in their revolution. At the Talas in July 751 the armies faced off for days (the sources — Arabic and Chinese, both partisan and late in their surviving forms — differ on numbers and sequence) before the Karluks attacked the Tang rear. Gao escaped with a remnant; the Tang held the Tarim for another generation, but the Pamir door had closed.

Paper and consequences

The story that Samarkand's paper industry began with Chinese prisoners taken at Talas comes from later Arabic authors (al-Tha'alibi); papermaking was indeed established in Samarkand by the later 8th century and spread through the Islamic world to Europe. The geopolitics were subtler: the Abbasids and Tang continued embassies, Arab troops even aided the Tang against An Lushan's rebels, and Tibet — not the caliphate — was the power that actually absorbed Central Asia in the war's aftermath. Talas is a battle whose legend (clash of civilisations) inverts its record (one episode in a multipolar steppe struggle).
