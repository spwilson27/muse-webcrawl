Arecoline is a nicotinic acid-based alkaloid found in the areca nut, the fruit of the areca palm (Areca catechu). It is the principal psychoactive compound responsible for the stimulant and parasympathomimetic effects of betel quid chewing.

Pharmacology

Arecoline is a partial agonist of muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (M1, M3, M4), producing increased salivation, alertness, and mild euphoria, along with sweating and gastrointestinal stimulation. It can cross the blood–brain barrier.

Health effects and research

Long-term exposure through betel quid use contributes to oral submucous fibrosis and oral cancer; arecoline promotes fibrogenesis in buccal tissues. It has been investigated experimentally in cognitive disorders, but its toxicity limits therapeutic use.
