Benjamin Apthorp Gould

Benjamin Apthorp Gould (September 27, 1824 – November 26, 1896) was a pioneering American astronomer known for his star catalogues of the southern sky, his founding of the Astronomical Journal, and his role in organizing early American scientific institutions.

Career

Gould graduated from Harvard College in 1844 and studied in Europe under Carl Friedrich Gauss. In 1849 he founded the Astronomical Journal, still a leading publication. He directed longitude determinations for the U.S. Coast Survey using the new telegraphic method, directed the Dudley Observatory in Albany, and helped found the National Academy of Sciences.

Work in Argentina

Invited by President Domingo Faustino Sarmiento, Gould moved to Córdoba, Argentina in 1870 to establish the Argentine National Observatory. There he produced the Uranometría Argentina (1879), cataloguing over 10,000 southern stars, and directed the massive Córdoba Durchmusterung. His meteorological work also led to the founding of Argentina's national weather service.

Legacy

Gould identified the Gould Belt, a ring of bright young stars tilted to the Milky Way's plane. The lunar crater Gould and the asteroid 2511 Patterson are among commemorations; he received the Royal Astronomical Society's Gold Medal in 1883 and the James Craig Watson Medal in 1887.
