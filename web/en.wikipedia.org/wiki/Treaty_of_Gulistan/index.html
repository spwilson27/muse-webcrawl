Treaty of Gulistan

The Treaty of Gulistan (Russian: Гюлистанский договор; Persian: عهدنامه گلستان) was a peace treaty concluded on 24 October 1813 between the Russian Empire and Qajar Iran (Persia), ending the Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813. It was signed at the village of Gulistan in the Karabakh region, with British envoy Sir Gore Ouseley mediating.

Terms

Iran ceded to Russia the khanates of Karabakh, Ganja, Shirvan, Baku, Derbent, Quba, and part of Talysh—broadly the territories of modern Dagestan, eastern Georgia, and most of the present-day Republic of Azerbaijan north of the Aras River. Russia also gained the exclusive right to maintain a naval fleet on the Caspian Sea. The treaty recognised Abbas Mirza as heir apparent to the Persian throne and promised Russian support for his succession.

Consequences

The imprecise boundary along the Aras and in Talysh left disputes that helped trigger the second Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828, settled by the still harsher Treaty of Turkmenchay. Gulistan marked the beginning of the end of Persian dominion in the Caucasus and remains a painful reference point in Iranian historical memory.
