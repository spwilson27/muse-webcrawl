Formwork

Formwork
PurposeMould for casting concrete structures
MaterialsTimber, plywood, steel, aluminium, plastic, fabric
TypesTraditional, engineered/modular, reusable, stay-in-place, insulated

Formwork is the term used for the temporary or permanent moulds into which concrete or similar materials are poured and allowed to harden in construction. Good formwork must be strong enough to withstand the pressure of wet concrete, rigid enough to hold the required shape, tight enough to prevent leakage, and economical to erect, strike (remove) and reuse.

Types

Traditional formwork is built on site from timber and plywood. Engineered formwork uses prefabricated modular panels of steel, aluminium or plastic that can be assembled rapidly and reused many times; specialist systems include climbing formwork for high-rise cores, slipforming for continuous pours, table forms for floor slabs, and tunnel forms. Stay-in-place formwork — including insulated concrete forms — remains part of the finished structure, while fabric formwork uses flexible membranes to create optimised structural shapes.

Practice

Formwork is a major cost component of reinforced-concrete construction, sometimes exceeding the cost of the concrete and reinforcement combined, and its failure during a pour can be catastrophic, so it is designed and checked carefully. Release agents are applied so panels strip cleanly, and striking times depend on the concrete reaching sufficient strength. Reusable systems and careful planning reduce waste and labour.
