Root-mean-square deviation of atomic positions

In bioinformatics, the root-mean-square deviation of atomic positions, or simply root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), is the measure of the average distance between the atoms (usually the backbone atoms) of superimposed proteins or other molecules. It is commonly used to quantify the structural similarity between two molecular conformations, for example a predicted structure and an experimentally determined reference structure.

Calculation

RMSD is calculated as the square root of the mean of the squared distances between corresponding atoms in two structures, after an optimal superposition (typically via the Kabsch algorithm) that minimises the RMSD itself. The result is usually expressed in ångströms (Å).

Applications

RMSD is widely used in molecular dynamics simulations to measure how much a structure drifts from a reference over time, in docking studies to assess the accuracy of predicted ligand poses, and in protein structure prediction benchmarks such as CASP. Values below about 2 Å are generally taken to indicate a close match for small molecules, while larger proteins may have higher acceptable thresholds. RMSD is sensitive to outliers and to the choice of atoms included in the calculation.
