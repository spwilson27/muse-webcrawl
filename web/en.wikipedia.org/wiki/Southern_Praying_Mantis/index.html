Southern Praying Mantis

Southern Praying Mantis (Chinese: 南派螳螂) is a Hakka Chinese martial art of the Pearl River Delta region. Despite its name, it is unrelated in technique to the Northern Praying Mantis style; the two share only the insect-inspired name and a legend of observing the mantis in combat.

Characteristics

The style is a close-range system built around short, explosive power generated from the waist and ribcage, delivered with the phoenix-eye fist (a single-knuckle punch), palm strikes, elbows and low kicks. Practitioners keep the chest hollowed and shoulders rounded ("floating ribs"), use the rolling bridge arms (gwat sau concepts of pressing and deflecting), and train sensitivity drills related to sticky hands. Footwork is compact, favouring the triangular and circular steps of Hakka kung fu.

Branches

Main branches include Chow Gar (associated with Chow Ah Naam and popularised internationally by grandmaster Ip Shui), Chu Gar, Jook Lum (Bamboo Forest Temple) and Kwong Sai Jook Lum, the latter two linked to the Jook Lum Gee temple tradition. All trace their ancestry to the monk Sam Dot and Hakka communities of Guangdong.
