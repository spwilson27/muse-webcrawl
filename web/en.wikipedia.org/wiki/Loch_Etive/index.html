Loch Etive (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Èite) is a 30 km long sea loch in Argyll and Bute, on the west coast of Scotland. It is reached from Connel, 5 km north of Oban. It stretches in a roughly north-easterly direction from its mouth to Glen Etive.

The name Etive is believed to mean "little fierce one" from the Gaelic Èite, a character in Irish mythology.

Geography

Loch Etive can be divided into two sections: the more southerly lower loch, from Connel to Bonawe, and the upper loch extending some 18 km to the head at Glen Etive. The loch is narrow — less than 2 km wide for most of its length — and reaches depths of up to 150 m. At Connel, the loch's entrance is narrowed by a submerged sill, over which the tidal streams produce the famous Falls of Lora, a tidal rapid that forms when the tide falls in the Firth of Lorn.

The loch is crossed at Connel by the Connel Bridge, a cantilever bridge carrying the A828 road. A railway bridge originally occupied the same site, built for the Callander and Oban Railway's Ballachulish branch.

History and culture

A number of traditional tales are associated with Loch Etive. In one, the princess Deirdre and her lover Naisi flee Ulster and live for a time on its shores before returning to Ireland. The hills around the head of the loch, in Glen Etive, have also become a popular filming location in modern times, most notably for the James Bond film Skyfall.
