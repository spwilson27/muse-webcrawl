Uninhabited island

An uninhabited island, desert island, or deserted island, is an island, islet or atoll that is not permanently populated by humans. Uninhabited islands are often depicted in films and stories about shipwrecked people, and are also used as stereotypes for the idea of "paradise". Some uninhabited islands are protected as nature reserves and some are owned by private individuals.

A small uninhabited tropical island.

Reasons for absence of population

Islands may be uninhabited because they lack fresh water, are too small or remote to support settlement, are too cold, hot, stormy or volcanic, are protected by law, or were depopulated by disease, war, disaster, or forced removal. The largest uninhabited island in the world is Devon Island in Nunavut, Canada, in the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, at about 55,000 square kilometres.

History

Many islands once inhabited are now deserted: populations vanished through introduced disease (as in parts of Polynesia), resource exhaustion (as argued for some Pacific islands), or relocation. Others, such as Henderson Island in the Pitcairn group, retain traces of earlier Polynesian settlement. On some islands, castaways survived for years before rescue — a reality that fed the literary genre of the "desert island" story, most famously Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe (1719), inspired in part by the real castaway Alexander Selkirk on the Juan Fernández Islands.

In popular culture

The desert island is a recurring setting in literature, film, and television, from The Swiss Family Robinson and Treasure Island to the film Cast Away and the long-running BBC radio programme Desert Island Discs, in which guests choose records they would take to a desert island.
