Slur (music)

A slur is a symbol in Western musical notation indicating that the notes it embraces are to be played without separation (that is, with legato articulation). This is signified by a curved line generally placed over the notes if the stems point downward, and under them if the stems point upwards.

"When a phrase ... is played ... the notes which are connected together by this curved line ... are ... not ... separated, in any way."

Performance

Slurs mean different things for different instruments:

  • For bowed string instruments, the notes should be played in one bow stroke.
  • For plucked string instruments such as guitar, the notes should be played without plucking the individual strings again (hammer-ons and pull-offs).
  • For wind instruments, the notes should be played without re-tonguing each note, except for special effects such as flutter-tonguing.
  • For vocal music, a slur over several notes indicates they are to be sung to a single syllable, a melisma.

Phrase marks

Slurs are closely related to phrase marks; the distinction in appearance is subtle and often a matter of convention and editorial judgement. Longer slurs spanning many bars generally indicate phrase structure, while shorter slurs denote legato articulation within a phrase.
