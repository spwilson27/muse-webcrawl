Yoni (Sanskrit: योनि, literally "womb," "source," or "abode") is the symbol of the Hindu goddess and of female creative energy, representing the vulva and the generative power of the divine feminine, Shakti. In temple iconography the yoni typically appears as the base — often a circular or square pedestal with a spout-like channel — from which the lingam, the symbol of the god Shiva, rises; the combined lingam-yoni is one of the most widely venerated emblems in Hindu worship.

Meaning and worship

The Sanskrit word has a wide semantic range: womb, origin, birthplace, and female genitalia. As a sacred symbol, the yoni stands for the goddess Devi in her many forms and for the creative matrix from which the universe issues. Water or other offerings poured over the lingam flow out through the yoni's channel (pranala), and worshippers may receive the sanctified liquid as a blessing.

History

Some scholars have linked yoni symbolism to Neolithic and Indus Valley imagery of fertility, though such identifications are debated. Carved yoni pedestals are found across South and Southeast Asia, from early historic Indian temples to the Hindu sites of Cambodia and Vietnam, and yoni worship also forms part of Tantric practice, in which the symbol is contemplated as the seat of cosmic energy.
