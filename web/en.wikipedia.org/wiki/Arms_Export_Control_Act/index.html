Arms Export Control Act

The State Department administers export controls under the Act

Enacted1976
Citation22 U.S.C. ch. 39
Administered byU.S. Department of State (DDTC)
Related rulesInternational Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR)

The Arms Export Control Act of 1976 (AECA), codified at 22 U.S.C. ch. 39, gives the President of the United States the authority to control the import and export of defense articles and defense services. It applies to the Foreign Military Sales (FMS) program, under which the U.S. government sells defense equipment to foreign governments, and to commercial sales licensed by the Department of State.

Provisions

The Act requires licenses for the export of items on the United States Munitions List, implemented through the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) administered by the Directorate of Defense Trade Controls (DDTC). It requires the executive branch to notify Congress of major arms sales above statutory thresholds, giving Congress an opportunity to block a sale by joint resolution. It also mandates reports on arms transfers, authorizes the President to designate countries as state sponsors of terrorism (restricting arms exports to them), and imposes penalties for unauthorized exports.

History

Congress enacted the AECA in the post-Vietnam and post-Watergate climate to increase legislative oversight of the executive's arms transfer policies, consolidating and amending authorities from the Foreign Military Sales Act of 1968 and the Mutual Security Act. High-profile controversies, including the Iran–Contra affair's diversion of arms sale proceeds, tested its framework.
