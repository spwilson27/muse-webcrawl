Solar Impulse

TypeExperimental solar-powered aircraft
DesignerSolar Impulse project (Bertrand Piccard, André Borschberg)
First flight3 December 2009 (Solar Impulse 1)
Known forFirst solar-powered circumnavigation of the Earth (2015–2016)

Solar Impulse was a Swiss long-range experimental solar-powered aircraft project, and also the name of the two aircraft built under it. Piloted alternately by Bertrand Piccard and André Borschberg, Solar Impulse 2 completed the first circumnavigation of the Earth by a solar-powered fixed-wing aircraft in July 2016, flying 42,438 km without using any fuel.

Solar Impulse 1

The first aircraft (HB-SIA) made its maiden flight in December 2009. With a wingspan of 63.4 m covered in about 11,600 solar cells and a weight of only 1,600 kg, it demonstrated 36-hour endurance in 2010 — the first solar aircraft to fly through a full day-night cycle — and made international and transcontinental flights, including a coast-to-coast crossing of the United States in 2013.

Solar Impulse 2 and the circumnavigation

Solar Impulse 2 (HB-SIB) was larger — 71.9 m wingspan (wider than a Boeing 747), 17,248 solar cells, 2,300 kg, four electric motors, and lithium batteries for night flying — and carried a single pilot in an unpressurised 3.8 m³ cockpit. The round-the-world flight began in Abu Dhabi on 9 March 2015. Borschberg's Nagoya-to-Hawaii leg of 117 hours 52 minutes set the world record for the longest solo flight by time. Battery damage forced a months-long stop in Hawaii; the flight resumed in April 2016 and Piccard landed back in Abu Dhabi on 26 July 2016 after 17 legs.

Legacy

The project was intended less as a practical transport prototype than as a demonstration of clean technologies and energy efficiency. Piccard went on to found the Solar Impulse Foundation and its World Alliance for Efficient Solutions, which catalogues profitable clean-technology solutions.
