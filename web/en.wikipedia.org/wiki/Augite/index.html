Augite

Augite
Category: Silicate mineral
Group: Pyroxene
Formula: (Ca,Na)(Mg,Fe,Al,Ti)(Si,Al)2O6
Crystal system: Monoclinic
Colour: Black, greenish black, brown
Mohs hardness: 5.5–6

Augite is a rock-forming single-chain inosilicate mineral of the pyroxene group. It is a common constituent of mafic and ultramafic igneous rocks such as basalt, gabbro and peridotite, and also occurs in high-temperature metamorphic rocks.

Properties

Augite crystallises in the monoclinic system and typically forms short, prismatic crystals of black to dark green colour with a vitreous lustre. It forms a solid solution series with diopside and hedenbergite, the calcium-rich pyroxenes, with substitution of magnesium, iron, aluminium and titanium in its structure. Translucent varieties are occasionally cut as collector's gemstones.

Occurrence

Augite is among the most common pyroxenes in terrestrial basalts and is also found in lunar basalts and in some meteorites, making it an important mineral in planetary geology.
