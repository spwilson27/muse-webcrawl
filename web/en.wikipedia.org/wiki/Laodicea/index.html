Laodicea

Laodicea (Greek: Λαοδίκεια, Laodikeia) is the name of several ancient cities founded during the Hellenistic period, principally by the Seleucid kings of Syria. The name honoured women of the royal house named Laodice—most famously Laodice, wife of Antiochus II Theos, and Laodice, mother of Seleucus I Nicator. Cities of this name are attested across Anatolia, Syria, Mesopotamia and Media.

Principal cities

  • Laodicea on the Lycus — in Phrygia, Anatolia, near modern Denizli in Turkey; a wealthy banking and textile city, and one of the Seven Churches of Asia addressed in the Book of Revelation.
  • Laodicea in Syria — modern Latakia, a major port on the Mediterranean coast of Syria, founded by Seleucus I.
  • Laodicea ad Libanum — in the Beqaa Valley region near Homs, Syria.
  • Laodicea in Mesopotamia — a city whose location is uncertain, possibly identifiable with a site in modern Iraq.
  • Laodicea in Media — identified with modern Nahavand in Iran.
  • Laodicea in Pontus and Laodicea Combusta ("the Burnt") in Lycaonia, near modern Ladik, Turkey.

The name

The name combines the Greek words laos ("people") and dikē ("justice"), and was borne by numerous women of the Macedonian and Seleucid royal families. Because "Laodicea" was a dynastic name, new foundations by successive Seleucid rulers repeatedly received it, leading to the profusion of homonymous cities, which ancient authors distinguished by geographical epithets ("on the Lycus", "by the sea", "near Lebanon").

Legacy

The most consequential of these cities is Laodicea on the Lycus, whose church received the lukewarm rebuke in Revelation 3:14–22—giving rise to the adjective "Laodicean", meaning indifferent or half-hearted, especially in religion. The Council of Laodicea, a regional church synod held there in the fourth century AD, produced canons, including an early list of biblical books, that influenced later canon law.

This article is about the name shared by several ancient cities. For the Phrygian city, see Laodicea on the Lycus.
