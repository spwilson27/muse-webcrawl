Damselfish

Damselfish are those fish belonging to the genera Abudefduf, Chromis, Dascyllus, Stegastes, Pomacentrus and many others in the family Pomacentridae. Along with the clownfishes (which form the subfamily Amphiprioninae), they make up one of the most familiar groups of tropical marine fishes.

Description

Damselfish are typically small, most species reaching only about 8–15 cm (3–6 in) in length, with deep, laterally compressed bodies and a single nostril on each side of the head. Many species are brilliantly coloured — electric blues, yellows and oranges — particularly as juveniles, though adults of some genera are drab brown or grey. Brightly coloured species are popular in the marine aquarium trade.

Distribution and habitat

Damselfish are found in tropical waters worldwide, chiefly in the Indo-Pacific, and are overwhelmingly associated with coral reefs, where they shelter among branching corals and rock crevices. A few species inhabit temperate waters or enter estuaries.

Behaviour

Many damselfish are highly territorial. Species of Stegastes farm patches of algae on the reef, weeding out unpalatable species and aggressively driving away much larger herbivores such as surgeonfish and parrotfish. Others, such as the Chromis species, form plankton-feeding aggregations above the reef. Males typically guard and aerate adhesive eggs laid on a cleared surface until they hatch.

In aquaria

Hardy and inexpensive, damselfish are often recommended as starter fish for marine aquariums, although their territorial aggression toward tankmates can be a problem in small systems.
