Itô's lemma

Itô's lemma, named after the Japanese mathematician Kiyosi Itô, is the fundamental differential rule of stochastic calculus — the analogue of the chain rule for functions of a stochastic process. It is indispensable in mathematical finance, where it underlies the derivation of the Black–Scholes equation.

A simulated Wiener process path; Itô's lemma describes how smooth functions of such paths evolve

Statement

Let Xt follow an Itô process dXt = μt dt + σt dBt, where Bt is a Wiener process, and let f(t, x) be twice continuously differentiable. Then f(t, Xt) is also an Itô process, and

df = ( ∂f/∂t + μ ∂f/∂x + ½σ² ∂²f/∂x² ) dt + σ ∂f/∂x dBt

The extra term ½σ² ∂²f/∂x² — arising because the quadratic variation of Brownian motion satisfies (dBt)² = dt — is the hallmark distinguishing Itô calculus from ordinary calculus.

History and use

Itô introduced the lemma in the 1940s as part of his construction of stochastic integrals. In finance, applying Itô's lemma to an option price as a function of the underlying stock price (modeled by geometric Brownian motion) yields the hedging argument behind the Black–Scholes–Merton model.
