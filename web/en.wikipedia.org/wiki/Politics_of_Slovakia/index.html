Politics of Slovakia


National Council of the Slovak Republic

The politics of Slovakia takes place in a framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic, with a multi-party system. The President of Slovakia is the head of state and the Prime Minister of Slovakia is the head of government. Legislative power is vested in the National Council of the Slovak Republic, a unicameral parliament of 150 members elected by proportional representation for four-year terms. Slovakia became an independent state on 1 January 1993 following the dissolution of Czechoslovakia, and joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and the eurozone in 2009.

Institutions

The president, directly elected since 1999 for a five-year term, appoints the prime minister and other officials and represents the state, though executive power rests mainly with the government. The Constitutional Court reviews the constitutionality of laws. Slovakia is a unitary state divided into eight self-governing regions (kraje) and numerous municipalities.

Recent politics

Recent decades have been dominated by figures such as Vladimír Mečiar in the 1990s and Robert Fico, whose Smer party has led several governments. The 2018 murder of journalist Ján Kuciak triggered mass protests and a change of government. Following the 2023 parliamentary election, Fico returned as prime minister for a fourth time at the head of a coalition, and survived an assassination attempt in May 2024.
