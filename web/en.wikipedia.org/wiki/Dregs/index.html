Dregs

Dregs are the solid residues or sediment that remain at the bottom of a container after the liquid has been poured off or consumed. The term is most commonly used for the sediment in wine, beer, coffee and tea, consisting of dead yeast cells (lees), tartrates, grape skins, coffee grounds or other particulate matter.

In winemaking and brewing

In wine, dregs or lees form during fermentation and ageing; contact with fine lees (sur lie ageing) can add texture and flavour. Dregs are typically removed by racking, fining and filtration before bottling, though some unfiltered wines retain sediment that is separated by decanting.

Figurative use

Figuratively, "the dregs" denotes the least valuable, most degraded part of something, as in "the dregs of society". The word derives from Old Norse dregg and Middle English dregges.
