Fin de siècle

Fin de siècle (French for "end of the century") is a term applied to the closing years of the 19th century, roughly 1880 to 1914, and to the cultural mood associated with them: a mixture of sophistication, decadence, world-weariness, and anticipation of a new era. Although French in origin, the spirit was felt across Europe and is often paired with the Belle Époque in France, the Victorian and Edwardian eras in Britain, and the Wilhelminism of Germany.

Cultural character

The period saw the flourishing of Symbolism, Decadence, Aestheticism, and early Modernism in literature — Joris-Karl Huysmans, Oscar Wilde, and Aubrey Beardsley among the emblematic figures — and of Art Nouveau in the visual arts, with artists such as Gustav Klimt and Alphonse Mucha. Thinkers and the public alike dwelt on themes of decline, degeneration, and transformation amid rapid industrialization, urbanization, and new sciences.

Legacy

The phrase is now used more generally for the end of any century and for cultural moments that combine exhaustion with anticipation. The fin de siècle proper ended abruptly with the First World War, which shattered the world the period's artists and writers had inhabited.
