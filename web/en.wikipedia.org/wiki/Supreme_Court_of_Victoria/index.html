The Supreme Court of Victoria is the superior court of the Australian state of Victoria, established in 1852 following the separation of the colony of Victoria from New South Wales. It is inferior to the High Court of Australia, but superior to the County Court of Victoria and the Magistrates' Court within the state court hierarchy.

Structure

The Court consists of a Trial Division and a Court of Appeal, and is headed by the Chief Justice of Victoria. It hears the most serious criminal cases, major civil litigation, and appeals from lower Victorian courts and tribunals. Its principal buildings are the historic Supreme Court buildings on William Street in Melbourne.

History

The court has sat in Melbourne since the gold-rush era and has presided over famous cases including trials arising from the Eureka Rebellion in 1855. Judges of the court are appointed by the Governor in Council and retire at the age of 70.
