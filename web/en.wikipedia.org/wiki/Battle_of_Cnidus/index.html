Battle of Cnidus

The Battle of Cnidus was a naval battle fought in 394 BC off Cnidus, on the southwestern coast of Asia Minor, between a Persian fleet commanded by the Athenian admiral Conon and the satrap Pharnabazus II, and a Spartan fleet under the navarch Peisander. The Persian-Athenian victory destroyed Spartan naval supremacy and marked a turning point in the Corinthian War.

The engagement off Cnidus, 394 BC.

Background

After Sparta's victory in the Peloponnesian War, Spartan fleets and garrisons dominated the Aegean, provoking resentment among former allies. Persia, seeking to weaken Sparta, financed a fleet under Conon — an Athenian exile who had survived the disaster at Aegospotami — to challenge Spartan sea power. Peisander, brother-in-law of the Spartan king Agesilaus II, sailed with some 85 triremes to meet a larger Persian-Phoenician fleet of about 90 or more ships near Cnidus.

The battle

Peisander attacked despite being outnumbered, reportedly fearing the disgrace of avoiding battle. His Greek allies on the left wing fled early in the action; Peisander's own ships fought on and were driven ashore or captured, and Peisander himself was killed. Sources report that the Persians captured some 50 Spartan triremes.

Consequences

The victory ended Sparta's brief naval empire. Conon and Pharnabazus sailed around the Aegean, expelling Spartan harmosts (governors) and encouraging democracies; Conon then returned to Athens with Persian money to rebuild the city's Long Walls and fortifications, restoring Athenian power a decade after its defeat in 404 BC.
