Saruman, also known as Saruman the White (and in Sindarin Curunír, "man of skill"), is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. He appears as the head of the Wizards (Istari) and leader of the White Council, but proves to be a traitor who has fallen to the desire for power, seeking the One Ring for himself and secretly building an army at Isengard. He is a central antagonist in The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), and is portrayed by Christopher Lee in Peter Jackson's film trilogies.

Story

Saruman was one of the five Maiar sent to Middle-earth around the year 1000 of the Third Age as Istari, emissaries to aid the free peoples against Sauron. Chief of the order and learned in the lore of the Rings of Power, he settled in the tower of Orthanc at Isengard, at the southern end of the Misty Mountains, and for centuries was a genuine ally; in 2463 he became head of the White Council, which drove Sauron from Dol Guldur.

Prolonged study of the Rings — and use of the palantír of Orthanc, through which Sauron ensnared him — corrupted him. Renouncing the White for "Saruman of Many Colours", he fortified Isengard, bred an army of Orcs and the fighting Uruk-hai, felled the trees of the valley, and made war on Rohan while secretly seeking the Ring. His servant Gríma Wormtongue poisoned the counsels of King Théoden. Saruman's plot was exposed when he tried to overawe Gandalf, whom he imprisoned on the pinnacle of Orthanc until the Eagle Gwaihir rescued him.

His armies were destroyed at the Battle of the Hornburg (Helm's Deep), and the Ents, enraged by the destruction of their trees, wrecked Isengard and flooded it, imprisoning Saruman in Orthanc. After Sauron's defeat Gandalf broke his staff and cast him from the order. Released, Saruman — now called Sharkey — travelled north with a handful of ruffians and seized the Shire in revenge, industrialising and despoiling it until the returning hobbits overthrew him in the Scouring of the Shire. At the very end, refused mercy by Frodo, he cursed Wormtongue, who cut his throat on the steps of Bag End; his spirit rose, looked west, and was blown away.

Characterisation

Tolkien presents Saruman as a study in corrupted intellect: his chief weapons are his voice — persuasive, subtle, almost hypnotic — and his knowledge ("Saruman the Wise"). Critics have read him as a figure of industrialisation and technocracy against Tolkien's pastoral values, and his fall mirrors that of his master Sauron in miniature: from servant of the powers to would-be tyrant, ending in pettiness and spite. His Scouring of the Shire was read by many early readers as an allegory of post-war England, an interpretation Tolkien denied beyond a general debt to his childhood experience of the countryside's destruction.
