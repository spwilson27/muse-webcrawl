Conjugation

Conjugation may refer to several related concepts in linguistics, mathematics, biology, and chemistry.

In linguistics

Grammatical conjugation is the variation of a verb in accordance with person, number, tense, aspect, mood, and voice. In languages such as Latin, Spanish, and French, verbs fall into conjugation classes with characteristic endings; compare French verb forms. The corresponding variation of nouns and adjectives is called declension.

In mathematics

Conjugation includes complex conjugation, mapping a + bi to a − bi; conjugation in group theory, where elements g and hgh−1 are conjugate; and the conjugate transpose of a matrix. Conjugate elements in field theory are roots of the same irreducible polynomial.

In biology and chemistry

Bacterial conjugation is the transfer of genetic material between bacteria through direct cell-to-cell contact, often via a pilus. In chemistry, conjugation describes overlapping p-orbitals in a molecule, as in conjugated double bonds, and in biochemistry the attachment of compounds such as glucuronic acid to make substances more water-soluble.
