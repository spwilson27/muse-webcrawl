Cauchy's integral formula

Cauchy's integral formula, named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy, is a central statement in complex analysis. It expresses the value of a holomorphic function inside a disk in terms of a line integral around the disk's boundary, and shows that a holomorphic function is completely determined by its values on the boundary.

FieldComplex analysis
Named afterAugustin-Louis Cauchy

Statement

If f is holomorphic on and inside a simple closed curve C, and a is a point inside C, then f(a) = (1/2πi) ∮ f(z)/(z − a) dz. Generalised versions give every derivative f⁽ⁿ⁾(a) as a similar integral, proving that holomorphic functions are analytic — representable by power series.

Consequences

The formula implies Cauchy's estimates on derivatives, Liouville's theorem, the maximum modulus principle, and Morera's and Goursat's theorems' converses. It underlies residue calculus: the integral picks out the value f(a) because 1/(z − a) has a single simple pole with residue 1. Compare Casorati–Weierstrass theorem.
