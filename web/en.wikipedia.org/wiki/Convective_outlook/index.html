Convective outlook

A Day 1 convective outlook issued by the Storm Prediction Center

A convective outlook is a forecast product issued by the Storm Prediction Center (SPC) of the United States National Weather Service that outlines areas at risk of severe thunderstorms over the coming days. Outlooks are issued for Day 1 (today), Day 2 (tomorrow), Day 3, and in probabilistic form for Days 4–8.

Risk categories

The categorical outlook uses a scale of increasing risk:

  • General thunderstorms — non-severe storms expected
  • Marginal risk — isolated severe storms possible
  • Slight risk — scattered severe storms possible
  • Enhanced risk — numerous severe storms possible
  • Moderate risk — widespread severe storms, some intense, likely
  • High risk — a major severe weather outbreak expected

High risk days are rare and reserved for the most serious tornado and derecho outbreaks. Day 1 outlooks are accompanied by separate probabilistic maps for tornadoes, damaging wind, and large hail; hatched areas denote a risk of especially significant severe weather, such as tornadoes of EF2 intensity or greater.

History

The SPC and its predecessors have issued convective outlooks since the 1950s, when the Severe Local Storms unit was established in Washington, D.C. The centre moved to Norman, Oklahoma, in 1997, co-locating with the National Severe Storms Laboratory. The categorical scale has been revised several times; the "enhanced" category was added in 2014.
