Great Lakes Storm of 1913

[Image: Great Lakes Storm of 1913]

The Great Lakes Storm of 1913, historically referred to as the "Big Blow", the "Freshwater Fury", or the "White Hurricane", was a blizzard with hurricane-force winds that devastated the Great Lakes region of the United States and Canada from 7 to 10 November 1913.

Impact

The storm was the deadliest and most destructive natural disaster to hit the lakes. More than 250 people were killed and 19 ships were destroyed, with 19 others stranded. Losses amounted to over $5 million in vessels alone — about $130 million in modern terms. The giant freighter Henry B. Smith and the Canadian steamer James Carruthers were among the ships lost with all hands; bodies washing ashore were often so ice-coated that identifying them was difficult.

Causes

Two storm systems converged over the lakes while a strong high-pressure system limited escape routes, producing sustained winds above 140 km/h and waves over 11 metres. The storm led to improvements in weather forecasting and ship construction on the Great Lakes.
