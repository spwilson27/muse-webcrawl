What Is It Like to Be a Bat?

"What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"
AuthorThomas Nagel
PublishedThe Philosophical Review, 1974
SubjectPhilosophy of mind; consciousness

"What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" is a paper by American philosopher Thomas Nagel, first published in The Philosophical Review in 1974. It is one of the most widely discussed articles in the philosophy of mind and a classic critique of physicalist reductionism about consciousness.

Argument

Nagel argues that an organism has conscious mental states if and only if there is something that it is like to be that organism — a subjective character of experience. Bats, which perceive the world largely through echolocation, have experiences whose subjective character humans cannot grasp merely by imagining webbed arms or sonar; we can only imagine what it would be like for us to behave as bats do. Nagel concludes that physicalist theories, which describe only objective facts, leave out this subjective character, and that an objective phenomenology would be needed to capture it.

Influence

The paper helped define the modern debate over qualia and the "hard problem of consciousness", and its question — what it is like to be a subject — became a standard way of framing the explanatory gap between physical processes and experience.
