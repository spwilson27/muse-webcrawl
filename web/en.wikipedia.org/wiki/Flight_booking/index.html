Flight booking

Flight booking is the process of reserving and purchasing air travel on a scheduled passenger flight. A booking records the passenger's itinerary in an airline's reservation system, generating a booking reference (record locator) and, once paid for, an electronic ticket. Bookings may be made directly with an airline, through a travel agency, or through online travel agencies and metasearch engines.

An electronic flight booking confirmation

History

For much of the twentieth century, flight bookings were made by telephone or in person through travel agents working with paper tickets. Airlines built computer reservation systems — such as Sabre, developed by American Airlines in the 1960s — which evolved into global distribution systems used by agents worldwide. The rise of the World Wide Web in the 1990s shifted much booking to airline websites and online travel agencies, and paper tickets were almost entirely replaced by e-tickets by 2008.

Process

A typical booking involves choosing an itinerary and fare class, providing passenger details exactly as they appear on identity documents, and paying by card or other means. The reservation is held in a passenger name record (PNR) containing the itinerary, contact details, and ticketing status. Travellers usually check in before departure — online from 24–48 hours ahead, or at the airport — to obtain a boarding pass.

Pricing

Airlines use revenue management systems that continuously adjust fares according to demand, booking patterns, and competition, so the price of a seat can vary greatly between passengers on the same flight. Fares are sold in booking classes with differing conditions for refunds, changes, and baggage.
