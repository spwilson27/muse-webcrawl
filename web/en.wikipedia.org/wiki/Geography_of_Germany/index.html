Geography of Germany

Topography of Germany

Area: 357,588 km²

Highest point: Zugspitze, 2,962 m

Longest river: Rhine (German section)

Germany is a country in Central Europe covering 357,588 square kilometres, bordered by Denmark, the North Sea and the Baltic Sea to the north; Poland and the Czech Republic to the east; Austria and Switzerland to the south; and France, Luxembourg, Belgium and the Netherlands to the west.

Physical regions

The terrain rises from north to south in three bands. The North German Plain, part of the Great European Plain, is a lowland of marshes, heathland and fertile farmland, cut by the Elbe, Weser and Ems rivers. The Central German Uplands (Mittelgebirge) are a belt of forested hills and low mountains including the Harz, the Thuringian Forest, the Ore Mountains and the Black Forest. In the south, the Alpine foothills and the German Alps in Bavaria contain the country's highest peak, the Zugspitze (2,962 m), on the Austrian border.

Rivers and lakes

The Rhine is Germany's most important commercial waterway, flowing north from Lake Constance to the Dutch border. The Danube rises in the Black Forest and flows east through Bavaria into Austria. Lake Constance (Bodensee) is the largest lake, shared with Austria and Switzerland.

Climate

Germany has a temperate seasonal climate moderated by the North Atlantic Drift: mild summers, cool winters, and rainfall throughout the year, with the northwest more maritime and the southeast more continental.
