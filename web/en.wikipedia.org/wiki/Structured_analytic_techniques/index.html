Structured analytic techniques

Structured analytic techniques are methods used in intelligence analysis to make analytical reasoning more explicit, systematic and resistant to cognitive bias. They were widely promoted in the United States intelligence community following the 9/11 attacks and the Iraq WMD intelligence failure, notably through the work of Richards J. Heuer Jr. and the CIA's Center for the Study of Intelligence.

Common techniques

Widely used techniques include Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH), in which analysts test multiple hypotheses against a matrix of evidence; key assumptions checks; devil's advocacy and red teaming, which challenge the prevailing assessment; indicators and signposts of future change; scenario analysis; and pre-mortem analysis, which imagines that a judgement has failed and works backwards to explain why.

Purpose

The techniques aim to externalise analysts' mental models, expose hidden assumptions, and ensure that alternative explanations and disconfirming evidence are considered before judgements are finalised.
