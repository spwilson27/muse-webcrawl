The Polizia di Stato (State Police) is one of Italy's national police forces and its principal civilian police service. It is responsible for public security, criminal investigation, highway, railway, postal and communications policing, immigration and border control, and the protection of public order. It operates under the Department of Public Security of the Ministry of the Interior, headed by the Chief of Police (Capo della Polizia).

History

The force traces its origins to the Corps of Public Security Guards founded in the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1852. It was reorganised several times under the Kingdom of Italy and the Fascist regime, during which it was militarised. A major reform in 1981 (Law 121/1981) demilitarised the force, renamed it the Polizia di Stato, and opened all roles to women.

Organisation

In each province the force is directed by a questore, based at the questura. Specialist divisions include the traffic police (Polizia Stradale), railway police (Polizia Ferroviaria), the postal and communications police responsible for cybercrime (Polizia Postale e delle Comunicazioni), anti-mafia and anti-terrorism units such as DIGOS, forensic police, and the elite counter-terrorism unit NOCS (Nucleo Operativo Centrale di Sicurezza). The State Police works alongside Italy's other forces, notably the military Carabinieri and the Guardia di Finanza.
