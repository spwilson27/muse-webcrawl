Economic inequality
Measures: Gini coefficient, income shares
Forms: Income, wealth, opportunity inequality

Economic inequality is the difference in how assets, wealth or income are distributed among individuals and populations. It is studied by economists, sociologists and political scientists, and measured by tools such as the Gini coefficient and income-share ratios.

Causes

Proposed drivers include technology and skill premiums, globalisation, labour-market institutions, tax and transfer policy, inheritance, education access, discrimination, and returns to capital exceeding growth — emphasised by Thomas Piketty. Within-country inequality rose in many nations from the 1980s, while global between-country inequality has fallen with growth in China and India.

Effects and policy

Debated effects include impacts on growth, health, crime, political influence and social mobility. Policy responses include progressive taxation, minimum wages, public education, social insurance and redistribution. See also Income inequality and Poverty.
