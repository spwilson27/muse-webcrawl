The Miami Dolphins are a professional American football team based in the Miami metropolitan area, competing in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) East division. They play their home games at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, Florida.

History

Founded in 1966 as an American Football League expansion team, the Dolphins joined the NFL in the 1970 merger. Under head coach Don Shula, hired in 1970, Miami became a powerhouse. The 1972 Dolphins completed the only perfect season in NFL history, going 17–0 and winning Super Bowl VII; they repeated as champions in Super Bowl VIII the following year. Shula became the winningest coach in NFL history, and quarterback Dan Marino, who played for the team from 1983 to 1999, set numerous passing records despite never winning a Super Bowl.

Identity

The team's colours are aqua, orange and white. Their fight song dates to the perfect-season era, and their former home, the Orange Bowl, hosted the franchise's glory years. Alumni in the Pro Football Hall of Fame include Shula, Marino, Bob Griese, Larry Csonka, Nick Buoniconti and Jason Taylor.
