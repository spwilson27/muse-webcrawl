James Byron Dean (February 8, 1931 – September 30, 1955) was an American actor. He became one of the defining cultural icons of teenage disillusionment and social estrangement, as expressed in the title of his most celebrated film, Rebel Without a Cause (1955), in which he starred as troubled teenager Jim Stark. His other two starring roles were as loner Cal Trask in East of Eden (1955) and as surly ranch hand Jett Rink in Giant (1956).

Early life

Dean was born in Marion, Indiana, and raised largely in Fairmount, Indiana, by an aunt and uncle after his mother's death when he was nine. He studied drama at UCLA but dropped out to pursue acting full-time, appearing in television dramas and in small stage roles in New York, including a Broadway production of André Gide's The Immoralist that caught the attention of director Elia Kazan.

Career

Kazan cast Dean in East of Eden, adapted from John Steinbeck's novel; his brooding, Method-inflected performance earned him a posthumous Academy Award nomination for Best Actor — the first posthumous acting nomination in Oscar history. Nicholas Ray's Rebel Without a Cause cemented his image: the red jacket, white T-shirt, and jeans became an enduring emblem of youthful rebellion. Dean then co-starred with Elizabeth Taylor and Rock Hudson in George Stevens's epic Giant.

Death

An enthusiastic racing driver, Dean was driving his Porsche 550 Spyder westward on Route 466 (now State Route 46) near Cholame, California, on September 30, 1955, en route to a race at Salinas, when he collided head-on with a Ford Tudor driven by Donald Turnupseed. Dean died almost instantly, at age 24. He received a second posthumous Oscar nomination for Giant.

Legacy

Dean's death in the middle of his brief career secured his legendary status. The American Film Institute ranked him the 18th greatest male star of classic Hollywood cinema. The phrase "live fast, die young" became inseparably associated with his image, and he remains an enduring reference point in discussions of youth culture and stardom.
