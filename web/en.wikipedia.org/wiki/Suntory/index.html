Suntory Holdings Limited (Japanese: サントリーホールディングス株式会社) is a Japanese multinational brewing and distilling company group. Established in 1899 as Kotobukiya by Shinjirō Torii, it is one of the oldest companies in Japan distributing alcoholic beverages and soft drinks, and one of the country's largest beverage makers. It is headquartered in Kita-ku, Osaka.

Suntory is best known internationally for its whisky brands, including Yamazaki and Hakushu single malts, and since 2014 for owning Beam Suntory (now Suntory Global Spirits), the American spirits company formed after Suntory's acquisition of Beam Inc. for approximately $16 billion.

History

Shinjirō Torii opened his first store, Torii Shōten, in Osaka in 1899, selling imported wines. In 1907 he launched Akadama Port Wine, a sweet fortified wine adapted to Japanese tastes. In 1923 Torii built Japan's first malt whisky distillery at Yamazaki, on the outskirts of Kyoto, and released the country's first authentic whisky, Shirofuda, in 1929; the company's Kakubin whisky followed in 1937 and became a long-running best seller.

The company was renamed Suntory in 1963, a name derived from "sun" and Torii's surname. Suntory entered the beer market in 1963 and expanded into soft drinks, coffee (including the Boss canned coffee brand), and health foods. In 2009 it acquired Orangina Schweppes Group, and in 2013 the drinks brands Lucozade and Ribena from GlaxoSmithKline.

Whisky

Suntory's Yamazaki single malt gained worldwide acclaim in the 2000s and 2010s, helping drive the global boom in Japanese whisky. The company operates the Yamazaki and Hakushu malt distilleries and the Chita grain distillery. Its blended whiskies include Hibiki, Kakubin, and Toki.

Suntory Hall and the arts

Suntory has a long tradition of corporate patronage of the arts. Suntory Hall, a concert hall in Tokyo's Ark Hills complex opened in 1986, is home to major orchestral performances, and the Suntory Museum of Art operates in Tokyo Midtown. The company's founder's philosophy, often summarized as "Yatte Minahare" ("go for it"), remains part of its corporate culture.
