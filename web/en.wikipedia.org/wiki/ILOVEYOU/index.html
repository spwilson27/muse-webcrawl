ILOVEYOU

ILOVEYOU, sometimes referred to as the Love Bug or Loveletter, was a computer worm that infected over ten million Windows personal computers beginning on 5 May 2000. It started spreading as an email message entitled "ILOVEYOU" with the attachment "LOVE-LETTER-FOR-YOU.txt.vbs", a Visual Basic script. It is often considered the first major virus to attract widespread attention from mainstream media, and its damage was estimated at US$5.5–8.7 billion worldwide.

Overview

The email subject line and attachment name were designed to exploit curiosity and social engineering: recipients believed they had received a love confession. When the attachment was opened, the worm activated: it overwrote music, image, and other files with copies of itself, mailed itself to all addresses in the victim's Microsoft Outlook address book, and attempted to download a password-stealing program.

Spread and impact

Because the worm mailed itself from the victim's own account to known contacts, it spread far faster than earlier worms such as Melissa. Corporate and government networks worldwide shut down email systems to stop it; affected organizations included the Pentagon, the CIA, the British Parliament, and large companies such as Ford and AT&T.

Origin

The worm was traced to the Philippines. Its author was later identified as Onel de Guzman, a computer student in Manila, who said the program was adapted from his thesis proposal for stealing internet passwords. Charges against him were dropped because the Philippines had no applicable computer-crime law at the time; the incident prompted the country to pass an e-commerce law covering such acts.
