Supernatural

The supernatural refers to phenomena beyond the laws of nature.

Supernatural refers to phenomena or entities that are beyond the laws of nature. The term is derived from Medieval Latin supernaturalis, from Latin super- (above, beyond, or outside of) + natura (nature). Although the corollary term "nature" has had multiple meanings since the ancient world, the term "supernatural" emerged in the medieval period and did not exist in the ancient world.

Concepts

Supernatural beliefs include phenomena such as deities, spirits, ghosts, miracles, magic, clairvoyance, and an afterlife. In theology and philosophy, the supernatural is contrasted with the natural: natural phenomena are in principle explicable by the laws of nature, whereas supernatural phenomena are attributed to agencies or causes that transcend those laws. Views differ sharply on whether supernatural entities exist at all — naturalism denies them, while most religions affirm them.

In religion and folklore

Many religions describe supernatural beings and events: angels, demons, gods, and miraculous interventions. Folklore around the world is rich with supernatural creatures, from vampires and werewolves to the kami of Shinto and the jinn of Islamic tradition. The anthropology of religion studies how such beliefs shape ritual, morality, and social organisation.

In popular culture

Supernatural themes pervade horror fiction, fantasy, television, and film — from Gothic novels to contemporary series such as the long-running Supernatural (2005–2020), which followed two brothers hunting monsters and demons across America.

Criticism

Philosophers of science and skeptics argue that supernatural explanations are untestable and that alleged supernatural phenomena have consistently yielded to natural explanations or failed to be verified. Methodological naturalism, the working assumption of science, proceeds without invoking supernatural causes.
