Morena (political party)

The National Regeneration Movement (Spanish: Movimiento Regeneración Nacional), commonly known by its acronym MORENA (also rendered Morena), is a left-wing political party in Mexico. Founded in 2011 as a civil association and registered as a political party in 2014 by Andrés Manuel López Obrador, Morena won the presidency and congressional majorities in the 2018 general election and retained the presidency in 2024 with Claudia Sheinbaum. It is the dominant party in Mexican politics, controlling the presidency, both chambers of Congress (in alliance with the PT and PVEM), and a majority of state governorships.

History

López Obrador, a former head of government of Mexico City and two-time presidential candidate of the PRD, founded Morena after the disputed 2006 election and his 2012 campaign. The movement organised nationwide opposition to the energy reforms of President Enrique Peña Nieto. Registered as a party in 2014, it performed strongly in the 2015 midterms and swept the 2018 elections: López Obrador won the presidency with about 53% of the vote, and the Morena-led coalition won majorities in Congress. In 2024, Claudia Sheinbaum, the party's candidate, won the presidency with about 60% of the vote, becoming Mexico's first woman president, while the coalition secured supermajorities enabling constitutional reforms.

Ideology and policies

Morena promotes what López Obrador called the "Fourth Transformation" of Mexican public life: combating corruption, austerity in government, social welfare programmes such as universal pensions and youth scholarships, state-led energy policy, and large infrastructure projects. Critics have raised concerns about democratic backsliding, the militarisation of public security, and reforms to the judiciary, including the introduction of popular elections for judges.
