Aristide Boucicaut

Aristide Boucicaut, founder of Le Bon Marché

Aristide Boucicaut (14 July 1810 – 26 December 1877) was a French entrepreneur who founded Le Bon Marché in Paris, generally credited as the first modern department store. Boucicaut pioneered many of the practices that define department-store retailing: fixed prices, free entry without obligation to buy, a wide assortment of goods under one roof, seasonal sales, mail-order catalogues and liberal returns policies.

Early life

Boucicaut was born in Bellême, in Normandy, the son of a hatter. After an apprenticeship in a draper's shop and work as an itinerant pedlar, he moved to Paris and found employment at the Petit Saint-Thomas, a novelty store, rising to head a department.

Le Bon Marché

In 1852 Boucicaut became a partner in Le Bon Marché, a small Paris shop founded by the Videau brothers, and soon took control. He expanded it aggressively, reinvesting profits and borrowing to build a vast new store on the Left Bank. Annual turnover grew from a few hundred thousand francs to tens of millions. Boucicaut's innovations included clearly marked fixed prices replacing haggling, low margins with high turnover, home delivery, advertising, and the famous January "white sale" of linens.

Boucicaut also introduced notable staff welfare measures, including lodging for unmarried employees, a provident fund, and profit-related benefits, partly to retain a loyal and respectable workforce.

Legacy

After his death, his widow Marguerite Boucicaut continued the business and became one of France's great philanthropists, funding among other things the Boucicaut Hospital in Paris. The couple's store served as the model for Émile Zola's novel Au Bonheur des Dames (1883), whose protagonist Octave Mouret is closely based on Boucicaut. Le Bon Marché survives today as a landmark Paris department store.
