Stress management

Relaxation techniques are a common element of stress management

Stress management consists of techniques and psychotherapies aimed at controlling a person's level of stress, especially chronic stress, in order to improve health and everyday functioning. Stress produces physiological changes through the sympathetic nervous system and the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis; unmanaged chronic stress is associated with cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety and impaired immune function.

Techniques

Commonly taught techniques include relaxation training (deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, guided imagery), meditation and mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), a structured programme developed by Jon Kabat-Zinn at the University of Massachusetts in the late 1970s; physical exercise; time management and problem-solving skills; cognitive restructuring, which challenges distorted appraisals of demands; and social support and sleep hygiene. Biofeedback uses real-time physiological signals to help people learn voluntary control over tension and arousal.

Models

Richard Lazarus's transactional model frames stress as arising when perceived demands exceed perceived resources, so interventions can target either the stressor (problem-focused coping) or the emotional response (emotion-focused coping). Hans Selye's earlier general adaptation syndrome described stress in terms of alarm, resistance and exhaustion stages. Workplace stress management is a major application area, addressed by occupational health programmes, workload redesign and employee assistance programmes.

Effectiveness

Evidence reviews find that cognitive-behavioural approaches and mindfulness programmes produce modest but reliable reductions in perceived stress and anxiety, while organisational-level interventions that reduce the stressors themselves may be more durable than techniques aimed only at individuals.
