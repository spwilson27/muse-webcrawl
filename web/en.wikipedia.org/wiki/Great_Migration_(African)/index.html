Great Migration (African)

The Great Migration is the annual, long-distance movement of enormous herds of grazing animals across the Serengeti–Mara ecosystem of Tanzania and Kenya, widely considered one of the greatest wildlife spectacles on Earth. Around 1.5 million wildebeest, together with some 200,000 zebras and large numbers of gazelles, travel a circuit of roughly 800–1,600 km (500–1,000 mi) in search of fresh grazing and water, following the seasonal rains.

Route and river crossings

The herds calve in the southern Serengeti in January–March, move northwest through the western corridor, and cross the crocodile-infested Grumeti and Mara rivers between about July and October, when thousands of animals drown or are taken by predators during the dramatic crossings. They then graze the Masai Mara before returning south as the short rains begin. The migration supports a rich predator community of lions, leopards, cheetahs, hyenas and Nile crocodiles, and sustains dung beetles, vultures and grassland cycles throughout the ecosystem.
