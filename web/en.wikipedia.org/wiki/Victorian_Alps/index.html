The Victorian Alps, also known locally as the High Country, are a mountainous region in the north-east of the Australian state of Victoria. They form the south-western portion of the Australian Alps and part of the Great Dividing Range, and contain the highest peaks in Victoria.

Geography

The region includes Mount Bogong (1,986 metres), the highest mountain in Victoria, along with Mount Feathertop (1,922 metres), Mount Hotham, and the Bogong High Plains. Major rivers, including the Mitta Mitta, Kiewa, Ovens and upper Murray tributaries, rise in the Alps. Much of the region is protected within the Alpine National Park, Victoria's largest national park.

Ecology

Slopes are clothed in snow gum and alpine ash forests, giving way above the treeline to alpine herbfields, mossbeds and snow-patch communities. The high country is home to endangered species such as the mountain pygmy possum and the Bogong moth, which historically aestivates in caves around Mount Bogong. The region is vulnerable to bushfire and to climate-driven changes in snowfall.

History and recreation

Aboriginal peoples visited the high country in summer for generations, gathering for ceremonies and to feast on Bogong moths. From the 1850s, European graziers drove cattle to summer pastures on the high plains, a tradition associated with "mountain cattlemen" and the huts scattered across the range. Today the Victorian Alps are a centre for snow sports, with resorts at Mount Hotham, Falls Creek, Mount Buller and Mount Buffalo, as well as bushwalking, mountain biking and touring along the Great Alpine Road.
