Product recall

A product recall is a request by a manufacturer, distributor or regulator for consumers to return a product, typically because of a safety defect, contamination, mislabelling or non-compliance discovered after distribution. Recalls may be voluntary or mandated, and remedies include repair, replacement or refund. They are costly to companies and can severely damage brand reputation.

Regulatory oversight

In the United States, the Consumer Product Safety Commission oversees most consumer goods, the Food and Drug Administration handles food, drugs and medical devices, and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration handles vehicles; the USDA covers meat and poultry. The European Union operates Safety Gate (formerly RAPEX), an alert system sharing information about dangerous non-food products. Recall classifications vary by severity; FDA drug and food recalls are graded Class I (reasonable probability of serious harm), Class II and Class III.

Notable recalls

Famous cases include the 1982 Tylenol tampering murders, which led to tamper-evident packaging across the industry; the Firestone tyre/Ford Explorer crisis of 2000–01; Toyota's unintended-acceleration recalls of 2009–10; Takata's airbag recalls, the largest in automotive history; and Samsung's 2016 Galaxy Note 7 battery recall, which ended the model. Food recalls for pathogens such as Salmonella and Listeria are frequent worldwide.
