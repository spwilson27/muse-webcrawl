Auction

TypesEnglish, Dutch, sealed-bid, Vickrey

An auction is a process of buying and selling goods or services by offering them up for bids, taking bids, and selling to the highest bidder — or, in reverse auctions, the lowest. Auctions have been recorded since antiquity and described by Herodotus in Babylon.

Types

In an English (open ascending) auction, bidding rises until only one bidder remains. In a Dutch auction, price falls from a high start until a bidder accepts. Sealed-bid auctions include first-price and second-price (Vickrey) formats. Auctions are used for art (Sotheby's, Christie's), government spectrum licences, electricity markets, online sales such as eBay, and procurement.

Theory

Auction theory, recognised by the 2020 Nobel Prize in Economics to Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson, studies bidder behaviour, the winner's curse, and revenue equivalence, and designs formats for complex sales.
