Jan Łukasiewicz

Born21 December 1878, Lwów, Austria-Hungary (now Lviv, Ukraine)
Died13 February 1956, Dublin, Ireland
FieldsLogic, philosophy, mathematics
Known forPolish notation; three-valued logic; Łukasiewicz logic

Jan Łukasiewicz (21 December 1878 – 13 February 1956) was a Polish logician and philosopher who is recognised as one of the most important historians of logic and a central figure of the Lwów–Warsaw school. He invented Polish notation, introduced many-valued (three-valued) logic, and made fundamental studies of Aristotle's syllogistic and the history of propositional logic.

Life and career

Łukasiewicz studied at the University of Lwów under Kazimierz Twardowski, became professor at Lwów and later at the University of Warsaw, where he served as rector. He was Poland's Minister of Education in 1919. After the Second World War, during which his manuscripts were destroyed, he emigrated to Ireland in 1946 and worked at the Royal Irish Academy in Dublin until his death.

Contributions

His parenthesis-free Polish notation (and its reverse form, RPN) later became influential in computer science. His three-valued logic (1920), motivated by the problem of future contingents and determinism, founded the study of many-valued and modal logics, and his axiomatisation of propositional calculus remains a classic of formal logic.
