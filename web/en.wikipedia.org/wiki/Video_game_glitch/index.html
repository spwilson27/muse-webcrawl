Video game glitch

A glitch in video games is a short-lived fault in a system, producing unexpected behaviour such as corrupted graphics, clipping through geometry, duplicated items, or physics anomalies. Unlike crashes, glitches often leave the game playable and are sometimes exploited deliberately.

Types

Common categories include graphical glitches, physics glitches (such as the backwards long jump in Super Mario 64), sequence breaks, duplication glitches, and out-of-bounds exploits. The famous Minus World in Super Mario Bros. and MissingNo. in Pokémon Red and Blue are canonical early examples.

Exploitation

Speedrunners systematically exploit glitches for competitive runs; communities distinguish glitchless and any% categories. Developers may patch harmful glitches while leaving beloved harmless ones intact, and some glitches — strafe-jumping, skiing in Tribes — became core mechanics in sequels.

Glitch hunting

Dedicated players and tool-assisted communities document glitches extensively, feeding both speedrunning and game preservation knowledge.
