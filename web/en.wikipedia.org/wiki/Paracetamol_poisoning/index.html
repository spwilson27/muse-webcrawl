Paracetamol poisoning

SpecialtyToxicology, emergency medicine
Main riskAcute liver failure
AntidoteAcetylcysteine (N-acetylcysteine)
Also calledAcetaminophen toxicity

Paracetamol poisoning, also known as acetaminophen toxicity, is poisoning caused by excessive use of the analgesic paracetamol (acetaminophen). It is one of the most common causes of poisoning worldwide and the leading cause of acute liver failure in many Western countries. Most overdoses are intentional self-poisonings, though unintentional toxicity can occur when several paracetamol-containing products are taken together or doses are repeatedly exceeded.

Mechanism

At therapeutic doses, paracetamol is mostly conjugated in the liver and excreted harmlessly. A small fraction is converted by cytochrome P450 enzymes into a toxic metabolite, NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine), which is normally detoxified by glutathione. In overdose, glutathione stores are overwhelmed; NAPQI accumulates and causes centrilobular necrosis of hepatocytes, which can progress to fulminant liver failure, kidney injury, encephalopathy, and death.

Course

Poisoning classically evolves in stages: nausea, vomiting, and malaise in the first 24 hours (sometimes minimal symptoms); a deceptive improvement with rising liver enzymes on days 2–3; peak liver injury, jaundice, coagulopathy, and possible liver failure on days 3–5; then recovery over one to three weeks in survivors. Severity is assessed with a serum paracetamol level plotted against time since ingestion on the Rumack–Matthew nomogram.

Treatment

Early treatment is highly effective. Activated charcoal may be given if the patient presents within about an hour. The specific antidote is acetylcysteine, which replenishes glutathione and can prevent or limit liver injury, especially when started within eight hours of ingestion; it remains beneficial later as well. Patients who develop acute liver failure may require intensive care and evaluation for liver transplantation using prognostic criteria such as the King's College criteria.
