Saturated fat

A saturated fat is a type of fat in which the fatty acid chains have all single bonds between carbon atoms, and are thus "saturated" with hydrogen atoms. Saturated fats are typically solid at room temperature.

Saturated fat
Structure: No double bonds in fatty acid chains
Sources: Butter, cheese, red meat, coconut oil
Examples: Stearic acid, palmitic acid

Sources

Animal products — meat, butter, cheese, cream — and tropical oils such as coconut and palm oil are rich in saturated fatty acids like palmitic and stearic acid.

Health

Many health authorities recommend limiting saturated fat because it raises LDL cholesterol, a risk factor for cardiovascular disease. The strength and interpretation of evidence — and effects of replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fats versus carbohydrates — remain subjects of research and debate.
