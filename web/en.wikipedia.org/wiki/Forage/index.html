Forage

Forage is a plant material (mainly plant leaves and stems) eaten by grazing livestock. Historically, the term forage has meant only plants eaten by the animals directly as pasture, crop residue, or immature cereal crops, but it is also used more loosely to include similar plants cut for fodder and carried to the animals, especially as hay or silage.

Common forage plants

While the term forage has a broad definition, the term forage crop is used to define crops, annual or biennial, which are grown to be utilized by grazing or harvesting as a whole crop. Important forage species include grasses such as ryegrass, timothy, and fescue; legumes such as alfalfa (lucerne), clover, and vetch, which fix nitrogen and provide high protein content; and broadleaf plants and brassicas such as forage rape and kale. Maize, sorghum, and oats are also widely grown for silage.

Use and management

Forage forms the basis of ruminant livestock production worldwide. Farmers manage forage through rotational grazing, cutting schedules, and conservation as hay, silage, or haylage to maintain feed supply during winter or dry seasons. The nutritional quality of forage depends on plant species, stage of maturity at harvest, soil fertility, and preservation method. Healthy forage systems also support soil health, water retention, and biodiversity.
