The Rails-to-Trails Conservancy (RTC) is an American nonprofit organization based in Washington, D.C., that works with communities to preserve unused rail corridors by transforming them into trails for walking, bicycling, and other recreation. Founded in 1986, RTC supports the creation of rail trails across the United States and maintains TrailLink, a database of trails, and the Rail-Trail Hall of Fame.

Background

As railroads abandoned thousands of miles of track in the mid-twentieth century, advocates sought to preserve the corridors rather than see them broken up and sold off. The Rails-to-Trails Conservancy was formed to champion the "railbanking" provisions of the amended National Trails System Act, under which a corridor can be preserved for interim trail use while remaining available for possible future rail service. The U.S. Supreme Court upheld railbanking in 1990.

Work

RTC provides technical assistance, small grants, policy advocacy, and legal support to trail projects. It counts more than a million members and supporters and reports over 25,000 miles of rail trails in the United States. Its signature route initiative, the Great American Rail-Trail, announced in 2019, aims to assemble a coast-to-coast multi-use trail of more than 3,700 miles from Washington, D.C. to Washington state, using existing trails for most of the route.
