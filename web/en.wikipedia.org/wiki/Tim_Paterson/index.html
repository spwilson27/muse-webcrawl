Tim Paterson (born 1956) is an American computer programmer, best known for creating 86-DOS, an operating system for the Intel 8086 processor which Microsoft bought and developed into MS-DOS. His name also lent itself to a character in the book and musical retellings of early personal computing history, and he has sometimes been nicknamed "the father of DOS".

86-DOS

Working at Seattle Computer Products, Paterson developed 86-DOS in 1980 because Digital Research's CP/M-86 was delayed. Originally called QDOS (Quick and Dirty Operating System), it adopted an API modelled on CP/M so that programs could be translated easily. Microsoft licensed the system in 1980 to supply an operating system for IBM's new personal computer, then purchased it outright in 1981 for $50,000.

Later work

Paterson worked for Microsoft during the early 1980s on the IBM PC project and again later in the decade, and founded his own company, Falcon Technology. He also maintained a long-running interest in competitive rally racing of vintage hardware designs and early microcomputer boards.
