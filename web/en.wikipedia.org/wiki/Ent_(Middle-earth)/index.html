Ent

Ents are a fictional race in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth: ancient, tree-like beings who act as the shepherds of the trees and guardians of the great forests. The most prominent Ent in The Lord of the Rings is Treebeard (Fangorn), described as one of the oldest living creatures in Middle-earth. The name derives from the Old English ent, meaning "giant".

Characteristics

Ents resemble the trees they tend, varying in bark, leaf and form like different species; they are immensely strong, with skin like bark and the power to crush stone and metal. They are long-lived, deliberate and slow to anger. Their language, Old Entish, is slow, sonorous and highly descriptive, suited to a people for whom nothing is worth doing in a hurry; Ents consider other races "hasty". In time, many Ents grow increasingly "tree-ish", standing still for long periods and eventually ceasing to move or speak.

History in the legendarium

The Ents were awakened by the Elves in the earliest ages, after Yavanna, fearing for the trees once the Children of Ilúvatar began to cut wood, obtained from Eru the promise that spirits would dwell in the trees. In the First Age the Ents ranged widely through the great forests of Beleriand and Eriador. By the Third Age their numbers had dwindled, confined largely to Fangorn Forest. The Entwives, who loved gardens and agriculture, had departed east long before and vanished — their fate is one of the enduring mysteries of Tolkien's world, and without them the Ents have no young.

The Entmoot and the Last March

Provoked by Saruman's destruction of their trees, the Ents hold an Entmoot in The Two Towers and resolve, after three days of deliberation, to march on Isengard. In the Last March of the Ents they tear down Saruman's industrial stronghold and divert the river Isen to flood it — a powerful expression of Tolkien's environmental themes. Their wild cousins, the Huorns, take part in the destruction of Saruman's army at Helm's Deep.
