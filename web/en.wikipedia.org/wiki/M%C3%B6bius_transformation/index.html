Möbius transformation

In geometry and complex analysis, a Möbius transformation, named after August Ferdinand Möbius, is a rational function of the form

f(z) = (az + b) / (cz + d),

where z is a complex number and a, b, c, d are complex constants with ad − bc ≠ 0. Möbius transformations are defined on the Riemann sphere and are exactly its orientation-preserving conformal automorphisms.

Properties

Every Möbius transformation is a composition of translations, rotations and scalings, and inversion in the unit circle — the elementary transformations of inversive geometry. They map generalized circles (circles and lines) to generalized circles, preserve cross-ratios and angles, and are determined uniquely by the images of any three distinct points. Composition corresponds to multiplication of the associated 2×2 matrices, making the Möbius group isomorphic to the projective linear group PGL(2, ℂ).

Classification and applications

By their fixed points and trace, Möbius transformations are classified as elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic, or loxodromic. They act as the isometries of hyperbolic geometry in the Poincaré disc and half-plane models, and appear throughout complex analysis, differential geometry, and the theory of Fuchsian groups and Kleinian groups.
