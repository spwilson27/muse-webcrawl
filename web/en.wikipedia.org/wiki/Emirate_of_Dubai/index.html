Emirate of Dubai

The Emirate of Dubai (Arabic: إمارة دبيّ, ʾImārat Dubayy) is one of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates. It is the most populous emirate in the UAE, with its capital, the city of Dubai, being the largest city in the country. The emirate covers an area of approximately 4,114 square kilometres (1,588 sq mi).

History

The Al Maktoum family has ruled Dubai since 1833, when Maktoum bin Butti led his clan out of Abu Dhabi and settled on the banks of Dubai Creek. Dubai developed as a trading port, famous for its pearl industry in the early 20th century. Following the discovery of oil in 1966, the emirate underwent rapid development under Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, who invested oil revenues in ports, airports, and infrastructure.

On 2 December 1971, Dubai joined six other emirates to form the United Arab Emirates. Although only a small fraction of its revenues now come directly from oil, Dubai has diversified into trade, logistics, tourism, aviation, real estate, and financial services.

Geography

Dubai is located on the southeastern coast of the Persian Gulf. It borders Abu Dhabi to the south, Sharjah to the northeast, and the Sultanate of Oman to the southeast. The exclave of Hatta lies in the Hajar Mountains near the Omani border.

Government

The ruler of Dubai is traditionally also the Vice President and Prime Minister of the UAE. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum has ruled the emirate since 2006.
