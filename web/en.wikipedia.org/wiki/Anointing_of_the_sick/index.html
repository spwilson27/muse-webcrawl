Anointing of the sick

Anointing of the sick, known in the Roman Catholic Church also by earlier names such as extreme unction, is a sacrament (among Roman Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, and some other Christians) administered to give strength and comfort to the ill, historically mainly to the dying. It involves anointing the sick person with blessed oil and prayers for healing and forgiveness.

Biblical basis

The rite is based principally on James 5:14–15: "Is anyone among you sick? Let them call the elders of the church to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord." Mark 6:13 also describes the apostles anointing the sick with oil.

Practice

In the Roman Catholic Church, since the Second Vatican Council the sacrament is no longer reserved to those in immediate danger of death; it may be received by any seriously ill person, and may be repeated. The minister is a priest or bishop, who anoints the forehead and hands with the Oil of the Infirm. Eastern Orthodox churches administer the sacrament (Holy Unction) often communally, especially during Holy Week.

Effects

Traditionally the sacrament is held to unite the sick person with Christ's suffering, strengthen them against temptation, forgive sins, and sometimes restore bodily health.
