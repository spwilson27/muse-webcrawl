Nephthys or Nebet-Het in ancient Egyptian (Greek: Νέφθυς) was a goddess in ancient Egyptian religion. A member of the great Ennead of Heliopolis, she was the daughter of Geb and Nut, sister of Isis, Osiris and Set, and the wife of Set. Her name means "Mistress of the (Temple) Enclosure." Nephthys is associated with mourning, the night, lamentation, childbirth, the dead and protection.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, Nephthys helps her sister Isis to search for and reassemble the dismembered body of Osiris after his murder by Set. Together the two sisters mourn the dead god and are depicted as kites at the head and foot of his bier. Nephthys is one of the four goddesses — with Isis, Neith and Serqet — who protect the canopic shrine and the coffin of the dead. In some traditions she is the mother of Anubis, god of mummification, by Osiris.

Role and imagery

Nephthys is usually depicted as a woman wearing a headdress in the shape of her name's hieroglyphs: a basket (neb) on top of a house plan (het). As a funerary goddess she appears on coffins and in the Book of the Dead, standing behind the throne of Osiris in the judgement hall. Along with Isis she symbolizes the two halves of the cosmos — Isis representing birth and growth, Nephthys death and decay — both essential to the cycle of existence.
