William John Evans (August 16, 1929 – September 15, 1980) was an American jazz pianist and composer who worked primarily as the leader of his trio. His use of impressionist harmony, block chords, innovative chord voicings, and trademark rhythmically independent "singing" melodic lines influenced a generation of pianists.

Evans is best known for his association with Miles Davis, with whom he recorded the landmark modal jazz album Kind of Blue (1959), the best-selling jazz album of all time. In 1959, Evans left Davis's group to form his own trio with bassist Scott LaFaro and drummer Paul Motian; their 1961 live recordings at the Village Vanguard, Sunday at the Village Vanguard and Waltz for Debby, are widely regarded as masterpieces of trio interplay.

Early life

Evans was born in Plainfield, New Jersey, to a Welsh mother and a father of Russian ancestry. He began piano lessons at age six and also studied flute and violin. After graduating from Southeastern Louisiana College with a degree in piano performance, he served in the U.S. Army as a flutist in a military band, then studied composition at the Mannes School of Music in New York.

Career

Evans's debut album, New Jazz Conceptions (1956), was recorded for Riverside Records. In 1958 he joined Miles Davis's sextet, influencing Davis's move toward modal improvisation. After leaving Davis, Evans formed the trio with LaFaro and Motian, revolutionising the role of the bass in the piano trio from timekeeper to conversational partner. LaFaro's death in a car accident in July 1961 devastated Evans, who withdrew from performing for months.

He later worked with bassists Chuck Israels, Eddie Gómez and Marc Johnson, and recorded the Grammy-winning album Conversations with Myself (1963), which used overdubbing to create a three-piano dialogue. In total Evans won seven Grammy Awards and was nominated for thirty-one.

Style and legacy

Evans's harmonies drew on Debussy, Ravel and Chopin, and his "rootless" left-hand voicings became standard practice in modern jazz. His final trio, with Marc Johnson and Joe LaBarbera, is documented on the recordings later released as The Last Waltz. Evans died in 1980, aged 51, from complications of long-term drug use and hepatitis. He was inducted into the DownBeat Jazz Hall of Fame in 1981.
