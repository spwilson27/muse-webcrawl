Mojibake

Mojibake (Japanese: 文字化け, "character transformation") is the garbled text that results when text is decoded using an unintended character encoding, or when characters cannot be represented in the encoding used for display. The result is sequences of apparently random symbols — for example, curly quotes in UTF-8 rendered under Windows-1252 as â€™ — that replace the intended characters.

Causes

Mojibake arises chiefly when text encoded in one encoding (such as UTF-8, Shift JIS, or EUC-JP) is interpreted under another, or when software silently assumes a default locale encoding. Japanese text was historically especially vulnerable because of the coexistence of Shift JIS, EUC-JP and ISO-2022-JP. Double-encoding — re-encoding already-encoded bytes as if they were characters — produces compounding layers of garbage that are harder to repair.

Repair and prevention

Because many mis-decodings are lossless transformations of the original bytes, mojibake can often be reversed by re-interpreting the text with the correct encoding pair, and libraries exist that detect and undo common cases. Prevention relies on consistently labelling encodings, using Unicode (UTF-8) end-to-end, and avoiding implicit conversions. In Chinese contexts, similar phenomena give rise to "luàn mǎ" (乱码, "messy code"). Mojibake remains common in old emails, legacy databases, and misconfigured web pages.
