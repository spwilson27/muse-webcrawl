Apocalypse Now

Apocalypse Now is a 1979 American epic war film produced and directed by Francis Ford Coppola. It stars Martin Sheen, Marlon Brando, Robert Duvall and Dennis Hopper. Loosely inspired by Joseph Conrad's novella Heart of Darkness, the film transfers the story to the Vietnam War.

Plot

Captain Benjamin Willard, a troubled special-operations officer, is sent upriver into Cambodia to assassinate Colonel Walter Kurtz, a decorated Green Beret who has gone rogue and set himself up as a ruler among a local group. Travelling by patrol boat, Willard passes episodes that dramatise the surreal violence and moral collapse of the war, including an air cavalry assault led by Lieutenant Colonel Kilgore.

Production

The production in the Philippines became notorious for delays, cost overruns, a typhoon that destroyed sets, and the health problems of its stars. The film premiered at Cannes, where it shared the Palme d'Or. Coppola later released Apocalypse Now Redux (2001) with additional footage and Apocalypse Now Final Cut (2019).

Legacy

The film is widely regarded as one of the greatest ever made, noted for its sound design, cinematography by Vittorio Storaro and its use of music such as Wagner's “Ride of the Valkyries” and The Doors' “The End”.
