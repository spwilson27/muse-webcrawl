Carron Company

The Carron Company was an ironworks established in 1759 near Falkirk, Scotland, on the banks of the River Carron. It was one of the foremost ironworks of the Industrial Revolution, at its peak employing over 5,000 workers and becoming the largest ironworks in Europe.

History

The company was founded by John Roebuck, Samuel Garbett, and Cadell interests, and was an early adopter of coke smelting. Engineer John Smeaton supplied its blowing machinery, and the works cast cannon for the Royal Navy during the Seven Years' War and American Revolutionary War.

The carronade

Carron is best known for the carronade, a short, large-calibre naval gun introduced in the 1770s, nicknamed the "smasher" by sailors for its devastating short-range fire. The works also produced domestic iron goods — stoves, grates, and the iconic red pillar-boxes — many stamped with the company name.

Later years

The company survived into the 20th century, receiving a royal charter in 1773 that it retained for over two centuries. Carron Company finally went into receivership in 1982, though the brand name continued in use for baths and other products.
