Loweswater is a small lake in the western part of the Lake District in Cumbria, England. It is one of the smaller and less visited of the Lakeland waters, lying in a quiet valley south of Lorton and west of Crummock Water, beneath the fells of Mellbreak and the Loweswater Fells.

Geography

The lake is about 1.8 kilometres long and is unusual among the Lake District lakes in that its outflow drains toward the centre of the district: water leaves via Dub Beck, which becomes Park Beck, and flows into Crummock Water. The surrounding valley is farmland and woodland, including the National Trust-owned Holme Wood on the southern shore, noted for its sessile oaks and red squirrels.

The village

The parish and small village of Loweswater stand just north of the lake. The village church, St Bartholomew's, was rebuilt in 1829 on the site of an earlier chapel. The Kirkstile Inn, at the foot of Mellbreak, is a long-established walkers' inn dating in part to the 18th century.

Recreation

A lakeside path through Holme Wood is popular with walkers, and the lake is fished for brown trout, perch, and pike. Because of its sheltered, rural setting, Loweswater is often described as one of the most peaceful of the Cumbrian lakes.
