Edmund Spenser

Edmund Spenser
Born: c. 1552/53, London
Died: 13 January 1599
Occupation: Poet
Major work: The Faerie Queene

Edmund Spenser (c. 1552/53 – 1599) was an English poet best known for The Faerie Queene, an epic poem and fantastical allegory celebrating the Tudor dynasty and Elizabeth I. He is recognised as one of the premier craftsmen of Modern English verse and one of the greatest poets in English.

Life

Educated at Merchant Taylors' School and Cambridge, Spenser served in Ireland from 1580 as secretary to the Lord Deputy and acquired Kilcolman Castle in County Cork. His Irish experience informed his prose tract A View of the Present State of Ireland. His castle was burned in the Nine Years' War; he died in London in 1599 and was buried in Poets' Corner, Westminster Abbey.

Works

The Shepheardes Calender (1579) launched his career. The Faerie Queene (1590, 1596) uses the nine-line Spenserian stanza he invented; his sonnet sequence Amoretti and marriage poem Epithalamion also endure. See also Elizabethan literature.
