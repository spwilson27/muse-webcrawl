Lord's Prayer

The Lord's Prayer, also known by its Latin incipit Pater Noster, is a central Christian prayer that Jesus taught his disciples. Versions appear in the Gospel of Matthew (6:9–13) and, in shorter form, in the Gospel of Luke (11:2–4). Matthew's version, given during the Sermon on the Mount, is the one most commonly used in worship.

Text

A widely used traditional English rendering (from the Book of Common Prayer and the King James Version, including the concluding doxology) reads:

Our Father, which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done, in earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive them that trespass against us. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. For thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory, for ever and ever. Amen.

Structure and use

The prayer addresses God as Father, followed by petitions: three concerning God's glory (name, kingdom, will) and three concerning human needs (daily bread, forgiveness, deliverance). The final doxology ("For thine is the kingdom...") appears in later manuscripts and is used in most Protestant liturgies; Roman Catholic liturgy historically omitted it from the prayer itself, though it is now said separately in the Mass.

The Lord's Prayer is used in virtually every Christian tradition, in catechesis, daily devotion, and public worship, and has been set to music countless times. Its petitions parallel Jewish prayers such as the Kaddish.
