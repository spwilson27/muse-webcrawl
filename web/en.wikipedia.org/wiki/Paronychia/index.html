Paronychia

Acute paronychia of the index finger.

Paronychia is an inflammation of the skin folds surrounding a fingernail or toenail (the paronychium). It is one of the most common infections of the hand and occurs when bacteria, most often Staphylococcus aureus, enter through a break in the skin around the nail, such as a hangnail or damage from nail biting or manicuring.

Types

Acute paronychia develops over a few hours to days, presenting with pain, redness, swelling and sometimes a collection of pus beside the nail. Chronic paronychia persists for at least six weeks, is usually caused by irritants and moisture with secondary fungal involvement (often Candida), and is common in people whose hands are frequently wet, such as dishwashers, bartenders and healthcare workers — as well as in small children who suck their thumbs.

Treatment

Early acute paronychia is treated with warm-water soaks several times a day and, where needed, oral antibiotics; an abscess requires incision and drainage. Chronic paronychia is managed primarily by keeping the skin dry, avoiding irritants, and using topical steroids or antifungal agents; the offending habits or occupational exposures must be addressed to prevent recurrence.
