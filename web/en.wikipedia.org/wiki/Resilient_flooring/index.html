Resilient flooring

Resilient flooring is a category of floor coverings that are firm yet have some elasticity or "give" underfoot, sitting between hard flooring (such as stone, ceramic tile or concrete) and soft flooring (such as carpet). The main types are linoleum, vinyl flooring (sheet vinyl, vinyl tile and luxury vinyl tile/plank), rubber flooring, cork flooring and vinyl composition tile (VCT). Resilient floors are widely used in hospitals, schools, retail premises and homes because they are durable, water-resistant, comparatively warm and quiet, and easy to clean.

Types

Linoleum, invented in the 1860s, is made from natural materials — linseed oil, rosin, cork dust and jute backing. Vinyl flooring, based on polyvinyl chloride (PVC), became dominant after the Second World War and ranges from inexpensive vinyl composition tile to cushioned sheet and rigid-core luxury vinyl plank that imitates wood. Rubber flooring, used in gyms, transport and industrial settings, offers slip resistance and impact absorption; cork provides warmth and resilience but needs sealing against moisture.

Properties and installation

Resilient floors are typically glued down, click-locked as floating floors, or laid loose in sheet form with welded seams (important for hygiene in healthcare). Performance characteristics include indentation resistance, abrasion class, slip resistance and acoustic damping. Environmental and health debates have centred on plasticisers and emissions from PVC products, driving growth in linoleum, cork and PVC-free alternatives.
