Rhaeto-Romance languages

The Rhaeto-Romance languages are a proposed subfamily of the Romance languages spoken in the central and eastern Alps. The group traditionally comprises three languages: Romansh in the Swiss canton of Graubünden, Ladin in the Dolomites of northern Italy, and Friulian in the Italian region of Friuli.

Name and history

The name derives from the Roman province of Raetia, which covered much of the region. The grouping was proposed in the nineteenth century, notably by the Italian linguist Graziadio Isaia Ascoli in 1873. These languages descend from the Vulgar Latin spoken in the Romanised Alpine valleys and were once spread over a much larger area before retreating before German from the north and Italian from the south.

The unity question

Whether Rhaeto-Romance forms a genuine genetic subgroup — the so-called Questione Ladina — has been debated for over a century. Some linguists treat the shared features as the result of common retention from Latin and parallel development in isolated mountain valleys rather than descent from a single "Proto-Rhaeto-Romance," and classify each language separately within the Gallo-Romance or Italo-Romance branches.

Status today

Romansh is a national language of Switzerland, with about 60,000 speakers, and exists in five written regional varieties alongside the artificial standard Rumantsch Grischun. Friulian is the largest of the three, with several hundred thousand speakers in north-eastern Italy, and Ladin is spoken by roughly 30,000 people in the Dolomite valleys. All three are recognised and protected as minority languages, but all are under pressure from German and Italian.
