AbbVie

AbbVie Inc. is an American biopharmaceutical company headquartered in North Chicago, Illinois. It was spun off from Abbott Laboratories on 1 January 2013, taking Abbott's proprietary pharmaceuticals and biologics businesses, while Abbott retained medical devices, diagnostics, nutritionals and branded generics. The name AbbVie combines "Abb" (for Abbott) with "vie", from the Latin root vita, meaning life.

History

At the time of the spin-off, AbbVie's principal product was Humira (adalimumab), a TNF inhibitor for autoimmune diseases that had become the world's best-selling drug, generating roughly US$20 billion in annual revenue at its peak. Richard A. Gonzalez, who led the spin-off, served as chairman and CEO from the company's founding until 2024, when Robert A. Michael succeeded him as CEO.

AbbVie has grown through major acquisitions, notably Pharmacyclics in 2015 (US$21 billion), adding the blood-cancer drug Imbruvica (ibrutinib), and Allergan in 2020 (about US$63 billion), which brought Botox, the eye drug Restasis, and a large aesthetics portfolio. Later deals included ImmunoGen and Cerevel Therapeutics, announced in 2023, to expand in oncology and neuroscience.

Products

Key products include Humira; Skyrizi (risankizumab) for psoriasis and other immune conditions; Rinvoq (upadacitinib); the leukaemia drug Venclexta (venetoclax); Imbruvica; Botox (onabotulinumtoxinA) for therapeutic and cosmetic uses; and the endometriosis drug Orilissa. After US biosimilar competition to Humira began in 2023, Skyrizi and Rinvoq became the company's leading growth drivers.
