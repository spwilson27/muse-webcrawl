Bösendorfer

L. Bösendorfer Klavierfabrik GmbH is an Austrian piano manufacturer, founded in Vienna in 1828 by Ignaz Bösendorfer. One of the world's oldest piano makers, it is renowned for hand-built grand pianos with a characteristically warm, singing tone. Since 2008 it has been a wholly owned subsidiary of Yamaha Corporation.

Instruments

Bösendorfer is famous for extending the keyboard beyond the standard 88 keys. The Model 225 has 92 keys, and the flagship Model 290 "Imperial", commissioned in 1909 at the suggestion of Ferruccio Busoni, has 97 keys extending down to C0. The extra bass strings add resonance and depth to the instrument's sound. The company builds roughly a few hundred pianos a year, using a high proportion of spruce in its rim construction, which contributes to its distinctive tonal character.

History

Ignaz Bösendorfer took over the workshop of Joseph Brodmann in 1828. The pianos' reputation for durability was cemented when Franz Liszt, notorious for breaking instruments, could not destroy a Bösendorfer during his concerts. In 1839 Bösendorfer became the first Viennese piano maker appointed Imperial and Royal Court and Chamber Piano Manufacturer. The Bösendorfer-Saal, opened in 1872, was one of Vienna's beloved concert halls until its demolition in 1913. After passing through several owners in the 20th century, the firm was acquired by Yamaha in 2008, while production remained in Wiener Neustadt.
