Crab-eating raccoon

The crab-eating raccoon (Procyon cancrivorus) is a species of raccoon native to marshy and jungle areas of Central and South America, from Costa Rica south through most of northern South America to northern Argentina and Uruguay. It is one of three species in the genus Procyon, alongside the common raccoon and the Cozumel raccoon.

Description

The crab-eating raccoon resembles its northern relative but has shorter, coarser fur, which is an adaptation to its warmer climate and semi-aquatic habits. Adults measure about 54 to 76 cm in body length with a tail of 25 to 38 cm, and weigh roughly 3 to 8 kg. The fur is brownish above and paler below, with the characteristic dark facial mask of raccoons, though the mask is less strongly marked than in the common raccoon. Its hind feet are somewhat webbed, aiding swimming.

Behaviour and diet

It is a nocturnal, largely solitary animal that forages along streams, lagoons, swamps and coastal areas. As its name suggests, it feeds heavily on crabs, but it also eats lobsters, crayfish, frogs, fish, insects, small turtles, eggs and fruit. Like other raccoons, it has highly sensitive, dexterous forepaws and often appears to "wash" its food in water, a behaviour linked to its tactile sense of touch rather than to cleanliness.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, being widespread and fairly common, though it is hunted in some areas for its fur and meat and can be affected locally by wetland loss.
