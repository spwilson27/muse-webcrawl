Madagascar

Baobab trees at sunset on Madagascar's Avenue of the Baobabs

Madagascar is an island country in the Indian Ocean, off the southeastern coast of Africa, across the Mozambique Channel from Mozambique. Its main island is the fourth-largest island in the world, about 587,000 square kilometres, and the country has a population of roughly 30 million, with its capital at Antananarivo.

Madagascar separated from Africa and then India tens of millions of years ago, and its long isolation produced extraordinary endemism: about 90 percent of its wildlife is found nowhere else, including lemurs, fossas, chameleons and six species of baobab. The Malagasy people descend from a mix of Austronesian voyagers from Indonesia and Bantu migrants from Africa. Deforestation threatens much of the island's unique biodiversity.
