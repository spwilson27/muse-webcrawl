Phospholipase

A phospholipase is an enzyme that converts phospholipids into fatty acids and other lipophilic substances. Phospholipases are classified according to the bond they cleave: phospholipase A1 and A2 remove fatty acids from the glycerol backbone, while phospholipase B, C and D act at other sites, with PLC and PLD generating important signalling molecules such as diacylglycerol and inositol trisphosphate.

Phospholipases play roles in digestion (notably pancreatic phospholipase A2), cell signalling, inflammation — via release of arachidonic acid — and membrane remodelling. Some animal venoms contain phospholipases that damage cell membranes.
