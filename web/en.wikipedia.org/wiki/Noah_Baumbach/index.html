Noah Baumbach, American filmmaker.

Noah Baumbach (born 3 September 1969) is an American film director and screenwriter known for literate, dialogue-driven comedies and dramas about families, divorce, ambition and New York intellectual life. He has received multiple Academy Award nominations, including for the screenplays of The Squid and the Whale (2005) and Marriage Story (2019), and co-wrote the blockbuster Barbie (2023) with Greta Gerwig.

Early life and career

Baumbach was born in Brooklyn to writer Jonathan Baumbach and critic Georgia Brown, and educated at Vassar College. He made his debut with Kicking and Screaming (1995), a comedy about post-college drift. After the less successful Mr. Jealousy (1997), his semi-autobiographical The Squid and the Whale — about his parents' divorce — won major Sundance awards and an Oscar nomination, establishing his reputation.

Later work

Baumbach has frequently collaborated with Wes Anderson (co-writing The Life Aquatic with Steve Zissou and Fantastic Mr. Fox) and with Greta Gerwig, with whom he made Greenberg (2010), Frances Ha (2012) and Mistress America (2015). Other films include While We're Young (2014), The Meyerowitz Stories (2017) and the Don DeLillo adaptation White Noise (2022). Marriage Story, starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, was widely acclaimed and earned Laura Dern an Academy Award.
