The Elbląg Canal (Polish: Kanał Elbląski; German: Oberländischer Kanal) is a canal in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship of northern Poland. It runs 80.5 kilometres (50.0 mi) from Lake Drużno, near the town of Elbląg, south to Lake Jeziorak near Iława and Ostróda. The canal is notable for its system of inclined planes, on which boats are carried overland on rail-mounted cradles to overcome a height difference of nearly 100 metres (330 ft) between lakes — a solution unique in the world in this form and still in working order.

History

The canal was designed by the Prussian engineer Georg Jacob Steenke to connect the ports of East Prussia with the Vistula Lagoon and the Baltic Sea, allowing timber, agricultural produce and other goods from the Oberland (Upper Land) region to reach Elbing (Elbląg) without relying on the Vistula, then partly under foreign control. Construction began in 1844 and the canal opened in stages between 1860 and 1861. Steenke studied inclined-plane systems in the United States — in particular the Morris Canal in New Jersey — before adapting the principle with water-powered machinery.

Inclined planes

Five inclined planes (Buczyniec, Kąty, Oleśnica, Jelenie and Całuny) carry vessels across dry land between water levels. Each ship is floated into a submerged cradle on rails; the cradle is then hauled up or down the slope by cables. The machinery is driven by waterwheels fed by the canal's own water, making the system largely self-powered. The planes overcome a total elevation difference of about 99.5 metres (326 ft), which would otherwise have required several dozen conventional locks.

The canal today

After decades of decline, the canal was restored and, following a major renovation completed in 2015, reopened to navigation. Passenger boats operating between Elbląg and Buczyniec, and between Ostróda and Miłomłyn, are a major tourist attraction of the region, often described as sailing "on water and on grass". The canal is listed as a historic monument and is regarded as one of the most remarkable feats of 19th-century hydraulic engineering in Europe.
