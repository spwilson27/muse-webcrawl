Enfield Town

Enfield market square and St Andrew's Church

Enfield Town, also known simply as Enfield, is a town in north London, England, and the historic centre of the London Borough of Enfield. It lies about 10 miles (16 km) north of central London, near the River Lea valley. Once a small market town in the county of Middlesex, it grew substantially with suburban development in the late 19th and 20th centuries.

History

Enfield is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Enefelde. It received a market charter in 1303 and developed as a market town serving the surrounding countryside; Enfield Market still operates in the town centre square. The parish church of St Andrew dates substantially from the 14th and 15th centuries. The royal palace of Elsyng once stood nearby, and Enfield Chase — a royal hunting ground — covered much of the surrounding area until its enclosure in the late 18th century; the New River, built in the early 17th century to supply London with water, passes through the locality.

The arrival of the railway in 1849 (Enfield Town station is a terminus of a branch from Liverpool Street) converted Enfield into a commuter suburb. The town also gave its name to the famous Lee–Enfield rifle, produced at the Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield Lock, and to Enfield Town F.C., a supporter-owned football club founded in 2001 after a dispute over the future of the original Enfield F.C.

Transport and features

Besides Enfield Town station (London Overground), the area is served by Enfield Chase station on the Hertford Loop line and by numerous bus routes. Local landmarks include the Enfield Town Library, the Palace Gardens shopping centre, Forty Hall (a 17th-century manor house to the north), and Gentleman's Row, a row of historic houses along the New River.
