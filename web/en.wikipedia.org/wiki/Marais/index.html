Le Marais ("The Marsh") is a historic district in Paris, France, spread across parts of the 3rd and 4th arrondissements on the Right Bank of the Seine. Once marshland, it was developed from the 13th century onward and became the favoured quarter of the French nobility in the 16th and 17th centuries. It preserves many grand hôtels particuliers (mansions) and contains the Place des Vosges, the city's oldest planned square, inaugurated in 1612.

History

After the aristocracy moved to Versailles and the Faubourg Saint-Germain, the Marais declined and filled with workshops and a dense working population. Having largely escaped Baron Haussmann's demolitions, it retained its pre-industrial street plan. In 1962 André Malraux's heritage law protected the district, and extensive restoration followed. The Marais is home to Paris's oldest Jewish community, centred on the Rue des Rosiers, and since the 1980s has also been the centre of the city's gay community, as well as a fashionable area of galleries, boutiques, and museums.

Landmarks

  • Place des Vosges, with the Maison de Victor Hugo.
  • Musée Picasso, Musée Carnavalet (museum of Paris history), and the Musée de l'Histoire de France in the Hôtel de Soubise.
  • Hôtel de Sully and Hôtel de Sens.
  • The Pletzl, the historic Jewish quarter around Rue des Rosiers.
  • Memorial de la Shoah and the Mémorial des Martyrs de la Déportation nearby.
