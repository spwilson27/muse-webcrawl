Customs

Customs is an authority or agency in a country responsible for collecting tariffs and for controlling the flow of goods, including animals, transports, personal effects, and hazardous items, into and out of a country. Traditionally, customs has been considered the fiscal subject that charges customs duties (tariffs) and other taxes on import and export; in recent decades its role has broadened to include security, trade facilitation, and enforcement of prohibitions and restrictions.

Functions

  • Assessing and collecting customs duties, excise and import VAT/GST.
  • Enforcing import and export prohibitions — narcotics, weapons, counterfeit goods, endangered species.
  • Applying rules of origin and trade preferences under free-trade agreements.
  • Collecting trade statistics and protecting domestic industry.
  • Border security screening of cargo and travellers' baggage.

Procedures

Goods crossing a border are normally declared on a customs declaration giving their classification under the Harmonized System, their customs value, and their origin. Most declarations are processed electronically with risk-based selectivity: low-risk consignments are cleared automatically ("green channel"), while others are subject to documentary checks or physical examination. Travellers typically pass through red and green channels at airports, declaring goods above duty-free allowances.

History

Duties on trade are ancient: portoria were levied in the Roman world, and medieval states charged tolls at ports, bridges, and city gates. Modern customs administrations developed alongside the nation-state and the growth of world trade; the World Customs Organization, founded in 1952, coordinates practices among its members.
