Rabbit

Kingdom: Animalia
Order: Lagomorpha
Family: Leporidae
Genera: Pentalagus, Bunolagus, Nesolagus, Romerolagus, Brachylagus, Sylvilagus, Oryctolagus, Poelagus

Rabbits are small mammals in the family Leporidae (which also includes the hares), which is in the order Lagomorpha (which also includes pikas). The European rabbit, Oryctolagus cuniculus, is the ancestor of the world's hundreds of breeds of domestic rabbit.

Biology

Rabbits are herbivores with long ears, large hind legs adapted for jumping, and short fluffy tails. They have four incisors in the upper jaw, including two small "peg teeth" behind the main pair, which distinguishes lagomorphs from rodents. Rabbits digest food in two steps — they produce soft caecal pellets (caecotrophs) that are re-ingested to absorb nutrients, a behaviour called caecotrophy. Male rabbits are called bucks, females does, and young kits.

Habitat and behaviour

Wild rabbits are native to Europe and northwestern Africa and have been introduced worldwide, sometimes destructively, as in Australia. Many species live in burrow systems called warrens and are social animals. Rabbits are prey animals and are generally crepuscular, most active at dawn and dusk.

Domestication and uses

Domestic rabbits are kept as pets and raised for meat, fur (including angora wool), and laboratory research. Rabbits feature prominently in folklore and popular culture, from Br'er Rabbit and the Easter Bunny to Bugs Bunny.
