Chain of custody

The chain of custody, in legal contexts, is the chronological documentation or paper trail that records the sequence of custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of physical or electronic evidence. It applies to the whole life cycle of a piece of evidence, from its collection at a crime scene or other source through its presentation in court and final disposal.

[Image: A sealed evidence bag with a chain of custody label]

Purpose

A documented chain of custody demonstrates that evidence presented in court is the same evidence that was originally collected and that it has not been tampered with, substituted, or contaminated. Gaps or inconsistencies in the chain may lead a court to exclude the evidence or to give it less weight, and attacks on the chain of custody are a standard defence strategy.

Procedure

Typical procedures include packaging and sealing evidence in tamper-evident containers, labelling, logging each transfer with dates, times and signatures, storing items in secure evidence rooms with restricted access, and maintaining equivalent audit trails for digital evidence, often supplemented by cryptographic hashes to detect alteration. The concept is applied beyond criminal law in fields such as forensic science, drug testing, food safety, and the handling of valuable goods and documents.
