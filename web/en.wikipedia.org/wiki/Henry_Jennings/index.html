Henry Jennings

Henry Jennings (died 1745) was an English privateer-turned-pirate who operated in the Caribbean during the early eighteenth century, in the post–Spanish Succession "Golden Age of Piracy". Based in Jamaica and later associated with the pirate haven at Nassau in the Bahamas, he is remembered for raiding the salvage camps of the sunken 1715 Spanish treasure fleet.

Career

Jennings first appears as a privateer captain commissioned by the governor of Jamaica during the War of the Spanish Succession. In early 1716, holding a privateering commission against the Spanish, he attacked the Florida salvage camps where divers were recovering silver from the wrecked 1715 fleet, seizing some 87,000 pieces of eight — an act that shaded into outright piracy. He later commanded the sloop Barsheba and mentored the young Charles Vane.

Retirement

Jennings accepted the King's pardon offered by Governor Woodes Rogers in 1718, retired to Bermuda as a wealthy and respectable planter, and is said to have died there in 1745 — a rare pirate captain who left the trade rich and died in his bed.
