Romanian language

Romanian (obsolete spelling: Roumanian; endonym: limba română) is an Eastern Romance language spoken by approximately 22–26 million people as a native language, primarily in Romania and Moldova. It is the official language of both countries and one of the official languages of the European Union.

Classification

Romanian belongs to the Eastern Romance branch of the Romance languages, descended from the Vulgar Latin spoken in the Roman provinces north of the Jireček Line. Its closest relatives are Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian and Istro-Romanian, sometimes treated together with Romanian as a single language with four dialects. Romanian is the only Eastern Romance language still widely spoken, and it preserves several features of Latin lost elsewhere, including a vestigial case system and a neuter gender.

History

The language formed between the 5th and 8th centuries AD. The oldest surviving text in Romanian is Neacșu's letter of 1521, written in Cyrillic, which remained the usual script until the 19th century. In the 18th and 19th centuries the language was modernized, adopting large numbers of French and Italian loanwords; earlier layers of borrowing include Slavic, Hungarian, Turkish and Greek vocabulary, reflecting the region's history.

Grammar

Romanian nouns have three genders (masculine, feminine and neuter — the last behaving as masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural) and inflect for two cases in addition to the vocative. The definite article is suffixed to the noun, as in other languages of the Balkan sprachbund, e.g. om "man", omul "the man". Verbs are conjugated in synthetic forms inherited from Latin, with a subjunctive widely used where other Romance languages prefer infinitives.

Vocabulary

Studies of the core vocabulary show that most of the fundamental lexicon is Latin in origin, with Slavic loans forming the largest later stratum and French loans dominating the modern, abstract vocabulary.
