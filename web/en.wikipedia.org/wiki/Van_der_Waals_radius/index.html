Van der Waals radius

The van der Waals radius, rw, of an atom is the radius of an imaginary hard sphere representing the distance of closest approach for another atom. It is named after Johannes Diderik van der Waals, winner of the 1910 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Determination

Van der Waals radii may be determined from the mechanical properties of gases, from critical points, from measurements of atomic spacing between pairs of unbonded atoms in crystals, or from optical and electrical properties. In practice, the radius is typically estimated as half the minimum separation between the nuclei of two identical non-bonded atoms.

Values

Representative values (in picometres, after Bondi, 1964) include hydrogen 120, carbon 170, nitrogen 155, oxygen 152, fluorine 147, chlorine 175 and sulfur 180. Van der Waals radii are generally larger than covalent radii, and they are widely used in molecular modelling, crystallography and the concept of van der Waals volume and surfaces.
