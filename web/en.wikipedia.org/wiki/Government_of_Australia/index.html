The Government of Australia is the federal government of the Commonwealth of Australia, a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. It consists of three branches — the executive, the legislature, and the judiciary — established by the Constitution of Australia, which came into force on 1 January 1901 at Federation.

Executive

Executive power is formally vested in the monarch and exercisable by the Governor-General as the monarch's representative, but by convention it is exercised by the Prime Minister and Cabinet, who must command the confidence of the House of Representatives. The Federal Executive Council advises the Governor-General.

Legislature

The Parliament of Australia is bicameral: the Senate (76 senators, 12 per state and 2 per territory) and the House of Representatives (151 members elected from single-member divisions). Both houses are directly elected, the Senate by proportional representation and the House by preferential voting.

Judiciary

The High Court of Australia is the apex court, with state and federal courts below it. The judiciary is independent of the other branches, a separation reinforced by Chapter III of the Constitution.

Federal structure

Australia's six states retain their own constitutions, parliaments, and governments; legislative powers are divided between the Commonwealth and the states, with certain powers exclusive to the Commonwealth and many exercised concurrently.
