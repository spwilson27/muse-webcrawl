The Israelites were a confederation of Iron Age Semitic-speaking tribes of the ancient Near East, who inhabited a part of Canaan during the tribal and monarchic periods. In the Hebrew Bible they are presented as the descendants of the patriarch Jacob — renamed Israel — organised into twelve tribes, who were led out of slavery in Egypt in the Exodus, received the Law at Mount Sinai, and settled in the Promised Land.

Archaeological picture

The earliest extrabiblical reference to "Israel" is the Egyptian Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE), which names Israel as a people in Canaan. Modern scholarship generally holds that the Israelites emerged largely indigenously from within Canaanite society in the hill country of the southern Levant during the collapse of the Late Bronze Age, rather than arriving as an external conquering group; hundreds of new small villages appear in the highlands in Iron Age I, with material culture continuous with Canaanite predecessors but marked by distinctive features such as four-room houses and an absence of pig bones.

The monarchies

According to the biblical account, the tribes united under kings — Saul, David and Solomon — before splitting into the northern Kingdom of Israel (capital Samaria) and the southern Kingdom of Judah (capital Jerusalem). The northern kingdom was destroyed by the Neo-Assyrian Empire in 722 BCE and much of its population deported, giving rise to the tradition of the "Ten Lost Tribes". Judah survived until Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon captured Jerusalem in 586 BCE and exiled its elites. After the Persian conquest of Babylon, Cyrus the Great permitted exiles to return and rebuild the Temple (completed 515 BCE); from this period the term "Jews" (Yehudim), originally denoting Judahites, becomes the common name for the people.

Religion

Early Israelite religion worshipped Yahweh alongside, in some strata, other deities of the Canaanite pantheon; over the monarchic and exilic periods it developed into an exclusive monotheism centred on Yahweh, the Jerusalem Temple, and the Torah. Samaritans, who survive today in small numbers, trace their descent to Israelites of the northern tribes and regard Mount Gerizim, not Jerusalem, as the chosen sanctuary.
