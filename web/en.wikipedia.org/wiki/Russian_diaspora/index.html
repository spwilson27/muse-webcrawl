Russian diaspora

The Russian diaspora comprises the global community of ethnic Russians and Russian speakers living outside Russia, including both voluntary emigrants and ethnic Russians who found themselves outside the Russian Federation's borders after the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991. Large communities exist in the former Soviet republics — notably Kazakhstan, Ukraine, Latvia, and Estonia — as well as in the United States, Israel, Germany, and Canada.

Waves of emigration

Russian emigration is often described in waves. The "White emigration" followed the Russian Revolution and Civil War (1917–1922), scattering exiles to Paris, Berlin, Harbin, and New York. Soviet-era emigration was tightly restricted, though significant numbers of Russian Jews and others left in the 1970s and again during perestroika. Economic and political emigration followed the collapse of the USSR in the 1990s, and a further large outflow — often called a fifth wave — followed Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, with hundreds of thousands departing for countries such as Georgia, Kazakhstan, Turkey, and the European Union.

Demographics

Estimates of the worldwide Russian diaspora vary widely, from around 20 to 30 million people depending on definitions. The Russian government has pursued policies toward "compatriots abroad", and the status of Russian-speaking minorities in the Baltic states and elsewhere remains a recurring theme in Russia's foreign relations.
