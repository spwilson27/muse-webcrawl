V for Vendetta


Author: Alan Moore
Illustrator: David Lloyd
Publisher: Vertigo (DC Comics)
Publication date: 1982–1989 (serialised)

V for Vendetta is a British graphic novel written by Alan Moore and illustrated by David Lloyd (with additional art by Tony Weare). Initially published between 1982 and 1985 in black and white as an ongoing serial in the short-lived British anthology Warrior, the story was completed and published by DC Comics in 1988–89.

Plot

Set in a dystopian future United Kingdom ruled by the fascist Norsefire party after a limited nuclear war, the story follows V, an anarchist revolutionary dressed in a Guy Fawkes mask, who plots to bring down the government. V rescues Evey Hammond, a young woman, from the secret police; she becomes his protégée and gradually uncovers his past as a victim of the Larkhill resettlement camp, where medical experiments gave him enhanced abilities but destroyed his sanity.

Themes

The work explores fascism, anarchism, surveillance and the power of ideas, encapsulated in V's assertion that "ideas are bulletproof." Moore wrote the series partly in reaction to Thatcherism in 1980s Britain.

Adaptations

A film adaptation directed by James McTeigue and written by the Wachowskis was released in 2005, starring Hugo Weaving as V and Natalie Portman as Evey. Sales of Guy Fawkes masks surged after the film, and the mask was later adopted by the hacktivist group Anonymous and the Occupy movement.
