Kaloyan (Bulgarian: Калоян; c. 1170 – October 1207), also known as Ioannitsa or Johannitsa, was a Bulgarian tsar (emperor) who ruled from 1197 until his death in 1207. He was the younger brother of the Asen brothers, Peter and Ivan Asen, who had led the uprising that restored the Bulgarian Empire in 1185. Kaloyan expanded and consolidated the Second Bulgarian Empire, winning recognition of his royal title from Pope Innocent III and inflicting a famous defeat on the Latin Emperor Baldwin I at the Battle of Adrianople in 1205.

Early life

As a youth, Kaloyan was sent as a hostage to Constantinople after a peace agreement with the Byzantine Empire, but he escaped and returned to Bulgaria. After the assassinations of his brothers Ivan Asen I (1196) and Peter II (1197), he ascended the throne in Tarnovo.

Reign

Kaloyan campaigned successfully against the Byzantines, capturing Varna in 1201 and extending Bulgarian rule in Macedonia. Seeking international legitimacy, he entered into negotiations with Pope Innocent III; in November 1204 a papal legate crowned him "King of the Bulgarians and Vlachs" (rex Bulgarorum et Blachorum), and his cousin was recognised as primate of the Bulgarian church, in a union with Rome that proved short-lived.

War with the Latins

After the Fourth Crusade created the Latin Empire of Constantinople, the Greek nobility of Thrace offered Kaloyan an alliance. On 14 April 1205, at the Battle of Adrianople, Bulgarian and Cuman forces crushed the Crusader army and captured Emperor Baldwin I, who died in captivity in Tarnovo. The victory checked Latin expansion in the Balkans. Byzantine sources hostile to him called him Skyloioannes ("John the Dog") and, for his treatment of captured Byzantines, "the Romanslayer" (Romaioktonos), a name said to have been embraced in reprisal for Basil II's treatment of Bulgarians.

Death

Kaloyan died in October 1207 while besieging Thessalonica — according to accounts, either from pleurisy or by assassination. He was succeeded by his nephew Boril.
