Yusuf (surah)

Chapter: 12 of the Quran
Verses: 111
Revelation: Meccan

Yusuf (Arabic: يوسف, Yūsuf, 'Joseph') is the twelfth chapter (surah) of the Quran, containing 111 verses (ayat). It is unique among the chapters in telling a single continuous story — the life of the prophet Yusuf (Joseph, son of Jacob) — from beginning to end. The surah itself calls it "the best of stories" (aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ).

Summary

Revealed in Mecca, traditionally said to be during a year of sorrow for the Prophet Muhammad, the chapter recounts Joseph's dream of eleven stars and the sun and moon prostrating to him; his brothers' jealousy and their casting him into a well; his sale into slavery in Egypt; his resistance to the advances of his master's wife and subsequent imprisonment; his interpretation of the king's dream of seven fat and seven lean cows; his rise to power over Egypt's storehouses; and finally the family's reunion and reconciliation in Egypt, fulfilling his childhood dream.

Significance

The Joseph story became one of the most beloved narratives in Islamic literature, retold in Persian, Turkish and Urdu poetry — most famously in Jami's Yusuf and Zulaikha — and in countless popular versions. The surah is read for themes of patience, chastity, trust in God and forgiveness, exemplified by Joseph's words to his brothers: "No blame rests on you this day."
