Twelve Years a Slave is an 1853 memoir and slave narrative by American Solomon Northup, as told to and edited by David Wilson. Northup, a free Black man living in New York, was kidnapped in Washington, D.C. in 1841 and sold into slavery in Louisiana, where he worked on plantations for twelve years before regaining his freedom in 1853. The book gives a detailed first-hand account of the domestic slave trade and plantation life along the Red River.

Synopsis

Lured to Washington by a false offer of musical work, Northup was drugged, beaten, and shipped to New Orleans, where he was sold under the name "Platt". He served masters including the relatively kind William Ford and the brutal Edwin Epps, on whose cotton plantation he spent ten years. After confiding in Samuel Bass, a Canadian carpenter opposed to slavery, letters reached Northup's family and New York officials; in January 1853 he was legally freed. His kidnappers were tried but acquitted.

Publication and reception

Published in 1853, months after Northup's liberation, the narrative sold some 30,000 copies and became a bestseller of abolitionist literature, frequently cited alongside Harriet Beecher Stowe's Uncle Tom's Cabin (to which it was dedicated). It fell out of print for a century before scholarly editions in 1968 re-established its accuracy and importance.

Adaptations

The book was adapted as the television film Solomon Northup's Odyssey (1984) and as Steve McQueen's film 12 Years a Slave (2013), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.
