Pambo

Pambo (c. 304–373) was an Egyptian monk and one of the Desert Fathers of early Christian monasticism. A disciple of Anthony the Great, he became a leading figure of the monastic colony at Nitria in the Egyptian desert and is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Roman Catholic traditions. His feast day is July 18 in the Coptic tradition.

Life

Pambo withdrew to the desert of Nitria, west of the Nile Delta, where he became a disciple of Anthony the Great. He was ordained a priest by Patriarch Athanasius of Alexandria and became abbot of a community of monks at Nitria. Among his own disciples were several later bishops and saints, including Pishoy and John the Dwarf. Pambo was noted for his asceticism, silence, and wisdom; many of his sayings are preserved in the Apophthegmata Patrum (Sayings of the Desert Fathers) and in the works of Palladius and other early monastic historians.

He is remembered for acts of charity and humility: when given gold by a wealthy visitor (by tradition, Melania the Elder), he distributed it among poor monasteries rather than keeping it for his own community. He died around 373 while weaving baskets, reportedly remarking that he did not recall ever eating bread he had not earned by the work of his hands.

Sayings

Among the sayings attributed to Pambo are reflections on guarding the tongue, avoiding judgment of others, and the value of silence. To a monk distressed by praise, he is said to have advised that one should not live for the approval of people but of God.
