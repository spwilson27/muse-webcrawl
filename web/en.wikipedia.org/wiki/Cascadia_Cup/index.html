The Cascadia Cup is the name of the trophy created in 2004 by the supporters of the Portland Timbers, Seattle Sounders FC, and Vancouver Whitecaps FC of North American soccer. It is awarded each season to the team that finishes with the best record in matches played between the three clubs, whose regional rivalry — the Cascadia rivalry — dates back to the North American Soccer League era in 1975 and is considered one of the most intense in North American soccer.

History

The three cities' teams first met in the original NASL in the mid-1970s, and the rivalry continued through the USL First Division years. In 2004 the supporters' groups — the Timbers Army, Emerald City Supporters, and Vancouver Southsiders — jointly created the Cascadia Cup. When Seattle joined Major League Soccer in 2009, followed by Vancouver and Portland in 2011, the cup continued at the MLS level, administered by the Cascadia Cup Council representing the supporters' groups. The cup competition is decided on points earned in regular-season league matches between the three teams, with tiebreakers such as head-to-head results and goal difference.

Culture

Cascadia matches are known for elaborate tifo displays, large travelling supporter contingents, and some of the highest attendances in MLS. The rivalry also carries a regional identity dimension, reflecting the shared Cascadia bioregion of the Pacific Northwest spanning Oregon, Washington, and British Columbia.
