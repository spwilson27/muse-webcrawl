Khitan (circumcision)

Khitān (Arabic: ختان) is the Islamic rite of male circumcision. Although not explicitly mentioned in the Quran, circumcision is regarded in Islamic tradition as part of the fitra, the natural practices associated with the prophets, and is widely practised among Muslims.

Practice

The age at which khitan is performed varies by region and family custom, from infancy to around puberty. In many communities the procedure is accompanied by celebrations. In Southeast Asia, ceremonies such as the Indonesian sunatan and the Philippine tuli mark the event as a rite of passage.

Views

Most Islamic schools of jurisprudence regard circumcision as recommended (sunna) or obligatory (wajib) for males. The practice predates Islam and is shared with Judaism and various traditional cultures.
