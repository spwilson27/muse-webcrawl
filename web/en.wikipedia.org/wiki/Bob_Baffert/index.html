Robert A. Baffert (born January 13, 1953) is an American racehorse trainer who trained the Triple Crown winners American Pharoah (2015) and Justify (2018). He is one of the most successful and recognisable trainers in the sport, distinguished by his white hair and record in the Kentucky Derby, which he has won six times. His career has also been marked by controversy over medication violations by his horses.

Early life and quarter horses

Baffert grew up on a cattle ranch in Nogales, Arizona, where his family raced Quarter Horses. He worked as a jockey, then trained Quarter Horses in Arizona and California, winning major futurities at Los Alamitos before switching to Thoroughbreds in the late 1980s. His breakthrough came with Thirty Slews, who won the 1992 Breeders' Cup Sprint.

Triple Crown campaigns

Baffert won his first Kentucky Derby with Silver Charm in 1997 and again with Real Quiet in 1998; both horses won the Preakness but lost the Belmont, Real Quiet by a nose. War Emblem won the Derby and Preakness in 2002. In 2015, American Pharoah swept the Derby, Preakness and Belmont to become the first Triple Crown winner in 37 years, and added the Breeders' Cup Classic. Justify repeated the sweep in 2018, retiring undefeated. Baffert won a record-equalling sixth Derby with Authentic in 2020.

Controversies

Several Baffert-trained horses failed post-race drug tests over his career. Most prominently, Medina Spirit, first past the post in the 2021 Kentucky Derby, tested positive for betamethasone and was ultimately disqualified, leading Churchill Downs to suspend Baffert (initially for two years, later extended through 2024). Baffert returned to the Kentucky Derby in 2025. His critics and defenders remain sharply divided over whether the violations reflected deliberate doping or environmental/therapeutic contamination.
