Obsidian

Obsidian is a naturally occurring volcanic glass formed when lava from a volcano cools so rapidly that atoms cannot arrange into a crystalline structure — an extreme case related to nucleation being suppressed. It is usually black or dark green, hard and brittle, with conchoidal fracture.

Formation and properties

Obsidian forms in rhyolitic lava flows with high silica and low water, cooling at flow margins. Varieties include snowflake, mahogany and rainbow obsidian, and Apache tears. Over geological time it devitrifies to perlite.

Historical use

Valued since the Stone Age for extremely sharp blades and arrowheads, obsidian was widely traded in Mesoamerica, the Mediterranean and the Near East; sourcing by trace elements maps ancient trade networks. Today it is used for surgical scalpels, jewellery and ornament.
