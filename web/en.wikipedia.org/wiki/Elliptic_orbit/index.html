Elliptic orbit

In astrodynamics or celestial mechanics, an elliptic orbit or elliptical orbit is a Kepler orbit with an eccentricity of less than 1; this includes the special case of a circular orbit, with eccentricity equal to 0. In a stricter sense, it is a Kepler orbit with eccentricity greater than 0 and less than 1, excluding a circular orbit.

Properties

The orbiting body moves along an ellipse with the central body at one focus. The point of closest approach is the periapsis, and the farthest point is the apoapsis. The speed of the body varies over the orbit, reaching its maximum at periapsis and its minimum at apoapsis, in accordance with Kepler's second law.

Orbital period

The orbital period of an elliptic orbit depends only on the semi-major axis, as given by Kepler's third law: the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

Examples

Most objects in orbit around the Sun follow elliptic orbits, including the planets. Highly eccentric comets travel in very elongated ellipses. Artificial satellites in geostationary transfer orbits and Molniya orbits also follow elliptic paths.
