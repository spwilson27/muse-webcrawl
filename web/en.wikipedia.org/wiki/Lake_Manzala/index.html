Lake Manzala (Arabic: بحيرة المنزلة) is a brackish coastal lagoon in the northeastern Nile Delta of Egypt, lying between the Damietta branch of the Nile and the Suez Canal, near the city of Port Said. It is the largest of the northern delta lakes of Egypt, though it has shrunk considerably from its historical extent through land reclamation.

Geography and economy

The lake is shallow — generally about a metre deep — and is separated from the Mediterranean Sea by a narrow sandy barrier through which several outlets connect it to the sea. It receives agricultural drainage water carrying nutrients and pollutants, including effluent from the Bahr el-Baqar drain, making water quality a persistent problem. Despite pollution, Lake Manzala has traditionally been Egypt's most productive inland fishery, supplying a large share of the country's fish catch, particularly tilapia and mullet, and supporting communities of fishermen around towns such as El Matareya.

Environment

The lake's marshes and open waters are an important habitat for migratory and wintering waterbirds on the African-Eurasian flyway. Egyptian government programmes have aimed at dredging, improving water exchange with the sea, and reducing pollution to restore the fishery and ecosystem.
