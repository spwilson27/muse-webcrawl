Apocalypse
FromGreek apokálypsis, "uncovering, revelation"
GenreApocalyptic literature; eschatology

An apocalypse (from Ancient Greek ἀποκάλυψις, "an uncovering" or "revelation") is a disclosure of knowledge or revelation, and in religious usage a literary genre in which a heavenly being reveals hidden truths and the coming end of the present world order to a human visionary. In modern usage the word has come to mean the end of the world itself, or any catastrophic event of world-ending scale.

Apocalyptic literature

The genre flourished in Second Temple Judaism and early Christianity. Its best-known examples are the Book of Daniel and the New Testament's Book of Revelation — traditionally called "the Apocalypse of John" — alongside works such as 1 Enoch, 4 Ezra and the Apocalypse of Abraham. Apocalypses typically feature symbolic visions, angelic interpreters, a sharp dualism between good and evil, and the expectation of divine judgement and cosmic renewal.

In wider usage

Related end-time doctrines appear in many traditions, including Ragnarök in Norse mythology and eschatologies in Islam, Hinduism and Buddhism. In secular and popular culture, "apocalypse" now describes scenarios such as nuclear war, pandemic, climate catastrophe or impact events, and post-apocalyptic settings are a staple of fiction, film and games.
