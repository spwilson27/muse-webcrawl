Hijikata Toshizō

Hijikata Toshizō (土方 歳三, May 31, 1835 – June 20, 1869) was a Japanese warrior of the late Edo period and the vice-commander of the Shinsengumi, the special police force that defended the Tokugawa shogunate in Kyoto during the Bakumatsu. Known for his strict discipline, he was nicknamed the "Demon Vice-Commander" (Oni no fukuchō).

Early life

Hijikata was born in Hino, a farming village west of Edo, the youngest son of a prosperous farmer. He worked as an apprentice and medicine peddler while practicing swordsmanship, eventually joining the Tennen Rishin-ryū school of Kondō Isami, alongside whom he would lead the Shinsengumi.

Shinsengumi

Formed in 1863, the Shinsengumi policed Kyoto against pro-imperial loyalists; Hijikata authored its severe internal code, which prescribed death for desertion or disgrace. He fought in the Ikedaya incident (1864) and rose as the corps' true organizer and second-in-command. A famous 1868 photograph shows him in Western dress during the final campaigns.

Death

After the shogunate fell in the Boshin War, Hijikata remained loyal, retreating north with Enomoto Takeaki's forces to the Republic of Ezo in Hokkaido. He was shot and killed on June 20, 1869, during the Battle of Hakodate, one of the last samurai to die fighting for the old order. He remains one of the most popular Bakumatsu figures in Japanese fiction and film.
