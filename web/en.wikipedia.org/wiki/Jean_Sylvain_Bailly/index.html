Jean Sylvain Bailly (15 September 1736 – 12 November 1793) was a French astronomer, mathematician, freemason, and political leader of the early part of the French Revolution. He presided over the Tennis Court Oath and served as the first mayor of Paris under the revolutionary government.

Scientific career

Bailly computed an orbit for Halley's Comet and wrote a respected History of Astronomy. He was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1763.

Revolution

Elected to the Estates-General of 1789, Bailly presided over the National Assembly and administered the Tennis Court Oath on 20 June 1789. Named mayor of Paris on 15 July, he authorized the Champ de Mars massacre of 1791, which destroyed his popularity.

Death

Arrested during the Reign of Terror, Bailly was guillotined on 12 November 1793.
