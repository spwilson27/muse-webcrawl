Hare Krishna (mantra)

The Hare Krishna mantra, also known as the mahamantra ("great mantra"), is a 16-word Vaishnava mantra composed of the names Hare, Krishna and Rama: "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare; Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare." It is central to Gaudiya Vaishnavism and to the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON).

History

The mantra appears in the Kali-Santarana Upanishad. The saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) popularised its public chanting (kirtana) across India. In the 1960s, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada brought the chant to the Western world, founding ISKCON in New York City in 1966.

Practice

Devotees chant the mantra in congregational singing (kirtan), in public processions, and individually on prayer beads (japa). The chant became a visible part of Western counterculture in the late 1960s, and was famously included in George Harrison's 1970 hit "My Sweet Lord".
