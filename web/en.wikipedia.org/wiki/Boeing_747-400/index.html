Boeing 747-400

The Boeing 747-400 is a large, long-range wide-body airliner produced by Boeing Commercial Airplanes, an advanced variant of the initial Boeing 747. The 747-400 was the best-selling model of the 747 family, with 694 delivered between 1989 and 2009. It introduced a two-crew glass cockpit, extended upper deck, winglets, and more efficient engines, and it served as a flagship aircraft for many of the world's major airlines for three decades.

Development

Boeing announced the 747-400 in 1985 to compete with newer twinjets and the McDonnell Douglas MD-11, combining structural and aerodynamic refinement of the classic 747 with a digital "glass cockpit" that eliminated the flight engineer, saving crew cost. Changes included 1.8 m (6 ft) tall winglets, an extended upper deck as standard, new engine options from General Electric (CF6), Pratt & Whitney (PW4000), and Rolls-Royce (RB211), increased maximum takeoff weight, and optional auxiliary fuel tanks. The prototype rolled out in January 1988 and flew on 29 April 1988; certification followed at the end of the year.

Variants

Variants included the passenger 747-400, the 747-400F freighter (without the extended upper deck), the 747-400M "Combi" with a side cargo door, the 747-400D high-density domestic version for Japan (without winglets), the 747-400ER and 747-400ERF extended-range versions, and special conversions such as the 747-400LCF "Dreamlifter" outsized freighter used to carry 787 components, and Boeing Converted Freighters (BCF). A 747-400 served as Air Force One (the VC-25A).

Operational history

The -400 became the workhorse of long-haul fleets at airlines including British Airways (the largest operator, with 57), Lufthansa, Cathay Pacific, Singapore Airlines, United, and Northwest. Rising fuel prices and the efficiency of large twinjets (777, A330, A350) led airlines to retire the type in growing numbers from the 2010s; the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 accelerated retirement, with British Airways and Qantas ending passenger 747 operations that year. Freighter versions remained in cargo service longer; the last 747-400, a freighter, was delivered in 2009, and passenger production ended in 2005.

Specifications (747-400)

Typical seating: 416 passengers in three classes (up to 660 maximum in the -400D). Length: 70.6 m (231 ft 10 in); wingspan: 64.4 m (211 ft 5 in); height: 19.4 m (63 ft 8 in). Maximum takeoff weight: 396,890 kg (875,000 lb). Range: about 13,490 km (7,285 nmi). Cruise speed: Mach 0.85 (913 km/h). Engines: four turbofans of roughly 250–275 kN thrust each.
