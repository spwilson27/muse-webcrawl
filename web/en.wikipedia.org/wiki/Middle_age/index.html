Middle age, also known as midlife, is the stage of life between young adulthood and old age. The exact range is debated, but it is most commonly defined as spanning from about 40 to 65 years of age. Middle age is characterised by continued career development (often reaching its peak), family responsibilities that may include caring for both children and ageing parents, and the first significant physical signs of ageing.

Definitions

Chronological definitions vary. The Oxford English Dictionary places middle age between about 45 and 65. Many psychologists, following developmental stage theories such as Erik Erikson's, locate middle adulthood from roughly 40 to 65, a period dominated by the psychosocial conflict of generativity versus stagnation — the desire to contribute to society and guide the next generation. As life expectancy has risen, proposals have been made to push the boundary of old age later and to treat the fifties and early sixties as an extended middle age.

Physical changes

During middle age, gradual physical changes accumulate: presbyopia (age-related long-sightedness), some loss of muscle mass and bone density, weight gain around the abdomen, greying and thinning hair, and reduced skin elasticity. Women experience the menopause, typically around age 50. The incidence of chronic conditions such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease rises, making midlife an important period for preventive health screening.

Psychological and social aspects

Popular culture associates midlife with the "midlife crisis", a period of self-questioning popularised in the 1960s; empirical research suggests such crises are far from universal, and many people report high life satisfaction and emotional stability in middle age. Middle-aged adults often occupy a "sandwich generation" position, supporting both dependent children and elderly parents, while also holding senior roles at work.
