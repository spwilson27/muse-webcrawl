Benedict of Nursia (Latin: Benedictus Nursiae; c. 480 – 547 AD), often known as Saint Benedict, was an Italian Christian monk and abbot, founder of the Benedictine order and of the abbey of Monte Cassino. He is a patron saint of Europe.

Life

Born in Nursia (modern Norcia) in Umbria, Benedict was sent to Rome to study but withdrew from city life, living as a hermit at Subiaco. He gathered followers and founded twelve monasteries before settling at Monte Cassino around 529, where he wrote his Rule.

Rule of Saint Benedict

The Rule, written around 516–540, balanced prayer, work and study — summarised as ora et labora — under an abbot, with vows of stability, conversion of life and obedience. It became the dominant monastic rule in Western Europe and shaped medieval civilisation, preserving learning through monasteries.

Legacy

Pope Pius XII called Benedict the "Father of Europe"; in 1964 Pope Paul VI proclaimed him patron of Europe. His twin sister Scholastica founded Benedictine convents for women.
