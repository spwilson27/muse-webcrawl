Cascadia (bioregion)

Cascadia is a bioregion and proposed political concept located within the Pacific Northwest of North America, generally corresponding to the watersheds of the Columbia and Fraser rivers and the Cascade Range.

Cascadia
Region: Pacific Northwest
Countries: Canada, United States
Major cities: Seattle, Portland, Vancouver
Named for: Cascade Range

Geography and ecology

The bioregion is characterised by temperate rainforests of Douglas-fir, western hemlock and western redcedar, abundant rainfall, and salmon-bearing rivers. Its boundaries are defined ecologically rather than politically, stretching roughly from coastal Alaska and British Columbia through Washington and Oregon to northern California, and east to parts of Idaho and Montana.

Cascadia movement

The term was popularised by geologist David McCloskey. A cultural and political movement advocates greater regional identity, environmental stewardship and, for some supporters, independence. The Doug flag — blue, white and green with a Douglas-fir — is a common symbol, also adopted by supporters of regional football clubs in the Cascadia Cup.
