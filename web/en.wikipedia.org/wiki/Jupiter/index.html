Jupiter

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest in the Solar System. A gas giant, Jupiter's mass is more than 2.5 times that of all the other planets combined, yet slightly less than one-thousandth the mass of the Sun. It is composed primarily of hydrogen, with a quarter of its mass being helium, and may have a rocky core of heavier elements.

Jupiter has been known since ancient times as a bright "wandering star", named after the Roman king of the gods. Its four largest moons — Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto — were seen by Galileo in 1610, the first clear evidence of celestial motions not centred on Earth. Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System, bigger than Mercury, and Europa is thought to hide a salty ocean beneath its ice crust, making it a leading target in the search for life.

Atmosphere

Jupiter's striped appearance comes from bands of clouds of ammonia and other compounds, circulating in opposite directions at different latitudes. The Great Red Spot is a giant anticyclonic storm larger than Earth that has raged for at least about 190 years, and probably much longer. Winds can exceed 400 km/h at band boundaries.

Exploration

Robotic spacecraft visiting Jupiter include Pioneer 10 and 11, Voyagers 1 and 2, Ulysses, Galileo (the first orbiter, 1995–2003), Cassini en route to Saturn, New Horizons en route to Pluto, and Juno, in orbit since 2016 studying the planet's interior, aurorae and magnetosphere — all missions of NASA, while ESA's JUICE mission, launched in 2023, is en route to study the icy moons.

Role in the Solar System

Jupiter's immense gravity shapes the asteroid belt, captures comets into short-period orbits, and is popularly said to shield the inner planets from impacts — though detailed studies suggest its effect both deflects some objects away and nudges others inward.
