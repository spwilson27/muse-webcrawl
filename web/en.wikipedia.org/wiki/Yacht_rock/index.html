Yacht rock is a broad music style and aesthetic commonly associated with soft rock of the mid-1970s to mid-1980s. Drawing on sources such as R&B, jazz and disco, typical yacht rock is characterised by polished production, breezy vocals, bouncy rhythms and an emphasis on melody over rock aggression. The term was coined retrospectively in 2005 by the creators of the online comedy series Yacht Rock, which dramatised the era's Southern California studio scene.

Characteristics

Yacht rock songs were frequently recorded by a tight circle of elite Los Angeles session musicians and songwriters. Core artists include Michael McDonald, the Doobie Brothers, Steely Dan, Toto, Christopher Cross, Kenny Loggins, Boz Scaggs and Hall & Oates. Lyrical themes tend toward sailing, leisure and gentle romantic melancholy — the persona of the carefree, affluent "yacht" lifestyle. Christopher Cross's "Sailing" (1980), which won multiple Grammy Awards, is often treated as the genre's emblem.

Revival

Originally a faintly mocking label, yacht rock has been embraced since the 2010s by collectors, DJs and streaming playlists; radio formats, tribute bands and festivals devoted to the style have flourished, and critics have reassessed the era's craftsmanship and musicianship.
