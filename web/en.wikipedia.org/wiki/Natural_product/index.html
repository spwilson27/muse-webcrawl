Natural product

A natural product is a chemical compound or substance produced by a living organism — found in nature — that usually has a pharmacological or biological activity. Within organic chemistry the term is often restricted to purified compounds isolated from natural sources, especially secondary metabolites, which are not essential for the organism's growth but confer ecological advantages such as defence against predators or competitors.

Classes

Natural products are grouped by biosynthetic origin and structure into classes including:

  • Alkaloids — nitrogen-containing compounds such as morphine, caffeine and quinine.
  • Terpenoids — built from isoprene units; examples include menthol, artemisinin and the carotenoids.
  • Polyketides — including many antibiotics such as erythromycin and tetracycline, and the statin lovastatin.
  • Nonribosomal peptides — such as penicillin and vancomycin.
  • Phenylpropanoids and flavonoids — widespread plant pigments and antioxidants.
  • Carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic-acid derivatives — primary metabolites sometimes also treated as natural products.

Uses and drug discovery

Natural products have been the source or inspiration for a large fraction of medicines: aspirin derives from willow-bark salicin, the cancer drug paclitaxel (Taxol) from the Pacific yew, and the antimalarial artemisinin from Artemisia annua. Because their structures have been shaped by evolution to interact with biological targets, natural products remain a major starting point for pharmaceutical research, though screening natural extracts fell out of fashion in the 1990s before renewed interest driven by genomics — genome mining can now reveal "cryptic" biosynthetic gene clusters that produce unknown compounds.

Sources

Plants, soil and marine microorganisms, fungi and marine invertebrates such as sponges are the richest sources. The systematic study of natural products drove much of the development of organic synthesis, as chemists sought to confirm structures and manufacture scarce compounds by total synthesis.
