Anthony Fokker

Anton Herman Gerard "Anthony" Fokker (6 April 1890 – 23 December 1939) was a Dutch aviation pioneer, aviation entrepreneur, aircraft designer, and aircraft manufacturer. He is best known for the fighter aircraft he produced in Germany during the First World War, including the Fokker Eindecker, the Fokker Dr.I triplane and the Fokker D.VII.

Early life

Fokker was born in Blitar, Java, in the Dutch East Indies (now Indonesia), where his father was a coffee plantation owner. The family returned to the Netherlands, and Fokker went to Germany to train as a mechanic. In 1910, at the age of 20, he designed and built his first aircraft, the Fokker Spin ("Spider").

First World War

At the outbreak of war in 1914, Fokker's company in Schwerin supplied aircraft to the German air service. His most important technical contribution was the synchronisation gear, which allowed a machine gun to fire through the arc of a spinning propeller, giving the Fokker Eindecker a decisive advantage in 1915–16 (the "Fokker Scourge"). Later designs, including the Dr.I triplane flown by Manfred von Richthofen (the "Red Baron") and the D.VII, considered among the finest fighters of the war, cemented his reputation.

Later career

After the war, Fokker smuggled aircraft and equipment out of Germany back to the Netherlands, where he founded a new company. He later moved to the United States, where his Atlantic Aircraft Corporation produced successful airliners such as the Fokker F.VII trimotor, used on pioneering flights including Richard Byrd's 1926 North Pole flight and the 1928 transpacific flight of the Southern Cross. Fokker died in New York in 1939. His company survived in the Netherlands until 1996.
