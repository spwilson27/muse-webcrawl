Bangladesh, officially the People's Republic of Bangladesh, is a country in South Asia, bordered by India and Myanmar and the Bay of Bengal. Its capital is Dhaka. With over 170 million people in a land area smaller than many countries with a tenth of its population, it is one of the most densely populated nations in the world.

The country occupies the great delta of the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers — a fertile, flood-prone plain that shapes its agriculture, climate vulnerability and culture.

History

The region of Bengal was a wealthy centre of trade and textiles under Mughal rule and then British India. At the Partition of India in 1947 it became East Pakistan, the eastern wing of Pakistan, separated from the west by 1,600 km of India. Discrimination against Bengalis in language, politics and economics culminated in the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971, after which Bangladesh became independent; the war involved mass atrocities against Bengalis, remembered as genocide by Bangladesh.

Independent Bangladesh has alternated between democratic and military rule, and has achieved notable development gains — in life expectancy, girls' education, microfinance and garment exports — while facing persistent political instability, most dramatically in 2024, when mass protests forced Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina from power and an interim government under Muhammad Yunus took office.

Economy and culture

Bangladesh is the world's second-largest garment exporter; remittances from migrant workers and agriculture (rice, jute, tea) are also central. The country is highly vulnerable to cyclones and to sea-level rise and river flooding from climate change.

Bengali (Bangla) is the official language — its defence sparked the Language Movement of 1952, commemorated worldwide as International Mother Language Day. Islam is the majority religion, with a significant Hindu minority.
