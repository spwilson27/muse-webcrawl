Suez Crisis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Suez Crisis, also known as the Second Arab–Israeli War, the Tripartite Aggression and (in the Anglophone world) the Suez Emergency, was the invasion of Egypt in late 1956 by Israel, followed by the United Kingdom and France, with the aim of regaining control of the Suez Canal, which Egypt's President Gamal Abdel Nasser had nationalised on 26 July 1956, and of removing Nasser from power. Israel invaded the Sinai on 29 October 1956; Britain and France issued a pre-arranged ultimatum (the secret Protocol of Sèvres), then bombed and landed forces at Port Said from 5 November.

The operation achieved its immediate military aims but collapsed politically: the United States (Eisenhower, furious at the secret collusion during his re-election week) and the Soviet Union both condemned it; US financial pressure on sterling forced a ceasefire on 6–7 November and complete Anglo-French withdrawal by 22 December; Israel withdrew from Sinai by March 1957 under US pressure, as a UN Emergency Force (Dag Hammarskjöld's innovation — the first UN peacekeeping deployment of its kind) interposed itself. The crisis humiliated Britain — Prime Minister Anthony Eden resigned in January 1957 — and France, confirmed American and Soviet superpower dominance in the Middle East, propelled Nasser to pan-Arab leadership, and is conventionally taught as the moment Britain's great-power pretensions ended.
