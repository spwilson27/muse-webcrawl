The Truce

[Hero image placeholder: cover image of Primo Levi's memoir of the journey home from Auschwitz.]

The Truce (Italian: La tregua), known in the United States as The Reawakening, is a book by the Italian author Primo Levi. Published in 1963, it is the sequel to If This Is a Man and recounts Levi's experiences after the liberation of Auschwitz, describing his long and circuitous journey home to Turin through Poland, Ukraine, Russia, Romania, Hungary, Austria and Germany.

Content

Following the liberation of Auschwitz by the Red Army in January 1945, Levi was sent to a transit camp at Katowice and then embarked on an eastward odyssey with thousands of other displaced people before finally turning west and reaching home in October 1945. The book is more episodic and picaresque than its predecessor, filled with vivid portraits of fellow travellers — including the unforgettable Greek survivor Mordo Nahum — and moments of recovered joy amid the devastation of post-war Europe.

Reception

The Truce won the Premio Campiello in 1963 and helped establish Levi's reputation in Italy. It was adapted into a 1997 film directed by Francesco Rosi, starring John Turturro as Levi.
