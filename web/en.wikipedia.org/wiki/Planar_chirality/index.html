Planar chirality


A planar-chiral metallocene

Planar chirality, also known as 2D chirality, is the special case of chirality for two dimensions. Most fundamentally, planar chirality is a mathematical term, finding use in chemistry, physics and related physical sciences, for example in astronomy, optics and metamaterials. In chemistry, planar chirality is exhibited by molecules that have no stereogenic centres but whose arrangement of atoms out of a reference plane makes them non-superimposable on their mirror images.

In chemistry

Planar chirality arises in compounds such as substituted paracyclophanes, in which two benzene rings are held face-to-face by bridging chains, and in 1,2-disubstituted metallocenes such as unsymmetrically substituted ferrocenes. It also occurs in some cyclophanes, ansa compounds, and certain alkenes held in constrained geometries.

Planar-chiral compounds are designated with the descriptors Rp and Sp (or pR/pS) according to the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules applied to a defined "pilot atom" and the atoms of the chiral plane. Planar-chiral ferrocenes and paracyclophanes are widely used as ligands and catalysts in asymmetric synthesis, including the Josiphos ligand family used in industrial hydrogenation.
