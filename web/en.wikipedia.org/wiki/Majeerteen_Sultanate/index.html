Majeerteen Sultanate

The Majeerteen Sultanate (Somali: Saldanadda Majeerteen), also known as the Majeerteen Kingdom, was a Somali sultanate centred in the Horn of Africa. Ruled by the Boqor of the Majeerteen clan, it was prominent from the mid-18th century into the early 20th century, controlling much of what is now northeastern Somalia, including the modern Puntland region.

History

The sultanate was established around 1800 by members of the Majeerteen Darod. Its capital was at Alula, and it maintained fortified settlements and trading posts along the Gulf of Aden and Indian Ocean coasts, engaging in trade in frankincense, livestock, and other goods with Arabian and Indian merchants. Under Sultan Osman Mahamuud, the sultanate reached its height in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Relations with colonial powers

In 1889, Sultan Osman signed a protectorate treaty with Italy, and the sultanate gradually came under Italian influence within Italian Somaliland. Along with the Sultanate of Hobyo, it retained a degree of autonomy until the Italian fascist campaigns of the 1920s, when both sultanates were forcibly incorporated into the colony, ending in 1927.
