United Principalities

The United Principalities (Romanian: Principatele Unite), formally the United Principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia (Romanian: Principatele Unite ale Moldovei și Țării Românești), was the personal union of the Danubian Principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia created on 24 January 1859 (Old Style 5 January) when both principalities elected Alexandru Ioan Cuza as their common ruler (domnitor). It was the nucleus of the modern Romanian state.

The United Principalities in the 1860s.

Background and the double election

The idea of uniting the two Romanian principalities, then under Ottoman suzerainty, gained momentum after the Crimean War. The 1856 Treaty of Paris and the 1858 Paris Convention created common institutions but kept the principalities formally separate. A legal loophole allowed the unionists to elect the same man in both: Cuza was elected in Moldavia on 5 January 1859 and in Wallachia on 24 January — a date Romanians celebrate as the Day of the Unification ("Little Union").

Reforms under Cuza

Cuza's reign (1859–1866) brought the administrative union of the two states (recognised internationally, first for his lifetime, in 1861–62), a single government and assembly seated in Bucharest, and major reforms: secularization of monastic estates (1863), a land reform (1864), a civil code and penal code, compulsory free primary education, and the founding of universities at Iași (1860) and Bucharest (1864). The state's name gradually became "Romania", though "United Principalities" remained in official use until the constitution of 1866.

Succession

Cuza's authoritarian methods alienated both liberals and conservatives; a coalition forced his abdication in February 1866. After a brief provisional government, the German prince Karl of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen was chosen as ruler, becoming Domnitor Carol I. The United Principalities declared full independence from the Ottoman Empire during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, recognised by the Treaty of Berlin (1878), and in 1881 Romania was proclaimed a kingdom.
