Electric energy consumption

Electric energy consumption is the amount of electrical energy used by a device, building, region or country over time, conventionally measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh) or, for nations, terawatt-hours (TWh). World electricity consumption has grown almost continuously since the late nineteenth century and reached roughly 29,000 TWh in the early 2020s.

World consumption

China is the largest consumer of electricity, using more than 8,500 TWh per year, followed by the United States at around 4,000 TWh, then India, Japan and Russia. Per-capita consumption varies enormously, from over 15,000 kWh per person in countries such as Canada and Norway to under 100 kWh in parts of sub-Saharan Africa.

Sectors

Industry is typically the largest consuming sector, followed by residential buildings, commercial and public services, and transport. Electrification of heating and vehicles is expected to raise demand substantially, while efficiency improvements in lighting, motors and appliances moderate growth.

Trends

After a pandemic-related dip and rebound, global demand growth accelerated in the mid-2020s, driven by air conditioning, data centres — including AI computing — and electric vehicles.
