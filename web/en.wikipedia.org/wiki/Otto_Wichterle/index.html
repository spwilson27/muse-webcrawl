Otto Wichterle

Otto Wichterle (27 October 1913 – 18 August 1998) was a Czech chemist, best known for his invention of modern soft contact lenses.

Wichterle worked on hydrophilic gels at the Institute of Macromolecular Chemistry in Prague. On Christmas Day 1961, using a home-built apparatus made from a children's Merkur construction set and a gramophone motor, he succeeded in spin-casting the first soft contact lenses from poly-HEMA hydrogel, a material he had patented with Drahoslav Lím.

The patents were later licensed and developed commercially, leading to widespread soft contact lens production. Wichterle also contributed to the development of polyamide fibres and served as president of the Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences after the Velvet Revolution.
