Top Gun is a 1986 American action drama film directed by Tony Scott and produced by Don Simpson and Jerry Bruckheimer, in association with Paramount Pictures. The screenplay was written by Jim Cash and Jack Epps Jr. and was inspired by an article titled "Top Guns", published in California magazine three years earlier. The film stars Tom Cruise as Lieutenant Pete "Maverick" Mitchell, a young naval aviator aboard the aircraft carrier USS Enterprise. He and his radar intercept officer, Lieutenant (junior grade) Nick "Goose" Bradshaw (Anthony Edwards), are given the chance to train at the United States Navy's Fighter Weapons School at Naval Air Station Miramar in San Diego, California.

Top Gun was released on May 16, 1986. Upon release, the film received mixed reviews from film critics, but despite this, it became a huge commercial hit, grossing $357 million globally against a production budget of $15 million. It was the highest-grossing film of 1986. The film maintained its popularity over the years and earned an IMAX 3D re-release in 2013. Additionally, the soundtrack won a Grammy Award for the film's second single, "Take My Breath Away", which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Plot

United States Navy aviators LT Pete "Maverick" Mitchell and his radar intercept officer (RIO) LTJG Nick "Goose" Bradshaw fly the Grumman F-14 Tomcat aboard the USS Enterprise. During an interception with two hostile MiG-28s over the Indian Ocean, Maverick performs a daring inverted maneuver to intimidate one of the enemy pilots while his wingman, Bill "Cougar" Cortell, is targeted by the other. After Cougar is nearly overwhelmed and Maverick saves him, Maverick is reprimanded but ultimately chosen, along with Goose, to attend the Navy's elite Fighter Weapons School — nicknamed "Top Gun" — at NAS Miramar after Cougar turns in his wings.

During training, Maverick clashes with fellow student Tom "Iceman" Kazansky, whose disciplined style contrasts with Maverick's reckless instincts. Maverick also pursues a romance with Charlotte "Charlie" Blackwood, a civilian astrophysicist and instructor at the school. During a training sortie, Maverick's F-14 enters a flat spin caused by jet wash, and he and Goose are forced to eject; Goose strikes the canopy and is killed. A board of inquiry clears Maverick of wrongdoing, but guilt shakes his confidence.

After briefly considering quitting, Maverick returns for the class's graduation and is immediately deployed to the Enterprise for a crisis operation. In a climactic dogfight against hostile MiGs, Maverick overcomes his fear of engaging the enemy, shoots down three aircraft alongside Iceman, and returns a hero. He accepts a position as an instructor at Top Gun.

Production

Producers Don Simpson and Jerry Bruckheimer developed the film after reading Ehud Yonay's May 1983 California magazine article about naval aviators at Miramar. The United States Navy provided extensive support, making F-14 squadrons aboard USS Enterprise and other carriers available for filming, and charged Paramount for flight time. Director Tony Scott was reportedly fired three times for exceeding the budget with extra aerial footage, but was rehired each time; the resulting aerial photography, including shots of aircraft flying low over the desert and near the camera plane, became one of the film's defining features.

Soundtrack

The film's soundtrack was a major success, reaching number one on the Billboard charts and eventually being certified multi-platinum. It featured Kenny Loggins's "Danger Zone" and "Playing with the Boys", and Berlin's "Take My Breath Away", which won the Academy Award and the Golden Globe for Best Original Song. The soaring instrumental "Top Gun Anthem", composed by Harold Faltermeyer and performed by guitarist Steve Stevens, also became iconic.

Reception and legacy

Top Gun received mixed reviews upon release, with critics praising its aerial sequences and energetic style but criticizing its plot and dialogue. It was nevertheless the highest-grossing film of 1986 worldwide, and it significantly boosted recruitment into the United States Navy; recruitment booths were reportedly set up in some theaters. The film was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry by the Library of Congress in 2015 as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

Sequel

A sequel, Top Gun: Maverick, was released in 2022, again starring Cruise and Kilmer, with Jennifer Connelly joining the cast. Directed by Joseph Kosinski, the sequel was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $1.4 billion worldwide and receiving an Academy Award nomination for Best Picture.
