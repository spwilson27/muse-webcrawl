Pig Latin

Pig Latin (also known as Igpay Atinlay) is a language game, or argot, in which English words are altered, usually by adding a fabricated suffix or by moving the onset of a word to its end and adding a suffix. It is most often used by children to disguise speech from adults, and is not a true secret language, as it is easily learned and widely known.

Rules

In the most common form of Pig Latin:

  • For words beginning with consonant sounds, the initial consonant or consonant cluster is moved to the end of the word and "ay" is added: "pig" becomes "igpay", "latin" becomes "atinlay".
  • For words beginning with vowel sounds, "way" (or a variant such as "yay" or "hay") is added to the end: "egg" becomes "eggway", "another" becomes "anotherway".

History

References to forms of word-play languages resembling Pig Latin appear as early as the 19th century; Thomas Jefferson was reported to have written letters in a comparable code. The modern form was described in print in the United States in the late 1800s and became a widespread playground pastime in the 20th century.

In popular culture

Pig Latin phrases occasionally appear in popular culture; in the Disney film The Lion King, the character Zazu refers to "Ix-nay on the upid-stay" ("nix on the stupid"). Words derived from Pig Latin, such as "ixnay" (from "nix") and "amscray" (from "scram"), have entered English slang.
