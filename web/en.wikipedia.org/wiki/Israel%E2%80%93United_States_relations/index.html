Israel–United States relations

Israel–United States relations are the bilateral relations between the State of Israel and the United States of America. The United States recognised Israel in 1948, minutes after its declaration of independence, and the two countries have since developed a close strategic, military, and economic partnership.

History

President Harry S. Truman extended de facto recognition to the provisional government of Israel on 14 May 1948. Relations deepened after the Six-Day War of 1967, when the United States became Israel's principal arms supplier and diplomatic supporter.

Military aid

Israel has been the largest cumulative recipient of United States foreign assistance since the Second World War. A ten-year memorandum of understanding signed in 2016 provides $38 billion in military aid for 2019–2028, much of it spent on systems such as the F-35 and missile defence programmes including Iron Dome.

Trade and diplomacy

The United States–Israel Free Trade Agreement of 1985 was the first free trade agreement signed by the United States. The US has frequently used its veto in the UN Security Council on resolutions critical of Israel, and it brokered the Camp David Accords (1978) and the Abraham Accords (2020).
