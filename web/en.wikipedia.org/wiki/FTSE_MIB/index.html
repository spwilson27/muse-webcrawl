The FTSE MIB (Milano Indice di Borsa) is the benchmark stock market index for the Borsa Italiana, the Italian national stock exchange. It comprises the 40 most liquid and capitalised Italian and foreign companies listed on the exchange, and is the successor of the S&P/MIB index, which it replaced in June 2009 after the London Stock Exchange Group's acquisition of Borsa Italiana.

Composition

The index is weighted by free-float-adjusted market capitalisation and reviewed quarterly. Major constituents have typically included the banks Intesa Sanpaolo and UniCredit, the insurer Generali, energy companies Eni and Enel, the carmaker Stellantis, Ferrari, and luxury and utility names. Individual company weightings are not capped as strictly as in some benchmark indices, so financial sector stocks have historically made up a large share of the index.

History

Italian equity benchmarks trace back to the BCI (Banca Commerciale Italiana) index of the early 20th century. The modern all-share MIB index began in the 1990s; the blue-chip series ran successively as the MIB 30, S&P/MIB and FTSE MIB. The index fell sharply during the euro-area sovereign debt crisis of 2011–12, when Italian bank shares came under severe pressure, and again during the COVID-19 crash of March 2020, before recovering in subsequent years.
