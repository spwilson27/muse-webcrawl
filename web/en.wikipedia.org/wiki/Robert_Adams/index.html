Robert Adams (born May 8, 1937) is an American photographer who has focused on the changing landscape of the American West. Raised in Colorado and trained as an English professor, he turned to photography full-time in 1970. His austere black-and-white images of tract housing, highways and cleared land along the Colorado Front Range documented suburban expansion with a quiet, elegiac clarity. His book The New West (1974) was a landmark of modern landscape photography, and he was a central participant in the influential 1975 exhibition New Topographics: Photographs of a Man-Altered Landscape at the George Eastman House.

Work

Adams's major sequences include Denver (1974), What We Bought: The New World (1995), Summer Nights, Walking (2009, revisiting negatives from the 1970s), and The Place We Live, the title of the retrospective exhibition organised by the Yale University Art Gallery in 2010. Later work turned to the Pacific Northwest, including photographs of clear-cut forests and of the sea at the mouth of the Columbia River. His writing on photography, notably Beauty in Photography (1981) and Why People Photograph (1994), is widely read.

Recognition

Adams received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1994 and the Hasselblad Award in 2009. Unlike photographers who emphasised untouched wilderness, he insisted that honest pictures of damaged land could still affirm beauty and form — "the coherence of the light" — an ethic that shaped generations of documentary landscape photographers.
