Recreational pilot certificate

A recreational pilot certificate is a pilot licence that allows a person to act as pilot in command of an aircraft for personal, non-commercial recreation, subject to limitations intended to reflect its reduced training requirements. In the United States it is issued by the Federal Aviation Administration under 14 CFR Part 61; similar certificates exist in other jurisdictions, such as the Recreational Aviation Australia pilot certificate.

United States privileges and limitations

An FAA recreational pilot may fly single-engine airplanes of no more than 180 horsepower with up to four seats, during daylight hours in good weather, generally within 50 nautical miles of the departure airport (further flight requires additional training and endorsements). Carrying more than one passenger, flying at night, or operating in controlled airspace above certain altitudes is prohibited without further training.

Requirements

Applicants must be at least 17 years old, log at least 30 hours of flight time, pass a written knowledge test and a practical test. The certificate has seen low uptake since the introduction of the sport pilot certificate in 2004.
