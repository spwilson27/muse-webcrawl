Ascot Racecourse is a British thoroughbred horse-racing venue at Ascot, Berkshire, about six miles from Windsor Castle. Founded by Queen Anne in 1711, it is one of Britain's leading racecourses and is closely associated with the Royal Family.

Royal Ascot

The course hosts Royal Ascot, a five-day meeting each June attended by the monarch and members of the Royal Family and famous for its dress code and pageantry. Its signature race is the Ascot Gold Cup, founded in 1807 and run over two and a half miles. In July Ascot stages the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes, one of Europe's most prestigious middle-distance races, and in October it hosts British Champions Day, the finale of the flat season.

History

The first race meeting was held on 11 August 1711 at the instigation of Queen Anne, who had identified the open heath as suitable for racing. The venue was extensively redeveloped in 2004–2006, when a new grandstand was built and the track realigned. Ascot stages both flat and jump racing, with about 26 days of racing a year.
