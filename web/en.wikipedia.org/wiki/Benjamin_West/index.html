Benjamin West (October 10, 1738 – March 11, 1820) was an American artist who painted famous historical scenes such as The Death of Nelson, The Death of General Wolfe and Benjamin Franklin Drawing Electricity from the Sky. He was the second president of London's Royal Academy, serving from 1792 to 1805 and again from 1806 until his death; as such he was, effectively, the leading painter in Britain for a generation, and historical painter to King George III.

Early life and Italy

Born in Pennsylvania to an innkeeper's family, West was remarkably self-taught; legend held that Native American friends taught him to make pigments from clay. Local patrons in Philadelphia sent him to Italy in 1760, where he spent three years studying the Old Masters — the first American-born artist to train in Europe. In Rome he met Johann Joachim Winckelmann and Gavin Hamilton and absorbed the emerging Neoclassical style.

London career

West settled in London in 1763 and never returned to America. His Death of General Wolfe (1770) revolutionised history painting by depicting a contemporary event with the figures in modern dress rather than classical costume, despite advice from Joshua Reynolds and others. George III appointed him historical painter to the court in 1772 and commissioned dozens of works; West was a founding member of the Royal Academy in 1768 and succeeded Reynolds as its president. His studio trained a generation of American artists, including John Singleton Copley, Gilbert Stuart, Charles Willson Peale, Rembrandt Peale and Washington Allston.

Later work

West's later years were dominated by vast religious canvases — Christ Healing the Sick (1811), painted for the Pennsylvania Hospital in Philadelphia, and Death on the Pale Horse (1817) — which anticipated Romantic apocalyptic painting. He died in London in 1820 and was buried in St Paul's Cathedral.
