The Type 61 (61式戦車, Roku-ichi Shiki sensha) was a main battle tank developed and used by the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF), built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. It was Japan's first domestically designed post-war tank, developed after the JGSDF found the American-supplied M24 Chaffee and M4 Sherman inadequate and the M41 and M47 too heavy or unavailable for Japan's terrain and rail transport limits. Production ran from 1961 to 1975, with about 560 vehicles built, and the type served until 2000.

Design

The Type 61 was constrained by Japan's railway loading gauge and road infrastructure to about 35 tonnes. Its layout was conventional for its era: a cast turret carrying a 90 mm rifled main gun — based on the American M36/M41 gun and produced domestically — with a distinctive muzzle brake and fume extractor, a cupola-mounted 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine gun, and a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun. The hull used welded steel armour of modest thickness, optimised for mobility in Japan's mountainous terrain rather than duelling heavier Soviet designs. Power came from a rear-mounted Mitsubishi V-12 air-cooled diesel producing about 570 hp, driving through torsion-bar suspension with six road wheels per side.

Variants and service

Variants included the Type 67 armoured vehicle-launched bridge, the Type 67 model armoured engineering vehicle, the Type 70 armoured recovery vehicle, and the Type 87 self-propelled anti-aircraft gun trainer derivatives. The Type 61 never saw combat. It was succeeded by the Type 74 from the mid-1970s, but remained in service with some units for a quarter-century more, finally retiring in 2000, with surviving examples preserved as gate guardians and museum pieces across Japan.
