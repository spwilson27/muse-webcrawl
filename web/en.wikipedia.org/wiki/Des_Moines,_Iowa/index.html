Des Moines is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Iowa. It is the county seat of Polk County, at the confluence of the Des Moines and Raccoon rivers in south-central Iowa. The city is a major centre of the U.S. insurance industry and has a sizable financial services and publishing sector. In politics it is famous for hosting the Iowa caucuses, traditionally the first major event of the U.S. presidential nomination process.

The skyline of Des Moines, dominated by the golden dome of the Iowa State Capitol.

History

The area was inhabited by the Ioway, Sauk and Meskwaki peoples. Fort Des Moines was established by the U.S. Army in 1843 at the junction of the two rivers. The settlement was incorporated as the City of Des Moines in 1851, and in 1857 it replaced Iowa City as the state capital. Coal mining, railroads, and later insurance drove its growth through the 19th and 20th centuries.

Economy

Des Moines is often called the "insurance capital" of the United States: major firms based or with large operations there include Principal Financial Group, Wells Fargo, Nationwide, and Athene. Publishing giant Meredith (Dotdash Meredith) was founded there, and the metro is a growing centre for data centres and agribusiness.

Culture

Annual events include the Iowa State Fair, one of the largest in the country, held each August on the east side of the city. The Des Moines Art Center, designed in part by I. M. Pei, and the John and Mary Pappajohn Sculpture Park anchor the city's arts scene. The gold-domed Iowa State Capitol, completed in 1886, is a prominent landmark.

Geography and climate

Des Moines has a humid continental climate with hot, humid summers and cold, snowy winters. Devastating floods in 1993 — part of the Great Flood of 1993 — inundated parts of the city and left it temporarily without drinking water.
