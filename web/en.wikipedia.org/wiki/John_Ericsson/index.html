John Ericsson (born Johan Ericsson; July 31, 1803 – March 8, 1889) was a Swedish-American inventor and mechanical engineer. One of the most influential engineers of the nineteenth century, he is chiefly remembered for designing the United States Navy's first ironclad warship, USS Monitor.

Early career in Sweden and England

Ericsson worked as a surveyor on Sweden's Göta Canal and served in the Swedish army before moving to London in 1826. There he developed steam fire engines, the "caloric" hot-air engine, and a screw propeller, which he patented and demonstrated on the Thames. His locomotive Novelty narrowly lost the Rainhill Trials of 1829 to Stephenson's Rocket.

Career in the United States

Ericsson emigrated to New York in 1839. With Captain Robert Stockton he built the screw-propelled warship USS Princeton, the U.S. Navy's first screw warship; after a gun explosion aboard killed the Secretary of State in 1844, Ericsson was unjustly blamed and distanced himself from the Navy for years.

USS Monitor

During the American Civil War, Ericsson designed the revolutionary ironclad USS Monitor, built in about one hundred days, whose revolving turret and low iron hull fought the Confederate ironclad Virginia at Hampton Roads on March 9, 1862 — the first battle between ironclad warships. The design spawned a whole class of monitors.

Later work and death

Ericsson later worked on torpedoes, solar engines, and the "destroyer" torpedo boat concept. He died in New York in 1889; his remains were returned to Sweden aboard USS Baltimore with great ceremony. He is commemorated by monuments in New York and Stockholm and by the John Ericsson Memorial in Washington, D.C.
