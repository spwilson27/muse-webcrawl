Carl Adam Petri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Carl Adam Petri (12 July 1926 – 2 July 2010) was a German mathematician and computer scientist. He is best known for inventing the Petri net in his 1962 doctoral dissertation at the Technical University of Darmstadt, a graphical and mathematical modelling language for describing distributed and concurrent systems that remains foundational in computer science, systems biology and business-process modelling.

Life

Petri was born in Leipzig. As a schoolboy he devised early ideas about concurrency while working in his father's chemistry library and, by his own account, sketched the concepts behind his nets at the age of thirteen, in 1939. After wartime service and imprisonment he studied mathematics at what is now Leibniz University Hannover and then Darmstadt, where he completed his doctorate in 1962 with the dissertation Kommunikation mit Automaten ("Communication with Automata"). Petri later worked at the University of Bonn and at the GMD (Gesellschaft für Mathematik und Datenverarbeitung) research institute at Sankt Augustin, where he directed the Institute for System Technology. He retired in 1991.

Petri nets

A Petri net consists of places, transitions and arcs, with tokens whose movement ("firing") models the flow of control and resources in a system. Unlike sequential models of computation, nets treat concurrency as fundamental rather than interleaved, which made them powerful for analysing deadlocks, synchronisation and distributed protocols. The formalism spawned an extensive research community, annual conferences from 1980 onward, and industrial applications from workflow management (where it underlies much of BPMN's semantics) to systems biology, where nets model biochemical pathways. Petri himself later broadened the work into a general "net theory" and reflections on the foundations of physics and information.

Honours

Petri received the Werner von Siemens Ring (1996), was elected to Academia Europaea, and was made an honorary professor of the University of Hamburg. He died in Siegburg in 2010, shortly before his 84th birthday.
