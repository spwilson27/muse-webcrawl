Lake Texcoco

Lake Texcoco (Spanish: Lago de Texcoco) was a natural saline lake in the Valley of Mexico, in central Mexico. The Mexica (Aztec) capital Tenochtitlan was built on an island in its western portion. After the Spanish conquest, the lake was progressively drained to control flooding of Mexico City.

History

Lake Texcoco was the largest of five interconnected lakes in the valley. Tenochtitlan was connected to the mainland by causeways, and chinampa agriculture flourished in the shallows. Colonial and later drainage projects — including the Huehuetoca canal and the Gran Canal — shrank the lake to a small remnant east of Mexico City.

Modern era

The exposed lakebed was the site of the cancelled Mexico City Texcoco Airport project; the area has since been developed as the Lake Texcoco Ecological Park, one of the largest urban park projects in the world, restoring wetlands and bird habitat.
