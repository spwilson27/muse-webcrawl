Robinson–Schensted correspondence

FieldCombinatorics, representation theory
Introduced byG. de B. Robinson (1938); Craige Schensted (1961)
MapsPermutation → pair of standard Young tableaux of the same shape

In mathematics, the Robinson–Schensted correspondence is a bijection between permutations and pairs of standard Young tableaux of the same shape. It was introduced by G. de B. Robinson in 1938 in work on the representation theory of the symmetric group and rediscovered by Craige Schensted in 1961 in connection with longest increasing subsequences.

The algorithm

Schensted's insertion algorithm builds a tableau by inserting the entries of the permutation one at a time: each new number replaces ("bumps") the leftmost larger entry in the first row, which is inserted into the next row, and so on. Recording where new boxes are added yields the second tableau of the pair. The correspondence shows, among other things, that the length of the longest increasing subsequence of a permutation equals the length of the first row of its tableaux, and the longest decreasing subsequence equals the first column.

Extensions and applications

The correspondence underlies the enumeration of standard Young tableaux (the hook-length formula), connects permutations with the representation theory of symmetric and general linear groups, and was extended by Knuth to the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth (RSK) correspondence for matrices. It also appears in probability, in the study of random permutations and longest increasing subsequences, and in integrable systems.
