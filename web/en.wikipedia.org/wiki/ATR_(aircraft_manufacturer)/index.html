ATR (French: Avions de Transport Régional; Italian: Aerei da Trasporto Regionale) is a Franco-Italian aircraft manufacturer headquartered in Toulouse, France. It was formed in 1981 as a joint venture between France's Aérospatiale (now Airbus) and Italy's Aeritalia (now Leonardo) to build regional turboprop airliners. ATR is the world market leader in the 50–78 seat turboprop segment, with over 1,700 aircraft delivered to more than 200 operators in about 100 countries.

Products

The company's two models are the 48-seat ATR 42 (first flight 1984) and the stretched 70-seat ATR 72 (first flight 1988). The current "-600" series, launched in 2007, features Pratt & Whitney Canada PW127 engines, a glass cockpit developed with Thales, and improved passenger comfort. Variants include freighter conversions and the ATR 72-600F purpose-built cargo version, plus the maritime patrol ATR 72MP/ASW for military customers.

Market position

Turboprops' lower fuel burn on short sectors — ATR claims roughly 40% less fuel than comparable regional jets — have kept the type dominant on routes under about 300 nautical miles. ATR's main historical rivals were Bombardier's Dash 8/Q400 (production ended 2021) and various regional jets. In 2019 ATR proposed the short take-off and landing ATR 42-600S. The company markets an "EVO" concept for a hybrid-electric evolution in the 2030s.
