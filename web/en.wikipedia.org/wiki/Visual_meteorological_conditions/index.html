Visual meteorological conditions

Visual meteorological conditions (VMC) is an aviation flight category in which weather conditions are good enough for pilots to fly the aircraft while maintaining visual separation from the terrain and other aircraft — that is, to fly by looking out of the window rather than relying solely on instruments. VMC is the opposite of instrument meteorological conditions (IMC). When conditions are at or better than specified minima of visibility and distance from cloud, flights may be conducted under visual flight rules (VFR); below those minima, instrument flight rules (IFR) are required.

VMC criteria

The exact minima are set by regulation and vary by country, airspace class, and altitude. Typical criteria combine:

  • Flight visibility — commonly 5 km or more in controlled airspace (3 statute miles under US rules), with reduced allowances in some uncontrolled airspace at low altitude.
  • Distance from cloud — aircraft must remain a specified horizontal and vertical distance from clouds (e.g., 1,500 m horizontally and 300 m vertically in many ICAO-based regimes; in the US commonly 500 feet below, 1,000 feet above, and 2,000 feet horizontally).
  • At higher altitudes (at and above FL100 in many states), visibility requirements increase, typically to 8 km.

Pilots are responsible for determining whether conditions are VMC before and during a VFR flight, and for remaining "well clear" of cloud. Special VFR clearances allow operations in controlled airspace below normal VMC minima under reduced criteria, subject to air traffic control approval.
