Transuranic waste

Transuranic waste (TRU)
Definition (US): Waste with >100 nCi/g of alpha-emitting transuranic isotopes, half-life >20 years
Main source: Nuclear weapons production
Disposal: Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP), New Mexico

Transuranic waste (TRU) is, as defined in United States law, waste contaminated with alpha-emitting transuranic radionuclides — elements with atomic number greater than uranium, such as plutonium and americium — above defined concentrations and half-lives, excluding high-level waste and spent fuel.

Sources and handling

Most TRU waste consists of clothing, tools, rags, debris and sludges from weapons research and plutonium production at sites such as Los Alamos, Hanford, Savannah River and Rocky Flats. It is categorised as contact-handled or remote-handled according to surface dose rate.

Disposal

Since 1999 US defence TRU waste has been disposed of in the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP), a deep geological repository in bedded salt near Carlsbad, New Mexico, about 655 m underground. A 2014 radiological release at WIPP, traced to a chemically reactive waste drum, suspended operations until 2017. See Radioactive waste and WIPP.
