Acid dissociation constant

The acid dissociation constant, Ka, is a quantitative measure of the strength of an acid in solution. It is the equilibrium constant for the reaction in which the acid HA dissociates into its conjugate base A− and a proton (usually written as H3O+ in water). The larger the value of Ka, the stronger the acid.

pKa

Because Ka values span many orders of magnitude, they are usually expressed as pKa = −log10 Ka. Strong acids such as hydrochloric acid have negative pKa values (about −7), acetic acid has pKa ≈ 4.76, and very weak acids such as water have pKa ≈ 14. A difference of one pKa unit corresponds to a tenfold difference in acid strength.

Polyprotic acids

Acids with more than one ionisable proton, such as phosphoric acid, dissociate stepwise, each step having its own constant (Ka1, Ka2, Ka3). Successive constants decrease sharply because removing a proton from an increasingly negative ion is progressively harder.

Applications

Together with the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation, pKa values predict the pH of buffer solutions and the charge state — and hence solubility, binding, and membrane permeability — of drug molecules at physiological pH.
