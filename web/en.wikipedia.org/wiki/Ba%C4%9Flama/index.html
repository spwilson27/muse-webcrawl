Bağlama

Bağlama
ClassificationPlucked string instrument (long-necked lute)
OriginAnatolia, Central Asian Turkic traditions
StringsUsually 7, in 3 courses (2+2+3)
RelatedSaz, tanbur, bouzouki, dutar

The bağlama (Turkish: bağlama, from bağlamak, "to tie") is a family of long-necked lutes and the most common traditional instrument in Turkey. It is central to Turkish folk music and to the Alevi-Bektashi ritual tradition, where it is sometimes called the saz.

Description

The bağlama has a deep, rounded body hollowed from a single piece of mulberry or juniper wood (or built from ribs), a long neck with movable tied frets, and typically seven metal strings grouped in three courses. The movable frets allow the microtonal intervals of Turkish makam music. It is played with the fingers or with a thin plectrum called a mızrap or tezene.

Types

The family ranges from the small cura, through the standard short-necked and long-necked bağlama, to the large divan bağlama and the bass meydan bağlama. Twentieth-century innovations include the electric bağlama, popularised in Anatolian rock.

Cultural role

The bağlama accompanies the aşık (minstrel) tradition of sung poetry and is indispensable in the cem ceremonies of Alevi communities. Noted performers include Aşık Veysel, Neşet Ertaş, Muharrem Ertaş and, in the electric style, Erkin Koray and Barış Manço recordings featuring the instrument.
