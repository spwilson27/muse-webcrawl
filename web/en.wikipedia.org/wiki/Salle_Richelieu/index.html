Salle Richelieu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Salle Richelieu]
Home of the Comédie-Française

The Salle Richelieu is a theatre in the Palais-Royal complex in Paris, France, and the principal home of the Comédie-Française, the French national theatre company founded in 1680. Located at 2 Rue de Richelieu, near the Louvre, it seats about 862 spectators in a horseshoe-shaped Italian-style auditorium, and is nicknamed "la Maison de Molière" (the House of Molière).

History

The hall was built as part of the Palais-Royal theatre developed under the architect Victor Louis, opening in 1790. It became the home of the Comédie-Française in 1799 and has been rebuilt and renovated several times, notably after fires in 1900 and during major modernisations in the later 20th century. The theatre contains famous furnishings including the armchair in which Molière collapsed while playing the title role of Le Malade imaginaire on 17 February 1673 — the so-called fauteuil de Molière, preserved in the foyer.

The building and performances

The auditorium retains classic red-and-gold décor with boxes, a royal box, and a chandelier; backstage areas house the company's workshops and the remarkable rotating stage machinery installed over the centuries. The Comédie-Française performs a rotating repertory of French classics — Molière, Racine, Corneille, Marivaux — alongside modern works, in the Salle Richelieu and its two other venues, the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier and the Studio-Théâtre. The square outside the theatre, Place Colette, was renamed for the writer in 1966.
