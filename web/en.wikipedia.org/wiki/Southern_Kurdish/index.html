Southern Kurdish (Kurdish: Kurdîy Xwarîn) is one of the main groups of Kurdish dialects, spoken in eastern Iraq and western Iran, principally in the provinces of Kermanshah and Ilam and in parts of Lorestan, as well as around Kirkuk and Khanaqin in Iraq. Together with Northern Kurdish (Kurmanji) and Central Kurdish (Sorani), it forms the conventional threefold division of the Kurdish language continuum.

Dialects

Recognised varieties include Kermanshahi, Kalhori, Feyli (Ilami), Laki (often debated as either Southern Kurdish or a separate language), Kordali, and the dialects of the Garrusi and Sanjabi areas. The Laki variety in particular occupies a transitional position between Southern Kurdish and Lori languages, and its classification is contested by both speakers and linguists.

Features

Southern Kurdish shares the Perso-Arabic writing system used for Sorani, though it lacks the standardised literary norm enjoyed by Sorani and Kurmanji and is less frequently used in publishing and broadcasting. Linguistically it shows features such as the loss of certain oblique-case distinctions found further north, distinctive pronominal systems, and verbal constructions influenced by neighbouring Persian and Lori, from which it has also borrowed considerable vocabulary. Because education and media in its region are dominated by Persian (in Iran) or Sorani and Arabic (in Iraq), Southern Kurdish is under pressure and many younger speakers shift toward the dominant languages in formal domains.
