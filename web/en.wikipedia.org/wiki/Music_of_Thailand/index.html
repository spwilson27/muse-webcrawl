Music of Thailand

The music of Thailand includes classical court music, regional folk traditions such as northeastern mor lam and northern music, and popular genres including luk thung, phleng phuea chiwit ("songs for life"), and Thai pop and rock.

Classical music

Thai classical music accompanies royal ceremonies, khon masked dance-drama, and theatre. Principal ensembles are the piphat (percussion and winds), khruang sai (strings), and mahori (combined). Instruments include the ranat ek and ranat thum xylophones, khong wong gong circles, saw string instruments, the pi oboe, and ching hand cymbals that mark the rhythmic cycle. Tuning divides the octave into seven roughly equal steps.

Folk music

In Isan (the northeast), mor lam is a sung narrative tradition accompanied by the khaen, a bamboo mouth organ, and the phin lute; electrified mor lam is a festival staple. Northern (Lanna) music features the salo, sueng, and khlui, while the south has traditions such as nora dance music and rong ngeng.

Popular music

Luk thung ("country music"), exemplified by singers Pumpuang Duangjan and Suraphol Sombatcharoen, blends rural themes with lush arrangements. Phleng phuea chiwit protest-folk emerged in the 1970s with bands like Caravan and Carabao. Modern Thai pop, rock, and hip-hop thrive alongside string orchestras descended from King Rama VI's era, and the Suntaraporn band popularized big-band luk krung music.
