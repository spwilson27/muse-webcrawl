Syria–Lebanon border clashes

The Syria–Lebanon border clashes refer to episodes of armed violence along the border between Syria and Lebanon, a frontier of about 375 kilometres that has never been fully demarcated. Tensions have recurred because of the mountains and smuggling routes of the Anti-Lebanon range, the spillover of the Syrian civil war from 2011 onward, and competing armed actors operating in the borderlands around the Beqaa Valley and the Qalamoun mountains.

Background

Lebanon and Syria gained independence from France in the 1940s without agreeing a precisely surveyed border. Disputed areas, including the Shebaa Farms, and the porous highlands between the two countries have repeatedly figured in regional conflicts. During the Syrian civil war, the border saw fighting involving the Syrian army, Lebanese armed groups, and jihadist organizations that seized Lebanese border towns such as Arsal in 2014 before being expelled in 2017.

Recent episodes

Following the fall of the Assad government in December 2024, new rounds of fighting broke out in early 2025 between Syrian government forces under the transitional authorities and Lebanese tribal and Hezbollah-linked gunmen in the Hermel–Qusayr border area. Exchanges of rockets and artillery killed soldiers and civilians on both sides and prompted deployments of the Lebanese Army and mediation to restore calm along the frontier.
