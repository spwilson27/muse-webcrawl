Base change

FieldMathematics (algebraic geometry, linear algebra, category theory)
Related notionsPullback, extension of scalars, fiber product

In mathematics, base change refers to a family of related constructions in which an object defined relative to one base (a ring, field, scheme, or category) is transferred to another base along a given morphism. The resulting object is typically formed by a pullback or, algebraically, by a tensor product.

Extension of scalars

In linear algebra and module theory, base change along a ring homomorphism R → S converts an R-module M into an S-module by extension of scalars, M ↦ S ⊗R M. A standard example is viewing a real vector space as a complex vector space by complexification. The dual process, restriction of scalars, goes in the opposite direction.

In algebraic geometry

Given a morphism of schemes X → S and a base-change morphism S′ → S, the base change of X is the fiber product X ×S S′ → S′. This construction underlies notions such as the fiber of a family over a point. Important theorems of "base change" type state that formation of higher direct images of sheaves commutes with base change under suitable hypotheses — for example, flat base change and the proper base change theorem in étale cohomology.

Change of basis

The phrase is also used more elementarily for a change of basis of a vector space: the matrix of a linear map changes by conjugation with the change-of-basis matrix. In category theory, base change functors arise in the calculus of correspondences and in six-functor formalisms, where pullback and pushforward along morphisms interact.
