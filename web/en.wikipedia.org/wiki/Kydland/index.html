Finn Erling Kydland (born 1 December 1943) is a Norwegian economist and professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara. He shared the 2004 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Edward C. Prescott "for their contributions to dynamic macroeconomics".

Work

With Prescott, Kydland published the seminal 1977 paper "Rules Rather than Discretion: The Inconsistency of Optimal Plans", showing that optimal economic policies can be time-inconsistent — a plan that seems best today may cease to be optimal later, so credible commitment and rules can outperform discretionary policymaking.

Their 1982 paper "Time to Build and Aggregate Fluctuations" founded real business cycle theory, modelling business cycles as the efficient response of the economy to real shocks, especially technology shocks, in a calibrated dynamic general-equilibrium framework.

Career

Kydland studied at the Norwegian School of Economics and received his PhD from Carnegie Mellon University in 1973, where Prescott was his adviser. He taught at Carnegie Mellon for many years before moving to UC Santa Barbara.
