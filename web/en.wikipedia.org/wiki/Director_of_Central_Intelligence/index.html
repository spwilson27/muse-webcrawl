Director of Central Intelligence

The Director of Central Intelligence (DCI) was the head of the United States Central Intelligence Agency from 1946 to 2005, acting as the principal intelligence adviser to the President and simultaneously as the nominal head of the wider United States Intelligence Community. The office was created under the National Security Act of 1947 and first held by Rear Admiral Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter (in its statutory form).

History

Notable holders included Allen Dulles, the longest-serving DCI; Richard Helms; William Colby; William J. Casey; William H. Webster; Robert Gates; John Deutch; George Tenet, who served from 1997 to 2004; and Porter Goss, the last holder. The DCI's dual role — running the CIA while coordinating other agencies whose budgets and personnel the DCI did not control — was long criticised as structurally weak.

Abolition

Following the September 11 attacks and the recommendations of the 9/11 Commission, the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004 abolished the position with effect from April 2005. Its community-wide functions passed to the new Director of National Intelligence, and leadership of the CIA to the Director of the Central Intelligence Agency.
