Vegetable oil

Vegetable oil
Main sourcesSoybean, palm, sunflower, rapeseed (canola), olive, peanut, cottonseed
CompositionTriglycerides of fatty acids
UsesCooking, food manufacture, soap, cosmetics, biodiesel

Vegetable oils, or vegetable fats, are oils extracted from seeds or other parts of plants. They are composed mainly of triglycerides and are liquid at room temperature, unlike most animal fats. Vegetable oils are among the world's most important agricultural commodities, exceeded in dietary importance only by cereals.

Production

Oil is recovered by mechanical pressing (expeller or cold pressing) or by solvent extraction, usually with hexane, followed by refining, bleaching and deodorising for neutral cooking oils. Global production is dominated by palm oil (from the fruit of the African oil palm, grown chiefly in Indonesia and Malaysia) and soybean oil, followed by rapeseed (canola) and sunflower oils. Olive oil is produced principally around the Mediterranean by pressing the whole fruit.

Uses

Beyond frying and salad dressings, vegetable oils are used in margarine, shortening (historically via hydrogenation, which creates trans fats now widely restricted), processed foods, soaps, paints, lubricants and biodiesel. The high-oleic and specialty markets include avocado, coconut (a saturated, semi-solid fat), sesame and flaxseed oils.

Health and environment

Nutritionally, vegetable oils are energy-dense but generally rich in unsaturated fatty acids, and olive-oil-rich Mediterranean diets are associated with cardiovascular benefits. Expansion of palm plantations has been linked to deforestation and peatland loss in Southeast Asia, prompting certification schemes and sustainability debates.
