Polski Fiat

Industry: Automotive

Origin: Poland

Parent: FSM / FSO under licence from Fiat

Polski Fiat ("Polish Fiat") was a Polish car brand, created under licence agreements between the Italian manufacturer Fiat and the Polish state. Cars were built by FSO in Warsaw and later by FSM in Bielsko-Biała and Tychy.

Before the Second World War

The brand was established in 1932, when Fiat licensed production of the Fiat 508 Balilla and other models to the Polish state works (PZInż). Production in Warsaw included cars, trucks and military vehicles until the outbreak of war in 1939 destroyed the factories.

Postwar production

The agreement was renewed in 1965, leading to the Polski Fiat 125p, based on the Fiat 125, which entered production at FSO in 1967 and was later sold simply as the FSO 125p after the Fiat licence expired. In 1973 FSM began building the Polski Fiat 126p, based on the Fiat 126. The little rear-engined 126p — nicknamed Maluch ("the toddler") — became the most popular car in Poland, with more than 3.3 million built before production ended in 2000. It motorised the country during the communist era, when private car ownership had previously been rare.

Legacy

After 1989 the Polish car industry was privatised; Fiat itself took over FSM in 1992, and its plants became Fiat Auto Poland, building modern small Fiats. Polski Fiat cars remain cultural icons in Poland, and the 126p is a sought-after classic.
