The Residencia de Estudiantes is a cultural institution and hall of residence in Madrid, founded in 1910 by the Junta para Ampliación de Estudios. Modelled partly on the Oxford and Cambridge collegiate ideal, it became the centre of Spain's Silver Age of culture.

Residents and visitors

Its most famous residents in the 1920s were poet Federico García Lorca, filmmaker Luis Buñuel and artist Salvador Dalí. Visiting lecturers included Albert Einstein, Marie Curie, Igor Stravinsky and Le Corbusier, and the Residencia published major works of the Generation of '27.

Today

Suppressed after the Spanish Civil War, the institution was revived in 1986 and now hosts researchers and creators, organises exhibitions and concerts, and preserves archives of the Spanish intellectual exile. It has been designated a European Heritage Label site.
