Rail transport speed records

The land speed record for rail vehicles is held by a modified French TGV trainset which reached 574.8 km/h (357.2 mph) on 3 April 2007 on the LGV Est line — the absolute world record for a conventional wheeled train on rails. Separate records apply to magnetic levitation (maglev) trains, where a Japanese SCMaglev test vehicle reached 603 km/h in 2015.

History

Speed records date from the earliest railways: the British steam locomotive Mallard reached 203 km/h (126 mph) in 1938, a record for steam traction that still stands. Electric traction took over after the Second World War; French SNCF locomotives broke 300 km/h in 1955, and the TGV programme progressively raised the record — 380 km/h in 1981, 482.6 km/h in 1989 and 515.3 km/h in 1990 — before the 2007 mark.

Maglev records

Maglev vehicles avoid wheel–rail contact. The JR Central SCMaglev MLX01 set the record of 581 km/h in 2003, improved to 603 km/h in April 2015 on the Yamanashi test track, making it the fastest land vehicle carrying passengers. The Shanghai Transrapid operates commercially at up to 431 km/h.

Operational records

In regular service, Chinese high-speed trains on the Beijing–Shanghai line have operated at up to 350 km/h, among the fastest scheduled rail services in the world, while TGV services run at up to 320 km/h.
