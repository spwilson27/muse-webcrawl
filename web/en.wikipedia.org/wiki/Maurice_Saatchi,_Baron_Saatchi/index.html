Maurice Nathan Saatchi, Baron Saatchi (born 21 June 1946) is a British-Iraqi businessman and life peer. With his brother Charles, he founded the advertising agency Saatchi & Saatchi in 1970, which grew into the world's largest advertising agency during the 1980s, and later co-founded M&C Saatchi.

Early life

Saatchi was born in Baghdad to a wealthy Iraqi Jewish family, which fled to London in 1947 amid rising persecution. He graduated from the London School of Economics in 1967 and worked in publishing and advertising before founding Saatchi & Saatchi with his brother Charles.

Advertising career

Saatchi & Saatchi became famous for campaigns such as "Labour Isn't Working" for the Conservative Party in 1979, widely credited with helping Margaret Thatcher's election victory. After a shareholder revolt in 1994, the brothers were forced out and founded M&C Saatchi in 1995, taking many clients with them.

Politics

Saatchi was made a life peer in 1996, taking the title Baron Saatchi of Staplefield. He served as co-chairman of the Conservative Party from 2003 to 2005 under Michael Howard. In the House of Lords he has promoted private member's bills, notably the Medical Innovation Bill, inspired by the death of his wife, novelist Josephine Hart, from ovarian cancer in 2011.
