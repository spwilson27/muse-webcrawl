Jayavarman II

Jayavarman II
Reign: c. 802–835 CE
Known for: Founder of the Khmer Empire
Posthumous name: Paramesvara

Jayavarman II (c. 770–835) is traditionally regarded as the founder of the Khmer Empire in Cambodia. Later inscriptions, notably the 11th-century Sdok Kak Thom stele, credit him with unifying the Khmer principalities and, in 802 CE, proclaiming himself universal monarch (chakravartin) in a ceremony on Phnom Kulen (Mount Mahendra).

Reign

According to tradition, Jayavarman had spent time at the court of Java — possibly as a hostage or in exile — before returning to Chenla. His consecration ritual, performed by the Brahman Hiranyadama, established the devaraja cult, in which the king was identified with the Hindu god Shiva. He moved his capital several times, including to Hariharalaya near the later site of Angkor.

Legacy

The empire he founded endured for over six centuries and produced the monumental temples of Angkor. His posthumous name was Paramesvara.
