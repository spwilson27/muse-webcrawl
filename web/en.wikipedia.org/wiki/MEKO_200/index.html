The MEKO 200 is a class of frigates designed by the German shipbuilder Blohm+Voss (now part of ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems) as part of the MEKO family of modular warships. It is one of the most widely exported frigate designs of the late twentieth century.

Design

The MEKO concept (from Mehrzweck-Kombination, "multi-purpose combination") is based on modularity: weapons, sensors, and combat systems are installed in standardised modules, allowing ships to be configured for each customer and refitted more easily. The MEKO 200 series introduced features such as a steel hull, shock-resistant equipment mounting, and subdivision intended to keep the ship afloat after damage.

Variants and operators

Major variants include the Australian and New Zealand Anzac class, the Turkish Yavuz and Barbaros classes, the Greek Hydra class, and the Portuguese Vasco da Gama class. The enlarged South African Valour class and the Chilean Adelaide-derived ships are sometimes grouped with the related MEKO 200 family developments. Several classes have received extensive mid-life upgrades, including new radars and missile systems.
