Amadis of Gaul

Original title: Amadís de Gaula
Author: Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo
Genre: Chivalric romance
English translation: from the French of Herberay des Essarts; later by Edwin Place and others

Amadis of Gaul is the English-language name and tradition of the Spanish chivalric romance Amadís de Gaula (1508), the story of the peerless knight Amadis and his beloved Oriana. In England the work became known chiefly through the French version of Nicolas de Herberay des Essarts (1540s), and Anthony Munday produced an English translation from the French at the end of the sixteenth century. The romance was one of the best-known foreign fictions in Elizabethan England.

The hero

Amadis, foundling son of King Perión, is the model of the perfect knight: unmatched in arms, constant in love, courteous to all. His exploits against enchanters and giants, his sufferings under Oriana's displeasure as the penitent Beltenebros, and his championing of the Firm Island made "Amadis" a byword for chivalric perfection across Europe; courtiers and poets from Sidney's circle onward invoked him as an ideal.

Reception in English literature

English writers treated Amadis as a touchstone of the romance genre. Edmund Spenser's The Faerie Queene works in the tradition Amadis exemplified, while later critics contrasted his idealised chivalry with the satire of Cervantes's Don Quixote, in which Amadis heads the library of romances that drove the knight mad — yet is the one book saved from burning. Modern English translations, notably Edwin B. Place's, have made the full Spanish text available to anglophone readers.
