Bajaj Group

The Bajaj Group is an Indian multinational conglomerate founded by Jamnalal Bajaj in Mumbai in 1926. Headquartered in Pune, the group comprises dozens of companies operating in automobiles, motorcycles, financial services, insurance, consumer electricals, sugar and steel. Its flagship company, Bajaj Auto, is among the world's largest manufacturers of motorcycles and three-wheelers.

History

Jamnalal Bajaj, a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi, founded the group during India's independence movement. His son Kamalnayan Bajaj expanded the business, and Rahul Bajaj, who led the group for decades, built Bajaj Auto into the maker of the iconic Bajaj Chetak scooter — a household symbol of India's middle class in the licence-raj era. After economic liberalisation in 1991, the group diversified strongly into finance; a 2008 demerger split the conglomerate's auto, finance and holding businesses into separate listed entities.

Principal companies

Group companies include Bajaj Auto, Bajaj Finserv, Bajaj Finance — one of India's largest non-bank financial companies — Bajaj Allianz insurance ventures, Bajaj Electricals, and sugar producer Bajaj Hindusthan. The family remains among India's wealthiest, and the group is known for the Bajaj family's philanthropic trusts and institutions.
