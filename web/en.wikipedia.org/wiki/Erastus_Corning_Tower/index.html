Erastus Corning Tower

Location: Empire State Plaza, Albany, New York
Height: 589 ft (180 m), 44 floors
Completed: 1973
Architect: Wallace Harrison (Harrison & Abramovitz)

The Erastus Corning Tower, also known as the Mayor Erastus Corning 2nd Tower, is a skyscraper in Albany, New York. At 589 feet (180 m) and 44 stories, it is the tallest building in Albany and the tallest in New York State outside New York City. It stands on the west side of the Empire State Plaza, the monumental state government complex built under Governor Nelson Rockefeller.

Architecture and use

Designed by Wallace Harrison of Harrison & Abramovitz, the tower is clad in Vermont pearl marble and glass in a spare modernist style, and houses New York State government offices, principally the Department of Health. It is named for Erastus Corning 2nd, the long-serving mayor of Albany (1942–1983). An observation deck on the 42nd floor, free to the public, offers views of the Hudson River, the Catskills and the Adirondack foothills; unlike most tall buildings it has no 43rd floor numbering quirk but is notable for high-speed elevators serving the deck.

History

The tower was completed in 1973 as part of the Empire State Plaza project, which displaced thousands of residents of downtown Albany neighbourhoods. Its marble cladding has periodically required repair, and large restoration programmes of the plaza have included work on the tower envelope.
