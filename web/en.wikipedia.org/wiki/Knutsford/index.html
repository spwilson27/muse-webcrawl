Knutsford is a market town in Cheshire, England. It lies 14 miles (23 km) south-west of Manchester, 9 miles (14 km) north-west of Macclesfield, and close to the M6 motorway and Manchester Airport. The town is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Cunetesford ("Canute's ford"), traditionally after King Canute, who is said to have forded the River Lily here after a battle. It is perhaps best known as the home of the Victorian novelist Elizabeth Gaskell and as the model for her fictional town of Cranford.

History

Knutsford received a market charter in 1292. Its prosperity in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries rested on coaching traffic, the silk and cotton trades, and its role as a genteel residential town; the narrow streets of the town centre, with their Italianate buildings erected under the patronage of the Egerton family of Tatton Park, date largely from this period. The railway arrived in 1862, making Knutsford a commuter town for Manchester. During the Second World War, General George S. Patton was stationed nearby, and the town hosted American troops and a prisoner-of-war camp.

Landmarks and culture

Tatton Park, a grand neoclassical mansion with extensive deer park, adjoins the town and hosts the RHS Flower Show Tatton Park. Other landmarks include the parish church of St John the Baptist, the Gaskell Memorial Tower, the Ruskin Rooms, and the Knutsford Heritage Centre. An annual May Day festival, dating from 1864, features a procession in which the streets are decorated with patterns sanded onto the pavement — a custom said to originate with King Canute shaking sand from his shoes. Knutsford was also the birthplace of the highwayman Edward "Ned" Higgins and home of mathematician Alan Turing in his later school years' holidays; more recently it is associated with the band The Charlatans' early base in nearby Northwich and with the writer of the Cranford novels.

Governance and transport

Knutsford is administered by Cheshire East Council and its own town council, and lies in the Tatton parliamentary constituency. The railway station is on the Mid-Cheshire line between Manchester Piccadilly and Chester. Junction 19 of the M6 is just east of the town.
