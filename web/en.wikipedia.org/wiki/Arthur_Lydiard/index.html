Arthur Lydiard

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Arthur Lydiard, running coach

Arthur Leslie Lydiard ONZ OBE (6 July 1917 – 11 December 2004) was a New Zealand runner and athletics coach. He has been lauded as one of the outstanding athletics coaches of all time and is credited with popularising the sport of running and making it commonplace across the world.

Lydiard's methods are based on developing a strong aerobic base built through long, steady mileage, which he called "marathon conditioning", before progressing to hill training and anaerobic speed work in a carefully periodised schedule lasting up to roughly 26 weeks before a peak race.

Athletes

His athletes, including Murray Halberg, Peter Snell, Barry Magee, and John Davies, won two gold medals and two bronze medals at the 1960 and 1964 Olympic Games. Snell, Halberg, and Magee finished first, second, and eighth over 10,000 metres at the 1960 Rome Olympics, an iconic achievement prepared on Lydiard's training grounds in the Waitākere Ranges near Auckland.

Jogging

Lydiard also founded the Auckland Jogging Club in 1961, beginning a worldwide movement of recreational jogging for health. In 1967 he introduced his training philosophy to Finland, where it influenced a generation of Finnish distance runners, and he later coached in Mexico, Venezuela, Denmark, and Japan.
