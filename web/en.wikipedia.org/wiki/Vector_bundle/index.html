Vector bundle

In mathematics, a vector bundle is a topological construction that makes precise the idea of a family of vector spaces parameterised by another space X: to every point x of the space X we associate a vector space V(x) in such a way that these vector spaces fit together to form another space of the same kind as X (e.g. a topological space, manifold, or algebraic variety), which is then called a vector bundle over X.

The simplest example is that of a trivial bundle, where the vector space is the same for every point: V(x) = Rn for all x, and the total space is the product X × Rn. A classic non-trivial example is the Möbius strip, which is a line bundle over the circle: the fibre twists as one travels around the base. The tangent bundle of a smooth manifold — which attaches the tangent space at each point — is one of the most important vector bundles in geometry.

Definition

A (real) vector bundle consists of topological spaces E (the total space) and X (the base space) with a continuous surjective map π : E → X (the projection) such that for every x ∈ X the fibre π−1(x) carries the structure of a finite-dimensional vector space, and every point of X has a neighbourhood U over which the bundle is homeomorphic to a product U × Rn in a way compatible with the vector space structures (local triviality). The number n is the rank (or fibre dimension) of the bundle. When the structure group can be reduced to the orthogonal group, the bundle can be given a fibre metric.

Morphisms and constructions

A vector bundle morphism between bundles over the same base is a continuous map of total spaces that covers the identity on the base and restricts to a linear map on each fibre. Standard constructions include direct sums (Whitney sum), tensor products, dual bundles, pullbacks along maps of bases, and quotient bundles. Smooth sections of a vector bundle — continuous choices of a vector in each fibre — form a module over the ring of smooth functions on the base, and for the tangent bundle these are exactly vector fields.

Classification

Vector bundles are organised and distinguished by characteristic classes, such as Stiefel–Whitney classes, Chern classes (in the complex case), and Pontryagin classes, which live in the cohomology of the base space. The study of vector bundles up to stable isomorphism leads to topological K-theory. Over algebraic varieties, vector bundles correspond to locally free sheaves, and on projective space they are governed by results such as the Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem over the projective line.

Examples

The tautological line bundle over projective space, whose fibre over a point (a line through the origin) is the line itself, is the universal example of a line bundle. The cotangent bundle, bundles of differential forms, and the normal bundle of a submanifold are further fundamental examples used throughout differential geometry, algebraic geometry and mathematical physics, where gauge fields are described as connections on principal bundles associated to vector bundles.
