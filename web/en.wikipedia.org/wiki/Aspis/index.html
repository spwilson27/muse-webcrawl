Aspis
Greek: ἀσπίς
Used by: Hoplites
Period: Archaic and Classical Greece
Diameter: ~0.9-1.0 m

An aspis (Ancient Greek: ἀσπίς, plural aspides) was a large, round, heavy wooden shield used by ancient Greek hoplite infantry. Constructed of wood overlaid with bronze, about a metre across and deeply dished, it was held by a double grip — the porpax armband and antilabe handgrip — which distributed its weight across the forearm.

Use in the phalanx

The aspis was central to phalanx warfare: each man's shield overlapped and partly protected his neighbour, so that discipline and cohesion determined survival. Losing one's shield was a disgrace; Spartan mothers are said to have told their sons to return "with it or on it".

Decoration

Shield faces were painted with blazons identifying the city or the bearer — the lambda of Sparta, the owl of Athens, or individual devices such as gorgoneia meant to terrify opponents.
