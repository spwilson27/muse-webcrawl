Remote work

Remote work (also called telecommuting, telework, work from home, or distributed work) is the practice of employees doing their jobs from a location other than a central office operated by the employer. Such locations can include an employee's home, a co-working space, or a café, enabled by technologies such as email, instant messaging, videoconferencing, and cloud computing.

History

The term "telecommuting" was coined in 1973 by Jack Nilles, who proposed telecommunications as a substitute for commuting during the oil crisis era. Remote work grew steadily with the spread of broadband internet in the 2000s and 2010s, then expanded dramatically in 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic forced offices to close worldwide. Many employers subsequently adopted hybrid models combining office and remote days.

Advantages and disadvantages

Proponents cite eliminated commutes, greater flexibility, lower overhead for employers, and access to a wider talent pool. Critics point to social isolation, difficulties in collaboration and mentoring, blurred work–life boundaries, and managerial concerns about monitoring productivity. Studies of productivity effects have produced mixed results depending on the job and the worker.
