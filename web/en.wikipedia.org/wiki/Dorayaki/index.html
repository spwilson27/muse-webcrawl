Dorayaki

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dorayaki (どら焼き) is a Japanese confection consisting of two small, round, castella-like pancakes wrapped around a filling of sweet red bean paste made from azuki beans. It is a popular wagashi throughout Japan and is famous as the favourite food of the manga and anime character Doraemon.

Name and history

The name comes from dora, Japanese for a gong, which the finished sweet resembles in shape. A popular legend tells of a samurai — sometimes identified as Saitō no Musashibō Benkei — who left his gong behind at a farmer's house, where it was used to fry the first dorayaki. The modern two-pancake form is credited to the Tokyo confectioner Usagiya in the early 20th century; earlier versions had a single folded layer.

Variations

Fillings now include custard, whipped cream, matcha cream, chestnut, and chocolate, and regional variants differ in pancake texture. A larger version called mikasa is sold in Nara. Dorayaki batter is typically made with eggs, sugar, honey, and flour, cooked on a griddle until golden, giving the pancakes a soft, honeyed crumb.
