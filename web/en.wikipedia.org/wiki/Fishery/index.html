Fishery

A fishery (plural fisheries) is an organised effort by humans to catch fish and other aquatic species, an activity known as fishing. A fishery typically deals with the capture of a defined stock or group of stocks (for example, "the North Sea cod fishery"), and the term also covers the places where fishing occurs and the industry built around it. Fisheries include commercial, artisanal, subsistence, and recreational types, in marine and fresh waters, and are complemented by fish farming (aquaculture), which by the 2020s produced more aquatic animals for human consumption than capture fisheries.


Commercial fishing vessels; world capture fisheries land roughly 90 million tonnes per year.

Scale and importance

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), global capture fisheries production is on the order of 90 million tonnes annually, with China, Indonesia, Peru, India, Russia, the United States, and Vietnam among the top producers. Fisheries and aquaculture together employ tens of millions of people — the great majority of them in small-scale and artisanal operations in developing countries — and fish supply about a fifth of the animal protein intake of billions of people. Major fisheries target anchoveta, Alaska pollock, skipjack tuna, and other high-volume species.

Management

Because fish stocks are a common-pool resource, unregulated fisheries tend toward overfishing. Fisheries management applies the results of fisheries science — stock assessment, population dynamics, and the concept of maximum sustainable yield — through tools such as total allowable catches, individual transferable quotas, closed seasons and areas, gear restrictions (mesh sizes), and marine protected areas. International fisheries in shared waters are coordinated through regional fisheries management organisations (RFMOs) and the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.

Problems

  • Overfishing: the FAO estimates that the share of stocks fished at biologically unsustainable levels has risen to roughly a third or more of assessed stocks.
  • Bycatch and discards: unintended catch of juveniles, seabirds, turtles, and marine mammals.
  • Illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing, subsidised fleet overcapacity, and destructive gear such as bottom trawls on sensitive habitats.
  • Climate change, which is shifting stock distributions poleward and across management boundaries.
  • Historic stock collapses, such as the Newfoundland northern cod moratorium of 1992, illustrate the social cost of management failure.
