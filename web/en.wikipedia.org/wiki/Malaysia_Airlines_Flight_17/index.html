Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 (MH17) was a scheduled passenger flight from Amsterdam to Kuala Lumpur that was shot down on 17 July 2014 over eastern Ukraine during the war in Donbas. All 283 passengers and 15 crew were killed. Contact with the aircraft, a Boeing 777-200ER, was lost when it was about 50 km (31 mi) from the Ukraine–Russia border, and wreckage fell near Hrabove in Donetsk Oblast, in territory controlled by pro-Russian separatists.

Investigation

The Dutch Safety Board concluded in October 2015 that the aircraft was destroyed by a Buk surface-to-air missile launched from an area controlled by pro-Russian fighters. The subsequent criminal investigation by the Dutch-led Joint Investigation Team (JIT) determined that the Buk system had been transported from Russia on the day of the shootdown and returned to Russia afterwards, and that it belonged to the Russian 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade.

Criminal trial

Four suspects — three Russians and one Ukrainian — were tried in absentia in the Netherlands. On 17 November 2022, the district court in The Hague convicted three of the men of murder and of causing the crash, sentencing them to life imprisonment; the fourth was acquitted. Russia has denied involvement and rejected the findings of the investigations.

Victims and aftermath

Among the victims were 193 Dutch nationals, 43 Malaysians, 27 Australians, and delegates travelling to an international AIDS conference in Melbourne, including researcher Joep Lange. The shootdown intensified international condemnation and sanctions against Russia, and led to lasting changes in how civil aviation assesses the risk of flying over conflict zones.
