Pokémon Red Version and Pokémon Blue Version are 1996 role-playing video games developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo for the Game Boy. They are the first instalments of the Pokémon video game series: released in Japan as Pocket Monsters Red and Pocket Monsters Green (with a special Blue version following), they launched internationally in 1998. The player travels the fictional Kanto region, catching and training 151 species of Pokémon to become Champion of the Pokémon League while thwarting the criminal organisation Team Rocket.

North American box art featuring Charizard (Red) and Blastoise (Blue).

Gameplay

Players choose a starter Pokémon — Bulbasaur, Charmander, or Squirtle — and capture wild Pokémon using Poké Balls. Battles are turn-based. Version-exclusive species encourage trading between cartridges via the Game Link Cable, a design inspired by creator Satoshi Tajiri's childhood interest in insect collecting. Completing the Pokédex of all 151 species, including the hidden Mew, is a central goal.

Reception and legacy

The games sold over 31 million copies combined, among the best-selling video games of all time, and ignited the worldwide "Pokémania" phenomenon. They were remade as Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen (2004) for Game Boy Advance and again reimagined in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Eevee! (2018). Famous glitches such as MissingNo. became part of gaming folklore.
