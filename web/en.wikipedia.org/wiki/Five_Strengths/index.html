Five Strengths

The Five Strengths (Sanskrit: pañcabalāni; Pali: pañca balāni) are one of the sets of qualities grouped among the thirty-seven aids to awakening (bodhipakkhiyādhammā) in Buddhism. When the five faculties (indriya) — faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration and wisdom — are developed and balanced to the point of becoming unshakable by their opposites, they are called the five strengths or powers.

The five strengths

Faith (śraddhā) — confidence in the Buddha's awakening and the teaching; as a strength it is unshaken by lack of faith or by false teachings.

Energy (vīriya) — vigorous effort in abandoning unwholesome states and cultivating wholesome ones, unshakeable by laziness.

Mindfulness (smṛti) — recollected awareness of body, feelings, mind and phenomena, unshaken by forgetfulness or inattention.

Concentration (samādhi) — collectedness and unification of mind, unshaken by distraction.

Wisdom (prajñā) — discernment of arising and passing away, unshaken by delusion.

Balancing the faculties

Buddhist commentary stresses that the strengths must be developed in balance: faith is balanced by wisdom (so that confidence is not blind), and energy by concentration (so that effort does not produce restlessness, nor calm produce sloth). Mindfulness, useful in all circumstances, protects the balance of the others. The same five qualities appear in Mahayana sources as well, and the strengths are said to be called "powers" because they cannot be overpowered by their opposing defilements.
