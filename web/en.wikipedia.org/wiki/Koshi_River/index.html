The Koshi River (Nepali: कोशी नदी) is a transboundary river flowing through Tibet, Nepal and India. It is formed by the confluence of three main streams — the Sun Koshi, the Arun and the Tamor — at Tribeni in eastern Nepal, and is accordingly called the Saptakoshi ("seven Koshis") for its seven upper tributaries in the Himalayas. It joins the Ganges in Bihar, India.

Course

The Arun, rising in Tibet, is the largest of the tributaries and cuts a deep gorge through the Himalaya between Everest and Makalu. After the Tribeni confluence, the Koshi flows south through the Chatra gorge, debouches onto the plains of the Terai and Bihar, and builds one of the largest alluvial megafans in the world — roughly 180 km long and 150 km wide.

Flooding — the "Sorrow of Bihar"

The Koshi carries an enormous sediment load, and its channel has migrated westward by more than 100 km over the past two centuries, inundating vast areas. Catastrophic floods have earned it the name "Sorrow of Bihar". In August 2008 a breach in the eastern embankment at Kusaha in Nepal diverted most of the river into an old channel, flooding districts in Nepal and Bihar and displacing millions.

Koshi Treaty and barrage

Under the Koshi Agreement of 1954 (revised 1966) between Nepal and India, the Koshi Barrage at Bhimnagar was built for flood control and irrigation. Water-sharing and embankment maintenance on the river remain recurring subjects of negotiation between the two countries, and a proposed high dam in Nepal has been debated for decades.
