Stormtroopers (Imperial Germany)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stormtroopers (German: Stoßtruppen or Sturmtruppen) were specialist assault soldiers of the Imperial German Army in the First World War. In the last years of the war, German units trained in "infiltration tactics" were formed into dedicated storm battalions, intended to break the deadlock of trench warfare by bypassing strongpoints, disrupting communications, and attacking command posts and artillery positions in the enemy's rear.

Development

The concept grew from experiments in 1915, including the assault detachment of Captain Willy Rohr, which developed combined-arms tactics using machine guns, flamethrowers, trench mortars, grenades, and light artillery working in close coordination. Under officers such as General Oskar von Hutier, the methods matured into the infiltration tactics used on the Eastern Front at Riga in 1917 and then on a massive scale in the German spring offensives of 1918 (Operation Michael and its sequels).

Tactics and equipment

Stormtroopers were typically young, fit volunteers, given better rations and extensive training. They attacked in small, self-contained squads after short, intense artillery preparations (often without prior registration), infiltrating between enemy positions rather than assaulting them head-on. Characteristic equipment included the MP 18 submachine gun, bundles of stick grenades, wire cutters, and light machine guns such as the MG 08/15. Their early successes in 1918 gained much ground but could not be sustained logistically, and the offensives ultimately failed.

Legacy

The stormtrooper concept influenced interwar infantry doctrine in many armies and German tactics in the Second World War. The name was later adopted — with a very different meaning — by the Nazi Party's Sturmabteilung (SA) paramilitary organization.
