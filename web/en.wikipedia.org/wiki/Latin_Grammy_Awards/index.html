Latin Grammy Awards

The Latin Grammy trophy

The Latin Grammy Awards (in Spanish, Premios Grammy Latinos) are annual awards presented by the Latin Recording Academy to recognize outstanding achievement in the Latin music industry, primarily for works recorded in Spanish or Portuguese. Often described as the Latin counterpart of the Grammy Awards, they were established in 2000, when the first ceremony was held on September 13, 2000, at the Staples Center in Los Angeles and broadcast on CBS.

History

The Latin Recording Academy was formed in 1997 by the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences as its first international venture, in response to the growth of Latin music in the United States and worldwide. The first Latin Grammys featured performances and awards across a growing number of categories spanning pop, rock, tropical, regional Mexican, Brazilian, jazz, and urban music.

Ceremony and categories

The awards are voted on by members of the Latin Recording Academy, comprising music professionals from Latin America, Spain, Portugal, and the United States. The most prestigious general-field awards are Album of the Year, Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best New Artist. The ceremony has usually been held in November, most often in Las Vegas, and is broadcast in the United States by Univision. In 2023 the ceremony was held outside the United States for the first time, in Seville, Spain.

Latin Grammy Hall of Fame and special awards

The Latin Recording Academy also presents special honours, including the Person of the Year award, Lifetime Achievement Awards, and the Latin Grammy Hall of Fame, which recognises historically significant recordings at least 25 years old.
