Lech Wałęsa (born 29 September 1943) is a Polish statesman, dissident and human rights activist who co-founded Solidarity (Solidarność), the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983 and served as President of Poland from 1990 to 1995.

Solidarity

Wałęsa worked as an electrician at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk. In August 1980 he climbed the shipyard fence to join and lead a strike that won the Gdańsk Agreement, legalizing free trade unions. Solidarity grew to some ten million members. When the communist government imposed martial law in December 1981, Wałęsa was interned for eleven months.

Nobel Prize and round table

Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983, Wałęsa remained the symbol of Polish resistance. Renewed strikes in 1988 forced the regime into the Round Table Talks of 1989, leading to semi-free elections and the first non-communist government in the Eastern bloc.

Presidency and later life

Wałęsa won the 1990 presidential election but lost his re-election bid in 1995 to Aleksander Kwaśniewski. His presidency oversaw Poland's transition to a market economy and its reorientation toward the West. He has remained an outspoken public figure; allegations concerning his 1970s contacts with the communist security service have been disputed and politically controversial.
