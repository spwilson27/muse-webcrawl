Tana River (Kenya)

LengthAbout 1,000 km (620 mi)
SourceAberdare Range / Mount Kenya region
MouthIndian Ocean at Ungwana Bay
BasinLargest river basin in Kenya

The Tana River is the longest river in Kenya, flowing roughly 1,000 kilometres from the Aberdare Range and the slopes of Mount Kenya eastward across the country's dry interior to the Indian Ocean near Ungwana Bay. It gives its name to Tana River County.

Course and use

Along its upper course the river is dammed in the Seven Forks scheme — including Masinga, Kamburu, Gitaru, Kindaruma, and Kiambere reservoirs — which together supply a large share of Kenya's hydroelectric power and irrigation water for the capital region. Downstream, the river supports farming and pastoralist communities of the Pokomo, Orma, and Somali peoples.

Ecology

The lower Tana's floodplain forests are among Kenya's most important remaining riverine ecosystems, home to endemic primates such as the Tana River red colobus and the Tana River mangabey. The Tana River Primate Reserve was established to protect them.
