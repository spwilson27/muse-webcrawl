Vasily Vasilyevich Andreyev (Russian: Василий Васильевич Андреев; 27 January 1861 – 26 December 1918) was a Russian musician, composer, and virtuoso performer on the balalaika. He is best known as the founder of the first orchestra of Russian folk instruments and for transforming the balalaika from a peasant folk instrument into a concert instrument with a standardised family of sizes.

Life and career

Andreyev was born in Bezhetsk, in Tver Governorate, into a merchant family. Trained initially on the violin, he encountered the balalaika while visiting his family's country estate, where he heard peasant musicians playing the simple three-stringed triangular instrument. Fascinated by its possibilities, he set about improving its construction and technique.

Working with the St. Petersburg instrument makers, Andreyev developed a chromatic balalaika and a whole family of instruments in different sizes — prima, sekunda, alto, bass, and contrabass — modelled on the string quartet. In 1888 he founded the Circle of Balalaika Players, which grew into the Great Russian Orchestra, the first professional orchestra of Russian folk instruments.

Legacy

The orchestra toured widely in Russia and abroad, including performances in Europe and the United States, popularising the balalaika internationally. Andreyev composed and arranged numerous pieces for the ensemble and championed the domra and gusli alongside the balalaika. The State Academic Russian Orchestra in St. Petersburg now bears his name.
