Texture (geology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In geology, texture refers to the physical appearance or character of a rock, determined by the size, shape, and arrangement of its constituent grains or crystals. Together with mineral composition and structure, texture is a primary criterion for classifying rocks, and it records information about the rock's formation history.

Igneous textures

Igneous textures reflect cooling history. Slow cooling underground yields coarse-grained phaneritic textures; rapid cooling at the surface yields fine-grained aphanitic textures; porphyritic texture (large phenocrysts in a fine matrix) records two-stage cooling. Glassy texture forms when lava quenches too fast for crystals to grow, and vesicular texture preserves gas bubbles, as in pumice and scoria.

Sedimentary and metamorphic textures

Sedimentary textures describe grain size, sorting, rounding, and packing of clasts, indicating transport and depositional environment. Metamorphic textures include foliated arrangements (slaty, schistose, gneissic) produced by directed pressure, and non-foliated (granoblastic) textures in rocks such as marble and quartzite.
