Anne Isabella Noel Blunt, 15th Baroness Wentworth (née King-Noel; 22 September 1837 – 15 December 1917), known as Lady Anne Blunt, was a British traveller, artist and horse breeder. A granddaughter of Lord Byron, she is celebrated for journeys through Arabia in the 1870s recorded in Bedouin Tribes of the Euphrates (1879) and A Pilgrimage to Nejd (1881), and for co-founding, with her husband Wilfrid Scawen Blunt, the Crabbet Arabian Stud in Sussex and the Sheykh Obeyd stud near Cairo.

Travels

Fluent in Arabic and an accomplished watercolourist, Lady Anne travelled with her husband from Syria through Mesopotamia to Baghdad, and in 1879–80 crossed the Nafud desert to Ha'il in the Nejd — among the first Europeans, and reputedly the first European woman, to make the journey. Her detailed journals, edited by her husband for publication, recorded tribal politics, geography and the Arabian horses they sought.

Crabbet Stud

The horses the Blunts imported from Arabia and Egypt in 1878 and later years founded the Crabbet Stud, whose bloodlines influenced Arabian horse breeding worldwide; a large share of modern purebred Arabians trace some ancestry to Crabbet horses. After her marriage broke down, Lady Anne lived permanently at Sheykh Obeyd in Egypt from 1911 until her death in 1917. Her daughter Judith Blunt-Lytton, 16th Baroness Wentworth, continued the stud.
