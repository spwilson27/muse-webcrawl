Eleanor Coppola

Eleanor Coppola
Born: May 4, 1936, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Died: April 12, 2024, Rutherford, California, U.S.
Occupation: Filmmaker, artist, writer
Spouse: Francis Ford Coppola

Eleanor Jessie Coppola (née Neil; May 4, 1936 – April 12, 2024) was an American documentary filmmaker, artist and writer. She was the wife of director Francis Ford Coppola and the mother of filmmakers Sofia Coppola and Roman Coppola.

Career

Coppola is best known for the documentary Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker's Apocalypse (1991), which chronicles the troubled production of her husband's film Apocalypse Now in the Philippines. Shot from footage she recorded on set in 1976–77 together with her diary of the production, the film, co-directed with Fax Bahr and George Hickenlooper, won an Emmy Award and is widely regarded as one of the finest documentaries about filmmaking. Her book Notes: The Making of Apocalypse Now (1979) was published during the production.

In later life she turned to narrative filmmaking, writing and directing the romantic comedy Paris Can Wait (2016), starring Diane Lane — making her, at 80, one of the oldest first-time feature directors — and Love Is Love Is Love (2020). She also worked as an installation and conceptual artist and wrote a memoir, Notes on a Life (2008).

Personal life

She met Francis Ford Coppola while working as an assistant art director on his directorial debut Dementia 13 (1963); they married in 1963. The couple lived in Napa Valley, California, where the family operates the Inglenook winery. Their eldest son, Gian-Carlos Coppola, died in a boating accident in 1986. Eleanor Coppola died at her home in Rutherford, California, in April 2024, aged 87.
