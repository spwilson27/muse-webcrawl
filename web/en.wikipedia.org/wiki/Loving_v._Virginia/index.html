Loving v. Virginia

Full case nameRichard Perry Loving, et ux. v. Virginia
DecidedJune 12, 1967
CourtSupreme Court of the United States
Citation388 U.S. 1
HoldingLaws banning interracial marriage violate the Equal Protection and Due Process Clauses of the Fourteenth Amendment

Loving v. Virginia, 388 U.S. 1 (1967), is a landmark civil rights decision of the United States Supreme Court that ruled state laws prohibiting interracial marriage unconstitutional. The case involved Mildred Loving, a woman of African American and Native American ancestry, and Richard Loving, a white man, who were criminally charged in Virginia for marrying in the District of Columbia in 1958 and returning home to Virginia, where their marriage was prohibited by the state's Racial Integrity Act of 1924.

Background

After pleading guilty to violating Virginia's ban, the Lovings were sentenced to a year in prison, with the sentence suspended for 25 years on condition they leave the state. The trial judge, Leon M. Bazile, wrote that God had created the races and placed them on separate continents, and that the fact He separated them showed he did not intend for them to mix. The Lovings moved to Washington, D.C., and in 1963, prompted in part by the civil rights movement and a near-fatal car accident involving their son, Mildred wrote to Attorney General Robert F. Kennedy, who referred her to the ACLU. Lawyers Bernard S. Cohen and Philip J. Hirschkop took the case through the courts.

Decision

On June 12, 1967, the Supreme Court unanimously struck down Virginia's law. Chief Justice Earl Warren's opinion held that marriage is "one of the vital personal rights essential to the orderly pursuit of happiness by free men" and "one of the 'basic civil rights of man'", and that restricting it on the basis of racial classifications violated the Equal Protection Clause and deprived the Lovings of liberty without due process. The decision invalidated the anti-miscegenation statutes remaining in 16 states.

Legacy

Loving has frequently been cited in later cases on marriage rights, including Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), which legalized same-sex marriage nationwide. June 12 is celebrated informally as "Loving Day". The story was dramatized in the 2016 film Loving, directed by Jeff Nichols. June 12, 1967 remained the date the last state bans on interracial marriage became unenforceable, though Alabama did not formally remove its (unenforceable) constitutional ban until 2000.
