Franco-Provençal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Franco-Provençal (also Francoprovençal, Patois, or Arpitan) is a Romance language spoken in east-central France, western Switzerland, and north-western Italy (the Aosta Valley and parts of Piedmont). It forms the third historical branch of the Gallo-Romance languages, alongside the langues d'oïl (including French) to the north and Occitan (langues d'oc) to the south, sharing features with both — hence the compound name coined by the Italian linguist Graziadio Isaia Ascoli in 1873.

Characteristics

Franco-Provençal never developed a single standard written form or a political centre; it remained a mosaic of local dialects (called simply patois by their speakers) centred on old cities such as Lyon, Grenoble, Geneva, Lausanne, and Aosta. Among its distinctive traits are the palatalisation of Latin /ka/ and /ga/, forms of the definite article, and extensive sound changes in stressed vowels that differ from village to village. Because it lacks a standard, it has often been classified as a group of dialects rather than a language.

Status

All varieties are severely endangered: transmission to children largely ceased during the 20th century as French (and, in Italy, Italian) became universal. Estimates of remaining speakers range from roughly 60,000 to 200,000, mostly older people; the Aosta Valley, where French itself is co-official and local dialect (valdôtain) retains prestige, is a relative stronghold. UNESCO lists Franco-Provençal as definitely to severely endangered. A modern standardised orthography (ORB, Orthographe de référence B) and the promoted name "Arpitan" (from Arpa, the Alps) support a small revival movement.
