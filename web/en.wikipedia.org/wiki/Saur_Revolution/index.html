Saur Revolution

The Saur Revolution (also spelled Sawr; from Saur, the second month of the Persian calendar used in Afghanistan) was a coup d'état on 27–28 April 1978 in which the People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA) overthrew the government of President Mohammed Daoud Khan, who was killed with most of his family. It established the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan under Nur Muhammad Taraki and set in train the upheavals that led to the Soviet invasion of 1979 and decades of war.

Background

Daoud had himself seized power from King Zahir Shah in 1973, proclaiming a republic, and initially relied on PDPA support before turning against the left. The funeral of a murdered PDPA ideologue, Mir Akbar Khyber, drew huge protests in April 1978; Daoud's arrest of PDPA leaders prompted the party's military sympathisers to strike first.

The coup

Armoured units and air force jets attacked the Arg palace in Kabul; after heavy fighting the palace fell and Daoud was shot. The PDPA's two factions, Khalq and Parcham, briefly united in government, with Taraki as president and Hafizullah Amin rising to power. The new regime proclaimed sweeping reforms — land redistribution, literacy campaigns, changes to marriage law and the emancipation of women — imposed abruptly on a deeply traditional, religious society.

Consequences

The reforms and the regime's violent repression of opponents sparked rural revolts and army mutinies through 1978–79. Taraki was murdered on Amin's orders in October 1979; in December the Soviet Union invaded, killed Amin, and installed Babrak Karmal, beginning the Soviet–Afghan War.
