Émile Benveniste (27 May 1902 – 3 October 1976) was a French linguist, one of the leading figures of twentieth-century structural linguistics and Indo-European studies. His work on the grammar of Indo-European languages, the vocabulary of Indo-European institutions, and the theory of language in discourse (énonciation) profoundly influenced linguistics, anthropology, and French theory.

Life

Benveniste was born in Aleppo, then in the Ottoman Empire, to a Sephardic Jewish family, and educated in Paris, where he studied under Antoine Meillet at the École pratique des hautes études. In 1937 he succeeded Meillet as chair of comparative grammar at the Collège de France. Arrested under Vichy France, he escaped and spent part of the war in Switzerland. He returned to his chair in 1945 and taught until a stroke in 1969 ended his career; he died in 1976.

Work

His early masterworks addressed Indo-European morphology: Origines de la formation des noms en indo-européen (1935) and Noms d'agent et noms d'action en indo-européen (1948). The essays collected in Problèmes de linguistique générale (Problems in General Linguistics, 1966; second volume 1974) developed his celebrated theory of subjectivity in language, distinguishing the semiotic from the semantic, analysing personal pronouns ("I" and "you" as positions in discourse), and contrasting histoire and discours as modes of utterance. Le Vocabulaire des institutions indo-européennes (1969; English: Indo-European Language and Society) reconstructed the social vocabulary of early Indo-European speakers — kingship, law, economy, kinship — and remains a classic of historical anthropology.
