Diepkloof Rock Shelter is a rock shelter in the Western Cape, South Africa, in which has been preserved some of the earliest known evidence of the human use of symbols, in the form of patterns engraved upon ostrich eggshell water containers. The shelter, overlooking the Verlorenvlei River about 17 km from the coast near Elands Bay, preserves a long Middle Stone Age sequence spanning roughly 130,000 to 45,000 years ago.

Engraved eggshells

More than 270 fragments of ostrich eggshell containers, dated to around 60,000 years ago and associated with the Howiesons Poort industry, bear deliberately engraved geometric patterns — hatched bands, parallel lines and cross-hatched motifs. Because similar motifs recur across many fragments, researchers interpret them as a shared graphic tradition: a rule-bound system of representation, and evidence that the containers communicated information about their owners or contents. The eggshells are regarded as some of the earliest unambiguous evidence of symbolic behaviour by anatomically modern humans.

Sequence and research

Excavations led by Christopher Henshilwood, Pierre-Jean Texier and colleagues documented industries including the Still Bay, known for finely made bifacial points, as well as evidence for early heat treatment of stone and the use of compound adhesives. In 2024 Diepkloof was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the serial site "The Emergence of Modern Human Behaviour: The Pleistocene Occupation Sites of South Africa", together with Blombos Cave and Sibhudu Cave.
