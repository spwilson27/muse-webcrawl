Juncaceae

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: common rush, Juncus]

Juncaceae is a family of flowering plants, commonly known as the rush family. It consists of 8 genera and about 464 known species of slow-growing, rhizomatous, herbaceous monocotyledonous plants that superficially resemble grasses and sedges.

Description

Rushes typically grow in wet, nutrient-poor soils and wetlands. They usually have round, solid or pith-filled stems and small, inconspicuous flowers with six tepals, pollinated by wind. The largest genus is Juncus (the true rushes), with around 300 species; Luzula, the woodrushes, is the second largest.

Uses

The pith of some Juncus species was historically used for rushlights, and the stems have been woven into mats (such as Japanese tatami coverings from Juncus effusus) and chair seats.
