Kala namak

Kala namak (Hindi: काला नमक, "black salt") is a kiln-fired rock salt used extensively in South Asian cuisines. It is manufactured from the raw natural salt sanchal and has a distinctive sulphurous, egg-like smell and taste caused by its hydrogen sulfide content. Despite the name, the finished ground salt is usually pinkish-grey to violet rather than black.

Production

Traditionally, raw salt is sealed in ceramic jars with charcoal and small quantities of harad seeds, amla, bahera bark or natron, then fired in a kiln for up to 24 hours. Under these reducing conditions, sodium sulfate in the raw salt is partly converted to sodium sulfide and hydrogen sulfide, and iron compounds give the lumps their dark colour. The salt melts, reacts, and is cooled and aged before sale. Modern production often uses furnaces and added sodium sulfate.

Culinary use

Kala namak is a key ingredient in chaat masala and is sprinkled on chaat, raitas, chutneys, salads, fruit, and drinks such as jaljeera and shikanji. Because of its striking resemblance to the taste of boiled eggs, it is popular in vegan cooking to imitate egg flavour in tofu scrambles and similar dishes. In Ayurvedic tradition it is considered a cooling spice and digestive aid.
