Net zero emissions

Net zero emissions refers to a state in which the greenhouse gases released into the atmosphere by human activity are balanced by greenhouse gases removed from the atmosphere. Achieving net zero is a central goal of international climate policy: the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has concluded that limiting warming to 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels requires global carbon dioxide emissions to reach net zero around 2050, with net zero for all greenhouse gases following later in the century.

Definitions

A target of "net zero" differs from "zero emissions": sources that are difficult to eliminate entirely (such as some agricultural or industrial emissions) may continue, provided they are offset by removals — through reforestation, ecosystem restoration, or technologies such as direct air capture with geological storage. Closely related terms include carbon neutrality (usually limited to carbon dioxide) and climate neutrality. The narrower goal of net zero is discussed in the context of energy systems and buildings as well.

Pledges and policy

Under the Paris Agreement (2015), parties aim to balance emissions and removals in the second half of the century. Since 2019 many countries, cities, and companies have adopted explicit net zero targets — frequently 2050, with China pledging carbon neutrality before 2060 and India by 2070. Frameworks such as the Science Based Targets initiative and ISO standards seek to define credible target-setting and limit reliance on offsets.

Pathways

IPCC and International Energy Agency scenarios emphasise rapid deployment of renewable energy, electrification of transport and heating, energy efficiency, halting deforestation, and reductions in methane, with carbon dioxide removal playing a supporting role for residual emissions. Critics warn that vague long-term pledges and heavy dependence on offsets or future removal technologies can delay near-term emissions cuts.
