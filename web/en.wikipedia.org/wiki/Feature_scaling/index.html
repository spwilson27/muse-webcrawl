Feature scaling

Feature scaling is a method used to normalise the range of independent variables or features of data. In data processing, it is also known as data normalisation and is generally performed during the data preprocessing step of machine learning. Scaling is important because features measured on very different scales otherwise dominate distance calculations and gradient-based optimisation, so algorithms such as k-nearest neighbours, support vector machines and neural networks usually perform better after scaling.

Rescaling (min-max normalisation)

Rescaling transforms values to a fixed range, commonly [0, 1], using the minimum and maximum of the feature:

x' = (x − min(x)) / (max(x) − min(x))

Standardisation (z-score normalisation)

Standardisation rescales a feature to have a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one:

x' = (x − μ) / σ

where μ is the mean and σ the standard deviation of the feature in the training data.

Other methods

Other approaches include mean normalisation, scaling to unit length (making each sample a unit vector), robust scaling using the median and interquartile range to reduce the effect of outliers, and non-linear transformations. The same transformation fitted on the training set must be applied to validation and test data to avoid data leakage.
