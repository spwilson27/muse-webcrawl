Regional lockout

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Publishers have divided the world into regions for games, DVDs and streaming media

Regional lockout (or region coding) is a class of digital rights management preventing the use of certain products or services, such as multimedia or a hardware device, outside a certain region or territory. A regional lockout may be enforced through physical means, through technological means such as detecting the user's IP address or using an identifying code, or through unintentional means introduced by devices only supporting certain regional technologies (such as video formats).

Reasons for use

Regional lockout is generally used to enforce different release dates in different regions, to allow price discrimination between markets, to comply with differing censorship or licensing laws, and to protect exclusive distribution agreements between publishers and regional distributors. In the video game industry, regional lockout was also historically tied to incompatible television standards (NTSC, PAL and SECAM).

Applications

Video games

Most home video game consoles released before the 2010s employed some form of regional lockout, implemented through cartridge shape, lockout chips (such as Nintendo's CIC in the NES and SNES), or disc region codes as on the PlayStation and Xbox families. Handheld systems such as the Game Boy were traditionally region-free. From the eighth generation onward, region-free hardware became the norm, though publishers may still restrict downloadable content and store accounts by region.

DVD and Blu-ray

DVD-Video discs are divided among up to eight regions, and a player will only play discs coded for its region (unless modified). Blu-ray Discs use three regions (A, B and C), while Ultra HD Blu-ray is region-free.

Streaming and software

Streaming services use geoblocking based on IP address to enforce territorial licensing. Printer manufacturers have used region coding in ink and toner cartridges, and smartphone and software activation systems sometimes restrict features by country.

Criticism and circumvention

Regional lockout is criticised for inconveniencing legitimate consumers, restricting imports, and enabling price discrimination. Circumvention methods include modchips, region-free players, imported hardware and virtual private networks; their legality varies by jurisdiction and is connected to laws against circumventing technological protection measures.
