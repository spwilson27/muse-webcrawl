Municipalities of Norway

Municipalities (kommuner) are the lowest level of public administration in Norway, below the counties (fylker). Following the regional reform that took effect in 2020, Norway has 356 municipalities (357 from 2024 with further county changes), each run by an elected municipal council headed by a mayor.

Responsibilities

Municipalities deliver most front-line welfare services: primary and lower-secondary schooling, kindergartens, primary health care, care for the elderly, water and sewage, local planning, and cultural services. They are financed by income taxes, state grants, and user fees.

Variation in size

Oslo, the largest municipality, has around 700,000 inhabitants and doubles as a county. The smallest, Utsira, has only about 200 residents. Many municipalities cover large rural or mountainous areas with sparse populations, and amalgamation of small municipalities has been a recurring policy debate.

History

Local self-government was established by the Formannskap laws of 1837, which created municipalities out of the old parishes. The number peaked at 747 in the 1930s before successive merger reforms reduced it.
