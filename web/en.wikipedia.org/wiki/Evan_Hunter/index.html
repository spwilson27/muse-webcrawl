Evan Hunter (born Salvatore Albert Lombino; October 15, 1926 – July 6, 2005) was an American author and screenwriter best known under the pen name Ed McBain. Under that name he created the 87th Precinct series of police procedural novels, one of the longest-running and most influential crime series in the genre.

Career

Lombino legally changed his name to Evan Hunter in 1952. He first achieved fame with the novel The Blackboard Jungle (1954), drawn from his experience teaching in a New York vocational school; the 1955 film adaptation helped popularize rock and roll with Bill Haley's "Rock Around the Clock." Under the McBain name, beginning with Cop Hater (1956), he wrote more than fifty 87th Precinct novels set in the fictional city of Isola, centred on detective Steve Carella and an ensemble cast — a model of ensemble realism widely credited with shaping modern police fiction and television procedurals.

Hunter also wrote the screenplay for Alfred Hitchcock's The Birds (1963), numerous standalone novels under his own name (including A Matter of Conviction and the Matthew Hope mystery series), children's books, and the novelization of The Birds' thematic successor projects. He received the Mystery Writers of America's Grand Master Award in 1986 — the first American so honoured since the 1970s.
