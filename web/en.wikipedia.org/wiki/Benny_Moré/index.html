Bartolomé Maximiliano Gutiérrez Moré (August 24, 1919 – February 19, 1963), better known as Benny Moré, was a Cuban singer, bandleader and songwriter. He is regarded as the greatest Cuban singer of all time, celebrated for his expressive tenor voice, instinctive musicianship and effortless command of every Cuban genre — son cubano, mambo, bolero, guaracha, cha-cha-chá and Afro-Cuban jazz. Cubans called him El Bárbaro del Ritmo, "the Barbarian of Rhythm".

Early life

Born into poverty in Santa Isabel de las Lajas in the former Santa Clara province, the eldest of eighteen children, Moré learned guitar and sang in local groups while working as a cane cutter and odd-jobber. He moved to Havana in 1940, busking and singing in bars before joining the Conjunto Matamoros in 1944, with which he travelled to Mexico in 1945 — and stayed, taking the stage name "Benny Moré".

Mexico and stardom

In Mexico City Moré became a star with the orchestra of Dámaso Pérez Prado, the "King of Mambo", recording hits such as "Bonito y sabroso", and appearing in films. Returning to Cuba in 1952, he sang with Bebo Valdés and with Ernesto Duarte before forming, in 1953, his own orchestra, the Banda Gigante — a big band of up to forty musicians that he conducted with a baton and an uncanny ear, despite being unable to read music. With the Banda Gigante he recorded enduring classics: "Qué bueno baila usted", "Santa Isabel de las Lajas", "Cienfuegos", "Dolor y perdón" and his tender boleros such as "Cómo fue".

Death and legacy

Moré's health, undermined by alcoholism and cirrhosis, collapsed in the early 1960s; he died in Havana in 1963 aged only 43, and his funeral drew tens of thousands. He remains a towering figure of Cuban music: his hometown of Lajas holds an annual festival in his honour, and singers from Celia Cruz to Oscar D'León have cited him as the supreme interpreter of Cuban song.
