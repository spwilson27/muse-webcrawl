Lough Ree

Lough Ree

Location: Midlands of Ireland

Surface area: 105 km² (41 sq mi)

Primary inflow/outflow: River Shannon

Counties: Westmeath, Longford, Roscommon

Lough Ree (Irish: Loch Rí, meaning "lake of the king") is a lake in the midlands of Ireland, the second of the three major lakes on the River Shannon, after Lough Derg and before Lough Allen. Lough Ree is the second largest lake on the Shannon and the third largest in Ireland by area.

Geography

The lake lies on the boundary between counties Longford and Westmeath to the east and Roscommon to the west. The town of Athlone stands at its southern end, where the Shannon leaves the lake, and the village of Lanesborough is at its northern end. Lough Ree contains many islands, the largest of which include Inchcleraun (Quaker Island), with the remains of an early Christian monastic settlement, and Inchmore and Inchturk.

History and legend

The lake features in Irish tradition and was a contested frontier in medieval times. Viking fleets used it as a base in the 9th century. The island monastery of Inchcleraun is associated with St Diarmuid and with the legend of Queen Medb, who is said to have died there.

Recreation

Lough Ree is popular for boating, cruising on the Shannon navigation, angling (especially for pike and coarse fish) and sailing, with marinas at Athlone and Lanesborough. It is designated a Special Protection Area for birds.
