Gas compressor

A gas compressor is a mechanical device that increases the pressure of a gas by reducing its volume. Compressors are used throughout industry: to transmit natural gas through pipelines, in refrigeration and air conditioning, in petrochemical and chemical plants, for air separation, and to supply compressed air for tools, brakes and control systems.

Types

  • Reciprocating compressors use pistons driven by a crankshaft to compress gas in cylinders. They can achieve high pressures and are common in smaller installations and high-pressure duties.
  • Rotary screw compressors trap gas between two meshing helical rotors and reduce its volume as it travels along the rotors; they deliver a smooth, continuous flow, usually of air or process gas.
  • Centrifugal compressors accelerate gas with a rotating impeller and convert velocity into pressure in a diffuser. They handle large volumes at moderate pressure ratios and are standard in pipelines and large plants.
  • Axial compressors pass gas through rows of rotating and stationary blades; they are used mainly in gas turbines and jet engines.
  • Scroll, vane and diaphragm compressors serve specialised duties, including oil-free and laboratory applications.

Thermodynamics

Compressing a gas raises its temperature, so multi-stage compressors often cool the gas between stages (intercooling) to reduce the work required and to protect materials and lubricants. Real compressors are compared against ideal isothermal or isentropic compression through efficiency figures.
