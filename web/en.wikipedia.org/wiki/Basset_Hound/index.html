Basset Hound

The Basset Hound is a short-legged breed of dog of the hound family, developed in France and Belgium for hunting hare and other small game. It is a scent hound with an exceptionally keen sense of smell — second only to the Bloodhound — long drooping ears, loose wrinkled skin, and a characteristically sad expression. The name derives from the French bas, meaning "low".

History

Low-slung hunting hounds were kept by French monks of the Abbey of St Hubert, and dwarf-legged strains were selected so hunters on foot could follow them. The modern breed was refined in the 19th century by French breeders such as the Comte le Couteulx de Canteleu, and crossed with Bloodhound stock after importation to Britain, where the breed was standardised and popularised.

Characteristics

Bassets are heavy-boned for their height, with very long ears that help sweep scent toward the nose, and loose skin folds. They are gentle, devoted, and famously stubborn, with a deep baying voice. Their long backs and short legs predispose them to intervertebral disc disease and ear infections, and they require weight management and regular ear cleaning. The breed is widely recognised in popular culture, from the droopy-eared dog of advertising to cartoon characters.
