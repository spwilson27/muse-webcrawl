Newborn

A newborn is an infant in the first hours, days and weeks after birth. In medical contexts the term usually refers to the neonatal period, the first 28 days of life, during which the child adapts from the environment of the womb to independent breathing, feeding and temperature regulation.

Characteristics

At birth an average full-term newborn weighs about 3.2–3.4 kg and measures roughly 50 cm. Reflexes such as rooting, sucking, grasping and the Moro (startle) reflex are present from birth. The skull bones are not yet fused, leaving soft fontanelles, and the umbilical cord stump dries and falls away within the first weeks. Newborns sleep 16 or more hours a day in short stretches and feed frequently, ideally on breast milk.

Care and screening

Standard care includes warmth and skin-to-skin contact, vitamin K administration, eye prophylaxis in some jurisdictions, and screening tests such as the Apgar score at birth, hearing screening, and heel-prick blood tests for metabolic disorders. The neonatal period carries the highest risk of mortality in childhood, chiefly from prematurity, birth complications and infection, and outcomes have improved markedly with modern neonatal intensive care.
