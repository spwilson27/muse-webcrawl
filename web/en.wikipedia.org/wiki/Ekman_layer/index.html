Ekman layer

Ekman layer
Named forVagn Walfrid Ekman
FieldOceanography, meteorology, fluid dynamics
RelatedEkman spiral, Ekman transport

The Ekman layer is the layer in a fluid where there is a force balance between pressure-gradient force, Coriolis force, and turbulent drag. It was first described by Swedish oceanographer Vagn Walfrid Ekman, who explained observations by Fridtjof Nansen that ice drifted at an angle to the wind during the Fram expedition.

Description

In the ocean surface Ekman layer, wind stress drives a current that veers with depth, forming the Ekman spiral: surface flow is about 45° to the wind (to the right in the Northern Hemisphere), turning further and weakening with depth. Net Ekman transport is 90° to the wind direction.

Significance

Ekman transport drives coastal upwelling and downwelling, nutrient supply, and ocean gyre circulation. An analogous boundary layer exists in the atmosphere near the ground, and bottom Ekman layers form above the seafloor.

Mathematics

Under steady flow with constant eddy viscosity, the horizontal momentum equations balance Coriolis acceleration against vertical turbulent stress divergence, yielding exponentially decaying spiral solutions with an e-folding Ekman depth.
