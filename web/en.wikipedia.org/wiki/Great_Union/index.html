Great Union

The Great Union (Romanian: Marea Unire) refers to the unification in 1918 of the provinces of Transylvania, Bessarabia, and Bukovina with the Kingdom of Romania, completing a process that had begun with the union of Moldavia and Wallachia in 1859. It is commemorated annually on 1 December, Great Union Day, the national day of Romania.

Background

Romania entered the First World War in 1916 on the Allied side, seeking the Romanian-inhabited territories of Austria-Hungary. After a disastrous campaign, the country signed a separate peace in May 1918, but re-entered the war in November as the Central Powers collapsed.

The unifications of 1918

Bessarabia's national council, Sfatul Țării, voted for union with Romania on 27 March/9 April 1918, and Bukovina's General Congress followed on 15/28 November. On 1 December 1918 the Great National Assembly of Alba Iulia, attended by 1,228 elected delegates and an estimated 100,000 people, proclaimed the union of Transylvania, the Banat, Crișana, and Maramureș with Romania, together with guarantees of national rights and democratic reform.

Recognition

The new frontiers were largely confirmed by the post-war treaties, including the Treaty of Trianon (1920) with Hungary. The resulting state, often called Greater Romania, reached its maximum territorial extent in the interwar period, before the losses of 1940.
