Overview

North East England is one of the nine official regions of England, comprising Northumberland, County Durham, Tyne and Wear, and the northern part of North Yorkshire (the Tees Valley). Its principal urban centres are Newcastle upon Tyne, Sunderland, Durham, Middlesbrough, and Darlington.

Geography and history

The region stretches from the Scottish border to the River Tees, bounded by the North Sea to the east and the Pennines to the west. Hadrian's Wall, built by the Romans, crosses the region, and the medieval kingdom of Northumbria shaped its early history. Durham Cathedral and Castle form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

In the nineteenth century the North East was a powerhouse of the Industrial Revolution, pioneering railways — the Stockton and Darlington Railway opened in 1825 — and leading in coal mining, shipbuilding on the Tyne, Wear, and Tees, and steelmaking. Deindustrialisation in the later twentieth century brought severe economic decline.

Governance

Devolution deals have created mayoral combined authorities for the North East and the Tees Valley, and in 2024 the first mayor of the new North East Combined Authority was elected. A 2004 referendum rejected a directly elected regional assembly.
