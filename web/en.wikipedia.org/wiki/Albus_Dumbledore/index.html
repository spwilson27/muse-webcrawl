Albus Percival Wulfric Brian Dumbledore is a fictional character in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series. He is the headmaster of the wizarding school Hogwarts, the founder of the Order of the Phoenix, and widely regarded within the story as the greatest wizard of the modern age. For most of the series he serves as a mentor and protector to the protagonist, Harry Potter.

Character

Dumbledore is portrayed as wise, whimsical and formidable: tall and thin, with silver hair and beard, half-moon spectacles and a fondness for sherbet lemons and eccentric pronouncements. Beneath the genial surface lies a strategist burdened by a guilty past — in his youth he briefly shared Gellert Grindelwald's ambition for wizard domination, a friendship that ended amid the death of his sister Ariana. In 1945 he defeated Grindelwald in a legendary duel and won the Elder Wand.

Role in the series

As headmaster, Dumbledore shelters Harry at Hogwarts, resists the Ministry of Magic's interference, and secretly prepares Harry for the confrontation with Lord Voldemort, researching Voldemort's Horcruxes. Cursed by a ring Horcrux and already dying, he arranges his own death at the hands of Severus Snape in Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince (2005), a mercy killing that preserves Snape's cover and spares Draco Malfoy. He appears to Harry in a liminal "King's Cross" in the final book to explain the choices that remain.

Reception

Dumbledore is frequently ranked among the most popular characters in children's literature, and has been compared to archetypal mentor figures such as Merlin and Gandalf. In 2007 Rowling revealed that she had always thought of Dumbledore as gay, a statement that drew wide commentary. On screen he was played by Richard Harris in the first two films and by Michael Gambon thereafter; Jude Law portrays a younger Dumbledore in the Fantastic Beasts films.
