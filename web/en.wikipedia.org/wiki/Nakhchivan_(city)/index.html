Nakhchivan (Azerbaijani: Naxçıvan; Armenian: Նախիջևան, Nakhijevan) is the capital and largest city of the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic, an exclave of Azerbaijan separated from the rest of the country by Armenia. The city lies on the banks of the Nakhchivanchay River, at the foot of the Zangezur Mountains, close to the borders with Armenia and Iran.

Nakhchivan is one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the region. Local tradition associates its founding with Noah, and the city's name is sometimes explained in folk etymology as deriving from words meaning "the place of descent" or "first landing". Archaeological evidence indicates human settlement in the area since the Neolithic period.

History

The area around Nakhchivan was inhabited from ancient times and formed part of various Armenian, Persian and Turkic polities over the centuries. In the 7th century the city came under Arab rule, and it later passed through the hands of several regional dynasties. In the medieval period Nakhchivan became a significant commercial and cultural centre on the trade routes between the Caucasus, Persia and Anatolia.

Under the Treaty of Turkmenchay in 1828, the khanates of Erivan and Nakhchivan were ceded by Qajar Persia to the Russian Empire. After the upheavals of the early 20th century, Nakhchivan was organised as an autonomous republic within Soviet Azerbaijan in 1924, a status it retains in the modern Republic of Azerbaijan.

Geography and climate

Nakhchivan sits in the foothills of the Zangezur range at an elevation of about 873 metres. The climate is continental and semi-arid, with hot, dry summers and cold winters. The distinctive cone-shaped Mount Ilandag (Snake Mountain) dominates the landscape northeast of the city and features in local legend.

Landmarks

The city is known for the Mausoleum of Momine Khatun, a 12th-century tower tomb built by the architect Ajami Nakhchivani, which has become a symbol of the city. Other notable sites include the Nakhchivan Khan's Palace, the mausoleum attributed to Noah in the old fortress area, and the Juma Mosque.

Transport

Nakhchivan International Airport serves the city with flights to Baku and international destinations. Because the exclave has no land connection to mainland Azerbaijan, air travel and transit through Iran are the principal links to the rest of the country.
