Ratnasambhava (Sanskrit: रत्नसम्भव, "Jewel-Born One"; Tibetan: རིན་ཆེན་འབྱུང་གནས།) is one of the Five Dhyani Buddhas (or Five Wisdom Buddhas) of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. He presides over the south, is golden-yellow in colour, and embodies the wisdom of equality — the realisation that all phenomena share the same essential nature.

Symbolism

Ratnasambhava is associated with the Ratna (jewel) family, the element of earth, the sense of smell, the feeling aggregate (vedanā), and the transmutation of the poison of pride and greed into the wisdom of equality. His emblem is the wish-fulfilling jewel, and he displays the varada mudra of generosity and gift-giving with his right hand.

Role in the mandala

In the five-Buddha mandala he occupies the southern quadrant, opposite Amoghasiddhi in the north; the central Buddha is Vairocana, with Akshobhya in the east and Amitabha in the west. His pure land is Śrīmat.

Mantra and consort

His seed syllable is Trāḥ and his mantra is commonly given as Oṃ Ratnasambhava Trāḥ. His consort is Māmakī, and his attendant bodhisattvas include Ratnapāṇi.
