Rashid Alievich Sunyaev (Russian: Раши́д Али́евич Сюня́ев; born 1 March 1943) is a Russian astrophysicist of Tatar descent, a leading figure in high-energy astrophysics and physical cosmology. He is a director at the Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics in Garching, Germany, and head of the High-Energy Astrophysics Department of the Russian Space Research Institute.

Biography

Sunyaev was born in Tashkent and educated at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, where he became a student of Yakov Zeldovich. He has spent his career between Moscow and Garching.

Scientific work

With Zeldovich he predicted the Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect (1969–1972): inverse Compton scattering of cosmic microwave background photons by hot gas in galaxy clusters, now a standard tool for measuring cluster properties and cosmological parameters. With Nikolai Shakura he developed the standard alpha-disk model of accretion onto black holes (1973), foundational to black-hole astrophysics. He also led work on the X-ray observatories Granat and Spectrum-Roentgen-Gamma (Spektr-RG).

Honours

Sunyaev has received the Gruber Prize in Cosmology (2003), the Crafoord Prize (2008), the Kyoto Prize (2011), the Dirac Medal, and numerous other honours; the asteroid 11759 Sunyaev is named after him.
