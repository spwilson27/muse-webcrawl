Wanda Landowska

Born: 5 July 1879, Warsaw, Congress Poland
Died: 16 August 1959 (aged 80), Lakeville, Connecticut, US
Occupation: Harpsichordist, pianist, musicologist
Known for: Revival of the harpsichord

Wanda Aleksandra Landowska (5 July 1879 – 16 August 1959) was a Polish harpsichordist and pianist whose performances, recordings, and writings played a central role in reviving interest in the harpsichord and in the keyboard music of the Baroque era in the twentieth century.

Career

A child prodigy trained in Warsaw and Berlin, Landowska settled in Paris in 1900, teaching piano at the Schola Cantorum while researching historical performance. Convinced that Bach and his contemporaries should be heard on the instruments for which they wrote, she commissioned the French piano maker Pleyel to build large modern harpsichords — iron-framed instruments with a 16-foot stop, controversial with purists but powerful in the concert hall. She toured worldwide, performing Bach's Goldberg Variations and other works largely abandoned to the piano, and founded the École de Musique Ancienne at Saint-Leu-la-Forêt in 1925.

War and later life

Fleeing the German invasion of France in 1940, Landowska emigrated to the United States with her companion Denise Restout, settling at Lakeville, Connecticut. Her famous harpsichords and library, seized in Paris, were partially restored after the war. In America she recorded extensively for RCA Victor, including the Goldberg Variations and the Well-Tempered Clavier, and premiered concertos written for her by Manuel de Falla and Francis Poulenc.

Legacy

Landowska's motto "You play Bach your way, and I'll play him his way" encapsulated her insistence on historical grounding, though her own romanticised registrations and heavy instruments were later superseded by the historically informed performance movement she helped to inspire. She remains the pivotal figure in the harpsichord's return to the concert stage.
