Piano Concerto No. 4 (Prokofiev)

Sergei Prokofiev (1891–1953).

Sergei Prokofiev's Piano Concerto No. 4 in B-flat major, Op. 53, for the left hand alone, was commissioned by the Austrian pianist Paul Wittgenstein, who had lost his right arm in the First World War. Prokofiev completed the concerto in 1931, but Wittgenstein — who also commissioned works from Ravel, Strauss, Hindemith and others — did not understand the music and never performed it, declaring that he did not understand a single note and would never play it in public.

Premiere and reception

Because of Wittgenstein's exclusive performing rights, the concerto remained unplayed during Prokofiev's lifetime. Prokofiev considered reworking it as a concerto for two hands but never did so. It was finally premiered on 5 September 1956 in Berlin by the German pianist Siegfried Rapp, himself an amputee who had lost his right arm in the Second World War, with the Berlin Radio Symphony Orchestra; an American premiere by Rudolf Serkin with the Philadelphia Orchestra under Eugene Ormandy followed in 1958.

Structure

The concerto is shorter and lighter in character than Prokofiev's second and third concertos, and is in four movements:

  1. Vivace
  2. Andante
  3. Moderato
  4. Vivace

The outer movements are brief and brilliant, functioning almost as a prelude and postlude around the longer central movements. Despite its late start, the work has entered the repertoire of concertos for the left hand, alongside the more famous examples by Ravel and Britten.
