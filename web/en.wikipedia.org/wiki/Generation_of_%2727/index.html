Generation of '27

The Generation of '27 (Generación del 27) was an influential group of Spanish poets and artists who emerged in the 1920s, blending avant-garde movements such as surrealism, futurism, and cubism with traditional Spanish forms and the legacy of the Golden Age poet Luis de Góngora.

Origins

The group's name derives from a gathering in Seville in December 1927 commemorating the tercentenary of Góngora's death, an event that symbolized the poets' fusion of classical tradition and modernist experimentation.

Members

Principal figures include Federico García Lorca, Rafael Alberti, Vicente Aleixandre, Pedro Salinas, Jorge Guillén, Dámaso Alonso, Gerardo Diego, Luis Cernuda, Emilio Prados, and Manuel Altolaguirre. Women writers of the circle, such as Concha Méndez and Ernestina de Champourcín, are sometimes grouped as Las Sinsombrero.

Legacy

The Spanish Civil War fractured the generation: Lorca was executed in 1936, and many others went into exile. Their work remains central to 20th-century Spanish literature, and Aleixandre received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1977.
