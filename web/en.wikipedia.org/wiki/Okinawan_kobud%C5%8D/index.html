Okinawan kobudō

Okinawan kobudō is a Japanese term referring to the weapon systems of Okinawan martial arts. Closely associated with karate, it trains practitioners in weapons traditionally adapted from agricultural and fishing implements, including the bō (staff), sai, tonfa, nunchaku, and kama.

History

Kobudō developed on Okinawa over centuries, influenced by trade and cultural exchange with China and Southeast Asia, and by restrictions on weapons imposed under the Ryukyu Kingdom and later Satsuma rule. Formal systems were organized in the 20th century by masters such as Taira Shinken, who catalogued kata and founded the Ryūkyū Kobudō Hozon Shinkōkai.

Weapons and training

Training emphasizes kata (forms), basic techniques, and partner drills. In addition to the five best-known weapons, systems may include the eku (oar), tekko, and surujin (weighted rope or chain).
