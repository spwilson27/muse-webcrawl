Scheveningen ([ˈsxeːvənɪŋə(n)]) is one of the eight districts of The Hague in the Netherlands, and the country's most famous seaside resort. Originally a fishing village on the North Sea coast, it developed in the 19th century into an elegant bathing resort centred on the grand Kurhaus hotel (opened 1885), with a long sandy beach, promenade and pier.

History

The village is first mentioned in the 13th century. A storm surge in 1470 destroyed much of the original settlement, which was rebuilt further inland. Scheveningen witnessed the Battle of Scheveningen in 1653, the final naval battle of the First Anglo-Dutch War, fought within sight of crowds on the shore. In 1818 Jacob Pronk built the first bathhouse, beginning the resort era; the arrival of the steam tram and later the Kurhaus made it a fashionable destination for European aristocracy. The fishing fleet, famous for its herring catch and the annual Vlaggetjesdag (Flag Day) celebrating the first new herring, still operates from the harbour.

Scheveningen today

Modern Scheveningen combines a working fishing port, fish restaurants and the Sea Life aquarium with beach clubs, the AFAS Circustheater and the renovated pier with its Ferris wheel. The name is also familiar to chess players: the Scheveningen variation of the Sicilian Defence is named for a 1923 tournament held there, and during the Second World War the town's name was used as a shibboleth — its Dutch pronunciation, impossible for most Germans, exposed infiltrators.
