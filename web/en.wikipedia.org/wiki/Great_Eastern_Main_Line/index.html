The Great Eastern Main Line (GEML) is a major railway line in England linking London Liverpool Street with Norwich, via Stratford, Chelmsford, Colchester, and Ipswich. It is one of the principal routes of the former Great Eastern Railway and today carries intercity, commuter, and freight services for the East of England, including boat trains to Harwich International port.

History

The line was built piecemeal by the Eastern Counties Railway and the Eastern Union Railway, reaching Ipswich in 1846 and Norwich in 1849. The companies merged into the Great Eastern Railway in 1862, which developed Liverpool Street station (opened 1874) and promoted suburban growth across Essex and east London. The route passed to the London and North Eastern Railway in 1923 and to British Rail in 1948. Electrification, begun with suburban services in the 1940s–50s using 1,500 V DC and later converted to 25 kV AC, was extended from Colchester to Norwich in 1987.

Route and services

Principal stations include Stratford, Shenfield (junction for Southend and Southminster branches), Chelmsford, Colchester, Manningtree (for Harwich), Ipswich (for Felixstowe and Lowestoft), Diss, and Norwich. Greater Anglia operates intercity trains between London and Norwich, with fastest journeys around 1 hour 45 minutes, alongside intensive commuter services into Liverpool Street. The Elizabeth line shares the eastern approach between Liverpool Street/Stratford and Shenfield.
