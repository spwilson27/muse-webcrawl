Consultation-liaison psychiatry

Consultation-liaison psychiatry, also known as psychosomatic medicine, is the subspecialty of psychiatry concerned with the diagnosis and treatment of psychiatric disorders in patients with complex medical conditions, and with the interface between mental and physical health. Practitioners work in general hospitals, providing consultations to medical and surgical teams (the consultation role) and fostering collaboration and education between psychiatry and other specialties (the liaison role).

FieldPsychiatry subspecialty
SettingGeneral hospitals, medical centres
Common conditionsDelirium, somatic symptom disorders, adjustment disorders, capacity assessments

Scope of practice

Consultation-liaison psychiatrists commonly evaluate delirium, dementia, depression and anxiety in the medically ill, somatic symptom and functional disorders, substance withdrawal, suicide risk after self-harm, and questions about a patient's decision-making capacity. They advise on psychotropic medication in the setting of organ failure, drug interactions, and pregnancy, and support teams caring for transplant, oncology, and burn patients.

History

The field grew with the establishment of psychiatric units in general hospitals in the early twentieth century and the psychosomatic movement of the 1930s–1950s. In the United States, psychosomatic medicine was recognized as a subspecialty by the American Board of Psychiatry and Neurology in 2003, with the first certificates issued in 2005; the name "consultation-liaison psychiatry" was adopted for the subspecialty in 2017.
