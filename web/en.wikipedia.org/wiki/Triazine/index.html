Triazine

Triazine is a class of nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds with the molecular formula C3H3N3: a six-membered aromatic ring containing three nitrogen atoms. There are three isomers — 1,2,3-triazine, 1,2,4-triazine, and 1,3,5-triazine — distinguished by the positions of the nitrogen atoms.

1,3,5-Triazine

The symmetrical 1,3,5-triazine (s-triazine) is the most important isomer and the parent of many commercial compounds. Its derivatives include melamine, used in resins and plastics; cyanuric chloride, a versatile reagent; and the triazine herbicides such as atrazine and simazine, among the most widely used weedkillers in agriculture, though their use is restricted in some jurisdictions over groundwater contamination.

Other isomers

1,2,4-Triazines appear in pharmaceuticals such as the anticonvulsant lamotrigine and in the chemistry of inverse electron-demand Diels–Alder reactions. 1,2,3-Triazines are less stable and less common. Related rings include tetrazines and the three-carbon-diazine family.
