Politics of Yemen

Sanaa, Yemen's capital, controlled by Houthi authorities since 2014

The politics of Yemen have been dominated since 2014 by civil war and the division of the country between the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), which controls Sanaa and much of northern Yemen, and the internationally recognised government, now represented by the Presidential Leadership Council. The conflict has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Unification and the Saleh era

The Republic of Yemen was created in 1990 by the unification of North Yemen and the formerly Marxist South Yemen. Ali Abdullah Saleh, president of the north since 1978, became president of the unified state and ruled for more than three decades, balancing tribal, military, and party networks. A brief civil war in 1994 saw northern forces defeat southern secessionists. During the 2011 Arab Spring uprising, Saleh was forced to hand power to his deputy, Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, under a Gulf-brokered transition plan.

Civil war

The Houthis, a Zaydi movement from the northern governorate of Saada that had fought a series of wars against Saleh's government, seized Sanaa in September 2014. In 2015 a Saudi-led coalition intervened militarily to restore Hadi's government. Saleh, who had allied with the Houthis, was killed by them in December 2017 after breaking with them. In April 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council chaired by Rashad al-Alimi, and a UN-brokered truce reduced fighting, though a permanent settlement has remained elusive. The Southern Transitional Council, backed by the United Arab Emirates, controls much of Aden and pursues renewed southern independence.

Institutions

Under the 1991 constitution, Yemen is a republic with an elected president and a bicameral legislature consisting of the Shura Council and the House of Representatives, though parliament's term expired long ago and elections have not been held since 2009.
