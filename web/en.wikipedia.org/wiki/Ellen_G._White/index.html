Ellen Gould White (née Harmon; 26 November 1827 – 16 July 1915) was an American author and co-founder of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Together with her husband James White and Joseph Bates, she helped shape the Sabbatarian Adventist movement that emerged from the Millerite movement after the "Great Disappointment" of 1844. Seventh-day Adventists regard her as a prophet, believing she exercised the spiritual gift of prophecy; she reportedly received some 2,000 visions and dreams during her public ministry.

Life and ministry

Born in Gorham, Maine, Ellen Harmon was struck in the face by a stone at age nine, an injury that ended her schooling and affected her health for years. As a teenager she embraced William Miller's teaching of Christ's imminent return; after 1844 she reported her first vision, affirming the Advent believers. In 1846 she married James White, and the couple travelled widely, preaching, publishing and organising the movement that formally became the Seventh-day Adventist Church in 1863. She spent nine years in Australia in the 1890s, helping establish the church there, and her final years at her home, Elmshaven, in California.

Writings and influence

White was one of the most prolific female writers of her century, producing some 100,000 pages of manuscripts; her books have been translated into more than 140 languages. Steps to Christ (1892) is among the most widely distributed Christian books ever published, and the five-volume "Conflict of the Ages" series — including The Great Controversy and The Desire of Ages — sets out her sweeping narrative of sacred history. Her counsels drove the church's emphasis on health reform (leading to institutions such as the Battle Creek Sanitarium and Loma Linda), education and publishing, though the extent of her literary borrowing from contemporary authors has been a subject of scholarly debate.
