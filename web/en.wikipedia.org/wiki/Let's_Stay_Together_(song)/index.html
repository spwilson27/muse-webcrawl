"Let's Stay Together" is a soul song by American singer Al Green, released as a single in November 1971 and as the title track of his 1972 album. Written by Green with producer Willie Mitchell and drummer Al Jackson Jr., and recorded at Royal Studios in Memphis with the Hi Rhythm Section, the song became Green's signature recording and a landmark of 1970s soul music.

Chart success

The single reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in February 1972, spending a week at the top, and topped the Billboard Hot Soul Singles chart for nine weeks. It was ranked by Rolling Stone among the greatest songs of all time and was selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress in 2010 for its cultural and historical significance.

Cover versions and legacy

Tina Turner recorded a celebrated cover version in 1983, produced by Heaven 17's B.E.F. project; it reached the top five in the United Kingdom and number 26 in the United States, relaunching her solo career ahead of the Private Dancer album. The song gained renewed attention in 1994 when it featured prominently in Quentin Tarantino's film Pulp Fiction. Its simple declaration of devotion — pledging to stay together "whether times are good or bad, happy or sad" — has made it a perennial wedding favourite. In January 2012, U.S. President Barack Obama sang the opening line of the song during a fundraiser appearance at the Apollo Theater in Harlem, briefly sending sales of Green's version soaring.
