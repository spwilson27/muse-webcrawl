Internship


An intern gaining workplace experience

An internship is a period of work experience offered by an organisation for a limited period of time. Once confined to medical graduates, internships are now used for a wide range of placements in businesses, non-profit organisations and government agencies. They are typically undertaken by students and graduates looking to gain relevant skills and experience in a particular field. Employers benefit from these placements because they often allow them to recruit and test potential employees; interns benefit from practical experience, professional contacts and, in many cases, academic credit.

Types

Internships may be paid or unpaid, full-time or part-time, and may last from a few weeks to a year. Common forms include:

  • Summer internships — structured programmes for students between academic years, heavily used in finance, law, technology and consulting.
  • Co-operative education (co-op) — alternating terms of study and paid work, integrated into a degree programme.
  • Research internships — placements in university or industrial laboratories.
  • Externships — short observational placements, common in medicine and law.
  • Virtual internships — remote placements, which expanded greatly after 2020.

In medicine, "internship" retains an older specific meaning: in the United States, the first year of residency training after graduation from medical school is still often called the intern year.

Unpaid internships and criticism

Unpaid internships are controversial. Critics argue that they favour candidates who can afford to work without pay, restrict social mobility, and can displace entry-level jobs. Several jurisdictions regulate them: in the United States, unpaid internships at for-profit companies must satisfy the Department of Labor's "primary beneficiary test" under the Fair Labor Standards Act; in the United Kingdom, interns who qualify as workers are entitled to the National Minimum Wage. Campaign groups and some employers have pushed for all internships to be paid.

History

The internship descends from apprenticeship systems in which novices learned a trade by working for a master. The modern white-collar internship expanded rapidly in the late 20th century, becoming a near-standard step in graduate recruitment in many industries.
