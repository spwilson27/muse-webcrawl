Static margin

In aircraft design, static margin is a measure of the longitudinal static stability of an aircraft. It is defined as the distance between the aircraft's centre of gravity (CG) and its neutral point (NP), expressed as a percentage of the mean aerodynamic chord (MAC).

Definition

Static margin = (NP position − CG position) / MAC × 100%. For an aircraft to be statically stable in pitch, the centre of gravity must lie ahead of the neutral point, giving a positive static margin. The further forward the CG, the greater the stability — but also the greater the control force and trim drag required. A negative static margin indicates inherent pitch instability, which may nevertheless be used in fighter aircraft whose flight control systems provide artificial stability.

Typical values

General aviation and transport aircraft are commonly designed with static margins of roughly 5% to 20% of the mean aerodynamic chord. Relaxed-static-stability combat aircraft may fly with negative static margins of several percent, relying on fly-by-wire control laws to remain controllable.
