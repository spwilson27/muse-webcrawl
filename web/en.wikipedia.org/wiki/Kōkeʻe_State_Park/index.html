Kōkeʻe State Park

Kōkeʻe State Park is a state park located at the northwestern end of Waimea Canyon, on the island of Kauaʻi in Hawaiʻi. The park encompasses about 4,345 acres (1,758 ha) of forest and mountainous terrain at elevations ranging roughly from 3,200 to 4,200 feet (975 to 1,280 m), adjoining Waimea Canyon State Park to the south and the Nā Pali Coast to the northwest.

Features

The park's signature viewpoints include the Kalalau Lookout and the Puʻu o Kila Lookout, which overlook the Kalalau Valley and the Nā Pali Coast from an elevation of about 4,000 feet. On clear days the lookouts offer views across the valley to the Pacific Ocean; when the trade winds are absent, clouds and fog frequently fill the valley.

Kōkeʻe is known for its high-elevation forests of native ʻōhiʻa lehua, koa, and introduced species, and for birdwatching: native Hawaiian forest birds such as the ʻapapane, ʻanianiau, and the Kauaʻi ʻamakihi can be found here, along with the introduced Japanese white-eye. The park contains more than 45 miles of hiking trails, including the Pihea, Alakaʻi Swamp, and Cliff trails, some of which lead into the Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve, a montane bog considered one of the wettest spots on Earth.

Facilities

The Kōkeʻe Natural History Museum, located in the park, provides exhibits on the geology, flora, and fauna of Kauaʻi. The park offers camping, cabins managed by a concessionaire, picnic areas, and hunting in designated units. The Kōkeʻe Lodge and its restaurant serve visitors at the park headquarters near the meadow.

History

The area was used historically as a summer retreat from the heat of the coastal lowlands; cabins were built in the early 20th century, and the park was expanded during the Great Depression with work by the Civilian Conservation Corps, whose camp buildings survive within the park.
