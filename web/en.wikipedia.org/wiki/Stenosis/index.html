Stenosis

Stenosis (from Ancient Greek στενός, "narrow") is the abnormal narrowing of a blood vessel or other tubular organ or structure, such as foramina and canals. It is also sometimes called a stricture (as in urethral stricture).

Types

Common examples include aortic valve stenosis and mitral stenosis of the heart valves; atherosclerotic narrowing of arteries such as carotid stenosis and coronary artery disease; spinal stenosis, narrowing of the vertebral canal compressing nerves; pyloric stenosis in infants; subglottic and tracheal stenosis of the airway; and esophageal or intestinal strictures. Causes range from congenital malformations and degenerative calcification to inflammation, scarring, tumours and atherosclerosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Stenoses are diagnosed by imaging (ultrasound, CT, MRI, angiography), pressure-gradient measurements, and the characteristic sounds, or murmurs and bruits, caused by turbulent flow, detectable by auscultation. Treatment depends on site and severity and may include medication, balloon angioplasty, stenting, endarterectomy, valve replacement or other surgical widening or bypass.
