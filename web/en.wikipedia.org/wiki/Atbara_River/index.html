The Atbara River (Arabic: نهر عطبرة; transliterated: Nahr 'Atbarah), also known as the Black Nile, is a river in northeast Africa. It rises in northwest Ethiopia, approximately 50 km north of Lake Tana and 30 km west of Gondar. It flows about 805 km (500 mi) to the Nile in north-central Sudan, joining it at the city of Atbara. Its tributary, the Tekezé (Setit) River, is perhaps the true upper course of the Atbara, as the Tekezé follows the longer course prior to the confluence of the two rivers.

Course and hydrology

For much of the year, it is little more than a stream. However during the rainy season in Ethiopia, from June to October, the Atbara rises some 18 m (59 ft) above its normal level. It is at that time a formidable barrier between the northern and central districts of the Amhara Region of Ethiopia. Besides the Tekezé, other important tributaries of the Atbara include the Shinfa River and the Guang River.

The discharge of the Atbara is highly seasonal: it carries a large share of the Nile's flood and sediment load in late summer, in some years contributing more than 10% of the Nile's total annual flow despite draining a much smaller basin, and nearly dries up in the dry season. Dams on the river in Sudan, including the Khashm el-Girba Dam, regulate flow for irrigation and power.

History

The Atbara valley saw the Battle of Atbara in April 1898 during the Mahdist War, when an Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener defeated Mahdist forces at the river's confluence with the Nile, a key step in the reconquest of Sudan.
