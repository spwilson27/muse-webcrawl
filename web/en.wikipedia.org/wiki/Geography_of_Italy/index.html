Geography of Italy

The Italian peninsula

Area: 301,340 km²

Highest point: Mont Blanc / Monte Bianco, 4,808 m

Longest river: Po, 652 km

Italy occupies a long, boot-shaped peninsula extending into the central Mediterranean Sea, together with the major islands of Sicily and Sardinia and many smaller island groups. It covers 301,340 square kilometres and is bordered by France, Switzerland, Austria and Slovenia to the north.

Mountains

The Alps form an arc across the north; their highest summit, Mont Blanc (Monte Bianco, 4,808 m), stands on the French border in the northwest. The Apennines run the length of the peninsula, reaching 2,912 m at Corno Grande in the Gran Sasso massif. Italy is geologically young and seismically active, with volcanoes including Etna in Sicily, Vesuvius near Naples and Stromboli in the Aeolian Islands.

Plains and rivers

The Po Valley (Pianura Padana) in the north is Italy's largest plain and its agricultural and industrial heartland, drained by the Po, the country's longest river. Other major rivers include the Adige, the Arno and the Tiber, on which Rome stands.

Seas and climate

The peninsula is surrounded by the Adriatic Sea to the east, the Ionian Sea to the southeast, the Tyrrhenian Sea to the west and the Ligurian Sea to the northwest. The climate is predominantly Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and mild wet winters in the south, and cooler, wetter conditions in the Alpine north.
