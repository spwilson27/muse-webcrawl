Jubilee Singers

The Fisk Jubilee Singers are an African-American a cappella ensemble of students at Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee, who perform Negro spirituals. Organised in 1871 by the university's treasurer, George L. White, the original nine-member group — most of them formerly enslaved — toured to raise money for the struggling school.

History

The Singers' tours of the United States and Europe introduced the spiritual, the sacred song of enslaved African Americans, to audiences around the world. They performed for President Ulysses S. Grant and, in Britain, for Queen Victoria, who is said to have declared that they must come from a "City of Gold". The funds they raised built Fisk's Jubilee Hall, completed in 1876 and now a National Historic Landmark. Their 1909 recording of "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is among the earliest recordings of the spiritual repertoire.

Legacy

The ensemble continues today as a celebrated student group. The original Singers are credited with preserving the spirituals as concert music and with establishing Fisk University's survival; their recording of "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.
