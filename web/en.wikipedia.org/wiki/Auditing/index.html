Auditing

For broader coverage of this topic, see Audit.

Auditing is the practice of examining and evaluating records, processes, systems or financial statements in order to provide assurance about their accuracy, reliability and compliance with applicable standards or rules. The word audit derives from the Latin audire, "to hear", reflecting an era in which accounts were presented orally before an examiner.

Financial auditing

A financial audit is an independent examination of the financial statements of an entity, whether profit oriented or not, irrespective of its size or legal form, when such an examination is conducted with a view to expressing an opinion thereon. The auditor perceives and recognises the propositions before them for examination, obtains evidence, evaluates the same and formulates an opinion on the basis of their judgement which is communicated through their audit report.

Financial audits are performed to ascertain the validity and reliability of information, and also provide an assessment of a system's internal control. As a result of an audit, stakeholders may effectively evaluate and improve the effectiveness of risk management, control, and the governance process of the entity. Statutory audits of companies are required by law in most jurisdictions; international work follows International Standards on Auditing (ISA) issued by the IAASB, while US audits of public companies are overseen by the PCAOB.

Types of audit

  • External audit — performed by an independent firm, culminating in an audit opinion (unqualified, qualified, adverse or a disclaimer).
  • Internal audit — an independent, objective assurance and consulting activity within an organisation, focused on risk management, control and governance.
  • Government/performance audit — examines economy, efficiency and effectiveness of public programmes.
  • Compliance audit — checks adherence to laws, regulations, policies or contracts.
  • Information systems audit — reviews IT controls and systems.
  • Other specialised audits — tax audits, forensic audits, environmental and energy audits, quality and safety audits, and due-diligence engagements.

The audit process

An engagement typically proceeds through planning and risk assessment, evaluation of internal controls, substantive testing of transactions and balances, and completion and reporting. Materiality and audit risk guide the nature, timing and extent of procedures. The largest global firms — Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG, the "Big Four" — audit most large listed companies.
