Anglo-French War (1213–1214)

Date1213 – 1214
LocationFrance, Flanders
ResultFrench victory; Truce of Chinon
BelligerentsKingdom of England and allies vs. Kingdom of France
CommandersJohn of England, Otto IV vs. Philip II of France

The Anglo-French War of 1213–1214 was King John of England's attempt to recover the Angevin territories in France lost to Philip II a decade earlier, through a grand alliance with Emperor Otto IV, the counts of Flanders and Boulogne, and other princes.

Campaign

John landed at La Rochelle in February 1214 and campaigned in Poitou and Anjou, while his allies invaded France from the northeast. Philip's son, Louis, forced John to retreat from Anjou. On 27 July 1214 Philip decisively defeated the allied imperial and Flemish army at the Battle of Bouvines, one of the most consequential battles of the Middle Ages.

Aftermath

The defeat ended John's hopes of recovering his continental lands and led to the Truce of Chinon in September 1214. The loss of prestige and the financial exactions of the campaign contributed directly to the baronial revolt that produced Magna Carta in 1215.
