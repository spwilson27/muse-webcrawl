Operation Chastise

Date16–17 May 1943

Operation Chastise, commonly known as the Dambusters Raid, was an attack on German dams carried out on the night of 16–17 May 1943 by Royal Air Force No. 617 Squadron, later called the Dam Busters, using special "bouncing bombs" developed by Barnes Wallis. The Möhne and Edersee dams were breached, causing catastrophic flooding of the Ruhr valley, while the Sorpe Dam was damaged.

A Lancaster of 617 Squadron releasing an Upkeep bomb

Background

Before the Second World War, the British Air Ministry identified the industrialised Ruhr valley and its dams as important targets. Barnes Wallis, an engineer at Vickers, designed the "Upkeep" bouncing bomb, a cylindrical mine spun backwards and dropped at low altitude so that it skipped across the water, over torpedo nets, sank against the dam wall and exploded at depth. Nineteen specially modified Avro Lancaster bombers of the newly formed 617 Squadron, commanded by Wing Commander Guy Gibson, trained for low-level night flying for the mission.

The raid

The aircraft attacked in three waves. Gibson's formation breached the Möhne Dam after several attacks; the Eder Dam was also breached, while attacks on the Sorpe Dam, an earthen dam attacked differently, caused only damage. Eight of the nineteen aircraft were lost, with 53 aircrew killed and three captured. Gibson received the Victoria Cross for leading the raid.

Effects

The flooding destroyed factories, mines, bridges and farmland, and killed around 1,600 people, many of them prisoners of war and forced labourers. Although the damage to German war production was repaired within months, the raid diverted large resources to dam defences and repair work, and was a major propaganda success for the Allies. The raid was immortalised in the 1955 film The Dam Busters.
