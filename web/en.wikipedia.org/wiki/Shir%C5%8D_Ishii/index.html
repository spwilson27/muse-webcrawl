Shirō Ishii

Born25 June 1892, Chiba, Japan
Died9 October 1959, Tokyo, Japan
OccupationArmy surgeon general; director of Unit 731

Shirō Ishii (1892–1959) was a Japanese microbiologist and lieutenant general of the Imperial Japanese Army who directed Unit 731, the covert biological and chemical warfare research programme based at Pingfang near Harbin in occupied Manchuria.

Unit 731

Under Ishii, Unit 731 conducted lethal human experimentation on prisoners — including deliberate infection with plague, cholera and anthrax, vivisection, and frostbite studies — and carried out biological warfare attacks on Chinese cities and farmland. Thousands of victims died at the Pingfang complex alone. Ishii also developed dissemination methods such as ceramic bombs containing plague-infected fleas.

Immunity and later life

After Japan's defeat in 1945, Ishii and his associates negotiated immunity from prosecution at the Tokyo war crimes trials in exchange for sharing their research data with the United States. He was never tried and lived quietly in Japan until his death from throat cancer in 1959. The immunity arrangement remains a subject of historical and ethical controversy.
