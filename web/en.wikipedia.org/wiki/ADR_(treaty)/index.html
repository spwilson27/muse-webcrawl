ADR (treaty)

ADR

Full name: Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road

Signed: 30 September 1957, Geneva

In force: 29 January 1968

Depositary: United Nations (UNECE)

Parties: more than 50 countries in Europe, Central Asia and North Africa

ADR, formally the Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road, is a 1957 United Nations treaty that governs the transnational transport of hazardous materials by road. Its name derives from the French title, Accord relatif au transport international des marchandises Dangereuses par Route. It was concluded under the auspices of the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) in Geneva and entered into force in 1968.

Provisions

ADR sets out the rules for how dangerous goods may be carried by road, principally in its two technical annexes, which are updated every two years. Annex A classifies dangerous substances and articles into nine classes (including explosives, gases, flammable liquids, toxic substances, corrosives, and radioactive material) and specifies requirements for their packing, labelling, and documentation. Annex B lays down requirements for the vehicles, tanks, equipment, driver training and certification, and the operations of carriage. Under ADR, vehicles carrying dangerous goods display characteristic orange-coloured plates bearing hazard identification (Kemler) numbers and UN substance numbers.

Scope

Although framed for international transport, ADR's rules are applied within the European Union to domestic journeys as well through EU directive, and most European states, together with several neighbouring countries, are contracting parties. Parallel agreements exist for rail (RID), inland waterways (ADN), sea (IMDG Code), and air (ICAO Technical Instructions).
