Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibody

Anti-CCP
Type: Autoantibody
Target: Citrullinated peptides
Disease association: Rheumatoid arthritis
Test: ELISA

Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies (anti-CCP) are autoantibodies produced by the immune system that target cyclic citrullinated peptides. They are a highly specific marker for rheumatoid arthritis.

Clinical significance

Anti-CCP testing has specificity of around 95-98% for rheumatoid arthritis, higher than rheumatoid factor, though sensitivity is similar at 60-70%. Positivity may precede clinical symptoms by years and is associated with more erosive disease.

Mechanism

Citrullination is a post-translational modification catalysed by peptidylarginine deiminases converting arginine to citrulline. In genetically susceptible individuals, especially those with the HLA-DR shared epitope, citrullinated proteins in synovium and lungs trigger autoimmunity.

Testing

Commercial assays are enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays using second- and third-generation cyclic peptides (CCP2, CCP3). Results are included in the 2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for rheumatoid arthritis.
