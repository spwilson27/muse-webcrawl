The Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) is an intergovernmental organization based at the Peace Palace in The Hague, Netherlands. Established in 1899 by the Hague Convention for the Pacific Settlement of International Disputes, it is the oldest global institution dedicated to the resolution of international disputes. Despite its name, the PCA is not a court in the conventional sense: it provides a registry and administrative support for arbitral tribunals constituted by agreement of the parties.

History

The PCA was created by the 1899 Hague Peace Conference, convened at the initiative of Tsar Nicholas II of Russia. The Convention was revised at the Second Hague Peace Conference in 1907. Its first case, the Pious Fund of the Californias dispute between the United States and Mexico, was heard in 1902. After a period of relative inactivity in the mid-twentieth century, the PCA experienced a revival from the 1990s onward as states increasingly turned to arbitration for territorial, maritime, and investment disputes.

Structure

The PCA's Administrative Council, composed of the diplomatic representatives of the contracting parties accredited to the Netherlands, oversees the International Bureau, which acts as registry and is headed by the Secretary-General. Each contracting party may nominate up to four persons of known competency in international law to the panel of Members of the Court, from whom arbitral tribunals may be appointed.

Activities

The PCA administers arbitration, conciliation, mediation, and fact-finding proceedings involving states, state entities, intergovernmental organizations, and private parties. Notable proceedings include the Eritrea–Ethiopia Boundary Commission, the Abyei Arbitration between Sudan and the Sudan People's Liberation Movement, the South China Sea Arbitration brought by the Philippines against China, and the Enrica Lexie incident arbitration between Italy and India. The PCA also frequently serves as appointing authority under the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules.

Finances

The PCA is funded principally by contributions from its contracting parties, assessed on a scale based on the United Nations system, supplemented by fees paid by parties for its registry services in certain categories of cases.
