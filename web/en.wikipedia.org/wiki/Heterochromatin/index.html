Heterochromatin

Heterochromatin is a tightly packed form of DNA, first distinguished by Emil Heitz (1928), that remains condensed through the cell cycle and is generally transcriptionally silent, in contrast to open euchromatin.

Types

Constitutive heterochromatin, rich in satellite repeats at centromeres and telomeres, is stable across cell types and marked by H3K9 methylation and HP1 proteins. Facultative heterochromatin can change state — the inactivated X chromosome (Barr body) in female mammals is a classic example.

Function

Heterochromatin maintains genome stability, silences transposable elements, supports centromere function, and helps regulate gene expression through position-effect variegation, first observed in Drosophila.

Formation

RNA interference, DNA methylation and histone-modifying complexes establish and spread heterochromatin in organisms from fission yeast to humans.
