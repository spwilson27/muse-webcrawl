The Canyon Diablo meteorite comprises the many fragments of the asteroid that created Meteor Crater (also called Barringer Crater) in Arizona, United States. Fragments are found in and around the crater, named for the nearby Canyon Diablo. The impact occurred about 49,000 years ago, when an iron-nickel asteroid roughly 50 metres across struck the Colorado Plateau.

Composition and significance

The meteorite is an iron octahedrite of the IAB group, composed mainly of kamacite and taenite with cohenite, schreibersite, troilite and graphite inclusions. It famously contains microscopic diamonds — including the hexagonal polymorph lonsdaleite — formed by shock during the impact. In the 1950s, Clair Cameron Patterson used lead isotope ratios measured in Canyon Diablo troilite to calculate the age of the Earth at 4.55 billion years, because the meteorite's lead had not been fractionated since the formation of the Solar System.

History

Native peoples knew of the fragments long before scientific recognition. Daniel Barringer's mining company collected many specimens in the early 1900s while unsuccessfully searching for the main mass, which had largely vaporised on impact. The largest recovered fragment, the Holsinger Meteorite (639 kg), is displayed at Meteor Crater's visitor centre.
