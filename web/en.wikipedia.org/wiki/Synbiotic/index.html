Synbiotic

Synbiotics refer to food ingredients or dietary supplements combining probiotics and prebiotics in a form of synergism, hence synbiotics. The term was introduced by Gibson and Roberfroid in 1995. The concept holds that a substrate selectively utilized by host microorganisms, combined with live microorganisms, confers a health benefit on the host.

Definition

In 2019, the International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics (ISAPP) updated the definition: a synbiotic is "a mixture comprising live microorganisms and substrate(s) selectively utilized by host microorganisms that confers a health benefit on the host." ISAPP distinguishes complementary synbiotics, in which probiotic and prebiotic components work independently, from synergistic synbiotics, in which the substrate is designed to be selectively utilized by the co-administered microorganism.

Examples

Typical combinations pair Bifidobacterium or Lactobacillus strains with fructooligosaccharides (FOS), galactooligosaccharides (GOS), or xylooligosaccharides (XOS). Synbiotics appear in yogurts, fermented foods, and supplements, and are studied for effects on gut health, immune function, and metabolic conditions, though clinical evidence for many claimed benefits remains limited.
