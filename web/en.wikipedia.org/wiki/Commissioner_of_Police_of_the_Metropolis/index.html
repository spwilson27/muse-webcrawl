The Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis is the head of London's Metropolitan Police Service, the largest police force in the United Kingdom. The commissioner is responsible for policing the Metropolitan Police District (Greater London, excluding the City of London), national counter-terrorism coordination, and protection of the Royal Family and senior government figures. The post is widely regarded as the most senior in British policing.

Appointment and accountability

The commissioner is appointed by the Crown on the recommendation of the Home Secretary, who must have regard to recommendations made by the Mayor of London through the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime (MOPAC). The commissioner holds the rank of a constable and is accountable to MOPAC and ultimately to Parliament; unlike other chief constables, the commissioner does not answer to a local police and crime commissioner independent of the mayor.

History

The office was created by the Metropolitan Police Act 1829, with Colonel Charles Rowan and barrister Richard Mayne serving as joint commissioners — originally titled justices of the peace for the force. Sir Robert Mark, commissioner from 1972 to 1977, became a notable reformer of corruption. Modern commissioners have included Ian Blair, Paul Stephenson, Bernard Hogan-Howe, Cressida Dick — the first woman to hold the post, serving 2017 to 2022 — and Mark Rowley, who took office in September 2022.
