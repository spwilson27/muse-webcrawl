Captain is a senior commissioned officer rank in most navies, equivalent to the army rank of colonel (NATO code OF-5). A naval captain typically commands a major warship — a cruiser, aircraft carrier, or large amphibious ship — or a significant shore installation. In the United States Navy and Coast Guard, captains wear the pay grade O-6 and are addressed as "captain" regardless of the vessel size commanded; by custom, the commanding officer of any ship is also called "the captain" as a title of office.

Insignia and seniority

In most Commonwealth navies a captain wears four gold rings on the sleeve, and in the US Navy four stripes with a line officer's star. The rank sits above commander and below commodore or rear admiral. Historically in the Royal Navy, a "post-captain" held substantive rank, while "master and commander" was a lower grade; officers commanding vessels below their rank could be given the courtesy title of captain.

Duties

As commanding officer, a captain bears absolute legal responsibility for the ship, its crew and its mission, including safety of navigation and discipline under maritime and military law. Captains also serve in senior staff and joint billets ashore as they progress toward flag rank.
