el-Kurru

Location: Northern Sudan, near the Nile
Period: Napatan, c. 860–650 BC burials
Part of: Gebel Barkal and Sites of the Napatan Region (UNESCO)

El-Kurru was one of the royal cemeteries used by the Nubian royal family of Kush and Egypt's 25th Dynasty. It lies in present-day northern Sudan, south of Gebel Barkal, near the Nile.

History

The cemetery contains tumulus tombs and later pyramid tombs of early Napatan kings, including ancestors of the Kushite pharaohs who ruled Egypt. Kings such as Kashta and Piye are associated with burials here before the royal cemetery shifted to Nuri.

Excavation

George Reisner excavated el-Kurru in 1918–1919 for Harvard University and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. Later work has documented decorated tomb chapels, including painted burial chambers.

Heritage

El-Kurru is included in the UNESCO World Heritage listing for Gebel Barkal and the Sites of the Napatan Region, inscribed in 2003.
