Salvador Guillermo Allende Gossens (26 June 1908 – 11 September 1973) was a Chilean socialist politician, who served as the 28th president of Chile from 1970 until his death in 1973. As a Marxist politician and a physician, he was the first Marxist to be elected president in a liberal democracy in Latin America.


Allende as president

Early life and career

Allende was born in Santiago into an upper-middle-class family and studied medicine at the University of Chile, graduating in 1933. In the same year he co-founded the Socialist Party of Chile. He served as a deputy, as Minister of Health under Pedro Aguirre Cerda (1939–1942), and as a senator from 1945. He ran unsuccessfully for the presidency in 1952, 1958 and 1964 before winning in 1970.

Presidency

Allende won the 1970 election with a plurality of about 36% as head of the Popular Unity coalition and was confirmed by Congress after agreeing to a statute of constitutional guarantees. His programme, the "Chilean way to socialism", included nationalisation of the copper industry (approved unanimously by Congress in 1971), agrarian reform, nationalisation of banks and large industries, and expanded social spending. His government recognised Cuba and the People's Republic of China.

His term was marked by severe economic difficulties, inflation, shortages, strikes and growing political polarisation, as well as covert intervention by the United States, which sought to destabilise his government. In August 1973 the Chamber of Deputies accused his government of unconstitutional acts.

Death and aftermath

On 11 September 1973, the Chilean military, led by General Augusto Pinochet, launched a coup d'état. During the assault on the presidential palace, La Moneda, Allende delivered a final radio address to the nation and then died; an official investigation in 2011 confirmed that he died by suicide. The coup inaugurated a military dictatorship that ruled Chile until 1990.
