Liposome

Liposome
StructureSpherical phospholipid bilayer vesicle
Typical size20 nm – several µm
Described byAlec Bangham and colleagues, 1960s
ApplicationsDrug delivery, gene transfection, cosmetics, vaccines

A liposome is a small artificial vesicle, spherical in shape, having at least one lipid bilayer. Because of their ability to enclose both water-soluble substances in their aqueous core and fat-soluble substances within their membrane, liposomes are widely used as delivery vehicles for pharmaceutical drugs and nutrients, and as model systems for cell membranes.

Discovery and structure

Liposomes were first described by the British haematologist Alec Bangham in the 1960s, observing that phospholipids dispersed in water spontaneously form closed bilayer structures. A liposome's membrane is typically composed of phospholipids such as phosphatidylcholine, often with cholesterol to modulate fluidity and stability. Vesicles are classified by size and number of bilayers (unilamellar or multilamellar) and may be produced by methods including sonication, extrusion and thin-film hydration. Their surfaces can be modified — for example by coating with polyethylene glycol ("PEGylation") — to prolong circulation in the bloodstream by reducing clearance by the immune system, or by attaching antibodies and ligands that target particular cells.

Applications

In medicine, encapsulating a drug in liposomes can improve its solubility, reduce its toxicity and alter where it accumulates in the body. Liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil/Caelyx), approved by the US FDA in 1995, was the first approved nano-drug and reduces the cardiac toxicity of the anticancer drug doxorubicin; liposomal amphotericin B is used against serious fungal infections, and liposomal formulations are used in some vaccines and anaesthetics. In research, liposomes serve as vehicles for transfection of nucleic acids into cells (lipofection), and lipid nanoparticles — related carriers — delivered the mRNA in major COVID-19 vaccines. Liposomes also appear in cosmetics and food technology as carriers for active ingredients and nutrients.
