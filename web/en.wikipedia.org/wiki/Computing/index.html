Computing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Colossus, an early electronic programmable computer

Computing is any goal-oriented activity requiring, benefiting from, or creating computing machinery. It includes the study and experimentation of algorithmic processes, and the development of both hardware and software. Computing has scientific, engineering, mathematical, technological, and social aspects. Major computing disciplines include computer engineering, computer science, cybersecurity, data science, information systems, information technology, and software engineering.

History

The history of computing predates the modern computer by millennia, beginning with counting aids such as the abacus and mechanical calculators by Blaise Pascal and Gottfried Leibniz. Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine (1830s) introduced the concept of a programmable machine, and Ada Lovelace wrote what is regarded as the first algorithm intended for such a machine. The twentieth century brought electromechanical and then electronic computers: the Z3, Colossus, and ENIAC of the 1940s, followed by the stored-program machines that define modern computing.

The invention of the transistor (1947) and the integrated circuit (1958) miniaturized and cheapened computation, leading to mainframes, minicomputers, personal computers in the 1970s and 1980s, and the networked, mobile, and cloud computing eras that followed.

Fields

Computing encompasses the theory of computation, algorithms and data structures, programming languages, operating systems, databases, computer networks, artificial intelligence, graphics, and human–computer interaction. The term is also used for the practical activity of using computers, as in cloud computing, scientific computing, and grid computing.
