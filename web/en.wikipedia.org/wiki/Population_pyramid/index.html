Population pyramid

A population pyramid (also called an age–sex pyramid or age structure diagram) is a graphical illustration that shows the distribution of various age groups in a population, typically of a country or region, forming the shape of a pyramid when the population is growing. Males are conventionally shown on the left and females on the right, measured by raw numbers or as a percentage of the total population.

Types

Demographers describe three broad shapes. An expansive pyramid has a wide base and narrow top, indicating high birth rates and a youthful population typical of rapidly growing developing countries. A constrictive pyramid has a narrower base than middle, reflecting low birth rates and an ageing population, as in much of Europe and Japan. A stationary pyramid has roughly equal cohorts until old age, characteristic of stable populations with low fertility and mortality.

Interpretation

Pyramids reveal dependency ratios, the demographic impact of wars, baby booms, migration, and disease, and are used to forecast demand for schools, pensions, and healthcare. The shape is closely tied to the stages of the demographic transition model.
