South African National Parks (SANParks) is the body responsible for managing South Africa's system of national parks. Founded in 1926 as the National Parks Board, SANParks conserves some 4 million hectares of protected land — around 4% of the country's area — spanning savanna, mountain, desert, fynbos, and marine environments.

History

The park system grew from the game reserves of the late 19th century, notably the Sabi Game Reserve proclaimed in 1898, which became Kruger National Park in 1926 under the National Parks Act championed by minister Piet Grobler and warden James Stevenson-Hamilton. The system expanded steadily through the 20th century to represent all of South Africa's major biomes.

Parks

The best-known parks include Kruger National Park, one of Africa's largest game reserves and home to the "Big Five"; Table Mountain National Park in Cape Town; Addo Elephant National Park in the Eastern Cape; Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, shared with Botswana; Augrabies Falls, Karoo, Mountain Zebra, and Golden Gate Highlands national parks; and the iSimangaliso and Mapungubwe parks near the northern borders. The Garden Route National Park protects the forests and lakes around Knysna and Tsitsikamma.

Role and challenges

SANParks combines conservation and commercial tourism: rest camps and concessions generate much of its revenue, funding anti-poaching and conservation work. Major challenges include rhino poaching, human–wildlife conflict, invasive species, climate change, and expanding access and benefit-sharing for communities living around the parks.
