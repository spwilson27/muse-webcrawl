Education in Turkey

Education in Turkey is supervised by the Ministry of National Education and is compulsory for twelve years. Since the 2012 reform known as "4+4+4", schooling is divided into three four-year stages: primary school, middle school and high school. Public education is free, and the language of instruction is Turkish.

Structure and examinations

Children begin primary school at around age six. At the end of middle school, placement in high schools is governed by the LGS examination for selective schools, while others enrol locally. Types of high school include Anatolian (general), science, vocational and technical, and Imam Hatip schools offering religious instruction alongside the general curriculum. University admission depends on the national YKS examination administered by ÖSYM. Turkey has more than 200 universities, including long-established institutions such as Istanbul University, Ankara University, and the technical universities in Istanbul and Ankara; a large share of students study through Anadolu University's open-education system.

History

The republican reforms of the 1920s unified education under the state, closed the medreses, adopted the Latin alphabet in 1928 and made education secular. Compulsory schooling was extended from five to eight years in 1997 and to twelve years in 2012, greatly raising enrolment, including of girls.

Issues

Turkish students score below the OECD average in PISA, and outcomes differ widely between regions and school types. Policy debates concern the growing number of Imam Hatip schools, curriculum content, the abolition of private cram schools (dershanes) in 2015, the integration of large numbers of Syrian refugee children, and centralised control over curricula and teacher appointments.
