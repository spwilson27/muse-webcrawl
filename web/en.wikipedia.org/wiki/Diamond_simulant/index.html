Diamond simulant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Cubic zirconia, the most common diamond simulant]

A diamond simulant, diamond imitation or diamond substitute is an object or material with gemological characteristics similar to those of a diamond. Simulants are distinct from synthetic diamonds, which are actual diamonds grown in a laboratory and share the same chemical and physical properties as natural diamonds. A simulant may be artificial, natural, or a combination, and it merely imitates the appearance of diamond without possessing its material identity.

Desired and actual properties

The value of a simulant depends on how closely it approaches diamond's brilliance (high refractive index), fire (dispersion), and durability (hardness). No simulant matches all three. Diamond has a refractive index of 2.417, dispersion of 0.044, and Mohs hardness 10. Common simulants differ measurably in thermal conductivity, specific gravity, and optical character, allowing gemologists to identify them with a thermal probe, loupe, or refractometer.

Common simulants

  • Cubic zirconia (CZ) — synthetic zirconium dioxide, produced cheaply since the 1970s; refractive index ~2.15–2.18, dispersion higher than diamond (~0.060), hardness 8–8.5.
  • Moissanite — silicon carbide; refractive index ~2.65–2.69 and doubly refractive, with very high thermal conductivity that can fool older testers; hardness 9.25.
  • White sapphire and topaz — natural colourless corundum or topaz, with lower brilliance.
  • Strontium titanate, rutile, YAG and GGG — earlier synthetic simulants with excessive fire or softness.
  • Rhinestones and paste — glass, sometimes foil-backed, used in costume jewellery since the eighteenth century.

Detection

Because simulants differ from diamond in thermal conductivity, electrical conductivity (moissanite), birefringence, inclusions, and facet wear, they can be distinguished by trained examination. Trade rules in many jurisdictions require that simulants be disclosed and never sold as "diamond" without qualification.
