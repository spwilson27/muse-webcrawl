Vapour barrier

A vapour barrier (or vapor barrier in American English) is a material used in building construction to resist the diffusion of moisture through wall, ceiling and floor assemblies. By limiting the movement of water vapour from the warm, humid side of an assembly to colder surfaces where it could condense, vapour barriers help prevent mould growth, rot and degradation of insulation.

Polyethylene sheeting is a common vapour barrier material in cold-climate construction.

How moisture moves

Water vapour moves through building assemblies both by air leakage and by diffusion. Air leakage usually carries far more moisture than diffusion, which is why modern building science emphasizes air barriers and careful sealing. Vapour diffusion is driven by differences in vapour pressure: in winter, indoor air typically has a higher vapour pressure than outdoor air, so moisture tends to migrate outward through the wall.

Permeance classes

Materials are rated by their water vapour permeance, measured in perms. Building codes commonly group vapour retarders into three classes: Class I (0.1 perm or less, effectively impermeable — e.g. sheet polyethylene, aluminum foil), Class II (0.1 to 1.0 perm — e.g. kraft-faced insulation, some paints), and Class III (1.0 to 10 perms — e.g. latex paint on gypsum board). Strictly, the term "vapour barrier" properly applies only to Class I materials; higher-perm materials are called vapour retarders.

Placement

In cold climates, the vapour barrier is traditionally placed on the warm-in-winter side of the insulation. In hot, humid climates the drive is reversed for much of the year, and an interior vapour barrier can trap moisture, so assemblies are designed to dry to the interior. Incorrect placement — or a double vapour barrier that traps moisture between two impermeable layers — is a common cause of concealed condensation and decay.

Materials

Common materials include 6-mil polyethylene sheet, aluminum foil laminates, kraft paper facing on fibreglass batts, foil-faced rigid foam, and vapour-retarding primers and paints. "Smart" vapour retarders change their permeance with humidity, resisting diffusion when dry and allowing drying when the assembly is wet.
