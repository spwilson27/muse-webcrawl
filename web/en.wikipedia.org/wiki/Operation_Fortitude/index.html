Operation Fortitude

Operation Fortitude was the code name for a World War II military deception operation employed by the Allied nations as part of the overall deception strategy (code named Operation Bodyguard) during the build-up to the 1944 Normandy landings (Operation Overlord). Fortitude was divided into two subplans, North and South, with the aim of misleading the German high command as to the location of the invasion.

Background

Fortitude was one of the most successful deception operations of the war. It built on the work of the London Controlling Section under John Bevan and drew on the Double Cross System, in which every German agent in Britain had been turned to feed controlled information to Berlin.

Fortitude North

Fortitude North was designed to threaten an invasion of Norway, tying down German forces there. A fictional British Fourth Army, headquartered in Edinburgh, was created, supported by dummy radio traffic and double-agent reports. In the event, Germany retained some 400,000 troops in Norway until the end of the war.

Fortitude South

Fortitude South aimed to convince the Germans that the main invasion would fall at the Pas-de-Calais, the shortest crossing point and the launching area for V-weapons. A fictional First United States Army Group (FUSAG), supposedly commanded by General George S. Patton, was "based" in Kent and Essex opposite Calais, complete with dummy landing craft, inflatable tanks, and fabricated radio nets. Double agents including Juan Pujol García ("Garbo") reported that the Normandy landings of 6 June 1944 were a diversion, a deception maintained for weeks after D-Day.

Effect

The deception persuaded Adolf Hitler and much of the German high command to hold the Fifteenth Army at the Pas-de-Calais during the critical first weeks of the Normandy campaign, contributing significantly to the success of the Allied lodgement.
