Kac determinant

The Kac determinant formula, introduced by mathematician Victor Kac, gives the determinant of the Gram matrix (the matrix of inner products, often called the Shapovalov form) of a basis of a Verma module of the Virasoro algebra, expressed as a function of the central charge c and the conformal weight h of the module.

Statement

For a Verma module of level n (the subspace spanned by states of total grade n), the determinant factorises into terms that vanish exactly when the module contains singular vectors:

det Mn(c, h) = an ∏1 ≤ rs ≤ n (h − hr,s(c))p(n − rs)

where p(k) is the partition function (the number of partitions of k), and hr,s(c) are the weights at which a singular vector appears at level rs, parametrised through the standard quantities h0 and α± associated with the central charge.

Significance

The zeros of the Kac determinant locate the values of (c, h) for which a Verma module is reducible, that is, contains a proper submodule generated by a null (singular) vector. Dividing out these null vectors yields the irreducible representations that underpin the classification of two-dimensional conformal field theories, including the unitary minimal models at central charges c = 1 − 6/(m(m+1)). The formula was conjectured from low-level computations and proved using the Jantzen filtration techniques adapted from the theory of Kac–Moody algebras.
