My Bloody Valentine (band)

My Bloody Valentine are an Irish-English rock band formed in Dublin in 1983 and consisting since 1987 of founding members Kevin Shields (vocals, guitar, sampler) and Colm Ó Cíosóig (drums, sampler), with Bilinda Butcher (vocals, guitar) and Debbie Googe (bass). Their music is characterised by dense layers of distorted, pitch-bent guitar textures and ethereal vocals, and they are credited with pioneering the subgenre known as shoegaze.

History

Early years and Isn't Anything (1983–1989)

Shields and Ó Cíosóig formed the band in Dublin in 1983, taking the name from the 1981 Canadian slasher film My Bloody Valentine. After early EPs with a post-punk sound and a move to London, the line-up stabilised with Butcher and Googe. Signing to Creation Records, they released the EP You Made Me Realise (1988) and their debut album Isn't Anything (1988), which became a landmark of the emerging shoegaze scene.

Loveless (1991)

Their second album, Loveless (1991), was recorded over two years in numerous studios at a famously large cost that nearly bankrupted Creation Records. Built from Shields's "glide guitar" technique — strumming while manipulating the tremolo arm — together with sampled drums and heavily treated vocals, the album was initially a modest commercial success but is now regarded as one of the greatest albums of all time.

Hiatus and reunion (1992–present)

Sessions for a follow-up stalled in the 1990s and the band effectively dissolved, though Shields contributed to Primal Scream and the soundtrack of Lost in Translation (2003). The band reunited for concerts in 2008 and released their third album, m b v, without prior announcement in 2013, alongside remastered reissues of their earlier work. In 2021 they signed with Domino Recording Company and their catalogue became widely available on streaming services.
