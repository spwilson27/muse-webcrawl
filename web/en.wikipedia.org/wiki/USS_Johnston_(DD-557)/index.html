USS Johnston (DD-557) was a Fletcher-class destroyer of the United States Navy, named after Lieutenant John V. Johnston. She is best known for her heroic action in the Battle off Samar on 25 October 1944, in which, as part of the small task unit "Taffy 3", she charged a vastly superior Japanese fleet to protect American escort carriers. She was sunk in the battle, but the sacrifice of Johnston and her sister ships helped force the Japanese Center Force to withdraw. Her wreck, located in 2021 at about 21,180 feet (6,460 m), is among the deepest shipwrecks ever surveyed.

Service history

Commissioned in October 1943 under Lieutenant Commander Ernest E. Evans, Johnston served in the Marshall Islands and the Solomons, providing shore bombardment and escort duty, and supported the invasions of Guam and the Palaus before joining Task Unit 77.4.3 ("Taffy 3") for the Leyte landings in the Philippines.

Battle off Samar

On the morning of 25 October 1944, Taffy 3's six escort carriers and their screen of three destroyers and four destroyer escorts were surprised by Admiral Kurita's Center Force of battleships, cruisers, and destroyers. Without waiting for orders, Evans turned Johnston toward the enemy, laying a smoke screen and delivering torpedo attacks that blew the bow off the heavy cruiser Kumano. Johnston then fought a gun duel with battleships and cruisers for over two hours before she was overwhelmed and sunk. Of her crew of 327, 186 — including Evans, who was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor — were lost.

The ship received the Presidential Unit Citation and six battle stars. In 2023–2024 the Navy announced that a new Arleigh Burke-class destroyer would bear Ernest Evans's name, and Evans was posthumously promoted in recognition of the action.
