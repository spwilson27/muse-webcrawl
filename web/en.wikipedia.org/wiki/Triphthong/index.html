Triphthong

In phonetics, a triphthong (from Greek tri, "three", and phthong, "sound") is a complex vowel sound that begins with one vowel quality and changes through a second to a third, all within a single syllable. Like a diphthong, which glides through two vowel qualities, a triphthong is a single phonological unit rather than a sequence of separate vowels.

In English

In Received Pronunciation and other non-rhotic accents of English, sequences such as /aɪə/ in fire, /aʊə/ in hour, /eɪə/ in layer, /ɔɪə/ in loyal, and /əʊə/ in lower are often analyzed as triphthongs. In many accents these are subject to "smoothing", by which the middle element is weakened or lost, so that fire may approach a long monophthong [aː] and become homophonous with neither far nor foyer exactly. In rhotic accents the final element is typically realized as an r-colored vowel instead.

In other languages

Romanian has well-known triphthongs such as /e̯ai̯/ and /e̯au̯/. Italian words like miei ("my", masculine plural) contain the triphthong /jɛi̯/ and buoi ("oxen") contains /wɔi̯/. Spanish words such as Paraguay and buey end in triphthongal sequences. Dutch words like mooi ("beautiful") are also sometimes cited.
