Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor

The Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) to honour an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a supporting role in a film. It was first presented at the 9th Academy Awards, held in 1937 for films of 1936, when it was won by Walter Brennan for Come and Get It.

History and records

Walter Brennan holds the record with three wins in the category (1936, 1938, 1940). Other multiple winners include Michael Caine, Robert De Niro, Anthony Quinn, Peter Ustinov, Melvyn Douglas, Jason Robards, Christoph Waltz, Mahershala Ali and Daniel Kaluuya-era contemporaries with two each. The youngest winner is Timothy Hutton, aged 20, for Ordinary People (1980); the oldest is Christopher Plummer, aged 82, for Beginners (2011). Initially, supporting winners received plaques rather than statuettes.

Selection

Nominees are chosen by members of the Academy's actors' branch by single transferable vote, and the winner is selected by the full voting membership. Since the 1940s, five nominees are usually named each year.

Recent winners

Recent recipients include Mahershala Ali (Green Book), Brad Pitt (Once Upon a Time in Hollywood), Daniel Kaluuya (Judas and the Black Messiah), Troy Kotsur (CODA), Ke Huy Quan (Everything Everywhere All at Once) and Robert Downey Jr. (Oppenheimer).
