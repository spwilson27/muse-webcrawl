Hydrogenase

A hydrogenase is an enzyme that catalyses the reversible oxidation of molecular hydrogen (H2): H2 ⇌ 2 H+ + 2 e−. Hydrogenases are found in many bacteria, archaea and some eukaryotes, where they allow organisms to use hydrogen as an energy source or to dispose of excess reducing equivalents as hydrogen gas.

Classification

Hydrogenases are classified by the metals in their active site. [NiFe]-hydrogenases are typically involved in hydrogen uptake and are relatively oxygen-tolerant. [FeFe]-hydrogenases have exceptionally high turnover rates for hydrogen production but are extremely oxygen-sensitive. A third group, the iron–sulfur cluster-free [Fe]-hydrogenases (Hmd), occur in methanogenic archaea and catalyse a step in the reduction of CO2 with H2. The active sites feature unusual carbon monoxide and cyanide ligands bound to iron, an arrangement once thought impossible in biology.

Significance

Because they interconvert protons, electrons and hydrogen at low overpotential using earth-abundant metals, hydrogenases inspire catalysts for fuel cells and for the production of hydrogen as a renewable fuel. They also play central roles in microbial metabolism — in nitrogen-fixing symbionts that recycle hydrogen evolved by nitrogenase, in gut microbes, and in pathogens such as Helicobacter pylori.
