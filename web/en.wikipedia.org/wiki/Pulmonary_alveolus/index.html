Pulmonary alveolus


Alveoli and their capillary network

A pulmonary alveolus (plural: alveoli, from Latin alveolus, "little cavity"), also known as an air sac or air space, is one of millions of hollow, distensible cup-shaped cavities in the lungs where pulmonary gas exchange takes place. Oxygen is exchanged for carbon dioxide through the blood–air barrier between the alveolar air and the pulmonary capillaries.

Structure

The alveoli are located in the alveolar sacs at the ends of the alveolar ducts, forming the great majority of the internal surface of the lungs. A typical pair of human lungs contains about 480 million alveoli, providing a total surface area of roughly 70 square metres. Each alveolus is about 200–300 micrometres in diameter and is wrapped in a fine mesh of capillaries.

Cells

Type I cells are thin, flat squamous cells that form the bulk of the alveolar surface and carry out gas exchange. Type II cells secrete pulmonary surfactant, which reduces surface tension and prevents alveolar collapse; they can also divide to replace damaged type I cells. Alveolar macrophages patrol the air spaces and clear dust and debris.

Function

During inhalation, fresh air fills the alveoli; oxygen diffuses across the blood–air barrier into capillary blood while carbon dioxide passes the other way, to be exhaled. Diseases affecting the alveoli include pneumonia, pulmonary edema, emphysema, and acute respiratory distress syndrome.
