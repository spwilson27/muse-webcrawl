Spring (device)


A helical compression spring.

A spring is a device typically made of an elastic material, most often spring steel, that stores mechanical energy when deformed and releases it when the load is removed. Within its elastic limit, a spring's force is described by Hooke's law: the force is proportional to the displacement, F = −kx, where k is the spring constant.

Types

Common forms include the coil (helical) spring, which may resist compression, tension, or torsion; the leaf spring, used in vehicle suspensions; the spiral or hairspring of watches; the torsion bar; and gas springs, which use compressed gas. Springs may be classified by how they receive load — compression, extension, torsion, or constant-force designs.

Uses

Springs are ubiquitous: in vehicle suspensions and mattresses, ballpoint pens and clothespins, clocks and watches (where the balance spring regulates timekeeping), valves, toys, and countless mechanisms that require a returning or cushioning force.

Physics

The energy stored in an ideal spring is ½kx². Combined with a mass, a spring forms a harmonic oscillator, the basis of models throughout physics and engineering. Real springs exhibit fatigue and can fail if repeatedly stressed beyond their design limits.
