Disposable soma theory of aging

The disposable soma theory of aging states that organisms age due to an evolutionary trade-off between growth, reproduction, and DNA repair maintenance. It was proposed by Thomas Kirkwood in 1977. The "disposable soma" refers to the soma — the body apart from the germ line — which is treated as disposable once reproduction has occurred.

Proposed byThomas Kirkwood

FieldEvolutionary biology, biogerontology

Theory

Because resources are limited, an organism must allocate energy among reproduction, growth, and somatic maintenance such as DNA repair. Natural selection is expected to favour allocations that maximize reproductive success, so maintenance of the body is set at a level lower than would be needed for indefinite survival. Accumulated molecular damage then leads to aging.

Evidence and criticism

The theory predicts that long-lived species invest more in repair and stress resistance, and that dietary restriction may extend lifespan by shifting resources from reproduction to maintenance. Critics note that aging patterns in some species, and cases where lifespan extends without obvious reproductive cost, do not fit simple versions of the trade-off.
