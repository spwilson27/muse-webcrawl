Paint

Paint is a liquid or paste material that, when applied to a surface, dries or cures into a solid coloured film. Paints serve to protect, colour and texture objects — buildings, vehicles, machinery and artworks — and have been made and used by humans for tens of thousands of years. A typical paint consists of pigments suspended in a binder (the film-forming vehicle), together with solvents and additives that control flow, drying and durability.

Composition

Pigments — finely ground insoluble particles such as titanium dioxide (white), iron oxides and carbon black — provide colour and opacity. The binder, commonly an acrylic emulsion in modern water-based paints or an alkyd or drying oil in solvent-based paints, forms the continuous film that adheres to the substrate. Solvents (water or organic liquids) adjust viscosity and evaporate during drying; cured films form by evaporation, oxidation, coalescence or chemical cross-linking depending on the binder. Additives in small amounts act as driers, anti-skinning agents, mildewcides, flow modifiers and defoamers.

History

The earliest paints were made from ochre, charcoal and other mineral pigments mixed with water, blood or animal fat, as seen in cave art tens of thousands of years old and in the sophisticated plaster and casein paints of ancient Egypt. Linseed-oil paints matured in the fifteenth century and underpinned European easel painting and house painting alike. Industrial manufacture of ready-mixed paint began in the nineteenth century; the twentieth century brought synthetic resins, emulsion ("latex") paints, and — after 1945 — the near-displacement of toxic lead pigments, whose use in household paint was progressively restricted and banned in many countries during the 1970s and after.

Types and finishes

Paints are classified by binder (acrylic, latex, alkyd, epoxy, polyurethane, silicone), by use (interior, exterior, marine, automotive, artists' paints such as oil, acrylic, watercolour and gouache), and by sheen — from flat/matte through eggshell and satin to semi-gloss and gloss. Primers prepare and seal substrates; undercoats build the film; topcoats provide final colour and protection.

Health and environment

Solvent-based paints release volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that contribute to air pollution and are now tightly regulated in many jurisdictions, driving the shift to water-based formulations. Lead and chromate pigments persist as hazards in old paintwork and industrial coatings, and paint waste is managed as a controlled material in many places.
