Fetal homicide

Fetal homicide or foeticide refers to laws that treat the killing of a fetus as a form of homicide, or the act itself of unlawfully killing a fetus. Such laws vary widely by jurisdiction in whether they exist, at what stage of pregnancy they apply, and whether they treat the fetus as a legal person or victim for criminal-law purposes.

United States

In the United States, the federal Unborn Victims of Violence Act of 2004 recognises a "child in utero" as a legal victim when injured or killed during the commission of certain federal crimes of violence, while expressly exempting lawful abortion and the pregnant woman's own conduct. A majority of U.S. states have enacted fetal homicide laws, many of them in the wake of high-profile cases such as the murder of Laci Peterson in 2002. The gestational thresholds vary from conception to viability or birth.

Legal debate

Fetal homicide laws are the subject of ongoing debate. Supporters argue they recognise the loss suffered by families; critics argue they may conflict with abortion rights or expose pregnant women to prosecution. Most statutes exempt legal abortion and medical care.
