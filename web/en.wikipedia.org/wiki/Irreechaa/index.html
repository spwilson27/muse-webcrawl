Irreechaa

Irreechaa (also spelled Irreessa or Irreecha Birraa) is the thanksgiving festival of the Oromo people of Ethiopia, celebrated to give thanks to Waaqa (God) for the blessings of the past year and to mark the end of the rainy season and the beginning of spring (birraa).

Celebration

The main festival is held at the beginning of October at Hora Arsadi, a lake near the city of Bishoftu (Debre Zeit) southeast of Addis Ababa, and since 2019 also at Hora Finfinnee in the capital. Led by Abbaa Gadaa, the leaders of the Gadaa system, celebrants dressed in traditional clothing gather at the lakeshore carrying green grass and flowers, which they dip in the water while singing and praying for peace, fertility and prosperity for the coming year. A related festival, Irreechaa Tulluu, is celebrated on hilltops at the end of the dry season in some areas.

Significance

Irreechaa is the largest cultural festival of the Oromo, Ethiopia's largest ethnic group, drawing millions of participants and serving as an expression of Oromo identity. The Gadaa system, of which the festival forms part, was inscribed by UNESCO on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016. The 2016 festival at Bishoftu was marred by a deadly crowd disaster during protests, in which scores of people died.
