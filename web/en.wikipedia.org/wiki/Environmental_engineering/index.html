Environmental engineering
DisciplineBranch of civil engineering
FocusWater and air quality, waste treatment, public health protection
Historical rootSanitary engineering

Environmental engineering is a professional engineering discipline that applies scientific and engineering principles to protect human populations from the effects of adverse environmental factors, to protect environments from the potentially deleterious effects of natural and human activities, and to improve environmental quality. It grew out of sanitary engineering and is commonly regarded as a sub-discipline of civil engineering.

Scope

Environmental engineers work on drinking-water treatment and supply, wastewater collection and treatment, air pollution control, solid and hazardous waste management, contaminated-site remediation, and environmental impact assessment. Typical tasks include designing treatment plants and distribution systems, modelling the transport of contaminants in water, air, and soil, and assessing risks to public health.

History

The field descends from ancient public works — Roman aqueducts and sewers, and the sanitation reforms of nineteenth-century cities responding to cholera — and took modern shape in the twentieth century as chemical and biological treatment processes were developed and environmental legislation created demand for specialists. Professional practice is typically licensed, and the work overlaps with environmental law and regulation.
