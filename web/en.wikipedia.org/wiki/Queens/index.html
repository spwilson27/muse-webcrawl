Queens is a borough of New York City, coextensive with Queens County, in the U.S. state of New York. Located at the western end of Long Island, it is the largest of the five boroughs in area, the second-most populous after Brooklyn, and — by some measures the most ethnically diverse urban area in the world — home to residents speaking some 130 languages.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Queens comprises diverse neighbourhoods including Astoria, Flushing, Jamaica, Long Island City, Forest Hills, Jackson Heights, and the Rockaways. It hosts two of the New York metropolitan area's three major airports, JFK International and LaGuardia, as well as Flushing Meadows–Corona Park (site of the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs and the US Open tennis championship), Citi Field, and the Queens Museum.

History

Queens County was one of the original twelve counties of the Province of New York in 1683, named for Queen Catherine of Braganza, wife of Charles II. The borough was created in 1898 when western Queens joined Greater New York; the eastern towns formed Nassau County in 1899. Twentieth-century bridges, tunnels, subways, and airports transformed Queens into a dense residential and commercial centre.

Demographics

Queens has large immigrant communities from Latin America, South and East Asia, and elsewhere; nearly half of its residents were born abroad. It is served politically by a borough president and is coterminous with the Queens County district attorney's jurisdiction.
