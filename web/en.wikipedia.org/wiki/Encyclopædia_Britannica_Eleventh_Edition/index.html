Encyclopædia Britannica Eleventh Edition

Encyclopædia Britannica, 11th edition
Published: 1910–1911
Volumes: 29
Editor: Hugh Chisholm
Publisher: Cambridge University Press / Horace Everett Hooper

The Encyclopædia Britannica Eleventh Edition (1910–11) is a 29-volume reference work, an edition of the Encyclopædia Britannica that is widely regarded as the finest edition of the encyclopaedia ever produced. It was developed during a period of transition in the encyclopaedia's British and American ownership and was published in part by Cambridge University Press. It is noted for the high quality and scholarly authority of its articles, many written by the leading scholars and public figures of the day.

Contents

The eleventh edition contains some 40,000 articles over more than 33,000 pages, with contributions from more than 1,500 authors, including Ernest Rutherford, Bertrand Russell, Marie Curie, and Sigmund Freud's contemporaries across science, history, and literature. Its long signed articles — for example on history, geography, and biography — are often treated as primary documents of Edwardian scholarship. The edition was also the first to be issued all at once rather than volume by volume over years, and the first sold extensively by door-to-door and instalment marketing in the United States.

Public domain status and reuse

Because of its age, the eleventh edition is in the public domain in many jurisdictions, and its text has been digitised by projects such as Project Gutenberg and Wikisource. Early editions of Wikipedia drew heavily on its articles as seed material. Modern readers are cautioned that its science, medicine, and social attitudes reflect its era, including some of the prejudices of British imperial culture, but as a snapshot of knowledge around 1911 it remains widely consulted.
