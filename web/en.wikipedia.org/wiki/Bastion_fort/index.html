Bastion fort

A bastion fort or trace italienne (a phrase coined by the 19th-century French historian of the Renaissance and Italian wars, Martin du Bellay) is a fortification in a style developed during the early modern period in response to the ascendancy of gunpowder weapons such as cannon, which rendered earlier medieval approaches based on high walls obsolete. It took the form of a polygon with bastions at the corners of the walls. The first fortress of this kind was constructed in Italy; the style spread throughout Europe in the later 15th and 16th centuries.

A plan of a typical bastion fort, showing the angular bastions projecting from the curtain walls.

Design

The defining features of the bastion fort are its low, thick, sloped walls of earth and masonry, which absorbed cannon fire, and its angular bastions, which allowed defenders to direct flanking fire along every face of the wall, eliminating dead ground. The works were surrounded by a dry or wet ditch, a covered way, and a gently sloping glacis that exposed attackers to fire while shielding the walls. Outworks such as ravelins, hornworks, and crownworks protected the curtains and gates.

History

The design emerged in Italy during the Italian Wars, with early examples at Poggio Imperiale and Nettuno, and was perfected by engineers such as Francesco di Giorgio Martini and the Sangallo family. The fortress of Palmanova, founded by Venice in 1593, is a celebrated example of the idealised star-shaped plan. In the 17th century the French engineer Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban systematised bastion fortification and siege warfare, building or rebuilding scores of fortresses along France's frontiers. The style remained dominant until rifled artillery and explosive shells in the mid-19th century made masonry forts vulnerable, prompting the transition to polygonal forts and dispersed ring fortresses.

Legacy

Star forts survive across Europe, the Americas, and colonial sites worldwide; many are preserved as historic monuments, and the fortifications of Vauban are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
