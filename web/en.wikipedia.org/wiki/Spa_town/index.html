Spa town

A spa town is a resort town based on a mineral spa, a place with mineral-rich spring water used to give medicinal baths. Patrons visit to "take the waters" for their purported health benefits. Historically many such towns also offered gambling, horse racing, dancing and other entertainments to their wealthy visitors. The word spa derives from the Belgian town of Spa, known since Roman times for its healing springs.

History

Mineral springs were used for bathing and healing in the ancient world; the Romans built baths at sites such as Aquae Sulis (modern Bath, England) and Aquae (Baden-Baden, Germany). Spa towns flourished across Europe in the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries, when "taking a cure" became a fashionable ritual combining prescribed drinking of mineral water, bathing, diet and social display. Grand hotels, pump rooms, colonnades and casinos were built to serve the visitors. In 2021 UNESCO inscribed the "Great Spa Towns of Europe" — eleven towns in seven countries, including Bath, Spa, Vichy, Baden-Baden, Karlovy Vary and Montecatini Terme — as a transnational World Heritage Site.

Characteristics

Spa towns typically combine a protected spring source, drinking halls and bathhouses, landscaped parks and promenades for gentle exercise, and an infrastructure of hotels and cultural venues. Many towns carry the element in their names: "Bad" in German (Bad Kissingen, Wiesbaden), "Bath" in English, "Bains" in French, and "-on-Sea" or "Royal" prefixes granted to fashionable resorts. In Germany, official spa status (Kurort) is legally regulated and tied to certified therapeutic resources.

Modern spas

With the rise of scientific medicine, the therapeutic claims of the waters faded, but many spa towns reinvented themselves as wellness, conference and heritage destinations. The word spa has broadened to cover day spas, hotel spas and treatments such as massage, mud baths and hydrotherapy far from any natural spring.
