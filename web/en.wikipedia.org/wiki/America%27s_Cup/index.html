America's Cup

The America's Cup is a sailing trophy contested between yacht clubs, first awarded in 1851 when the schooner America won a race around the Isle of Wight. Governed by the Deed of Gift of 1857, it is the oldest international trophy in sport and is renowned for technological innovation, legal wrangling, and enormous cost. The defender races a single challenger (chosen through challenger selection series) in a match-race series.

History

The New York Yacht Club held the Cup for 132 years — the longest winning streak in sports — until Australia II, with its secret winged keel, won in 1983. Subsequent eras saw Dennis Conner's campaigns, the 1988 "Big Boat" mismatch and court battles, and New Zealand's victories from 1995. In the 2010s the event shifted to foiling catamarans and then to foiling AC75 monohulls, with Team New Zealand defending successfully in Auckland (2021) and Barcelona (2024).

The Deed of Gift

Challenges are governed by a 19th-century trust deed, supplemented by mutual-consent protocols covering boat class, venue, and format. Disputes repeatedly reached New York courts, making litigation part of the Cup's character.
