Feta

[Image: Cubes of feta cheese in brine with olives]

Overview

Feta is a Greek brined white cheese made from sheep's milk or a mixture of sheep and goat milk. It is formed into blocks and aged in brine, giving it a salty, tangy flavour and a crumbly, slightly grainy texture. Feta has PDO (protected designation of origin) status in the European Union: only cheese made in specific regions of Greece by traditional methods may bear the name there.

Uses

Feta is essential to Greek salad (horiatiki), spanakopita and many Balkan and Eastern Mediterranean dishes; it is baked, grilled and crumbled over watermelon in summer salads. Outside the EU, "feta-style" cheeses are made from cow's milk in many countries, which typically differ in texture and sharpness.
