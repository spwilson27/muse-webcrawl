SANAA (Sejima and Nishizawa and Associates) is an architectural firm based in Tokyo, Japan, founded in 1995 by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa. The firm's work is characterized by lightness, transparency and fluid spatial organization, often using glass, white surfaces and thin structural elements. In 2010 Sejima and Nishizawa jointly received the Pritzker Architecture Prize.

Notable works

  • 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa (2004) — a low circular glass building whose galleries are scattered like a small town within.
  • New Museum, New York (2007) — a stack of seven offset white boxes on the Bowery, the firm's first building in the United States.
  • Rolex Learning Center, EPFL (2010) — a single undulating slab pierced by courtyards, functioning as a continuous landscape of study space.
  • Louvre-Lens (2012) — a series of low, gently reflective pavilions in a former coal-mining landscape in northern France.
  • Grace Farms (2015) — a sinuous "River" building in New Canaan, Connecticut, flowing down its hillside site under one long roof.

Approach

SANAA's designs dissolve conventional divisions between rooms and between inside and outside. Circulation is treated as a field rather than a corridor, and programs are arranged so that activities remain visually connected. Both partners also maintain independent practices: Sejima directs Kazuyo Sejima & Associates, and Nishizawa the Office of Ryue Nishizawa.
