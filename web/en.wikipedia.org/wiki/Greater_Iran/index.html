Greater Iran

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greater Iran (Persian: ایران بزرگ, Irān-e Bozorg) refers to the regions of the Caucasus, West Asia, Central Asia, and parts of South Asia that have significant Iranian cultural influence due to having been ruled by or having close cultural ties to historical Iranian polities and peoples. It roughly corresponds to the territory on the Iranian plateau and its bordering plains.

The concept does not describe a present-day political state; rather, it denotes a cultural and historical sphere bound together by the Persian language, Iranian languages, Nowruz celebrations, and shared artistic, architectural, and literary traditions.

Extent

Greater Iran encompasses all of modern Iran and Afghanistan, Tajikistan, and parts of Pakistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, Iraq (particularly Kurdistan and Mesopotamia), and the North Caucasus, including Dagestan and Ossetia. Persian-speaking and other Iranic peoples are distributed across this whole region.

History

The Iranian world took shape under the Achaemenid Empire (550–330 BC), whose rule stretched from Anatolia to the Indus. Parthian and Sasanian empires perpetuated the idea of Ērānshahr, "the Empire of the Iranians". After the Arab conquests, Persian culture experienced a renaissance under dynasties such as the Samanids, and Persian became the lingua franca of courts from the Balkans to India under the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal empires.

Culture

Shared features of the region include the celebration of Nowruz (the Persian New Year), Persianate art and miniature painting, Sufi poetry in Persian, and the influence of figures such as Rumi, Hafez, Ferdowsi, and Omar Khayyam.
