Teatro Amazonas

The Teatro Amazonas (Amazon Theatre) is an opera house located in Manaus, in the heart of the Amazon rainforest in Brazil. It is the home of the Amazonas Philharmonic Orchestra and hosts the annual Festival Amazonas de Ópera. Built during the height of the Amazon rubber boom, it is regarded as one of the most beautiful opera houses in the world and a symbol of the wealth generated by rubber in the late 19th century.

History

Construction began in 1884 and, after stoppages, was completed in 1896. The theatre was inaugurated on 31 December 1896, with the first opera, Ponchielli's La Gioconda, performed on 7 January 1897. Materials were imported from Europe: steel from England, Murano glass chandeliers, tiles from Alsace, and wood from Brazilian forests finished in Italy.

Architecture

The building's distinctive dome is covered with 36,000 ceramic tiles painted in the colours of the Brazilian flag — green, yellow and blue. The central chandelier is composed of 198 glass lamps from Murano. The stage curtain, painted by the Italian artist Domenico De Angelis, depicts the meeting of the waters of the Rio Negro and the Solimões.

Decline and restoration

With the collapse of the rubber trade after 1912, Manaus declined and the theatre fell into disuse. After decades of neglect it was restored and reopened in 1990. A major programme of performances resumed in 1997 with the creation of the Amazonas Opera Festival.
