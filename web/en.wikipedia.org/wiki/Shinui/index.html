Shinui

Shinui (Hebrew: שינוי, "change" or "alteration") is a concept in Jewish law (halakha) referring to performing an action in an unusual or indirect manner, rather than in the normal, direct way. Doing something with a shinui can change its legal status — most notably on Shabbat and festivals, where acts done in a backhanded or otherwise abnormal fashion may be treated more leniently than the same acts done normally.

Use on Shabbat

Many melakhot (categories of prohibited work) are biblically forbidden only when performed in their usual manner (kederech). Performing them with a shinui — for example, writing with one's non-dominant hand, carrying an object between one's elbows or on the back of the hand, or pressing a button with a knuckle — may reduce the act to a rabbinic prohibition, which can matter when the act is needed for a sick person or another permitted purpose.

Other contexts

The term also appears in discussions of acquisition (kinyan), theft and restitution (where a thief who materially alters a stolen object may acquire it and owe only its value), and ritual procedure, where departures from the standard manner of performing a mitzvah can affect its validity. In a broader legal-philosophical sense, shinui illustrates how halakha evaluates not only what is done but the manner and intention with which it is done.
