Bora (wind)

Bora
TypeKatabatic wind
RegionEastern Adriatic coast — Slovenia, Croatia, Montenegro; also Novorossiysk (Black Sea)
DirectionFrom the northeast, descending from the Dinaric Alps
Recorded gustsOver 200 km/h in Croatia (unofficially up to 248 km/h)

The bora is a regional katabatic wind that blows from the northeast onto the eastern coast of the Adriatic Sea. In Italy it is called bora, in Slovenia burja, and in Croatia bura. Cold, dry, and notoriously gusty, it funnels through gaps in the Dinaric Alps and can rage for days, most often in winter. A similar wind at Novorossiysk on Russia's Black Sea coast is called the nordost ("northeaster").

Meteorology

The bora forms when a pool of cold continental air accumulates behind the Dinaric mountain barrier under high pressure while lower pressure sits over the warmer Adriatic. The dense air spills over passes — classically near Senj and through the Vratnik pass, and toward Trieste — and accelerates downhill, producing severe, turbulent gusts. Meteorologists distinguish a cyclonic ("dark") bora, accompanied by cloud and rain, from a clear-sky anticyclonic ("white") bora.

Effects

The bora shapes life along the coast: it clears the air, cures the region's famous air-dried hams (such as Dalmatian pršut), and historically drove the design of houses and drystone walls. It also disrupts traffic — bridges such as the Krk and Maslenica crossings close in extreme gusts — and endangers shipping. Gusts at Senj and on the A1 motorway have been measured among the strongest in Europe, exceeding 200 km/h.
