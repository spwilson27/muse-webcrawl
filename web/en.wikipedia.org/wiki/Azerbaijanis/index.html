Azerbaijanis (Azerbaijani: Azərbaycanlılar), also called Azeri Turks, are a Turkic ethnic group native to the region of Azerbaijan in the South Caucasus and northwestern Iran. They are the largest ethnic group in the Republic of Azerbaijan and form the second-largest ethnic group in neighbouring Iran, where the majority of the world's Azerbaijanis live, concentrated in the provinces of East and West Azerbaijan, Ardabil and Zanjan. Sizeable diaspora communities exist in Turkey, Russia, Georgia, and the West.

History

The ethnogenesis of the Azerbaijanis involved the Turkification, between the 11th and 14th centuries, of the earlier Iranian- and Caucasian-speaking populations of the region through settlement by Oghuz Turkic tribes, layered onto older populations of Atropatene, Caucasian Albania and Arran. The region was subsequently ruled by the Seljuks, Mongols, Turkmen confederations (Qara Qoyunlu and Aq Qoyunlu), and the Safavid Empire, founded by Shah Ismail I of Ardabil, under which Twelver Shi'ism became entrenched. The treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828) divided Azerbaijanis between the Russian and Persian empires, a division reflected today in the border along the Aras River.

Language

The Azerbaijani language belongs to the Oghuz branch of the Turkic languages and is closely related to Turkish and Turkmen. It is written in a modified Latin alphabet in the Republic of Azerbaijan (since 1991), in the Perso-Arabic script in Iran, and was written in Cyrillic during the Soviet period. North and South Azerbaijani form a dialect continuum with largely mutual intelligibility.

Culture

Azerbaijani culture blends Turkic, Persian and Caucasian elements. It is internationally known for mugham vocal-improvisational music (inscribed by UNESCO), the improvisational poetry contests of ashiks, carpet weaving, miniature painting, and cuisine featuring plov, dolma, kebabs and black tea served in armudu glasses. Novruz, the spring new year, is the most important traditional holiday. The epic of Kitabi Dede Gorgud and the poetry of Nizami (claimed in the region's literary heritage), Fuzuli and, in the modern era, the satirist Sabir and composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov, are central cultural reference points.

Religion

The great majority of Azerbaijanis are Twelver Shia Muslims, with a Sunni minority concentrated in northern parts of the Republic of Azerbaijan. Levels of observance vary widely: the Republic of Azerbaijan is constitutionally secular, while Iranian Azerbaijanis practise within the Islamic Republic's framework. Pre-Islamic Zoroastrian heritage is reflected in Novruz customs and in sites such as the Ateshgah fire temple near Baku.
