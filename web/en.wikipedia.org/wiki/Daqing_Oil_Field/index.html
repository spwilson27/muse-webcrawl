Daqing Oil Field

The Daqing Oil Field (Chinese: 大庆油田; pinyin: Dàqìng Yóutián) is the largest oil field in the People's Republic of China, located between the Songhua River and Nen River in Heilongjiang province. Discovered on 26 September 1959, it transformed China from a net oil importer into a self-sufficient producer and became a national symbol of industrial achievement. The city of Daqing grew up around the field and takes its name ("Great Celebration") from its discovery, announced on the eve of the 10th anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic.

History

After discovery, tens of thousands of workers were mobilized in the "Daqing battle" (Daqing huizhan) from 1960, developing the field under harsh conditions on the Songliao Plain. The campaign produced propaganda icons such as "Iron Man" Wang Jinxi, a drilling team leader celebrated as a model worker. For decades Daqing produced around 50 million tonnes of crude oil per year — roughly half of China's output — and it passed a cumulative total of more than 2.5 billion tonnes of production in the 2020s.

Geology

The field lies in the Songliao Basin, a large Cretaceous rift basin in northeast China. The main reservoirs are fluvial and deltaic sandstones of the Cretaceous Quantou, Qingshankou, Yaojia and Nenjiang formations, sealed by lacustrine mudstones which are also the source rocks.

Operations and decline

The field is operated by Daqing Oilfield Company, a subsidiary of PetroChina. Output declined steadily from its long plateau, to around 30 million tonnes per year by the mid-2020s, partly offset by shale oil development in the Gulong shale and by enhanced oil recovery using waterflooding, polymer flooding and ASP (alkali-surfactant-polymer) flooding, in which Daqing is a world leader.

Legacy

"Learn from Daqing in industry" was a national campaign slogan under Mao Zedong, and the "Daqing spirit" and "Iron Man spirit" remain fixtures of Chinese political culture.
