National Capital Region (India)

National Capital Region (NCR)
Country: India
Core: National Capital Territory of Delhi
States covered: Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan
Area: about 55,000 km²
Population: over 58 million (2021 estimates)
Planning body: National Capital Region Planning Board (NCRPB)

The National Capital Region (NCR) is a metropolitan planning region in northern India centered on Delhi, the national capital. It encompasses the entire National Capital Territory of Delhi together with districts of the neighboring states of Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, making it one of the world's largest urban agglomerations by population.

Constituent areas

Major cities in the NCR include Delhi, Gurugram (Gurgaon), Noida and Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, and Faridabad, along with satellite and counter-magnet towns such as Meerut, Sonipat, Panipat, Rohtak, Alwar, and Bulandshahr. The region was formally constituted under the National Capital Region Planning Board Act, 1985, which created the NCR Planning Board to coordinate development, infrastructure, and land use across state boundaries and to ease population pressure on Delhi itself.

Transport and economy

The NCR is served by an expanding transport network that includes the Delhi Metro, national highways and expressways such as the Delhi–Meerut Expressway, and the Regional Rapid Transit System (RRTS), whose first corridor connects Delhi with Ghaziabad and Meerut. The region is a major economic hub for information technology, manufacturing, finance, and services, with Gurugram and Noida hosting large corporate and IT sectors.

Issues

Rapid growth has brought severe challenges, most notably chronic air pollution—Delhi is frequently ranked among the world's most polluted capitals—along with groundwater depletion, traffic congestion, and pressure on housing and public services.
