Wali Sanga

The Wali Sanga (Javanese: ꦮꦭꦶꦱꦔ, "Nine Saints") are nine revered saints credited in Javanese tradition with spreading Islam in Java, Indonesia, during the 15th and 16th centuries. Known collectively as the Nine Saints of Java, they are venerated to this day, and their tombs are major pilgrimage destinations.

The nine saints

The composition of the nine varies somewhat between traditions, but the list most commonly given is:

  • Sunan Gresik (Maulana Malik Ibrahim), regarded as the first of the saints, buried at Gresik
  • Sunan Ampel (Raden Rahmat) of Surabaya, founder of the Ampel mosque and pesantren
  • Sunan Bonang (Makhdum Ibrahim), son of Sunan Ampel, associated with Tuban
  • Sunan Drajat, his brother, associated with Lamongan
  • Sunan Giri (Raden Paku), founder of the Giri religious centre near Gresik
  • Sunan Kalijaga (Raden Said), famed for using wayang puppetry, gamelan and Javanese arts to teach Islam
  • Sunan Kudus (Ja'far Shadiq), founder of Kudus, whose mosque minaret resembles a Hindu temple tower
  • Sunan Muria, associated with Mount Muria
  • Sunan Gunungjati (Syarif Hidayatullah), founder of Cirebon and Banten

Historical role

The Wali Sanga worked along the north coast of Java (the Pasisir), where trade with Muslim merchants from Arabia, Persia, India and China was concentrated. Rather than imposing Islam by force, tradition credits them with adapting existing Javanese culture — incorporating Hindu-Buddhist architectural forms into mosques, tolerating local customs, and composing songs and stories in Javanese. Their preaching is traditionally linked with the decline of the Hindu-Buddhist Majapahit empire and the rise of Muslim sultanates such as Demak, Cirebon and Banten.

Because the sources for their lives are largely later Javanese chronicles (babad) and oral tradition, historians treat many details as legendary, but the saints' importance in Javanese Islam is undisputed.
