Quds Force

The Quds Force (Persian: نیروی قدس, romanized: Nirū-ye Qods, lit. "Jerusalem Force") is one of five branches of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), specializing in unconventional warfare and foreign military intelligence operations. It is responsible for carrying out extraterritorial operations abroad and supports non-state actors in many countries, including Hezbollah in Lebanon, Hamas in Palestine, and the Houthis in Yemen.

Emblem of the Quds Force

History

The Quds Force was founded during the Iran–Iraq War as a special unit deployed into Iraqi Kurdistan. It expanded its role after the war, conducting operations in Afghanistan, Lebanon, and the Balkans. During the Iraq War, it was accused by the United States of supplying explosively formed penetrator weapons to Iraqi insurgents.

Major General Qasem Soleimani commanded the Quds Force from 1998 until his assassination by a United States drone strike at Baghdad International Airport on January 3, 2020. He was succeeded by Esmail Qaani.

Organization and activities

The force is organized into regional directorates and is estimated to number in the thousands. Its activities range from training and arming allied militias to intelligence gathering and covert action. The United States designated the IRGC, including the Quds Force, as a Foreign Terrorist Organization in April 2019, the first time the designation was applied to part of another state's military.
