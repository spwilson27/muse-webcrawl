Tabs of the United States Army

In the United States Army, tabs are cloth insignia worn on the shoulder sleeve that indicate a special skill, qualification or unit affiliation. Tabs are worn above the shoulder sleeve insignia (unit patch) on the left shoulder of the uniform. Some tabs are awarded to individuals who complete demanding courses of training; others are part of a unit's insignia and are worn only while assigned to that unit.

Individual skill tabs

The best-known individual tabs are the Ranger tab, awarded to graduates of Ranger School; the Special Forces tab, awarded on completion of the Special Forces Qualification Course; and the Sapper tab, awarded to graduates of the Sapper Leader Course at Fort Leonard Wood. The President's Hundred tab is awarded to soldiers who place in the top one hundred competitors in the President's Match rifle and pistol competitions and is one of the few tabs that may be worn permanently regardless of assignment.

Unit tabs

Certain units incorporate a tab into their shoulder sleeve insignia, such as the Airborne tab worn above the patch of airborne units, the Mountain tab of the 10th Mountain Division, and the Arctic tab. These are worn only while a soldier serves with the unit in question.

Order of precedence

Army regulations specify that only three individual skill tabs may be worn at one time, in a prescribed order of precedence, and govern the colours and subdued versions worn on camouflage uniforms.
