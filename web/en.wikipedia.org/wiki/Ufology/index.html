Ufology

Ufology is the investigation of unidentified flying objects (UFOs) by people known as ufologists, who have many sub-groups and factions. Though there have been official studies on the subject, ufology has generally not been embraced by academia and is often considered a borderline to pseudoscientific field; mainstream scientists generally argue that nearly all sightings are misidentified natural phenomena, aircraft, or hoaxes.

History

The modern era of ufology began in 1947 with pilot Kenneth Arnold's sighting of objects near Mount Rainier, Washington, which the press dubbed "flying saucers". Reports multiplied, and the US Air Force ran successive investigations — Project Sign (1948), Project Grudge, and Project Blue Book (1952–1969), which examined over 12,000 reports. The 1968 Condon Report concluded further scientific study was unlikely to be productive, and Blue Book closed in 1969. Civilian groups such as NICAP, APRO and later MUFON (founded 1969) carried on investigations. The closed field was revived in public debate by the Roswell incident literature, the 2004 USS Nimitz encounters and the 2017 press revelations of the Pentagon's AATIP program, followed by congressional hearings and the rebranding of UFOs as UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena).

Methods and classifications

Astronomer J. Allen Hynek, a Blue Book consultant who became a prominent ufologist, proposed the "close encounter" classification (CE-1 to CE-3) popularised by the 1977 film Close Encounters of the Third Kind. Ufologists compile sighting databases, interview witnesses, and examine photographs and radar data. The extraterrestrial hypothesis, the psychosocial hypothesis and other explanations compete within the field.

Reception

Critics, including skeptics such as Philip J. Klass, contend that ufology lacks the methods and falsifiability of science, while supporters argue that official dismissal and stigma have impeded legitimate enquiry into a real aerial-phenomena problem. Recent government offices such as the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO, established 2022) have resumed official collection of reports.
