Infix notation

Infix notation is the notation commonly used in arithmetical and logical formulae and statements. It is characterized by the placement of operators between operands — "infixed operators" — such as the plus sign in 2 + 2.

Precedence and associativity

Because multiple operators may appear in one expression, infix notation relies on rules of operator precedence and associativity, supplemented by parentheses, to determine the order of evaluation. For example, multiplication is conventionally performed before addition, so 2 + 3 × 4 equals 14 rather than 20.

Comparison with other notations

Infix notation contrasts with prefix notation (Polish notation), in which the operator precedes its operands, and postfix notation (reverse Polish notation), in which it follows them. Prefix and postfix forms need no parentheses or precedence rules, which makes them convenient for stack-based evaluation in computers.

Conversion

Infix expressions are commonly converted to postfix form using Dijkstra's shunting-yard algorithm, after which they can be evaluated with a simple stack machine.
