Daniel Kleitman

Daniel J. Kleitman (born October 4, 1934) is an American mathematician and professor of applied mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). His research interests include combinatorics, graph theory, genomics and operations research. He is known, among other things, for the Kleitman–Winston algorithm for counting lattice paths and for his work on the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem in extremal combinatorics.

Biography

Kleitman was born in Brooklyn, New York. He studied physics at Cornell University, graduating in 1954, and earned his PhD in physics from Harvard University in 1958 under Julian Schwinger. He was a postdoctoral researcher and assistant professor at Brandeis University before joining the MIT faculty in 1966, where he shifted his research focus from theoretical physics to combinatorics.

Research

Kleitman's work spans extremal set theory, partially ordered sets, and graph theory. He proved results on intersecting families of sets related to the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem, worked on the combinatorics of the hypercube, and contributed to the study of sorting networks and lattice paths. He collaborated with Paul Erdős on multiple papers; his Erdős number is 1. He also served as a mathematical consultant on the 1997 film Good Will Hunting.

Selected publications

Kleitman has authored and co-authored more than 200 papers in combinatorics and related fields, including work with Noga Alon, Béla Bollobás and Joel Spencer.
