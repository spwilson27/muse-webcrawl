Hong Kong nationalism

Hong Kong nationalism is an ideology that promotes Hong Kong as a distinct political community. It gained attention in the 2010s, particularly among younger Hong Kongers, amid debates over Hong Kong's autonomy under the "one country, two systems" framework following the 1997 handover from Britain to China. Some adherents argue that a distinct "Hong Kong nation" exists.

Origins

Ideas of a distinct Hong Kong identity developed during the colonial era and grew after the handover. Scholar Chin Wan's 2011 work On the Hong Kong City-State is often cited as an early articulation of localist theory. The University of Hong Kong's magazine Undergrad published a 2014 volume on Hong Kong nationhood that drew a rebuke from Chief Executive Leung Chun-ying.

Development

The 2014 Umbrella Movement and the rise of localist political groups accelerated discussion. Parties and groups such as Hong Kong Indigenous and the short-lived Hong Kong National Party (banned in 2018) advocated varying degrees of self-determination or independence, positions the Beijing and Hong Kong governments deem violations of the Basic Law. The 2020 National Security Law effectively ended public advocacy of Hong Kong nationalism.

Scholarly debate

Academics debate whether Hong Kong nationalism qualifies as ethnic, civic or cultural nationalism, and how it relates to the broader pro-democracy and localist movements.
