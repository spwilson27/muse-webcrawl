Fader

Fader
TypeAudio control device
Common formsLinear (sliding), rotary, crossfader
Used onMixing consoles, DJ mixers, lighting desks

A fader is any device used for fading, especially when it is a knob or button that slides along a track or slot. A knob which rotates is usually not considered a fader, although it is electrically and functionally equivalent in most cases. The most common use of a fader is in audio engineering, where it controls the level of a signal on a mixing console.

Types

Linear faders (also called channel faders) slide along a straight track and are the primary level controls on professional mixing consoles; their position gives an immediate visual indication of relative channel levels. Rotary faders or pots perform the same function with a rotating knob and are common on smaller mixers. A crossfader on a DJ mixer fades between two sources, typically the two turntable or deck channels, and is central to scratching and beatmatching techniques.

Implementation

Traditional faders are potentiometers that directly attenuate an analogue signal. In modern digital and automated consoles, faders may instead control a voltage-controlled amplifier (VCA) or send position data to a digital signal processor, allowing fader movements to be recorded and replayed (flying faders) during mix automation. Fader taper — the relationship between physical position and gain — is usually approximately logarithmic to match human perception of loudness, with decibel markings printed along the track.

Other uses

Faders are also used on lighting control consoles to set the intensity of stage lighting channels, and in video production switchers for transitions. The term "fade" in film and broadcasting derives from the same gradual increase or decrease of signal or image.
