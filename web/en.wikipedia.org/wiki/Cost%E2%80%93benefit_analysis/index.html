Cost–benefit analysis

Cost–benefit analysis (CBA) is a systematic approach to estimating the strengths and weaknesses of alternatives by comparing their total expected costs against their total expected benefits, expressed in monetary terms. It is widely used by governments and businesses to decide whether a project or policy is worthwhile.

Method

Analysts list all costs and benefits, assign monetary values (including intangible effects such as time savings or environmental damage), discount future values to the present using a discount rate, and compute measures such as net present value and the benefit–cost ratio.

Criticism

Critics note that CBA depends on contested valuations, may underweight distributional effects on the poor, and can be manipulated through optimistic assumptions.
