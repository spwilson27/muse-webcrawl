Lesotho Highlands Water Project

LocationLesotho and South Africa
Treaty signed1986
Key structuresKatse Dam, Mohale Dam, Polihali Dam
PurposeWater transfer and hydroelectric power

The Lesotho Highlands Water Project (LHWP) is a large, multi-phase water supply project under development since the 1980s between the governments of Lesotho and South Africa. It diverts water from the Senqu River system in the highlands of Lesotho — a mountainous, water-rich but economically poor enclaved kingdom — northward to South Africa's industrial Gauteng province around Johannesburg, while generating hydroelectric power for Lesotho. It is one of the largest infrastructure projects of its kind in the world.

Phases

The project treaty was signed in 1986. Phase I, completed in the late 1990s and early 2000s, included the Katse Dam on the Malibamat'so River — at 185 metres one of Africa's tallest dams — the Mohale Dam, delivery tunnels through the Maluti Mountains, and the 'Muela hydroelectric power station. Phase II, whose construction began in the 2010s, centres on the Polihali Dam on the Senqu River and a transfer tunnel connecting the Polihali reservoir to the Katse reservoir, increasing the volume of water transferred to South Africa.

Benefits and controversy

Lesotho receives royalties for the water, a major source of government revenue, as well as hydropower that made the country largely self-sufficient in electricity. South Africa gains a reliable supply for its economic heartland. The project has also been controversial: tens of thousands of highland residents were displaced or lost land and grazing to reservoirs, with compensation programmes frequently criticized as inadequate; environmental effects on downstream flows have been a concern; and the project was the subject of a major corruption scandal in the late 1990s and 2000s, in which Lesotho prosecuted its own project chief executive and several international engineering firms for bribery — a landmark case in international anti-corruption enforcement.
