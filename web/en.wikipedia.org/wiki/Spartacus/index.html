Spartacus

Spartacus (Greek: Σπάρτακος; Latin: Spartacus; c. 103–71 BC) was a Thracian gladiator who, along with Crixus, Gannicus, Castus, and Oenomaus, was one of the escaped slave leaders in the Third Servile War.

Origins

Ancient sources say Spartacus was a Thracian who served in the Roman army, was enslaved, and trained as a gladiator at Capua in the school of Lentulus Batiatus.

The revolt

In 73 BC Spartacus and about 70 gladiators escaped, seized weapons, and defeated Roman forces sent against them, gathering an army of perhaps 70,000 escaped slaves on Mount Vesuvius. They defeated several Roman armies in 73–72 BC and ranged across Italy. Spartacus reportedly wished to cross the Alps to freedom, but his army turned south.

Defeat and death

Marcus Licinius Crassus took command with eight legions, restored discipline, and trapped the rebels in Bruttium. Spartacus was killed in battle near the Silarus River in 71 BC; his body was never found. Some 6,000 captured followers were crucified along the Appian Way. Pompey, returning from Spain, destroyed remnants and claimed credit.

Legacy

Spartacus became a symbol of resistance to oppression, invoked by revolutionaries, in Howard Fast's novel and Stanley Kubrick's 1960 film Spartacus, and by the German Spartacus League.
