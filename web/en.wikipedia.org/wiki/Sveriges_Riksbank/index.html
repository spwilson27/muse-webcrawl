Sveriges Riksbank

Sveriges Riksbank, or simply the Riksbank, is the central bank of Sweden. Founded in 1668, it is the world's oldest surviving central bank and the world's third oldest bank in continuous operation. It is responsible for monetary policy — maintaining price stability with an inflation target of 2 per cent — issuing Swedish krona banknotes and coins, and overseeing the payment system.

Established: 1668

History

The Riksbank succeeded Stockholms Banco, founded by Johan Palmstruch, which in 1661 issued Europe's first banknotes but collapsed after over-issuing them. In 1668 the Swedish parliament (Riksdag) took banking under its own control as the Riksens Ständers Bank — hence the bank's historic subordination to parliament rather than the Crown or government. It received a monopoly on banknote issue in the late 19th century. In 1968, on its tercentenary, the bank established the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, often called the Nobel Prize in Economics.

Governance and policy

An eleven-member General Council, appointed by the Riksdag, oversees the bank and appoints its Executive Board, which takes monetary policy decisions, including the policy rate. Since 1993 the Riksbank has pursued an explicit inflation target, announced in advance, of 2 per cent CPI inflation. The bank also operates RIX, the central payment system for Swedish kronor, and holds Sweden's foreign-exchange reserves.

Notable episodes

During the Swedish banking crisis of the early 1990s, the Riksbank famously raised its overnight marginal lending rate to 500 per cent in September 1992 in a doomed defence of the krona's fixed exchange rate, which was abandoned that November. The bank was among the pioneers of negative policy rates (2015–2019) and of large-scale asset purchases, and it has been a leading explorer of central bank digital currency with its e-krona project.
