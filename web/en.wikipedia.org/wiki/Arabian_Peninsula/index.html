The Arabian Peninsula is a peninsula in Western Asia, situated northeast of Africa on the Arabian Plate. It is the largest peninsula in the world, at about 3.2 million km². The peninsula consists of Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait, and parts of southern Iraq and Jordan. It is bounded by the Red Sea to the west and southwest, the Gulf of Aden and Arabian Sea to the south and southeast, and the Gulf of Oman and the Persian Gulf to the east.

Geography and climate

Most of the peninsula is desert — the Arabian Desert covers much of Saudi Arabia, including the Rub' al Khali (Empty Quarter), the largest continuous sand desert in the world. The southwest (Yemen and Oman) is mountainous and catches monsoon rains from the Indian Ocean. Water is scarce: there are no permanent rivers of size, and countries such as Saudi Arabia depend heavily on groundwater and desalination. The peninsula holds a large share of the world's proven petroleum reserves, which transformed its economies after oil was discovered in Bahrain (1932) and Saudi Arabia (1938).

History

Arabia was home to ancient trading kingdoms dealing in frankincense and myrrh, and its ports linked the Mediterranean with India along the Silk Road sea lanes. In the 7th century CE, Islam arose in the Hejaz cities of Mecca and Medina and spread rapidly across and beyond the peninsula. For centuries the interior was largely left to Bedouin tribes and local emirates while Ottoman and later British influence held the coasts; the modern map emerged in the 20th century, above all with the unification of Saudi Arabia in 1932.
