Juanita mummy

Mummy Juanita (Spanish: Momia Juanita), also known as the Inca Ice Maiden and the Lady of Ampato, is the well-preserved frozen body of a girl of about 12–15 years who was sacrificed as an offering to the Inca gods around 1440–1480. She was discovered in 1995 near the summit of Mount Ampato in southern Peru and is one of the best-preserved mummies from the pre-Columbian Americas.

Discovery

Anthropologist Johan Reinhard and his Peruvian climbing partner Miguel Zárate found the mummy in September 1995 after volcanic ash from the nearby Sabancaya eruption had melted the ice cap and exposed a ceremonial platform near Ampato's summit (6,288 m). Wrapped in fine alpaca textiles, she was accompanied by grave goods including pottery, figurines of gold, silver and spondylus shell, and food offerings.

Scientific findings

Studies indicate she died from a blow to the head as part of the Inca capacocha ritual. CT scans and DNA analysis, along with analysis of her stomach contents and hair chemistry, have revealed details of her diet — which shifted to maize and animal protein before the sacrifice — and her general good health. Because her body froze rather than desiccating, her skin, organs, blood and even stomach contents survived.

Exhibition

After a period of international exhibition, including display at the National Geographic Society in Washington, D.C., the mummy is housed at the Museo Santuarios Andinos of the Catholic University of Santa María in Arequipa, Peru, kept in a specially refrigerated glass case.
