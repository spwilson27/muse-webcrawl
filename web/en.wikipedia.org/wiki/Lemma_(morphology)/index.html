Lemma (morphology)

Lemmas serve as the headwords of dictionary entries

In morphology and lexicography, a lemma (plural lemmas or lemmata) is the canonical, dictionary form of a word — the form chosen as the headword under which a set of related word forms (a lexeme) is listed. For English verbs the lemma is the infinitive without "to" (go subsumes goes, going, went, gone); for nouns it is usually the singular (cat, not cats). The process of reducing word forms to their lemmas is called lemmatisation.

Conventions across languages

Lemma conventions vary with a language's morphology. Latin dictionaries list verbs by the first-person singular present indicative (amō, "I love") together with principal parts, and nouns by the nominative singular with the genitive. Ancient Greek verbs are likewise cited in the first person singular. Arabic dictionaries traditionally organise entries by consonantal root, and Hebrew lexica by the third-person masculine singular of the past tense. In languages with extensive inflection, choosing a lemma involves explicit conventions so that users can find any inflected form.

Lemma versus stem

A lemma should be distinguished from a stem: a stem is the part of a word to which affixes attach, which may not be a complete or pronounceable word, whereas a lemma is always a full citation form. Similarly, lemmatisation differs from stemming in computational linguistics — stemming crudely truncates words (studies → studi), while lemmatisation uses vocabulary and morphological analysis to return the true dictionary form (studies → study, better → good).

Applications

Lemmatisation is a standard step in natural language processing, improving information retrieval, text mining, corpus linguistics, and machine translation by grouping inflections under a single entry; word-frequency counts are likewise usually reported by lemma. In psycholinguistics, the lemma is also the name of an abstract level of lexical representation in models of speech production, mediating between meaning and grammatical encoding.
