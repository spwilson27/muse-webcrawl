Daniel K. Inouye International Airport

IATA/ICAO: HNL / PHNL
Location: Honolulu, Hawaii, U.S.
Opened: 1927 (as John Rodgers Airport)
Named for: Senator Daniel K. Inouye
Runways: 4, including the offshore Reef Runway

Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (IATA: HNL) is the main airport serving Honolulu and the island of Oahu in Hawaii. Located about 5 km northwest of downtown Honolulu, it is one of the busiest airports in the United States and the principal Pacific gateway to the Hawaiian Islands, shared by civilian traffic and adjacent Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam.

History

The airport opened in 1927 as John Rodgers Airport and was renamed Honolulu Airport in 1947 and Honolulu International Airport in 1951. Its famous Reef Runway (8R/26L), built on an artificial reef offshore and opened in 1977, was designated an emergency landing site for the Space Shuttle. In 2017 the airport was renamed for Daniel K. Inouye, the longtime U.S. senator from Hawaii.

Facilities

The airport has three terminals and four runways, with distinctive open-air walkways and gardens in Hawaiian, Japanese and Chinese styles between concourses. It serves as a hub for Hawaiian Airlines and handles interisland, mainland U.S., trans-Pacific and military traffic.
