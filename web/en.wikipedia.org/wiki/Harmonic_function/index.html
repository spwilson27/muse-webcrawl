Harmonic function

In mathematics, mathematical physics and stochastic processes, a harmonic function is a twice continuously differentiable function u defined on an open subset of Euclidean space that satisfies Laplace's equation:

Δu = ∂²u/∂x₁² + ∂²u/∂x₂² + … + ∂²u/∂xₙ² = 0.

Harmonic functions are the steady-state solutions of the heat and diffusion equations and describe gravitational and electrostatic potentials in charge-free regions.

Properties

Harmonic functions enjoy a remarkably rigid structure. They satisfy the mean value property: the value at any point equals the average of the values over any ball or sphere centred at that point. Consequently they obey the maximum principle — a non-constant harmonic function cannot attain a maximum or minimum inside its domain. Harmonic functions are automatically infinitely differentiable and analytic, and Liouville's theorem states that a bounded harmonic function on all of Euclidean space must be constant.

Connections

In two dimensions, the real and imaginary parts of every holomorphic function are harmonic, and every harmonic function on a simply connected domain has a harmonic conjugate. Harmonic functions on domains are uniquely determined by their boundary values (the Dirichlet problem), a fact proven in generality through Perron's method and the theory of Brownian motion, where harmonic functions describe expected values of functions of a random walker's position.

Examples

Examples include the potentials 1/r in three dimensions and log r in two, the temperature distribution in a body at equilibrium, polynomials such as Re(zⁿ), and the spherical harmonics used to solve problems in spherical symmetry.
