The vacuum magnetic permeability (variously vacuum permeability, permeability of free space, or the magnetic constant) is the magnetic permeability of a classical vacuum. It is a physical constant, conventionally denoted μ0, which relates the magnetic field B produced in vacuum to the magnetic field strength H:

B = μ0H

Value

Until the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units, μ0 had the exact defined value 4π×10−7 H/m, a consequence of the former definition of the ampere in terms of the force between two current-carrying wires. Since 20 May 2019 the ampere is instead defined by fixing the elementary charge, so μ0 became an experimentally determined quantity, measured via the fine-structure constant. Its value is now very close to, but no longer exactly, 4π×10−7 H/m.

Relation to other constants

Vacuum permeability is linked to the vacuum permittivity ε0 and the speed of light c by the relation μ0ε0c2 = 1, reflecting the electromagnetic nature of light described by Maxwell's equations. Together with ε0 it determines the impedance of free space, about 376.73 Ω.
