Wild West shows

Placeholder image: a Buffalo Bill's Wild West poster.

Wild West shows were travelling vaudeville performances in the United States and Europe that existed between 1870 and 1920. The shows introduced a romanticised version of the American frontier, beginning with the first western frontier play in 1874, and were the longest-running and most popular form of outdoor entertainment of their era. They are the origin of many enduring cowboy and frontier stereotypes in popular culture.

Buffalo Bill's Wild West

The most famous of the shows was Buffalo Bill's Wild West, founded by William F. "Buffalo Bill" Cody in 1883 in North Platte, Nebraska. Its arena spectacles featured re-enacted battles (including Custer's Last Stand), stagecoach attacks, sharpshooting exhibitions by Annie Oakley and others, trick riding, roping, and displays by Native American performers — the Lakota leader Sitting Bull toured with the show for a season in 1885. The show toured Europe to great acclaim, performing for Queen Victoria in 1887 during her Golden Jubilee.

Other shows and legacy

Competitors and successors included Pawnee Bill's Wild West, the Miller Brothers' 101 Ranch Wild West Show, and numerous smaller troupes; performers such as Will Rogers, Tom Mix, and Lucille Mulhall began their careers in the shows. The genre declined with the rise of cinema — western films absorbed its performers and imagery — and the last major shows folded during the Great Depression. Wild West shows profoundly shaped international images of the American West and influenced the modern rodeo.
