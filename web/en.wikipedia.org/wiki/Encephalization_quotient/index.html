Encephalization quotient

Encephalization quotient
Developed byHarry Jerison (1973)
MeasuresActual brain mass relative to that expected for body size

The encephalization quotient (EQ) is a measure of relative brain size, defined as the ratio between actual brain mass and predicted brain mass for an animal of a given body mass, based on an allometric regression across reference species. Proposed by Harry Jerison in 1973, it is used as a rough proxy for cognitive capacity across species.

Values

By definition the average mammal has EQ ≈ 1. Humans score highest at roughly 7–8; dolphins around 4–5; chimpanzees about 2–2.5; dogs and cats near 1–1.2; rats about 0.4. Jerison's formula uses brain mass E = 0.12 P^(2/3) (P = body mass in grams, for mammals).

Criticism

EQ depends on the reference group and exponent chosen, conflates structures with different functions, and correlates only loosely with intelligence within groups. Alternatives include absolute neuron counts (e.g. Suzana Herculano-Houzel's work) and residual brain-size measures.
