Index on Censorship

Type: Magazine and campaigning organisation

Founded: 1972

Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

Focus: Freedom of expression

Index on Censorship is a London-based non-profit organisation and quarterly magazine that campaigns for freedom of expression worldwide and publishes censored writers, journalists, and artists. Its name refers to the Index Librorum Prohibitorum, the Catholic Church's historical list of prohibited books.

History

The organisation grew out of Writers and Scholars International, founded in 1968 by poet Stephen Spender and others in response to appeals from Soviet dissidents. The magazine was launched in 1972 under founding editor Michael Scammell, publishing work by writers such as Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, Milan Kundera, Václav Havel, and Nadine Gordimer whose writing was banned in their home countries.

Activities

Alongside the magazine, Index on Censorship runs advocacy and monitoring projects documenting violations of free expression, including censorship of the arts and digital restrictions. Since 2001 it has presented the annual Freedom of Expression Awards, honouring journalists, campaigners, artists, and digital activists who have defended free speech under pressure.
