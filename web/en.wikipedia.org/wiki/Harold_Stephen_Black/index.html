Harold Stephen Black (April 14, 1898 – December 11, 1983) was an American electrical engineer who revolutionized the field of applied electronics by inventing the negative feedback amplifier in 1927.

Career

Black graduated from Worcester Polytechnic Institute and joined Western Electric, later working at Bell Laboratories. He spent much of his career working on telephone repeater amplifiers, which had to boost voice signals repeatedly over long-distance lines without accumulating distortion.

Negative feedback amplifier

Black conceived the idea of negative feedback on August 2, 1927, while crossing the Hudson River on the Lackawanna Ferry on his way to work. By feeding a portion of an amplifier's output back to its input out of phase, distortion and variations in gain could be greatly reduced at the cost of some amplification. His patent application, filed in 1928, was not granted until 1937 because the concept seemed so counterintuitive to examiners. The invention became foundational to control theory, audio engineering, and telecommunications.

Awards

Black received numerous honours, including induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. He published the classic paper "Stabilized Feedback Amplifiers" in 1934.
