Chattogram

Skyline of Chattogram, Bangladesh's principal seaport.

Chattogram (Bengali: চট্টগ্রাম, Chôṭṭôgram), formerly known officially as Chittagong, is the second-largest city in Bangladesh and the capital of Chittagong Division. Located on the banks of the Karnaphuli River near its mouth on the Bay of Bengal, it is the country's chief seaport, handling the great majority of Bangladesh's imports and exports, and a major commercial and industrial centre. The city was renamed Chattogram in 2018 to reflect the Bengali pronunciation.

History

The port has been a trading post since antiquity, visited by Arab, Persian and Chinese merchants, and known to Europeans after the Portuguese established a settlement in the 16th century — the "Porto Grande" of Bengal. The region passed between the Arakanese, the Mughals, who conquered it in 1666 under Shaista Khan, and finally the British East India Company in 1760. In 1930 the city saw the Chittagong armoury raid, an uprising led by Surya Sen against British rule. During the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971, the declaration of independence was broadcast from the Kalurghat radio station in the city.

Economy

Chattogram's economy is anchored by its port, shipbreaking yards at Sitakunda — among the largest in the world — steel, shipbuilding, textiles and garments, petroleum refining, fertilizer and tea trading; the Chittagong Tea Auction handles the country's tea crop. The city is served by Shah Amanat International Airport and by the Karnaphuli Tunnel, a road tunnel under the river opened in 2023, the first of its kind in South Asia.

Geography

The city occupies hilly terrain unusual in deltaic Bangladesh, between the coast and the Chittagong Hill Tracts to the east, and has a tropical monsoon climate with heavy rains and exposure to Bay of Bengal cyclones. It is a gateway to Cox's Bazar and the hill districts, and home to institutions including the University of Chittagong.
