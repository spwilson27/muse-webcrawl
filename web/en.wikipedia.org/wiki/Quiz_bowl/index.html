Quiz bowl

Quiz bowl is a family of quiz-based competitions that test players on a wide variety of academic subjects. Standardized formats of quiz bowl generally feature two teams of up to four players each competing in a head-to-head contest, in which one player from each team buzzes in to answer a toss-up question. Winning players earn their team the right to answer a series of bonus questions, often referred to as a bonus cycle, the combined answers to which are read by a moderator.

History

Quiz bowl tournaments have existed since the 1940s, when College Bowl, a popular radio and later television program, became popular. The format that would become modern quiz bowl grew out of these early competitions. National Academic Quiz Tournaments (NAQT), founded in 1996, standardized many rules and pyramidal question writing that characterize contemporary competitions. The Academic Competition Federation (ACF) organizes college tournaments including ACF Fall, ACF Winter, ACF Regionals, and ACF Nationals.

Gameplay

Matches consist of toss-up questions read by a moderator. Questions are typically written in a pyramidal style, beginning with obscure clues and progressing to easier ones, rewarding deeper knowledge with earlier buzzes. A correct answer, called a power if given before a designated point in the question, earns extra points. An incorrect interruption of a toss-up is called a neg and costs points in most formats; the other team then may answer after the question is completed. Correct toss-ups unlock bonus questions, usually three related parts worth ten points each, on which the team may confer.

Question subjects

Question distributions cover literature, history, science, fine arts, religion, mythology, philosophy, social science, geography, current events, and popular culture ("trash"). University-level competitions tend to emphasize academic subjects, while high-school formats vary by organizer.

Organizations

Major governing and question-producing organizations include NAQT, ACF, and Partnerships for Academic Competitive Excellence (PACE), which runs the National Scholastic Championship. In the United Kingdom, a related tradition developed around University Challenge, broadcast since 1962.
