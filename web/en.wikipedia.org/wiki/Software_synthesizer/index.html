Software synthesizer

A software synthesizer or softsynth is a computer program that generates digital audio, usually for music. A computer program that generates sound in real time, responding to input from a performer or sequencer, it is analogous to a hardware synthesizer but implemented entirely in software.

A virtual analog softsynth interface mimicking hardware knobs and sliders

History

Real-time software synthesis became practical on general-purpose computers in the 1990s as processor speeds rose. Steinberg's VST plug-in standard (1996) let instruments run inside digital audio workstations, and Native Instruments' Reaktor and Generator (later Generator/Reaktor line) demonstrated fully software modular synthesis. Propellerhead's Reason (2000) packaged a rack of softsynths and effects as a self-contained studio.

Types

  • Virtual analog: models subtractive synthesis circuits of classic hardware.
  • Sample-based: plays back and processes recorded sounds (romplers and samplers such as Kontakt).
  • FM and additive: generates tones from modulated or summed partials (e.g. FM8, Razor).
  • Wavetable: sweeps through stored single-cycle waveforms (e.g. Serum, Vital).
  • Physical modelling: simulates acoustic processes mathematically.
  • Modular environments: VCV Rack, Pure Data and SuperCollider allow building instruments from primitives.

Integration

Softsynths are typically hosted as plug-ins (VST, AU, AAX, CLAP) in a digital audio workstation, controlled via MIDI, MPE or CV-like automation. Latency depends on audio buffer sizes and driver architecture; modern interfaces achieve round-trip latencies below 10 ms, making live performance practical.

Comparison with hardware

Software instruments offer total recall, unlimited instances (subject to CPU) and low cost per voice, but lack tactile control unless paired with MIDI controllers. Their sound generation is related to the software-defined approach in radio: moving function from dedicated hardware into general-purpose computation.
