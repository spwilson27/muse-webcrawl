Wi-Fi 6 is the sixth generation of the Wi-Fi standard, based on the IEEE 802.11ax specification. Adopted by the Wi-Fi Alliance under its generational naming scheme, Wi-Fi 6 was designed primarily to improve performance in dense, high-device-count environments such as stadiums, offices, and apartment buildings, rather than only to raise peak single-client speeds. It operates in the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands; the variant operating also in the 6 GHz band is marketed as Wi-Fi 6E.

Key features

802.11ax introduces orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA), allowing an access point to serve many clients in a single transmission window; uplink and downlink multi-user MIMO (MU-MIMO) with up to eight spatial streams; 1024-QAM modulation for higher throughput; target wake time (TWT) to improve battery life for phones and IoT devices; and BSS colouring to reduce interference between overlapping networks. It also mandates support for WPA3 security in certified devices.

Adoption

The Wi-Fi Alliance began certifying Wi-Fi 6 devices in September 2019, and the standard was formally approved by the IEEE in February 2021. Wi-Fi 6 support became standard in flagship smartphones and laptops in the early 2020s, and the generation was succeeded by Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be).
