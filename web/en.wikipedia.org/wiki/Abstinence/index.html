Abstinence

Practice ofSelf-restraint
Common formsSexual abstinence, teetotalism, fasting

Abstinence is the practice of self-enforced restraint from indulging in bodily activities that are widely experienced as giving pleasure. Most frequently, the term refers to sexual abstinence, abstinence from alcohol (teetotalism), or abstinence from food — that is, fasting. The practice may arise from religious prohibitions, health considerations, family planning, or personal and philosophical commitments.

Forms

Sexual abstinence means refraining from some or all aspects of sexual activity, and is promoted in abstinence-only sex education and by religious traditions that value chastity before marriage or celibacy in religious life. Abstinence from alcohol and other drugs is central to recovery programmes such as Alcoholics Anonymous and to movements like the temperance movement and Straight Edge. Dietary abstinence includes fasting in Lent, Ramadan and Yom Kippur, as well as rules such as abstaining from meat on Fridays in Catholic practice.

Health and debate

Sudden abstinence after heavy dependence can produce withdrawal symptoms, which for alcohol and benzodiazepines may be dangerous without medical supervision. Abstinence-based approaches to addiction and sex education remain debated, with critics advocating harm reduction or comprehensive education and supporters emphasising moral, religious or health grounds.
