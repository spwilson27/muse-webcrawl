Othmar Ammann

Othmar Hermann Ammann
Born: 26 March 1879, Feuerthalen, Switzerland
Died: 22 September 1965, Rye, New York, US
Occupation: Civil engineer
Known for: George Washington Bridge, Verrazzano–Narrows Bridge

Othmar Hermann Ammann (March 26, 1879 – September 22, 1965) was a Swiss-American civil engineer whose bridge designs dramatically reduced the cost of crossing wide water barriers, contributing significantly to the motor age in the New York metropolitan area. His designs include the George Washington Bridge, the Bayonne Bridge, the Triborough Bridge complex, the Bronx–Whitestone Bridge, the Throgs Neck Bridge and, in his eighties, the Verrazzano–Narrows Bridge.

Early life and career

Ammann studied engineering at the Eidgenössisches Polytechnikum (now ETH Zurich) and emigrated to the United States in 1904. He worked on the Queensboro Bridge and was assistant to the great bridge engineer Gustav Lindenthal on the Hell Gate Bridge. After the collapse of the Quebec Bridge in 1907, in which he was involved as an investigator, he gained a reputation as an analyst of bridge failures.

The George Washington Bridge

As chief engineer of the Port of New York Authority, Ammann designed the George Washington Bridge across the Hudson River (opened 1931). With a main span of 1,067 m (3,500 ft), it nearly doubled the previous world record for suspension bridges, yet was completed ahead of schedule and under budget. Its slender, flexible deck — designed with deflection theory that Ammann helped pioneer — demonstrated that stiffening trusses could be much lighter than previously assumed, making very long spans economical. In the same year his Bayonne Bridge, a steel arch of 510 m, became the longest of its type in the world.

Later works

Ammann also engineered the Lincoln Tunnel under the Hudson, and his last great work, the Verrazzano–Narrows Bridge (1964), with a main span of 1,298 m, was the world's longest suspension span on completion. He received the National Medal of Science in 1964, the first civil engineer so honoured. He died in Rye, New York, in 1965.
