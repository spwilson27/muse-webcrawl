The Museum of London Docklands, formerly the Museum in Docklands, is a history museum in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, part of the Museum of London group. It opened in 2003 at West India Quay in Canary Wharf and is housed in a Grade I listed Georgian warehouse — No. 1 Warehouse of the West India Docks, built in 1802 to store sugar, coffee and rum imported from the Caribbean.

Collections and galleries

The museum tells the story of the River Thames, the Port of London and the Docklands from Roman times to the present. Galleries cover the Roman port, the medieval and Tudor waterfront, the construction of the great docks, London's involvement in the transatlantic slave trade — addressed in the permanent gallery London, Sugar & Slavery, opened in 2007 — the sailortowns of Wapping and Limehouse, the Blitz on the docks, and the decline of the port and regeneration of Docklands in the 1980s and 1990s. A popular children's gallery, Mudlarks, is aimed at under-8s. The museum also houses the archive of the Port of London Authority.

The museum is a short walk from Canary Wharf and Heron Quays DLR stations. Admission is free.
