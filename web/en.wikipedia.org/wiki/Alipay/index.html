Alipay (Chinese: 支付宝) is a third-party mobile and online payment platform established in Hangzhou, China, in December 2004 by Alibaba Group and its founder Jack Ma. Operated by Ant Group, it is one of the dominant payment systems in China, together with Tencent's WeChat Pay, and one of the largest mobile payment platforms in the world.

History

Alipay began as an escrow service for Alibaba's Taobao marketplace: buyers' money was held until they confirmed satisfactory receipt of goods, solving a trust problem in early Chinese e-commerce. It expanded into a full digital wallet supporting bill payment, peer-to-peer transfers, transport and retail purchases, largely via QR codes scanned by phone. In 2011 the service was controversially spun out of Alibaba into a company controlled by Jack Ma, later named Ant Financial and then Ant Group. Associated services grew to include the Yu'e Bao money-market fund, the Sesame Credit scoring system, and the Huabei consumer-credit product.

International use

Through the Alipay+ network, Ant partners with local wallets across Asia, and Alipay acceptance has spread to merchants worldwide catering to Chinese travellers. In China itself, Alipay and WeChat Pay together displaced cash for most everyday transactions.

Regulation

Following the suspension of Ant Group's record initial public offering in November 2020, Chinese regulators required Ant to restructure as a financial holding company, and Alipay's payment business came under closer supervision by the People's Bank of China.
