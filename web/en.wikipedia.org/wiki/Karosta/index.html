Karosta

Karosta is a neighbourhood in the northern part of Liepāja, Latvia. It was constructed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as a naval base for the Russian Empire, and later served as a major base of the Soviet Baltic Fleet. Its name derives from the Latvian words kara osta, "war port".

History

Construction of the naval port began around 1890 under Tsar Alexander III and continued under Nicholas II, who named it the Port of Alexander III. The complex included dry docks, barracks, officers' housing, a hospital, a manège (riding hall), water towers, and the monumental St. Nicholas Naval Cathedral (1901–1903). After Latvian independence, the base served the Latvian navy; during the Soviet era, Karosta became a closed military city, at times housing a large submarine base, inaccessible to civilians.

Present day

Since the withdrawal of Russian forces in 1994, much of Karosta has been left to decay, creating an internationally unusual landscape of abandoned military structures. Notable attractions include the cathedral, the Karosta Prison — a former military jail now operated as a museum and hostel — the breakwater, and the ruins of coastal artillery batteries. The area occupies roughly a third of Liepāja's territory with a much smaller share of its population.
