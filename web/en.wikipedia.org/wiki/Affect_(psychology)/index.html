Affect (psychology)

Affect
Field: Psychology
Components: Valence, arousal, motivational intensity
Related: Emotion, mood, feeling

Affect, in psychology, refers to the underlying experience of feeling, emotion or mood. It is a central concept in the study of emotion and is distinguished from cognition and behaviour.

Dimensions

Researchers commonly describe affective states along dimensions of valence (pleasant versus unpleasant) and arousal (calm versus excited). Core affect theory, associated with James Russell and Lisa Feldman Barrett, treats these basic feelings as a continuously present neurophysiological state that is then interpreted as specific emotions.

In clinical practice

In psychiatry, "affect" also describes the externally observable expression of emotion; clinicians note, for example, flat, blunted or labile affect during mental status examinations.
