Lester Horton (January 23, 1906 – November 2, 1953) was an American dancer, choreographer, and teacher. He developed the Horton modern dance technique and founded one of the first racially integrated dance companies in the United States, the Lester Horton Dance Theater in Los Angeles.

Early life

Horton was born in Indianapolis, Indiana. Fascinated as a youth by Native American culture and dance, he studied with local teachers and performed with the Denishawn-influenced companies before settling in Los Angeles in the late 1920s, where he began choreographing for little-theatre productions.

Dance Theater

In 1932 Horton began presenting concerts with the Lester Horton Dancers. His company, based from 1948 at the Dance Theater on Melrose Avenue, was a landmark of the Los Angeles arts scene and notable for its multiracial casting at a time of segregation. His dancers included Alvin Ailey, Carmen de Lavallade, Bella Lewitzky, and James Truitte.

Horton technique

The Horton technique is built on anatomical studies of the body, emphasising flat backs, lateral stretches, tilts, and long, fortifying lines designed to strengthen the dancer for a wide range of movement styles. It remains a core curriculum at the Ailey School and other institutions. Horton's best-known choreographic work is Beloved (1948), a duet about a woman murdered by her husband, based on a true story.
