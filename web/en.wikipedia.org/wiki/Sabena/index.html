Sabena

IATA: SN · ICAO: SAB

Founded: 23 May 1923

Ceased operations: 7 November 2001

Hub: Brussels Airport

Headquarters: Brussels, Belgium

Sabena, an abbreviation of Société Anonyme Belge d'Exploitation de la Navigation Aérienne, was the national airline of Belgium from 1923 until its bankruptcy in 2001. It was one of the oldest airlines in the world, founded in the aftermath of the First World War, and operated from its hub at Brussels Airport.

History

Sabena began operations in 1923, taking over routes pioneered by the military-backed SNETA. It built an important network to the Belgian Congo, inaugurating a celebrated Brussels–Léopoldville route in 1935. After the Second World War the airline expanded across the Atlantic and into Asia, operating aircraft from the Douglas DC-3 to the Boeing 747, and it was an early operator of the Sud Aviation Caravelle.

In the 1990s the Belgian state sold a 49.5 percent stake to Swissair, whose own collapse in October 2001 dragged Sabena down with it. Burdened by debt and unable to secure further funding, Sabena declared bankruptcy on 7 November 2001. Its regional subsidiary Delta Air Transport continued as SN Brussels Airlines, which later merged into Brussels Airlines.

Fleet and network

At its peak Sabena served more than 90 destinations in Europe, Africa, North America and Asia, and was especially strong in Central and West Africa, a legacy of Belgium's colonial-era links with the Congo. The airline was known for its in-flight service and its training academy.
