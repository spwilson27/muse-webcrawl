The Sunda Strait (Indonesian: Selat Sunda) is the strait separating the Indonesian islands of Java and Sumatra. It connects the Java Sea to the Indian Ocean and takes its name from Sunda, the historic kingdom and the Sundanese people of western Java. The strait runs roughly northeast to southwest, narrowing to about 24 km at its northeastern end, where it is also shallow — in places only about 20 m deep — which makes navigation hazardous for large vessels.

History

The strait was long a vital artery of the spice trade and the principal route from Europe to the East Indies before many ships shifted to the Strait of Malacca. It contains the Krakatoa archipelago, whose 1883 eruption was one of the deadliest and most destructive volcanic events in recorded history. In March 1942 the Battle of Sunda Strait saw the cruisers HMAS Perth and USS Houston sunk by Japanese forces. On 22 December 2018 a flank collapse of the Anak Krakatau volcano generated a tsunami that struck the strait's coasts, killing more than 400 people.

Transport

Busy ferry services link Merak on Java with Bakauheni on Sumatra, one of the heaviest-used crossings in Indonesia. A bridge across the strait has been proposed repeatedly since the 1960s but remains unbuilt, owing to cost and the region's volcanic and seismic risks. The planned crossing would be among the longest sea bridges in the world.
