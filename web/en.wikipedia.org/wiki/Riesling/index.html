Riesling (pronounced "REES-ling") is an aromatic white grape variety originating in the Rhine region of Germany, and the wine made from it. Regarded by many critics as one of the world's finest white wine grapes, Riesling is noted for its high natural acidity, pronounced floral and fruit aromas, transparency to terroir, and exceptional ability to age for decades.

History and characteristics

The first documented mention of Riesling dates to 1435, in a cellar inventory from Rüsselsheim near the Rhine. DNA analysis indicates a parentage involving Gouais blanc. The grape ripens late, buds late (avoiding spring frosts) and is winter-hardy, thriving in cool climates on slate and other poor soils in Germany's Mosel, Rheingau, Pfalz and Nahe regions, as well as Alsace in France, Austria's Wachau, New York's Finger Lakes and Australia's Clare and Eden Valleys.

Styles

Riesling wines span a unique range: steely dry (trocken) wines, off-dry Kabinett, late-harvest Spätlese and Auslese, and intensely sweet Beerenauslese, Trockenbeerenauslese and Eiswein made from botrytis-affected or frozen grapes. With age, fine Rieslings develop a characteristic petrol-like aroma (from the compound TDN), alongside honey, lime and stone-fruit notes. German wine law classifies quality by must weight, and top dry wines from great sites are labelled Grosses Gewächs.
