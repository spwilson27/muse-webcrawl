C. F. Martin & Company (often referred to as Martin) is an American guitar manufacturer established in 1833 by Christian Frederick Martin. It is highly regarded for its steel-string acoustic guitars and is credited with inventing or popularising many of the features of the modern acoustic guitar, including X-bracing and the dreadnought body shape. The company has remained family-owned and is based in Nazareth, Pennsylvania.

History

Martin, a German immigrant trained in the Viennese guitar-making tradition, opened a shop in New York in 1833 and moved to Nazareth in 1839. In the 1850s he developed the X-braced top that became standard for steel-string guitars. In 1916 the company produced the large-bodied "dreadnought" for the Oliver Ditson Company, later selling it under its own name; the D-18 and D-28 dreadnoughts became archetypes of the flat-top guitar. Martin also made highly regarded ukuleles in the 1920s and, later, archtop and electric guitars, though acoustics remained its core.

Instruments and legacy

Martin's naming system combines body size and ornamentation level (e.g. D-28, 000-18). Its guitars have been played by artists from Gene Autry and Hank Williams to Elvis Presley, Bob Dylan, Eric Clapton and John Mayer, and pre-war Martin dreadnoughts are among the most valuable production guitars in the world. The company also manufactures strings and operates a museum and visitor centre at its Nazareth factory.
