Romanos IV Diogenes

Romanos IV Diogenes (Greek: Ῥωμανός Διογένης; c. 1030 – 1072) was a Byzantine emperor from 1068 to 1071. A capable general from a distinguished Cappadocian family, he married the empress Eudokia Makrembolitissa, widow of Constantine X Doukas, and was crowned to defend the empire against the Seljuk Turks. His reign is chiefly remembered for the catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071.

Reign

Romanos had been convicted of plotting against the Doukas regency when Eudokia pardoned and married him, making him senior emperor and guardian of her sons. He campaigned energetically in the east in 1068–1070, rebuilding fortresses and checking Turkish raids, though intrigue by the Doukas faction and the courtier Michael Psellos undermined him at home.

Manzikert and downfall

In 1071 Romanos led a large army to recover Armenia and confront Sultan Alp Arslan. At Manzikert (19 August 1071) treachery in the rearguard and Seljuk feigned retreat led to the army's rout and the emperor's capture — the first Byzantine emperor taken prisoner in battle. Alp Arslan released him for a ransom and treaty, but in Constantinople the Doukai deposed him; defeated in the ensuing civil war, Romanos was blinded so brutally that he died of his wounds in 1072. The loss of Anatolia to the Turks followed within a generation.
