Tellurium

Tellurium is a chemical element with the symbol Te and atomic number 52. It is a brittle, mildly toxic, rare, silver-white metalloid in the chalcogen group, chemically related to selenium and sulfur. Elemental tellurium is occasionally found in nature, but it is more often obtained as a by-product of copper refining.

History

Tellurium was discovered in 1783 by Franz-Joseph Müller von Reichenstein in gold ores from Transylvania, and was named by Martin Heinrich Klaproth from the Latin tellus, "earth." Its scarcity delayed study of the element for decades.

Applications

Tellurium is used in cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film solar cells, thermoelectric devices (as bismuth telluride), phase-change memory and rewritable discs, and as an alloying additive in steel and copper. It is one of the rarest stable elements in the Earth's crust, comparable in scarcity to platinum.
