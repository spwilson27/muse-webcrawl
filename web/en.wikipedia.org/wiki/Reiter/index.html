Reiter

Type: Heavy cavalry
Period: Mid-16th to 17th centuries
Origin: German states
Weapons: Wheel-lock pistols, sword
Also called: Schwarzreiter ("black riders")

Reiter (German for "rider") were a type of heavy cavalry that originated in Germany in the mid-sixteenth century and spread throughout Europe. Armed principally with pistols and a sword, and protected by a cuirass and helmet, reiters displaced the lance-armed gendarme as the dominant form of cavalry in many armies.

Equipment and tactics

Reiters typically carried two or more wheel-lock pistols and fought in deep formations, sometimes employing the caracole, in which successive ranks rode forward, discharged their pistols and wheeled away to reload. In practice, the best reiters increasingly charged home with the sword after firing. Their darkened armour earned some regiments the name Schwarzreiter, "black riders". Many served as mercenaries in the French Wars of Religion, the Dutch Revolt and the wars of the Baltic.

Decline

As firearms infantry grew more effective and cavalry tactics shifted toward shock action, the distinct reiter type faded during the seventeenth century, merging into the general category of cuirassiers and other heavy cavalry.
