The Combat Vehicle 90 (CV90; Swedish: Stridsfordon 90, Strf 90) is a family of Swedish tracked armoured combat vehicles designed by the Swedish Defence Materiel Administration (FMV) and manufactured by Hägglunds (now BAE Systems Hägglunds). Development began in the mid-1980s, and the vehicle entered Swedish service in 1994. The CV90 was designed for sub-arctic conditions, with high mobility in snow and wetlands, strong firepower and protection, and low radar and infrared signatures.

Design

The baseline Swedish Strf 90 mounts a Bofors 40 mm L/70 autocannon in a two-man turret, giving it unusually heavy firepower for an infantry fighting vehicle; export versions generally use 30 mm or 35 mm Bushmaster chain guns. The vehicle carries a crew of three plus up to eight infantry in the rear compartment. Later marks added mine protection, all-round armour against 30 mm rounds (with add-on packages), air conditioning, and digital vehicle electronics. Variants include air-defence (Lvkv 90), forward command, forward observation, armoured recovery, mortar (Mjölner twin 120 mm) and engineering versions.

Service history

Sweden deployed CV90s to Liberia and Afghanistan. Export customers include Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, Finland, Estonia, Switzerland and Slovakia; Denmark used its CV9035s in combat in Afghanistan. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, Sweden donated CV9040s to Ukraine in 2023, where they saw front-line use, and Ukraine subsequently agreed local production arrangements. The platform has been progressively upgraded, with the Mk IV standard offering a new engine, active damping and a growth path for active protection systems.
