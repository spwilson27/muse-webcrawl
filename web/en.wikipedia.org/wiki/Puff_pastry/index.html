Puff pastry

Puff pastry
French: pâte feuilletée
Ingredients: flour, butter, water, salt
Method: lamination with repeated folding (“turns”)

Puff pastry, also known as pâte feuilletée, is a light, flaky pastry made from a laminated dough composed of alternating layers of dough and butter, rolled and folded repeatedly.

Production

A base dough (détrempe) encloses a block of butter (beurrage). The package is rolled out and folded — typically six single turns — creating hundreds of layers. In the oven, water in the dough turns to steam, separating the layers while the butter melts.

Uses

Puff pastry is used for vol-au-vent, mille-feuille (Napoleon), sausage rolls, turnovers, beef Wellington, and the base of many tarts. It contrasts with Danish pastry, a yeasted laminated dough.

History

Recipes appear in early modern European cookbooks; Antonin Carême systematised the five-turn method in the 19th century.
