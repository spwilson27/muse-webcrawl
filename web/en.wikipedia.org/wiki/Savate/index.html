Savate

Savate, also known as boxe française (French boxing), is a French martial art and combat sport that combines elements of Western boxing with elegant kicking techniques. Unlike many other kickboxing styles, savate fighters wear shoes and strike principally with the foot, with kicks delivered using the toe, heel, sole, and instep; knee strikes are not permitted.

Savate
Origin: France, 19th century
Also called: Boxe française
Focus: Striking with fists and feet (shod)
Hardness: Light to full contact (assaut / combat)

History

Savate began as a street fighting method among sailors and dock workers in the ports of southern France — its name comes from the old French word for a worn shoe or slipper. In the 1820s Michel Casseux opened a salle in Paris and began codifying its kicks, while Charles Lecour later combined savate footwork with English boxing hand techniques after losing a friendly bout to an English boxer; Lecour is credited with founding modern boxe française around the 1830s–1850s. The sport was popular in the Belle Époque, appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1924 Paris Olympics, and was revived after the Second World War by figures such as Pierre Baruzy.

Technique and competition

Savate technique is named in French: kicks include the fouetté (roundhouse), chassé (side/piston kick), revers (reverse kick), and the low coup de pied bas. Competition has two forms: assaut, light-contact bouts judged on technique, and combat, full-contact bouts held in a ring. Practitioners progress through glove colours (blue, green, red, white, yellow, and silver) as grades. Related disciplines under the French federation include canne de combat (stick fighting), which shares its footwork heritage.
