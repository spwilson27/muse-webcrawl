The Church of Cyprus (Greek: Ἐκκλησία τῆς Κύπρου, romanized: Ekklisía tis Kýprou) is one of the autocephalous churches of the Eastern Orthodox communion, centred on the island of Cyprus. It is headed by the Archbishop of Nova Justiniana and All Cyprus, based in Nicosia.

History

According to tradition, the church was founded by the apostles Paul and Barnabas, a native of Cyprus, during their missionary journey recorded in Acts 13 (c. AD 45). Its autocephaly was recognized at the Council of Ephesus in 431, affirmed against the claims of the Patriarchate of Antioch, and its independence was confirmed by the Byzantine emperor Zeno after the reported discovery of Barnabas's tomb.

The church survived Arab raids, Frankish (Lusignan) and Venetian rule — during which it was subordinated to the Latin hierarchy — and the Ottoman conquest of 1571, which restored the Orthodox bishops and made the archbishop the ethnarch of the Greek Cypriots. Under British colonial rule the church became central to the movement for enosis (union with Greece); Archbishop Makarios III served as the first President of the Republic of Cyprus (1960–1977).

Today

The church comprises the Archdiocese and metropolitan sees including Paphos, Kition (Larnaca), Kyrenia, Limassol, and Morphou, though the sees in the north have operated in exile from Greek Cypriot areas since the Turkish invasion of 1974, which left many churches in the occupied territory damaged, looted, or converted to other uses. The majority of Greek Cypriots belong to the church.
