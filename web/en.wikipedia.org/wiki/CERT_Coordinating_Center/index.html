The CERT Coordination Center (CERT/CC) is a center for internet security at the Software Engineering Institute (SEI) of Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. It was the first computer security incident response team, established by the United States Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) in November 1988.

The CERT Coordination Center is based at Carnegie Mellon University.

History

CERT/CC was created in direct response to the Morris worm, the first major internet worm, which struck in November 1988 and disabled a substantial fraction of the machines then connected to the internet. DARPA charged the SEI with establishing a coordination center that could serve as a single point of contact for responding to computer security emergencies.

The original mission was incident response — helping system administrators deal with intrusions — but the center's role quickly expanded to include vulnerability analysis, publishing security advisories, and developing secure coding standards and training.

Activities

CERT/CC coordinates disclosure of software vulnerabilities between researchers and vendors, maintains the Vulnerability Notes Database, and publishes advisories on widely exploited flaws. Its researchers work on secure coding standards for languages such as C, C++, Java, and Perl, on malware analysis, and on insider-threat research. Many other national and organizational CERTs around the world were modelled on CERT/CC, and the CERT name is a registered trademark of Carnegie Mellon University, licensed to incident response teams worldwide.
