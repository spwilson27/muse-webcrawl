United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime

The United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (UNTOC), also called the Palermo Convention, is a 2000 United Nations-sponsored multilateral treaty against transnational organized crime. It was adopted by the UN General Assembly on 15 November 2000 and entered into force on 29 September 2003. The convention is supplemented by three protocols dealing with specific areas of organized crime.

The convention was adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2000.

Contents

The convention requires states parties to criminalize participation in an organized criminal group, money laundering, corruption, and obstruction of justice, and provides frameworks for extradition, mutual legal assistance, joint investigations, witness protection, confiscation of criminal proceeds, and law-enforcement cooperation. It defines an "organized criminal group" as a structured group of three or more persons acting in concert to commit serious crimes for financial or material benefit.

The protocols

  • Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children (Palermo Protocol) — the first global instrument with an agreed definition of human trafficking.
  • Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air.
  • Protocol against the Illicit Manufacturing of and Trafficking in Firearms, Their Parts and Components and Ammunition.

Implementation

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) serves as the secretariat and guardian of the convention. A Conference of the Parties oversees implementation; in 2018 states parties established a formal review mechanism for the convention and its protocols. The convention has been ratified by the great majority of UN member states.
