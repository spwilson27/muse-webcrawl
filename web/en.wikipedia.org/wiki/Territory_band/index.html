Territory band

Territory bands were jazz and dance ensembles in the United States during the 1920s through the 1940s that toured extensively within a defined geographic region, or "territory," rather than holding long-term engagements in major metropolitan venues. They were especially important in the Midwest, Southwest, and South.

Organization

Territory bands were booked through booking agencies and organized on circuits that took them through ballrooms, dance halls, hotels, and one-night stands in smaller cities and towns. Because they rarely recorded or broadcast nationally, many accomplished territory bands remained regionally famous but nationally obscure.

Notable bands

Famous territory bands included Bennie Moten's Kansas City Orchestra, the Blue Devils of Walter Page, Andy Kirk and His Twelve Clouds of Joy, Jay McShann's orchestra, and the bands of Alphonso Trent, Troy Floyd, and Terrence Holder. The Southwest territory bands were instrumental in developing the Kansas City jazz style, with its emphasis on riffs and blues, and launched the careers of musicians such as Count Basie, Lester Young, Charlie Parker, and Ben Webster.

Decline

The territory band system declined after World War II due to wartime travel restrictions, the rise of recording and radio, musicians' union pressures, and changing economics of big band touring, though regional touring bands continued in rhythm and blues and other genres.
