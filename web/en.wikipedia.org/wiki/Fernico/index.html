Fernico

CompositionIron, nickel, cobalt alloy
RelatedKovar
UseGlass-to-metal seals

Fernico is a family of iron–nickel–cobalt alloys whose name derives from their principal constituents: Fe (iron), Ni (nickel) and Co (cobalt). The alloys were developed for making reliable hermetic seals between metal conductors and glass, because their thermal expansion can be matched to that of certain glasses over a wide temperature range.

Properties and grades

Fernico grades are closely related to the better-known alloy Kovar and share its characteristic expansion behaviour: a coefficient close to that of borosilicate glass, with an inflection point in the expansion curve at a few hundred degrees Celsius. Different grades adjust the nickel and cobalt content slightly to suit particular glasses and ceramics. The alloys are magnetic at room temperature and can be brazed, welded and plated.

Applications

Fernico and its relatives were widely used for the lead-in wires and pins of vacuum tubes, incandescent and discharge lamps, and later for feedthroughs in semiconductor packages, microwave tubes, and hermetic connectors in aerospace and medical devices, where a vacuum-tight glass or ceramic seal is required.
