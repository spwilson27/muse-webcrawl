Southend-on-Sea

Southend-on-Sea
County: Essex, England
Status: City (granted 2022)
Population: about 180,000
Known for: Southend Pier, the world's longest pleasure pier

Southend-on-Sea, commonly referred to as Southend, is a coastal city and unitary authority area in south-eastern Essex, England. It lies on the north side of the Thames Estuary, about 40 miles (64 km) east of central London. Originally the "south end" of the village of Prittlewell, Southend developed into a seaside resort during the 18th and 19th centuries, aided by royal patronage and the arrival of the railway in 1856. City status was granted in 2022, in honour of the town's murdered MP, Sir David Amess.

Attractions

Southend Pier is the city's most famous landmark: at 1.34 miles (2.16 km) it is the world's longest pleasure pier, served by its own railway. The town offers seven miles of seafront with beaches, the Adventure Island amusement park, the Kursaal entertainment hall, and the Cliffs Pavilion theatre. Shoeburyness, at the eastern end of the borough, gives access to the Thames Estuary and the historic Shoebury Ranges.

Economy and transport

Southend is a significant commuter town for London, served by two railway lines (to Fenchurch Street and Liverpool Street). London Southend Airport, just north of the town in Rochford district, expanded greatly as a low-cost airline base in the 2010s. Tourism, retail, and the public sector are major employers; the town also hosts a campus of the University of Essex and South Essex College.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times, and Prittlewell Priory dates to the 12th century. The resort grew after Princess Charlotte of Wales and later Princess Victoria stayed in the 1800s–1810s; the original pier opened in 1830. Southend became a county borough in 1914 and a unitary authority in 1998.
