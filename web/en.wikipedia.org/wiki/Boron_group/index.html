Boron group

The boron group consists of the chemical elements in group 13 of the periodic table: boron (B), aluminium (Al), gallium (Ga), indium (In), thallium (Tl), and nihonium (Nh). The elements in the boron group are characterized by having three valence electrons. These elements have also been referred to as the triels.

Characteristics

Boron is a metalloid, while the other well-characterised members are poor metals. Reactivity and metallic character increase down the group, although thallium shows the inert pair effect, favouring the +1 oxidation state over the group oxidation state of +3. Boron forms predominantly covalent compounds, while the heavier elements form increasingly ionic chemistry.

Occurrence and uses

Boron is rare in the Earth's crust and occurs in borate minerals such as borax; its compounds are used in glass, detergents, and semiconductors. Aluminium, by contrast, is the most abundant metal in the crust and is extracted from bauxite. Gallium and indium are obtained as by-products of zinc and aluminium processing and are important in semiconductors such as gallium arsenide and indium tin oxide. Thallium is highly toxic and was historically used in rat poisons. Nihonium is a synthetic element known only from a handful of atoms produced in laboratories.

History

Boron was isolated in 1808 by Davy, Gay-Lussac, and Thénard; aluminium was produced by Ørsted and Wöhler in the 1820s; gallium (1875), indium (1863), and thallium (1861) were all discovered through spectroscopy or careful chemical analysis in the 19th century.
