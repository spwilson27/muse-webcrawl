Education for sustainable development

Education for sustainable development (ESD) is an approach to teaching and learning that seeks to empower people to assume responsibility for creating and enjoying a sustainable future. It integrates key sustainable development issues — including climate change, biodiversity, poverty reduction and sustainable consumption — into teaching and learning at all levels.

History

The concept grew out of environmental education and was given prominence by Chapter 36 of Agenda 21, adopted at the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro. The United Nations declared 2005–2014 the UN Decade of Education for Sustainable Development, led by UNESCO. In 2015, Sustainable Development Goal 4.7 committed countries to ensuring that all learners acquire the knowledge and skills needed to promote sustainable development by 2030. UNESCO's subsequent "ESD for 2030" framework continues this work.

Characteristics

ESD is generally characterised by:

  • An interdisciplinary and holistic approach, rather than a separate subject;
  • Values-driven learning, based on the principles underpinning sustainable development;
  • Promotion of critical thinking and problem solving;
  • Use of varied pedagogical methods, including participatory and learner-centred approaches;
  • Local relevance combined with a global perspective.

Relationship to other fields

ESD overlaps with environmental education, global citizenship education and development education, but differs in covering the full social, economic and environmental dimensions of sustainability rather than the environment alone.
