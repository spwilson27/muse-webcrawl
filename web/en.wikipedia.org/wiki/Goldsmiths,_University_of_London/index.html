Goldsmiths, University of London, is a public research university in New Cross, south-east London, and a constituent college of the University of London. Founded in 1891 by the Worshipful Company of Goldsmiths as the Goldsmiths' Technical and Recreative Institute, it was acquired by the University of London in 1904 and developed a particular reputation for the arts, humanities and social sciences, as well as for teacher training and computing.

History and campus

The institute occupied the former Royal Naval School building in New Cross, its main hall fronting Lewisham Way. Goldsmiths suffered severe bomb damage during the Second World War and expanded considerably in the post-war decades, acquiring degree-awarding powers through the University of London. Its campus combines the Victorian main building with later additions, including the Ben Pimlott Building, known for its distinctive rooftop sculpture, and a purpose-built media and arts complex.

Academics and reputation

Goldsmiths is especially noted for art and design: many of the Young British Artists, including Damien Hirst, studied there in the late 1980s, and the college was central to the emergence of Britpop through alumni such as Blur. It is also known for media and communications, anthropology, sociology and cultural studies, and for the Centre for Cultural Studies associated with Stuart Hall's legacy in the field. Alumni include the film director Steve McQueen and the musician James Blake.
