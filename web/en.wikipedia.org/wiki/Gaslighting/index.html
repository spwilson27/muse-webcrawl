Gaslighting

Gaslighting is a form of psychological manipulation in which a person or group seeks to sow self-doubt and confusion in a target's mind, causing them to question their own memory, perception, or sanity.

Gaslighting
Type: Psychological manipulation
Origin of term: Play Gas Light (1938)

Origin

The term derives from Patrick Hamilton's 1938 play Gas Light and its film adaptations (1940 and 1944, the latter starring Ingrid Bergman). In the story, a husband secretly dims the home's gas lights and denies any change when his wife notices, making her doubt her perception.

Techniques

Common techniques include withholding, countering (disputing the victim's memory), blocking and diverting, trivialising feelings, and forgetting or denying events and promises. Gaslighting is discussed in contexts of abusive relationships, workplaces, medicine and politics.

Use and recognition

The word entered clinical literature in the 1960s–70s and spread widely in popular usage in the 2010s–2020s. Merriam-Webster named "gaslighting" its Word of the Year for 2022. Critics warn the term is sometimes over-extended to ordinary disagreement or lying, diluting its specific meaning of reality-undermining manipulation. See also psychological manipulation and emotional abuse.
