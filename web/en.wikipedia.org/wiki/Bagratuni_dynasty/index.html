The Bagratuni or Bagratid dynasty (Armenian: Բագրատունի) was an Armenian noble and later royal dynasty that ruled parts of Armenia from the early medieval period, reaching its height with the Bagratid Kingdom of Armenia (c. 885–1045). A cadet branch, the Georgian Bagrationi, claimed descent from the same family and ruled Georgia for a millennium. The Bagratunis were known in earlier centuries by the office of aspet (master of the horse / coronant), the hereditary right to crown Armenian kings.

Origins

The family's origins are obscure and were wrapped in legend; Armenian historians such as Movses Khorenatsi traced them to a Jewish noble, Shambate, while the Bagratids themselves later claimed descent from King David. In the Parthian and Sasanian periods, the Bagratunis held lands at Sper and in the Chorokhi valley, and under Arab rule they gradually became the foremost princely house of Armenia, filling the office of presiding prince (ishkhan).

Kingdom of Armenia

In about 885, Ashot I was recognised as King of Armenia by both the Abbasid caliph and the Byzantine emperor. His successors, notably Smbat I and Ashot II "the Iron", consolidated the kingdom against Arab emirs and internal rivals. Under Ashot III "the Merciful", the capital was moved to Ani, which under Gagik I became a famed city of churches and trade — tradition calls it the "City of 1001 Churches".

The kingdom sponsored a golden age of Armenian architecture, manuscript illumination and historiography. Internal division among cadet lines (Kars, Lori) and pressure from Byzantium weakened it; in 1045 Ani fell to the Byzantines, and shortly after to the Seljuk Turks, ending Bagratid rule in Armenia. Descendants continued as the Kiurikian kings of Lori and, in Georgia, the Bagrationi line flourished.
