Electrical connector

An electrical connector is an electromechanical device used to create an electrical connection between parts of an electrical circuit, or between different electrical circuits, joining them into a larger circuit. The connection may be removable (as for portable equipment), require a tool for assembly and removal, or serve as a permanent joint.

Construction

Connectors consist of plugs (male-ended) and jacks or sockets (female-ended) with metal contacts, usually pins and receptacles, held in an insulating housing. Contacts are often gold- or tin-plated to resist corrosion. Key properties include current and voltage ratings, contact resistance, mating cycles, and mechanical features such as keying, latching and strain relief.

Types

Common types include terminal blocks and crimp terminals for wiring; power plugs and sockets; coaxial RF connectors (BNC, SMA); audio and video connectors (RCA, HDMI, XLR); data connectors (RJ45 Ethernet, USB, D-subminiature); board-to-board and edge connectors inside electronics; and high-reliability circular connectors for aerospace and military use (MIL-DTL-5015 and similar standards).

Design considerations

Engineers select connectors by signal integrity, environmental sealing (IP ratings), size, cost and safety approvals. Poor connectors are a common source of intermittent faults and overheating.
