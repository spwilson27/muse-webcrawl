Deed of Gift

Deed of Gift may refer in general to a legal document used to effect a voluntary transfer of property without consideration, or more specifically to several notable instruments, most famously the deed governing the America's Cup sailing competition.

In law

A deed of gift is a formal written document by which a donor transfers ownership of real or personal property to a donee gratuitously. In common law systems, a valid gift generally requires donative intent, delivery of the property (or of the deed), and acceptance by the donee. A deed of gift is frequently used for gifts of land, charitable donations of objects to museums, and transfers of valuables within families, and it may be recorded to provide evidence of the transfer and of any conditions attached to it. Unlike a transfer by will, a gift by deed takes effect during the donor's lifetime (inter vivos).

America's Cup Deed of Gift

The most famous instrument of the name is the Deed of Gift of the America's Cup, the document that governs competition for the America's Cup trophy. After the syndicate of the New York Yacht Club won the cup in 1851 with the schooner America, the surviving members transferred the cup to the club by a deed of gift dated 8 July 1857, dedicating it as a perpetual challenge trophy open to foreign yacht clubs. Revised deeds followed in 1882 and 1887, the latter being the operative version that, as amended and interpreted by the New York courts (the deed is administered under New York trust law), still governs the event.

The deed sets the basic rules of the competition: the cup is held in trust, challengers must be foreign yacht clubs conducting annual regattas on the sea or an arm of the sea, matches are arranged on terms agreed by "mutual consent", and if the parties cannot agree, default terms in the deed apply — including the famous provision under which a defender may be required to race a challenger that has given ten months' notice, in vessels meeting the deed's broad dimensions. Disputes over the deed have repeatedly been litigated in New York, and on rare occasions — in 1988 and 2010 — the competition has proceeded directly under the deed's default terms in so-called "Deed of Gift matches".

Other uses

The term is also used for deeds recording gifts of land to public bodies, deeds of gift in Islamic law (hiba), and instruments by which collectors donate works of art or archives to institutions, often with conditions concerning display, attribution, or reversion.
