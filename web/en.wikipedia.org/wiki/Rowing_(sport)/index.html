Rowing (sport)

Rowing, often called crew in the United States, is a sport in which athletes race boats (racing shells) on rivers, lakes or the sea using oars. It is among the oldest Olympic sports, having been on the programme since 1900 (the 1896 regatta was cancelled by weather), with women's events added in 1976.

Boat classes

Olympic and World Championship racing is over 2,000 metres in classes including the single sculls, double sculls, quadruple sculls, coxless pair, coxless four and the eight (with coxswain), in openweight and lightweight categories. In sculling each rower has two oars; in sweep rowing, one. World Rowing (formerly FISA) governs the sport internationally, and the World Rowing Championships are held annually.

Technique and training

The rowing stroke divides into the catch, drive, finish and recovery, with power supplied predominantly by the legs on a sliding seat. Rowers train extensively on ergometers ("ergs"), and the sport is noted for its extreme aerobic demands — elite rowers have among the highest recorded VO2 max values. Famous regattas include the Boat Race, Henley Royal Regatta and the Head of the Charles Regatta.
