Lake Elmenteita is a shallow soda lake in the Great Rift Valley of Kenya, about 120 km northwest of Nairobi and just south of Lake Nakuru. Its name derives from the Maasai word muteita, meaning "dusty place," referring to the dry, dusty conditions of the surrounding area in the dry season.

Ecology

The alkaline lake supports large growths of blue-green algae (Spirulina), which sustain lesser flamingos, and its fish—tilapia introduced in the 1960s—support fish eagles, pelicans, and other birds. More than 400 bird species have been recorded in the area. The lake and the surrounding Soysambu Conservancy also support Rothschild's giraffe, zebra, eland, and other wildlife. Because it is endorheic and very shallow, its size and salinity fluctuate greatly with rainfall; during the drought of 2009 it nearly dried up.

Conservation

Lake Elmenteita was designated a Ramsar site in 2005 and in 2011 was inscribed, together with Lake Nakuru and Lake Bogoria, as part of the Kenya Lake System in the Great Rift Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site.
