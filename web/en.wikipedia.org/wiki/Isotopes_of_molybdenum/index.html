Isotopes of molybdenum

Molybdenum (atomic number 42) has 39 known isotopes, from mass number 81 to 119. Seven of these occur naturally; of those, six are stable, while molybdenum-100 is radioactive with a very long half-life of about 7 × 1018 years, decaying by double beta decay to ruthenium-100. Natural molybdenum is a mixture dominated by molybdenum-98 (about 24%) and molybdenum-96 (about 17%).

Molybdenum-99

The synthetic isotope molybdenum-99 (half-life about 66 hours) is of great medical importance: it decays to metastable technetium-99m, the most widely used radioisotope in diagnostic nuclear medicine. Most of the world's Mo-99 supply is produced by fission of uranium-235 targets in research reactors, and its short supply chain has led to periodic shortages and programmes for alternative production routes.
