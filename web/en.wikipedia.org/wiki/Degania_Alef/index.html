Degania Alef (Hebrew: דְּגַנְיָה א', D'ganya Alef) is a kibbutz in northern Israel, situated south of the Sea of Galilee near the Jordan River. Founded in 1910, it is regarded as the first kibbutz (originally called a kvutza) and is often honoured as the "mother of group settlement" in Israel.

History

Degania was established in 1909–1910 by a group of young pioneers of the Second Aliyah on land purchased by the Jewish National Fund at Umm Juni, south of the Sea of Galilee. In 1920 a second group founded neighbouring Degania Bet, after which the original settlement took the name Degania Alef ("Degania A"). A. D. Gordon, the philosopher of Labour Zionism, was among its notable members, and the writer Yosef Haim Brenner and the military leader Joseph Trumpeldor passed through it. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Degania's defenders helped halt the Syrian advance into the Jordan Valley; a disabled Syrian tank remains at the kibbutz entrance as a memorial.

Economy and culture

The kibbutz economy combines agriculture (dairy, poultry, bananas, dates and other crops) with industry, including a factory producing diamond-cutting tools. The Beit Gordon museum and nature study centre commemorates A. D. Gordon.
