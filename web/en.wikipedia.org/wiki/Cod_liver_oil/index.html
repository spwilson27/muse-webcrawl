Cod liver oil

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cod liver oil is a dietary supplement derived from the liver of cod fish, most commonly Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua). As well as the omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA) found in other fish oils, it is a rich natural source of vitamin A and vitamin D.

History

The oil was traditionally produced in northern fishing communities such as Norway's Lofoten Islands and in Newfoundland, by fermenting or steaming cod livers. From the nineteenth century it was marketed medicinally, and in the 1920s it became a standard preventive and cure for rickets once its vitamin D content was understood; generations of children were dosed with it by the spoonful, a byword for an unpleasant-tasting tonic.

Uses and cautions

Today cod liver oil is taken as a supplement for its vitamin D, vitamin A, and omega-3 content. Because vitamin A accumulates in the body, excessive doses can cause hypervitaminosis A, and the oil is generally not recommended at high doses during pregnancy. Modern products are purified to remove pollutants such as PCBs and are often flavoured.
