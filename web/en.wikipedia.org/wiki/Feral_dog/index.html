Feral dog

A feral dog is a dog that is living in a wild state, having had no significant contact with humans or having reverted from a domesticated condition. The term is applied both to formerly owned dogs that have become wild and to their offspring born outside human care. Feral dogs are distinct from free-ranging owned dogs and from truly wild canids such as wolves and dingoes, though the dingo is sometimes described as a feral descendant of early domestic dogs.

Behaviour and ecology

Feral dogs typically avoid people and may form packs that hunt cooperatively, though many subsist by scavenging near settlements, rubbish dumps and slaughterhouses. They can prey on livestock and native wildlife, compete with wild carnivores, and act as reservoirs of rabies and other diseases that threaten both wildlife and human health.

Populations and control

Large feral populations exist in parts of Asia, Africa, Latin America and rural regions elsewhere. Management strategies include capture and removal, sterilisation campaigns, and fencing of sensitive habitats. On some islands, eradication programmes have been undertaken to protect endangered seabirds and reptiles from dog predation.
