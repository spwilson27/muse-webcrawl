Unfolded protein response

The unfolded protein response (UPR) is a cellular stress response related to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). It is activated when unfolded or misfolded proteins accumulate in the ER lumen — a condition called ER stress — and acts to restore protein-folding homeostasis by reducing protein synthesis, increasing the production of molecular chaperones, expanding the ER, and enhancing the degradation of misfolded proteins. If homeostasis cannot be restored, the UPR instead triggers apoptosis.

Signalling pathways

In mammals three ER transmembrane sensors initiate the UPR, all normally held inactive by the chaperone BiP/GRP78:

  • IRE1α splices XBP1 mRNA to produce a transcription factor that upregulates chaperones and ER-associated degradation (ERAD) components; it can also degrade other mRNAs (RIDD).
  • PERK phosphorylates eIF2α, attenuating general translation while selectively allowing synthesis of the transcription factor ATF4, which induces the pro-apoptotic factor CHOP under prolonged stress.
  • ATF6 travels to the Golgi apparatus, where site-specific proteases release its cytosolic transcription-factor domain, which drives expression of chaperone genes.

Medical relevance

Dysregulated UPR signalling is implicated in diabetes (including the Wolcott–Rallison syndrome caused by PERK mutations), neurodegenerative diseases involving protein aggregation, inflammatory bowel disease, viral infection, and cancer, where tumour cells exploit the UPR to survive hypoxia and nutrient deprivation. The pathway is an active target for drug development in these areas.
