Civil Guard

Civil Guard is a name used by several military and paramilitary police forces around the world, generally modelled on the gendarmerie concept — a force with military status tasked with civilian policing duties, particularly in rural areas.

Spain

The best-known Civil Guard is the Guardia Civil of Spain, founded in 1844. It is one of the country's two national police forces, alongside the National Police Corps, and is responsible for highway patrol, rural policing, border and coastal surveillance, environmental protection, and the guarding of official buildings. Its motto is El honor es mi divisa ("Honour is my emblem"). Members traditionally wore the distinctive tricorn hat (tricornio).

Other countries

Forces with the name Civil Guard have existed in many other countries, including the Philippines during the Spanish colonial period, Peru, Costa Rica, and Israel (the Mishmar Ezrahi, a volunteer reserve of the Israel Police founded in 1974). In the Philippines, the Guardia Civil operated from 1868 until the Spanish–American War. Several countries in Latin America and Europe adopted the Spanish model in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
