Capital equipment

Image placeholder: Machinery as capital equipment in a factory.

Capital equipment is durable goods used by a business or organisation in the production of other goods and services over an extended period, generally more than one year. It is a category of capital goods and appears on the balance sheet as a fixed asset, depreciated over its useful life.

Characteristics

Capital equipment includes machinery, tools, vehicles, buildings' installed systems, computers, and other physical assets that are not consumed in a single production cycle and are not intended for resale to customers. Unlike raw materials or supplies, capital equipment provides value through repeated use, though it wears out, becomes obsolete, or requires maintenance over time.

Accounting treatment

The purchase of capital equipment is a capital expenditure (capex) rather than an operating expense: its cost is capitalised and allocated over its useful life through depreciation. Businesses plan such purchases through capital budgeting, weighing expected returns, financing costs, and tax treatment. In governmental and military budgeting, "capital equipment" similarly denotes major durable purchases funded through capital or procurement budgets rather than annual operating funds.

Examples

Examples include lathes and CNC machines in manufacturing, MRI scanners in hospitals, aircraft for airlines, servers for data centres, and construction cranes.
