International Data Group

International Data Group, Inc. (IDG) is an American media, events, and market-research company focused on technology. Founded in 1964 in Newtonville, Massachusetts, by Patrick Joseph McGovern, it grew into one of the world's largest technology media companies.

Businesses

IDG's best-known units include IDC (International Data Corporation), its market-intelligence subsidiary founded in 1964, and publishing brands such as Computerworld, PCWorld, Macworld, CIO, and InfoWorld. The company also ran technology events and, from 1993 to 2015, owned the "For Dummies" publishing imprint's parent company IDG Books, later spun off.

Global expansion

IDG was a pioneer among Western media companies in China, establishing joint ventures there from 1980, and McGovern made more than 100 trips to the country. Its venture-capital arm, IDG Capital, became a major investor in Chinese technology firms.

Ownership

After McGovern's death in 2014, IDG was acquired in 2017 by China Oceanwide Holdings, and in 2021 it was sold to private-equity funds managed by Blackstone for a reported $1.3 billion.
