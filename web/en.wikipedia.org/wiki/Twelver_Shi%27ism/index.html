Twelver Shi'ism

Twelver Shi'ism (Ithnā ʿAshariyya) is the largest branch of Shia Islam, comprising the majority of Shia Muslims. Twelvers believe in a line of Twelve Imams, divinely guided successors of the Prophet Muhammad beginning with Ali ibn Abi Talib. The twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed to be in occultation (ghayba) since 874 CE and to return as the Mahdi at the end of time.

Adherentsc. 150–200 million

Key doctrineImamate; Occultation of the 12th Imam

Beliefs and practice

Twelver theology includes the principles of divine unity, justice, prophecy, imamate and resurrection. Jurisprudence (Ja'fari school) relies on the Quran, the hadith of the Prophet and the Imams, consensus and reason. During the occultation, senior scholars (marjaʿ al-taqlīd) serve as reference points for religious guidance; observances such as Ashura commemorate the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala (680 CE).
