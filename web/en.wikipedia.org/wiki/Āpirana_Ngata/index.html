Āpirana Ngata

Sir Āpirana Ngata, regarded as one of the most important Māori leaders of the 20th century.

Sir Āpirana Turupa Ngata (3 July 1874 – 14 July 1950) was a prominent New Zealand statesman, lawyer and Māori leader. Often regarded as the foremost Māori politician to have served in the Parliament of New Zealand, he is known for his advocacy of Māori land development and his efforts to preserve and revive Māori language and culture.

Early life and education

Ngata was born at Te Araroa on the East Coast, of Ngāti Porou descent. He attended Te Aute College and then Canterbury University College and the University of Auckland, becoming the first Māori to complete a university degree in New Zealand (a BA in 1893), followed by a law degree; he was admitted to the bar in 1897.

Political career

Ngata won the Eastern Maori seat in 1905 and held it until 1943. He served as Minister of Native Affairs from 1928 to 1934, driving an ambitious programme of Māori land development schemes that brought large areas of Māori land into farming. He was a central figure in the Young Māori Party and worked closely with Te Puea Hērangi and others on Māori welfare and cultural renewal.

Cultural legacy

Ngata collected and published Māori song and poetry, most famously in the anthology Ngā Mōteatea, supported the revival of carving and kapa haka, and helped establish the Māori Battalion's reputation in the Second World War. His portrait appears on the New Zealand fifty-dollar note.
