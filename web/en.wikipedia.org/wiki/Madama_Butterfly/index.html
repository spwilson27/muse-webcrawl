Madama Butterfly

Madama Butterfly is an opera in three acts (originally two) by Giacomo Puccini, with an Italian libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa. Based partly on David Belasco's play adapted from John Luther Long's short story, it premiered at La Scala, Milan, on 17 February 1904.

Madama Butterfly
ComposerGiacomo Puccini
Premiere17 February 1904, La Scala, Milan
SettingNagasaki, Japan, early 1900s

Synopsis

U.S. naval officer B. F. Pinkerton marries the young Japanese geisha Cio-Cio-San ("Butterfly") in Nagasaki, treating the marriage as a temporary arrangement while she takes it with complete sincerity. He leaves for America; years later he returns with an American wife, and Butterfly, who has borne his son, takes her own life. Famous numbers include the aria "Un bel dì, vedremo" and the humming chorus.

Reception

The premiere was a fiasco, but Puccini revised the score, and the opera became one of the most frequently performed works in the repertoire worldwide.
