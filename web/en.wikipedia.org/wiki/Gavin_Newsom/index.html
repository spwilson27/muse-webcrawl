Gavin Christopher Newsom (born October 10, 1967) is an American politician and businessman serving as the 40th governor of California since 2019. A member of the Democratic Party, he served as lieutenant governor of California from 2011 to 2019 and as the 42nd mayor of San Francisco from 2004 to 2011.

Early life and business career

Newsom was born in San Francisco to Tessa and William Newsom III, a state appeals court judge. He attended Santa Clara University, graduating in 1989 with a degree in political science. In 1992 he co-founded the PlumpJack Group, a hospitality business that began with a wine shop and grew to include wineries, restaurants and hotels; investor Gordon Getty was a major backer.

Mayor of San Francisco

Newsom entered politics in the 1990s, serving on San Francisco's Parking and Traffic Commission and then the Board of Supervisors. He was elected mayor in 2003 and took office in January 2004. Weeks into his term he directed the city to issue marriage licenses to same-sex couples, a move later invalidated by the courts but one that drew national attention to the marriage-equality debate. His administration also launched the Care Not Cash programme for homeless residents and Universal health coverage initiative Healthy San Francisco.

Governor of California

After two terms as lieutenant governor under Jerry Brown, Newsom was elected governor in 2018, defeating Republican John Cox. As governor he placed a moratorium on executions in California, expanded Medi-Cal coverage, and issued wide-ranging executive orders during the COVID-19 pandemic, including the first statewide stay-at-home order in the United States in March 2020.

Newsom survived a recall election in September 2021 and was re-elected in 2022. His governorship has emphasised housing production, climate policy, homelessness funding and social issues, and he has been a prominent national figure within the Democratic Party.
