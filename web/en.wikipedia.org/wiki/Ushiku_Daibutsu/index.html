Ushiku Daibutsu

The Ushiku Daibutsu (Japanese: 牛久大仏) is a statue located in Ushiku, Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. Completed in 1993, it stands a total of 120 metres (390 ft) tall, including the 10-metre-high base and 10-metre-high lotus platform, making it one of the tallest statues in the world. The bronze statue depicts Amitābha Buddha and was built by the Higashi Hongan-ji branch of Jōdo Shinshū Buddhism.

The Ushiku Daibutsu held the record for the world's tallest statue from 1993 to 2008.

Description

The statue weighs over 4,000 tonnes and is constructed of bronze plates over a steel frame. Inside the statue, visitors can take an elevator to an observation level 85 metres above the ground, behind the Buddha's chest, where windows look out over the surrounding Kantō plain; on clear days Mount Fuji and the Tokyo Skytree can be seen. The interior contains several floors of exhibits, including a hall with thousands of small golden Buddha statues and displays explaining the construction.

Details of the statue are correspondingly immense: each eye is about 2.5 metres across, the nose about 1.2 metres, and the mouth about 2 metres wide. The left hand, held palm-up at the statue's side, is some 18 metres long.

Significance

The Daibutsu was erected to commemorate the birth of Shinran (1173–1263), founder of the Jōdo Shinshū school, who was born in the Kantō region. At its completion it surpassed all previous statues in height; it was in turn surpassed by China's Spring Temple Buddha (2008) and later by India's Statue of Unity (2018). It remains among the tallest statues in the world and a major pilgrimage and sightseeing destination in Ibaraki.
