Dillenia

Dillenia is a genus of flowering plants in the family Dilleniaceae, native to tropical and subtropical regions of southern Asia, Australasia, and the Indian Ocean islands. The genus comprises roughly 60–100 species of evergreen or semi-evergreen trees and shrubs, and is named after the German botanist Johann Jacob Dillenius.

Notable species

The best-known species is Dillenia indica, the elephant apple, a medium-sized tree of South and Southeast Asia whose large, sour, fibrous fruits are used in curries, chutneys and traditional medicine, and are eaten by elephants and other wildlife. Other species include Dillenia philippinensis (katmon) of the Philippines and Dillenia suffruticosa, a pioneer shrub common in disturbed habitats in Southeast Asia.

Description

Members of the genus have large, simple, strongly veined leaves and showy white or yellow flowers, often with numerous stamens. Several species are planted as ornamentals and shade trees in tropical gardens.
