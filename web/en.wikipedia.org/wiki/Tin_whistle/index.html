Tin whistle

The tin whistle, also called the penny whistle, is a simple six-holed woodwind instrument. It is a type of fipple flute, in the same class as the recorder, and is strongly associated with Irish and Scottish traditional music.

Description

The player blows into the mouthpiece, where a fipple directs air against a sharp edge, and opens or closes six finger holes to change pitch. Most whistles are diatonic, most commonly in the key of D. Modern whistles are made of brass, nickel, or plastic despite the historical name.

History

Flutes of this type are ancient, but the modern tin whistle dates to 19th-century England, where Robert Clarke began manufacturing cheap tinplate whistles in 1843 — hence the "penny" whistle, affordable to working-class musicians.

Repertoire

The whistle is a staple of Irish sessions, playing jigs, reels, and slow airs, with ornamentation such as cuts, rolls, and crans borrowed from uilleann piping technique.
