The Adventist Development and Relief Agency (ADRA) is a global humanitarian organisation operated by the Seventh-day Adventist Church. It provides community development assistance and disaster relief in countries around the world, regardless of the religious or political affiliation of the recipients, and maintains a network of country offices coordinated by ADRA International, headquartered in Silver Spring, Maryland.

History

ADRA's origins trace to 1956, when the Seventh-day Adventist Church established the Seventh-day Adventist Welfare Service (SAWS) to ship food and relief supplies overseas. The organisation was renamed the Seventh-day Adventist World Service in the 1970s as its work expanded from relief into long-term development, and adopted the name Adventist Development and Relief Agency in 1984 to reflect that dual mandate.

Work

ADRA's programmes span emergency response to earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, and conflicts; food security and livelihoods; primary health care, nutrition, and water and sanitation; education; and disaster preparedness. It has responded to crises including the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, the 2010 Haiti earthquake, and the Syrian refugee crisis. ADRA holds consultative status with United Nations bodies and works with institutional donors such as USAID and the European Union as well as with private supporters.
