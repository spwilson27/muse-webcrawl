BioMed Central (BMC) is a United Kingdom-based scientific publisher specialising in open access journals. Founded in 2000, BioMed Central was a pioneer of the open access publishing model, in which research articles are freely available to readers online and publication costs are met by article processing charges paid by authors or their institutions. In 2008 the company was acquired by Springer (now part of Springer Nature).

History

BioMed Central grew out of the Current Science Group founded by Vitek Tracz. It launched its first journals in 2000, arguing that the traditional subscription model unduly restricted access to publicly funded research. The publisher was among the first to make peer-reviewed articles available under Creative Commons licences, and its journals are indexed in PubMed Central. Its flagship titles include the BMC series of journals across biology and medicine, such as BMC Biology and BMC Medicine.

Publishing model

BMC journals conduct conventional peer review but publish all accepted articles open access. Membership schemes allow institutions to cover charges centrally. In later years BMC expanded into open data initiatives and campaigns for open research, and its journals adopted registered reports and transparent peer review options on some titles.

Reception

BioMed Central's commercial success demonstrated that open access publishing could be financially viable, influencing the founding of publishers such as the Public Library of Science (PLOS). Like other open access publishers, it has had to police paper-mill submissions and maintain editorial quality across a large journal portfolio.
