Libation

A libation is a ritual pouring of a liquid, most often wine, oil, milk, honey or water, as an offering to a deity, spirit, or the dead. Libations were central to religious practice in ancient Greece and Rome and are found in many cultures worldwide, including ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, Judaism, Hinduism, Shinto and various African and Afro-diasporic traditions.

Greco-Roman practice

In ancient Greece a libation (spondē) typically accompanied prayer, oaths, treaties, meals and sacrifices: a portion of wine was poured on the ground or an altar from a shallow bowl (phiale) before the remainder was drunk. Libations to the dead (choai) were poured entirely onto the grave. In Rome, wine was poured on altars and at funerals, and the patera was the characteristic vessel.

Other traditions

In ancient Israel, drink offerings of wine accompanied burnt offerings at the Tabernacle and Temple. In Hindu practice, liquids such as water, milk and soma have been poured as offerings since Vedic times. Libations of rum or other drinks feature in Vodou and related traditions, and pouring a drink for the dead survives informally in modern customs in many countries.
