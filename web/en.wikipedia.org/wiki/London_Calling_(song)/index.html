"London Calling" is a song by the British punk rock band the Clash. It was released as a single from the band's double album of the same name in December 1979. Written by Joe Strummer and Mick Jones, the song's apocalyptic, politically charged lyrics reflect anxiety about nuclear war, flooding of the Thames, police brutality, and racial and social unrest in London.

Composition and lyrics

The title alludes to the BBC World Service station identification phrase "This is London calling", used during World War II broadcasts to occupied countries. Strummer's lyrics survey a catalogue of looming disasters — "a nuclear error", the coming ice age, and the Thames flooding London — while mocking the complacency of the "phony Beatlemania" that had "bitten the dust". The song's driving, reggae-influenced bassline by Paul Simonon and its martial rhythm depart from straight punk toward the broader sound of the album.

Reception and legacy

The single reached number 11 on the UK Singles Chart. "London Calling" is widely regarded as one of the greatest songs of all time; Rolling Stone ranked it number 15 on its list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time. The accompanying album is frequently cited among the finest ever made. In 2012, the song featured in the opening ceremony of the London Olympic Games. Bassist Paul Simonon owns the bass guitar he famously smashed on stage at the New York Palladium in 1979, photographed by Pennie Smith for the album cover.
