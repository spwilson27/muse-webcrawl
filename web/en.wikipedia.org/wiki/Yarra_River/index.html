The Yarra River (Woiwurrung: Birrarung) is a perennial river in east-central Victoria, Australia. It flows some 242 kilometres westward from its source in the Yarra Ranges through the Yarra Valley and Melbourne before emptying into Hobsons Bay in northern Port Phillip. Melbourne, founded on its banks in 1835, grew around the river, whose lower reaches form the heart of the city's central business district.

History

For thousands of years the river was central to the Wurundjeri people of the Kulin nation, who called it Birrarung, often rendered "river of mists and shadows". European settlement began in 1835 when John Batman's party selected "the place for a village" near the falls at the limit of tidal influence. The upstream gold rushes of the 1850s (notably at Warrandyte) and later industrial pollution badly degraded the river; the Falls were dynamited and the river straightened and widened in the late 19th century to control flooding and improve shipping, creating Coode Island and the modern Port of Melbourne channels.

Course and geography

The upper catchment in the Yarra Ranges National Park is protected and supplies much of Melbourne's drinking water via the Upper Yarra Reservoir and the Thomson Reservoir scheme. At Warburton and Healesville the river is a clear mountain stream; it meanders through the broad Yarra Valley wine region at Yarra Glen, passes Warrandyte gorge and the rapids at Wonga Park, and enters the suburbs at Templestowe. Tributaries include the Plenty, Merri Creek, Darebin Creek, Gardiners Creek, and the Maribyrnong River near the mouth.

Use and culture

The riverbanks in central Melbourne — Southbank, the Royal Botanic Gardens, Birrarung Marr, and the Melbourne Park sporting precinct — are among the city's best-known public spaces. Longstanding jokes about the Yarra's brown, muddy colour (locally "the river that flows upside down") reflect its naturally turbid lower reaches. Recent decades have seen sustained clean-up efforts, and in 2017 the Victorian Parliament passed the Yarra River Protection (Wilip-gin Birrarung murron) Act, the first Australian legislation to recognise a river and its Traditional Owners in this way.
