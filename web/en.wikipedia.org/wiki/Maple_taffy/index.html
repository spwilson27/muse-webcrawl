Maple taffy

Maple taffy
Alternative namesMaple toffee; tire d'érable; tire sur la neige
Place of originCanada (Quebec)
Main ingredientsMaple syrup, snow

Maple taffy (also known as maple toffee, and in French as tire d'érable or tire sur la neige) is a sugar candy made with maple syrup. Hot, boiled maple syrup is poured in strips onto clean snow, where the cold causes it to thicken rapidly into a soft, chewy taffy. The taffy is then lifted with a wooden stick or fork and rolled up to be eaten. The technique originates in Quebec and is traditional in eastern Canada and New England, where maple sap is harvested.

Preparation

Maple syrup is boiled to about 112 °C (234 °F), slightly beyond the concentration used for syrup, and poured in thin ribbons onto fresh snow packed into trays or troughs. Within seconds to a minute or two the syrup sets into a pliable sheet or strip, which is wound around a flat wooden paddle or popsicle stick. Because it melts quickly, it is eaten on the spot.

Cultural significance

Maple taffy is a hallmark of the cabane à sucre (sugar shack) tradition of Quebec, where families visit maple groves during the spring sugaring-off season and taffy on snow is served as part of a traditional meal. It is also a standard feature of winter festivals such as the Quebec Winter Carnival and Ottawa's Winterlude, and of maple festivals across Ontario, the Maritimes, and the northeastern United States.
