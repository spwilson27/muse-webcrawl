Electric displacement field

In physics, the electric displacement field, also called electric flux density and usually denoted by D, is a vector field that accounts for the effects of free and bound charge within materials. It appears in Maxwell's equations and simplifies them in the presence of dielectrics.

D = ε0E + P

Definition

The displacement field combines the electric field E with the polarisation density P of the material, where ε0 is the vacuum permittivity. In a linear, isotropic dielectric, D = εE, where ε is the permittivity of the material.

Gauss's law

In differential form, the divergence of D equals the free charge density: ∇ · D = ρf. This is one of Maxwell's equations, and it makes D convenient for calculating fields from free charges alone, particularly in capacitors filled with dielectric.

Boundary conditions

At an interface between two media, the normal component of D changes by the free surface charge density, while the tangential component of E is continuous in the absence of free surface currents. Displacement current, the time derivative of D, is the term Maxwell added to Ampère's law.
