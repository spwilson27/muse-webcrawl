Webinar

A webinar is an online seminar, lecture, workshop, or presentation conducted over the World Wide Web using videoconferencing software. The word is a portmanteau of "web" and "seminar". Webinars may be live, allowing real-time interaction between presenters and audience through text chat, polls, and question-and-answer sessions, or they may be recorded and viewed on demand.

Features

Typical webinar platforms support screen sharing, slide presentations, live audio and video of one or more presenters, text chat, polling, hand-raising, and recording. Attendees usually join through a web browser or a dedicated application after registering in advance. Webinars are widely used in education, corporate training, marketing, and product demonstrations, because they allow a presenter to reach a geographically dispersed audience without travel costs.

History

Real-time text conferencing originated with systems such as IRC in the late 1980s, and web-based conferencing products appeared in the mid-1990s. Companies such as WebEx (founded 1995) and GoToMeeting popularized web conferencing for business use in the 2000s. The term "webinar" was trademarked in 1998 by Eric R. Korb. Adoption grew rapidly in the 2010s and especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, when remote work and online education became widespread.
