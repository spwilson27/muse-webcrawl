Alliance of Liberals and Democrats for Europe group

The Alliance of Liberals and Democrats for Europe (ALDE) was a liberal political group in the European Parliament from 2004 to 2019. It united MEPs from the ALDE Party and the European Democratic Party, and for most of its existence was the third or fourth largest group in Parliament. In 2019, joined by Emmanuel Macron's La République En Marche, it was succeeded by Renew Europe.

History

ALDE was formed on 20 July 2004, at the start of the sixth European Parliament, from the former European Liberal Democrat and Reform Party (ELDR) group and centrist parties of the new European Democratic Party. Under leaders including Graham Watson and, from 2009, former Belgian prime minister Guy Verhofstadt, the group frequently held the balance of power between the centre-right EPP and the centre-left S&D groups.

Ideology and policies

The group advocated a strongly pro-European, federalist outlook: completing the single market, free trade agreements, civil liberties and human rights, rule-of-law conditionality within the EU, and a common approach to climate and energy policy. It generally supported economic liberalism combined with social liberal positions on issues such as LGBTQ rights and privacy.

Composition

Member parties included Germany's Free Democratic Party, the Netherlands' VVD and D66, Spain's Ciudadanos, the Czech ANO 2011, the British Liberal Democrats (until Brexit), and liberal parties across Scandinavia, the Baltic states and central Europe. After the 2014 election the group held 67 seats in the eighth Parliament.

Succession

Following the 2019 European elections, ALDE re-formed with the addition of Macron's delegation as Renew Europe, adopting the new name to reflect the enlarged coalition; Verhofstadt stepped aside and Dacian Cioloș became the new group's first leader.
