Visa Inc. is an American multinational payment card services corporation headquartered in San Francisco, California. It operates the world's largest retail electronic payments network, facilitating funds transfers via Visa-branded credit, debit and prepaid cards issued by member banks.

History

Visa began in 1958 as BankAmericard, launched by Bank of America in Fresno, California — famously including an unsolicited mass mailing of 60,000 real cards. The programme was licensed to banks internationally, and in 1970 it was spun out as a cooperative association of member banks under Dee Hock, who developed its decentralised organisational model. The name "Visa" was adopted in 1976 because it was short, pronounceable in many languages and evoked a travel visa.

The associations restructured into a single public company in a 2007–2008 IPO that was then the largest in U.S. history; Visa Europe was acquired and merged back in 2016.

Operations

Visa does not issue cards, extend credit or set consumer rates; it provides the VisaNet network and brand to financial institutions, earning fees from payment volume. It processes tens of trillions of dollars annually across billions of transactions, and its interchange fees have drawn repeated antitrust scrutiny and litigation from merchants.
