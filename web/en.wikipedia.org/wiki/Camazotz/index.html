Camazotz

In Maya mythology, Camazotz (also Camazot, from K'iche' kama zotz', 'death bat') is a bat god associated with night, death and sacrifice. He is a prominent figure in the K'iche' epic the Popol Vuh.

MythologyMaya
AbodeXibalba (underworld)
SymbolBat

In the Popol Vuh

The Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque, during their trials in Xibalba, spend a night in the House of Bats (Zotzi' Ja), where Camazotz swoops down and snips off Hunahpu's head. The head is used as a ball in the Mesoamerican ballgame before being restored with the help of a squash.

Origins

Bat deities appear in earlier Mesoamerican cultures, including Zapotec art, and bats were linked to caves — entrances to the underworld — blood and decapitation. A giant bat figure is known from Copán.

In popular culture

Camazotz has appeared in modern fantasy, games and comics, sometimes compared with Batman-like figures, and the name was given to an extinct vampire bat species, Desmodus draculae-era discussions aside. See Popol Vuh, Maya mythology, Xibalba.
