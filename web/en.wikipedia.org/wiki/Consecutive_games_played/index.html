Consecutive games played

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Consecutive games played streaks are endurance records in professional sports in which an athlete appears in every game — or every scheduled game — for an extended run of seasons without missing one to injury, rest, or suspension. Such players are often described as "iron men". The most famous streaks belong to baseball, where the long season and daily schedule make the achievement especially demanding.

Major League Baseball

The Major League Baseball record is held by Cal Ripken Jr. of the Baltimore Orioles, who played in 2,632 consecutive games from 30 May 1982 to 19 September 1998, breaking the record of 2,130 held by Lou Gehrig on 6 September 1995. Gehrig's mark had stood since 1939 and was long considered unbreakable; his streak ended when he removed himself from the lineup owing to the illness (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis) that later bore his name. Other notable streaks include Everett Scott (1,307), Steve Garvey (1,207), and Miguel Tejada (1,152).

Other sports

  • NFL: Quarterback Brett Favre started 297 consecutive regular-season games (321 including playoffs) from 1992 to 2010, the record for consecutive starts by an NFL player. Punter Jeff Feeney — rather, punter and holder records are led by appearances rather than starts.
  • NBA: A. C. Green played in 1,192 consecutive games from 1986 to 2001, the NBA record.
  • NHL: Doug Jarvis played 964 consecutive games between 1975 and 1987; defenceman Keith Yandle later reached 989 games, and Phil Kessel surpassed both, reaching 1,064 consecutive games.
  • Association football: Streaks are typically measured in consecutive league appearances; goalkeepers and durable outfield players hold club-specific records, since international breaks and rotation make national records harder to compare.

Significance and modern attitudes

Streaks are celebrated as evidence of durability and professionalism, but modern sports science has made teams more willing to rest healthy players ("load management"), so long streaks are rarer than they were. Players sometimes keep appearances alive with token late-game entries, prompting debate about whether streaks should require a minimum involvement.
