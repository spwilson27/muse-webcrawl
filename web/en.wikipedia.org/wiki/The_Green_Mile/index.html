The Green Mile

[Image: A film still from The Green Mile — a gentle giant of a prisoner holding a small mouse in his hands while a guard watches kindly.]

The Green Mile is a 1999 American fantasy drama film written and directed by Frank Darabont and based on Stephen King's 1996 novel of the same name. It stars Tom Hanks as Paul Edgecomb, a death-row corrections officer in Louisiana during the Great Depression, and Michael Clarke Duncan as John Coffey, a gentle, supernaturally gifted prisoner convicted of a terrible crime. The film was a critical and commercial success and received four Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture.

Plot

In 1935, Edgecomb oversees "the Green Mile" — the green-linoleum-floored corridor of death row at Cold Mountain Penitentiary — where executions are carried out by electric chair. The arrival of the enormous, childlike Coffey, convicted of murdering two young girls, changes everything: Coffey heals Edgecomb's infection by touch, revives a dead mouse, and reveals through a vision that the real killer was another inmate, the sadistic "Wild Bill" Wharton. Convinced of Coffey's innocence but unable to free him, Paul offers him escape; Coffey refuses, weary of the world's pain, and goes to his execution. Paul, cursed with unnatural longevity, lives to 108, outliving everyone he loved.

Reception

Critics praised the performances, especially Duncan's breakthrough role, and the film's emotional power, though some found its three-hour length excessive. It earned about $290 million worldwide and remains one of the most popular of all Stephen King adaptations; Darabont had earlier directed The Shawshank Redemption from another King prison story.
