Wakayama, Wakayama

Wakayama (Japanese: 和歌山市, Wakayama-shi) is the capital city of Wakayama Prefecture in the Kansai region of Japan, located at the mouth of the Kino River on the Kii Channel, south of Osaka. A former castle town of the Kishū Tokugawa family, it is known for Wakayama Castle, the Wakanoura bay celebrated in classical poetry, and as a gateway to the sacred sites of the Kii Mountain Range.

History

The area was home to the Saika confederacy of gunner-mercenaries in the Sengoku period, who resisted Oda Nobunaga. Toyotomi Hideyoshi built Wakayama Castle in 1585; under the Tokugawa shogunate the city became the seat of the Kishū branch, one of the three great collateral houses (gosanke), which supplied the eighth shogun, Tokugawa Yoshimune. The modern city was incorporated in 1889. Much of the centre was destroyed by American air raids in July 1945; the castle keep, burned in the raids, was rebuilt in concrete in 1958.

Geography and economy

Wakayama occupies the northwestern corner of the Kii Peninsula, facing Awaji Island across the Kii Channel. Steel, chemicals and textiles historically anchored its economy at the Sumitomo Metal works; today services, tourism and food processing predominate. The city is under an hour from Osaka by JR and Nankai trains, and a ferry links its port to Tokushima on Shikoku.

Sights

Beyond the castle, attractions include Wakanoura and Kataonami beach, Kishū Tōshō-gū shrine, Kimiidera temple (second stop on the Saigoku pilgrimage), and the Marina City resort complex on an artificial island. Wakayama ramen — a pork-and-soy tonkotsu style — is a celebrated local dish.
