Ocala, Florida

Ocala is a city and the county seat of Marion County in north-central Florida, United States. The city had a population of about 63,000 at the 2020 census, and its metropolitan area held roughly 375,000 people. Ocala is known as the "Horse Capital of the World", a title reflecting the hundreds of thoroughbred farms in the surrounding county.

History

The name derives from Ocali, a Timucua village recorded by the Hernando de Soto expedition in 1539. The modern town grew around Fort King, a United States Army post of the Second Seminole War established in 1827; the fort site is now a National Historic Landmark. Ocala was incorporated in 1869 and rebuilt in brick after a devastating fire in 1883, earning it the nickname "Brick City".

Horse industry

Marion County's limestone-rich soils support a major thoroughbred breeding industry dating to the 1940s; Florida Derby winners and Triple Crown champion Affirmed were raised locally. The World Equestrian Center, one of the largest equestrian venues in the United States, opened in Ocala in 2021. Nearby Silver Springs, one of Florida's oldest attractions, is famous for its glass-bottom boats and artesian springs.
