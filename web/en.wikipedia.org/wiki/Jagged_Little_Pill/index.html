Jagged Little Pill is the third studio album by Canadian singer-songwriter Alanis Morissette, released on June 13, 1995, through Maverick Records. Her first album released internationally, it marked a sharp departure from the dance-pop of her earlier Canadian releases toward an alternative rock sound built on personal, confessional songwriting, developed with producer and co-writer Glen Ballard in Los Angeles.

Singles and reception

The album produced a string of hit singles — "You Oughta Know," "Ironic," "You Learn," "Hand in My Pocket," "Head over Feet," and "All I Really Want" — and was propelled by heavy rotation on MTV and alternative radio. "You Oughta Know," with its furious post-breakup lyrics and contributions from Red Hot Chili Peppers members Flea and Dave Navarro, became an anthem of female anger in popular music.

Commercial success and awards

Jagged Little Pill topped the charts in thirteen countries, including ten weeks atop the US Billboard 200, and became one of the best-selling albums of all time, with worldwide sales estimated at 33 million copies. At the 1996 Grammy Awards, Morissette won four awards including Album of the Year and Best Rock Album; at 21, she was then the youngest artist to win Album of the Year. The album was later certified 17× platinum in the United States and is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of the 1990s.

Legacy

The album's success is widely credited with opening commercial space for confessional female singer-songwriters in mainstream rock. It was adapted into a stage musical that premiered in 2018 and transferred to Broadway in 2019. In 2015, the album's 20th anniversary was marked with a collector's edition and an acoustic companion release.
