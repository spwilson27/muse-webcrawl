Robert Raikes

Robert Raikes
Born: 14 September 1736, Gloucester, England
Died: 5 April 1811, Gloucester
Known for: Founding the Sunday school movement

Robert Raikes (14 September 1736 – 5 April 1811) was an English philanthropist and Anglican layman, generally credited with founding the Sunday school movement in England. A Gloucester newspaper proprietor and campaigner for prison reform, Raikes began holding classes for poor working children on Sundays in 1780; within a few years the idea had spread nationally, and by 1831 Sunday schools in Britain were teaching some 1.25 million children.

Life

Raikes was born in Gloucester, the son of Robert Raikes the elder, founder of the Gloucester Journal, which the younger Raikes inherited and edited. Inspired by his work visiting prisoners in Gloucester's gaols and by concern for children employed in factories and chimney-sweeping who spent their only free day, Sunday, in the streets, he resolved to offer them basic education and religious instruction.

The Sunday schools

In July 1780 Raikes engaged a teacher, Mrs Meredith, to instruct a group of boys in Sooty Alley, Gloucester; the children were required to arrive washed and with combed hair, and were taught reading — using the Bible as the textbook — along with the catechism and basic manners. Raikes publicised the experiment in his newspaper in 1783, and the movement spread rapidly through the churches, both Anglican and Nonconformist. The Sunday School Society was founded in London in 1785 to support the schools with funding and materials.

Legacy

The Sunday schools taught literacy to generations of working-class children before state elementary education became compulsory, and are seen as precursors of the public school system. Raikes's role is commemorated by a statue in Gloucester Park and another on the Victoria Embankment in London, and the bicentenary of the movement in 1880 was marked by national celebrations.
