The PlayStation Vita (officially abbreviated PS Vita) is a handheld game console developed and marketed by Sony Computer Entertainment. It is the successor to the PlayStation Portable and part of the PlayStation brand of gaming devices. It was released in Japan on December 17, 2011, and in North America, Europe, and other regions in February 2012.

Hardware

The Vita features a 5-inch (13 cm) OLED touchscreen on the original PCH-1000 model, two analog sticks—a first for a mainstream handheld at the time—a rear touchpad, a quad-core ARM Cortex-A9 CPU, and a quad-core PowerVR GPU. Two models were sold: a Wi-Fi-only version and a more expensive 3G/Wi-Fi version. Games were distributed on proprietary PlayStation Vita Cards and digitally through the PlayStation Store; the system required proprietary memory cards, whose high prices were widely criticized.

A revised model, the PCH-2000 series (often called the Vita Slim), was released in Japan in October 2013 and in the West in 2014. It replaced the OLED screen with an LCD, was thinner and lighter, added 1 GB of internal storage, and used a standard micro-USB port for charging.

Software and features

The Vita's launch lineup included entries in major Sony franchises such as Uncharted: Golden Abyss and Wipeout 2048. The system's library came to include acclaimed titles such as Persona 4 Golden, Gravity Rush, and Tearaway, along with a large catalogue of Japanese role-playing games, visual novels, and independent titles. Features included Remote Play with the PlayStation 4, cross-buy and cross-save with PlayStation 3 and PS4, the "near" location service, and augmented-reality applications.

Reception and legacy

Although praised for its hardware quality and screen, the Vita struggled commercially, especially in Western markets, where smartphone gaming and the competing Nintendo 3DS limited its audience. Sales are estimated at roughly 10–15 million units worldwide. Sony ended production of the hardware and physical game cards in 2019. The Vita retained a devoted fan community, particularly for JRPGs and indie games, and is often regarded as an underappreciated system whose commercial failure reflected market timing rather than quality.
