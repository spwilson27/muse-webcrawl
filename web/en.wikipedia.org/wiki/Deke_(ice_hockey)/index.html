Deke (ice hockey)

A deke, short for "decoy", is a technique in ice hockey in which a player draws an opposing player or goaltender out of position or fakes a move in one direction while going another, in order to skate past with the puck. The word is a Canadianism derived from "decoy" and is used both as a noun and a verb: a player may "deke" a defender or "deke out" a goalie.

Techniques

Common dekes include the forehand-to-backhand drag, the toe drag (pulling the puck back with the toe of the stick blade), the shoulder fake, and the "Datsyukian" deke named after Pavel Datsyuk, famed for his creative puckhandling. In a shootout or breakaway, skaters often use a sequence of stickhandling feints to force the goaltender to commit before shooting or sliding the puck around them. Skilled stickhandlers such as Patrick Kane and Connor McDavid are known for deking opponents at full speed.

In other sports

The term is also used in lacrosse and other sports for similar faking moves, and has passed into general slang meaning to evade or outwit someone.
