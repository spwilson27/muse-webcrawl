Bosnian Church

The Bosnian Church (Serbo-Croatian: Crkva bosanska) was a Christian church in medieval Bosnia and Herzegovina. It was independent of both the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches and was long accused by both of heresy. Its adherents called themselves krstjani ("Christians") or "good Bosnians".

Background

Bosnia lay between the Catholic West and the Orthodox East, and its mountainous isolation encouraged an autonomous church. From the time of Ban Kulin around 1200, Rome repeatedly demanded action against alleged Bosnian heretics; the Bilino Polje abjuration of 1203 saw Bosnian church leaders renounce errors before a papal legate while keeping their own organisation.

Beliefs and organisation

The church was headed by a bishop called the djed ("grandfather"), assisted by elders known as strojnici. Its monks lived in small houses called hiže. Scholars debate whether the church held dualist, Bogomil beliefs; much of the evidence comes from hostile Catholic sources, and many modern historians regard the Bosnian Church as essentially a schismatic but orthodox-rite body.

Decline

The church came under pressure from the Franciscan vicariate in Bosnia and from Bosnian kings seeking papal favour. It faded rapidly after the Ottoman conquest of Bosnia in 1463, its remaining adherents converting over time to Catholicism, Orthodoxy, or Islam. The carved tombstones known as stećci are often popularly associated with its followers.
