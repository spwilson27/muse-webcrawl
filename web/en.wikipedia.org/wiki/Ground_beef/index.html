Ground beef, beef mince or minced beef is beef that has been finely chopped with a knife, meat grinder or mincing machine. It is used in many recipes including hamburgers, bolognese sauce and meatloaf, and is a staple of cuisines worldwide.

Contents and grading

Ground beef is made from the trimmings and less tender cuts of beef, commonly chuck, round and sirloin. In the United States it is sold by lean percentage: "regular" ground beef may contain up to 30% fat, while ground chuck, ground round and ground sirloin are progressively leaner. Under US regulations, products labelled "hamburger" may contain added beef fat, whereas "ground beef" may not; both are capped at 30% fat. Debates over additives — notably the use of lean finely textured beef, dubbed "pink slime" by critics — led to labelling and sourcing changes in the 2010s.

Food safety

Because grinding distributes surface bacteria throughout the meat, ground beef carries a higher risk of contamination with pathogens such as E. coli O157:H7 and Salmonella than intact cuts. Health authorities recommend cooking ground beef to an internal temperature of 71 °C (160 °F). Large outbreaks in the 1990s prompted the US Department of Agriculture to declare E. coli O157:H7 an adulterant in ground beef and to require hazard-analysis controls in packing plants.

Uses

Ground beef is the basis of the hamburger patty and appears in dishes such as meatloaf, meatballs, chili con carne, tacos, shepherd's pie (properly made with lamb, its beef version being cottage pie), kofta, and pasta sauces. Regional variants use seasonings and binders ranging from breadcrumbs and egg to rice and spices.
