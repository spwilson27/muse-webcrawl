Battle of Yamen

The Battle of Yamen (Chinese: 崖門海戰), also known as the Battle of Mount Ya, was a naval battle fought on 19 March 1279 between the Song dynasty and the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty. It is regarded as the last major battle of the Song dynasty and marked the complete conquest of China by the Mongols under Kublai Khan.

Background

By 1276 the Yuan forces had captured the Song capital at Lin'an (Hangzhou), and the dowager empress had surrendered. Loyalist officials, however, enthroned two young princes in succession and continued resistance in the south. After the death of the boy emperor Duanzong in 1278, his younger brother Zhao Bing was proclaimed emperor at Yamen, an island anchorage near modern Xinhui in Guangdong. The Song court commander Zhang Shijie prepared a final defensive position there.

The battle

Zhang Shijie lashed together roughly a thousand ships into a defensive formation in the bay, covering their wooden sides with mud to resist fire arrows. The Yuan commander Zhang Hongfan blockaded the anchorage, cutting off supplies of fresh water and firewood, and on 19 March 1279 attacked from several directions, using the tide and music to mask the assault. Boarding parties overwhelmed the chained Song fleet.

Seeing defeat was inevitable, the chancellor Lu Xiufu first drove his own family to their deaths and then, carrying the eight-year-old emperor Zhao Bing on his back, leapt into the sea. Many officials and members of the court followed them in mass suicide; tradition holds that a hundred thousand bodies floated in the waters afterward.

Aftermath

The battle ended the Song dynasty after more than three centuries and completed the Mongol conquest of China, uniting the whole country under the Yuan dynasty. The site is commemorated today in Xinhui, and the death of Lu Xiufu with the child emperor remains one of the most famous episodes of dynastic loyalty in Chinese history.
