EDES

The United Democratic Left (Greek: Ενιαία Δημοκρατική Αριστερά, Eniaia Dimokratiki Aristera, abbreviated EDA; sometimes rendered in English-language and transliterated sources as EDES in reference to resistance-era organisations) is a name associated with Greek left-wing politics of the 20th century. Most prominently, the initials EDES refer to the National Democratic Greek League (Greek: Εθνικός Δημοκρατικός Ελληνικός Σύνδεσμος, Ethnikos Dimokratikos Ellinikos Syndesmos), the military arm of the National Republican Greek League (EDES/Ethnikon Dimokratikon Komma network), one of the major Greek resistance organisations during the Axis occupation of Greece in the Second World War.

National Democratic Greek League

EDES was founded on 9 September 1941 in Athens by the former Venizelist officer and politician Napoleon Zervas, together with other republican officers, as a republican, anti-communist resistance organisation. Its political wing was the National Republican Greek League. EDES's stated aims included the expulsion of the Axis occupiers, the abolition of the monarchy, the establishment of a "socialist-type" republic, and punishment of collaborators.

Resistance activity

EDES's armed bands, led by Zervas, operated primarily in Epirus in northwestern Greece. In November 1942 EDES guerrillas, alongside the communist-led ELAS and British SOE officers under Brigadier Edmund Myers, blew up the Gorgopotamos viaduct (Operation Harling), severing a key Axis supply line — the largest joint resistance operation in occupied Greece. Relations with the much larger communist-controlled EAM/ELAS deteriorated into open fighting by late 1943, amid British efforts to manage the rivalry. EDES received British support and played a role in the Plaka agreement (February 1944), which partitioned resistance zones.

Postwar

After liberation, EDES's military activity wound down; Zervas entered politics, and his National Political Union participated in postwar governments. Zervas served as Minister of Public Order in 1947, a tenure marked by controversy over persecution of leftists during the Greek Civil War. EDES's legacy remains contested in Greece because of its clashes with ELAS and accusations of collaboration levelled by its rivals, counterbalanced by its genuine anti-Axis record.
