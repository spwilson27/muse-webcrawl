Feces

Feces (or faeces), also called stool, are the solid or semi-solid remains of food that was not digested in the small intestine and was broken down by bacteria in the large intestine. Feces contain a relatively small amount of metabolic waste products such as bacterially altered bilirubin and dead epithelial cells from the lining of the gut. They are discharged through the anus during defecation.

Composition

About 75% of an average fecal sample is water; of the remaining solid fraction, roughly 25–54% is bacterial biomass, with the rest made up of undigested food residue, fibre, protein, fat, and inorganic matter. The brown colour comes from stercobilin, a breakdown product of bile pigments. The Bristol stool scale is used clinically to classify stool form.

Ecology and uses

In ecosystems, feces return nutrients to the soil and are exploited by coprophagous animals, dung beetles, and fungi. Human feces are a vehicle for disease transmission when sanitation fails, but treated biosolids are used as fertiliser, and fecal microbiota transplantation is an effective treatment for recurrent Clostridioides difficile infection.
