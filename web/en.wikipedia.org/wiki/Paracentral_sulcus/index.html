Paracentral sulcus

The paracentral sulcus is a groove (sulcus) of the cerebral cortex located on the medial surface of each cerebral hemisphere. It runs roughly perpendicular to the central sulcus and forms part of the boundary of the paracentral lobule, which wraps around the termination of the central sulcus on the medial surface of the brain.

Anatomy

The paracentral lobule contains the medial continuation of the primary motor cortex anteriorly and the primary somatosensory cortex posteriorly, corresponding to representation of the lower limb, hip, and trunk. The paracentral sulcus typically marks the anterior boundary of this lobule, while the marginal segment of the cingulate sulcus forms its posterior boundary.

Clinical significance

Lesions involving the paracentral lobule may produce weakness and sensory loss confined largely to the contralateral leg. The region is a frequent site of parasagittal meningiomas, which can compress the medial motor strip.
