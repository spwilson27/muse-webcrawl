Miklós Horthy de Nagybánya (18 June 1868 – 9 February 1957) was a Hungarian admiral and statesman who served as Regent of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1920 to 1944. A former commander-in-chief of the Austro-Hungarian Navy, he led the counter-revolutionary forces that overthrew the Hungarian Soviet Republic in 1919.

Early life and naval career

Horthy entered the Austro-Hungarian Naval Academy at Fiume at age fourteen and rose steadily through the ranks. During the First World War he distinguished himself at the Battle of the Strait of Otranto in 1917 and was appointed commander-in-chief of the fleet in 1918, the last to hold that post.

Regency

Elected regent in March 1920, Horthy ruled a kingdom without a king under the Treaty of Trianon, which had stripped Hungary of much of its territory. His regime was conservative and authoritarian, passing anti-Jewish laws in the late 1930s and aligning Hungary with Nazi Germany in hopes of regaining lost lands. Hungary joined the Axis invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941.

In October 1944, after Horthy attempted to negotiate an armistice with the Soviet Union, Germany forced his resignation and installed the Arrow Cross regime. He spent the postwar years in exile in Portugal, where he wrote his memoirs and died in 1957. He was reburied in Hungary in 1993.
