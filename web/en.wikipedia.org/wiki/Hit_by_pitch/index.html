Hit by pitch

In baseball, a hit by pitch (HBP) is an event in which a batter or his clothing or equipment (other than his bat) is struck directly by a pitch from the pitcher; the batter is then entitled to take first base without being put out, provided he does not swing and, in modern rules, makes an attempt to avoid being hit.

Rules

Under the Official Baseball Rules, if the pitch is in the strike zone, or the batter swings, the pitch is simply a strike. Otherwise the ball is dead, the batter is awarded first base, and other runners advance only if forced. A hit by pitch is not counted as a hit or an at-bat, but counts as a plate appearance and for on-base percentage. The rule was introduced in the National League in 1887.

Tactics and danger

Some batters deliberately crowd the plate or lean into pitches to reach base; Craig Biggio, who was hit 285 times, second only to Hughie Jennings's 287, was famous for not moving out of the way. Hit by pitches can cause serious injury: Ray Chapman of Cleveland was killed by a pitch from Carl Mays in 1920, the only on-field fatality in Major League history, an event that sped the adoption of clean balls and, eventually, batting helmets. Intentional "brushback" or "beanball" pitches can provoke warnings, ejections and brawls and are policed by umpires.

Records

Hughie Jennings holds the career record with 287 HBPs, and also the single-season record of 51 (1896). The modern single-season record is 50, set by Ron Hunt in 1971. Don Baylor was hit 267 times and Frank Robinson 198 times. Pitchers' career records for hitting batters are led by Gus Weyhing with 277.
