Wyoming Stock Growers Association

Wyoming Stock Growers Association
Founded: 1872
Headquarters: Cheyenne, Wyoming
Type: Trade association
Industry: Cattle ranching

The Wyoming Stock Growers Association (WSGA) is an American cattle trade association founded in 1872 in Cheyenne, in what was then Wyoming Territory. It is among the oldest livestock organizations in the United States and historically wielded enormous political and economic power in Wyoming, effectively dominating territorial politics during the open-range cattle boom of the 1880s.

History

The association was organized by wealthy cattle barons, many of them absentee owners from the eastern United States and Britain, to protect their herds, standardize brands, coordinate roundups, and lobby against cattle theft. Working through its stock inspectors and brand inspection system, the WSGA policed the range, blacklisted suspected rustlers, and pressed for favorable legislation. Its influence extended into the Wyoming legislature, the governorship, and the state's congressional delegation.

Johnson County War

The association is most notorious for its role in the Johnson County War of 1892, when it hired gunmen from Texas and elsewhere to invade Johnson County and eliminate alleged rustlers and small settlers who competed with the big outfits. The invasion ended when federal troops intervened, and public backlash badly damaged the association's standing.

Modern role

Today the WSGA is a conventional industry association representing cattle producers, working on brand inspection, animal health, public lands grazing, and legislative advocacy. It publishes the Wyoming Livestock Roundup and holds an annual convention.
