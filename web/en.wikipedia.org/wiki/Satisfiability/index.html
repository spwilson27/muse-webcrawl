Satisfiability

In mathematical logic, a formula of propositional logic or predicate logic is satisfiable if it can be interpreted under an assignment or model that makes it true. A formula that is not satisfiable is unsatisfiable and is false under every assignment.

Boolean satisfiability problem

The Boolean satisfiability problem (SAT) asks whether there exists an assignment of truth values to the variables of a given Boolean formula that makes the formula evaluate to true. SAT was the first problem proven to be NP-complete, in the Cook–Levin theorem (1971). Its restricted form 3-SAT, where the formula is in conjunctive normal form with three literals per clause, is one of Karp's 21 NP-complete problems.

Satisfiability modulo theories

Satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) generalises SAT by considering formulas over richer background theories such as integer arithmetic, arrays, and bit vectors. SMT solvers such as Z3 and CVC5 are widely used in software verification, program analysis, and test-case generation.

In model theory

In first-order logic, satisfiability is closely related to validity: a sentence is valid if and only if its negation is unsatisfiable. By the completeness theorem, unsatisfiability of a first-order formula can be established syntactically, although by Church's theorem the general decision problem for first-order satisfiability is undecidable.

Solvers

Despite the worst-case exponential complexity of SAT, modern conflict-driven clause-learning solvers routinely decide industrial instances with millions of variables, powering hardware verification, planning, and cryptanalysis.
