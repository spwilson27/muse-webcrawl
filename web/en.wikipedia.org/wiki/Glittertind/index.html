Glittertind (official form on maps: Glittertinden) is the second-highest mountain in Norway and in Scandinavia. It lies in the municipalities of Lom and Vågå in Innlandet county, within the Jotunheimen mountain area. For much of the twentieth century, the ice cap on its summit made it higher than Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m), but the glacier has shrunk, and its rock summit is about 2,449 m above sea level; including the remaining ice, the summit reaches about 2,452 m.

Geography and climbing

The mountain rises above the Glittertind glacier, between the valleys of Visdalen and Veodalen. The normal route from the Spiterstulen lodge in Visdalen is a popular though long hike, commonly done on skis in spring and on foot in summer; the summit offers wide views across Jotunheimen. The first recorded ascent was made in 1841 by Harald Nicolai Storm Wergeland and Hans Sletten.

Climate significance

The shrinking of Glittertind's summit ice cap, which has lost several metres of thickness since the 1960s, is frequently cited as a visible example of glacier retreat in Norway attributed to climate warming.
