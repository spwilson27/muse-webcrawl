Within Our Gates is a 1920 American silent film by the director Oscar Micheaux. It is the earliest surviving "race film" made by an African-American director. The film portrays the contemporary racial situation in the United States during the Jim Crow era, the years of the Great Migration of African Americans from the agricultural South to the industrial North, and the era of lynching. It was produced at a time when the Ku Klux Klan was resurgent following the success of D. W. Griffith's The Birth of a Nation (1915), which depicted the Klan as heroic; Micheaux's film is often regarded as a direct response to Griffith's.

Plot

The film follows Sylvia Landry (Evelyn Preer), a young African-American schoolteacher from the South who travels to Boston to raise funds for a rural school for Black children in the fictional town of Piney Woods. Rebuffed by a wealthy white philanthropist whose secretary opposes education for Black people, Sylvia is helped by a Black doctor, Dr. V. Vivian, with whom she falls in love. In an extended flashback, Sylvia recounts her family history: her adoptive parents, tenant farmers, were lynched by a white mob after her father was falsely accused of murdering a white landowner, and Sylvia herself narrowly escaped sexual assault. The flashback also reveals that Sylvia is the daughter of the wealthy white Gridlestone family, whose patriarch had supported her education. In the present, Dr. Vivian encourages Sylvia to remain in the United States and work for racial uplift.

Production and censorship

Micheaux produced the film through his own company, touring with prints to Black audiences across the country. The film's depiction of lynching and attempted rape provoked concern among censors: the Chicago Board of Censors initially refused it a permit, fearing it would incite further unrest after the Chicago race riot of 1919, and Micheaux had to campaign and make cuts before it was shown, with the support of prominent Black leaders. Copies of the film were thought lost for decades, until a print, titled La Negra ("The Black Woman"), was found in Spain in the 1970s. The Library of Congress restored the film from that print, reconstructing intertitles from the script and contemporary materials.

Legacy

In 1992, Within Our Gates was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." Film historians value the film as a remarkably direct depiction of racial violence and as evidence of the ambitions and complexity of early Black independent cinema.
