The United Federation of Teachers (UFT) is the labor union representing most teachers and other professional staff in the New York City public school system. It is Local 2 of the American Federation of Teachers and also affiliated with New York State United Teachers (NYSUT).

History

The UFT was founded in 1960 through the merger of the Teachers Guild with other teacher groups, and won its first collective bargaining election in 1961. Under its first president, Charles Cogen, and then Albert Shanker (president from 1964), the union conducted major strikes in 1967 and 1968 — the latter over the community-control experiment in the Ocean Hill–Brownsville district of Brooklyn, a defining episode in the city's racial and labor politics. Later presidents included Sandra Feldman and Randi Weingarten, who went on to lead the AFT. Michael Mulgrew became president in 2009.

Activities

In addition to collective bargaining for teachers, paraprofessionals, school nurses, and other titles, the UFT operates programs such as the Teacher Center for professional development and the Dial-A-Teacher homework help line.
