Mouflon

Mouflon
Scientific nameOvis gmelini (European mouflon often Ovis aries musimon)
FamilyBovidae
Native rangeSouthwest Asia; Corsica and Sardinia
DietHerbivore — grasses, shrubs

The mouflon is a wild sheep native to the Caspian region from eastern Turkey and Armenia to Iran, and — through early human introduction — to the Mediterranean islands of Corsica and Sardinia. It is one of the two animals from which modern domestic sheep are descended, the other being the urial. Rams carry large, curling horns; ewes of some populations are hornless or have small horns.

Taxonomy and distribution

The mouflon is usually classified as Ovis gmelini, with the Cypriot mouflon among its subspecies. The European mouflon of Corsica and Sardinia descends from very early domestic sheep that became feral on the islands thousands of years ago, and is often treated as a subspecies of the domestic sheep, Ovis aries musimon. Mouflon have been introduced for hunting across much of continental Europe — including Spain, France, Germany, and the Czech Republic — and to places such as Hawaii and parts of the United States.

Behaviour

Mouflon inhabit rugged, mountainous terrain. They live in sexually segregated groups outside the autumn rut, when mature rams clash, charging and striking horns with an audible crack. Their keen eyesight and wariness make them a prized and challenging game animal.

Conservation

The Cypriot mouflon, the national animal of Cyprus (it appears on Cypriot euro coins), is protected after nearly being hunted to extinction in the 20th century. In their native Asiatic range, populations are under pressure from poaching and competition with livestock.
