Vinca major

Vinca major, with the common names bigleaf periwinkle, greater periwinkle, and blue periwinkle, is a species of flowering plant in the family Apocynaceae, native to the western Mediterranean region. It is an evergreen perennial with trailing stems that root at the nodes, widely grown as a groundcover.

Description

Vinca major is a trailing vine growing to about 25 cm tall and spreading indefinitely. The glossy, dark green leaves are broadly ovate to heart-shaped, 3–9 cm long, with a hairy margin, borne in opposite pairs on arching stems. The flowers, produced mainly from early spring to autumn, are violet-purple (occasionally white in cultivars) and about 3–5 cm in diameter, with five petals. It resembles the smaller Vinca minor, from which it differs by its larger, hairy-margined leaves and larger flowers.

Cultivation and ecology

The species tolerates deep shade and poor soils and is used to cover banks and shaded areas; variegated cultivars such as 'Variegata' are popular. It has been introduced to many temperate regions worldwide, including North America, Australia, and New Zealand, where it has become an invasive weed of woodlands and streamsides, smothering native undergrowth. Like other periwinkles, the plant contains alkaloids and is toxic if ingested.
