Harald Greycloak

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harald Greycloak (Old Norse: Haraldr gráfeldr; c. 935 – c. 970) was King of Norway from about 961 until his death around 970. He was a son of Eric Bloodaxe and Gunnhild, and belonged to the Fairhair dynasty. His byname "Greycloak" is said to derive from an incident involving the trade in grey cloaks or furs with Icelandic merchants.

Reign

After the death of King Haakon the Good, Harald and his brothers, backed by their uncle Harald Bluetooth of Denmark, seized power in Norway. Harald eliminated rival petty kings, including Tryggve Olafsson and Gudrød Bjørnsson, consolidating his rule. As a Christian king ruling a largely pagan population, he is remembered in the sagas for destroying pagan temples, which made him unpopular.

Death

Around 970 Harald was lured to Denmark, ostensibly to receive support, and was killed in an ambush at Hals in the Limfjord, in a plot associated with Haakon Sigurdsson, who then took control of Norway under Danish overlordship. The main sources for Harald's life are the kings' sagas, including Heimskringla.
