Punta

Punta is an Afro-indigenous dance and cultural music tradition of the Garifuna people of the Caribbean coasts of Belize, Honduras, Guatemala, and Nicaragua. In December 2001, punta — together with Garifuna language and dance generally — was proclaimed by UNESCO as part of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

Characteristics

Punta is traditionally performed at wakes, funerals, and ancestral celebrations. Dancers move their hips in a fast circular motion while keeping the upper body relatively still, accompanied by call-and-response singing, mostly by women, over polyrhythmic drumming on primero (lead) and segunda (bass) drums, with shakas (maracas), conch shells, and turtle shells struck as percussion. The songs, generally in the Garifuna language, comment on daily life, history, and moral themes.

Punta rock

In the late 1970s the Belizean artist Pen Cayetano and the Turtle Shell Band electrified punta, fusing it with calypso, soca, and rock instrumentation to create punta rock. The new genre spread through the Garifuna diaspora and the wider Caribbean, producing stars such as Andy Palacio, whose 2007 album Wátina won international acclaim, and bands such as Honduras's Banda Blanca, whose "Sopa de Caracol" became a huge Latin hit in 1991.

Cultural significance

Punta is a central marker of Garifuna identity, preserving the language and collective memory of a people descended from West African, Arawak, and Island Carib ancestors who resisted enslavement on St. Vincent and were exiled to Central America in 1797.
