Esau

Parents: Isaac and Rebecca
Siblings: Jacob (twin brother)
Spouses: Judith, Basemath, Mahalath (Adah and Aholibamah in another account)
Children: Eliphaz and others; ancestor of the Edomites
Other names: Edom ("red")

Esau (Hebrew: עֵשָׂו, ʿĒśāv) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the elder twin son of Isaac and Rebecca and the twin brother of Jacob. He is described as a skilful hunter and a man of the field, in contrast to Jacob, a quiet man dwelling in tents. Esau is regarded as the progenitor of the Edomites, the people of Edom (also called Seir), south-east of the Dead Sea.

Birth and the birthright

According to the Book of Genesis, Esau was born first, emerging red and hairy, and so was called Esau and also Edom ("red"). As the firstborn he was entitled to the birthright — the double portion and leadership of the family. Returning famished from hunting one day, he sold his birthright to Jacob for bread and a stew of red lentils, an act for which he was said to have "despised his birthright" (Genesis 25).

The stolen blessing

When Isaac was old and nearly blind, he called Esau to hunt game and prepare a meal so that he might bless him before his death. Rebecca, favouring Jacob, had Jacob disguise himself with goat skins to imitate his brother's hairy arms and bring food first. Isaac, deceived, gave Jacob the blessing of dominion. Esau's anguished cry — "Have you only one blessing, my father? Bless me, even me also, O my father" — is one of the most famous pleas in Genesis. Esau hated Jacob and planned to kill him after Isaac's death, prompting Jacob's flight to Haran.

Reconciliation and descendants

Years later the brothers met again; after Jacob's wrestling at the Jabbok, Esau ran to meet him, embraced him, and kissed him — a reconciliation scene read in different ways by later interpreters. Esau settled in Mount Seir, and his descendants are listed in Genesis 36 as the chiefs and kings of Edom. He married women including Judith and Basemath, daughters of Hittite/Hivite clans, and Mahalath, a daughter of Ishmael.

In later tradition

In rabbinic tradition Esau became an ambivalent and often negative figure, associated with Rome and, in medieval times, with Christendom; the Malachi prophecy "Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated" is quoted in the New Testament (Romans 9). In Islam, Esau is known as ʿĪṣ (عيص) and is mentioned as an ancestor of the Romans (Rûm).
