Berlin: Symphony of a Great City

Ruttmann's film follows Berlin through a single day, from dawn trains to nightlife.

Berlin: Symphony of a Great City (German: Berlin: Die Sinfonie der Großstadt) is a 1927 German silent film directed by Walter Ruttmann. A landmark of the "city symphony" genre, it portrays a day in the life of Berlin through rhythmic montage rather than narrative, and remains one of the most celebrated documentary-style films of the Weimar era.

Production

The film grew from an idea by Carl Mayer, the screenwriter of The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, who proposed composing a "melody of images" of the metropolis. Ruttmann, an avant-garde artist who had made abstract animated films, directed, while the celebrated cinematographer Karl Freund shot much of the footage with hidden and lightweight cameras to capture unposed street life. Edmund Meisel composed the original orchestral score, performed live at the premiere at Berlin's Tauentzienpalast on 23 September 1927.

Structure and content

Divided into five acts corresponding to the phases of a day, the film opens with a train racing toward the sleeping city and empty dawn streets, then accelerates into the morning rush: workers, trams, machines, shop shutters, schoolchildren and stock-exchange telephones. Midday brings lunch breaks and zoo animals; afternoon turns to sport, shopping and leisure; evening closes with theatres, neon signs, dance halls and fireworks. Ruttmann cross-cuts humans, animals and machines into visual rhymes — marching legs with cattle, typewriters with factory pistons — creating a portrait of the modern city as a single organism, influenced by Soviet montage and Dziga Vertov's earlier experiments.

Reception and legacy

The film was praised for its virtuoso editing and attacked by some critics, including Siegfried Kracauer, for aestheticising the city while glossing over social misery. It inspired a wave of city symphonies in Europe and America and is now regarded as both a supreme work of montage cinema and an invaluable record of Weimar Berlin before its destruction in the Second World War.
