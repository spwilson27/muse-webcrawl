Life

James Hargreaves (c. 1720 – 22 April 1778) was an English carpenter and weaver of Stanhill, Lancashire, who invented the spinning jenny, one of the key inventions of the early Industrial Revolution. Largely illiterate, he worked as a carpenter and hand-loom weaver, and is said to have conceived the jenny after seeing a spinning wheel overturned, its spindle continuing to rotate upright — suggesting several spindles could be driven vertically at once.

The spinning jenny

By 1764 Hargreaves had built a frame spinning yarn on multiple spindles simultaneously — eight at first, soon sixteen, and eventually over a hundred — multiplying a single worker's output of cotton weft. Named, by tradition, after his daughter (or wife) Jenny, the machine's rovings were drawn out by a moving carriage while the spindles twisted and wound the thread. In 1768 a mob of hand spinners, fearing for their livelihoods, broke into Hargreaves' house and smashed his jennies; he moved to Nottingham, where he patented the jenny in 1770 and built a small mill in partnership.

Later years

Hargreaves prospered modestly but not spectacularly from his invention, spending his later years defending his patent against widespread infringement (the courts and a manufacturers' settlement brought him some £3,000–4,000). He died in Nottingham in 1778. The jenny, combined with Arkwright's water frame and Crompton's mule (itself a jenny-frame hybrid), mechanised cotton spinning and underpinned the factory system.
