Crunchyroll is an American subscription video on-demand over-the-top streaming service owned by Sony, focused on the streaming of East Asian media, principally Japanese anime. Founded in 2006, it grew from a video-sharing site hosting user-uploaded fansubbed content into the world's largest licensed anime streaming platform, offering thousands of episodes and films, often subtitled within hours of Japanese broadcast ("simulcast").

Since 2021 Crunchyroll has been operated by Crunchyroll, LLC, a joint venture between Sony Pictures Entertainment and Sony Music Entertainment Japan's Aniplex. Sony also folded rival service Funimation into Crunchyroll in 2022, consolidating most of the Western anime streaming market under the Crunchyroll brand.

History

Crunchyroll began in 2006 as a for-profit video site where users uploaded Asian media, including unlicensed fansubbed anime. Venture funding from Venrock (2008) prompted a pivot: Crunchyroll removed infringing content and struck its first licensing deal for Naruto Shippuden with TV Tokyo in 2008–2009, becoming a legitimate distributor. The Chernin Group acquired a majority stake in 2013, and in 2014 Crunchyroll joined the Otter Media joint venture (AT&T and Chernin), which later became part of WarnerMedia. AT&T agreed in December 2020 to sell Crunchyroll to Sony's Funimation for about $1.175 billion; the deal closed in August 2021. In March 2022, Sony announced Funimation's catalogue would move to Crunchyroll, and the Funimation service was sunset in April 2024.

Services and content

Crunchyroll offers free ad-supported streaming and premium tiers providing ad-free viewing, offline downloads, and simultaneous streams. Its flagship offering is simulcasting — new episodes available professionally subtitled shortly after Japanese broadcast — plus dubs produced after broadcast, a large back catalogue licensed from Japanese studios and publishers, manga (Crunchyroll Manga), games publishing (Crunchyroll Games), merchandise through its store, and original co-productions and "Crunchyroll Originals" such as Tower of God, The God of High School, and Solo Leveling (the latter a major award winner).

Business and industry role

Crunchyroll is often credited with creating the legal streaming market for anime outside Japan and with pioneering simulcast licensing, which compressed the window exploited by piracy sites. Revenue is shared with Japanese licensors through licensing fees and royalties. Its scale — over 13–15 million paying subscribers by 2024–2025 and services in more than 200 countries and territories — made it a central gatekeeper of global anime distribution, raising industry debate about consolidation under Sony, which also owns the Aniplex production studio and distribution arms.

Events and awards

The annual Crunchyroll Anime Awards, first held in 2017, are fan-voted and judged awards for anime of the year and categories such as best animation, direction, and voice performance, staged in Tokyo in recent years. The company also runs the Crunchyroll Expo (2017–2022) and exhibits widely at anime conventions. Its mascot is "Crunchyroll Hime."
