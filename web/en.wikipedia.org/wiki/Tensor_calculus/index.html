Tensor calculus

Tensor calculus (the Ricci calculus, or absolute differential calculus) is the extension of vector calculus to tensor fields — quantities that vary over a manifold and transform under coordinate changes according to definite rules. Developed by Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and his student Tullio Levi-Civita around 1890–1900, it became famous as the mathematical language of Einstein's general theory of relativity (1915).

Notation

Tensor calculus uses index notation: contravariant components carry upper indices, covariant components lower indices, and repeated indices in a product are summed (Einstein summation convention). Operations include contraction, raising and lowering indices with the metric tensor, and the covariant derivative, which generalises partial differentiation to curved spaces via Christoffel symbols.

Key objects

Central constructions include the Riemann curvature tensor, its contractions the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature, and the metric tensor itself. Tensor densities and the Levi-Civita symbol extend the formalism to volume and orientation.

Applications

Beyond relativity, tensor calculus is fundamental in continuum mechanics (stress and strain tensors), differential geometry, and modern machine learning, where multi-dimensional arrays are loosely called tensors.
