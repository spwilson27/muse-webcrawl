Positional asphyxia

Positional asphyxia, also known as postural asphyxia, is a form of asphyxia which occurs when someone's position prevents them from breathing adequately. Deaths may also occur in people who are under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or who are otherwise unable to reposition themselves out of dangerous positions.

Mechanism

Positional asphyxia occurs when the position of the body interferes with the mechanics of respiration. Common mechanisms include compression of the chest wall, obstruction of the airway by neck flexion, or inverted positions in which the weight of the abdominal contents presses against the diaphragm. Because breathing requires continuous muscular effort, a restrained or incapacitated person may be unable to maintain adequate ventilation and can die within minutes.

Restraint and custody deaths

The concept is frequently discussed in forensic pathology in connection with deaths in police custody. Prone restraint, particularly when a person is handcuffed face down with pressure applied to the back (sometimes called hog-tying or the maximal restraint position), has been associated with sudden deaths. Contributing factors identified in the research include obesity, stimulant intoxication (such as cocaine or methamphetamine), alcohol intoxication, and an underlying heart condition. The extent to which restraint position itself is causative remains debated among medical examiners.

Other contexts

Positional asphyxia is also a recognized risk for infants placed in car seats or inclined sleepers for prolonged periods, and for intoxicated adults who collapse into positions that obstruct the airway. It has additionally been reported in industrial and entrapment accidents.
