Hertzsprung–Russell diagram

The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (abbreviated as H–R diagram, HR diagram or HRD) is a scatter plot of stars showing the relationship between the stars' absolute magnitudes or luminosities versus their stellar classifications or effective temperatures. The diagram was created independently in 1911 by Ejnar Hertzsprung and by Henry Norris Russell in 1913, and represented a major step towards an understanding of stellar evolution.

Features

Most stars occupy a diagonal band called the main sequence, running from hot, luminous stars at the upper left to cool, dim stars at the lower right. Above the main sequence lie the giants and supergiants; below it lie the white dwarfs. Gaps such as the Hertzsprung gap appear between the main sequence and the giant branch.

Interpretation

The diagram allows astronomers to infer a star's evolutionary state. Stars spend most of their lifetimes on the main sequence fusing hydrogen; their position there is determined mainly by mass. Clusters of stars, whose members share an age, trace characteristic patterns whose main-sequence turn-off point reveals the cluster's age.
