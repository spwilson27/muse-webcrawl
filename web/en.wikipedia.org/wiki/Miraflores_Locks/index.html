Miraflores Locks

The Miraflores Locks (Spanish: Esclusas de Miraflores) are one of three sets of locks in the Panama Canal, located on the Pacific side near Panama City. Completed in 1913 and opened with the canal in 1914, they lift or lower ships 16.5 metres (54 feet) in two steps between sea level and the level of Miraflores Lake.

Operation

The locks consist of two parallel flights, each with two chambers, allowing simultaneous two-way traffic. Each chamber is about 33.5 metres (110 feet) wide and 300 metres (1,000 feet) long. Ships are guided through the chambers by electric locomotives known as "mules" running on tracks along the lock walls. Water flows by gravity through culverts to raise or lower vessels.

Visitor center

The Miraflores Visitor Center, opened in 2003, offers observation decks, exhibits and a museum explaining the canal's history and operation, and is one of Panama's most popular tourist attractions. The locks were complemented in 2016 by the much larger Cocolí locks of the expanded canal nearby.
