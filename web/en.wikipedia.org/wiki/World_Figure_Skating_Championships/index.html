The World Championships are the most prestigious annual event in figure skating.

The World Figure Skating Championships is an annual figure skating competition sanctioned by the International Skating Union (ISU), in which elite skaters compete for the title of World Champion in men's singles, women's singles, pair skating and ice dance. Together with the Olympic competition, it is regarded as the most prestigious event in the sport, and it determines many of the entry quotas for the following year's championships and Olympics.

History

The first championships were held in St. Petersburg in 1896 and were open only to men; Gilbert Fuchs of the German Empire won the inaugural title. Women's competition began in 1906 in Davos, after Madge Syers had controversially entered — and placed second in — the open event of 1902, prompting the ISU to create a separate women's championship. Pairs were added in 1908 and ice dance in 1952. The championships were interrupted by the two world wars and cancelled in 1961 after the entire United States team was killed in the crash of Sabena Flight 548 en route to Prague; the 2020 edition in Montreal was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic. After the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, skaters from Russia and Belarus were excluded from ISU competition.

Format and qualification

Skaters qualify through their national federations, with the number of entries per country in each discipline (one to three) earned by placements at the previous year's championships. Since the adoption of the ISU Judging System in 2004, performances are scored on technical elements and program components across a short program (or rhythm dance) and a free skate (or free dance); the highest combined score wins. Only skaters meeting minimum technical scores and age requirements may compete.

Notable champions

Sweden's Ulrich Salchow won a record ten men's titles in the early 1900s. Norway's Sonja Henie likewise won ten consecutive women's titles (1927–1936) before becoming a film star. Other dominant champions include Katarina Witt of East Germany, Michelle Kwan of the United States, Irina Rodnina of the Soviet Union in pairs, and in the modern era Japan's Yuzuru Hanyu and ice dancers Gabriella Papadakis and Guillaume Cizeron of France. American skater Ilia Malinin, the first to land a quadruple Axel in competition, won the men's title in 2024 and 2025.
