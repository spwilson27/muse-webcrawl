Net metering

TypeElectricity billing mechanism
Used forDistributed generation, esp. rooftop solar
RelatedFeed-in tariff, smart grid

Net metering (also known as net energy metering or NEM) is an electricity billing mechanism that allows consumers who generate some or all of their own electricity to use that electricity at any time, rather than only when it is generated. Excess generation is exported to the grid and credited against electricity consumed at other times.

How it works

A bidirectional meter records electricity flowing in both directions. When a customer's solar panels produce more than the premises consume, the surplus flows to the distribution network and the meter effectively runs backwards or records a credit. At night or on cloudy days the customer draws from the grid, offsetting earlier credits, typically over a monthly billing period.

Policy and debate

Net metering policies vary widely by country and state. Supporters argue it encourages renewable energy, reduces peak demand and defers grid investment. Utilities and critics argue that full retail-rate credits shift fixed grid costs onto non-solar customers. Reforms in places such as California (NEM 3.0, 2023) and Hawaii have moved toward net billing, valuing exports at avoided-cost or time-varying rates rather than the full retail rate.

Variants

Variants include time-of-use net metering, virtual net metering for multi-tenant buildings, and aggregate metering for farms and campuses. Net metering is distinct from a feed-in tariff, under which all generation is sold at a set price.
