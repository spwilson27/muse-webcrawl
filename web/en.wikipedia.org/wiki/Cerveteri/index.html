Cerveteri is a town in the Metropolitan City of Rome, Lazio, Italy, known in antiquity as Caere, one of the most powerful cities of the Etruscan civilization. It is famed for its vast necropolises, above all the Banditaccia necropolis, whose rock-cut tombs imitate Etruscan houses and streets; together with Tarquinia it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004.

History

Caere was a wealthy maritime power, trading with Greeks and Phoenicians and connected to the sea by its port of Pyrgi, where bilingual gold tablets in Etruscan and Phoenician were found. The city was an ally of Rome in early conflicts — it sheltered Roman priests and sacred objects during the Gallic sack of Rome in 390 BC — but later rebelled and was subdued, becoming a Roman municipium.

Archaeology

Excavations have yielded masterpieces of Etruscan art including the Sarcophagus of the Spouses (now in the Louvre and the Villa Giulia), the Regolini-Galassi tomb treasures, and painted and relief-decorated tombs such as the Tomb of the Reliefs. Much material from Cerveteri is displayed in the Museo Nazionale Cerite in the town itself.
