The Emirate of Umm Al Quwain (Arabic: أم القيوين, "Mother of Two Powers") is the second-smallest and least populous of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates. It lies on the Persian Gulf coast between the emirates of Sharjah and Ras Al Khaimah, and its capital is the city of Umm Al Quwain.

History

The Al Mualla (Al Ali) family founded the modern emirate in the 18th century, building Umm Al Quwain Fort in 1768. The emirate signed the General Maritime Treaty with Britain in 1820 and later became one of the Trucial States, joining the UAE in 1971. Important archaeological sites include Ed-Dur and Tell Abraq, which demonstrate the region's ancient trading links with Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley.

Economy and attractions

Traditional activities include fishing, dhow building and date farming. Modern attractions include the Dreamland Aqua Park, one of the largest water parks in the region, the mangrove-fringed Khor Al Beidah lagoon—a noted birdwatching site—and the offshore island of Al Siniyah.
