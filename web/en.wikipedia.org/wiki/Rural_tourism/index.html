Rural tourism
ScopeTourism in rural and countryside areas
FormsAgritourism, farm stays, village tourism, eco-tourism, heritage tourism, wine tourism
Key featuresLow density, locally owned businesses, landscape and tradition based experiences

Rural tourism focuses on actively participating in a rural lifestyle. It can be a variant of ecotourism in which visitors travel to rural areas to experience agriculture, village life, crafts, food traditions, festivals, and landscapes away from urban centres. Many villages can facilitate tourism because villagers are hospitable and eager to welcome or host visitors, and because tourism provides income that supports rural communities and helps maintain buildings, crafts, and farmland that might otherwise be abandoned.

Definition and forms

Rural tourism is generally defined as tourism taking place in the countryside, characterised by low population density, small-scale and often family-owned accommodation, and experiences based on the natural environment, agriculture, heritage, and rural ways of life. Common forms include:

  • Agritourism — visits to working farms, harvest participation, and farm stays
  • Village tourism — lodging in villages, homestays, and participation in local festivals and crafts
  • Wine and food tourism — vineyard routes, cheese and olive-oil trails, farmers' markets
  • Nature and activity tourism — hiking, cycling, horse riding, fishing, and birdwatching
  • Heritage tourism — historic villages, vernacular architecture, museums of rural life

Benefits and challenges

Rural tourism can diversify farm incomes, create employment — particularly for women and young people — and give economic value to landscape conservation and traditional building maintenance. It is promoted by many governments and by the European Union's rural development programmes as a tool against rural depopulation.

Challenges include seasonality, the cost of infrastructure in remote areas, variable service quality, pressure on fragile environments, and the risk that tourism displaces the agricultural and communal life that attracted visitors in the first place. Community involvement in planning is widely regarded as essential to balancing benefits and costs.

By country

In France, the Gîtes de France network (founded 1951) institutionalised farm and village lodging. Italy's agriturismo sector is regulated by law and links accommodation to working farms. Spain developed networks of casas rurales and heritage village hotels (Paradores in historic buildings). In India, the Ministry of Tourism has promoted rural tourism clusters and homestays showcasing crafts and village life. The World Tourism Organization (UN Tourism) promotes a "Best Tourism Villages" initiative recognising villages committed to sustainable, community-based tourism.
