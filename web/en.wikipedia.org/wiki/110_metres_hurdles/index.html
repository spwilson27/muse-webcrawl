110 metres hurdles

The 110 metres hurdles is a track and field hurdling event run by men (the corresponding women's event is the 100 metres hurdles). The race covers 110 metres with ten hurdles 42 inches (1.067 m) high. The first hurdle is placed 13.72 metres (15 yards) from the start, the hurdles are spaced 9.14 metres (10 yards) apart, and the run-in from the last hurdle to the finish is 14.02 metres.

Sport: Athletics

Category: Hurdles / sprint

Olympic Games: Since 1896

History

Hurdling over 120 yards was contested in England from the 1830s, and the event was included in the first modern Olympic Games at Athens in 1896, converted to the metric 110 metres. Early hurdles were heavy wooden barriers; the modern L-shaped hurdle, designed to tip forward when struck, was introduced in the 1930s, greatly reducing the penalty for hitting a barrier. The event has been dominated historically by athletes from the United States.

Technique

Sprinters take eight strides to the first hurdle and three strides between hurdles, attacking the barrier with the lead leg extended and the trail leg snapped through horizontally. Because the hurdles are higher and the spacing tighter relative to stride length than in the women's event, rhythm and minimal vertical displacement are crucial. Knocking over hurdles is permitted provided it is not deliberate and the athlete does not trail a leg outside a hurdle.

Records and leading athletes

The world record is 12.80 seconds, set by Aries Merritt of the United States in Brussels on 7 September 2012. The record had previously stood at 12.87 (Dayron Robles, 2008) and before that at 12.88, held jointly by Liu Xiang, the 2004 Olympic champion. Other greats of the event include Allen Johnson, Colin Jackson (whose 12.91 stood as the world record from 1993 to 2006), Roger Kingdom, Renaldo Nehemiah and Grant Holloway, a multiple world champion and the 2024 Olympic gold medallist.
