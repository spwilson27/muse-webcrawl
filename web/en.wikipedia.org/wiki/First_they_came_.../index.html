First they came ...

AuthorMartin Niemöller
LanguageGerman
Date1946 (first published versions)
SubjectComplicity and silence under Nazism

"First they came ..." is the opening phrase of a famous confession in prose-poem form by the German Lutheran pastor Martin Niemöller (1892–1984). It reflects on the cowardice of German intellectuals and clergy who failed to resist the Nazis as they purged group after group.

Text

Versions differ in the order and identity of the groups named, as Niemöller improvised the passage in speeches after the Second World War. One widely quoted version reads:

First they came for the socialists, and I did not speak out — because I was not a socialist.
Then they came for the trade unionists, and I did not speak out — because I was not a trade unionist.
Then they came for the Jews, and I did not speak out — because I was not a Jew.
Then they came for me — and there was no one left to speak for me.

Background

Niemöller had initially welcomed aspects of the Nazi rise to power before becoming a leader of the Confessing Church and spending 1937–1945 in concentration camps, including Sachsenhausen and Dachau. After the war he became a leading voice of German repentance and disarmament. The text is displayed at numerous Holocaust memorials, including the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum and Yad Vashem.
