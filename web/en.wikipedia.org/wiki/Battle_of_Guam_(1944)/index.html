Battle of Guam (1944)

The Second Battle of Guam (21 July – 10 August 1944) was the American recapture of the Japanese-held island of Guam, a U.S. territory in the Mariana Islands captured by Japan in 1941. It was fought during the Pacific War of World War II.

Background

Guam, the largest of the Marianas, had been seized by Japan days after Pearl Harbor. For its recapture as part of Operation Forager, the United States assembled the III Amphibious Corps under Major General Roy Geiger, comprising the 3rd Marine Division, the 1st Provisional Marine Brigade, and the Army's 77th Infantry Division — some 55,000 troops — against a Japanese garrison of about 18,500 under Lieutenant General Takeshi Takashina. The invasion was preceded by weeks of naval bombardment, the longest of the Pacific war to that point.

Battle

Landings took place on 21 July 1944 on either side of the Orote Peninsula. Japanese counterattacks, including a major night attack on 25–26 July, were defeated with heavy losses. Takashina was killed on 28 July. U.S. forces drove north, liberating the capital, Agana, and on 10 August organised resistance was declared ended, though Japanese holdouts remained in the jungle for years — the last, Sergeant Shoichi Yokoi, was found in 1972. American casualties were about 1,750 killed; nearly the entire Japanese garrison died.
