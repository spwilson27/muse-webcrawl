Amphiphile

An amphiphile (from Greek amphis, both, and philia, love), or amphipathic compound, is a chemical compound possessing both hydrophilic (water-loving, polar) and lipophilic (fat-loving, nonpolar) properties. Such molecules self-assemble in water into structures such as micelles, bilayers and vesicles.

ExamplesSoaps, detergents, phospholipids
Structures formedMicelles, liposomes, lipid bilayers

Structure and behaviour

A typical amphiphile has a polar head group and a hydrocarbon tail. Above the critical micelle concentration, amphiphiles aggregate so tails avoid water while heads remain exposed. In biology, phospholipids form the double-layer membranes of cells.

Examples and uses

Soaps and detergents clean by emulsifying oils. Lecithin is a food emulsifier. Amphiphilic polymers and peptides are studied for drug delivery, including liposomal medicines, and for antimicrobial action.
