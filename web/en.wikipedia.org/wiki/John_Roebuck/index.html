John Roebuck

John Roebuck of Kinneil FRS (1718 – 17 July 1794) was an English industrialist, inventor and physician who played an important role in the early Industrial Revolution. He is best known for devising the lead chamber process for the manufacture of sulphuric acid and for financing James Watt's early work on the steam engine.

Sulphuric acid

Roebuck studied medicine at Edinburgh and Leiden and practised in Birmingham. In 1746 he introduced the lead chamber process, in which sulphur and nitre were burned in large lead-lined chambers, dramatically reducing the cost of sulphuric acid — a key industrial chemical. He established a works at Prestonpans in Scotland.

Iron and coal

With Samuel Garbett and William Cadell, Roebuck founded the Carron Company ironworks near Falkirk in 1759, which became famous for its carronade cannon. He leased coal mines at Bo'ness and undertook ambitious harbour works to supply them.

Watt partnership

In 1768 Roebuck took a two-thirds share in James Watt's patent for the separate-condenser steam engine and financed its development. Financial difficulties forced Roebuck to relinquish his share to Matthew Boulton in 1773, just before the engine's commercial success; Watt and Boulton compensated him with an annuity.
