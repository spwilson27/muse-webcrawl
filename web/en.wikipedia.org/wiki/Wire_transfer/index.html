Wire transfer

A wire transfer, or bank wire, is a method of electronic funds transfer from one person or institution to another, typically between bank accounts. Wire transfers are usually fast — often settled the same business day — and are considered secure, but they are generally irrevocable once sent, which makes them a target for fraud.

Systems

In the United States, domestic transfers use the Federal Reserve's Fedwire system, while large-value transfers between banks may also clear through CHIPS. Internationally, most wire instructions are communicated over the SWIFT network of standardized messages between banks, with settlement occurring through correspondent (nostro/vostro) accounts. Within Europe, SEPA transfers provide a harmonized, cheaper alternative for euro payments.

Process and costs

The sender provides the recipient's name, account number (in many countries an IBAN), and the bank's routing code or SWIFT/BIC. Banks typically charge fees for sending and sometimes receiving wires, and intermediary banks may deduct charges from the transferred amount in international payments. Exchange-rate margins add to the cost of cross-border wires, prompting competition from specialist remittance services.

Fraud

Because wires settle quickly and are hard to reverse, they feature in scams such as business email compromise and real-estate closing fraud, in which victims are tricked into sending funds to accounts controlled by criminals.
