Hypoxia (medicine)

Hypoxia is a condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of an adequate supply of oxygen at the tissue level. It is classified as generalized, affecting the whole body, or local, affecting a region. Hypoxia differs from hypoxemia, which refers specifically to low oxygen content in arterial blood, although the two frequently coexist.

Cyanosis is a classic — though late and unreliable — sign of hypoxia.

Classification

The classical types are hypoxemic hypoxia (low arterial oxygen, from lung disease, hypoventilation or altitude), anaemic hypoxia (reduced oxygen-carrying capacity, as in anaemia or carbon monoxide poisoning), stagnant or circulatory hypoxia (inadequate perfusion, as in shock or heart failure), and histotoxic hypoxia (cells unable to use oxygen, as in cyanide poisoning). At high altitude, the falling partial pressure of oxygen causes acute mountain sickness and, in pilots, a well-recognised impairment of judgement that makes time of useful consciousness a key safety concept.

Signs and symptoms

Generalized hypoxia produces breathlessness, tachycardia, confusion, euphoria or agitation, and, as it progresses, cyanosis, loss of consciousness and death. Chronic hypoxia causes pulmonary hypertension and secondary polycythemia. Cells adapt partly through hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF), transcription factors that increase erythropoietin and angiogenesis — the discovery of which was recognised by the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Treatment

Treatment aims to restore oxygen delivery: supplemental oxygen, airway and ventilatory support, transfusion where carrying capacity is the problem, and treatment of the underlying cause.
