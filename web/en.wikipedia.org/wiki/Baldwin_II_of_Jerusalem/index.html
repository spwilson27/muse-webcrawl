Baldwin II, also known as Baldwin of Bourcq, was count of Edessa from 1100 to 1118 and king of Jerusalem from 1118 until his death. He was a cousin of Baldwin I of Jerusalem, alongside whom he had travelled on the First Crusade.

Reign

As count of Edessa, Baldwin was captured by the Turks in 1104 and held for four years. After succeeding Baldwin I in Jerusalem, he faced continual warfare on the kingdom's northern frontier. He was captured again in 1123 by the Artuqid emir Balak and imprisoned at Kharput until ransomed in 1124. During his absence the kingdom was governed by regents, and Venetian crusaders helped capture Tyre in 1124.

Family and succession

Baldwin married Morphia of Melitene, an Armenian noblewoman, and had four daughters, the eldest of whom, Melisende, married Fulk of Anjou. Having no sons, Baldwin arranged for Melisende to succeed him jointly with Fulk, a succession that took effect on his death in 1131. During his reign the Knights Templar, founded around 1119, were headquartered in the al-Aqsa Mosque, granted to them by Baldwin.
