Illative case

The illative case (abbreviated ILL; from Latin illatus, "brought in") is a grammatical case used in some languages to indicate motion into or inside something — the English equivalent of "into the house". It is one of the locative cases, alongside the inessive ("in") and elative ("out of").

Usage

The illative is a core case in Finnic languages such as Finnish, Estonian and Karelian, and also occurs in Hungarian and Lithuanian. In Finnish it is formed with endings such as -hVn, -seen or -siin: for example, talo ("house") → taloon ("into the house"). In Hungarian the illative suffix is -ba/-be, as in házba ("into the house").

In Finnish

Finnish has three external and three internal locative cases; the illative belongs to the internal series (inessive–elative–illative: "in", "from in", "into"). Short and long illative forms exist depending on the word's stem shape and gradation.

Other languages

Illative-like forms also appear in some Samoyedic and other Uralic languages, and historically in Baltic languages. In Lithuanian the illative, still used in dialects and fixed expressions, is increasingly replaced by the preposition į with the accusative.
