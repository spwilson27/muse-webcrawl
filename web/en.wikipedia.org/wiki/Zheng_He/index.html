Zheng He

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A Ming treasure ship under sail, its nine masts towering over accompanying junks]

Zheng He (1371–1433 or 1435), born Ma He, was a Chinese Muslim eunuch, mariner and diplomat of the early Ming dynasty who commanded seven great treasure voyages (1405–1433) across the Indian Ocean. His fleets — hundreds of ships, including huge 'treasure ships', and tens of thousands of men — visited Southeast Asia, India, Arabia and East Africa, projecting Ming power, enrolling tributaries and exchanging envoys and exotica (including a giraffe presented as the auspicious qilin). After his death the voyages were discontinued and China turned away from state-sponsored oceanic expansion.

The voyages

Commissioned by the Yongle Emperor, who had usurped the throne and sought legitimacy and glory, the fleets sailed in 1405, 1407, 1409, 1413, 1417, 1421 and 1431. They fought pirates at Palembang, intervened in Ceylon and Sumatra, exchanged embassies with Bengal, Hormuz, Aden, Mogadishu and Malindi, and left trilingual inscriptions (Chinese, Tamil, Persian) at Galle. Zheng He, a Hui Muslim from Yunnan captured and castrated as a boy, rose through the Yongle prince's household to become the emperor's most trusted servant.

End and memory

Zheng He died on the seventh voyage (1433, at sea or soon after; his tomb at Nanjing is probably a cenotaph). Court factions hostile to eunuch power and expensive fleets ended the programme; the ships rotted, and records were partly suppressed — later tradition blames the official Liu Daxia for hiding the archives. Remembered in temples across Southeast Asia (as Sanbao, 'Three Treasures'), Zheng He was revived in 20th-century China as a symbol of peaceful maritime outreach, in deliberate contrast to European armed expansion.
