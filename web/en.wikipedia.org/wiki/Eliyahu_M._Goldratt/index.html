Eliyahu Moshe Goldratt (March 31, 1947 – June 11, 2011) was an Israeli business management guru and physicist. He originated the Theory of Constraints (TOC), a management philosophy centred on identifying the single limiting factor — the constraint — that stands in the way of achieving a goal, and systematically improving it. He is best known as the author of the business novel The Goal (1984), one of the best-selling management books of all time.

Life and career

Goldratt was born in the British Mandate of Palestine into a rabbinic family; his father, Avraham-Yehuda Goldrat, served as a member of the first Knesset. Goldratt studied physics, earning a BSc from Tel Aviv University and a master's and PhD from Bar-Ilan University. Drawn into manufacturing problems through work with friends running a factory, he developed production-scheduling software marketed as Optimized Production Technology (OPT) and founded the company Creative Output.

The Goal and the theory of constraints

Frustrated that managers would not read technical explanations, Goldratt presented his ideas as a novel. The Goal, co-written with Jeff Cox, follows plant manager Alex Rogo, who has ninety days to save his factory and learns — guided by a physicist named Jonah — to focus on throughput, inventory, and operational expense, and to apply the five focusing steps: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, and repeat. The book has sold millions of copies and remains required reading in many operations courses, though its commercial success initially led some academics to dismiss its ideas.

Later work

Goldratt founded the Avraham Y. Goldratt Institute (1986) to spread TOC, and extended the theory beyond manufacturing into project management — Critical Chain (1997) — distribution, marketing, and strategy. His "thinking processes", including the current reality tree and the evaporating cloud conflict-resolution diagram, aimed to apply scientific cause-and-effect logic to organisational problems. Later books include It's Not Luck (1994), The Haystack Syndrome, and the novel The Choice (2008), written with his daughter Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag. He died of complications from lung cancer in 2011.
