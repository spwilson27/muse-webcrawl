Coney Island

Coney Island is a neighborhood and entertainment area in the southwestern part of the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. It is a peninsula — formerly a true island, separated from mainland Brooklyn by Coney Island Creek, part of which has been filled in. The neighborhood is famous for its beach, boardwalk, and amusement parks.

History

The name derives from the Dutch Konijn Eiland, "Rabbit Island", for the wild rabbits once found there. Developed as a seaside resort from the mid-nineteenth century, Coney Island became world famous between about 1880 and the Second World War for its great amusement parks — Steeplechase Park, Luna Park, and Dreamland — and attractions such as the Cyclone roller coaster (1927) and the Wonder Wheel (1920). It declined after the war as families moved to the suburbs and air conditioning and automobiles changed leisure habits.

Revival

From the 2000s, city rezoning and investment brought new rides, a restored Luna Park (opened 2010), minor-league baseball at the Brooklyn Cyclones' stadium, and the continued popularity of the original Nathan's Famous hot dog stand (founded 1916) and its annual hot dog eating contest. The New York Aquarium, opened in 1896, is the oldest continuously operating aquarium in the United States. The Riegelmann Boardwalk stretches along the Atlantic beach.

Culture

Coney Island is home to the Mermaid Parade and sideshow traditions maintained by Coney Island USA, and remains an emblem of American popular amusement culture.
