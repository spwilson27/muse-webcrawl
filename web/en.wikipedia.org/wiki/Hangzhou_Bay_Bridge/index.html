The Hangzhou Bay Bridge (simplified Chinese: 杭州湾大桥; traditional Chinese: 杭州灣大橋) is a long, high-speed highway bridge with two cable-stayed portions across the mouth of Hangzhou Bay in the eastern coastal region of China. It connects the municipalities of Jiaxing and Ningbo in Zhejiang province.

Construction of the bridge was completed on 14 June 2007, and an opening ceremony was held on 26 June 2007. The bridge was not opened to public traffic until 1 May 2008, after a considerable period of testing and evaluation. At 35.673 km (22 mi) in length, it was among the longest trans-oceanic bridges in the world at the time of its completion, cutting the road distance between Ningbo and Shanghai from about 400 km to 180 km and the driving time from four hours to about two.

Design

The bridge has two main cable-stayed spans — a northern span of 448 m and a southern span of 318 m — and is designed to withstand severe conditions, including some of the world's strongest tidal bores on the Qiantang River, typhoons, and earthquakes. Midway across stands a service centre built on an offshore platform in the bay.

Related crossings

A second road link, the Jintang Bridge, and a separate Hangzhou Bay rail bridge for the Tongyu–Jiaxing–Ningbo high-speed railway, have since been built or planned to ease congestion on the corridor between Shanghai and Ningbo.
