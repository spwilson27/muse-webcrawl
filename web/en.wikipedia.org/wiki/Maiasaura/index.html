Maiasaura

Maiasaura (from the Greek μαῖα, meaning "careful mother lizard") is a large herbivorous hadrosaurid ("duck-billed") dinosaur genus that lived in the area currently covered by the state of Montana and the Canadian province of Alberta, in the Upper Cretaceous Period about 76 million years ago.

Discovery and behaviour

Maiasaura was discovered by Laurie Trexler in 1978 in the Two Medicine Formation near Choteau, Montana, and described by Jack Horner and Robert Makela in 1979. The discovery of nests, eggs and hatchlings at a site called "Egg Mountain" provided the first evidence of parental care in dinosaurs: adults apparently tended and fed their young in nesting colonies, which inspired the genus name. Maiasaura grew to about 9 metres long, walked on two or four legs, and had the flattened, toothless beak and hundreds of grinding cheek teeth typical of hadrosaurs.
