McLaren Racing Limited is a British motor racing team based at the McLaren Technology Centre in Woking, Surrey, England. The team is a subsidiary of the McLaren Group, which also controls McLaren Automotive. McLaren is best known as a Formula One constructor, the second-oldest active team and the second-most successful in the sport, having won more than 190 races, 12 Drivers' Championships and numerous Constructors' Championships.

History

The team was founded in 1963 by New Zealander Bruce McLaren, who was killed testing a Can-Am car at Goodwood in 1970. Under Teddy Mayer and then Ron Dennis, McLaren became a dominant force: Niki Lauda won the 1984 drivers' title, and the partnership with Honda brought four consecutive titles for Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna between 1988 and 1991, including the record-breaking 1988 season in which McLaren won 15 of 16 races. Later champions included Mika Häkkinen (1998, 1999) and Lewis Hamilton (2008). After a period in the midfield, the team returned to the front under Zak Brown and Andrea Stella, winning the Constructors' Championship in 2024, its first since 1998.

Other series

McLaren also competes in the IndyCar Series as Arrow McLaren, in Formula E and Extreme E (until 2025), and has won the Indianapolis 500, the 24 Hours of Le Mans (1995) and the Can-Am championship.
