The Strait of Messina (Italian: Stretto di Messina) is a narrow strait between the eastern tip of Sicily and the western tip of Calabria in southern Italy. It connects the Tyrrhenian Sea to the north with the Ionian Sea to the south, within the central Mediterranean.

Characteristics

The strait is about 3.1 km wide at its narrowest point and is known for strong tidal currents and whirlpools, which inspired the ancient Greek myths of the monsters Scylla and Charybdis, said to menace sailors on opposite sides of the channel. Ferries carry trains and vehicles between Villa San Giovanni and Messina; the crossing is also a famous open-water swimming challenge.

Bridge proposals

A bridge across the strait has been proposed since Roman times and seriously planned since the 1960s. The Strait of Messina Bridge project — a single-span suspension bridge of about 3.3 km, which would be the longest in the world — has been repeatedly approved, suspended, and revived, most recently relaunched by the Italian government in 2023.
