Avatar (computing)

In computing, an avatar is a graphical representation of a user, the user's character, or persona. Avatars can be two-dimensional icons used on Internet forums and other online communities, or three-dimensional models in virtual worlds, online games, and virtual reality environments. The word derives from the Sanskrit avatāra, the descent of a deity to Earth in Hindu theology.

History

One of the earliest uses of the term in computing was in the 1985 role-playing game Ultima IV: Quest of the Avatar, in which the player character was called "the Avatar". Lucasfilm's Habitat (1986), an early graphical online world, popularised the term for a user's in-world body. Neal Stephenson's 1992 novel Snow Crash brought the concept to a wide audience through its depiction of the Metaverse.

Types

Icon avatars are small images displayed beside usernames on forums, blogs, and social media; services such as Gravatar provide globally recognised avatars across sites. Three-dimensional avatars inhabit platforms such as Second Life and VRChat, and massively multiplayer games, where they may be highly customised and animated in real time, sometimes tracked from the user's own body movements in VR.
