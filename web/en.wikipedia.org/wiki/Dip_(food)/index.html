Dip (food)

Dip
Type: Sauce / accompaniment
Served with: Chips, crudités, bread, crackers
Examples: Guacamole, hummus, salsa
Related: Sauce, Hummus

A dip or dipping sauce is a common condiment into which foods — such as chips, crackers, raw vegetables, bread or dumplings — are dipped before eating. Unlike sauces poured over food, dips are typically thick and served in a shared bowl.

Common dips

Well-known examples include guacamole, hummus, salsa, tzatziki, baba ghanoush, French onion dip, spinach-artichoke dip, queso, and sour-cream-based ranch dip. Sweet dips include chocolate and caramel sauces for fruit.

Serving and etiquette

Dips are staples of parties and casual dining. 'Double-dipping' — dipping a partially eaten item again into a shared bowl — is widely considered poor etiquette and a hygiene concern.

Fondue

Swiss fondue, in which bread is dipped into communal melted cheese, is a cooked relative of the dip tradition.
