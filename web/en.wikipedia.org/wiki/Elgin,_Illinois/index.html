Elgin is a city in Cook and Kane counties in the northern part of the U.S. state of Illinois. Located along the Fox River about 35 miles northwest of Chicago, it is one of the larger cities of the Chicago metropolitan area, with a population of roughly 115,000.

History

Elgin was founded in 1835 and named after the Scottish hymn "Elgin". It became nationally known for the Elgin National Watch Company, founded in 1864, which grew into one of the largest watch manufacturers in the United States, and for the Elgin Board of Trade's butter and dairy trade. The city also gave its name to the Elgin Marbles controversy only indirectly — the marbles are named for a Scottish earl, not the city.

Geography and economy

The city straddles the Fox River, with a historic downtown on its banks that includes the Grand Victoria Casino riverboat. Modern employers include manufacturing, healthcare, and education institutions such as Judson University and Elgin Community College.

Arts and landmarks

Elgin is home to the Elgin Symphony Orchestra, one of Illinois' largest professional orchestras, and to a notable stock of Victorian-era houses, Sears catalogue homes, and the Elgin Public Museum in Lords Park.
