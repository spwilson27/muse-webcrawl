Nth root

In mathematics, an n-th root of a number x is a number r which, when raised to the power n, yields x: rn = x, where n is a positive integer called the degree of the root. A root of degree 2 is called a square root and a root of degree 3 a cube root. Roots are written using the radical symbol √, with the degree shown in the "notch" of the radical: n√x. Roots can also be expressed as fractional powers: n√x = x1/n.

Real roots

Every positive real number has exactly one positive real n-th root, called the principal root. Negative numbers have a real n-th root only when n is odd; for even n, roots of negative numbers are not real. For example, ∛(−8) = −2, while −16 has no real square root.

Complex roots

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, every non-zero complex number has exactly n distinct complex n-th roots, known as roots of unity when the number is 1. These roots are evenly spaced around a circle in the complex plane, separated by angles of 2π/n, and can be computed using De Moivre's formula from the polar form of the number.

Computing roots

N-th roots can be calculated by the n-th root algorithm (a generalisation of Newton's method), using logarithms, or via infinite series. Historically, roots were extracted by labour-intensive digit-by-digit algorithms taught in schools before electronic calculators.
