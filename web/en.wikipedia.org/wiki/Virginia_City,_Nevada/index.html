Virginia City, Nevada

StateNevada
CountyStorey County

Virginia City is a census-designated place (CDP) that is the county seat of Storey County, Nevada, and the largest community in the county. It was the prototype of a mining boomtown: it sprang up as a boomtown on the Comstock Lode, the first major silver deposit discovery in the United States, in 1859. At its peak in the mid-1870s, the city had an estimated population of 25,000.

C Street, Virginia City's historic main street

History

The discovery of the Comstock Lode in 1859 drew thousands of miners, prospectors and speculators to the eastern slopes of the Sierra Nevada. Virginia City became one of the richest and most famous mining towns of the American West, financing San Francisco's growth and contributing to Nevada's statehood during the Civil War. Its mines ultimately produced hundreds of millions of dollars in silver and gold. The young Mark Twain worked as a reporter for the town's newspaper, the Territorial Enterprise, from 1862 to 1864, and it was there that Samuel Clemens first used his famous pen name. A devastating fire in 1875 destroyed much of the city, which was promptly rebuilt in brick. As the ore bodies declined after the 1870s, the population dwindled.

Today

The decline left a remarkably preserved 19th-century mining town. The Virginia City Historic District is a National Historic Landmark, and the town's preserved boardwalks, saloons, churches and the Fourth Ward School attract numerous tourists. Tourism is the basis of the modern economy, with museums, historic mansions such as the Mackay Mansion, and rides on the Virginia and Truckee Railroad.
