Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act

The Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act is a federal statute enacted by the 109th United States Congress and signed into law by President George W. Bush on July 27, 2006. The Act is named after Adam Walsh, a six-year-old boy abducted from a Florida shopping mall and murdered in 1981; his father, John Walsh, became a prominent victims' advocate and campaigned for the legislation.

Provisions

The Act's first title, the Sex Offender Registration and Notification Act (SORNA), established a comprehensive national system for registering sex offenders, replacing the patchwork of earlier federal standards. It classifies offenders into three tiers based on the nature of the offence, with registration periods of 15 years, 25 years or life, and requires in-person periodic verification and registration in every jurisdiction where the offender lives, works or attends school. Other titles addressed child exploitation enforcement, DNA fingerprinting of offenders, civil commitment of sexually dangerous persons, and funding for background checks of foster and adoptive parents.

Implementation and debate

States were required to substantially implement SORNA or lose a portion of federal justice funding, but implementation proved slow and costly, and many states retained their own systems for years. Critics, including some judges and legal scholars, have questioned the Act's application to juvenile offenders, its retroactivity, and whether registration regimes of this breadth measurably improve public safety. Supporters argue that uniform national standards close loopholes that allowed offenders to evade registration by moving between states.
