György Ligeti

György Sándor Ligeti (28 May 1923 – 12 June 2006) was a Hungarian-Austrian composer of contemporary classical music. He has been described as "one of the most important avant-garde composers in the latter half of the twentieth century" and "one of the most innovative and influential among progressive figures of his time".

Life

Born in Transylvania, Romania, to a Hungarian Jewish family, Ligeti studied at the conservatory in Cluj and at the Franz Liszt Academy of Music in Budapest. He survived the Holocaust, in which most of his family perished, and remained in Hungary after the war, but emigrated to Austria following the Hungarian Revolution of 1956. He worked at the electronic music studio of Westdeutscher Rundfunk in Cologne and became an Austrian citizen in 1968.

Music

Ligeti is best known for works such as Atmosphères (1961), the Requiem (1963–65), Lux Aeterna (1966) and Lontano (1967), which develop dense, slowly evolving textures he termed micropolyphony. Several of his works became widely known through their use in Stanley Kubrick's films 2001: A Space Odyssey, The Shining and Eyes Wide Shut. His later output includes the opera Le Grand Macabre (1977), the Piano Concerto, the Études for piano, and the Violin Concerto. He was also influenced by African polyrhythm, the player-piano studies of Conlon Nancarrow, and fractal geometry.
