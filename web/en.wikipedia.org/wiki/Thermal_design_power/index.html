Thermal design power

The thermal design power (TDP), sometimes called the thermal design point, is the maximum amount of heat, measured in watts, that a computer chip — typically a CPU or GPU — is expected to generate under a defined workload, and for which the cooling system of the computer must be designed to dissipate. The term was popularised by Intel, which defines the TDP of its processors as the power dissipation under a "high-complexity workload" at the base clock frequency and a specified maximum case temperature. AMD has used similar figures, variously styled TDP and, for some GPU products, "total board power".

Interpretation

TDP is a specification for thermal engineers rather than a direct measure of electrical power consumption. A processor may briefly draw power well above its TDP when boosting under turbo algorithms (Intel publishes separate short-duration and long-duration power limits, PL2 and PL1), and under most real workloads it draws considerably less. Consequently two chips with the same nominal TDP may behave quite differently in a thin laptop versus a desktop with a large cooler. Typical values range from a few watts for mobile system-on-chip parts, through 15–45 W for laptop processors and 65–125 W for mainstream desktop CPUs, to 250 W and beyond for high-end desktop and workstation chips, and several hundred watts for server processors and flagship GPUs.

History and criticism

The concept grew out of the "power wall" era of the early 2000s, when the end of Dennard scaling made heat removal the principal constraint on processor performance. Reviewers and system builders have long criticised TDP figures as marketing-dependent: definitions differ between vendors and generations, boost behaviour makes the number a poor predictor of sustained power draw, and motherboard makers' firmware settings often set long-duration limits well above the nominal TDP. Both Intel and AMD have consequently published additional metrics — Intel's "Processor Base Power" and "Maximum Turbo Power" (from the 12th-generation Core processors), and AMD's measured socket power — though "TDP" remains the common shorthand in retail specifications.
