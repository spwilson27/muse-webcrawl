Halite

Halite (pronounced /ˈhælaɪt/), commonly known as rock salt, is a type of salt, the mineral (natural) form of sodium chloride (NaCl). Halite forms isometric crystals. The mineral is typically colorless or white, but may also be light blue, dark blue, purple, pink, red, orange, yellow or gray depending on inclusion of other materials, impurities, and structural or isotopic abnormalities in the crystals.

Occurrence

Halite occurs in vast beds of sedimentary evaporite minerals that result from the drying up of enclosed lakes, playas, and seas. Famous deposits include the Khewra Salt Mine in Pakistan, the Wieliczka Salt Mine in Poland, and thick salt beds beneath Detroit, Michigan, and in Germany's Stassfurt district.

Uses

Rock salt is used in food preparation and preservation, in the chemical industry as a source of sodium and chlorine, and extensively for de-icing roads in winter. Large crystals of impure halite are spread on icy surfaces to lower the freezing point of water.

Properties

Halite has a hardness of 2.5 on the Mohs scale, perfect cubic cleavage, and a characteristically salty taste. It is highly soluble in water.
