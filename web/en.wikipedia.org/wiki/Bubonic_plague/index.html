Bubonic plague
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Bubonic plague is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, transmitted to humans mainly by the bite of infected fleas carried by rodents (classically black rats). It is named for the painful swollen lymph nodes — buboes — in the groin, armpit or neck; untreated, it is often fatal, and the infection can become septicaemic or pneumonic (the latter transmissible person to person).

Plague caused three great pandemics: the Plague of Justinian (from 541), the Black Death (from 1346) and the Third Pandemic (from 1855, mainly in Asia). Alexandre Yersin identified the bacillus in Hong Kong in 1894. Today plague persists in rodent reservoirs in parts of Africa (notably Madagascar), Asia and the western United States, with typically a few thousand human cases a year worldwide; antibiotics (streptomycin, gentamicin, doxycycline) are effective if given early.
