Fascist Italy is the period of the Kingdom of Italy between 1922 and 1943 when the country was governed by the National Fascist Party under Benito Mussolini. Following the March on Rome of October 1922, Mussolini was appointed prime minister by King Victor Emmanuel III and progressively dismantled Italy's liberal parliamentary system, establishing a one-party dictatorship by 1926. Fascist Italy pursued an aggressive expansionist foreign policy, conquered an empire in Africa, aligned itself with Nazi Germany in the Axis, and entered the Second World War in June 1940.

Rise of Fascism

Fascism arose from the turmoil of post–First World War Italy — economic crisis, inflation, strikes and the "mutilated victory" grievance over territorial gains. Mussolini founded the Fasci di combattimento in 1919; its paramilitary Blackshirts (squadristi) violently suppressed socialist and trade-union organisations with the tacit support of landowners, industrialists and much of the state apparatus. On 28 October 1922 the March on Rome prompted the king to invite Mussolini to form a government.

The regime

Mussolini styled himself Il Duce ("the Leader"). The Acerbo Law (1923), the murder of the socialist deputy Giacomo Matteotti (1924) and the exceptional laws of 1925–26 destroyed political opposition; all parties except the PNF were banned. The regime built a corporatist economic system, censored the press, created mass organisations such as the Opera Nazionale Balilla for youth, and settled its long dispute with the papacy through the Lateran Treaty of 1929, which created Vatican City. In 1938 the regime enacted antisemitic racial laws under German influence.

Empire and war

In 1935–36 Italy invaded and annexed Ethiopia, proclaiming an Italian Empire and driving Italy away from Britain and France and toward Hitler's Germany. Italy intervened in the Spanish Civil War on Franco's side, annexed Albania in 1939, and signed the Pact of Steel with Germany. Italy entered the Second World War on 10 June 1940 but suffered defeats in Greece, North Africa and East Africa; the Allied invasion of Sicily in July 1943 (see Italian campaign) triggered Mussolini's fall.

Fall

On 25 July 1943 the Fascist Grand Council voted no confidence in Mussolini, and the king had him arrested. Marshal Badoglio's government signed the Armistice of Cassibile with the Allies on 8 September 1943. Germany occupied northern Italy and rescued Mussolini, who headed the puppet Italian Social Republic (the "Republic of Salò") until his capture and execution by partisans in April 1945.
