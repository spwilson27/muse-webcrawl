Kaieteur National Park

Kaieteur National Park is a national park located in the Potaro-Siparuni region (Region 8) of Guyana, in the Pakaraima Mountains at the edge of the Guiana Shield. Established in 1929 — among the earliest protected areas in South America created under British colonial administration — it covers roughly 242 square miles (627 km²) of mostly pristine Amazonian rainforest and is centred on Kaieteur Falls, one of the world's most powerful single-drop waterfalls.

Kaieteur Falls

The Potaro River drops 226 metres (741 feet) in a single uninterrupted leap over a sandstone and conglomerate escarpment, with a width of about 113 metres and an average flow near 660 cubic metres per second. The falls were brought to outside attention in 1870 by geologist Charles Barrington Brown. In Patamona tradition the name Kaieteur ("old man falls") recalls Kai, a chief who sacrificed himself by paddling over the falls to save his people from a raiding party.

Wildlife and environment

The park's nutrient-poor sandstone soils support distinctive flora, including carnivorous plants such as sundews and bladderworts around the falls. Notable fauna include the Guianan cock-of-the-rock, the golden rocket frog (Anomaloglossus beebei), which lives in the water held by giant tank bromeliads near the falls, white-collared swifts nesting behind the curtain of water, jaguars, and giant otters. Tourism is limited and typically by light aircraft from Georgetown to the airstrip near the falls, often combined with visits to Orinduik Falls.
