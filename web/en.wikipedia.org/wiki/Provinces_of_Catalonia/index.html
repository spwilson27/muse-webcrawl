Provinces of Catalonia

Provinces: Barcelona, Girona, Lleida, Tarragona
Created: 1822 / 1833 provincial division of Spain
Alternative division: Vegueries (Catalan law)

The autonomous community of Catalonia in northeastern Spain is divided into four provinces: Barcelona, Girona, Lleida, and Tarragona. The provinces date from Spain's nineteenth-century provincial divisions and serve as electoral districts and units of the Spanish state administration.

Role

Each province has a provincial council (diputació), though in Catalonia many functions are exercised instead by the Generalitat de Catalunya and by the smaller comarques (counties). Catalan statute law provides for an alternative territorial division into vegueries, but the four-province structure remains the operative one for Spanish state purposes.

The provinces

Barcelona province is by far the most populous, with over five million inhabitants. Girona covers the Costa Brava and eastern Pyrenees; Lleida is the large, agricultural western interior; Tarragona covers the southern coast and the Ebro delta.
