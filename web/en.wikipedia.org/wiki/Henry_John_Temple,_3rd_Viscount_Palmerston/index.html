Henry John Temple, 3rd Viscount Palmerston (20 October 1784 – 18 October 1865), known as Lord Palmerston, was a British statesman who dominated British foreign policy from the 1830s to the 1860s and served twice as Prime Minister. Popularly nicknamed "Pam", he embodied assertive liberal interventionism backed by Royal Navy "gunboat diplomacy".

Early career

An Anglo-Irish peer (his viscountcy was Irish, allowing him to sit in the Commons), Palmerston entered Parliament in 1807 and served as Secretary at War for nearly twenty years. Switching from the Tories to the Whigs over Catholic emancipation and parliamentary reform, he became Foreign Secretary in 1830.

Foreign Secretary

In three spells at the Foreign Office (1830–34, 1835–41, 1846–51) Palmerston secured Belgian independence, upheld the Ottoman Empire against Muhammad Ali's Egypt in the Oriental Crisis of 1840, prosecuted the First Opium War against China, and in the Don Pacifico affair of 1850 defended the rights of a British subject with his famous civis Romanus sum speech. Dismissed by Lord John Russell in 1851 for approving Louis-Napoleon's coup, he took his revenge by toppling the government weeks later.

Prime Minister

Palmerston became Prime Minister in 1855 during the Crimean War, bringing energy to its prosecution and negotiating the Treaty of Paris. Out of office briefly in 1858–59, he returned after the meeting at Willis's Rooms that effectively founded the Liberal Party. His second ministry conducted the Second Opium War, supported Italian unification, maintained neutrality (with sympathy for the North) during the American Civil War, and alarmed the public with invasion panics and fortification programmes directed at France. He died in office on 18 October 1865, two days short of his 81st birthday, and remains the last British prime minister to die in office.
