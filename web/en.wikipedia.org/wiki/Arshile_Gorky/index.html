Arshile Gorky (born Vostanik Manoug Adoian, Armenian: Ոստանիկ Մանուկ Ատոյեան; c. April 15, 1904 – July 21, 1948) was an Armenian-American painter who had a seminal influence on Abstract Expressionism. As a young man fleeing the Armenian genocide, he spent most of his life as a national of the United States, reinventing his biography and taking his professional name in homage to the writer Maxim Gorky.

Early life

Gorky was born in the village of Khorkom near Lake Van in the Ottoman Empire. In 1915, during the Armenian genocide, his family fled to Russian-controlled territory; his mother died of starvation in Yerevan in 1919. Gorky emigrated to the United States in 1920, studied in Boston and then settled in New York, where he taught at the Grand Central School of Art.

Career

Through the 1920s and 1930s Gorky absorbed the styles of Cézanne, Picasso, Miró and Kandinsky — he was wryly dubbed a "Picasso of Washington Square" — while producing the double portrait The Artist and His Mother, based on a childhood photograph. In the 1930s he worked for the WPA Federal Art Project, including murals for Newark Airport. In the early 1940s, influenced by the exiled European Surrealists and Roberto Matta, he developed his mature style of fluid, biomorphic abstraction, seen in works such as Garden in Sochi, The Liver is the Cock's Comb (1944) and How My Mother's Embroidered Apron Unfolds in My Life (1944), which profoundly affected younger New York painters such as Willem de Kooning.

Final years and death

Gorky's last years were marked by a series of calamities: a studio fire in 1946 destroyed much of his work, he underwent a colostomy for cancer the same year, and in June 1948 he broke his neck and injured his painting arm in a car accident. On 21 July 1948 he died by suicide at his home in Sherman, Connecticut.

Legacy

Gorky is regarded as the pivotal figure linking European Surrealism to American Abstract Expressionism. Major retrospectives have been held at the Museum of Modern Art, the Whitney Museum and Tate Modern, and his works are held in leading museums worldwide.
