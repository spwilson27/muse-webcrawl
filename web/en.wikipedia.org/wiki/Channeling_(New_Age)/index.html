Channeling (New Age)

Channeling is a practice within the New Age movement in which a person, the "channel" or "channeler," claims to receive and relay communications from a non-physical entity — such as an ascended master, an extraterrestrial intelligence, an angel, or a disembodied spirit collective. The channeled material is usually delivered in trance or a relaxed meditative state, in spoken or written form.

Channelers claim to relay messages from discarnate entities, sometimes before live audiences.

History

Channeling descends from 19th-century Spiritualism, in which mediums communicated with the dead, and from Theosophy, whose founders claimed contact with hidden "Masters." In the 20th century it took modern form with figures such as Edgar Cayce, the "sleeping prophet," and Jane Roberts, whose Seth Material (from 1963) popularised the very term "channeling." Other influential channels include J. Z. Knight, said to channel the entity Ramtha, and Helen Schucman, who transcribed A Course in Miracles. The practice became a hallmark of the New Age milieu of the 1970s and 1980s.

Types

Practitioners distinguish "trance channeling," in which the channel's ordinary consciousness is displaced and the entity speaks directly through the body, from "conscious" or "mental" channeling, in which impressions are received and translated by the channel's own mind. Automatic writing is a related written form.

Views and criticism

Believers hold that channeled teachings offer spiritual guidance and insight into the nature of reality. Skeptics regard channeling as a performance drawing on the channeler's own subconscious, cold reading and audience expectation, noting that claims made by purported entities are unverifiable and often contradictory. No channeled communication has been validated under controlled scientific conditions.
