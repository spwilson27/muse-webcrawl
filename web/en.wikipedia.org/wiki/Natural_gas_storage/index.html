Natural gas storage

Natural gas storage refers to the facilities and methods used to store natural gas for later use, principally to balance seasonal and short-term fluctuations in supply and demand. Because gas production is relatively steady while consumption peaks sharply in winter for heating, gas is injected into storage during low-demand summer months and withdrawn during cold periods.

Types of underground storage

Most large-scale storage is underground, in three main types of facility:

  • Depleted oil and gas reservoirs — the most common and usually cheapest option, reusing wells, gathering systems and the geological knowledge built up during production. A large portion of the stored volume is "cushion gas" that must remain in the reservoir to maintain pressure.
  • Aquifers — porous water-bearing rock formations capped by impermeable layers; they require more cushion gas and careful development but are used where no depleted fields exist.
  • Salt caverns — cavities leached from underground salt formations. They hold less gas but allow very rapid injection and withdrawal, making them valuable for short-term balancing and trading.

Above-ground storage

Gas may also be stored as liquefied natural gas (LNG) in insulated tanks, or in low-pressure gasholders historically used by town gas works. LNG peak-shaving plants store liquefied gas to meet extreme daily demand.

Operation and markets

The difference between total capacity and working gas capacity, injection and withdrawal rates, and the seasonal price spread all shape the commercial value of storage. In the United States the Energy Information Administration publishes weekly storage reports that are closely watched by gas markets. Storage levels in Europe became a major policy concern after the supply disruptions of 2022, leading to mandatory filling targets ahead of winter.
