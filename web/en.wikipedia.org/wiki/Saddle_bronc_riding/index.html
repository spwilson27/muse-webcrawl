Saddle bronc riding

Saddle bronc riding is a rodeo event that involves a participant riding a bucking horse (sometimes called a bronc or bronco) that attempts to throw the rider off. It is one of the oldest events in rodeo and is often called rodeo's "classic event". Unlike bareback riding, the rider uses a specialized western-style saddle without a horn, holding on to a single thick rein attached to the horse's halter.

Technique

The rider must stay on the horse for eight seconds while performing a controlled, rhythmic spurring motion. At the start of the ride, the rider's spurs must be held over the horse's shoulders until the horse's front feet hit the ground after the initial jump out of the chute — a rule known as the "mark out"; failure to mark out results in disqualification. The rider holds the rein in one hand and must not touch the horse or himself with the free hand.

Scoring

Two judges score the ride on a scale of 0 to 25 each for the rider and 0 to 25 each for the horse, giving a maximum combined score of 100. Points are awarded for control, spurring technique, timing with the horse's bucking, and the difficulty of the horse. After the eight-second whistle, pickup riders help the competitor dismount safely.

Equipment

The saddle is a modified stock saddle with free-swinging stirrups and no horn. The bronc rein, made of braided rope, is attached to the horse's halter, and a flank strap encourages the horse to buck. Broncs are bred and selected for their bucking ability and are valuable rodeo livestock.
