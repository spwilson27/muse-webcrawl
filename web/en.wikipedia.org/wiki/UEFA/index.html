The Union of European Football Associations (UEFA; French: Union des associations européennes de football) is the governing body of association football in Europe. It is one of the six continental confederations of FIFA and the largest and wealthiest of them, representing 55 national football associations across Europe.

History

UEFA was founded on 15 June 1954 in Basel, following consultations between the French, Italian and Belgian associations; its first headquarters were in Paris before moving to Bern and, in 1995, to Nyon on Lake Geneva. Founding figures included Henri Delaunay, the French administrator whose idea for a European national-team championship became the UEFA European Championship, first held in 1960.

Competitions

UEFA organises Europe's principal club competitions: the UEFA Champions League (formerly the European Cup, founded in 1955), the UEFA Europa League, the UEFA Europa Conference League (launched in 2021) and the UEFA Women's Champions League. For national teams it runs the European Championship ("the Euros") every four years and, since 2018, the UEFA Nations League. It also governs futsal and youth tournaments across the continent.

Membership

Membership extends beyond strictly European states: the associations of transcontinental and Asian countries including Turkey, Russia, Israel, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Georgia are UEFA members, as are the constituent countries of the United Kingdom, which compete separately. Gibraltar's association was admitted in 2013 and Kosovo's in 2016, bringing the total to 55.
