Thomas Artsruni


An Armenian historian, in a medieval illumination

Thomas Artsruni (Armenian: Թովմա Արծրունի, Tovma Artsruni; fl. 9th–10th centuries) was an Armenian historian, author of the History of the House of the Artsrunik, a major source for the history of medieval Armenia and the Caucasus.

Life and work

Thomas was a member of the Artsruni princely house, which ruled the kingdom of Vaspurakan in south-eastern Armenia, around Lake Van. Little is known of his biography; he wrote at the court of the Artsruni kings in the late 9th and early 10th centuries.

His History begins with the legendary origins of the Armenians and the Artsruni family and continues to about 904 AD. It covers the Arab domination of Armenia, the rise of the Bagratuni and Artsruni dynasties, and the wars and diplomacy of the period, mixing legendary genealogies with detailed contemporary political narrative. An anonymous continuator later extended the work into the 1120s.

Significance

Alongside the histories of Łazar P'arpec'i, Movses Khorenatsi, and John Mamikonian, Thomas Artsruni's work is a principal primary source for early medieval Armenian history. It was edited and translated into French by Marie-Félicité Brosset in the 19th century, and an English translation by Robert W. Thomson appeared in 1985.
