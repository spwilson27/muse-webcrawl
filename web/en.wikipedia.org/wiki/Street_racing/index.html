Street racing

Street racing is a form of unsanctioned and illegal motor racing that takes place on public roads. Racing in the streets is considered an ancient hazard, as horse racing occurred on streets for centuries, and street racing in automobiles is likely as old as the automobile itself. It became especially prevalent during the heyday of hot rodding (1960s), muscle cars (1970s), Japanese imports (1990s), sports cars (2020s), and exotic cars (2020s).

Types

Street races may be spontaneous or planned and coordinated. Common formats include drag races over a set distance, roll races beginning from a moving start, and circuit-style races on improvised courses. Car meets may precede races, and participants may wager money or vehicle titles ("racing for pink slips").

Dangers and legality

Street racing is illegal in most jurisdictions and carries penalties including fines, licence suspension, vehicle impoundment, and imprisonment. It is substantially more dangerous than organised racing because it lacks safety measures such as roll cages inspection, controlled environments, and medical personnel, and because it endangers spectators and uninvolved road users. Numerous deaths of drivers, passengers, spectators, and bystanders have been attributed to street racing.

In popular culture

Street racing culture has been depicted in films such as The Fast and the Furious franchise and in video games including the Need for Speed series. Authorities and motorsport organisations promote sanctioned track events and drag strips as safer alternatives.
