Ben Hall (bushranger)

Born9 May 1837, Wallis Plains (Maitland), New South Wales
Died5 May 1865 (aged 27), near Forbes, New South Wales
OccupationStockman; bushranger
Known forLeading a gang in over 600 robberies without killing anyone

Ben Hall (9 May 1837 – 5 May 1865) was an Australian bushranger active in New South Wales in the 1860s. Together with his gang — at various times including John Gilbert, John Dunn and Frank Gardiner's former associates — Hall is credited with committing more than 600 robberies, including holding up whole towns, yet he was famous for never killing anyone. He remains one of the best-known figures of the Australian bushranging era, and ballads and folklore cast him as a gentleman outlaw driven to crime by police persecution.

Life

Hall was born at Wallis Plains (Maitland) to convict parents and worked as a stockman in the Lachlan district, leasing land near Forbes. In 1862 he was arrested as a suspected accomplice of Frank Gardiner's gang after the Eugowra gold-escort robbery, but was acquitted. Following further arrests, the collapse of his marriage and the burning of his home by police, Hall took to bushranging in earnest in 1863.

His gang bailed up towns such as Canowindra and Bathurst, robbed coaches, race meetings and homesteads, and repeatedly humiliated the police, often with theatrical courtesy to their victims. In 1865, under the newly passed Felons Apprehension Act, Hall was declared an outlaw who could be shot on sight. Betrayed by an informer, he was ambushed at dawn on 5 May 1865 by police at Goobang Creek near Forbes and shot dead, reportedly receiving some thirty wounds. He was 27. His associates Gilbert and Dunn were killed or captured shortly afterwards.

Legacy

Hall is commemorated in folk songs such as "The Streets of Forbes", in the grave at Forbes cemetery, and in numerous books and films, including the 2016 feature The Legend of Ben Hall. The debate over whether he was a folk hero or simply a criminal continues, but his reputation for restraint distinguishes him from later bushrangers such as Ned Kelly.
