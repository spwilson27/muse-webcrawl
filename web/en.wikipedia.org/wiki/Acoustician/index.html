Acoustician

An acoustician is a scientist or engineer who specializes in acoustics, the science of sound and vibration. Acousticians study how sound is produced, transmitted, and received, and apply that knowledge to fields ranging from concert hall design and noise control to medical ultrasound and underwater communications.

Roles and specializations

Acousticians work in many subfields, including:

  • Architectural acoustics — designing the sound of rooms, halls, and buildings
  • Environmental acoustics — assessing and controlling community noise
  • Musical acoustics — the physics of musical instruments and performance spaces
  • Psychoacoustics — how people perceive sound
  • Underwater acoustics — sound propagation in the ocean, sonar, and marine life
  • Speech acoustics — production and perception of speech
  • Engineering acoustics — transducers, microphones, loudspeakers, and signal processing
  • Bioacoustics and medical acoustics — animal sound and diagnostic ultrasound

Education and practice

Most acousticians hold degrees in physics, mechanical or electrical engineering, mathematics, music, or architecture, often followed by graduate study in acoustics. Many work as consultants, advising architects and engineers on the acoustic design of buildings and products; others work in universities, industry, government laboratories, and the military. Professional societies such as the Acoustical Society of America and the Institute of Acoustics (UK) support the profession.
