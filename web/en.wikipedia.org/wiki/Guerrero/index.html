Guerrero, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Guerrero (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Guerrero), is one of the 32 federal entities that comprise the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 85 municipalities. The capital city is Chilpancingo and the largest city is Acapulco. It is located in southwest Mexico and is bordered by the states of Michoacán to the north and west, the State of Mexico and Morelos to the north, Puebla to the northeast, Oaxaca to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the south. The state has a population of about 3.5 million.

The state is named after Vicente Ramón Guerrero Saldaña, one of the leaders in the Mexican War of Independence and the second president of Mexico. It is the only Mexican state named after a president.

History

The territory was home to Olmec-influenced cultures and later to the Mezcala culture, and formed part of the tributary sphere of the Aztec Empire. After the Spanish conquest, the port of Acapulco became the terminus of the Manila galleon trade with the Philippines. Guerrero was created as a state in 1849, carved from parts of Mexico, Puebla and Michoacán. During the Mexican Revolution and again in the later 20th century, the state saw significant agrarian and guerrilla conflict, and it remains one of Mexico's poorest states, with a large indigenous population speaking Nahuatl, Mixtec, Tlapanec and Amuzgo languages.

Geography and economy

The Sierra Madre del Sur range dominates the state, separating the coastal plain from the Balsas River basin. Tourism is centred on the "Triángulo del Sol" of Acapulco, Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo and the silver-mining town of Taxco. Agriculture includes maize, sesame, coffee, mango and coconut; opium poppy cultivation in the mountains has made the state a focus of Mexico's drug conflict. In October 2023 Acapulco was devastated by Hurricane Otis, one of the strongest Pacific hurricanes to make landfall in Mexico.
