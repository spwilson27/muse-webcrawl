The Battle of Camarón, fought on 30 April 1863 at the Hacienda Camarón near Camarón de Tejeda in Veracruz, Mexico, is the most celebrated episode in the history of the French Foreign Legion. A company of 65 legionnaires under Captain Jean Danjou held off a Mexican force of some 2,000 men for an entire day during the French intervention in Mexico.

Background

France had intervened in Mexico in 1861–62, initially alongside Britain and Spain, then alone in support of installing the Emperor Maximilian. As the French army besieged Puebla, a convoy carrying munitions, supplies and three million francs in gold was sent from Veracruz. On 30 April 1863 the 3rd Company of the Foreign Regiment — 3 officers and 62 men, many of them sick-list substitutes — was sent ahead to scout the road and took position in the ruined Hacienda de la Trinidad at Camarón.

The battle

Attacked in the morning by Mexican cavalry and then by steadily growing infantry forces under Colonel Francisco de Paula Milán, the legionnaires refused repeated surrender demands and fought from the hacienda's walls and courtyard all day in extreme heat, without food or water. Danjou was killed in the early afternoon. By evening only a handful of men remained; the last five, out of ammunition, fixed bayonets and charged. Struck by their courage, the Mexicans spared the survivors. French losses were nearly total; Mexican casualties were estimated at 190–500 killed and wounded.

Legacy

Camerone (as it is known in French) is commemorated every 30 April as the Foreign Legion's principal day of honour, with Captain Danjou's wooden hand — he had lost his left hand in an accident in 1853 — paraded as the Legion's most precious relic. The battle secured the convoy's passage but could not save the intervention, which collapsed in 1867.
