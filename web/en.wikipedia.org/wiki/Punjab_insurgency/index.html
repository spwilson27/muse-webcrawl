Punjab insurgency

The Punjab insurgency was an armed separatist conflict in the Indian state of Punjab, roughly from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s, in which Sikh militant groups sought an independent homeland called Khalistan.

Course

Unrest grew from political and religious grievances and the rise of preacher Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale, whose followers occupied the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar. In June 1984 the Indian Army stormed the complex in Operation Blue Star, killing Bhindranwale and many others. In October 1984 Prime Minister Indira Gandhi was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, triggering anti-Sikh riots in Delhi and elsewhere in which thousands were killed.

A decade of militancy, assassinations, bombings and counter-insurgency followed. Under police chief K. P. S. Gill, security forces largely suppressed the movement by the mid-1990s, though the campaign was accompanied by widespread allegations of extrajudicial killings and enforced disappearances.
