Munsell color system

The Munsell color system is a color space that specifies colors based on three properties: hue, chroma (colorfulness), and value (lightness). It was created by Albert H. Munsell, an artist and teacher, and published in A Color Notation (1905) and the Munsell Book of Color (1929). Unlike purely theoretical systems, the Munsell system was designed so that its steps are perceptually uniform — equal numeric steps correspond roughly to equal perceived differences.

Dimensions

  • Hue is arranged in a circle of five principal hues — red, yellow, green, blue, and purple — with five intermediate hues between them, each divided into ten steps.
  • Value runs vertically from 0 (black) to 10 (white), forming the central axis of the "color tree."
  • Chroma, the departure from neutral gray, increases outward from the axis, with its maximum varying by hue and value.

A color is specified in the order hue, value, chroma — for example, 5R 4/6 is a moderately dark, moderately saturated red.

Applications

The system is widely used in soil science (the Munsell Soil Color Charts), dentistry, art education, archaeology, and industrial color specification. The U.S. National Bureau of Standards validated its underlying renotation against spectrophotometric measurements in the 1940s.
