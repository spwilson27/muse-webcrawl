Lunar deity

A lunar deity is a deity who represents the Moon, or an aspect of it, in mythology and religion. Lunar deities occur across cultures worldwide and may be male or female; in many traditions the Moon god is male (such as Mesopotamian Sin, Egyptian Khonsu, Norse Máni and Japanese Tsukuyomi), while in classical Greco-Roman religion the Moon is embodied by goddesses such as Selene and Luna.

Selene, Greek goddess of the Moon, driving her chariot

Roles and associations

Lunar deities are commonly associated with the measurement of time and calendars, the tides, fertility and menstrual cycles, darkness and night, hunting, and cycles of death and rebirth. Because the Moon visibly waxes and wanes, lunar gods often embody change and renewal. In some traditions a lunar deity "chases" or is pursued by a solar deity across the sky, as with Norse Máni and Sól, who are destined to be devoured by wolves at Ragnarök.

Examples

Notable lunar deities include Sin (Nanna) of Mesopotamia; Thoth and Khonsu of Egypt; Chandra of Hinduism; Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto of Shinto; Chang'e, the Chinese goddess who lives on the Moon; Selene, Artemis and Hecate of Greece (the three sometimes combined as a triple goddess); Luna and Diana of Rome; Máni of Norse mythology; and the Māori goddess Hina. In Abrahamic-influenced folklore, the "Man in the Moon" is a common personification.
