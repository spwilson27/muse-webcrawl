Madinat al-Zahra

Madinat al-Zahra (Arabic: مدينة الزهراء, "the radiant city") was a fortified palace-city built in the tenth century by Abd al-Rahman III, the first caliph of Córdoba, on the western outskirts of Córdoba in present-day Spain. It served as the seat of government of the Caliphate of Córdoba.

History

Construction began around 936–940, shortly after Abd al-Rahman III proclaimed the caliphate. Built on terraced levels on the slopes of the Sierra Morena, the city contained the caliphal palace, government offices, mosques, gardens, barracks, and workshops, and was supplied by aqueducts. It flourished for only a few decades: during the civil wars that destroyed the caliphate in the early eleventh century, Madinat al-Zahra was sacked and abandoned around 1010, and its ruins were quarried for building materials for centuries.

Archaeology

Excavations began in 1911 and have uncovered roughly a tenth of the walled area, including the reception hall known as the Salón Rico, richly decorated with carved vegetal ornament. In 2018 the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Caliphate City of Medina Azahara."
