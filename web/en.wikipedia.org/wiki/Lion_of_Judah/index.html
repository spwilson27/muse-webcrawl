Lion of Judah

The Lion of Judah (Hebrew: אריה יהודה Aryeh Yehudah) is a Jewish national and cultural symbol, traditionally regarded as the symbol of the Israelite tribe of Judah. According to the Torah, the tribe consists of the descendants of Judah, the fourth son of Jacob. The association between Judah and the lion can first be found in the blessing given by Jacob to his son Judah in the Book of Genesis.

In Judaism

The lion appears in Jacob's blessing: "Judah is a lion's whelp" (Genesis 49:9). Jewish tradition regarded the lion as a symbol of the kingship of the House of David, and the motif decorates Torah ark curtains, synagogue mosaics and ceremonial objects. Lions flank the Tablets of the Law in many synagogue depictions.

In Christianity

In Christian tradition, the Lion of Judah is a title of Jesus Christ, drawn from the Book of Revelation: "the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered" (Revelation 5:5). The image is prominent in hymns, heraldry and in C. S. Lewis's Chronicles of Narnia, where Aslan the lion represents Christ.

In Ethiopia and Rastafari

The Lion of Judah was the central emblem of imperial Ethiopia, appearing on the flag of the Ethiopian Empire, whose emperors claimed descent from Solomon and the Queen of Sheba through the Solomonic dynasty. Haile Selassie I bore the title "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah". In the Rastafari movement, the lion is a symbol of Haile Selassie and of African pride, and features on its flags and in its music and iconography.
