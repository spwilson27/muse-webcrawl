Corning Tower

Location: Empire State Plaza, Albany, New York
Height: 180 m (589 ft), 44 stories
Completed: 1973
Architect: Wallace Harrison

The Erastus Corning Tower, commonly called the Corning Tower, is a skyscraper in Albany, New York, the tallest building in New York State outside New York City. Standing 180 metres (589 feet) with 44 stories, it dominates the Empire State Plaza complex of state government buildings.

History and design

The tower was built as part of Governor Nelson Rockefeller's Empire State Plaza project and was completed in 1973 to designs by architect Wallace K. Harrison. It is named for Erastus Corning 2nd, the longtime mayor of Albany. The slab-like tower is clad in marble and glass and houses offices of New York State agencies, principally the Department of Health.

Observation deck

The Corning Tower Observation Deck, on the 42nd floor, is open to the public free of charge and offers views over Albany, the Hudson River, and toward the Catskill and Adirondack mountains and the Berkshires in Massachusetts. On clear days it is possible to see for many miles in every direction.
