John Smeaton

John Smeaton FRS (8 June 1724 – 28 October 1792) was a British civil engineer responsible for the design of bridges, canals, harbours and lighthouses. He was also a capable mechanical engineer and an eminent physicist. Smeaton was the first self-proclaimed "civil engineer", and is often regarded as the "father of civil engineering."

Eddystone Lighthouse

Smeaton's most famous work was the third Eddystone Lighthouse (1759), built of interlocking dovetailed granite blocks and set in hydraulic lime of his own devising. The tower stood for over a century and its tapered profile became the model for lighthouse design worldwide; when it was replaced, Smeaton's tower was re-erected on Plymouth Hoe, where it still stands.

Engineering work

Smeaton conducted systematic experiments on waterwheels and windmills, improving their efficiency, and advised on the Forth and Clyde Canal, harbour works at Ramsgate and elsewhere, and numerous bridges and drainage schemes. In 1791 he founded the Society of Civil Engineers, later renamed the Smeatonian Society in his honour.

Science

A Fellow of the Royal Society, Smeaton published research on the power of water and wind and on the properties of cements, rediscovering and improving upon the lost Roman art of making lime mortar that sets under water.
