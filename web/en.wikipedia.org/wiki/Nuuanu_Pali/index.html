Nuʻuanu Pali

LocationOʻahu, Hawaii, United States
RangeKoʻolau Range
SiteNuʻuanu Pali State Wayside

The Nuʻuanu Pali is a section of the windward cliff (pali in Hawaiian) of the Koʻolau mountain range on the island of Oʻahu, Hawaii. The Pali Highway (Hawaii State Highway 61) passes through tunnels bored into the cliff to a lookout, the Nuʻuanu Pali State Wayside, which offers panoramic views of the windward coast, including Kāneʻohe, Kāneʻohe Bay and Kailua. The pass is famous for its strong trade winds, which funnel through the gap in the mountains.

History

The Pali is the site of the Battle of Nuʻuanu, fought in May 1795, one of the bloodiest battles in Hawaiian history. Forces of Kamehameha I, invading from Hawaiʻi island, defeated the Oʻahu army of Kalanikūpule; hundreds of Oʻahu warriors were driven over the cliff edge to their deaths. When the first road over the Pali was built in 1898, workers found numerous skulls and bones at the base of the cliff, believed to be those of the fallen warriors.

Two large stones near the highway, the Hapuʻu and Ka-lae-hau-ola, are said to be stones associated with offerings by travellers crossing the pass in old Hawaiʻi, and the area figures in Hawaiian legends, including stories of the goddess Pele and of the demigod Kamapuaʻa. The modern Pali Highway tunnels opened in 1959, replacing the winding Old Pali Road, parts of which remain as a scenic route.
