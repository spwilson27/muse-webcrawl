Writers Guild of America

The Writers Guild of America (WGA) is the joint name of two United States labor unions representing writers in film, television, radio, and new media: the Writers Guild of America West (WGAW), based in Los Angeles, and the Writers Guild of America, East (WGAE), based in New York City. Together they negotiate the Minimum Basic Agreement (MBA) with the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP), which sets minimum compensation, residuals, credit determination procedures, and working conditions for screen and television writers.

History

The guild descends from the Screen Writers Guild, founded in 1933, which won recognition after struggles with the studios in the 1930s. In 1954 the screen and television writers' organizations reorganized as the Writers Guild of America, with eastern and western branches. During the McCarthy era the guild was entangled in the Hollywood blacklist, a period it later formally apologized for; beginning in the 1990s it worked to restore credits of blacklisted writers.

Strikes

The WGA has struck several times, including in 1960, 1973, 1981, and 1985. The 1988 strike lasted 22 weeks (155 days) over residuals for foreign and videocassette markets. The 2007–08 strike lasted 100 days and centered on residuals for new media (internet streaming and downloads). In 2023 the WGA struck for 148 days (May 2 – September 27), alongside the SAG-AFTRA strike, winning gains on streaming residuals, minimum staffing in television writers' rooms, and protections concerning the use of artificial intelligence.

Credits and awards

The guild arbitrates screenwriting credits (for example "Written by", "Screenplay by", and "Story by") when disputes arise, using its Screen Credits Manual. It also presents the annual Writers Guild of America Awards for film, television, and other media.
