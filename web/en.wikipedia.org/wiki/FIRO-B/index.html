FIRO-B

The FIRO-B (Fundamental Interpersonal Relations Orientation–Behavior) is a psychological test created by William C. Schutz in 1958, originally developed from his work with groups for the US Navy. It measures how a person characteristically relates to others, based on Schutz's theory that interpersonal behaviour is driven by three needs: inclusion, control and affection.

Scales

For each of the three needs, the instrument distinguishes behaviour the respondent expresses towards others from behaviour they want from others, yielding six scales: expressed and wanted inclusion, expressed and wanted control, and expressed and wanted affection (in the modern revision, affection is often reframed as openness). The questionnaire contains 54 items, and results are used mainly for team building, leadership development and coaching rather than clinical diagnosis.

Use and criticism

The FIRO-B is published by The Myers-Briggs Company and is frequently administered alongside instruments such as the MBTI in organizational settings. While popular in training contexts, psychologists have critiqued the depth of its psychometric evidence compared with trait models such as the Big Five.
