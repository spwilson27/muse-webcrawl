Overview

Thomas Telford (1757–1834) was a Scottish civil engineer, architect and stonemason who became the pre-eminent road, bridge and canal builder of Britain's industrial age — nicknamed the "Colossus of Roads". Apprenticed as a stonemason and largely self-taught in engineering, he built or improved some 1,000 miles of road and 120 bridges in the Highlands alone, besides harbours, churches and canals across Britain and Sweden.

Works

His masterpieces include the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct (1805) — the tallest canal aqueduct in Britain, now a World Heritage Site — the Caledonian Canal (1822), the Menai Suspension Bridge (1826, then the world's longest suspension span), the Göta Canal in Sweden, and the London–Holyhead road (the A5). In 1820 he became the first president of the Institution of Civil Engineers. The new town of Telford in Shropshire is named for him.
