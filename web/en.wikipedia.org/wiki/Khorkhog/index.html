Khorkhog

Khorkhog (Mongolian: хорхог) is a traditional Mongolian dish of meat — usually mutton or goat — cooked with hot stones in a sealed container. It is often described as the authentic Mongolian barbecue, in contrast to boodog, in which the meat is cooked inside the animal's skin.

Preparation

Smooth stones are heated in a fire until glowing, then layered with pieces of meat, and often potatoes, carrots, cabbage and onions, inside a metal pot or milk churn, which is sealed and sometimes placed over further heat. The stones cook the meat from within while the vessel steams it; a little water or vodka may be added. After cooking, the hot, greasy stones are passed hand to hand, as holding them is traditionally believed to be good for health. The resulting broth is drunk alongside the meat and vegetables.

Cultural role

Khorkhog is a festive, communal dish prepared for guests and celebrations, particularly in the countryside. Its preparation is labour-intensive and is generally reserved for special occasions.
