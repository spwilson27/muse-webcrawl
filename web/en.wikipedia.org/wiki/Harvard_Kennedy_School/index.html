Harvard Kennedy School

Harvard Kennedy School (HKS)
Founded: 1936
Location: Cambridge, Massachusetts
Parent: Harvard University
Degrees: MPP, MPA, PhD and executive programmes

The Harvard Kennedy School, formally the John F. Kennedy School of Government, is the school of public policy and government of Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

History

Founded in 1936 as the Graduate School of Public Administration with a gift from Lucius Littauer, it was renamed in 1966 for President John F. Kennedy. Its campus on the Charles River includes the Institute of Politics, founded 1966.

Academics and centres

HKS offers the Master in Public Policy, Master in Public Administration (including the mid-career Mason programme), and doctoral degrees, plus executive education for officials worldwide. Research centres include the Belfer Center, Ash Center, Carr Center and the Center for Public Leadership.

People

Alumni include heads of government, ministers, central bankers and heads of international organisations around the world.
