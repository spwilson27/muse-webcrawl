Emission standard

Placeholder image: emissions testing of a motor vehicle

Emission standards are the legal requirements governing air pollutants released into the atmosphere. Emission standards set quantitative limits on the permissible amount of specific air pollutants that may be released from specific sources over specific timeframes. They are generally designed to achieve air quality standards and to protect human life.

Regulated sources

Standards may govern emissions from motor vehicles, power stations, industrial plants, small equipment such as lawn mowers and diesel generators, and other sources. Regulated pollutants commonly include nitrogen oxides (NOx), particulate matter (PM), carbon monoxide (CO), hydrocarbons and sulphur dioxide (SO2); greenhouse gas standards separately cap carbon dioxide (CO2).

Vehicle emission standards

Many countries have adopted vehicle standards modelled on two systems: the European emission standards (Euro 1 through Euro 6, with Euro 7 in preparation), expressed in grams per kilometre, and the United States federal standards administered by the Environmental Protection Agency under the Clean Air Act (Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3), supplemented by California's stricter rules. China (China 6), India (Bharat Stage VI) and Japan operate their own regimes. Test cycles such as the WLTP and requirements for on-board diagnostics and real-driving emissions testing accompany the numerical limits.

Implementation

Manufacturers typically comply using technologies such as catalytic converters, diesel particulate filters, selective catalytic reduction with urea injection, and exhaust gas recirculation. The 2015 Volkswagen emissions scandal, in which "defeat devices" detected test conditions and altered engine behaviour, led to tighter real-world testing and enforcement worldwide.
