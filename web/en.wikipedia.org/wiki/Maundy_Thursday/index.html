Maundy Thursday

Maundy Thursday, also known as Holy Thursday, is the Thursday of Holy Week in the Christian liturgical calendar, commemorating the Last Supper of Jesus Christ with his apostles, the institution of the Eucharist, and Jesus's washing of his disciples' feet. It falls on the day before Good Friday and initiates the Paschal Triduum, the three-day observance of Christ's passion, death and resurrection culminating at Easter.

Name

"Maundy" derives from the Latin mandatum — "commandment" — from the words of Jesus in John 13:34, Mandatum novum do vobis ("A new commandment I give unto you"), spoken after the foot-washing. In many languages the day is simply "Holy Thursday" or, in German, Gründonnerstag.

Observances

The Mass of the Lord's Supper, celebrated in the evening, includes the rite of the washing of the feet (mandatum), in which the priest washes the feet of twelve members of the congregation. In Catholic tradition, the Chrism Mass, at which the bishop blesses the holy oils, is held on this day (or earlier in Holy Week). After Mass, altars are stripped and the Blessed Sacrament is carried in procession to an altar of repose for adoration, recalling Christ's agony in Gethsemane. In Britain, the monarch distributes specially minted Maundy money to elderly recipients at the Royal Maundy service, a ceremony whose roots lie in the medieval royal practice of washing the feet of the poor. Maundy Thursday is a public holiday in many historically Christian countries, including Spain, Denmark, Norway and the Philippines.
