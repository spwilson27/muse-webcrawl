Washington State Capitol

The Washington State Capitol in Olympia, Washington, is the complex of buildings housing the Washington State Legislature and offices of the governor and other officials. Its centrepiece, the Legislative Building, was completed in 1928 and is crowned by the tallest self-supporting masonry dome in the United States, at 287 feet (87 m).

History

Washington became a state in 1889 with Olympia as its capital. After earlier wooden and interim capitols — including a building purchased from Thurston County — the state commissioned New York architects Walter Wilder and Harry White, whose design for a capitol group was selected in 1911. The Temple of Justice was finished in 1920 and the Legislative Building in 1928, just before the Depression halted further construction.

Architecture

The Legislative Building is built of Wilkeson sandstone with a dome supported by four massive corner piers. Its interior features marble finishes, bronze doors depicting scenes from state history, and a 10,000-pound Tiffany chandelier in the rotunda. The campus, laid out with Olmsted-influenced landscaping, also includes the Insurance Building, the John L. O'Brien and John A. Cherberg buildings, and the governor's mansion.
