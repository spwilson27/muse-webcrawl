The Mauna Kea Observatories (MKO) are a group of independent astronomical research facilities located on the summit of Mauna Kea on the island of Hawaii, United States. The facilities are situated in the 525-acre Mauna Kea Science Reserve, established in 1968 and administered by the University of Hawaiʻi. The summit's altitude, aridity, and exceptionally stable atmosphere make it among the best sites in the world for optical, infrared, and submillimetre astronomy.

History and controversy

Site testing in the 1960s, led by Gerard Kuiper, identified Mauna Kea's excellence, and the first major telescope, the UH 2.24 m, was completed in 1970. The reserve has since grown to thirteen working telescopes. Development has been opposed by Native Hawaiians, for whom the summit is sacred, and by environmental groups concerned about the endangered wēkiu bug. The proposed Thirty Meter Telescope became the focus of major protests in 2014 and 2019. In 2022, Hawaiʻi created the Mauna Kea Stewardship and Oversight Authority to transition management of the mountain away from the University of Hawaiʻi.
