Billing (performing arts)

In the performing arts, billing is the order, size and prominence with which performers' and creators' names appear in credits and advertising — on posters, marquees, playbills and film title sequences. Billing signals status and drawing power, and it is a frequent subject of contractual negotiation and dispute.

A marquee: billing made visible in light bulbs.

Top billing and co-billing

The performer named first, usually in the largest type, receives "top billing". Two stars may share the summit through devices such as diagonal or stacked billing, in which names are arranged so that each can be read as first. In film posters, the "billing block" — the dense column of credits at the bottom — follows conventions refined by studio practice and guild agreements.

Special forms

Particular phrases carry meaning: "starring" and "featuring" rank a performer; "and" or "with" before a final name often grants a distinguished position to a veteran actor in a supporting role ("last billing"); "introducing" marks a debut. In theatre, the star's name above the title is the classical mark of pre-eminence, and contracts may specify billing in minute detail — relative type size, placement and parity with co-stars.

Disputes

Billing quarrels are legendary in show business, from vaudeville to the "possessory credit" ("A film by...") contested by directors and writers. Agents negotiate billing alongside salary because credit order affects an artist's future market value.
