Right whale

Family: Balaenidae
Genus: Eubalaena
Species: North Atlantic, North Pacific, southern
Length: 13–19 m
Conservation: Endangered to Critically Endangered

Right whales are three species of large baleen whales of the genus Eubalaena: the North Atlantic right whale (E. glacialis), the North Pacific right whale (E. japonica) and the southern right whale (E. australis). They are distinguished by stocky bodies, no dorsal fin, a strongly arched mouth line, and rough patches of skin called callosities on the head.

Name and whaling

Whalers considered them the "right" whales to hunt: they swim slowly near shore, float when killed, and yield large amounts of oil and baleen. Centuries of whaling nearly exterminated them before international protection in the 1930s.

Conservation

The southern right whale has recovered substantially off Argentina, South Africa and Australia. The two northern species remain among the rarest large whales — only about 370 North Atlantic right whales survive — threatened mainly by ship strikes and entanglement in fishing gear.
