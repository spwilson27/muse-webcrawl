Institutional repository

An institutional repository (IR) is an archive for collecting, preserving, and disseminating digital copies of the intellectual output of an institution, particularly a research institution. Such repositories are an important part of the open access movement: they make scholarly works — journal articles (preprints and postprints), theses and dissertations, conference papers, datasets, reports and teaching materials — freely available online, increasing their visibility and citation.

History and purpose

Institutional repositories proliferated in the early 2000s following the Budapest Open Access Initiative (2002), which identified self-archiving in repositories as one of two strategies for achieving open access. Universities established IRs to showcase their research output, preserve institutional records, and counter the rising costs of journal subscriptions. Many funders and institutions have adopted open-access mandates requiring deposit of published articles in a repository (the "green road" to open access).

Software

Most repositories run on open-source platforms, principally DSpace (developed by MIT and HP), EPrints (University of Southampton), and Fedora-based systems such as Islandora and Samvera. Repositories expose metadata through the OAI-PMH protocol (Open Archives Initiative Protocol for Metadata Harvesting), allowing aggregators such as CORE, BASE and OpenAIRE to index content across thousands of repositories. Registries including OpenDOAR and ROAR track their growth worldwide.

Contents and challenges

Beyond publications, IRs increasingly host research data, digitised special collections, and electronic theses. Persistent challenges include persuading researchers to deposit their work, managing copyright and publisher embargo policies, long-term digital preservation, and integrating repositories with research information systems and funder reporting workflows.
