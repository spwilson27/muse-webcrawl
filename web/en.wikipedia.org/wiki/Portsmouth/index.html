Portsmouth

Country: England
County: Hampshire
Population: about 208,000
Nickname: Pompey

Portsmouth is a port city in Hampshire, on the south coast of England, located mainly on Portsea Island. It is the United Kingdom's only island city and one of its most densely populated places. Portsmouth has been a significant naval port for centuries and is home to the Royal Navy's Portsmouth Naval Base and the Portsmouth Historic Dockyard.

History

Although there was settlement in the area earlier, Portsmouth was founded as a town in 1180 by the Anglo-Norman merchant Jean de Gisors. Henry VII built the world's first dry dock there in 1495, and the town grew with the navy. Charles Dickens was born in Portsmouth in 1812. The city was heavily bombed during the Second World War Blitz because of its dockyard.

Historic Dockyard

The Historic Dockyard preserves several famous ships: HMS Victory, Nelson's flagship at Trafalgar; HMS Warrior, the first iron-hulled warship; and the remains of the Tudor warship Mary Rose, raised from the Solent in 1982 and displayed in its own museum.

Landmarks and culture

The 170-metre Spinnaker Tower, opened in 2005, is the city's modern landmark. Portsmouth's football club, Portsmouth FC (nicknamed "Pompey"), won the FA Cup in 2008. The University of Portsmouth and the ferry port, with services to France, Spain and the Isle of Wight, are also important to the city.
