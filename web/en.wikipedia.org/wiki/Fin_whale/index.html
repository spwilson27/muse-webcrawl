Fin whale

The fin whale (Balaenoptera physalus), also known as the finback whale or common rorqual, is a species of baleen whale and the second-largest animal species on Earth after the blue whale. The longest recorded fin whales were 26–27 metres (85–89 ft), and they can weigh up to roughly 70–80 tonnes.

Description

The fin whale is slender and streamlined, with a distinctive colouration: the body is grey or brown above and white below, and the head is asymmetrically coloured — the right lower jaw is bright white while the left is black. It has a prominent dorsal fin set far back on the body, from which it takes its name. Like other rorquals, it feeds by lunging through schools of krill, fish, and squid, expanding its pleated throat pouch and filtering prey through baleen plates.

Distribution and status

Fin whales are found in all major oceans, mainly in deep offshore waters from polar to temperate latitudes, and are rarer in the tropics. They were heavily hunted during the twentieth-century whaling era — hundreds of thousands were killed — and the species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, with populations recovering slowly since the end of commercial whaling.
