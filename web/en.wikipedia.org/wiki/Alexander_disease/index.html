Alexander disease

SpecialtyNeurology
ClassificationLeukodystrophy
CauseMutations in the GFAP gene
OnsetUsually infancy or early childhood (adult forms exist)
Named afterWilliam Stewart Alexander

Alexander disease is a rare, progressive neurological disorder, one of the leukodystrophies, characterised by the destruction of white matter in the brain together with the formation of Rosenthal fibres — abnormal protein deposits in astrocytes. It is caused by mutations in the gene encoding glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP).

Signs and symptoms

The infantile form, the most common, presents in the first years of life with an enlarged head (macrocephaly), developmental delay, seizures, spasticity, and failure to thrive, and is usually fatal in childhood. Juvenile and adult forms progress more slowly and may feature speech and swallowing difficulties, ataxia, and autonomic problems.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is suggested by characteristic MRI changes in the frontal white matter and confirmed by genetic testing for GFAP mutations. There is no cure; treatment is supportive, and research into reducing GFAP production, including antisense oligonucleotide approaches, is ongoing.
