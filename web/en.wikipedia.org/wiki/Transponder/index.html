Transponder

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A transponder (short for transmitter-responder) is an electronic device that produces a response when it receives a radio-frequency interrogation. Transponders are used in aviation, satellite communications, motor sport, keyless entry systems, road tolling, and oceanography, among many other fields.

Aviation

Aircraft carry transponders that respond to interrogation from secondary surveillance radar, transmitting a four-digit identification code ("squawk") assigned by air traffic control, along with the aircraft's altitude (Mode C). Modern Mode S transponders transmit a unique 24-bit address and extended data, and form the basis of the ADS-B position broadcasts and the TCAS collision-avoidance system. Codes such as 7500 (hijack), 7600 (radio failure) and 7700 (general emergency) have standard meanings worldwide.

Satellites

In a communications satellite, a transponder receives an uplink signal at one frequency, amplifies it, and retransmits it on a different downlink frequency to avoid interference. A typical transponder has a bandwidth of 36 MHz and can carry one analogue television channel or many digital channels and data streams.

Other uses

RFID tags are a form of passive transponder, powered by the interrogating signal, used for inventory, toll collection and access control. Timing transponders in racing record lap times as vehicles pass detection loops, and SONAR transponders are used in undersea positioning and oceanographic research.
