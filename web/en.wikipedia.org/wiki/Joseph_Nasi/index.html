Joseph Nasi

Joseph Nasi (c. 1524 – 1579), born João Micas and known in the Ottoman Empire as Yasef Nassi, was a Portuguese Jewish courtier, financier, and diplomat who rose to become one of the most influential figures at the court of Sultans Suleiman the Magnificent and Selim II. Made Duke of Naxos — the last holder of that title — he promoted Jewish settlement in Tiberias and played a significant, controversial role in Ottoman foreign policy.

From Lisbon to Constantinople

Nasi was born into a converso family of bankers in Lisbon; his aunt was the celebrated Gracia Mendes Nasi. Fleeing the Inquisition, the family moved through Antwerp and Venice before settling in Constantinople in the 1550s, where Joseph openly returned to Judaism, married his cousin Reyna, and built a fortune in trade, tax farming, and finance. His wealth and connections won him the favour of the future Selim II.

Duke of Naxos and statesman

On Selim's accession in 1566, Nasi was granted the Duchy of the Archipelago (Naxos), ruling the Cycladic islands through a deputy. He encouraged the Ottoman attack on Venetian Cyprus (1570–71) — Venetian propaganda blamed him personally for the war — and was reportedly promised the kingship of Cyprus, which he never received. He obtained concessions for Jewish settlement, receiving Tiberias in Galilee, which he rebuilt and repopulated with Jewish refugees, an early proto-Zionist experiment that also aimed at silk production.

Nasi's influence waned after Selim's death in 1574, though he retained his titles and wealth until his death in 1579. In European literature he was long depicted through hostile legends — as a scheming court Jew blamed for wars and for the great fire of the Venetian Arsenal in 1569 — while modern scholarship presents him as a skilled financier and an unusually powerful Jewish statesman of the early modern Mediterranean.
