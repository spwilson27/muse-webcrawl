The Eden Valley is the valley of the River Eden in Cumbria, north-west England. Running between the Lake District fells to the west and the North Pennines to the east, it extends from the river's source near Mallerstang south of Kirkby Stephen, northward through Appleby-in-Westmorland and Penrith to Carlisle and the Solway Plain. It is a predominantly rural, agricultural valley known for its red sandstone villages, castles and wide views.

Geography

The valley is floored by fertile Permian and Triassic red sandstone, which gives the local building stone its distinctive colour, seen in Penrith and Appleby castles and in many village churches. Its sheltered position in the rain shadow of the Lake District makes it one of the driest parts of Cumbria. The Eden, one of England's major salmon rivers, is designated a Special Area of Conservation for its wildlife, including otters, Atlantic salmon and white-clawed crayfish.

Settlements and history

Penrith, the valley's largest town, is an historic market town and former administrative centre of the Eden District, which existed from 1974 until local government reorganisation in 2023 placed the valley in the unitary authority of Westmorland and Furness. Appleby-in-Westmorland, the former county town of Westmorland, is famous for the annual Appleby Horse Fair, one of the largest gatherings of Gypsies and Travellers in Europe, held each June since the eighteenth century. The Settle–Carlisle railway crosses the head of the valley, and the A66 and M6 skirt its northern end. Notable sites include Long Meg and Her Daughters stone circle near Little Salkeld, Eden Valley's prehistoric monuments, and the castles of Brough, Brougham and Pendragon.
