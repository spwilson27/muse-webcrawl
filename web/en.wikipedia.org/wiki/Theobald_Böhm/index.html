Overview

Theobald Böhm (9 April 1794 - 25 November 1881) was a Bavarian flutist, composer, goldsmith, and inventor who redesigned the flute into essentially the instrument used today. A virtuoso flutist at the Bavarian court, Böhm sought to overcome the acoustic limitations of the old conical, small-holed flute.

The Boehm system flute

In 1832 Böhm introduced a flute with larger tone holes placed according to acoustic principles rather than finger convenience, covered by keys operated through a system of ring keys and rods. In 1847 he patented his definitive design: a cylindrical-bore flute (with a tapered, parabolic head joint) made of metal, with large holes and a sophisticated key mechanism.

The Boehm system greatly improved the flute's intonation, power, and evenness across registers. Though initially resisted, it was adopted across Europe and remains the standard for the Western concert flute; its key principles were also adapted to the oboe, clarinet, and other woodwinds. Böhm's treatise on flute construction codified the acoustic reasoning behind the design.
