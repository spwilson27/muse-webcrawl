Scottish people

The Scottish people (Scots: Scots Fowk; Scottish Gaelic: Albannaich) are an ethnic group and nation native to Scotland. Historically, they emerged in the early Middle Ages from an amalgamation of two Celtic-speaking peoples, the Picts and Gaels, who founded the Kingdom of Scotland in the 9th century. In the following centuries, other peoples such as Angles, Normans, and Norse contributed to the ethnogenesis of the Lowland Scots.

History

The Kingdom of Scotland unified under the House of Alpin in the 9th century, gradually extending over Pictland, Strathclyde, and Lothian. The Wars of Scottish Independence preserved Scotland's separate statehood, which lasted until the Acts of Union 1707 joined Scotland with England in the Kingdom of Great Britain, while Scots law, the Church of Scotland, and a distinct education system survived.

Languages

Scottish people historically spoke Scottish Gaelic in the Highlands and Scots in the Lowlands; today English (in its Scottish standard form) is near-universal, with Gaelic and Scots recognised as minority languages.

Diaspora

Large Scottish diasporas exist in the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and elsewhere, with tens of millions claiming Scottish ancestry. Scottish culture — tartan, Highland games, whisky, bagpipes, and writers such as Robert Burns and Walter Scott — has had a global influence.
