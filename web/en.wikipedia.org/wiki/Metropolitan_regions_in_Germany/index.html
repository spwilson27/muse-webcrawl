Metropolitan regions in Germany

The metropolitan regions in Germany (Metropolregionen) are eleven officially designated urban agglomerations recognised by the Standing Conference of Federal and State Ministers responsible for Spatial Planning (MKRO) as "European metropolitan regions". Unlike the strictly administrative regions of some countries, they are planning and marketing cooperations between cities, districts, states and business, intended to raise the international profile of Germany's polycentric urban system.

History

The first metropolitan regions were designated by the MKRO in 1995, covering Berlin–Brandenburg, Hamburg, Munich, Rhine–Ruhr, Rhine–Main, Stuttgart and the Saxon triangle. Further regions were added in 1997 and 2005, bringing the total to eleven. The Rhine–Ruhr region, with over ten million inhabitants, is the largest and one of the biggest polycentric urban areas in Europe.

Functions

The regions have no constitutionally defined powers; instead they coordinate regional planning, transport, tourism, inward investment and lobbying. German spatial planning is deliberately polycentric, so the metropolitan regions are presented as complementary "engines" of development rather than rivals to a single capital.
