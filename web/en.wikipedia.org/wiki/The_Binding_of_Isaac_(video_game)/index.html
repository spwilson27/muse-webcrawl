The Binding of Isaac (video game)

The Binding of Isaac is a roguelike action-adventure video game designed by Edmund McMillen and developed with Florian Himsl, released for Microsoft Windows and OS X in September 2011. Loosely retelling the biblical story of the same name, it follows the child Isaac as he flees through his basement from his mother, who believes God has commanded her to sacrifice him. The game was a surprise commercial and critical success and a landmark of the modern roguelike revival.

Gameplay

The player controls Isaac (and unlockable characters) through procedurally generated, room-based dungeons viewed from above, in the manner of the dungeons of The Legend of Zelda. Isaac attacks by firing his tears in four directions. Each run is different: floors, enemies, bosses, and the hundreds of collectable items — which stack and combine in elaborate, often grotesque ways, visibly mutating Isaac — are randomised. Death is permanent, though persistent unlocks reward repeated play. The game features multiple endings and extensive hidden content.

Development and expansions

McMillen, previously known for Super Meat Boy, built the game in Flash with Himsl as a side project exploring religion, childhood, and roguelike design. An expansion, Wrath of the Lamb, followed in 2012. A full remake, The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth, developed with Nicalis and released in 2014, rebuilt the game in a new engine with expanded content; it received expansions Afterbirth (2015), Afterbirth+ (2017), and Repentance (2021), the last incorporating the acclaimed fan mod Antibirth. Ports reached consoles and mobile platforms.

Reception and legacy

Critics praised the game's depth, replayability, dark humour, and Danny Baranowsky's soundtrack, though its religious themes and child-endangerment premise were controversial in places. The Binding of Isaac is credited with helping popularise the roguelike/roguelite genre in the 2010s, influencing a generation of indie games, and it remains actively played, with daily runs, challenge modes, and a large modding community.
