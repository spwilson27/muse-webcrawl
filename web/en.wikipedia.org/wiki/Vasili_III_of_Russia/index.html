Vasili III of Russia

Vasili III Ivanovich (Russian: Василий III Иванович; 25 March 1479 – 3 December 1533) was Grand Prince of Moscow and all Russia from 1505 until his death in 1533. He was the son of Ivan III and Sophia Palaiologina, and father of Ivan IV, the first Tsar of Russia.

Reign

Vasili continued his father's centralisation and expansion of Muscovy. He annexed the last autonomous Russian lands — Pskov (1510), Ryazan (1521) and Novgorod-Seversk — and captured Smolensk from Lithuania in 1514 during the Fourth Muscovite–Lithuanian War, though later wars were less successful. He also fought the Crimean and Kazan Tatars, building defence lines to the south.

Domestic policy

He strengthened grand-princely authority over the boyars and the church, supported the Josephite position on church landholding, and limited the privileges of appanage princes. New fortifications and the Kitay-gorod wall in Moscow date in part to his reign's initiatives.

Marriages and succession

His first marriage to Solomonia Saburova was childless; he had it annulled, controversially, and married Elena Glinskaya in 1526. She bore Ivan (the future Ivan the Terrible) in 1530 and Yuri in 1532. Vasili died in 1533, leaving Elena as regent for the three-year-old Ivan IV.
