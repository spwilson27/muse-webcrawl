The Colleoni Chapel (Italian: Cappella Colleoni) is a Renaissance mausoleum and chapel in Bergamo, Lombardy, Italy, built between 1470 and 1476 as the funerary monument of the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni, captain-general of the Republic of Venice. Designed and executed by the sculptor-architect Giovanni Antonio Amadeo, it stands beside the basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore in Bergamo's Città Alta and is considered a masterpiece of Lombard Renaissance art.

Architecture and decoration

The chapel's facade is faced in polychrome marble — white, red and black — arranged in geometric patterns, with a large rose window, medallions of Roman emperors, and reliefs of the labours of Hercules and scenes from the Old Testament. Inside, Colleoni's tomb, decorated with reliefs of the Passion and surmounted by a gilded wooden equestrian statue of the condottiero, stands against the wall; opposite is the tomb of his beloved daughter Medea, who died in 1470. The dome frescoes of scenes from the lives of St Mark, St John the Baptist and St Bartholomew were painted by Giovanni Battista Tiepolo in 1732–33.

History

Colleoni demolished the sacristy of Santa Maria Maggiore to make room for his chapel, an act of famous self-assertion. When his tomb was opened in 1969, his remains were found intact, resolving a legend that the body had been lost.
