Christmas Day (25 December) is an annual festival commemorating the birth of Jesus Christ, observed primarily on 25 December as a religious and cultural celebration by billions of people around the world. A feast central to the liturgical year, it follows the season of Advent and initiates the season of Christmastide, which historically in the West lasts twelve days and culminates on Twelfth Night.

History

The date of Jesus's birth is not stated in the Gospels. By the early to mid-4th century, the Western Christian Church had placed Christmas on 25 December, a date later adopted in the East; some Eastern churches celebrate it on 7 January in the Gregorian calendar because they follow the Julian calendar date of 25 December. Proposed explanations include calculation from the traditional date of the Annunciation (25 March) and the alignment of the feast with the Roman winter solstice festivals.

Customs

Popular modern customs include gift-giving (associated with Santa Claus and Saint Nicholas), decorating Christmas trees, attending church services such as Midnight Mass, singing carols, and sharing a festive meal — traditionally roast turkey or ham in many English-speaking countries. Christmas Day is a public holiday in most countries, including many with small Christian minorities.
