Antonio de Padua María Severino López de Santa Anna y Pérez de Lebrón (21 February 1794 – 21 June 1876), usually known as Santa Anna, was a Mexican general, politician and caudillo who dominated Mexican politics for a quarter-century. He served as president of Mexico on multiple occasions between 1833 and 1855, alternating periods of power with retirements to his hacienda, and styled himself at various times liberal, conservative and dictator — he had himself called "His Most Serene Highness" during his final regime.

Career

Born in Xalapa, Veracruz, Santa Anna joined the colonial army as a cadet and fought against insurgents in the War of Independence before switching to Agustín de Iturbide's Army of the Three Guarantees in 1821. He rose to prominence repelling the Spanish invasion attempt at Tampico in 1829, earning the title "Hero of Tampico".

Texas and war with the United States

Having centralised power under the Siete Leyes, Santa Anna personally led the army into Texas in 1836 to crush the Texas Revolution. His forces stormed the Alamo and executed prisoners at Goliad, but on 21 April 1836 Sam Houston surprised and routed his army at San Jacinto; Santa Anna was captured and signed treaties recognising Texan independence. In the Pastry War against France (1838) he lost a leg, which he later had buried with full military honours. During the Mexican–American War (1846–48) he commanded at Buena Vista, Cerro Gordo and the defence of Mexico City, but Mexico lost the war and half its territory under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.

Final years

Recalled from exile in 1853, he ruled as dictator until the Revolution of Ayutla drove him out in 1855 — after he had sold the Mesilla valley to the United States in the Gadsden Purchase. He spent years in exile (in Cuba, the United States, Colombia and St. Thomas), intriguing for a return; a famous anecdote credits his introducing chicle to the United States, seeding the chewing-gum industry. Allowed back under amnesty in 1874, he died in Mexico City in 1876, impoverished and largely forgotten.
