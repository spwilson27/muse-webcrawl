Cloaca Maxima

Cloaca Maxima
LocationRome, Italy
Builtc. 600 BC (open channel); vaulted later
BuildersTarquinius Priscus / Tarquinius Superbus (tradition)
PurposeDrainage of the Roman Forum; later sewage
OutletRiver Tiber

The Cloaca Maxima (Latin for "Greatest Sewer") is one of the world's earliest sewage systems, built in ancient Rome to drain the marshy lowlands between the hills — including the site of the Roman Forum — into the River Tiber. Traditionally credited to the Etruscan kings of Rome around 600 BC, it began as an open channel and was later roofed over with a massive stone vault.

Construction

The main channel runs from the Forum through the Velabrum to the Tiber near the Ponte Rotto. Large stretches were built with huge blocks of tuff and peperino stone fitted without mortar; the vaulted section is wide enough, in places, for a hay cart to pass. Agrippa, as aedile in 33 BC, famously inspected and cleaned the system.

Significance

By draining the Forum valley, the Cloaca made the civic heart of Rome possible. It carried storm water, drainage from public baths and latrines, and street waste. Remarkably, portions of the original drain still function within Rome's modern sewer network after more than 2,500 years.
