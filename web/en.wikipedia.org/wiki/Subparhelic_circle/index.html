Subparhelic circle
TypeAtmospheric optical phenomenon (halo)
CauseReflection of sunlight in plate-shaped ice crystals
LocationBelow the horizon

The subparhelic circle is a rare halo, an optical phenomenon appearing as a white horizontal circle below the horizon. It is the counterpart of the parhelic circle, which passes through the sun at the same altitude above the horizon; the subparhelic circle instead passes through the subsun, the point directly below the sun.

Formation

Like the parhelic circle, the subparhelic circle is produced when sunlight reflects off the vertical faces of plate-shaped ice crystals in cirrus clouds. Rays reflected internally within the crystals form the circle at the elevation of the subsun. Bright spots known as subparhelia may appear on the circle at 120° azimuth from the sun, analogous to the parhelia (sun dogs) seen on the parhelic circle.

Observation

Because it lies below the horizon, the subparhelic circle can only be seen from aircraft, mountains, or other elevated vantage points looking down over ice-crystal clouds. It is seldom photographed and is among the least commonly reported halo phenomena.
