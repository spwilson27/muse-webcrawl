Praecipua

Praecipua is a traditional name historically applied to stars, most notably to 38 Lyncis in the constellation Lynx, and in some older catalogues associated with Alpha Coronae Borealis. The name derives from the Latin praecipua, meaning "principal" or "chief", indicating the principal star of its grouping.

Usage

Traditional star names such as Praecipua come from historical star atlases and catalogues of the 17th to 19th centuries, when Latin designations were commonly given to the brightest stars of the smaller, fainter constellations introduced by astronomers such as Johannes Hevelius, who created the constellation Lynx in 1687.

Modern status

The International Astronomical Union's Working Group on Star Names (WGSN) has standardised proper names for many stars; many traditional names have fallen out of common use and are retained mainly in historical literature. 38 Lyncis is a multiple star system roughly 120 light-years from Earth.
