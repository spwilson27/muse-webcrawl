Beauty and the Beast is a 1991 American animated musical romantic fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. It is based on the 1756 fairy tale of the same name by Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont. The film tells of a prince cursed to spend his days as a hideous monster, who seeks to regain his humanity by earning the love of a young woman, Belle, whom he imprisons in his castle.

Plot

A selfish prince is cursed by an enchantress to live as a Beast until he learns to love another and be loved in return before the last petal falls from an enchanted rose. Years later, the bookish Belle trades her freedom for her father Maurice's, becoming the Beast's prisoner. Aided by the castle's enchanted servants — Lumière the candelabra, Cogsworth the clock, and Mrs. Potts the teapot — Belle gradually sees past the Beast's exterior, while the vain hunter Gaston, rejected by Belle, stirs the village into storming the castle. Belle's declaration of love breaks the spell moments before the last petal falls.

Production

The film was the third of the Disney Renaissance period. Songs were written by composer Alan Menken and lyricist Howard Ashman, who also served as executive producer; Ashman died of AIDS-related complications eight months before the film's release, and the film is dedicated to him. The ballroom sequence pioneered the blending of hand-drawn characters with a computer-generated background.

Reception and legacy

Beauty and the Beast premiered at the New York Film Festival and was a critical and commercial success, grossing $424.9 million worldwide. It became the first animated film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and won Academy Awards for Best Original Score and Best Original Song for its title song. In 2002 it was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry. A Broadway musical adaptation opened in 1994, and a live-action remake was released in 2017.
