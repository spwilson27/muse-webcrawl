Painted churches of Moldavia

LocationMoldavia, northeastern Romania
Built15th–16th centuries
DesignationUNESCO World Heritage Site (1993, extended 2010)

The painted churches of Moldavia are a group of Romanian Orthodox churches in Suceava County, in the historical region of Moldavia in northeastern Romania, famed for the vivid Byzantine frescoes covering their exterior walls. Eight of the churches were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993, with a ninth added in 2010.

History

Most were built under Stephen the Great and his successor Petru Rareș in the 15th and 16th centuries, as votive monuments after victories over the Ottomans. The exterior paintings — including full Last Judgments, the Tree of Jesse, and the Siege of Constantinople — taught Bible stories to villagers and soldiers who could not read.

Notable churches

The best known include Voroneț, called the "Sistine Chapel of the East" for its intense blue pigment and Last Judgment; Sucevița, the largest and most completely painted; Moldovița; Humor; and Probota, the oldest of the group.
