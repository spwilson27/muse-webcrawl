The European People's Party (EPP) is a European political party with Christian-democratic, liberal-conservative, and conservative member parties. Founded in 1976 by Christian democratic parties of the European Community, it has since broadened to include centre-right parties of other traditions. It has been the largest group in the European Parliament since 1999, and EPP parties have long supplied a large share of European Commission presidents (including Ursula von der Leyen), European Council members, and national leaders.

History

The EPP held its founding congress in Brussels in 1976, uniting Christian democrats in the run-up to the first direct European Parliament elections of 1979. After 1989 it expanded eastward and absorbed conservative parties — including Spain's People's Party and, for a time, Britain's Conservatives (who later left to form the ECR). Hungarian party Fidesz was suspended in 2019 and departed the EPP group and party in 2021 amid rule-of-law disputes.

Member parties and positions

Major member parties include Germany's CDU/CSU, France's Les Républicains, Italy's Forza Italia, Poland's Civic Platform, and Greece's New Democracy. The EPP advocates a social market economy, fiscal discipline, strong EU institutions, Atlanticism, and — increasingly since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine — firm support for Kyiv and European defence.
