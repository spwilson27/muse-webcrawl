Immolation

Immolation is the act of offering, or slaying as a sacrificial offering. The word derives from the Latin immolare, originally meaning to sprinkle with meal (mola salsa) in preparation for sacrifice. In extended usage it denotes killing or destruction by fire, and specifically self-immolation, the act of setting oneself on fire.

Religious usage

In Roman religion, immolation was the preliminary rite of sprinkling the victim with salted meal and wine before slaughter. In Catholic theology, the term is applied to the sacrifice of the Mass and to Christ as the sacrificial victim ("the Immolated Lamb"). Hindu tradition historically included sati, the immolation of a widow on her husband's funeral pyre, now outlawed.

Self-immolation

Self-immolation has been used as a radical form of protest, famously by the Vietnamese Buddhist monk Thích Quảng Đức in Saigon in 1963 to protest the persecution of Buddhists, and by the Tunisian street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi in 2010, whose death helped ignite the Arab Spring. In Tibet, more than a hundred monks, nuns and laypeople have self-immolated since 2009 in protest against Chinese rule. The practice has religious roots in some strands of Mahayana Buddhism and Jainism (sallekhana by fasting is a distinct, non-violent-by-fire practice).

Other senses

In immunology, "immolation" is occasionally used metaphorically for the destruction of a pathogen or cell, and the word appears in military and figurative contexts for complete destruction by fire.
