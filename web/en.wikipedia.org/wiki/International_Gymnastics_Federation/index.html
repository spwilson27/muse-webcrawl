International Gymnastics Federation

The FIG governs artistic, rhythmic, trampoline and other gymnastics disciplines

The International Gymnastics Federation (French: Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, FIG) is the international governing body for competitive gymnastics. Founded on 23 July 1881 in Liège, Belgium, it is the oldest international sports federation in the world. Its headquarters are in Lausanne, Switzerland, and it has more than 160 affiliated national federations. The FIG is recognised by the International Olympic Committee and organises the World Gymnastics Championships and the gymnastics events of the Olympic Games.

Disciplines

The FIG governs seven disciplines: artistic gymnastics (the traditional apparatus events for men and women), rhythmic gymnastics, trampoline gymnastics (including tumbling and double mini-trampoline), acrobatic gymnastics, aerobic gymnastics, parkour (added in 2017, controversially, over objections from the parkour community), and "Gymnastics for All", its non-competitive mass-participation programme.

Scoring and the Code of Points

The FIG publishes a Code of Points for each discipline, revised every Olympic cycle. In artistic gymnastics, the code in force since 2006 replaced the famous "perfect 10" system with an open-ended score combining a difficulty (D) score, which credits the content of the routine, and an execution (E) score, which starts at 10 and deducts for errors. The change followed judging controversies at the 2004 Athens Olympics. Skills in the Code are named after the first gymnast to perform them successfully at a major FIG competition.

History

Founded as the Bureau des Fédérations Européennes de Gymnastique by Nicolas Cupérus, the body renamed itself the FIG in 1922 as membership spread beyond Europe. Gymnastics has been part of the Olympic programme since the first modern Games in 1896 (artistic), with rhythmic gymnastics added in 1984 and trampoline in 2000. Long-serving presidents include Charles Cazalet, Yuri Titov and Bruno Grandi; Morinari Watanabe of Japan became president in 2017.
