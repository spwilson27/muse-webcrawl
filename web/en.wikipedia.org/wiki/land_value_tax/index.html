Land value tax

A land value tax (LVT) is a levy on the value of land without regard to buildings, personal property and other improvements upon it. It is also known as a location value tax, a point valuation tax, a site valuation tax, split rate taxation, or a site-value rating.

Rationale

Land value tax has been referred to as "the perfect tax" by some economists because, unlike other taxes, it does not cause economic inefficiency: the supply of land is fixed, so taxing its value does not reduce the amount of land available. Henry George popularised the idea in his 1879 book Progress and Poverty.

Implementation

Land value taxes are levied in various forms in countries including Denmark, Estonia, parts of Australia, and some jurisdictions in the United States such as Pennsylvania.
