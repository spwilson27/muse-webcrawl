Enoch L. Johnson

Enoch Lewis "Nucky" Johnson
Born: 20 January 1883, Galloway Township, New Jersey
Died: 9 December 1968, Atlantic City, New Jersey
Occupation: Political boss, racketeer
Known for: Control of Atlantic City during Prohibition

Enoch Lewis "Nucky" Johnson (1883–1968) was an American political boss and racketeer who dominated politics and organised crime in Atlantic City, New Jersey, for some thirty years. As treasurer of Atlantic County and leader of its Republican machine, Johnson controlled patronage, gambling, prostitution and — during Prohibition — bootlegging in the resort city, taking a cut of nearly every illicit dollar earned there.

Rise to power

Johnson was the son of a county sheriff and rose through Atlantic County Republican politics, succeeding his mentor Louis Kuehnle as boss in 1911 after Kuehnle's conviction for graft. He held a succession of offices — sheriff, county treasurer, and party leader — while rarely seeking the spotlight of higher office himself. From his base, famously including a floor of the Ritz-Carlton on the Boardwalk, he dispensed jobs, charity and favours in exchange for votes and kickbacks.

Prohibition era

Under Johnson, Atlantic City styled itself "The World's Playground", openly flouting Prohibition: liquor flowed into its hotels and speakeasies, and gambling and vice operated under police protection bought through his organisation. He allied with major underworld figures — hosting the Atlantic City Conference of mob leaders in 1929 — and his income, drawn from percentages on gambling and liquor, ran to hundreds of thousands of dollars a year, none of it declared for tax.

Downfall and later life

Federal investigators, prompted by his conspicuous spending, built a tax evasion case with help from IRS agent William Frank and journalist-turned-agent intelligence. Johnson was convicted of income tax evasion in 1941 and served four years of a ten-year sentence at Lewisburg Penitentiary. Released in 1945, he lived quietly in Atlantic City until his death in 1968. He was the model — with the name changed at his estate's insistence — for Nucky Thompson in the television series Boardwalk Empire.
