Luna 9

Luna 9 was an uncrewed space mission of the Soviet Union's Luna programme. On 3 February 1966 it became the first spacecraft to achieve a survivable soft landing on the Moon, and it transmitted the first photographic views from the lunar surface.

Mission

Launched on 31 January 1966, the spacecraft consisted of a cruise stage and a spherical 99-kilogram landing capsule. As it descended toward Oceanus Procellarum, the capsule was ejected, cushioned by an inflatable airbag system, and bounced to rest on the surface before opening four petal-like panels to stabilise itself and expose its instruments and camera.

Results

Over three days, until its batteries ran down on 6 February, Luna 9 returned panoramic images of the surrounding terrain, showing a rough, rock-strewn surface that bore the lander's weight — settling fears that a spacecraft might sink into deep lunar dust. The signals were also picked up by the Jodrell Bank Observatory in England, which famously decoded the picture transmissions using equipment adapted from newspaper picture faxes, publishing the images before their official Soviet release.

Luna 9's landing, at roughly 7.1° N, 64.4° W in Oceanus Procellarum, proved that robotic and later crewed landings were feasible; the American Surveyor 1 followed four months later.
