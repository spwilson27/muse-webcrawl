Crawshay family


Image placeholder: Crawshay family

The Crawshay family were a dynasty of ironmasters who built and ran the Cyfarthfa Ironworks at Merthyr Tydfil in South Wales, one of the largest ironworks in the world during the Industrial Revolution.

History

Richard Crawshay (1739–1810), born in Normanton, Yorkshire, moved to London and then to Wales, where he leased and later owned the Cyfarthfa works, supplying cannon during the wars with France and profiting from the puddling process. His son William Crawshay I and grandson William Crawshay II, known as the "Iron King", expanded the business enormously, building Cyfarthfa Castle in 1824–25 as the family home overlooking the works.

Legacy

Later generations included Robert Thompson Crawshay, the last of the family to run the works, and Rose Mary Crawshay, a noted philanthropist and women's rights campaigner. The family's rise and decline mirrors the fortunes of the Welsh iron industry in the nineteenth century.
