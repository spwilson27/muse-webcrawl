Major League Baseball on ESPN

Major League Baseball on ESPN is the presentation of Major League Baseball (MLB) games by the ESPN family of networks. The title is sometimes seen in the form ESPN Major League Baseball, which is used in on-air graphics.

History

ESPN began televising MLB games in 1990 under a four-year deal, carrying weeknight games and the Sunday night showcase. Its flagship telecast became Sunday Night Baseball, launched in 1990, which for decades held exclusive national rights to the Sunday evening window. ESPN also carried Wednesday Night Baseball and, on ESPN2, additional games, plus extensive coverage of the Home Run Derby during All-Star week, which became one of the network's most popular annual events.

Postseason

From 1996, ESPN networks televised Division Series games, and under the rights agreements in force in the 2010s and 2020s ESPN carried the expanded Wild Card Series/round; its radio arm, ESPN Radio, has broadcast the World Series since 1998. In 2025, MLB and ESPN mutually opted out of the final years of their national contract (which ran through 2028), leaving the network's future package — which continued to include Sunday Night Baseball in 2025 — subject to renegotiation.

Programming and personnel

Studio coverage centres on Baseball Tonight. Notable broadcasters in ESPN's baseball history include Jon Miller and Joe Morgan (the long-running Sunday Night team), Dan Shulman, Karl Ravech, Eduardo Pérez, David Cone, Jessica Mendoza, and Alex Rodriguez. ESPN's coverage is known for innovations such as the K-Zone pitch-tracking graphic.
