The province of Brindisi (Italian: provincia di Brindisi) is a province in the Apulia region of Italy. Its capital is the city of Brindisi, an important Adriatic port that was the terminus of the Roman Via Appia. The province was created in 1927 from territory of the former province of Lecce.

Geography

The province lies on the Salento peninsula's northern edge, bordered by the Adriatic Sea to the east and by the provinces of Bari, Taranto and Lecce. The landscape is a limestone plateau (the Murge) stepping down to a coastal plain of olive groves — some olive trees here are over a thousand years old — and vineyards.

Main towns

Besides Brindisi, municipalities include Ostuni, the famous "white city" of whitewashed houses; Fasano, with the Safari Zoo and the ancient site of Egnazia; Francavilla Fontana; Mesagne; and Cisternino, counted among Italy's most beautiful villages. Tourism in the masserie (fortified farmhouses converted to hotels), agriculture (olive oil, wine such as Ostuni DOC), and the port and petrochemical industries of Brindisi sustain the economy.
