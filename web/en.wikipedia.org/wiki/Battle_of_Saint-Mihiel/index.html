Battle of Saint-Mihiel

Date12–15 September 1918
LocationSaint-Mihiel, Meuse, France
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States and France vs. German Empire
CommandersJohn J. Pershing vs. Georg von Fuchs

The Battle of Saint-Mihiel was a major First World War battle fought from 12 to 15 September 1918, in which the American Expeditionary Forces under General John J. Pershing, supported by the French Army, assaulted the Saint-Mihiel salient, a German-held bulge into the Allied lines south of Verdun. It was the first large-scale operation undertaken principally by the United States Army in the war.

Background

The salient had existed since 1914, cutting the Paris–Nancy railway and threatening Verdun. Pershing insisted on an independent American army operation before joining the general Allied autumn offensives. Unknown to the Allies, the Germans had already begun withdrawing from the salient to a shorter line when the attack fell.

The battle

After a four-hour artillery preparation by some 3,000 guns, American divisions attacked both faces of the salient on 12 September, with a secondary French attack at its tip. Over 1,400 aircraft under Colonel William "Billy" Mitchell — the largest air armada of the war — supported the assault, and light tanks led by Lt. Col. George S. Patton accompanied the infantry. The pincers met at Vigneulles on the morning of 13 September, trapping thousands of Germans; by 16 September the salient was eliminated.

Aftermath

The Americans took about 15,000 prisoners and 443 guns for roughly 7,000 casualties, restoring the railway and freeing the region. The victory validated the AEF as an independent army, though critics noted the disorganised German withdrawal had eased the task. Pershing's forces immediately redeployed westward for the Meuse–Argonne offensive, which began on 26 September.
