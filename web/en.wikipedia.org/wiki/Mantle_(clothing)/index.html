Mantle (clothing)

A mantle is a type of loose garment, usually worn over indoor clothing to serve the same purpose as an overcoat. Technically, the mantle is a type of cape or cloak, typically sleeveless, fastening at the neck or shoulder, and ranging in length from hip-length to sweeping the floor.

History

Mantles were worn from antiquity through the Middle Ages and into the early modern period across Europe. The Greek himation and the Roman pallium were mantle-like wraps. In the medieval period, mantles formed part of ceremonial dress, often lined with fur and decorated with heraldic arms; they survive today in the coronation robes of monarchs, the vestments of some religious orders, and the ceremonial dress of orders of chivalry, such as the mantle of the Order of the British Empire.

Women's fashion

In the 19th century the term was applied to a woman's loose, cape-like outer garment, often trimmed with lace, jet or fringe, and fashionable in various forms in the 1850s–1890s. The word is also used figuratively, as in "taking up the mantle" of a predecessor, a phrase deriving from the biblical account of Elijah's mantle falling to Elisha.
