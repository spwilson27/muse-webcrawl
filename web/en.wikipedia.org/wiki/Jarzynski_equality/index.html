Jarzynski equality

[Image: Non-equilibrium work process]

The Jarzynski equality (JE) is an equation in statistical mechanics that relates free energy differences between two states to the irreversible work done during a transition between the same two states. It was derived by Christopher Jarzynski in 1997. Unlike the familiar statement that work is greater than or equal to the free energy difference (following from the second law of thermodynamics), the Jarzynski equality is an exact relation that holds for processes of arbitrary speed.

Statement

⟨ e−βW ⟩ = e−βΔF

Here β = 1/(kBT) is the inverse temperature, W is the work done on the system during a single realisation of the process, ΔF is the free energy difference between the initial and final equilibrium states, and the angle brackets denote an average over many repetitions of the process.

Significance

By Jensen's inequality, the Jarzynski equality implies the second-law statement ⟨W⟩ ≥ ΔF. More importantly, it provides a practical method of measuring free energy differences from non-equilibrium experiments: for example, force-spectroscopy experiments that stretch single molecules such as RNA can recover equilibrium free energies from repeated irreversible pulling trajectories.

Related results

The equality is closely related to the fluctuation theorems, the Crooks fluctuation theorem, and the older work of Bochkov and Kuzovlev. It has been verified experimentally in systems ranging from colloidal particles in optical traps to single biomolecules, and is used widely in computational chemistry for free energy calculations.
