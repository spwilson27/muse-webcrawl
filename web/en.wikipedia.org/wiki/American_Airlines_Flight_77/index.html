American Airlines Flight 77

American Airlines Flight 77 was a scheduled domestic passenger flight from Washington Dulles International Airport in Virginia to Los Angeles International Airport. On September 11, 2001, the Boeing 757-223 operating the route was hijacked by five al-Qaeda terrorists as part of the September 11 attacks and deliberately crashed into the Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia, at 09:37 EDT. The impact killed all 64 people aboard, including the hijackers, and 125 people in the building.

Flight and hijacking

The flight departed Dulles at 08:20 EDT, delayed about ten minutes. The hijackers, led by pilot-hijacker Hani Hanjour, seized the aircraft about 35 minutes into the flight, moving passengers to the rear; passenger Barbara Olson telephoned her husband, Solicitor General Theodore Olson, reporting the hijacking. Hanjour turned the aircraft back toward Washington.

Crash

At 09:37 the aircraft, descending in a sweeping turn, struck the western facade of the Pentagon at about 530 mph (850 km/h), penetrating the building's outer rings and igniting a fire that caused a section of the facade to collapse. The impacted area was in a wedge recently renovated with blast-resistant features, which limited casualties. Passengers' remains and the flight recorders were recovered from the wreckage.

Aftermath

The Pentagon Memorial, dedicated in 2008, commemorates the 184 victims (excluding the hijackers) with individual benches arranged by birth year. The crash site was repaired within about a year of the attack.
