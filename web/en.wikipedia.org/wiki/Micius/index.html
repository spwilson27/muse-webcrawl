Micius

Micius (Chinese: 墨子; pinyin: Mòzǐ; c. 470 – c. 391 BC), born Mo Di (墨翟), was a Chinese philosopher during the Hundred Schools of Thought period, early in the Warring States era. He founded the school of Mohism, which argued strongly against the Confucianism of his day.

Life

Mozi was born in the State of Lu, the home state of Confucius. Little is known for certain about his life; later tradition portrayed him as a humble craftsman or artisan who rose through learning rather than birth. He may have begun as a follower of Confucian teaching before breaking away to found his own school, criticizing Confucian ritual as wasteful and its emphasis on family as partial.

Philosophy

The core of Mozi's teaching is impartial care (兼愛, jiān'ài), often translated "universal love": people should care for others as they care for themselves, without distinction of family, rank, or state. He argued that partiality is the root of social disorder, theft, and war. Related doctrines include opposition to aggressive warfare, frugality and opposition to elaborate funerals and music, meritocratic promotion of the worthy regardless of birth, and belief in ghosts and spirits who reward good and punish evil.

Mozi also developed an early form of consequentialist reasoning: doctrines should be judged by whether they benefit the people, enrich the state, and increase its population. His followers, the Mohists, organized themselves into a disciplined order under a Grand Master and became renowned experts in defensive siegecraft, traveling to dissuade rulers from attacking weaker states.

The Mozi text

The Mozi is a collection of writings associated with Mozi and his school, comprising 71 chapters of which 53 survive. Its core chapters present his doctrines in triads of parallel essays. Later chapters contain important early Chinese work on logic, definitions, optics, and mechanics, including a description of the camera obscura principle.

Legacy

Mohism was a major rival of Confucianism for several centuries but declined sharply after the Qin unification, and Mozi's ideas were largely neglected until renewed scholarly interest in the Qing dynasty and the modern era. In 2016 China named its first quantum communications satellite Micius in his honour, recognizing his early work on optics.
