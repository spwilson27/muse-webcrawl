Generating set of a group

In abstract algebra, a generating set of a group is a subset of the group such that every element of the group can be expressed as a combination (under the group operation) of finitely many elements of the subset and their inverses. A group with a finite generating set is called finitely generated.

Definitions

If S is a subset of a group G, the subgroup generated by S, written ⟨S⟩, is the smallest subgroup of G containing S; equivalently it consists of all finite products of elements of S and their inverses. S is a generating set for G when ⟨S⟩ = G. Generators together with relations among them give a presentation of the group.

Examples

The integers under addition are generated by {1} (or by {1, −1} without inverses). The symmetric group on n letters is generated by the transpositions (1 2) and the cycle (1 2 ⋯ n). A cyclic group is one generated by a single element. The modular group is generated by the maps S: z ↦ −1/z and T: z ↦ z + 1.

Free groups and rank

A free group on a set S has no relations except those forced by the group axioms; every group is a quotient of a free group on its generating set. For finitely generated abelian groups, the minimal number of generators is related to the rank in the fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups.
