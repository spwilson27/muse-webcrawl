Entropy production

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Entropy production (or generation) is the amount of entropy generated during processes involving an irreversible transformation in a thermodynamic system. The second law of thermodynamics requires that the entropy production of an isolated system can never be negative: it is zero for fully reversible processes and positive for irreversible ones.

Entropy balance

For any system, the rate of change of entropy can be written as an entropy balance: entropy change = entropy transfer across the boundary (with heat and mass flows) + internal entropy production. Whereas entropy can be transferred and can increase or decrease within a system, the internal production term is always non-negative. Processes that produce entropy include heat conduction across a finite temperature difference, friction, unrestrained expansion (free expansion), mixing of different substances, and chemical reactions proceeding at finite rates.

Non-equilibrium thermodynamics

In the thermodynamics of irreversible processes, developed by Lars Onsager and Ilya Prigogine, entropy production is expressed as a sum of products of generalized fluxes and thermodynamic forces: σ = Σ Jk Xk ≥ 0. Prigogine proposed a principle of minimum entropy production for systems in stationary non-equilibrium states near equilibrium, while dissipative structures can arise far from equilibrium in systems with sufficient entropy export.
