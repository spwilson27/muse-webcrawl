Myristica fragrans

KingdomPlantae
OrderMagnoliales
FamilyMyristicaceae
GenusMyristica
SpeciesM. fragrans
Binomial nameMyristica fragrans Houtt.

Myristica fragrans is an evergreen tree indigenous to the Maluku Islands (the "Spice Islands") of Indonesia. It is important as the main source of the spices nutmeg and mace. The tree is also grown commercially in the Caribbean, notably in Grenada, and in the Indian state of Kerala.

The ripe fruit splits open to reveal the seed (nutmeg) wrapped in its lacy red aril (mace).

Description

Myristica fragrans is a small evergreen tree, usually 5–13 m (16–43 ft) tall but occasionally reaching 20 m. The alternately arranged leaves are dark green, 5–15 cm long, and glossy. The species is dioecious: bell-shaped, pale yellow male and female flowers are borne on separate trees. The fruit is a fleshy drupe, pendulous and pear-shaped, 6–9 cm long; when ripe, its husk splits into two halves along a ridge, exposing the shiny purplish-brown seed, 2–3 cm long, surrounded by a bright red, net-like aril.

Products

The seed, ground or grated, is the spice nutmeg; the dried aril surrounding it is mace, a somewhat more delicate spice. Nutmeg butter is expressed from the seed, and essential oils from nutmeg contain compounds such as myristicin, safrole, and elemicin. The pericarp (husk) is used locally to make jams, pickles, and candies.

History of cultivation

Nutmeg was among the most valuable spices of early modern trade, carried from the Banda Islands through Arab and Venetian merchants into Europe. The Dutch East India Company waged brutal campaigns in the 17th century to monopolise production, including the near-extermination of the Bandanese in 1621. To break the monopoly, seedlings were later transplanted to Mauritius, and then to Grenada, which became known as the "Island of Spice".

Toxicity

In culinary quantities nutmeg is harmless, but large doses are psychoactive and toxic, owing largely to myristicin; nutmeg poisoning can cause convulsions, hallucinations, nausea, and palpitations.
