Washington, officially the State of Washington, is a state in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. Named for George Washington, it was formed from the western part of the Washington Territory and admitted to the Union in 1889 as the 42nd state.

Geography

The state is bordered by Oregon to the south, Idaho to the east, British Columbia to the north, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The Cascade Range divides the wet, forested west — home to the Puget Sound metropolis of Seattle — from the drier Columbia Plateau in the east, an agricultural region of wheat and apples. Mount Rainier, at 14,410 feet (4,392 m), is the state's highest peak; other volcanoes include Mount St. Helens, which erupted catastrophically in 1980.

Economy

Washington's economy is driven by aerospace (Boeing), software and technology (Microsoft, Amazon), shipping, agriculture and hydroelectric power from Columbia River dams. It is the nation's leading producer of apples, hops and sweet cherries, and notable for having no state income tax, relying instead on sales and property taxes.

History

Indigenous peoples lived in the region for millennia before European exploration in the late 18th century. After decades of joint Anglo-American occupation, the Oregon Treaty of 1846 fixed the boundary at the 49th parallel. Washington Territory was created in 1853.
