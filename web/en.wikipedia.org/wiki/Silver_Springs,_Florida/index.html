Silver Springs is an unincorporated community and census-designated place (CDP) in Marion County, Florida, United States, east of Ocala. It is famous for Silver Springs, a group of artesian springs feeding the Silver River, and for the historic tourist attraction built around them, widely regarded as one of Florida's first tourist attractions.

History

The springs drew visitors from the 1870s, when glass-bottom boats — developed by Hullam Jones and Phillip Morrell around 1878 — allowed tourists to view the exceptionally clear spring water and its aquatic life. The attraction grew under owners Carl Ray and W.M. "Shorty" Davidson from the 1920s, hosting film productions including Tarzan movies and the television series Sea Hunt. During segregation, a separate attraction, Paradise Park, served African American visitors from 1949 to 1969.

Geography and springs

The Silver Springs group discharges hundreds of millions of gallons of water per day from the Floridan aquifer into the Silver River, a 4.5-mile run joining the Ocklawaha River. Declining flows and nitrate pollution have been environmental concerns since the 2000s.

State park

In 2013 the attraction was absorbed into Silver Springs State Park, combining the former attraction grounds with the existing state park; glass-bottom boat tours continue under state operation.
