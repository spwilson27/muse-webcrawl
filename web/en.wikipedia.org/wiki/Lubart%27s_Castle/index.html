Lubart's Castle, also known as Lutsk Castle, is a medieval fortress in Lutsk, Ukraine, and one of the best-preserved castles in the country. It was built in the mid-14th century by Liubartas, the last ruler of the united Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, on the site of an earlier wooden stronghold above the Styr River.

History

A fortified settlement existed on the site from the 11th century. After Liubartas made Lutsk his residence, the wooden walls were rebuilt in brick with three towers — the Entrance Tower, the Styrova Tower, and the Vladycha (Bishop's) Tower. In 1429 the castle hosted a congress of European monarchs, attended by the Holy Roman Emperor Sigismund and Grand Duke Vytautas of Lithuania, which addressed the defence of Europe against the Ottomans and the coronation of Vytautas. The castle later passed through Lithuanian, Polish, and Russian hands and was repeatedly modified, but retained its medieval core.

Architecture

The castle forms an irregular triangle of brick walls adapted to the terrain, with Gothic and Renaissance elements added over the centuries. The Entrance Tower, housing a museum of bells, is the dominant feature of the Lutsk skyline.

Today

Lubart's Castle is a national architectural monument and museum reserve, hosting exhibitions, art festivals, and the annual "Night at Lutsk Castle" event. Its image appears on the Ukrainian 200-hryvnia banknote.
