Lever House

Lever House
Location: 390 Park Avenue, New York City
Completed: 1952
Architect: Gordon Bunshaft, Skidmore, Owings & Merrill
Style: International Style
Height: 94 m (307 ft), 21 floors

Lever House is a historic office building at 390 Park Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Built from 1950 to 1952 as the headquarters of the soap company Lever Brothers, a subsidiary of Unilever, it was designed by Gordon Bunshaft of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill in the International Style.

Architecture

The building consists of a horizontal two-storey base covering the whole site, raised on pilotis above an open ground-floor plaza and courtyard, with a slender 21-storey slab tower occupying only about a quarter of the lot — a deliberate contrast to the masonry office buildings around it, and a response to the city's 1916 Zoning Resolution. Its façade is a curtain wall of blue-green heat-resistant glass and stainless steel. The open plaza beneath the building set a precedent imitated by the Seagram Building and many later Park Avenue towers.

History and preservation

Lever House was designated a New York City landmark in 1982 and added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1983. After Unilever relocated, the building was renovated around 2000 under new ownership, with its glass curtain wall restored; its ground floor and courtyard have since hosted public art installations and a restaurant.
