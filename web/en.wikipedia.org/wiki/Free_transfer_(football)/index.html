Free transfer (football)

A free transfer, also known as a Bosman transfer, involves a professional footballer transferring from one club to another without a transfer fee. This can occur when a player's contract expires or when it is terminated by mutual agreement. Free transfers became widespread following the Bosman ruling of 1995.

The Bosman ruling

On 15 December 1995 the European Court of Justice ruled in favour of Belgian footballer Jean-Marc Bosman, holding that clubs could not demand a transfer fee for a player whose contract had expired when moving between clubs in different European Union countries, and that quotas on foreign EU players were unlawful. The ruling transformed the labour market for footballers within Europe.

Mechanics

Under FIFA regulations, a player whose contract has expired, or will expire within six months, may negotiate with other clubs, subject to rules on approaches and compensation for young players. Because no fee is paid to the former club, free agents often command higher signing-on fees and wages; clubs, in turn, save on transfer fees but may pay inflated salaries. A player may also be released from his contract and join a new club on a free transfer outside the normal transfer windows, as free agents can usually be signed at any time.

Impact

The free transfer system shifted bargaining power towards players and their agents, encouraged shorter contracts and strategic contract run-downs, and led clubs to sell players before their contracts entered their final year to avoid losing them for nothing.
