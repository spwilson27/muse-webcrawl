The Voith Group is a German multinational family-owned technology company headquartered in Heidenheim an der Brenz, Baden-Württemberg. Founded in 1867, it operates in around 60 countries and is a major supplier of equipment for the paper industry, hydropower generation and industrial drive technology.

History

Johann Matthäus Voith took over his father's locksmith workshop in 1825; his son Friedrich Voith founded the company in 1867 and developed it around wood grinders for the paper industry. The company built its first water turbines in the 1870s — including equipment for early power stations such as Niagara — and added paper machines and, in the twentieth century, hydrodynamic couplings and transmissions. The Voith Schneider Propeller, a cycloidal marine propulsion system invented by Ernst Schneider and commercialised by Voith from the late 1920s, became standard on tugboats and ferries.

Divisions

The group is organised into three divisions. Voith Hydro supplies turbines, generators and automation for hydroelectric plants, including equipment for some of the world's largest stations. Voith Paper builds complete paper production lines, stock preparation, clothing and services; its machines produce a large share of the world's paper. Voith Turbo makes hydrodynamic, mechanical and electric drive and braking systems — automatic transmissions and retarders for buses and trucks, rail vehicle drives and couplings, and marine propulsion. Voith also holds a significant stake in the robotics company KUKA lineage businesses historically, and has invested in digital and industrial-services ventures.
