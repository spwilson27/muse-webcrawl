L.A. Live

L.A. Live is an entertainment complex in the South Park district of downtown Los Angeles, California, adjacent to the Crypto.com Arena and the Los Angeles Convention Center. Developed by the Anschutz Entertainment Group (AEG), the complex opened in stages beginning in 2007 and is centered on Peacock Place, an open-air plaza (originally named Xbox Plaza) surrounded by restaurants, nightclubs, hotels, and performance venues.

Venues

The centerpiece of L.A. Live is the Peacock Theater (formerly the Nokia Theatre and Microsoft Theater), a 7,100-seat concert and awards-show venue that has hosted the Emmy Awards, the ESPY Awards, and American Music Awards. The complex also includes the Grammy Museum, which opened in December 2008, the smaller Club Nokia (later The Novo) concert venue, a Lucky Strike bowling lounge, and a Regal Cinemas multiplex.

Hotels and broadcasting

A 54-story tower shared by JW Marriott and Ritz-Carlton hotels, along with Ritz-Carlton Residences, opened in 2010 and anchors the site. ESPN maintains a broadcasting facility and studios at L.A. Live across from the arena, producing the West Coast edition of SportsCenter there.

Development

The project was conceived as part of the revitalization of downtown Los Angeles around the arena (opened 1999 as Staples Center). The first phase, including the theater and plaza, debuted in October 2007; total development cost was reported at roughly $2.5 billion.
