Honda NSX

The Honda NSX, marketed in North America as the Acura NSX, is a two-seater, mid-engined sports car manufactured by Honda. The name NSX stands for "New Sportscar eXperimental". Its origins date to the 1984 HP-X concept; the production car debuted at the 1989 Chicago Auto Show.

First generation (1990–2005)

Designed by a team led by chief designer Masahito Nakano and executive chief engineer Shigeru Uehara, the NSX was the first production car with an all-aluminium semi-monocoque body. Formula One champion Ayrton Senna contributed to chassis development. Its 3.0-litre (later 3.2-litre) V6 used VTEC variable valve timing and titanium connecting rods, revving to 8,000 rpm. Despite supercar performance, the NSX was praised for everyday reliability and usability.

Second generation (2016–2022)

After a ten-year hiatus, a hybrid second generation was launched, built in Marysville, Ohio. It combined a twin-turbocharged 3.5-litre V6 with three electric motors for a total of 573 hp (600 hp in the 2021 Type S), all-wheel drive, and a nine-speed dual-clutch transmission. Production ended in 2022 with the limited Type S.

Legacy

The NSX forced European supercar makers to improve reliability and ergonomics; Gordon Murray cited it as a major influence on the McLaren F1.
