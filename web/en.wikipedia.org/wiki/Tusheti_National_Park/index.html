Tusheti National Park is a protected area in the Tusheti region of northeastern Georgia, on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus. Together with the Tusheti Protected Landscape and the strict nature reserve, it safeguards one of the largest relatively untouched mountain ecosystems in the Caucasus.

Landscape

The park spans deep gorges of the Alazani headwaters, alpine meadows, pine and birch forests, and peaks rising above 4,000 metres. The region is cut off by snow for much of the year and is reached mainly by the dramatic Abano Pass road from Kakheti.

Wildlife

The park protects East Caucasian tur, chamois, red deer, brown bear, wolf, and lynx, as well as raptors including the bearded vulture and golden eagle. Endemic Caucasian flora cover the alpine zones, and the area is noted for ancient pine forests on north-facing slopes.

Culture

Tusheti is home to the Tush people, whose fortified stone villages — Dartlo, Omalo, Diklo, and Shenako among them — with their medieval defensive towers, form part of the protected landscape. Seasonal sheep transhumance between Tusheti and the Shiraki lowlands remains a defining feature of local life.
