Clifford Geertz

Born: 23 August 1926, San Francisco, USA

Died: 30 October 2006, Philadelphia

Clifford James Geertz was an American anthropologist, the leading proponent of interpretive (symbolic) anthropology, arguing that cultures are webs of significance to be read through "thick description".

Work

Based on fieldwork in Java, Bali and Morocco, his essay collection The Interpretation of Cultures (1973) contains the famous analysis of the Balinese cockfight as a story a society tells itself about itself, and defined religion as a cultural system of symbols. Later works — Negara on the Balinese theatre state, Local Knowledge, Works and Lives — extended his interpretive approach to politics and to anthropological writing itself.

Influence

At the University of Chicago and then Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study, Geertz reshaped anthropology and influenced history, literary studies and social theory, moving the field away from the evolutionism of Edward Tylor toward meaning-centred ethnography.
