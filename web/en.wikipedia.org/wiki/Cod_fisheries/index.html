Cod fisheries

Drying Atlantic cod for stockfish, Norway

Principal species: Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua), Pacific cod (Gadus macrocephalus)

Major regions: North Atlantic, Barents Sea, Grand Banks, North Pacific

Cod fisheries are the commercial fisheries targeting cod — principally Atlantic cod and Pacific cod, and historically Alaska pollock and haddock in some definitions. Cod has been an important economic resource for nations around the North Atlantic for more than a thousand years: dried and salted cod (stockfish and klippfisk) kept for months without refrigeration, making cod one of the first globally traded food commodities and a driver of exploration, settlement and conflict.

History

Viking-age Norwegians traded dried cod from the Lofoten Islands, and by the late Middle Ages the Hanseatic League's Bergen trade made stockfish a staple across northern Europe. After John Cabot's 1497 voyage reported the immense cod stocks of the Grand Banks off Newfoundland, fleets from England, France, Spain and Portugal fished there for centuries; salt cod became a provisioning staple of navies, colonies and the Atlantic slave trade's West Indian plantations. The "Cod Wars" between the United Kingdom and Iceland (1958–1976) were fought over fishing limits in the North Atlantic.

Collapse and management

Industrial trawling in the 20th century massively increased catches, and several stocks collapsed. The most famous is the northern cod stock off Newfoundland, where Canada declared a moratorium in 1992 after the stock fell to about one percent of its historical biomass, putting tens of thousands of people out of work — an event often cited as the classic case of fisheries overexploitation. North Sea and Baltic cod have also suffered severe declines. Some stocks, notably Barents Sea cod managed jointly by Norway and Russia, have recovered under strict quotas, while Atlantic cod remains listed as vulnerable. In 2024 Canada lifted the northern cod moratorium with a small commercial quota, ending the 32-year ban.

Present-day fisheries

The largest contemporary cod fisheries operate in the Barents Sea and around Iceland, Norway, Alaska (Pacific cod) and the Faroe Islands. Management tools include total allowable catches, individual transferable quotas, seasonal closures, mesh-size rules and marine protected areas. Changing ocean temperatures are shifting cod distribution poleward, complicating management.
