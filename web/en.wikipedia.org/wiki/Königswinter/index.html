Königswinter

Königswinter is a town in the Rhein-Sieg district of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, on the eastern bank of the Rhine about 12 km south-east of Bonn. It is best known as the gateway to the Siebengebirge hills and the Drachenfels.

Geography and sights

The town's landmark is the Drachenfels ("Dragon's Rock", 321 m), crowned by a ruined castle of 1138 and reached since 1883 by the Drachenfelsbahn, Germany's oldest cog railway. Near the summit stands Schloss Drachenburg, a flamboyant villa-castle built in 1882–84. Other attractions include the Nibelungenhalle, the Sea Life aquarium and the Petersberg, whose grand hotel hosted the Petersberg Agreement of 1949 and for decades served as the guest house of the German federal government.

History

Königswinter's name ("King's winter") is traditionally linked to a stay by Charlemagne. Vineyards on the Rhine slopes long supported local wine production, and from the 19th century the town became a popular excursion destination for Bonn and Cologne.
