Tianmen Mountain (Chinese: 天门山, Tiānmén Shān, "Heaven's Gate Mountain") is a mountain in Tianmen Mountain National Park, Zhangjiajie, in northwestern Hunan Province, China. Rising to 1,518.6 metres immediately south of Zhangjiajie city, it is famed for Tianmen Cave, an enormous natural arch piercing the mountain's cliff — at roughly 131 metres high, one of the highest natural arches in the world — reached by a monumental staircase of 999 steps.

Tianmen Cave

According to local records, the cave formed in the year 263 CE, when part of the cliff face collapsed to leave the arch; the mountain, previously called Songliang, was renamed Tianmen, "Gate of Heaven". The arch is about 131.5 metres high, 57 metres wide and 60 metres deep, and is often wreathed in mist. Below it, the stairway of 999 steps — nine being an auspicious number in Chinese culture, associated with the emperor and eternity — climbs to the opening; a road with 99 hairpin bends, the Tongtian Avenue, winds up the mountain face beside it.

Access and attractions

The mountain is reached by the Tianmen Mountain Cableway, a gondola lift running about 7.5 kilometres from Zhangjiajie railway station area to the summit, among the longest passenger cableways in the world. On the summit plateau sit the rebuilt Tianmenshan Temple, a Buddhist centre first established in the Ming dynasty, and a network of cliff-hugging plank paths, including glass-bottomed skywalks attached to the cliff face more than a thousand metres above the valley floor.

Stunts and events

The mountain's arch and bends have hosted famous stunts: in 1999 three aircraft flew through Tianmen Cave before a large crowd; in 2011 the wingsuit flyer Jeb Corliss flew through the arch — and subsequent wingsuit flights, drift runs up the 99 bends and a celebrated "stairway" climb by a hybrid SUV in 2018 (the Range Rover Sport Dragon Challenge, ascending the 999 steps at a 45-degree angle) have made the mountain a byword for spectacle as well as scenery.
