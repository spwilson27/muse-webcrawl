Chrysler Building

The Chrysler Building is an Art Deco skyscraper at 405 Lexington Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Completed in 1930, it was briefly the world's tallest building at 1,046 feet (319 m) before being surpassed by the Empire State Building in 1931.

Chrysler Building
Location: 405 Lexington Ave, New York
Completed: 1930
Height: 319 m (1,046 ft), 77 floors
Architect: William Van Alen

Design

Designed by William Van Alen for Walter Chrysler, it is famous for its terraced stainless-steel crown with triangular windows, eagle gargoyles modelled on Chrysler hood ornaments, and ornate lobby. A secretly assembled spire (the vertex) let it overtake 40 Wall Street in the "race into the sky".

History

It housed Chrysler Corporation headquarters until the 1950s and remains an iconic, designated New York City landmark and National Historic Landmark.
