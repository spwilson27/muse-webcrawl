Coram (charity)

Coram is a British children's charity group, headquartered in London. It traces its origins to the Foundling Hospital, established by the philanthropist Thomas Coram in 1739, and is often described as the United Kingdom's first children's charity. Today Coram works in adoption, fostering, children's rights, legal services, education and family support.

History

Thomas Coram, a shipwright and merchant, campaigned for seventeen years for a royal charter to establish a hospital for abandoned children in London. The Foundling Hospital received its charter in 1739 and opened in Hatton Garden before moving to a purpose-built site in Bloomsbury. The artist William Hogarth and the composer George Frideric Handel were early benefactors; Handel's performances of Messiah raised funds for the charity.

In the 20th century the hospital moved out of London, first temporarily to Redhill and then to Berkhamsted in Hertfordshire, and residential care ended. The charity was renamed the Thomas Coram Foundation for Children, and from 1999 simply Coram.

Work today

Coram's activities include Coram Adoption and CoramBAAF (formed from the British Association for Adoption and Fostering), the Children's Legal Centre, Coram Life Education, Coram Family and Childcare, and the Coram Institute for research. Its campus at Brunswick Square, on part of the Foundling Hospital site, also hosts the Foundling Museum, which tells the story of the Hospital and its children.
