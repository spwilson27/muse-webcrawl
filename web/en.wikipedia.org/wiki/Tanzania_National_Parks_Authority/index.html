Tanzania National Parks Authority

Serengeti National Park, one of the parks administered by TANAPA

The Tanzania National Parks Authority (Swahili: Mamlaka ya Hifadhi za Taifa Tanzania), commonly known by its acronym TANAPA, is a parastatal organisation of the Government of Tanzania responsible for the management and conservation of the country's national parks. Its headquarters are in Arusha.

History

TANAPA's origins date to 1959, when the first national parks administration was established under the National Parks Ordinance during the British colonial administration of Tanganyika; Serengeti was the first park gazetted, in 1951. After independence, the organisation was reconstituted as a parastatal body, and the network of parks expanded steadily across the country.

Role and responsibilities

TANAPA manages Tanzania's national parks for conservation, wildlife protection and tourism. Its duties include anti-poaching operations, ecological research and monitoring, maintenance of park infrastructure such as roads and airstrips, community conservation programmes for villages bordering the parks, and promotion of tourism. Revenue comes principally from park entry and concession fees paid by visitors and tour operators.

National parks

TANAPA administers more than twenty national parks, including Serengeti, Kilimanjaro, Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Arusha, Ruaha, Katavi, Gombe, Mahale Mountains, Mikumi, Udzungwa Mountains, Saadani, Rubondo Island, Saanane Island, Burigi-Chato, Ibanda-Kyerwa, Rumanyika-Karagwe, Kigosi, Ugalla River and Nyerere (created from the northern part of the former Selous Game Reserve in 2019). Together the parks cover a substantial fraction of Tanzania's land area and protect ecosystems ranging from savanna and miombo woodland to montane forest and wetlands.

Governance

The authority operates under the Ministry of Natural Resources and Tourism and is led by a Director General and a board of trustees. Conservation areas such as the Ngorongoro Conservation Area are managed separately by the NCAA, and game reserves by the Tanzania Wildlife Management Authority (TAWA).
