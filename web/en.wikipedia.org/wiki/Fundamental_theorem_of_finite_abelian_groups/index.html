Fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups

The fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups, also known as the fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups in its more general form, states that every finite abelian group can be expressed as a direct product of cyclic groups whose orders are prime powers.

Statement

Equivalently, every finite abelian group is isomorphic to a product of cyclic groups Zn1 × Zn2 × … × Znk where each ni divides the next (the invariant factor decomposition), or uniquely to a product of cyclic groups of prime-power order (the elementary divisor decomposition). The two decompositions are related by the Chinese remainder theorem.

Consequences

The theorem completely classifies finite abelian groups: there are finitely many isomorphism types of each order n, counted by multiplying partition numbers of the exponents in the prime factorization of n. It generalizes to finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain, of which abelian groups are the special case over the integers.
