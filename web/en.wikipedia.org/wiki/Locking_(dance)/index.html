Locking (dance)

Locking (originally called Campbellocking) is a style of funk dance and one of the original street dance styles associated with hip hop dance. It was created in Los Angeles in the late 1960s and early 1970s by Don "Campbellock" Campbell. Locking is characterised by fast, distinct arm and hand movements, exaggerated poses, rhythmic freezes — the "locks" from which it takes its name — and playful interaction with the audience, all performed with a loose, funky groove.

History

Campbell developed the style accidentally: trying to reproduce a dance called the Funky Chicken at a party, he kept pausing — "locking" — between moves, and onlookers turned the pauses into part of the dance. He performed on the television show Soul Train and in 1973 formed the dance troupe The Lockers, whose members included Fred Berry (later "Rerun" on What's Happening!!), Toni Basil and Adolfo "Shabba Doo" Quiñones. Their appearances on Soul Train, Saturday Night Live and other shows spread locking nationwide.

Moves and character

Classic locking vocabulary includes the Lock, the Point, the Wrist Twirl, the Muscle Man (Up-Lock), the Scoo B Doo, the Stop and Go, the Funky Scoop and "giving yourself five". Dancers typically wear colourful, flamboyant outfits — striped socks, suspenders, oversized hats and knickers — echoing the original Lockers' look. Unlike the competitive aggression of some other street styles, locking emphasises humour, showmanship and audience engagement; dancers frequently point at, mime to, or high-five spectators mid-routine.

Influence

Locking strongly influenced hip hop dance, pop choreography (including Michael Jackson's routines, which borrowed from street dancers), and later styles such as popping, with which it is often taught alongside though it predates it. It remains a core category in international street dance battles and is practised worldwide, particularly in Japan, France and South Korea.
