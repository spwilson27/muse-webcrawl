Romantic orientation

Romantic orientation, also called affectional orientation, describes the sex or gender with which a person is most likely to have a romantic relationship or fall in love. It is distinguished from sexual orientation because romantic attraction and sexual attraction can differ: a person may, for example, be asexual but homoromantic, or heterosexual and biromantic.

Terms

Common terms mirror those for sexual orientation with the suffix -romantic: heteroromantic, homoromantic, biromantic, panromantic and aromantic (experiencing little or no romantic attraction). The distinction is most often discussed within the asexual community, where the split attraction model separates romantic from sexual attraction, but it is also used by people whose orientations are not split.

Discussion

For most people romantic and sexual orientation coincide, and the usefulness of separating them is debated. Within aromantic and asexual communities the terms are standard vocabulary for describing experience and identity, and some people combine labels — for instance, "aromantic asexual" (aroace).
