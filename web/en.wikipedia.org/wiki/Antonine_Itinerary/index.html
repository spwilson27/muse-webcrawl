Antonine Itinerary

The Antonine Itinerary (Latin: Itinerarium Antonini Augusti) is a famous itinerarium, a register of the stations and distances along the major roads of the Roman Empire. Despite its name linking it to the emperor Antoninus (probably Caracalla), the surviving text is generally dated to the late 3rd century AD, around the time of Diocletian.

Contents

The work lists some 256 routes covering about 53,000 miles (85,000 km) of roads across Europe, North Africa and the Near East, naming staging posts (mansiones and mutationes) and the distances between them, usually in Roman miles. A maritime section, the Itinerarium Maritimum, lists sea routes, ports and crossings in the Mediterranean.

Significance

The Itinerary is one of the principal sources for the geography of the Roman road network and for identifying Roman place-names with modern locations, especially in Roman Britain, where its routes have been used to map the road system and name otherwise unrecorded settlements. It survives only in medieval manuscript copies, the earliest from the 7th–8th centuries.
