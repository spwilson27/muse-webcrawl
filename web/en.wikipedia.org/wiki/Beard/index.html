Beard

A beard is the hair that grows on the chin, upper lip, cheeks and neck of human beings, usually males. Facial hair is a secondary sex characteristic: it typically appears during puberty under the influence of androgens, and its density and distribution vary widely between individuals and populations. Throughout history beards have carried shifting associations with wisdom, masculinity, status, religious devotion or, conversely, with neglect and unconventionality; shaving customs have swung correspondingly between beard-wearing and clean-shaven norms.

History

In ancient Egypt shaving was prized, though pharaohs wore ceremonial false beards. Ancient Greek men commonly wore beards — philosophers were recognisable by them — until Alexander the Great ordered his soldiers shaved, and Roman fashion followed; later emperors such as Hadrian revived the beard. Early medieval European rulers and clergy varied in custom; the Norman clean-shaven style confronted Anglo-Saxon beards at the time of the Conquest. Beards flourished in the sixteenth century, waned in the eighteenth and early nineteenth, then returned triumphantly in Victorian Britain (encouraged partly by soldiers returning from the Crimea and India), before receding again with the mass shaving of the First World War era, when a clean-shaven face was needed to seal a gas mask. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century fashion has cycled repeatedly, with a marked revival of full beards in the 2010s.

Religion and custom

Several religions prescribe or favour beards: many Orthodox Jews, Sikhs (for whom uncut hair, kesh, is an article of faith), Rastafarians, and many Muslim men, following the practice of Muhammad, wear beards; Amish men shave the moustache but grow the beard after marriage. Conversely, some institutions restrict them — many armed forces, police and fire services require short hair or clean-shaven faces for uniformity and equipment seals, with growing accommodation for religious practice.

Styles

Named styles include the full beard, the goatee, the chinstrap, the Van Dyke, mutton chops (sideburns without a chin beard), the circle beard and designer stubble. The study and measurement of beards is termed pogonology, and competitive beard-growing has its own world championships.
