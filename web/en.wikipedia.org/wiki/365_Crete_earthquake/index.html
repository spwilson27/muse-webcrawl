The 365 Crete earthquake was a submarine earthquake that struck off the coast of Crete in the early morning of 21 July AD 365. It is estimated to have had a magnitude of at least 8.5, making it one of the largest known earthquakes in the Mediterranean in historical times.

Geology

The earthquake occurred along the Hellenic arc, where the African plate subducts beneath the Aegean. The western part of Crete was raised by as much as 9 metres (30 feet) relative to sea level — uplifted shorelines and harbours, such as at Phalasarna, remain visible today — while other areas subsided.

Tsunami and impact

The quake generated an enormous tsunami that devastated the eastern Mediterranean. The historian Ammianus Marcellinus described the sea retreating and then surging back at Alexandria, Egypt, where ships were left stranded on rooftops and thousands died. Effects were reported from the Nile Delta to the Adriatic and possibly as far as Spain. Damage and deposition from the event have been identified at sites across the region, and the day of the disaster was commemorated annually in Alexandria for centuries as a "day of horror".
