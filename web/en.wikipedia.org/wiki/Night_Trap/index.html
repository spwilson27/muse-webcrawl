Night Trap

Developer Digital Pictures

Release 1992 (Sega CD)

Genre Interactive movie, survival horror

Mode Single-player

Night Trap is an interactive movie video game developed by Digital Pictures and released in 1992 for the Sega CD. Filmed in 1987 as Scene of the Crime, a proof-of-concept for Hasbro's cancelled NEMO video game console, it casts the player as an agent monitoring security cameras in a house where teenage girls are staying, activating traps to capture invading vampiric creatures known as Augers. The game stars Dana Plato as an undercover agent posing as one of the guests.

Gameplay

The player watches live-action full-motion video feeds from eight rooms and must switch between cameras and trigger traps at the right moments to capture the Augers while ensuring the safety of the house's occupants. Capturing enough Augers and protecting all the characters is required to reach the best ending.

Controversy and legacy

Night Trap became notorious in 1993 when it was featured in United States Senate hearings on video game violence, alongside Mortal Kombat; the controversy contributed to the creation of the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) in 1994. Although retailers pulled the game amid the uproar, its content is comparatively tame. It was ported to several other systems, and a 25th anniversary edition was released in 2017. The game is remembered as a landmark of the full-motion video era and of the video game rating debate.
