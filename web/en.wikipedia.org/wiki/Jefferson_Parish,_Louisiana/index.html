Jefferson Parish, Louisiana

Parish seat: Gretna
Largest community: Metairie
Population: approx. 440,000
Named for: Thomas Jefferson

Jefferson Parish is a parish in the US state of Louisiana, immediately west of New Orleans. It extends from Lake Pontchartrain in the north to the Gulf of Mexico, including Grand Isle, Louisiana's only inhabited barrier island. Its parish seat is Gretna, and its largest community is the unincorporated suburb of Metairie. With roughly 440,000 residents, it is among Louisiana's most populous parishes.

History

The parish was established in 1825 and named for President Thomas Jefferson, who had acquired the territory in the Louisiana Purchase. Its upriver (East Bank) suburbs such as Metairie and Kenner grew rapidly after the Second World War as part of the New Orleans metropolitan area, while its West Bank communities — Gretna, Marrero, Harvey, Westwego — developed around river industries and shipbuilding. The southern portion of the parish is largely water and wetlands, historically tied to fishing, oystering, and oil and gas activity.

Hurricane Katrina

In August 2005 Jefferson Parish suffered severe flooding when its drainage pump stations were evacuated and, later, canal floodwalls on its border with New Orleans failed in the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina. The scandal over the evacuation of pump operators led to litigation and changes in parish emergency policy; Marrero congressman and parish leaders subsequently reformed the system so pumps would be operated remotely and personnel sheltered in place during storms.

Geography and communities

Jefferson Parish includes the cities of Gretna, Kenner, Harahan, Westwego, and Grand Isle, the towns of Jean Lafitte and Grand Isle, and large unincorporated areas including Metairie, River Ridge, Terrytown, and Harvey. Jean Lafitte National Historical Park and Preserve's Barataria Preserve, with boardwalks through cypress swamps, lies in the parish's southern wetlands.
