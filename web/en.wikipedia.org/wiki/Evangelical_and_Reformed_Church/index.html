The Evangelical and Reformed Church (E&R) was a Protestant Christian denomination in the United States, formed in 1934 by the union of the Evangelical Synod of North America and the Reformed Church in the United States. Both bodies were rooted in German immigrant communities. In 1957 the E&R merged with the Congregational Christian Churches to form the United Church of Christ (UCC).

History

The Reformed Church in the United States traced its origins to German Reformed congregations organised in Pennsylvania in the 1720s under leaders such as Michael Schlatter, and to the theology of the Heidelberg Catechism (1563). The Evangelical Synod grew out of the Prussian Union of Lutheran and Reformed churches and was organised among German immigrants in Missouri in 1840. Their 1934 union, consummated in Cleveland, joined two traditions that had already been cooperating closely.

Theology and notable figures

The E&R was known for producing two of the most influential American theologians of the 20th century: the brothers Reinhold Niebuhr and H. Richard Niebuhr, both ordained in the Evangelical Synod tradition, as well as the theologian Paul Tillich, who taught at its seminaries after emigrating from Germany. The denomination maintained seminaries including Eden Theological Seminary in St. Louis and Lancaster Theological Seminary in Pennsylvania.
