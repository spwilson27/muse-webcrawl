Doppelbock (German for "double bock") is a strong lager style that originated with Franciscan monks of the Order of Minims at Munich's Neudeck ob der Au monastery in the 17th century. Brewed as "liquid bread" to sustain the brothers through Lenten fasting, the original beer — Paulaner's Salvator, first served publicly in 1780 — gave the style its naming convention: many doppelbocks carry names ending in -ator, such as Ayinger Celebrator and Augustiner Maximator.

Characteristics

Doppelbocks are rich, malty and full-bodied, from deep gold to very dark brown, with flavours of dark bread, toast, caramel, toffee and dried fruit, subdued hop bitterness, and 7–12% alcohol by volume. They are bottom-fermented and cold-lagered. The stronger spring releases are celebrated at Munich's Starkbierfest ("strong beer festival"), the city's "fifth season".

History

The style descends from the bock beers of Einbeck adapted by Munich brewers; the Paulaner monks' extra-strong version was sold to the public from the late 18th century and became a civic institution. Modern examples are brewed worldwide, and the strongest variants overlap with the "eisbock" style produced by partial freezing.
