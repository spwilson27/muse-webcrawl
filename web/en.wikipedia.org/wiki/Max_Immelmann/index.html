Max Immelmann

Max Immelmann
Born: September 21, 1890, Dresden, German Empire
Died: June 18, 1916, near Sallaumines, France
Service: Imperial German Air Service
Victories: 15

Max Immelmann (21 September 1890 – 18 June 1916) was a German First World War flying ace credited with 15 aerial victories. Along with Oswald Boelcke, he was one of the first German fighter aces, pioneering fighter aviation in the Fokker Eindecker. His name is attached to the Immelmann turn, an aerobatic manoeuvre combining a half loop with a half roll.

Career

Flying the Fokker E.I in 1915, Immelmann scored the first victories of the German air service alongside Boelcke. He became the first pilot awarded the Pour le Mérite, Germany's highest military decoration, after his eighth victory in January 1916; the award became popularly known as the "Blue Max" in his honour.

Death

Immelmann was killed on 18 June 1916 during combat with British F.E.2b aircraft near Sallaumines. The cause of his death remains disputed: British accounts credited the crew that engaged him, while German investigation suggested his interrupter gear failed and he shot off his own propeller.
