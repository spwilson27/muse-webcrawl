Western Satraps

The Western Satraps, or Western Kshatrapas (35–405 CE), were Indo-Scythian (Saka) rulers of the western and central parts of India, in the present-day states of Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Madhya Pradesh. Their state, or states, covered an area of about 350,000 km² and at times included much of western India.

History

The Western Satraps were contemporaries and frequent rivals of the Satavahana dynasty to their south-east. The dynasty was founded by Nahapana (c. 70–95 CE), whose coins and inscriptions are found widely in Maharashtra and Gujarat. His successor line, the Kardamaka dynasty founded by Chashtana (c. 78–130 CE), produced the dynasty's most famous ruler, Rudradaman I, whose Junagadh rock inscription of about 150 CE is the first major inscription in classical Sanskrit.

Decline

The Western Satraps survived for nearly four centuries before being conquered by Chandragupta II of the Gupta Empire around 395–400 CE, after which their silver coinage was imitated by the Guptas in the region.

Coinage

The Western Satraps issued abundant silver coins modelled on earlier Indo-Greek issues, typically bearing the king's bust on the obverse and chaitya and river symbols on the reverse, with legends in Greek script on one side and Brahmi or Kharoshthi on the other. These dated coins are a crucial source for reconstructing the chronology of ancient India.
