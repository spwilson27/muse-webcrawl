Armen Firman

[Image: Armen Firman — Artist's impression of Armen Firman's rocket chair, 17th-century Córdoba]

Armen Firman (died 852), also reported as Armen Firnas in some accounts, is the subject of a 17th-century account describing an early attempt at explosive-propelled flight in Umayyad Córdoba. According to this much later retelling, Firman constructed a chair fitted with rockets and launched himself from a height, covering some distance before crashing and breaking his back. The story is almost certainly conflated with the better-known tale of Abbas Ibn Firnas, the 9th-century polymath of Córdoba said to have made a glide with a winged apparatus in 875.

Source and reliability

The sole source for Armen Firman is the account of the Moroccan historian Ahmed Mohammed al-Maqqari (c. 1578–1632), writing some seven centuries after the supposed event. Al-Maqqari's narrative is primarily about Ibn Firnas; Firman appears in it as a bystander figure who attempted a rocket-assisted flight of his own, imitating or preceding Ibn Firnas. Historians of aviation treat the episode as legend rather than documented fact, and the "rocket chair" motif resembles the later Chinese legend of Wan Hu.

Legacy

Despite its dubious historicity, the tale of Armen Firman is frequently repeated in popular histories of rocketry as one of the earliest (legendary) human rocket flights, and is a fixture of lists of early aviation pioneers.
