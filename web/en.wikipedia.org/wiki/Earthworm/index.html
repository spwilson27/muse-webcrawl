Earthworm

An earthworm is a soil-dwelling terrestrial invertebrate that belongs to the phylum Annelida. The most commonly known species is Lumbricus terrestris. Earthworms are vital to soil health, aerating and enriching it as they burrow and consume decaying organic matter.

Earthworm
Phylum: Annelida
Class: Clitellata
Order: Opisthopora
Diet: Detritivore

Anatomy

Earthworms have a segmented, tube-shaped body, no limbs or eyes, a clitellum used in reproduction, and setae for movement. They breathe through moist skin and have a closed circulatory system with multiple aortic arches acting as hearts.

Ecology and reproduction

They are hermaphrodites that exchange sperm and lay cocoons. Charles Darwin's final book, The Formation of Vegetable Mould Through the Action of Worms (1881), documented their role in soil formation.
