Rugby league is a full-contact team sport played with an oval ball on a rectangular field by two teams of thirteen players. It originated in 1895 in Huddersfield, England, when clubs in the industrial north broke away from the Rugby Football Union over payments to working-class players ("broken-time" payments), forming the Northern Union. Out of this schism grew a distinct code with its own rules, designed to be faster and more spectator-friendly.

Gameplay

Teams advance the ball by running and passing it backwards; the defending team tackles the ball carrier. A side has a set of six tackles to score before possession changes hands. Points are scored by grounding the ball over the try line (a try, 4 points), kicking goals from conversions, penalties or the field of play (2 points), or drop goals. League abolished most set pieces found in rugby union: scrums are uncontested restarts and there are no line-outs or rucks, keeping the ball in play longer.

Geography and competitions

Rugby league is strongest in northern England, Australia (where the National Rugby League is one of the country's most popular competitions), New Zealand and Papua New Guinea — where it is the national sport — with significant play in France and the Pacific Islands. The premier club competitions are Australia's NRL and the northern hemisphere's Super League; the Rugby League World Cup was first held in 1954, and Australia has won the men's tournament a record number of times.

History

The sport turned openly professional early, spread to Australia and New Zealand in 1907–08, and survived bans in Vichy France. Since 1995 English league has been a summer sport under the Super League banner; the 1995–97 "Super League war" in Australia reshaped the game there.
