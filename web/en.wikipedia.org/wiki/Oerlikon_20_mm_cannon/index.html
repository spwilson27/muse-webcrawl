The Oerlikon 20 mm cannon is a series of autocannons developed by the Swiss firm Werkzeugmaschinenfabrik Oerlikon, based on the Becker cannon designed in Germany during the First World War. It became one of the most widely used light anti-aircraft and aircraft weapons of the Second World War, serving with both Allied and Axis forces.

Design

The Oerlikon operates on the blowback principle with advanced primer ignition, allowing a comparatively light and simple mechanism. It was fed from 60-round drum magazines in its classic naval form and fired high-explosive, armour-piercing, and incendiary 20 mm ammunition. Different length barrels gave rise to variants such as the 20 mm/70 (1.8 m barrel) used by the United States Navy.

Service history

Adopted by the Royal Navy and the US Navy from 1940–41, the Oerlikon became the standard Allied light anti-aircraft gun, mounted in enormous numbers on warships of every size and credited with a large share of aircraft brought down by naval gunfire at close range. It also armed aircraft — notably in the form of the Hispano-Suiza developments used in British fighters — and was licence-produced or copied in Germany (as the MG FF) and Japan (as the Type 99). Later Oerlikon designs, such as the 35 mm KD series, descend from the same family of weapons.
