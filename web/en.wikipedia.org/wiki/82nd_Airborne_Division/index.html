The 82nd Airborne Division is an airborne infantry division of the United States Army based at Fort Liberty, North Carolina. Nicknamed the "All American" division, it was originally formed in 1917 with soldiers from all 48 states, giving rise to its distinctive "AA" shoulder patch. It specialises in parachute assault operations and serves as the core of the Army's Immediate Response Force, able to deploy anywhere in the world within 18 hours.

History

The division fought in the First World War in the Meuse-Argonne offensive, where Sergeant Alvin York performed his famous exploit. Reorganised in 1942 as the Army's first airborne division under Matthew Ridgway, it made combat jumps in Sicily, at Salerno, in Normandy on D-Day and in the Netherlands during Operation Market Garden, then fought in the Battle of the Bulge and in Germany in 1945.

Post-war service

The 82nd deployed to the Dominican Republic in 1965, Vietnam, Grenada (1983), Panama (1989), the Gulf War (1991), Kosovo, Afghanistan and Iraq. Elements of the division also responded to Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and supported the 2021 Kabul evacuation. Its paratroopers famously wear maroon berets and jump boots, and the division's motto is "All the way!"
