Myricetin

Chemical formula: C15H10O8
Molar mass: 318.24 g/mol
Appearance: Yellow crystals
Class: Flavonol

Myricetin is a member of the flavonoid class of compounds, specifically a flavonol. It is commonly found in many grapes, berries, fruits, vegetables, herbs, tea, wine and nuts, and is one of the phenolic compounds present in red wine. Its glycoside forms include myricitrin.

Sources

Rich dietary sources of myricetin include cranberries, rutabaga, sweet potato leaves, blueberries, and various herbs and teas. The compound derives its name from the bayberry genus Myrica, from which it was first isolated.

Properties and research

Myricetin is structurally similar to quercetin and kaempferol and shares many of their properties. In laboratory studies it has shown antioxidant activity, and it has been investigated for possible effects on diabetes, inflammation and cancer cell growth, as well as for inhibiting enzymes such as xanthine oxidase. Evidence for health benefits in humans remains limited, and myricetin is sold as a dietary supplement ingredient in some markets.
