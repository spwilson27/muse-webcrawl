Gerhard Gentzen

Gerhard Karl Erich Gentzen (24 November 1909 – 4 August 1945) was a German mathematician and logician who founded modern proof theory.

Gerhard Gentzen
Born: 24 November 1909, Greifswald, Germany
Died: 4 August 1945, Prague
Fields: Mathematical logic, proof theory
Known for: Natural deduction, sequent calculus, consistency proof for arithmetic

Work

In his 1934–35 papers Gentzen introduced natural deduction and the sequent calculus, and proved the cut-elimination theorem (Hauptsatz), showing cut rules can be eliminated from proofs. In 1936 he proved the consistency of Peano arithmetic using transfinite induction up to ε0, a landmark in Hilbert's programme after Gödel's incompleteness theorems.

Life

A student of Hermann Weyl at Göttingen, Gentzen worked there and later in Prague during World War II. Arrested after the Prague uprising in May 1945, he died of starvation in prison in August 1945, aged 35. His work underlies modern structural proof theory and influenced contemporaries of Gottlob Frege's logical tradition.
