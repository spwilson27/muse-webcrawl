Council of People's Commissars

[Image: Council of People's Commissars]

The Council of People's Commissars (Russian: Совет народных комиссаров, abbreviated Sovnarkom) was the highest collegial body of executive and administrative authority of the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic and, from 1923, of the Soviet Union.

History

The Sovnarkom was established by the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets on 8 November 1917 (26 October, Old Style), immediately after the October Revolution. Vladimir Lenin was its first chairman, and its members — known as People's Commissars rather than ministers — headed commissariats for foreign affairs, war, internal affairs, finance, labour and other branches of government. Leon Trotsky was the first People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs.

The first Sovnarkom issued the early decrees of the Soviet government, including the Decree on Peace and the Decree on Land. Under the 1918 Russian constitution and the 1924 Soviet constitution, the council was formally responsible to the Congress of Soviets and its Central Executive Committee.

Transformation

On 15 March 1946 the Council of People's Commissars of the USSR was transformed into the Council of Ministers, and the people's commissariats became ministries, bringing Soviet terminology into line with that of other states. Equivalent councils existed in each union republic.

Chairmen

Chairmen of the USSR Sovnarkom were Lenin (1923–1924), Alexei Rykov (1924–1930), Vyacheslav Molotov (1930–1941) and Joseph Stalin (1941–1946).
