Flight plan

PurposeNotification of intended flight to air traffic services
Filed byPilot or flight dispatcher
FormatsICAO flight plan form; FAA domestic form

A flight plan is a document filed by a pilot or flight dispatcher with a local air traffic services authority prior to departure, declaring the intended route and details of a flight. Flight plans are generally required for instrument flight rules (IFR) operations and for flights in controlled airspace, and are recommended for visual flight rules (VFR) flights over remote areas so that search and rescue can be alerted if the flight becomes overdue.

Contents

A standard ICAO flight plan includes the aircraft identification and type, flight rules and type of flight, number and type of aircraft, wake turbulence category, equipment and surveillance capabilities, departure and destination aerodromes, estimated off-block time, cruising speed and level, the route (including airways, waypoints and standard departures/arrivals), total estimated elapsed time, alternate aerodromes, fuel endurance, persons on board, and emergency and survival equipment.

Filing and activation

Plans may be filed by telephone, radio, or electronically through flight service stations, airline dispatch systems, or apps. An IFR plan is typically activated when the flight receives its clearance; a VFR plan must be activated after takeoff and closed on arrival, failing which an overdue-aircraft search may be initiated. Repetitive flight plans are used for scheduled airline operations on fixed routes.
