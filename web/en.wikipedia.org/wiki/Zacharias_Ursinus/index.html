Zacharias Ursinus (18 July 1534 – 6 May 1583) was a German Reformed theologian and professor, best known as the principal author of the Heidelberg Catechism (1563), one of the most influential confessional documents of the Reformed tradition.

Life

Born Zacharias Baer in Breslau (he Latinised his surname to Ursinus, from ursus, "bear"), he studied at Wittenberg under Philipp Melanchthon, then at Geneva under John Calvin, and in Paris and other centres of learning. In 1561, the Elector Palatine Frederick III appointed him professor of theology at the University of Heidelberg, where he directed the Collegium Sapientiae.

The Heidelberg Catechism

Frederick III commissioned a catechism to unify and instruct his territories in the Reformed faith. Ursinus, then in his late twenties, was its chief author, working with the court preacher Caspar Olevianus, who is often credited as co-author. The catechism, structured in 129 questions and answers arranged for preaching over 52 Lord's Days, is famed for its warm, pastoral tone, opening with the question: "What is your only comfort in life and in death?" It remains a standard of the Dutch Reformed, German Reformed, and many Presbyterian churches.

Later years

When the Lutheran Elector Louis VI succeeded in 1576, Ursinus and other Reformed professors were expelled from Heidelberg. He was invited to Neustadt an der Weinstraße, where he taught at the Casimirianum academy until his death in 1583. His posthumously published lectures and commentaries on the catechism long served as textbooks of Reformed dogmatics.
