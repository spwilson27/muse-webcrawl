List of volcanoes in the United States

The United States is home to more than 160 volcanoes that have been active in the past 10,000 years, monitored by the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). Most are in Alaska, the Cascade Range of the Pacific Northwest, Hawaii, and the western U.S. rift zones. The USGS ranks 18 volcanoes as "very high threat," including Kīlauea, Mount St. Helens, Mount Rainier, and the Yellowstone Caldera.

Notable volcanoes by region

Cascade Range

  • Mount St. Helens (Washington) — catastrophic eruption on May 18, 1980; renewed dome-building 2004–2008.
  • Mount Rainier (Washington) — most topographically prominent U.S. peak; major lahar hazard.
  • Mount Hood (Oregon), Mount Baker (Washington), Mount Adams (Washington)
  • Three Sisters, Mount Jefferson, Newberry Volcano (Oregon)
  • Mount Shasta, Lassen Peak (California) — Lassen erupted 1914–1917.
  • Crater Lake (Mount Mazama) — climactic eruption ~7,700 years ago.

Alaska

  • Novarupta — site of the largest eruption of the 20th century (1912), in Katmai National Park.
  • Mount Redoubt, Augustine Volcano, Mount Spurr, Akutan, Shishaldin, Pavlof, Aniakchak, Mount Veniaminof

Hawaii

  • Kīlauea — one of the world's most active volcanoes; erupting almost continuously 1983–2018 and again since 2020.
  • Mauna Loa — largest shield volcano on Earth; erupted in 2022 after 38 quiet years.
  • Mauna Kea, Hualālai, Kamaʻehuakanaloa (Lōʻihi) — submarine volcano off the Big Island.

Elsewhere

  • Yellowstone Caldera (Wyoming) — supervolcano; last caldera eruption 640,000 years ago.
  • Long Valley Caldera and Mono–Inyo chain (California)
  • Craters of the Moon (Idaho), Sunset Crater (Arizona), Capulin Volcano (New Mexico)
  • Valles Caldera (New Mexico)
