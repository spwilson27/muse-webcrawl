United States foreign aid

United States foreign aid is aid given by the United States government to other governments, international organisations and individuals. The US is the world's largest single donor of official development assistance in absolute dollar terms, although several countries give more as a share of gross national income.

Agencies and programmes

Foreign assistance is administered principally through the United States Agency for International Development (USAID), the Department of State, and the Department of Defense. Major programmes have included the Marshall Plan after the Second World War, the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR), the Millennium Challenge Corporation, and humanitarian food assistance. Aid is broadly divided into military assistance and economic/development assistance.

Recipients and debate

Major recipients have varied over time, with Israel, Egypt, and more recently Ukraine among the largest. Foreign aid is a recurring subject of public debate; polls often show that Americans overestimate its share of the federal budget, which is around one percent.
