Khene

The khene (also spelled khaen, kaen; Lao: ແຄນ) is a Lao mouth organ whose pipes pass through a wooden windchest. It is the national instrument of Laos and is also central to the music of the Lao-speaking Isan region of northeastern Thailand.

Construction

The khene consists of two rows of bamboo pipes of graduated lengths bound together, each fitted with a small free metal reed. The pipes pass through a carved wooden mouthpiece or windchest; the player blows or draws air through it and opens or closes finger holes to sound notes. Instruments commonly have 16 pipes, though 6- and 18-pipe versions exist. Because the free reeds sound on both exhalation and inhalation, the khene can sustain continuous drones and chords.

Music

The khene accompanies mor lam singing and folk dance, playing pentatonic melodies over drones. Skilled players perform solo repertoire imitating the sounds of nature. Khene music of the Lao people was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.
