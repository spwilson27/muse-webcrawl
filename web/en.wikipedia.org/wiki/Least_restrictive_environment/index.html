Least restrictive environment

Least restrictive environment (LRE) is a principle in United States special education law, established by the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), requiring that students with disabilities be educated with their non-disabled peers to the maximum extent appropriate, and that removal to separate classes or schools occur only when education in regular classes cannot be achieved satisfactorily even with supplementary aids and services.

Legal basis

First mandated by the Education for All Handicapped Children Act of 1975 (P.L. 94-142) and retained in IDEA, LRE decisions are made individually by each student's IEP team. Schools must maintain a continuum of placements, from full-time general education with support, through resource rooms and self-contained classes, to separate schools and residential facilities.

Interpretation

Court decisions such as Daniel R.R. v. State Board of Education (1989), Oberti v. Board of Education (1993), and Sacramento City USD v. Rachel H. (1994) developed tests balancing the benefits of inclusion against the effect on the education of the student and classmates. LRE is related to, but not identical with, the inclusion and mainstreaming movements.
