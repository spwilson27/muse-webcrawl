Peter Chen

Peter Pin-Shan Chen (born 3 January 1947) is a Taiwanese-American computer scientist who is best known as the inventor of the entity–relationship (ER) model, introduced in his landmark 1976 paper "The Entity-Relationship Model — Toward a Unified View of Data", published in ACM Transactions on Database Systems.

Career

Chen received his bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from National Taiwan University in 1968 and his PhD in computer science and applied mathematics from Harvard University in 1973. He taught at the MIT Sloan School of Management and UCLA before becoming the M.J. Foster Distinguished Chair Professor at Louisiana State University, where he taught for many years. He later returned to Taiwan and has been associated with National Tsing Hua University and Academia Sinica.

Contributions

The ER model and its graphical notation became foundational to database design and influenced conceptual modeling in software engineering, including information engineering and early UML class diagrams. Chen received numerous honors, including the ACM Software System Award, the IEEE Harry Goode Award, and induction as an ACM Fellow and IEEE Fellow.
