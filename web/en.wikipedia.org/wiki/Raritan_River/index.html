Raritan River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Raritan River is a river in New Jersey, the United States, and the largest river located entirely within the state. It is formed by the confluence of its North and South Branches near Bridgewater and flows generally eastward for about 111 kilometres (69 miles), passing New Brunswick and emptying into Raritan Bay at Perth Amboy, between New York City and the Atlantic Ocean.

Course and watershed

The Raritan's watershed drains roughly 2,800 square kilometres of central New Jersey, including parts of Hunterdon, Somerset, Middlesex, Mercer, Morris, Union and Monmouth counties. Major tributaries include the Millstone River, Green Brook, Lawrence Brook and the South River. The river is tidal below New Brunswick, and its lower valley was historically an important corridor for commerce: the Delaware and Raritan Canal, opened in 1834, ran along its banks connecting the Delaware River with New York Harbor.

Environment

Industrialisation left the lower Raritan heavily polluted, and it remains subject to long-running remediation efforts at former industrial sites along its banks. Cleanup programmes, dam removals and habitat restoration have improved fish passage and water quality, and the river supports recreation including rowing — Rutgers University's crew rows on the Raritan at New Brunswick — fishing and paddling.
