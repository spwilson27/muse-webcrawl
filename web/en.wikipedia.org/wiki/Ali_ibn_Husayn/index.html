Ali ibn Husayn

Ali ibn Husayn ibn Ali (c. 659 – c. 713), known as Zayn al-Abidin ("Ornament of the Worshippers") and al-Sajjad ("the one who constantly prostrates"), was the fourth Shia Imam. The only son of Husayn ibn Ali to survive the Battle of Karbala, he is revered for his piety and supplications.

Bornc. 659 CE, Medina
Diedc. 713 CE, Medina
PredecessorHusayn ibn Ali

Life

Ali was ill during the Battle of Karbala (680) and thus spared, then taken captive with the women of the family to Kufa and Damascus, where tradition holds he and his aunt Zaynab addressed Yazid's court. He lived quietly in Medina thereafter, avoiding the revolts of the period and devoting himself to worship and charity — reportedly feeding the poor of Medina in secret.

Works

The al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya ("The Psalms of Islam"), a collection of supplications attributed to him, is among the most treasured devotional texts in Shia Islam. The Risalat al-Huquq (Treatise on Rights) sets out duties toward God, self and others.

Death

He died in Medina around 713 and is buried in al-Baqi cemetery beside his uncle Hasan. Shia tradition attributes his death to poisoning by the Umayyads.
