Fuji Speedway

Fuji Speedway (富士スピードウェイ, Fuji Supīdowei) is a motorsport race track standing in the foothills of Mount Fuji in Oyama, Suntō District, Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan. It was built in the early 1960s. In the 1980s, Fuji Speedway was used for the FIA World Sportscar Championship and national racing. Originally managed by Mitsubishi Estate Co., Fuji Speedway was acquired by Toyota Motor Corporation in 2000.

History

The circuit was conceived in 1963 as a NASCAR-style superspeedway, but the project was scaled back and completed as a road course, opening in December 1965. Its original layout included a long, steeply banked first corner that produced a series of serious accidents and was abandoned in the mid-1970s in favour of a reconfigured first section.

Fuji hosted the first Formula One Japanese Grand Prix in 1976 — the famous championship decider in heavy rain in which James Hunt won the title after Niki Lauda withdrew — and again in 1977. The circuit returned to the Formula One calendar in 2007 and 2008 after a major renovation designed by Hermann Tilke, before the race moved back to Suzuka.

The circuit today

The modern Grand Prix layout is 4.563 km long, featuring one of the longest main straights in motorsport at about 1.475 km. Fuji Speedway is a regular venue for the FIA World Endurance Championship's 6 Hours of Fuji, Super GT and Super Formula, and it served as the finish venue for the road cycling races at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games.
