Galvanic cell

A galvanic cell or voltaic cell, named after the scientists Luigi Galvani and Alessandro Volta, respectively, is an electrochemical cell in which an electric current is generated from spontaneous oxidation-reduction reactions. A common apparatus consists of two different metals, each immersed in separate beakers containing their respective metal ions in solution that are connected by a salt bridge or separated by a porous membrane.

Volta was the inventor of the voltaic pile, the first electrical battery. The common usage of the word "battery" has evolved to include a single galvanic cell, but the first batteries had multiple galvanic cells.

Description

In its simplest form, a half-cell consists of a solid metal (called an electrode) that is submerged in a solution; the solution contains cations (+) of the electrode metal and anions (−) to balance the charge of the cations. The full cell consists of two half-cells that are usually connected by a salt bridge, which permits the ions in solution to move from one half-cell to the other, completing the circuit.

Two different metals in electrical contact with each other will cause a reaction in which one metal is oxidized and the other is reduced. In a galvanic cell, when an electrode is exposed to an electrolyte at the electrode–electrolyte interface, the atoms of the metal electrode tend to generate ions in the electrolyte solution and leave the electrons at the electrode, causing the electrode to take on a negative charge. Meanwhile, the electrolyte solution tends to deposit dissolved metal ions onto the electrode so that eventually the electrode takes on a positive charge.

Galvanic corrosion

Galvanic corrosion occurs when two different metals have physical or electrical contact with each other and are immersed in a common electrolyte, or when the same metal is exposed to electrolyte with different concentrations. In a galvanic couple, the more active metal (the anode) corrodes at an accelerated rate and the more noble metal (the cathode) corrodes at a slower rate.
