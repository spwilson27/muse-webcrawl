T-tail

ConfigurationEmpennage: horizontal stabilizer mounted on top of the vertical fin
First common useLate 1940s–1950s (Gloster Javelin, Handley Page Victor)
ExamplesBoeing 727, DC-9/MD-80 family, Tupolev Tu-154, C-17 Globemaster III
Main drawbackDeep-stall susceptibility

A T-tail is an aircraft empennage configuration in which the horizontal stabilizer (tailplane) is mounted on top of the vertical fin, so that the tail surfaces form the shape of a capital letter T when viewed from the front or rear. The design became common on jet aircraft from the 1950s onward.

Advantages

Mounting the tailplane high keeps it clear of the disturbed airflow behind the wing and fuselage, and clear of engine exhaust, at most flight conditions, making the elevators more effective and allowing a smaller vertical fin. The arrangement is almost obligatory on rear-engined airliners such as the Boeing 727 and the DC-9 family, where the fin root offers a natural mount, and it is used on military transports such as the C-17 and Il-76 so that the rear fuselage can be freed for a loading ramp.

Disadvantages — the deep stall

The classic hazard of the T-tail is the deep stall (or super stall): at very high angles of attack the wing's wake can blanket the elevated tailplane, destroying elevator authority so that the nose cannot be lowered to recover. The danger was demonstrated tragically by the crash of the BAC One-Eleven prototype in 1963, which killed its crew, and by the loss of a Hawker Siddeley Trident in the Staines crash investigation period; the accidents drove the development of stick shakers and stick pushers, now standard on T-tail types. A T-tail also requires a heavier, stronger fin structure and complicates maintenance access to the tailplane actuators.

Related configurations

Variants include the cruciform tail, where the tailplane sits partway up the fin, and the twin tail and V-tail. Many modern regional jets and business jets retain T-tails, while large wide-body airliners with underwing engines overwhelmingly use conventional low tailplanes.
