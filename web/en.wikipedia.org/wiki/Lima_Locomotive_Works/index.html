The Lima Locomotive Works was an American firm based in Lima, Ohio, that designed and manufactured steam locomotives from the 1870s to the 1940s. With Baldwin and Alco it was one of the "Big Three" US steam builders.

History

The company began as the Lima Machine Works, building agricultural machinery and, from 1878, the geared Shay locomotive patented by Ephraim Shay, of which more than 2,700 were built for logging and industrial railroads. Reorganised as the Lima Locomotive Works in 1916, it became famous in the 1920s for William E. Woodard's "Super Power" designs — beginning with the A-1 Berkshire 2-8-4 of 1925 — which used large fireboxes carried on four-wheel trailing trucks to sustain high horsepower.

Products

Lima built celebrated late-era steam including Berkshire 2-8-4s, the Southern Pacific GS class 4-8-4s, and the last new steam locomotive it delivered in 1949, Nickel Plate Road 2-8-4 No. 779. In 1947 Lima merged with the Hamilton Press & Machinery Company to form Lima-Hamilton, which built a short line of diesel locomotives before production ended in 1951.

Legacy

Preserved Lima locomotives include several operating Berkshires, among them Nickel Plate Road 765 and Pere Marquette 1225.
