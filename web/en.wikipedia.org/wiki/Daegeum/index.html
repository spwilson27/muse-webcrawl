Daegeum

The daegeum (also spelled taegŭm) is a large transverse flute made of bamboo used in traditional Korean music. It has a buzzing membrane that gives it a distinctive timbre and is one of the three bamboo flutes of the Silla period, together with the junggeum and sogeum — collectively called the samjuk.

Description

The daegeum is about 80 cm long, with six finger holes and an additional hole covered by a thin membrane (usually reed or rice paper) under a raised cover; the membrane vibrates sympathetically, producing the instrument's characteristic buzzing sound. Notable repertoire includes solo pieces such as Cheongseonggok and heavy use in court and folk ensembles and in sanjo.

Legend

According to legend, the flute derives from the Manpasikjeok, a magical flute made from bamboo given to King Sinmun of Silla (reigned 681–692), which was said to calm enemies, cure disease and end droughts when played.
