3M Company, originally the Minnesota Mining and Manufacturing Company, is an American multinational conglomerate headquartered in Maplewood, Minnesota. It produces more than 60,000 products used in homes, industry, healthcare and electronics, and is a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

History

Five businessmen founded the company in Two Harbors in 1902 to mine corundum, which turned out to be low-grade anorthosite. The struggling firm pivoted to sandpaper, moved to St. Paul, and grew through inventions such as waterproof sandpaper, masking tape (1925) and Scotch transparent tape (1930). Mid-century innovations included Scotchgard, reflective sheeting and copying technology.

Famous products

The Post-it Note, developed by Spencer Silver and Art Fry and launched in 1980, became one of the world's most recognisable office products. 3M is also a major maker of N95 respirators, which saw surging demand during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Controversies

3M has faced extensive litigation over PFAS "forever chemicals" produced at its plants, agreeing in 2023 to pay up to $10.3 billion to settle claims by US public water systems, and over allegedly defective military earplugs, settled for about $6 billion the same year. In 2024 it spun off its healthcare division as Solventum.
