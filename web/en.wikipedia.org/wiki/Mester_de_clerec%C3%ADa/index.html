Mester de clerecía

Mester de clerecía ("Ministry of Clergy") is a Spanish literary genre that flourished in the 13th and 14th centuries, written by learned clerics and poets in a regular, learned verse form. It is conventionally contrasted with the mester de juglaría, the oral tradition of travelling minstrels.

Characteristics

Works of the mester de clerecía are composed in cuaderna vía: monorhymed quatrains of alexandrine lines (fourteen syllables with a caesura). Unlike the irregular minstrel verse, the clerecía poets counted syllables precisely and wrote for a literate audience, drawing on Latin sources, religious themes and moral instruction.

Principal works

The most important author associated with the genre is Gonzalo de Berceo, the first named poet in the Spanish language, whose Milagros de Nuestra Señora (Miracles of Our Lady) exemplifies the style. Other works include the Libro de Alexandre, the Libro de Apolonio, the Poema de Fernán González, and, in the 14th century, Juan Ruiz's Libro de buen amor.
