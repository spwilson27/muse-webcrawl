Capital punishment in China

Image placeholder: A people's court in China.

Capital punishment is a legal penalty in the People's Republic of China. It is enforced for a wide range of offences, and China is believed to execute more people each year than the rest of the world combined, although exact figures are a state secret and are not published.

Legal framework

The Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China provides the death penalty for offences including murder, drug trafficking, robbery, corruption and other economic crimes, and crimes endangering state security. Since 2007, all death sentences must be reviewed and approved by the Supreme People's Court. Chinese law also provides for a suspended death sentence ("death sentence with reprieve"), typically a two-year suspension after which the sentence is usually commuted to life imprisonment.

Method and scale

Executions are carried out by lethal injection or shooting. Human rights organisations such as Amnesty International estimate executions in the thousands annually, but because statistics are classified, no reliable total exists. Reforms since the 2000s have reduced the number of capital offences — amendments in 2011 and 2015 removed the death penalty for a number of non-violent economic crimes — and official statements indicate the number of executions has declined substantially from earlier decades.

Debate

Public opinion surveys in China generally show majority support for the death penalty, particularly for violent crime and corruption. International human rights groups criticise the system's scale, the use of capital punishment for non-violent offences, and the lack of transparency.
