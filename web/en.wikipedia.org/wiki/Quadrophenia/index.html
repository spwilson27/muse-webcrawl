Quadrophenia is the sixth studio album by the English rock band the Who, released in October 1973. A double album and the group's second rock opera, written entirely by Pete Townshend, it tells the story of Jimmy, a young mod in 1965 London and Brighton whose name and title pun on schizophrenia — split into four personalities, each representing one member of the band.

Story

Jimmy, a disaffected mod working as a beach refuse collector, clashes with his parents, loses his girlfriend and travels to Brighton, scene of the 1964 mod–rocker battles. Disillusioned to find his idol, the "Ace Face", working as a bellboy, he steals a boat, sails to a rock in the sea and, in a moment of clarity amid the rain, reconciles the warring parts of himself.

Music and reception

The album weaves four themes ("Helpless Dancer", "Is It in My Head?", "Bell Boy", "Love, Reign o'er Me") through tracks such as "The Real Me", "5:15", "I'm One" and "Drowned". It reached No. 2 in both the UK and US charts and is routinely ranked among the greatest albums of all time. A film adaptation directed by Franc Roddam appeared in 1979, and a stage musical followed in 2005.
