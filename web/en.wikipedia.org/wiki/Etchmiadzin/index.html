Etchmiadzin (Armenian: Էջմիածին, Ejmiatsin), officially Vagharshapat, is a city in the Armavir Province of Armenia, about 18 km west of Yerevan. It is the seat of the Catholicos of All Armenians, the supreme head of the Armenian Apostolic Church, and is considered the spiritual centre of the Armenian nation.

History

The city was founded as Vardgesavan and refounded as Vagharshapat by King Vagharsh I around the 2nd century AD, serving as a capital of the Kingdom of Armenia. According to tradition, after Armenia became the first Christian state in 301 AD, Saint Gregory the Illuminator had a vision of Christ descending from heaven and striking the ground with a golden hammer to show where a church should be built. The name Etchmiadzin derives from this vision: "the Only-Begotten descended" (ech miatsin).

Etchmiadzin Cathedral

Etchmiadzin Cathedral, built in 301–303 AD on the site indicated by the vision, is considered the oldest cathedral in the world built by a state as its official church. The present structure is largely the result of rebuilding in the 5th and 7th centuries and renovation in the 17th century. Together with nearby churches — Saint Hripsime, Saint Gayane and Shoghakat — it is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The cathedral complex, the Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin, includes the catholicosal residence, a seminary, museums and the treasury, which holds relics such as the Holy Lance (Geghard) and a fragment of Noah's Ark.

Modern city

The city was officially renamed Vagharshapat in 1992, though "Etchmiadzin" remains in universal use. It is a centre of Armenian religious life and pilgrimage, and home to the Gevorkian Theological Seminary.
