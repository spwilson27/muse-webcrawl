The Elizabeth line is a railway line through central London operated by Transport for London (TfL). It runs services on infrastructure built for the Crossrail project — new tunnels under central London — combined with existing lines to Heathrow Airport and Reading in the west, and to Abbey Wood and Shenfield in the east. It is named in honour of Queen Elizabeth II.

History

Crossrail was one of Europe's largest infrastructure projects, authorised in 2008 and constructed from 2009. The central section between Paddington and Abbey Wood opened on 24 May 2022, named the Elizabeth line, with through services across the full route phased in over the following year until the complete timetable began in May 2023. Total project cost rose to roughly £19 billion.

Route and services

The line is 73 miles (118 km) long with 41 stations, ten of them newly built in the central section, including Paddington, Bond Street, Tottenham Court Road, Farringdon, Liverpool Street, Whitechapel and Canary Wharf. In the central core, up to 24 trains per hour run in each direction at peak. Outside the tunnels, trains share tracks with other operators on the Great Western and Great Eastern main lines.

Rolling stock

Services are operated by a fleet of 70 nine-car Class 345 Aventra electric multiple units built by Bombardier (later Alstom) in Derby. Each 200-metre train can carry around 1,500 passengers and features walk-through carriages, air conditioning and step-free access in the central section.

Usage

The Elizabeth line quickly became one of Britain's busiest railways, carrying over 200 million passengers in its first full years of operation and substantially relieving congestion on the Central and District lines of the London Underground.
