Compulsive behavior

Compulsive behavior (or compulsion) is defined by performing an act persistently and repetitively, even when it does not result in an actual reward or pleasure. Compulsive behaviors are typically actions a person feels driven to perform repeatedly, often in an attempt to reduce the apprehension associated with an obsession, despite recognizing — at least in insight-retaining cases — that the behavior is excessive or unreasonable. The classic example is obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD), in which obsessions (intrusive, unwanted thoughts or urges) provoke compulsions such as washing, checking, counting, ordering, or mental rituals performed to neutralize anxiety.

Forms

Compulsions are grouped into checking, contamination/washing, symmetry/ordering, and taboo-thought/mental-ritual dimensions in OCD. Related phenomena include compulsive behaviors in addiction, where drug seeking becomes habitual and stimulus-driven; body-focused repetitive behaviors such as trichotillomania (hair-pulling) and excoriation (skin-picking) disorder; hoarding; compulsive shopping; and repetitive behaviors seen in autism, Parkinson's disease treated with dopamine agonists, and as side effects of some medications.

Mechanisms

Neuroscientific research implicates cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits and the balance between goal-directed behavior and habit systems: in compulsivity, behavior appears to shift from deliberate, outcome-sensitive control toward automatic habits. This framework has led some researchers to view compulsivity as a trans-diagnostic dimension spanning OCD, addiction, and eating disorders, complementary to impulsivity (acting without adequate forethought).

Treatment

First-line treatments include cognitive behavioral therapy with exposure and response prevention (ERP), in which patients confront feared stimuli while refraining from performing rituals, and pharmacotherapy with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs). Refractory cases may be treated with adjunctive antipsychotics or neuromodulation techniques such as deep brain stimulation.
