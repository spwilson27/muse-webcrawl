Building

A building is a structure with a roof and walls standing more or less permanently in one place, such as a house or factory. Buildings come in a variety of sizes, shapes, and functions, and have been adapted throughout history for many factors, from building materials available, to weather conditions, land prices, ground conditions, specific uses, and aesthetic reasons. To better understand the concept, the list of nonbuilding structures uses the opposite term.

Types

Buildings are broadly classified by use as residential (houses, apartment blocks), commercial (offices, shops, hotels), industrial (factories, warehouses), institutional (schools, hospitals, government buildings), and agricultural (barns, silos). They may also be classified by height — low-rise, mid-rise, and high-rise — with a skyscraper being any very tall, continuously habitable building with many storeys.

History

Shelter is a basic human need, and the earliest structures were simple huts and tents. Monumental building began with materials such as mud brick, stone, and timber in early civilisations; the pyramids of Egypt and ziggurats of Mesopotamia demonstrate large-scale organised construction. Roman engineering introduced concrete, arches, and domes at scale. The Industrial Revolution brought iron and steel frames, plate glass, and the elevator, enabling the modern skyscraper. The twentieth century added reinforced concrete, curtain walls, and prefabrication, while contemporary practice increasingly emphasises energy performance and sustainability.

Construction and systems

A building consists of a substructure (foundations) and superstructure (frame, walls, floors, roof), together with internal services: plumbing, electrical systems, heating, ventilation and air conditioning, lifts, and fire protection. Design and construction are governed by building codes that set standards for safety, health, accessibility, and increasingly energy use. The design team typically includes an architect, structural and services engineers, and a builder or general contractor.

Environmental impact

Buildings account for a large share of global energy use and carbon emissions, both in construction materials (embodied carbon, notably in cement and steel) and in operation. Green building standards such as LEED and BREEAM rate buildings on energy, water, materials, and indoor environmental quality, and regulations in many countries now require minimum performance standards for new and existing stock.
