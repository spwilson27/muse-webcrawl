Stream restoration

Stream restoration or river restoration is the set of activities that reverse environmental degradation of a river or stream and restore it to a healthier, more natural state, in order to improve biodiversity, water quality, floodplain function, recreation, and landscape development. Restored streams should be self-sustaining and resilient to floods and droughts.

Approaches

Techniques include removing or modifying dams and culverts to restore fish passage; re-meandering straightened (channelised) reaches; reconnecting floodplains and wetlands; stabilising eroding banks with vegetation and bioengineering; planting riparian buffer strips that filter runoff; and restoring natural flow regimes by changing reservoir releases or reducing abstraction. In urban settings, buried streams are sometimes "daylighted" back to the surface.

Rationale and results

Many waterways were degraded by channelisation, pollution, flow alteration, and removal of riparian vegetation. Restoration is promoted under frameworks such as the EU Water Framework Directive and U.S. Clean Water Act mitigation programmes. Evaluations show mixed results: projects reliably improve habitat structure and aesthetics, but recovery of water quality and biological communities can lag, especially where catchment-scale pressures (nutrients, stormwater, fine sediment) are unaddressed.
