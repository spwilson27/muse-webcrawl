Cizre

Cizre (Kurdish: Cizîr) is a city in Şırnak Province, southeastern Turkey, situated on the Tigris River just west of the tripoint with Syria and Iraq. It has a population of roughly 130,000–150,000 and is predominantly Kurdish.

History

Cizre, known in classical sources as Beth Zabday and in Arabic as Jazirat ibn Umar ("the island of Ibn Umar"), developed on a bend of the Tigris that made it a natural river crossing and trading post between Mesopotamia and Anatolia. In the Middle Ages it was an important centre in Upper Mesopotamia, associated with scholars such as the inventor and engineer al-Jazari (1136–1206), author of the Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices. Local tradition associates the town with the tomb of Noah and with Melayê Cizîrî, the classic Kurdish poet.

Recent history

The city was a focus of the Kurdish–Turkish conflict; prolonged curfews and clashes in 2015–16 caused extensive destruction in several neighbourhoods and many civilian deaths, drawing criticism from the United Nations and human-rights organisations.
