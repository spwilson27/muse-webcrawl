Shanxi

Capital: Taiyuan
Area: ~156,000 km²
Population: ~35 million
Province of: People's Republic of China

Shanxi (Chinese: 山西, "west of the mountains") is a landlocked province in North China, bordered by Hebei to the east, Henan to the south, Shaanxi to the west, and Inner Mongolia to the north. Its capital and largest city is Taiyuan. The province takes its name from its position west of the Taihang Mountains, and most of its territory lies on the Loess Plateau east of the Yellow River, which forms its western and southern borders.

History

Shanxi is one of the cradles of Chinese civilization; the legendary sage-kings Yao, Shun, and Yu are associated with its south. In the Spring and Autumn period it formed the core of the state of Jin, whose partition in 453 BC among the Han, Zhao, and Wei families inaugurated the Warring States period. The city of Pingyao, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserves a Ming- and Qing-era walled city and was a national banking centre: Shanxi merchants (Jinshang) founded China's first draft banks, the piaohao, in the nineteenth century.

Economy

Shanxi has long been China's leading coal-producing province, with roughly a third of the country's known coal deposits, and its economy is heavily oriented toward coal, coke, metallurgy, and chemicals. The province is famous for its aged black vinegar, its hand-cut noodles, and historic sites including the Yungang Grottoes near Datong, Mount Wutai (a sacred Buddhist mountain), and the Hanging Temple near Hunyuan.

Geography

The province is a high plateau, averaging over 1,000 metres in elevation, framed by the Taihang Mountains in the east and the Lüliang Mountains in the west, with the Fen River valley running between them. Its climate is cold and dry, with hot summers and severe winters.
