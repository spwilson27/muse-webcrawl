Angband is a dungeon-crawling roguelike video game derived from Umoria, itself a version of Moria. It is named after Angband, the fortress of Morgoth in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Silmarillion, and the game reproduces many elements of Tolkien's legendarium as the player descends through 100 dungeon levels to defeat Morgoth.

Gameplay

The player creates a character from combinations of race and class and explores procedurally generated levels rendered originally in ASCII characters. The town above the dungeon provides shops for supplies. Gameplay emphasises resource management, identification of magical items, and permanent death: a dead character's save file is lost. Defeating Sauron at level 99 precedes the final confrontation with Morgoth.

Development and variants

Angband was created in 1990 by Alex Cutler and Andy Astrand at the University of Warwick and was subsequently maintained by a succession of developers, becoming one of the most influential open-source roguelikes. Its clean, well-organised codebase spawned dozens of variants (or "*bands"), including ZAngband, ToME (which later evolved into Tales of Maj'Eyal), Sil, and NPPAngband.
