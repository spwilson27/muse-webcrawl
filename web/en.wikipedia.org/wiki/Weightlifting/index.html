Weightlifting (or Olympic weightlifting) is a strength sport in which athletes attempt a maximum-weight single lift of a barbell loaded with weight plates. The two competition lifts are the snatch and the clean and jerk.

The lifts

In the snatch, the barbell is lifted from the floor to overhead arms in a single continuous motion, caught in a squat. In the clean and jerk, the bar is first pulled to the shoulders (the clean), then driven overhead with a split or squat jerk. A third lift, the clean and press, was removed from competition in 1972.

Competition

Athletes compete in bodyweight categories and get three attempts in each lift; the sum of the best successful snatch and clean and jerk is the total. Lifts are judged by three referees. Lifters wear singlets and often use chalk, belts and wrist wraps.

History

Weightlifting featured at the first modern Olympics in 1896 and has been a permanent fixture since 1920; women's events were added at Sydney 2000. Historically dominant nations include the Soviet Union, China, Bulgaria, Turkey and Iran. The sport has repeatedly battled doping scandals, prompting retests of stored samples and sanctions against national federations.

Related disciplines

Weightlifting training underpins many sports, and its lifts are practised in CrossFit. It is distinct from powerlifting (squat, bench press, deadlift) and bodybuilding, which judges physique rather than weight lifted.
