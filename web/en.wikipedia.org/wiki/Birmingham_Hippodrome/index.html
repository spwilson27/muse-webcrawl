The Birmingham Hippodrome is a theatre in the Chinese Quarter of Birmingham, England, on Hurst Street. It is the home of the Birmingham Royal Ballet and, alongside touring productions, hosts performances by Welsh National Opera and DanceXchange. With an audience capacity of around 2,000, it is among the busiest single-stage theatres in the United Kingdom, welcoming well over half a million visitors in a typical year.

History

The theatre opened in 1899 as a circus and variety venue called the Tower of Varieties; it was rebuilt and reopened as a Hippodrome in 1900 under the ownership of theatre magnate Oswald Stoll's circuit. Over the twentieth century it presented variety, revue, musicals, opera and ballet, and hosted stars from Charlie Chaplin to the Beatles. The building was remodelled in the 1960s and extensively redeveloped in 2001, when a new glass-fronted foyer and improved facilities were added.

Present day

The Hippodrome is famous for its annual pantomime, one of the largest in the country, as well as seasons of musicals, dance, opera and comedy. It became the home of Birmingham Royal Ballet in 1990 when the company moved from London's Sadler's Wells. The theatre is operated by an independent charitable trust and receives no regular public subsidy for its core programme.
