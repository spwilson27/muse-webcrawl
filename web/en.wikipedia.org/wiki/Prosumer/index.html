Prosumer

A prosumer is an individual who both consumes and produces. The word is a portmanteau of the words producer and consumer, and it has several distinct usages:

  • in futurology and marketing, a consumer who participates in the production of goods and services (the sense popularised by Alvin Toffler);
  • in media and the internet, a user who both creates and consumes content;
  • in energy, a household or organisation that both consumes and produces electricity, e.g. with rooftop solar panels;
  • in the equipment market, "prosumer" grade products sit between consumer and professional quality.

Toffler's concept

Marshall McLuhan and Barrington Nevitt predicted in 1972 that with electronic technology the consumer would become a producer. Alvin Toffler coined "prosumer" in The Third Wave (1980), arguing that the sharp producer/consumer split of the industrial ("Second Wave") era would soften in the post-industrial "Third Wave": consumers would increasingly design, make or modify what they use, from DIY and self-service to mass customisation. Don Tapscott developed the idea for the digital economy, describing prosumers who co-create products with firms.

Digital media

On the internet, prosumption describes user-generated content: bloggers, video creators, wiki editors and social media users who both produce and consume information. Scholars such as George Ritzer have debated whether digital prosumption empowers users or amounts to unpaid labour exploited by platforms ("free labour").

Energy prosumers

In the electricity sector, a prosumer generates power — typically with rooftop photovoltaic panels — consumes part of it, and exports the surplus to the grid. Prosumers are a central feature of decentralised energy systems and smart grids, raising new questions about tariffs, grid management and energy communities; EU energy legislation (the 2018 Renewable Energy Directive) formally recognises "renewables self-consumers" and citizen energy communities.
