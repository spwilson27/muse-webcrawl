F. Ross Johnson

Frederick Ross Johnson (13 December 1931 – 29 December 2016) was a Canadian businessman, best known as the chief executive of RJR Nabisco whose attempt to take the company private in 1988 triggered the largest leveraged buyout contest of its era, won by Kohlberg Kravis Roberts (KKR) for about US$25 billion. The affair was chronicled in the bestselling book Barbarians at the Gate (1989) by Bryan Burrough and John Helyar, and in a subsequent television film in which Johnson was played by James Garner.

Career

Johnson rose through Canadian business, becoming president of Standard Brands and engineering its 1981 merger with Nabisco, then the 1985 merger of Nabisco with R. J. Reynolds that created RJR Nabisco. As CEO he became a symbol of 1980s corporate extravagance, with a large fleet of corporate jets and generous executive perks that Burrough and Helyar dubbed the "RJR Air Force".

The buyout

In October 1988, after RJR's share price sagged following the launch of the smokeless Premier cigarette, Johnson proposed a management buyout at $75 per share. A bidding war with KKR followed, ending with KKR's winning bid of $109 per share. Johnson left the company in 1989 and later ran his own investment firm, RJM Group. He died of pneumonia in Florida in 2016.
