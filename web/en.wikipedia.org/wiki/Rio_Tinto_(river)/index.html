Rio Tinto (river)

The Rio Tinto (Spanish: Río Tinto, "red river") is a river in southwestern Spain that rises in the Sierra Morena mountains of Andalusia and flows about 100 km to the Gulf of Cádiz at Huelva. It is famous for its deep reddish colour, caused by dissolved iron and other heavy metals.

Acidity and mining

The river runs through one of the largest pyrite and sulfide deposits on Earth, mined for copper, gold, and silver since the era of the Tartessians and Phoenicians, and intensively by the Rio Tinto Company from 1873. Weathering of the ores — both natural and mining-accelerated — gives the water an extremely low pH (around 2), supporting acidophile bacteria and algae that oxidize the metals and colour the river.

Astrobiology

Because extremophile life thrives in its acidic, metal-rich waters, the Rio Tinto is used by NASA and ESA scientists as a terrestrial analogue for conditions on Mars, and its subsurface ecosystem has informed the design of drilling instruments for Mars missions.
