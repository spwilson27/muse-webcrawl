Battle of Fredericksburg

The Battle of Fredericksburg was fought December 11–15, 1862, in and around Fredericksburg, Virginia, during the American Civil War. The Union Army of the Potomac, commanded by Major General Ambrose Burnside, launched repeated frontal assaults against entrenched Confederate defenders under General Robert E. Lee and suffered heavy losses in one of the most one-sided battles of the war.

Background

Burnside, who had replaced George B. McClellan in November 1862, planned to cross the Rappahannock River at Fredericksburg and advance on Richmond before Lee could react. Delays in the arrival of pontoon bridges allowed Lee to concentrate the Army of Northern Virginia on the heights west of the town.

The battle

Union engineers built bridges under fire on December 11, and fighting in the streets preceded the main attacks of December 13. On the Union left, George Meade's division briefly pierced Stonewall Jackson's line before being driven back. On the right, brigade after brigade advanced across open ground toward a sunken road and stone wall at Marye's Heights, defended by James Longstreet's troops; none reached the wall. Union casualties were roughly 12,600, against about 5,300 Confederate.

Aftermath

Burnside withdrew across the river on the night of December 15. The defeat damaged Northern morale, and his disastrous "Mud March" in January 1863 led to his replacement by Joseph Hooker.
