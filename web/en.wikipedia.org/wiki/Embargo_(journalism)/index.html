Embargo (journalism)

In journalism and public relations, a news embargo or press embargo is a request or requirement given by a source that the information or news provided by that source not be published until a certain date or certain conditions have been met. The understanding is that if the embargo is broken by reporting before the embargo period has elapsed, the source will apply sanctions, such as cutting off the offender's access to further information in the future.

Uses

Embargoes are commonly used by scientific journals, government agencies, and companies to give journalists time to prepare coverage of studies, statistics, product launches, and speeches. Journalists who accept material under embargo voluntarily agree to the condition, though the practice is sometimes criticized as a form of information management by sources.

Breaking an embargo

When a news outlet publishes embargoed material early, other outlets holding the material generally consider themselves released from the embargo. Deliberately breaking embargoes can damage a publication's relationship with sources.
