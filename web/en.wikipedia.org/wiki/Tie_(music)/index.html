Tie (music)

In music notation, a tie is a curved line connecting the note heads of two notes of the same pitch, indicating that they are to be played as a single sound with a duration equal to the sum of the individual notes' durations. A tie is similar in appearance to a slur, but a slur joins notes of different pitches and indicates phrasing or legato articulation.

Uses

Ties allow a note to be sustained across a bar line, or to create durations not available from a single note value — for example, a dotted duration across a beat — and they are essential for syncopated rhythms in which a note crosses the middle of a measure. In vocal music, a tie differs from the joining of two syllables under a melisma.

Notation

The tie is drawn as an arc between note heads; when note stems point up the tie is usually placed above, and vice versa. Multiple tied notes can be chained to sustain a pitch over long passages.
