International recognition of Palestine


Map of diplomatic recognition of the State of Palestine.

The international recognition of Palestine concerns the diplomatic recognition of the State of Palestine, proclaimed on 15 November 1988 in Algiers by the Palestine Liberation Organization. A large majority of United Nations member states — more than 140 — recognise Palestine as a state, and Palestine has held non-member observer state status at the United Nations since 2012.

History

Following the 1988 declaration of independence, recognition came rapidly from most Arab, African and Asian states, as well as much of the socialist bloc. In 1993–1995 the Oslo Accords established the Palestinian Authority and mutual recognition between Israel and the PLO, but did not resolve final status issues such as borders, Jerusalem and refugees.

Status at the United Nations

On 29 November 2012 the UN General Assembly voted 138–9 to grant Palestine non-member observer state status, upgrading its representation from an observer entity. Palestine has since joined numerous UN agencies and treaties. In April 2024 a bid for full UN membership was vetoed in the Security Council by the United States, though the General Assembly subsequently affirmed Palestine's eligibility and granted it additional rights of participation.

Recognition since 2023

The Gaza war that began in 2023 prompted a new wave of recognitions, with several Caribbean and European states — including Spain, Ireland and Norway in May 2024 — extending recognition, bringing the total to more than three quarters of UN member states.
