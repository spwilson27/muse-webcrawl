A. P. Giannini

Amadeo Pietro Giannini (May 6, 1870 – June 3, 1949), also known as A. P. Giannini, was an Italian-American banker who founded the Bank of Italy, which later became Bank of America. He is credited as the inventor of many modern banking practices, most notably as one of the first bankers to offer banking services to middle-class Americans, rather than only the wealthy, and to lend on character and future prospects.

Bank of Italy

Giannini, the son of Italian immigrants and a successful produce broker, founded the Bank of Italy in San Francisco in 1904 to serve immigrants and working people ignored by other banks. After the 1906 earthquake he famously rescued funds from the vaults and lent from a plank desk on the waterfront to rebuild the city. He pioneered statewide branch banking in California.

Bank of America and legacy

His growing network was renamed Bank of America in 1930. Giannini financed industries from California agriculture and water projects to Hollywood films and the Golden Gate Bridge bonds during the Depression. He also established the Giannini Foundation. His populist branch-banking model reshaped American retail banking alongside institutions such as Wells Fargo and what became Citigroup.
