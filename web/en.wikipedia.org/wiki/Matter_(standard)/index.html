Matter (standard)

Matter, formerly Project Connected Home over IP (Project CHIP), is a royalty-free connectivity standard for smart-home and Internet of Things devices, designed to improve interoperability and security across manufacturers and ecosystems. It is developed by the Connectivity Standards Alliance (CSA), whose members include Apple, Google, Amazon, Samsung, and hundreds of other companies. Matter runs as an application layer over existing network technologies, principally Thread, Wi-Fi, and Ethernet, using IPv6.

History

The project was announced in December 2019 by the Zigbee Alliance (later renamed the Connectivity Standards Alliance) together with Amazon, Apple, and Google. The first specification, Matter 1.0, was released on 4 October 2022, accompanied by a certification programme and an open-source reference implementation (SDK) hosted on GitHub. Subsequent releases extended the standard: version 1.1 (2023) improved support for battery-powered devices, 1.2 (2023) added device types such as robot vacuums, refrigerators, and air conditioners, 1.3 (2024) added energy reporting and appliances including ovens and cooktops, and 1.4 (2024) broadened energy-management support for solar panels, batteries, and water heaters.

Technical overview

Matter devices are commissioned using setup codes and passcodes, with device attestation certificates establishing authenticity. Communication is encrypted, local-first control is required — devices must work without a cloud connection — and "multi-admin" support lets a device belong to several ecosystems simultaneously. Low-power mesh networking is provided by Thread, while a Matter bridge can bring older devices using protocols such as Zigbee or Z-Wave into a Matter fabric.

Adoption

Major platforms including Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, and Samsung SmartThings support Matter, and certified products span lighting, locks, thermostats, sensors, televisions, and appliances.
