Navan Fort

Navan Fort (Irish: Eamhain Mhacha) is an ancient ceremonial site near Armagh in Northern Ireland. It is traditionally identified as the royal seat of the kings of Ulster and the capital of the mythical Ulaid, and features prominently in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, where it is the court of King Conchobar mac Nessa and his Red Branch warriors.

The site

The monument occupies a hilltop enclosed by a large circular bank and ditch about 250 metres across — unusually, the ditch lies inside the bank, indicating a ceremonial rather than defensive purpose. Within stands a great flat-topped mound some 50 metres wide. Excavations in the 1960s and 1970s revealed that the mound covers a remarkable sequence of Iron Age structures, culminating in a huge circular timber building about 40 metres in diameter, built — according to dendrochronology — in 95/94 BCE. This building was filled with a cairn of stones, deliberately burned, and then buried under turf, apparently in a single great ritual event.

Mythology and history

The name Eamhain Mhacha is explained in legend by the goddess or queen Macha, said to have marked out the fort's outline with her brooch after racing the king's horses while heavily pregnant. The site is linked in the tales with Cú Chulainn and the events of the Táin Bó Cúailnge. It forms part of a wider ritual landscape that includes Haughey's Fort, the King's Stables (an artificial pool that yielded votive deposits, including a Barbary ape skull) and Loughnashade.

Visitor centre

The Navan Centre interprets the site and its mythology for visitors. Navan Fort is a scheduled historic monument in state care and one of the most important archaeological sites in Ireland.
