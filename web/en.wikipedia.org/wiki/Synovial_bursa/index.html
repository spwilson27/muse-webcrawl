Synovial bursa

A synovial bursa (plural bursae or bursas) is a small fluid-filled sac lined by synovial membrane with an inner capillary layer of synovial fluid. It provides a cushion between bones and tendons or muscles around a joint, reducing friction and allowing free movement.

Structure and types

Bursae are found throughout the body where skin, muscles, or tendons must slide over bone. They are classified by location as subcutaneous, submuscular, subtendinous, or subfascial. Well-known examples include the subacromial bursa of the shoulder, the olecranon bursa at the elbow, and the prepatellar bursa of the knee. Some bursae, known as adventitious bursae, develop later in life in response to unusual friction.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of a bursa is called bursitis, which can result from repetitive movement, prolonged pressure, infection, or systemic inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and gout.
