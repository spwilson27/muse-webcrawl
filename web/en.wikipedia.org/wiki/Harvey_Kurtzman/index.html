Harvey Kurtzman (October 3, 1924 – February 21, 1993) was an American cartoonist, writer, and editor. He is best known as the founding editor of the satirical magazine Mad in 1952, and for his meticulously researched war comics Two-Fisted Tales and Frontline Combat for EC Comics.

Career

Kurtzman began drawing professionally as a teenager and served in the United States Army during World War II. After the war he freelanced for various publishers before joining EC Comics, where his war titles, written and laid out by Kurtzman and drawn by artists such as Jack Davis, Wally Wood, and John Severin, were praised for their anti-glorification stance and historical accuracy.

In 1952 EC publisher William Gaines asked Kurtzman to create a humor comic, Tales Calculated to Drive You Mad, which rapidly became a cultural phenomenon. When Kurtzman left EC in 1956 after a dispute over control, he went on to edit a succession of humor magazines, including Trump (funded by Hugh Hefner), Humbug, and Help! From 1962 to 1988 he worked with Will Elder on the Little Annie Fanny strip for Playboy.

Legacy

Kurtzman is regarded as one of the most influential figures in American comics; the Harvey Awards, presented annually in the comics industry, are named in his honor. His satirical style influenced generations of cartoonists and comedians.
