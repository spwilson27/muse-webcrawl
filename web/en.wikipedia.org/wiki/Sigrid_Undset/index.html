Sigrid Undset

Sigrid Undset (20 May 1882 – 10 June 1949) was a Danish-born Norwegian novelist. She was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1928, principally for her powerful descriptions of Northern life during the Middle Ages.

Works

Her best-known work is Kristin Lavransdatter, a trilogy of historical novels published between 1920 and 1922, following a woman's life in 14th-century Norway from birth to death. She also wrote the four-volume Olav Audunssøn series, contemporary novels such as Jenny (1911), and biographies.

Life

Born in Kalundborg, Denmark, she grew up in Kristiania (Oslo). She converted to Catholicism in 1924, a faith that deeply informed her later writing. During the German occupation of Norway she fled to the United States, returning after the Second World War. She died in Lillehammer in 1949.
