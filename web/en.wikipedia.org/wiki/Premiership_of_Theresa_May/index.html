Premiership of Theresa May

The premiership of Theresa May began on 13 July 2016, when May was appointed prime minister by Queen Elizabeth II following David Cameron's resignation after the Brexit referendum, and ended on 24 July 2019 when May resigned and was succeeded by Boris Johnson. May, previously home secretary for six years, was the second woman to hold the office after Margaret Thatcher.

Brexit negotiations

May's premiership was dominated by the UK's withdrawal from the European Union. She triggered Article 50 in March 2017 and set out her approach in the Lancaster House speech, favouring departure from the single market and customs union. Hoping to increase her majority, she called a snap election in June 2017 but lost her overall majority and governed with support from the Democratic Unionist Party.

The withdrawal agreement negotiated with the EU, together with the controversial Northern Ireland backstop, was rejected three times by the House of Commons in the "meaningful votes" of early 2019, by record margins. Unable to secure passage and under pressure from her own party, May announced her resignation on 24 May 2019, tearfully concluding that she had done her best.

Domestic policy

May entered office promising to tackle "burning injustices", but domestic legislation was largely crowded out by Brexit. Her government oversaw the Grenfell Tower fire response and introduced the Race Disparity Audit. Her leadership survived a confidence vote in December 2018, but dissent over Brexit continued until her departure.
