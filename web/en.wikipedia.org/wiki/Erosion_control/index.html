Erosion control

Erosion control is the practice of preventing or controlling wind or water erosion in agriculture, land development, coastal areas, river banks and construction. Effective erosion controls handle surface runoff and are important techniques in preventing water pollution, soil loss, wildlife habitat loss and human property loss.

Principles

Erosion happens when soil particles are detached by raindrop impact or running water and transported away. Control measures work by (1) covering the soil to absorb raindrop energy, (2) slowing runoff so particles settle out, (3) stabilising the surface with roots or structures, and (4) diverting or safely conveying concentrated flows. Best practice is to prevent erosion at the source rather than rely on sediment capture downstream.

Methods

  • Vegetation and mulching — seeding, hydroseeding, sod, and straw or wood-fibre mulch protect bare soil; plants' root systems bind the soil.
  • Erosion control blankets and geotextiles — biodegradable mats (coir, jute, straw) armour slopes until vegetation establishes.
  • Sediment controls — silt fences, sediment basins, check dams and inlet protection trap sediment-laden runoff, especially on construction sites.
  • Landform techniques — contour farming, terracing, grassed waterways, windbreaks and strip cropping reduce slope length and wind speed in agriculture.
  • Hard armouring — riprap, gabions, retaining walls and seawalls protect banks and shores where flow energy is high.
  • Chemical stabilisation — soil binders, tackifiers and dust suppressants for temporary stabilisation.

Regulation

Many countries require erosion and sediment control plans for construction and mining. In the United States, the Clean Water Act's stormwater permits (e.g. the EPA Construction General Permit) require Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plans (SWPPPs) with inspected and maintained controls. The Dust Bowl of the 1930s drove the creation of soil conservation services and remains the classic case study of agricultural erosion control at national scale.
