Jewellery (or jewelry in American English) consists of decorative items worn for personal adornment, such as brooches, rings, necklaces, earrings, pendants, bracelets and cufflinks. Made often from gemstones and precious metals (gold, silver, platinum), or from glass, shells, beads and modern materials, jewellery has been worn for adornment, status, ritual, currency and safekeeping of wealth since prehistory — shell beads from over 100,000 years ago are among the earliest ornaments known.

Styles track civilisations: Egyptian gold and lapis, Greek cameos, Roman intaglios, medieval reliquary jewels, Renaissance enamel, Victorian mourning jewellery, Art Nouveau and Art Deco, and today's studio and mass-market design. 'Crown jewels' are state regalia; the industry centres run from Antwerp (diamonds) to Jaipur (coloured stones), and ethical sourcing of gems and metals is a major modern concern.
