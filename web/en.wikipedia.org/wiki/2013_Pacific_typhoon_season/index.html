2013 Pacific typhoon season

2013 Pacific typhoon season
First system formed: 3 January 2013
Last system dissipated: 3 December 2013
Strongest storm: Haiyan — 895 hPa, 230 km/h (10-min)
Tropical depressions: 49
Total storms: 31
Typhoons: 13
Total fatalities: 8,200+
Total damage: ~$22.7 billion

The 2013 Pacific typhoon season was an above-average season in the annual cycle of tropical cyclone formation in the western North Pacific, and was notable for Typhoon Haiyan, one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded at landfall.

Season summary

The season produced 31 named storms, 13 of which became typhoons. Storms affected the Philippines, Taiwan, China, Japan, Vietnam and other parts of East and Southeast Asia. Responsibility for naming storms lies with the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) and PAGASA for the Philippine area.

Typhoon Haiyan

Typhoon Haiyan (known as Yolanda in the Philippines) made landfall in November 2013 with one-minute sustained winds estimated at 315 km/h. Its storm surge devastated Tacloban City and surrounding areas in Leyte and Samar, killing more than 6,300 people in the Philippines alone and causing billions of dollars in damage. Other significant storms included Typhoon Utor, Typhoon Fitow and Typhoon Usagi.

Season effects

The name Haiyan was retired from the naming lists after the season because of the destruction it caused, as were Utor and Fitow.
