Chastushka

Chastushka
OriginRussian Empire, late 19th century
FormFour-line rhymed verse, usually humorous, satirical, or romantic
MeterTrochaic, typically four feet
PerformanceSung, often with balalaika, garmon, or bayan accompaniment; frequently improvised in contests

A chastushka (Russian: частушка, plural chastushki) is a traditional type of short Russian humorous folk song with high beat frequency, combining music and poetry. The name derives from the word chasty, meaning "frequent", reflecting both the rapid tempo and the quick-fire delivery. A typical chastushka consists of a single rhymed quatrain of trochaic tetrameter with an ABAB or ABCB rhyme scheme.

Origins and themes

Chastushki appeared in the last third of the 19th century in the Russian countryside and spread rapidly with the factory and the city, becoming the dominant form of popular folk verse by the early 20th century. Their subject matter is nearly unlimited: love and courtship (the largest category), village gossip, jokes about mothers-in-law, work, drinking, and pointed satire about current events, officials, and politics. In the Soviet period chastushki were composed both as official propaganda celebrating collectivization and as clandestine anti-Soviet satire; collectors gathered hundreds of thousands of texts.

Performance

Chastushki are sung solo or antiphonally, often by young women, and traditionally accompanied by the garmon (Russian button accordion), balalaika, or bayan; singers frequently compete, improvising new verses in response to one another in a manner comparable to a rap battle. The first two lines often set up a scene or parallel from nature, and the last two deliver the punchline — a structure rooted in the older lyric song.

In culture

The chastushka's punchy quatrain has influenced Russian popular humor and political satire down to the present day, and verses about events from the Russo-Japanese War to the space age testify to the form's adaptability. Folklorists regard the chastushka as one of the most productive folk genres in Russian tradition.
