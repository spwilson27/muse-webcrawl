Crème anglaise

Crème anglaise (French for "English cream") is a light pouring custard used as a sauce or cream base in desserts. It is made by whisking egg yolks and sugar and slowly adding hot milk or cream, usually flavoured with vanilla, then cooking gently until it coats the back of a spoon.

Preparation

The sauce is cooked to about 82–85 °C (180–185 °F): thick enough to coat but below the temperature at which the yolks curdle. It contains no starch, unlike pastry cream, so it remains pourable. It may be served warm or cold.

Uses

Crème anglaise accompanies cakes, poached fruit and desserts such as île flottante, and forms the base of bavarian cream and of many ice creams when churned and frozen.
