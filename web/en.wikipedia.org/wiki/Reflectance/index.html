Reflectance


Specular and diffuse reflection from a surface.

The reflectance of the surface of a material is its effectiveness in reflecting radiant energy. It is the fraction of incident electromagnetic power that is reflected at the boundary. Reflectance depends on the wavelength of the radiation, the angle of incidence, the polarisation, and the material's refractive index and surface roughness.

Definitions

Reflectance ρ is the ratio of reflected flux to incident flux. For a thick, opaque layer, reflectance is closely related to the amplitude reflection coefficient of the Fresnel equations, squared and averaged where necessary. Closely related quantities include reflectivity (a property of the material itself, for a semi-infinite medium) and albedo, the diffuse reflectance of a surface in planetary science.

Specular and diffuse reflectance

A smooth, polished surface reflects light specularly, at the mirror angle; a rough surface scatters light in many directions, giving diffuse reflectance. Most real surfaces show a combination, described by reflectance models such as the bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF), widely used in computer graphics and remote sensing.

Applications

Reflectance measurements underpin spectroscopy, the characterisation of optical coatings, solar-energy analysis, and satellite observation of the Earth's surface, where the reflectance spectra of vegetation, water, and rock identify land cover.
