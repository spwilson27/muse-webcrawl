The Khitan people (Khitan: 𐑪𐑑𐑢𐑭; Chinese: 契丹; pinyin: Qìdān) were a nomadic people originating from the Mongolia–Manchuria region that rose to prominence in the 4th century CE. They founded the Liao dynasty (916–1125), which ruled over present-day northern China, Mongolia, and parts of Siberia, and later the Qara Khitai (Western Liao) empire in Central Asia.

Origins and early history

The Khitan emerged from the Xianbei confederation and were first recorded in Chinese histories during the 4th century, living along the upper reaches of the Liao River in what is now Inner Mongolia. For centuries they lived under the influence of neighbouring powers, including the Göktürks and the Uyghur Khaganate, and paid tribute to the Chinese Tang dynasty.

The Liao dynasty

In 916, the Khitan leader Abaoji (Emperor Taizu) unified the Khitan tribes and proclaimed himself emperor, founding the state that became known as the Liao dynasty. In 936 the Khitan acquired the Sixteen Prefectures of northern China from the Later Jin dynasty, and in 1005 they compelled the Song dynasty to sign the Chanyuan Treaty, inaugurating a century of peace in which Song paid annual tribute to Liao. The Liao state developed a dual administrative system, governing its nomadic population through traditional tribal institutions and its settled agricultural population with a Chinese-style bureaucracy.

Language and scripts

The Khitan language is generally regarded as para-Mongolic. In the 920s the Khitan created two writing systems: the "large script," modelled in part on Chinese characters, and the "small script," influenced by the Old Uyghur alphabet. Both scripts have been only partially deciphered.

Later history and legacy

The Liao dynasty fell to the Jurchen Jin dynasty in 1125. A Khitan prince, Yelü Dashi, led a remnant westward and founded the Qara Khitai (Western Liao) in Central Asia, which survived until the Mongol conquest of 1218. Many Khitan were subsequently absorbed into the Mongols and other populations. The name "Kitay" for China in Russian and medieval European languages (whence "Cathay") derives from the Khitan.
