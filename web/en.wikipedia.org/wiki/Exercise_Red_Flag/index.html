Exercise Red Flag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Exercise Red Flag is an advanced aerial combat training exercise hosted by the United States Air Force. It is conducted several times a year at Nellis Air Force Base, Nevada, using the enormous Nevada Test and Training Range, with a parallel series, Red Flag – Alaska, held at Eielson Air Force Base. The exercise pits "Blue" friendly forces against "Red" aggressor forces using realistic threat replication, including aggressor squadrons flying US fighters painted and operated in enemy colours and tactics, surface-to-air missile simulators and electronic warfare.

Origins

Red Flag was established in 1975 (the first exercise began in November of that year) in response to lessons of the Vietnam War. Analysis — the Project Red Baron studies — showed that an aircrew's chances of survival rose dramatically after completing about ten combat missions; losses were concentrated in a pilot's earliest sorties. Red Flag was designed to give aircrews those first ten "missions" in training, in an environment as close to real war as possible, so that the steepest part of the learning curve would be climbed over Nevada rather than in combat.

The exercise today

Each Red Flag typically lasts two to three weeks and involves dozens of units and over a hundred aircraft from the US Air Force, Navy, Marine Corps and Army, plus allied air forces from around the world. Scenarios combine air-to-air combat, strike packages, suppression of enemy air defences, combat search and rescue, dynamic targeting and space and cyber support, flown day and night over roughly 12,000 square miles of instrumented range. The exercise is run by the 414th Combat Training Squadron at Nellis. Red Flag has inspired sister exercises abroad, such as Indradhanush, and remains the benchmark for large-force air combat training; a typical participant quote describes it as "the closest thing to war without actually being shot at."
