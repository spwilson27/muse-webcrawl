Normed division algebra

In mathematics, a normed division algebra A over the real or complex numbers is a division algebra over R or C equipped with a norm ||·|| satisfying ||xy|| = ||x|| ||y|| for all x, y in A, together with a multiplicative identity element. By a celebrated theorem of Adolf Hurwitz (1898), the only finite-dimensional normed division algebras over the real numbers are the real numbers R, the complex numbers C, the quaternions H, and the octonions O, of dimensions 1, 2, 4, and 8 respectively.

Properties

In any normed division algebra, the norm comes from an inner product via the polarization identity, and the algebra carries a conjugation (involution) x ↦ x̄ such that x x̄ = ||x||2. The multiplicative property of the norm is equivalent to a sum-of-squares identity: the product of two sums of n squares is again a sum of n squares, and such identities exist only for n = 1, 2, 4, 8.

The four algebras form a hierarchy obtained by the Cayley–Dickson construction, each step doubling the dimension while losing a structural property:

  • R — ordered, commutative, associative
  • C — loses ordering, remains commutative and associative
  • H — loses commutativity, remains associative
  • O — loses associativity, remains alternative

Applying the Cayley–Dickson process once more yields the 16-dimensional sedenions, which are no longer a division algebra: they contain zero divisors, so the norm cannot be multiplicative.

Hurwitz's theorem

Hurwitz's theorem, published posthumously in 1923 from his 1898 work, states that a finite-dimensional real normed division algebra must have dimension 1, 2, 4, or 8. Equivalent formulations occur in the theory of composition algebras. Jacobson and others later generalized the classification using quadratic forms, showing that (unital) composition algebras over any field have dimension 1, 2, 4, or 8.

Over the complex numbers

Over C, the only finite-dimensional normed division algebra is C itself: any such algebra would contain C and, being a division algebra over an algebraically closed field compatible with the norm, cannot be larger.

Applications

Normed division algebras appear in topology (the only parallelizable spheres are S0, S1, S3, and S7), in the theory of spin groups, and in theoretical physics, where the octonions play a role in certain approaches to quantum mechanics, string theory, and the classification of exceptional Lie groups.
