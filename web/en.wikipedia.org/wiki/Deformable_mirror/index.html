Deformable mirror

A deformable mirror is a mirror whose surface can be reshaped by actuators to correct wavefront distortions. It is the key component of adaptive optics systems in astronomy, ophthalmology, microscopy and laser communications.

Operation

A wavefront sensor measures atmospheric turbulence aberrations hundreds of times per second; a control system drives piezoelectric or MEMS actuators behind a thin reflective facesheet to cancel them, sharpening telescope images beyond the seeing limit.

Types

Designs include segmented mirrors, bimorph mirrors, and microelectromechanical (MEMS) mirrors with thousands of actuators. Large observatories and the Extremely Large Telescope use deformable secondary mirrors with over a thousand actuators.
