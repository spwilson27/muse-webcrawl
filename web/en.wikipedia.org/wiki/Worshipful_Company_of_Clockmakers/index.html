The Worshipful Company of Clockmakers is one of the livery companies of the City of London. It was established by royal charter granted by Charles I in 1631 and is ranked 61st in the order of precedence of the livery companies. Its motto is Tempus rerum imperator, Latin for "Time, commander of all things". It is the oldest surviving horological institution in the world.

History

Before 1631, London clockmakers fell under the guild authority of the Blacksmiths' Company, and foreign (especially French and Dutch) clockmakers worked in the city outside guild control. The charter gave the new company powers to regulate the trade within ten miles of London, search for and destroy faulty work, and control apprenticeships. Leading early members included Ahasuerus Fromanteel, whose family introduced the pendulum clock to England, and the company counted Thomas Tompion, the "father of English watchmaking", among its masters.

Museum and library

The company maintains the Clockmakers' Museum, founded in 1814 and regarded as the oldest specialist horological museum in the world. Its collection includes John Harrison's marine timekeeper H5 and his precision regulator, early English watches, and the archive of the company. The museum was long housed at Guildhall and moved in 2015 to galleries within the Science Museum in South Kensington.

Today

Like other livery companies, the Clockmakers now function principally as a charitable and professional body, supporting horological education, apprenticeships and awards, while its members include working clock and watch makers, collectors and historians.
