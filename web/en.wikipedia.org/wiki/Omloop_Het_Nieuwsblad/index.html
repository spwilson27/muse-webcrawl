The Omloop Het Nieuwsblad is a one-day professional road cycling race held annually in Flanders, Belgium, traditionally on the last Saturday of February. It is the traditional opening race of the Belgian cycling season and the first of the spring cobbled classics.

History

The race was first held in 1945 as the Omloop Het Volk, organised by the newspaper Het Volk. When that newspaper was absorbed by Het Nieuwsblad, the race took its current name from 2009. It joined the UCI World Tour in 2017; a women's race has been held since 2006.

Course

The route starts and finishes in Ghent (with a period finishing in Ninove) and crosses the Flemish Ardennes, featuring short, steep, cobbled climbs such as the Muur van Geraardsbergen and the Bosberg, which for many years formed the finale when the race finished in Ghent's suburbs.

Winners

Belgian riders dominate the honours list. Multiple winners include Ernest Sterckx, Joseph Bruyère, Peter Van Petegem, Philippe Gilbert, Greg Van Avermaet, and Wout van Aert.
