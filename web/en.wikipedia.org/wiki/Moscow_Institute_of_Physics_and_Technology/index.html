The Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT; Russian: Московский физико-технический институт), also known as Phystech, is a public research university located in Dolgoprudny, Moscow Oblast, Russia. It was founded in 1946 by Nobel laureates Pyotr Kapitsa, Nikolay Semyonov, and Lev Landau, among others, to train elite researchers for the Soviet Union's physics institutes. MIPT is widely considered one of Russia's leading institutions in physics, mathematics, and computer science.

The Phystech system

MIPT is famous for the "Phystech system," developed by its founders: a highly selective admission process, often through national olympiads; a rigorous core curriculum in mathematics and physics; and, from the third year, placement of students in "base institutes" — research institutes of the Academy of Sciences and industry laboratories — where they work on real research problems under practising scientists. The institute's founders insisted that education be conducted by those actively engaged in research.

History

The institute began in 1946 as the Department of Physics and Technology of Moscow State University, championed by Kapitsa, who was dismissed by Stalin the same year; it became an independent institute in 1951. Its graduates staffed the Soviet nuclear, space, and computing programmes. In recent decades MIPT has expanded into life sciences, computer science, and engineering, runs an English-taught international programme, and is regularly ranked among Russia's top three universities. Alumni and faculty include multiple Nobel laureates and founders of companies such as ABBYY and Acronis.
