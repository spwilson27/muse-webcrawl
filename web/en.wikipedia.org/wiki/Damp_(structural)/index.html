Damp (structural)

Structural dampness is the presence of unwanted moisture in the structure of a building, resulting either from intrusion from outside or condensation within structures. It is a common cause of deterioration, damaging plaster, timber and decoration, and can aggravate health problems through mould growth.

Types

Rising damp is the capillary rise of groundwater through porous masonry where a damp-proof course is absent or bridged; penetrating damp results from leaks and rain driven through walls; and condensation forms where moist air meets cold surfaces, often the most common cause in modern homes.

Treatment

Remedies address the source: repairing leaks, installing or restoring a damp-proof course, improving ventilation and heating, and removing contaminated plaster. Misdiagnosis, especially of rising damp, is common in the building trade.
