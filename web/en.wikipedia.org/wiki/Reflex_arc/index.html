Reflex arc

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A reflex arc is the neural pathway that mediates a reflex — a fast, involuntary response to a stimulus. Its classic form runs from a sensory receptor through a sensory neuron, one or more interneurons in the spinal cord or brainstem, and a motor neuron to an effector muscle or gland, allowing action before (or without) conscious perception.

Examples

In the knee-jerk (patellar) reflex, tapping the tendon stretches the quadriceps, whose muscle spindles excite motor neurons directly, extending the leg, while interneurons relax the opposing hamstrings. Withdrawal reflexes pull a limb from pain, crossed-extensor reflexes shift weight to the other leg, and the pupillary light reflex adjusts the pupil via the brainstem.

Clinical use

Clinicians test reflexes with a tendon hammer to localise damage in the Nervous system: exaggerated reflexes suggest upper motor neuron lesions, while absent reflexes point to lower motor neuron or peripheral nerve problems. Newborns show primitive reflexes (grasp, rooting, Moro) that normally disappear in infancy.
