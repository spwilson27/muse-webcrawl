Quintus Tullius Cicero (102 BC – 43 BC) was a Roman statesman and military officer, the younger brother of the celebrated orator Marcus Tullius Cicero. He served as aedile, praetor (62 BC), governor of the province of Asia, and as a legate under Julius Caesar in Gaul and under his brother in Cilicia.

Quintus Cicero, soldier and author, younger brother of the orator.

Career

Quintus accompanied Caesar to Britain in 54 BC, where his camp was famously attacked by the Nervii and successfully defended — an episode recounted in Caesar's Commentarii de Bello Gallico. In the civil war he first sided with Pompey, was pardoned by Caesar, and later attempted to reconcile with the victors. He was proscribed by the Second Triumvirate and killed in 43 BC along with his son, in the same proscription that claimed his brother's life.

Writings

He is credited with the Commentariolum Petitionis ("Handbook of Electioneering"), a letter of advice on running for the consulship purportedly addressed to his brother, though its authenticity has been debated since antiquity. Four of his tragedies and some poetry are mentioned by ancient sources but are lost. His brother's extensive correspondence preserves many letters to him, including the long literary letter on the writing of history and the dialogue De Legibus, in which Quintus appears as an interlocutor.
