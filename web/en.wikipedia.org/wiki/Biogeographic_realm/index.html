A biogeographic realm or biogeographical realm is the broadest biogeographic division of Earth's land surface, based on the geographic distribution patterns of terrestrial organisms. Realms are subdivided into bioregions, which are in turn divided into biomes and ecoregions. The concept descends from the faunal regions proposed by Alfred Russel Wallace in the 19th century, refined by later work on both animals and plants.

Realms reflect the evolutionary history of the organisms within them: their boundaries often correspond to barriers — oceans, mountain ranges, and deserts — that have separated evolving biotas over geological time. Two realms may have similar climates and similar biomes (such as desert or tropical rainforest) yet contain very different species, because their organisms descend from different ancestral stocks.

The eight terrestrial realms

  • Nearctic — North America and Greenland
  • Palearctic — Europe, North Africa, and northern and central Asia
  • Afrotropical (Ethiopian) — sub-Saharan Africa and southern Arabia
  • Indomalayan (Oriental) — South and Southeast Asia
  • Australasian — Australia, New Guinea, New Zealand and nearby islands
  • Neotropical — Central and South America and the Caribbean
  • Oceanian — the Pacific islands of Polynesia, Micronesia and Fiji
  • Antarctic — Antarctica and some southern islands

The World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) system, widely used in conservation planning, divides these realms into 867 terrestrial ecoregions grouped into biomes. Marine and freshwater realms are classified separately. Some schemes merge the Nearctic and Palearctic into a single Holarctic realm, reflecting their historically connected faunas.

History

The division of the world into faunal regions was pioneered by Philip Sclater for birds (1858) and Alfred Russel Wallace for animals generally in The Geographical Distribution of Animals (1876). Botanists such as Adolf Engler developed parallel floristic kingdoms; the two traditions differ in places, notably in the recognition of a Cape floristic kingdom in South Africa.
