Chrism

Chrism, also called myrrh, holy anointing oil, or consecrated oil, is a consecrated oil used in the Anglican, Assyrian, Catholic, Nordic Lutheran, Old Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Latter-day Saint churches in the administration of certain sacraments and ecclesiastical functions.

Composition and consecration

Chrism is made of olive oil mixed with a fragrant substance, usually balsam. In the Roman Catholic Church it is consecrated by the diocesan bishop together with his priests at the Chrism Mass, traditionally celebrated on Maundy Thursday. In Eastern Orthodox practice the chrism (myron) is prepared and consecrated by the primates of autocephalous churches, a process taking several days during Holy Week.

Uses

Chrism is used in baptism (anointing after the water bath), in confirmation (called chrismation in the East, where it is administered immediately after baptism, including to infants), in the ordination of priests and bishops, and in the consecration of altars, churches, and bells. In some traditions the anointing of monarchs at coronation also employs chrism or a related holy oil.

Name

The word derives from the Greek χρῖσμα (chrisma), "anointing", from the same root as Christ, "the Anointed One".
