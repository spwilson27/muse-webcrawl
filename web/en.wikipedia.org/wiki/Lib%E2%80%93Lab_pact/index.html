Lib–Lab pact

Lib–Lab pact is a name applied to several working arrangements between the British Liberal Party (and later the Liberal Democrats) and the Labour Party. The term usually refers to the 1977–1978 parliamentary agreement under which the Liberals supported James Callaghan's minority Labour government in exchange for consultation on policy.

The 1977–78 pact

By early 1977, by-election losses had erased Labour's small Commons majority. Prime Minister James Callaghan and Liberal leader David Steel negotiated a formal agreement in March 1977: the Liberals would vote with the government on confidence and supply motions, and in return Liberal MPs were consulted in advance on legislation. The pact stabilised the government for some eighteen months but frustrated many in both parties, and Steel ended it in mid-1978. Labour governed on as a minority until losing a confidence vote in March 1979.

Earlier and later arrangements

The name is also used for the Gladstone–MacDonald pact of 1903, an electoral arrangement between Liberals and the Labour Representation Committee, and informally for later cooperation, including joint constitutional reform discussions in the 1990s.
