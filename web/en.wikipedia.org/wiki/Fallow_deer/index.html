The fallow deer is a species of deer known by the scientific name Dama dama. It is native to southern Europe and possibly parts of western Asia, but has been introduced around the world. Historically, fallow deer were dispersed by the Phoenicians, Romans, and Normans into much of Europe and later by colonists to the Americas, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, largely as an ornamental park deer and game animal.

Description

The fallow deer is a medium-sized deer: adult males (bucks) stand about 85–95 cm at the shoulder and weigh 60–100 kg; females (does) are smaller, at 75–85 cm and 30–50 kg. The common coat colour is a rich chestnut-brown with white spots in summer, fading to a greyer, less distinctly spotted coat in winter; however, the species is unusually variable, with common colour morphs including menil (paler spotting), black (melanistic), and white (leucistic, but not albino). Only bucks carry antlers, which in mature animals are broad and flattened ("palmate") with numerous points — unique among European deer — and are shed each spring and regrown by late summer.

Behaviour and ecology

Fallow deer are grazing and browsing herbivores of open woodland, parkland, and farmland edges, feeding mainly on grasses, herbs, acorns, and browse. They are social animals, with does and young forming herds separate from bucks outside the breeding season. During the autumn rut (October–November), bucks groan repeatedly at mating stands or leks, competing for groups of does; a single fawn, spotted and well camouflaged, is born in June after a gestation of about 230 days.

Distribution

Fossil and genetic evidence suggests the species survived the last ice age in Mediterranean refugia — in Italy, Sicily, and the Balkans — before human-mediated spread across Europe, including introduction to Britain by the Romans (and again by the Normans). Free-living populations now occur in most European countries and established introduced populations exist in the United States, Argentina, Australia, New Zealand, and elsewhere.

Use and status

Fallow deer have been kept in deer parks for centuries and are farmed commercially for venison and velvet antler. The IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The closely related Persian fallow deer (Dama mesopotamica), sometimes treated as a subspecies, is endangered and survives mainly in Iran and in reintroduced populations in Israel.
