In Greek mythology, Lethe (Ancient Greek: Λήθη, "forgetfulness" or "oblivion") is one of the five rivers of Hades, the underworld, and also the name of the spirit (daemon) of forgetfulness and oblivion, a daughter of Eris (Strife). The word survives in the medical and literary vocabulary of forgetfulness — as in the river's adjective "Lethean".

The river

Drinking from Lethe caused complete forgetfulness. In Plato's Myth of Er, closing the Republic, souls about to be reborn drink from the river of Unmindfulness in the plain of Lethe before returning to earthly life, losing memory of what they have seen. Later traditions, including Virgil's Aeneid and Dante's Purgatorio (where Lethe's waters wash away the memory of sin), made the river a fixture of the literary afterlife. Lethe was paired in Orphic tradition with Mnemosyne, Memory, whose spring initiates were instructed to drink from instead, so as to retain their identity.

Other associations

A real river near Cádiz in Spain — the Guadalete — was identified in antiquity with Lethe. The name also denotes a genus of butterflies (the treebrows) and appears throughout Western literature as a symbol of oblivion, from Milton and Keats to modern fantasy.
