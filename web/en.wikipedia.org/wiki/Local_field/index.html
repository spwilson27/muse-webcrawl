Local field

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Valuation rings in a local field

In mathematics, a local field is a field that is complete with respect to a discrete valuation and whose residue field is finite. Local fields arise in number theory as the completions of global fields (such as the rational numbers or function fields) at their individual "places", and the strategy of studying a global problem one place at a time — the local–global principle — is fundamental to modern algebraic number theory.

Classification

Every local field is isomorphic to one of the following: the real numbers R or complex numbers C (the archimedean cases); a finite extension of the p-adic numbers Qp for some prime p (non-archimedean local fields of characteristic zero, with residue field of characteristic p); or the field of formal Laurent series Fq((t)) over a finite field (characteristic p). A non-archimedean local field has a ring of integers which is a discrete valuation ring with a unique maximal ideal; elements admit valuations in the integers, and the field is totally disconnected and locally compact, making harmonic analysis and Haar measure available. The study of extensions of local fields — ramification theory, the local reciprocity law of local class field theory, and the structure of the absolute Galois group — is much more tractable than in the global case and feeds into the Langlands programme. If local fields are assembled at all places of a global field one obtains the adele ring, the setting for Tate's thesis and modern automorphic forms.
