Miscellany

A miscellany is a collection of literary pieces, produced by one or several authors, differing in topic, form, or both, and assembled into a single volume. Miscellanies range from ancient collections of epigrams and anecdotes to the poetic miscellanies popular in sixteenth- to eighteenth-century England, such as Tottel's Miscellany (1557), which introduced the sonnets of Wyatt and Surrey to print.

History

The form descends from classical and medieval compilations — Greek anthologies, florilegia and commonplace books — in which excerpts of verse and prose were gathered for edification and pleasure. In the early modern period, printed poetic miscellanies became a principal means by which new verse circulated, often mixing poems by well-known and anonymous writers. The term is also applied to collections of shorter prose works, essays and anecdotes.

Modern usage

Today "miscellany" denotes any heterogeneous collection, in publishing and beyond; the plural "miscellanea" is used in cataloguing for works treating diverse subjects.
