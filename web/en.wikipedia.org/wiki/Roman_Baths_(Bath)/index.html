Roman Baths (Bath)

The Roman Baths are well-preserved thermae in the city of Bath, Somerset, England. A temple was constructed on the site between AD 60 and 70 in the first few decades of Roman Britain. Its presence led to the development of the small Roman urban settlement known as Aquae Sulis around the site, dedicated to the goddess Sulis Minerva.

The complex

The site includes the Sacred Spring, which rises at 46 °C and supplied the baths with over a million litres of water daily; the Roman Temple; the Great Bath, a lead-lined pool once roofed by a vaulted hall; and the Roman Bath House with its caldarium, tepidarium, and frigidarium. Finds include the gilt bronze head of Sulis Minerva and around 130 curse tablets inscribed in Latin and thrown into the spring.

History and museum

The baths fell into ruin after the Roman withdrawal, and the spring was built over in medieval times as the King's Bath. Rediscovered from the eighteenth century onward, the complex now houses a museum and is one of the most visited heritage attractions in Britain, though visitors no longer bathe in the ancient pools.
