Polystyrene

Polystyrene (PS) is a synthetic aromatic hydrocarbon polymer made from the monomer known as styrene. Polystyrene can be solid or foamed. General-purpose polystyrene is clear, hard, and brittle. It is an inexpensive resin per unit weight and is among the most widely produced plastics.

Forms

Polystyrene is manufactured as solid sheet and moulded products, as expanded polystyrene (EPS) foam — widely known by the Dow trademark Styrofoam for extruded polystyrene (XPS) insulation board — and as high-impact polystyrene (HIPS) blended with rubber for toughness.

Uses

Applications include disposable food containers and cups, packaging, insulation panels, model kits, CD cases, and laboratory ware. Foamed polystyrene is used for thermal insulation and protective packaging because it consists mostly of trapped air.

Environmental issues

Polystyrene is slow to biodegrade and is a common component of litter, especially in foam form. Recycling is technically feasible but often uneconomical; many municipalities have restricted single-use polystyrene food service items. Its monomer, styrene, is classified as a possible carcinogen.
