Elsevier

Elsevier (Dutch: [ˈɛlzəviːr]) is a Dutch academic publishing company specialising in scientific, technical, and medical content. It is part of the RELX Group and is one of the largest scholarly publishers in the world, publishing thousands of journals — including The Lancet and Cell — and tens of thousands of books. Its products include the ScienceDirect platform, the Scopus abstract and citation database, and the Mendeley reference manager.

History

The modern company was founded in 1880 in Rotterdam and took its name from the House of Elzevir, a family of Dutch publishers active in the 16th and 17th centuries, though there is no direct connection. In 1993 Elsevier merged with the British publisher Reed International to form Reed Elsevier (now RELX). The company has since shifted heavily toward digital distribution and analytics services for researchers and institutions.

Criticism

Elsevier has long been criticised for high subscription prices, bundling practices, and high profit margins, which contributed to the "serials crisis" in academic libraries. In 2012 thousands of researchers joined "The Cost of Knowledge" boycott, pledging not to publish, referee, or edit for Elsevier journals. Universities in several countries have cancelled or renegotiated big-deal contracts with the company, while open-access mandates have pushed it toward article-processing-charge models.
