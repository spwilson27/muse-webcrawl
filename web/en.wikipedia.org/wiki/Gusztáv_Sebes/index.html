Gusztáv Sebes

Gusztáv Sebes (born Gusztáv Scharenpeck; 22 January 1906 – 30 January 1986) was a Hungarian football player and coach, best known as the architect of Hungary's celebrated "Golden Team" (Aranycsapat) of the 1950s, the Mighty Magyars who went unbeaten for four years, won the 1952 Olympic title, and finished runners-up at the 1954 World Cup.

As deputy minister of sport as well as national coach, Sebes concentrated the best players at army club Honvéd, and developed a fluid 3-2-3-2 system with a deep-lying centre-forward, Nándor Hidegkuti, that influenced coaching worldwide; its most famous result was the 6–3 victory over England at Wembley in 1953, followed by a 7–1 win in Budapest. After the 1956 Hungarian Revolution the team dispersed and Sebes's influence ended.
