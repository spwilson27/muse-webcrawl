Dāna

TermDāna (Sanskrit, Pali)
MeaningGiving, generosity, charity
TraditionsHinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism

Dāna (Sanskrit and Pali: दान) is the practice of cultivating generosity and giving without expectation of reward. It is a central virtue in the dharmic religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism — encompassing charity, almsgiving, donations and the voluntary sharing of material goods, food, knowledge and protection.

In Hinduism

In Hindu tradition, dāna is one of the fundamental virtues and duties described in the Vedas, Upanishads and dharmashastra literature. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad names self-restraint (dama), giving (dāna) and compassion (dayā) as the three cardinal virtues. Classical texts classify gifts, praise giving to worthy recipients at the proper time and place, and regard generosity as a means of spiritual merit and a duty of householders.

In Buddhism

In Buddhism, dāna is the first of the perfections (pāramitā) cultivated by a bodhisattva and the first of the meritorious practices taught to laypeople. Giving — especially offerings of food to the monastic sangha — generates merit, weakens attachment and greed, and prepares the mind for meditation. Texts distinguish the gift of material things, the gift of fearlessness, and the supreme gift of the Dhamma itself. The merit of a gift is held to depend on the purity of the giver's intention, the gift, and the recipient.

In Jainism and Sikhism

Jain tradition recognises four kinds of dāna: the gift of food (āhāra), medicine (auṣadha), learning (śāstra or jñāna) and fearlessness (abhaya). In Sikhism, sharing one's earnings — expressed in the principle of vaṇḍ chhakṇā and in the institution of langar, the free community kitchen — is a core religious duty alongside honest work and remembrance of God.
