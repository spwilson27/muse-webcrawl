Pushforward (differential)

In differential geometry, the pushforward (also called the differential, or, historically, the derived map) of a smooth map between manifolds is the induced linear map between the tangent spaces at corresponding points. If F : M → N is smooth and p ∈ M, the pushforward dFp : TpM → TF(p)N sends a tangent vector at p to a tangent vector at F(p). It is the manifold analogue of the Jacobian matrix, and satisfies the chain rule.

Definition

Given a tangent vector X at p, regarded either as a derivation on smooth functions or as the velocity of a curve through p, the pushforward is defined by composing with F: if γ is a curve in M with γ(0) = p and velocity X, then dFp(X) is the velocity at F(p) of the composed curve F ∘ γ in N. Equivalently, as a derivation, dFp(X) acts on a function g on N by X(g ∘ F).

Properties

  • Linearity: dFp is a linear map between tangent spaces.
  • Chain rule: d(G ∘ F)p = dGF(p) ∘ dFp.
  • Coordinates: in local charts, dFp is represented by the Jacobian matrix of F.
  • Diffeomorphisms: if F is a diffeomorphism, dFp is a linear isomorphism.

Pushforward of vector fields

When F is a diffeomorphism, a vector field on M can be pushed forward pointwise to a vector field on N; for a general smooth map this is not possible, and instead one uses the notion of F-related vector fields. The dual construction for covector fields and differential forms — pulling forms back along F — is the pullback. The pushforward of vector fields is closely related to the Lie bracket and to the differential of a flow, which transports vectors along the integral curves of a vector field.
