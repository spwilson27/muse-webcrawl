Realtor

Realtor (capitalised and styled REALTOR®) is a registered collective membership mark of the National Association of Realtors (NAR) in the United States, identifying a real estate professional who is a member of the association and subscribes to its code of ethics. Although often used generically to mean any real estate agent or broker, the term properly applies only to NAR members — about half of licensed agents in the United States.

History

The term was coined in 1915 by Charles N. Chadbourn, a Minneapolis real estate developer and later president of the association, to distinguish members of what was then the National Association of Real Estate Exchanges from non-members. The association adopted the term in 1916 and secured trademark protection; in 1949 and 1950 the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office registered "Realtor" as a collective mark, a status upheld against later challenges arguing that the word had become generic.

Membership and the code of ethics

Realtors belong simultaneously to local, state and national associations. The NAR Code of Ethics, first adopted in 1913 and regularly revised, imposes duties to clients, the public and other realtors that in some respects exceed legal requirements. Membership also typically carries access to the local Multiple Listing Service (MLS), through which member brokers share listings.

The Canadian Real Estate Association licenses the mark in Canada, where "REALTOR" likewise denotes a member agent.
