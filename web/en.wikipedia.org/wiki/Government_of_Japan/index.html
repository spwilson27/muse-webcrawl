Government of Japan

Formation: 1947 (Constitution of Japan)
Head of state: Emperor (symbolic)
Head of government: Prime Minister
Legislature: National Diet
Judiciary: Supreme Court

The Government of Japan is the central government of Japan. It operates under the framework established by the Constitution of Japan, which took effect on 3 May 1947. Japan is a unitary state and a constitutional monarchy in which the power of the Emperor is limited to a largely ceremonial role. The government has three branches: the legislative National Diet, the executive Cabinet, and the judicial Supreme Court.

The Emperor

Under Article 1 of the Constitution, the Emperor is "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the People." He performs ceremonial acts of state — appointing the Prime Minister and the Chief Justice (as designated by the Diet and Cabinet respectively), promulgating laws, and receiving foreign ambassadors — but possesses no powers relating to government. The Imperial House is the world's oldest continuous hereditary monarchy.

Legislative branch

The National Diet is the highest organ of state power and the sole law-making authority. It is bicameral: the House of Representatives (465 members, four-year terms) and the House of Councillors (248 members, six-year terms). The House of Representatives is the more powerful of the two, able to override the upper house on budgets, treaties and the designation of the prime minister.

Executive branch

Executive power is vested in the Cabinet, headed by the Prime Minister, who is designated by the Diet from among its members and formally appointed by the Emperor. The Prime Minister appoints and may dismiss the Ministers of State, most of whom must be Diet members. The Cabinet is collectively responsible to the Diet; it must resign if the House of Representatives passes a no-confidence resolution or rejects a confidence resolution, unless the House is dissolved within ten days. Ministries and agencies — including Finance, Foreign Affairs, Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), and the powerful bureaucracy — administer national policy.

Judicial branch

The judiciary is independent. The Supreme Court, headed by the Chief Justice, is the court of last resort and has the power of constitutional review. Below it are high courts, district courts, family courts and summary courts.

Local government

Japan is divided into 47 prefectures, each with an elected governor and assembly, and below them municipalities (cities, towns and villages) with elected mayors and councils. The Constitution guarantees local self-government.
