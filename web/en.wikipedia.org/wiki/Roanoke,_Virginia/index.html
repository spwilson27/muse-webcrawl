Roanoke is an independent city in the Commonwealth of Virginia, United States. It is located in Southwest Virginia along the Roanoke River, in the valley between the Blue Ridge and Allegheny mountain ranges. As of the 2020 census, the population was 100,011. Roanoke is the largest municipality in Southwest Virginia and the principal city of the Roanoke metropolitan area.

History

The town was first established as Big Lick in 1852, named for a large outcropping of salt near the Roanoke River that attracted wildlife. In 1882 it became a junction of the Norfolk and Western Railway (later Norfolk Southern), and in 1884 it was chartered as the city of Roanoke — a name derived from an Algonquian word for shell "money" (rawrenock). The railway's locomotive shops made Roanoke a major railroad town and the builder of famous steam locomotives such as the Class J.

Landmarks

The city's best-known landmark is the Roanoke Star, a large illuminated star atop Mill Mountain, erected in 1949 and among the world's largest free-standing illuminated man-made stars. Other attractions include the Taubman Museum of Art, the Virginia Museum of Transportation, the historic City Market (dating to 1882), the O. Winston Link Museum, and the Blue Ridge Parkway, which passes along the city's edge. Roanoke brands itself as a centre for outdoor recreation, with the Roanoke River Greenway and extensive mountain biking trails.

Economy

Historically dominated by the railroad, Roanoke's economy today is led by healthcare (Carilion Clinic is the region's largest employer), education, and retail and services for a large surrounding trade area. Norfolk Southern's operations remain significant, though its corporate headquarters relocated to Atlanta in 2021.
