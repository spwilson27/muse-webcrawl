Europa (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Europa was a Phoenician princess, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre, after whom the continent of Europe was named. Zeus, captivated by her, transformed himself into a gentle white bull, enticed her onto his back, and swam across the sea to Crete, where she became the first queen of Crete.

Myth

In Crete Europa bore Zeus three sons: Minos, Rhadamanthus and Sarpedon, who became famed judges of the dead. She later married Asterion, king of Crete, who adopted her sons. Zeus gave her gifts including Talos, the bronze guardian, a hound that never failed to catch its quarry, and an infallible javelin.

In art

The abduction of Europa is a recurrent subject in Western art, painted by Titian, Rembrandt, Rubens and many others; Titian's version (1560–62) is now in the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum, Boston. The motif appears on the Greek €2 coin and in European iconography generally.
