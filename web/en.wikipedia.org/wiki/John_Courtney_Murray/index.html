John Courtney Murray SJ (September 12, 1904 – August 16, 1967) was an American Jesuit priest and theologian known for his work on religious freedom and church–state relations. His book We Hold These Truths (1960) argued that Catholic teaching could be reconciled with American constitutional democracy.

Murray taught at Woodstock College in Maryland and edited the journal Theological Studies. His views on religious liberty were controversial in the 1950s and he was directed to stop publishing on church–state questions, but he was later vindicated at the Second Vatican Council.

Vatican II

As a peritus (theological adviser) at the Council, Murray was a principal architect of Dignitatis humanae (1965), the declaration on religious freedom that affirmed the right of persons and communities to immunity from coercion in religious matters. He appeared on the cover of Time magazine in 1960 and is regarded as one of the most influential American Catholic thinkers of the 20th century.
