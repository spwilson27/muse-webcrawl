Cessna 210

The Cessna 210 Centurion is a six-seat, high-performance, retractable-gear single-engine aircraft manufactured by Cessna. Introduced in 1960 and produced until 1986, a total of 9,240 were built. It was developed from the Cessna 182 Skylane, with which it shared its basic fuselage, adding retractable landing gear and a more powerful engine.

Development

The original Model 210, first flown in January 1957, was powered by a 260 hp Continental IO-470 engine and retained the strut-braced wing of its predecessors. In 1967 the 210G introduced the distinctive cantilever (strutless) wing that characterized later models, together with a larger cabin. Turbocharged versions were sold as the Turbo Centurion and Turbo Centurion II, and a pressurized variant, the P210 Pressurized Centurion, was offered from 1978, allowing flight at altitudes up to about 23,000 feet. Production ended in 1986 when Cessna halted manufacture of piston single-engine aircraft; unlike the smaller singles, the 210 was not returned to production when Cessna resumed piston aircraft manufacture in the 1990s.

Specifications (typical, later models)

Later normally aspirated Centurions are powered by a 300 hp Continental IO-520, cruise at roughly 160–170 knots, and have a range of around 900 nautical miles. The aircraft seats one pilot and five passengers. The type has been widely used for personal transport, air taxi work, and utility operations.

Safety record

The 210's complex hydraulic retractable gear system was a frequent source of maintenance problems and gear-up landings. In 2020 the FAA issued an airworthiness directive requiring inspection of the wing carry-through spar for corrosion and fatigue cracking following a fatal in-flight breakup in Australia.
