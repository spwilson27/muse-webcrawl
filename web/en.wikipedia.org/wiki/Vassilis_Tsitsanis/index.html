Vassilis Tsitsanis (Greek: Βασίλης Τσιτσάνης; 18 January 1915 – 18 January 1984) was a Greek songwriter and bouzouki player, regarded as one of the most important figures of rebetiko music and a founding figure of the later laïko genre. He composed more than 500 songs, many of which remain standards in Greece.

Life and career

Tsitsanis was born in Trikala to parents from Epirus. He began playing the bouzouki as a teenager and moved to Athens in 1936 to study law, but soon turned to music professionally, making his first recordings in 1937. During the Axis occupation of Greece he lived in Thessaloniki, where he ran a taverna and wrote some of his most famous songs, including "Synefiasmeni Kyriaki" ("Cloudy Sunday", 1943), considered one of the greatest Greek popular songs.

After the war he returned to Athens and recorded extensively, collaborating with singers such as Sotiria Bellou, Marika Ninou and Stelios Kazantzidis. His sophisticated harmonies and incorporation of Western elements helped move rebetiko from the margins into mainstream Greek culture.

Death and legacy

Tsitsanis died in London on his 69th birthday, 18 January 1984, while undergoing heart surgery, and was buried in Athens. A museum dedicated to him opened in his native Trikala, and his songs continue to be performed and recorded by successive generations of Greek musicians.
