Sheck Exley

Born: April 1, 1949
Died: April 6, 1994 (aged 45), El Zacatón, Tamaulipas, Mexico
Occupation: Cave diver, mathematics teacher
Known for: Pioneering cave diving safety and deep diving records

Sheck Exley (April 1, 1949 – April 6, 1994) was an American cave diver and a pioneer of the sport, widely regarded as one of the greatest cave and deep divers of all time. A high-school mathematics teacher in Florida, he explored and mapped many of the flooded cave systems of Florida and Mexico and wrote foundational texts on diving safety.

Achievements

Exley was the first person to log 1,000 cave dives and survived an extraordinary number of record-setting deep dives. In 1988 he descended to 780 feet (238 m) in Mexico's Nacimiento del Río Mante, then a world record. He co-founded the National Speleological Society's Cave Diving Section and methodically analysed cave-diving fatalities, producing the safety rules — concerning training, guidelines, gas management, and equipment — that remain the basis of cave-diving instruction. His books include Basic Cave Diving: A Blueprint for Survival (1979) and his autobiography Caverns Measureless to Man (1994).

Death

Exley died on April 6, 1994, while attempting to descend below 300 metres (about 1,000 feet) in El Zacatón, a water-filled sinkhole in Tamaulipas, Mexico — at the time the deepest known dive attempt. His body was recovered from a depth of over 800 feet; the cause of death was never conclusively established, with high-pressure nervous syndrome and gas-related complications among the possibilities discussed.
