National Gallery Singapore

Location: Civic District, Singapore
Opened: November 24, 2015
Collection: Singapore and Southeast Asian art
Buildings: Former Supreme Court and City Hall

National Gallery Singapore is an art museum in the Civic District of Singapore. It houses the world's largest public collection of Singaporean and Southeast Asian art, with over 8,000 works.

Buildings

The gallery occupies two restored national monuments — the former Supreme Court Building and City Hall — linked by a dramatic glass-and-metal canopy roof. The City Hall wing was where the Japanese formally surrendered in 1945 and where Singapore's first prime minister, Lee Kuan Yew, was sworn in.

Collections and exhibitions

Its long-term galleries, including the DBS Singapore Gallery and UOB Southeast Asia Gallery, survey art from the 19th century to the present. The gallery organises major touring exhibitions, a children's biennale and the Keppel Centre for Art Education, and has partnered with institutions such as Tate Britain and the Centre Pompidou.
