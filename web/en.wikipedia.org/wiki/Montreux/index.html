Montreux is a Swiss municipality and town on the shoreline of Lake Geneva at the foot of the Alps. It belongs to the district of Riviera-Pays-d'Enhaut in the canton of Vaud in Switzerland. Montreux has a population of approximately 26,000, with about 51,000 people living in the wider agglomeration.

Geography

Montreux lies on the north shore of Lake Geneva, sheltered by mountain peaks, and enjoys a mild microclimate that has earned the region the nickname of the Vaud Riviera. Vineyards, notably the nearby Lavaux terraced vineyards, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, line the surrounding slopes.

Culture

The town is world-famous for the Montreux Jazz Festival, founded in 1967, which takes place every July. The lakeside resort also inspired the Deep Purple song "Smoke on the Water", written after a fire at the Montreux Casino in 1971 during a concert by Frank Zappa. The medieval island castle of Château de Chillon stands just south of the town.

Montreux was a favourite retreat of many artists and writers. Freddie Mercury of Queen lived and recorded in Montreux; a statue of Mercury stands on the lakeside promenade.
