International Taekwon-do Federation

The ITF was founded by General Choi Hong-hi in 1966 to promote his style of taekwondo.

The International Taekwon-do Federation (ITF) is an international taekwondo organization founded on 22 March 1966 in Seoul, South Korea, by General Choi Hong-hi, regarded as the founder of taekwondo. The ITF promotes the style of taekwondo codified by Choi, distinguished by the spelling "Taekwon-do", its set of patterns (tul) known as the Chang Hon forms, and its emphasis on the theory of power and sine-wave motion in technique.

History

Choi, a South Korean army general, had led the naming of taekwondo in 1955 and founded the Korea Taekwondo Association, but broke with the Seoul authorities and established the ITF with nine founding countries in 1966. After falling out with the South Korean government, Choi moved the ITF headquarters to Toronto, Canada, in 1972 — the year the rival World Taekwondo Federation (now World Taekwondo) was established in Seoul with the Kukkiwon. The ITF later relocated its headquarters to Vienna, Austria. Choi promoted taekwondo in North Korea from the 1980s, which further deepened the schism between the ITF and South Korea-based bodies.

Splits

Following Choi's death in 2002, disputes over his succession produced multiple organisations claiming the ITF name and legacy, including groups led by Choi's son Choi Jung-hwa, by the North Korea-backed Chang Ung, and by the Vienna-based leadership (later headed by Ri Yong-son and others). Several of these bodies hold their own world championships and claim continuity with the original federation, making "the ITF" a contested designation in the taekwondo world.

Practice

ITF competition traditionally includes patterns, sparring (semi-contact, point-stop in many championships), power breaking, and special technique breaking. Its 24 Chang Hon patterns are said to represent the 24 hours of a day, symbolising the whole of a practitioner's life devoted to taekwondo.
