Turkmen (Türkmençe, Түркменче) is a Turkic language spoken by the Turkmens of Central Asia, mainly in Turkmenistan, where it is the official language, as well as in northeastern Iran and northwestern Afghanistan. It belongs to the Oghuz branch of the Turkic family, alongside Turkish and Azerbaijani, though it has also been strongly influenced by the Kipchak languages and retains features such as long vowels and the interdental fricatives inherited from Common Turkic.

Features

Turkmen is an agglutinative language with vowel harmony, subject–object–verb word order, and no grammatical gender. It preserves a contrast between long and short vowels that is distinctive among Oghuz languages. The standard language is based on the Teke dialect, though Yomut and other dialects are widely spoken.

Writing system

Turkmen was historically written in the Perso-Arabic script. In the Soviet period it was switched first to a Latin alphabet in 1928 and then to Cyrillic in 1940. After independence in 1991, Turkmenistan adopted a new Latin-based alphabet (Täze Elipbiý), containing letters such as Ň, Ý, Ž, and $, which became the official script, though Cyrillic remains in use among some communities, and the Perso-Arabic script is used by Turkmens in Iran and Afghanistan.
