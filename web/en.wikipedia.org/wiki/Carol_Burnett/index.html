Carol Creighton Burnett (born 26 April 1933) is an American actress, comedian, singer, and writer whose career spans seven decades of television. She is best known for The Carol Burnett Show (1967–1978), one of the most celebrated variety and sketch comedy programs in American television history, and is widely regarded as a pioneer for women in comedy.

Early life

Burnett was born in San Antonio, Texas, and raised in Hollywood, California, largely by her grandmother, in a one-room apartment near Hollywood Boulevard. After studying musical comedy at UCLA, she moved to New York, where she gained attention in the revue format and as a regular on The Garry Moore Show, and scored a Broadway success as Princess Winnifred in Once Upon a Mattress (1959).

The Carol Burnett Show

Premiering on CBS in 1967, The Carol Burnett Show ran for 11 seasons and 279 episodes, winning 25 Emmy Awards. With repertory players Harvey Korman, Vicki Lawrence, Lyle Waggoner, and later Tim Conway, the show produced enduring sketches including "The Family" (the origin of Mama's Family), the soap-opera parody "As the Stomach Turns," and the famous Gone with the Wind parody "Went with the Wind!", in which Burnett wore a curtain-rod dress designed by Bob Mackie that is now in the Smithsonian. Her signature sign-off—a tug on her left ear—was a private signal to her grandmother.

Later career and honors

Burnett has appeared in films including Annie (1982), as Miss Hannigan, and continued to act on stage and television into her nineties, including a role in the Apple TV+ series Palm Royale (2024). Her honors include the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2005), the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor (2013), the Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award, and the Golden Globes' Carol Burnett Award, created in 2019 and named for her as its first recipient. Her memoirs include One More Time and This Time Together.
