Meles Zenawi Asres (Ge'ez: መለስ ዜናዊ ኣስረስ; 8 May 1955 – 20 August 2012) was an Ethiopian politician and former guerrilla leader who ruled Ethiopia from 1991 until his death in 2012. As chairman of the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) and the ruling EPRDF coalition, he led the forces that overthrew the Derg military regime in 1991, served as President of the transitional government (1991–1995), and then as Prime Minister under the new federal constitution from 1995.

Early life and guerrilla years

Meles was born in Adwa in northern Ethiopia. He studied medicine at Addis Ababa University but abandoned his studies in 1974 to join the newly formed TPLF, taking the nom de guerre "Meles" from a fellow student executed by the Derg. He rose through the front's Marxist leadership, becoming its chairman in 1989, and in the same year brokered the formation of the EPRDF alliance that captured Addis Ababa in May 1991.

Rule

As head of government Meles introduced ethnic federalism, restructuring Ethiopia into regions based largely on ethnolinguistic lines, and oversaw a new constitution (1995) that enshrined a right of secession, exercised by Eritrea in 1993 after a referendum. His government pursued a "developmental state" model — state-led investment in infrastructure, agriculture and industry — that delivered rapid economic growth through the 2000s. He was a prominent African voice in international climate negotiations and a close Western ally in counterterrorism in the Horn of Africa.

His rule was also criticised for authoritarianism: the disputed 2005 elections were followed by a crackdown in which nearly 200 protesters were killed, and subsequent elections were swept by the EPRDF amid opposition complaints. Relations with Eritrea collapsed into the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000.

Death

Meles died on 20 August 2012 in Brussels, where he was receiving medical treatment; the cause was reported as an infection complicating an undisclosed illness. He received a state funeral in Addis Ababa and was succeeded as prime minister by Hailemariam Desalegn.
