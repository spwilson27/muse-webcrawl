Pondicherry

Pondicherry (officially Puducherry)
Capital of: Puducherry union territory, India
In Tamil: புதுச்சேரி (Puducherry)
Nickname: "The French Riviera of the East"

Pondicherry, officially renamed Puducherry in 2006, is the capital and largest city of the union territory of Puducherry in India. Located on the Coromandel Coast of the Bay of Bengal, about 135 km south of Chennai, it was the principal settlement of French India and retains a distinct Franco-Tamil character.

History

The area has ancient trading links — Roman-era commerce is attested at nearby Arikamedu. The French East India Company established a trading post at Pondicherry in 1674 under François Martin; the town changed hands several times between the French, Dutch, and British during the Carnatic Wars, with figures such as Joseph François Dupleix expanding French influence in India from here. France formally transferred its Indian possessions to India de facto in 1954 and de jure in 1962–63 following the Treaty of Cession (1956), and Pondicherry became a union territory.

Cityscape and culture

The old town east of a canal — the French Quarter (or "White Town") — is laid out in a grid with colonial villas, the Raj Niwas, the French Consulate, the Alliance Française, and the Sri Aurobindo Ashram, founded by Sri Aurobindo and Mirra Alfassa ("The Mother") in 1926. The experimental township of Auroville lies just outside the city. The Promenade Beach and Rock Beach front the Bay of Bengal, with a statue of Mahatma Gandhi and a French war memorial. Tamil is the predominant language, with French still taught and spoken by some residents; the city is a popular domestic tourist destination known for its cafés, churches such as the Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Jesus, and temples including the Manakula Vinayagar Temple.
