Élie Cartan

Élie Joseph Cartan (9 April 1869 – 6 May 1951) was a French mathematician who did fundamental work in the theory of Lie groups, differential systems (the PDE system, equivalent to differential forms) and differential geometry. He also made significant contributions to general relativity and, indirectly, to quantum mechanics. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the twentieth century.

Life

Cartan was born in the village of Dolomieu, Isère, the son of a blacksmith. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, where he was a student of Gaston Darboux and Sophus Lie. He taught at the universities of Montpellier, Lyon and Nancy before settling in Paris, first at the Sorbonne and later at the École Normale Supérieure. His son, Henri Cartan, was also an influential mathematician.

Work

Cartan's doctoral thesis concerned Lie algebras: he completed the classification of the simple complex Lie algebras begun by Wilhelm Killing, and gave the criterion based on the Killing form. He developed the theory of exterior differential calculus and the method of moving frames (repère mobile), which became central tools of modern differential geometry, and he introduced the notion of a Cartan connection. In 1913 he introduced spinors, later fundamental in physics as representations of the rotation group. His work on Riemannian geometry, on the equivalence problem for differential systems, and with Albert Einstein on the Einstein–Cartan theory of gravitation, placed him at the centre of the mathematics of his era—even though, because of his difficult, notation-heavy style, his influence on his contemporaries was less than it might have been.
