In the folklore of northern England, a barghest (also spelled barguest or bargest) is a monstrous black dog with huge teeth, claws and fiery eyes. It serves as an omen of death: to see one, or to hear its howl, is said to foretell the death of the witness or of someone near to them. The barghest belongs to a widespread family of spectral black dogs in British tradition.

Etymology

The origin of the word is debated. One derivation treats it as a compound of Old English or Norse elements meaning "barrow-ghost" — a spirit associated with burial mounds. Another connects it with German Berg-geist, "mountain spirit", or with "burh-ghast", the ghost of a town. The name is especially associated with Yorkshire, including the city of York, where a barghest was said to haunt the narrow lanes called snickelways.

Characteristics and legends

The barghest is described as a gigantic hound, sometimes headless or able to vanish in a flash of flame, whose chains clank as it stalks lonely roads, churchyards and places of execution. It cannot cross running water. In some tales the creature is a shapeshifter able to take human or other animal form; in others it is simply an apparition whose appearance portends disaster. Whitby tradition tells of a barghest haunting the harbour town, and the spectral dog of Trollers Gill in the Yorkshire Dales is another well-known example.

In popular culture

Barghests appear in fantasy literature, role-playing games and video games, usually as fearsome canine monsters of the underworld. The creature is among the folkloric sources often cited for the spectral hound in Arthur Conan Doyle's The Hound of the Baskervilles, alongside Black Shuck of East Anglia.
