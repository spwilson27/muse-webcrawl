Alvin Cullum York (December 13, 1887 – September 2, 1964), also known by his rank as Sergeant York, was an American soldier who was one of the most decorated United States Army soldiers of World War I. He received the Medal of Honor for his actions during the Meuse-Argonne Offensive. On October 8, 1918, York led an attack that killed at least 28 German soldiers, captured 132 others and 35 machine guns, and silenced a position that had pinned down his battalion near Chatel-Chéhéry, France.

A Tennessee farmer and former conscientious objector on religious grounds, York was initially denied an exemption and drafted in 1917; long discussions with his officers about the Bible and war persuaded him that fighting could be justified. Before the war he had been a hard-drinking brawler; a religious conversion in 1914 made him a devout member of a pacifist-leaning church, which made his eventual decision to fight all the more notable in American popular memory.

Early life

York was born in a two-room log cabin in Pall Mall, Tennessee, the third of eleven children. His father, a blacksmith, died when York was young, and York helped raise his siblings, receiving only a few months of schooling. He became an expert marksman through hunting and turkey shoots in the Tennessee hills.

Action of 8 October 1918

During the Meuse-Argonne Offensive, York's patrol of seventeen men worked behind German lines to silence machine-gun nests. When fire killed or wounded half the patrol, York, by then a corporal, charged a nest single-handedly with rifle and pistol; his marksmanship forced the surrender of a large body of German troops, whom the surviving Americans marched back to their lines. Promoted to sergeant, he was awarded the Medal of Honor, presented by General John J. Pershing.

Later life

York returned home to national fame but declined most commercial offers. He devoted himself to improving education in his poor rural region, founding the York Agricultural Institute in Jamestown, Tennessee, and worked to bring roads and schooling to Fentress County. The 1941 film Sergeant York, starring Gary Cooper, retold his story and won Cooper an Academy Award; York insisted proceeds support his educational work.

Legacy

The Sergeant Alvin C. York State Historic Park in Pall Mall preserves his home and gristmill. York remains among the best-known American enlisted heroes of the First World War, and his story is frequently cited in discussions of conscience, faith and military service.
