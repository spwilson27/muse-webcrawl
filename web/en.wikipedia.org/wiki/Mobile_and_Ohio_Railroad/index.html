Mobile and Ohio Railroad

Reporting mark: M&O
Locale: Alabama, Mississippi, Tennessee, Kentucky, Illinois, Missouri
Dates of operation: 1848–1940
Successor: Gulf, Mobile and Ohio Railroad

The Mobile and Ohio Railroad was a railroad in the Southern United States. Chartered in 1848 in both Alabama and Mississippi, it was envisioned to link the port of Mobile, Alabama, on the Gulf of Mexico with the Ohio River near Cairo, Illinois, giving the cotton-producing interior a direct outlet to the Gulf that bypassed the Mississippi River route to New Orleans. When completed in 1861, just as the American Civil War began, its 486-mile (782 km) main line was one of the longest railroads in the world built under a single charter.

History

Construction began in 1852 and proceeded northward from Mobile through Mississippi and western Tennessee. The line suffered severe damage during the Civil War, when both armies tore up track, burned bridges, and seized rolling stock. Rebuilt after the war, the railroad struggled financially in the postwar South and passed through receivership in the 1870s. Under later management it extended its reach, gaining access to St. Louis, Missouri, and building branches including lines to Montgomery, Alabama. Long controlled by the Southern Railway as an affiliated property, the Mobile and Ohio nevertheless retained its own identity for decades.

Merger

In 1940 the Mobile and Ohio merged with the Gulf, Mobile and Northern Railroad to form the Gulf, Mobile and Ohio Railroad (GM&O), creating a system running from the Gulf of Mexico to Chicago and Kansas City. The GM&O in turn merged with the Illinois Central Railroad in 1972 to form Illinois Central Gulf.

Legacy

The historic Mobile and Ohio passenger station in Mobile, Alabama, a Spanish Mission Revival building completed in 1907, survives as a landmark and bus terminal. Much of the original main line remains in service today under the Canadian National Railway, successor to the Illinois Central.
