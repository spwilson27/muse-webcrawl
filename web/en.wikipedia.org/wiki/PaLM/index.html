PaLM (Pathways Language Model) is a large language model developed by Google, announced in April 2022. It is a dense, decoder-only Transformer trained on 780 billion tokens of text and code, and is named for Google's Pathways system for large-scale training across many accelerator pods. The largest version, PaLM 540B, has 540 billion parameters and was trained on 6,144 TPU v4 chips — at the time the largest TPU configuration publicly described.

Capabilities

The PaLM paper reported state-of-the-art results on most of 29 English NLP benchmarks the authors tested, strong performance on reasoning tasks when prompted with chain-of-thought examples, and surprising abilities such as explaining jokes, solving multi-step arithmetic and word problems, and writing and debugging code. The model also performed competitively on multilingual tasks, having been trained on text in over 120 languages. Two smaller versions, PaLM 62B and PaLM 8B, were described in the same report. A vision-language extension, PaLM-E, and instruction-tuned and medical variants (Flan-PaLM and Med-PaLM) followed.

PaLM API and products

In March 2023 Google opened an API for PaLM and integrated the model into consumer and enterprise products; a PaLM-powered system underlay the first releases of the Bard chatbot. In May 2023 Google announced PaLM 2, a more efficient successor trained with an improved data mixture and model scaling, which powered updated Bard and a number of features across Google services. The PaLM branding was subsequently retired in favor of the Gemini model family from late 2023.

Reception and discussion

PaLM intensified discussion of scaling laws — the observation that language-model capabilities keep improving predictably with model size, data and compute — and of the risks of very large models, including bias, misinformation and environmental cost. The accompanying paper included an unusually extensive analysis of training-data documentation, bias and toxicity, and responsible-AI considerations.
