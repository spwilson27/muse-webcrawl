Béarnaise sauce

Béarnaise sauce is a sauce made of clarified butter emulsified with egg yolks and flavoured with a reduction of white wine vinegar, shallots and tarragon. It is a "child" of hollandaise sauce, from which it differs by its seasonings, and is traditionally served with steak — famously in steak frites and chateaubriand.

History

The sauce is credited to chef Jean-Louis-François Collinet and was first served in 1836 at the Pavillon Henri IV restaurant in Saint-Germain-en-Laye. Despite the name, it was not created in Béarn; it honours Henry IV, who was born in that province.

Preparation

A reduction of vinegar, wine, shallots, tarragon stems and peppercorns is whisked into egg yolks over gentle heat, and warm clarified butter is added gradually to form a stable emulsion, finished with chopped tarragon and chervil.
