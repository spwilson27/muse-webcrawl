Lorenzaccio is a French romantic drama in five acts by Alfred de Musset, written in 1834 and published in the collection Un Spectacle dans un fauteuil. Set in Medici Florence, it is generally considered Musset's masterpiece and one of the greatest French plays of the 19th century, although it was not staged until 1896, decades after the author's death.

Plot

Lorenzo de' Medici — nicknamed "Lorenzaccio" (a contemptuous diminutive) by the Florentines — is the cousin and debauched companion of Duke Alessandro de' Medici, the tyrannical ruler of Florence. Lorenzo affects corruption: he drinks, brawls, and panders to the Duke, and republicans who once hoped in him now despise him as a coward and libertine.

In secret, Lorenzo is preparing to assassinate the Duke, having concluded that only tyrannicide can free Florence. Yet the years of feigned vice have corroded his own idealism, and he doubts whether the Florentines will use the liberty he buys them. He lures the Duke to his rooms with the promise of an amorous assignation and murders him — but the republicans fail to act, the Medici install Cosimo as the new duke, and Lorenzo, a fugitive in Venice, is himself assassinated by a bounty hunter. The murder changes nothing.

Themes

The play explores the corruption of the idealist who must wear the mask of vice, the futility of isolated heroic action, and the political apathy of a people habituated to tyranny. Written in the disillusionment following the July Revolution of 1830, it reflects Musset's pessimism about the generation of young liberals who had failed to transform France.

Performance history

Regarded in its century as an unstageable closet drama — with some 39 scenes, dozens of characters, and constant changes of location — Lorenzaccio was first performed in 1896 with Sarah Bernhardt in the title role. It has since entered the repertoire of the Comédie-Française and is a staple of French theatre; its Shakespearean scope and political despair are frequently compared to Hamlet.
