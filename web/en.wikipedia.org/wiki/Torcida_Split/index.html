Torcida Split is an ultras group of supporters of the Croatian football club HNK Hajduk Split. Founded on 28 October 1950, it is regarded as the oldest ultras group in Europe. The group's name derives from the Portuguese word torcida, meaning a group of supporters or fans, which the founders adopted after reports of Brazilian fans at the 1950 FIFA World Cup.

History

Torcida was founded in 1950 by a group of Hajduk Split fans, many of them students and young workers in Zagreb, who had been inspired by accounts of the passionate Brazilian supporters at the World Cup in Brazil that year. The group was among the first in Europe to organize coordinated support involving banners, chants, and choreographed displays. Under Yugoslavia, the group had a complicated relationship with the authorities; in later decades Torcida became closely associated with Croatian identity, particularly during and after the Croatian War of Independence in the 1990s.

The group occupies the north stand of Stadion Poljud and is known for elaborate tifos, pyrotechnics, and intense support at derby matches, especially the Eternal Derby against Dinamo Zagreb, whose supporters are the Bad Blue Boys.

Rivalries and friendships

Torcida's main rivals are the Bad Blue Boys of Dinamo Zagreb. The group maintains friendly relations with the supporters of Benfica (No Name Boys) and some other European groups.
