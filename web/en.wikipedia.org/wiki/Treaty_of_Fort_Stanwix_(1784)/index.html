Treaty of Fort Stanwix (1784)

Signed22 October 1784
LocationFort Stanwix, New York
PartiesUnited States, Iroquois Confederacy

The Treaty of Fort Stanwix of 1784 was a treaty between the United States and Native Americans from the Iroquois Confederacy, signed at Fort Stanwix (present-day Rome, New York) on October 22, 1784. It was one of the first treaties the United States concluded with Native Americans following the American Revolutionary War.

Background

During the Revolutionary War, four of the six Iroquois nations had sided with the British, while the Oneida and Tuscarora supported the Americans. After the British defeat, the United States treated the hostile Iroquois nations as conquered peoples, despite the fact that Britain had ceded land in the Treaty of Paris (1783) that it did not itself control.

Terms

American commissioners, including Oliver Wolcott, Richard Butler, and Arthur Lee, demanded significant land cessions. Under the treaty the Iroquois ceded all claims to the Ohio country and a strip of land along the Niagara River, and gave up a tract in western Pennsylvania. The Six Nations retained their core lands in New York, and the treaty nominally exempted the lands of the Oneida and Tuscarora, allies of the United States.

Criticism and aftermath

The treaty was deeply controversial. Many Iroquois leaders denied that the delegates present had authority to cede lands, and the Western Confederacy of tribes living in the ceded territory rejected it entirely, contributing to the Northwest Indian War. Within the Confederacy itself, the treaty aggravated internal divisions; Mohawk leader Joseph Brant denounced the negotiations. The cessions were partly renegotiated in later agreements, but the 1784 treaty set the pattern for aggressive American treaty-making in the Old Northwest.
