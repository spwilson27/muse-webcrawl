Prader–Willi syndrome

FieldMedical genetics
CauseLoss of paternal genes on chromosome 15q11–13
Frequency1 in 10,000–30,000 births

Chromosome 15, the locus of PWS

Prader–Willi syndrome (PWS) is a genetic disorder caused by the loss of function of specific genes on chromosome 15. It is characterised by weak muscle tone and feeding difficulties in infancy, followed in childhood by insatiable hunger (hyperphagia) that can lead to life-threatening obesity, along with intellectual disability and distinctive behavioural features.

Genetics

In most cases PWS results from a deletion of paternal DNA in the 15q11–13 region; other cases arise from maternal uniparental disomy 15 or imprinting defects. The syndrome is named after Swiss physicians Andrea Prader and Heinrich Willi, who described it in 1956 together with Alexis Labhart.

Management

There is no cure; management centres on strict supervision of food access, growth hormone therapy, physical activity and behavioural support.
