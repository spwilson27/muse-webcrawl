White Buses

The White Buses was a Swedish Red Cross operation in the spring of 1945, in the closing weeks of the Second World War, to rescue concentration camp inmates from areas under Nazi control and transport them to safety in neutral Sweden. The expedition, led by Count Folke Bernadotte, vice-president of the Swedish Red Cross, brought about 15,345 prisoners to safety, roughly half of them Scandinavians.

Background

Bernadotte negotiated with Heinrich Himmler, who hoped to use the releases in a separate peace feeler to the Western Allies. The convoys used buses, trucks, and ambulances painted white with red crosses to distinguish them from military vehicles — though one convoy was mistakenly attacked by Allied aircraft.

Operation

Beginning in March 1945, the expedition gathered Scandinavian prisoners at Neuengamme camp near Hamburg and transported them to Denmark and Sweden. As the war collapsed the operation expanded to prisoners of many nationalities, including thousands of Jewish women evacuated from Ravensbrück. Buses travelled as far as Dachau and Theresienstadt.

Legacy

The White Buses remain a celebrated episode of humanitarian rescue; surviving buses are preserved at the Yad Vashem memorial in Israel and in Sweden.
