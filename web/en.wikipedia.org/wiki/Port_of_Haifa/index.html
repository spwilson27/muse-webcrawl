The Port of Haifa is the largest of Israel's three international seaports, located in downtown Haifa at the foot of Mount Carmel on the Mediterranean Sea. Together with its newer satellite, the Bayport terminal, it handles the majority of Israel's container traffic and is a principal gateway for cargo serving Israel and, through overland routes, parts of Jordan and the eastern Mediterranean.

History

Haifa's sheltered bay had served shipping since antiquity, but the modern port was a creation of the British Mandate for Palestine: construction under engineer Sir Palmer and the harbour works of the early 1930s produced a dredged deep-water harbour with breakwaters, officially opened in 1933. During the Second World War, Haifa was a key Allied naval and oil terminal — the Iraq Petroleum Company pipeline from Kirkuk ended there, feeding the Haifa refineries. In 1948 the port was the scene of mass immigration to the new State of Israel and of the departures of Palestinian refugees during the Nakba. The Kishon port area east of the main harbour was added in the 1960s, and the Carmel container terminal opened in 2010.

Privatisation and Bayport

In 2021 the new Haifa Bayport, built and operated by Shanghai International Port Group (SIPG), opened on reclaimed land adjacent to the existing port — part of a national programme to introduce competition between terminals. The state-owned Haifa Port Company itself was privatised in 2023, sold to a consortium led by India's Adani Ports with Israel's Gadot Group for about ₪4 billion. The port complex now includes specialised terminals for containers, grain (the Dagon silo), chemicals, oil, general cargo, and a passenger terminal serving cruise ships and ferries.

Operations

Haifa's terminals work around the clock except on the Sabbath and holidays, and the port connects to Israel's rail and road networks, with a rail link into the port area. Its strategic position has also made it sensitive to regional conflict: the port has faced boycott campaigns, missile threats during wars in Lebanon and Gaza, and scrutiny in Israel and the United States over Chinese involvement in the Bayport terminal given the presence of the Israeli Navy's main base — with its submarine flotilla — in the same harbour.
