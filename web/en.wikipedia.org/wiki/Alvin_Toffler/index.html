Alvin Toffler

Alvin Toffler (October 4, 1928 – June 27, 2016) was an American writer, futurist, and businessman known for his works discussing modern technologies, including the digital revolution and the "information age". A former associate editor of Fortune magazine, he is best known for the bestsellers Future Shock (1970) and The Third Wave (1980), several of them co-authored with his wife, Heidi Toffler.

Early life and career

Toffler was born in New York City to Jewish immigrants from Poland and studied English at New York University. After graduating he worked for five years in industrial jobs, including in a steel mill, experience he later drew on when writing about work and organisations. He then became a journalist, working for newspapers and for Fortune, and wrote for IBM and other organisations on the social impact of computing.

Major ideas

In Future Shock Toffler argued that accelerating technological and social change produces a psychological state of disorientation — "too much change in too short a period of time". The Third Wave described history as successive "waves": the agricultural revolution (First Wave), the industrial age (Second Wave), and the post-industrial information age (Third Wave), characterised by decentralisation, demassification, and the rise of the "prosumer". Powershift (1990) examined the shifting sources of power from violence and wealth toward knowledge.

Influence

The Tofflers' books were translated into dozens of languages and influenced politicians, executives, and reformers, particularly in China, where The Third Wave circulated widely among officials in the 1980s. The couple founded the consultancy Toffler Associates.
