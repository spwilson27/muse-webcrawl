Mahdist State

The Mahdist State, also known as Mahdist Sudan, was a state based on a religious and political movement launched in Sudan in 1881 by Muhammad Ahmad bin Abdullah, who proclaimed himself the Mahdi — the prophesied redeemer of Islam. The state lasted until 1898, when it was destroyed by an Anglo-Egyptian army.

The Mahdist War

Rising against Turco-Egyptian rule and foreign influence, Muhammad Ahmad's followers, the Ansar (dervishes), won a string of victories, annihilating an Egyptian army under Hicks Pasha at Shaykan in 1883 and capturing Khartoum in January 1885 after a long siege in which General Charles George Gordon was killed. The Mahdi died of typhus a few months later, in June 1885, and was succeeded by the Khalifa Abdallahi ibn Muhammad, who consolidated the state from its new capital, Omdurman.

Government

The Mahdist State was a theocratic Islamic state governed by sharia, with the Khalifa as absolute ruler. It maintained armies, a treasury (Beit el Mal), coinage, a postal service, and courts, while waging jihad against Egypt and Ethiopia and suppressing internal revolts. The population suffered severely from war, famine, and disease during the 1885–1891 period.

Downfall

Determined to avenge Gordon and secure the Nile, Britain authorized the reconquest of Sudan under Herbert Kitchener. Advancing methodically with railways and gunboats, the Anglo-Egyptian army destroyed the Mahdist host at the Battle of Omdurman on 2 September 1898; the Khalifa was hunted down and killed at Umm Diwaykarat in November 1899. Sudan then became the Anglo-Egyptian condominium.
