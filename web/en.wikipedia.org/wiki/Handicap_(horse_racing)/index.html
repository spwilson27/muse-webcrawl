Handicap (horse racing)

A handicap race in horse racing is a race in which the horses carry different weights, allocated by the handicapper. A better horse will carry a heavier weight, to give it a disadvantage when racing against slower horses, in the hope of making the race more competitive. The skill in betting on a handicap race lies in predicting which horse is "ahead of the handicapper".

How handicapping works

An official handicapper assesses each horse's performances and assigns it a rating. In a handicap, the highest-rated horse carries top weight, and the others carry proportionally less according to the difference in their ratings — typically one pound per rating point. As a horse wins, its rating is raised, so it must carry more weight in future handicaps.

Notable handicap races

Famous handicaps include the Grand National over steeplechase fences at Aintree, the Melbourne Cup in Australia, the Cesarewitch and the Cambridgeshire at Newmarket, and the Ebor Handicap at York. In the United States, handicap races such as the Santa Anita Handicap were historically among the richest races in the calendar.
