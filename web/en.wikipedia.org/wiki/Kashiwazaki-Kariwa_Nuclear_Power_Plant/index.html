Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Nuclear Power Plant

The Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Nuclear Power Plant (柏崎刈羽原子力発電所) is a nuclear power station in Niigata Prefecture, Japan, straddling the city of Kashiwazaki and the village of Kariwa on the coast of the Sea of Japan. Operated by Tokyo Electric Power Company (TEPCO), its seven boiling water reactors have a total rated capacity of about 8,212 MW, making it the largest nuclear power station in the world by net electrical output.

History

Construction began in 1980 and the first unit entered service in 1985; units 6 and 7, opened in 1996–97, were the world's first Advanced Boiling Water Reactors. The site covers about 4.2 km².

2007 earthquake and shutdowns

The July 2007 Chūetsu offshore earthquake (magnitude 6.6) shook the plant beyond parts of its design basis, causing a transformer fire and minor radioactive releases; all reactors shut down safely but the station remained largely closed for years while seismic defences were upgraded. After the 2011 Fukushima disaster the remaining operating units were progressively idled, and the plant has been offline pending regulatory approval and local consent for restart. Units 6 and 7 passed Nuclear Regulation Authority safety reviews in 2017, but a series of security lapses — including an unauthorised ID-card use and inoperative intrusion-detection equipment — led the NRA to issue an effective ban on restart preparations in 2021, lifted in late 2023 after corrective measures.
