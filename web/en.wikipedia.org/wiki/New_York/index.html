Overview

New York is a state in the northeastern United States. It is the fourth-most populous US state, and is dominated economically and culturally by New York City — the largest city in the country, at its southeastern corner — while its capital is Albany on the Hudson River. To avoid confusion with the city, the state is often called 'New York State'.

The region was home to the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy and Algonquian peoples before Dutch colonists founded New Netherland in the 1620s; the English seized it in 1664 and renamed it for the Duke of York. New York was one of the original Thirteen Colonies and ratified the US Constitution in 1788.

Geography

Residents distinguish 'the City' from 'upstate' — everything north and west. Upstate New York includes the Hudson Valley, the Catskill and Adirondack mountains (Adirondack Park is the largest protected area in the contiguous United States), the Finger Lakes wine country, Buffalo and Rochester on Lake Erie and Lake Ontario, and Niagara Falls on the Canadian border. The Erie Canal (1825) made New York the young republic's commercial gateway to the Great Lakes.
