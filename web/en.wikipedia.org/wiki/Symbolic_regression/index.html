Symbolic regression

[Image placeholder: symbolic regression]

Symbolic regression is a type of regression analysis that searches the space of mathematical expressions to find the model that best fits a dataset, both in terms of accuracy and simplicity. Unlike traditional regression, which fits parameters of a predetermined model form (such as a polynomial of fixed degree), symbolic regression searches over both the structure of the model and its parameters.

Method

The technique is most often implemented with genetic programming, in which candidate expressions are represented as trees built from variables, constants, and functions, and are evolved through selection, crossover, and mutation. Fitness combines prediction error with a parsimony pressure penalising complexity, so that compact, interpretable formulas are favoured. The result is often a Pareto front of expressions trading accuracy against simplicity.

Applications

Because it produces human-readable formulas, symbolic regression is used for scientific discovery — recovering physical laws from data — and for interpretable modelling in engineering and finance. Notable software includes the commercial Eureqa package (which famously re-derived equations of motion from pendulum data), the open-source PySR, and gplearn.

Challenges

The search space of expressions is enormous, so symbolic regression is computationally expensive and can overfit or produce bloated expressions without suitable complexity control and validation.
