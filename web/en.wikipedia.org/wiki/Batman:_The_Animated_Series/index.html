Batman: The Animated Series (often shortened to Batman TAS or BTAS) is an American animated superhero television series based on the DC Comics superhero Batman. Developed by Bruce Timm and Eric Radomski and produced by Warner Bros. Animation, it originally aired on Fox Kids from 1992 to 1995 with a total of 85 episodes. It is widely praised for its thematic complexity, film-noir aesthetics, darker tone, and voice acting, and is frequently ranked among the greatest animated series — and greatest Batman adaptations — of all time.

Production and style

Capitalising on the success of Tim Burton's live-action films, the series adopted a distinctive visual style dubbed "Dark Deco": Gotham City drawn with 1940s Art Deco design, black backgrounds (animators drew on black paper rather than white), and muted colours. Danny Elfman's film theme was adapted for the opening, with Shirley Walker becoming the series' principal composer. Stories balanced action for children with adult themes of loss, obsession, and corruption.

Characters and legacy

Kevin Conroy's grave Batman and Mark Hamill's manic Joker became definitive portrayals for a generation. The series reinvented villains such as Mr. Freeze — given a tragic backstory in the Emmy-winning episode "Heart of Ice" — and created an entirely new character, Harley Quinn (written by Paul Dini, voiced by Arleen Sorkin), who proved so popular she entered the comics canon. It won four Emmy Awards and spawned the DC Animated Universe, including Superman: The Animated Series, Batman Beyond, and Justice League, as well as the feature film Batman: Mask of the Phantasm (1993). A streamlined sequel, The New Batman Adventures, followed in 1997.
