Jajang

Born590, Silla (Korea)
Died658
ReligionBuddhism
Known forFounding Tongdosa; establishing the Korean Vinaya tradition

Jajang (590–658), also known as Jajang Yulsa, was a celebrated Buddhist monk of the Korean kingdom of Silla. He is credited with founding the temple of Tongdosa, one of the Three Jewels Temples of Korea, and with establishing the Vinaya (monastic discipline) school in Korea.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family related to the Silla royal house, Jajang travelled to Tang China in 636 to study Buddhism. There he is said to have received relics of the Buddha, including a skull fragment, a tooth, and a piece of the Buddha's robe, from the bodhisattva Manjusri in a vision or encounter on Mount Wutai.

Tongdosa and the relics

Returning to Silla in 643, Jajang founded Tongdosa in 646 and enshrined the Buddha relics there, which is why the temple's main hall contains no Buddha statue — the relics in the stupa courtyard serve as the object of veneration. He also advised Queen Seondeok on the construction of the nine-storey wooden pagoda of Hwangnyongsa, intended to protect the kingdom from its enemies.

Legacy

Jajang's commentaries and his work in organising the Korean sangha under the precepts made him one of the most influential figures in the history of Korean Buddhism.
