Creek (stream)

A creek is a small to medium-sized natural stream of water, smaller than a river and larger than a brook. The term is used especially in North America and Australia, and there is no fixed standard of size that separates a creek from a brook, run, kill, branch or river; the names are largely a matter of local custom and history.

Usage and regional variants

In British English, "creek" traditionally means a narrow tidal inlet of the sea or the mouth of a stream — a meaning preserved in coastal place names. In the United States, Canada and Australia, the word was extended to freshwater streams, and thousands of waterways bear names such as "Mill Creek" or "Dry Creek". Some American regions use distinctive terms: kill in the former Dutch colonies of the Hudson and Delaware valleys (from Dutch kil), run in parts of the mid-Atlantic and Midwest, and branch or fork in the South. In Australia and New Zealand a creek may be a substantial seasonal watercourse that dries up for part of the year.

Characteristics

Creeks form part of a watershed's drainage network, carrying water from springs, rainfall and melting snow into larger streams and rivers. Many are shallow enough to wade or ford. Ecologically, creeks and headwater streams support diverse aquatic life, move nutrients and sediment downstream, and form riparian corridors used by wildlife. Because of their small size they are sensitive to changes in land use, water extraction and pollution.
