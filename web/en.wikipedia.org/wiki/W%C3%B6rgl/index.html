Wörgl

Wörgl is a city in the Austrian state of Tyrol, in the district of Kufstein, with a population of roughly 14,000. It lies in the Inn valley near the confluence of the Inn and Brixentaler Ache rivers and is an important railway and road junction.

The Wörgl experiment

Wörgl became internationally famous during the Great Depression as the site of the "Miracle of Wörgl". In 1932, mayor Michael Unterguggenberger introduced a local stamp scrip — "work certificates" that lost 1% of their value each month unless a stamp was purchased. The demurrage currency circulated rapidly, funded public works, and sharply reduced local unemployment, until the Austrian National Bank asserted its monopoly on currency and had the scheme banned in 1933.

Transport and economy

The city's main railway station is a hub on the line between Innsbruck and Salzburg/Munich, with a branch into the Brixen valley. Wörgl markets itself as an "energy city" for its renewable-energy initiatives.
