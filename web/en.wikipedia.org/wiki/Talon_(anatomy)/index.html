Talon (anatomy)

In anatomy, a talon is a sharp, hooked claw found on some animals, most notably birds of prey. The word derives from Latin talus, meaning ankle or heel, through Old French. Talons are used by raptors to catch, hold, and kill prey.

In birds of prey

Raptors such as eagles, hawks, falcons, and owls have strong feet with curved talons, powered by powerful leg muscles and a locking mechanism in the toes that lets them maintain grip with little effort. The hallux (rear toe) talon of large eagles is especially large and serves as the main killing instrument. Falcons, by contrast, rely more on a notched beak to dispatch prey, having talons adapted mainly for grasping.

Other uses

The term is also applied more loosely to the claws of predatory mammals, certain dinosaurs (such as the sickle claw of dromaeosaurids), and in dentistry to the talon cusp, an extra cusp on the front teeth.
