Holy Week

Holy Week
Observed byChristians worldwide
BeginsPalm Sunday
EndsHoly Saturday (followed by Easter Sunday)
ObservancesProcessions, the Paschal Triduum, Tenebrae, Stations of the Cross

Holy Week is the most sacred week in the Christian liturgical year, commemorating the final week of Jesus's life in Jerusalem, from his triumphal entry on Palm Sunday through his Last Supper, crucifixion and burial, culminating in the celebration of his resurrection at Easter. It is the final week of Lent in Western Christianity.

Elaborate Holy Week processions, such as those of Seville's Semana Santa, are famous in Spain and Latin America.

The days of Holy Week

Palm Sunday recalls Jesus's entry into Jerusalem, greeted by crowds waving palm branches; blessed palms are distributed in many churches. The following days (Holy Monday to Wednesday, the latter sometimes called Spy Wednesday) recall events including the anointing at Bethany and Judas's agreement to betray Jesus. Maundy Thursday commemorates the Last Supper, the institution of the Eucharist and Jesus's washing of the disciples' feet; its name derives from mandatum ("commandment"). Good Friday solemnly commemorates the crucifixion and death of Jesus, with services including the veneration of the Cross and the Stations of the Cross. Holy Saturday is a day of quiet waiting, ending with the Easter Vigil after nightfall.

The Paschal Triduum

In Roman Catholic and many other traditions, the Easter (Paschal) Triduum — from the evening of Maundy Thursday through Easter Sunday — forms a single great celebration of the Paschal mystery: Christ's passing from death to life. The Easter Vigil, with the lighting of the Paschal candle, readings tracing salvation history, baptisms and the first Mass of Easter, is its climax.

Customs around the world

Holy Week is marked by penitential processions in Spain (the cofradías of Seville and Málaga), the Philippines (including self-flagellation and passion plays), Latin America, Malta and Sicily. Eastern Orthodox churches observe Holy and Great Week with long services such as the Bridegroom Matins and the Epitaphios procession on Good Friday evening. In many countries Good Friday and Easter Monday are public holidays.
