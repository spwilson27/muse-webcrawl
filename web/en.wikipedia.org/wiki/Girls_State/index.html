Girls State

Girls State is a summer leadership and citizenship program sponsored by the American Legion Auxiliary for high school girls in the United States. The program, first held in 1937, is designed to educate young women about the duties, privileges, and responsibilities of franchised citizenship, with a particular emphasis on the workings of state government.

History

The Girls State program was organized in 1937 by the American Legion Auxiliary as a counterpart to the Boys State program, which the American Legion had founded two years earlier. The first sessions were held in a small number of states, but the program expanded rapidly, and by the mid-twentieth century nearly every state held an annual Girls State session. Over the decades, hundreds of thousands of young women have participated, and alumnae include governors, members of Congress, judges, and civic leaders.

Program

Participants, often called "citizens," are typically high school juniors selected by their schools and local Auxiliary units on the basis of leadership, character, and scholarship. During a session lasting about a week, citizens are assigned to mythical cities and counties, form political parties (conventionally the Nationalists and the Federalists), draft party platforms, campaign for office, and hold elections for municipal, county, and state offices — including a Girls State governor. The week usually culminates in an inauguration ceremony and a visit to the actual state capitol, where participants may meet state officials and use the legislative chambers.

In addition to elections, the program includes instruction in parliamentary procedure, public speaking, and the structure of American government. Many states also operate a Girls Nation program, in which two senators selected from each Girls State travel to Washington, D.C., to simulate the workings of the United States Senate.
