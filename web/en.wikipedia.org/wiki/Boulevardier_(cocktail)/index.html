Boulevardier (cocktail)

TypeCocktail
Base spiritBourbon or rye whiskey
IngredientsWhiskey, Campari, sweet vermouth
OriginParis, 1920s
RelatedNegroni

The Boulevardier is a cocktail made with whiskey, Campari, and sweet vermouth. It is essentially a Negroni in which whiskey replaces gin, giving a richer, warmer drink associated with 1920s Paris.

History

The drink is credited to Erskine Gwynne, an American writer living in Paris who founded a monthly magazine called The Boulevardier, named for the fashionable Parisian "man-about-town." The recipe was popularized by bartender Harry McElhone of Harry's New York Bar in Paris and appeared in his 1927 book Barflies and Cocktails. Long overshadowed by the Negroni, the Boulevardier enjoyed a revival during the 21st-century craft cocktail movement.

Preparation

The classic proportions combine 1½ parts bourbon or rye whiskey, 1 part Campari, and 1 part sweet vermouth, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled glass (often over a large ice cube), and garnished with an orange twist or cherry. Rye gives a spicier profile; bourbon a rounder one. Some modern recipes equalize the whiskey to 2 parts for a more spirit-forward drink.

Variations

The Old Pal, a drier relative, uses rye with dry vermouth and Campari. Swapping proportions and amari generates a family of related stirred drinks in the Negroni template.
