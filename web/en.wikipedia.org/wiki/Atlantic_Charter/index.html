Atlantic Charter

The Atlantic Charter was a joint declaration released by U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt and British Prime Minister Winston Churchill on 14 August 1941, following their meeting aboard warships at Placentia Bay, Newfoundland. It set out a vision for the post-war world.

Points

The charter's eight points included: no territorial aggrandisement by the United States or the United Kingdom; no territorial changes against the wishes of the peoples concerned; respect for the right of peoples to choose their own form of government; equal access to trade and raw materials; international economic cooperation; freedom from fear and want; freedom of the seas; and the abandonment of the use of force, with disarmament of aggressor nations pending a permanent system of security.

Legacy

The Charter was endorsed by the Allied nations in the Declaration by United Nations of January 1942 and influenced the founding of the United Nations and the post-war international order.
