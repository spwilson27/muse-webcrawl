Zuni Pueblo, New Mexico

Zuni Pueblo (Zuni: Halona Idiwan'a) is a census-designated place in McKinley County, New Mexico, United States, and the principal settlement of the Zuni Tribe, a federally recognized Native American tribe. The population was about 6,300 at the 2010 census.

Geography and community

The pueblo lies on the Zuni River, a tributary of the Little Colorado River, in western New Mexico near the Arizona border, at an elevation of roughly 1,900 metres. It is the largest of the New Mexico pueblos by population, and most residents are Zuni people, the majority of whom speak the Zuni language — a language isolate unrelated to any other known language.

History

The Zuni are descendants of the Ancestral Pueblo peoples. Halona, on the site of the modern pueblo, was one of the settlements encountered by Coronado's expedition in 1540 during its search for the fabled Seven Cities of Cibola. Zuni artists are renowned for their silverwork, turquoise inlay jewelry, and carved stone fetishes.
