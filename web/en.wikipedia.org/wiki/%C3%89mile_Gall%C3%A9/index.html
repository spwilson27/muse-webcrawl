Émile Gallé

Émile Gallé (8 May 1846 – 23 September 1904) was a French artist and designer who worked in glass, ceramics, and furniture, and was a central figure of the Art Nouveau movement. Based in Nancy, he founded and led the École de Nancy, an alliance of artists and manufacturers devoted to naturalistic design. His cameo glass vases decorated with flowers, insects, and landscapes, often inscribed with lines of poetry, became emblematic of French Art Nouveau.

Life and work

Gallé was born in Nancy, where his father Charles ran a business producing glassware and faience. After studying philosophy, botany, and drawing, and training in glassmaking at Meisenthal, he took over the family firm in the 1870s. His botanical knowledge deeply informed his designs, which drew on the plants and insects of Lorraine.

Glass and furniture

Gallé perfected techniques of cameo glass — layered glass etched and carved so motifs stand out in relief — along with enamelling and acid etching. His workshops produced both unique exhibition pieces (pièces uniques) and serial wares signed "Gallé". In furniture, he pioneered marquetry with many native woods to create pictorial, plant-inspired decoration.

École de Nancy and legacy

In 1901 Gallé became president of the newly founded École de Nancy, which united figures such as Louis Majorelle and the Daum brothers. Ill with leukaemia, he died in 1904; his firm continued under his widow until 1914, with production resuming until 1931. His work is held by major museums, including the Musée de l'École de Nancy.
