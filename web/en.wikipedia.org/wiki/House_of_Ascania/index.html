The House of Ascania (German: Askanier) is a dynasty of German rulers that takes its name from Ascania Castle near Aschersleben. Counts of Ballenstedt from the 11th century, the Ascanians rose to prominence under Albert the Bear, who became the first Margrave of Brandenburg in 1157, and his descendants ruled Brandenburg until 1320, Saxony, Anhalt, and Saxe-Lauenburg, and supplied electors of Saxony.

History

Albert the Bear inherited Brandenburg from the Slavic prince Pribislav and consolidated German rule east of the Elbe. The margravial line died out in 1320, after which Brandenburg passed to other dynasties. The Anhalt branch, descending from Albert's youngest son Bernhard, fragmented into numerous principalities — Anhalt-Dessau, Anhalt-Köthen, Anhalt-Bernburg and others — reunified as the Duchy of Anhalt in 1863 and ruled by Ascanian dukes until the German revolution of 1918. A Saxon branch held the Electorate of Saxony (Saxe-Wittenberg) until 1422 and Saxe-Lauenburg until 1689. Catherine the Great of Russia was born an Ascanian princess of Anhalt-Zerbst.

Legacy

The head of the house in the modern era is Eduard, Prince of Anhalt. Ascanian names persist in Brandenburg's geography — Albert's title "the Bear" is echoed in Berlin's bear emblem — and in the Anhalt region of Saxony-Anhalt.
