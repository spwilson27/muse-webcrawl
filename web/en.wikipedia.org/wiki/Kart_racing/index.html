Kart racing

Kart racing, or karting, is a form of open-wheel motorsport using small, open, four-wheeled vehicles called karts or go-karts. It is widely regarded as the first step in a racing driver's career, and many Formula One drivers began in karting.

History

The first kart was built in Los Angeles in 1956 by Art Ingels. The sport spread rapidly in the United States and Europe, with formal championships organised from the 1960s onward.

Karts and classes

Karts range from rental machines with small four-stroke engines to racing karts with 125 cc two-stroke engines capable of speeds over 130 km/h. Competition classes are divided by age and engine type, including Cadet, Junior and Senior categories, and gearbox classes such as KZ.

As a stepping stone

Drivers such as Ayrton Senna, Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton began their careers in karts. The sport teaches racecraft, car control and mechanical sympathy at relatively low cost.
