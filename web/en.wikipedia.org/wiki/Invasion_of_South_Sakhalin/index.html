Invasion of South Sakhalin

Date: 11–25 August 1945
Location: Karafuto (southern Sakhalin Island)
Result: Soviet victory
Belligerents: Soviet Union vs Empire of Japan

The Invasion of South Sakhalin, also known in Japanese as the Battle of Karafuto, was the Soviet invasion of the Japanese-controlled southern part of Sakhalin Island (Karafuto Prefecture) in August 1945, during the final phase of the Second World War. It formed part of the wider Soviet–Japanese War launched on 9 August 1945.

Background

Under the Treaty of Portsmouth (1905), Sakhalin had been divided at the 50th parallel: the south went to Japan, the north to Russia (later the USSR). At the Yalta Conference in February 1945 the Allies agreed that the Soviet Union would regain southern Sakhalin in return for entering the war against Japan.

The operation

Soviet forces of the 16th Army, supported by the Pacific Fleet's Northern Pacific Flotilla, began operations on 11 August 1945, attacking across the land border and making amphibious landings at ports including Esutoru (Uglegorsk), Toro (Shakhtyorsk) and Maoka (Kholmsk). Japanese forces — chiefly the 88th Division — resisted but were undermined by Japan's general surrender decision following the imperial rescript of 15 August; fighting nevertheless continued until about 25 August as Soviet units secured the island.

Aftermath

The entire island came under Soviet control. The Japanese civilian population, some 400,000 people, was largely repatriated to Japan in the following years, while Koreans brought to Karafuto as labourers were in many cases left behind. Southern Sakhalin was incorporated into the Soviet Union (now Russia) as part of Sakhalin Oblast.
