Nishat Bagh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nishat Bagh terraces overlooking Dal Lake

Nishat Bagh (Urdu: نشاط باغ, "Garden of Joy" or "Garden of Delight") is a terraced Mughal garden on the eastern shore of Dal Lake in Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir, India, set against the Zabarwan mountain range. It is the largest of the famous Mughal gardens of Kashmir and one of the most popular tourist destinations in Srinagar.

History

The garden was laid out in 1632–33 by Mirza Abul Hasan, known as Mirza Asaf Khan, an elder brother of the empress Nur Jahan and governor of Kashmir. According to tradition, the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan admired the garden so greatly on a visit in 1633 that he repeatedly expressed his desire for it, hoping his father-in-law would gift it to him; when no gift followed, the emperor is said to have ordered the water supply — shared with his own Shalimar Bagh upstream — to be cut off, leaving Nishat dry until the intercession of a faithful servant restored the flow. The garden later passed through periods of neglect under Afghan and Sikh rule and was restored under the Dogra maharajas.

Layout

Nishat Bagh is built on twelve terraces representing the twelve signs of the zodiac, rising from the lake shore up the hillside. A central water channel fed by the Gopi Tirith spring descends through the terraces in cascades and pools lined with fountains, flanked by lawns, flowerbeds, and chinar and cypress trees. The uppermost terrace was reserved for the ladies of the court, reflecting the garden's original role as a private pleasure retreat. From every level there are celebrated views across Dal Lake to the Pir Panjal mountains.

Today

Together with Shalimar Bagh and Chashme Shahi, Nishat Bagh forms the trio of classic Mughal gardens of Srinagar. It is open to the public and is especially visited in spring, when its flowerbeds are in bloom, and in autumn, when the chinar leaves turn red.
