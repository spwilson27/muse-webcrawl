Oliver Eaton Williamson (September 27, 1932 – May 21, 2020) was an American economist and professor at the University of California, Berkeley. He was a leading figure in transaction cost economics and the new institutional economics, and shared the 2009 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Elinor Ostrom "for his analysis of economic governance, especially the boundaries of the firm".

Life and career

Williamson studied at MIT's Sloan School of Management and received his PhD in economics from Carnegie Mellon in 1963, where Herbert A. Simon influenced his thinking on bounded rationality. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, the University of Pennsylvania and Yale, before returning to Berkeley in 1988 as professor at the Haas School of Business and the law school.

Work

Building on Ronald Coase's insight that firms exist to economise on the costs of using the market, Williamson developed a theory predicting which transactions will be organised within firms (hierarchies), which through markets, and which through hybrid forms. Key variables are asset specificity, uncertainty and frequency, combined with the behavioural assumptions of bounded rationality and opportunism. His framework has been widely applied in organisation theory, antitrust, corporate governance and public policy.
