Perfect 10 (gymnastics)

A perfect 10 is the maximum possible score in artistic gymnastics under the scoring system used for most of the twentieth century, in which a routine began from a start value of 10.0 points and judges deducted for errors in execution, form, and composition. The score became one of the most famous numbers in sport after Romanian gymnast Nadia Comăneci received the first perfect 10 in Olympic history at the 1976 Montreal Olympics.

History

Under the traditional Code of Points, gymnasts were judged out of 10.0, combining difficulty, execution, and artistry into a single mark. A flawless routine could in principle earn a 10.0, but for decades judges were reluctant to award it. At Montreal on 18 July 1976, the 14-year-old Comăneci performed a compulsory routine on the uneven bars that the judges scored 10.00. The scoreboard, not programmed to display a four-digit score, showed "1.00" instead. Comăneci received six more perfect 10s during those Games.

Other gymnasts subsequently earned perfect 10s, including Nellie Kim, Mary Lou Retton, Lavinia Miloșovici, and Daniela Silivaș. The system produced celebrated moments but was criticised for compressing scores at the top and failing to distinguish difficulty adequately.

End of the perfect 10

After the 2004 Athens Olympics, the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) adopted an open-ended Code of Points, effective 2006, in which the final score is the sum of a difficulty (D) score and an execution (E) score beginning at 10.0. Because the difficulty component is uncapped, there is no longer a single maximum score, and the "perfect 10" disappeared from elite gymnastics. The perfect 10 survives in NCAA collegiate gymnastics in the United States, which continues to use a 10.0-based system.
