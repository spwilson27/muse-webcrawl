Eorcenberht (died 14 July 664) was King of Kent from 640 until his death, succeeding his father Eadbald. He was the first king in English history who, according to the Venerable Bede, ordered the destruction of pagan idols throughout his kingdom and commanded the observance of Lent by royal authority.

Reign

Unlike his father, who had relapsed into paganism before returning to Christianity, Eorcenberht was a staunch Christian king. Bede reports that he was also the first king to order that idols be abandoned and destroyed. His reign saw Kent firmly within the Christian orbit of the Augustinian mission based at Canterbury.

Family

Eorcenberht married Seaxburh, daughter of King Anna of East Anglia; she later became abbess of Ely. Their sons Ecgberht and Hlothhere succeeded to the Kentish throne in turn, and their daughters included Eormenhild, mother of the Mercian royal saint Werburgh.

Death

He died on 14 July 664, the same year as the great plague and the Synod of Whitby, and was succeeded by his son Ecgberht.
