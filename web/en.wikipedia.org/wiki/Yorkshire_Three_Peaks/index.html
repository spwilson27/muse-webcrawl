Yorkshire Three Peaks

PeaksPen-y-ghent (694 m), Whernside (736 m), Ingleborough (723 m)
Challenge routeAbout 39 km (24 miles), ~1,585 m ascent
Traditional targetUnder 12 hours
LocationYorkshire Dales, England

The Yorkshire Three Peaks are the three highest distinct mountains of the Yorkshire Dales in northern England: Pen-y-ghent, Whernside, and Ingleborough. They form the basis of the Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge, a circular walk of about 39 kilometres (24 miles) linking the three summits, traditionally completed within 12 hours.

The peaks

Whernside (736 m) is the highest point in modern North Yorkshire, a long whale-backed ridge above Ribblesdale. Ingleborough (723 m) is a flat-topped massif crowned by the remains of an Iron Age hillfort and ringed by limestone pavement and potholes. Pen-y-ghent (694 m) — its name probably meaning "hill of the winds" in Cumbric — rises steeply above Horton-in-Ribblesdale and is distinguished by its millstone grit cap over limestone terraces.

The challenge

The route usually starts and ends at Horton-in-Ribblesdale, climbing Pen-y-ghent first, crossing via the Ribblehead Viaduct to Whernside, then over Ingleborough and back to Horton. The challenge was popularized in the mid-20th century and the first recorded completion is usually credited to members of the Clayton-le-Moors Harriers or teachers from Giggleswick School in 1954. Today thousands attempt it annually, many raising money for charity; the Three Peaks Race, a fell race over the route held since 1954, is usually won in under three hours. Erosion from the walk's popularity has required extensive path restoration by the national park authority, using stone pitching and flagstones.

Distinction

The Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge is frequently confused with the National Three Peaks Challenge (Ben Nevis, Scafell Pike, and Snowdon). The Yorkshire version is a single continuous walk requiring no driving between mountains.
