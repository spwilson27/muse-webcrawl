Jongmyo

Location: Jongno District, Seoul, South Korea
Built: 1394 (periodically enlarged)
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1995)
Function: Royal Confucian ancestral shrine of the Joseon dynasty

Jongmyo (Korean: 종묘) is a Confucian royal ancestral shrine in Seoul, South Korea, dedicated to the memorial services for the deceased kings and queens of the Korean Joseon dynasty (1392–1897). It is the oldest royal Confucian shrine preserved and the ritual ceremonies held there link the present to a tradition of the 14th century. Jongmyo was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1995.

Architecture

The shrine's main hall, Jeongjeon, is one of the longest wooden buildings in the world, its severe, unadorned façade extending over 100 metres. Spirit tablets of 19 Joseon kings and their queens are enshrined there; those of 30 further kings are kept in the second hall, Yeongnyeongjeon. Unusually for East Asia, Jongmyo stands as a separate structure opposite the Sajik altars, flanking the palace of Gyeongbokgung according to Confucian protocol, and it survived substantially intact while other royal shrines were destroyed in invasions.

Jongmyo Jerye

The annual rite known as Jongmyo Jerye, held on the first Sunday of May, re-enacts the Confucian memorial ceremony for Joseon royalty, accompanied by the court music and dance known as Jongmyo Jeryeak—performed by descendants of the Yi royal family and musicians in traditional dress. The ceremony and its music were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008 (originally proclaimed a Masterpiece in 2001).
