General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper

[Image placeholder: MQ-9 Reaper]

The General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper (sometimes called the Predator B) is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) capable of remotely controlled or autonomous flight operations, developed by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems primarily for the United States Air Force. It is a larger, more powerful successor to the MQ-1 Predator, designed for long-endurance intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR) and precision strike.

Design and capabilities

The Reaper is powered by a roughly 950-horsepower turboprop engine, cruises far faster than the Predator, and can operate at altitudes up to about 50,000 feet with an endurance exceeding 27 hours. It carries a multispectral targeting system combining electro-optical/infrared sensors, laser designator, and synthetic-aperture radar, and can be armed with up to about 3,800 lb (1,700 kg) of ordnance, including AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bombs, and GBU-38 JDAMs. A crew of two — a pilot and a sensor operator — controls the aircraft from a ground control station, often on another continent, via satellite link.

Operational use

Introduced in 2007, the MQ-9 has been flown extensively in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Libya, and elsewhere for surveillance and airstrikes. Operators include the U.S. Air Force, U.S. Marine Corps, NASA (as the Ikhana research aircraft), the UK Royal Air Force (as the Protector/Reaper), France, Italy, Spain, India, and others. Its use in targeted strikes — including the 2020 strike that killed Iranian general Qasem Soleimani — has made it a focal point in debates over drone warfare.
