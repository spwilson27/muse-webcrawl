Exophora

Image placeholder: Exophoric versus endophoric reference

In linguistics, exophora is the reference of an expression to something outside the text, in the surrounding situation. Exophoric reference relies on the shared context of the speaker and listener — the physical environment, gestures, or common knowledge — rather than on other parts of the discourse. It is the opposite of endophora, which refers to items within the text itself.

Explanation

Expressions such as "this one", "that man over there", "you", and "here" are typically exophoric: their interpretation depends on the situation in which they are uttered. For example, in the sentence "He's the one I told you about", spoken while pointing at a man across the room, the pronoun "he" refers exophorically. First- and second-person pronouns are inherently exophoric, since they identify participants in the speech event rather than entities introduced by the text.

The term derives from Greek exo ("outside") and phora ("carrying"). The related phenomenon of deixis covers context-dependent expressions generally, while exophora specifically concerns reference to extralinguistic context. Homophora is a subtype in which the referent is identified through shared cultural knowledge, as in "the sun" or "the Queen".
