Lewis Allan Reed (2 March 1942 – 27 October 2013) was an American musician, singer, and songwriter. He was the guitarist, vocalist, and principal songwriter of the rock band the Velvet Underground and had a solo career that spanned five decades. Though the Velvet Underground sold poorly in their lifetime, they became one of the most influential bands in rock and alternative music; Reed's deadpan delivery and literary, streetwise lyrics — about drugs, sex, and New York City life — rewrote what rock songs could address.

The Velvet Underground

Reed formed the Velvet Underground in New York in 1964 with John Cale, Sterling Morrison, and Maureen Tucker. Championed by Andy Warhol, who managed them and designed the banana cover of their 1967 debut The Velvet Underground & Nico, the band fused rock with avant-garde drone and noise. Reed left in 1970 after Loaded, which contained "Sweet Jane" and "Rock & Roll".

Solo career

Produced by David Bowie and Mick Ronson, Reed's second solo album Transformer (1972) produced his only top-20 single, "Walk on the Wild Side", and the ballad "Perfect Day". His subsequent career was famously uncompromising: the bleak concept album Berlin (1973), the double album of feedback Metal Machine Music (1975), the acclaimed New York (1989), and the song cycle Magic and Loss (1992). In 2011 he collaborated with Metallica on Lulu.

Death and honours

Reed, who had long-standing liver disease (he received a liver transplant in 2013), died of liver disease on 27 October 2013 at his home in Southampton, New York, aged 71. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice: with the Velvet Underground (1996) and as a solo artist (2015).
