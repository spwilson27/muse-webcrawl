Checkpoint Charlie (officially "Checkpoint C") was the best-known Berlin Wall crossing point between East Berlin and West Berlin during the Cold War (1947–1991), as well as the only crossing point for foreigners and members of the Allied forces. It is located at the junction of Friedrichstraße with Zimmerstraße and Mauerstraße in the Friedrichstadt neighborhood.

History

After the construction of the Berlin Wall in August 1961, the Western Allies designated three crossing points for their personnel, named after the NATO phonetic alphabet: Checkpoint Alpha at Helmstedt, Checkpoint Bravo at Dreilinden, and Checkpoint Charlie in central Berlin.

In October 1961, Checkpoint Charlie was the scene of a famous confrontation ("stand-off") between American and Soviet tanks, triggered by a dispute over the right of an American diplomat to enter East Berlin. For sixteen hours the tanks faced each other before withdrawing in a choreographed de-escalation.

After reunification

The checkpoint was dismantled on 22 June 1990. A replica of the guardhouse and the famous sign ("You are leaving the American sector") now mark the spot, a major tourist attraction. The site and its history are documented in the nearby Mauermuseum – Museum Haus am Checkpoint Charlie, founded by Rainer Hildebrandt.
