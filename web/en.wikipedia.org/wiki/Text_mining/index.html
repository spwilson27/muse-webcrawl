Text mining

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Text mining, text data mining (TDM), or text analytics is the process of deriving high-quality information from text. It involves the discovery by computer of new, previously unknown information by automatically extracting information from different written resources. Text mining is an interdisciplinary field that draws on information retrieval, data mining, machine learning, statistics, and computational linguistics.

Tasks

Typical text mining tasks include text categorization, text clustering, concept/entity extraction, document summarization, sentiment analysis, and entity relation modeling. Preprocessing commonly involves tokenization, stemming or lemmatization, part-of-speech tagging, and parsing; documents are frequently represented numerically using the bag-of-words model, TF–IDF weighting, or learned embeddings such as word2vec and transformer-based language models.

Applications

Text mining is used in business intelligence and customer feedback analysis, biomedical literature mining, fraud detection, spam filtering, legal e-discovery, social media monitoring, and research assessment. Its growth parallels the increasing availability of unstructured text data on the Web and within organizations.

History

The field has roots in 1980s work on automatic content analysis and in the Message Understanding Conferences (MUC) that drove information extraction research in the 1990s. Modern neural approaches, beginning with recurrent networks and culminating in transformer architectures, have substantially improved accuracy on most text mining tasks.
