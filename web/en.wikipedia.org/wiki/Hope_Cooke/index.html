Hope Cooke (born 24 April 1940) is an American writer and lecturer who became the queen consort of the Himalayan kingdom of Sikkim through her marriage to its last king, Palden Thondup Namgyal. Her marriage and life at court in Gangtok made her internationally known in the 1960s, and she later chronicled her experiences in the memoir Time Change.

Early life

Cooke was born in San Francisco and raised in New York City. She attended Sarah Lawrence College, where she studied Asian history and comparative religion. While travelling in India in 1959 she met Palden Thondup Namgyal, then the crown prince of Sikkim, in Darjeeling. After a courtship conducted largely by correspondence, they were engaged and married in March 1963 in a Buddhist ceremony in Gangtok.

Queen of Sikkim

Cooke became queen (gyalmo) when her husband ascended the throne in 1965. She worked on development and cultural projects and helped publicise Sikkim abroad, but the couple's position grew precarious as Sikkim's status as an Indian protectorate came under strain. In 1973, amid political unrest, she left Sikkim with her children for New York. A 1975 referendum abolished the monarchy and Sikkim became a state of India. The marriage ended in divorce in 1980.

Later life

Living in New York, Cooke wrote Time Change: An Alternative View of History (1980) and Seeing New York: History Walks for Armchair and Footloose Travelers, and became known for leading historical walking tours of the city.
