The Zemsky Sobor (Russian: зе́мский собо́р, "Assembly of the Land") was a parliament of the Tsardom of Russia's estates — clergy, boyars, gentry, merchants, and sometimes townsfolk and state peasants — convened by the tsar between the mid-16th and late 17th centuries. More than fifty sobors are recorded.

History

Ivan the Terrible convened the first Zemsky Sobor in 1549 as part of his reforms. The institution reached its height during the Time of Troubles: the sobor of 1598 elected Boris Godunov as tsar, and the great sobor of 1613 elected Michael Romanov, founding the Romanov dynasty. In 1649 a sobor approved the Sobornoye Ulozheniye, the law code that formalised serfdom.

Decline

As the tsars consolidated absolute power, full assemblies fell out of use after the 1650s; the last sobors of any kind met in the 1680s. Historians debate whether the Zemsky Sobor was a genuinely representative institution comparable to Western estates-general or merely a consultative body summoned to ratify royal decisions.
