Transpose

In linear algebra, the transpose of a matrix is an operator which flips a matrix over its diagonal; that is, it switches the row and column indices of the matrix A by producing another matrix, often denoted by AT.

Definition

The transpose of a matrix A is defined as the matrix AT whose entries satisfy (AT)ij = Aji. If A is an m × n matrix, then AT is an n × m matrix. The transpose was introduced in 1858 by the British mathematician Arthur Cayley.

Properties

For matrices A and B and scalar c, transposition satisfies: (AT)T = A; (A + B)T = AT + BT; (AB)T = BTAT; and (cA)T = cAT. A square matrix is symmetric if it equals its transpose, and orthogonal if its transpose equals its inverse. The determinant of a matrix equals the determinant of its transpose.

Transpose of a linear map

Corresponding to the matrix operation, the transpose (or dual) of a linear map between vector spaces is the map between their dual spaces induced by composition. In inner product spaces, the related concept of the adjoint operator generalises the conjugate transpose.
