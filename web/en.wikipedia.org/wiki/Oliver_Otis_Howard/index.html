Oliver Otis Howard

Oliver Otis Howard (November 8, 1830 – October 26, 1909) was a United States Army career officer and Union general in the American Civil War. Known as the "Christian General" for his deep religious convictions, he lost his right arm at the Battle of Fair Oaks in 1862, an action for which he later received the Medal of Honor.

Civil War

Howard commanded a brigade at First Bull Run and rose to command the XI Corps of the Army of the Potomac, which was routed at Chancellorsville and defeated again on the first day at Gettysburg. Transferred west, his corps fought at Chattanooga, and he later commanded the Army of the Tennessee on General Sherman's March to the Sea and through the Carolinas.

Reconstruction and later career

After the war Howard headed the Freedmen's Bureau (1865–1872), the federal agency charged with assisting formerly enslaved people in the South. He was a founder of Howard University in Washington, D.C., chartered in 1867, and served as its president from 1869 to 1874. In the west he commanded troops in campaigns against the Nez Perce in 1877 and the Apache. He also helped found Lincoln Memorial University in Tennessee. Howard died in Burlington, Vermont, in 1909.
