The Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta, or California Delta, is a large inland river delta and estuary in Northern California. The Delta is formed at the western edge of the Central Valley by the confluence of the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers and lies just east of where the rivers enter Suisun Bay.

The Delta is an inverted delta: unlike most deltas, which fan out toward the sea, its channels converge westward through the Carquinez Strait toward San Francisco Bay. It covers roughly 1,153 square miles and contains hundreds of miles of waterways, dozens of islands protected by levees, and some of California's most productive farmland.

Geology and ecology

The Delta formed as meltwater from Sierra Nevada glaciers met the rising post-glacial sea, depositing sediment behind the constriction at the Carquinez Strait. Before reclamation, it was a vast tidal marsh of tule reeds, home to waterfowl, tule elk, and grizzly bears, and to Native American peoples including the Miwok and Yokuts.

Today the estuary is a critical habitat for fish such as Chinook salmon and the endangered Delta smelt, and a major stop on the Pacific Flyway. Much of the land on Delta islands has subsided well below sea level through peat oxidation, making the islands dependent on aging levees.

Water supply

The Delta is the hub of California's water delivery system. The Central Valley Project and State Water Project pump water from the southern Delta into aqueducts serving farms in the San Joaquin Valley and cities in Southern California. About two-thirds of Californians receive at least some of their drinking water from the Delta.

Exports are controversial: environmentalists argue pumping harms the estuary, farmers argue for greater reliability, and proposed conveyance projects such as a peripheral canal or tunnel have been debated for decades.

Recreation and communities

The Delta hosts houseboating, fishing, and waterskiing, and historic river towns such as Locke, Walnut Grove, and Rio Vista. Agriculture—pears, tomatoes, corn, wine grapes, and asparagus—employs thousands.
