Brèche de Roland

The Brèche de Roland (Spanish: Brecha de Rolando) is a natural gap, some 40 metres across and 100 metres deep, at an elevation of about 2,807 metres in the Pyrenees, on the border between France and Spain. It lies in the Monte Perdido massif above the Cirque de Gavarnie in the French Pyrénées National Park, and opposite the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park in Spain.

Legend

According to legend, the breach was cut by Roland, the heroic nephew of Charlemagne and hero of the Chanson de Roland. After the Battle of Roncevaux Pass in 778, Roland is said to have tried to destroy his indestructible sword Durendal by striking it against the rock, so that it would not fall into enemy hands; instead of breaking, the sword cleaved the gap in the mountain ridge. Versions of the legend place Durendal lodged in a cliff face at Rocamadour, where Roland threw it.

Geology and access

The gap cuts through the limestone cliffs of the cirque walls and forms a striking landmark visible from Gavarnie. It is a popular objective for hikers and climbers, approached from the French side via the Sarradets refuge (Refuge des Sarradets, also called Refuge de la Brèche de Roland) and from the Spanish side from Ordesa via the Góriz refuge and the icy slopes below the breach. The crossing can be hazardous in poor weather and when snow-covered.
