Prague Linguistic Circle

The Prague Linguistic Circle (Czech: Pražský lingvistický kroužek), founded in 1926, was a group of linguists and literary scholars — largely Czech and Russian émigré — whose work founded structural linguistics and made major contributions to phonology, poetics and semiotics. It is also known as the Prague School.

Members and programme

Leading figures included Vilém Mathesius (the Circle's president), Roman Jakobson, Nikolai Trubetzkoy, Jan Mukařovský, Bohuslav Havránek and René Wellek. The Circle's collective Theses, presented at the First Congress of Slavists in Prague in 1929, set out a functional-structural programme: language is a system of means adapted to purposes, and linguistic phenomena should be studied in terms of the functions they serve.

Contributions

Trubetzkoy's posthumous Principles of Phonology (1939) systematised phonemic analysis, distinguishing phonetics from phonology and classifying phonological oppositions. Jakobson developed distinctive-feature theory and his model of the functions of language. Mukařovský extended structural methods to literature and aesthetics, influencing narratology and the theory of the standard language.

Legacy

The German occupation of Czechoslovakia and the deaths of Mathesius and Trubetzkoy broke up the Circle by 1939, though its journal Slovo a slovesnost continued and a successor circle was refounded in 1990. Prague School ideas profoundly influenced structuralism worldwide, including the work of Claude Lévi-Strauss.
