Drain field


Schematic of a septic tank and drain field system.

A drain field, also known as a leach field, leach drain or septic drain field, is an underground network of perforated pipes that forms the final stage of a septic system. It receives partially treated wastewater (effluent) from a septic tank and distributes it over a large area of soil, where further treatment occurs as the liquid percolates downward.

Design

A typical drain field consists of a series of trenches, usually 30 to 90 centimetres (1 to 3 feet) deep, filled with gravel or coarse sand and containing perforated distribution pipes. Effluent flows by gravity or by pump from the septic tank into the pipes and seeps out through the perforations into the surrounding soil. A layer of geotextile fabric or untreated building paper is often laid over the gravel to prevent soil from migrating into the trench while still allowing water and air to pass.

Function

The soil beneath the drain field acts as a living filter. As effluent trickles through the unsaturated zone, bacteria and other microorganisms colonise the surfaces of soil particles and pipe gravel, forming a biological mat (biomat) that digests pathogens, phosphorus and nitrogen compounds. By the time the water reaches the water table it has usually been substantially purified. The size of the field required depends on the volume of wastewater, typically estimated from the number of bedrooms in a dwelling, and on the percolation rate of the soil, measured by a percolation test ("perc test").

Failure

Drain fields fail when the soil becomes clogged with an excessively thick biomat, when solids escape an unmaintained septic tank, or when the field is hydraulically overloaded. Symptoms include slow drains, sewage odours, lush green patches of grass over the field, and effluent surfacing at ground level. Heavy vehicle traffic and tree roots can also damage distribution pipes. Modern alternatives include chamber systems, mound systems, sand filters and aerobic treatment units for sites with poor soil or high water tables.
