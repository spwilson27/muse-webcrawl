Sarsaparilla

Sarsaparilla is a preparation made from the roots of tropical American vines of the genus Smilax, particularly Smilax aristolochiifolia and related species, native to Mexico and Central America. It was introduced to Europe in the 16th century and became a popular medicinal drink.

History and uses

Spanish colonists learned of sarsaparilla from Indigenous peoples, who used it for various ailments. Europeans adopted it as a supposed cure for syphilis and as a "blood purifier," and it was an official drug in pharmacopoeias for centuries, though its efficacy against syphilis was never established. Sarsaparilla extract — often combined with sassafras, licorice, and wintergreen — became a flavouring for soft drinks, and a drink called sarsaparilla remains popular in parts of Asia, Australia, and Latin America. Similar flavoured sodas survive in Taiwan, Malaysia, and the Philippines, where it is known as sarsi.

Botanical notes

The name is also loosely applied to unrelated plants, including Indian sarsaparilla (Hemidesmus indicus) and wild sarsaparilla (Aralia nudicaulis) of North America.
