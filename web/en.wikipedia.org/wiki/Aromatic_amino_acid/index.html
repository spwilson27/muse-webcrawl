Aromatic amino acid

An aromatic amino acid is an amino acid that includes an aromatic ring in its side chain. The standard aromatic amino acids are phenylalanine, tryptophan and tyrosine; histidine is sometimes included in the group.

Properties

The aromatic side chains absorb ultraviolet light (strongly at around 280 nm for tryptophan and tyrosine), a property used to estimate protein concentration spectrophotometrically. Phenylalanine is hydrophobic, tyrosine is polar owing to its hydroxyl group, and tryptophan's indole ring is the largest amino-acid side chain.

Biosynthesis and roles

Plants and microorganisms synthesise aromatic amino acids via the shikimate pathway, which animals lack — hence phenylalanine and tryptophan are essential nutrients for humans. They are precursors of neurotransmitters and hormones: tyrosine gives rise to dopamine and adrenaline, and tryptophan to serotonin and melatonin.
