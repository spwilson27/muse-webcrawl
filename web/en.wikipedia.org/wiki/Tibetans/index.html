Tibetans

Tibetans are an East Asian ethnic group native to Tibet. Their total population is about 7.5 million, with roughly 7 million living within China — in the Tibet Autonomous Region and in Tibetan autonomous prefectures of Qinghai, Sichuan, Gansu and Yunnan — and a diaspora of more than one hundred thousand, principally in India, Nepal and Bhutan, with smaller communities worldwide.

Language and culture

Tibetans speak Tibetic languages, a group of related languages and dialects including Central Tibetan (Ü-Tsang), Khams and Amdo varieties, written in the Tibetan script, traditionally said to have been created by Thonmi Sambhota in the seventh century. Traditional livelihoods divide between farming in the river valleys — growing highland barley, wheat, peas and rapeseed — and nomadic or semi-nomadic pastoralism on the plateau, herding yaks, sheep and goats. Barley is the staple grain, ground into tsampa and used to make butter tea, the characteristic drink of the region.

Religion

Most Tibetans practise Tibetan Buddhism, which combines Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions, while a minority follow the indigenous Bön religion and small numbers are Muslim or Catholic. Monasteries of the Gelug, Nyingma, Kagyu and Sakya schools historically served as centres of learning, art and governance; the Dalai Lama and Panchen Lama are the best-known religious figures. Pilgrimage, prayer flags, prayer wheels, mani stones and circumambulation of sacred sites such as Mount Kailash and the Jokhang Temple in Lhasa are central features of religious life.

History

The Tibetan Empire of the seventh to ninth centuries, founded by Songtsen Gampo, was a major Central Asian power. After its fragmentation, Tibet was successively influenced or administered by the Mongol Yuan dynasty, the Phagmodrupa, Rinpungpa and Tsangpa rulers, the Khoshut Khanate, and from the eighteenth century the Qing dynasty, before the Ganden Phodrang government of the Dalai Lamas governed until 1959, when the 14th Dalai Lama fled to India and a government-in-exile was established at Dharamshala.
