B-roll

B-roll footage being cut into a news package in a video editing suite.

In video production, B-roll (also written B roll or b-roll) is supplemental or alternative footage intercut with the main shot. The term contrasts with A-roll, the main footage that carries the narrative — typically an interview subject speaking to camera or a principal scene. News packages, documentaries, corporate videos and online video creators all make heavy use of B-roll.

Uses

B-roll serves several purposes. It illustrates what a speaker is talking about — showing the factory, street or product being discussed while the voice continues in voice-over. It covers edits: when two segments of an interview are joined, cutting away to B-roll hides the jump cut. It sets a scene with establishing shots of locations, adds visual variety to hold attention, and can show cause and effect or reactions (the "noddy" shot in television interviews). Stock footage libraries license pre-shot B-roll of common subjects for productions that cannot film everything themselves.

Origin of the term

The term derives from the era of physical film and early videotape editing. In traditional two-machine and film-splicing workflows, editors prepared an "A roll" containing the primary shots and a separate "B roll" containing the alternate shots that would be dissolved to or cut in; checkerboard printing in film laboratories likewise used A and B rolls to make transitions invisible. The terminology survived the transition to non-linear digital editing, where B-roll now simply means secondary footage placed on an upper track of the timeline.
