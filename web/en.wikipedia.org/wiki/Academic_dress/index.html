Academic dress

Academic dress is a traditional form of clothing worn by students, graduates and academics at universities and other educational institutions. It typically consists of a long gown or robe, a hood indicating the degree and institution, and a cap such as a mortarboard or Tudor bonnet. Its origins lie in the everyday dress of medieval scholars, who were usually clerics.

History

The universities of Oxford and Cambridge developed distinctive systems of gowns and hoods in the Middle Ages, codified over the centuries. The hood's colours and lining indicate the wearer's degree and university. In the United States, the Intercollegiate Code of 1895 standardised the shapes of gowns and the colours of hood linings and velvet by field of study — for example, white for arts and letters, golden yellow for science and purple for law.

Components

The gown differs by degree: bachelor's gowns have pointed sleeves, master's gowns have oblong sleeves closed at the end, and doctors' gowns have bell-shaped sleeves often faced with coloured velvet and chevrons. The hood is worn over the gown, and the cap — mortarboard, tam or bonnet — completes the dress for most occasions.

Modern use

Academic dress is now worn chiefly at graduation ceremonies, matriculation and formal university occasions, though at a few institutions, notably Oxford and Cambridge, it retains a role in examinations and formal dinners.
