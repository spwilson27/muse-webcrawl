DVD-RW is a rewritable optical disc format invented by Pioneer in November 1999 and approved by the DVD Forum. It has a storage capacity similar to a DVD-R, approximately 4.7 GB, but unlike DVD-R, a DVD-RW disc can be rewritten over 1,000 times.

History

Pioneer developed the format as a recordable companion to its DVD-R standard. Early DVD-RW drives appeared in Japanese PC kits in 1999 and in consumer players soon afterwards. The format competed with DVD+RW, developed by the DVD+RW Alliance, and with Panasonic's DVD-RAM.

Technology

DVD-RW uses a phase-change alloy recording layer similar to CD-RW. A laser heats spots on the disc to change them between crystalline and amorphous states, altering reflectivity. Sequential video recording is supported, and the VR mode allows in-place editing on compatible recorders.

Compatibility

Older DVD recorders were unable to read DVD-RW discs recorded in some modes, but modern drives handle the format widely. DVD-RW discs must be finalized for playback on non-compliant players.
