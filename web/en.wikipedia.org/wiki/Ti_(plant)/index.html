Ti (plant)

Cordyline frutescens, commonly known as ti (also written tī), is an evergreen flowering plant in the asparagus family, Asparagaceae. Native to tropical Southeast Asia and Papuasia, it was carried throughout the Pacific by Austronesian voyagers as a canoe plant and is now widely grown as an ornamental.

Description

It is a palm-like shrub growing to about 3–4 metres, with spirally arranged lance-shaped leaves that in cultivated varieties range from green through red and purple to variegated. It is not closely related to the true lilies despite its older name Cordyline terminalis.

Uses

In Hawaiʻi, tī (kī) leaves are used for hula skirts, food wrapping (as in lau lau), thatching, and offerings, and the plant is regarded as sacred to the goddess Laka and the god Lono. Elsewhere in the Pacific the starchy rhizomes were baked as food, and in Hawaiʻi fermented and distilled into the liquor ʻōkolehao.
