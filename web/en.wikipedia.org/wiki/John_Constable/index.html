John Constable RA (11 June 1776 – 31 March 1837) was an English landscape painter in the Romantic tradition. Born in Suffolk, he is known principally for revolutionising the genre of landscape painting with his pictures of Dedham Vale, the area surrounding his home — now known as "Constable Country" — which he invested with an intensity of affection. "I should paint my own places best," he wrote, "painting is but another word for feeling."

Early life

Constable was born in East Bergholt, a village on the River Stour in Suffolk, the second son of Golding Constable, a wealthy corn merchant who owned Flatford Mill and a mill in Dedham. Though expected to enter the family business, he was permitted to pursue art and in 1799 entered the Royal Academy Schools in London, where he studied old masters including Claude Lorrain and Jacob van Ruisdael.

Career and the "six-footers"

Constable developed a distinctive naturalistic style based on open-air oil sketches, meticulous observation of clouds and weather, and a rejection of the artificial conventions of academic landscape painting. Between 1819 and 1825 he painted a series of large canvases known as the "six-footers," including The White Horse, Stratford Mill, and The Hay Wain (1821). When The Hay Wain was exhibited at the Paris Salon of 1824, it won a gold medal and was widely admired by French artists, influencing the Barbizon School and later the Impressionists. In his own country, recognition came slowly: he sold only twenty paintings in England during his lifetime and was not elected a full member of the Royal Academy until 1829, at the age of 52.

Personal life

After a long courtship opposed by her family, Constable married Maria Bicknell in 1816; they had seven children. Maria's death from tuberculosis in 1828 devastated him, and he wore mourning black for the rest of his life. His lectures on the history of landscape painting, delivered in the 1830s, remain valuable records of his artistic philosophy.

Death and legacy

Constable died in London on the night of 31 March 1837, apparently of heart failure, and was buried beside Maria in Hampstead. Today he is regarded, with J. M. W. Turner, as one of the greatest British landscape painters. His oil sketches, once treated as preparatory works, are now prized for their freshness and spontaneity, and his full-scale sketch for The Hay Wain hangs in the Victoria and Albert Museum.
