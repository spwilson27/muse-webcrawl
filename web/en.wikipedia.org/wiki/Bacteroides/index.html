Bacteroides

Bacteroides, anaerobic Gram-negative rods
DomainBacteria
PhylumBacteroidota
OrderBacteroidales
FamilyBacteroidaceae
GenusBacteroides
Type speciesBacteroides fragilis

Bacteroides is a genus of Gram-negative, obligate anaerobic bacteria. Bacteroides species are non-endospore-forming bacilli and may be motile or nonmotile, depending on the species. They are among the most abundant organisms in the human gastrointestinal tract, where the genus may constitute a substantial share of the gut microbiota, and they are also found in the oral cavity and female genital tract.

Role in the gut

In a healthy gut, Bacteroides species such as B. thetaiotaomicron and B. uniformis help digest complex polysaccharides that human enzymes cannot break down, producing short-chain fatty acids and other metabolites used by the host. Their extensive repertoires of carbohydrate-active enzymes and polysaccharide utilization loci allow them to harvest energy from dietary fibre and host mucins. They also contribute to immune system development and to colonization resistance against pathogens.

Clinical significance

Outside the gut, Bacteroides can act as opportunistic pathogens. Bacteroides fragilis is the species most frequently isolated from anaerobic infections, including intra-abdominal abscesses, pelvic infections, and bacteraemia, typically following perforation or surgery. Its polysaccharide capsule promotes abscess formation. Bacteroides infections are treated with agents such as metronidazole, carbapenems, or beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations, although resistance is increasing. Because Bacteroides are so characteristic of faecal contamination, their molecular markers are used to track sewage pollution in water.

Taxonomy

The genus was long a repository for many anaerobic Gram-negative rods; numerous former members have been moved to genera such as Prevotella, Porphyromonas, and Parabacteroides. In 2022 several well-known species, including B. vulgatus and B. dorei, were proposed for transfer to the genus Phocaeicola, a change that remains in flux across databases.
