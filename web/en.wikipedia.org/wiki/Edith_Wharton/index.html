Edith Wharton (born Edith Newbold Jones; January 24, 1862 – August 11, 1937) was an American writer and designer. She was a Pulitzer Prize winner and was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature. Wharton drew upon her insider's knowledge of the upper-class New York "aristocracy" to realistically portray the lives and morals of the Gilded Age.

Works

Her major works include the novels The House of Mirth (1905), Ethan Frome (1911) and The Age of Innocence (1920), which won the 1921 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction, making her the first woman to win the prize. She also wrote on architecture and design, including The Decoration of Houses (1897) with Ogden Codman Jr.

Life

Wharton designed and built The Mount, her estate in Lenox, Massachusetts. During the First World War she carried out extensive relief work in France, where she lived for the rest of her life.
