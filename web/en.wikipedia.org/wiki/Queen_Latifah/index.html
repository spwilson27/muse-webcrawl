Queen Latifah (born Dana Elaine Owens, March 18, 1970) is an American rapper, singer, and actress. She has received various accolades, including a Grammy Award, a Primetime Emmy Award, a Golden Globe Award, three Screen Actors Guild Awards, and two NAACP Image Awards, in addition to a nomination for an Academy Award. In 2006, she became the first hip hop artist to receive a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

Queen Latifah, a pioneering figure in hip hop and a celebrated screen actress.

Music career

Latifah signed with Tommy Boy Records in 1988 and released her debut album All Hail the Queen in 1989, featuring the hit single "Ladies First". Her third album, Black Reign (1993), included "U.N.I.T.Y.", which won a Grammy Award for Best Rap Solo Performance and became an anthem for women's empowerment in hip hop. She later recorded albums of jazz and soul standards, including The Dana Owens Album (2004).

Acting career

She starred as Khadijah James in the sitcom Living Single (1993–1998) and received critical acclaim and an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress for her role as Matron "Mama" Morton in the film Chicago (2002). Other notable roles include Set It Off (1996), Bringing Down the House (2003), Hairspray (2007), and the lead role in the television series The Equalizer (2021–2025). She has hosted the syndicated talk show The Queen Latifah Show in two runs.
