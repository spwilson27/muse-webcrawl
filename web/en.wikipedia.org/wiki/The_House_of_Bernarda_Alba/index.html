The House of Bernarda Alba

The House of Bernarda Alba (Spanish: La casa de Bernarda Alba) is a play by the Spanish dramatist Federico García Lorca, completed in 1936, shortly before his assassination during the Spanish Civil War. It was first performed in 1945 in Buenos Aires.

Plot

After the death of her second husband, the domineering matriarch Bernarda Alba imposes a traditional eight-year mourning period on her household, confining her five daughters — Angustias, Magdalena, Amelia, Martirio and Adela — within the walls of the house. When the eldest daughter becomes engaged to Pepe el Romano, the younger sisters' repressed desires ignite: Adela, the youngest, conducts a secret affair with Pepe, and Martirio jealously betrays her. When Bernarda fires at Pepe and Martirio lies that he is dead, Adela hangs herself. Bernarda's final concern is only the family's reputation: she insists Adela died a virgin and demands silence.

Themes

Subtitled "a drama of women in the villages of Spain," the play explores repression, authoritarianism, conformity and the position of women in a rigidly patriarchal, honour-bound society. Critics also read it as a critique of fascism and social intolerance in the Spain of Lorca's time. No male character appears on stage, yet men dominate the women's lives from offstage.

Productions and adaptations

The play, the last of Lorca's "rural trilogy" alongside Blood Wedding and Yerma, is one of the most performed Spanish plays worldwide. It has been adapted as an opera, as films, and into numerous translations and modern-dress productions.
