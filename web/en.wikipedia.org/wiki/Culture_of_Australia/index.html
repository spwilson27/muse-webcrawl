Culture of Australia

The Sydney Opera House, designed by Jørn Utzon, is a symbol of modern Australia.

The culture of Australia is a Western culture derived primarily from Britain and Ireland, enriched by the world's oldest living Indigenous cultures and by large-scale immigration from Europe, Asia and beyond after 1945. Australia is a multicultural, English-speaking society whose values are often summarized as egalitarianism, "mateship," informality and a "fair go."

Indigenous cultures

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have inhabited the continent for at least 65,000 years, maintaining hundreds of languages and rich traditions of art, music (including the didgeridoo) and Dreaming stories tied to Country. Indigenous art, from rock painting to the Western Desert acrylic movement, is now internationally celebrated.

Arts and media

Australian literature runs from bush ballads and Henry Lawson to Nobel laureate Patrick White and contemporary writers. The Heidelberg School defined a national landscape painting style. Australian cinema produced classics such as Picnic at Hanging Rock, Mad Max and Crocodile Dundee, and Australian actors and musicians are prominent worldwide.

Sport and lifestyle

Sport is central to national life: cricket, Australian rules football, rugby league and union, soccer, surfing and swimming command huge followings, and the Melbourne Cup horse race famously "stops the nation." The beach and the backyard barbecue are enduring institutions, and Australian English is rich in slang.
