Atom transfer radical polymerization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) is an example of a reversible-deactivation radical polymerization, a controlled/"living" radical polymerization technique. It was independently discovered by Mitsuo Sawamoto and by Krzysztof Matyjaszewski and Jin-Shan Wang in 1995. ATRP allows the synthesis of polymers with predetermined molecular weights, narrow molecular weight distributions (low dispersity), and complex architectures such as block, graft and star copolymers.

Mechanism

The method relies on a dynamic equilibrium between a low concentration of actively propagating radicals and a predominant amount of dormant species — alkyl halides capped chains. A transition metal complex, most commonly copper(I) with a nitrogen-based ligand such as bipyridine or PMDETA, reversibly abstracts a halogen atom from the dormant chain end, generating a radical and the higher-oxidation-state metal halide complex, which can deactivate the radical again. Because radicals are present only briefly, termination is suppressed and chains grow uniformly.

Components

InitiatorAlkyl halide (e.g. ethyl α-bromoisobutyrate)
CatalystCu(I) halide / ligand complex
MonomersStyrenes, (meth)acrylates, acrylonitrile
Typical dispersity1.05–1.3

Key components are the monomer, an initiator with a transferable halogen, a catalyst composed of a transition metal and ligand, and solvent. The rate and control depend on the equilibrium constant of activation/deactivation, tuned through ligand choice.

Variants

Variants developed to reduce catalyst loading and improve practicality include ARGET ATRP (activators regenerated by electron transfer), ICAR ATRP, SARA ATRP, photoinduced ATRP and electrochemically mediated eATRP, some operating at parts-per-million catalyst levels.

Applications

ATRP is used to prepare well-defined functional polymers for coatings, adhesives, dispersants, biomaterials and surface modification, although removal of metal catalyst residues remains a consideration for large-scale use.
