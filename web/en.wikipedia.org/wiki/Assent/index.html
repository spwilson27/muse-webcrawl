Assent

Assent
General meaningAgreement or approval; the expression of consent
Constitutional formRoyal assent — approval of legislation by a head of state
Epistemic formMental acceptance of a proposition as true

Assent is the act of agreeing to, approving, or permitting something; in law and philosophy it has more specific senses. The word derives from the Latin assentiri — ad ("to") plus sentire ("to feel, think") — and is generally used of a deliberate, often formal expression of agreement, as distinct from consent, which often implies authorisation by someone with the power to give or withhold it.

Royal assent

In constitutional monarchies and in Commonwealth realms, royal assent is the formal approval by the monarch (or a representative, such as a governor-general) that converts a bill passed by the legislature into an Act of Parliament. In the United Kingdom assent is now signified by commission under the Royal Assent Act 1967 and is, by convention, never refused; the last refusal was by Queen Anne in 1708. In republics the analogous step is presidential assent or promulgation — for example, legislation passed by an assembly such as the Assembly of the Republic takes effect after promulgation by the President.

In philosophy and psychology

Philosophers since the Stoics have used assent (sunkatathesis) for the mind's act of accepting an impression or proposition as true, a central concept in ancient epistemology and, in modern form, in theories of belief and judgement. John Henry Newman's Grammar of Assent (1870) famously distinguished real from notional assent.
