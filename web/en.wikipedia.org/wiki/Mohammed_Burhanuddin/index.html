Mohammed Burhanuddin

Syedna Mohammed Burhanuddin (6 March 1915 – 17 January 2014) was the 52nd Dā'ī al-Mutlaq (absolute missionary) and spiritual head of the Dawoodi Bohra community, a denomination of Ismaili Shia Islam. He led the community for nearly five decades, from 1965 until his death in 2014 at the age of 98.

Early life

Born in Surat, Gujarat, he was the son of the 51st Dā'ī, Syedna Taher Saifuddin, whom he succeeded in 1965. He was educated in religious sciences, memorised the Qur'an as a youth, and received a doctorate from Al-Azhar University in Cairo, among other academic honours.

Leadership

During his tenure Burhanuddin promoted education, women's literacy, healthcare and business enterprise among the Bohras, a traditionally mercantile community spread across India, East Africa, Yemen, the Gulf, Europe and North America. He oversaw major construction and restoration projects, including the reconstruction of historic mosques and the development of the Saifee Hospital in Mumbai, and he established the Saifee Burhani Upliftment Trust redevelopment project in Bhendi Bazaar, Mumbai, one of India's largest urban renewal schemes.

He was widely decorated: he received honours from Egypt and Jordan, the Padma Shri from India in 2015 (posthumously announced), and honorary doctorates from universities including Aligarh Muslim University.

Death and succession

Burhanuddin died of a cardiac arrest in Mumbai on 17 January 2014. His funeral drew an enormous crowd in Mumbai during which a stampede caused several deaths. He was buried at Raudat Tahera mausoleum in Mumbai beside his father. He was succeeded as 53rd Dā'ī by his son Mufaddal Saifuddin, though the succession was disputed by another claimant, Khuzaima Qutbuddin, and litigated in the Bombay High Court.
