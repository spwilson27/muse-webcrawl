The ArmaLite AR-10 is a 7.62×51mm NATO battle rifle designed by Eugene Stoner in the late 1950s and manufactured by ArmaLite (then a division of the Fairchild Aircraft Corporation). When introduced in 1956, the AR-10 used an innovative combination of a straight-line barrel/stock design with a phenolic composite stock and forged alloy parts, resulting in a weapon significantly easier to control in automatic fire and over 1 lb (0.45 kg) lighter than other infantry rifles of the day.

History and development

ArmaLite was founded in 1954 as a division of Fairchild Engine and Airplane Corporation. Its first chief engineer, Eugene Stoner, designed the AR-10 around the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge with a novel direct gas impingement system, an inline stock, a carrying handle housing the rear sight, and a charging handle within that carrying handle. The design was tested by the U.S. Army at Springfield Armory in 1957, but the Army chose the M14 instead.

Production and service

ArmaLite licensed production to the Dutch firm Artillerie-Inrichtingen (AI) at Hembrug, which produced AR-10s for contracts with Sudan, Portugal, and others. Portuguese paratroopers used the rifle extensively in the colonial wars in Africa during the 1960s and 1970s. The AR-10 was later scaled down into the AR-15, which became the M16 rifle.

Modern AR-10 pattern rifles

Following the expiry of patents, many manufacturers produce "AR-10 pattern" semi-automatic rifles chambered in .308 Winchester/7.62×51mm and other cartridges, popular for hunting, competition, and designated marksman roles. ArmaLite itself reintroduced the AR-10 name for modern semi-automatic rifles in the 1990s.
