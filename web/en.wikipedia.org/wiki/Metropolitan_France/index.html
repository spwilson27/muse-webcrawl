Metropolitan France (French: France métropolitaine or la Métropole), also known as European France, is the area of France located in Europe. It comprises mainland France and nearby islands in the Atlantic Ocean, the English Channel and the Mediterranean Sea, including Corsica.

Terminology

The term contrasts with Overseas France (France d'outre-mer): the overseas departments and regions (French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Mayotte, Réunion) and the overseas collectivities and territories. Together, metropolitan and overseas France form the French Republic. Because of its roughly hexagonal shape, mainland France is often called l'Hexagone.

Geography and demography

Metropolitan France covers about 551,695 km² and holds roughly 96% of the republic's population. Its 96 departments are those numbered 01–95 plus the two Corsican departments (2A, 2B). The five overseas departments have three-digit numbers (971–976). The INSEE uses the term in statistics to distinguish totals for the European territory from the whole republic.

Status

Some laws and regulations apply only in metropolitan France, or apply differently overseas, reflecting differences in geography and status recognised by the French Constitution. Corsica has a special territorial collectivity status within the metropolitan territory.
