Natural (music)

The natural sign ♮ on a staff

In music theory, a natural is an accidental which cancels previous accidentals and represents the unaltered pitch of a note. A note is referred to as "natural" when it is not flat (♭) or sharp (♯), and as a "natural sign" (♮) when written as an accidental.

Notation and use

The natural sign (♮) is placed before a notehead to cancel a sharp or flat applied earlier in the same bar, or implied by the key signature. For example, if a bar contains F♯ and the composer wishes to return to F natural later in the same bar, the second note is marked with a natural sign. Naturals remain in effect only for the remainder of the bar in which they appear, like other accidentals in modern notation practice.

The term also applies to instruments and harmonics: on brass instruments, a "natural" note is one produced without valves or slides, from the open harmonic series — hence the historical natural horn and natural trumpet. On string instruments, a natural harmonic is one produced on an open string, as opposed to an artificial harmonic.

History

The natural sign derives from the square form of the letter B (b quadratum) used in medieval notation to distinguish B natural from the rounded B (b rotundum) that indicated B flat — the origin of both the natural and sharp signs.
