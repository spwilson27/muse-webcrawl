Kali Yuga

Kali Yuga (Sanskrit: कलियुग, "age of Kali" or "age of vice") is the fourth and worst of the four yugas (world ages) in Hindu cosmology, following Satya (Krita), Treta and Dvapara Yuga. It is characterised by conflict, ignorance and the decline of dharma. Hindu tradition dates its beginning to 3102 BCE, traditionally the year of Krishna's departure from the world, shortly after the Kurukshetra War.

Description

The yuga cycle is often pictured as a bull representing dharma standing on four legs in Satya Yuga and losing one leg in each succeeding age; in Kali Yuga it stands on a single leg. Puranic texts such as the Bhagavata Purana describe the age's symptoms: rulers become corrupt, lifespans and virtue shrink, wealth replaces merit as the mark of worth, and falsehood prevails in disputes. Kali here is the demon of discord, not the goddess Kālī, with whom he is sometimes confused.

Chronology

A full cycle of four yugas (a mahayuga) lasts 4,320,000 years, of which Kali Yuga is the final 432,000 years. On the traditional reckoning the present era began in 3102 BCE, so roughly 427,000 years of the age remain. At its end, Vishnu is prophesied to appear as the avatar Kalki, riding a white horse, to destroy the wicked and inaugurate a new Satya Yuga.

Interpretations

Reformers and teachers such as Swami Sri Yukteswar proposed alternative, much shorter yuga cycles based on precession; others interpret the ages symbolically as states of human consciousness. The concept strongly influenced later Indian religions and was borrowed into Jain and Sikh thought, and it features in modern popular culture and astronomy discussions of the 3102 BCE epoch, which also opens the count of the Kali Yuga era used in Indian calendars.
