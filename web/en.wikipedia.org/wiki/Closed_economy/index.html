Closed economy

A closed economy is an economy that does not interact with other economies in the world: there are no exports, no imports, and no capital flows. It is the opposite of an open economy. No modern country is completely self-sufficient in this sense, but the closed economy is a fundamental simplifying model in macroeconomics.

[Image: Diagram of the circular flow of income in a closed economy]

In macroeconomic models

In a closed economy, total output (GDP) is exhausted by consumption, investment, and government purchases: Y = C + I + G. National saving — the sum of private and public saving — must equal investment, so the real interest rate adjusts to balance the market for loanable funds. Many introductory models, including the Solow growth model and basic IS–LM analysis, are first developed for the closed-economy case before trade and capital mobility are added.

Autarky

A policy of deliberate economic self-sufficiency is called autarky. Historical approximations include Tokugawa Japan under sakoku, Hoxha-era Albania, and North Korea under the Juche ideology, though all maintained some trade. Economists generally argue that closing an economy forfeits the gains from comparative advantage, though governments may restrict openness for security, infant-industry, or balance-of-payments reasons using tariffs, quotas, and capital controls.
