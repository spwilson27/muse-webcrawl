Battle of Imphal

Battle of Imphal
Date8 March – 3 July 1944
LocationImphal, Manipur, British India (modern India)
ResultDecisive Allied victory
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom, British India vs. Empire of Japan, Indian National Army
CommandersWilliam Slim, Geoffrey Scoones vs. Renya Mutaguchi, Subhas Chandra Bose (INA)

The Battle of Imphal took place in the region around the city of Imphal, the capital of Manipur in northeast India, from March until July 1944. Together with the simultaneous Battle of Kohima, it marked the turning point of the Burma campaign: the Japanese Fifteenth Army's invasion of India (Operation U-Go) was destroyed, suffering some of the heaviest losses in Japanese military history.

Background

In 1944 Lieutenant General Renya Mutaguchi persuaded his superiors to launch an offensive across the Chindwin River to seize Imphal and Kohima, cut the supply line to China, and — he hoped — spark an uprising in British India with the aid of Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army. The plan gambled on capturing Allied supply dumps, since the Japanese carried only about three weeks' rations through some of the most difficult terrain in the world.

The battle

General William Slim, commanding the British Fourteenth Army, anticipated the offensive and withdrew Geoffrey Scoones's IV Corps to the Imphal plain, where Allied armour and airpower could be used and supplies flown in. From March the Japanese divisions closed around Imphal, cutting its roads and isolating the garrison, which was sustained by air supply — including the airlift of the 5th Indian Division from the Arakan. Bitter fighting raged at positions such as Nungshigum, Shenam Saddle and Bishenpur, and the parallel siege of Kohima to the north was relieved in June. On 22 June the Imphal–Kohima road was reopened; on 3 July the Japanese called off the offensive.

Aftermath

Of roughly 85,000 Japanese troops committed, more than 50,000 were lost, most to starvation and disease during the disastrous retreat across the flooded Chindwin. Allied casualties were about 12,500. Japan never again threatened India, and Slim's Fourteenth Army went on to reconquer Burma in 1945. In 2013 Imphal–Kohima was voted "Britain's Greatest Battle" in a National Army Museum poll.
