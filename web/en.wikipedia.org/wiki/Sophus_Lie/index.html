Sophus Lie

Marius Sophus Lie
Born: 17 December 1842, Nordfjordeid, Norway
Died: 18 February 1899, Christiania (Oslo)
Field: Mathematics
Known for: Lie groups, Lie algebras, continuous symmetry of differential equations

Marius Sophus Lie (17 December 1842 – 18 February 1899) was a Norwegian mathematician who created the theory of continuous symmetry — now known through the concepts of Lie groups and Lie algebras — and applied it to geometry and differential equations.

Life and work

Studying at the University of Christiania, Lie was inspired by the work of Poncelet and Plücker. In 1869–70 he travelled to Berlin and Paris, where with Felix Klein he absorbed the ideas that led both men to group-theoretic approaches to geometry. Lie became professor at Christiania in 1872 and later succeeded Klein at Leipzig (1886), where he built a research school; his health declining, he returned to Norway in 1898.

Contributions

Lie's central idea was to do for differential equations what Galois theory had done for algebraic equations: classify them by their symmetry groups. Continuous transformation groups (Lie groups) and their infinitesimal generators (Lie algebras) became fundamental throughout mathematics and theoretical physics, from differential geometry to quantum mechanics and the Standard Model.
