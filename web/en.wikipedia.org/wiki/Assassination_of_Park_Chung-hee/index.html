Assassination of Park Chung-hee

Date26 October 1979
LocationKCIA safe house near the Blue House, Seoul, South Korea
TargetPark Chung-hee, President of South Korea
PerpetratorKim Jae-gyu, Director of the KCIA
DeathsPark Chung-hee; Cha Ji-chul; several bodyguards

The assassination of Park Chung-hee, president of South Korea since 1961, occurred on 26 October 1979 (the "10.26 incident") during a private dinner at a Korean Central Intelligence Agency (KCIA) safe house in Seoul. Park was shot by his own intelligence chief, Kim Jae-gyu.

The shooting

At the dinner, attended by Park, Kim, presidential chief bodyguard Cha Ji-chul and chief secretary Kim Gye-won, tensions over the handling of anti-government protests in Busan and Masan escalated. Kim left the room, returned with a pistol, and shot Cha and then Park, who died of his wounds. Kim's accomplices killed several presidential bodyguards elsewhere in the compound. Kim later claimed he acted to restore democracy; historians still debate his motives, which may have included rivalry with Cha and fear of dismissal.

Aftermath

Martial law was declared and Prime Minister Choi Kyu-hah became acting president. Kim Jae-gyu and his co-conspirators were tried by a military court and hanged in May 1980. Within weeks, Major General Chun Doo-hwan consolidated power in the coup of 12 December 1979, leading to his own authoritarian presidency. Park's killing ended the Yushin system and 18 years of his rule.
