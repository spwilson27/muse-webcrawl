Wormhole

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Artistic rendering of a wormhole: a tunnel through space connecting two distant regions, stars visible through it]
A wormhole as a shortcut through spacetime.

A wormhole is a hypothetical structure which connects disparate points in spacetime. It may be visualised as a tunnel with two ends at separate points in spacetime (different locations, different points in time, or both). Wormholes are consistent with the general theory of relativity, but whether they actually exist is unknown.

The first type of wormhole solution was the Schwarzschild wormhole (the Einstein–Rosen bridge, 1935), found to be unstable and untraversable. In 1988 Kip Thorne and Mike Morris showed that a traversable wormhole could be kept open by 'exotic matter' violating the averaged null energy condition — matter with negative energy density, which quantum effects supply only in tiny quantities (Casimir effect). Wormholes feature in the ER = EPR proposal (Maldacena and Leonard Susskind, 2013), which equates Einstein–Rosen bridges with Quantum entanglement, and in science fiction as the standard device for interstellar travel; no observational evidence for wormholes exists.

Time travel and paradoxes

A traversable wormhole with its mouths moved appropriately becomes a time machine in general relativity, raising causality paradoxes that Hawking's 'chronology protection conjecture' (1992) proposes quantum effects will always prevent. The study of such solutions — though speculative — has sharpened understanding of energy conditions, quantum inequalities and the limits of semiclassical gravity.
