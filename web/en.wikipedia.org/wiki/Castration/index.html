Castration

Castration is the removal or inactivation of the gonads — the testes in males or the ovaries in females — by surgical, chemical, or other means. It eliminates most production of sex hormones such as testosterone and has effects on fertility, sexual characteristics, and behaviour.

History and human practice

Castrated men, or eunuchs, served as officials, harem guards, and servants in many ancient and imperial courts, including those of Assyria, China, the Byzantine Empire, and the Ottoman Empire. In Europe, boy singers were castrated from the 16th to the 19th century to preserve their voices, producing the castrati of Baroque opera. Castration has also been used historically as a punishment and, in the 20th century, coercively in eugenics programmes; chemical castration using antiandrogens is used in some jurisdictions for certain sex offenders and as treatment for hormone-sensitive cancers.

Animal husbandry

Castration of livestock — producing steers, geldings, barrows, capons and wethers — is routine to reduce aggression, prevent unwanted breeding, and improve meat quality. In companion animals the procedures are called neutering, spaying (females) and castration (males), and are widely practised for population control and health reasons.
