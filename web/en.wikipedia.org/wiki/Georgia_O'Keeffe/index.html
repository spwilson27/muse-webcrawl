Georgia O'Keeffe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A monumental close-up of a flower filling the whole canvas, petals swelling in red and pink]

Georgia Totto O'Keeffe (November 15, 1887 – March 6, 1986) was an American modernist painter known for her paintings of enlarged flowers, New York skyscrapers and New Mexico landscapes. O'Keeffe has been called the 'Mother of American modernism'.

Promoted by the photographer Alfred Stieglitz, whom she married in 1924, she became famous for flower paintings such as Red Canna and Black Iris and for city views like Radiator Building – Night, New York. From 1929 she spent increasing time in New Mexico, whose bones, crosses and adobe architecture — around Taos, Ghost Ranch and Abiquiú — dominated her later work. In 2014 her Jimson Weed/White Flower No. 1 sold for $44.4 million, then the highest price for a work by a woman artist.
