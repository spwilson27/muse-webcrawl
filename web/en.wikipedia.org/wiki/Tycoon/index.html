Tycoon

Tycoon
MeaningA wealthy, powerful business magnate
OriginJapanese taikun (大君), "great lord"
Entered English1850s (title for the Tokugawa shōgun)
SynonymsMagnate, mogul, baron, industrialist

A tycoon is a wealthy, powerful person in business or industry. The term derives from the Japanese taikun (大君), literally "great lord", a title used by the shōgun of Japan in dealings with foreign powers in the mid-19th century. American officials who negotiated with Japan in the 1850s brought the word home, where it was jocularly applied to Abraham Lincoln by his staff before settling into its modern meaning of a dominant business figure.

Usage

"Tycoon" belongs to a family of near-synonyms — magnate, mogul, baron ("robber baron"), czar, and magnate compounds such as "railroad tycoon", "media tycoon", and "property tycoon" — each signalling control of a particular industry. The word carries connotations of flamboyant wealth and personal dominance beyond the neutral "industrialist" or "businessperson", and is frequently applied to figures such as Cornelius Vanderbilt, John D. Rockefeller, and, in the modern era, press barons and technology founders.

In popular culture

The word is embedded in business-simulation video games, most famously Railroad Tycoon (1990) and the many "Tycoon" titles it spawned, in which players build business empires. The "Last Tycoon" archetype, from F. Scott Fitzgerald's unfinished novel The Last Tycoon about a Hollywood studio mogul, has further fixed the term in cultural usage.
