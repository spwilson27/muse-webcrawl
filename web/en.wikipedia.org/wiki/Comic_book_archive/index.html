Comic book archive

A comic book archive or comic archive file is a type of archive file for the purpose of sequential viewing of images, especially comic books. The idea was popularized by the CDisplay sequential image viewer; since then, numerous comic book viewers for different platforms have been created.

Design

A comic book archive is an ordinary archive file — typically ZIP, RAR, 7z, or TAR — containing scanned page images (usually JPEG, PNG, or WebP) named in reading order, distinguished only by its file extension:

  • .cbz — ZIP archive
  • .cbr — RAR archive
  • .cb7 — 7z archive
  • .cbt — TAR archive
  • .cba — ACE archive (rare)

Reader software sorts the enclosed images alphabetically and displays them page by page, often with two-page spreads, zooming, and library management. Optional metadata, such as a ComicInfo.xml file, can record title, issue, author, and credits. The format supports no encryption or rights management, which makes it popular both for legitimate digital distribution by independent creators and for unauthorized scans ("scanslations" and pirated comics).

Software

Popular readers have included CDisplay and CDisplayEx on Windows, YACReader and Calibre (which has built-in comic support), and various mobile applications on Android and iOS. Comic servers such as Komga and Kavita organize archives into streaming libraries.
