Elimelech of Lizhensk

Elimelech Weisblum of Lizhensk
Born: 1717, Tykocin, Poland
Died: 1787, Leżajsk, Poland
Known for: Author of Noam Elimelech

Elimelech Weisblum of Lizhensk (1717–1787), often called the Noam Elimelech after his work, was a rabbi and one of the founding figures of Hasidic Judaism in Poland. A disciple of Dov Ber, the Maggid of Mezeritch (himself the chief disciple of the Baal Shem Tov), Elimelech settled in Leżajsk (Yiddish: Lizhensk) in Galicia and became the leading Hasidic master of his generation in Poland, spreading the movement from that region.

Life

Elimelech and his brother, Zusha of Anipoli, spent years in self-imposed exile as wandering preachers, a Hasidic practice known as galut, before Elimelech established his court at Leżajsk. His pupils—including Yaakov Yitzchak, the Seer of Lublin; Menachem Mendel of Rymanów; Abraham Yehoshua Heshel of Apt; and the Maggid of Kozhnitz—went on to head the major Hasidic courts of nineteenth-century Poland, making him the ancestor of much of Polish Hasidism.

Teachings

His book Noam Elimelech ("The Pleasantness of Elimelech"), published posthumously in 1788, is a commentary on the weekly Torah portions and a classic of Hasidic literature. It develops the doctrine of the tzadik: the righteous leader as a channel through whom divine blessing flows to the community, and to whom followers owe attachment and support. The work's gentle tone and its instruction that one should see the good in every person made it one of the most widely read Hasidic books; many families traditionally place it at a woman's bedside during childbirth as a protection.

Legacy

Elimelech's grave in Leżajsk remains a major pilgrimage site; tens of thousands of visitors, many from abroad, gather there each year on the anniversary of his death, 21 Adar. His prayer Tefillah Kodem HaTefillah, recited before the morning service—asking to see the merit of others and none of their faults—is printed in many prayer books.
