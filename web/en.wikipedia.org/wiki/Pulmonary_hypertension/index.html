Pulmonary hypertension (PH or PHTN) is a condition of increased blood pressure in the arteries of the lungs. Symptoms include shortness of breath, fainting, tiredness, chest pain, swelling of the legs, and a fast heartbeat. The condition may make it difficult to exercise. Onset is typically gradual.

Definition and classification

Pulmonary hypertension is defined haemodynamically by a mean pulmonary arterial pressure above 20 mmHg measured at right heart catheterisation. The World Health Organization clinical classification divides PH into five groups: (1) pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), including idiopathic, heritable, and drug- or disease-associated forms; (2) PH due to left heart disease; (3) PH due to lung diseases and/or hypoxia; (4) chronic thromboembolic PH (CTEPH), caused by organised blood clots; and (5) PH with unclear or multifactorial mechanisms. In PAH, the small pulmonary arteries are narrowed by vasoconstriction, vascular remodelling, and in-situ thrombosis; heritable PAH is most often caused by mutations in the BMPR2 gene.

Diagnosis

Suspicion arises from symptoms, an accentuated pulmonary component of the second heart sound, and findings on ECG or chest imaging. Echocardiography estimates pulmonary pressures and excludes left-heart causes; the definitive test is right heart catheterisation, which measures pressures and pulmonary vascular resistance directly and tests vasoreactivity.

Treatment

Treatment targets the underlying group and cause. PAH-specific drugs act on three pathways: endothelin receptor antagonists (e.g. bosentan, macitentan), phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (e.g. sildenafil, tadalafil) and other agents of the nitric oxide pathway, and prostacyclin analogues and receptor agonists (e.g. epoprostenol, selexipag); the activin-signalling inhibitor sotatercept was added in 2024. CTEPH may be curable by pulmonary endarterectomy surgery or treated with balloon pulmonary angioplasty. Supportive care includes diuretics, oxygen, and anticoagulation in selected patients; lung or heart–lung transplantation is an option for advanced disease. Untreated PAH historically had a median survival under three years from diagnosis; modern therapy has substantially improved prognosis, though it remains a serious progressive condition.
