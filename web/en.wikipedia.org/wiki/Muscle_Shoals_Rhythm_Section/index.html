The Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section is a group of American session musicians based in Muscle Shoals, Alabama, who performed on many hit records from the 1960s to the 1980s — including songs by Aretha Franklin, Wilson Pickett, the Rolling Stones, Bob Dylan and Paul Simon. Informally called The Swampers, they were immortalised in Lynyrd Skynyrd's 1974 song "Sweet Home Alabama": "Now Muscle Shoals has got the Swampers / And they've been known to pick a song or two."

The studio at 3614 Jackson Highway, Sheffield, founded by the Swampers in 1969.

Career

The core four — keyboardist Barry Beckett, drummer Roger Hawkins, guitarist Jimmy Johnson and bassist David Hood — first worked together at Rick Hall's FAME Studios, backing Arthur Alexander, Wilson Pickett ("Mustang Sally," "Land of 1000 Dances") and Aretha Franklin's breakthrough 1967 sessions ("I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You," "Respect"). In 1969 they left FAME to found their own Muscle Shoals Sound Studio at 3614 Jackson Highway in neighbouring Sheffield.

There they recorded the Staple Singers' "I'll Take You There," Paul Simon's "Kodachrome," Bob Seger's "Old Time Rock and Roll," and sessions for the Rolling Stones (who cut "Brown Sugar" and "Wild Horses" at the studio in December 1969), Traffic, Rod Stewart, Bob Dylan and Dire Straits. Their playing was notable for a deep, unhurried groove rooted in southern soul and R&B — remarkable to many visitors because the Swampers were all white musicians backing predominantly Black artists in the segregated South.

Legacy

The group was inducted into the Alabama Music Hall of Fame, and their story is told in the 2013 documentary Muscle Shoals. David Hood remained active as a bassist into the 2020s; the original Jackson Highway studio was restored and reopened as a working studio and museum.
