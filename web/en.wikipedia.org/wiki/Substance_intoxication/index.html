Substance intoxication

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Substance intoxication is a condition in which a person's mental and physical functioning is temporarily altered following the recent ingestion of a psychoactive substance, such as alcohol, cannabis, opioids, stimulants, or sedatives. The changes result from the substance's effects on the central nervous system and vary with the drug, the dose, and the individual's tolerance and state.

Signs and symptoms

Common features of intoxication include disturbed perception, impaired judgement, changes in mood and behaviour, slurred speech, unsteady gait, and reduced attention and memory. Specific syndromes differ by substance: alcohol intoxication typically produces disinhibition and incoordination; stimulant intoxication produces euphoria, agitation, and increased energy; opioid intoxication produces sedation, constricted pupils, and depressed respiration; hallucinogen intoxication produces perceptual distortions. Severe intoxication can progress to stupor, coma, respiratory failure, or death — in which case it constitutes poisoning or overdose.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on a history of recent substance use and the characteristic pattern of signs, supplemented where needed by blood or urine testing. Clinicians must distinguish intoxication from withdrawal states, head injury, metabolic disorders, and psychiatric illness, which can mimic or accompany it.

Management

Treatment is mainly supportive: protecting the airway, monitoring vital signs, and allowing time for the substance to be metabolised. Specific antidotes exist for some substances, including naloxone for opioid overdose and flumazenil (used cautiously) for benzodiazepine overdose. Agitated patients may require sedation and a safe, supervised environment. Assessment for underlying substance use disorder and referral to treatment are recommended once the acute episode resolves.

Legal and social aspects

Intoxication in public is an offence in many jurisdictions, and driving while intoxicated is widely criminalised because of its contribution to road deaths. In criminal law, voluntary intoxication is generally not a defence, though it may be relevant to specific charges in some legal systems.
