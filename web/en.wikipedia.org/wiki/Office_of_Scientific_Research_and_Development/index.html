Office of Scientific Research and Development

Abbreviation: OSRD

Formed: June 28, 1941

Dissolved: December 31, 1947

Director: Vannevar Bush

Parent: Office for Emergency Management

The Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) was an agency of the United States federal government created to coordinate scientific research for military purposes during World War II. Established by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on June 28, 1941, it superseded and expanded the National Defense Research Committee (NDRC) and was headed throughout its existence by engineer Vannevar Bush, who reported almost directly to the President.

Organization and work

The OSRD let contracts to universities and industry rather than building large government laboratories, pioneering the model of federally funded research performed at academic institutions that shaped postwar American science. Its divisions worked on radar (through the MIT Radiation Laboratory), the proximity fuze, sonar, rockets, and new medical treatments, including the mass production of penicillin and antimalarial research. Its Committee on Medical Research coordinated wartime advances in blood substitutes, insecticides such as DDT, and vaccines.

The atomic bomb

The OSRD's Section S-1 supervised early American work on uranium and nuclear fission. As the project grew, OSRD transferred the engineering and production effort to the Army's Manhattan Engineering District in 1942–43 while retaining scientific oversight of key research contracts; Bush remained a central figure in atomic policy through the end of the war.

Legacy

Bush's 1945 report to the President, Science, the Endless Frontier, distilled the OSRD experience into the case for a permanent federal science agency and led, after several years of debate, to the creation of the National Science Foundation in 1950. The OSRD itself wound down after the war and was formally terminated on December 31, 1947.
