Canadian content

Canadian content (abbreviated CanCon, Cancon or Can-Con) refers to the cultural requirement, established by the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC), that radio and television broadcasters in Canada air a certain percentage of content that was at least partly written, produced, presented, or otherwise contributed to by persons from Canada. The rules are intended to promote Canadian culture and the domestic production industry in the face of the much larger United States media market.

Music

For music, the MAPL system determines whether a recording qualifies as Canadian: points are awarded for Music composed by a Canadian, Artist who is Canadian, Performance recorded or performed live in Canada, and Lyrics written by a Canadian. Commercial radio stations must generally devote 35% of popular music aired during the week to Canadian content.

Television

Television requirements have varied over time; conventional broadcasters have historically been required to devote a proportion of the broadcast day and of prime time to Canadian programming, alongside expenditure requirements administered through the CRTC and support funds such as the Canada Media Fund.

Debate

Supporters argue CanCon sustains artists such as The Tragically Hip, Rush and Arcade Fire and preserves a distinct cultural voice; critics argue quotas can promote mediocrity, are difficult to apply to streaming services, and amount to cultural protectionism. The Online Streaming Act (2023) extended CRTC authority to online platforms.
