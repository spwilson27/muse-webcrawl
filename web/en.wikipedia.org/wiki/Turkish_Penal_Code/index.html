Turkish Penal Code

The Turkish Penal Code (Turkish: Türk Ceza Kanunu) is the principal criminal code of the Republic of Turkey. The current code, Law No. 5237, was adopted in 2004 and entered into force on 1 June 2005, replacing the 1926 code that had been modelled on the Italian Zanardelli Code.

Structure

The code is divided into a general part, covering principles such as legality, intent, culpability, attempt, and sentencing, and a special part defining offences against persons, property, society, and the state. The 2005 reform abolished the death penalty for all crimes, strengthened provisions on torture and organised crime, and removed many discriminatory provisions affecting women.

Controversial provisions

Article 301, which criminalised "denigrating Turkishness" (later amended to "the Turkish nation"), was used to prosecute writers including Nobel laureate Orhan Pamuk and journalist Hrant Dink, and provoked sustained criticism from the European Union; a 2008 amendment required the Justice Minister's permission for prosecutions. Anti-terrorism provisions within the code and the separate Anti-Terror Law have also drawn scrutiny for their use against journalists and politicians.
