Vesicular monoamine transporter

The vesicular monoamine transporter (VMAT) is a transport protein integrated into the membrane of synaptic vesicles that pumps monoamine neurotransmitters — dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin and histamine — into vesicles for storage and subsequent release.

Isoforms

Two isoforms exist: VMAT1 (gene SLC18A1), expressed mainly in neuroendocrine cells such as adrenal chromaffin cells, and VMAT2 (gene SLC18A2), expressed predominantly in neurons of the central nervous system.

Pharmacology

VMAT uses the proton gradient across the vesicle membrane to drive uptake. Reserpine irreversibly blocks VMAT, depleting monoamines — an early (now rarely used) treatment for hypertension and psychosis whose depressive side effects helped inspire the monoamine hypothesis of depression. Tetrabenazine and its derivatives valbenazine and deutetrabenazine reversibly inhibit VMAT2 and are used to treat chorea in Huntington's disease and tardive dyskinesia. Amphetamines disrupt VMAT function, contributing to their stimulant effects.
