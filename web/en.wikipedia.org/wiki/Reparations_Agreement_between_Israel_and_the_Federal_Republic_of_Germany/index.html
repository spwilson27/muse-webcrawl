Reparations Agreement between Israel and the Federal Republic of Germany

Reparations Agreement
Signed10 September 1952, Luxembourg City Hall
PartiesIsrael; Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany)
Amount3 billion Deutsche Marks to Israel, plus 450 million DM to the Claims Conference

The Reparations Agreement between Israel and the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Luxemburger Abkommen; Hebrew: הסכם השילומים, "Heskem HaShillumim") was signed on 10 September 1952 in Luxembourg and entered into force on 27 March 1953. Under the agreement, West Germany undertook to pay Israel 3 billion Deutsche Marks over 14 years, largely in goods and services, as compensation for the costs of absorbing Jewish refugees and as moral restitution for the Holocaust; a further 450 million DM was paid to the Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany (Claims Conference) for victims outside Israel.

Negotiations and controversy

The agreement followed Chancellor Konrad Adenauer's September 1951 declaration acknowledging Germany's obligation to make moral and material amends. Negotiations, held at Wassenaar in the Netherlands, were fiercely controversial in Israel: Prime Minister David Ben-Gurion's government argued the payments were essential to the young state's economy, while the opposition, led by Menachem Begin, denounced accepting "blood money" and staged mass protests outside the Knesset in January 1952. The Knesset approved negotiations by a narrow margin.

Implementation and impact

Reparations goods — including ships, railway equipment, machinery and fuel — played a major role in Israel's economic development in the 1950s, and the agreement laid the groundwork for the eventual establishment of diplomatic relations between Israel and West Germany in 1965. German payments to individual survivors through related programmes continued for decades.
