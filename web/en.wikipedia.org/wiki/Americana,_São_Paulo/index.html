Americana is a municipality located in the Brazilian state of São Paulo. It is part of the Metropolitan Region of Campinas and the larger Campinas microregion. The city is unusual in Brazil for having been founded largely by immigrants from the United States — former Confederates who emigrated after the American Civil War — together with later waves of Italian immigrants.

History

The settlement began in 1866, when Confederate exiles led by Colonel William Hutchinson Norris, a former Alabama state senator and veteran of the Mexican–American War, established a community near the Santa Bárbara area. The community, initially called Vila dos Americanos ("Village of the Americans"), grew around a railway station on the line between Campinas and Rio Claro. The town was officially incorporated as part of Santa Bárbara d'Oeste before becoming a separate municipality in 1924, taking the name Americana in honour of its American founders.

The Confederate settlers — known in Brazil as Confederados — introduced new agricultural techniques, including the steel plough and the cultivation of watermelon and cotton varieties. Their descendants still hold an annual festival, the Festa Confederada, at the Campo Cemetery in neighbouring Santa Bárbara d'Oeste, where many of the original immigrants are buried.

Geography and economy

Americana lies about 115 kilometres northwest of the state capital, São Paulo city. Historically its economy centred on textiles; the Carioba district was a major textile mill complex. Today the municipality has a diversified industrial base including metallurgy, chemicals and food processing, and a strong services sector serving the Campinas metropolitan area.

Culture

The city's Confederate heritage remains a defining cultural feature. The Immigration Museum (Museu da Imigração) in Santa Bárbara d'Oeste documents the history of the Confederado community. Americana is also known for its large rodeos and country-music festivals, reflecting its agricultural roots.
