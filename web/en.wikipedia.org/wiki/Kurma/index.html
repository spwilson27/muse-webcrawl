Kurma

Kurma (Sanskrit: कूर्म, "tortoise") is the second avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu, following Matsya the fish and preceding Varaha the boar, in the traditional list of the Dashavatara.

Mythology

In the Kurma narrative, the devas (gods) and asuras (demons) joined forces to churn the cosmic Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthana) in order to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. Mount Mandara served as the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope. When the mountain began to sink into the ocean, Vishnu took the form of a great tortoise and supported it upon his back. From the churning emerged numerous treasures, including the goddess Lakshmi, the moon, the wish-granting cow Kamadhenu, the poison halahala — which Shiva drank — and finally Dhanvantari bearing the nectar.

Temples and worship

Kurma is rarely the principal deity of temples; the best known is the Sri Kurmanatha temple at Srikurmam in Andhra Pradesh, one of the few shrines dedicated specifically to this avatar. The story is told in the Mahabharata, the Bhagavata Purana and the Vishnu Purana.
