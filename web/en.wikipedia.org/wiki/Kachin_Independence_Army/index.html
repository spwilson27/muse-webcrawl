Kachin Independence Army
Founded: 1961
Political wing: Kachin Independence Organisation
Area: Kachin State and northern Shan State, Myanmar
Headquarters: Laiza

The Kachin Independence Army (KIA) is the armed wing of the Kachin Independence Organisation (KIO), a political organisation representing the Kachin people of northern Myanmar. Founded in 1961, it is one of the largest ethnic armed organisations in the country.

History

The KIA was formed in response to the centralisation policies of the Burmese government after the 1962 coup. It fought a long insurgency for Kachin autonomy, controlling substantial territory along the Chinese border. A ceasefire signed in 1994 held for 17 years before collapsing in June 2011, after which fighting resumed around Laiza, the KIO's headquarters, displacing large numbers of civilians.

Recent conflict

Following the 2021 military coup in Myanmar, the KIA cooperated at times with resistance forces opposed to the junta and expanded its operations. The conflict has centred on jade-mining areas such as Hpakant and on strategic transport routes in Kachin State.

Organisation

The KIA is organised into brigades across Kachin and northern Shan State and administers territory, taxation and services in areas under KIO control.
