College of Bishops

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The bishops of a church act corporately in matters of faith and governance.

The College of Bishops is a term used in several Christian traditions to describe the body of all bishops of a church acting together as a corporate body. In Roman Catholic theology, the college of bishops, together with and under its head the Pope, is the subject of supreme and full authority over the universal Church.

Catholic theology

The Second Vatican Council's dogmatic constitution Lumen gentium (1964) taught that episcopal consecration confers membership in the college of bishops in hierarchical communion with the Pope and the other members. The college exercises its authority most solemnly in an ecumenical council, and in dispersed fashion when the bishops, in communion with the Pope, agree that a teaching is to be held definitively. This doctrine of collegiality balanced the definition of papal primacy and infallibility made at the First Vatican Council (1870).

Other traditions

In Anglicanism, the bishops of the Anglican Communion meet at the Lambeth Conference, roughly every ten years, though its resolutions are advisory rather than legislative. In the Church of England, the House of Bishops forms one of the three houses of the General Synod. In Eastern Orthodoxy, authority rests with synods of bishops of each autocephalous church, such as the Holy Synod.
