Scissors

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scissors are hand-operated cutting instruments consisting of a pair of blades pivoted so that the sharpened edges slide against each other when the handles (bows) are closed. Scissors are used for cutting various thin materials, such as paper, cardboard, metal foil, cloth, rope, and wire. A large variety of scissors and shears exist for specialised purposes. Hair-cutting scissors and kitchen shears are functionally specialized forms, as are surgical scissors used in medicine.

History

The earliest cutting tools of the scissor type were spring shears, in which two bronze blades were joined by a flexible strip of metal; examples date to ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt around 1500 BCE. Pivoted scissors of the modern form were invented by the Romans around 100 CE and were made in bronze and iron. Scissors were manufactured in quantity from cast steel in Hangzhou, China, from the seventeenth century, and in Europe the English city of Sheffield became a major centre of production — Robert Hinchliffe of Sheffield is credited with first mass-producing modern scissors from cast steel in 1761. The German Solingen and Japanese Seki also became famous scissor-making centres.

Construction

Modern scissors consist of two blades joined by a pivot, with handles formed into finger loops (bows). Most scissors are designed for right-handed users; left-handed scissors have the blades reversed so the cutting edge remains visible and the natural squeezing motion works with the pivot. The mechanical advantage of scissors depends on where the material is placed along the blades: cuts near the pivot apply greater force, suited to tougher material, while cuts near the tips move faster and suit light material.

Specialised types

Types include dressmaker's shears, embroidery scissors, pinking shears (which cut a zigzag edge that resists fraying), thinning scissors for hairdressing, trauma shears for cutting clothing in first aid, bandage scissors with a flattened lower blade, tin snips for sheet metal, and hedge shears and pruning shears for gardening. In the game rock paper scissors, the hand gesture for scissors imitates the tool with two extended fingers.
