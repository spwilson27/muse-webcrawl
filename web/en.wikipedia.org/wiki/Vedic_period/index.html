The Vedic period, or Vedic age (c. 1500 – c. 500 BCE), is the period in the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age of the history of India when the Vedic literature, including the Vedas, was composed in the northern Indian subcontinent, between the decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation and the rise of the Mahajanapada kingdoms.

Overview

The period is conventionally divided into an Early Vedic phase (c. 1500–1000 BCE), associated with the composition of the Rigveda in the Punjab region by Indo-Aryan-speaking peoples, and a Later Vedic phase (c. 1000–500 BCE), during which society expanded eastward into the Ganges plain, the kingdom of Kuru emerged, and the Brahmanas, Aranyakas and early Upanishads were composed.

Society and religion

Vedic society was organized around kinship and a developing varna hierarchy of Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras. Religion centred on ritual sacrifice (yajna) performed by priests, offerings to deities such as Indra, Agni and Varuna, and the ritual drink soma. In the later period, speculative texts — the Upanishads — developed concepts of atman, brahman, karma and rebirth that became foundational to later Hinduism.

Legacy

The religious practices, Sanskrit language and social institutions of the Vedic period form the historical foundation of classical Hindu culture, and Vedic texts remain authoritative in Hindu tradition as śruti ("that which is heard").
