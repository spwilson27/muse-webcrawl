The Palatinate (German: die Pfalz; Palatine German: die Palz) is a historical region of Germany. In its modern usage it denotes the southern part of the state of Rhineland-Palatinate, west of the Rhine and south of Rheinhessen, comprising about a quarter of the state. Historically, the name is associated with the Electoral Palatinate, one of the most important territories of the Holy Roman Empire.

Geography

The Palatinate is bounded by the Rhine plain to the east, the Nahe to the north, and France (Alsace) to the south. Its western half is dominated by the Palatinate Forest (Pfälzerwald), one of the largest continuous forests in Germany and, with the French Northern Vosges, a UNESCO transboundary biosphere reserve. Along the eastern edge of the hills runs the German Wine Route (Deutsche Weinstraße), the oldest tourist wine route in Germany, through the second-largest wine-growing region in the country. The mild climate supports not only vines but also almond, fig, and kiwi trees.

History

The counts palatine of the Rhine rose to prominence in the 12th and 13th centuries; by the Golden Bull of 1356 the Count Palatine was one of the seven prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire, with his residence at Heidelberg. The Electoral Palatinate adopted Calvinism in the 16th century and was devastated repeatedly in the Thirty Years' War and the wars of Louis XIV, which destroyed Heidelberg and Mannheim and drove large numbers of "Palatines" to emigrate to England, Ireland, and North America in the 18th century.

After the Napoleonic era the Palatinate west of the Rhine went to Bavaria, remaining a Bavarian district (the "Rheinpfalz") until 1946, while territories east of the Rhine went to Baden and Hesse. The Hambach Festival of 1832 at Hambach Castle near Neustadt became a landmark demonstration for German unity and democracy. In 1946 the region became part of the new state of Rhineland-Palatinate; a referendum in 1956 confirmed that it would remain so.

Culture

The region is known for its Palatine German dialects, its wine festivals — culminating in the Dürkheimer Wurstmarkt, among the world's largest wine festivals — and hearty cuisine including Saumagen, famously the favourite dish of Chancellor Helmut Kohl. Its castles, headed by Hambach Castle and Trifels, and the cities of Speyer (with its Romanesque imperial cathedral, a UNESCO site) and Kaiserslautern are major landmarks.
