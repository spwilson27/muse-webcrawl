Oirats

Regions: Western Mongolia, Xinjiang, Kalmykia (Russia), Qinghai

Languages: Oirat (Clear Script), other Mongolic varieties

Religion: Tibetan Buddhism; historically Tengrism and shamanism

Related groups: Mongols, Kalmyks

The Oirats (Mongolian: Ойрад) are the westernmost group of the Mongols, whose ancestral home is in the Altai region of western Mongolia and Dzungaria in northern Xinjiang. The name means "forest people" or, in another interpretation, "close allies." Historically the Oirats were organized as the Four Oirat confederation — the Choros (later Dzungars), Torghuts, Dörbets, and Khoshuts — and they spoke (and in places still speak) the Oirat language, written since 1648 in the Clear Script (Todo bichig) created by the monk Zaya Pandita.

History

The Oirats appear in history as forest tribes northwest of the Mongolian plateau who submitted to Genghis Khan in 1207. After the fall of the Yuan dynasty they grew in power as the chief rivals of the eastern Mongols: under Esen Taishi in the fifteenth century they briefly unified Mongolia and captured the Ming emperor at Tumu in 1449. In the seventeenth century the confederation fragmented into major migrations and states. The Torghuts migrated west to the Volga steppe, forming the Kalmyk Khanate — their descendants, the Kalmyks, are the only traditionally Buddhist ethnic group native to Europe. The Khoshuts established a khanate in the Kokonor (Qinghai) region and intervened in Tibet, while the Choros-led Dzungar Khanate became a major Central Asian empire and the last great steppe power, until it was destroyed by the Qing dynasty in the 1750s with catastrophic loss of life. Part of the Volga Kalmyks famously returned east to Dzungaria in 1771.

Modern distribution

Today Oirat-descended populations live in western Mongolia (including Dörbet, Bayad, and Torghut communities), in Xinjiang and Qinghai in China, and in the Republic of Kalmykia in Russia. Oirat dialects and the Clear Script survive, though under pressure from Khalkha Mongolian, Chinese, and Russian.
