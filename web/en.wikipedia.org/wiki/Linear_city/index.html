Linear city
ConceptUrban planning model

A linear city is an urban plan for an elongated urban formation that is proposed to consist of two parallel rows of functional zones and a central transport corridor. The city is designed to run parallel to a river or transport artery so that residents live in close proximity to both nature and their places of work, while the single spine provides efficient movement along the settlement's length.

Origins

The idea was first developed by the Spanish engineer and urban planner Arturo Soria y Mata, who in 1882 proposed the Ciudad Lineal ("Linear City") for Madrid: a narrow city about 500 metres wide stretching for tens of kilometres around a central boulevard containing roads and railways, with utilities buried beneath it. A five-kilometre section was built from 1894, with tram service and one-storey houses with gardens; most of the district was later absorbed into Madrid's conventional growth, though the Ciudad Lineal survives as a district name.

Soviet and later schemes

The concept strongly influenced Soviet planners of the 1920s and 1930s. Nikolay Milyutin's functional linear scheme organised industry, housing and green zones in parallel bands, and linear plans were drawn up for new industrial cities such as Magnitogorsk and for the rebuilding of Stalingrad. In the post-war period linear principles appeared in parts of planned cities and in proposals for linear development along motorways and coasts. In 2021 Saudi Arabia announced The Line at Neom, an extreme linear-city proposal 170 kilometres long, later reported to be scaled back; the announcement revived international discussion of the model's merits and practicality.

Criticism

Critics note that a single spine creates congestion and vulnerability (one blocked corridor can sever the city), that linear plans scale poorly beyond a certain length, and that real cities naturally develop centres and cross-connections the model suppresses. Supporters reply that linear organisation maximises access to transport and countryside and provides a rational structure for growth.
