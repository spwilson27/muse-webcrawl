Madame Roland

Madame Roland (17 March 1754 – 8 November 1793), born Jeanne-Marie (Manon) Phlipon, was a French revolutionary writer and political figure. As the wife of Jean-Marie Roland, Minister of the Interior, she hosted a Paris salon that became the gathering place of the Girondins, and her influence on the politics of the early French Revolution was considerable.

Early life

Manon Phlipon was educated largely by her own reading: Plutarch's Lives shaped her republican convictions from childhood. In 1780 she married Jean-Marie Roland, an inspector of manufactures twenty years her senior, and assisted him in his official and literary work.

Revolution

During the Revolution the Rolands' salon drew leading Girondin deputies such as Brissot and Buzot. Madame Roland drafted much of her husband's official correspondence and became a symbol of republican virtue. When the Girondins fell in June 1793 she was arrested, and after five months in prison she was tried and guillotined on 8 November 1793.

Writings and legacy

In prison she composed her memoirs, the Appel à l'impartiale postérité, a classic of revolutionary literature. According to tradition, on the scaffold she cried, "O Liberty, what crimes are committed in thy name!" Her husband, learning of her death, took his own life two days later.
