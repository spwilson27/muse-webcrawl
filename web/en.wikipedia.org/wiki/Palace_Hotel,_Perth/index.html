The Palace Hotel in Perth, Western Australia, is a landmark heritage building on St Georges Terrace, at the corner of William Street, in the Perth central business district. Completed in 1897 during the Western Australian gold boom, it was for decades the city's grandest hotel, celebrated for its lavish interiors, its domed banking-chamber-like saloon and its role in the social life of the gold rush era.

History

The hotel was built for the entrepreneur William Zimpel on the site of the earlier Freemasons' Hotel, opening in March 1897 to considerable acclaim for its ornamentation: marble, cedar and stained glass, an elaborate triple-storey façade and what was claimed to be the finest bar in the colony. It hosted politicians, mining magnates and visiting dignitaries, and survived the temperance-era alterations that stripped many Perth hotels of their verandahs and ornament.

Preservation campaign and later use

In the 1970s, redevelopment plans for the site prompted one of Western Australia's most prominent heritage campaigns. The building was ultimately retained when the site was redeveloped as the Bankwest Tower (later 108 St Georges Terrace) in the 1980s: the hotel's façades and principal interiors were preserved and converted to a banking chamber and offices, with the tower rising behind and above the retained heritage fabric. The building is listed on the State Register of Heritage Places.

Architecture

The Palace Hotel is a three-storey stuccoed brick building in the Federation Free Classical style, richly modelled with pediments, pilasters and a central cupola, and notable for the quality of its surviving interiors, including the former saloon bar with its timber and glass fittings.
