Reincarnation is the religious or philosophical belief that the soul or consciousness, after death, begins a new life in a new body. It is central to the Indian religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism — where rebirth within samsara is governed by Karma and liberation from the wheel is the ultimate goal. Versions appear in Greek thought (Pythagoras, Plato's Myth of Er), some indigenous traditions worldwide, and modern Theosophy and New Age movements.

Variations and study

Buddhist rebirth explicitly denies a permanent soul, explaining continuity by causal process rather than an entity that migrates — a subtlety often missed in popular accounts. Western researchers such as Ian Stevenson collected children's reports of past-life memories, findings mainstream science treats cautiously; meanwhile belief in reincarnation polls surprisingly high even in historically Christian countries.
