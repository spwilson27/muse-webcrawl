The Italian Football Federation (Italian: Federazione Italiana Giuoco Calcio, FIGC) is the governing body of football in Italy. Based in Rome, it organises the Italian football leagues, the Coppa Italia, and the Italy national football teams at every level, from the senior men's and women's sides down to the youth and futsal teams.

History

The federation was founded in Turin on 26 March 1898 as the Federazione Italiana Football, at a time when the game was dominated by clubs in the north-west of the country such as Genoa, Torino and Internazionale Torino. The first Italian championship was held the same year and won by Genoa. Italy was a founding member of FIFA in spirit if not in fact, joining the world body in 1905, and the national team played its first match in 1910, beating France 6–2 in Milan.

Under the FIGC, Italy became one of the most successful football nations in the world. The national team won the FIFA World Cup in 1934 and 1938 under coach Vittorio Pozzo, and again in 1982 and 2006. The federation was a founding member of UEFA in 1954. In 2006 the FIGC was embroiled in the Calciopoli scandal; commissioner Luca Pancalli oversaw reforms, and the federation later introduced financial licensing and video assistant referees in Serie A.

Competitions

The FIGC oversees Serie A, Serie B and Serie C through their respective leagues, as well as the Coppa Italia and Supercoppa Italiana. It also governs amateur football through the Lega Nazionale Dilettanti, women's football, futsal and beach soccer.

National teams

The senior men's team, the Azzurri, has won four World Cups and two European Championships (1968 and 2020). The women's team, the Azzurre, has reached the World Cup quarter-finals. Italy's youth teams have repeatedly won UEFA age-group titles.
