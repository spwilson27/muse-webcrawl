Mysore Narasimhachar Srinivas (16 November 1916 – 30 November 1999) was an Indian sociologist and social anthropologist, widely regarded as the founder of modern Indian sociology. He is best known for the concepts of Sanskritization and the dominant caste, and for his fieldwork-based studies of village India.

Life and work

Srinivas studied at the University of Bombay under G. S. Ghurye and at Oxford under A. R. Radcliffe-Brown and E. E. Evans-Pritchard. His doctoral research on the Coorgs of South India was published as Religion and Society among the Coorgs of South India (1952). While doing fieldwork in the village he called Rampura in Karnataka, his field notes were destroyed by fire; from memory he later wrote the celebrated ethnographic memoir The Remembered Village (1976).

He coined the term Sanskritization for the process by which lower castes adopt the rituals and practices of higher castes to raise their status, and dominant caste for a caste that wields preponderant economic and political power in a locality. He taught at the Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda and the University of Delhi, where he built the Delhi School of Economics' sociology department, and later co-founded the Institute for Social and Economic Change in Bangalore. He received the Padma Bhushan in 1977.
