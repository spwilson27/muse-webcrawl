Reflective listening

Reflective listening
Field: Counselling, communication
Origin: Carl Rogers's client-centered therapy
Core technique: Restating the speaker's words and feelings for confirmation

Reflective listening is a communication strategy in which the listener seeks to understand what the speaker is saying and feeling, and then offers that understanding back to the speaker — restating or paraphrasing the content and emotion — for confirmation. It is a central skill of Carl Rogers's client-centered (person-centered) therapy and of motivational interviewing.

Technique

Rather than questioning, advising, or judging, the reflective listener paraphrases the message ("So you're feeling overwhelmed because..."), reflects feelings explicitly, and uses summarising reflections to pull together several statements. Simple reflections repeat the speaker's words; complex reflections add a tentative inference about meaning or emotion, which the speaker can accept, correct, or extend. Rogers held that accurate empathic reflection, combined with unconditional positive regard, helps speakers clarify their own experience and feel genuinely understood.

Applications

Beyond psychotherapy, reflective listening is taught in motivational interviewing for behaviour change, crisis negotiation, mediation and conflict resolution, nursing and medicine, teaching, and management training. It is distinguished from mere repetition ("parroting") by its focus on meaning and affect rather than exact wording.
