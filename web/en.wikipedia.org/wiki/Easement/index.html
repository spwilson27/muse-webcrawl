Easement

An easement is a nonpossessory right to use or enter onto the real property of another without possessing it. It is best typified in the right of way which one landowner, A, may enjoy over the land of another, B.

Easement
Area: Property law
Types: Appurtenant, in gross, express, implied, prescriptive

Types

Easements may be express (by deed or will), implied by prior use or necessity, or prescriptive through long open use. An easement appurtenant benefits a dominant tenement; an easement in gross benefits a person or utility.

Creation and termination

Easements are created by grant, reservation or prescription, and can end by release, abandonment, merger or expiry. Common examples are rights of way, utility lines, drainage and conservation easements.
