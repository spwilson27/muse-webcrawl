The Language of God: A Scientist Presents Evidence for Belief is a 2006 book by the American geneticist Francis Collins, who led the Human Genome Project. In it, Collins — a former atheist who became an evangelical Christian — argues that science and Christian faith are compatible, and describes his own journey from atheism to belief.

Contents

Drawing on his experience sequencing the human genome, which he calls "the language of God", Collins presents the case for theistic evolution, which he terms "BioLogos": the view that evolution is the mechanism through which God created life. He discusses the Big Bang, the fine-tuning of physical constants, and the moral law as pointers toward a creator, while explicitly rejecting young Earth creationism and intelligent design as scientifically and theologically untenable. The book closes with an appeal for harmony between the scientific and spiritual worldviews.

Reception

The book became a New York Times bestseller and a focal point in debates about science and religion. It drew praise from many scientists and theologians, and criticism both from atheists such as Sam Harris, who faulted its arguments for faith, and from creationists who objected to its acceptance of evolution. Collins went on to found the BioLogos Foundation in 2007 to promote the dialogue the book advocated.
