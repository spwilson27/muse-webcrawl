Dale Earnhardt

Ralph Dale Earnhardt Sr. (April 29, 1951 – February 18, 2001) was an American professional stock car driver and team owner, widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers in NASCAR history. Nicknamed "The Intimidator", "The Man in Black", and "Ironhead", he raced from 1975 until his death in 2001, spending most of his career driving the No. 3 Chevrolet for Richard Childress Racing in the NASCAR Winston Cup Series.

Career

Earnhardt, the son of short-track racer Ralph Earnhardt, won the Winston Cup Rookie of the Year award in 1979 and his first Cup championship in 1980. He went on to win seven Winston Cup titles (1980, 1986, 1987, 1990, 1991, 1993, and 1994), a record he shares with Richard Petty and Jimmie Johnson. He recorded 76 Cup Series victories, including the 1998 Daytona 500 — a win that had eluded him for twenty years — and four IROC championships. His aggressive, hard-charging style and black No. 3 GM Goodwrench car made him one of the sport’s most popular and marketable figures. As an owner, he founded Dale Earnhardt, Inc., which fielded cars for his son Dale Earnhardt Jr. and others.

Death and legacy

Earnhardt was killed in a crash on the final lap of the 2001 Daytona 500, when his car was turned into the outside wall in turn four while he was defending third place for his son and teammate Michael Waltrip, who won the race. He died of a basilar skull fracture. His death was a watershed moment for NASCAR safety, leading to mandatory use of head-and-neck restraints (such as the HANS device), the installation of SAFER barriers at tracks, improved seats and harnesses, and the development of the Car of Tomorrow. Earnhardt was among the first class inducted into the NASCAR Hall of Fame in 2010.
