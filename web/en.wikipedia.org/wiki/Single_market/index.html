Single market

A single market is a type of trade bloc in which most trade barriers have been removed (for goods) with some common policies on product regulation, and freedom of movement of the factors of production (capital and labour) and of enterprise and services. The goal is that the movement of capital, labour, goods, and services between the members is as easy as within them. The physical (borders), technical (standards) and fiscal (taxes) barriers among the member states are removed to the maximum extent possible. These barriers obstruct the freedom of movement of the four factors of production.

A common market is usually referred to as the first stage towards the creation of a single market. It usually is built upon a free trade area with no tariffs for goods and relatively free movement of capital, and is often tied to the elimination of most or all non-tariff barriers as well. A single market, however, allows for deeper integration than a common market. Common rules and regulations are created so that goods, services, capital, and labour move freely for the entire area.

European single market

The best-known example is the European single market of the European Union, formally established on 1 January 1993 following the Single European Act of 1986 and the completion programme led by the European Commission under Jacques Delors. It guarantees the "four freedoms": free movement of goods, services, capital, and persons across the EU's member states, and also extends (with variations) to the European Economic Area states Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway, and partially to Switzerland through bilateral agreements.

Features

Compared with a customs union or common market, a single market typically involves:

  • Elimination of internal border controls for goods and harmonisation or mutual recognition of product standards;
  • Common competition and state-aid rules;
  • Freedom of establishment for businesses and mutual recognition of professional qualifications;
  • Removal of restrictions on cross-border provision of services, including financial services "passporting";
  • Free movement of workers and citizens' residence rights.

Other examples and proposals

The concept has informed integration projects elsewhere, including the CARICOM Single Market and Economy in the Caribbean, the Eurasian Economic Union's internal market, and proposals within the African Continental Free Trade Area. Single markets may be a step toward economic union and, ultimately, political union.
