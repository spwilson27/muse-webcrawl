Order of Culture

The Order of Culture (Japanese: Bunka-kunshō) is a Japanese order established on 11 February 1937. It is awarded by the Emperor of Japan to individuals who have made outstanding contributions to the development and advancement of Japanese culture in the fields of art, literature, scholarship and science.

Overview

The order has a single class and is conferred annually, traditionally on Culture Day (3 November). The badge, designed around paulownia flowers, is suspended from a light purple ribbon and is presented by the Emperor in person at a ceremony at the Imperial Palace in Tokyo. Recipients are selected by the Cabinet on the recommendation of the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology.

Recipients

Honourees have included painters, novelists, kabuki actors, film directors, and scientists, including several Nobel laureates. Recipients of the Order of Culture may also be designated Persons of Cultural Merit, a related honour established by law in 1951 that carries a special government pension.
