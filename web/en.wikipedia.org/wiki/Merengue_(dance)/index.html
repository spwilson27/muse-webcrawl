Merengue is a style of music and dance originating in the Dominican Republic, where it is considered the national dance. Partners dance closely in a two-step rhythm, shifting weight from foot to foot with characteristic hip movements.

Steps and style

The basic merengue step is simple: dancers march in place or travel around the floor, stepping on each beat while bending the knees so the hips sway naturally. Partners may dance in closed position, in a one- or two-handed hold, or separated, with turns and wraps built over the basic step. Its accessibility — often summarised as "one, two" — makes it a common first dance taught in Latin dance classes.

History

Merengue developed in the 19th century from a blend of European contradance figures and African rhythms. Promoted officially under the Trujillo regime in the 20th century, it spread across the Caribbean and Latin America and became a staple of international ballroom and social dancing; a faster ballroom version is also danced in competitions.
