Domed stadium

A domed stadium is a sports stadium with a roof that completely covers the playing field and spectator areas, allowing events to proceed regardless of weather. Domes may be fixed or retractable, and their roofs may be supported by air pressure, rigid frames, or cable systems.

History

The first modern domed stadium was Houston's Astrodome (1965), nicknamed the "Eighth Wonder of the World", which pioneered indoor professional baseball and football and introduced artificial turf ("AstroTurf") after its translucent roof panels had to be painted over because of glare. The Louisiana Superdome (1975) and Seattle's Kingdome (1976) followed in the United States.

Types

Air-supported domes, such as the former Metrodome in Minneapolis, hold a fabric roof up with slightly pressurized interior air. Rigid domes use steel or concrete shells. Retractable-roof stadiums — beginning with Toronto's SkyDome (now Rogers Centre, 1989) — combine weather protection with an open-air feel and a natural-grass option, and have largely supplanted fixed domes in new construction.

Issues

Domed stadiums change the character of traditionally outdoor sports: wind and rain cease to matter, artificial light replaces daylight, and acoustics become louder. Their high construction and energy costs, and the failure of the Metrodome's roof under snow in 2010, are frequent points of debate.
