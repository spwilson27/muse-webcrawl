Punched tape

Punched tape, or paper tape, is a form of data storage consisting of a long strip of paper in which holes are punched to represent characters or data. It was widely used in telegraphy, teleprinters, early computers and machine tools through much of the 20th century.

Formats

Tapes came in several widths encoding five to eight channels (bits) per row, with a smaller feed-hole track for sprocket transport. Five-hole tape served Baudot/Murray telegraph codes, while eight-hole ASCII tape became standard for computing and numerically controlled machines. Mylar tape offered greater durability.

Decline

Paper tape was largely displaced by magnetic and then semiconductor storage by the 1980s, though it persisted in some industrial and telecommunications niches.
