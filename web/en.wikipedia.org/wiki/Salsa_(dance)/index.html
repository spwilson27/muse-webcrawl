Salsa (dance)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Salsa is a Latin dance, associated with salsa music, that originated in the Caribbean and among Hispanic communities in New York City in the mid-20th century. It is a partner dance with roots in Cuban son, mambo, cha-cha-chá, and Puerto Rican and other Afro-Caribbean traditions.

Basic steps

Salsa is danced to music in 4/4 time. The basic step involves three steps over four beats—steps on beats 1, 2, 3, holding on 4, then steps on 5, 6, 7, holding on 8 (in eight-beat phrasing)—with a characteristic forward-and-back motion. Partners dance in an open or closed hold, exchanging turns, cross-body leads, and intricate hand changes, often improvising around the clave rhythm of the music.

Styles

Major regional styles include:

  • New York style ("on 2") — dancers break on the second beat; associated with Eddie Torres and the Palladium era.
  • Los Angeles style ("on 1") — break on the first beat, with showy dips and tricks.
  • Cuban style (casino) — circular partner movement with group rueda de casino formations.
  • Colombian (Cali) style — fast footwork and acrobatic figures.

History

The dance evolved in the ballrooms and clubs of New York in the 1950s–1970s, where the Palladium Ballroom's mambo dancers mixed with the Fania Records salsa scene. The name "salsa" ("sauce") marketed the spicy blend of Caribbean rhythms. From the 1980s onward, international congresses, schools, and social-dance scenes spread salsa worldwide.
