Penrose process

Proposed1969, by Roger Penrose
Applies toRotating (Kerr) black holes
Region usedErgosphere
EffectExtraction of rotational energy

The Penrose process (also called the Penrose mechanism) is a theoretical process, proposed by physicist Roger Penrose in 1969, by which energy can be extracted from a rotating black hole.

Mechanism

A rotating (Kerr) black hole is surrounded by an ergosphere, a region in which spacetime itself is dragged around so strongly that no object can remain stationary. Penrose showed that an object entering the ergosphere could split in two: one fragment falls into the black hole on a trajectory with negative energy relative to a distant observer — possible only inside the ergosphere — while the other escapes carrying more energy than the original object brought in. The black hole loses a corresponding amount of rotational energy and angular momentum.

Significance

The process demonstrates that up to about 29 percent of a maximally rotating black hole's mass-energy is in principle extractable. Related ideas, such as the Blandford–Znajek process, are invoked to explain the powerful jets of active galactic nuclei, though the original particle-splitting mechanism requires implausibly high relative velocities and is not thought to operate directly in nature.
