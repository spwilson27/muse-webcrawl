Johnny Got His Gun

Johnny Got His Gun is an anti-war novel by American author and screenwriter Dalton Trumbo, published in 1939. It tells the story of Joe Bonham, a young American soldier who wakes in a hospital bed after being struck by an artillery shell in the First World War, having lost his limbs, eyes, ears, mouth and nose, yet retaining full consciousness.

Plot

Trapped inside his own mind, Joe drifts between memory and hallucination as he comes to understand the extent of his injuries. He eventually learns to communicate by tapping his head in Morse code, and asks to be displayed publicly as a warning against war — a request the military refuses, leaving him imprisoned in his body.

Reception and adaptations

The novel won a National Book Award and became a classic of anti-war literature, though Trumbo, a pacifist when it was written, later cooperated in suppressing it during the Second World War and the Korean War. Trumbo adapted and directed a film version in 1971. The Metallica song "One" (1988) and its video drew directly on the novel and film.
