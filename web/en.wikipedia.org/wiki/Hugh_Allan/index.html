Sir Hugh Allan (29 September 1810 – 9 December 1882) was a Scottish-Canadian shipping magnate, financier and railway promoter. He founded the Montreal Ocean Steamship Company — better known as the Allan Line — which dominated transatlantic emigrant and mail traffic between Britain and Canada, and built one of the largest private fortunes in 19th-century Canada. His name is inseparable from the Pacific Scandal of 1873, which brought down the government of Sir John A. Macdonald.

Early life and the Allan Line

Allan was born at Saltcoats, Scotland, the second son of Captain Alexander Allan, founder of the Glasgow shipping firm of Allan and Co. In 1826 he sailed to Montreal, where he worked as a clerk before joining his family's Canadian agency. By the 1850s he controlled the Allan shipping interests in Canada. In 1856, with his brother Andrew, he established a regular steamship service between Liverpool, Quebec and Montreal subsidized by the Canadian government's mail contract; the Allan Line carried hundreds of thousands of emigrants to Canada and was famous for fast Royal Mail steamers such as the Canadian and Indian. He also built the Ravenscrag mansion (1861) in Montreal's Golden Square Mile.

Railways and the Pacific Scandal

Allan invested in railways (including the Montreal Northern Colonization Railway), banks — he was president of the Merchants' Bank of Canada — telegraph and manufacturing. In 1872 his syndicate, the Canada Pacific Railway Company, was awarded the charter to build Canada's transcontinental railway. It emerged during the 1873 election-funding revelations that Allan and his American backers had contributed some $350,000 to the Conservative Party's 1872 election fund in expectation of the contract; telegrams in Allan's own hand ("I must have another ten thousand") were published. The scandal forced Macdonald's resignation in November 1873 and destroyed Allan's hope of building the railway, though he faced no criminal charges.

Honours and death

Allan was knighted by Queen Victoria in 1871. He died in Edinburgh in December 1882 while visiting Scotland, and was buried in Mount Royal Cemetery, Montreal. The Allan Line was absorbed by Canadian Pacific Steamships in 1915–17, and Ravenscrag later became part of McGill University's Allan Memorial Institute.
