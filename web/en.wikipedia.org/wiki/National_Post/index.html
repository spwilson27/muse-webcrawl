National Post

The National Post is a Canadian English-language broadsheet newspaper published in Toronto, Ontario. Founded in 1998 by media magnate Conrad Black, it was intended as a conservative national newspaper to rival the Globe and Mail. It is owned by Postmedia Network, which publishes numerous other Canadian dailies.

History

Black conceived the paper partly in conflict with existing Canadian newspaper owners and partly to provide a right-of-centre voice in national affairs. The National Post launched on 27 October 1998, absorbing the Financial Post business section, whose name is still used for the paper's finance coverage. Early editors included Ken Whyte. The paper struggled financially and was sold fully to Canwest Global Communications in 2001; following Canwest's bankruptcy, it passed to Postmedia in 2010.

Editorial stance and content

The National Post is generally conservative in its editorial positions, supporting free markets and smaller government, with columnists drawn largely from the Canadian right. It publishes national and international news, the Financial Post business section, sports, and arts coverage, and in 2017 ended its Monday print edition, later reducing print publication further while expanding digital content.
