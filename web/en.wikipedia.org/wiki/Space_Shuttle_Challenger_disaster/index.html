Space Shuttle Challenger disaster

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Space Shuttle Challenger breaking apart 73 seconds after launch, with branching white smoke trails against a blue sky]
Challenger breaks up, 28 January 1986.

On 28 January 1986 the Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after liftoff from Kennedy Space Center, killing all seven crew members: commander Dick Scobee, pilot Michael Smith, mission specialists Ellison Onizuka, Judith Resnik and Ronald McNair, payload specialist Gregory Jarvis, and Christa McAuliffe, a schoolteacher selected as the first participant in the Teacher in Space project.

The disaster was caused by the failure of O-ring seals in the right solid rocket booster, whose rubber had lost resilience in the unusually cold weather that morning — engineers at contractor Morton Thiokol had warned against launching in such cold, but were overruled. Hot gas burned through the booster joint, and the external tank collapsed, tearing the orbiter apart in the resulting aerodynamic forces; the crew cabin survived intact until ocean impact.

Aftermath

The Rogers Commission, whose members included Neil Armstrong and Sally Ride, exposed deep flaws in NASA's decision-making and safety culture. Physicist Richard Feynman's televised demonstration — dropping a piece of O-ring rubber in ice water to show it lost elasticity — became famous. The shuttle fleet was grounded for nearly three years, and the boosters were redesigned with a third O-ring and joint heaters.
