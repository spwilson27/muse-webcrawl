The Well-Tempered Clavier

ComposerJohann Sebastian Bach
CatalogueBWV 846–893
ComposedBook 1: 1722; Book 2: c. 1738–1742
FormTwo sets of preludes and fugues in all 24 major and minor keys

The Well-Tempered Clavier (German: Das Wohltemperierte Klavier), BWV 846–893, is two sets of preludes and fugues in all 24 major and minor keys for keyboard by Johann Sebastian Bach. In the German of the period, Klavier could denote a variety of keyboard instruments, including harpsichord, clavichord and organ.

Contents

Each set contains twenty-four prelude-and-fugue pairs, ascending chromatically from C major and C minor through every major and minor key. Book 1 was compiled in 1722, "for the profit and use of musical youth desirous of learning, and especially for the pastime of those already skilled in this study", in Bach's words. Book 2 was compiled around 1738–1742. The collection demonstrates the practical advantages of a well temperament — a tuning system in which all keys are usable, though not necessarily equal temperament.

Legacy

Circulating in manuscript throughout the 18th century, the collection profoundly influenced composers from Mozart and Beethoven to Chopin and Shostakovich, whose own sets of 24 preludes and fugues were modelled on it. Hans von Bülow called the work the "Old Testament" of piano literature (Beethoven's sonatas being the "New Testament"). The Prelude in C major that opens Book 1, with its unbroken arpeggios, is among the best-known pieces of classical music; Charles Gounod built his Ave Maria upon it.
