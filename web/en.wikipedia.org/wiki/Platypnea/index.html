Platypnea

Platypnea is shortness of breath (dyspnea) that is relieved when lying down and worsens when sitting or standing upright. It is the opposite of orthopnea, the breathlessness on lying flat characteristic of heart failure. Platypnea is usually accompanied by orthodeoxia — a fall in arterial oxygen saturation on assuming the upright posture — and the combination is called platypnea–orthodeoxia syndrome. The word comes from the Greek platy ("flat") and pnoia ("breath").

Causes

Platypnea arises when the upright posture increases right-to-left shunting of blood or worsens ventilation–perfusion matching. Recognised causes fall into three groups:

  • Intracardiac shunts — most classically a patent foramen ovale or atrial septal defect, especially after pneumonectomy, with aortic dilatation, or with pericardial disease, where standing directs inferior vena cava flow across the atrial septum.
  • Pulmonary vascular shunts — hepatopulmonary syndrome in advanced liver disease, in which dilated vessels at the lung bases shunt blood when upright, and pulmonary arteriovenous malformations, as in hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia.
  • Ventilation–perfusion mismatch — diseases affecting the lung bases, such as emphysema or basal fibrosis, in which gravity increases blood flow to the worst-ventilated regions when standing.

Diagnosis and treatment

The key investigation is measurement of oxygen saturation or arterial blood gases first supine and then upright: a drop in saturation of more than about 5% (or PaO2 by more than 4 mmHg) on standing defines orthodeoxia. Contrast ("bubble") echocardiography, sometimes performed upright, can demonstrate an intracardiac or intrapulmonary shunt, supplemented by CT or pulmonary angiography. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: percutaneous closure of a patent foramen ovale or septal defect can abolish the symptom, embolisation can treat arteriovenous malformations, and in hepatopulmonary syndrome liver transplantation is definitive.
