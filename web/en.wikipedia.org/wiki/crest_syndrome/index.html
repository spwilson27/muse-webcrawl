CREST syndrome

CREST syndrome, also known as the limited cutaneous form of systemic sclerosis (lcSSc), is a multisystem connective tissue disorder. The acronym "CREST" refers to the five main features: calcinosis, Raynaud's phenomenon, esophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, and telangiectasia.

SpecialtyRheumatology
SymptomsCalcium deposits, Raynaud's phenomenon, esophageal problems, thickened finger skin, small dilated blood vessels
CausesAutoimmune
TreatmentSymptomatic

Signs and symptoms

Calcinosis is the formation of calcium deposits in the skin, most commonly on the fingers, hands, face, and trunk. Raynaud's phenomenon causes the fingers and toes to turn white or blue in response to cold or stress. Esophageal dysmotility can cause food to feel stuck in the mid or lower chest and may lead to gastroesophageal reflux. Sclerodactyly is the thickening and tightening of the skin of the fingers and toes. Telangiectasias are small dilated blood vessels visible on the face and hands.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is generally made from the clinical features, supported by blood tests for specific autoantibodies, particularly anticentromere antibodies, which are strongly associated with the limited form of the disease.

Treatment

There is no cure; treatment is directed at the individual symptoms. Calcium channel blockers may help Raynaud's phenomenon, proton pump inhibitors are used for reflux, and immunosuppressants may be used in some cases.
