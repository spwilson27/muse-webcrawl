Sulforaphane

Sulforaphane
IUPAC name1-Isothiocyanato-4-(methanesulfinyl)butane
FormulaC6H11NOS2
PrecursorGlucoraphanin
SourcesBroccoli (esp. sprouts), cabbage, kale, Brussels sprouts

Sulforaphane is an organosulfur compound of the isothiocyanate group, obtained from cruciferous vegetables such as broccoli, Brussels sprouts, and cabbage. It is formed when the enzyme myrosinase converts the glucosinolate glucoraphanin upon damage to the plant, as by chewing or chopping. Young broccoli sprouts contain especially high concentrations of glucoraphanin.

Biological activity

Sulforaphane is a potent activator of the Nrf2 signalling pathway, inducing phase II detoxification enzymes such as glutathione S-transferases and NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1. It has been widely studied in laboratory and animal models for possible anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial effects, including activity against Helicobacter pylori. Clinical research has examined broccoli sprout preparations in conditions ranging from autism spectrum disorder to prostate cancer recurrence and detoxification of air pollutants; results are preliminary and no therapeutic use has been established.
