The M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System (M270 MLRS) is an American self-propelled multiple launch rocket system developed in the late 1970s and fielded from 1983. Mounted on a tracked chassis derived from the Bradley Fighting Vehicle, it carries two launcher pods, each holding six 227 mm rockets or one ATACMS tactical ballistic missile, giving a full load of twelve rockets or two missiles.

Development

The system grew out of a US Army requirement in the 1970s for a general-support rocket artillery weapon able to deliver large volumes of fire rapidly and then displace before counter-battery fire could respond. Vought (later Lockheed Martin) won the competition, and the M270 entered service in 1983. Its rockets originally carried the M77 dual-purpose submunition warhead; later guided variants, the GMLRS family, added GPS/inertial guidance for precision strikes at ranges beyond 70 km, with extended-range versions reaching roughly 150 km.

Design

The three-person crew operates from an armoured cab. The launcher is aimed and fired from within the cab using a computerised fire-control system, and a full ripple of twelve rockets can be fired in under a minute. Reloading uses an integral hoist to exchange the two pods in a matter of minutes. The tracked chassis provides cross-country mobility comparable to the armoured formations the system supports.

Operational history

The M270 saw its combat debut in the 1991 Gulf War, where Iraqi troops reportedly nicknamed its submunition salvos "steel rain". It has since been used in Iraq and Afghanistan, and systems donated by the United States, United Kingdom, Germany and others have been employed by Ukraine following the 2022 Russian invasion. Wheeled HIMARS launchers fire the same rocket and missile families from a lighter truck chassis.

Operators

Operators have included the United States, the United Kingdom (where it is known as the M270B1), Germany, France, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Israel and several other nations.
