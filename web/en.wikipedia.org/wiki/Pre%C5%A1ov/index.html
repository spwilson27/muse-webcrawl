Prešov (Hungarian: Eperjes, German: Eperies or Preschau) is a city in eastern Slovakia. It is the seat of the Prešov Region and, with a population of about 84,000, the third-largest city in the country. The city lies in the Košice Basin at the confluence of the Torysa and Sekčov rivers.

History

First mentioned in 1247, Prešov received royal free town privileges in 1374 and prospered as a centre of trade in salt, wine and linen. It was a stronghold of the Reformation in Upper Hungary and the site of the 1687 "Prešov butchery," when Imperial authorities executed Protestant burghers. The historic centre preserves the elongated main street (Hlavná ulica) with the Gothic Church of St. Nicholas, the Neptune fountain, and burgher houses from the Renaissance and Baroque periods.

Culture and education

Prešov is home to the University of Prešov, founded in 1997, and is a centre of Slovak Greek Catholic life, housing the seat of the Archeparchy of Prešov as well as an Orthodox theological faculty. The Solivary salt works at nearby Solivar, now a technical museum, document the city's historic salt industry. Football club 1. FC Tatran Prešov, founded in 1898, is among the oldest in Slovakia.
