Cambridgeshire is a ceremonial county in the East of England. It borders Lincolnshire to the north, Norfolk to the north-east, Suffolk to the east, Essex and Hertfordshire to the south, and Bedfordshire and Northamptonshire to the west. The county town is the city of Cambridge, famous for its university. The modern ceremonial county includes the unitary authority of Peterborough, and the historic county also once included the Isle of Ely.

Geography

Much of the northern and eastern part of the county lies within the Fens, a low-lying marshy region largely below sea level that was drained in the 17th–19th centuries and is now some of the most fertile farmland in England. The principal rivers are the Great Ouse, the Nene and the Cam. The south of the county is gently rolling chalk and clay country, including part of the Gog Magog Hills near Cambridge.

History

The area was settled in Roman times and later formed part of the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of East Anglia. The University of Cambridge was founded in 1209. In 1974 the historic county was merged with the Isle of Ely and parts of neighbouring counties to form the modern administrative county; Peterborough became a unitary authority in 1998 but remains in the ceremonial county.

Settlements

The largest settlements are Peterborough and Cambridge, followed by Wisbech, St Neots, Ely, Huntingdon, March, Whittlesey, Chatteris and St Ives. Ely is a small cathedral city on a former "isle" of higher ground in the Fens.

Economy

Agriculture dominates the Fens, while Cambridge and its surroundings host a major high-technology cluster known as "Silicon Fen", centred on software, electronics and biotechnology, together with the university, publishing and tourism.
