U2

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: U2 performing in a stadium: Bono at the microphone stand, the Edge's guitar chiming, the vast screen behind them glowing]

U2 are an Irish rock band formed in Dublin in 1976, comprising Bono (lead vocals), the Edge (guitar, keyboards), Adam Clayton (bass) and Larry Mullen Jr. (drums) — a lineup unbroken since school. From post-punk beginnings they became one of the world's biggest bands: The Joshua Tree (1987) and Achtung Baby (1991) are their twin peaks, and their live shows set the standard for stadium spectacle.

Early albums (Boy, 1980; War, 1983, with 'Sunday Bloody Sunday') built a reputation for earnest, anthemic intensity; the live album and film Under a Blood Red Sky and Rattle and Hum (1983, 1988) mark the first era's close. The Joshua Tree — 'With or Without You', 'I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For', 'Where the Streets Have No Name' — made them the biggest band in the world from a small country, and its themes (America, faith, loss) their permanent territory. Song titles cited only.

Reinvention and later career

Achtung Baby and the Zoo TV tour (1991–93) answered earnestness with irony, glam and media overload — a reinvention few stadium acts have matched; further experiments (the Passengers project, Pop, 1997) met mixed fates. The 2000s restored anthemic form (All That You Can't Leave Behind) and live supremacy: the 360° Tour (2009–11) was the highest-grossing tour in history at its close, and their 2023–24 Sphere residency in Las Vegas inaugurated a new era of venue technology. Bono's activism (debt relief, AIDS) and the band's tax affairs draw commensurate praise and scrutiny in Ireland and beyond.

Honours

U2 have won 22 Grammy Awards — the most by a band — entered the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2005 (inducted by Bruce Springsteen), and received Amnesty International's Ambassador of Conscience Award. Their catalogue is controlled as fiercely as their brand: the unsolicited 2014 iTunes release of Songs of Innocence into every user's library remains a landmark of marketing overreach.
