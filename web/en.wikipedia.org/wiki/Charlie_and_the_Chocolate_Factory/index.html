Charlie and the Chocolate Factory is a 1964 children's novel by British author Roald Dahl. It tells the story of Charlie Bucket, a poor but kind-hearted boy who wins a golden ticket to tour the magical chocolate factory of the eccentric chocolatier Willy Wonka, along with four far less pleasant children who come to comically sticky ends. It is one of the best-loved and best-selling children's books of all time.

Plot

Charlie Bucket lives in poverty with his parents and four grandparents in a small house overshadowed by Wonka's enormous factory. When Willy Wonka announces that five Golden Tickets have been hidden in his chocolate bars, Charlie miraculously finds the last one. Accompanied by Grandpa Joe, he tours the factory's wonders — the chocolate river, the nut room, the inventing room — staffed by the diminutive Oompa-Loompas. One by one the other children — gluttonous Augustus Gloop, spoilt Veruca Salt, gum-chewing Violet Beauregarde and television-obsessed Mike Teavee — are eliminated by their own vices, each departure marked by an Oompa-Loompa song. Charlie alone remains, and Wonka reveals the prize: the factory itself, which Charlie will inherit.

Background and reception

Dahl drew on his schooldays near the Cadbury factory and on chocolate companies' real-life rivalry and spying. The book was first published in the United States in 1964 and in Britain in 1967. It was followed by the sequel Charlie and the Great Glass Elevator (1972). Over the years some elements — notably the original depiction of the Oompa-Loompas as African pygmies — were revised by Dahl in later editions.

Adaptations

The novel has been filmed twice: as Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory (1971), starring Gene Wilder, and as Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (2005), directed by Tim Burton and starring Johnny Depp. A stage musical premiered in London's West End in 2013, and the prequel film Wonka (2023), starring Timothée Chalamet, imagined Wonka's early life.
