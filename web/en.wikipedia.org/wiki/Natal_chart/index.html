Natal chart

A natal chart — also called a birth chart or nativity — is a map of the positions of the Sun, Moon, planets and other astrological points at the exact moment and place of a person's birth. In Western astrology the chart is the foundation of the horoscope, and astrologers interpret it as a symbolic picture of the individual's character, tendencies and life pattern. The scientific consensus is that astrology is a pseudoscience, and controlled tests have not found natal-chart interpretations to perform above chance.

Components

The chart is drawn as a circle divided into twelve signs of the zodiac and twelve houses. The Ascendant (rising sign) is the sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth and anchors the house system; the Midheaven marks the sign culminating overhead. The Sun, Moon and planets are plotted by sign and house, and geometric angles between them — conjunctions, sextiles, squares, trines and oppositions — are counted as "aspects" and interpreted as harmonious or challenging relationships between the chart factors they connect.

Interpretation

Astrologers read the chart holistically: the Sun sign is taken to indicate core identity, the Moon sign emotional nature, and the Ascendant outward manner. Planets in signs and houses, and the aspects between them, are combined into a narrative reading. Different schools vary their methods: tropical and sidereal zodiacs place the signs differently, and several house systems (Placidus, Whole Sign, Equal House and others) are in use. Traditions outside the West, such as Vedic astrology (Jyotisha) and Chinese astrology, use different charts and cycles for the same purpose.

History

Personal natal astrology arose in Hellenistic Egypt around the 2nd century BC, systematised in works such as Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos, and was practised through the Islamic world and medieval Europe. The precise birth-time recording needed for house placement became common only with the spread of clocks and birth registration. In the 20th century, psychological astrology reframed the chart as a tool of personality description, and newspaper "star sign" columns reduced the practice for most readers to the Sun sign alone.
