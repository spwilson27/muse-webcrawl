Learning management system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A learning management system (LMS) is a software application for the administration, documentation, tracking, reporting, automation, and delivery of educational courses, training programs, materials, or learning and development programs. The learning management system concept emerged directly from e-learning.

Features

Common LMS features include course content delivery, student registration and enrolment, assignment submission, quizzes and assessments, gradebooks, discussion forums, calendars, and reporting on learner progress and completion. Systems used in corporate training emphasize compliance tracking and skills management, while those in higher education support blended and fully online courses. Interoperability standards such as SCORM, xAPI (Experience API), and LTI allow content and tools from different vendors to work within one system.

Examples

Widely used systems include the open-source Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard Learn, D2L Brightspace, Schoology, and Google Classroom. During the COVID-19 pandemic, learning management systems became essential infrastructure for schools and universities teaching remotely.

History

The first LMSs appeared in the 1990s alongside the growth of the World Wide Web, evolving from earlier computer-based training and PLATO systems of the 1960s–70s. By the 2010s, cloud-hosted systems had largely replaced locally installed software in education and business.
