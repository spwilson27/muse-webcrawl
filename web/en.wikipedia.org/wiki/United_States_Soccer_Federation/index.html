United States Soccer Federation

The United States Soccer Federation (USSF), commonly referred to as U.S. Soccer, is the official governing body of the sport of soccer in the United States. Founded in 1913 as the United States Football Association, it is a member of FIFA and of CONCACAF. Headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, the federation administers the men's and women's national teams, sanctions professional and amateur leagues, and oversees referee and coaching development.

History

The federation was founded on April 5, 1913, as the United States Football Association and was granted provisional FIFA membership the same year, becoming one of FIFA's earliest members outside Europe. It organized the first U.S. Open Cup in 1914. The United States finished third at the inaugural FIFA World Cup in 1930, still its best men's result. In 1945 the word "Soccer" was added to the name, and in 1974 the organization became the United States Soccer Federation.

National teams

USSF fields national teams at multiple levels. The women's national team is the most successful in the world, winning four FIFA Women's World Cups (1991, 1999, 2015, 2019) and multiple Olympic gold medals. The men's national team has appeared in eleven World Cups and famously defeated England at the 1950 tournament. The federation also oversees futsal, beach soccer, and para national teams.

Domestic competitions

The federation sanctions the professional league pyramid, including Major League Soccer (Division I men's), the National Women's Soccer League (Division I women's), the USL Championship and MLS Next Pro, and organizes the Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup, the oldest ongoing national soccer competition in the United States.
