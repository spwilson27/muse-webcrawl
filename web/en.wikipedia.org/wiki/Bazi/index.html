Bazi

Bazi (Chinese: 八字; pinyin: bāzì; Wade–Giles: pa-tzu), also known as the Four Pillars of Destiny (四柱命理; sìzhù mìnglǐ), is a Chinese astrological and calendrical system of fortune-telling based on a person's date and time of birth. The name refers to the eight Chinese characters — two for each of the four "pillars" of year, month, day and hour — derived from the sexagenary cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches. Bazi analysis interprets the balance and interaction of the Five Elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, water) in the chart to characterise a person's destiny, personality, career, relationships and periods of fortune.

Method

Each pillar pairs a Heavenly Stem (representing yin/yang and an element) with an Earthly Branch (associated with a zodiac animal and hidden stems). The day pillar's stem — the "Day Master" — represents the self; the practitioner evaluates its strength and the chart's elemental balance, then maps "Ten Gods" relationships between the Day Master and the other characters. Luck is read through ten-year "luck pillars" and annual cycles that interact with the natal chart. Compatibility of the eight characters between partners (hehun) is traditionally checked before marriage.

History

Precursors of birth-data divination appear in Tang-dynasty methods associated with Li Xuzhong, who used year, month and day. The mature four-pillar system, using the hour as well and centred on the day stem, was systematised in the Song dynasty by Xu Ziping, giving the method its alternative name, Ziping Bazi. Classic Ming and Qing commentaries, such as the Sanming Tonghui and Qiongtong Baojian, remain standard references, and Bazi is still widely consulted in Chinese communities worldwide.
