Americana (music)

Americana
Stylistic originsCountry, folk, bluegrass, blues, rock and roll, R&B, roots rock
Cultural origins1990s, United States (term); roots in 20th-century American vernacular music
Typical instrumentsAcoustic guitar, fiddle, banjo, mandolin, dobro, upright bass, pedal steel

Americana is an amalgam of American roots music formed by the confluence of the shared and varied traditions that make up the musical ethos of the United States, specifically merged from folk, country, bluegrass, blues, rhythm and blues, rock and roll, and other external influences. The Americana Music Association defines it as contemporary music that incorporates elements of various American roots music styles, resulting in a distinctive roots-oriented sound.

History

The term gained currency as a radio format in the mid-1990s, drawing on the alternative country movement and artists who fell outside mainstream country and rock categories. The Americana Music Association was founded in 1999 and held its first Americana Music Festival and Conference in Nashville. In 2010 the Grammy Awards added a Best Americana Album category, and in 2016 Billboard launched an Americana/Folk albums chart.

Artists

Artists associated with Americana include Emmylou Harris, Lucinda Williams, Steve Earle, Gillian Welch, Ryan Adams, Jason Isbell, Brandi Carlile, the Avett Brothers, Mumford & Sons, and Tyler Childers, alongside foundational influences such as Bob Dylan, The Band, Gram Parsons, and Johnny Cash.
