The North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) is a United States and Canadian bi-national organization charged with the missions of aerospace warning, aerospace control and maritime warning for North America. Aerospace warning includes the detection, validation and warning of attacks against the continent by aircraft, missiles or space vehicles.

NORAD continuously monitors North American airspace.

History

NORAD was established during the Cold War, with its agreement signed in 1958, amid fears of Soviet bomber attacks over the Arctic. Its famous underground combat operations center was built inside Cheyenne Mountain in Colorado in the 1960s. Radar lines such as the DEW Line watched the far north, and NORAD's warning systems were central to nuclear deterrence strategy.

Missions today

After the September 11 attacks, NORAD's air sovereignty mission expanded under Operation Noble Eagle, with fighters on alert to intercept aircraft within North America. NORAD also operates missile-warning and space-surveillance sensors in support of United States Space Command. The commander of NORAD is simultaneously the commander of United States Northern Command.

NORAD Tracks Santa

Each Christmas Eve since 1955, NORAD has run the "NORAD Tracks Santa" program, reporting the location of Santa Claus's sleigh — a tradition that began with a misprinted telephone number in a department-store advertisement and became a beloved public outreach activity.
