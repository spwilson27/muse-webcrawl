Major Occultation

The Major Occultation (al-Ghaybah al-Kubrā), in Twelver Shia Islam, is the period that began in 329 AH (941 CE) and continues to the present day, during which the twelfth and final Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed to be alive but hidden from the world, and will remain so until he returns at the end of time to establish justice.

Background

The Major Occultation followed the Minor Occultation (874–941 CE), during which the hidden Imam was said to communicate with his followers through four successive deputies (the Four Gates, or Abwāb). Shortly before the death of the fourth deputy, Ali ibn Muhammad al-Samarri, in 941, a signed letter (tawqīʿ) attributed to the Imam announced that the period of deputyship had ended: there would be no further special deputy, and anyone claiming to see or represent the Imam before his return would be a liar.

Consequences

During the Imam's absence, religious guidance falls to qualified jurists (mujtahids), a doctrine later elaborated into the theory of marjaʿiyya (following a source of emulation) and, in the Islamic Republic of Iran, the doctrine of wilāyat al-faqīh (guardianship of the jurist). Believers are encouraged to pray for the hastening of the Mahdi's reappearance (ẓuhūr), and the era of waiting (intiẓār) is a central theme of Twelver devotion, commemorated especially on Mid-Sha'ban, the Imam's birthday.
