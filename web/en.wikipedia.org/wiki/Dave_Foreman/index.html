Dave Foreman

Dave Foreman (18 October 1946 – 19 September 2022) was an American environmental activist and author. He co-founded the radical environmental movement Earth First! in 1980 and is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in the modern wilderness preservation movement.

Foreman advocateded large-scale wilderness protection and rewilding in North America.

Early life and career

Born in Albuquerque, New Mexico, Foreman studied history at the University of New Mexico. After early work with conservative and Republican campaigns, he became a lobbyist in Washington, D.C. for The Wilderness Society during the 1970s, representing the organization during the RARE II roadless-area review. Disillusioned with the compromises of mainstream environmentalism, he left Washington in 1980.

Earth First!

With several colleagues, Foreman founded Earth First! in the southwestern United States, adopting the motto "No compromise in defense of Mother Earth!" The movement promoted direct action and civil disobedience in defense of wilderness, and Foreman edited its journal through the 1980s. His book Ecodefense: A Field Guide to Monkeywrenching (1985) became a controversial manual of sabotage techniques. In 1989 he was arrested by the FBI in a case involving an alleged plot against power lines; he ultimately pleaded guilty to a reduced conspiracy charge.

Later work

Foreman later distanced himself from confrontation and founded the Wildlands Project (now the Wildlands Network) to promote continental-scale networks of protected areas, and the Rewilding Institute. His later books include Rewilding North America (2004) and Man Swarm and the Killing of Wildlife (2011), which addressed human population growth. He died in Albuquerque in 2022.
