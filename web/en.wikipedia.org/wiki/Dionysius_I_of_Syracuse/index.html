Dionysius I of Syracuse

Reign405 – 367 BC
Bornc. 432 BC, Syracuse
Died367 BC, Syracuse
SuccessorDionysius II

Dionysius I (Greek: Διονύσιος; c. 432 – 367 BC), also known as Dionysius the Elder, was a tyrant of Syracuse in Sicily who ruled from 405 BC until his death. He made Syracuse the most powerful Greek city in the western Mediterranean and waged long wars against Carthage.

Rise to power

Dionysius rose as a military commander during the Carthaginian invasion of Sicily. Elected general with extraordinary powers in 405 BC amid the failures of the Syracusan democracy, he seized control of the city, fortified the island of Ortygia as his stronghold, and ruled as tyrant.

Wars with Carthage

Dionysius fought four wars against Carthage for control of Sicily, with mixed success; he conquered much of the island's east and campaigned in southern Italy, capturing Rhegium in 386 BC. His court supported military innovation, and tradition credits his engineers with early forms of the catapult.

Court and reputation

A patron of literature who fancied himself a tragic poet, Dionysius hosted figures such as Plato, whose visits to Syracuse ended unhappily, and the courtier Damocles, whose story of the sword suspended by a hair became proverbial. Later writers made Dionysius an archetype of the suspicious, cruel tyrant, though he kept power for 38 years and died in office, succeeded by his son Dionysius II.
