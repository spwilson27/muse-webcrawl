Operation Nachshon (Hebrew: מבצע נחשון, Mivtsa Nachshon) was a military operation of the Haganah during the 1947–48 Civil War in Mandatory Palestine, carried out in April 1948. Its goal was to open the blockaded road from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem and deliver supplies to the besieged Jewish population of Jerusalem. It was the first operation of Plan Dalet and the first in which the Haganah operated in brigade strength. The operation was named after the biblical figure Nahshon, who, according to tradition, was the first to enter the Red Sea during the Exodus.

Background

By early 1948 Arab irregular forces had cut the main Tel Aviv–Jerusalem road, particularly around the Bab el-Wad (Sha'ar HaGai) gorge, and Jerusalem's roughly 100,000 Jewish residents faced shortages of food and supplies. Arms recently arrived from Czechoslovakia allowed the Haganah to mount a larger offensive effort.

The operation

From 5 April 1948, some 1,500 troops — drawn from the Palmach's Harel Brigade and the Givati Brigade, among others — attacked Arab positions controlling the corridor, capturing villages including al-Qastal, whose commander Abd al-Qadir al-Husayni was killed on 8 April during fighting to retake it. Between 9 and 16 April, three large convoys of hundreds of trucks carried supplies into Jerusalem. During the same period, on 9 April, Irgun and Lehi fighters attacked the village of Deir Yassin, where more than one hundred villagers were killed, an event that became one of the most controversial of the war.

Aftermath

Although the convoys relieved Jerusalem temporarily, Arab forces retook key positions after the Haganah brigades were withdrawn, and the road was again cut; Jerusalem remained besieged until an improvised bypass, the "Burma Road", was opened in June 1948. Nevertheless, Nachshon marked a turning point: the Haganah shifted from defensive to offensive operations in the final weeks of the Mandate.
