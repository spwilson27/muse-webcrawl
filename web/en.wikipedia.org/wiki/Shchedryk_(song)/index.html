Shchedryk (song)

"Shchedryk" (Ukrainian: «Щедрик») is a Ukrainian New Year carol, or shchedrivka, arranged by the composer Mykola Leontovych in 1916. Its melody — a simple four-note ostinato — underlies the famous English-language Christmas song "Carol of the Bells", with lyrics written by Peter J. Wilhousky in 1936.

Origins

The song descends from a traditional Ukrainian folk chant sung at the New Year, which in pre-Christian times fell in spring; its text tells of a swallow flying into a household to sing of the prosperity the family will enjoy in the coming year — livestock, crops and a loving wife. Leontovych worked on his choral arrangement from 1901 to 1919, producing several versions. It was premiered in Kyiv in December 1916 by the Kyiv University choir.

Spread and fame

The Ukrainian Republic Capella under Oleksander Koshetz toured Europe and the Americas after the First World War to promote Ukrainian culture; their performance of "Shchedryk" at Carnegie Hall in October 1922 introduced the melody to American audiences. Wilhousky, an NBC radio arranger of Ukrainian descent, fitted it with the bell-themed English lyrics (he said the music reminded him of handbells), and "Carol of the Bells" became a Christmas standard, recorded by countless choirs, orchestras and popular artists.

Cultural significance

Leontovych was assassinated by a Soviet agent in 1921, and "Shchedryk" has come to symbolize Ukrainian cultural identity and resilience. A recording of the original arrangement was inscribed in Ukrainian cultural heritage lists, and the song featured prominently in centenary celebrations in 2022.
