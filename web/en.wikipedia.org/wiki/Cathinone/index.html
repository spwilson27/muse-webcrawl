Cathinone

IUPAC name: 2-amino-1-phenyl-1-propanone
Source: Khat (Catha edulis)
Class: Stimulant

Cathinone is a monoamine alkaloid found in the shrub Catha edulis (khat) and is chemically similar to ephedrine, cathine, and amphetamine. It is the principal psychoactive constituent of fresh khat leaves, chewed for centuries in the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula.

Pharmacology

Cathinone acts as a central nervous system stimulant, promoting release of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin. It degrades relatively quickly after harvest to the less potent cathine, which is why khat is traditionally consumed fresh.

Legal status and derivatives

Cathinone is a Schedule I substance under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances in many jurisdictions. Synthetic cathinones, such as mephedrone, MDPV, and methylone — sometimes sold as 'bath salts' — are a large family of designer stimulants, most now widely controlled.
