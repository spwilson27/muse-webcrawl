Transient receptor potential channel

TRP channels
Type: Ion channel superfamily
Subfamilies: TRPC, TRPV, TRPM, TRPA, TRPP, TRPML, TRPN
Permeability: Cations (Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+)

Transient receptor potential channels (TRP channels) are a group of ion channels mostly located on the plasma membrane of numerous animal cell types. They mediate sensations such as pain, temperature, taste, pressure, and vision.

History

The family is named after a Drosophila mutant whose photoreceptors produced a transient rather than sustained response to light; the trp gene was cloned in 1989. Mammalian homologues were discovered through the 1990s, including the capsaicin receptor TRPV1.

Structure and subfamilies

TRP subunits have six transmembrane segments and assemble as tetramers forming cation-permeable pores. Mammalian subfamilies include canonical (TRPC), vanilloid (TRPV), melastatin (TRPM), ankyrin (TRPA), polycystin (TRPP), and mucolipin (TRPML). TRPM8 senses cold and menthol; TRPA1 responds to irritants such as mustard oil.

Function and disease

TRP channels act as polymodal cellular sensors. Mutations cause diseases including polycystic kidney disease (TRPP2), mucolipidosis type IV, and pain and skin disorders, making the family a major drug target.
