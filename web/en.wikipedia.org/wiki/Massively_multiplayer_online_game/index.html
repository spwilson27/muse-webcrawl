Massively multiplayer online game

A massively multiplayer online game (MMOG) is a video game that is capable of supporting large numbers of players simultaneously on a server, typically hundreds or thousands, within a shared persistent virtual world. MMOGs generally require a network connection and feature a persistent game world that continues to exist and evolve even when individual players are offline. The most common subgenre is the massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG).

History

MMOGs grew out of earlier networked games such as multi-user dungeons (MUDs) and graphical online games of the 1980s and early 1990s. Ultima Online (1997) and EverQuest (1999) popularised the MMORPG form, and World of Warcraft (2004) became a cultural phenomenon, peaking at around 12 million subscribers in 2010. Other genres later adopted the format, including first-person shooters (Planetside, Destiny), strategy games, and social worlds such as Second Life.

Characteristics

MMOGs are defined by the size of their player population, persistence of the game world, and a continuous flow of new content and events. They commonly include social features such as guilds or clans, chat systems, and player-versus-player and player-versus-environment gameplay. Business models have shifted from monthly subscriptions to free-to-play with in-game purchases, expansions, and battle passes.

The genre's communities have been widely studied by sociologists and psychologists for the social bonds they create, the economies that develop inside virtual worlds, and phenomena such as game addiction. Some MMOGs have developed real-money economies in which virtual items are bought and sold for real currency.
