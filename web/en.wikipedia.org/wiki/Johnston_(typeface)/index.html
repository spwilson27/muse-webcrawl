Johnston (typeface)

Category: Sans-serif
Classification: Humanist sans-serif
Designer: Edward Johnston
Commissioned by: Underground Electric Railways Company of London
Date released: 1916

Johnston (or Johnston Sans) is a humanist sans-serif typeface designed by Edward Johnston and commissioned in 1913 by Frank Pick of the Underground Electric Railways Company of London. Introduced in 1916, it became the corporate typeface of London Underground and later of Transport for London, and is widely considered the first modern sans-serif designed for a large public identity programme.

Design

Johnston's letterforms are based on classical Roman proportions inscribed in squares and circles, giving the alphabet a distinctive clarity. Characteristic features include the diamond-shaped tittle (dot) on the i and j, the open tail of the lowercase a in early versions, and the perfectly circular O. Johnston's aim was a typeface of "bold simplicity" that could not be mistaken for commercial advertising lettering.

History and influence

The typeface was cut by the Monotype Corporation and used across Underground posters, station nameboards, signage, and timetables. In the 1980s it was redrawn by Banks & Miles as New Johnston, which remains in use by Transport for London today. Johnston's design profoundly influenced British typography: his pupil Eric Gill derived Gill Sans from it, and it set the template for the humanist sans-serif genre.
