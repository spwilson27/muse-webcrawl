Bromothymol blue (also known as bromothymol sulfone phthalein and BTB) is a pH indicator. It is mostly used in applications that require measuring substances that would have a relatively neutral pH, such as swimming pool water and the water in aquariums. It is also used in the laboratory as a biological slide stain and to demonstrate photosynthesis and respiration in cells.

Colours and chemistry

Bromothymol blue acts as a weak acid in solution. In acidic conditions it appears yellow, in neutral solution green, and in alkaline solution blue. Its transition range is approximately pH 6.0 to 7.6, with a pKa of about 7.1, close to neutral pH, which makes it especially useful for measuring near-neutral solutions.

Uses

Because carbon dioxide dissolved in water forms carbonic acid and lowers pH, bromothymol blue is used to detect CO2: blowing exhaled air through a straw into a BTB solution turns it from blue or green toward yellow as respired CO2 acidifies it. This is a classic school experiment demonstrating respiration. It is also used in obstetric practice in the bromothymol (Nitrazine-type) test for ruptured membranes, and in pools and aquaria for water testing.
