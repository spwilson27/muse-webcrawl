Ruth

Ruth (Hebrew: רוּת, Rūt) is the central figure of the biblical Book of Ruth, one of the shortest books of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. A Moabite widow, she is celebrated for her loyalty to her Israelite mother-in-law Naomi and becomes the great-grandmother of King David.

The narrative

During a famine, Naomi's family migrates from Bethlehem to Moab, where her sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. When Naomi's husband and both sons die, she resolves to return to Bethlehem; Orpah stays behind, but Ruth insists on accompanying her, in words that became famous: "Whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God."

In Bethlehem Ruth gleans in the fields of Boaz, a wealthy kinsman of Naomi's late husband, who shows her special kindness. Following Naomi's plan, Ruth presents herself to Boaz at the threshing floor by night. Boaz, after settling the claim of a nearer kinsman-redeemer, marries Ruth. Their son Obed becomes the father of Jesse and grandfather of David, placing the Moabite Ruth in the royal — and, in the Gospel of Matthew, the messianic — genealogy.

Interpretation

Set in the time of the Judges, the book is often read as a story of hesed (loving-kindness) crossing ethnic boundaries, and as a gentle counterpoint to the exclusivism of Ezra and Nehemiah. Jewish tradition reads the Book of Ruth at the festival of Shavuot, the season of the barley harvest in which the story is set. Ruth is also a traditional given name, and "ruth" survives archaically in English as a word for compassion or sorrow.
