Jaffa orange

Cultivar'Shamouti' (Jaffa) orange
OriginNear Jaffa, Ottoman Palestine, mid-19th century
TraitsThick peel, few seeds, sweet; excellent for export

The Jaffa orange, also known as the Shamouti orange, is a sweet, almost seedless orange cultivar developed by Arab farmers near the city of Jaffa in Ottoman Palestine in the mid-19th century. With its thick, tough peel and long keeping qualities, it became famous as an export fruit shipped around the world.

History

The Shamouti appeared as a mutation on a tree of the Baladi orange near Jaffa around the 1840s–50s. Citrus groves expanded around Jaffa under Arab growers, and exports through Jaffa port grew rapidly in the late Ottoman period, with oranges crated and shipped to Europe. Jewish immigrants also entered citrus growing from the 1880s; by the Mandate era "Jaffa" was a celebrated brand and citrus was Palestine's leading export, managed through cooperative marketing boards.

After 1948

The 1948 war displaced many Arab growers and transformed the industry; Israel developed Jaffa into a national symbol and major export brand, marketed by the Citrus Marketing Board. Competition from Spain, Morocco and others, water costs and urbanisation later reduced groves sharply. In recent decades the Jaffa brand has been revived and licensed. The orange remains a symbol claimed in both Israeli and Palestinian heritage — of Jaffa's Arab groves and of Israeli agriculture.

Characteristics

Shamouti fruit is oval, sweet with low acidity, easy to peel, with very few seeds; its thick rind resists bruising, suiting it to long sea voyages before refrigeration.
