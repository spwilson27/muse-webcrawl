Bamyan

The Bamyan valley and the Buddha niches

Bamyan (Dari: بامیان), also spelled Bamiyan, is the capital of Bamyan Province in the Hazarajat region of central Afghanistan. Situated at an elevation of about 2,550 metres in a valley of the Hindu Kush, some 130 kilometres northwest of Kabul, it is the largest town in Hazarajat and the cultural centre of the Hazara people.

History

Bamyan lay on the Silk Road between China, Persia and India, and became a flourishing Buddhist centre between the 2nd and 9th centuries CE. Its cliff caves housed thousands of monks, and its two giant Buddha statues — 55 and 38 metres tall — were carved in the 6th–7th centuries. The valley passed through Ghaznavid, Ghurid and Mongol rule; Genghis Khan's forces destroyed the nearby fortress city of Shahr-e Gholghola in 1221 after the death of his grandson. The region later became a heartland of the Hazara. The Taliban dynamited the Buddhas in March 2001. Since then Bamyan has been one of Afghanistan's more peaceful provinces, known for the Band-e Amir National Park — Afghanistan's first national park, a chain of turquoise travertine lakes west of the town — and for developing skiing and tourism, including the Afghan Ski Challenge.
