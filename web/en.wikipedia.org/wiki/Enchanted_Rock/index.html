Enchanted Rock is a pink granite mountain located in the Llano Uplift of the Texas Hill Country, about 17 miles (27 km) north of Fredericksburg in Texas, United States. The massive exfoliation dome rises approximately 425 feet (130 m) above the surrounding terrain to an elevation of 1,825 feet (556 m), and covers roughly 640 acres. It is one of the largest rock formations of its kind (a batholith) in the United States and is protected as the Enchanted Rock State Natural Area.

Geology

The rock is part of the Town Mountain Granite batholith, formed from magma that cooled underground about 1.08 billion years ago during the Grenville orogeny. Erosion of the overlying rock exposed the granite, and weathering along sheet joints produces its rounded, domed form and characteristic peeling slabs.

History and folklore

Archaeological evidence shows human visitation for at least 11,000 years. Tonkawa, Apache and Comanche peoples regarded the rock as a sacred place; its name derives from legends and from the creaking, groaning sounds the cooling rock makes at night, as well as reports of strange lights. The site was designated a National Natural Landmark in 1971 and was purchased by the State of Texas in 1978. Today it is a popular destination for hiking, rock climbing and stargazing, and is an International Dark Sky Park.
