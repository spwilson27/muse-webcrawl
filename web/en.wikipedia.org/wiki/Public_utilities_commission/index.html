Public utilities commission

A public utilities commission is a regulatory body, found in each U.S. state and in various other jurisdictions, that oversees public utilities — electricity, natural gas, water, telecommunications and sometimes transport — which operate as natural monopolies or franchised services. In exchange for an exclusive service territory, the utility accepts regulation of its rates and service standards.

Functions

The core function is rate regulation: in formal rate cases the commission determines the utility's revenue requirement — prudent operating costs plus a fair return on its rate base (invested capital) — and allocates it among customer classes. Commissions also license facilities, approve construction of power plants and transmission lines (certificates of public convenience and necessity), set service-quality and safety standards, handle consumer complaints, and increasingly oversee decarbonisation and grid-modernisation plans.

Examples

The California Public Utilities Commission, founded in 1911 as the Railroad Commission, is among the most influential, regulating investor-owned energy and water utilities, rail safety and rideshare companies. Texas's Public Utility Commission gained prominence after the February 2021 winter storm Uri blackouts. Commissioners are appointed by governors in most states; in a minority (such as Arizona's Corporation Commission) they are elected. Similar bodies exist internationally, for example provincial utility boards in Canada.

Issues

Criticism focuses on regulatory capture by the industries overseen, the complexity that favours utility lawyers over consumers (many states fund consumer advocates or intervenors), and the tension between guaranteed returns and incentives for efficiency — addressed elsewhere by price-cap regulation such as the UK's RPI−X model.
