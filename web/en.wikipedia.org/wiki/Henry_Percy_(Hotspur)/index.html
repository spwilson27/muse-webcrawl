Sir Henry Percy (20 May 1364 – 21 July 1403), nicknamed Hotspur, was an English knight and the eldest son of Henry Percy, 1st Earl of Northumberland. The Scots gave him his nickname for the speed of his advances in border warfare. He is a major character in Shakespeare's Henry IV, Part 1.

Career

Hotspur fought the Scots at the Battle of Otterburn (1388), where he was captured and ransomed, and campaigned in France and Wales. The Percys helped Henry Bolingbroke seize the throne as Henry IV in 1399 but quarrelled with the king over ransoms, rewards and the treatment of prisoners from the Welsh revolt of Owain Glyndŵr.

Rebellion and death

In 1403 Percy rebelled against Henry IV. At the Battle of Shrewsbury on 21 July 1403 he was killed — by some accounts struck in the face by an arrow when he raised his visor. His body was later exhumed and quartered; Shakespeare dramatised his rivalry with Prince Hal, though in reality Hotspur was old enough to be the prince's father.
