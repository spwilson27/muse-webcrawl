Radia Perlman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Radia Perlman
Born18 December 1951, Portsmouth, Virginia, US
Known forInventing the spanning-tree protocol (1985)
Nickname'Mother of the Internet' (a title she dislikes)
[Image: Portrait photograph of Radia Perlman speaking at a technical conference]

Radia Joy Perlman (born 1951) is an American computer programmer and network engineer, known principally for her invention of the spanning-tree protocol (STP), which is fundamental to the operation of network bridges and switches — and thus of Ethernet local area networks everywhere.

Working at Digital Equipment Corporation in 1985, Perlman solved the problem of loops in bridged networks — which otherwise produce broadcast storms — with an elegant distributed algorithm by which bridges elect a root and prune links into a loop-free tree, reconfiguring automatically when links fail. She documented it in a famous poem ('Algorhyme': I think that I shall never see / a graph more lovely than a tree…) in her paper. STP was standardised as IEEE 802.1D and remains embedded in every switched LAN, though faster successors (RSTP, TRILL — her own later design) have refined it.

Perlman's broader career spans routing protocol design (IS-IS, still used in large provider networks) and network security; her textbook Interconnections is a standard reference. She is often styled 'Mother of the Internet', a label she resists — noting the Internet had many parents and that her contribution, though essential, is the humble plumbing of the local network.
