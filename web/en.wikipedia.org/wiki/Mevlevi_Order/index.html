The Mevlevi Order (Turkish: Mevlevilik) is a Sufi order that originated in Konya, Anatolia, from the teachings of the 13th-century Persian poet, mystic, and theologian Jalal al-Din Muhammad Rumi — called Mevlana ("our master") by his followers. The order is famous for the sama ceremony, in which dervishes perform a meditative whirling dance accompanied by music, earning its members the name "Whirling Dervishes." UNESCO proclaimed the Mevlevi Sama Ceremony a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005.

History

After Rumi's death in 1273, his son Sultan Walad and successors organised the order, centred on Rumi's mausoleum in Konya. Mevlevi lodges (tekkes) spread across the Ottoman Empire, attracting sultans, composers, and calligraphers; the order produced much of Ottoman classical music's repertoire. In 1925 the Turkish Republic closed Sufi lodges under its secularisation laws, and the Konya lodge became a museum, though sama performances later resumed as cultural events and the order survived abroad and informally in Turkey.

Practices

Initiates traditionally underwent a 1,001-day retreat of prayer, study of Rumi's Masnavi, and service in the lodge kitchen. The sama combines the ney (reed flute), chanting, and the semazen's turning — right palm raised to receive divine blessing, left palm turned down to channel it to the earth — symbolising the soul's ascent and the planets' revolution.
