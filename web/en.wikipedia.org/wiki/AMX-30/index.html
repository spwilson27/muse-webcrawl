The AMX-30 is a main battle tank designed by GIAT and first delivered to the French Army in 1966. Reflecting French doctrine of the period — influenced by the belief that armor could not defeat modern shaped-charge weapons — the AMX-30 was lightly armored for its class but highly mobile and powerfully armed with a 105 mm gun firing the unique Obus G spin-stabilized HEAT round, whose charge did not rotate with the shell.

Development

The AMX-30 began in 1957 as a joint Franco-German-Italian project for a common European tank; when the project collapsed, France continued alone with the AMX-30 while Germany developed the Leopard 1, which it resembles in concept. The definitive AMX-30B entered production at Roanne in 1966. The modernized AMX-30B2 of 1979 added an integrated fire-control system, laser rangefinder, and an improved engine.

Service

The AMX-30 was exported to Spain (built under licence as the AMX-30E), Greece, Cyprus, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela, Chile, and others. Saudi AMX-30s saw combat against Iraq in the 1991 Gulf War, and French AMX-30B2s served in Operation Daguet in the same war. In French service the type was replaced by the Leclerc in the 1990s, with the last French AMX-30 variants retiring in 2011. The chassis spawned a large family including the AMX-30D recovery vehicle, the AuF1 self-propelled howitzer, the Pluton missile launcher, and the Roland and AMX-30SA anti-aircraft vehicles.
