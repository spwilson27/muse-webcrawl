Hubert de Givenchy

Count Hubert James Marcel Taffin de Givenchy (20 February 1927 – 10 March 2018) was a French fashion designer who founded the house of Givenchy in 1952. He is best known for his elegant haute couture and ready-to-wear designs and for his long collaboration with the actress Audrey Hepburn.

Career

Born in Beauvais, Givenchy studied law briefly before turning to fashion, working for Jacques Fath, Robert Piguet, Lucien Lelong and Elsa Schiaparelli. Opening his own house at the age of 24, he presented his first collection in 1952, built on "separates" such as the Bettina blouse, named for the model Bettina Graziani. His mentor was Cristóbal Balenciaga, whose sculptural approach to clothing shaped Givenchy's own restrained elegance.

Audrey Hepburn

Givenchy designed Hepburn's costumes for Sabrina (1954) and went on to dress her on and off screen for four decades; the little black dress he created for Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961) became one of the most famous garments in cinema history. He also designed for Jacqueline Kennedy, Grace Kelly and the Duchess of Windsor. His first perfume, L'Interdit (1957), was made for Hepburn and launched the Parfums Givenchy line.

Later life

Givenchy sold the house to LVMH in 1988 but remained its artistic director until retiring in 1995; he was succeeded by John Galliano, then Alexander McQueen. In retirement he championed Balenciaga's legacy and restored the gardens of his home, the Manoir du Jonchet. He died in Neuilly-sur-Seine in March 2018, aged 91.
