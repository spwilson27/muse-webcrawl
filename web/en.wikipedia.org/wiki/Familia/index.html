Familia

In ancient Rome, a familia (plural familiae) was a legally defined unit consisting of all those under the authority of a single head of household, the pater familias. It could include the wife, children, descendants in the male line, adopted members, and slaves of the household; under Roman law the pater familias held patria potestas — power of life, property, and legal representation — over its free members.

Meaning and usage

The word's primary legal sense was closer to "household" or "estate" than to the modern nuclear family: it could denote the persons subject to one man's authority, or the property (including slaves, referred to as the familia rustica or familia urbana) belonging to it. Only Roman citizens could form a familia in the strict legal sense; upon the death of the pater familias, each adult son became head of his own familia.

The kinship group of blood relatives, by contrast, is often described with terms such as gens and cognatio. The familia is the root of the word "family" in many modern languages, though the meanings have diverged.
