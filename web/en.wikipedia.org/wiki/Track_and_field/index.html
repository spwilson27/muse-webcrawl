Track and field is a sport that includes athletic contests based on running, jumping and throwing skills. Known as athletics in most of the world, it takes its name from the athletics stadium, where a running track surrounds the field for jumping and throwing events. It is the centrepiece of the Summer Olympic Games, descending directly from the contests of ancient Olympia, where the stadion footrace was the sole event of the first recorded Games in 776 BC.

Events include sprints (100 m, 200 m, 400 m), middle- and long-distance races (800 m to 10,000 m, plus the marathon on the road), hurdles and steeplechase, relays, jumps (long, triple, high, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin), plus the combined decathlon and heptathlon. The biennial World Athletics Championships, first held in Helsinki in 1983, and the annual Diamond League circuit are the sport's premier non-Olympic stages.

Great athletes

Usain Bolt (100 m/200 m world records of 9.58 s and 19.19 s) is the sport's biggest modern star. Other era-defining figures include Jesse Owens (four golds at Berlin 1936), Carl Lewis (nine Olympic golds), Florence Griffith Joyner (whose 1988 sprint records still stand), Paavo Nurmi, Emil Zátopek, and distance greats Haile Gebrselassie, Kenenisa Bekele and Eliud Kipchoge, the two-time Olympic marathon champion and first man to run the distance in under two hours (in a non-record-eligible event in 2019).
