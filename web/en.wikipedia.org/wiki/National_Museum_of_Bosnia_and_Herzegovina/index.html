The National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina (Bosnian: Zemaljski muzej Bosne i Hercegovine) is the largest and most important museum institution in Bosnia and Herzegovina, located in Sarajevo. Founded in 1888 under Austro-Hungarian rule, it holds around four million artefacts in departments of archaeology, ethnology, and natural history, together with a library of some 300,000 volumes.

Collections

The museum's most famous treasure is the Sarajevo Haggadah, a 14th-century illuminated Jewish manuscript brought to Bosnia by Sephardic Jews expelled from Spain, preserved today in a secure vault room. Other notable holdings include the Roman mosaics of the region, medieval Bosnian tombstones (stećci), a rich ethnographic collection of Balkan dress and crafts, and extensive botanical collections in the museum's botanical garden, laid out between its four purpose-built pavilions opened in 1913.

History

The museum survived the siege of Sarajevo (1992–1995) despite shell damage to its buildings. Chronic disputes over which level of government should fund it after the Bosnian War led to a funding crisis and its closure from October 2012 until September 2015, when an international campaign helped secure its reopening.
