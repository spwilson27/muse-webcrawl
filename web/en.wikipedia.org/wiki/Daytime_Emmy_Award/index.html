The Daytime Emmy Awards, or Daytime Emmys, are an American accolade bestowed by the New York–based National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences in recognition of excellence in American daytime television programming. They are considered one of the major American television awards, alongside the Primetime Emmy Awards.

History

Daytime categories were first added to the Emmy Awards in 1972, and a separate Daytime Emmy ceremony was first held in 1974, hosted by Barbara Walters and Peter Marshall and broadcast from New York. The ceremony historically honoured soap operas, talk shows, game shows, children's programming and morning shows.

Categories

Major categories include Outstanding Drama Series, Outstanding Talk Series, Outstanding Game Show, and acting awards for lead and supporting performers in drama and talk programming. Since 2022, children's and family categories have been spun off into the separate Children's and Family Emmy Awards.

Ceremony

The awards are usually presented in spring or early summer. The ceremony moved among network television, cable and streaming outlets over the years as daytime viewing habits changed.
