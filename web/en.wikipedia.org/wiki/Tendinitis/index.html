Tendinitis

Tendinitis (also spelled tendonitis) is inflammation or irritation of a tendon — the thick fibrous cord that attaches muscle to bone. It most often develops from repetitive motion or overuse, and causes pain and tenderness just outside a joint. Common sites include the shoulder (rotator cuff), elbow, wrist, knee, and Achilles tendon.

Signs and causes

Symptoms typically include a dull ache, tenderness, and mild swelling that worsens with movement of the affected limb. Causes include sudden injury, repetitive occupational or sporting activity, poor posture, and age, as tendons become less flexible. Some infections and antibiotics (such as fluoroquinolones) can also provoke tendon problems.

Treatment

Most cases resolve with rest, ice, analgesics such as NSAIDs, and physiotherapy; corticosteroid injections are sometimes used, while severe cases with tendon rupture may require surgery. Modern research distinguishes acute inflammatory tendinitis from chronic degenerative tendinopathy, in which inflammatory cells are largely absent.
