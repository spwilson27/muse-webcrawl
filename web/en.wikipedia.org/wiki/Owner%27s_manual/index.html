Owner's manual

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An owner's manual (also called an instruction manual or user guide) is an instructional book or booklet supplied with almost all technologically advanced consumer products such as vehicles, home appliances and computer peripherals. Information contained in the owner's manual typically includes safety instructions; assembly, installation and set-up instructions; instructions for normal operations; product features; specifications; maintenance and troubleshooting instructions; and warranty terms.

Contents

Manuals for automobiles usually cover operation, maintenance schedules, fluid specifications, tyre pressures and basic troubleshooting. Appliance and electronics manuals typically describe controls, modes of operation, cleaning, and error codes. In many jurisdictions, safety warnings in manuals are legally significant and form part of a manufacturer's duty to warn users of hazards.

Formats

Traditionally printed and stored in a car's glove compartment or an appliance's packaging, manuals are increasingly provided as PDF documents or web-based help, sometimes accessed through QR codes on the product. The shift has drawn criticism where printed copies are no longer supplied.
