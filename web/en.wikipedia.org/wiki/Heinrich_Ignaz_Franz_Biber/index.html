Heinrich Ignaz Franz Biber

Born: 12 August 1644, Wartenberg, Bohemia
Died: 3 May 1704, Salzburg
Occupation: Composer, violinist
Era: Baroque

Heinrich Ignaz Franz Biber (12 August 1644 (baptised) – 3 May 1704) was a Bohemian-Austrian composer and violinist. Biber worked in Graz and Kroměříž before he illegally left his employer, Prince-Bishop Karl Liechtenstein-Kastelkorn, and settled in Salzburg, where he remained for the rest of his life and rose to the rank of Kapellmeister.

He is widely regarded as one of the most important composers for the violin in the history of music, demanded the highest technical feats from the performers of his works, in both ordinary playing and in scordatura — the mistuning of strings to achieve particular effects.

Works

Biber's best-known work is the cycle of fifteen Mystery Sonatas (also called the Rosary Sonatas, c. 1676), for violin and basso continuo, each sonata depicting one of the Mysteries of the Rosary and each using a different scordatura tuning; the cycle concludes with a monumental Passacaglia for solo violin, one of the earliest such works. His other works include the Battalia à 10 (1673), a vividly programmatic battle piece; the eight Sonatas for violin and continuo of 1681; masses, including the enormous Missa Salisburgensis for 53 voices attributed to him; and the Sonatae tam aris quam aulis servientes (1676).

Legacy

Biber's music was largely forgotten until the 20th-century early music revival, when performers such as Eduard Melkus and later John Holloway, Andrew Manze and others recorded his violin works. He was ennobled by Emperor Leopold I in 1690, taking the name Biber von Bibern.
