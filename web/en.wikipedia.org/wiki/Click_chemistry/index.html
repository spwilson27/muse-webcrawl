Click chemistry

Click chemistry is a class of simple, high-yielding chemical reactions that generate substances quickly and reliably by joining small modular units together. The concept was introduced by K. Barry Sharpless and colleagues at the Scripps Research Institute in 2001, drawing inspiration from the way nature builds complex molecules from a limited set of building blocks.

Criteria

Sharpless proposed that a reaction qualifies as a "click" reaction if it is modular, wide in scope, gives very high yields, generates only inoffensive byproducts removable without chromatography, and is stereospecific. The process should use simple reaction conditions, readily available starting materials, no solvent or a benign solvent such as water, and simple product isolation.

The azide–alkyne cycloaddition

The flagship click reaction is the copper(I)-catalysed azide–alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC), in which an organic azide and a terminal alkyne combine to give a 1,4-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole. The reaction was reported independently in 2002 by the groups of Sharpless and of Morten Meldal. Copper catalysis accelerates the classical Huisgen cycloaddition by roughly seven orders of magnitude and enforces a single regioisomer.

Bioorthogonal variants

Because copper is toxic to living systems, Carolyn Bertozzi developed strain-promoted azide–alkyne cycloaddition (SPAAC), in which a strained cyclooctyne reacts with azides without any metal catalyst. This made click chemistry usable inside living cells and organisms, a field Bertozzi termed bioorthogonal chemistry. Other widely used click-type reactions include thiol–ene coupling, Diels–Alder reactions, and the sulfur(VI) fluoride exchange (SuFEx) introduced by Sharpless in 2014.

Applications

Click chemistry is used in drug discovery, polymer and materials science, bioconjugation, DNA labelling, and the assembly of molecular machines. In 2022 Sharpless, Meldal, and Bertozzi shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for the development of click chemistry and bioorthogonal chemistry."
