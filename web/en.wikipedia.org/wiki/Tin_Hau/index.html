Tin Hau (Chinese: 天后; Cantonese Yale: Tīn Hauh), literally "Queen of Heaven", is a Chinese sea goddess also known as Mazu. She is the deified form of Lin Moniang, a shamaness from Fujian said to have lived in the 10th century and to have rescued seafarers in distress. Tin Hau is the patron deity of sailors, fishermen, and coastal communities throughout Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, Fujian, and the Chinese diaspora.

Worship

More than sixty Tin Hau temples exist in Hong Kong alone, many founded by fishing communities. The goddess's birthday, on the 23rd day of the third lunar month, is celebrated with festivals, parades, and offerings, notably at Joss House Bay and in Yuen Long. The Hong Kong neighbourhood of Tin Hau, near Causeway Bay, takes its name from a local temple, as does the MTR station serving it.

Mazu

Under the name Mazu, the goddess's belief was inscribed by UNESCO in 2009 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. In imperial China she was progressively granted higher titles, culminating in "Empress of Heaven" (Tianhou).
