Seodaemun Prison

The preserved cell blocks of Seodaemun Prison History Hall.

Seodaemun Prison is a former prison in Seodaemun District, Seoul, South Korea. Built under Japanese rule and opened in 1908 as Gyeongseong Prison, it became notorious as a place of confinement, torture, and execution for Korean independence activists during the Japanese colonial period (1910–1945). It continued to operate as a prison after liberation, holding political prisoners under successive South Korean governments, before closing in 1987. The site is now preserved as the Seodaemun Prison History Hall, a museum and memorial.

History

Construction began in 1907 and the prison opened on 21 October 1908, initially with a capacity of about 500 inmates. As Japanese colonial control tightened, the facility was repeatedly expanded. Following the March 1st Movement of 1919, in which millions of Koreans protested for independence, thousands of demonstrators were arrested and many were held at Seodaemun, which became severely overcrowded. The prison's name changed several times, becoming Seodaemun Prison in 1923.

Among its most famous inmates was Yu Gwan-sun, a teenage organizer of independence protests who died in the prison in 1920 after torture and abuse. Many other activists were executed at the prison's execution building or died from torture, malnutrition, and disease. Records and survivor testimony describe systematic torture in interrogation rooms in the prison's basement and security buildings.

Post-liberation use

After Korea's liberation in 1945, the prison was renamed Seoul Prison and later Seoul Detention Center. Under the authoritarian governments of the Republic of Korea, it held democracy activists and political dissidents alongside criminal offenders. It remained in use until 6 November 1987, when inmates were transferred to a new facility in Uiwang and the prison closed.

Museum

Rather than demolishing the entire complex, the government preserved several buildings, and the Seodaemun Prison History Hall opened in 1998 within Independence Park. Preserved and restored structures include cell blocks, the execution building and its associated tunnel through which bodies were removed, the women's cell block (Yeooksa), interrogation and torture rooms, and watchtowers. Exhibits document the independence movement and the prison's role in Korea's modern history. Parts of the site are designated as Historic Site No. 324 of South Korea. The museum hosts commemorative ceremonies, particularly around Liberation Day (15 August) and the anniversary of the March 1st Movement.
