Fentanyl is a potent synthetic opioid drug used medically as an analgesic and anaesthetic adjunct. It was first synthesised by Paul Janssen in 1960 and is roughly 50 times stronger than heroin and 100 times stronger than morphine by weight. Illicitly manufactured fentanyl and its analogues have become a leading cause of drug overdose deaths in North America.

Medical use

Fentanyl is used for severe pain, in anaesthesia, and for chronic pain in opioid-tolerant patients via transdermal patches; lozenges, nasal sprays and sublingual forms treat breakthrough cancer pain. It acts primarily as an agonist at the μ-opioid receptor, with rapid onset and short duration when given intravenously because of its high lipid solubility.

Adverse effects

Side effects include respiratory depression (the usual cause of death in overdose), sedation, nausea, constipation, muscle rigidity and dependence. Overdose is treated with the opioid antagonist naloxone, sometimes requiring repeated or higher doses. Potent analogues such as carfentanil are used only in veterinary medicine for large animals.

Illicit use and public health

Since roughly 2013, illicitly produced fentanyl — much of it manufactured from precursor chemicals sourced internationally and trafficked by criminal organisations — has been mixed into heroin, counterfeit prescription pills and stimulants, sharply increasing overdose mortality in the United States and Canada. Its potency means small quantities are easily smuggled, and its presence is often unknown to users. Public-health responses include naloxone distribution, fentanyl test strips and drug-checking services.

History

Janssen Pharmaceutica developed fentanyl as part of a family of synthetic opioids related to pethidine (meperidine). It entered clinical use in the 1960s under the trade name Sublimaze and was later marketed in patch (Duragesic), lozenge (Actiq) and other formulations.
