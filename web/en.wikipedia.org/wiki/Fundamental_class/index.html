Fundamental class

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sum of the triangles of a closed orientable surface, taken with consistent orientation, represents its fundamental class.

In mathematics, the fundamental class is a homology class [M] ∈ Hn(M, ∂M) associated to a connected orientable compact manifold of dimension n, which corresponds to the generator of the homology group Hn(M, ∂M; Z) ≅ Z. Intuitively, the fundamental class can be thought of as the sum of the top-dimension simplices of a suitable triangulation of the manifold.

The fundamental class is the essential ingredient of Poincaré duality: cap product with [M] induces isomorphisms Hk(M) → Hn−k(M) between the cohomology and homology of a closed orientable n-manifold.

Definition

For a closed (boundaryless) orientable n-manifold M, Hn(M; Z) ≅ Z, and the fundamental class is a chosen generator — a choice equivalent to choosing an orientation. For manifolds with boundary, the class lives in relative homology Hn(M, ∂M). For non-orientable manifolds, or with coefficients in Z/2, a mod-2 fundamental class always exists, since every manifold is Z/2-orientable.

Applications

  • Poincaré duality and its consequences, including the intersection form on 4-manifolds.
  • Degree of a map: for a map f : M → N between closed orientable n-manifolds, the degree is defined by f*[M] = deg(f)·[N].
  • Cohomology operations and characteristic classes: dual classes of submanifolds are obtained by pushing forward their fundamental classes; in algebraic geometry, the fundamental class of a variety anchors the Chow ring.
  • Complex geometry: the fundamental class [X] of a compact complex manifold is used to define integration of differential forms over X.
