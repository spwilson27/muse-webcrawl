Banchan

Banchan
Korean name반찬 (banchan)
CourseSide dishes
Place of originKorea
ServingCommunal, in small dishes

Banchan (Korean: 반찬) are small side dishes served along with cooked rice in Korean cuisine. They are set in the middle of the table to be shared, and a typical meal features several kinds — traditionally three for commoners, five, seven, nine or twelve for royal and ceremonial tables, excluding soup and rice. In Korean restaurants, banchan are often served free of charge before the main dishes and refilled on request.

Types

  • Kimchi – fermented vegetables, almost always present, most famously napa cabbage (baechu-kimchi) and cubed radish (kkakdugi)
  • Namul – seasoned steamed or stir-fried vegetables, such as spinach (sigeumchi-namul) and bean sprouts (kongnamul)
  • Bokkeum – stir-fried dishes, such as spicy stir-fried pork or squid
  • Jorim – foods braised in seasoned soy sauce, such as soy-braised beef (jangjorim)
  • Jjim – steamed dishes, such as steamed eggs (gyeran-jjim)
  • Jeon – savoury pancakes, such as scallion pancakes (pajeon)
  • Muchim – raw or blanched ingredients tossed in seasoning

Cultural notes

The number and elaborateness of banchan historically signalled the status of the household; palace cuisine (surasang) featured twelve side dishes. Because the dishes are shared, diners traditionally take them with personal chopsticks onto individual plates. The practice is thought to have been influenced by Buddhist traditions of communal eating and by the need to supplement a rice-centred diet with varied, preserved foods through Korea's cold winters.
