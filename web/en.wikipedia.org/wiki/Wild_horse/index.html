Wild horse

The wild horse (Equus ferus) is a species of the genus Equus that includes the domestic horse (E. f. caballus) and the endangered Przewalski's horse (E. f. przewalskii). The tarpan, the Eurasian wild form, went extinct in the early 20th century.

True wild versus feral

Most free-roaming "wild" horses today — American mustangs, Australian brumbies, the Konik and Exmoor populations — are feral descendants of domestic horses. Przewalski's horse, once extinct in the wild, was reintroduced to Mongolia from zoo stock; genetic studies indicate it descends from early Botai domestication yet remains the closest living wild relative.

Evolution and behaviour

Horses evolved in North America, spread to Eurasia over Beringia, and died out in the Americas around 10,000 years ago before Spanish reintroduction. Wild and feral herds form harems led by a stallion with mares and foals, plus bachelor bands, grazing steppe and semi-arid ranges.

Management

Feral populations spark debate over land use, roundups and fertility control in the US West and Australia.
