Commercial driver's license

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A commercial driver's license (CDL) is a driver's license required in the United States to operate large, heavy or placarded hazardous-material vehicles in commerce. The federal Commercial Motor Vehicle Safety Act of 1986 established national minimum standards, administered by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), while individual states issue the licenses.

Classes

  • Class A — any combination of vehicles with a gross combination weight rating of 26,001 pounds or more, provided the vehicle being towed exceeds 10,000 pounds (for example, tractor-trailers).
  • Class B — any single vehicle of 26,001 pounds or more, or such a vehicle towing another vehicle not exceeding 10,000 pounds (for example, straight trucks, large buses and dump trucks).
  • Class C — vehicles not covered by Class A or B that are designed or used to carry 16 or more passengers including the driver, or that carry hazardous materials requiring placards.

Endorsements and requirements

Additional endorsements are required for particular operations, including hazardous materials (H), tank vehicles (N), passengers (P), school buses (S), doubles and triples (T), and combinations of these. Applicants must pass knowledge and skills (road) tests, hold a commercial learner's permit first, meet medical certification requirements, and complete entry-level driver training from a registered provider under rules that took effect in 2022. CDL holders are subject to stricter traffic-enforcement standards than ordinary drivers — for example, a lower blood-alcohol limit of 0.04% while operating a commercial vehicle — and serious offences can lead to disqualification of the license.
