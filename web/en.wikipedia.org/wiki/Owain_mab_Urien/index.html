Owain mab Urien

Owain mab Urien (Middle Welsh: Owain ap Urien) was a late sixth-century prince of Rheged, a Brittonic kingdom of the Hen Ogledd (the "Old North") in what is now northern England and southern Scotland. He was the son of King Urien of Rheged and is celebrated in the poetry of Taliesin, who sang at Urien's court. In later Welsh tradition and in Arthurian legend he was transformed into the knight Ywain (Yvain, Owain), one of the Knights of the Round Table.

Historical figure

Owain appears in the early Welsh poems attributed to Taliesin, which praise Urien and his son's fighting against the Angles of Bernicia. The poem Gweith Argoed Llwyfein ("The Battle of Argoed Llwyfein") celebrates Owain's part in a victory over the Bernician leader Fflamddwyn, usually identified with Theodric of Bernicia. Welsh genealogies make Owain the brother of Rhun and other sons of Urien. According to later tradition, Owain was killed fighting Morcant Bulc, a rival British king who envied the family's successes; his death is placed around 595.

He is a major figure in the Welsh tale The Dream of Rhonabwy, set in the twelfth century but drawing on older material, in which Owain plays a game of gwyddbwyll (a board game akin to chess) against King Arthur while their armies — Owain's ravens and Arthur's men — battle in the distance.

Arthurian legend

In Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae and subsequent romances, Owain became Yvain, the Knight of the Lion. Chrétien de Troyes devoted a romance to him, Yvain, the Knight of the Lion (c. 1180), whose story was retold in Welsh as Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain, one of the Three Welsh Romances associated with the Mabinogion. In the tale, Owain defends a magical fountain in the forest of Brocéliande, marries its lady, Laudine, and, after breaking a promise to her, redeems himself through a series of adventures aided by a lion he has rescued.
