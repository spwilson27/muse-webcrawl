Schizoaffective disorder is a mental disorder characterized by abnormal thought processes and an unstable mood. The diagnosis is made when the person experiences symptoms of both schizophrenia (such as hallucinations, delusions, and disorganized speech or behavior) and a mood disorder, either bipolar disorder or major depression. The main criterion is the presence of psychotic symptoms for at least two weeks without any mood symptoms present.

Signs and symptoms

Schizoaffective disorder is defined by mood disorder-free psychosis in the context of a long-standing psychotic and mood disorder. Psychosis must meet criterion A for schizophrenia, which may include delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thinking and speech, or disorganized or catatonic behavior. Both delusions and hallucinations are classic symptoms of psychosis. Mood episodes — mania, hypomania, mixed episodes, or depression — are present for the majority of the duration of the illness.

Types

Two types of schizoaffective disorder are recognized: the bipolar type, which includes episodes of mania and sometimes major depression, and the depressive type, which includes only major depressive episodes.

Causes

A combination of genetic and environmental factors is thought to play a role. Genetic factors include genes involved in dopaminergic, serotonergic, and glutamatergic signaling. Environmental factors include early stress, cannabis use, and prenatal exposure to infection or malnutrition.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on observed behavior, reported experiences, and reported symptoms. It belongs to the schizophrenia spectrum and is distinguished from schizophrenia by the prominence of mood episodes, and from mood disorders by the persistence of psychotic symptoms during periods without mood disturbance.

Treatment

Treatment typically involves a combination of antipsychotic medication, mood stabilizers, and antidepressants, along with psychotherapy and psychosocial rehabilitation.

Prognosis

Outcomes are generally considered to be intermediate between those of schizophrenia and mood disorders, though there is considerable individual variation.
