E-Commerce Directive

TitleDirective 2000/31/EC
Adopted8 June 2000
In force17 July 2000
AreaEuropean Union

The E-Commerce Directive (Directive 2000/31/EC) is a European Union directive on certain legal aspects of information society services, in particular electronic commerce, in the Internal Market. Adopted on 8 June 2000, it established the basic legal framework for online services in the EU for more than two decades.

Key provisions

  • Internal market clause (country of origin): information society service providers are principally regulated by the member state in which they are established, and other member states may not restrict incoming services for reasons within the directive's scope.
  • Transparency requirements: service providers must supply basic information about themselves and make commercial communications (including advertising and unsolicited email) clearly identifiable.
  • Electronic contracts: member states must ensure their legal systems allow contracts to be concluded electronically, and set rules on the information to be given when orders are placed.
  • Intermediary liability: Articles 12–15 give "mere conduit", caching and hosting providers conditional exemptions from liability for third-party content, and prohibit imposing a general obligation to monitor content. Hosting providers lose protection once they have actual knowledge of illegal content and fail to act expeditiously.

Reform

The liability framework and much of the directive's approach were modernised by the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065), which replaced the intermediary-liability articles of the directive while leaving many of its other provisions in force. After Brexit, the directive's rules as implemented continued to apply in the United Kingdom in amended form.
