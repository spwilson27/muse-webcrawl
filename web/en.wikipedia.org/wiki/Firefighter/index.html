Firefighter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Firefighters wear protective turnout gear and self-contained breathing apparatus.

A firefighter (or fire fighter or fireman) is a first responder trained in firefighting, primarily to control and extinguish fires that threaten life and property, as well as to rescue persons from dangerous situations. Firefighters work under the auspices of a fire department, typically a public service agency.

Duties

Beyond suppressing structure and wildland fires, firefighters respond to vehicle accidents, hazardous material incidents, technical rescues, and medical emergencies — in many jurisdictions the majority of calls are for emergency medical services. Duties also include fire prevention inspections, public education, and maintaining equipment and apparatus.

Training and equipment

Firefighters are trained in the use of hose lines, ladders, ventilation, forcible entry, and search and rescue. They wear personal protective equipment ("turnout gear") made of heat-resistant fabrics and a self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) for working in smoke. The work is physically demanding and hazardous; firefighters face elevated risks of burns, smoke inhalation, and certain cancers from exposure to combustion products.

History

Organized firefighting dates to ancient Rome's Corps of Vigiles. Modern municipal fire departments developed in the 19th century alongside steam-powered pumpers and professional, paid companies.
