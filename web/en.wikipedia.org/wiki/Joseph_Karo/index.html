Joseph Karo

Joseph ben Ephraim Karo (1488 – March 24, 1575), also spelled Caro or Qaro, was a prominent Sephardic rabbi and the author of the Shulchan Aruch, the most widely accepted code of Jewish law ever written. Known in Hebrew by the acronym Maran ("Our Master"), he was also a mystic of the Safed circle in Ottoman Palestine.

Life

Karo was born in Toledo, Spain, in 1488. After the expulsion of the Jews from Spain in 1492, his family fled to Portugal and then to the Ottoman Empire, settling in Nikopol (in modern Bulgaria) and later Salonica and Adrianople, where he served as rabbi and began his great legal commentary. Around 1536 he emigrated to Safed in the Galilee, then a flourishing centre of Jewish scholarship and Kabbalah, where he headed a yeshiva and was ordained by Jacob Berab in the attempt to revive the ancient semikhah. He died in Safed in 1575 and is buried in its old Jewish cemetery.

Works

Karo's monumental Beit Yosef ("House of Joseph"), on which he laboured for some twenty years, is a comprehensive commentary on the Arba'ah Turim of Jacob ben Asher, tracing every law to its Talmudic and medieval sources. His distilled digest of its conclusions, the Shulchan Aruch ("Set Table", printed in Venice in 1565), became — together with the Ashkenazic glosses of Moses Isserles (the Mappah) — the authoritative code of halakha for most of the Jewish world down to the present day.

Karo also kept a mystical diary, published as Maggid Mesharim, recording decades of nightly visitations from a maggid, an angelic mentor he identified with the personified Mishnah. Though his central place in Safed's Kabbalistic milieu is sometimes overshadowed by his younger contemporary Isaac Luria, Karo's house hosted prayer and study circles in which law and mysticism were united.
