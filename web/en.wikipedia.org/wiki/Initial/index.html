Initial

In a written or published work, an initial is a letter at the beginning of a word, a chapter, or a paragraph that is larger than the rest of the text. The word is derived from the Latin initialis, meaning "of the beginning." Initials are often rendered in a decorative typeface or elaborately ornamented, especially in manuscripts and fine printed books. A related everyday sense of the word refers to the first letters of a person's names, used as an abbreviation or a form of signature.

Types

When the enlarged letter sits within the paragraph and following lines of text wrap around it, it is called a drop cap (or dropped initial). When the letter's baseline aligns with the first line and it rises above the text, it is a raised cap. In illuminated manuscripts, initials could be historiated, containing a recognizable scene or portrait within the letter, or inhabited, filled with figures, animals, or foliage that do not form a narrative scene. Medieval scribes and rubricators left space for initials to be added by an illuminator, and guide letters were often written faintly in the margin.

History

Enlarged initials developed in late antique and early medieval manuscripts, reaching extraordinary elaboration in Insular Gospel books such as the Book of Kells, where the Chi Rho page turns the Greek letters of Christ's name into a full-page composition. Carolingian and Romanesque manuscripts favoured inhabited initials, and Gothic books added pen-flourishing. Early printers initially left blank spaces for initials to be painted by hand before printing them directly in woodcut or metal type.

Initials as names

The term also denotes the first letters of personal names. Many people sign documents with their initials, and some are publicly known by them, such as the authors J. R. R. Tolkien and J. K. Rowling. Sequences of initials can form initialisms and acronyms. In some legal and formal contexts, each page of a document is initialled to show it has been read and agreed.
