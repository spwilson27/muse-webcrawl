Lixouri (Greek: Ληξούρι) is a town and a municipality on the island of Cephalonia (Kefalonia), in the Ionian Islands of Greece. It is the island's second-largest settlement after the capital, Argostoli, which lies directly across the Gulf of Argostoli from it. Lixouri is the principal town of the Paliki peninsula, the western lobe of Cephalonia, and has been a separate municipality since Greece's local government reforms of 2019.

History

Lixouri grew in the 16th and 17th centuries under Venetian rule and became the island's leading commercial port, exporting currants, wine and olive oil; its merchants and shipowners built the grand town houses that still line its streets. It was the birthplace of the satirical poet Andreas Laskaratos (1811–1901), whose statue — famously seated with its back to the sea and the town's clergy with whom he quarrelled — stands on the waterfront, and of the scholar and sailor Iakovos Typaldos. The town was badly damaged by the great Ionian earthquake of August 1953 and again, on a smaller scale, by earthquakes in early 2014, and was substantially rebuilt.

Geography and sights

The town sits on a sheltered bay with a busy waterfront of cafés, a working harbour, and ferry connections to Argostoli (about 30 minutes across the gulf). Nearby attractions include the red-sand beach of Xi, the beaches of Petani and Lepeda, the Kipouria monastery on the west coast cliffs, and the library and museum housed in the Iakovateios Library, a notable 19th-century mansion. Lixouri is known across Greece for its carnival traditions and for the celebrated musical culture of the Paliki peninsula, including its philharmonic society, founded in 1836.
