President of the Government of Catalonia

President of the Government of Catalonia
Catalan: President de la Generalitat de Catalunya
Residence: Casa dels Canonges, Barcelona
Seat: Palau de la Generalitat
Appointer: King of Spain (on nomination by the Parliament of Catalonia)

The president of the Government of Catalonia (Catalan: president de la Generalitat de Catalunya) is the head of government of Catalonia, the leader of the Executive Council, and the highest representative of the Generalitat de Catalunya, the autonomous government of the Spanish region of Catalonia.

Role and election

The president is elected by the Parliament of Catalonia from among its members and formally appointed by the King of Spain. The president directs the Government (Govern), appoints and dismisses its ministers (consellers), represents the Spanish state in Catalonia in an ordinary capacity, and promulgates laws in the King's name. The official residence is the Casa dels Canonges in Barcelona's Gothic Quarter, adjoining the Palau de la Generalitat.

History

The Generalitat traces its origins to the medieval Catalan Corts and its permanent deputation established in the 13th–14th centuries. The institution was abolished in 1714 after the War of the Spanish Succession, restored briefly in 1931–1939 under Francesc Macià and Lluís Companys, suppressed under Franco, and re-established in 1977 with the return from exile of Josep Tarradellas. Post-Statute presidents have included Jordi Pujol (1980–2003), Pasqual Maragall, José Montilla, Artur Mas, Carles Puigdemont, Quim Torra, Pere Aragonès, and Salvador Illa (from 2024).

The 2017 crisis

After the unauthorized independence referendum of 1 October 2017 and the Catalan parliament's declaration of independence, the Spanish government invoked Article 155 of the Constitution, dismissed President Carles Puigdemont and his government, and imposed direct rule until regional elections restored self-government in 2018.
