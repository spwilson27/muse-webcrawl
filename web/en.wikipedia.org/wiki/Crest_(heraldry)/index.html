Crest (heraldry)

A crest sits above the helmet in a full heraldic achievement.

A crest is a component of a heraldic display that consists of the device borne on top of the helm. Originating in the decorative sculptural devices worn by knights in tournaments, the crest became a standard element of a full heraldic achievement, displayed above the shield and helmet and usually resting on a wreath (torse) or coronet.

Origins

Crests originated in the 13th and 14th centuries as three-dimensional ornaments, made of light materials such as leather, wood, or papier-mâché, worn on helmets during tournaments so that the bearer could be recognised. Over time they came to be depicted two-dimensionally in armorial rolls and grants of arms, and became hereditary like other elements of a coat of arms.

Use

In English and Scottish heraldry, the crest is granted as part of the arms and is inherited. In Scottish practice, the crest is also used in the form of a crest badge worn by clan members to show allegiance to their chief. Crests should not be confused with badges, which are separate heraldic devices, or with the shield itself: popular usage that calls a coat of arms a "family crest" is technically incorrect.
