Observatory

An observatory is a facility equipped and staffed for observing natural phenomena, most commonly astronomical events. Astronomical observatories house telescopes and other instruments used by astronomers to study celestial objects and phenomena, and are typically located at high altitude or in remote areas to avoid light pollution and atmospheric turbulence.

History

Early observatories date to antiquity, with structures aligned to solstices and equinoxes. Systematic observatories appeared in the Islamic world and later in Europe; Uraniborg, built by Tycho Brahe in the 16th century, was among the most advanced pre-telescopic observatories. The invention of the telescope transformed observatory design, and institutions such as the Royal Observatory, Greenwich (founded 1675) combined timekeeping, navigation, and research.

Types

  • Optical observatories — reflectors and refractors observing visible light.
  • Radio observatories — arrays of dish antennas detecting radio emission.
  • Space observatories — telescopes in orbit above the atmosphere, such as Hubble and Webb.
  • Other facilities — observatories also exist for volcanology, meteorology, and seismology.

Site selection

Good observing sites combine dark skies, stable air ("seeing"), dry climate, and high elevation. Premier sites include the Atacama Desert in Chile, Mauna Kea in Hawaii, and the Canary Islands.
