Analog-to-digital converter

An analog-to-digital converter (ADC, A/D, or A-to-D) is a system that converts an analog signal, such as a sound picked up by a microphone or light entering a digital camera, into a digital signal. The conversion involves sampling the signal at discrete time intervals and quantising its amplitude into a finite number of levels, usually represented as a binary number proportional to the input voltage.

Resolution and sampling rate

Resolution, expressed in bits, is the number of discrete values the converter can produce: an 8-bit ADC encodes the input into one of 256 levels, while a 16-bit audio converter offers 65,536. The sampling rate must satisfy the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem — at least twice the highest frequency present — to avoid aliasing. Real converters are limited by quantisation error, thermal noise, jitter and nonlinearity, summarised by measures such as signal-to-noise ratio and effective number of bits (ENOB).

Architectures

  • Flash ADC — compares the input against a ladder of reference voltages in parallel; extremely fast but limited to low resolutions.
  • Successive approximation (SAR) — narrows the value by binary search using an internal DAC; a good balance of speed and resolution, common in microcontrollers.
  • Sigma-delta (ΔΣ) — oversamples the signal and shapes quantisation noise; dominates audio and precision measurement at high resolutions (16–24 bits).
  • Pipelined — resolves the signal in stages; used in video and communications at high sample rates.
  • Dual-slope / integrating — very accurate and noise-rejecting but slow; classic choice for digital multimeters.

Applications

ADCs are embedded in virtually every system that interfaces digital processors with the physical world: audio recording and telephony (pulse-code modulation), digital imaging sensors, medical instruments, oscilloscopes, software-defined radio, automotive sensors and scientific data acquisition. The complementary device that converts digital signals back to analog form is the digital-to-analog converter (DAC).
