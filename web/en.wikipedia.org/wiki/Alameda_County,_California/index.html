Alameda County is a county located in the U.S. state of California, in the East Bay region of the San Francisco Bay Area. As of the 2020 census, its population was 1,682,353, making it the seventh-most populous county in the state. Its county seat and largest city is Oakland.

History

The county was formed on 25 March 1853 from parts of Contra Costa and Santa Clara counties. The name derives from Alameda Creek, named by Spanish explorers for its poplar-shaded banks. The region was home to Ohlone peoples for millennia before Spanish missionisation; American settlement accelerated after the Gold Rush, and the completion of the transcontinental railroad at Oakland in 1869 made the county a transportation hub.

Geography and communities

The county extends from San Francisco Bay eastward across the Berkeley and Oakland Hills to the Livermore Valley. Its fourteen incorporated cities include Oakland, Fremont, Hayward, Berkeley, San Leandro, Livermore, Alameda, Pleasanton, Dublin and Union City. It hosts the University of California, Berkeley, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

Government and transport

Alameda County is governed by a five-member Board of Supervisors. Transport includes BART and Amtrak rail, the Port of Oakland, Oakland International Airport, and Interstate highways 80, 580, 680 and 880.
