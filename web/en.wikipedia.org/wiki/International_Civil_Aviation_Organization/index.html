The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations that coordinates the principles and techniques of international air navigation and fosters the planning and development of international air transport to ensure safe and orderly growth. Its headquarters are in Montreal, Canada.

History

ICAO was established by the Convention on International Civil Aviation (the Chicago Convention), signed on 7 December 1944 by 52 states; the organisation came formally into existence on 4 April 1947 when the Convention entered into force. A provisional body, PICAO, operated from 1945. As of the 2020s, 193 of the UN's member states are parties to the Chicago Convention and members of ICAO.

Standards and activities

ICAO's core work is the development of Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs), contained in 19 Annexes to the Chicago Convention, covering subjects such as personnel licensing, rules of the air, aeronautical charts, airworthiness, air traffic services, accident investigation and environmental protection. More than 12,000 standards and practices support the safe, secure and efficient operation of international aviation. ICAO also:

  • allocates the three-letter location codes and four-letter airport codes used in aviation, and the airline designator codes used in flight plans;
  • standardises machine-readable travel documents, including passport specifications;
  • audits member states' safety and security oversight through its Universal Safety Oversight Audit Programme;
  • coordinates global air navigation planning, including the promotion of aviation English as the common language of radiotelephony;
  • administers CORSIA, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, adopted in 2016 to address CO2 emissions from international flights.

Governance

ICAO is governed by an Assembly of all member states, which meets at least once every three years, and a Council of 36 states elected by the Assembly, which sits in near-continuous session and adopts SARPs. The Secretariat, headed by a Secretary General, is organised in bureaux including Air Navigation and Air Transport, supported by seven regional offices.
