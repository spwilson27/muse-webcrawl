Kazimierz Kuratowski

Kazimierz Kuratowski (Polish pronunciation: [kaˈʑimjɛʂ kuraˈtɔfskʲi]; 2 February 1896 – 18 June 1980) was a Polish mathematician and logician. He was one of the leading representatives of the Warsaw School of Mathematics.

Education and career

Kuratowski studied mathematics in Glasgow and Warsaw, earning his doctorate in 1921 under Stefan Mazurkiewicz and Zygmunt Janiszewski. He taught at Lwów Polytechnic and later at the University of Warsaw, and after the Second World War served as director of the Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences.

Work

His best-known result is Kuratowski's theorem, characterising planar graphs as those containing no subdivision of K5 or K3,3. He also gave the closure-complement problem, the Kuratowski–Ulam theorem, and the standard set-theoretic definition of an ordered pair, (x, y) = {{x}, {x, y}}. His two-volume monograph Topology became a standard reference in point-set topology.
