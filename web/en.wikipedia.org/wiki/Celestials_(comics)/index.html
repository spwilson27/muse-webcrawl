Celestials (comics)

The Celestials are fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. They are a race of ancient, enormously powerful cosmic beings, created by Jack Kirby and first appearing in The Eternals #2 (1976). Depicted as towering figures encased in colourful armour, the Celestials travel the universe seeding life and judging the worth of the species they create.

Fictional history

Among the oldest beings in the universe, the Celestials visited Earth millions of years ago and experimented on early hominids, producing three branches of humanity: the godlike Eternals, the monstrous Deviants, and ordinary humans carrying a latent potential for mutation. They returned periodically in "Hosts" to assess their experiments, with the Fourth Host's judgement of Earth forming the climax of Kirby's original Eternals saga. Individual Celestials include Arishem the Judge, who renders verdicts on worlds; Hargen the Measurer; Jemiah the Analyzer; and the sleeping Celestial Tiamut, the Dreaming Celestial, buried beneath California.

Powers and nature

Celestials stand up to 2,000 feet (about 600 metres) tall and possess power on a cosmic scale: energy manipulation, matter restructuring and near-total invulnerability. Their true forms and origins were long a mystery; later stories revealed that they were created by the First Firmament, the personification of the first Marvel cosmos, and that a war among them shattered it into the multiverse. The armour is a containment shell; a slain Celestial's energies were used by other races, and in the 2018 Avengers run the progenitor Celestial's corpse on prehistoric Earth explained the unusual abundance of superhumans on the planet.

In other media

The Celestials appear in the Marvel Cinematic Universe: the severed head of a Celestial forms the space station Knowhere in Guardians of the Galaxy (2014), Ego is revealed as a Celestial in its sequel, and Arishem and Tiamut feature centrally in Eternals (2021), which ends with Tiamut's emergence from the Indian Ocean halted mid-rise.
