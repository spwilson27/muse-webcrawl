Marketing mix

The marketing mix is a framework of the controllable tools a firm blends to produce the response it wants in the target market. It is most commonly summarised as the four Ps: product, price, place and promotion.

The four Ps

Product — the goods or services offered, including design, quality, features and branding. Price — the amount charged, discounts and terms. Place — distribution channels and availability. Promotion — advertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations. The formulation was popularised by E. Jerome McCarthy in 1960, building on Neil Borden's earlier concept of the mix.

Extensions

For services, Booms and Bitner extended the mix to seven Ps by adding people, process and physical evidence. Robert Lauterborn proposed a consumer-oriented four Cs counterpart: customer solution, cost, convenience and communication.
