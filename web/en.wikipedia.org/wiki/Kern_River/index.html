The Kern River is a river in California, about 164 miles (264 km) long. It drains the southern Sierra Nevada, rising in the high country near Mount Whitney and flowing south-west through a deep granite canyon before emerging into the San Joaquin Valley near Bakersfield. It was named by explorer John C. Frémont in 1845 for Edward M. Kern, the artist and topographer of his expedition, after Kern nearly drowned crossing it.

Course

The river's headwaters lie in Sequoia National Park. Its two main upper branches, the North Fork (the "Upper Kern") and the South Fork, cut the Kern Canyon — at places nearly 3,000 feet deep. Below their confluence the river is impounded by Isabella Dam, forming Lake Isabella, a major reservoir for irrigation and flood control. The lower Kern then flows through the Kern River Canyon past Bakersfield, where most of its remaining water is diverted for agriculture and groundwater recharge. In wet years the river historically fed Buena Vista Lake and, through overflow, Tulare Lake.

Recreation and hazards

The Upper Kern is a noted whitewater rafting and kayaking river, and its waters support the Kern River rainbow trout, a subspecies endemic to the basin. The river is also notorious for drownings: steep banks, cold snowmelt water and swift currents near Bakersfield have led local authorities to post an often-quoted count of lives lost since 1968, warning the public that the river is dangerous even where it appears calm.
