7.62×39mm

7.62×39mm
Type: intermediate rifle cartridge
Country of origin: Soviet Union
Associated weapons: AK-47, AKM, SKS, RPD.

The 7.62×39mm is an intermediate cartridge developed in the Soviet Union during the Second World War. It is inseparable from the global history of the Kalashnikov family: the AK-47 and AKM rifles, the SKS carbine, the RPD light machine gun and many later designs were chambered for it. Its tapered steel case and moderate power made it reliable in automatic weapons and effective at the combat ranges Soviet planners considered most likely.

Origins

Soviet designers studied wartime experience showing that most infantry firefights occurred well inside the maximum range of full-power rifle cartridges. Influenced in part by German work on intermediate rounds and by domestic experiments, they produced a cartridge shorter and less powerful than the 7.62×54mmR but far more capable than pistol ammunition. The result balanced controllable automatic fire, useful range and compact magazines.

Design

The rimless, bottlenecked case is 39 mm long and has a pronounced taper that aids extraction, especially in dirty or adverse conditions. Standard ball ammunition typically uses a bullet of about 7.9 g (122 grains) at roughly 715 m/s from a rifle-length barrel. Military production in many countries used steel cases and Berdan primers, while commercial loadings are widely made with brass cases and expanding bullets for hunting and sport.

Influence

Because AK-pattern weapons spread through wars, aid programmes, licensed production and copies, 7.62×39mm became one of the most widely encountered rifle cartridges in the world. China, Yugoslavia, Finland, Egypt and many other states produced weapons or ammunition in the calibre. Although numerous militaries later adopted smaller high-velocity rounds, the cartridge remains common in civilian shooting and in military and irregular service.
