Marie Antoinette watch

The Marie Antoinette watch (Breguet No. 160, the "Grande Complication") is a celebrated pocket watch made by the workshop of Abraham-Louis Breguet. It is often described as the most complicated watch of its era and one of the most valuable timepieces in the world.

Commission and construction

According to tradition, the watch was commissioned in 1783 by an admirer of Queen Marie Antoinette of France—often identified as Count Hans Axel von Fersen—with the stipulations that gold should replace other metals wherever possible, that every known complication should be included, and that there was no limit on cost or time. Work began in 1783 but was interrupted by the French Revolution and Breguet's exile; Marie Antoinette was executed in 1793 without ever seeing it. The watch was finally completed in 1827, four years after Breguet's death, by his son's workshop.

Its complications include a minute repeater, perpetual calendar, equation of time, chronograph, power reserve indicator, thermometer, and a self-winding ("perpétuelle") mechanism, executed in gold with sapphire bearings.

Theft and recovery

The watch entered the collection of the L.A. Mayer Museum for Islamic Art in Jerusalem through the bequest of Sir David Lionel Salomons. In 1983 it was stolen, along with dozens of other watches from the Salomons collection, by master thief Na'aman Diller. It remained missing for more than two decades before being recovered in 2006–2007 after items from the theft surfaced for sale; the watch was returned to the museum, where it is again on display.
