Cesar Chavez (born Cesario Estrada Chavez; March 31, 1927 – April 23, 1993) was an American labor leader and civil rights activist. Along with Dolores Huerta, he co-founded the National Farm Workers Association (NFWA), which later merged to become the United Farm Workers (UFW) labor union. Ideologically, his worldview combined left-wing politics with Roman Catholic social teachings.

Early life

Chavez was born in Yuma, Arizona, to a Mexican American family. After his family lost their farm during the Great Depression, they became migrant farm workers in California, and Chavez left school after the eighth grade to work in the fields full-time. He served in the United States Navy from 1946 to 1948. In 1952 he joined the Community Service Organization (CSO), a Latino civil rights group, where he learned community organizing under Fred Ross and rose to become its national director before leaving in 1962 to organize farm workers.

Farm workers movement

In 1962 Chavez and Huerta founded the NFWA in Delano, California. In 1965 the union joined the Delano grape strike begun by Filipino American workers of the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee, and in 1966 Chavez led a 300-mile march from Delano to Sacramento to draw attention to the strikers' cause. The ensuing nationwide grape boycott gained support from students, churches, and civil rights organizations, and by 1970 most table-grape growers had signed contracts with the union.

Chavez embraced nonviolence, inspired by Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr., and undertook several fasts, including a 25-day fast in 1968 to reaffirm the movement's commitment to nonviolence and a 36-day "Fast for Life" in 1988 to protest pesticide use. The movement's rallying cry, "Sí, se puede" ("Yes, we can"), coined by Huerta, became a lasting slogan of Latino activism.

Later life and legacy

Chavez died in 1993 in San Luis, Arizona, near his birthplace. He was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1994. Cesar Chavez Day is a state holiday in California and several other states, and in 2014 President Barack Obama proclaimed March 31 as a national Cesar Chavez Day. His gravesite and the movement's headquarters at Keene, California, form the César E. Chávez National Monument.
