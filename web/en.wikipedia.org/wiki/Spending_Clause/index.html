Spending Clause

The Spending Clause is the clause of the United States Constitution that grants Congress the power to levy taxes and spend money for the general welfare of the United States. It appears in Article I, Section 8, Clause 1, which provides that "The Congress shall have Power To lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises, to pay the Debts and provide for the common Defence and general Welfare of the United States." Because it authorizes both taxation and spending, the provision is also known as the Taxing and Spending Clause.

Historical background

The clause was adopted at the Constitutional Convention of 1787 against the background of the Articles of Confederation, under which the national government lacked an effective power to raise revenue. The reference to the "general Welfare" sparked an enduring debate between two readings: the view associated with James Madison, that the spending power is limited to the other enumerated powers of Congress, and the broader view associated with Alexander Hamilton, that Congress may spend for any purpose it judges to serve the general welfare, so long as the spending itself is general rather than local in character.

Supreme Court interpretation

In United States v. Butler (1936), the Supreme Court adopted the Hamiltonian reading, holding that the power to tax and spend is not confined to the other enumerated powers, although it struck down the particular statute before it on Tenth Amendment grounds. Later decisions, including Steward Machine Co. v. Davis (1937) and Helvering v. Davis (1937), upheld broad federal spending programs such as Social Security.

In South Dakota v. Dole (1987), the Court articulated limits on Congress's use of conditional spending to influence state policy: the spending must serve the general welfare, conditions must be unambiguous, conditions must be related to the federal interest in the program, and the conditions must not violate other constitutional provisions or coerce the states. The coercion limit was given effect in National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius (2012), where the Court held that threatening states with the loss of all existing Medicaid funding was unconstitutionally coercive.
