Polish science fiction

Polish science fiction is a major tradition within Polish literature, internationally best known through Stanisław Lem, author of Solaris. It has flourished since the late 19th century, often blending philosophy, satire and hard science fiction.

Polish science fiction

History

Early figures include Jerzy Żuławski (On the Silver Globe trilogy, 1901–1911). After WWII, Lem dominated, alongside Janusz A. Zajdel, Edmund Wnuk-Lipiński, Marek Oramus and later Jacek Dukaj. The magazine Fantastyka (founded 1982) nurtured generations, including Andrzej Sapkowski's fantasy.

Themes

Polish SF under communism often used allegory to evade censorship, exploring totalitarianism, contact with aliens and the limits of knowledge.
