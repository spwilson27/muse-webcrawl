Music of Canada

The music of Canada has reflected the diverse influences of its many peoples—Indigenous First Nations, French, British, and later immigrant communities—and has produced internationally successful artists in nearly every genre, from folk and rock to hip-hop and classical music.

History

Indigenous peoples of Canada have musical traditions stretching back thousands of years, centred on drumming, singing, and dance. French settlers brought folk songs and fiddle music; British and American influences followed. In the 20th century, Canadian content regulations (the MAPL system, introduced in 1971) requiring radio stations to play a proportion of Canadian music helped build a domestic industry.

Popular music

Canada has produced major rock and pop acts including Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Leonard Cohen, Rush, The Band, Bryan Adams, Alanis Morissette, Shania Twain, Céline Dion, Michael Bublé, Arcade Fire, and Justin Bieber. Canadian hip-hop artists Drake, The Weeknd, and Kardinal Offishall achieved global success. The Juno Awards, established in 1970, are Canada's principal music awards.

Folk and regional traditions

Cape Breton fiddling, Québécois folk music, Newfoundland folk songs, and the music of the Métis are enduring regional traditions. Stan Rogers and Stompin' Tom Connors became icons of Canadian folk.

Classical and jazz

Canada's orchestras include the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Montreal Symphony Orchestra. Pianist Glenn Gould became one of the most celebrated classical musicians of the 20th century. Jazz figures include Oscar Peterson, Maynard Ferguson, and Diana Krall.
