Greene and Greene

Greene and Greene was an architectural firm established by brothers Charles Sumner Greene (1868–1957) and Henry Mather Greene (1870–1954), influential early 20th century American architects. Active primarily in California, they designed many houses in the American Craftsman style, and are regarded as exemplars of the Arts and Crafts movement in architecture.

Biography

The Greene brothers were born in Brighton, Cincinnati, Ohio, and raised in St. Louis, Missouri. They trained at the Manual Training School of Washington University and then at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. After apprenticeships in Boston, they moved to Pasadena, California, in 1893 to join their parents, and founded their practice there in 1894.

The "ultimate bungalows"

The firm's most celebrated works are the so-called "ultimate bungalows" designed between 1907 and 1909: large, exquisitely crafted houses in which the brothers designed not only the buildings but also the furniture, lighting fixtures, and landscaping. The best known are the Gamble House (1908) in Pasadena, built for David and Mary Gamble of Procter & Gamble, and the Robert R. Blacker House (1907) in Pasadena. Other ultimate bungalows include the Thorsen House in Berkeley and the Freeman Ford House.

Style

Greene and Greene's designs fused influences from Japanese architecture and joinery, Swiss chalets, and English Arts and Crafts design. Signature elements include broad overhanging eaves, exposed rafter tails and structural joinery, sleeping porches, and the extensive use of fine woods such as teak and mahogany, with hand-rubbed finishes. Charles Greene supervised the furniture and decorative details with extraordinary attention to craftsmanship.

Later years and legacy

After World War I, commissions declined and Charles moved to Carmel, California, in 1916, while Henry continued the practice in Pasadena. The partnership dissolved in 1922. Their work was largely forgotten until a revival of interest in the 1950s; the Gamble House is now a National Historic Landmark and museum owned by the City of Pasadena and administered by the University of Southern California.
