The Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine (Ukrainian: Верховна Рада України, "Supreme Council of Ukraine") is the unicameral parliament of Ukraine. It is the country's sole legislative body under the Constitution of Ukraine.

Composition and elections

The Rada consists of 450 People's Deputies elected for five-year terms. Under the electoral system used since 1998 and reinstated after 2014, half of the seats were filled by proportional representation on closed party lists with a 5% threshold and half in single-member constituencies; a later electoral code adopted fully proportional open-list elections, though it had not yet been used in a national election as of the early 2020s. Elections scheduled during martial law following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 were postponed.

Powers

The Verkhovna Rada adopts laws and the state budget, approves the programme of the Cabinet of Ministers, appoints and dismisses certain senior officials, ratifies international treaties, and can override a presidential veto by a two-thirds majority. Constitutional amendments require approval by two-thirds of the deputies. The parliament is headed by a Chairman (speaker), assisted by vice-chairmen.

History

The name derives from the Supreme Soviet of the Ukrainian SSR, whose successor body declared Ukraine's independence in August 1991. The parliament building in Kyiv, completed in 1939, stands on Mykhaila Hrushevskoho Street next to the Mariinskyi Palace.
