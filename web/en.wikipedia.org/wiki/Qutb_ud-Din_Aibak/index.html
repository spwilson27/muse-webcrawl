Qutb ud-Din Aibak (1150–1210) was a Turkic general of the Ghurid sultan Muhammad Ghori who became the first sultan of Delhi and the founder of the Mamluk dynasty (also called the Slave dynasty) of the Delhi Sultanate. A former slave who rose through military talent, he governed the Ghurid conquests in northern India after his master's assassination in 1206 and ruled, nominally from Lahore, until his death in a polo accident in 1210.

Early life and career

Born in Turkestan, Aibak was sold into slavery as a boy and acquired by Muhammad Ghori, whose service he entered and in which he rose to commander and governor. "Qutb ud-Din" is an honorific meaning "Axis of the Faith". Following the Ghurid victory at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192) over Prithviraj Chauhan, Aibak was left in charge of the Indian territories and extended Ghurid control over Delhi, Ajmer, Meerut and other centres, campaigning in the Doab and against the Gahadavalas of Kannauj.

Sultan of Delhi

On Muhammad Ghori's death in 1206, Aibak was raised to power at Lahore and received formal manumission and recognition as ruler. His four-year reign was spent consolidating the north Indian conquests and managing relations with rival Ghurid successors in Ghazna and Khwarizm. In 1210 he died after falling from his horse while playing chaugan (polo) at Lahore. He was succeeded briefly by Aram Shah, then by his son-in-law and fellow former slave Iltutmish, under whom the sultanate took firm shape.

Building and reputation

Aibak began two of India's most famous monuments: the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque in Delhi and the Qutub Minar victory tower (completed by Iltutmish), as well as the Adhai Din ka Jhonpra mosque in Ajmer. Persian chroniclers praised his generosity—his nickname Lakhbaksh, "giver of lakhs", reflected largesse to scholars and the poor.
