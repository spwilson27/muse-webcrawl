Aerobatics

A competition aerobatic aircraft performing at an airshow.

Aerobatics is the practice of flying manoeuvres involving aircraft attitudes that are not used in conventional passenger-carrying flights — including loops, rolls, spins, hammerheads, and combinations judged in competition or performed for display at airshows. Aerobatic flying demands specially strengthened aircraft, precise energy management, and rigorous training, and pilots may experience loads from −10 to +10 g or more.

Manoeuvres

Basic figures include the loop, aileron and barrel rolls, the Immelmann turn, split S, Cuban eight, stall turn (hammerhead), and deliberate spins. Advanced unlimited figures add vertical rolls, snap (flick) rolls, tailslides, and tumbling gyroscopic manoeuvres such as the Lomcovák. Sequences are notated with the Aresti system of symbols and flown within a marked "aerobatic box" one kilometre square.

Competition

Competitions are governed internationally by the FAI Aerobatics Commission (CIVA) and nationally by bodies such as the International Aerobatic Club in the United States and the British Aerobatic Association. Classes from Sportsman to Unlimited culminate in the World Aerobatic Championships, flown in aircraft such as the Extra 300, MXS, and Sukhoi Su-26/31 series. Aerobatics also survives as a racing spectacle in events descended from the Red Bull Air Race.

History

The first loop is credited to Pyotr Nesterov in 1913; wartime dogfighting in 1914–1918 spread manoeuvres such as the Immelmann, and interwar barnstormers turned them into public entertainment. Military pilots still train in basic aerobatics, and display teams — the Red Arrows, Blue Angels, and others — perform formation aerobatics worldwide.

Safety

Because aerobatics are often flown at low altitude with high energy, accidents have historically been a leading cause of airshow fatalities; modern regulation imposes minimum altitudes, crowd-line distances, and pilot competency evaluations.
