Enid

Enid is a city in Garfield County, Oklahoma, United States. It is the county seat and, with a population of roughly 50,000, one of the larger cities in northwestern Oklahoma. Enid serves as a regional centre for wheat farming and energy, and is home to Vance Air Force Base on its southern edge.

History

Enid was founded during the Cherokee Outlet land run of September 1893. Its name, according to popular tradition, was taken from Enid, a character in Alfred, Lord Tennyson's Idylls of the King. A famous early dispute over the townsite's railroad station — the Rock Island line built its depot some distance from the platted town — led townspeople to build their own station; in the ensuing conflict a train was ditched, giving rise to Enid's local "railroad war" lore. Enid grew as a wheat-shipping and milling centre, was briefly an oil boom town in the 1920s, and later developed grain storage and processing industries so extensive that it was sometimes called the "Wheat Capital" of the region.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the eastern edge of the Great Plains, amid wheat fields and pasture. Agriculture, oil and gas, and the nearby air base anchor the economy; Phillips University, a private Christian university, operated in Enid from 1907 until its closure in 1998, while Northern Oklahoma College maintains a campus in the city.

Culture

Enid hosts the Cherokee Strip Regional Heritage Center, commemorating the 1893 land run, and the annual Tri-State Music Festival. Notable natives include astronaut Owen Garriott, author Marcia Brown and Pulitzer Prize–winning poet Jorie Graham spent part of her career nearby; the singer and actor Michael Hedges was born in Enid.
