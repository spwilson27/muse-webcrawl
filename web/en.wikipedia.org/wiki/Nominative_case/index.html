Nominative case

In grammar, the nominative case (abbreviated NOM) is one of the grammatical cases of a noun or other part of speech, which generally marks the subject of a verb or the predicate noun or predicate adjective — as opposed to its object or other verb arguments. Generally, the noun "that is doing something" is in the nominative, and the nominative is often the form listed in dictionaries.

Characteristics

The nominative case is the citation form of nouns in many languages: the form used in dictionaries and word lists, and the form in which a noun appears when no other case is called for. In Latin, for example, the first declension noun puella ("girl") is the nominative singular form under which the word is conventionally listed. In English, nouns do not have a distinct nominative form, but personal pronouns do: I, he, she, we, and they are nominative, contrasted with the oblique forms me, him, her, us, and them.

The nominative is also the case used for a noun predicated of the subject in a copular sentence, as in Latin Caesar est imperātor ("Caesar is the commander"), where both Caesar and imperātor appear in the nominative.

History

The term derives from Latin cāsus nōminātīvus, "case of naming," itself a translation of the Greek ptôsis onomastikḗ. In the grammatical tradition descending from Dionysius Thrax, the nominative was regarded as the "upright" case (orthḗ), from which the other, "oblique," cases were thought to fall away — the origin of the word "case" itself, from Latin cāsus, "a falling."

Nominative–accusative alignment

Languages in which the nominative marks both the subject of an intransitive verb and the agent of a transitive verb, while the patient of a transitive verb takes a different (accusative) case, are said to show nominative–accusative alignment. This is the most common alignment type in the world's languages and is standard across Indo-European languages, in contrast to ergative–absolutive alignment found in languages such as Basque and many languages of the Caucasus and Australia.
