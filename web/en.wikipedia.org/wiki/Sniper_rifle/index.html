Sniper rifle

A sniper rifle is a high-precision, long-range rifle designed for use by a sniper. Built on high accuracy requirements, sniper rifles are fitted with a telescopic sight and chambered in specialist high-performance military calibres, such as 7.62×51mm NATO, .300 Winchester Magnum, .338 Lapua Magnum or .50 BMG for anti-materiel roles. The term is often used interchangeably with "precision rifle" and "marksman rifle", though a designated marksman rifle is typically a semi-automatic filling a mid-range role within an infantry squad.

Characteristics

Sniper rifles differ from standard service rifles in features such as heavy free-floating barrels for consistent harmonics, adjustable stocks with cheek risers, match-grade triggers, bipods, threaded muzzles for suppressors, and rails for optics and night-vision devices. Accuracy is commonly specified in minutes of angle (MOA), with true sniper rifles expected to group under 1 MOA — often around 0.5 MOA — with match ammunition. Rifles are usually bolt-action for rigidity and reliability, though semi-automatic systems such as the M110 and Mk 13's counterparts are widely used.

History

Scoped precision rifles emerged in the American Civil War and the Boer War; all major armies fielded sniper variants of service rifles in the World Wars (e.g. the scoped Mosin–Nagant, Karabiner 98k and Lee-Enfield No. 4 Mk I (T)). Purpose-built designs followed: the Soviet SVD (Dragunov) of 1963 pioneered the semi-automatic marksman concept, while the US Marine Corps' M40 and the British L96/AWM (Accuracy International Arctic Warfare) established the modern bolt-action pattern. Very-long-range records during the conflicts in Afghanistan and Iraq stretched engagement distances beyond 2,400 m.

Calibres and roles

  • 7.62×51mm / .308 Winchester — classic intermediate-range cartridge, effective to ~800 m.
  • .300 Winchester Magnum and .338 Lapua Magnum — extended range to ~1,200–1,500 m.
  • 6.5mm Creedmoor — adopted by several special-operations forces for flatter trajectory and low recoil.
  • .50 BMG — anti-materiel rifles (e.g. Barrett M82) for vehicles, equipment and extreme range.

Civilian use

Precision rifles closely related to military designs are widely used in civilian long-range target competition (such as the Precision Rifle Series and F-Class) and in varmint and deer hunting.
