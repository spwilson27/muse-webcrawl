Influencer marketing

Influencer marketing is a form of social media marketing involving endorsements and product placement from influencers — people regarded as having expert knowledge or social influence in their field, typically with a substantial following on platforms such as Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. Brands pay or otherwise compensate influencers to promote products to their audiences, trading on the trust and parasocial relationships creators build with followers.

Categories of influencers

Influencers are often classified by follower count: mega-influencers and celebrities (millions of followers), macro-influencers, micro-influencers (roughly 10,000–100,000), and nano-influencers with small but highly engaged audiences. Marketing research often finds engagement rates decline as audience size grows, making smaller creators attractive for niche products.

Regulation

Advertising regulators in many countries — including the US Federal Trade Commission and the UK Advertising Standards Authority — require clear disclosure of paid partnerships (e.g. "#ad") because undisclosed endorsements are considered deceptive. Enforcement actions and guidance have tightened as the industry has grown.

Criticism

The field has faced criticism over fraudulent followers and engagement, undisclosed advertising, promotion of unhealthy products to young audiences, and high-profile failures such as the Fyre Festival promotion. "Virtual influencers" — computer-generated personas — have also drawn debate.

Scale

Influencer marketing grew from a niche practice in the 2010s into a global industry valued in the tens of billions of dollars annually.
