The Inuit Circumpolar Council (ICC; Inuit: Inuit Issittormiut Siunnersuisooqatigiiffiat) is a multinational non-governmental organization representing the approximately 180,000 Inuit of Alaska (United States), Canada, Greenland, and Chukotka (Russia). Founded in 1977 by Eben Hopson of Barrow, Alaska, as the Inuit Circumpolar Conference, it works to promote Inuit rights and interests on an international level and to strengthen unity among Inuit across national borders.

History

The first Inuit Circumpolar Conference was held in Barrow (now Utqiaġvik), Alaska, in June 1977, bringing together delegates from Alaska, Canada, and Greenland; Soviet restrictions prevented Chukotka Inuit from attending until 1992. The organization adopted its current name, Inuit Circumpolar Council, in 2006. Its general assembly meets every four years in different Inuit communities, electing an executive council and chair; the assemblies have been held in locations including Nuuk, Inuvik, Kuujjuaq, and Ilulissat.

Work and status

The ICC holds consultative status with the United Nations Economic and Social Council and is a Permanent Participant of the Arctic Council, the intergovernmental forum of Arctic states. It advocates on issues including climate change and its effects on sea ice and subsistence hunting, Arctic shipping, contaminants transported to the Arctic, language and cultural preservation (including Inuktitut and related languages), and Inuit self-determination. It issued the Circumpolar Inuit Declaration on Sovereignty in the Arctic in 2009 and a Declaration on Inuit Rights in 2022. Prominent leaders have included Sheila Watt-Cloutier, who chaired the ICC from 2002 to 2006 and campaigned internationally on climate change and persistent organic pollutants.

Member organizations

National members include ICC Alaska, ICC Canada, ICC Greenland, and the Association of Indigenous Peoples of Chukotka. The ICC has international offices and a rotating chairmanship among the member countries.
