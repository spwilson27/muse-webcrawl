Seuthopolis

Seuthopolis (Ancient Greek: Σευθόπολις) was an ancient hellenistic-type city founded by the Thracian king Seuthes III between 325 and 315 BC in what is now Bulgaria. Its ruins lie at the bottom of the Koprinka Reservoir in the Valley of the Thracian Rulers, near the modern town of Kazanlak.

History

Seuthopolis was built on the site of an earlier settlement and served as the capital of the Odrysian kingdom under Seuthes III. The city was constructed according to a Hippodamian grid plan, with straight streets intersecting at right angles, and was surrounded by a fortified wall about 890 metres long. It contained an agora, temples, and a palace complex on its acropolis-like citadel in the northeastern part of the city.

The city was short-lived: it was abandoned after only a few decades, around 270 BC, probably following a flood or an invasion. Its ruins remained largely undisturbed until the 20th century.

Excavations

Seuthopolis was excavated between 1948 and 1954 during the construction of the Koprinka Reservoir (then named the Georgi Dimitrov Reservoir). The excavations, led by Dimitar P. Dimitrov, yielded rich finds, including inscriptions, pottery, coins, and architectural remains. A long Greek inscription found in the bath complex of the palace provided important information about the dynasty of Seuthes III and his successors.

After the reservoir was filled, the city was submerged. In the 21st century, projects were proposed to drain or isolate the site within a circular dam so that Seuthopolis could be exposed again as a tourist attraction, though these plans have not been realised.
