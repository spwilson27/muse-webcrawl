Apollonius's theorem

In geometry, Apollonius's theorem relates the length of a median of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. It states that the sum of the squares of any two sides of a triangle equals twice the square of the median to the third side plus twice the square of half the third side.

Statement

For a triangle with sides a, b, c and median m drawn to side a, the theorem gives: b² + c² = 2m² + 2(a/2)². It is a special case of Stewart's theorem and can be proved using the law of cosines or vectors.

Relation to the parallelogram law

Apollonius's theorem is equivalent to the parallelogram law: doubling the triangle along its median produces a parallelogram whose diagonal relation yields the result. In the special case of a right triangle, it reduces to a form of the Pythagorean theorem. The theorem is named after Apollonius of Perga, the ancient Greek geometer famous for his work on conic sections.
