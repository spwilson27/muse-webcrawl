Harper Lee

Nelle Harper Lee (April 28, 1926 – February 19, 2016) was an American novelist whose 1960 novel To Kill a Mockingbird won the 1961 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction and became a classic of modern American literature. Lee received numerous accolades and honorary degrees in recognition of her single famous work, including the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2007. In 2015 an earlier draft of the novel, Go Set a Watchman, was published as a sequel, generating considerable controversy.

Early life

Lee was born in Monroeville, Alabama, the youngest of four children of Amasa Coleman Lee, a lawyer and state legislator, and Frances Cunningham Finch. Her childhood friend and neighbour was the future writer Truman Capote, on whom she based the character Dill in To Kill a Mockingbird; Capote in turn based the character Idabel in Other Voices, Other Rooms on her. She studied law at the University of Alabama but left before completing her degree, moving to New York City in 1949 to pursue writing while working as an airline reservations clerk. In 1956 her friends Michael and Joy Brown gave her a year's wages as a Christmas gift so she could write full-time.

To Kill a Mockingbird

Working with editor Tay Hohoff at J. B. Lippincott, Lee spent two and a half years revising her manuscript. Published on 11 July 1960, To Kill a Mockingbird — narrated by the young Scout Finch and centred on her father Atticus Finch's defence of a Black man falsely accused of rape in a segregated Southern town — was an immediate bestseller. It won the Pulitzer Prize in 1961, and the 1962 film adaptation starring Gregory Peck won three Academy Awards. The novel has sold more than 40 million copies and is widely read in schools.

Later life and Go Set a Watchman

Lee assisted Capote with the research for his book In Cold Blood (1966) but published almost nothing further for decades, living quietly between Monroeville and New York and shunning publicity. In February 2015 it was announced that an unpublished early draft, Go Set a Watchman — written in the mid-1950s and featuring an adult Scout confronting a segregationist Atticus — would be published that July. The book's release prompted debate about Lee's capacity to consent, though investigations found no evidence of coercion. Lee died in her sleep in Monroeville on 19 February 2016.
