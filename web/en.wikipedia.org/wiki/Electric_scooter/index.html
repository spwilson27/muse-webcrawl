Electric scooter

The term electric scooter covers two kinds of vehicle: motorised motor scooters powered by an electric motor, and lightweight stand-up kick scooters fitted with an electric motor, often called e-scooters. Both use rechargeable batteries and have become common forms of urban transport.

Stand-up e-scooters

Motorised stand-up scooters became widely popular from 2017 with the launch of dockless scooter-sharing services such as Bird and Lime, which placed fleets of rentable scooters in cities worldwide, unlocked by smartphone app. Typical models have a range of 25–45 km and a top speed limited to about 25 km/h. Their rapid spread prompted cities to regulate parking, speed and use on pavements, and raised safety debates concerning riders, pedestrians and road traffic.

Seated electric scooters

Electric versions of the classic step-through motor scooter (in the Vespa style) are especially common in Asia. Manufacturers such as NIU, Yadea and Gogoro sell millions of units, used for commuting and delivery work; in China they form the largest class of electric two-wheelers.
