Dan Boneh

Dan Boneh (born 1969) is an Israeli-American professor and cryptographer at Stanford University, where he holds the Rajeev Motwani Professorship in Computer Science. He is one of the leading researchers in applied cryptography and computer security, widely known for his work on pairing-based cryptography, identity-based encryption, and secure systems.

Education and career

Boneh received his B.Sc. in computer science from the Technion — Israel Institute of Technology in 1989, and his Ph.D. from Princeton University in 1996 under the supervision of Richard Lipton. After a postdoctoral position, he joined the Stanford University faculty, where he heads the applied cryptography group.

Research

With Matthew K. Franklin, Boneh developed a practical identity-based encryption scheme from the Weil pairing, a work that helped launch the field of pairing-based cryptography. He also co-developed, with Xavier Boyen, the Boneh–Boyen short signature scheme, and with Ben Lynn and Hovav Shacham the BLS short signature scheme, which is used in many blockchain systems. His research spans cryptanalysis, secure voting, web security, and side-channel attacks; he co-developed timing attack techniques against cryptographic implementations. Boneh is also known for his massive open online course on cryptography.

Awards

Boneh received the 2014 ACM Prize in Computing (the ACM Infosys Foundation Award) for contributions to the theory and practice of cryptography and security. He has also received the Gödel Prize, the RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics, and a MacArthur Foundation grant for early work. He is a fellow of the ACM and a member of the National Academy of Engineering.
