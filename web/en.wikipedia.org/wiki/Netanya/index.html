Netanya

Netanya (Hebrew: נְתַנְיָה, lit. "God has given") is a city in the Central District of Israel, and the capital of the Sharon plain. It is located between Poleg stream and Wingate Institute in the south and Avihayil stream in the north, on the Mediterranean coast. Its 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) of beaches have made the city a popular tourist resort. The city is known as the "Israeli Riviera".

History

Netanya was founded in 1929 by citrus growers of the Bnei Binyamin association as a moshava (farming village). It was named after Nathan Straus, an American Jewish philanthropist and co-owner of Macy's department store, in the hope of receiving a donation; when this did not materialize as expected, the name, similar to the Hebrew phrase meaning "God gave", was retained. The city grew rapidly after Israeli independence in 1948, absorbing many Jewish immigrants, and became a center of the diamond polishing industry as well as tourism.

Geography

The city sits on limestone cliffs overlooking the Mediterranean Sea. The Poleg Nature Reserve lies to its south, and the winter pond (brekha) and iris reserve are local natural attractions. Netanya is located about 30 km north of Tel Aviv.

Economy and tourism

Tourism and services dominate the local economy, with numerous hotels along the beachfront. The city is home to the Wingate Institute, Israel's national center for physical education and sport, and to Netanya Academic College. Maccabi Netanya is the city's principal football club.
