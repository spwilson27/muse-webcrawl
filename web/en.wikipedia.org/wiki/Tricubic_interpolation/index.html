Tricubic interpolation

In mathematics, tricubic interpolation is a method for obtaining values at arbitrary points in three-dimensional space from data defined on a regular grid, by fitting a cubic polynomial within each grid cell. It is the three-dimensional extension of bicubic interpolation and cubic Hermite interpolation.

Method

Within a unit cube, the interpolant is a polynomial of the form p(x,y,z) = Σi,j,k≤3 aijk xi yj zk, with 64 coefficients. These are determined by matching, at the eight corners of the cube, the function values, the three first derivatives, the three mixed second derivatives, and the third mixed derivative — or by deriving the needed derivatives from the corner values with finite differences (the Catmull–Rom style approach). Tricubic interpolation is C1-continuous across cell boundaries, giving smoother results than trilinear interpolation, at greater computational cost.

Applications

Tricubic interpolation is used in medical imaging (resampling CT and MRI volumes), volume rendering, fluid simulation, and texture mapping in computer graphics, wherever smooth reconstruction of volumetric data is needed.
