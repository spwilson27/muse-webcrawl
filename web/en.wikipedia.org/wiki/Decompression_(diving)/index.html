Decompression (diving)

Decompression in diving is the reduction of ambient pressure experienced by a diver during ascent, and the controlled elimination of dissolved inert gases (mainly nitrogen) from the body's tissues.

If a diver ascends too quickly, dissolved gas can form bubbles in tissues and blood, causing decompression sickness ("the bends"). Divers therefore follow decompression schedules — staged stops at set depths — or no-stop limits derived from decompression models and tables, today usually computed by a dive computer.

History

Early decompression research was driven by caisson workers and naval diving; J.S. Haldane published the first decompression tables in 1908.
