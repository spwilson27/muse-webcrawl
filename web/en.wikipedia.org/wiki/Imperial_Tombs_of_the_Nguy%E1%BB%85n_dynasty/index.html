LocationHuế, Vietnam
Built19th–20th centuries

Tomb of Emperor Khải Định

The Imperial Tombs of the Nguyễn dynasty are a group of mausoleums built for the emperors of the Nguyễn dynasty (1802–1945) in and around Huế, the imperial capital of Vietnam. Scattered along the banks of the Perfume River and in the surrounding hills southwest of the citadel, the tombs combine Vietnamese architectural traditions with feng shui principles and, in later examples, French influences.

Principal tombs

The best known tombs include those of Gia Long, the dynasty's founder; Minh Mạng, whose tomb is noted for its harmonious layout of pavilions, temples, and lakes; Thiệu Trị; Tự Đức, whose extensive complex includes a palace where the emperor lived and worked during his lifetime; Dục Đức; Đồng Khánh; and Khải Định, the last tomb built, an eclectic structure of reinforced concrete blending Vietnamese and Western styles with elaborate glass and porcelain mosaics.

Design

Each tomb typically consists of two main parts: the tomb area itself, containing the burial enclosure and stele pavilion housing a biography of the emperor composed by his successor, and a temple area for worship, with courtyards lined by stone statues of mandarins, elephants, and horses. Sites were chosen according to geomantic principles, with water features such as lakes and canals playing a central role in the landscape design.

Heritage status

The tombs form part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993. The sites suffered damage during the wars of the twentieth century but have undergone extensive restoration and are among Vietnam's most visited historical attractions.
