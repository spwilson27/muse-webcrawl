Charles X (born Charles Philippe, Count of Artois; 9 October 1757 – 6 November 1836) was King of France from 1824 until his abdication in 1830 in the July Revolution. The younger brother of Louis XVI and Louis XVIII, he was the last French monarch of the senior Bourbon line to rule.

Life and reign

As Count of Artois, he was a leader of the ultra-royalist émigrés during the Revolution and Napoleonic Wars. Succeeding his brother Louis XVIII in 1824, his lavish medieval-style coronation at Reims signalled reactionary intent. His government under the Prince de Polignac indemnified émigrés, passed an Anti-Sacrilege Act, and restricted the press. In 1830 French forces invaded and began the conquest of Algeria.

July Revolution

Charles's July Ordinances of 25 July 1830 suspended press freedom and dissolved the newly elected chamber, provoking the "Trois Glorieuses" uprising in Paris. He abdicated on 2 August in favour of his grandson the Duke of Bordeaux, but the throne passed instead to Louis Philippe. Charles died in exile at Gorizia in 1836.
