Eric Allan Dolphy Jr. (June 20, 1928 – June 29, 1964) was an American jazz multi-instrumentalist and bandleader. Primarily a player of the alto saxophone, bass clarinet, and flute, Dolphy was one of several multi-instrumentalists to gain prominence during the same era. His use of the bass clarinet helped to establish the instrument within jazz, and he extended the vocabulary and boundaries of the alto saxophone more than any other musician of his generation.

Early life

Dolphy was born in Los Angeles to Eric Allan Dolphy Sr. and Sadie Dolphy, immigrants from Panama. He took up the clarinet at age six and the oboe a year later, and by his teens he was playing alto saxophone in Los Angeles clubs. He studied at Los Angeles City College and played in the band of drummer Roy Porter.

Career

Dolphy first gained national attention as a member of Chico Hamilton's quintet in 1958. Moving to New York, he became closely associated with Charles Mingus, John Coltrane, and Ornette Coleman. His album Out to Lunch! (1964), recorded for Blue Note Records, is regarded as one of the masterpieces of avant-garde jazz, featuring compositions such as "Hat and Beard" and "Something Sweet, Something Tender".

Dolphy died in Berlin in 1964 from complications of diabetes that had gone undiagnosed. His angular melodic lines, wide intervallic leaps, and interest in sounds resembling human speech remain hugely influential on later saxophonists and clarinettists.

Discography (selected)

Studio albums include Outward Bound (1960), Out There (1960), Far Cry (1960), Out to Lunch! (1964), and the posthumous Iron Man and Conversations (1963 recordings).
