Cuyahoga River

Placeholder image: Cuyahoga River

The Cuyahoga River is a river located in Northeast Ohio that bisects the City of Cleveland and feeds into Lake Erie. It is about 85 miles (137 km) long and drains roughly 813 square miles. Its name is believed to derive from an Iroquoian or Mohawk word meaning "crooked river," reflecting its U-shaped course: it flows southwest from its headwaters in Geauga County, then turns sharply northwest through Akron and the Cuyahoga Valley to Cleveland.

History

The river was historically significant as part of the boundary in the Treaty of Greenville (1795) and as a transportation corridor; the Ohio and Erie Canal paralleled its lower course in the 1820s–30s. Industrial growth in Cleveland and Akron made it a working river lined with steel mills, refineries, and factories.

Pollution and fires

The Cuyahoga became notorious for catching fire due to oil and industrial debris floating on its surface. Documented fires occurred at least 13 times, beginning in 1868; a major 1952 fire caused over a million dollars in damage. The fire of June 22, 1969, though relatively small, attracted national attention after coverage in Time magazine and became a symbol of American water pollution, helping spur the environmental movement, the creation of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (1970), and passage of the Clean Water Act (1972) and Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement.

Recovery

Decades of cleanup, wastewater treatment improvements, and dam removals have greatly improved water quality; fish species returned, and in 2019 the Ohio EPA declared fish from the river safe to eat. The Cuyahoga Valley National Park, established as a national recreation area in 1974 and redesignated a national park in 2000, protects a 33,000-acre stretch of river valley between Cleveland and Akron. Removals of dams, including the Gorge Dam project, are intended to restore free-flowing conditions on the middle river.
