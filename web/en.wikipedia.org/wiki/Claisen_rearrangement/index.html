Claisen rearrangement

Claisen rearrangement
Reaction typeRearrangement reaction ([3,3]-sigmatropic)
Discovered byRainer Ludwig Claisen, 1912
VariantsIreland–Claisen, Johnson–Claisen, Eschenmoser–Claisen, aromatic Claisen

The Claisen rearrangement is a powerful carbon–carbon bond forming reaction in organic chemistry, discovered by Rainer Ludwig Claisen in 1912. It is a [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement in which an allyl vinyl ether, heated, rearranges to a γ,δ-unsaturated carbonyl compound; the aromatic version converts an allyl phenyl ether into an ortho-allyl phenol.

Mechanism

The reaction is a concerted, pericyclic process passing through a chair-like six-membered transition state. Because the rearrangement is suprafacial on both components, the stereochemistry of the starting allyl fragment is transferred predictably to the new carbon–carbon bond, making the reaction highly valuable in stereoselective synthesis. It is driven by formation of the strong carbonyl bond and is essentially irreversible.

Variants

Several major variants extend its scope: the Johnson–Claisen rearrangement (allylic alcohols with triethyl orthoacetate give γ,δ-unsaturated esters), the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement (silyl ketene acetals from allyl esters rearrange at low temperature, enabling enolate geometry control), and the Eschenmoser–Claisen rearrangement (producing γ,δ-unsaturated amides). Related aza-Claisen rearrangements introduce nitrogen. The Claisen rearrangement is also performed in nature: the enzyme chorismate mutase catalyzes a Claisen rearrangement of chorismate to prephenate in the biosynthesis of aromatic amino acids — a classic example of enzymatic catalysis of a pericyclic reaction.
