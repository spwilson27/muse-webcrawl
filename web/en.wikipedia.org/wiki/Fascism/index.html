Fascism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fascism is a far-right, authoritarian and ultranationalist political ideology and movement characterised by a dictatorial leader, centralised autocracy, militarism, forcible suppression of opposition, belief in a natural social hierarchy, subordination of individual interests to the perceived good of the nation or race, and strong regimentation of society and the economy. It rose to prominence in early-20th-century Europe, first in Italy under Benito Mussolini (in power 1922–1943) and most destructively in Nazi Germany.

Origins and rise

Fascism emerged from the upheaval of the First World War, combining revolutionary nationalism, anti-communism, anti-liberalism and veterans' paramilitarism. Mussolini's Fasci Italiani di Combattimento (1919) took power after the March on Rome (1922); fascist or fascist-influenced regimes followed in Germany (1933), Spain (after civil war, 1939) and elsewhere. The movements glorified violence, youth and national rebirth, and mobilised mass parties, propaganda and spectacle.

Definitions

Scholars debate a generic definition. Roger Griffin's influential account identifies fascism's core as palingenetic ultranationalism — the myth of national rebirth. Robert Paxton defines it through "mobilising passions" and stages of development; Umberto Eco listed features of "Ur-Fascism". Definitions matter because the label is also used loosely in polemics; historians distinguish fascism proper from other authoritarianisms (conservative, military or clerical regimes) by its revolutionary, mass-mobilising character.

Practice and consequences

In power, fascist regimes abolished independent parties, unions and press freedom, built police states, pursued imperial expansion and, in the Nazi case, perpetrated the Holocaust and a war of annihilation in Europe. The defeat of the Axis in 1945 discredited fascism, though neo-fascist movements persisted at the margins, and the term remains central to debates about extremism, populism and democratic backsliding.
