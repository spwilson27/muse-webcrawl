Hurricane Florence

Hurricane Florence was a powerful and long-lived Cape Verde hurricane that caused catastrophic flooding in the Carolinas in September 2018. Florence was the sixth named storm, third hurricane, and first major hurricane of the 2018 Atlantic hurricane season. It made landfall near Wrightsville Beach, North Carolina, on 14 September 2018 as a Category 1 hurricane, and its slow movement thereafter produced record-breaking rainfall and river flooding.

Meteorological history

Florence developed from a tropical wave off the west coast of Africa on 31 August–1 September 2018 near Cape Verde. Moving westward across the Atlantic, it intensified rapidly and reached peak strength as a Category 4 hurricane with maximum sustained winds of 150 mph (240 km/h) and a minimum pressure of 937 mbar while still far out at sea — unusually, it weakened to a tropical storm mid-ocean before re-strengthening. As it approached the United States East Coast, wind shear and an eyewall replacement cycle weakened it, but the storm's expanding wind field and slow forward speed greatly increased its storm surge and rainfall potential.

Impacts

Florence caused at least 54 deaths and an estimated US$24 billion in damage, mostly in the Carolinas. Rainfall totals exceeded 35 inches (890 mm) in parts of North Carolina, setting state records; the Cape Fear, Lumber, and other rivers crested at record levels, flooding towns such as Lumberton and inundating portions of Interstate 95. Floodwaters overtopped coal-ash ponds and swine-waste lagoons, raising contamination concerns. Because of the damage, the name Florence was retired by the World Meteorological Organization in 2019 and replaced with Francine.
