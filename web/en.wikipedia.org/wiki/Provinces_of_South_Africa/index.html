Provinces of South Africa

South Africa is divided into nine provinces. The current provincial boundaries date from the end of apartheid: on 27 April 1994, the day of the first democratic elections, the four former provinces and the ten Bantustans were reorganised into nine provinces under the interim constitution, retained in the 1996 Constitution.

Government

Each province has its own legislature and provincial government headed by a premier. Provinces have exclusive competence over matters such as provincial roads, health and education (shared concurrently with the national government in several areas), and they are subdivided into metropolitan and district municipalities.

Geography and economy

Gauteng, the smallest province by area, is the economic heartland of the country and its most populous province. The Northern Cape is the largest by area but the most sparsely populated. KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng vie for the largest population, each with well over ten million residents.
