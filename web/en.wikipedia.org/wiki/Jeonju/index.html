Jeonju (Korean: 전주, 全州) is the capital and largest city of North Jeolla Province, South Korea. It is both urban and rural due to the closeness of Wanju County, which almost entirely surrounds Jeonju. The city has a population of around 650,000 and is an important tourist centre famous for Korean food, its historic buildings, sports activities and innovative festivals.

History

Jeonju was the capital of the Later Baekje kingdom, founded by Gyeon Hwon in 892, and an important regional centre under the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties. The Joseon dynasty's founder, Yi Seong-gye (King Taejo), came from the Jeonju Yi clan, and the city houses Gyeonggijeon Shrine, which preserves his portrait. In 1894 the Donghak Peasant Revolution began in the region, and rebel forces occupied Jeonju Castle before their defeat.

Culture and food

Jeonju is widely regarded as a capital of Korean gastronomy: in 2012 it was designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. Its most famous dish is Jeonju bibimbap, rice mixed with vegetables, beef and gochujang, traditionally served in a brass bowl. The Jeonju Hanok Village contains over 800 traditional Korean houses (hanok) and draws millions of visitors. The city also hosts the Jeonju International Film Festival, held annually since 2000.

Sport

Jeonju is home to the Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors football club, one of the most successful teams in the K League, which plays at the Jeonju World Cup Stadium, built for the 2002 FIFA World Cup.
