Graham (hill)

A Graham is a hill in Scotland with a height between 600 and 762 metres (1,969 and 2,500 feet) above sea level and a prominence of at least 150 metres (492 feet). The list of Grahams is one of several classifications of hills used by hillwalkers in the British Isles, alongside the Munros, Corbetts, and Marilyns.

History

The list was drawn up by Alan Dawson and named after Fiona Torbet (née Graham), who had compiled a similar list. The Grahams were first published in Dawson's book The Relative Hills of Britain (1992). Following a resurvey and a revision of the height band in 2022, the list comprises 219 hills; the qualifying band was widened from the original 2,000–2,499 feet to 600–762 metres.

Characteristics

Because Grahams must have a prominence of at least 150 metres, every Graham is also a Marilyn. They are distributed across the Scottish Highlands and Islands, and many are less frequented than the higher Munros (hills over 3,000 feet). Completing a round of all the Grahams is a recognised hillwalking challenge, though less celebrated than a round of the Munros.
