The House of Hohenzollern is a German dynasty of princes, electors, kings and emperors, originating from the Swabian counts of Zollern in the 11th century. Its Brandenburg-Prussian branch ruled Prussia and, from 1871 to 1918, the German Empire. A cadet branch, Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, ruled Romania from 1866 to 1947.

Origins

The family takes its name from Hohenzollern Castle near Hechingen in Swabia, first mentioned in 1061. The counts of Zollern rose through service to the Holy Roman Emperors. In 1415, Frederick VI of Nuremberg, a Hohenzollern burgrave, was enfeoffed with the Margraviate of Brandenburg, becoming Elector Frederick I — the foundation of the family's northern power.

Brandenburg-Prussia

Under the "Great Elector" Frederick William (r. 1640–1688), Brandenburg was rebuilt after the Thirty Years' War and its standing army created. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia as Frederick I at Königsberg. Frederick II ("the Great", r. 1740–1786) made Prussia a European great power through the Silesian Wars and the Seven Years' War. The Prussian Hohenzollerns led the unification of Germany in 1871, when Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles.

German Empire and fall

The dynasty ruled the German Empire under Wilhelm I, Frederick III and Wilhelm II. Defeat in the First World War led to the November Revolution of 1918 and Wilhelm II's abdication and exile in the Netherlands, ending over 500 years of Hohenzollern rule in Brandenburg and Prussia. The family's immense property was partly confiscated, a settlement litigated into the 1920s and revisited after German reunification.

Romanian branch

Prince Karl of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen became ruler of Romania in 1866 as Carol I, and the branch reigned as kings of Romania until Michael I was forced to abdicate by the communist government in 1947. The Spanish succession crisis caused by an earlier candidacy of Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen was a trigger of the Franco-Prussian War of 1870.

Today

The head of the Prussian line since 1994 is Georg Friedrich, Prince of Prussia. The Swabian (Sigmaringen) line descends in parallel. Family seats include Hohenzollern Castle and the Cecilienhof Palace in Potsdam.
