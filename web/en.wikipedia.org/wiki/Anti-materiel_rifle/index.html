Anti-materiel rifle

An anti-materiel rifle (AMR) is a large-calibre rifle designed to engage military equipment (materiel) — such as vehicles, parked aircraft, communications gear, and unexploded ordnance — rather than personnel, though in practice such rifles are also used for long-range anti-personnel fire. Most are chambered for heavy cartridges such as the .50 BMG (12.7×99mm NATO) or the Soviet 14.5×114mm.

History

The concept descends from the anti-tank rifles of the World Wars, such as the German Tankgewehr M1918 and the Soviet PTRS-41, which became obsolete as tank armor thickened. Interest revived in the 1980s with rifles like the Barrett M82, adopted by the U.S. military as the M107, which combined semi-automatic operation, heavy-calibre power, and relative portability.

Characteristics and use

Anti-materiel rifles are typically bolt-action or semi-automatic, weigh 10–20 kg, and have effective ranges exceeding 1,500 metres. Specialized ammunition — armor-piercing, incendiary, and explosive types such as Raufoss Mk 211 — enhances effects against equipment. In addition to combat roles, AMRs are used for explosive ordnance disposal, allowing technicians to destroy ordnance from a safe distance.
