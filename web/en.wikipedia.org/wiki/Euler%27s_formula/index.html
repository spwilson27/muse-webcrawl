Euler's formula

Euler's formula, named after Leonhard Euler, is a mathematical formula in complex analysis that establishes the fundamental relationship between the trigonometric functions and the complex exponential function: eix = cos x + i sin x, for any real number x.

Euler's identity

Setting x = π gives Euler's identity, eiπ + 1 = 0, celebrated for linking five fundamental mathematical constants: e, i, π, 1, and 0. The formula interprets points on the unit circle in the complex plane as complex exponentials, and it underlies Fourier analysis, signal processing, and the phasor representation of alternating currents.

Proof and derivation

The formula can be derived by comparing the Taylor series expansions of eix, cos x, and sin x, or by showing that both sides satisfy the same differential equation with the same initial value. It generalizes to give the polar form of complex numbers, z = r eiθ, and de Moivre's formula for powers of complex numbers.

Other uses of the name

"Euler's formula" also refers to several other results by Euler, including the polyhedron formula V − E + F = 2 and the formula relating the number of faces, vertices, and edges in planar graphs.
