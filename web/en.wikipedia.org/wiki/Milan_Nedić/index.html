Milan Nedić

Milan Nedić (2 September 1878 – 4 February 1946) was a Yugoslav general and politician who served as prime minister of the German-installed Government of National Salvation in the Territory of the Military Commander in Serbia during the Second World War.

Career

A career officer, Nedić distinguished himself in the Balkan Wars and the First World War and rose to Chief of the General Staff and Minister of the Army and Navy of Yugoslavia. After the Axis invasion and partition of Yugoslavia in April 1941, the German military administration appointed him in August 1941 to head a Serbian collaborationist government, hoping he could suppress resistance and administer the territory.

Government of National Salvation

Nedić's government cooperated with the occupation authorities; Serbian state forces assisted German operations, and the regime participated in repressive measures and the persecution of Jews, Roma, and resistance members. Nedić argued he was shielding Serbs from worse reprisals, a claim historians continue to debate. After the liberation of Belgrade he fled to Austria, was extradited to Yugoslavia in 1946, and died in custody in Belgrade, officially by suicide, before trial.
