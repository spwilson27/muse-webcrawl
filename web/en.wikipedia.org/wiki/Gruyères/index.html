Gruyères (French: [ɡʁɥijɛʁ]; Arpitan: Gruviéres) is a town in the district of Gruyère in the canton of Fribourg, Switzerland. Its medieval old town, closed to traffic, sits on a hill above the Saane valley and is one of Switzerland's most visited heritage sites. The town gives its name to Gruyère cheese and to the surrounding region of La Gruyère.

History

Gruyères was the seat of the counts of Gruyère, who ruled the upper Saane valley from the 11th century until 1554, when the last count, Michael, went bankrupt and his lands were divided between Fribourg and Berne. The 13th-century Gruyères Castle (Château de Gruyères), residence of nineteen counts, crowns the hilltop; it is now a museum displaying eight centuries of regional art and history.

Sights

The cobbled main street, lined with flower-decked houses, leads up to the castle. The town is also home to the H. R. Giger Museum, opened in 1998 in the Château St. Germain, devoted to the Swiss artist who designed the creature in Alien, with the fantastical Giger Bar opposite. Nearby, at Pringy, the demonstration dairy La Maison du Gruyère shows how the famous cheese is made, and the alpine village of Moleson offers skiing and summer pastures.
