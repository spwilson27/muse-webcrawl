The Battle of Orchomenus was fought in 86 BC between a Roman army under Lucius Cornelius Sulla and the forces of Mithridates VI of Pontus, commanded by his general Archelaus. Together with the preceding Battle of Chaeronea, it destroyed Pontic power in Greece and effectively decided the First Mithridatic War in Rome's favour.

Background

After his victory at Chaeronea earlier in 86 BC, Sulla faced a fresh Pontic army that had landed in Boeotia and taken position on the plain near Orchomenus, ground well suited to Archelaus's superior cavalry and scythed chariots. Sulla, short of cavalry himself, protected his army by digging an extensive network of trenches and earthworks to restrict the enemy's movement.

The battle

Over two days of fighting, the Pontic attacks were repeatedly frustrated by Sulla's fieldworks. When the Roman lines wavered, Sulla is said to have seized a standard and rallied his men in person. The Pontic scythed chariots proved ineffective, and a final Roman assault drove the enemy back into their camp and then into the marshes of Lake Copais, where vast numbers were killed. Ancient sources give exaggerated figures, but Archelaus's army was annihilated as an effective force, the general himself escaping by boat after hiding in the marshes.

Aftermath

With Greece cleared of Pontic armies, Sulla crossed to Asia in 85 BC and imposed the Peace of Dardanus on Mithridates. Orchomenus, with Chaeronea, remained a showcase of Sulla's generalship, and he commemorated the victories with trophies on the battlefield — one of which, found near Orchomenus, is associated with an inscribed monument to the campaign.
