Ari Emanuel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ari Emanuel, CEO of Endeavor and TKO Group Holdings.

Ariel Zev Emanuel (born April 2, 1961) is an American businessman and talent agent, best known as the chief executive officer of Endeavor, an entertainment and media agency that owns the talent agency WME. He was a founding partner of the Endeavor Talent Agency and a central figure in its merger with the William Morris Agency in 2009, forming William Morris Endeavor (WME).

Career

Emanuel worked at the agencies CAA and ICM before co-founding Endeavor in 1995. Under his leadership, Endeavor expanded aggressively beyond talent representation into sports, events, and content ownership, acquiring the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) in 2016 for about $4 billion and taking the company public in 2021. In 2023, Endeavor combined the UFC with WWE to form TKO Group Holdings, with Emanuel as chief executive. He is widely regarded as one of the most powerful executives in Hollywood.

In popular culture

Emanuel is said to be the inspiration for the character Ari Gold, the brash talent agent played by Jeremy Piven in the HBO series Entourage. He is the brother of Rahm Emanuel, former White House Chief of Staff and mayor of Chicago, and of oncologist and bioethicist Ezekiel Emanuel.

Personal life

Emanuel grew up in Wilmette, Illinois, in a family of Jewish background; his father was a pediatrician and activist. He attended Macalester College. He has been involved in Democratic politics and philanthropy, and has written and spoken about living with ADHD and dyslexia.
