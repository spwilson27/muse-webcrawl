Abdülmecid I (25 April 1823 – 25 June 1861) was the 31st sultan of the Ottoman Empire. He succeeded his father Mahmud II on 2 July 1839. His reign saw the proclamation of the Edict of Gülhane in 1839, which inaugurated the Tanzimat period of reorganization and reform in the empire.

Reign

Abdülmecid's reign was notable for the Tanzimat reforms, which sought to modernize the empire's administration, military, and legal system and to grant a measure of equality to non-Muslim subjects. The Hatt-ı Hümayun of 1856 reaffirmed these guarantees. During his reign the empire fought the Crimean War (1853–1856) alongside Britain, France, and Sardinia against Russia.

Architecture

Abdülmecid commissioned the Dolmabahçe Palace on the Bosphorus, completed in 1856, which became the empire's main palace, replacing Topkapı. He also sponsored extensive restorations of mosques and secular buildings in Istanbul.

Death

He died of tuberculosis on 25 June 1861, aged 38, and was succeeded by his brother Abdülaziz.
