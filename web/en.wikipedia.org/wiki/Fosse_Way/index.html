Fosse Way

The Fosse Way was a Roman road in England that linked Isca Dumnoniorum (Exeter) in the southwest with Lindum Colonia (Lincoln) in the northeast, through Ilchester, Somerton, Bath, Cirencester and Leicester. It joins up with the road network at Lincoln to continue a route to York, Hadrian's Wall and Carlisle.

Route

The word fosse is derived from the Latin fossa, meaning ditch. For the first few decades after the Roman invasion of Britain in AD 43 the Fosse Way marked the western frontier of Roman rule — it may have originated as a defensive ditch later filled in and converted into a road, or it may have simply been a road with ditches on either side. The road is remarkable for its extremely direct route: from Lincoln to Ilchester in Somerset, a distance of 182 miles (293 km), it never deviates more than 6 miles (10 km) from a straight line.

Modern survival

Many sections of the Fosse Way form modern roads: the A46 between Lincoln and Leicester and on toward Bath, the A429 between Cirencester and Warwickshire, and the A37 in Somerset. Other parts survive as minor lanes, bridleways and parish boundaries. It remains one of the best-known Roman roads in Britain.
