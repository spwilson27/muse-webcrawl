The Washington Metro, often abbreviated as the Metro and formally the Metrorail, is a rapid transit system serving the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area. It is administered by the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA), a tri-state compact agency.

System

The system opened in 1976 with the Red Line between Rhode Island Avenue and Farragut North. It now comprises six colour-coded lines, 129 miles of track and 98 stations, including the Silver Line extension to Dulles International Airport and Loudoun County completed in 2022. Downtown stations feature the celebrated Brutalist design of architect Harry Weese — great vaulted coffered ceilings in cast concrete.

Operations

Metro is among the busiest rapid-transit systems in the United States, carrying federal workers, residents and tourists, with peak pre-pandemic ridership of over 200 million trips a year. Fares are distance-based using the SmarTrip card. The 2009 Red Line collision, the system's deadliest accident, led to major safety reforms and the eventual replacement of the oldest railcars.
