Spider-Man is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Steve Ditko, he first appeared in the anthology comic book Amazing Fantasy #15 (August 1962). His alter ego is Peter Parker, a high-school student from Queens, New York, who gains spider-like abilities after being bitten by a radioactive spider.

Character biography

Orphaned and raised by his Aunt May and Uncle Ben, Peter initially uses his powers for personal gain; after he lets a burglar escape who later murders Uncle Ben, he learns the lesson summed up in the famous phrase "with great power comes great responsibility". Unlike earlier teenage sidekicks, Spider-Man was a teenage hero in his own right, juggling school, work as a freelance photographer for the Daily Bugle, and relationships with figures such as Gwen Stacy and Mary Jane Watson, while battling villains including the Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, Venom and the Kingpin.

Powers and equipment

Spider-Man possesses proportional strength, speed and agility of a spider, the ability to cling to surfaces, and a precognitive "spider-sense" warning him of danger. Originally, Peter used mechanical web-shooters of his own invention firing a fluid adhesive; in some versions he produces organic webbing.

In other media

Spider-Man is one of the most commercially successful superheroes, starring in animated series, video games and films, including Sam Raimi's trilogy (2002–2007) starring Tobey Maguire, the Amazing Spider-Man films with Andrew Garfield, the Marvel Cinematic Universe films with Tom Holland, and the acclaimed animated Spider-Verse films centred on Miles Morales. The character has been described by Stan Lee's biographers as a landmark in making superheroes relatable, flawed and young.
