Heijō-kyō

Heijō-kyō
Location: Nara, Japan
Capital: 710–740, 745–784
Palace: Heijō Palace

Heijō-kyō (平城京, also Heijōkyō) was the capital city of Japan during most of the Nara period, from 710 to 740 and again from 745 to 784. It stood on the site of present-day Nara.

Layout

The city was laid out in a grid pattern modelled on the Chinese Tang capital Chang'an, divided by the broad Suzaku Avenue into eastern and western halves, with the Heijō Palace at the northern end. Great temples such as Tōdai-ji, Kōfuku-ji, Yakushi-ji and Daian-ji were established within and around the city.

History

Empress Genmei moved the capital to Heijō in 710. After a period of temporary capitals in the 740s, the court returned, then moved to Nagaoka-kyō in 784 and to Heian-kyō in 794. The palace site was excavated in the 20th century and is now a museum site; the reconstructed Suzaku Gate and Daigokuden hall commemorate the 1300th anniversary in 2010.
