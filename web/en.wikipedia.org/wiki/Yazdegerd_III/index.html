Yazdegerd III (Middle Persian: 𐭩𐭦𐭣𐭪𐭥𐭲𐭩 Yazdegerd; 624–651) was the last Sasanian King of Kings of Iran, reigning from 632 to 651. A grandson of Khosrow II, he was raised on the throne as a child amid the civil war that followed the disastrous war with Byzantium, and his reign coincided with the Arab Muslim conquest of Persia, which extinguished the Sasanian Empire after more than four centuries.

Accession

After the overthrow of Khosrow II in 628, the empire fell into chaos: a rapid succession of monarchs, including the queens Borandukht and Azarmidokht, was raised and deposed by rival noble factions. In 632 the magnates crowned Yazdegerd, a young grandson of Khosrow living at Istakhr in Fars, at the Anahita temple there. He never exercised firm control over the fractious aristocracy and military.

Arab conquest

The united Arab forces of the Rashidun Caliphate invaded Iraq and defeated the Sasanians at the Battle of al-Qadisiyyah (636), opening Mesopotamia; Ctesiphon, the capital, fell in 637. Yazdegerd fled eastward, regrouping at Nihavand, where his last great army was destroyed in 642 — the "Victory of Victories". Thereafter he retreated from province to province, seeking aid from local rulers and even, reportedly, from Tang China, while Arab armies absorbed the empire piecemeal.

Death and legacy

In 651 Yazdegerd was killed at Merv in Khorasan — later accounts say by a miller who coveted his jewels, or at the instigation of the local governor Mahoe. His death marks the conventional end of the Sasanian Empire and of pre-Islamic Persian kingship. His son Peroz and grandson Narsieh sought refuge at the Tang court and served as Tang generals. The Zoroastrian calendar's era counts from Yazdegerd's accession, still used by Zoroastrians today. His story was embellished in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh and in Persian historical romance.
