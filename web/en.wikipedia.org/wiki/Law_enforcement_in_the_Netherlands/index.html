Law enforcement in the Netherlands

Law enforcement in the Netherlands is centred on the National Police (Nationale Politie), a single nationwide force created on 1 January 2013 by merging the former 25 regional police forces and the national police services agency. The force is organised into ten regional units, a central unit and a police services centre, under a single Chief of Police, and falls under the Ministry of Justice and Security.

Other agencies

The Royal Marechaussee is a gendarmerie branch of the armed forces responsible for border policing, airport security at Schiphol, and guarding the royal palaces. Financial and tax crime is investigated by the FIOD, and there are special investigating services for areas such as the environment, transport and social affairs. Municipal enforcement officers (handhaving, commonly "BOAs") handle local bylaws and minor public-order matters but are not full police.

Character

Dutch policing traditionally emphasises community policing (wijkagenten, neighbourhood officers) and proportional use of force; Dutch police officers are armed, though specialist units — the Dienst Speciale Interventies — handle counterterrorism and high-risk arrests.
