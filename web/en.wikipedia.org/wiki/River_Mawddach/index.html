The River Mawddach (Welsh: Afon Mawddach) is a river in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It rises in the mountains of southern Snowdonia (Eryri) amid the peaks around Dduallt and Rhobell Fawr, gathers tributaries including the Afon Eden and Afon Gain, and flows south-west past Dolgellau before widening into the broad tidal Mawddach Estuary, which meets Cardigan Bay at Barmouth (Abermaw). The river is about 45 kilometres long.

The estuary

The Mawddach Estuary is celebrated for its scenery: John Ruskin wrote that there was "no better walk" than from Barmouth to Dolgellau along its shores, and the estuary backed by Cadair Idris is considered one of the finest landscapes in Wales. At Barmouth the estuary is crossed by Barmouth Bridge (Pont Abermaw), a long wooden railway viaduct opened in 1867 carrying the Cambrian Coast Line, with a pedestrian walkway alongside. The Mawddach Trail, a walking and cycling route of about 9.5 miles, follows the disused railway line along the southern shore from Dolgellau to Barmouth through Penmaenpool, where a Victorian toll bridge crosses the river.

History

The upper valley was the scene of a small gold rush in the 19th and early 20th centuries: the Clogau and Gwynfynydd mines in the "Dolgellau gold belt" produced the Welsh gold traditionally used for royal wedding rings. Shipbuilding once flourished along the estuary, and the river supported salmon and sea-trout fisheries. Much of the catchment lies within Snowdonia National Park (Parc Cenedlaethol Eryri), and the estuary's saltmarshes and mudflats are protected for their birdlife and are grazed by sheep producing salt-marsh lamb.
