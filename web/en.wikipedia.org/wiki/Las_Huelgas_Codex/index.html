Las Huelgas Codex

The Las Huelgas Codex (Codex Las Huelgas, Burgos, Monasterio de Las Huelgas, MS IX) is a music manuscript compiled in the early 14th century at the Cistercian convent of Santa María la Real de Las Huelgas near Burgos, Spain. It is one of the most important sources of medieval polyphony, preserving music of the ars antiqua period that was sung by the convent's nuns.

Contents

The codex contains 186 compositions, including organa, conductus, motets, sequences, and hymns, written for one to four voices. Most of the pieces are anonymous, though some are attributed to composers of the Notre Dame school in Paris. Unusually for a women's convent, the repertoire demands virtuoso singing, suggesting the nuns of Las Huelgas achieved a high standard of musical performance.

History and study

The manuscript remained at the convent for centuries, little known until it was described in the 1920s and published in facsimile by Higinio Anglés in 1931. Modern editions and recordings — notably by ensembles specializing in medieval music — have made its repertoire widely known. The codex is significant evidence for the transmission of Parisian polyphony to Spain and for the musical culture of medieval female religious communities.
