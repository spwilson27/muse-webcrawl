Battle of the Gabbard

The Battle of the Gabbard, also called the Battle of Nieuwpoort, took place on 2 and 3 June 1653 (12 and 13 June in the Dutch calendar) during the First Anglo-Dutch War. It was fought off the Gabbard shoal in the North Sea between the fleets of the Commonwealth of England and the Dutch Republic, and ended in a major English victory.

Background

After the indecisive fighting of 1652 and the Dutch defeat at the Battle of Portland earlier in 1653, the English fleet under Generals-at-Sea George Monck and Robert Blake sought a decisive engagement to establish a blockade of the Dutch coast. The Dutch admiral Maarten Tromp put to sea with a fleet of about 98 warships, in part to cover homeward-bound Dutch merchant shipping.

The battle

The fleets met off the Gabbard bank on 2 June. The English, fighting in a disciplined line ahead, made effective use of their heavier artillery over two days of fighting. The Dutch rear under Michiel de Ruyter held its own, but the centre and van were badly mauled. On the second day the Dutch withdrew toward their own coast, losing transports and warships in the retreat; the action continued in Dutch waters off Nieuwpoort.

Aftermath

The victory allowed the English to impose a close blockade on the Dutch coast, strangling Dutch trade and contributing to a severe economic crisis in the Republic. The war at sea continued with the Battle of Scheveningen two months later, in which Tromp was killed, and ended with the Treaty of Westminster in 1654.
