John o' Groats is a village in Caithness, in the far north of Scotland. It is popularly regarded as the northernmost settlement of the British mainland and is the traditional starting or finishing point of the end-to-end journey between John o' Groats and Land's End in Cornwall, a distance of about 874 miles (1,407 km) by the shortest road route.

Name and history

The name is traditionally said to derive from Jan de Groot, a Dutchman who in the 15th century obtained a licence from James IV of Scotland to operate a ferry to Orkney. The nearby headland of Duncansby Head is in fact the true northernmost point of mainland Britain.

Tourism

The village draws visitors completing the Land's End to John o' Groats journey, and ferries run to Orkney during the summer. The famous signpost, erected in 1964, points distances to destinations including Land's End and New York.
