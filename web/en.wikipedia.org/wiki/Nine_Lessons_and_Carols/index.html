Nine Lessons and Carols

A Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols is a service of Christian worship traditionally celebrated near Christmas. The story of the fall of man, the promise of the Messiah, and the birth of Jesus is told in nine short Bible readings (lessons) from Genesis, the prophetic books and the Gospels, interspersed with the singing of Christmas carols, hymns and choir anthems. The format is best known from the service held annually on Christmas Eve at King's College Chapel, Cambridge, and broadcast worldwide by the BBC.

History

The original service was created by Edward White Benson, then Bishop of Truro and later Archbishop of Canterbury, and held in a wooden shed serving as Truro's cathedral on Christmas Eve 1880. Benson intended to draw people away from the pubs on Christmas Eve and into church. In 1918 the Dean of King's College, Cambridge, Eric Milner-White, adapted the format for the college chapel, drawing on Benson's structure; the King's service has been held every year since, even during the Second World War, and broadcast by the BBC since 1928 (with a single interruption in 1930).

The King's College service

The Cambridge service traditionally opens with a solo treble singing the first verse of "Once in Royal David's City" and ends with "O Come, All Ye Faithful" and "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing". The nine lessons are read by members of the college and university community, from a chorister to the Provost. Since 1982 the college has commissioned a new carol each year from a contemporary composer, a practice that has produced works by composers including Judith Weir, Harrison Birtwistle and Thomas Adès. The live broadcast is heard by millions on BBC Radio 4 and the World Service, and a television version, Carols from King's, is recorded earlier in December.

Structure

The lessons trace salvation history: the fall in Genesis 3; God's promise to Abraham; Isaiah's prophecies of the Messiah; the annunciation and birth narratives from the Gospels of Luke and Matthew; and finally the prologue of John's Gospel on the Word made flesh. Between readings, carols and anthems reflect on the text just heard, and congregational hymns allow all present to sing.
