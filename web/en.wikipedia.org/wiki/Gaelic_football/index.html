Gaelic football (Irish: Peil Ghaelach; short name Peil, Ulster Scots: Gaelic fitbaw), commonly known simply as Gaelic, GAA or football, is an Irish team sport. A form of football, it is played between two teams of 15 players on a rectangular grass pitch. The objective of the sport is to score by kicking or hand-passing the ball into the other team's goal (3 points) or between two upright posts above the goal and over a crossbar 2.5 metres above the ground (1 point).

Rules

Players advance the round ball — slightly smaller than a soccer ball — by carrying (for at most four steps), soloing (kicking it back into one's own hands), hand-passing with an open hand, and kicking. Tackling is restricted to shoulder-to-shoulder contact and dispossessing the ball carrier. Matches last 60 to 70 minutes at senior inter-county level. Scores are written in the form goals–points, e.g. 2–14.

History and organisation

Football games called caid were played in Ireland for centuries. The modern game was codified after the founding of the Gaelic Athletic Association in 1884, which standardised rules, promoted Irish sport and culture, and organised the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship, first played in 1887. The final is held at Croke Park in Dublin, one of Europe's largest stadiums, and draws crowds of over 82,000. County teams such as Kerry, Dublin and Tyrone dominate the honours list; the sport is strictly amateur, and a separate ladies' game is governed by the Ladies' Gaelic Football Association. A hybrid, International Rules football, is played periodically against Australian rules footballers.
