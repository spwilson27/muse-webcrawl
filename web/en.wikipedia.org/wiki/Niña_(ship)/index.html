Niña (ship)

La Niña (Spanish for "The Girl") was one of the three Spanish ships used by Christopher Columbus in his first voyage of exploration across the Atlantic Ocean in 1492, along with the Pinta and the flagship Santa María. A small caravel originally named the Santa Clara, she was Columbus's personal favourite of the fleet: he sailed her in heavy weather on the return voyage and used her again on later expeditions.

The first voyage

The Niña belonged to Juan Niño of Moguer and sailed in 1492 under the command of Vicente Yáñez Pinzón, with a crew of about two dozen. On the first crossing she was originally rigged with triangular lateen sails, which were changed to square sails in the Canary Islands for better following winds. After the Santa María ran aground off Hispaniola on Christmas Eve 1492, Columbus transferred his flag to the Niña for the return voyage. In February 1493 she survived a violent Atlantic storm that separated her from the Pinta, reaching the Azores and then Lisbon before returning to Palos de la Frontera on 15 March 1493.

Later service

Columbus took the Niña on his second voyage (1493–1496), where she served as flagship of the large colonising fleet and was damaged but survived a hurricane in 1495 — the first hurricane recorded by Europeans in the Americas. She also made an unauthorised trading voyage to Rome's markets in 1496, being captured by pirates en route but escaping, and returned to the Caribbean on Columbus's third voyage in 1498. Her later fate is uncertain; records last place her in the Indies in the early 1500s.

Replicas

A full-scale sailing replica of the Niña, built in Brazil for the 1992 quincentenary by traditional methods without power tools, has toured widely as a floating museum, logging tens of thousands of nautical miles. Another replica was built for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, and a replica at Corpus Christi, Texas, was one of a trio built by Spain for the quincentenary.
