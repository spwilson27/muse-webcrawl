Garissa County is a county in northeastern Kenya, one of the 47 counties established under the 2010 constitution. Its capital and largest town is Garissa, on the Tana River. The county borders Somalia to the east and is inhabited predominantly by ethnic Somalis, whose livelihoods centre on pastoralism.

Geography and economy

Garissa County is arid to semi-arid lowland, watered by the seasonal Ewaso Ng'iro and the permanent Tana River, which supports irrigated farming around Garissa town. Livestock — camels, cattle, goats and sheep — dominate the economy, and the town's livestock market is one of the largest in East Africa. The county hosts the Dadaab refugee complex near the Somali border, one of the largest refugee settlements in the world, established in 1991 following Somalia's civil war.

Security

The county has suffered repeated attacks by the Somali militant group al-Shabaab, most notoriously the 2015 assault on Garissa University College. Cross-border tensions and drought also strongly affect the region.
