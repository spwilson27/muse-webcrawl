University of Fribourg (Switzerland)

The University of Fribourg (French: Université de Fribourg; German: Universität Freiburg; Latin: Universitas Friburgensis) is a public university in the Swiss city of Fribourg. Founded in 1889, it is Switzerland's only bilingual university, offering courses in both French and German, the two languages of its canton. It has a strong tradition in theology, law, humanities, economics, and science.

History

The university was founded in 1889 by the Catholic canton of Fribourg under the initiative of Georges Python, initially with faculties of law and humanities (letters). The Faculty of Theology, entrusted to the Dominicans, followed in 1890, and science in 1896; economics and social sciences grew out of the law faculty in the twentieth century, and a Faculty of Medicine was established in the 2010s, beginning with a bachelor's program. The main Miséricorde building, inaugurated in 1941, is a notable example of modernist architecture by Denis Honegger, a student of Auguste Perret.

Bilingualism and notable people

Students may study in French, German, or both, and can earn a bilingual degree — a distinctive feature in Swiss higher education. The university hosts institutes such as the Adolphe Merkle Institute for nanosciences. Alumni include the writer and Nobel laureate Władysław Reymont (honorary), former Swiss federal councillors, and the economist Joseph Deiss, a former president of the Swiss Confederation who also served as president of the United Nations General Assembly.
