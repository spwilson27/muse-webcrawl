The Annals of Mathematics is a mathematical journal published bimonthly by Princeton University and the Institute for Advanced Study. It is one of the most prestigious journals in mathematics, and is consistently ranked among the top journals in the field.

History

The journal began in 1874 at the University of Virginia under the name The Analyst, founded and edited by Joel E. Hendricks. After a decade it ceased publication and was revived in 1884 as the Annals of Mathematics, published at Harvard. In 1899 it moved to Princeton University, and from 1928 it was jointly edited with the newly founded Institute for Advanced Study from 1933 onward.

During the 1930s the journal became a leading outlet for research in topology, algebra and analysis, publishing work by figures such as John von Neumann and Kurt Gödel. Solomon Lefschetz, editor from 1928 to 1958, greatly raised its standing.

Notable papers

Among the celebrated results published in the Annals are Andrew Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem (1995, jointly with a companion paper by Taylor and Wiles), the solution of the Poincaré conjecture's precursors in geometric analysis, and major papers by Jean-Pierre Serre, Michael Atiyah and many Fields Medalists. The journal accepts papers across all areas of pure mathematics.

Editorial policy

The Annals is known for an unusually rigorous refereeing process and a small acceptance rate. Manuscripts are handled by a board of editors based largely at Princeton and the Institute for Advanced Study.
