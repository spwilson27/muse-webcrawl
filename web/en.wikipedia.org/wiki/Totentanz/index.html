Totentanz

Totentanz (German for "dance of death") may refer to several related topics:

Art and literature

  • The Danse Macabre (French) or Totentanz (German), an artistic genre of late-medieval allegory in which Death personified leads a procession of dancing skeletons and representatives of every social station — pope, emperor, king, peasant — to the grave. Famous examples include the murals formerly in the Marienkirche, Lübeck (destroyed in 1942, replaced by a modern window), the Totentanz of Basel, and the woodcuts of Hans Holbein the Younger.
  • Totentanz, the fifteenth of Hugo Distler's 14 Sprüche (Op. 12 No. 2, 1934), a motet cycle for the Sunday of the Dead.

Music

  • Totentanz (Franz Liszt), S. 126, a piece for piano and orchestra composed between 1838 and 1849, based on the plainchant Dies irae and inspired by frescoes of the Triumph of Death. It is among Liszt's most macabre and virtuosic works, structured as a set of variations.
  • Works by several other composers have used the title, including pieces by Thomas Adès (2013, for mezzo-soprano, baritone and orchestra, setting the text of the Lübeck Totentanz).

Other uses

  • Totentanz, a 1905 play by August Strindberg is better known as The Dance of Death.
