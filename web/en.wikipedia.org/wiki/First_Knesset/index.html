First Knesset

The First Knesset was the legislature of Israel between 1949 and 1951. It was elected on 25 January 1949 as the Constituent Assembly, charged with drafting a constitution for the new State of Israel, and shortly after convening it renamed itself the First Knesset and became a regular legislature, deciding that the constitution would instead be built up chapter by chapter as Basic Laws.

Election and composition

The elections of 25 January 1949, held during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, used nationwide proportional representation with a single electoral district and a 1% threshold. Mapai, the labour party led by David Ben-Gurion, won the largest share with 46 seats, followed by the left-wing Mapam with 19, the United Religious Front with 16 and the right-wing Herut, led by Menachem Begin, with 14. Twelve lists in all won seats; voter turnout was high despite the ongoing war.

Work of the Knesset

The assembly first met in Tel Aviv and elected Chaim Weizmann as Israel's first President before moving to Jerusalem in December 1949. Its foundational acts included the Transition Law (1949), sometimes called the "small constitution"; the Law of Return (1950), granting every Jew the right to immigrate to Israel; and the Harari Decision of June 1950, which resolved that the constitution would be enacted piecemeal as Basic Laws rather than in a single document — a compromise that still shapes Israeli constitutional law. The Knesset also legislated for the absorption of mass immigration, which roughly doubled the young state's Jewish population, and for the administration of military government over Arab citizens.

Government

Ben-Gurion formed a coalition of Mapai with the religious parties, the Progressive Party and others, excluding both Herut and the communists. The first government fell in October 1950 over disputes concerning immigrant transit camps and religious education, and a reconstituted coalition lasted only until February 1951, when the Knesset voted itself out of office over religious education in the camps. Elections to the Second Knesset were held on 30 July 1951.
