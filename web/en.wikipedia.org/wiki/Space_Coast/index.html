The Space Coast is a region in the U.S. state of Florida around the Kennedy Space Center (KSC) and Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. It is one of several themed "coasts" in Florida. The name arose in the 1960s, as the nation's crewed space programme transformed the sleepy coastal communities of Brevard County into the centre of the Space Race.

Geography and communities

The region is generally identified with Brevard County, which stretches along some 70 miles of Atlantic shoreline on Florida's east-central coast. Communities include Titusville, Cape Canaveral, Cocoa, Cocoa Beach, Rockledge, Melbourne, Indialantic, Melbourne Beach and Palm Bay, divided by the Indian River Lagoon and Banana River from mainland to barrier islands. The 321 telephone area code, introduced in 1999, was deliberately chosen to echo the launch countdown "3-2-1".

Space industry

Rockets have been launched from Cape Canaveral since 1950. The area hosted the Mercury, Gemini and Apollo programmes — all crewed U.S. spaceflights since 1968 have been launched from Kennedy Space Center — followed by the Space Shuttle (1981–2011) and, in the twenty-first century, a commercial renaissance led by SpaceX, United Launch Alliance and Blue Origin. The economy of the region remains deeply tied to aerospace contractors and the space tourism drawn by launch viewing.

Tourism

Besides launches, attractions include the Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex, the Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge, Port Canaveral (one of the world's busiest cruise ports), and the beaches of Cocoa Beach, famous as a surfing centre and the setting of the 1960s sitcom I Dream of Jeannie.
