Basset horn

ClassificationSingle-reed aerophone (clarinet family)
RangeWritten F below the alto clarinet; usually in F
Related instrumentsClarinet, alto clarinet, basset clarinet

The basset horn (German: Bassetthorn) is a member of the clarinet family, similar to the clarinet but larger and therefore lower in pitch. It is typically a transposing instrument in F, sounding a perfect fifth below the written notes, and its range extends down to low C — the extra low notes being the "basset" notes that give the instrument its name.

History

The basset horn emerged in the mid-18th century, probably in Bavaria, with early instruments having a curved or angled body. It enjoyed a vogue in the late 18th and early 19th centuries before falling into obscurity, and was revived in the 20th century.

Repertoire

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was fond of the instrument, using it in his Requiem, the operas La clemenza di Tito and Così fan tutte, and chamber works; he wrote his Clarinet Concerto originally for a related basset clarinet. Mendelssohn composed two concert pieces for clarinet, basset horn and piano, and Richard Strauss used basset horns in several operas including Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier.

Construction

Modern basset horns are usually made of grenadilla wood with a metal bell, either straight or in a compact angled form, and are played with a clarinet-style single-reed mouthpiece.
