Russian sturgeon

The Russian sturgeon (Acipenser gueldenstaedtii), also known as the diamond sturgeon or Danube sturgeon, is a species of fish in the family Acipenseridae. It is found in the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov and the Caspian Sea, and in the rivers that flow into them, including the Danube and the Volga.

Description and life cycle

The Russian sturgeon can grow to over 2 metres in length and more than 100 kg in weight, although such sizes are now rare. It is anadromous: adults migrate from the sea up rivers to spawn. Like other sturgeons it is long-lived and matures late, females typically not spawning until their teens.

Caviar and conservation

The species is one of the principal sources of caviar (marketed as osetra), as well as of valuable flesh. Overfishing, poaching and the damming of spawning rivers have caused severe declines, and the species is listed as critically endangered by the IUCN. Catch restrictions, CITES controls and aquaculture have been introduced to relieve pressure on wild stocks.
