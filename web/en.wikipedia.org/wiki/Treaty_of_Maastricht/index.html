Treaty of Maastricht

The Treaty of Maastricht, formally the Treaty on European Union (TEU), was signed on 7 February 1992 in Maastricht, Netherlands, and entered into force on 1 November 1993. It founded the European Union and laid the legal groundwork for the single currency, the euro.

Structure

The treaty organized the Union into three pillars: the European Communities, a Common Foreign and Security Policy, and cooperation in justice and home affairs. It introduced European Union citizenship, expanded the powers of the European Parliament through co-decision, and committed member states to economic and monetary union.

The euro criteria

The treaty's convergence criteria ("Maastricht criteria") required candidate states to keep inflation, government deficits (below 3% of GDP), public debt (below 60% of GDP), exchange rates, and long-term interest rates within defined limits before adopting the euro, which was launched in 1999 under the European Central Bank.

Ratification

Ratification was protracted: Danish voters initially rejected the treaty in a 1992 referendum before approving it with opt-outs in 1993, French voters approved it only narrowly, and in the United Kingdom it provoked a major parliamentary battle within the governing Conservative Party.
