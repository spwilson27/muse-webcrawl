Falkland Palace

Falkland Palace, in Falkland, Fife, Scotland, is a royal palace of the Scottish Kings. Built between 1501 and 1541 by James IV and James V on the site of an earlier castle, it is one of the finest examples of Renaissance architecture in Scotland, its south façade decorated with buttresses, medallions and statues in the French style — James V employed French masons for the work.

History

The original castle belonged to the Earls of Fife; Robert Stewart, Duke of Albany, was accused of starving David, Duke of Rothesay — heir to Robert III — to death there in 1402. The palace became a favourite hunting retreat of the Stewart monarchs, set beside the Lomond Hills. Mary, Queen of Scots, often stayed at Falkland, playing tennis in its court; the real tennis court built in 1539 survives as the oldest still in use in Britain. James VI hunted here, and Charles I and Charles II both visited. After 1660 the palace declined; Cromwell's troops burned part of it. In the late 19th century the 3rd Marquess of Bute restored it, and in 1952 his descendants placed it in the care of the National Trust for Scotland, acting for the Crown.

Today

The palace preserves the royal apartments, the Chapel Royal with its painted ceiling of 1633, and extensive gardens restored in the 1940s–50s. It is open to the public and remains, formally, a royal palace owned by the Crown.
