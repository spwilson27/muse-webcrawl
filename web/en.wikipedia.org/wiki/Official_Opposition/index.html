Official Opposition

The Official Opposition is, in the Westminster system and systems derived from it, the largest political party or coalition in a legislature that is not in government. Its leader is entitled Leader of the Opposition, its senior members form a shadow cabinet, and it presents itself as a government-in-waiting. The phrase "His Majesty's Most Loyal Opposition" — first used jokingly in the British House of Commons in 1826 — captures the constitutional bargain: the opposition opposes the government of the day while remaining loyal to the state itself.

Role and functions

The Official Opposition scrutinises the executive through debates, select committees and question sessions — most visibly Prime Minister's Questions — proposes alternative policies, and seeks to persuade the electorate that it is fit to govern. In the United Kingdom the opposition is allocated specific parliamentary time ("opposition days"), its leader receives a statutory salary and briefings on privy counsellor terms, and its parties receive public funding ("Short money" in the Commons, "Cranborne money" in the Lords) to support their parliamentary work.

Selection

The Official Opposition is normally simply the largest party not in government. Where this is unclear — for example after inconclusive elections or where two parties are close in seats — the Speaker of the House determines which party holds the status, having regard to numerical strength. In legislatures with several opposition parties, only the largest enjoys the formal title and its privileges.

In other countries

Canada recognises "His Majesty's Loyal Opposition" in both the federal Parliament and provincial legislatures, and the leader's residence, Stornoway, is provided by the state. Australia, New Zealand and India follow similar conventions; in India the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha is a statutory office under a 1977 Act, though the post has sometimes been vacant when no opposition party held the required tenth of seats. Many Commonwealth Caribbean and Pacific legislatures replicate the institution.
