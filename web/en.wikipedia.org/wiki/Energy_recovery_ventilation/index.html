Energy recovery ventilation

Energy recovery ventilation (ERV) is the energy recovery process in residential and commercial HVAC systems that exchanges the energy contained in building exhaust air and uses it to treat (precondition) the incoming outdoor ventilation air. An energy recovery ventilator transfers both heat and moisture between the two airstreams, unlike a heat recovery ventilator (HRV), which transfers heat only.

How it works

In an ERV, exhaust air and fresh intake air pass through opposite sides of an enthalpy exchange core — typically a fixed plate of water-permeable material, or a rotating enthalpy wheel. In winter, warmth and humidity from the outgoing air pass to the cold, dry incoming air; in summer, heat and moisture from hot incoming air are transferred to the cooler exhaust stream. Well-designed systems recover 70–85 percent of the exhaust energy.

Benefits

By preconditioning ventilation air, ERVs reduce heating and cooling loads, allow smaller HVAC equipment, and help control indoor humidity — retaining moisture in dry winter climates and limiting excess humidity in summer. They are a standard component of energy-efficient and Passive House building design, where continuous fresh-air supply must be reconciled with minimal energy loss.
