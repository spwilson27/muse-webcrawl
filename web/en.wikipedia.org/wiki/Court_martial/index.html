Court martial

A court martial (plural courts martial) is a military court that determines the guilt of members of the armed forces subject to military law and imposes punishment for offences such as desertion, mutiny, disobedience or conduct unbecoming.

Procedure

Courts martial are convened by commanding officers and typically consist of a panel of officers, sometimes with a military judge presiding over questions of law. Many systems, such as the United States Uniform Code of Military Justice, provide graded types — summary, special and general courts martial — with increasing maximum punishments, and an appellate structure. The term is also used as a verb: to be "court-martialled".
