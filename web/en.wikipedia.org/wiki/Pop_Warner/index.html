Pop Warner

Glenn Scobey Warner (April 5, 1871 – September 7, 1954), most commonly known as Pop Warner, was an American college football coach at various institutions who is credited with several still-popular innovations in the sport. He is regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of American football.

Career

Warner coached at Georgia, Cornell, Carlisle, Pittsburgh, Stanford, and Temple over a career spanning 1895 to 1938, compiling a record of 319 wins, 106 losses, and 32 ties. At the Carlisle Indian School he coached the legendary Jim Thorpe. His Pittsburgh teams won national championships in 1915, 1916, and 1918, and his Stanford team won the 1927 Rose Bowl.

Innovations

Warner is credited with popularising the single-wing and double-wing formations, the spiral punt, the screen pass, the three-point stance, numbering players' jerseys, and improvements such as shoulder and thigh pads.

Legacy

The Pop Warner Little Scholars youth football organisation, founded in 1929, bears his name and remains the largest youth football programme in the United States. Warner was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1951.
