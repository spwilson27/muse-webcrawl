Thomas à Kempis

Thomas à Kempis (c. 1380 – 25 July 1471), born Thomas Hemerken, was a German-Dutch canon regular and the presumed author of The Imitation of Christ, one of the most widely read devotional books in Christianity. He was a leading figure of the Devotio Moderna movement.

Life

Thomas was born in Kempen in the Rhineland, from which his Latin name derives. Educated by the Brethren of the Common Life at Deventer, he entered the Augustinian priory of Mount St. Agnes (Agnietenberg) near Zwolle in 1399, where his brother Jan was prior. He was professed in 1406 and ordained priest in 1413, spending the rest of his long life copying manuscripts, writing devotional works and directing novices.

The Imitation of Christ

De imitatione Christi, composed in the 1420s, consists of four books of spiritual counsel emphasising humility, detachment from worldly affairs and inward devotion. Attributed to Thomas by strong early tradition and supported by his authenticated writings in the same style, it has been translated into more languages and printed in more editions than almost any book besides the Bible.

Other works and legacy

Thomas also wrote chronicles of his monastery, lives of Gerard Groote and Florens Radewyns, hymns and meditations. He died during an outbreak of plague in 1471 and is venerated as a spiritual master, though his cause for canonisation was never completed.
