Early childhood education
Ages: Birth to 8 years
Settings: Preschool, kindergarten, childcare
Key thinkers: Froebel, Montessori, Piaget, Vygotsky

Early childhood education (ECE) is a branch of education theory relating to the teaching of children from birth up to the age of eight, traditionally equivalent to third grade. It encompasses preschool, nursery school and kindergarten.

History

Friedrich Froebel founded the kindergarten in 1837, emphasising play. Maria Montessori developed her method of self-directed activity in the early 20th century. Jean Piaget's stages of cognitive development and Lev Vygotsky's social learning theory shaped modern practice.

Approaches and evidence

Approaches range from play-based and Montessori to Reggio Emilia and structured curricula. Research such as the Perry Preschool Project links high-quality ECE to better schooling, earnings and health outcomes, especially for disadvantaged children, though quality and dosage matter. Issues include access, funding, workforce pay and school readiness. See also Kindergarten.
