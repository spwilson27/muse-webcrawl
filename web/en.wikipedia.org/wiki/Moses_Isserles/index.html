Moses Isserles (Hebrew: משה איסרלישׂ; February 22, 1520 – May 1, 1572), also known by the acronym Rema (רמ״א), was an eminent Polish Ashkenazi rabbi, talmudist, and posek (decisor of Jewish law). He is best known for his HaMappah ("the Tablecloth"), a set of glosses on the Shulchan Aruch of Joseph Karo which adapted Karo's Sephardic code to the customs of Ashkenazi Jewry. Together, the two works became the authoritative code of Jewish law for most of the Jewish world.

Life

Isserles was born in Kraków to a wealthy and learned family; his father Israel was a founder of the Remah Synagogue, which still stands in the Kazimierz district. After the death of his first wife in an epidemic, he studied in Lublin under Shalom Shachna, whose daughter he married, and later returned to Kraków, where he founded a yeshiva and supported it from his own funds. He corresponded with scholars across Europe and corresponded even with the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus's milieu in Kraków; he was knowledgeable in philosophy, history, and astronomy, unusual among the Polish rabbis of his day.

Works

Besides the Mappah, which is printed as an integral part of standard editions of the Shulchan Aruch, Isserles wrote Darkhei Moshe, a commentary on the Arba'ah Turim; Torat ha-Chatat on dietary laws; responsa; and treatises including Mechir Yayin on the Book of Esther and a work on the philosophy of religion. His tombstone, in the Old Jewish Cemetery beside the Remah Synagogue, bears the inscription that "from Moses (Maimonides) to Moses (Isserles) there arose no one like Moses" — a pilgrimage site on Lag BaOmer, the anniversary of his death.
