Musical quotation

Musical quotation is the practice of directly quoting another work in a new composition. The quotation may be a melody, a chord progression, a rhythm or a larger passage, and it may serve as homage, parody, commentary or structural material.

History and examples

Quotation has been used by composers for centuries, from Renaissance parody masses built on earlier motets to the self-quotation and borrowed themes of the Romantic era. Charles Ives made quotation a central technique, weaving hymns, marches and popular tunes into works such as the Concord Sonata and his symphonies. In the 20th century, composers such as Luciano Berio (in Sinfonia), Alfred Schnittke and George Crumb used extensive quotation and collage, while popular music adopted the related practice of sampling.

Related techniques

Musical quotation is related to variation, paraphrase, parody and allusion, but differs in presenting the borrowed material recognisably and essentially intact. In modern popular music, sampling raises legal questions of copyright and clearance that classical quotation historically did not.
