Died11 October 1953, Westport, Connecticut, US

James Earle Fraser (November 4, 1876 – October 11, 1953) was an American sculptor. He is best known for The End of the Trail, depicting a weary Native American on horseback, and for designing the Buffalo nickel (Indian Head nickel), issued by the United States Mint from 1913 to 1938.

Life and training

Fraser grew up in the Dakota Territory, where his father worked for a railroad; the experience gave him sympathy for Native Americans displaced by settlement. He studied at the Art Institute of Chicago and in Paris at the École des Beaux-Arts and the Académie Julian, and worked as an assistant to the sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens.

Major works

His works include the pediment figures and statues for the National Archives Building and the Supreme Court in Washington, D.C. (including Authority of Law and Contemplation of Justice), statues of Alexander Hamilton and Albert Gallatin at the Treasury Building, the Benjamin Franklin statue at the Franklin Institute, and numerous medals and monuments. His wife, Laura Gardin Fraser, was also a noted sculptor and medallist.
