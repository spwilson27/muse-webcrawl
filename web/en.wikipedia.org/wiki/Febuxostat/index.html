Febuxostat

Febuxostat, sold under the brand name Uloric among others, is a medication used to treat gout by lowering high levels of uric acid in the blood. It is a non-purine selective inhibitor of xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that produces uric acid, and is generally reserved for people who cannot tolerate allopurinol, the usual first-line treatment.

Medical uses

Febuxostat is used for the long-term management of hyperuricemia in patients with gout. It is not recommended for people with high uric acid levels but no symptoms. Treatment is taken once daily by mouth, and like other urate-lowering therapies it may provoke gout flares when started.

Safety

Common side effects include nausea, joint pain and abnormal liver function tests. A large cardiovascular outcomes trial (CARES, 2018) found higher rates of cardiovascular and all-cause death with febuxostat than with allopurinol in patients with gout and established cardiovascular disease, leading the US Food and Drug Administration to add a boxed warning and limit its use; a later European trial (FAST, 2020) did not find an increased cardiovascular risk. Febuxostat should not be combined with azathioprine or mercaptopurine, whose breakdown it inhibits.

History

Febuxostat was discovered by scientists at the Japanese company Teijin and was approved in the European Union in 2008 and in the United States in 2009.
