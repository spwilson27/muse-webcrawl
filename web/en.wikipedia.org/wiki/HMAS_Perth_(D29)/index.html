HMAS Perth (D29) was the lead ship of the Perth-class guided missile destroyers of the Royal Australian Navy (RAN). Built in the United States as a modified version of the US Navy's Charles F. Adams class, she was commissioned in 1965 and served for 34 years, including deployments during the Vietnam War. She was the second RAN ship named for the city of Perth; the first, a light cruiser, was sunk at the Battle of Sunda Strait in 1942.

Design and construction

The Perth class was ordered in the early 1960s as Australia's first guided-missile warships, bringing the RAN into the missile age with the Tartar surface-to-air missile system. Perth displaced about 4,700 tons at full load, was 133.2 metres long, and was armed with a single-arm Mk 13 missile launcher for Tartar (later Standard) missiles, two 5-inch/54 calibre guns in single turrets, Ikara anti-submarine missile launchers — an Australian system fitted in place of the American ASROC — and torpedo tubes. Her crew numbered about 330.

Perth was laid down by the Defoe Shipbuilding Company at Bay City, Michigan, on 21 September 1962, launched on 26 September 1963 by Lady Beale, wife of the Australian ambassador to the United States, and commissioned into the RAN at Boston on 17 July 1965 under Captain G. R. Griffiths.

Service history

After work-up with the US Navy, Perth made three deployments to the Vietnam War between 1967 and 1971, operating on the gunline off Vietnam providing naval gunfire support to allied ground forces and screening US carriers in the Gulf of Tonkin. On 18 October 1967 she was hit by a shell from a North Vietnamese shore battery, which wounded several sailors. For her Vietnam service she received the United States Navy Unit Commendation — one of only a few RAN ships so honoured — and the battle honour "Vietnam 1967–71".

Through the 1970s and 1980s Perth was extensively modernised: her Tartar system was replaced by Standard SM-1 missiles, new radars and combat data systems were fitted, and she later received the Phalanx close-in weapon system. She took part in countless exercises with allied navies, made deployments throughout the Indian Ocean and Southeast Asia, and supported Australia's regional presence during the Cold War and after.

Decommissioning and fate

Perth was decommissioned at Sydney on 15 October 1999, her place taken by the newer Anzac-class frigates. Rather than being scrapped, the ship was gifted to Western Australia and deliberately scuttled on 24 November 2001 in King George Sound off Albany — close to where the first ANZACs embarked in 1914 — to form an artificial reef and dive wreck. She lies upright in about 32 metres of water and has become one of Australia's most popular wreck dives, colonised by dense marine growth and visited by divers who can explore her bridge, guns and missile launcher.
