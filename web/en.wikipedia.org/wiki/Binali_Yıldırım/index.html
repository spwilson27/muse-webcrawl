Binali Yıldırım (born December 20, 1955) is a Turkish politician who served as the 27th and most recent prime minister of Turkey from 2016 to 2018 and as Speaker of the Grand National Assembly from 2018 to 2019. He was Leader of the Justice and Development Party (AK Party) from 2016 to 2017, when the position of Prime Minister was abolished following the 2017 constitutional referendum and the subsequent transition to a presidential system.

Before becoming prime minister, Yıldırım served as Minister of Transport, Maritime and Communication almost continuously from 2002 to 2013 and again from 2015 to 2016. Between 2003 and 2016, he oversaw numerous large infrastructure projects, including the expansion of high-speed rail, the construction of the Marmaray tunnel under the Bosphorus, and the growth of Turkish Airlines' domestic network.

Early life and education

Yıldırım was born in Refahiye, in Erzincan Province, into a Kurdish-Alevi family. He studied naval architecture and ocean engineering at Istanbul Technical University, and later completed postgraduate studies at the World Maritime University in Malmö, Sweden. He worked in the shipbuilding industry and served as general director of the Istanbul Fast Ferries Company (İDO) from 1994 to 2000, during the mayoralty of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan.

Political career

Yıldırım was a founding member of the AK Party in 2001 and was elected to parliament for Istanbul in the 2002 general election. As transport minister he became known for ambitious motorway, airport, and railway projects. Following Ahmet Davutoğlu's resignation in 2016, Yıldırım was elected party leader and appointed prime minister. In 2019 he was the AK Party's candidate for Mayor of Istanbul, losing the closely contested election — and its controversial re-run — to Ekrem İmamoğlu.

Personal life

Yıldırım is married to Semiha Yıldırım, a retired teacher; they have three children. His first name, Binali, derives from Arabic and means "son of Ali", reflecting his family's Alevi heritage.
