Radhanath Sickdar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A 19th-century survey office scene: Indian computers at long desks with brass theodolites and calculation tables]

Radhanath Sickdar (1813-1870) was an Indian mathematician and surveyor who, working for the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India, first computed the height of the mountain then known as Peak XV — and concluded it was the highest in the world. The mountain was later named Mount Everest.

Sickdar was the Survey's 'Chief Computer', leading the human computers in Dehradun who reduced the survey's observations. In 1852 he reported his result to his superior Andrew Waugh; after further checking, Waugh announced the finding in 1856.

Recognition

For a long time Sickdar's role was little known outside the Survey, and histories credited Waugh and George Everest instead. Modern accounts and the Survey of India's records have restored his central part in the measurement.

Career

Educated at Hindu College, Calcutta, Sickdar was also involved in founding scientific communication in Bengali and worked on the survey's publications before retiring in 1862.
