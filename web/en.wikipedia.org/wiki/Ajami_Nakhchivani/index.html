Ajami Nakhchivani

Ajami ibn Abubakr Nakhchivani (Azerbaijani: Əcəmi Naxçıvani; fl. 12th–13th centuries) was a medieval Muslim architect and the founder of the Nakhchivan school of architecture in present-day Azerbaijan. Working in the city of Nakhchivan under the Eldiguzid atabegs of Azerbaijan, he is renowned for mausoleum towers of refined brickwork and geometric ornament that became classics of Islamic architecture in the Caucasus and influenced tomb-tower design far beyond the region.

Works

Two signed monuments in Nakhchivan are securely attributed to Ajami:

  • The Yusuf ibn Kuseyir Mausoleum (1162), an eight-sided brick tomb tower whose facets are filled with geometric brick patterns and crowned by a pyramidal roof.
  • The Momine Khatun Mausoleum (1186), built for the wife of the Eldiguzid ruler Muhammad Jahan Pahlavan. Originally about 34 metres high (its upper portion has been lost), the ten-sided tower is decorated with intricate interlacing geometric ornament, glazed turquoise insets, and a band of Kufic inscription in brick.

Ajami is also associated with other monuments of the Eldiguzid period in and around Nakhchivan, including minarets and the city's Juma mosque complex.

Style and influence

Ajami's buildings are distinguished by the complete fusion of structure and ornament: patterns are not applied decoration but formed by the brickwork itself, with glazed tiles used sparingly to pick out inscriptions and medallions. His towers show a sophisticated command of geometry — star polygons and interlaced bands that anticipate later developments in Islamic geometric design. The Nakhchivan school he founded shaped mausoleum architecture across Azerbaijan and eastern Anatolia for centuries; the much later Gonbad-e Qabus tradition and the tomb towers of Maragheh and Ardabil all show affinities with his work.

Legacy

The Momine Khatun Mausoleum remains the emblem of the city of Nakhchivan and appears on Azerbaijani currency and official imagery. A metro station in Baku, Memar Ajami, is named in his honour ("memar" meaning architect).
