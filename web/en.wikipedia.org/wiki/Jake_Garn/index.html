Edwin Jacob "Jake" Garn (born 12 October 1932) is an American politician and member of the Republican Party who served as a United States senator representing Utah from 1974 to 1993. He is best known to the general public as the first sitting member of the United States Congress to fly in space.

Career

Garn, a former insurance executive and Utah Air National Guard pilot, served as mayor of Salt Lake City before winning election to the Senate in 1974. In the Senate he chaired the Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee and co-authored the Garn–St Germain Depository Institutions Act of 1982, which deregulated savings and loan associations.

Spaceflight

As chair of the subcommittee overseeing NASA's budget, Garn pressed to fly aboard the Space Shuttle and in April 1985 served as a payload specialist on mission STS-51-D aboard Discovery, conducting medical experiments on space adaptation syndrome. The episode's notoriety led to NASA's biomedical unit for motion-sickness severity being jokingly named the "Garn scale". A training simulator, the Jake Garn Mission Simulator and Training Facility, was named for him.
