NatureServe

NatureServe is a non-profit organisation based in Arlington, Virginia, United States, that provides scientific data, tools and services related to biodiversity conservation. It coordinates a network of natural heritage programmes and conservation data centres across the United States, Canada and Latin America, maintaining standardised information on the distribution and conservation status of species and ecosystems.

History

NatureServe grew out of the natural heritage programme network begun by The Nature Conservancy in 1974, when the first state heritage programme was established in South Carolina to inventory rare species and natural communities. In 1994 the network's central science organisation became the Association for Biodiversity Information, and in 2001 it was renamed NatureServe and became independent of the Conservancy.

Conservation status ranks

NatureServe is known for its conservation status assessment system, which assigns ranks at global (G), national (N) and subnational (S) scales, from 1 (critically imperilled) to 5 (secure), with additional categories for extinct (X) and possibly extinct (H) elements. These NatureServe ranks are widely used by government agencies, including in assessments under endangered-species legislation, and complement the IUCN Red List. Its public database, NatureServe Explorer, provides free information on tens of thousands of species and ecosystems in North America.

Work

The organisation produces analyses of species at risk, climate-change vulnerability assessments, ecological classification systems, and tools such as the NatureServe Vista decision-support software. Member programmes in each US state and Canadian province collect field data using common standards, giving the network one of the most detailed biodiversity datasets in the Western Hemisphere.
