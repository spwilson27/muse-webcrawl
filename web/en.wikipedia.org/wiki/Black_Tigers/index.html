The Black Tigers (Tamil: கரும்புலிகள்) were an elite suicide-attack wing of the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), the Tamil separatist organisation that fought the Sri Lankan government from 1983 to 2009.

Formation and operations

The unit's first attack was carried out on 5 July 1987, when "Captain Miller" drove a truck bomb into a Sri Lankan Army camp at Nelliyady in the Jaffna Peninsula, killing dozens of soldiers. Black Tiger cadres — both men and women — subsequently carried out well over 200 suicide attacks against military, political and economic targets, more than any comparable organisation of the period. Their most prominent victims included former Indian prime minister Rajiv Gandhi, assassinated by a female Black Tiger in 1991, and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa, killed in 1993. Sea Tiger and later air units adopted similar suicide tactics.

Selection and culture

Volunteers were selected by the LTTE leadership, trained in secrecy and commemorated as martyrs (Maaveerar). Before a mission, a cadre traditionally shared a final meal with LTTE leader Velupillai Prabhakaran. Fallen Black Tigers were honoured on 5 July, observed as Black Tigers Day. The unit ceased to exist when the LTTE was militarily destroyed in May 2009.
