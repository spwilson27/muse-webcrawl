Étienne-Jules Marey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Étienne-Jules Marey (5 March 1830 – 15 May 1904) was a French physiologist and pioneer of photography and cinema. He is best known for chronophotography, a technique for recording multiple phases of movement on a single photographic plate, which he used to study the locomotion of humans and animals and which laid essential groundwork for cinematography and biomechanics.

Work

Marey began his career in medicine, inventing instruments to record physiological processes graphically, including an improved sphygmograph for measuring blood pressure. Influenced by Eadweard Muybridge's sequential photographs of a galloping horse, Marey developed the chronophotographic gun (1882), a camera shaped like a rifle that captured twelve images per second on a rotating plate. He later used fixed-plate and film-strip cameras at his Station Physiologique in Paris to analyze walking, running, jumping, and the flight of birds and insects.

Legacy

Marey's "geometric chronophotographs"—in which subjects wore black suits marked with white lines, reducing the body to abstract trajectories of points and lines—influenced both science and modern art, notably Marcel Duchamp's Nude Descending a Staircase. His graphical method shaped experimental physiology, cardiology, and the science of motion.
