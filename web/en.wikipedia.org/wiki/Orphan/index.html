Orphan

An orphan is a child whose parents have died, are unknown or have permanently abandoned them. In common usage only a child who has lost both parents is called an orphan, while a child who has lost one parent may be called a half-orphan; legal and aid-agency definitions vary and may include children with a surviving parent who cannot care for them. UNICEF and UNAIDS estimate that tens of millions of children worldwide have lost one or both parents, with HIV/AIDS, war and poverty among the leading causes.

Care of orphans

Historically, orphans were cared for by extended family, religious institutions and orphanages. In many developed countries large residential orphanages have largely been replaced by foster care and adoption, and international policy now generally favours family-based care over institutionalisation, which research associates with developmental harm. Orphans have also been a major theme in literature, from folk tales to works such as Oliver Twist and Anne of Green Gables.

Orphan works and other meanings

The word is used metaphorically in several fields: an "orphan work" in copyright law is a work whose rights-holder cannot be found; in typography an orphan is a single short line left at the bottom of a page; and "orphan drugs" are medicines developed for rare diseases.
