Hywel Dda

Reign: c. 920 – 949/950
Title: King of Deheubarth; later ruler of most of Wales
Died: 949 or 950
Father: Cadell ap Rhodri

Hywel ap Cadell (c. 880 – 949/950), commonly known as Hywel Dda ("Hywel the Good"), was a Welsh king who ruled Deheubarth in south-west Wales and, by the middle of the 10th century, came to rule most of modern Wales. He is credited with the codification of Welsh law — the Cyfraith Hywel ("Laws of Hywel") — which remained in force in parts of Wales until the Laws in Wales Acts of the 16th century.

Reign

Hywel inherited Seisyllwg from his father Cadell ap Rhodri and, with his brother Clydog, ruled jointly before Clydog's death in 920 left him sole ruler. Through marriage to Elen ferch Llywarch he acquired Dyfed, forming the kingdom of Deheubarth. In the 940s, following the deaths of neighbouring rulers, Hywel added the northern kingdoms of Gwynedd and Powys, briefly uniting most of Wales. His reign is notable for peaceable relations with England: Hywel visited the court of Æthelstan, witnessed charters there, and issued coinage at Chester bearing his name — the only Welsh king of the period known to have minted coins.

Laws

Tradition holds that Hywel summoned an assembly at Whitland (Hendy Gwyn ar Daf) of representatives from across Wales to systematise Welsh law, producing codes covering the king and court, land law, and the law of women, theft, and suretyship, and the famous fixed values (galanas) for homicide compensation. Surviving manuscripts of the laws are medieval redactions (the Iorwerth, Blegywryd, and Cyfnerth versions), written centuries later, but they consistently attribute the code to Hywel's initiative.

Legacy

Hywel died in 949 or 950, after which his realm fragmented among his sons and rival dynasties. He is remembered in Welsh tradition as one of its great lawgivers; the Senedd building's debating chamber in Cardiff Bay is named Hywel Dda in his honour near the site's Tŷ Hywel building.
