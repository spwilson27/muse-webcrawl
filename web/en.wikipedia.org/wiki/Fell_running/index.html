Fell running

Fell running, also known as hill running, is the sport of running and racing, off-road, over upland country where a significant gradient is climbed. It differs from trail running in that it is mainly off-path, often crossing pathless moor and bog, and requires mountain navigation skills. The sport is most closely associated with northern England — especially the Lake District — and with Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Races

Races are classified by ascent and distance (categories A, B and C by severity, and short, medium and long by length). Events range from short village-show races of a few kilometres to classic long challenges such as the Bob Graham Round, a 66-mile circuit of 42 Lakeland peaks. Historic professional "guides races" were held at agricultural shows from the 19th century; the amateur sport organised itself with the founding of the Fell Runners Association in 1970, which publishes an annual calendar of hundreds of races and oversees English championships.

Skills and safety

Fell runners are expected to be self-reliant: races commonly require competitors to carry waterproof clothing, a map, compass, whistle and emergency food, and navigation in mist over pathless terrain is part of the discipline. Notable fell runners include Billy Bland, Joss Naylor and Jasmin Paris, who in 2019 won the 268-mile Montane Spine Race outright.
