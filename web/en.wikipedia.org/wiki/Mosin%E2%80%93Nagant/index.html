The Mosin–Nagant is a five-shot, bolt-action, internal magazine-fed military rifle developed between 1882 and 1891. Combining designs by Russian Captain Sergei Mosin and Belgian designer Léon Nagant, it was adopted by the Russian Empire in 1891 and became one of the most widely produced military rifles in history.

Design

The rifle fires the 7.62×54mmR cartridge and loads through a five-round integral magazine using stripper clips. Its name in Russian service was the "three-line rifle", a reference to the calibre measured in an old unit. Numerous variants were produced, including dragoon, Cossack, and carbine models, and the shortened Model 1891/30 became the standard Soviet infantry rifle.

Service history

Mosin–Nagants served in the Russo-Japanese War, both World Wars, the Russian Civil War, and countless later conflicts. During the Second World War, scoped sniper versions were used to great effect by marksmen such as Vasily Zaitsev and Lyudmila Pavlichenko, and by Finnish sniper Simo Häyhä with a Finnish-built variant. The rifle remains common in civilian markets and has appeared in conflicts well into the 21st century.
