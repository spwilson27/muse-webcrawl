Green New Deal

The Green New Deal (GND) is the name of various proposed economic stimulus programs in the United States that aim to address climate change and economic inequality. The name refers to the New Deal, a set of reforms and public works projects undertaken by President Franklin D. Roosevelt in response to the Great Depression of the 1930s.

The 2019 resolution

On February 7, 2019, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Senator Ed Markey introduced a non-binding resolution (H. Res. 109) "Recognizing the duty of the Federal Government to create a Green New Deal". The resolution proposed a "10-year national mobilization" to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions, create millions of jobs, invest in infrastructure, secure clean air and water, and promote justice and equity for frontline and vulnerable communities.

Origins of the term

The term was popularized by journalist Thomas Friedman in 2007 and was used by the Green Party of the United States, whose presidential candidates including Jill Stein adopted a Green New Deal platform beginning in 2012. Similar proposals have appeared internationally, including the European Green Deal announced by the European Commission in December 2019.

Goals and provisions

Proposals under the Green New Deal banner generally call for a rapid transition to renewable energy, public investment in green infrastructure and transportation, a federal job guarantee in some versions, and measures addressing social and racial inequality. The 2019 resolution also referenced goals such as affordable housing, universal access to healthy food, and economic security.

Reception and debate

The Green New Deal has been the subject of extensive political debate. Supporters argue that the scale of the climate crisis demands a mobilization comparable to the New Deal or World War II efforts. Critics have questioned its cost, feasibility, and scope. The resolution was blocked in the Senate in March 2019. Elements of the agenda influenced later legislation, including the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, the largest climate investment in U.S. history.
