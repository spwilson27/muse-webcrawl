Port of Keelung

The harbour at Keelung, northern Taiwan.

The Port of Keelung is a seaport located in Keelung City in northern Taiwan, adjacent to the East China Sea. It is operated by the Taiwan International Ports Corporation. The natural harbour, surrounded by hills, has long served as the principal gateway for the Taipei metropolitan area.

History

The harbour was developed during the Qing dynasty and expanded under Japanese rule (1895–1945), when Keelung became Taiwan's principal port of connection with Japan. After the Second World War it grew into one of the world's busiest container ports; in the mid-1980s it ranked among the top container ports globally.

Decline and role today

Keelung's growth has been constrained by the mountains and dense urban development surrounding the harbour, and it cannot easily accommodate the largest container ships. Much of northern Taiwan's container traffic shifted to the newer Port of Taipei, and Taiwan's largest port is now Kaohsiung in the south. Keelung remains important for cross-strait and coastal shipping, ferry services to outlying islands such as Matsu, and cruise ship calls.

Features

The port is notable for its narrow entrance, shipbreaking and ship repair facilities in the surrounding area, and the nightly Miaokou Night Market near the waterfront, one of Taiwan's most famous food markets.
