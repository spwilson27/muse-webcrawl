County borough

A county borough was a type of borough in England and Wales, and in Ireland, that was independent of the control of the county council that administered the surrounding area. County boroughs were first created by the Local Government Act 1888, which divided England and Wales into administrative counties and granted the largest towns a status that allowed them to run all their own local government services.

History

Before 1888, boroughs were towns with charters of self-government, but many of their functions had been taken over by the newly created county councils or ad hoc bodies. The 1888 Act created 59 county boroughs in England and two in Wales, generally from towns with populations of at least 50,000, although some smaller historic towns were included. Existing county corporate towns, such as Bristol and Norwich, also became county boroughs.

Further towns were granted county borough status during the twentieth century, usually after campaigns for greater autonomy. By 1974 there were 83 county boroughs in England and Wales. In Ireland, the corresponding term was also used; the cities of Belfast and Londonderry were county boroughs in Northern Ireland, and in the Republic of Ireland the term was later replaced by "city council" designations.

Abolition

The Local Government Act 1972 abolished county boroughs in England and Wales with effect from 1 April 1974, replacing them with metropolitan and non-metropolitan districts within a two-tier county structure. Some former county boroughs later regained single-tier status as unitary authorities following the local government reviews of the 1990s.
