The Battle of Grozny (1999–2000) was the siege and capture of the Chechen capital by Russian forces during the Second Chechen War. Unlike the improvised and costly assault of 1994–95, the Russians methodically encircled the city and subjected it to one of the heaviest bombardments since 1945, declaring it liberated on 6 February 2000.

Background

War resumed in autumn 1999 after the incursion of Chechen-based militants into Dagestan and a series of apartment bombings in Russia blamed on Chechens. Under Prime Minister Vladimir Putin, Russian forces advanced into Chechnya behind massive artillery and air strikes, reaching Grozny's outskirts in November 1999. The city was defended by an estimated 2,000–3,000 fighters under Aslan Maskhadov, Shamil Basayev and others; tens of thousands of civilians remained trapped inside.

The siege

Russian forces sealed the city and, from late December, fought inward through the suburbs using artillery, rockets and thermobaric weapons rather than exposed armoured columns. Ultimatums urged civilians to leave by a designated corridor, and the city's districts — most of them already ruined from the first war — were systematically destroyed. The United Nations later described Grozny as the most destroyed city on earth.

The breakout

On the night of 31 January–1 February 2000, the surviving Chechen fighters attempted to break out southwest toward Alkhan-Kala. Deceived by a bribed passage plan, the column walked into a minefield near Alkhan-Yurt; hundreds were killed or wounded, Basayev losing a foot to a mine, though Maskhadov and other commanders escaped to the mountains. On 6 February Russia raised its flag over the city.

Aftermath

The fall of Grozny did not end the war, which continued as a guerrilla insurgency for years, but it restored Russian control over the capital and cemented Putin's political ascendancy. The city's population had fallen from over 400,000 before the wars to a small fraction of that number, and reconstruction took decades.
