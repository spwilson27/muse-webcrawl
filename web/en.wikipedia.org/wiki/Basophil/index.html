Basophil

Basophils are a type of white blood cell. Basophils are the least numerous of the granulocytes, representing about 0.5% to 1% of circulating white blood cells. However, they are the largest type of granulocyte, measuring roughly 10–14 micrometres in diameter. They are responsible for inflammatory reactions during an immune response, as well as in the formation of acute and chronic allergic diseases, including anaphylaxis, asthma, atopic dermatitis and hay fever. They also produce compounds that coordinate immune responses, including histamine and serotonin that induce inflammation, heparin that prevents blood clotting, and they may compete with mast cells in their functions.

Structure

Basophils contain large cytoplasmic granules that obscure the cell nucleus under the microscope. When unstained, the nucleus is visible and it usually has two lobes. The granules stain deep purple with basic dyes such as haematoxylin, and they contain histamine, heparin, and various enzymes. The granules are water-soluble and can be partially washed out during standard staining procedures, sometimes giving the cytoplasm a vacuolated appearance.

Function

Basophils appear in many specific kinds of inflammatory reactions, particularly those that cause allergic symptoms. Like mast cells, basophils express the high-affinity IgE receptor FcεRI on their surface; when allergen-specific IgE bound to this receptor is cross-linked by allergen, the cells degranulate, releasing histamine and other mediators. Basophils also secrete interleukin-4 (IL-4) and can drive T helper 2 (Th2) immune responses. Together with eosinophils, they play a role in defence against parasites, particularly helminths.

Clinical significance

An elevated basophil count is termed basophilia, while a reduced count is basopenia. Basophilia can occur in myeloproliferative disorders such as chronic myeloid leukaemia, in hypersensitivity reactions, and in some inflammatory conditions. Basophils have also been implicated in the itch response and in chronic allergic skin diseases.

History

Basophils were first described in 1879 by the German physician Paul Ehrlich, who had earlier noted the staining properties of granulocytes in his work on blood cells.
