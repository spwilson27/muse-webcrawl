Power usage effectiveness

Power usage effectiveness (PUE) is a ratio that describes how efficiently a computer data center uses energy; specifically, how much energy is used by the computing equipment in contrast to the cooling and other overhead that supports the equipment.

PUE is the ratio of the total amount of energy used by a computer data center facility to the energy delivered to computing equipment:

PUE = Total Facility Energy / IT Equipment Energy

An ideal PUE is 1.0: everything not used directly by the computing equipment counts as overhead, including cooling, lighting, and power distribution losses. The metric was developed by The Green Grid, a non-profit consortium, and published in 2007.

Interpretation

A PUE of 2.0 means that for every watt used by the IT equipment, an additional watt is consumed in overhead. Enterprise data centers historically averaged values around 2.0, while hyperscale operators have reported values in the 1.1–1.2 range using techniques such as free-air cooling, hot-aisle/cold-aisle containment, high-efficiency power distribution, and siting facilities in cool climates.

Related metrics

The inverse of PUE is data center infrastructure efficiency (DCiE). Related metrics include water usage effectiveness (WUE), carbon usage effectiveness (CUE), and partial PUE (pPUE), which measures a subsystem of a facility. Critics note that PUE says nothing about the efficiency of the IT equipment or software itself, and can be gamed by increasing IT load; it is therefore often reported alongside utilisation and productivity metrics.

Standards

In 2016 the metric was adopted as a global standard under ISO/IEC 30134-2:2016. Reporting conventions distinguish measurement categories by where and how often the energy is metered.
