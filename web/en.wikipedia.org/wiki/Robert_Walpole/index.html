Robert Walpole, 1st Earl of Orford (26 August 1676 – 18 March 1745), known as Sir Robert Walpole between 1725 and 1742, was a British statesman and Whig politician generally regarded as the de facto first Prime Minister of Great Britain.

Early career

Educated at Eton and King's College, Cambridge, Walpole entered Parliament in 1701. He rose as Secretary at War and Treasurer of the Navy, was impeached by Tories in 1712 and briefly imprisoned in the Tower.

Premiership

After handling the South Sea Bubble crisis (1720), Walpole became First Lord of the Treasury and Chancellor of the Exchequer in 1721, dominating politics for over 20 years — the longest premiership in British history. He kept Britain at peace, lowered taxes, managed patronage through the Commons, and resided at 10 Downing Street. His fall followed the unpopular War of Jenkins' Ear.

Legacy

Walpole built Houghton Hall in Norfolk and amassed a major art collection, later sold to Catherine the Great. His son Horace Walpole was a noted writer. See also Rump Parliament, Rotten borough.
