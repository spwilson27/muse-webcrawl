Cholula massacre

The Cholula massacre was an attack carried out in October 1519 by the forces of Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés and his Tlaxcalan allies against the city of Cholula, one of the largest and most sacred cities of pre-Columbian Mexico.

Events

Cortés claimed to have uncovered a plot to ambush his men, a justification repeated in his letters to Charles V. Spanish and Tlaxcalan forces killed thousands of unarmed Cholultecans, including nobles gathered in the temple courtyard, and the city was sacked and partly burned. Death toll estimates vary widely, from around 3,000 to far higher figures.

Aftermath

The massacre spread terror through the region ahead of the Spanish advance on Tenochtitlan and remains a contested episode in accounts of the conquest, with Spanish chronicles, indigenous codices and later historians differing on the plot's existence and the scale of the killing.
