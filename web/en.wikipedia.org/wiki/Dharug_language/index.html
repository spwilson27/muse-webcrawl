The Dharug language, also called the Sydney language or Eora, is an Aboriginal Australian language of the Yuin–Kuric group that was traditionally spoken in the region of present-day Sydney, New South Wales. It was the language of the Darug people and of the clans around Sydney Harbour known collectively as the Eora.

History

Dharug was first recorded by British colonists after the arrival of the First Fleet in 1788; the notebooks of William Dawes, who learned from the young Patyegarang, preserve the most detailed early grammar and vocabulary. Colonisation rapidly displaced the language, and the last fluent first-language speakers died in the nineteenth or early twentieth century.

Revival

Since the 1990s, Dharug descendants and linguists have worked to revive the language from the historical records. A standardised spelling has been developed, the language is taught in some Sydney schools, and Dharug words survive in Sydney place names — such as Parramatta, Woolloomooloo and Bondi — and in Australian English borrowings including "wallaby", "dingo" and "boomerang" (via related forms). Whether the coastal and inland varieties were one language or two closely related ones (Dharug and Dharawal) remains debated.
