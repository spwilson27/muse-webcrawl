Velimir Khlebnikov, born Viktor Vladimirovich Khlebnikov (9 November [O.S. 28 October] 1885 – 28 June 1922), was a Russian poet and writer, a central figure of the Russian avant-garde and co-founder, together with David Burliuk, Vladimir Mayakovsky and others, of Russian Futurism. He is best known for his experiments with poetic language, including the invented transrational language zaum, and for works such as the "supersaga" Zangezi.

Life

Khlebnikov was born in Malye Derbety in the Astrakhan Governorate, the son of an ornithologist and forester, and grew up with a strong interest in nature, mathematics and Slavic mythology. He studied mathematics and natural sciences at Kazan University and later in Saint Petersburg, where he joined the Futurist group Hylaea and contributed to the landmark 1912 manifesto A Slap in the Face of Public Taste. He lived an itinerant life, served in the army during the First World War and the Russian Civil War, and travelled to Persia with the Red Army in 1921. He died of malnutrition and illness in the village of Santalovo in 1922, aged 36.

Work

Khlebnikov sought to discover the fundamental units of poetic meaning and the mathematical laws of history. His zaum poems, such as the "Incantation by Laughter" (1910), build words from Russian roots and sounds beyond ordinary sense. Other major works include the prose-poem Ka, the play-like supersaga Zangezi (1922), and his numerological writings on the "Tables of Destiny", in which he claimed historical events recur at intervals governed by powers of 2 and 3. He styled himself "President of Planet Earth" and imagined a society of such presidents, signing manifestos in that role.

Legacy

Largely neglected after his death, Khlebnikov was championed by the linguist Roman Jakobson and later scholars, and is now regarded as one of the most original poets of the twentieth century, influencing Russian poetry, sound poetry and conceptual writing.
