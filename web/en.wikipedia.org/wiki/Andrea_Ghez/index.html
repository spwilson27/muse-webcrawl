Andrea Mia Ghez (born 16 June 1965) is an American astrophysicist and professor at the University of California, Los Angeles. She shared one half of the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics with Reinhard Genzel 'for the discovery of a supermassive compact object at the centre of our galaxy' — the black hole Sagittarius A* — the other half going to Roger Penrose. She is the fourth woman to receive the Physics Nobel.

From the mid-1990s, using the Keck telescopes with speckle imaging and then adaptive optics, Ghez's group tracked individual stars orbiting an unseen mass at the Galactic Centre. The star S2 (S02) completes an orbit in about 16 years with a pericentre of only 17 light-hours, confining ~4 million solar masses to a region smaller than the Solar System — conclusive evidence for a supermassive black hole. The same orbits now test General relativity in extreme gravity (gravitational redshift detected 2018–19).

Career

Ghez studied at MIT and took her PhD at Caltech (1992). At UCLA she built the Galactic Center Group and pioneered the high-resolution techniques — her friendly rivalry with Genzel's European team, working the same stars with different telescopes, drove two decades of rapid progress. She has also worked on star formation and binary stars, and is a prominent public communicator of astronomy.
