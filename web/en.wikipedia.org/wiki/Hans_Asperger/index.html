Hans Asperger

Hans Asperger
Born: 18 February 1906, Hausbrunn, Austria-Hungary
Died: 21 October 1980, Vienna, Austria
Field: Paediatrics
Known for: Describing "autistic psychopathy"; Asperger syndrome (named after him)

Hans Asperger (18 February 1906 – 21 October 1980) was an Austrian paediatrician, medical theorist, and medical professor. He is best known for his early studies on atypical neurology, specifically in children. His work on what he called "autistic psychopathy" was little noticed in his lifetime but was brought to wide attention by Lorna Wing in 1981, after which the diagnosis "Asperger syndrome" was named for him.

Career

Asperger studied medicine at the University of Vienna and worked at the University Children's Hospital, where he directed the special education ("Heilpädagogik") unit. In 1944 he published his postdoctoral thesis describing four boys with social difficulties, intense narrow interests, and good language and cognitive skills.

Controversy

Research published in 2018 by historian Herwig Czech documented Asperger's cooperation with the Nazi child "euthanasia" programme, including referrals of children to the Am Spiegelgrund clinic, where many were killed. The findings have prompted debate about his legacy and about retaining his name in diagnosis; the DSM-5 (2013) and ICD-11 folded Asperger syndrome into autism spectrum disorder.
