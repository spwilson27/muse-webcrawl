Autonomous regions of China

Autonomous regions are a first-level administrative division of the People's Republic of China. Like provinces, an autonomous region has its own local government, but under the Constitution of the People's Republic of China, an autonomous region is granted a higher level of autonomy, with nominal rights to establish regional legislation and to administer affairs concerning the titular ethnic minority group associated with the region.

The five regions

China has five autonomous regions: Inner Mongolia (established 1947, for the Mongols), Xinjiang (1955, for the Uyghurs), Guangxi (1958, for the Zhuang), Ningxia (1958, for the Hui), and Tibet (1965, for the Tibetans). Together they cover a large share of China's land area, particularly in the north and west, though in several of them the Han Chinese now form the largest population group.

Governance

Each region has a People's Government chaired by a member of the titular ethnic group, and a regional People's Congress that may enact autonomy regulations subject to approval by the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress. In practice, the highest authority in each region is the regional secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, a position not restricted by ethnicity. Below the regional level, China also maintains autonomous prefectures and autonomous counties for other minority groups.
