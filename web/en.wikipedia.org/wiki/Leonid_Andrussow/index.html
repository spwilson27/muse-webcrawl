Leonid Andrussow

Leonid Andrussow
Born: 28 November 1896, Riga, Russian Empire
Died: 15 December 1988, Paris, France
Known for: Andrussow oxidation process

Leonid Andrussow (28 November 1896 – 15 December 1988) was a German chemical engineer of Baltic origin. He is known for inventing the Andrussow process, the principal industrial method for manufacturing hydrogen cyanide, a key feedstock for nylon, acrylics and other chemicals.

Career

Andrussow studied chemistry in Berlin and worked at BASF in Ludwigshafen, researching the catalytic oxidation of ammonia–methane mixtures. In 1927 he patented the process in which methane and ammonia react with oxygen over a platinum–rhodium gauze at about 1,200 °C to produce hydrogen cyanide and water: 2 CH4 + 2 NH3 + 3 O2 → 2 HCN + 6 H2O. The first industrial plant based on the process came into operation in the 1930s.

Later work

Andrussow also made contributions to rocket propellant chemistry, publishing on self-igniting (hypergolic) mixtures such as nitric acid with amines, and wrote extensively on the physicochemical properties of liquids, including the "Andrussow equation" relating vapour pressure to temperature. After the Second World War he continued his research career in France.
