Gorm the Old

Reign: c. 936 – c. 958/964
Successor: Harald Bluetooth
Spouse: Thyra
Issue: Harald Bluetooth, Knut Danaást, Gunnhild
Dynasty: House of Jelling
Burial: Jelling, Denmark

Gorm the Old (Danish: Gorm den Gamle; Old Norse: Gormr gamli), also called Gorm the Languid, was a ruler of Denmark, reigning from c. 936 to his death c. 958 or a few years later. He ruled from Jelling, and made the oldest of the Jelling stones in honour of his wife Thyra. Gorm is the first historically recognized king of Denmark; his son Harald Bluetooth succeeded him.

Reign and family

According to the chroniclers Adam of Bremen and Saxo Grammaticus, Gorm was the son of Harthacnut, a semi-legendary king who came from "Northmannia" (Norway or Normandy) and took power in Denmark in the early 10th century. Gorm's epithet "the Old" likely refers to his status as the oldest known ancestor of the Danish royal line. His wife Thyra is celebrated on the Jelling stones; later tradition credited her with ordering the construction of the Danevirke fortifications, earning her the title "Ornament of Denmark" (Danebod) on the smaller Jelling stone.

The Jelling stones

The smaller Jelling stone, raised by Gorm, reads in runes: "King Gorm made this monument in memory of Thyra, his wife, Denmark's ornament." It is the oldest known use of the name "Denmark" within the country's borders. His son Harald raised the larger stone nearby, proclaiming that he "won for himself all of Denmark and Norway and made the Danes Christian." Gorm was originally buried in a chamber in the great North Mound at Jelling; dendrochronology dates the chamber to 958/959. His remains were later re-interred, probably by Harald, in the church at Jelling, where a skeleton of a man of his period has been identified as possibly his.
