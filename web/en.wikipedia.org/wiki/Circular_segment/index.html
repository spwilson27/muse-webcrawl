Circular segment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In geometry, a circular segment is the region of a circle cut off by a secant or chord: it is the area between a chord of the circle and the arc that the chord subtends. The segment is the smaller part when the chord is not a diameter; the larger part is also sometimes called a segment, with the smaller usually meant by default.

Formulas

Let R be the radius of the circle and θ the central angle subtended by the chord, measured in radians. The area of the segment is

A = (R²/2)(θ − sin θ).

The chord length is c = 2R sin(θ/2), and the sagitta (height) of the segment — the distance from the chord's midpoint to the arc — is h = R − R cos(θ/2) = R(1 − cos(θ/2)). Conversely, given chord length and height, the radius can be recovered as R = (h² + (c/2)²)/(2h).

Applications

Circular segments appear in engineering and architecture wherever arched openings, partially filled horizontal tanks and pipes, or curved roofs are analysed; the segment area formula is used to compute the volume of liquid in a partly filled cylindrical vessel lying on its side.
