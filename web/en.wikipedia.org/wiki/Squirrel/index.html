Squirrel

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Order: Rodentia
Family: Sciuridae

Squirrels are members of the family Sciuridae, a family of small and medium-sized rodents that includes tree squirrels, ground squirrels (including marmots, chipmunks and prairie dogs), and flying squirrels. There are more than 280 species, native to the Americas, Eurasia and Africa, and introduced to Australia.

Description

Squirrels typically have slender bodies, bushy tails and large eyes, with soft, silky fur that varies in colour between species. Their hind legs are longer than their forelegs, and their sharp claws and reversible hind feet suit them for climbing. Ever-growing incisors allow them to gnaw hard nuts and seeds. Tree squirrels range from the tiny African pygmy squirrel, about 10 cm long, to the large giant squirrels of Asia.

Behaviour and diet

Most squirrels are diurnal herbivores feeding on seeds, nuts, fruits, buds and fungi, though some take insects and small vertebrates. Many species cache food in scattered hoards, and forgotten caches help disperse tree seeds, making squirrels important agents of forest regeneration. Tree squirrels build nests (dreys) in branches or holes, while ground squirrels live in burrow systems and may hibernate.

Relationship with humans

Squirrels are familiar animals in parks and gardens. The introduced grey squirrel has displaced the native red squirrel across much of Britain and Ireland and is regarded as an invasive species in several countries. Squirrels also appear widely in folklore and children's literature.
