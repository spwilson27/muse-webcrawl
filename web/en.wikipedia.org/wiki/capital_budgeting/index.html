Capital budgeting

Capital budgeting (or investment appraisal) is the planning process used by organisations to determine whether long-term investments — such as new machinery, new plants, new products, or research and development projects — are worth funding through the firm's capital structure. It is a central topic in corporate finance.

Techniques

The main evaluation techniques are:

  • Net present value (NPV): the sum of the present values of a project's future cash flows discounted at the cost of capital, minus the initial investment. Projects with positive NPV are accepted.
  • Internal rate of return (IRR): the discount rate at which NPV equals zero; a project is accepted if the IRR exceeds the hurdle rate.
  • Payback period: the time required for cumulative cash flows to recover the initial outlay.
  • Profitability index and modified internal rate of return (MIRR).

Considerations

Because capital is limited, firms often face capital rationing and must rank mutually exclusive projects. Analysis must account for risk, inflation, taxation, and the time value of money; techniques such as sensitivity analysis, scenario analysis, and real-options valuation are used to handle uncertainty. Only incremental, after-tax cash flows are relevant, and sunk costs are excluded.
