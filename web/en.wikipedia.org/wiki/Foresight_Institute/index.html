Foresight Institute

Founded1986
FoundersK. Eric Drexler, Christine Peterson
TypeNonprofit research institute
FocusNanotechnology, AI safety, longevity, neurotechnology

The Foresight Institute is a San Francisco-based nonprofit research institute founded in 1986 to promote the development and beneficial use of transformative technologies, particularly molecular nanotechnology. It was co-founded by K. Eric Drexler, author of Engines of Creation (1986), and Christine Peterson, who coined the term "open source" in connection with its popularization.

Activities

The Institute is known for the Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology, awarded since 1993 for advances toward molecular manufacturing, named for physicist Richard Feynman, whose 1959 talk "There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom" is regarded as a founding document of the field. Foresight also runs the Foresight Fellowship supporting early-career researchers, salons and conferences on topics including artificial intelligence safety, longevity biotechnology, whole-brain emulation, and molecular machines.

Early work emphasized guidelines for the safe development of molecular nanotechnology and debates over scenarios such as "grey goo". In recent decades the Institute broadened its focus to several technology areas it considers pivotal for the long-term future of humanity.
