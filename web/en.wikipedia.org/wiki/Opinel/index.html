Opinel

Opinel
Founded: 1890
Founder: Joseph Opinel
Headquarters: Chambéry, Savoie, France
Products: Folding knives, kitchen knives, tools

The Opinel knife is a popular French folding knife, prized for its simplicity, low price and distinctive beechwood handle. Designed by Joseph Opinel in 1890 in the village of Albiez-le-Vieux in Savoie, it has become an icon of French everyday culture and is sold in sizes numbered from No. 2 to No. 13 according to blade length.

Design

The original design consists of just a handful of parts: a blade, a wooden handle, a metal bolster (ferrule) with a rivet acting as the pivot, and — on larger models — the Virobloc, a rotating locking collar patented by Marcel Opinel in 1955 that locks the blade open (and on modern versions, closed as well). Blades are available in carbon steel, which sharpens easily but stains, and in stainless steel.

The crowned hand

The blade is stamped with the Opinel emblem, the "crowned hand": the blessing hand of Saint John the Baptist, combined with the crown of the Dukes of Savoie. The mark dates to 1909, when Joseph Opinel registered it.

Cultural status

Cheap, robust and ubiquitous, the knife became the standard pocket knife of French farmers, hikers and picnickers. In 1985 the Victoria and Albert Museum in London included it in an exhibition of the world's best-designed objects, and it has been cited alongside designs such as the Porsche 911. The company remains family-owned, based in Chambéry, and produces millions of knives a year.
