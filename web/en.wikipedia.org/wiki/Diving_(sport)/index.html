Diving (sport)

Governing bodyWorld Aquatics (formerly FINA)
Olympic events3 m springboard; 10 m platform; synchronised 3 m; synchronised 10 m
Olympic since1904
JudgingPanel scores execution; multiplied by degree of difficulty

Diving is the sport of jumping or falling into water from a platform or springboard, usually while performing acrobatics. It combines elements of gymnastics and dance, and is judged both on the elegance and precision of the dive and on its technical difficulty.

Competition

International competition is held on the 3-metre springboard and the 10-metre platform, individually and in synchronised pairs, in which two divers perform identical dives simultaneously and are also judged on their synchronisation. Divers perform a list of dives from the five groups (forward, backward, reverse, inward, and twisting), in straight, pike, tuck or free positions. Each dive has a degree of difficulty; judges' execution scores (after discarding high and low marks) are multiplied by it. A "rip" entry, with minimal splash, is the mark of a well-executed finish. Springboard diving rewards height and bounce from the flexible board; platform diving is performed from a rigid tower.

History

Diving grew out of gymnastics movements performed over water in Germany and Sweden and appeared at the 1904 St Louis Olympics as "fancy diving". Greg Louganis of the United States, a double gold medallist in 1984 and 1988, is widely regarded as the greatest diver of the twentieth century; since the 1990s China has dominated the sport, winning the great majority of Olympic titles in the twenty-first century. High diving from 20 m (women) and 27 m (men) has been added to the World Aquatics Championships programme.
