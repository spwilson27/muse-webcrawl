Oxazoline

Formula (parent): C₃H₅NO

Ring: Five-membered; one O, one N, one C=N double bond

Common derivative: 2-oxazolines

Uses: Ligands (BOX, PyBOX); protecting groups; polymers

Oxazoline is a five-membered heterocyclic compound containing one oxygen and one nitrogen atom and one double bond. Of the three possible isomers, the 2-oxazolines — with the double bond between carbon and nitrogen adjacent to the oxygen — are by far the most important, serving as monomers, protecting groups and ligands in organic and coordination chemistry.

Synthesis

2-Oxazolines are commonly made by cyclising N-(2-hydroxyethyl)amides, or from amino alcohols and nitriles or carboxylic acids. The saturated analogue is oxazolidine, and the aromatic, fully unsaturated relative is oxazole.

Applications

Ligands: Chiral bis(oxazoline) ("BOX") and pyridine-bis(oxazoline) ("PyBOX") ligands chelate metals through their two nitrogen donors and are workhorses of asymmetric catalysis, including cyclopropanation and Diels–Alder reactions; oxazoline-bearing phosphines (PHOX ligands) are standard in palladium-catalysed allylic substitution.

Synthesis: 2-Oxazolines (notably Evans' oxazolidinone relatives in auxiliary chemistry, and Meyer's use of 2-methyloxazoline as a masked carboxylic acid) protect and direct functional groups.

Polymers: Cationic ring-opening polymerisation of 2-oxazolines gives poly(2-oxazoline)s, water-soluble "pseudo-peptide" polymers investigated as alternatives to polyethylene glycol in biomedicine.
