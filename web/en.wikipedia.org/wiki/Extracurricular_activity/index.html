Extracurricular activity

Image placeholder: Students in after-school activities

An extracurricular activity (ECA) or extra academic activity (EAA) or cultural activities is an activity, performed by students, that falls outside the realm of the normal curriculum of school or university education. Such activities are generally voluntary (as opposed to mandatory), social, philanthropic, and often involve others of the same age.

Examples

Extracurricular activities include sports teams, academic clubs, debate and speech, Model United Nations, student government, school publications, music and theatre ensembles, volunteer and community service, scouting, and part-time work experience. Schools, colleges, and universities support such activities to promote socialisation, leadership, teamwork, and personal development beyond classroom instruction.

Significance

Research generally finds that participation in structured extracurricular activities is associated with higher academic achievement, better school engagement, and improved social skills, although effects vary by activity and intensity. In university admissions, particularly in the United States, extracurricular involvement is considered alongside grades and test scores as an indicator of an applicant's interests and initiative. Critics note that heavy schedules can cause stress, and that access to activities is unequal across socioeconomic groups.
