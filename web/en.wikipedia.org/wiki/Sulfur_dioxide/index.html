Sulfur dioxide (IUPAC-recommended spelling: sulphur dioxide, also sulfur(IV) oxide) is the chemical compound with the formula SO2. It is a toxic, colourless gas responsible for the odour of burnt matches. It is released naturally by volcanic activity and is produced industrially by burning sulfur or sulfur-containing fossil fuels.

Production and uses

Most sulfur dioxide is generated by roasting sulfide ores or burning elemental sulfur and is immediately converted, via the contact process, into sulfuric acid — the largest-volume industrial chemical. Sulfur dioxide is also used as a preservative and antioxidant in winemaking and dried fruit (E220), as a refrigerant historically, and as a bleaching agent.

Environmental and health effects

Sulfur dioxide is a major air pollutant. In the atmosphere it oxidises to sulfur trioxide and sulfuric acid, a principal component of acid rain, which damages forests, soils, lakes, and buildings. It irritates the eyes and respiratory tract and can trigger bronchoconstriction, particularly in people with asthma. Emission controls, low-sulfur fuels, and flue-gas desulfurisation have sharply reduced emissions in many countries since the 1970s. Volcanic eruptions can inject large quantities of SO2 into the stratosphere, forming sulfate aerosols that temporarily cool the climate.
