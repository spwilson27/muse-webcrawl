The Alexander Archipelago is a group of about 1,100 islands off the southeastern coast of Alaska, forming the island chain of the Alaska Panhandle. The islands are the tops of a submerged coastal mountain range, separated from the mainland and each other by deep, fjord-like channels.

Geography and ecology

The largest islands include Prince of Wales Island, Chichagof Island, Admiralty Island, Baranof Island, and Revillagigedo Island. The archipelago is covered by the temperate rainforest of the Tongass National Forest and supports dense populations of brown bears, wolves (including the Alexander Archipelago wolf), Sitka deer, and bald eagles.

History and communities

The islands are the traditional territory of the Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian peoples. Communities such as Ketchikan, Sitka, Petersburg, and Wrangell depend on fishing, timber, and tourism, and are connected principally by the Alaska Marine Highway ferry system and air travel, as there are few roads between them.
