Diameter

Image: Circle with radius, diameter, circumference, and chord labelled

In geometry, a diameter of a circle is any straight line segment that passes through the centre of the circle and whose endpoints lie on the circle. It is the longest chord of the circle, and its length is twice the length of the radius. Both definitions are also valid for the diameter of a sphere.

In more modern usage, the length of a diameter is also called the diameter. In this sense one speaks of the diameter rather than a diameter. For a circle of radius r, the diameter d satisfies d = 2r, and the circumference is πd.

Generalizations

The definitions given above are only valid for circles, spheres and convex bodies. The diameter of a subset of a metric space is the least upper bound of the distances between all pairs of points in the subset; for a plane figure it corresponds to the greatest distance between any two points of the figure. For a convex shape in the plane, the diameter can be found by rotating a pair of parallel supporting lines (calipers) around the shape.

Symbol

The symbol ⌀ is sometimes used in engineering and drafting to indicate the diameter of a circle or cylindrical feature, as in "⌀ 55 mm". It resembles the letter ø but is a distinct symbol.
