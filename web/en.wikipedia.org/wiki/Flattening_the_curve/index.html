Flattening the curve

Flattening the curve is a public health strategy used to slow down the spread of an infectious disease, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic. The "curve" refers to the projected number of people who contract the disease over a period of time, plotted on an epidemic curve. A flatter curve assumes the same total number of infections may occur, but spread over a longer period, so that fewer people are seeking care at any given time and health care systems are not overwhelmed.

Concept

Without protective measures, an epidemic tends to peak early and sharply, potentially exceeding the capacity of hospitals — represented in diagrams as a horizontal "health care system capacity" line. Protective measures such as social distancing, hand hygiene, isolation of cases, school and workplace closures, and mask-wearing delay and reduce the peak, keeping demand within capacity and buying time for treatments and vaccines to be developed. The idea was illustrated in a widely circulated 2007 US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention guidance graphic for pandemic influenza, and popularised in March 2020 by charts adapted by Drew Harris and published by The Economist and others; the slogan "flatten the curve" spread widely on social media under hashtags such as #FlattenTheCurve.

Background

The strategy contrasts with "raising the line" — increasing health care capacity (beds, ventilators, staff, testing) — and the two approaches are complementary. Modelling, notably the March 2020 Imperial College London report on COVID-19, distinguished "mitigation" (flattening) from "suppression" (reducing transmission enough to shrink the epidemic), informing lockdown policies in many countries.
