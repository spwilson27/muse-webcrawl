Margarita

A margarita is a cocktail consisting of tequila, triple sec (orange liqueur), and lime juice, often served with salt on the rim of the glass. It is traditionally served in a margarita glass, a stepped-diameter variation of the champagne coupe, and can be shaken with ice, blended with ice (a frozen margarita), or served straight up.

History

The origin of the margarita is disputed, with several invention claims from Mexico and Texas in the 1930s and 1940s. One popular account attributes it to Carlos “Danny” Herrera of Tijuana in 1938, who is said to have created it for a customer allergic to many spirits but not tequila; another credits Texas socialite Margaret Sames, who said she mixed the drink at her Acapulco home in 1948. Whatever its precise origin, the drink belongs to the daisy family of cocktails — margarita is Spanish for “daisy”.

Variations

Common variations substitute other liqueurs or fruits, such as the Tommy’s margarita, which replaces triple sec with agave syrup. The International Bartenders Association recipe specifies tequila, Cointreau and fresh lime juice in roughly a 7:4:3 ratio.
