Portuguese Water Dog

The Portuguese Water Dog (Portuguese: cão de água português) is a working breed of dog originally from the Algarve coast of Portugal. For centuries it worked aboard fishing boats, herding fish into nets, retrieving lost tackle and carrying messages between boats.

History

The breed nearly became extinct in the twentieth century as fishing mechanised. In the 1930s the Portuguese shipping magnate Vasco Bensaude began a preservation programme from stock gathered in the Algarve, on which the modern breed is founded. Exported to the United States in the 1960s and 1970s, it gained American Kennel Club recognition in 1983.

Description

It is a medium-sized, robust dog with a distinctive single coat of curly or wavy hair — black, brown or white — that does not shed, making the breed a popular choice for allergy sufferers, though no dog is truly hypoallergenic. Traditional clips include the "lion clip", with the muzzle and hindquarters shaved, and the full "retriever clip". Webbed feet and a powerful swimming ability reflect its aquatic work.

Temperament and fame

Portuguese Water Dogs are intelligent, energetic, loyal and sometimes stubborn, excelling in obedience, agility and water sports. The breed became world-famous in 2009 when Bo, a Portuguese Water Dog, became the First Dog of the United States as the pet of President Barack Obama and his family; a second, Sunny, followed in 2013.
