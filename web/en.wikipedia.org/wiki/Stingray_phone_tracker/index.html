Stingray phone tracker

The Stingray is an IMSI-catcher, a cellular phone surveillance device, manufactured by Harris Corporation. Initially developed for the military and intelligence communities, the Stingray and similar devices have been widely adopted by federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies in the United States and elsewhere. The device mimics a legitimate cell phone tower, prompting mobile phones in an area to connect to it, which allows operators to locate phones and identify their IMSI numbers, and in some configurations to intercept communications.

Capabilities and use

Stingray devices act as a "cell-site simulator": phones within range connect to the device rather than to a genuine tower, enabling tracking of a target phone's location with high precision. The technology collects identifying information from all phones in the area, not only the target's, which has raised civil liberties concerns. Law enforcement agencies have frequently been bound by non-disclosure agreements with the manufacturer, leading to controversy over secrecy in criminal proceedings.

Legal issues

The use of cell-site simulators has been challenged in court on Fourth Amendment grounds. In 2015, the U.S. Department of Justice adopted a policy generally requiring a search warrant supported by probable cause for federal use of the technology, and several states have enacted similar warrant requirements.
