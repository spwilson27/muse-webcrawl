The Pedro Miguel Locks are one of the three sets of locks of the Panama Canal, located on the Pacific side of the isthmus. Unlike the multi-step Gatún Locks on the Atlantic side and the two-step Miraflores Locks nearby, Pedro Miguel consists of a single chamber, which raises or lowers ships by about 9.5 metres (31 feet) between Miraflores Lake and the level of Gatún Lake at the southern end of the Culebra Cut.

Operation

A ship travelling from the Pacific to the Atlantic first passes under the Bridge of the Americas, transits the two-step Miraflores Locks, crosses the small Miraflores Lake, and enters Pedro Miguel for the final single lift before entering the Culebra (Gaillard) Cut toward Gatún Lake. Southbound ships reverse the sequence. The lock chambers of the original canal measure 1,000 feet long and 110 feet wide, defining the original Panamax ship size; gates are massive steel miter gates, and ships are guided by electric towing locomotives ("mules") running on rails along the lock walls.

Setting

The locks are situated beside the town of Pedro Miguel and the former Canal Zone community of Paraíso. The Centennial Bridge crosses the canal just north of the locks. The Pedro Miguel locksite, together with Miraflores, is among the most visited viewpoints for observing canal transits, and the nearby Miraflores Visitor Center presents the canal's history and operation.
