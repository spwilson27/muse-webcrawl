John Goodwin Tower (September 29, 1925 – April 5, 1991) was an American politician who served as a United States Senator from Texas from 1961 to 1985. The first Republican senator from Texas since Reconstruction, he was a leading congressional voice on defence matters.

Senate career

Tower won a special election in 1961 for the seat vacated by Lyndon B. Johnson, an early sign of the Republican Party's rise in the South. A conservative on most issues — he voted against the Civil Rights Act of 1964 on constitutional grounds — he rose to chair the Senate Armed Services Committee from 1981 to 1984 and was known for his expertise in military policy.

After the Senate

Tower chaired the 1987 Tower Commission investigating the Iran–Contra affair. In 1989 President George H. W. Bush nominated him as Secretary of Defense, but the Senate rejected the nomination amid personal and professional allegations — a rare defeat for a former senator. He died in a plane crash in Georgia in 1991.
