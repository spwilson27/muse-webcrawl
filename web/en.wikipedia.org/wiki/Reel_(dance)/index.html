Reel (dance)

The reel is a folk dance type, and the accompanying music type, originating in Scotland and Ireland. Reels are also danced throughout the Scottish and Irish diaspora. In Scottish country dancing and Irish dance, the reel is one of the principal dance types, performed in 2/2 or 4/4 time with quick, lively steps.

Music

Reel music is in simple time — either cut time (2/2) or 4/4 — played at a fast tempo, with a continuous stream of quavers (eighth notes) and characteristic patterns of repeated notes and rolls (in Irish music) or bowing figures (in Scottish and North American fiddling). Famous reels include "The Wind That Shakes the Barley" and "The Silver Spear" in the Irish tradition. In Scotland the reel is associated with the strathspey as a contrasting slower, dotted-rhythm type; the two are often paired.

Dance forms

In Scottish country dancing, reels of three or four dancers — in which dancers trace a figure-of-eight-like path (a "reel of three") — form a basic figure, while the eightsome reel is a popular ceilidh dance for eight dancers. In Highland dancing, the Highland fling and the sword dance are distinct, but reels also feature in competition dances such as the "Highland reel". Irish stepdance includes both soft-shoe (light) reels and hard-shoe (treble) reels.

In North America

Reels crossed the Atlantic with emigrants and became central to old-time fiddle music, contra dancing and square dancing, and to the French-Canadian and Cape Breton fiddle traditions. The Virginia reel is a well-known American folk dance derived from the form.
