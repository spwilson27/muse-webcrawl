Bhopal disaster

[Image placeholder: Union Carbide plant, Bhopal]

The Bhopal disaster or Bhopal gas tragedy was a chemical accident on the night of 2–3 December 1984 at the Union Carbide India Limited (UCIL) pesticide plant in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India. Considered the world's worst industrial disaster, over 500,000 people in the small towns around the plant were exposed to highly toxic methyl isocyanate (MIC) gas.

Water entered a storage tank containing about 42 tonnes of MIC, triggering an exothermic runaway reaction that vented the gas over the sleeping city. Estimates of deaths vary widely: the official immediate death toll was 2,259, while the Madhya Pradesh government confirmed 3,787 deaths related to the gas release; other estimates put the number of deaths within weeks at 8,000 or more, with thousands more in following years. Over half a million injuries were recorded.

Aftermath

Union Carbide settled with the Government of India in 1989 for US$470 million. Criminal cases dragged on for decades; in 2010 seven former UCIL employees were convicted of causing death by negligence and received two-year sentences. Site contamination and compensation for victims remain subjects of litigation and activism.
