List of countries by net international investment position

The net international investment position (NIIP) is the difference between a country's external financial assets and its external liabilities — in effect, whether a nation is a net creditor or a net debtor to the rest of the world. A positive NIIP means residents own more assets abroad than foreigners own in the country; a negative NIIP means the reverse. Figures below are approximate values compiled from IMF data, expressed in US dollars; the ratio of NIIP to GDP is often more informative than the absolute figure.

The United States has by far the world's largest negative NIIP in absolute terms, reflecting decades of current-account deficits and foreign purchases of US assets; even so, the US historically earns more on its foreign assets than it pays on its liabilities, an effect sometimes called the "exorbitant privilege". Japan has been the world's largest net creditor for over three decades, though China and Germany have rivalled it in recent years.
