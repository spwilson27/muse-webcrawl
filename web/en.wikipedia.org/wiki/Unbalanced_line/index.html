Unbalanced line

In electrical engineering, an unbalanced line (or single-ended signalling) is a transmission line in which the voltages on the conductors are referenced to a common ground: typically one conductor carries the signal while the other is the ground return. The most familiar example is coaxial cable, whose central conductor carries the signal and whose outer shield serves as the return path and ground. Unbalanced lines contrast with balanced lines, in which two conductors carry equal and opposite signals and neither is grounded.

Coaxial cable: the signal travels on the centre conductor and returns on the grounded shield.

Characteristics

Unbalanced transmission is simple and cheap, and coaxial cable gives good shielding against external interference at radio frequencies. However, because the signal is referenced to ground, noise and interference picked up on the ground or shield—such as ground-loop hum—can enter the signal path, and the line offers little rejection of common-mode interference. It is therefore standard for consumer audio connections (RCA cables), antenna feeds, and RF equipment, but professional audio and long-distance telecommunications generally prefer balanced lines for their noise rejection. A balun converts between balanced and unbalanced lines.
