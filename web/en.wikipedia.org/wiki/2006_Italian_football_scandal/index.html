The 2006 Italian football scandal, known in Italy as Calciopoli, was a match-influence scandal that erupted in May 2006 when transcripts of wiretapped telephone conversations were published showing that club officials — most prominently Juventus general manager Luciano Moggi — had cultivated relationships with referee designators and federation officials to influence officiating appointments during the 2004–05 and 2005–06 Serie A seasons.

Investigations and verdicts

Sporting trials in the summer of 2006 moved at extraordinary speed, partly so the following season could begin. Juventus were stripped of their 2005 and 2006 league titles (the 2006 title was reassigned to Inter Milan) and relegated to Serie B for the first time in the club's history, starting with a points deduction later reduced to nine. Milan, Fiorentina, Lazio and Reggina received points penalties but remained in Serie A. Moggi and Juventus executive Antonio Giraudo received lifetime bans from football roles.

Aftermath

The scandal broke in the same summer Italy won the 2006 FIFA World Cup, with several Juventus players in the squad. Many star players left Juventus, though Gianluigi Buffon, Alessandro Del Piero and others stayed to win Serie B at the first attempt. Subsequent criminal trials in Naples produced mixed outcomes, with some convictions later overturned or time-barred, and continuing debate in Italy over the fairness of the rushed sporting process.
