Koreatown (Korean: 코리아타운), or K-Town, is an ethnic Korean enclave in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, centred on West 32nd Street between Fifth Avenue and Broadway, a stretch officially co-named "Korea Way". Unlike the larger residential Korean communities of Flushing, Queens, Manhattan's Koreatown is primarily a commercial district of restaurants, shops and businesses serving the wider metropolitan Korean American population.

History

Korean businesses began concentrating on West 32nd Street in the late 1970s and 1980s, drawn by proximity to the Garment District, Herald Square retail and Korean import-export and wholesale firms in the area. Korean restaurants, bookstores, grocery markets and karaoke bars multiplied along the block, and by the 1990s the strip had become the recognised centre of Korean commerce in Manhattan.

Character

The single block of 32nd Street west of Fifth Avenue packs dozens of Korean barbecue restaurants, bakeries, dessert cafés, beauty shops and 24-hour eateries into a few hundred metres. Above street level, the buildings house Korean banks, travel agencies, hair salons, acupuncture clinics, clubs and offices. Larger waves of visitors arrive at night and on weekends; the district is also a gathering point for Korean cultural events and for watching Korean national sports teams.
