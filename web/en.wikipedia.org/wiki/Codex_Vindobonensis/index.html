Codex Vindobonensis

A manuscript page of the Vienna Dioscurides (Codex Vindobonensis med. gr. 1).

Codex Vindobonensis (Latin for "Vienna Codex", from Vindobona, the Roman name of Vienna) is a name applied to a number of important manuscripts held by the Austrian National Library in Vienna, distinguished by their shelfmarks. The term is therefore ambiguous and usually refers to one of several famous codices.

Notable examples

The most celebrated is the Codex Vindobonensis med. gr. 1, the Vienna Dioscurides, an early sixth-century Byzantine illuminated manuscript of De materia medica by Dioscorides, made in Constantinople around 512 CE for the imperial princess Juliana Anicia. It contains nearly 400 full-page paintings of plants and is the oldest surviving illustrated herbal.

Other manuscripts called Codex Vindobonensis include the Codex Vindobonensis 2554, a lavishly illuminated French moralized Bible (Bible moralisée) of the thirteenth century; the Codex Vindobonensis 387, a Carolingian astronomical compendium; the Codex Vindobonensis lat. 1 and related codices of the Vienna Coronation Gospels group; and the Codex Vindobonensis Mexicanus I, a sixteenth-century Mexican pictorial manuscript — one of the few pre- and early post-conquest Mesoamerican screenfolds in European collections. The library's holdings also include the unique manuscript of parts of the Waltharius and numerous glossed legal and theological codices catalogued under the generic name.

See also

Other famous codices follow the same convention of being named for their city of preservation, such as the Codex Alexandrinus and the Codex Sinaiticus, both early manuscripts of the Bible.
