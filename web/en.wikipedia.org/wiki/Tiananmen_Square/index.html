Tiananmen Square

Tiananmen Square or Tian'anmen Square (天安门广场; pinyin: Tiān'ānmén Guǎngchǎng) is a city square in the centre of Beijing, China, named after the Tiananmen ("Gate of Heavenly Peace") located to its north, which separates it from the Forbidden City. The square contains the Monument to the People's Heroes, the Great Hall of the People, the National Museum of China, and the Mausoleum of Mao Zedong. Enlarged to its present size under Mao Zedong, it covers about 109 acres (440,000 m²) and is among the largest public squares in the world.

History

The Tiananmen gate was built in 1417 during the Ming dynasty. The area south of it was a walled corridor of imperial offices; after the fall of the Qing and the founding of the Republic, and especially after 1949, the space was cleared and progressively expanded into a monumental square. On 1 October 1949 Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China from the Tiananmen rostrum. In 1958–1959, for the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic, the square was enlarged and its landmark buildings — the Great Hall of the People and the Museum of Chinese History and Revolution — were constructed.

Events

The square has been the site of many major political events, including the May Fourth Movement demonstrations of 1919, the April 1976 Tiananmen Incident following the death of Zhou Enlai, and the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests, which culminated in a military crackdown on 4 June 1989. Mass parades and flag-raising ceremonies continue to be held there.

Layout

The Monument to the People's Heroes, a granite obelisk completed in 1958, stands near the centre. The Mausoleum of Mao Zedong, built in 1977 after Mao's death, lies to the south. Chang'an Avenue runs along the square's northern edge.
