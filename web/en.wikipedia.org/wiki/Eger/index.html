Eger

Eger

Country: Hungary

County: Heves

Population: Approximately 50,000

Known for: Eger Castle, Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood") wine, baroque old town

Eger (German: Erlau; Latin: Agria) is the county seat of Heves County and the second largest city in northern Hungary, lying on the Eger Stream at the foot of the Bükk Mountains. It is famous for its castle, its wines — above all the red blend Egri Bikavér — its baroque historic centre, and its thermal baths.

History

Eger was one of the earliest Hungarian settlements, and became the seat of a bishopric founded by King Stephen I in the eleventh century. The town is best known for the Siege of Eger in 1552, when a small garrison under István Dobó repelled a vastly larger Ottoman army — a defence immortalised in Géza Gárdonyi's novel Eclipse of the Crescent Moon. The Ottomans captured the town in 1596 and held it for 91 years, leaving behind monuments such as the northernmost Ottoman minaret in Europe. Eger was liberated in 1687 and flourished in the eighteenth century under its bishops, who endowed it with much of its baroque architecture.

Wine

The Eger wine region is one of Hungary's historic districts. Its flagship red, Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood of Eger"), is a blend of several varieties, and the region also produces the white Egri Leányka. Wine cellars are carved into the rhyolite tuff hills around the town, most famously along the Szépasszonyvölgy ("Valley of the Beautiful Woman").

Sights

Principal sights include Eger Castle, the neoclassical Eger Basilica (one of Hungary's largest churches), the Lyceum with its historic library and astronomical museum, the Ottoman minaret, and the Turkish-era thermal baths.
