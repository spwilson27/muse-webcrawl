Gas to liquids

Gas to liquids (GTL) is a refinery process that converts natural gas or other gaseous hydrocarbons into longer-chain hydrocarbons, such as gasoline or diesel fuel. Methane-rich gases are converted into liquid synthetic fuels through the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis and related processes. GTL allows stranded or flared natural gas resources to be transported and sold as liquids.

The Pearl GTL plant in Qatar is the world's largest GTL facility.

Process

GTL proceeds in three main stages. First, natural gas is reformed with steam and/or oxygen to produce synthesis gas (syngas), a mixture of hydrogen and carbon monoxide. Second, the Fischer–Tropsch reaction polymerises the syngas over cobalt or iron catalysts into long-chain liquid hydrocarbons and waxes. Third, the products are refined by hydrocracking and distillation into finished fuels and speciality products.

Products and advantages

GTL diesel has a very high cetane number and contains essentially no sulfur or aromatics, producing lower particulate emissions than conventional diesel. GTL base oils are used in premium lubricants, and the process also yields naphtha and normal paraffins for chemicals. Synthetic aviation fuel blends from GTL have been approved for commercial use.

Economics and criticism

GTL plants are extremely capital-intensive: Shell's Pearl GTL in Qatar, with a capacity of about 140,000 barrels per day, cost an estimated US$18–19 billion. Critics note that GTL fuels have a high carbon footprint relative to conventional petroleum and that the process is energy-hungry. Commercial plants operate in Qatar, Malaysia, South Africa (alongside coal-to-liquids) and Nigeria.
