Mary Eastey

Mary Eastey (née Towne; baptised 8 August 1634 – 22 September 1692) was a victim of the Salem witch trials in colonial Massachusetts. She was accused of witchcraft, released, then re-arrested after protesting her accusation, convicted, and hanged. Her sisters Rebecca Nurse and Sarah Cloyce were also accused; Nurse was hanged, while Cloyce survived.

Life

Mary Towne was born in Great Yarmouth, England, daughter of William Towne and Joanna (née Blessing). The family emigrated to Salem, Massachusetts, around 1640 and later settled in Topsfield. She married Isaac Eastey (also spelled Easty), a farmer, in 1655; they had eleven children.

Accusation and trial

Eastey was first accused in April 1692, largely through the afflicted girls' claims. Examined by magistrates, she maintained her dignity and was released in May after the accusers wavered. When she was accused again days later, she protested in a famous petition asking not for her own life but that no more innocent blood be shed, and requesting that her accusers be examined — one of the most moving documents of the trials. She was convicted on 9 September 1692 and hanged on 22 September.

Legacy

In 1711 the Massachusetts legislature reversed her attainder and paid compensation to her family. Mary Eastey is commemorated at the Salem Witch Trials Memorial and in Tercentenary memorials in Danvers. Her final petition is frequently quoted in accounts of the trials as a model of reasoned protest against injustice.
