Odeon Cinemas

Founded: 1928 (as part of Oscar Deutsch's cinema business)

Founder: Oscar Deutsch

Headquarters: Manchester, England

Parent: AMC Theatres (via Odeon Cinemas Group)

Area served: United Kingdom and Ireland

Odeon Cinemas is a British cinema chain and, under the Odeon Cinemas Group, part of the largest cinema exhibition group in Europe. It operates cinemas throughout the United Kingdom and Ireland, and the wider group runs the UCI, Nordic, and other brands across Europe. Since 2016 the group has been owned by the American chain AMC Theatres.

History

The chain was built by Oscar Deutsch, a Birmingham scrap-metal dealer who opened his first cinema in 1928 and adopted the name "Odeon" — a popular name for cinemas derived from the Greek odeon, a building for musical performances; Deutsch's publicity claimed it stood for "Oscar Deutsch Entertains Our Nation". During the 1930s the chain expanded rapidly, commissioning striking modernist and art deco cinemas, many designed by architect Harry Weedon and his practice, recognizable by their streamlined faience-clad towers and fins.

After Deutsch's death in 1941, control passed to J. Arthur Rank's Rank Organisation, under which Odeon became one of the two dominant British circuits, handling releases from major studios. The chain passed through several owners in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, merged with UCI in the UK market in 2004 under Terra Firma ownership, and was sold in 2016 to AMC Theatres for about £921 million, creating the world's largest cinema group at the time.

Cinemas and brands

The flagship Odeon Luxe Leicester Square in London, opened in 1937, remains a principal venue for royal and world film premieres. The company has developed premium formats, including iSense screens, Dolby Cinema auditoriums, and the Odeon Luxe recliner-seat brand, alongside its Limitless subscription membership scheme.
