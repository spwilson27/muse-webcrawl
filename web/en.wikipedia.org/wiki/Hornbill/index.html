Hornbill

Hornbills
Family: Bucerotidae
Order: Bucerotiformes
Species: about 55–60, in 15 genera
Range: tropical and subtropical Africa and Asia

The hornbills are a family, Bucerotidae, of birds found in tropical and subtropical Africa, Asia and Melanesia. They are characterised by a long, down-curved bill which is frequently brightly coloured and sometimes has a casque on the upper mandible. Both the scientific and English names of the family refer to the shape of the bill, "buceros" meaning "cow horn" in Greek.

Description

Hornbills range in size from about 100 grams (black dwarf hornbill) to over 6 kilograms (southern ground hornbill). They are omnivorous, feeding on fruit and small animals. Notably, they are monogamous breeders, and the female of most species seals herself inside a tree-hollow nest using mud and droppings, leaving a narrow slit through which the male feeds her and the chicks until they fledge. Ground hornbills (family Bucorvidae in some classifications) are terrestrial and do not seal the nest.

Conservation

Many Asian hornbills are threatened by hunting and habitat loss. The helmeted hornbill has been heavily poached for its solid casque ("red ivory"), carved in China and Japan, and is listed as Critically Endangered. Hornbills are important seed dispersers in tropical forests and hold cultural significance in many societies; the rhinoceros hornbill is the national bird of Malaysia.
