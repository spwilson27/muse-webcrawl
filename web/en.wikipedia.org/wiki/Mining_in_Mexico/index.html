Mining in Mexico

Mining in Mexico has been a central activity of the economy of Mexico since the Spanish colonial era. Mexico is among the world's leading producers of silver — frequently ranked first globally — and an important producer of gold, copper, zinc, lead, fluorite, and other minerals.

History

Pre-Hispanic peoples worked gold, silver, and copper for ornament and tools. Spanish colonization launched large-scale silver mining from the 1540s, at Zacatecas, Guanajuato, Taxco, and San Luis Potosí, using the patio amalgamation process; Mexican silver financed the Spanish Empire and circulated worldwide as the peso. After independence, British and then American capital entered the sector. The revolutionary-era constitution of 1917 asserted national ownership of subsoil resources. Reforms in the 1990s opened mining concessions widely to foreign companies, and Canadian firms became major operators.

Industry today

Major companies include Grupo México, Fresnillo plc, Peñoles, and Goldcorp/Newmont's Peñasquito mine in Zacatecas, one of the country's largest gold and silver producers. Mining contributes a few percent of GDP and is concentrated in the northern states of Sonora, Zacatecas, Chihuahua, Durango, and Coahuila. The sector faces criticism over water use, environmental damage, conflicts with local and indigenous communities over land and concessions, and the safety of workers, issues central to debates about the economy of Mexico.
