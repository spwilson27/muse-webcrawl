Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (novel)

Saturday Night and Sunday Morning is the first novel by British author Alan Sillitoe, published in 1958. It won the Author's Club First Novel Award and was adapted into a successful 1960 film of the same name directed by Karel Reisz and starring Albert Finney. The novel is regarded as a landmark of the "Angry Young Men" movement and of working-class "kitchen sink realism".

Plot

The novel follows Arthur Seaton, a young Nottingham factory worker at a bicycle factory, who spends his wages on weekend drinking and affairs — including with Brenda, the wife of a workmate — in defiance of the drab conformity he sees around him. After Brenda becomes pregnant and Arthur is beaten by soldiers related to another of his conquests, he settles toward engagement with the respectable Doreen, while inwardly resolving never to let the world grind him down.

Themes

Sillitoe drew on his own experience of factory work in Nottingham. The novel portrays post-war working-class life with unusual frankness: its hero's credo — "Don't let the bastards grind you down" — became famous, and its descriptions of consumer pleasures and resentment of authority captured a new mood in 1950s Britain.

Adaptations

The 1960 film adaptation, part of the British New Wave, made a star of Albert Finney. The novel was also adapted for the stage and radio.
