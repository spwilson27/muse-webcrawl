Victoria is a state in southeastern Australia. It is the second-smallest state by land area (after Tasmania) and the second-most populous (after New South Wales), with a population of around 6.8 million, most of whom live in and around the capital and largest city, Melbourne. Victoria is bordered by New South Wales to the north, South Australia to the west, and the Bass Strait and Tasman Sea to the south.

The Melbourne skyline across the Yarra River.

History

Aboriginal Victorians occupied the land for tens of thousands of years before European settlement. The colony of Victoria was separated from New South Wales in 1851, named in honour of Queen Victoria. The Victorian gold rush of the 1850s, centred on Ballarat and Bendigo, transformed the colony: Melbourne became one of the largest and richest cities in the British Empire, and events such as the Eureka Rebellion of 1854 entered Australian democratic folklore. Victoria became a state of the Commonwealth of Australia at Federation in 1901, and Melbourne served as the temporary national capital until 1927.

Geography and economy

The state ranges from the alpine country of the Great Dividing Range in the northeast (including Mount Bogong, its highest peak) through the fertile Gippsland plains to the volcanic Western District and the Mallee in the northwest. Its coast includes Port Phillip Bay and the famous Great Ocean Road with the Twelve Apostles sea stacks. Victoria's economy is dominated by services, finance, education, and manufacturing centred on Melbourne, with significant agriculture and food processing in regional areas.

Culture and sport

Victoria is the heartland of Australian rules football, and Melbourne hosts the Australian Open tennis, the Melbourne Cup horse race, and the Australian Formula One Grand Prix. The state is also known for its public holidays, cafe culture, and historic gold-rush towns.
