Laboratory of Molecular Biology

The Medical Research Council Laboratory of Molecular Biology (MRC LMB) is a research institute in Cambridge, England, dedicated to understanding biological processes at the molecular level. Founded in 1947 as the MRC Unit for Research on the Molecular Structure of Biological Systems, it is one of the birthplaces of modern molecular biology; scientists who worked there have won about a dozen Nobel Prizes.

History

The unit began in the Cavendish Laboratory under Max Perutz and John Kendrew, who determined the first structures of proteins (haemoglobin and myoglobin). Francis Crick and James Watson, working in the unit, proposed the double helix structure of DNA in 1953. In 1962 the laboratory moved to a purpose-built building on the Addenbrooke's site; Sydney Brenner, Hugh Huxley, and César Milstein led successive generations. Fred Sanger developed DNA sequencing there, and later milestones include monoclonal antibodies (Milstein and Georges Köhler), the ribosome structure (Venki Ramakrishnan), and cryo-electron microscopy advances (Richard Henderson). A new building opened on the Cambridge Biomedical Campus in 2013.

Notable alumni and Nobel laureates

Nobel-associated figures include Perutz, Kendrew, Crick, Watson, Sanger, Brenner, Milstein, Köhler, Aaron Klug, John Walker, Ramakrishnan, Henderson, and Michael Rosbash, among others.
