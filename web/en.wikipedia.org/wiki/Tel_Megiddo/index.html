Tel Megiddo

Tel Megiddo
Location: Northern Israel, Jezreel Valley
Type: Tell / ancient city
Part of: Megiddo
UNESCO World Heritage Site (2005, Biblical Tels)

Tel Megiddo is an archaeological tell in northern Israel, near the modern kibbutz of Megiddo, overlooking the Jezreel Valley. It was a major fortified city from the Bronze Age through the Iron Age, controlling the Via Maris trade route between Egypt and Mesopotamia. The name is linked in Christian tradition to Armageddon.

History

Megiddo was inhabited from about 5000 BCE. In the 15th century BCE Pharaoh Thutmose III defeated a Canaanite coalition at the Battle of Megiddo. Later it became an Israelite administrative centre under Solomon and Ahab, famous for its stables, water system and palaces.

Archaeology

Excavations by the Oriental Institute of Chicago (1925–1939) and later Tel Aviv University revealed some 20–26 occupation layers, a Bronze Age temple, a Canaanite gate, Solomon's gate, and a rock-cut water tunnel.
