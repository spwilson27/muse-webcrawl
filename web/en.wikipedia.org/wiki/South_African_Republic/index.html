The South African Republic (Dutch: Zuid-Afrikaansche Republiek, abbreviated ZAR), also known as the Transvaal Republic, was an independent Boer republic in Southern Africa that existed from 1852 to 1902. Founded by Voortrekkers who had migrated north from the British Cape Colony during the Great Trek, it occupied roughly the territory of the later South African province of Transvaal, with its capital at Pretoria.

Establishment

British recognition of the republic's independence came with the Sand River Convention of 17 January 1852, by which Britain renounced claims north of the Vaal River. The republic adopted a constitution (Grondwet) in 1858, formally restricting political rights to white burghers, and Marthinus Wessel Pretorius became its first president. The early republic was beset by feuding factions, an empty treasury, and conflicts with neighbouring African peoples, notably the Pedi under Sekhukhune.

Annexation and restoration

Bankrupt and embattled, the republic was annexed by Britain in 1877 under Sir Theophilus Shepstone, who proclaimed it the Transvaal Colony. Boer resentment culminated in the First Boer War (1880–81), in which Boer commandos defeated British forces at Majuba Hill. The Pretoria Convention of 1881 restored self-government under British suzerainty, and the London Convention of 1884 largely removed the suzerainty clause while retaining the name South African Republic.

The Kruger era and gold

Paul Kruger, president from 1883, dominated the republic's final decades. The discovery of gold on the Witwatersrand in 1886 transformed the ZAR into the richest state in southern Africa and its largest city, Johannesburg, grew almost overnight, populated largely by foreign uitlanders whom Kruger's government refused full political rights. British pressure, the failed Jameson Raid of 1895–96, and disputes over uitlander franchise and the dynamite monopoly led to the Second Boer War (1899–1902). Although the republic's government went into exile and Pretoria fell in 1900, Boer commandos waged guerrilla war until the Treaty of Vereeniging of 31 May 1902 ended the republic and annexed its territory to the British Empire.

Legacy

The ZAR's flag, the Vierkleur, and its institutions influenced later South African statehood; the territory joined the Union of South Africa as Transvaal Province in 1910. The republic's racial order — constitutional inequality and land dispossession — formed part of the background to 20th-century segregation and apartheid.
