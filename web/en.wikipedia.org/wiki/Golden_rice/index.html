Golden rice

Golden rice is a variety of rice (Oryza sativa) produced through genetic engineering to biosynthesize beta-carotene, a precursor of vitamin A, in the edible parts of the rice. It is intended to address vitamin A deficiency, which causes blindness and increased mortality in children in parts of Africa and Southeast Asia where rice is a staple food.

Development

Golden rice was created by Ingo Potrykus of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology and Peter Beyer of the University of Freiburg, working in the 1990s with support from the Rockefeller Foundation. The original golden rice (GR1) used genes from daffodil and the bacterium Erwinia uredovora to produce about 1.6 µg/g of carotenoids. A second version, GR2, developed by the company Syngenta, replaced the daffodil gene with the corresponding gene from maize and increased carotenoid levels up to 23 times, to about 37 µg/g.

The project faced complex intellectual-property issues: dozens of patented techniques were involved, and arrangements were made so that farmers earning less than a set threshold could use the seed free of charge under a humanitarian licence.

Regulation and deployment

Golden rice received food-safety approvals in Australia, New Zealand, Canada and the United States in 2017–2018. In 2019 it was approved for commercial propagation in the Philippines, and in 2021 the Philippines became the first country to issue a biosafety permit for its commercial production. Its deployment has also been slowed by litigation and by opposition from some environmental and anti-GMO organisations, who argue that vitamin A deficiency is better addressed through dietary diversification and supplementation.

Reception

Golden rice has been one of the most debated genetically modified crops. Supporters, including more than 150 Nobel laureates who signed an open letter in 2016, describe it as a safe tool against a preventable cause of childhood blindness and death; critics question its efficacy, agronomic performance and the appropriateness of a technological fix for a problem of poverty and diet.
