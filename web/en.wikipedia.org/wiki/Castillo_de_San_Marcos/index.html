Castillo de San Marcos

The Castillo de San Marcos (Spanish for "St. Mark's Castle") is the oldest masonry fort in the continental United States, located on the western shore of Matanzas Bay in St. Augustine, Florida. It was designed by the Spanish engineer Ignacio Daza and built between 1672 and 1695 by the Spanish Empire to protect Florida and the Atlantic trade route.

Construction

The fort is built of coquina, a shell-stone quarried on nearby Anastasia Island. Its soft, porous structure proved remarkably effective: instead of shattering under cannon fire, cannonballs tended to sink into the walls. The star-shaped fort, with four bastions named San Carlos, San Agustín, San Pablo, and San Pedro, guarded the inlet and the town, succeeding a series of wooden forts.

History

The Castillo was never taken by force. It withstood British sieges in 1702 and 1740. After the Seven Years' War it passed to Britain (1763), was returned to Spain in 1783, and became United States territory in 1821, when it was renamed Fort Marion. It served as a prison for Native American captives, including Seminole leader Osceola and, later, Plains Indians and Apache prisoners such as Geronimo's band. In 1933 it was transferred to the National Park Service and in 1942 Congress restored its original name.

Today

The Castillo de San Marcos National Monument is a major historic attraction, with reenactments, cannon firings, and exhibits on Spanish colonial Florida.
