International Health Regulations

The International Health Regulations (IHR) are a legally binding instrument of international law adopted by the World Health Organization (WHO) that define countries' rights and obligations in handling public health events and emergencies with potential to cross borders.

History

The regulations originated in 19th-century international sanitary conferences aimed at controlling cholera and plague. The first International Sanitary Regulations were adopted in 1951 and renamed the International Health Regulations in 1969. A major revision was adopted in 2005, entering into force in 2007, in response to SARS and growing concern over emerging diseases.

PHEIC

Under the IHR, the WHO Director-General may declare a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) — a formal designation used for events such as the 2009 swine flu pandemic, the 2014–2016 Ebola epidemic in West Africa, and COVID-19 in 2020.

Core capacities

States parties are required to develop minimum core capacities for surveillance and response, and to notify WHO of events that may constitute a PHEIC through their National IHR Focal Points.
