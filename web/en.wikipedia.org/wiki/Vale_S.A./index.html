Vale S.A. (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈvali]) is a Brazilian multinational mining corporation, one of the largest mining companies in the world and the world's largest producer of iron ore and nickel. Founded in 1942 as the state-owned Companhia Vale do Rio Doce (CVRD), it was privatised in 1997 and adopted the name Vale in 2007.

Operations

Vale's core business is iron ore, mined principally in Brazil's Iron Quadrangle in Minas Gerais and at the vast Carajás complex in Pará — home to S11D, the largest iron ore mine in the world — and shipped globally through company-owned railways and ports such as Ponta da Madeira. The company is also the world's leading nickel producer, with mines in Canada (Sudbury and Voisey's Bay), Indonesia and Brazil; a major copper producer; and a producer of pellets, manganese and energy-transition metals. It operates in around 20 countries.

History

CVRD was created by the Brazilian federal government in 1942 under Getúlio Vargas, taking over British-owned iron ore interests at Itabira with Allied support during the Second World War. Over the following decades it expanded into aluminium, steel, shipping and railways. Privatisation in 1997 transferred control to a consortium led by CSN and pension funds, while the government retained a golden share. In 2006 Vale acquired the Canadian nickel giant Inco for about US$17 billion, then the largest foreign acquisition by a Brazilian company.

Dam disasters

Vale was the co-owner (with BHP, through Samarco) of the Fundão tailings dam whose collapse at Mariana in November 2015 killed 19 people and devastated the Doce River basin. On 25 January 2019, Vale's own Córrego do Feijão dam at Brumadinho, Minas Gerais, burst, killing 270 people — one of the worst industrial disasters in Brazilian history. The company has since committed to eliminating its upstream tailings dams and has faced billions of dollars in reparations, fines and legal settlements, alongside a wholesale overhaul of its safety governance.
