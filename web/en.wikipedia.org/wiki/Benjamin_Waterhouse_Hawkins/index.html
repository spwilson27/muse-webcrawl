Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins

Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins (8 February 1807 – 27 January 1894) was an English sculptor and natural history artist famed for creating the first life-size models of dinosaurs, the Crystal Palace Dinosaurs, which remain on display in London.

Crystal Palace models

Working under the direction of anatomist Richard Owen, Hawkins built some 33 life-sized concrete and iron sculptures of extinct animals for the grounds of the relocated Crystal Palace at Sydenham, unveiled in 1854. On New Year's Eve 1853 he famously hosted a dinner for 21 scientists inside the mould of his Iguanodon model. Though later science showed some reconstructions to be inaccurate, the sculptures were the first attempt to present extinct animals at full scale to the public and remain Grade I listed monuments.

Work in America

In the 1860s Hawkins lectured in the United States and, with palaeontologist Edward Drinker Cope, mounted the duck-billed dinosaur Hadrosaurus at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia — the world's first mounted dinosaur skeleton. He began a similar prehistoric menagerie for New York's Central Park, the Palaeozoic Museum, but the project was destroyed in 1871 by vandals associated with the Tweed Ring.

Later life

Hawkins produced anatomical teaching models and framed artwork for Princeton and the Smithsonian, and returned to England, where he continued illustrating and lecturing until his death in 1894.
