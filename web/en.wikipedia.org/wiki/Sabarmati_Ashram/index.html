Sabarmati Ashram

Location: Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India

Established: 25 May 1915 (Kochrab); relocated 17 June 1917

Founder: Mahatma Gandhi

Coordinates: 23.06° N, 72.58° E

Sabarmati Ashram (also known as Gandhi Ashram or Satyagraha Ashram) is located in the Sabarmati suburb of Ahmedabad, Gujarat, adjoining the Ashram Road, on the banks of the Sabarmati River, 4 miles (6.4 km) from the town hall. This was one of the many residences of Mahatma Gandhi, who lived at Sabarmati (Gujarat) and Sevagram (Wardha, Maharashtra) when he was not travelling across India or in prison.

History

Gandhi established the ashram first at Kochrab in May 1915, shortly after his return from South Africa, and moved it to the present 36-acre site on the Sabarmati riverbank in June 1917. He lived there from 1917 to 1930, conducting experiments in farming, animal husbandry, cow breeding, khadi production and constructive work connected with the freedom struggle.

On 12 March 1930, Gandhi launched the famous Salt March (Dandi March) from the ashram, marching 241 miles with 78 followers to the coastal village of Dandi to protest the British salt tax. He vowed not to return to the ashram until India had gained independence; he never returned, and the ashram community was disbanded in 1933.

The site today

The ashram is now a museum and national monument administered by the Sabarmati Ashram Preservation and Memorial Trust. It includes Hriday Kunj, Gandhi's own cottage; the Gandhi Smarak Sangrahalaya museum designed by Charles Correa; Magan Niwas; Upasana Mandir, an open-air prayer ground; and Vinoba Kutir. The museum houses letters and photographs relating to Gandhi's life, including the famous exchange with Adolf Hitler addressed "Dear friend".
