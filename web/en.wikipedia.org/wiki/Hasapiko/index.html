Hasapiko

Hasapiko
OriginGreek; from Constantinople (Istanbul)
MeterTypically 2/4 or 4/4
Name meaning"Of the butcher" (Greek hasapis, butcher)
Related dancesSirtaki, hasaposerviko, zeibekiko

The hasapiko (Greek: χασάπικο) is a Greek folk dance with roots in Constantinople. Its name derives from hasapis, meaning butcher, as it was traditionally the dance of the butchers' guild of the city.

Description

The hasapiko is danced in a line or open circle, with dancers holding shoulders or hands, and consists of improvised variations on a set of core steps led by the first dancer. It typically begins slowly (the hasapiko vary, or "heavy" hasapiko) and may accelerate into the faster grigoro hasapiko. The slow form uses a stately 4/4 rhythm and intricate, deliberate steps.

Sirtaki

A choreographed combination commonly called sirtaki, created for the 1964 film Zorba the Greek, begins in the slow hasapiko style and builds to the fast hasaposerviko. The sirtaki, rather than any single traditional dance, became internationally emblematic of Greek dancing.
