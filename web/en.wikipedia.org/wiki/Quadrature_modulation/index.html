Quadrature modulation

Quadrature modulation is a method of modulating a carrier signal using two component signals that are 90 degrees out of phase with each other: an in-phase (I) component and a quadrature (Q) component. By varying the amplitudes of the I and Q components, both the amplitude and phase of the transmitted signal can be controlled, allowing two independent signals to share the same bandwidth.

Principle

The transmitted signal can be written as s(t) = I(t) cos(2πfct) − Q(t) sin(2πfct). At the receiver, multiplying by the cosine and sine references and low-pass filtering recovers I and Q separately. Plotting I against Q gives the constellation diagram used to visualise digital modulation schemes.

Applications

Quadrature modulation underlies quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) and phase-shift keying used in Wi-Fi, LTE and 5G radio, digital television, cable modems and software-defined radio. Analogue colour television systems also used quadrature modulation to encode colour information in the NTSC and PAL standards.
