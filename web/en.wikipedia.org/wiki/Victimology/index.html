Victimology

Victimology is the study of victimisation, including the psychological effects on victims, the relationship between victims and offenders, and the interactions between victims and the criminal justice system and other social groups and institutions.

History

The field emerged in the 1940s and 1950s with studies by Hans von Hentig and Benjamin Mendelsohn, who examined victim–offender relationships. Early concepts such as 'victim precipitation' proved controversial and were criticised, especially by feminist scholars, for blaming victims. Modern victimology emphasises victim surveys, victims' rights, compensation, and support services.

Key topics

Research covers patterns of victimisation revealed by crime surveys, repeat victimisation, fear of crime, the impact of trauma, secondary victimisation by the justice process, and restorative approaches involving victims, including those promoted by Howard Zehr.
