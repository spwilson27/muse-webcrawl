Nanda Empire

The Nanda Empire was an ancient Indian empire based in Magadha, ruling much of northern India during the fourth century BCE, roughly from 345 to 322 BCE. The Nandas succeeded the Shaishunaga dynasty at Pataliputra and were themselves overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya, founder of the Maurya Empire.

History

Greek and Roman writers, drawing on accounts of Alexander's invasion, describe the Nanda king (called Agrammes or Xandrames) as enormously wealthy and commanding a vast army of infantry, cavalry, chariots, and elephants — a force whose reputation reportedly helped persuade Alexander's troops to refuse to march further east at the Hyphasis. The founder, Mahapadma Nanda, is described in Puranic texts as a powerful conqueror who subdued many neighbouring kingdoms and is sometimes called the first empire-builder of India.

Fall

The last Nanda ruler, Dhana Nanda, was deposed by Chandragupta Maurya with the counsel of Chanakya, a story elaborated in later Sanskrit drama and legend. Nanda rule is remembered for a large standing army, an organised tax system, and great accumulated treasure.
