Tacrolimus

Trade namesPrograf, Protopic, Advagraf, others
Drug classCalcineurin inhibitor; macrolide
RoutesOral, intravenous, topical
FormulaC44H69NO12
Elimination half-life11–33 hours (variable)

Tacrolimus, sold under the brand name Prograf among others, is an immunosuppressive drug. After allogeneic organ transplantation, the risk of organ rejection is moderate; to prevent this, tacrolimus is given to reduce the activity of the patient's immune system and so lower the risk of rejection. It is also used topically in the treatment of atopic dermatitis (eczema), severe refractory uveitis, and vitiligo.

History

Tacrolimus was discovered in 1987 in a soil sample from Mount Tsukuba, Japan, in a fermentation broth of the bacterium Streptomyces tsukubaensis. It was originally designated FK-506. Although structurally unrelated to ciclosporin, it proved to be a far more potent immunosuppressant in vitro. It was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 1994 for use in liver transplantation and subsequently for kidney and other organ transplants.

Mechanism of action

Tacrolimus is a macrolide calcineurin inhibitor. Inside T lymphocytes it binds to the immunophilin FKBP12, and the resulting complex inhibits calcineurin, a calcium-dependent phosphatase. This blocks the dephosphorylation of the transcription factor NFAT and thereby prevents the transcription of interleukin-2 and other early cytokines essential for T-cell activation and proliferation.

Adverse effects

Important adverse effects include nephrotoxicity (kidney damage), neurotoxicity (tremor, headache, and in severe cases posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome), hyperglycemia and new-onset diabetes, hypertension, and an increased risk of infection and malignancy common to immunosuppressive therapy. Because tacrolimus has a narrow therapeutic index, blood concentrations are routinely monitored. The drug is metabolised by the CYP3A enzyme system and interacts with many other medicines, including grapefruit juice, which raises its blood levels.

Topical use

As an ointment (Protopic), tacrolimus is used for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis in patients who do not respond adequately to conventional therapies such as topical corticosteroids. It is particularly valued for use on sensitive areas such as the face and skin folds because, unlike steroids, it does not cause skin atrophy.
