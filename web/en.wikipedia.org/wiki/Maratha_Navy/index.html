Maratha Navy

Active: c. 1654 – 1756
Country: Maratha Empire
Type: Navy
Notable commanders: Shivaji, Kanhoji Angre, Tulaji Angre

The Maratha Navy was the naval arm of the Maratha Empire of India, founded by Chhatrapati Shivaji in the mid-17th century. Regarded as the foundation of the modern Indian Navy's heritage, it defended the Konkan coast against the Siddis of Janjira, the Portuguese, the Dutch, and the British.

Foundations

Shivaji recognised that control of the coast required a fleet. Around 1654–1659 he built his first vessels and fortified sea forts such as Vijaydurg, Sindhudurg, and Suvarnadurg. The navy used shallow-draft grabs (ghurābs) and gallivats (galbats) suited to the creeks and estuaries of the western Indian coast.

The Angre era

Under Sarkhel Kanhoji Angre (1669–1729), the fleet reached its zenith, dominating the coast between Bombay and Goa, levying the chauth on shipping and repeatedly defeating European squadrons. After his death, succession disputes split the fleet; his son Tulaji Angre was finally defeated in 1756 when the British and the Peshwa jointly destroyed Vijaydurg, ending the Maratha Navy as a major force.

Legacy

Shivaji is honoured as the "Father of the Indian Navy"; the Indian Navy's training and logistical installations in Maharashtra, including INS Shivaji and INS Angre, commemorate the Maratha naval tradition.
