Armenian national liberation movement

Armenian fedayi (volunteer fighters) of the 1890s–1900s.

The Armenian national liberation movement was the Armenian effort, spanning roughly from the 1860s to the early 1920s, to secure autonomy or independence for the Armenian-populated regions of the Ottoman Empire and the Caucasus, culminating in the establishment of the First Republic of Armenia in 1918.

Origins

National consciousness among Armenians grew in the 19th century with cultural revival, education and the press. In the Ottoman Empire, Armenians lived under the millet system; demands for reform and security intensified after the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, when the Treaty of Berlin promised reforms in the "Armenian provinces" that were never implemented.

Parties and armed struggle

Political parties — the Armenakan Party (1885), the Social Democrat Hunchakian Party (1887) and the Armenian Revolutionary Federation (Dashnaktsutyun, 1890) — organised political and armed activity. Fedayi guerrilla bands resisted massacres and state violence in the 1890s and 1900s, a period marked by the Hamidian massacres of 1894–96 and the Adana massacre of 1909.

First World War and independence

During the First World War the Ottoman government carried out the Armenian genocide, deporting and massacring the Armenian population of Anatolia. In the Caucasus, Armenian volunteer units fought alongside Russian forces. After the collapse of the Russian Empire, Armenians proclaimed the Republic of Armenia on 28 May 1918 following the battles of Sardarabad, Bash Abaran and Karakilisa. The republic was international recognised briefly (Treaty of Sèvres, 1920) but was invaded by Turkish and Soviet forces and incorporated into the USSR by 1921–22.
