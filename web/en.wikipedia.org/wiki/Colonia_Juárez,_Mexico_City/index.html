Colonia Juárez, Mexico City

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Colonia Juárez, between Paseo de la Reforma and the historic centre.

Colonia Juárez is one of the 19th-century neighbourhoods (colonias) of central Mexico City, in the Cuauhtémoc borough. It lies just west of the historic centre, bounded by Paseo de la Reforma to the north and Avenida Chapultepec to the south. Within it is the Zona Rosa, a well-known commercial, dining, and nightlife district.

History

The land was subdivided in the 1870s–1900s from the hacienda of La Teja and was laid out with streets named after European cities — Londres, Hamburgo, Génova, Berlín, Copenhague. During the Porfiriato it became one of the capital's most fashionable districts, filled with French-style mansions built for wealthy elites. After the mid-20th century many houses were replaced by office blocks, hotels, and apartment towers, and the area declined after the 1985 earthquake, which hit it hard; it has since undergone sustained revival as a centre for restaurants, galleries, and creative businesses.

Zona Rosa

The Zona Rosa ("Pink Zone"), developed as a bohemian café district in the 1950s and 1960s, is known for its nightlife, its large Korean community, and as the historic centre of Mexico City's LGBTQ scene. Landmarks in and around the colonia include the Angel of Independence on Reforma, the Museo de Cera (wax museum), the Plaza del Ángel, and numerous protected Porfirian houses.
