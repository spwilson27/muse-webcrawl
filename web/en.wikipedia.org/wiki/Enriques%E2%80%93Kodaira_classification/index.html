Enriques–Kodaira classification

The Enriques–Kodaira classification groups compact complex surfaces into ten types according to their Kodaira dimension and other birational invariants. Begun by Federigo Enriques in the early twentieth century and completed and made rigorous by Kunihiko Kodaira in the 1960s, it is the cornerstone of the theory of algebraic surfaces and a model for higher-dimensional classification programmes.

The ten classes

Surfaces of Kodaira dimension −∞ are the rational surfaces and the ruled surfaces (classes VII surfaces, with first Betti number 1 and Kodaira dimension −∞, form the tenth and most mysterious class, including Hopf and Inoue surfaces). Surfaces of Kodaira dimension 0 are K3 surfaces, Enriques surfaces, abelian surfaces (complex tori) and bielliptic (hyperelliptic) surfaces. Properly elliptic surfaces have Kodaira dimension 1, and surfaces of general type have Kodaira dimension 2.

Invariants

The classification is organised by numerical invariants — the plurigenera, irregularity, geometric genus and self-intersection of the canonical divisor — and each class has characteristic geometry: Enriques surfaces, for example, are quotients of K3 surfaces by a fixed-point-free involution, while surfaces of general type are the two-dimensional analogues of curves of genus at least two and display the richest geometry.

Scope and extensions

The classification covers both algebraic (Kähler) and non-Kähler surfaces. In positive characteristic the analogous classification was completed by work of Enrico Bombieri and David Mumford, with some additional exotic classes. The success of the surface classification inspired the minimal model programme in higher dimensions.
