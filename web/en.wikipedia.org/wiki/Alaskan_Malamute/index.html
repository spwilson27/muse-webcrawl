Alaskan Malamute

A grey-and-white Alaskan Malamute

The Alaskan Malamute is a large breed of dog originally developed in Alaska for heavy freight work as a sled dog. Named after the Mahlemut (Malamute) Inupiat people of the Kotzebue Sound region, who bred the dogs to haul heavy loads over long distances in harsh Arctic conditions, it is one of the oldest Arctic sled dog breeds. The Malamute is the official state dog of Alaska.

Characteristics

Malamutes are powerful, heavily boned dogs with a broad head, erect ears and a plumed tail carried over the back. Their dense double coat — a coarse outer layer over a thick, woolly undercoat — insulates them in extreme cold. Unlike the faster, lighter Siberian Husky, the Malamute was bred for strength and endurance in pulling heavy freight rather than for racing speed.

Temperament and care

The breed is typically affectionate, loyal and playful with people, but strong-willed and independent, requiring consistent training and substantial exercise. Malamutes have a high prey drive and a pronounced pack instinct, and they shed heavily, especially during seasonal "coat blows". Health concerns include hip dysplasia, inherited polyneuropathy, cataracts and chondrodysplasia in certain lines.

History

During the Klondike Gold Rush the dogs were in high demand for freighting supplies, and Malamutes later served on Admiral Byrd's Antarctic expeditions and in the Second World War. The American Kennel Club recognised the breed in 1935, based principally on the Kotzebue strain developed by Arthur Walden and the Seeley kennels.
