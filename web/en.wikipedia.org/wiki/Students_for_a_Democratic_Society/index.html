Students for a Democratic Society

Students for a Democratic Society (SDS) was a national student activist organization in the United States that was one of the principal representations of the New Left. Founded in 1960, it attracted broad student participation and developed rapidly amid the civil rights movement and the escalation of the Vietnam War, before disintegrating amid factional conflict in 1969.

Founding and the Port Huron Statement

SDS grew out of the Student League for Industrial Democracy. In June 1962, about sixty members met at a United Auto Workers camp in Port Huron, Michigan, and adopted the Port Huron Statement, a manifesto drafted largely by Tom Hayden. It criticized racism, poverty, militarism, and the alienation of modern life, and called for "participatory democracy" in American political and economic institutions.

Growth

Through the mid-1960s, SDS chapters spread across American campuses. The organization organized community projects through its Economic Research and Action Project (ERAP), supported civil rights organizing, and became a leading force in the movement against the Vietnam War, sponsoring the April 1965 March on Washington that drew some 25,000 protesters. By 1968, SDS claimed around 100,000 members.

Decline and split

At its June 1969 national convention in Chicago, SDS split between competing factions, including the Worker Student Alliance and the Revolutionary Youth Movement. A faction of the latter became the militant Weather Underground. The organization effectively dissolved, holding its last convention in 1969, though a new organization using the SDS name was founded by students in 2006.
