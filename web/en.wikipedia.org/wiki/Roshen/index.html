Roshen

Roshen Confectionery Corporation is a Ukrainian confectionery manufacturing group, one of the world's largest confectionery producers. It was founded in 1996 by the Ukrainian businessman and later president Petro Poroshenko. The company's name derives from its founder's surname, with the first and last syllables removed.

Operations

Roshen produces several hundred types of confectionery — chocolate bars and candies, cakes (including the famous Kyiv cake), biscuits, wafers, jellies and marshmallows — at factories in Kyiv, Kremenchuk, Vinnytsia and other Ukrainian cities, as well as plants in Klaipėda, Lithuania, and Bonbonetti in Budapest, Hungary. Its products are exported to dozens of countries in Europe, North America and Asia, and the company operates a large chain of branded retail stores in Ukraine.

History

Poroshenko assembled the group in the 1990s by acquiring state confectionery factories during privatisation. A factory the company owned in Lipetsk, Russia, became politically contentious after 2014; it was raided and had its assets seized by Russian authorities before Roshen halted its operations there in 2017. When Poroshenko became President of Ukraine in 2014 he promised to sell the company; he instead transferred his stake into a blind trust in 2016.
