Sea Wolf (missile)


Type: Surface-to-air missile
Origin: United Kingdom
Manufacturer: MBDA (formerly British Aircraft Corporation)
In service: 1979 – c. 2020s (Royal Navy)

Sea Wolf is a naval surface-to-air missile system designed and built by the British Aircraft Corporation (later MBDA) as a point-defence weapon for warships against low-level aircraft and anti-ship missiles. It entered service with the Royal Navy in 1979 aboard Type 22 frigates, replacing the earlier Seacat system, and was also fitted in a vertical-launch version (VL Sea Wolf) on Type 23 frigates.

Design and service

Sea Wolf is a Mach 2+ missile guided by command line-of-sight via radar or television tracking, with a range of roughly 1–10 km depending on variant and an HE-fragmentation warhead with a proximity fuze. Fully automatic, the system could react in seconds to shore-hugging and sea-skimming threats.

Sea Wolf was first used in combat during the Falklands War of 1982, where it was credited with several aircraft destroyed. It remained the Royal Navy's principal point-defence missile for decades aboard Type 22 and Type 23 frigates, exported to several other navies, and was replaced in Royal Navy service by the Sea Ceptor (CAMM) system.
