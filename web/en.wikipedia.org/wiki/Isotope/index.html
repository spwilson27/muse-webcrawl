Isotope

Isotopes are distinct nuclear species (nuclides) of the same chemical element: they have the same number of protons (hence the same element and nearly identical chemistry) but different numbers of neutrons and thus different mass numbers. The term was coined by Margaret Todd and popularised by Frederick Soddy, from Greek for 'equal place' (in the periodic table).

Some isotopes are stable (e.g. carbon-12, carbon-13); others are radioactive (carbon-14, uranium-235 vs stable-ish uranium-238). Isotope ratios are tools in geology and archaeology (dating, provenance) and medicine (tracers) and industry; separating isotopes of uranium (enrichment) is the key industrial step in nuclear power and weapons. Nearly 300 stable nuclides and thousands of radioactive ones are known.
