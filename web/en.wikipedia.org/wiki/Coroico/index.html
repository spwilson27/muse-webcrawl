Coroico is a small town in the Nor Yungas Province of the La Paz Department of Bolivia. Perched on a hillside at about 1,782 metres (5,846 ft) in the subtropical Yungas region where the Andes descend toward the Amazon Basin, it is a popular weekend resort for residents of La Paz and a base for travellers exploring the region's cloud forests, coca plantations and coffee farms.

History

The settlement grew during the colonial period; its full historic name was Coroico Viejo, and a new town (Coroico Nuevo) was established nearby. The area played a role in the Bolivian War of Independence, and the town retains a number of colonial-era buildings. The surrounding Yungas have long been the principal coca-growing region supplying the legal coca market of Bolivia, and Coroico is a centre for Afro-Bolivian communities descended from enslaved Africans brought to work in colonial mines and plantations.

Transport

Coroico is famous as the destination of the North Yungas Road from La Paz, long known as the "Road of Death" (Camino de la Muerte) for the hundreds of travellers who died each year on its narrow, unpaved, cliff-edge track. The old road has become a major mountain-biking attraction. A modern paved bypass, opened in 2006, now carries most motor traffic between La Paz and the Yungas, sharply reducing fatalities.

Tourism

The town offers hotels, swimming pools and views across forested ridges. Nearby attractions include waterfalls such as those at San Félix, the Afro-Bolivian community of Tocaña, and guided visits to coca and coffee farms.
