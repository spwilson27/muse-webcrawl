Internet of things
Devices: sensors, appliances, vehicles
Connectivity: Wi-Fi, cellular, LPWAN

The Internet of things (IoT) describes devices with sensors, processing ability, software and other technologies that connect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the Internet or other communications networks. The field has evolved due to the convergence of multiple technologies, including ubiquitous computing, commodity sensors, and increasingly powerful embedded systems.

History

The concept of a network of smart devices was discussed as early as 1982, when a modified Coca-Cola vending machine at Carnegie Mellon University became the first ARPANET-connected appliance. The term "Internet of things" was coined by Kevin Ashton of Procter & Gamble in 1999.

Applications

IoT applications include smart homes, industrial automation, healthcare monitoring, agriculture, and smart cities. Consumer devices such as smart speakers, thermostats, and wearable fitness trackers are among the most visible examples.

Concerns

The rapid growth of IoT devices has raised concerns about security and privacy, as many devices ship with weak default credentials and limited update mechanisms.
