Constanze Mozart (born Maria Constanze Cäcilia Josepha Johanna Aloysia Weber; 5 January 1762 – 6 March 1842) was a German singer and the wife of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. Following his death in 1791, she became the primary custodian of his legacy, promoting performances of his music and publishing the first biography of the composer.

Life

Constanze was the daughter of Fridolin Weber, a musician and music copyist, in Zell im Wiesental. Her elder sister Aloysia had been courted by Mozart in Mannheim; Mozart met Constanze in Vienna in 1781, where the Weber family had moved, and married her on 4 August 1782 at St Stephen's Cathedral. The couple had six children, of whom two survived infancy: Carl Thomas and Franz Xaver Wolfgang, both of whom became musicians.

After Mozart's death

Left with debts after Mozart's death in December 1791, Constanze obtained a pension from the emperor and organized benefit concerts of his music, which proved highly successful. She sold his manuscripts to the publisher Johann Anton André and in 1809 married Danish diplomat Georg Nikolaus von Nissen, who had been a lodger in her home. Together they wrote the first biography of Mozart (published 1828), drawing on her memories and the family correspondence. She also arranged for the completion and early performances of Mozart's Requiem, which had been completed by Franz Xaver Süssmayr.

Assessment

Nineteenth-century writers, hostile to her for various reasons, often portrayed her as unworthy of Mozart; modern scholarship emphasises her competence and enterprise in preserving his works and establishing his posthumous reputation.
