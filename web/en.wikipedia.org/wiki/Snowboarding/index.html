Snowboarding

Snowboarding is a recreational and competitive winter sport that involves descending a snow-covered surface while standing on a snowboard that is almost always attached to the rider's feet using special boots set onto a mounted binding. The development of snowboarding was inspired by skateboarding, sledding, surfing, and skiing. It was developed in the United States, where it became popular around the world, and became a Winter Olympic Games sport in 1998.

History

The modern snowboard traces to 1965, when Michigan engineer Sherman Poppen lashed two skis together as a toy "Snurfer" for his daughters. Jake Burton Carpenter founded Burton Snowboards in 1977 and, with pioneers such as Tom Sims and Dimitrije Milovich, turned the snurfer into a manufactured board with bindings; early ski resorts largely banned the devices, and riders hiked backcountry terrain. Through the 1980s and 1990s resorts admitted snowboarders, professional tours formed, and riders like Terje Håkonsen and Craig Kelly defined freestyle and freeride styles. Snowboarding debuted at the 1998 Nagano Winter Olympics and grew into one of the Games' signature sports, with stars such as Shaun White and Chloe Kim.

Styles and disciplines

  • Freeride — riding natural, ungroomed terrain, including backcountry and steep alpine lines.
  • Freestyle — tricks on jumps, rails, boxes and in the halfpipe and slopestyle courses.
  • Alpine/carving — hard-boot racing on groomed pistes: parallel giant slalom and slalom.
  • Snowboard cross (boardercross) — several riders racing simultaneously over banked turns, rollers and jumps.
  • Big air — a single large jump judged on aerial tricks.

Equipment and technique

Riders stand sideways on the board, in regular (left foot forward) or goofy stance, steering by shifting weight between toe and heel edges. Boards vary by discipline in length, flex, sidecut and camber profile; boots and bindings range from soft freestyle setups to rigid alpine plate systems. Helmets and wrist guards are common protective gear; the typical beginner injury is a wrist fracture from falling.

Culture

Snowboarding grew from a countercultural youth movement with close ties to skate and surf culture — its own music, dress and film scene — into a mainstream industry worth billions, while retaining an anti-establishment streak visible in debates over Olympic governance and competition formats.
