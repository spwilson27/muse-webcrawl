The East Slavic languages are one of the three regional subgroups of the Slavic languages, spoken by the East Slavic peoples in Eastern Europe and Siberia. The group comprises Russian, Ukrainian and Belarusian, together with Rusyn, whose status as a separate language or as a dialect grouping is debated. Together they have roughly 240 million native speakers, the great majority of them speakers of Russian.

History

The East Slavic languages descend from a common ancestor, Old East Slavic (also called Old Russian), the language of Kievan Rus' from roughly the 10th to the 14th centuries, written in Cyrillic and strongly influenced in its literary form by Old Church Slavonic. After the Mongol invasions and the political division of the Rus' lands between the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the rising principality of Moscow, the common language diverged. The chancery language of Lithuania (Ruthenian) developed towards modern Belarusian and Ukrainian, while the dialect of Moscow became the basis of Russian.

Classification

  • Russian — the most widely spoken Slavic language, official in Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, and widely used across the former Soviet Union.
  • Ukrainian — official in Ukraine; notable for its preservation of the vocative case and the phoneme /ɦ/.
  • Belarusian — co-official with Russian in Belarus; characterised by features such as akanye and the fricative /ɣ/.
  • Rusyn — spoken by Rusyns in the Carpathians (Ukraine, Slovakia, Poland, Serbia); codified regionally and recognised as a minority language in several states.

Features

Shared East Slavic developments from Proto-Slavic include pleophony (polnoglasie) — the insertion of a vowel resolving liquid diphthongs, as in Proto-Slavic *gordъ "town" giving Russian gorod, Ukrainian horod, Belarusian horad (compare Polish gród) — the change of initial *je- to o- (as in odin "one"), and the merger of the nasal vowels into plain vowels. All are written in the Cyrillic script with national variants of the alphabet: Russian and Belarusian use the letter ы/Ы, Ukrainian uses ї and є, and Belarusian has і and ў.
