NMDA receptor antagonist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

NMDA receptor antagonists are a class of drugs that work to antagonize, or inhibit the action of, the N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR). They are commonly used as anesthetics for animals and humans; the state of anesthesia they induce is referred to as dissociative anesthesia.

Several synthetic opioids function additionally as NMDAR-antagonists, such as pethidine, levorphanol, methadone, dextropropoxyphene, tramadol and ketobemidone.

Mechanism of action

The NMDA receptor is an ionotropic receptor that allows for the transfer of electrical signals between neurons in the brain and in the spinal column. For electrical signals to pass, the NMDAR must be open. In order for it to remain open, glutamate and glycine must bind to it. An NMDAR with bound glutamate and glycine, but with an antagonist bound in the channel, is not able to conduct current through the cell membrane.

Most NMDA receptor antagonists are uncompetitive channel blockers: they bind inside the ion channel pore and physically prevent ion flow. Ketamine, phencyclidine (PCP), dizocilpine (MK-801) and memantine are classic examples. Others, such as dextromethorphan, act as antagonists at additional sites; still others, including ifenprodil, selectively block receptors containing the GluN2B subunit.

Uses

Anesthesia

Ketamine is used as a dissociative anesthetic in human and veterinary medicine, producing a trance-like state while providing pain relief, sedation, and amnesia. Its favorable cardiovascular profile makes it valuable in emergency and battlefield medicine.

Neurological disorders

Memantine is approved for the treatment of moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's disease, where it is thought to protect neurons against excitotoxic damage caused by excessive glutamatergic activity. Amantadine has been used in Parkinson's disease. NMDA antagonists have also been investigated for depression, neuropathic pain, epilepsy, and traumatic brain injury.

Research

Because NMDA receptor hypofunction has been proposed to play a role in schizophrenia, NMDA antagonists such as PCP and ketamine have long been used as pharmacological models of the disease in both human volunteers and laboratory animals. In 2019, esketamine, the S-enantiomer of ketamine, was approved in the United States as a nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression.
