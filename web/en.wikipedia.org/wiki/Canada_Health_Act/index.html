Canada Health Act

Canada Health Act
Enacted: 1984
Type: federal legislation
Criteria: public administration, comprehensiveness, universality, portability, accessibility

The Canada Health Act is the federal legislation governing Canada's publicly funded health care system ("medicare"). Passed unanimously in 1984, it sets the conditions provinces and territories must meet to receive full federal cash contributions for insured hospital and physician services under the Canada Health Transfer.

Five criteria

The Act requires provincial plans to satisfy five criteria: public administration (non-profit operation), comprehensiveness (all medically necessary hospital and physician services insured), universality (all residents covered on uniform terms), portability (coverage maintained when moving or travelling), and accessibility (reasonable access without financial barriers).

User charges and enforcement

The Act defines and discourages extra-billing by physicians and user charges by hospitals for insured services, authorising dollar-for-dollar deductions from federal transfers to provinces that permit them. Federal–provincial disputes over private clinics, diagnostic services, and virtual care have repeatedly tested its interpretation.
