Banda El Recodo

OriginMazatlán, Sinaloa, Mexico
Years active1938–present
GenresBanda, ranchera
FounderCruz Lizárraga

Banda El Recodo de Cruz Lizárraga is a Mexican banda ensemble from Mazatlán, Sinaloa. Founded in 1938 by clarinetist Cruz Lizárraga, it is widely credited with popularising the brass banda format in Mexico and internationally, earning the title "La Madre de Todas las Bandas" ("The Mother of All Bands").

History

Cruz Lizárraga (1918–1995) formed the band in the village of El Recodo, near Mazatlán, and named it after the village. Beginning with performances at local festivities, the group made its first recordings in the 1950s and pioneered bringing banda — previously a local folk form dominated by wind instruments — to formal concert stages, radio, and television in Mexico City. El Recodo's huge output, numbering well over 200 albums, helped define the modern Sinaloan banda sound of brass, woodwinds, and percussion without strings.

Musical style and influence

The band performs rancheras, cumbias, corridos, waltzes, and pop arrangements adapted to the banda format. Its innovations included standardised uniforms, choreography, and the incorporation of a lead singer performing romantic repertoire alongside dance instrumentals. Nearly every major banda since, from Banda Machos to Banda MS, traces lineage to El Recodo's model. After Cruz Lizárraga's death, the band continued under the leadership of his sons and later his widow and family members, remaining one of Mexico's best-selling regional acts.

Recognition

El Recodo has received numerous awards, including Latin Grammy Awards for its albums, and has performed in venues such as the Auditorio Nacional in Mexico City and toured the United States, Europe, and Japan.
