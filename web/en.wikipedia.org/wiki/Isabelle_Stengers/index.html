Isabelle Stengers
Born: 1949, Brussels, Belgium
Nationality: Belgian
Occupation: Philosopher
Known for: Philosophy of science, cosmopolitics

Isabelle Stengers (born 1949) is a Belgian philosopher of science. She is known for her work on the philosophy of science, her collaboration with the chemist Ilya Prigogine, and her concept of "cosmopolitics," developed in dialogue with the thought of Alfred North Whitehead, Gilles Deleuze, and Bruno Latour.

Life and career

Stengers is the daughter of the historian Jean Stengers. Trained in chemistry and philosophy, she became a professor at the Université libre de Bruxelles, where she taught philosophy of science.

Work

With Ilya Prigogine she co-authored Order Out of Chaos (French: La Nouvelle Alliance, 1979), an influential account of irreversibility, dissipative structures, and the implications of non-equilibrium thermodynamics for the philosophy of science.

Her multi-volume Cosmopolitics (1996–1997) examines the practices of the modern sciences, proposing a "cosmopolitical" approach that slows down reasoning and resists the dismissal of non-scientific practices in the name of science. Other works include The Invention of Modern Science (2000), Thinking with Whitehead (2011), and In Catastrophic Times: Resisting the Coming Barbarism (2015), which engages with the climate crisis and the concept of Gaia, drawing on the work of James Lovelock and the writings of activists such as those in the "slow science" movement.

Concepts

  • Cosmopolitics — a proposal that the "cosmos" in cosmopolitanism remain open to the divergent worlds and practices that political representation tends to exclude.
  • Ecology of practices — an approach to comparing knowledge practices by how they construct obligations and requirements.
  • Slow science — a critique of fast, competitive, metric-driven research.
