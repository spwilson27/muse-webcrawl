The island of Hawaii (Hawaiian: Hawaiʻi), often called the Big Island, is the largest and southeasternmost island of the Hawaiian archipelago in the Pacific Ocean. With an area of 4,028 square miles it is larger than all the other Hawaiian islands combined, and continues to grow through volcanic activity.

Geology

The island is built from five shield volcanoes: Kohala, Mauna Kea (a dormant volcano and the highest point in the state), Hualālai, Mauna Loa and Kīlauea, the latter two active and protected within Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park. Kīlauea erupted almost continuously from 1983 to 2018, and Mauna Loa erupted in 2022 for the first time in 38 years. Offshore, the young submarine volcano Kamaʻehuakanaloa (Lōʻihi) is forming the next island.

History and economy

Hawaii was the home island of Kamehameha I, who unified the archipelago by 1810; Captain James Cook was killed at Kealakekua Bay on the island in 1779. Hilo, on the wet windward coast, is the county seat, while Kailua-Kona on the dry leeward coast is a resort centre. The economy rests on tourism, agriculture (including Kona coffee and macadamia nuts), ranching and astronomy — the Mauna Kea Observatories are among the world's premier telescope sites.
