Dysautonomia

Dysautonomia, autonomic dysfunction, or autonomic neuropathy is a condition in which the autonomic nervous system (ANS) does not work properly. This may affect the heart, bladder, intestines, sweat glands, pupils, and blood vessels.

Dysautonomia
Specialty: Neurology
Symptoms: Tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, gastroparesis, anhidrosis

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms vary widely and include postural tachycardia, syncope, exercise intolerance, fatigue, bowel and bladder problems, abnormal sweating, and pupillary abnormalities.

Causes

It can be congenital or acquired, from diabetes, amyloidosis, autoimmune diseases such as Guillain-Barré syndrome, Parkinson's disease, multiple system atrophy, and genetic disorders such as familial dysautonomia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis uses tilt table testing, autonomic reflex screens and sweat tests. There is no cure for many forms; treatment is symptomatic with fluids, salt, compression garments, midodrine and fludrocortisone.
