Orange juice is a liquid extract of the orange tree fruit, produced by squeezing or reaming oranges. It comes in several different varieties, including blood orange, navel oranges, valencia orange, clementine, and tangerine. As well as variations in oranges used, some varieties include differing amounts of juice vesicles, known as "pulp" in North America. Orange juice is the most popular fruit juice worldwide, and it has long been a staple part of the Western breakfast.

Production

Commercial juice is usually pasteurised and sold either as "not from concentrate" (NFC) or as frozen concentrated orange juice (FCOJ), which is evaporated under vacuum, frozen, and reconstituted with water before sale. FCOJ is traded as a commodity on the ICE Futures exchange in New York. Brazil is the world's largest producer and exporter of orange juice, accounting for the majority of global trade, followed by the United States, where Florida is the main producing state. Extraction uses mechanical reamers or whole-fruit extractors that press the fruit and separate the juice from peel oils and pith to limit bitterness.

Nutrition and health

Orange juice is a rich source of vitamin C and a moderate source of folate, potassium and thiamine. A 250 ml glass contains roughly 110–120 kcal and about 21–26 g of sugars, comparable to soft drinks, which has prompted public-health debate: unlike whole fruit, juice lacks most of the fibre and is easy to overconsume. Some products are fortified with calcium and vitamin D. The juice's acidity (pH ~3.5) can contribute to dental erosion if consumed frequently.

History

Freshly squeezed juice was a luxury until the development of the frozen concentrate process during the Second World War, when the US Army sought a palatable source of vitamin C for soldiers. The USDA Citrus Products Station in Florida perfected FCOJ in 1948, and post-war marketing campaigns — including the slogan "a day without orange juice is like a day without sunshine", popularised by Bing Crosby for Minute Maid — made it a breakfast staple in North America and Europe.
