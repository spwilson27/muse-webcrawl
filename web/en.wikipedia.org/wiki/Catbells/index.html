Catbells is a fell in the English Lake District, in the county of Cumbria. It rises to 451 metres (1,480 ft) on the western shore of Derwentwater, close to the town of Keswick. Despite its modest height, it is one of the most popular fells in the district, owing to its distinctive shape, its fine views, and its accessibility to novice walkers.

Name

The name is thought to derive from "Cat Bields" — the shelter of the wild cat — referring to wild cats once believed to inhabit the fell. Alfred Wainwright, who included Catbells in his Pictorial Guide to the Lakeland Fells, described it as a family fell.

The walk

The usual ascent begins at Hawes End, reached by launch across Derwentwater from Keswick or by road. A well-worn path climbs the northern ridge over Skelgill Bank to the summit, a small rocky top with a short scramble near the top. The walk is frequently combined with the longer circuit over Maiden Moor and High Spy. Mrs Tiggy-Winkle, the hedgehog washerwoman in Beatrix Potter's tale, was said to live in the area of the Newlands Valley beneath the fell, and the hill features in her story's setting.

Geology

Catbells is formed of the Skiddaw Group of mudstones and siltstones, sheared and folded during the Caledonian orogeny, and gives its name to the Catbells Member of the local stratigraphy.
