GMSL

Gigabit Multimedia Serial Link (GMSL) is a serial link technology developed by Maxim Integrated (now part of Analog Devices) for high-speed transmission of video, audio, and control data over a single coaxial cable or shielded twisted pair. It is widely used in automotive applications, particularly for connecting cameras and displays to electronic control units.

A GMSL link carries forward video data and a reverse control channel over one cable.

Overview

GMSL uses a serializer/deserializer (SerDes) architecture. A serializer at the source converts parallel video and control data into a high-speed serial stream, while a deserializer at the destination recovers the parallel data. The link supports a bidirectional control channel, allowing peripherals such as image sensors to be configured over the same cable that carries video.

Generations

The first generation of GMSL supported data rates up to about 3.12 Gbit/s. GMSL2 increased the forward rate to 6 Gbit/s with a 187.5 Mbit/s reverse channel, and added features important for advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS), including support for power over coax, cable diagnostics, and functional-safety features. Later generations have pushed rates higher to serve multiple high-resolution cameras and displays in a vehicle.

Applications

  • Automotive surround-view and rear-view cameras
  • In-vehicle infotainment displays
  • Industrial and machine-vision cameras
  • Medical imaging equipment

GMSL competes with other automotive SerDes standards such as Texas Instruments' FPD-Link.
