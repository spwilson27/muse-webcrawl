Change control

A change control board reviewing requests

Change control is a systematic approach to managing all changes made to a product or system, ensuring that no unnecessary changes are made, that all changes are documented, and that services are not unnecessarily disrupted. The method is used in project management, software engineering, and regulated industries such as pharmaceuticals and aviation.

Process

A typical change control process includes: recording and classifying a change request; assessing its impact, cost, and risk; obtaining approval from a change authority or change control board; implementing the change; and verifying and closing the record. Uncontrolled change is a common source of project failure and is sometimes described as scope creep.

In regulated industries

Under good manufacturing practice (GMP) and standards such as ISO 9001, formal change control is mandatory: any change to a validated process, specification, or document must be evaluated and approved before implementation, with a complete audit trail retained.
