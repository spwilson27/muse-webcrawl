The Beijing National Stadium, commonly known as the Bird's Nest, is a stadium at the Olympic Green in Beijing, China. Built for the 2008 Summer Olympics and Paralympics, it hosted the opening and closing ceremonies, athletics, and the football final. In 2022 it became the only stadium in the world to have hosted the ceremonies of both a Summer and a Winter Olympic Games.

Design and construction

The stadium was designed by the Swiss firm Herzog & de Meuron in collaboration with Chinese architect Li Xinggang, with artist Ai Weiwei as artistic consultant. Its form derives from an external lattice of some 42,000 tonnes of steel, whose seemingly random interwoven members — inspired by Chinese crazed-pottery patterns and the image of a bird's nest — are in fact the primary load-bearing structure. The seating bowl sits inside the lattice as an independent structure. The stadium is 330 metres long, 220 metres wide, and 69 metres high, and its construction, begun in December 2003, cost roughly 2.3 billion yuan (about US$428 million).

History

During the 2008 Games the stadium witnessed Usain Bolt's world-record sprints in the 100 and 200 metres. After the Olympics, seating was reduced to 80,000 and the venue struggled to find regular tenants, hosting occasional football matches (including the Supercoppa Italiana), concerts, and the 2015 World Championships in Athletics. For the 2022 Winter Olympics it again hosted the opening and closing ceremonies, directed by Zhang Yimou, who had also directed the 2008 ceremonies.
