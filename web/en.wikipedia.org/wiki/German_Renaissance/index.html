German Renaissance

A German Renaissance work of art

The German Renaissance, part of the Northern Renaissance, was a cultural and artistic movement that spread among German thinkers and artists between the late 15th and early 17th centuries, developing from the Italian Renaissance. Many areas of the arts and sciences were influenced, notably by the spread of Renaissance humanism to the various German states and principalities. There were numerous developments in the fields of architecture, the visual arts and science, and German printing would achieve dominance in Europe by the 16th century.

Background

The movement was strongly influenced by the Reformation and the invention of the printing press. Unlike Italy, where the Renaissance was driven by wealthy city states and princely courts, in Germany it spread through Imperial Free Cities, universities and the courts of prince-electors. The rediscovery of classical antiquity combined with new humanist scholarship shaped by figures such as Johannes Reuchlin and Desiderius Erasmus.

Art

The best-known artist of the German Renaissance is Albrecht Dürer of Nuremberg, whose woodcuts and engravings spread his fame throughout Europe. Other major artists included Matthias Grünewald, Albrecht Altdorfer, Hans Holbein the Younger and Lucas Cranach the Elder. Painting in this period fused late Gothic traditions with Italian perspective, anatomy and classical themes.

Architecture

Renaissance architecture arrived more slowly, often grafted onto Gothic structures. Examples include the Fugger Chapel in Augsburg, Heidelberg Castle and numerous town halls and residences built by Italian or Italian-trained architects. Ornate gables and classical ornament became hallmarks of the style in the German lands.
