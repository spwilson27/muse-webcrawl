Road bicycle racing is the cycle sport discipline of road cycling, held on paved roads. It is the most popular form of bicycle racing, and has been an Olympic sport since the first modern Games in 1896. Races take the form of one-day events or stage races lasting up to three weeks, contested by professional teams whose riders cooperate tactically even though individual victories and classifications are at stake.

History

The first documented bicycle race was held on 31 May 1868 at the Parc de Saint-Cloud in Paris. Long-distance city-to-city races such as Paris–Brest–Paris (1891) and Bordeaux–Paris established the sport in France, Belgium and Italy. The Tour de France was created in 1903 by the newspaper L'Auto to boost circulation, and the Giro d'Italia followed in 1909. The sport's governing body, the UCI, was founded in Paris in 1900.

Race formats

  • One-day races, including the five Monuments: Milan–San Remo, the Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège and the Giro di Lombardia.
  • Stage races, from short events to the three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — each about three weeks long, with flat, mountain and time-trial stages and classifications for overall time (general classification), points, mountains and youth.
  • Individual and team time trials, raced against the clock.
  • Criteriums, short races on closed city circuits, especially popular in the United States and the Low Countries.

Tactics

Riding in the slipstream of others can save a rider up to roughly 40 per cent of energy, so races are fundamentally collective. Teams protect a leader, chase breakaways, and launch sprinters from the peloton ("bunch"); riders known as domestiques fetch food and water and sacrifice their own chances for the leader. Crosswinds can split the field into echelons, while mountain stages reward climbers and descents test handling and nerve.

Doping

The sport has a long history of performance-enhancing drug use, from amphetamines in the early peloton — implicated in the death of Tom Simpson on Mont Ventoux in 1967 — to the EPO era of the 1990s and 2000s, the Festina affair of 1998, and the stripping of Lance Armstrong's seven Tour titles in 2012. Anti-doping is now administered under the World Anti-Doping Code, with a biological passport tracking riders' blood values.
