Kindred is a 1979 novel by American writer Octavia E. Butler. It combines science-fiction time travel with a slave narrative.

Plot

On her 26th birthday in 1976 Los Angeles, Dana, a Black writer, is pulled back in time to a Maryland plantation in 1815 whenever Rufus Weylin, the white son of a slaveholder and her own ancestor, is in mortal danger. She returns to the present when she fears for her own life. Repeated journeys force Dana to endure and witness slavery's brutality to ensure Rufus survives to father her ancestor Hagar — while trying to preserve her own humanity and marriage to Kevin, who is once stranded in the past with her.

Themes and reception

The novel explores power, race, gender, complicity and the lasting trauma of slavery. Though Butler resisted calling it science fiction, preferring "grim fantasy," Kindred became her best-known work, widely taught in schools and adapted as a graphic novel (2017) and a television series (2022).
