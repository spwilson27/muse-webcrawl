Kasuga-taisha

Kasuga-taisha (春日大社) is a Shinto shrine in Nara, Japan, founded in 768 CE. It is the shrine of the Fujiwara family, Japan's most powerful clan for much of the Heian period, and is famous for its thousands of bronze and stone lanterns. Together with other monuments of ancient Nara, it is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

According to tradition, the shrine was established when the Fujiwara invited the god Takemikazuchi from Kashima Shrine to protect the new capital at Nara; the deity is said to have arrived riding a white deer, which is why the deer of Nara Park are treated as sacred messengers of the gods. The shrine enshrines four main kami, including deities of the Fujiwara and their ancestral Nakatomi clan.

As the tutelary shrine of the Fujiwara, Kasuga received imperial patronage and was ranked among the highest shrines of the state. The Kasuga Wakamiya festival, founded in 1136, remains one of Japan's celebrated festivals. In the grounds grows the Kasugayama Primeval Forest, preserved as a sacred area where hunting and logging have been prohibited since 841.

Architecture and lanterns

The shrine buildings, rebuilt roughly every twenty years for centuries in earlier times, are the archetype of the Kasuga-zukuri style, with vermilion-lacquered woodwork and cypress-bark roofs. Kasuga is renowned for its approximately 3,000 lanterns: stone lanterns line the approach paths and bronze lanterns hang from the buildings, donated over the centuries by worshippers. Twice a year, at the Mantōrō festivals in February and August, all the lanterns are lit at once.
