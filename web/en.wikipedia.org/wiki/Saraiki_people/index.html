Saraiki people

The Saraiki people are an ethnolinguistic group native to central and southern Pakistan, principally the southern districts of Punjab province and adjoining areas of Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan. They speak Saraiki, an Indo-Aryan language of the Lahnda group, and number in the tens of millions. The historic city of Multan is the cultural heart of the Saraiki region, sometimes called the Saraikistan or Rohi-Thal area.

History

The Saraiki-speaking region, centred on the ancient city of Multan — a major trading post and pilgrimage centre famous for its Sufi shrines — has historically stood at the crossroads of Punjab, Sindh and the western highlands. The term "Saraiki" itself was standardised in the 20th century to describe the related dialects (Multani, Derawali, Riasti and others) of the region. Linguists long debated whether Saraiki is a separate language or a dialect of Punjabi; it is now generally classified as a distinct language of the Lahnda (Western Punjabi) group, written in a Perso-Arabic script with additional letters for its implosive consonants.

Culture

Saraiki culture is deeply marked by Sufism: the poetry of saints such as Khwaja Ghulam Farid of Mithankot — author of beloved kafis set in the Cholistan desert landscape — is sung across the region. Other hallmarks include Multani blue pottery, camel-skin craft, the ajrak block-printed cloth, and festivals at Sufi shrines (urs). The agricultural economy of the region rests on cotton, wheat, sugarcane and mango orchards watered by the Indus and its tributaries.

Identity and politics

Since the 1960s a Saraiki identity movement has campaigned for recognition of Saraiki language and culture and, prominently, for a separate Saraiki province (Saraikistan) in southern Punjab — a demand taken up at various times by major national parties and reflected in parliamentary resolutions, though the province has not been created.
