Electronic Road Pricing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Electronic Road Pricing (ERP) is an electronic toll collection scheme adopted in Singapore to manage traffic demand by road pricing. Implemented by the Land Transport Authority in September 1998, it replaced the earlier paper-based Area Licensing Scheme, which had required vehicles entering the restricted central zone during peak hours to display a purchased licence.

How it works

Overhead gantries at charging points register vehicles passing beneath them. Each vehicle carries an in-vehicle unit with a stored-value smart card (originally the CashCard, later NETS FlashPay and EZ-Link cards). Charges are deducted automatically as the vehicle passes, with rates varying by location, time of day, and vehicle type, and reviewed periodically against target traffic speeds.

Effects and development

The ERP system is credited with keeping traffic flowing in the central business district and expressways, and Singapore is frequently cited as a pioneer of congestion pricing. A next-generation, satellite-based ERP system (ERP 2.0) using on-board units with distance-based charging capability has been progressively rolled out to replace gantry-based charging.
