Godot (pronounced "guh-DOH") is a free and open-source game engine released under the permissive MIT license. Developed by Argentine software engineers Juan Linietsky and Ariel Manzur beginning in 2007 for use by Latin American game studios, it was released to the public in 2014 and is now maintained by a large community of contributors under the coordination of the Godot Foundation.

Features

Godot provides an integrated development environment for creating 2D and 3D games, built around a scene-and-node architecture in which game elements are composed from reusable building blocks. Scripting is supported in GDScript, a Python-like language designed for the engine, as well as C# and, through the GDExtension system, C, C++ and other languages. The engine exports to desktop, mobile, web and console platforms and includes editors for animation, tiles, shaders and visual scripting (removed in version 4 in favour of other approaches).

History

Godot 1.0 was released in December 2014. Version 3.0 (2018) added a new renderer and C# support. Godot 4.0, released in March 2023 after several years of development, was a major rewrite introducing the Vulkan-based renderer, improved 3D workflows and a revamped physics system. The engine's popularity grew markedly in 2023 after controversial pricing changes at the commercial Unity engine prompted many independent developers to migrate.

The engine is named after Samuel Beckett's play Waiting for Godot, reflecting its long development and the developers' sense of always waiting for it to be finished.
