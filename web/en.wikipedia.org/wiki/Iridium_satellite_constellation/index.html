Iridium satellite constellation

OperatorIridium Communications
Satellites66 operational (plus spares)
OrbitLow Earth orbit, ~780 km, polar
First launch1997

The Iridium satellite constellation provides worldwide voice and data coverage using a network of low Earth orbit satellites operated by Iridium Communications Inc. Named for the element iridium (atomic number 77), the original design called for 77 satellites; the deployed system uses 66 operational satellites in six polar orbital planes, plus in-orbit spares.

History

Conceived by Motorola engineers in the late 1980s, the system launched from 1997 and began service in November 1998 — but high handset and call prices and competition from terrestrial mobile networks drove Iridium into bankruptcy in 1999. A new company bought the assets in 2000 for a fraction of their cost and rebuilt the business around government, maritime, aviation and remote-industry users.

Technology

Satellites cross-link by Ka-band radio, routing calls in space without needing nearby ground stations. Handsets communicate in L-band, which penetrates weather better than higher frequencies. The constellation also hosts Aireon aircraft-tracking and maritime AIS payloads. Bright reflections from the original satellites' antennas produced predictable "Iridium flares" visible from Earth.

Iridium NEXT

Between 2017 and 2019, SpaceX Falcon 9 launches deployed 75 second-generation Iridium NEXT satellites, enabling the broadband Iridium Certus service and replacing the first generation, most of which was de-orbited. In 2009 an Iridium satellite collided with the defunct Russian Kosmos 2251, a landmark space-debris event.
