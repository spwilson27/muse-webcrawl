The emblem of Israel (Hebrew: סמל מדינת ישראל) depicts a temple menorah surrounded by an olive branch on each side, with the word Israel written in Hebrew below it. The emblem was adopted on 10 February 1949, less than a year after the establishment of the state.

Design and symbolism

The central image of the seven-branched menorah is based on the depiction carved on the Arch of Titus in Rome, showing the menorah carried from the Temple in Jerusalem after its destruction in 70 CE; the design thus symbolises the return of the Jewish people to their land. The olive branches flanking the menorah symbolise peace.

History

The new state held a design competition in 1948. The winning entry, submitted by graphic designers Gabriel and Maxim Shamir, drew on the proposal of Otte Wallish and W. Struski; after revision by the selection committee, the Shamir brothers' design combined their menorah with the olive branches of the Wallish proposal.

Use

The emblem appears on official documents, government buildings, Israeli passports and courtrooms, and a monumental bronze menorah modelled on it — the Knesset Menorah, sculpted by Benno Elkan — stands opposite the Knesset in Jerusalem.
