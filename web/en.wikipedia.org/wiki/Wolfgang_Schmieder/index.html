Wolfgang Schmieder

Wolfgang Schmieder (May 29, 1901 – November 8, 1990) was a German musicologist and librarian, best known as the creator of the Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis (BWV), the standard catalogue of the works of Johann Sebastian Bach.

Schmieder was born in Bromberg (now Bydgoszcz, Poland). He studied musicology at the University of Heidelberg, where he completed his doctorate in 1927 with a dissertation on the works of the composer Gottlieb Muffat. He then trained as a librarian and joined the music department of the Saxon State Library in Dresden, where he worked on the cataloguing of music manuscripts and prints.

Wolfgang Schmieder (1901–1990)

The BWV catalogue

In 1946, the Leipzig publisher Breitkopf & Härtel commissioned Schmieder to compile a complete thematic catalogue of Bach's works. The result, published in 1950 as Thematisch-systematisches Verzeichnis der musikalischen Werke von Johann Sebastian Bach, assigned each composition a BWV number that became the universal reference in scholarship, publishing, and recording. Schmieder prepared a substantially revised and expanded second edition in 1990, the year of his death; a further revised edition overseen by other scholars appeared decades later.

Later career

After the Second World War, Schmieder moved to Frankfurt am Main, where he headed the music department of the Frankfurt University Library (Stadt- und Universitätsbibliothek) from 1946 until his retirement in 1963, rebuilding war-damaged collections and continuing work on cataloguing projects including the Répertoire International des Sources Musicales (RISM). He died in Fürstenfeldbruck in 1990.
