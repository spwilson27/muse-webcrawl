Willy Wonka is a fictional character created by the British author Roald Dahl. He is the eccentric founder and owner of the Wonka Chocolate Factory, and appears in Dahl's children's novels Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (1964) and its sequel Charlie and the Great Glass Elevator (1972). Wonka is famous for his extraordinary confectionery inventions — including everlasting gobstoppers and a chocolate river — and for the contest in which five children who find Golden Tickets in Wonka Bars are given a tour of his secretive factory.

Character

Wonka is depicted as brilliant, whimsical and unpredictable, with a mischievous sense of humour and a tendency to speak in quotations and riddles. Having shut his factory to spies from rival chocolate makers, he staffs it entirely with the Oompa-Loompas, a people he rescued from Loompaland and who are paid in cacao beans. During the factory tour, four ill-behaved children meet comic misfortunes, and Wonka ultimately chooses the honest Charlie Bucket as his heir.

Portrayals

Gene Wilder's portrayal in the 1971 film Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory became iconic, blending warmth with an unsettling edge. Johnny Depp played a more childlike, socially awkward Wonka in Tim Burton's Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (2005), which added a backstory involving Wonka's dentist father. Timothée Chalamet portrayed a young Wonka in the prequel musical film Wonka (2023), which depicts his arrival in a European city and his first encounters with the Oompa-Loompas and rival chocolatiers.

Cultural impact

The character has become one of the most recognisable figures in children's literature, and "Willy Wonka" is widely used as a byword for an eccentric inventor-entrepreneur. The real "Wonka" brand of sweets, launched in connection with the 1971 film, was sold under various owners for decades. The character also gave rise to popular internet memes, notably the "Condescending Wonka" image derived from Wilder's performance.
