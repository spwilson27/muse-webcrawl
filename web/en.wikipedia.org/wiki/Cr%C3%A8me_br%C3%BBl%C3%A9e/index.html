Crème brûlée

Crème brûlée is a dessert consisting of a rich custard base topped with a layer of hardened caramelised sugar. It is normally served slightly chilled; the heat from the caramelising process tends to warm the top of the custard while leaving the centre cool.

History

The earliest known recipe appears in François Massialot's 1691 cookbook. Similar custards exist elsewhere — the Catalan crema catalana and the English "burnt cream" associated with Trinity College, Cambridge. The dessert's popularity surged in the 1980s as a signature dish of modern French restaurants.

Preparation

The custard — typically cream, egg yolks, sugar and vanilla — is baked in ramekins, often in a water bath. Shortly before serving, sugar is sprinkled on top and caramelised with a blowtorch, a salamander grill, or a red-hot iron disc.
