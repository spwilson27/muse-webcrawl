John Paul Goode (November 21, 1862 – August 22, 1932) was an American geographer and cartographer at the University of Chicago. He is best known for the Goode homolosine projection, an interrupted pseudo-cylindrical equal-area map projection widely used for world maps in atlases and textbooks.

Career

Goode studied at the University of Minnesota and took his doctorate at the University of Pennsylvania. After teaching in Illinois, he joined the University of Chicago's geography department, one of the first in the United States, and worked there for the rest of his career. He was a leading figure in popularising geography education and worked closely with the Rand McNally publishing company on school atlases.

The homolosine projection

In 1923 Goode published his interrupted homolosine projection, which combines the sinusoidal projection near the equator with Mollweide's projection at higher latitudes, interrupting the oceans (or, in alternative versions, the continents) to minimise distortion of land areas. Goode's school atlases made his name familiar to generations of students.
