T'Challa is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. He is the king and protector of the fictional African nation of Wakanda and holds the ceremonial title of the Black Panther. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Fantastic Four #52 (July 1966), making him the first Black superhero in mainstream American comics.

Character biography

T'Challa is the son of King T'Chaka, who was killed by the adventurer Ulysses Klaw. After earning the title of Black Panther, T'Challa rules Wakanda, an unconquered and technologically advanced isolationist nation rich in the metal vibranium. Enhanced by the heart-shaped herb, he possesses superhuman senses, strength, and agility, and is a skilled scientist, strategist, and martial artist. He is frequently a member of the Avengers and other superhero teams.

In other media

Chadwick Boseman portrayed T'Challa in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, in Captain America: Civil War (2016), the solo film Black Panther (2018), Avengers: Infinity War, and Avengers: Endgame. Following Boseman's death in 2020, Marvel chose not to recast the role, and Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022) centred on the character's in-universe death and succession.
