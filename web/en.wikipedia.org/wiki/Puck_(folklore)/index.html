Puck (folklore)

In English folklore, Puck, also known as Robin Goodfellow, is a mischievous nature sprite, fairy or trickster figure. Puck delights in playing pranks on mortals — misleading travellers, curdling milk and tripping up the unwary — but can also do household chores in exchange for offerings.

Origins

The name derives from Old English puca, a kind of goblin or demon, with cognates across Germanic and Celtic traditions. By the early modern period Puck had become a largely comic, roguish figure rather than a feared one.

In literature

Puck is most famous as a central character in Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream (c. 1595), where he serves Oberon and delivers the epilogue. He also appears in Kipling's Puck of Pook's Hill and many later works.
