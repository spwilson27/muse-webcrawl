Social science fiction

Social science fiction is a subgenre of science fiction concerned with the social sciences — anthropology, sociology, psychology, politics and economics — rather than the "hard" physical sciences. Instead of asking how a technology works, it asks how societies and individuals respond to change, often by extrapolation or satire.

Characteristics and history

Classic examples include Ursula K. Le Guin's The Left Hand of Darkness (1969) and The Dispossessed (1974), which use alien worlds to explore gender and anarchism; Isaac Asimov's Foundation series, built around the fictional science of "psychohistory"; and the dystopias of Aldous Huxley's Brave New World and Margaret Atwood's The Handmaid's Tale. The 1960s New Wave movement pushed the genre toward such "soft" themes and literary experimentation.

Social science fiction frequently overlaps with utopian and dystopian fiction, political fiction and satire. Anthropological science fiction is a closely related label for works centred on imagined cultures.
