The Toronto Star is a Canadian English-language broadsheet daily newspaper based in Toronto, Ontario, and published by Toronto Star Newspapers Ltd., a division of Torstar Corporation. Founded in 1892 as the Evening Star and renamed the Toronto Daily Star in 1900, it has long been among Canada's largest-circulation newspapers and is known for its centre-left, social-justice editorial tradition.

History

The paper was founded in 1892 by striking printers from the Toronto News. Its formative publisher was Joseph E. Atkinson, who took control in 1899 and ran the paper until his death in 1948. Atkinson instilled the principles that still guide the paper — a strong, united Canada, social justice, individual and civil liberties, community engagement, the rights of working people, and government accountability — known collectively as the Atkinson Principles.

Under Atkinson the Star became Canada's largest newspaper, pioneering radio broadcasting through its own station and sending a young Ernest Hemingway to Europe as a correspondent in the 1920s. The paper moved to One Yonge Street in 1971. For decades it was held under the terms of Atkinson's will by the Atkinson Foundation and a group of trustees committed to preserving its character.

Recent years

Like most metropolitan dailies, the Star has faced steep declines in print circulation and advertising. In 2020 Torstar was taken private by NordStar Capital, a company controlled by Jordan Bitove and Paul Rivett, after a contested bidding process. The Star maintains bureaus in Ottawa and Queen's Park and publishes investigative reporting that has won numerous National Newspaper Awards and the Michener Award.
