Transporter bridge

A transporter bridge, also known as a ferry bridge or aerial transfer bridge, is a type of movable bridge that carries a segment of roadway or a gondola across a river. The gondola is slung from a tall span by wires or a metal frame and travels back and forth like a ferry, allowing road traffic to cross without obstructing the passage of tall-masted ships below.

History

The concept was developed in the 1890s, notably by the French engineer Ferdinand Arnodin. The first transporter bridge, the Vizcaya Bridge across the Nervión at Portugalete near Bilbao, Spain, opened in 1893 and remains in use; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Around two dozen transporter bridges were built, mostly in Europe and South America, before the type was superseded by cheaper conventional bridges.

Surviving examples

Few transporter bridges survive in operation. Well-known examples include the Vizcaya Bridge in Spain; the Newport Transporter Bridge and the Middlesbrough Transporter Bridge in the United Kingdom; the Rochefort-Martrou Transporter Bridge in France; the Osten Transporter Bridge in Germany; and the Puente Transbordador in Buenos Aires, Argentina.
