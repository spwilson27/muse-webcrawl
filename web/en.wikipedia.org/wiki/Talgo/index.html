Talgo (officially Patentes Talgo, S.A.) is a Spanish manufacturer of railway trains, founded in 1942 to develop the designs of engineer Alejandro Goicoechea. The name is an acronym of Tren Articulado Ligero Goicoechea Oriol — "light articulated train" — combining Goicoechea's name with that of financier José Luis Oriol.

Technology

Talgo trains are distinguished by their articulated design: short, low car bodies rest on shared single-axle running gear with independently rotating wheels, rather than conventional bogies. This gives a low centre of gravity, light weight, and a natural passive tilting that lets trains take curves faster. Talgo is also renowned for its variable-gauge axles, which allow trains to pass automatically between Iberian gauge and standard gauge (and, in some designs, broad gauge) — a system in service since the late 1960s that connects Spain's standard-gauge high-speed lines with its legacy network.

Products and services

Talgo trainsets operate in Spain (including the high-speed Talgo 350, marketed as Avril and the trains of the Avlo and Ouigo España services), Germany, Denmark, the United States (the Pacific Northwest's Amtrak Cascades), Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Saudi Arabia (the Haramain high-speed railway), and Egypt, among other countries.
