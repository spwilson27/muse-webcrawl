Helsinki Treaty

Helsinki Treaty
Signed: 23 March 1962, Helsinki
Parties: Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden
Purpose: Nordic co-operation framework

The Helsinki Treaty, formally the Treaty of Co-operation between Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, was signed on 23 March 1962 in Helsinki and entered into force on 1 July 1962. It is the foundational agreement for official Nordic co-operation.

Content

The treaty sets out co-operation in legal, cultural, social, economic, transport, environmental and other fields, and provides the framework for the Nordic Council (founded 1952) and the Nordic Council of Ministers (founded 1971). It has been amended several times, notably to reflect European integration and the accession of Finland and Sweden to the European Union in 1995.

Later developments

The day of signing, 23 March, is celebrated as Nordic Day. Periodic proposals to revise the treaty have addressed defence and foreign-policy co-operation among the Nordic states.
