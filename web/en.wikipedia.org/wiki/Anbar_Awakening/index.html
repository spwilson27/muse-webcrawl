Anbar Awakening

Date: 2005–2007
Location: Al Anbar Governorate, Iraq
Part of: Iraq War

The Anbar Awakening (Arabic: صحوة الأنبار), also called the Sunni Awakening, was a movement in Iraq's Al Anbar Governorate beginning in 2005 in which Sunni Arab tribal sheikhs and former insurgents turned against al-Qaeda in Iraq and allied with United States forces. The movement dramatically reduced violence in Anbar and became a model for similar "Sons of Iraq" programmes across the country.

Background

After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, Anbar, a predominantly Sunni province, became the heartland of the insurgency. Al-Qaeda in Iraq, led by Abu Musab al-Zarqawi, imposed a harsh rule in areas it controlled, assassinating tribal leaders, banning customs such as smoking, and interfering with smuggling revenues on which tribes depended. Zarqawi's killing of Sunni tribal figures and the group's indiscriminate bombings alienated the tribes, some of which had begun fighting al-Qaeda as early as 2005.

The Awakening Council

In September 2006, Sheikh Abdul Sattar Abu Risha formed the Anbar Salvation Council (also called the Awakening Council), an alliance of tribes that pledged to fight al-Qaeda alongside American forces. US commanders in Ramadi, notably Colonel Sean MacFarland, supported the tribes with money, weapons and joint operations. Violence in Ramadi, once among the most dangerous cities in Iraq, fell sharply during 2007. Sattar was assassinated by al-Qaeda in September 2007, days after meeting US President George W. Bush, but the movement continued under his brother.

Spread and consequences

The Awakening model spread beyond Anbar as the US military hired roughly 100,000 mostly Sunni fighters into local security units known as the Sons of Iraq, a key component of the 2007 "surge" strategy. Violence across Iraq declined markedly in 2007–2008. After 2008 the Iraqi government of Nouri al-Maliki was slow and reluctant to integrate Awakening fighters into the security forces, contributing to Sunni grievances that later fuelled instability.
