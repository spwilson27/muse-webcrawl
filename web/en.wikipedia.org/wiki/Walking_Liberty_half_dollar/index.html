The Walking Liberty half dollar is a silver 50-cent piece or half dollar coin that was issued by the United States Mint from 1916 to 1947. It was designed by Adolph A. Weinman, a well-known sculptor and engraver, and is widely considered one of the most beautiful United States coins.

Design

The obverse depicts a full-length figure of Liberty striding toward the dawn of a new day, draped in the American flag, carrying branches of laurel and oak, with her right hand extended. The reverse shows a bald eagle perched on a mountain crag, wings unfolded, fearless in spirit, with a small mountain pine sapling springing from a rock. Weinman's initials appear on both sides.

History

The coin replaced the Barber half dollar in 1916 as part of a celebrated redesign of American coinage that also produced the Mercury dime (also by Weinman) and the Standing Liberty quarter. Early issues had the mint mark on the obverse, but it was moved to the reverse in 1917. Striking quality varied, and the design details — notably Liberty's head, hand, and the eagle's breast feathers — are often weakly struck, making well-struck examples prized by collectors. The series ended in 1947 and was replaced by the Franklin half dollar in 1948.

Legacy

Weinman's obverse design was revived in 1986 for the American Silver Eagle bullion coin programme, on which it remains in use.
