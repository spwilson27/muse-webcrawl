Super Mario World is a platform video game developed by Nintendo EAD and published by Nintendo for the Super Nintendo Entertainment System (SNES). It was released in Japan in November 1990 as a launch title for the system and in North America in 1991. The game follows Mario and Luigi as they rescue Princess Toadstool and Dinosaur Land from Bowser and the Koopalings.

Gameplay

As in earlier Mario games, the player runs and jumps through side-scrolling levels, defeating enemies and collecting coins and power-ups. Super Mario World introduced Yoshi, a dinosaur Mario can ride who can eat enemies and fruit and gain abilities from coloured Koopa shells, and the Cape Feather, which lets Mario fly. A world map connects 72 levels with 96 exits; finding secret exits unlocks shortcuts and the hidden Star World and Special World. It was also the first Mario game to let the player store an extra power-up in a reserve box.

Development and reception

Directed by Takashi Tezuka and produced by Shigeru Miyamoto, the game took three years to develop and was designed to showcase the SNES's expanded colour palette and graphical effects. Koji Kondo's score reworked a small set of melodies across many arrangements. Super Mario World sold more than 20 million copies, making it the best-selling SNES game, and is frequently cited among the greatest video games of all time for its level design and precise controls. It was re-released for the Game Boy Advance as Super Mario World: Super Mario Advance 2 in 2001, and Yoshi went on to star in his own series beginning with Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island.
