Broker-dealer

FieldFinancial services
Regulators (US)SEC, FINRA
ActivitiesBrokerage (agency) and dealing (principal) in securities

A broker-dealer is a person, company, or other organization that engages in the business of trading securities for its own account or on behalf of its customers. Broker-dealers are at the heart of the securities and derivatives trading process.

Functions

When executing trade orders on behalf of a customer, the institution is said to be acting as a broker (agent). When executing trades for its own account, the institution acts as a dealer (principal), buying and selling securities from its own inventory and profiting from the bid–ask spread. Many firms act in both capacities, which creates potential conflicts of interest addressed by regulation and disclosure rules.

Regulation

In the United States, broker-dealers must register with the Securities and Exchange Commission under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and belong to a self-regulatory organization such as FINRA. They are subject to net capital requirements, customer protection rules such as segregation of customer assets, and suitability or best-interest standards when making recommendations to retail customers.
