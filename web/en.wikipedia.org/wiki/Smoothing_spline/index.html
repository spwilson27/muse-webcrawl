Smoothing spline

Smoothing splines are function estimates, f̂(x), obtained from a set of noisy observations yi of the target f(xi), in order to balance a measure of goodness of fit of the estimate with a derivative-based measure of the smoothness of the estimate. They provide a means for smoothing noisy data, and are a popular tool in nonparametric regression.

Definition

Let x1 ≤ ... ≤ xn be a set of observations, modeled by the relation yi = f(xi) + εi where the εi are independent, zero-mean noise terms. The cubic smoothing spline estimate of the function f is defined to be the minimizer of:

Σi (yi − f̂(xi))² + λ ∫ (f̂″(x))² dx

The smoothing parameter λ controls the trade-off between fidelity to the data and smoothness: as λ approaches zero the spline interpolates the data, while as λ approaches infinity it approaches a least-squares straight line. The minimizer is a natural cubic spline with knots at the distinct values of the covariates.

Choice of smoothing parameter

The parameter λ may be selected by cross-validation, generalized cross-validation (GCV), or by treating the spline as a mixed model and using (restricted) maximum likelihood estimation of variance components.

Extensions

Multivariate smoothing splines, known as thin-plate splines, extend the penalized-fit idea to data in several dimensions. Smoothing splines form the foundation of generalized additive models, in which a response is modeled as a sum of smooth functions of predictor variables.
