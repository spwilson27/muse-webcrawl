Targeted protein degradation

Targeted protein degradation (TPD) is a therapeutic strategy in drug discovery that uses small molecules to induce the selective destruction of disease-causing proteins by the cell's own protein-disposal machinery, principally the ubiquitin–proteasome system. Unlike conventional inhibitors, which merely block a protein's active site, degraders remove the protein entirely.

Mechanism

The best-known degraders are PROTACs (proteolysis-targeting chimeras): heterobifunctional molecules with one end binding the target protein and the other recruiting an E3 ubiquitin ligase such as cereblon or VHL. Bringing the two together causes the target to be tagged with ubiquitin and destroyed by the proteasome. Because the degrader is released afterwards, it acts catalytically. Molecular glues achieve a similar effect with a single small molecule that stabilises a ligase–target interaction.

Applications

TPD is attractive for so-called "undruggable" proteins that lack enzymatic pockets, including transcription factors and scaffolding proteins. Clinical candidates have been developed against androgen and oestrogen receptors in cancer, and research extends to neurodegenerative diseases.
