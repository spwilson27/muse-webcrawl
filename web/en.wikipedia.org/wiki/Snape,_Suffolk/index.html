Snape, Suffolk

Snape is a village in the civil parish of Snape with Thorington, in East Suffolk, England, on the River Alde. It is known internationally for Snape Maltings, a concert hall complex associated with the composer Benjamin Britten and the Aldeburgh Festival.

County: Suffolk
District: East Suffolk
Population: Under 1,000

History

Snape was a small port and shipbuilding centre in the Saxon era; the nearby Sutton Hoo ship burial reflects the area's early importance. Snape Priory was founded in the 12th century. In the 19th century the village became famous for Snape Maltings, a large malting complex built by Newson Garrett (1840s onward) that supplied malt for brewing.

Snape Maltings

After malting ceased in the 1960s, the complex was converted into a concert hall, opened in 1967 by Benjamin Britten and Peter Pears as the home of the Aldeburgh Festival. The site now includes shops, galleries and the Britten Pears Arts organisation.
