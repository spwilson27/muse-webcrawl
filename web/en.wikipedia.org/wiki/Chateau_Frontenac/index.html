Château Frontenac

LocationQuebec City
Opened1893
StyleChâteauesque railway hotel
[Image: Château Frontenac above the St Lawrence]
Château Frontenac above the St Lawrence

The Château Frontenac is a grand railway hotel in Quebec City, Canada, opened in 1893, whose copper turrets above the St Lawrence make it one of the most photographed hotels in the world.

Overview

Built by the Canadian Pacific Railway in the château style it made canonical, the hotel stands on Cap Diamant at the edge of Old Quebec, near the site of the Château St-Louis, seat of French and British governors. It hosted the Quebec Conferences of 1943 and 1944, where Roosevelt, Churchill and Mackenzie King planned the later war and D-Day. Named for the Comte de Frontenac, the twice-governor who defied an English fleet in 1690 ("I will answer you from the mouth of my cannons"), the hotel anchors the Dufferin Terrace promenade over the old town and the funicular to the Petit-Champlain quarter.
