Buck–boost converter

TypeSwitched-mode power supply
TopologyNon-isolated DC–DC converter
Output polarityInverted (negative) relative to input
ComponentsInductor, switch, diode, capacitor

The buck–boost converter is a type of DC-to-DC converter whose output voltage magnitude can be either greater than or less than the input voltage magnitude. It is a class of switched-mode power supply (SMPS) conceptually similar to combining a boost converter and a buck converter into a single circuit.

Principle of operation

In the basic inverting topology, a switch (usually a transistor) alternately connects the inductor to the input source, storing energy in its magnetic field, and then disconnects it so the inductor discharges into the load through a diode. Because the inductor discharges with reversed polarity, the output voltage is negative relative to the input. With duty cycle D, the steady-state output is approximately −Vin × D/(1−D) in continuous conduction mode, so outputs above and below the input magnitude are obtained by varying D below or above 50 per cent.

Applications and variants

Buck–boost converters are used where a regulated rail must be maintained from a source that varies both above and below it, such as a battery. Non-inverting variants include the SEPIC, Ćuk converter and cascaded buck–boost designs. Isolated forms based on the flyback topology are widely used in low-power adapters.
