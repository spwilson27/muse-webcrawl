Sodium polyacrylate

Sodium polyacrylate (ACR, ASAP) is a sodium salt of polyacrylic acid with the chemical formula [−CH2−CH(CO2Na)−]n. It is a superabsorbent polymer (SAP) with the ability to absorb 100 to 1000 times its mass in water.

Properties

Sodium polyacrylate is an anionic polyelectrolyte with negatively charged carboxylic groups in the main chain. When dry it is a white powder; exposed to water, the sodium ions hydrate and the cross-linked polymer network swells into a gel, trapping the liquid. Absorption is much weaker in salty water because the extra cations screen the charges and reduce the osmotic driving force.

Applications

The most common use is in disposable diapers and other hygiene products, where the polymer gel absorbs and retains urine. It is also used in artificial snow, instant gel powder toys and demonstrations, water-retaining granules for agriculture, spill-control and cable water-blocking, and — in expanded forms — in instant snow for film and decoration. Food-grade forms are restricted, and the material expands visibly when used in novelty products.

Safety and environment

The polymer itself is considered non-toxic, though residual acrylic acid monomer is controlled by regulation. Superabsorbent polymers are not readily biodegradable, which has prompted research into bio-based alternatives.
