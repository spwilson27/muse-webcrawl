John Balliol

John Balliol (c. 1249 – late 1314), known disparagingly as Toom Tabard ("empty coat"), was King of Scots from 1292 to 1296. His brief reign ended with his forced abdication by Edward I of England, a humiliation that helped spark the Wars of Scottish Independence.

Accession

After the death of Margaret, Maid of Norway in 1290 left the Scottish succession disputed, thirteen competitors claimed the throne. Edward I of England, invited to arbitrate the "Great Cause," chose Balliol, whose claim through his mother Devorguilla of Galloway made him the senior descendant of David I. Balliol was crowned at Scone on 30 November 1292.

Reign and downfall

Edward treated Scotland as a vassal kingdom and repeatedly humiliated Balliol by hearing appeals from Scottish courts. In 1295 Balliol's council concluded the Auld Alliance with France. Edward invaded in 1296, sacked Berwick, and defeated the Scots at Dunbar. Balliol surrendered, was stripped of his royal insignia — hence "Toom Tabard" — and imprisoned in the Tower of London.

Later life

Released into papal custody in 1299, Balliol retired to his family's estates in Picardy, France, where he died around 1314. His son Edward Balliol later made unsuccessful bids for the Scottish crown during the Second War of Scottish Independence.
