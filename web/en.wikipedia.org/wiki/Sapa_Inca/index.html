Sapa Inca

The Sapa Inca (Quechua: Sapa Inka, "the only Inca") was the emperor of the Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu), the largest empire in pre-Columbian America. Regarded as a descendant and earthly representative of the sun god Inti, the Sapa Inca held absolute political and religious authority.

Role and regalia

The emperor wore the mascapaicha, a fringed royal headdress with a scarlet tassel, as his crown. He was carried in a litter, ate from gold and silver vessels, and his person was considered sacred. On his death, his mummified body (mallki) was preserved, consulted as an oracle, and maintained by his royal descent group (panaca), which also kept his palaces and estates.

List of rulers

Traditional lists begin with the semi-legendary Manco Cápac, founder of Cusco. The empire's great expansion began under Pachacuti (reigned c. 1438–1471), followed by Topa Inca Yupanqui and Huayna Capac. After Huayna Capac's death, a civil war between his sons Huáscar and Atahualpa weakened the empire; Atahualpa was captured by Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca in 1532, ending independent Inca rule, though a Neo-Inca State survived at Vilcabamba until 1572.
