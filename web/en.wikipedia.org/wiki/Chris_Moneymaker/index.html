Christopher Bryan Moneymaker (born November 21, 1975) is an American poker player who won the Main Event at the 2003 World Series of Poker (WSOP). His victory — as an amateur accountant who earned his seat in an $86 online satellite tournament — is credited with sparking the mid-2000s "poker boom," a surge in the game's popularity often called the Moneymaker effect.

Early life

Moneymaker was born in Atlanta, Georgia, and grew up and studied in Tennessee, earning a master's degree in accounting from the University of Tennessee. He worked as an accountant in Tennessee and played poker as a hobby. His surname, often assumed to be a stage name, is genuine — a modification of an ancestral German name.

2003 World Series of Poker

In 2003 Moneymaker won a seat in the $10,000 WSOP Main Event through a satellite on the online site PokerStars. It was his first major live tournament. He outlasted a field of 839 players and defeated professional Sam Farha heads-up, winning $2.5 million. A celebrated bluff against Farha on the river of a key hand became one of the most replayed moments in televised poker. ESPN's enhanced coverage of that year's Main Event, showing players' hole cards, amplified the impact of an amateur winning the title.

The Moneymaker effect

Moneymaker's win convinced millions of casual players that an unknown amateur could beat the professionals. Participation in the WSOP Main Event more than tripled the following year, online poker grew explosively, and poker programming spread across television. Moneymaker signed with PokerStars as an ambassador, a role he held until 2020, before joining Americas Cardroom in 2021.

Later career

Moneymaker finished second in a World Poker Tour event in 2004 and has continued to play professionally, adding further tournament cashes and appearing in poker media. He was inducted into the Poker Hall of Fame in 2019. His memoir, Moneymaker: How an Amateur Poker Player Turned $40 into $2.5 Million at the World Series of Poker, was published in 2005.
