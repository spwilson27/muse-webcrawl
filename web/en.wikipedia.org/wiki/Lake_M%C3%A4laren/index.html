Lake Mälaren

Mälaren, historically also Lake Malar, is the third-largest freshwater lake in Sweden, after Vänern and Vättern. It covers about 1,140 square kilometres and stretches roughly 120 kilometres westward from Stockholm, where it meets the Baltic Sea.

The lake has a complex shoreline with some 1,200 islands and several distinct basins and bays. Important towns on its shores include Västerås, Eskilstuna, Södertälje and Uppsala (via the Fyris River and Ekoln bay). Two UNESCO World Heritage Sites stand on its islands: Birka and Hovgården, and Drottningholm Palace.

In Norse mythology and early Swedish history Mälaren was a central waterway for trade and settlement. Post-glacial rebound means the lake was once a bay of the Baltic; it became a freshwater lake around the 11th–12th centuries as the land rose and the Stockholm outlet narrowed.
