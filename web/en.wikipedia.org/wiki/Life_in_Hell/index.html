Life in Hell is a comic strip by Matt Groening that ran weekly from 1977 to 2012. A sardonic satire of life, love, work, and school, it starred Binky, a depressed one-eared rabbit; Sheba, his girlfriend; the identical gay twins Akbar and Jeff; and Bongo, Binky's one-eared son. The strip became a fixture of alternative newspapers across the United States, most prominently the Los Angeles Reader and later the LA Weekly.

History

Groening, who had moved to Los Angeles and found conditions hellish, began drawing the strip in 1977 and first published it in the avant-garde magazine Wet; it debuted in the Los Angeles Reader in 1978. Book collections such as Love Is Hell, Work Is Hell, and School Is Hell became underground bestsellers in the 1980s, and the strip's deadpan lists and "How-To" guides defined a generation of alternative comics. Fans included producer James L. Brooks, who asked Groening to create animated shorts for The Tracey Ullman Show; Groening instead invented The Simpsons (1989), whose success eventually eclipsed the strip. Groening ended Life in Hell in June 2012, citing the decline of alternative weeklies.

Style and themes

Drawn in a deliberately crude style with heavy use of cross-hatching, the strip's recurring features included "The Los Angeles Way of Death," "Forbidden Words," single-panel gags of Binky in various states of existential dread, and the feud between media moguls Akbar and Jeff, proprietors of innumerable interchangeable businesses.
