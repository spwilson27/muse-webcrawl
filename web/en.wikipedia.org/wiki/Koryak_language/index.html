Koryak (Koryak: нымылан, Nymylan) is a Chukotko-Kamchatkan language spoken by the Koryak people in the northern part of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East, mainly in Koryak Okrug. It is closely related to Chukchi and somewhat more distantly to Alutor and Kerek.

Status

Koryak is severely endangered. Of roughly 8,000 ethnic Koryaks, only a minority — mostly older people — speak the language fluently, as younger generations shift to Russian. The 2010 Russian census recorded about 1,700 speakers. Dialects include Chavchuven (spoken by reindeer-herding nomads), Palana, Karaga and Apuka varieties.

Grammar

Koryak is an agglutinative, polysynthetic language with noun incorporation and ergative alignment. It has a relatively small consonant inventory but makes extensive use of vowel harmony: vowels fall into dominant and recessive sets, and suffix vowels change to harmonise with the stem. Word order is flexible, with basic SOV tendencies.

Writing

A Cyrillic-based orthography for Koryak was developed in the 1930s, replacing a short-lived Latin alphabet. Koryak is taught in some primary schools in Koryak Okrug, and a small body of literature, textbooks and a newspaper page are published in the language, but Russian dominates education and media.
