Creation

James Bond is a fictional British secret agent created in 1953 by the writer and former naval intelligence officer Ian Fleming in the novel Casino Royale. Fleming, writing at his Jamaican estate Goldeneye, named his hero after the American ornithologist James Bond, author of Birds of the West Indies, deliberately choosing a name he considered plain and un-heroic. Fleming wrote twelve Bond novels and two short-story collections before his death in 1964; authors including Kingsley Amis, John Gardner, Raymond Benson, Sebastian Faulks, and Anthony Horowitz continued the literary series.

The character

Bond is a commander in the Royal Navy and an officer of the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6) holding the 00 codename 007 — a licence to kill. Fleming gave him a fondness for gambling, fast cars (a Bentley, later the film-famous Aston Martin DB5), vodka martinis "shaken, not stirred," cigarettes, and short, intense love affairs; a scarred, cold-eyed physical portrait based partly on Fleming himself. In the novels Bond's backstory, sketched in an obituary in You Only Live Twice, includes Scottish and Swiss parents killed in a climbing accident, schooling at Eton and Fettes, and wartime service.

The novels

The Fleming canon runs from Casino Royale, Live and Let Die, Moonraker, Diamonds Are Forever, From Russia, with Love, Dr. No, and Goldfinger to Thunderball, The Spy Who Loved Me, On Her Majesty's Secret Service, You Only Live Twice, and The Man with the Golden Gun. The books' formula — exotic locales, a megalomaniac villain, a glamorous "Bond girl," gambling set pieces, and torture-and-escape sequences — defined the modern spy thriller.

The films

The Eon Productions film series began with Dr. No (1962), starring Sean Connery, and grew into one of the longest-running and highest-grossing franchises in cinema, with 25 Eon films through No Time to Die (2021). Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan, and Daniel Craig have played Bond, each era reflecting its period. The films are famous for pre-title sequences, the gun-barrel opening, Monty Norman's (arranged by John Barry) theme, gadget-laden cars from "Q Branch," and villains such as Goldfinger, Blofeld of SPECTRE, and Le Chiffre.

Cultural impact

Bond shaped the spy genre and British popular identity for two generations, spawning imitators from The Man from U.N.C.L.E. to Jason Bourne, and enduring debates over the character's sexism, violence, and imperial nostalgia. The 007 brand extends to video games (notably GoldenEye 007), and Bond's Olympic stadium appearance with Queen Elizabeth II in 2012 confirmed his status as a national icon.
