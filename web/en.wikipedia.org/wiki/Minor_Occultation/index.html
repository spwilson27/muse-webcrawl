Minor Occultation

Al-Mahdi, the twelfth Imam in Twelver Shia belief

The Minor Occultation (Arabic: الغيبة الصغرى, al-ghayba al-ṣughrā) is a concept in Twelver Shia Islam referring to the first of two periods of occultation of the twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi. It began in 874 CE (260 AH), after the death of the eleventh Imam, Hasan al-Askari, and lasted until 941 CE (329 AH).

The four deputies

During the Minor Occultation, the Hidden Imam is believed to have communicated with his followers through four successive deputies (sufara): Uthman ibn Sa'id al-Asadi, his son Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Uthman, Abu al-Qasim Husayn ibn Ruh al-Nawbakhti, and Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Samarri. Letters and rulings attributed to the Imam, known as tawqīʿāt, were transmitted through them.

End and Major Occultation

Shortly before al-Samarri's death in 941, a final letter announced the beginning of the Major Occultation, during which no special deputy would be appointed and the Imam would remain hidden until his return near the end of time.
