Spin group

In mathematics, the spin group, denoted Spin(n), is a Lie group that is the simply connected double cover of the special orthogonal group SO(n). It arises naturally in the study of Clifford algebras and plays a fundamental role in differential geometry and quantum mechanics, where it provides the correct mathematical framework for the quantum-mechanical concept of spin.

As a manifold, Spin(n) coincides with SO(n) for n > 2 except that it is simply connected, whereas SO(n) has fundamental group Z/2. The double covering homomorphism Spin(n) → SO(n) maps two elements of the spin group to each rotation; intuitively, a full 720° rotation—not 360°—is required to return a spinor to its original state, a fact demonstrated by Dirac's belt trick.

Construction

The spin group can be constructed inside the Clifford algebra Cl(n) of an n-dimensional Euclidean space. Elements of Spin(n) are products of an even number of unit vectors in the Clifford algebra, acting on vectors by conjugation; this conjugation action is a rotation, giving the covering map onto SO(n).

Low-dimensional examples

In low dimensions the spin groups coincide with other classical groups: Spin(1) ≅ Z/2; Spin(2) ≅ U(1), the circle group; Spin(3) ≅ SU(2), the group of unit quaternions, which covers the rotation group SO(3) of ordinary three-dimensional space; Spin(4) ≅ SU(2) × SU(2); Spin(5) ≅ Sp(2); and Spin(6) ≅ SU(4). These "accidental isomorphisms" underlie many special features of low-dimensional geometry and physics.

Applications

Spin groups and their representations—spin representations—are essential in quantum field theory, where particles such as electrons are described by spinor fields transforming under Spin(3) rather than SO(3). In geometry, a spin structure on a Riemannian manifold is a lift of its oriented orthonormal frame bundle to a Spin(n) bundle, permitting the definition of spinor fields and the Dirac operator.
