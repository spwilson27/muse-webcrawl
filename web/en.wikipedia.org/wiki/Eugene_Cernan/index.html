Eugene Cernan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eugene Cernan
Born14 March 1934, Chicago, Illinois
Died16 January 2017 (aged 82)
MissionsGemini 9A, Apollo 10, Apollo 17
Cernan, dusted grey, aboard Challenger.

Eugene Andrew Cernan (1934–2017) was an American naval aviator and astronaut who, as commander of Apollo 17 in December 1972, became the eleventh person to walk on the Moon — and the last, a distinction he held for the rest of his life and treated as a trust. His final words from the surface, a pledge that 'we shall return', framed his decades of advocacy for a return to deep space.

Cernan's career traced Apollo's whole arc: a gruelling, near-disastrous spacewalk on Gemini 9A (1966), when his visor fogged and he overheated in an ill-fitting suit — the 'spacewalk from hell' that taught NASA to redesign EVA around underwater training; the Apollo 10 dress rehearsal; and Apollo 17, the longest and most productive lunar mission, with Harrison Schmitt.

Last man

Cernan's memoir and the 2014 documentary share the title The Last Man on the Moon. He died in 2017 still holding the title; the Artemis program is expected, at last, to pass the honour to a successor.
