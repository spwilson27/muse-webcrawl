Dhrupad

Dhrupad
Cultural origins: Medieval India
Typical instruments: Vocals, pakhawaj, tanpura, rudra veena

Dhrupad is a genre of Hindustani classical music and is considered the oldest surviving form of that tradition. The word derives from dhruva (fixed, immovable) and pada (verse), reflecting its structured, meditative character.

History

Dhrupad has its roots in temple music and the chanting traditions of ancient India, and it flourished in the royal courts of northern India from the 15th century onwards. The Mughal emperor Akbar's court included the legendary musician Tansen, regarded as one of the greatest dhrupad singers. The genre declined in popularity during the 19th and 20th centuries as khayal became dominant, but it survived through hereditary musician families (gharanas) such as the Dagar family, whose members led a 20th-century revival.

Structure

A dhrupad performance typically begins with an extended improvised alap, in which the singer explores the raga syllable by syllable, followed by a composed song (bandish) set to a tala such as chautal (12 beats), accompanied by the pakhawaj drum. Compositions are often devotional, in Braj Bhasha, and the singing is marked by precise intonation and elaborate rhythmic interplay.
