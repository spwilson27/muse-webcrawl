The Speaker of the Parliament of Lebanon is the presiding officer of the Lebanese Chamber of Deputies (Parliament). Under the unwritten National Pact of 1943, confirmed by the Taif Agreement of 1989, the office is reserved for a Shia Muslim, while the presidency goes to a Maronite Christian and the premiership to a Sunni Muslim — the "troika" at the head of the Lebanese state.

Role and powers

The Speaker presides over parliamentary sessions, directs the chamber's agenda, and represents the legislature. Since the Taif amendments increased the Speaker's term from one year to the full four-year parliamentary term and strengthened the office's role in consultations, the speakership has become one of the most powerful positions in the country.

History

Notable speakers include Sabri Hamadeh, Kamel Asaad, and Hussein el-Husseini, under whom the Taif Agreement was adopted. Nabih Berri, leader of the Amal Movement, has held the office continuously since 1992, making him one of the longest-serving parliamentary speakers in the world and a central figure in Lebanese politics for more than three decades.
