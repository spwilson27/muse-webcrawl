Central angle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A central angle is an angle whose apex (vertex) is the center O of a circle and whose legs (sides) are radii intersecting the circle in two distinct points A and B. The central angle is subtended by an arc between those two points, and the length of the arc is the central angle of a circle of radius one (measured in radians). The central angle is also known as the arc's angular distance.

Measure

The size of a central angle Θ is

0° ≤ Θ < 360°  or  0 ≤ Θ < 2π (in radians).

When defining or drawing a central angle, it must be specified whether the arc subtended is the minor arc (< 180°) or the major arc (> 180°). If the points of intersection are diametrically opposite, the angle is a straight angle of 180°.

Relation to arc length

For a circle of radius r, the arc subtended by a central angle of Θ radians has length

s = rΘ.

This relationship is the basis of the definition of the radian: one radian is the central angle subtending an arc equal in length to the radius.

Relation to inscribed angles

The inscribed angle theorem states that an angle inscribed in a circle is half the measure of the central angle that subtends the same arc. Consequently, all inscribed angles subtending the same chord or arc are equal, and an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle.

Great circles

On a sphere, the central angle between two points — the angle at the sphere's centre formed by the radii to those points — determines their great-circle distance, which is the radius multiplied by the angle in radians. This is fundamental in navigation and geodesy.
