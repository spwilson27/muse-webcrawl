Evaporated milk, known in some countries as "unsweetened condensed milk", is a shelf-stable canned cow's milk product in which about 60% of the water has been removed. It differs from sweetened condensed milk, which contains added sugar; evaporated milk is unsweetened and is sterilised in the can, giving it a slightly darker colour and caramelised flavour.

Production

Fresh milk is evaporated under vacuum, homogenised, canned and heat-sterilised. The process was commercialised in the 1880s–1890s, notably by John B. Meyenberg and the companies that became Carnation and PET, and it was valued before refrigeration because the sealed cans kept for many months.

Uses

Reconstituted with water it substitutes for fresh milk; undiluted it adds richness to coffee, sauces, pies and desserts such as tres leches cake and pumpkin pie.
