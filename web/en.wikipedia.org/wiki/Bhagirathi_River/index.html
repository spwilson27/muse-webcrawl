Bhagirathi River

Bhagirathi River
LocationUttarakhand, India
SourceGaumukh, Gangotri Glacier
MouthConfluence with the Alaknanda at Devprayag, forming the Ganges
Lengthc. 205 km (127 mi)

The Bhagirathi is a turbulent Himalayan river in the Indian state of Uttarakhand, and one of the two headstreams of the Ganges, the major river of Northern India; the other is the Alaknanda. In Hindu tradition and culture, the Bhagirathi is considered the source stream of the Ganges, although hydrologically the Alaknanda is larger.

Course

The river rises at Gaumukh, at the foot of the Gangotri Glacier, at an elevation of about 4,356 m (14,291 ft). It flows through the pilgrim town of Gangotri, then through deep gorges past Harsil and Uttarkashi, before being joined by tributaries such as the Jadh Ganga and the Bhilangna. At Devprayag it meets the Alaknanda, and the combined stream takes the name Ganga (Ganges).

Dams

The Tehri Dam, one of the tallest dams in the world, is built on the Bhagirathi near Tehri, and its reservoir submerges the old town of Tehri. Other projects include the Koteshwar Dam downstream. Hydroelectric development on the river has been controversial because of seismic risk, ecological concerns, and the river's religious significance; several projects were cancelled or suspended following protests, including by religious figures, and a 2013 flood disaster in the region.

Religious significance

In Hindu mythology, King Bhagiratha performed penance to bring the goddess Ganga down from heaven to earth to purify the ashes of his ancestors, the sons of King Sagara; the river is named after him. The town of Gangotri on its banks is one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites.
