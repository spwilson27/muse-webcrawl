Antizyme

Antizymes are a family of small proteins that regulate cellular polyamine levels. Ornithine decarboxylase antizyme 1 (OAZ1) binds to ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) — the rate-limiting enzyme of polyamine biosynthesis — and targets it for degradation by the 26S proteasome in a ubiquitin-independent manner, a rare mechanism in protein turnover.

Regulation

Antizyme synthesis is itself controlled by polyamines through a distinctive mechanism: high polyamine levels stimulate a +1 ribosomal frameshift in the OAZ1 mRNA, allowing production of the full-length functional protein. Antizyme also inhibits the cellular uptake of polyamines, providing dual feedback control. A related protein, antizyme inhibitor (AZIN1), binds antizyme more tightly than ODC does and can reactivate ODC; AZIN1 editing and overexpression have been linked to several cancers.

Family members

Vertebrates possess multiple antizymes (OAZ1, OAZ2, and the testis-specific OAZ3), with roles extending beyond polyamine control, including interactions with proteins involved in cell-cycle regulation.
