Battle of Harlaw

The Battle of Harlaw was fought on 24 July 1411 near Inverurie in Aberdeenshire, Scotland. It was one of the bloodiest battles on Scottish soil, between Highland and Islands forces of Donald of Islay, Lord of the Isles, and a Lowland army led by Alexander Stewart, Earl of Mar.

Background

Donald claimed the Earldom of Ross through his wife Mariota Leslie. When the earldom was granted instead within the regent Albany's family, Donald raised a large host — tradition says 10,000 men — from the Isles and Ross and marched toward Aberdeen to enforce his claim by fire and sword.

Battle

Mar met him at Harlaw with knights, men-at-arms and burgesses of Aberdeen, including Provost Robert Davidson, who was killed. The Highland charge inflicted heavy losses but Mar's armoured men held. Fighting lasted until nightfall with perhaps 1,000 dead on each side in traditional accounts — earning it the name "Red Harlaw". Donald withdrew to the Isles.

Aftermath

Neither side won a clear victory, but Donald's advance was stopped and he later submitted and gave up his claim for the time being. The battle lives on in ballads such as "The Battle of Harlaw" and in Aberdeen's civic memory.
