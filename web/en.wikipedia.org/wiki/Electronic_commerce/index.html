Electronic commerce

Electronic commerce (or e-commerce) is the activity of electronically buying or selling products on online services or over the Internet. E-commerce draws on technologies such as mobile commerce, electronic funds transfer, supply chain management, Internet marketing, online transaction processing, electronic data interchange (EDI), inventory management systems, and automated data collection.

Forms

E-commerce is commonly classified by the parties involved: business-to-consumer (B2C), as in online retail stores such as Amazon and Alibaba; business-to-business (B2B); consumer-to-consumer (C2C), as on eBay and marketplace platforms; and consumer-to-business (C2B). Modern e-commerce spans online storefronts, marketplaces, social commerce, subscription services, and digital distribution of media.

History

Precursors include EDI over value-added networks in the 1970s and early teleshopping systems. Online shopping was pioneered by Michael Aldrich in 1979, and the field took off with the World Wide Web: the first secure retail transaction over the web is often dated to 1994, and Amazon and eBay both launched in 1995. Growth accelerated with broadband, smartphones, and digital payments such as PayPal, Alipay, and mobile wallets.

Scale and impact

Global e-commerce sales amount to several trillion US dollars annually; China is the largest market, with online sales exceeding a quarter of total retail. E-commerce has reshaped logistics, retail employment, advertising, and consumer behaviour, and is regulated through consumer-protection, taxation, and data-protection law in most jurisdictions.
