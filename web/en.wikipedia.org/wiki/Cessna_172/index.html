Cessna 172

The Cessna 172 Skyhawk is an American four-seat, single-engine, high-wing fixed-wing aircraft made by the Cessna Aircraft Company. First flown in 1955, it is the most successful aircraft in history measured by units produced, with more than 44,000 built. It remains in production today and is the most popular flight-training aircraft in the world.

Development

The 172 was developed from the earlier Cessna 170, replacing its tailwheel undercarriage with a tricycle gear and introducing a squared-off vertical fin. Early models used a 145 hp Continental O-300 engine; later variants adopted Lycoming engines of up to 180 hp. Production was interrupted in 1986 amid the US general-aviation slump and resumed in 1996 as the 172R Skyhawk, followed by the fuel-injected 172S Skyhawk SP, which features a glass cockpit with Garmin G1000 avionics in recent production.

Operational history

The 172 is used worldwide for flight training, personal flying, aerial photography and light utility work. A Cessna 172 flown by Robert Timm and John Cook holds the world endurance record: in 1958–59 they remained airborne for 64 days, 22 hours and 19 minutes over the Nevada desert, refuelling from a truck driving beneath them. In 1987 the German teenager Mathias Rust flew a rented Cessna 172 from Helsinki to Moscow, landing near Red Square and penetrating Soviet air defences.

Variants

Numerous variants include the Reims-built F172 in France, the retractable-gear 172RG Cutlass, the floatplane versions on amphibious floats and the military T-41 Mescalero trainer used by the US Air Force and Army.
