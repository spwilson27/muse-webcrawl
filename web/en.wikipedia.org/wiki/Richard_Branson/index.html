Richard Branson

Sir Richard Charles Nicholas Branson
Born: 18 July 1950, Blackheath, London
Occupation: Businessman, author
Known for: Founding the Virgin Group

Sir Richard Charles Nicholas Branson (born 18 July 1950) is a British business magnate, investor and author who co-founded the Virgin Group in the 1970s, which today controls more than 400 companies in various fields. He is one of Britain's best-known entrepreneurs, famous also for record-breaking balloon flights and his spaceflight venture.

Early ventures

Branson, who has dyslexia and left school at sixteen, began with a student magazine and a mail-order record business. He opened a chain of record stores, Virgin Records, in 1972, and launched the Virgin Records label, whose first release, Mike Oldfield's Tubular Bells (1973), was a huge success. The label later signed the Sex Pistols and many major acts; Branson sold it to Thorn EMI in 1992 for about $1 billion.

Virgin Group

Branson expanded the Virgin brand into airlines (Virgin Atlantic, founded 1984), rail (Virgin Trains), telecommunications (Virgin Mobile), health clubs, finance and many other sectors, licensing the Virgin name widely. His space tourism company Virgin Galactic flew Branson himself to the edge of space aboard VSS Unity in July 2021, days ahead of Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin flight.

Adventures and personal life

Branson made several attempts on world records in boats and hot-air balloons, including the first transatlantic balloon crossing in 1987 with Per Lindstrand. Knighted in 2000 for services to entrepreneurship, he lives much of the time on Necker Island in the British Virgin Islands. He has written several bestselling autobiographies, including Losing My Virginity (1998).
