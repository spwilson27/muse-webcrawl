Exonumia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Exonumia is the study and collecting of coin-like objects that are not, or were not, legal tender currency — items such as tokens, medals, medallions, scrip, badges, encased postage stamps, elongations and other numismatic paraphernalia. The word was coined in the mid-20th century from "exo-" (outside) and "numia" (of numismatics); collectors of such material are called exonumists. In British usage the field overlaps with paranumismatica.

Categories

Major categories include:

  • Tokens — privately issued substitutes for coinage, from 17th–19th-century British Conder tokens and American Civil War tokens and Hard Times tokens, to transit tokens, subway and bus fare tokens, telephone tokens, and casino chips.
  • Medals — commemorative, prize, campaign and religious medals, from Renaissance portrait medals to modern challenge coins used by military units.
  • Scrip — company-issued money such as coal-company and lumber scrip redeemable only at the company store.
  • Merchant and advertising pieces — good-for tokens, encased coins, elongated "pressed pennies", wooden nickels and store cards.
  • Badges, fobs and membership pieces, tax tokens, ration tokens and love tokens engraved on smoothed coins.

Collecting

Exonumia appeals to collectors interested in local history, advertising art and social history, since tokens typically name a specific merchant, town and trade. The field is supported by specialist societies — notably the Token and Medal Society (TAMS), founded in the United States in 1960 — and by catalogues that organise material by issuer, locality and type. Because most exonumia was produced in small quantities and spent hard or thrown away, survival rates can be low, and rare local tokens can be as valuable to specialists as official coinage.
