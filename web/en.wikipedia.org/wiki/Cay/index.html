Cay

A cay (pronounced /kiː/ "key"), also spelled caye or key, is a small, low-elevation, sandy island on the surface of a coral reef. Cays occur in tropical environments throughout the Pacific, Atlantic and Indian Oceans, including in the Caribbean and on the Great Barrier Reef and Belize Barrier Reef.

Formation

Cays form when ocean currents transport loose sediment across a reef flat, where the current slows and deposits sand and gravel. Vegetation may take root as the island stabilises, and guano from seabirds enriches the soil. Because they consist of loose sediment, cays can shift, grow or erode significantly during tropical storms and hurricanes.

Ecology and use

Vegetated cays provide nesting habitat for seabirds and sea turtles. Some cays support small tourist resorts or fishing camps; famous examples include Heron Island on the Great Barrier Reef and the Florida Keys, where "key" is the standard American spelling, as in Key West.
