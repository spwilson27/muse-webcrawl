DigiCert

Type: Private
Industry: Internet security
Founded: 2003
Headquarters: Lehi, Utah, United States

DigiCert, Inc. is an American digital security company headquartered in Lehi, Utah, with offices around the world. It is a certificate authority (CA) that issues TLS/SSL certificates and provides public key infrastructure (PKI) and validation services for websites, connected devices, and software code signing. DigiCert is one of the largest certificate authorities in the world.

History

DigiCert was founded in 2003 by Ken Bretschneider. In 2015, private equity firm Thoma Bravo acquired a majority stake in the company. In 2017, DigiCert acquired Symantec's Website Security and related PKI solutions business — including the Symantec, GeoTrust, Thawte, VeriSign and RapidSSL brands — for about $950 million plus stock, after browsers began distrusting Symantec-issued certificates. The acquisition made DigiCert the provider of roughly a third of the world's TLS certificates at the time.

Acquisitions and expansion

Subsequent acquisitions included QuoVadis (2019), Mocana (2022), and Vercara (formerly Neustar's security services, 2024). DigiCert has been involved in developing standards through the CA/Browser Forum and in work on post-quantum cryptography readiness.

Controversies

In 2024, DigiCert announced the mass revocation of more than 80,000 certificates after discovering a bug in its domain control validation process, requiring affected customers to reissue certificates on short notice. The episode drew attention to the operational weight of the CA/Browser Forum's 24-hour-to-five-day revocation timelines for validation errors.
