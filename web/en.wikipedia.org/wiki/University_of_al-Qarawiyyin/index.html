University of al-Qarawiyyin

The University of al-Qarawiyyin (Arabic: جامعة القرويين), also written Al-Karaouine, is a university in Fez, Morocco. Founded as a mosque by Fatima al-Fihri in 857–859, it incorporated a madrasa that UNESCO and Guinness World Records describe as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution in the world.

History

Fatima al-Fihri, daughter of a wealthy merchant from Kairouan (hence Qarawiyyin, 'of the Kairouanese'), endowed the mosque and school with her inheritance. Scholars including Ibn Khaldun and Leo Africanus studied or taught there; its library, one of the world's oldest, holds manuscripts over a thousand years old.

Education

Teaching traditionally centred on Islamic law (Maliki fiqh), theology, Arabic grammar, logic, mathematics, and astronomy, with students seated around a scholar. It became a state university in 1963 and today focuses on religious and legal sciences, with degrees in modern formats.
