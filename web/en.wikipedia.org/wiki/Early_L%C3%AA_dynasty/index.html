The Early Lê dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Tiền Lê), sometimes called the Former Lê dynasty, was a dynasty of Vietnam that ruled from 980 to 1009. It followed the Đinh dynasty and was succeeded by the Lý dynasty. The dynasty was founded by Lê Hoàn, a general who took the throne after the death of Emperor Đinh Tiên Hoàng.

History

Lê Hoàn rose to power as commander-in-chief (Thập Đạo tướng quân) under the Đinh dynasty. When Emperor Đinh Tiên Hoàng and his heir were assassinated in 979, the child emperor Đinh Toàn ascended the throne, and Lê Hoàn became regent. Facing an invasion by China's Song dynasty in 980, the court — led by Empress Dowager Dương Vân Nga — offered the throne to Lê Hoàn, who accepted and was crowned emperor.

As Emperor Lê Đại Hành, he defeated the Song invasion at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 981 and also campaigned successfully against Champa in the south, sacking its capital Indrapura. His reign consolidated the independence of the Vietnamese state, known at the time as Đại Cồ Việt.

Later reign and fall

Lê Đại Hành died in 1005 and was succeeded by his son Lê Trung Tông, who was murdered after only three days by his brother Lê Long Đĩnh. Long Đĩnh's reign was notorious for cruelty and debauchery, and when he died in 1009 the court official Lý Công Uẩn was elevated to the throne, founding the Lý dynasty.
