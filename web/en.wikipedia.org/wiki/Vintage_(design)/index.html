Vintage (design)

Vintage, in the context of design, fashion, and material culture, refers to items that originate from a previous era and are valued for their quality, craftsmanship, rarity, or representative style. The word derives from the French vendange (grape harvest) and was borrowed from winemaking, where a vintage denotes the year in which a wine's grapes were harvested.

Definition and distinctions

There is no universally fixed age threshold, but vintage items are commonly at least 20 to 30 years old. An item over 100 years old is generally classed as an antique, while "retro" usually describes new items deliberately made in an older style rather than genuinely old objects. Vintage clothing in particular became fashionable from the 1990s onward, as consumers and designers sought out distinctive garments from the 1920s through the 1980s.

Markets and culture

Vintage goods are traded through specialist shops, flea markets, estate sales, auctions, and online platforms. Demand is driven by sustainability concerns, nostalgia, and the perceived durability of older manufacturing. Organized scenes exist around vintage automobiles, watches, cameras, furniture (notably mid-century modern), and typography and graphic design, with prices for rare pieces reaching large sums.
