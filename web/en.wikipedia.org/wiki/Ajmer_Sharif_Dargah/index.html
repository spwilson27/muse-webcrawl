The Ajmer Sharif Dargah is a Sufi shrine (dargah) of the revered Sufi saint Muin al-Din Chishti, known as Khwaja Gharib Nawaz ("Benefactor of the Poor"), located at Ajmer in Rajasthan, India. It is one of the most important pilgrimage sites of Sufism in South Asia and attracts devotees of all faiths.

History

Moinuddin Chishti, founder of the Chishti order in India, settled in Ajmer in the early 1190s and died there in 1236. His tomb became a place of pilgrimage; the shrine complex grew over the centuries with patronage from Mughal emperors — Akbar visited repeatedly and built a mosque there, while Shah Jahan added the white marble mosque and other structures. The great gate (Buland Darwaza) is attributed to the Sultan of Malwa, and later rulers of Hyderabad and others contributed gates and cauldrons.

The complex

The tomb itself lies beneath a marble dome with a silver railing and marble screen, covered daily with rose petals by devotees. The complex contains two enormous deghs (cauldrons) used to cook sweetened rice distributed to pilgrims, mosques, courtyards and the saint's cenotaph chamber. Qawwali devotional singing is performed at the shrine.

Urs festival

The annual Urs, marking the saint's death anniversary, draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims over six days. The shrine is also famous for its tradition of communal harmony, drawing Hindu, Muslim, Sikh and other visitors alike.
