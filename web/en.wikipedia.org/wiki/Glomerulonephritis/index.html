Glomerulonephritis

Glomerulonephritis (GN) refers to a group of diseases that injure the glomeruli, the tiny filtering units of the kidney, usually through immune-mediated inflammation. It may present acutely with visible blood in the urine, swelling, and high blood pressure (a nephritic picture), with heavy protein loss causing the nephrotic syndrome, or silently, discovered only through abnormal urine tests; untreated, many forms progress to chronic kidney failure.

Causes and classification

Glomerulonephritis may be primary (confined to the kidney) or secondary to systemic disease or infection. Well-known forms include IgA nephropathy (Berger's disease), the commonest primary GN worldwide; post-streptococcal GN, a childhood illness following throat or skin infection; membranous nephropathy; minimal change disease; focal segmental glomerulosclerosis; lupus nephritis in systemic lupus erythematosus; ANCA-associated vasculitis; and anti-GBM (Goodpasture) disease, a rapidly progressive form with crescents of proliferating cells in the glomeruli. Diagnosis often requires kidney biopsy with light microscopy, immunofluorescence, and electron microscopy.

Signs and treatment

Features include haematuria (sometimes producing "cola-coloured" urine), proteinuria, oedema, hypertension, and declining kidney function measured by an elevated creatinine or reduced glomerular filtration rate. Treatment depends on the specific disease: blood-pressure control and inhibitors of the renin–angiotensin system protect the kidney in most forms, while immunosuppressive drugs such as corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide, or rituximab are used for inflammatory and rapidly progressive types. End-stage disease requires dialysis or transplantation.
