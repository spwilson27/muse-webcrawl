Qisas

Qisas (Arabic: قصاص, qiṣāṣ, "accountability", "following up after", "retribution") is an Islamic term meaning "retaliation in kind", "eye for an eye", or retributive justice. In traditional Islamic law (sharia), the doctrine of qisas provides for a punishment analogous to the crime: in cases of murder, the victim's family may demand the execution of the murderer, accept monetary compensation (diyya, blood money), or forgive the offender.

Quranic basis

The Quran prescribes qisas for murder (2:178–179) while encouraging forgiveness and compensation as preferable. Classical jurisprudence elaborated detailed rules about intent, equality of status, and the rights of the victim's heirs (wali al-dam), who may choose retaliation, compensation, or pardon.

Modern law

Qisas remains part of the penal law of several countries, including Saudi Arabia and Iran, where it applies to murder and intentional bodily injury; in Iranian law the victim or family may demand exact retribution, accept diyya, or forgive. Its application — for example, blinding sentences for acid attacks — has drawn international attention and criticism from human rights organisations.
