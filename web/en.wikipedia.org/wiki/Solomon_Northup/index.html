Solomon Northup (born July 10, 1807 or 1808; died c. 1864) was an American abolitionist and the primary author of the memoir Twelve Years a Slave. A free-born African American from New York, he was the son of a freed slave and free woman of color. A farmer and a professional violinist, Northup had been a landowner in Washington County, New York. In 1841, he was offered a traveling musician's job and went to Washington, D.C. (where slavery was legal); there he was drugged, kidnapped, and sold as a slave. He was shipped to New Orleans and sold to a planter in Louisiana's Red River region, where he was held in bondage for 12 years until he was able to secure messages to his family and friends in New York, who invoked a state law protecting free citizens, and secured his release on January 3, 1853.

Twelve Years a Slave

With editor David Wilson, Northup published his memoir the year of his release. It became a bestseller and a key text of the abolitionist movement, giving a detailed first-person account of the domestic slave trade and plantation life in Louisiana. His owners included William Ford, John Tibeats, and Edwin Epps, on whose cotton plantation Northup spent ten years.

Later life and legacy

After regaining freedom, Northup lectured for abolitionist causes, and helped prosecute — unsuccessfully — his kidnappers. His later life and the date and place of his death are uncertain; he may have died around 1864. The memoir was adapted into the 2013 film 12 Years a Slave, directed by Steve McQueen, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.
