Ultra-high-definition television

Ultra HD (UHDTV)
Resolutions4K UHD: 3840 × 2160; 8K UHD: 7680 × 4320
Aspect ratio16:9
StandardITU-R Recommendation BT.2020 / BT.2100
PredecessorHigh-definition television (HDTV)
FeaturesWide colour gamut, high dynamic range (HDR), higher frame rates

Ultra-high-definition television (UHDTV), also known as Ultra HD or UHD, comprises two digital video formats defined by ITU-R Recommendation BT.2020: 4K UHD (2160p), with a resolution of 3840 × 2160 pixels, four times that of 1080p HDTV; and 8K UHD (4320p), at 7680 × 4320 pixels, sixteen times 1080p.

Resolution comparison: 8K UHD, 4K UHD, Full HD and standard definition.

History

Japan's NHK began research into ultra-high-definition systems ("Super Hi-Vision") in the 1990s and demonstrated experimental 8K systems in the 2000s. Consumer 4K televisions appeared from 2012–13, and the Ultra HD Blu-ray format launched in 2016. Streaming services adopted 4K widely in the late 2010s; 8K consumer sets followed from 2018, though native 8K content remains scarce.

Technical features

Beyond resolution, the UHD standard introduced the Rec. 2020 wide colour gamut, support for frame rates up to 120 Hz, and later, via BT.2100, high dynamic range using the PQ and HLG transfer functions. Consumer branding such as "4K Ultra HD" certification requires these features in combination, along with HDCP 2.2-protected HDMI 2.0 (or later) inputs.

Adoption

By the early 2020s, 4K sets accounted for the majority of new television sales in major markets, and most new scripted streaming content is produced in 4K HDR. Broadcast UHD channels exist in several countries, particularly for sport, but most terrestrial broadcasting remains in HD or SD.
