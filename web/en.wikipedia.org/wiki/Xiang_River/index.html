Xiang River

Length: approx. 856 km
Source: Guangxi
Mouth: Dongting Lake, Hunan
Basin: approx. 94,600 km²

The Xiang River (Chinese: 湘江; pinyin: Xiāngjiāng) is the chief river of Hunan Province in south-central China and the largest river flowing into Dongting Lake. It is one of the main tributaries of the Yangtze River system. The province's abbreviation, Xiang, derives from the river's name.

Course

The river rises in the mountains of northern Guangxi near Xing'an County, where the ancient Lingqu Canal (built under the Qin dynasty) connects it with the Li River. It flows northeast through Hunan past Yongzhou, Hengyang, Zhuzhou, Xiangtan, and the provincial capital Changsha, where Orange Isle (Juzizhou) divides its channel. North of Changsha it enters Dongting Lake, whose waters eventually join the Yangtze.

History and culture

In Chinese legend, the river goddesses Ehuang and Nüying, wives of the sage-king Shun, wept by the Xiang after his death, staining the bamboo on its banks — the origin of spotted bamboo and of the "Xiang consorts" of poetry. The Xiang Army of the 19th century took its name from the river's province. In December 1934, the Battle of Xiang River was a costly engagement for the Red Army during the Long March.

Economy

The Xiang is navigable for much of its length and has long served as a transport artery for Hunan. Its basin contains significant deposits of manganese, lead, and zinc, and its waters support intensive agriculture, including rice cultivation.
