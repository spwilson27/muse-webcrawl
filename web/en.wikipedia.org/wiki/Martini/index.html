Martini

TypeCocktail
Primary alcoholGin (or vodka)

The martini is a cocktail made with gin and vermouth, garnished with an olive or a lemon twist. Over the years it has become one of the best-known mixed alcoholic beverages. The International Bartenders Association recipe combines 60 ml of gin with 10 ml of dry vermouth, stirred with ice and strained into a chilled cocktail glass.

History and variations

The drink evolved from the 19th-century Martinez and Marguerite cocktails; by the early 20th century the modern dry style — less sweet vermouth, more gin — had taken hold, and the ratio kept drying out through the century. A martini made with vodka instead of gin is a vodka martini; ordering it "dirty" adds olive brine, and a "Gibson" is garnished with a cocktail onion. The question of whether a martini should be shaken or stirred was famously popularised by Ian Fleming's James Bond, whose preference for a "shaken, not stirred" vodka martini (the Vesper in Casino Royale) departs from bartending convention.

Cultural impact

The martini is an icon of mid-century American drinking culture, associated with the three-martini lunch, and its conical stemmed glass is among the most recognised symbols of cocktails worldwide. Numerous unrelated drinks served in the same glass are marketed as "martinis", such as the espresso martini and appletini.
