Asmara (Tigrinya: ኣስመራ, Arabic: أسمرة) is the capital and most populous city of Eritrea, in the country's Central Region. It sits at an elevation of 2,325 metres (7,628 ft), making it the sixth highest capital in the world by altitude. The city is known for its well-preserved colonial Italian modernist architecture, for which it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017 — the first in Eritrea.

History

Asmara originated from villages founded in the 12th century. It grew after Italian colonisation from the 1880s, becoming capital of Italian Eritrea in 1897. Under Italian rule, especially in the 1930s, it was rebuilt with Art Deco and modernist buildings, earning the nickname Piccola Roma. After the Eritrean War of Independence, Asmara became capital of independent Eritrea in 1991–1993 following the referendum on independence from Ethiopia.

Geography and climate

Asmara lies on the northwestern edge of the Eritrean Highlands and the Great Rift Valley escarpment. It has a cool semi-arid climate, with mild days, cold nights, and a rainy season roughly from June to September.

Architecture

Notable buildings include the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Rosary, the Fiat Tagliero Building (a futuristic petrol station shaped like an aeroplane), the Asmara Theatre, and the Great Mosque of Asmara. The UNESCO listing, “Asmara: a Modernist City of Africa,” recognised over 400 buildings.

Economy and transport

Asmara is Eritrea's industrial and cultural centre, with textiles, food processing and brewing. It is served by Asmara International Airport and historically by the Eritrean Railway to Massawa on the Red Sea.
