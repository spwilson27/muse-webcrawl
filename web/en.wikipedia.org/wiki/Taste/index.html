Taste

SystemGustatory system
OrgansTongue, taste buds
Basic tastesSweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami

The gustatory system or sense of taste is the sensory system that is partially responsible for the perception of taste. Taste is the perception stimulated when a substance in the mouth reacts chemically with taste receptor cells located on taste buds in the oral cavity, mostly on the tongue. Taste, along with olfaction and trigeminal nerve stimulation (registering texture, pain and temperature), determines the flavor of food and beverages.

Basic tastes

Five basic tastes are widely recognized: sweetness, sourness, saltiness, bitterness and umami (savory). Sweetness is produced by sugars and related compounds; sourness by acids; saltiness chiefly by sodium ions; bitterness by a large family of chemically diverse compounds detected by the TAS2R receptors; and umami by glutamates and aspartates, the taste of foods such as meat broths and aged cheese. Research continues into further candidates, including fat taste ("oleogustus") and kokumi.

Anatomy and physiology

Taste buds are found mainly within papillae on the tongue—fungiform, foliate and circumvallate papillae—and also on the soft palate, epiglottis and upper esophagus. Each bud contains 50–100 receptor cells. Signals travel via the facial (VII), glossopharyngeal (IX) and vagus (X) cranial nerves to the nucleus of the solitary tract, then to the thalamus and the gustatory cortex. The familiar "tongue map" assigning different tastes to different regions is a misconception: all taste qualities can be sensed across the tongue, though sensitivity varies slightly.

Function

The taste system serves as a gatekeeper for ingestion: sweetness signals energy-rich carbohydrates, saltiness helps regulate electrolyte balance, umami indicates protein, while intense sourness and bitterness warn of unripe, spoiled or potentially toxic foods. Taste preferences are shaped by genetics (for example, supertasters who are highly sensitive to the bitter compound PROP), age, learning and culture.

Disorders

Taste disorders include ageusia (complete loss), hypogeusia (reduced sensitivity) and dysgeusia (distorted taste). Causes range from viral infections, including COVID-19, to medications, zinc deficiency and nerve damage.
