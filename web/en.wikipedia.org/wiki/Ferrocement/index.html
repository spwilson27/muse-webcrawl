Ferrocement

Ferrocement (also called ferro-cement) is a construction material consisting of wire mesh and cement mortar, applied in thin layers over a framework of metal rods to form a strong, lightweight shell. It differs from conventional reinforced concrete in the density and distribution of its reinforcement and its much smaller thickness.

History

The technique originated in France in the 1840s with Joseph-Louis Lambot, who built ferrocement boats and patented the material as "ferciment"; it was later championed by Italian engineer Pier Luigi Nervi, whose ferrocement yachts and buildings demonstrated its strength. Ferrocement enjoyed a boat-building boom in the 1960s and 1970s because amateurs could build large hulls cheaply.

Applications

Besides boats, ferrocement is used for water tanks, roofs, silos, and low-cost housing, especially in developing countries, because it uses little material and needs modest skills and equipment. Its disadvantages include labor intensity and vulnerability to corrosion of the mesh if poorly covered.
