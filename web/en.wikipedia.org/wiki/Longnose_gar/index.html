The longnose gar (Lepisosteus osseus) is a species of gar in the family Lepisosteidae. It is found along the east coast of North and Central America in freshwater lakes and as far west as Kansas and Texas and the northern parts of Mexico. It is distinguished from other gars by its extremely long, narrow snout, which is more than twice as long as the rest of the head.

Description

Longnose gar are typically 60–100 cm (24–39 in) long, though specimens approaching 2 m have been recorded. The body is olive-brown, covered in hard, interlocking ganoid scales, and the beak is lined with sharp conical teeth suited to gripping fish. Like other gars, it has a vascularised swim bladder that allows it to breathe air, letting it survive in warm, low-oxygen waters.

Ecology

Longnose gar are ambush predators of fish such as shad, sunfish and catfish, hunting mostly at night in slow-moving rivers, lakes and estuaries. They spawn in spring over gravel or vegetation in shallow water. Their eggs are adhesive and toxic to many animals. Although not usually sought as food, they are taken by bowfishers and are sometimes regarded as a nuisance species by anglers; ecologically they serve as apex predators in many of the systems they inhabit.
