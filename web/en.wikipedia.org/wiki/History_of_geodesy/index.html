History of geodesy

DisciplineGeodesy
Key milestonesEratosthenes' measurement; meridian arcs; satellite geodesy

The history of geodesy is the history of measuring the Earth's size, shape and gravity field, from ancient estimates of its circumference to modern satellite techniques.

Antiquity to the Enlightenment

Eratosthenes (3rd century BCE) estimated Earth's circumference from shadow angles at Syene and Alexandria with remarkable accuracy. Posidonius and later Islamic astronomers refined the figure. In 1617 Willebrord Snell introduced triangulation; French expeditions to Peru and Lapland (1730s) confirmed Newton's prediction that Earth is an oblate spheroid.

The classical era

Nineteenth-century geodesists — Gauss, Bessel, Clarke — computed reference ellipsoids from arc measurements and invented least-squares adjustment; national surveys built triangulation networks worldwide. Gravimetry added knowledge of the geoid, and Airy and Pratt proposed isostasy.

Satellite geodesy

From the 1960s, satellite Doppler, laser ranging, VLBI, GPS/GNSS and dedicated gravity missions (GRACE, GOCE) yielded centimetre-level positions, plate-motion measurements and time-varying gravity fields, making geodesy central to Earth science.
