The English Folk Dance and Song Society (EFDSS) is an organisation dedicated to promoting English folk dance, music and song. It was formed in 1932 by the merger of the Folk-Song Society (founded 1898) and the English Folk Dance Society (founded 1911 by Cecil Sharp). Its headquarters is Cecil Sharp House in Camden Town, London, which also houses the Vaughan Williams Memorial Library, the principal folk archive in England.

History

Cecil Sharp began collecting folk songs in Somerset in 1903 and founded the English Folk Dance Society in 1911 to teach and promote morris dancing, sword dancing and country dancing. The Folk-Song Society, founded in 1898, had published a journal of collected songs. Following Sharp's death in 1924, the two bodies merged in 1932. Cecil Sharp House, built in 1930, was the first purpose-built headquarters for a folk organisation in England and was badly damaged by bombing in 1940, after which it was restored.

Activities

The society publishes the Folk Music Journal and the English Dance and Song magazine, runs courses, dances and festivals, supports folk artists through bursaries and showcases, and maintains the Vaughan Williams Memorial Library, named after the composer Ralph Vaughan Williams, a former president of the society. It organises the annual programme of concerts, ceilidhs and education projects at Cecil Sharp House and works with schools and community groups across England.
