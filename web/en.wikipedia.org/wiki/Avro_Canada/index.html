Avro Canada

Founded: 1945
Defunct: 1962
Headquarters: Malton, Ontario
Notable products: CF-100 Canuck, CF-105 Arrow, C102 Jetliner

A.V. Roe Canada, commonly known as Avro Canada, was a Canadian aircraft manufacturer founded in 1945 when Hawker Siddeley acquired the Victory Aircraft plant in Malton, Ontario. It became one of Canada's largest industrial enterprises during the early Cold War.

Products

Avro Canada produced the CF-100 Canuck all-weather interceptor, the only Canadian-designed fighter to reach mass production; the C102 Jetliner, one of the world's first jet airliners; and the CF-105 Arrow, an advanced supersonic interceptor cancelled by the Diefenbaker government on 20 February 1959 — a date remembered as "Black Friday" in Canadian aviation. The company also built the Orenda jet engine and experimented with the VZ-9 Avrocar flying saucer.

Closure

After the Arrow cancellation, the company declined rapidly and its operations were wound down by 1962. Many of its engineers later joined NASA and other aerospace programmes.
