Richard Allen (bishop)

Richard Allen (February 14, 1760 – March 26, 1831) was an American minister, educator and writer, and the founder of the African Methodist Episcopal Church (AME), the first independent Black denomination in the United States. Born into slavery in Delaware, he purchased his freedom, became a Methodist preacher, and in 1816 was elected the AME Church's first bishop.

Early life

Allen was born enslaved and was sold with his family to a Delaware farmer, Stokely Sturgis. Converted to Methodism as a teenager, he taught himself to read and write, worked extra jobs, and bought his freedom in 1780 for ,000 in Continental money. He took the surname Allen and travelled as an itinerant preacher.

Philadelphia and Mother Bethel

In 1786 Allen began preaching at St. George's Methodist Episcopal Church in Philadelphia, where Black membership grew. After Black worshippers were pulled from their knees during prayer and segregated in a new gallery, Allen and Absalom Jones led a walkout in 1792. Allen founded the Bethel African Methodist Episcopal Church in 1794 in a converted blacksmith's shop — "Mother Bethel."

The AME Church

In April 1816, Black Methodist congregations from several cities met in Philadelphia and organised the African Methodist Episcopal Church, electing Allen its first bishop. He was also a co-founder of the Free African Society (1787), helped organise relief during the 1793 yellow fever epidemic, and opposed colonisation schemes, advocating full citizenship for African Americans.
