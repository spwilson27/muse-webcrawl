Camille Jordan

Marie Ennemond Camille Jordan (1838–1922).

Marie Ennemond Camille Jordan (5 January 1838 – 22 January 1922) was a French mathematician, known both for his foundational work in group theory and for his influential textbook Cours d'analyse, which reshaped the teaching of mathematical analysis. His name is attached to several fundamental concepts in algebra, topology and measure theory.

Career

Jordan studied at the École Polytechnique and first worked as an engineer, publishing mathematics in his spare time. His 1870 Traité des substitutions et des équations algébriques gave the first comprehensive account of Galois theory and of permutation groups, and introduced composition series and the theorem now called the Jordan–Hölder theorem. He later held chairs at the École Polytechnique and the Collège de France, and in 1881 was elected to the French Academy of Sciences.

Contributions

  • Jordan normal form — the canonical block-diagonal form of a linear operator over an algebraically closed field.
  • Jordan curve theorem — the statement that a simple closed curve divides the plane into an interior and an exterior; Jordan's 1887 proof, long and subtle, was the first rigorous one.
  • Jordan measure — an early finitely additive notion of content for sets in the plane and space.
  • Jordan's lemma and the Jordan totient function also bear his name, as does the characterisation of finite linear groups in his work on the classification of equations.
