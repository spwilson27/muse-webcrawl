Argolic Gulf

View over the Argolic Gulf from the Palamidi fortress at Nafplio.

The Argolic Gulf (Greek: Αργολικός κόλπος), also known as the Gulf of Argolis or Gulf of Nafplio, is a gulf of the Aegean Sea off the east coast of the Peloponnese, Greece. It is about 50 km long and up to 30 km wide. Its chief port is Nafplio, at its northwestern end; the town of Argos lies a short distance inland. The beaches at Tolo, on the west shore, and the village of Astros are summer resorts.

Geography

The gulf is bounded to the west by the mountainous Argolid peninsula's eastern shore and the Arcadian coast, and to the east by the rugged Argolic (Epidaurus) peninsula. The rivers flowing into its head include the Inachos. In antiquity its shores hosted important Mycenaean and classical sites, among them Tiryns, the sanctuary of Asclepius at Epidaurus across the peninsula, and Lerna, one of the oldest settled sites in Greece. Islands in and near the gulf include Spetses and Hydra near its mouth.

History

The gulf was a maritime heartland of Bronze Age Greece: Mycenae's outlet to the sea. In the War of Greek Independence, Nafplio became the first capital of modern Greece (1828–1834) and the gulf saw naval operations against Ottoman-Egyptian forces. Today the region is a popular destination for sailing, with marinas at Nafplio, Tolo and Porto Cheli nearby.
