Zork is a series of interactive fiction (text adventure) computer games. The original game, written in 1977–1979 by Tim Anderson, Marc Blank, Dave Lebling and Bruce Daniels at the MIT Dynamic Modelling Group, ran on a PDP-10 mainframe and was inspired by Colossal Cave Adventure. The authors went on to found Infocom and split the game into a trilogy for home computers: Zork I: The Great Underground Empire (1980), Zork II: The Wizard of Frobozz (1981), and Zork III: The Dungeon Master (1982).

Gameplay

The player explores the ruins of the Great Underground Empire, collecting treasures and solving puzzles by typing natural-language commands ("take lamp", "kill troll with sword") interpreted by a sophisticated parser for its time. Familiar elements include the brass lantern, the elvish sword that glows in the presence of danger, the jewelled egg, the leaflet, the infamous grue — creatures that devour adventurers in darkness — and the greeting "Hello, sailor". The games were implemented in a portable virtual machine (the Z-machine) using the ZIL language, allowing Infocom to publish them on many platforms.

Sequels and legacy

Further titles followed, including Enchanter (1983) and its sequels, Beyond Zork (1987), Zork Zero (1988), and, after Infocom's acquisition by Activision, graphical entries: Return to Zork (1993), Zork Nemesis (1996) and Zork: Grand Inquisitor (1997), plus the online Legends of Zork (2009). Zork is consistently cited among the most influential video games ever made; the original mainframe version and the trilogy are preserved and playable, and the games' source code has been released for historical study. The phrase "It is pitch black. You are likely to be eaten by a grue" has become a long-running cultural reference.
