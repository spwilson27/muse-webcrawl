Thomas Keating

Thomas Keating (7 March 1923 – 25 October 2018) was an American Trappist monk and priest of the Cistercian Order of the Strict Observance, known as one of the principal architects of Centering Prayer, a method of contemplative prayer that emerged from the monastic milieu of Vatican II.

Life

Born in New York City, Keating attended Deerfield Academy and studied at Yale and Fordham before entering the Trappist Abbey of Our Lady of the Valley in Rhode Island in 1944. He was ordained a priest in 1949. In 1958 he became superior of St. Benedict's Monastery in Snowmass, Colorado, and in 1961 was elected abbot of St. Joseph's Abbey in Spencer, Massachusetts, serving until 1981.

Centering Prayer and Contemplative Outreach

At St. Joseph's, Keating, together with William Meninger and M. Basil Pennington, developed Centering Prayer, drawing on the anonymous fourteenth-century classic The Cloud of Unknowing and the teaching of John Cassian. In 1984 he co-founded Contemplative Outreach to teach the practice internationally. His books include Open Mind, Open Heart (1986), The Mystery of Christ and Invitation to Love. Keating was also prominent in interreligious dialogue, hosting the Snowmass Conferences of contemplatives from world religions.

Death

He retired to St. Benedict's Monastery in Snowmass and died in 2018, aged 95.
