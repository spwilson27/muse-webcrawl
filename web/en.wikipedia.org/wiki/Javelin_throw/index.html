Javelin throw

Javelin throw
World record (men): 98.48 m, Jan Železný (1996)
World record (women): 72.28 m, Barbora Špotáková (2008)

The javelin throw is a track and field event in which an athlete throws a javelin — a spear-like implement — as far as possible. It has been part of the Olympic Games since 1908 for men and 1932 for women.

Technique and rules

The thrower runs down a runway about 30 m long and releases the javelin before the foul line, gripping it at the cord in the centre of gravity. The throw is measured from the foul line to where the tip first strikes the ground within the sector. In the 1980s, distances had grown so long that the men's javelin was redesigned in 1986 (and the women's in 1999) to move the centre of gravity forward, shortening throws and ensuring the javelin lands tip-first.

Records

Jan Železný of the Czech Republic set the current men's world record of 98.48 m in 1996; Barbora Špotáková holds the women's record of 72.28 m from 2008. Uwe Hohn's 104.80 m throw in 1984 with the old design remains the longest ever.
