Poe's law

Poe's law is an adage of Internet culture stating that, without a clear indicator of the author's intent, it is impossible to create a parody of extreme views so obviously exaggerated that it cannot be mistaken by some readers for a sincere expression of the views being parodied.

Origin

The law is attributed to Nathan Poe, who formulated it in 2005 during an online debate about creationism on the message board christianforums.com. Poe wrote: "Without a winking smiley or other blatant display of humor, it is utterly impossible to parody a Creationist in such a way that someone won't mistake it for the genuine article." The observation echoed longstanding difficulties with satire, but the Internet version emphasises the absence of tone cues in text and the size and diversity of online audiences.

Implications

Poe's law is frequently invoked in discussions of online extremism, trolling, and irony, where genuinely held beliefs and parodies of them become indistinguishable. It is related to, and sometimes cited alongside, the concept of "Poe" as a noun: a person whose stated views cannot be determined to be sincere or parodic.
