Sam Nzima

Sam Nzima (8 August 1934 – 12 May 2018) was a South African photographer best known for taking the iconic photograph of Hector Pieterson during the Soweto uprising of 16 June 1976. The image, showing the mortally wounded Pieterson being carried by Mbuyisa Makhubo with his sister Antoinette Sithole running beside them, became one of the most famous photographs of the apartheid era and helped turn international opinion against the South African government.

Early life

Nzima was born in Lilydale, a village in what is now Mpumalanga province. He developed an interest in photography as a schoolboy, borrowing a teacher's camera and taking portraits of his classmates. After a period working in Johannesburg, he became a photojournalist.

Career

Nzima joined the newspaper The World as a photographer. On 16 June 1976, students in Soweto marched in protest against the enforced use of Afrikaans as a medium of instruction in schools. Police opened fire on the marchers, and Nzima captured the moment when 12-year-old Hector Pieterson, who had been shot, was carried away by fellow student Mbuyisa Makhubo. Nzima hid the film in his socks to prevent it from being confiscated by police, and the photograph was published the following day.

The publication of the photograph placed Nzima under intense police harassment. He fled Johannesburg, and The World was banned in 1977. Nzima returned to his home region and for many years ran a liquor store, largely leaving photography. In later years he received recognition for his work and campaigned for copyright acknowledgement and compensation for the image, which had been reproduced worldwide, often without his permission.

Death and legacy

Nzima died on 12 May 2018 in a hospital in Nelspruit (Mbombela), aged 83. The Hector Pieterson photograph is preserved at the Hector Pieterson Museum in Soweto and remains a central symbol of resistance to apartheid.
