Rudd Concession

The Rudd Concession was a written concession for exclusive mining rights in Matabeleland, Mashonaland and other adjoining territories in what is now Zimbabwe. It was granted by King Lobengula of the Ndebele to Charles Rudd, James Rochfort Maguire and Francis Thompson, acting for Cecil Rhodes's interests, on 30 October 1888.

Terms

In exchange for 1,000 Martini-Henry rifles, 100,000 rounds of ammunition, a monthly payment of £100, and a gunboat on the Zambezi (never delivered), the grantees received the right to 'do all things necessary' to win minerals, with a promise — made orally by missionary-interpreter Charles Helm — that no more than ten white men would enter the country. The written text contained no such limit.

Consequences

Rhodes used the concession to obtain a royal charter for the British South Africa Company (1889). Lobengula, realising he had been deceived, repudiated it and sent envoys to Queen Victoria, but the Pioneer Column occupied Mashonaland in 1890, founding Salisbury (Harare). Conflict led to the First Matabele War (1893-94) and the fall of Lobengula, son of Mzilikazi.
