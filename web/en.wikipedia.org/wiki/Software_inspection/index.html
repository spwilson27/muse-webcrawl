Software inspection

Software inspection is a formal peer-review process for finding defects early.

Software inspection is a formal, structured peer-review technique in which a team examines a software work product — code, requirements, designs, or test plans — line by line to detect defects as early as possible. The method was defined by Michael Fagan at IBM in the 1970s and is often called the Fagan inspection. Inspections are consistently reported among the most effective defect-removal methods in software engineering, with well-run processes finding a majority of defects before testing begins, at lower cost than fixing them later.

Process

A Fagan inspection follows defined stages with assigned roles. Planning: a moderator organizes the inspection and selects participants. Overview: the author presents the product and its context. Preparation: inspectors study the material individually, using checklists and standards. Inspection meeting: the team walks through the product together, logging defects without debating solutions; the author does not defend the work, and managers are normally excluded so that the meeting is not used for appraisal. Rework: the author fixes the logged defects. Follow-up: the moderator verifies the corrections and collects process data, such as defect rates and inspection rates, for process improvement. Entry and exit criteria govern when a product is ready for inspection and when it has passed.

Related techniques

Inspection differs from less formal code reviews, walkthroughs, and technical reviews in its defined roles, checklists, data collection, and entry/exit criteria. Modern lightweight descendants include the pull-request reviews of contemporary version-control workflows, pair programming, and tool-assisted static analysis, which automate part of what human inspectors once did. Research from the 1980s onward examined optimal team size, reading techniques (such as perspective-based reading), and meeting effects, sometimes questioning whether the formal meeting adds value beyond individual preparation.
