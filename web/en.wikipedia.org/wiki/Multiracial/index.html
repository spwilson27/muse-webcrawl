Multiracial

Multiracial describes people whose ancestry derives from more than one racial or ethnic group, as those categories are socially defined. The term is used in demography, sociology, and everyday identity, particularly in countries such as the United States, Brazil, South Africa, and the United Kingdom, where census and survey instruments increasingly allow respondents to identify with more than one race.

Terminology

Related terms include mixed race, biracial (for people of two racial backgrounds), and numerous historically specific labels that arose in colonial and slaveholding societies, many of which are now considered dated or offensive. Contemporary usage favors self-identification: a person's multiracial identity is generally taken to be whatever they report.

Demographics

Multiracial populations have grown rapidly in many countries. In the United States, the 2020 census reported that the population identifying as two or more races had increased substantially since 2010, reflecting both demographic change and changes in how responses were processed. Brazil's population has long included a large proportion of people of mixed European, African, and Indigenous ancestry, and in South Africa the Coloured population forms a distinct, long-established community.

History

Mixed-ancestry populations arose wherever different peoples came into sustained contact through migration, trade, colonization, and enslavement. Legal regimes in many jurisdictions historically sought to police racial boundaries through anti-miscegenation laws, which in the United States were not struck down nationwide until Loving v. Virginia (1967).

Identity and society

Research on multiracial identity examines how individuals negotiate belonging across communities, how institutions classify them, and how experiences of discrimination or acceptance vary with appearance, family, and social context.
