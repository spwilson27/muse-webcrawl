The gaur (Bos gaurus), also known as the Indian bison, is a bovine native to South and Southeast Asia. It is the largest extant species of wild cattle: bulls stand up to about 2.2 metres at the shoulder and can weigh 1,500 kilograms or more.

Description

The gaur has a deep chestnut to blackish coat, white "stockings" on the lower legs, and a prominent muscular ridge along the back. Both sexes carry horns that grow from the sides of the head and curve upward. A distinctive ridge between the horns forms a hollowed or bossed forehead.

Distribution and behaviour

Gaur occur in fragmented populations from India and Nepal through mainland Southeast Asia to Peninsular Malaysia. They inhabit forested hills and grasslands, feed mainly by grazing and browsing, and live in herds of 8 to 20 animals led by older cows. Adults have few natural predators besides tigers.

Conservation

The species is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List, having been eliminated from much of its range by habitat loss, hunting and diseases transmitted from domestic cattle. The largest populations survive in India. A domesticated form, the gayal or mithun, is kept in northeastern India and neighbouring regions.
