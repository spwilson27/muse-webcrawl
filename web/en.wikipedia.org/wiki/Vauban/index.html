Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban (1 May 1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly referred to simply as Vauban, was a French military engineer and Marshal of France who worked under Louis XIV. He is generally considered the foremost military engineer of his age, famed for his skill in designing fortifications and his mastery of both their construction and the tactics for breaking into them. He also advised Louis on how France could feel more secure through a system of defensive rings and suggested a radical plan of fortifying France's borders into an iron belt (the pré carré), which would hold through the Wars of the French Revolution and beyond.

Early life and career

Vauban was born into a family of minor Burgundian nobility at Saint-Léger-de-Foucheret (now Saint-Léger-Vauban) in the Morvan. Orphaned at a young age and raised by a local priest, he received an education in mathematics, science and geometry. At seventeen he joined the regiment of the Prince de Condé during the Fronde, but was captured by royal troops and, impressed by his ability, was persuaded to change sides. By 1655 he had been made a royal engineer after distinguishing himself at sieges such as Sainte-Menehould and Stenay.

Siege warfare

Vauban transformed siege warfare. His method of parallels — three successive trenches dug parallel to a fortress's walls, linked by zigzag approach trenches and supported by ricochet fire — made the capture of fortresses a near-certainty given time, and greatly reduced casualties among the attackers. His first great demonstration was the siege of Maastricht in 1673, where the system was used in a fully developed form. Over his career he directed or supervised some 48 sieges, and was never defeated in one; he also fortified or improved around 300 strongholds, of which some 37 were built wholly new.

Fortifications

Vauban's fortresses adapted the bastioned trace to terrain with extraordinary subtlety: low profiles, mutually supporting bastions, outworks, covered ways and glacis, often backed by deliberate inundation defences. His masterpieces include Lille, Besançon, Belfort, Mont-Dauphin, Briançon, Neuf-Brisach and the coastal works at Dunkirk and Brest. In 2008, twelve representative sites were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Fortifications of Vauban".

Later years and writings

Created Marshal of France in 1703, Vauban spent his last years writing widely on economics, taxation and strategy. His Projet d'une dîme royale (1707), proposing a single proportional tax, was suppressed by the crown. He died in Paris on 30 March 1707. Vauban's works remain foundational in the history of military engineering, and his name is synonymous with the great age of bastioned fortification.
