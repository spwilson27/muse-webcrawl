Vigilante

A vigilante is a person who engages in vigilantism: the act of preventing, investigating and punishing perceived offenses and crimes without legal authority. A vigilante is, by definition, a person who does not work with the police or other law enforcement agencies; in the United States and other countries, such acts of vigilantism are generally illegal. The term derives from Spanish vigilante, meaning "watchman" or "guard".

History

Vigilantism has a long history, arising particularly on frontiers where formal law enforcement was weak. In the American West, vigilance committees formed in mining camps and towns such as San Francisco (1851 and 1856) to combat crime, sometimes lynching accused criminals. In the rural American South and elsewhere, vigilante groups have been associated with racial violence, including lynching and the Ku Klux Klan.

More recent examples include neighbourhood watch organisations, the Guardian Angels founded in New York City in 1979, and online "vigilantes" who expose alleged criminals on the internet. Controversial cases, such as the 1984 Bernhard Goetz subway shooting, have fuelled public debate over self-defence and extrajudicial punishment.

In popular culture

The vigilante is a stock figure in fiction, from Zorro and the Lone Ranger to comic-book superheroes such as Batman and the Punisher, and films like Death Wish (1974) and Taxi Driver (1976). These portrayals often romanticise vigilantism while raising ethical questions about justice outside the law.
