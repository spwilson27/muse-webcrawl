Guernésiais

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Guernésiais

Guernésiais, also known as Dgèrnésiais, Guernsey Norman or Guernsey French, is the variety of the Norman language spoken in Guernsey, one of the Channel Islands. It is descended from the Norman French brought to the islands in the medieval period and developed in relative isolation, retaining features lost in continental Norman and standard French.

Status

Guernésiais is severely endangered. Once the everyday language of most of the island's population, it was displaced by English during the 20th century, a process accelerated by the evacuation of Guernsey children to England during the German occupation of 1940–1945. Census figures in the early 21st century recorded only a few percent of the population — roughly 1,300 people — as speakers, most of them elderly. Revival efforts include classes, signage, literature and an annual cultural festival (Lé Viaer Marchi), and the language has gained increased official recognition alongside Jèrriais in Jersey and Auregnais in Alderney.

Features

Guernésiais differs from standard French in pronunciation, vocabulary and some grammar, preserving older Norman traits and absorbing influences from English. Its literature includes the works of the 19th-century poet George Métivier, often called the "Burns of Guernsey", who wrote extensively in the language.

Example

The Lord's Prayer and short phrases such as Bianv'nu ("welcome") are commonly used to illustrate the language. Guernsey banknotes and cultural materials have carried Guernésiais words as part of the island's identity.
