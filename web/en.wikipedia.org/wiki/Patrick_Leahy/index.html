Patrick Leahy


Born: 31 March 1940, Montpelier, Vermont, U.S.
Office: President pro tempore of the U.S. Senate (2012–2015; 2021–2023); U.S. Senator from Vermont (1975–2023)
Party: Democratic

Patrick Joseph Leahy (born 31 March 1940) is an American politician and attorney who served as a United States senator from Vermont from 1975 to 2023. A member of the Democratic Party, he was the longest-serving U.S. senator in Vermont history and, at his retirement, the third-longest-serving senator in American history. He served twice as president pro tempore of the Senate, placing him third in the line of presidential succession during those periods.

Early life and career

Leahy was born in Montpelier, Vermont, and raised in Burlington. He graduated from Saint Michael's College and earned a law degree from Georgetown University Law Center. He served as State's Attorney of Chittenden County from 1966 to 1974, gaining national attention for his prosecutorial work.

Senate career

Elected in 1974 in the post-Watergate wave at age 34, Leahy was the first Democrat ever elected to the Senate from Vermont. He chaired the Agriculture Committee and, most prominently, the Judiciary Committee, where he presided over numerous Supreme Court confirmation hearings. He was a leading voice on civil liberties, privacy, and opposition to the Iraq War, and authored the Leahy Laws conditioning U.S. military aid on human rights standards abroad. He also chaired the Appropriations Committee in his final term.

Personal life

Leahy is a noted fan of Batman and has made cameo appearances in several Batman films and voiced a character in animation; royalties were donated to charity. He announced in November 2021 that he would not seek a ninth term and retired in January 2023, succeeded by Peter Welch.
