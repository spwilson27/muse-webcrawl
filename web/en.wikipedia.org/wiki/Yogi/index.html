Yogi

A yogi in meditation.

A yogi (Sanskrit: योगी, yogī) is a practitioner of yoga, including a sannyasin or practitioner of meditation in Indian religions. The feminine form, used on occasion in English, is yogini. The term has historically been applied to advanced practitioners of spiritual disciplines centred on meditation, breath control, bodily postures and ethical observances.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Sanskrit root yuj, "to yoke" or "to unite", the same root that gives yoga itself. In classical texts a yogi was one who had attained union with the divine or with ultimate reality through sustained practice. Over the centuries the title has been used within Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism for ascetics, monks and adepts of meditative and tantric traditions alike.

Practice

Traditional yogis typically follow a regimen that combines moral disciplines (such as the yamas and niyamas of Patañjali's eight-limbed yoga), physical postures (āsana), breath regulation (prāṇāyāma) and meditation (dhyāna), directed toward liberation (mokṣa) from the cycle of rebirth. Renunciate yogis historically lived in monasteries, hermitages or wandering communities, sometimes affiliated with orders such as the Nāth tradition associated with Gorakhnāth, whose hatha yoga techniques profoundly shaped later practice.

In the modern world

In contemporary usage, especially in the West, "yogi" commonly denotes anyone who practises yoga regularly, including modern postural yoga taught in studios. It has also been applied honorifically to teachers and spiritual figures, and appears in popular culture in names such as the baseball player Yogi Berra (a nickname unrelated to practice) and the cartoon character Yogi Bear.
