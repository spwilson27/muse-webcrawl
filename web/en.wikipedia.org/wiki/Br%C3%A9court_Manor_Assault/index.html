The Brécourt Manor Assault was a successful assault by United States Army paratroopers on a German artillery position during the early hours of the Normandy landings on 6 June 1944 (D-Day). It is often cited as a classic example of a small unit attacking a larger, well-entrenched force using superior tactics.

Background

German artillery at Brécourt Manor, consisting of four 105 mm howitzers, was firing on Utah Beach and hindering the landings of the U.S. 4th Infantry Division. The guns had to be destroyed.

The assault

First Lieutenant Richard Winters, with a small force of about 13 paratroopers from E Company, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division, attacked the position, which was defended by roughly 50 German soldiers. Moving trench to trench, Winters's men destroyed all four guns using TNT blocks placed down the barrels and captured a map detailing German gun positions throughout the Cotentin Peninsula. The action is still used as a teaching case at military academies.

Legacy

The assault was portrayed in the second episode of the television miniseries Band of Brothers. Winters was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross for his leadership.
