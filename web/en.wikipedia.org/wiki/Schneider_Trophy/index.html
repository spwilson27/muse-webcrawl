Schneider Trophy

The Schneider Trophy, more fully the Coupe d'Aviation Maritime Jacques Schneider, was a trophy awarded annually (and later biennially) to the winner of a race for seaplanes and flying boats. It was first offered in 1912 by Jacques Schneider, a French financier and aircraft enthusiast, to encourage progress in water-based aviation. The races were held twelve times between 1913 and 1931.

History

Early races were won by France, Italy, and the United States. The contest became a famous rivalry between Italy, the United States, and Britain, driving rapid advances in engine power and aerodynamics. Under the rules, a country winning three consecutive races would retain the trophy permanently.

In 1931 the British team, flying the Supermarine S.6B designed by R. J. Mitchell and funded by Lady Lucy Houston after government support was withdrawn, won for the third successive time and kept the trophy outright. The S.6B's development led directly to the Rolls-Royce Merlin engine lineage and to Mitchell's Supermarine Spitfire.

Legacy

The Schneider Trophy races are credited with spurring the high-speed aircraft technology of the interwar years. The trophy itself is on display at the Science Museum in London alongside the S.6B.
