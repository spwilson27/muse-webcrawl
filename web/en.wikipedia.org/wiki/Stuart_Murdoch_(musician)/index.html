Stuart Murdoch (musician)

Stuart Murdoch performing on stage

Stuart Lee Murdoch (born 25 August 1968) is a Scottish musician, writer and filmmaker, best known as the lead singer, songwriter and founder of the indie pop band Belle and Sebastian.

Early life

Murdoch was born in Clarkston, East Renfrewshire, and grew up in Ayr. In the early 1990s he was diagnosed with myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome, which left him unable to work for several years; during his recovery he began writing songs in earnest and attended Stow College in Glasgow, where a music business course project led to the recording of what became Belle and Sebastian's debut album.

Career

Belle and Sebastian's debut, Tigermilk (1996), was initially pressed in a run of only 1,000 copies before the band signed to Jeepster Recordings; the follow-up, If You're Feeling Sinister (1996), is regarded as a landmark of indie pop, and the band won the Brit Award for Best Newcomer in 1999 despite being virtually unknown to the wider public. Murdoch's songs—literate, whimsical and often semi-autobiographical—anchor albums including The Boy with the Arab Strap (1998), Dear Catastrophe Waitress (2003) and Girls in Peacetime Want to Dance (2015).

Other work

Murdoch wrote and directed the musical film God Help the Girl (2014), which grew out of his side project of the same name with guest vocalists, and published a semi-autobiographical book, The Celestial Café (2010), drawn from his online diaries. A memoir, Nobody's Empire, centred on his experience of chronic illness, appeared in 2024–2025.
