Kalambaka

Kalambaka (Greek: Καλαμπάκα, Kalabáka, sometimes Kalampaka) is a town in the Trikala regional unit of Thessaly, Greece. It is best known as the gateway to the Meteora monastery complex, whose Eastern Orthodox monasteries are perched atop towering sandstone rock pillars immediately above the town.

Geography

Kalambaka lies at the northwestern edge of the Plain of Thessaly, near the Pinios River and the foothills of the Pindus mountains. The Meteora rock formations, rising some 400 metres above the plain, dominate the town's skyline.

History

The site is identified with the ancient town of Aiginion, mentioned by Strabo and Livy, and later with the Byzantine town of Stagoi, a bishopric attested from the 10th century. The town's Byzantine Church of the Dormition of the Virgin (Koimisis tis Theotokou) contains frescoes and a unique marble pulpit. Under Ottoman rule the town was a modest market centre; it was burned by German occupation forces in 1943 during the Second World War and largely rebuilt afterwards.

Meteora

From the 11th century, hermits and later monastic communities occupied the caves and summits of the Meteora pillars. At their peak there were 24 monasteries; six remain active and open to visitors, and the complex was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1988. Tourism associated with Meteora is the mainstay of Kalambaka's economy.

Transport

The town is served by a railway station on the Palaiofarsalos–Kalambaka line, with trains to Athens and Thessaloniki, and by road connections to Trikala, Ioannina, and Larissa.
