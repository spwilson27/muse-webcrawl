Orinoco Belt

Orinoco Belt (Faja Petrolífera del Orinoco)
Location: Southern Orinoco River basin, Venezuela
Area: about 55,000 km²
Resource: Extra-heavy crude oil

The Orinoco Belt (Spanish: Faja Petrolífera del Orinoco) is a vast deposit of extra-heavy crude oil in the southern strip of the Orinoco River basin in Venezuela. It is among the largest petroleum accumulations in the world, with technically recoverable resources often estimated in the hundreds of billions of barrels, rivalling the Canadian oil sands.

Geology and production

The belt stretches some 600 kilometres east to west. Its oil is very heavy — typically 8–10° API — and must be blended with lighter crude or upgraded into synthetic oil before refining. Development blocks such as Junín, Ayacucho, Carabobo and Boyacá were opened to joint ventures between the state company PDVSA and foreign partners.

Significance

Certification of the belt's reserves in the 2000s made Venezuela's proven reserves the largest in the world on paper, though production has been limited by the cost and complexity of extraction and by the country's wider economic difficulties.
