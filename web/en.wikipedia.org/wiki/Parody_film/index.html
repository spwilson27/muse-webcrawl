Parody film

A parody film or spoof film is a subgenre of comedy film that pastiches and mocks other film genres, specific movies or cinematic conventions. Parody films imitate the style of their targets with deliberate exaggeration for comic effect, relying on the audience's familiarity with the source material.

History

Early screen parodies mocked westerns and melodramas in the silent era. The genre flourished from the 1970s with Mel Brooks's Blazing Saddles (1974) and Young Frankenstein (1974), and the Zucker, Abrahams and Zucker team's Airplane! (1980) and The Naked Gun series, which spoofed disaster and police films with rapid-fire gags. The Scary Movie franchise (2000–) applied the formula to horror.

Characteristics

Spoofs typically string together references and set-piece imitations rather than sustained plots, and recycle genre clichés — dramatic speeches, training montages, villain monologues — in absurd contexts. Critically maligned "reference comedies" of the 2000s showed the genre's dependence on timely targets.
