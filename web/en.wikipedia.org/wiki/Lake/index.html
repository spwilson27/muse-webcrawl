Lake

[Image: A mountain lake with forested shores]
A mountain lake with forested shores

A lake is an often naturally occurring, relatively large and fixed body of water on or near the Earth's surface, located in a basin or interconnected basins surrounded by dry land. Lakes lie on land and are not part of the ocean, although, like the much larger oceans, they form part of the Earth's water cycle. Most lakes are freshwater and account for almost all the world's surface fresh water.

The largest lake by surface area is the Caspian Sea, which is called a sea but is hydrologically a lake; the largest freshwater lake by area is Lake Superior in North America, and the deepest and largest by volume is Lake Baikal in Siberia, which holds roughly a fifth of the world's surface fresh water.

Formation

Lakes form in many ways: tectonic basins (Baikal, Tanganyika), volcanic craters (Crater Lake), glacial scouring (the Great Lakes, the Finnish lake district), river meanders cut off as oxbow lakes, and artificial reservoirs behind dams. Some lakes are endorheic, with no outflow, and become saline, like the Great Salt Lake and the Dead Sea.

Ecology and use

Lakes support rich ecosystems and are vital for drinking water, irrigation, fisheries, transport and recreation. Nutrient pollution can cause eutrophication and harmful algal blooms, a problem in lakes worldwide.
