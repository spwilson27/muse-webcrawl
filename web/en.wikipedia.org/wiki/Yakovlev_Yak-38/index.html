Yakovlev Yak-38

Role: VTOL strike fighter
Manufacturer: Yakovlev
First flight: 1971
Introduced: 1976
Retired: 1991
Primary user: Soviet Naval Aviation

The Yakovlev Yak-38 (NATO reporting name: "Forger") was a Soviet naval VTOL (vertical take-off and landing) strike fighter developed by the Yakovlev design bureau. It was the Soviet Union's first and only operational VTOL carrier aircraft, serving aboard the Kiev-class aircraft carriers from 1976 until 1991.

Design

Unlike the single-engine Harrier, the Yak-38 used a combination of one main vectored-thrust turbojet (a Tumansky R-28) and two small dedicated lift jets (RD-38s) mounted vertically behind the cockpit. The lift jets operated only during take-off and landing and were dead weight in forward flight, sharply limiting range, payload, and hot-weather performance. The aircraft featured an automatic ejection system that fired the pilot out if engine failure occurred during hover — a system credited with saving several lives.

Service

About 230 Yak-38s were built, including the two-seat Yak-38U trainer. Deployments to the Mediterranean and a trial deployment to Afghanistan in 1980 exposed severe limitations: in hot conditions the aircraft sometimes could not lift off with any useful weapons load. Soviet pilots reportedly nicknamed it a "mast threat" rather than a combat asset. Nevertheless it gave the Soviet Navy early experience with carrier aviation, paving the way for later conventional carriers. It was retired shortly after the dissolution of the USSR, its successor Yak-141 program cancelled.
