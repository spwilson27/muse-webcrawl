Canadian English

Canadian English (CanE, CE, en-CA) is the set of varieties of English native to Canada. It shares features with both American English and British English, with its own vocabulary, spelling conventions and pronunciation.

Spelling

Canadian spelling mixes British and American forms: colour, centre, travelled (British-like) but program, tire, aluminum often American-like. Government and major dictionaries such as the Canadian Oxford Dictionary codify preferences.

Pronunciation

Hallmarks include Canadian raising — the diphthongs in about and writer raised before voiceless consonants, popularly heard as 'aboot' — the cot–caught merger in most regions, and a distinctive pronunciation of words like progress and schedule varying by speaker.

Vocabulary

Distinctive terms include toque (knit hat), loonie and toonie (coins), washroom, poutine, hydro (electricity), eh, and double-double (coffee). Influences come from French, Indigenous languages and immigration.

Regional varieties

Atlantic Canadian English, Quebec English, Ontario English and Western Canadian English differ noticeably, with Newfoundland English the most distinct.
