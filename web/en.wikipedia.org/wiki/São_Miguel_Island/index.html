São Miguel Island

Archipelago: Azores

Area: 744.7 km²

Highest point: Pico da Vara, 1,105 m

Population: approx. 137,000

Largest city: Ponta Delgada

São Miguel Island (Portuguese for Saint Michael) is the largest and most populous island in the Portuguese archipelago of the Azores. Nicknamed "The Green Island" (Ilha Verde) for its lush pastures, it forms part of the eastern group of the archipelago together with Santa Maria Island. The island's capital, Ponta Delgada, is also the largest city of the Azores.

Geography

São Miguel is about 65 km long and between 8 and 16 km wide, with an area of roughly 745 km². It is built from three main volcanic complexes: the Sete Cidades massif in the west, whose caldera holds the famous twin lakes Lagoa Azul and Lagoa Verde; the Água de Pau (Fogo) volcano in the centre, with the crater lake Lagoa do Fogo; and the Furnas and Nordeste complexes in the east. The highest point is Pico da Vara at 1,105 m.

History

The island was probably discovered between 1426 and 1439 during the Portuguese voyages of exploration sponsored by Henry the Navigator, and settlement began in the 1440s under the command of Gonçalo Velho Cabral. The first capital, Vila Franca do Campo, was largely destroyed by a devastating earthquake and landslide in 1522, after which Ponta Delgada rose to prominence and became the capital in 1546.

Attractions

The Furnas valley is famous for its hot springs, fumaroles, and cozido, a stew cooked underground in volcanic heat. The Cha Gorreana estate is one of the oldest tea plantations in Europe. Sete Cidades, Furnas Lake, and the islet of Vila Franca do Campo are major natural attractions, and pineapple greenhouses near Ponta Delgada produce the Azorean pineapple.

Economy

The economy is based on agriculture (especially dairy cattle and pasture), fishing, and a growing tourism sector. The island is served by João Paulo II Airport near Ponta Delgada.
