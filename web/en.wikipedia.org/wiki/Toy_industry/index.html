Toy industry

The toy industry encompasses the design, manufacture, marketing, and sale of toys and games worldwide. It is a global business worth tens of billions of dollars annually, dominated by a small number of large multinational manufacturers alongside many smaller specialist firms. The industry is highly seasonal, with a large share of sales concentrated in the weeks before Christmas and other gift-giving holidays.

Mass production of plastic toys on a factory line.

History

Toy production was a cottage industry until the 19th century, when German centres such as Nuremberg and Sonneberg became famous for tinplate toys, wooden toys, and dolls exported around the world. The United States and Japan emerged as manufacturing centres in the 20th century, and from the 1970s onward production shifted heavily to China, which came to manufacture the large majority of the world's toys.

Major firms include Mattel (maker of Barbie and Hot Wheels), Hasbro (maker of Monopoly, Play-Doh, and Transformers), the Danish Lego Group, Japan's Bandai Namco, and Germany's Playmobil. The industry consolidated through mergers and licensing deals, and character licensing from films, television, and video games became a central marketing strategy.

Market characteristics

The toy business is driven by short product cycles, rapidly shifting fads, and intense holiday-season competition. Manufacturers introduce thousands of new products each year, most of which fail commercially. "Hot toy" phenomena — such as Cabbage Patch Kids in the 1980s, Tickle Me Elmo in the 1990s, and various electronic crazes — can create shortages and resale markets. Retail has likewise shifted, with the decline of specialist chains such as Toys "R" Us (which entered bankruptcy in 2017) and the rise of big-box stores and e-commerce.

Safety and labour issues

The industry has faced recurring controversies over product safety — including major recalls in 2007 involving lead paint in toys manufactured in China — and over labour conditions in toy factories. These issues have led to stricter testing standards, third-party certification, and industry codes of practice such as the ICTI Ethical Toy Program.

Trends

Recent trends include the growth of the "kidult" market of adult collectors, the expansion of video games and digital play at the expense of traditional categories, subscription and STEM-oriented educational toys, and increased attention to sustainability in materials and packaging.
