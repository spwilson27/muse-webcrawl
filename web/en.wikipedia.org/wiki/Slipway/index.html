Slipway

A slipway, also known as a boat ramp or launch or boat deployer, is a ramp on the shore by which ships or boats can be moved to and from the water. They are used for building and repairing ships and boats, and for launching and retrieving small boats on trailers towed by automobiles.

Nautical terms of the slipway include the ways, which are timber, wood or concrete structures on which the ship is built or repaired; a cradle, a frame structure mounted on the ways on which the ship sits; and the keel blocks, transverse sections of timber or concrete on which the keel and bilge of the ship rest.

Simple slipways

In its simplest form, a slipway is a plain ramp, typically made of concrete, stone, gravel or even wood. The height of the tide can limit its usability: unless the ramp continues well below the low water level, it may not be usable at low tide. Normally there is a flat paved area adjoining the top of the ramp.

The earliest slipways are thought to be the ancient Greek slipways used for hauling warships out of the water to protect the hulls and keep them ready for launch.

Shipbuilding slipways

For building large ships, slipways descend to the water at the end of the slipway so the vessel can float off at launching. Slipways for larger ships are often constructed of concrete with steel reinforcements. When a vessel is being constructed, it is supported by keel blocks and bilge blocks which take the strain of the load of the ship.

The first use of a shipbuilding slipway in England is uncertain, but by the 16th century slipways were used in royal dockyards such as Chatham and Portsmouth.

Launching

Ships are commonly launched down the slipway using greased ways. The vessel slides down the launching ways on a cradle until it floats free. This dramatic event traditionally marks the first time the ship enters the water.
