ASCII Corporation (株式会社アスキー) was a Japanese publishing company and technology firm founded in Tokyo in 1977 by Kazuhiko Nishi and Akio Gunji. Named after the ASCII character code, the company grew out of the magazine ASCII, one of Japan's earliest personal-computer publications, and became a central institution of the Japanese PC industry.

MSX and Microsoft

Nishi, who had worked with Bill Gates in the early days of Microsoft, became Microsoft's representative in Japan and led the creation of the MSX home computer standard (1983), promoted by ASCII and Microsoft and adopted by many Japanese manufacturers. ASCII also developed semiconductors and peripherals, and published magazines including LOGiN and Famitsu (the latter through its Enterbrain subsidiary).

Games and later years

ASCII published and developed video games, and its software division created the RPG Maker and Fighter Maker game-creation tools. Financial difficulties in the late 1990s led to restructuring and investment by CSK/SEGA interests. In 2008 ASCII merged with MediaWorks to form ASCII Media Works, a subsidiary of Kadokawa, which continued the ASCII brand on publications.
