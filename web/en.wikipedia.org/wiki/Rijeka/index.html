Rijeka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rijeka (lit. 'River'; Italian: Fiume) is the principal seaport and the third-largest city in Croatia. It is located in Primorje-Gorski Kotar County on Kvarner Bay, an inlet of the Adriatic Sea, about 130 km southwest of Zagreb. With a population of roughly 108,000 in the city proper (2021 census) and around 200,000 in the wider urban area, Rijeka is an industrial, transport and university centre and the county seat.

History

The site was a Roman municipium called Tarsatica. In the Middle Ages it developed as a fortified town under various rulers, passing in 1466 to the Habsburgs. As corpus separatum attached to the Hungarian Crown from 1779, Fiume grew into a major port of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, shared with the Croatian port of Sušak across the Rječina river.

After the First World War the city became a flashpoint: in 1919 the Italian poet Gabriele D'Annunzio seized it and ruled the Italian Regency of Carnaro until evicted in 1920. The Free State of Fiume (1920–1924) was then annexed by Italy. Following the Second World War the city was ceded to Yugoslavia, its Italian population largely emigrating, and it was reunified with Sušak as Rijeka in the Socialist Republic of Croatia; since 1991 it has been part of independent Croatia.

Economy and culture

The Port of Rijeka is Croatia's largest cargo port, and the city retains shipbuilding, oil-refining and logistics industries. The University of Rijeka was founded in 1973. Rijeka was, with Galway, a European Capital of Culture in 2020. Landmarks include Trsat Castle, the City Tower on the Korzo promenade, St Vitus Cathedral with its distinctive rotunda, and the lively Rijeka Carnival held before Lent.

Transport

Rijeka is connected by motorway and rail to Zagreb and Central Europe, and Rijeka Airport is located on the nearby island of Krk, linked by one of Croatia's longest bridges.
