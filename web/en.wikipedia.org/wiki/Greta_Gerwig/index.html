Greta Celeste Gerwig (born 4 August 1983) is an American actress, screenwriter and film director. She first attracted attention through her work in low-budget independent "mumblecore" films in the late 2000s, and rose to prominence as an actress in films such as Greenberg (2010) and Frances Ha (2012), the latter of which she co-wrote with Noah Baumbach.

Directing career

Gerwig's solo directorial debut, the coming-of-age drama Lady Bird (2017), set in her hometown of Sacramento and starring Saoirse Ronan, was met with widespread acclaim; it earned five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director for Gerwig. Her adaptation of Little Women (2019) received six nominations including Best Picture and Best Adapted Screenplay.

Her third film, the fantasy comedy Barbie (2023), co-written with Baumbach and starring Margot Robbie and Ryan Gosling, grossed over $1.4 billion worldwide, making Gerwig the first solo female director of a billion-dollar film and Barbie the highest-grossing film of 2023. It received eight Academy Award nominations including Best Picture.

Personal life

Gerwig was raised in Sacramento and educated at Barnard College, where she studied English and philosophy. She has been in a relationship with filmmaker Noah Baumbach since 2011; they have two sons.
