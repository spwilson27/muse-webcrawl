Linear span

In mathematics, the linear span (also called the linear hull or just span) of a set of vectors in a vector space is the smallest linear subspace containing that set — equivalently, the set of all finite linear combinations of the vectors in the set.

Definition

Given vectors v1, ..., vk, their span consists of all vectors of the form a1v1 + ... + akvk where the ai are scalars from the underlying field. The span of the empty set is the zero subspace. A set of vectors spans a space if its span equals the whole space; a spanning set that is linearly independent is a basis.

Examples

In R3, two non-parallel vectors span a plane through the origin, and three non-coplanar vectors span all of R3. The span of a single non-zero vector is the line through it and the origin.
