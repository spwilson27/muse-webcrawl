Gene Baur

Gene Baur (born 1962), formerly known as Gene Bauston, is an American animal rights activist, author, and the co-founder and president of Farm Sanctuary, the first North American sanctuary for rescued farmed animals, founded in 1986.

Early life and activism

Baur grew up in California and worked as an actor and model before becoming an activist. In the 1980s he began investigating factory farms, stockyards, and slaughterhouses, documenting conditions for farmed animals. In 1986 he co-founded Farm Sanctuary in Watkins Glen, New York, after rescuing a sheep — named Hilda — found alive on a pile of dead animals at a stockyard.

In the organization's early years, Baur funded the sanctuary partly by selling veggie hot dogs at Grateful Dead concerts. Farm Sanctuary later expanded to additional shelters and became a leading voice in campaigns against practices such as gestation crates, battery cages, and foie gras production.

Writing

Baur is the author of Farm Sanctuary: Changing Hearts and Minds About Animals and Food (2008), a New York Times best seller, and co-author of Living the Farm Sanctuary Life (2015). He has a bachelor's degree in sociology and a master's degree in agricultural economics.
