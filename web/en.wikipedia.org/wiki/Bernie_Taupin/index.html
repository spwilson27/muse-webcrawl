Bernard John Taupin (born 22 May 1950) is an English lyricist and visual artist. He is best known for his lifelong songwriting partnership with singer Elton John, for whom he has written the lyrics to most of his songs, including "Your Song", "Rocket Man", "Candle in the Wind", "Daniel", "Goodbye Yellow Brick Road", and "Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me".

Early life and meeting Elton John

Taupin was born near Sleaford in Lincolnshire and grew up on a farm. In 1967 he answered an advertisement in the New Musical Express placed by Liberty Records seeking songwriters. A young pianist, Reginald Dwight, had answered the same advertisement; neither passed the audition, but an executive gave Dwight an unopened envelope of Taupin's lyrics, and the two began writing together by post — the start of one of the most successful partnerships in popular music.

Career

The pair's usual method is famously separate: Taupin writes lyrics alone and sends them to John, who composes the music, often within the hour. Their collaboration produced a string of acclaimed albums in the 1970s, including Goodbye Yellow Brick Road and Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy, the latter an autobiographical account of their early partnership. Taupin has also written for other artists, co-writing hits such as "We Built This City" for Starship and Heart's "These Dreams". He and John wrote the songs for the stage musicals The Lion King adaptations, Billy Elliot the Musical and others, and won an Academy Award for "(I'm Gonna) Love Me Again" from the biopic Rocketman (2019). Taupin has additionally pursued a career as a visual artist, exhibiting large abstract works in the United States.

Recognition

Taupin and John were inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1992. In 2023 Taupin was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in the Musical Excellence category. His memoir, Scattershot, was published in 2023.
