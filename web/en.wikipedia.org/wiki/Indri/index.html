Indri

Indri (Indri indri)
Order: Primates
Family: Indriidae
Range: Eastern Madagascar
Status: Critically endangered

The indri (Indri indri), also called the babakoto, is one of the largest living lemurs, with a head-body length of about 64–72 cm (25–28 in) and a weight of between 6 and 9.5 kg (13 and 21 lb). It has a black and white coat and is found in the rainforests of eastern Madagascar.

The indri is a vertical clinger and leaper.

Behaviour

The indri is a diurnal tree-dweller that moves by vertical clinging and leaping among the trunks of large trees. It lives in small family groups — typically a monogamous breeding pair and their offspring — and maintains communication with other groups through loud, haunting songs that can carry for kilometres through the forest. These duetting calls are one of the most distinctive sounds of the Malagasy rainforest.

Diet

It feeds mainly on leaves, supplemented by fruit, flowers and seeds. Like other folivorous primates it has a relatively low-energy lifestyle, resting for long periods between feeding bouts.

Conservation

The indri is listed as critically endangered, threatened principally by habitat destruction from slash-and-burn agriculture, logging and fuelwood collection, and in some areas by hunting despite traditional taboos (fady) that protect it. The indri has never survived for long in captivity, and no zoo population exists, so its survival depends entirely on protecting its wild habitat in reserves such as Andasibe-Mantadia National Park.

In Malagasy culture

The Malagasy name babakoto is often translated "ancestor", and many local legends treat the indri as a sacred ancestor of humankind, pointing to its upright posture, short tail and human-like hands.
