Catechin is a flavan-3-ol, a type of flavonoid, found in many plants and plant-derived foods and drinks, including tea, apples, grapes, cocoa, and the bark extracts catechu (from Acacia catechu) and gambier, from which the compound takes its name. It is a secondary metabolite that functions as an antioxidant in plants.

Chemistry

Catechin has two benzene rings (the A and B rings) joined by a dihydropyran heterocycle (the C ring) bearing a hydroxyl group on carbon 3. The molecule has two chiral centres, giving rise to stereoisomers; the most common natural form is (+)-catechin (2R,3S). Its mirror-image epimer with a cis configuration is epicatechin, another abundant dietary flavonoid.

Occurrence and metabolism

Green tea is an especially rich source of catechins, including epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), epicatechin gallate, epigallocatechin, and epicatechin, which together account for much of the beverage's bitterness and have been widely studied for possible health effects. In the human body, catechins are extensively metabolised by gut microbiota and hepatic enzymes, and their bioavailability is low.

Uses

Beyond food, catechin-rich extracts have historical uses in tanning leather and dyeing. Isolated catechin is sold as a dietary supplement, though health claims for catechin supplements remain under regulatory scrutiny.
