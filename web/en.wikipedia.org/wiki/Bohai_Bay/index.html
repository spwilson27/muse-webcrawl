Bohai Bay (Chinese: 渤海湾) is one of the three bays forming the Bohai Sea, the innermost gulf of the Yellow Sea, on the coast of northeastern China. It borders Hebei province and the municipality of Tianjin.

Geography

The bay is the westernmost of the Bohai Sea's three bays (with Laizhou Bay to the south and Liaodong Bay to the north). The Hai River and other rivers discharge into it, carrying heavy sediment loads that make its waters shallow and muddy. The port of Tianjin, serving Beijing, is China's largest artificial harbour and lies on its shore.

Economy and environment

The bay hosts major offshore oil fields, extensive salt pans, and the Binhai New Area development zone. It has suffered serious pollution and ecological pressure from the rapid industrialisation of the surrounding Bohai Economic Rim; oil spills, including a 2011 leak from the Penglai fields, raised national attention.
