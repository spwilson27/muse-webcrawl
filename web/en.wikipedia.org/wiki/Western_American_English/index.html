Western American English

Western American English is a variety of American English that unifies most of the western United States as a single dialect region, encompassing areas west of roughly the Mississippi River plains states through the Rocky Mountains to the Pacific coast states.

Features

The Western dialect is characterised by the cot–caught merger for most speakers, a fronted pronunciation of the vowel in words like goose, and a relatively low degree of internal variation compared with eastern dialect regions — a consequence of the region's more recent settlement by English speakers. Notable local sub-varieties include California English, Utah English and Pacific Northwest English.

Study

The West was mapped as a distinct region in the Atlas of North American English (2006) by William Labov and colleagues, who noted its ongoing role in sound changes spreading across North America, including the fronting of back vowels.
