Virtual reality

Virtual reality (VR) is a simulated experience that employs head-mounted displays and three-dimensional near-eye displays to give the user an immersive feel of a virtual world. Applications include entertainment (particularly video games), education (such as medical or military training) and business (such as virtual meetings). VR is distinguished from augmented reality, which overlays digital content on the real world rather than replacing it.

Technology

A VR system combines a headset with stereoscopic displays, lenses and head tracking (gyroscopes, accelerometers and cameras) so the virtual scene responds to the wearer's movements with low latency. Hand controllers, haptic feedback, eye tracking and spatial audio deepen immersion. "Inside-out" tracking uses cameras on the headset itself; earlier systems used external base stations. Key qualities sought are a wide field of view, high resolution and refresh rate, and minimal motion-to-photon delay, which also reduces cybersickness.

History

Precursors include panoramic paintings, stereoscopes and Morton Heilig's Sensorama (1962). Ivan Sutherland built the first head-mounted display system, the "Sword of Damocles", in 1968. The term "virtual reality" was popularised by Jaron Lanier's VPL Research in the 1980s. Consumer attempts in the 1990s (Nintendo's Virtual Boy) failed, but the 2012 Oculus Rift Kickstarter revived the field; standalone headsets such as Meta Quest (2019–) made VR mass-market, and Apple's Vision Pro (2024) pushed mixed-reality "spatial computing".

Applications

Beyond gaming, VR is used for flight and surgical simulation, exposure therapy for phobias and PTSD, architectural visualisation, virtual tourism, remote collaboration and social platforms. Research continues into its effects on health, including simulator sickness, eye strain and psychological impacts of prolonged immersion.
