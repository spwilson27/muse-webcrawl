Clochán

RegionIreland
MaterialDry stone
Also calledBeehive hut

A clochán is a dry-stone hut with a corbelled roof, commonly called a beehive hut, found on the southwestern coast of Ireland. The name derives from the Irish word for a stone structure.

Construction

Clochán are built entirely without mortar: successive rings of stone are laid, each slightly overlapping the one below, until the walls converge to a small opening closed by a capstone. This corbelling technique produces a weatherproof, dome-like structure.

Distribution and history

Fine examples survive on the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry and on the monastic island of Skellig Michael, where monks' cells form part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Their dates are debated, with examples ranging from early medieval monastic use to much later agricultural shelters.
