The Employee Benefits Security Administration (EBSA) is an agency of the United States Department of Labor responsible for administering and enforcing the fiduciary, reporting and disclosure provisions of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA). It oversees roughly 2.8 million private-sector retirement and health plans and welfare benefit plans covering some 153 million American workers, retirees and their families.

History

The agency's functions originated with the pension and welfare plan administration created around ERISA's enactment in 1974; it was long known as the Pension and Welfare Benefits Administration (PWBA) before being renamed EBSA in 2003 to reflect its enforcement mission.

Functions

EBSA interprets ERISA through regulations and advisory opinions, conducts civil and criminal investigations of plan fiduciaries, brings enforcement actions, grants prohibited-transaction exemptions, and provides compliance assistance and consumer help to plan participants—recovering billions of dollars in benefits through its enforcement and informal dispute-resolution programs. It also collects the annual Form 5500 reports filed by employee benefit plans.
