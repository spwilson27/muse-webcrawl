Forest ecology

Forest ecology is the scientific study of the interrelated patterns, processes, flora, fauna, and ecosystems within forests. The management of forests is known as forestry, silviculture, and forest management. A forest ecosystem is a natural woodland unit consisting of all plants, animals, and micro-organisms (biotic components) in that area functioning together with all of the non-living physical factors of the environment (abiotic components).

Scope

Forest ecology studies forests at scales from individual organisms and stands to landscapes and biomes. Key topics include energy flow and nutrient cycling, the roles of disturbance such as fire, windthrow, and herbivory, succession after disturbance, and the interactions among trees, understory plants, soil organisms, and wildlife. Forests play a major role in the global carbon cycle, storing large amounts of carbon in biomass and soils.

Communities and ecosystems

Forest ecologists classify forests into types such as boreal, temperate, and tropical forests, each with characteristic species composition and structure. Vertical structure — canopy, understory, shrub, and forest floor layers — creates distinct microclimates and habitats. Dead wood, including snags and fallen logs, is an important habitat feature and nutrient reservoir.

Relation to management

Forest ecology underlies practices in forestry and conservation, informing harvest planning, reforestation, prescribed burning, and the protection of biodiversity. It overlaps with landscape ecology, conservation biology, and soil science.
