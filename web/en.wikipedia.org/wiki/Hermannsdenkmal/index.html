Hermannsdenkmal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hermannsdenkmal (German for "Hermann Monument") is a monument in the Teutoburg Forest near Detmold in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Completed in 1875, it commemorates the Cherusci war chief Arminius (in German, Hermann) and his victory over three Roman legions at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, a defining symbol of German nationalism in the 19th century.

Description

The statue, designed by Ernst von Bandel, stands about 53.5 metres (176 ft) high including its pedestal; the copper figure of Arminius alone is some 26 metres tall and raises a sword 7 metres long toward France, Germany's rival at the time of construction. Building began in 1841 and the monument was inaugurated by Kaiser Wilhelm I in 1875, four years after German unification.

History and reception

Raised largely by public subscription, the monument became an icon of the new German Empire and later a popular tourist attraction, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors a year. A similar monument, the Hermann Heights Monument, was built by German-American settlers in New Ulm, Minnesota.
