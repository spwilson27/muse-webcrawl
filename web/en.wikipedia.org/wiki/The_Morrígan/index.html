The Morrígan, also known as Mórrígan or the Phantom Queen, is a figure from Irish mythology associated with war, battle, fate, and sovereignty. Her name is usually interpreted as "great queen" or "phantom queen". She often appears as a crow or raven flying above the battlefield, inciting warriors to battle, foretelling doom, or bringing about the death of heroes.

Triplicity

The Morrígan is frequently described as a triple goddess, sometimes comprising three sisters — Badb, Macha, and Nemain (or Anand) — though the groupings vary between texts and scholars debate whether the Morrígan is one goddess with three aspects or a collective name. Related war-goddesses share her crow imagery and her power to sow panic and confusion.

In the tales

In the Ulster Cycle, particularly the Táin Bó Cúailnge, the Morrígan confronts the hero Cú Chulainn: when he spurns her advances, she attacks him in the forms of an eel, a wolf, and a heifer, and he wounds her each time; later she appears to him as an old woman milking a cow, and his blessing of her heals her wounds. Before his final battle she is linked to the Washer at the Ford, seen washing his bloodied clothes as an omen of death. In the mythological cycle's Cath Maige Tuired, she mates with the Dagda before the battle against the Fomorians and promises to destroy their king.

Interpretation

Scholars have connected the Morrígan with sovereignty goddesses who confer kingship, with older European figures of fate and battle-frenzy, and with the later folklore figure of the banshee. Her cave at Oweynagat in County Roscommon is described in tradition as a gateway to the Otherworld.
