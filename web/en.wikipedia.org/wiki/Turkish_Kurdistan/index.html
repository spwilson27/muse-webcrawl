Turkish Kurdistan (Kurdish: Bakurê Kurdistanê, "Northern Kurdistan") is the unofficial name for the parts of eastern and southeastern Turkey inhabited predominantly by Kurds. It forms the northwestern portion of the greater Kurdish region, which also spans parts of Iraq, Iran, and Syria. Major cities include Diyarbakır, Van, Şanlıurfa, Mardin, Batman, Hakkâri, and Şırnak.

Geography

The region is mountainous, lying largely within the Eastern Anatolia and Southeastern Anatolia regions. The Tigris and Euphrates rivers rise here, and Lake Van, Turkey's largest lake, lies in the east. The terrain includes high plateaus, deep valleys, and the foothills of the Zagros and Taurus mountains.

History

Kurdish emirates existed in the region under Ottoman suzerainty. After the First World War, the Treaty of Sèvres (1920) envisaged possible Kurdish autonomy, but the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) left the Kurds divided among four states without one of their own. A series of Kurdish revolts — Sheikh Said (1925), Ararat (1927–1930), and Dersim (1937–1938) — were suppressed by the Turkish Republic.

From 1984, the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) waged an armed insurgency against the Turkish state, leading to decades of conflict, village evacuations in the 1990s, and tens of thousands of deaths. Periods of ceasefire and peace talks alternated with renewed fighting. Kurdish political parties have repeatedly won municipalities in the region, though elected mayors have at times been replaced by state-appointed trustees.

Demographics

Kurds form the majority in most provinces of the southeast, though large Kurdish populations also live in western Turkish cities such as Istanbul, which is sometimes described as the largest Kurdish city in the world.
