The term fashion industry refers to the industry surrounding the design, manufacture, distribution, marketing, retailing, advertising and promotion of all types of apparel, from the most rarefied and expensive haute couture to ordinary mass-produced clothing. Sometimes the broader term "fashion industries" is used to include related sectors such as textiles, footwear, accessories, cosmetics and jewellery.

Structure

The industry is usually divided into segments:

  • Haute couture — made-to-measure garments from Paris houses regulated by the Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode.
  • Luxury and designer ready-to-wear — brands sold under houses such as Chanel, Dior, Gucci and Prada, largely owned by conglomerates like LVMH and Kering.
  • High-street / fast fashion — rapidly produced inexpensive clothing (Inditex's Zara, H&M, Shein, ASOS).
  • Mass market and value retail — supermarkets' and discounters' own labels.

A global value chain connects fibre production (cotton in India and the US, synthetics from petrochemicals), textile mills (heavily concentrated in China), dyeing and finishing, and cut-make-trim garment factories, chiefly in Bangladesh, Vietnam, Cambodia and Turkey, before distribution to retail markets in Europe, North America and East Asia.

History

The modern industry emerged with 19th-century Paris couture and department stores, then mass production and standard sizing in the early 20th century. Post-1945, manufacturing shifted progressively to lower-wage countries; the phase-out of the Multi-Fibre Arrangement quotas in 2005 accelerated the concentration of garment production in Asia. E-commerce and social media reshaped marketing and demand from the 2010s.

Labour and environment

Garment work employs tens of millions, mostly women, often at low wages and in unsafe conditions; the Tazreen Fashions fire (2012) and Rana Plaza collapse (2013) in Bangladesh prompted the Accord on Fire and Building Safety and wider scrutiny. Environmentally the industry is a major consumer of water and chemicals and a source of greenhouse-gas emissions and textile waste, with less than 1% of clothing recycled into new clothing. Regulatory responses include France's anti-fast-fashion proposals and EU ecodesign and extended-producer-responsibility rules.
