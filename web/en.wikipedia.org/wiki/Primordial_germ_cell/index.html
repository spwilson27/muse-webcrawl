Primordial germ cell

Migration of primordial germ cells to the developing gonads

Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are the embryonic precursor cells of the gametes — the sperm and eggs — in sexually reproducing animals. They are set aside early in development, before the formation of the gonads, and later migrate to the developing gonadal ridges, where they proliferate and eventually differentiate into gametes through gametogenesis.

Specification

Animals specify their germline in one of two broad ways. In some organisms, such as Drosophila, C. elegans, and many fish and amphibians, PGCs are determined by germ plasm — maternally deposited cytoplasm containing specialized RNAs and proteins that is inherited by a few cells during cleavage. In mammals, by contrast, PGCs are specified by induction (epigenesis): signals such as BMP4 from extraembryonic tissues induce a small group of epiblast cells to adopt germ cell fate. In mice this involves the transcription factors BLIMP1 (PRDM1), PRDM14, and AP2γ (TFAP2C).

Migration and development

In mouse embryos, PGCs appear around day 7 of development in the extraembryonic mesoderm near the allantois, then move through the hindgut and its mesentery to colonize the genital ridges by about day 10.5–11.5, proliferating from roughly 40 founder cells to about 25,000. In human embryos, PGCs are specified around the second to third week and migrate via the hindgut to the gonadal ridges by about the fifth week. Guiding cues include the chemokine SDF1 and its receptor CXCR4, and the KIT ligand/KIT signaling pathway; PGCs that stray from the path normally die, and survivors outside the gonads can give rise to teratomas.

Epigenetic reprogramming

PGCs undergo extensive epigenetic reprogramming, including genome-wide DNA demethylation and the erasure of parental genomic imprints, which are later re-established according to the sex of the individual during gametogenesis. This resetting is essential for normal development of the next generation and makes PGCs a major model for studying epigenetic mechanisms.

Applications

PGCs can be cultured in vitro as embryonic germ cells, which are pluripotent. In birds, cultured PGCs are used for genetic modification and for conserving endangered species and rare poultry breeds. In mammals, functional gametes have been produced from stem cells via PGC-like cells in mice, a line of research with potential implications for fertility treatment.
