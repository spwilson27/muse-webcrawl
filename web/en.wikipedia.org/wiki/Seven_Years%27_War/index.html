Seven Years' War

The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) was a global conflict that pitted Great Britain and Prussia against France, Austria, Russia, Spain and Sweden. Sometimes described as the first "world war," it was fought across Europe, North America (as the French and Indian War), India, West Africa, the Caribbean and the Philippines.

Date1756–1763
ResultTreaties of Paris and Hubertusburg; British gains in North America and India
BelligerentsBritain, Prussia, Hanover vs. France, Austria, Russia, Spain, Sweden

Course

The war grew out of the Diplomatic Revolution of 1756, which reversed longstanding alliances. Prussia under Frederick the Great survived against a coalition in Europe, while Britain, under the leadership of William Pitt the Elder, won decisive victories over France in North America — including the capture of Quebec in 1759 — and in India, where Robert Clive's victory at Plassey (1757) had already shifted the balance.

Consequences

The Treaty of Paris (1763) confirmed Britain as the dominant colonial power: France ceded Canada and Louisiana east of the Mississippi. Prussia retained Silesia. British war debt led to new taxes on the American colonies, contributing to the American Revolution.
