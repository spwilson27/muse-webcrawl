Urs

Urs (from Arabic: عرس, romanized ʿurs, literally "wedding") is the death anniversary of a Sufi saint, usually held at the saint's dargah (shrine or tomb). In most Sufi orders such as Naqshbandiyyah, Suhrawardiyya, Chishtiyya, Qadiriyya, etc. the concept of urs exists and is celebrated with enthusiasm. The devotees refer to their saints as lovers and God as the beloved.

In the urs, the saint's devotees and followers congregate at the shrine to attend religious congregations (majalis), hold devotional musical performances such as qawwali, and pay homage. Urs festivals are usually presided over by a custodian or current leader of an order. Many urs gatherings include a food festival, bazaar, and various kinds of shops.

Meaning and significance

The urs is seen as a moment of "spiritual wedding" because the saint, regarded as a lover of God, is believed to be reunited with the Beloved, God, after death. Unlike a funeral, which marks separation, the urs celebrates the saint's union (wisal) with God. This concept is closely related to the Sufi notion of fana, the annihilation of the self in God.

Celebrations

Urs festivals are held throughout the Muslim world, particularly in South Asia, where famous examples include the urs of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti at Ajmer in India and the urs of Data Ganj Baksh at Lahore in Pakistan. Large crowds gather, sometimes numbering in the hundreds of thousands, and the celebrations can last several days. Rituals often include the washing of the tomb, the offering of floral tributes and a ceremonial cloth (chadar), and the recitation of prayers. Pilgrims believe that the saint can intercede on their behalf.
