Shipping Forecast

The Shipping Forecast is a BBC Radio broadcast of weather reports and forecasts for the seas around the British Isles. It is produced by the Met Office and specifically covers 31 maritime areas, whose names — including Viking, Forties, Cromarty, Dogger, Fisher, German Bight, Humber, Thames, Dover, Wight, Portland, Plymouth, Biscay, Trafalgar, FitzRoy, Sole, Lundy, Fastnet, Irish Sea, Shannon, Rockall, Malin, Hebrides, Fair Isle, Faeroes and Southeast Iceland — are part of British cultural life. The forecast is broadcast four times daily on BBC Radio 4, including the much-loved late-night edition at 00:48, introduced by the tune "Sailing By".

History

Marine weather warnings for British coastal waters were first issued after a Royal Charter storm in 1859 prompted Robert FitzRoy to establish storm warnings; telegraphed gale warnings began in 1861, and the first radio shipping forecast was broadcast in 1924. The modern list of areas took shape after the Second World War, with areas added or renamed over time: Helgoland became German Bight (1956), Finisterre was renamed FitzRoy in 2002 to avoid confusion with a Spanish area of the same name, and Trafalgar appears only in the late-night broadcast. The forecast is also transmitted by HM Coastguard via Navtex and VHF.

Format

Each broadcast follows a strict pattern and word limit (about 350 words for the main bulletins). After a general synopsis, each area receives, in fixed order, its wind direction and speed (in Beaufort scale terms), precipitation, visibility and, where relevant, icing. Gale warnings are given first for affected areas, followed by reports from coastal stations. The measured, hypnotic delivery of the late-night edition has made the forecast a cultural institution: it has inspired poetry (Seamus Heaney's "The Shipping Forecast"), songs by Radiohead, Blur and Jethro Tull, appearances in literature and film, and affectionate parody, and generations of listeners have used it as an aid to sleep.
