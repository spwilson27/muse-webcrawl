Duchy of Parma

Duchy of Parma and Piacenza (Ducato di Parma e Piacenza)
Existed: 1545–1802, 1814–1859
Capital: Parma
Ruling houses: Farnese, Bourbon-Parma

The Duchy of Parma, formally the Duchy of Parma and Piacenza, was an Italian state created in 1545 by Pope Paul III for his illegitimate son Pier Luigi Farnese, carved from the Papal States in Emilia-Romagna.

Farnese era

The Farnese dukes ruled for nearly two centuries. Alessandro Farnese, governor of the Spanish Netherlands, was the dynasty's most famous soldier; later dukes such as Ranuccio I and Francesco made Parma a notable court and university city, building the vast Palazzo della Pilotta and its Farnese Theatre (1618), the first permanent proscenium theatre.

Bourbon era

When the Farnese line died out in 1731, the duchy passed via Elisabeth Farnese to her son Charles of Bourbon, then to the Austrian Habsburgs, and in 1748 to Philip of Spain, founder of the House of Bourbon-Parma. Under the minister Guillaume du Tillot the duchy became a model of enlightened reform. Marie Louise of Austria, Napoleon's widow, ruled from 1814 to 1847 and was much loved. The duchy was annexed to Piedmont-Sardinia in 1859–60 during Italian unification.

Legacy

The name survives in Parma's famous products — Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese and Prosciutto di Parma — and in the title Duke of Parma, still claimed by the Bourbon-Parma family.
