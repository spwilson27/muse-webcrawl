The Odeon of Agrippa was a large odeon (roofed theatre for musical performances) located in the centre of the Ancient Agora of Athens. It was built around 15 BC as a gift to the city by Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa, the son-in-law and general of the emperor Augustus.

Description

The building was a two-storey structure measuring about 51.4 by 43.2 metres, with a rectangular auditorium seating roughly 1,000 spectators. Its most ambitious feature was a wide roof spanning some 25 metres without internal supports — one of the largest clear spans attempted in the ancient world. The exterior was decorated with pillars carved as giants and tritons, some of which were later reused in the facade of the Gymnasium of the Giants.

History

The great roof collapsed around AD 150, after roughly a century and a half of use. The odeon was rebuilt as a smaller hall with about 500 seats and a new north facade. The complex was destroyed during the Herulian invasion of AD 267. In the 5th century AD a large building complex, sometimes called the Gymnasium or Palace of the Giants, was constructed over the site, incorporating the giant and triton pillars.
