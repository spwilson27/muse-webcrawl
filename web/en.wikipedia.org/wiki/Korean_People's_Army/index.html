The Korean People's Army (KPA; Korean: 조선인민군) is the military force of North Korea and the armed wing of the ruling Workers' Party of Korea. Founded in 1948, shortly before the proclamation of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, it is one of the largest standing armies in the world, with an estimated 1.28 million active personnel, supplemented by millions of reservists and paramilitary forces.

Organisation

The KPA comprises the Ground Force, the Navy, the Air Force, the Strategic Force (responsible for ballistic missiles), and the Special Operations Force. Under the songun ("military-first") policy the army long enjoyed priority in resource allocation; political officers and party committees permeate every level of command. Conscription is among the longest in the world, with men historically serving around a decade.

Capabilities and doctrine

Although much of its conventional equipment is of ageing Soviet and Chinese design, the KPA fields large artillery forces concentrated near the Demilitarized Zone and has developed nuclear weapons and a diverse ballistic missile arsenal, including intercontinental-range missiles tested from 2017. It fought the Korean War (1950–1953), which ended in armistice, and its forces remain deployed along the DMZ facing South Korean and US forces.
