Faster Payments Service

CountryUnited Kingdom
Launched2008
OperatorPay.UK
SettlementNear real-time, 24/7

The Faster Payments Service (FPS) is a United Kingdom banking initiative that enables near-instant electronic payments between bank accounts, available 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Payments typically arrive within seconds, and the vast majority within two hours at most.

History

Before Faster Payments, most UK interbank transfers used BACS and took three working days to clear. Following pressure from the Office of Fair Trading and the government, the banking industry built the new service, which launched in May 2008 — making the UK one of the first countries with a real-time retail payment system. It is now operated by Pay.UK, which also runs BACS and the cheque image clearing system.

Operation

Customers send payments through online, mobile, or telephone banking using a sort code and account number. The single immediate payment limit has risen over time to £1 million for participating banks, though individual banks set lower customer limits. Faster Payments underpins most UK mobile and internet transfers, as well as services such as Paym (until its closure) and open-banking payments. Because payments are irrevocable and instant, the system has also been targeted by authorised push payment fraud, prompting the Confirmation of Payee name-checking service in 2020.
