Asterales is an order of flowering plants (angiosperms) within the asterid clade of the eudicots. It contains around 35,000 species in 11 families, roughly a tenth of all flowering plants. The order is dominated by the sunflower family, Asteraceae, one of the two largest families of plants, which includes sunflowers, daisies, lettuce, artichokes and chrysanthemums.

Characteristics

Members of Asterales commonly store the carbohydrate inulin rather than starch, often have fused anthers, and present pollen by a "plunger" mechanism in which the style pushes pollen out for visiting insects. In Asteraceae, the flowers are reduced to small florets packed into a dense head (capitulum) that mimics a single flower, an arrangement associated with their great ecological success.

Families and importance

Under the APG IV system the order includes Asteraceae, Campanulaceae (bellflowers, including lobelias), Goodeniaceae, Stylidiaceae (triggerplants), Menyanthaceae, Calyceraceae and several small families. Economically important members include food crops (lettuce, sunflower oil seed, artichoke, chicory, stevia), ornamentals (dahlia, marigold, gerbera, aster) and herbs such as chamomile, echinacea and tarragon.

Evolution

Molecular and fossil evidence places the origin of the order in the Late Cretaceous, possibly in the Southern Hemisphere; the Asteraceae in particular diversified rapidly as open, seasonal habitats expanded during the Cenozoic, and now occur on every continent except Antarctica.
