Arecibo Telescope

The Arecibo Telescope was a 305 m (1,000 ft) spherical reflector dish radio telescope built into a natural sinkhole at the Arecibo Observatory located near Arecibo, Puerto Rico.

Decommissioned2020 (collapsed 1 Dec 2020)

History

Completed in 1963, it was the world's largest single-aperture telescope until 2016, when China's FAST was completed. It was used for radio astronomy, atmospheric science and radar astronomy, including mapping Venus and asteroids.

Discoveries

At Arecibo, Russell Hulse and Joseph Taylor discovered the first binary pulsar (1974), earning the Nobel Prize. The telescope sent the Arecibo message in 1974 and participated in SETI searches. It measured Mercury's rotation period as 59 days.

Collapse

After cable failures in August and November 2020, the NSF announced decommissioning. On 1 December 2020 the instrument platform fell and destroyed the dish. Plans have been discussed for a next-generation facility.
