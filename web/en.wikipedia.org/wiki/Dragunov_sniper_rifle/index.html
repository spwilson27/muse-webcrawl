Dragunov sniper rifle

Type Designated marksman rifle / sniper rifle
Place of origin Soviet Union
In service 1963–present
Designer Yevgeny Dragunov
Cartridge 7.62×54mmR
Action Gas-operated, rotating bolt
Effective range ~800 m
Feed system 10-round detachable box magazine

The Dragunov sniper rifle (Russian: Снайперская Винтовка Драгунова, Snayperskaya Vintovka Dragunova, abbreviated SVD) is a semi-automatic designated marksman rifle chambered in 7.62×54mmR, developed in the Soviet Union by Yevgeny Dragunov and adopted in 1963.

Design and role

Unlike Western sniper rifles of its era, which were precision bolt-action weapons for trained snipers, the SVD was designed to extend the effective range of an ordinary infantry squad: a designated marksman in each platoon carried it. It is gas-operated with a short-stroke piston and a rotating bolt, fed from a 10-round magazine, and normally fitted with the PSO-1 4× telescopic sight, which includes a stadiametric rangefinder and an illuminated reticle.

Service

The SVD served with Warsaw Pact armies and was widely exported and licence-produced, including versions made in China (Type 79/85) and Iran. It has seen use in conflicts from the Vietnam War era to the present day, and modernised Russian variants (SVDS, SVDM) remain in service.
