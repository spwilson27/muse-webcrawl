Nanjing (Chinese: 南京; pinyin: Nánjīng; historically romanized as Nanking) is the capital of Jiangsu province in eastern China and one of the country's most historically significant cities. Situated on the lower Yangtze River, it has served as the capital of China under several dynasties and governments, and remains a major center of education, research, transport and tourism.

Nanjing's skyline, mixing Ming-era walls with modern towers.

History

Founded in antiquity and first made a capital in 229 CE by the state of Eastern Wu, Nanjing was the capital of the early Ming dynasty, whose founder, the Hongwu Emperor, built its massive city walls — among the longest surviving in the world — and is buried at the Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum. It later became the capital of the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom and, from 1927, of the Republic of China.

In December 1937, during the Second Sino-Japanese War, Japanese forces captured the city and perpetrated the Nanjing Massacre, in which large numbers of Chinese civilians and disarmed soldiers were murdered. The event remains a central and painful episode in modern Chinese memory, commemorated by a memorial hall in the city.

Modern city

Today Nanjing is a major rail and river hub, home to leading universities such as Nanjing University, and a center for software, chemicals and automotive manufacturing. Landmarks include the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum on Purple Mountain, the Confucius Temple district and the Nanjing Yangtze River Bridge, completed in 1968.
