Pousada

A pousada is a type of inn or guesthouse in Portuguese-speaking countries. In Portugal, Pousadas de Portugal is a chain of luxury hotels established in 1941, typically housed in historic buildings such as castles, monasteries, and palaces, and classified into categories including historic, historic design, nature, and charm. The Portuguese network has been operated by the Pestana Group since 2003.

In Brazil

In Brazil, a pousada is a small, independently run guesthouse, inn, or bed-and-breakfast-style establishment, common in beach towns and tourist destinations. They range from simple family homes renting rooms to upscale boutique lodgings, and differ from hotels mainly in their smaller scale and more personal character.

History in Portugal

The first Portuguese pousada opened in Elvas in 1942, part of a state project to promote regional tourism and preserve monuments. Architecturally significant examples include the Pousada de Santa Maria do Bouro, a converted Cistercian monastery designed by Eduardo Souto de Moura, and the Pousada do Castelo in Óbidos.
