The Simpsons

The Simpsons is an American animated sitcom created by Matt Groening for the Fox Broadcasting Company. It satirises American life through the Simpson family — Homer, Marge, Bart, Lisa and Maggie — in the fictional town of Springfield.

History

Groening conceived the family while waiting in producer James L. Brooks's office, and the Simpsons first appeared as animated shorts on The Tracey Ullman Show in 1987. The half-hour series premiered in 1989 and quickly became a cultural phenomenon and Fox's first top-30 hit. It is the longest-running American animated series, longest-running sitcom, and longest-running scripted primetime series in US television history.

Characters and setting

Homer is the dim, donut-loving safety inspector at Springfield Nuclear Power Plant; Marge the patient homemaker with tall blue hair; Bart the mischievous ten-year-old; Lisa the gifted, saxophone-playing eight-year-old; and Maggie the silent baby. Hundreds of supporting characters — Mr. Burns, Ned Flanders, Krusty the Clown, Apu, Chief Wiggum — populate Springfield, whose location is deliberately never specified.

Cultural impact

The show's "Golden Age" (roughly seasons 2–10) is considered among the finest television ever made. It has won dozens of Emmy Awards, the 1997 Peabody Award, and a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. Homer's "D'oh!" entered the Oxford English Dictionary. The Simpsons Movie (2007) grossed over $536 million, and the franchise spans comics, video games, a theme-park area and vast merchandising. Lines and predictions from the show — including a joke about a Donald Trump presidency — are famous worldwide.
