PayPal Holdings, Inc. is an American multinational financial technology company operating an online payments system in the majority of countries that support online money transfers. It serves as an electronic alternative to traditional paper methods such as checks and money orders. The company operates as a payment processor for online vendors, auction sites and many other commercial and personal users.

History

PayPal was established in December 1998 as Confinity, a company that developed security software for handheld devices, by Max Levchin, Peter Thiel, Luke Nosek and Ken Howery. Its online payment service was launched in 1999. In March 2000 Confinity merged with X.com, an online banking company founded by Elon Musk. The combined company adopted the name PayPal in 2001 and went public in 2002.

In October 2002 PayPal was acquired by eBay for $1.5 billion in stock, becoming eBay's default payment method. Many members of PayPal's early team — the so-called "PayPal Mafia" — went on to found or fund companies including Tesla, SpaceX, LinkedIn, YouTube and Palantir. In 2014 eBay announced it would spin off PayPal into an independent publicly traded company, which was completed in July 2015.

Services

PayPal allows users to send and receive money online, linked to a bank account, credit card or PayPal balance. The company expanded through acquisitions including the mobile payment service Venmo (via its 2013 acquisition of Braintree), the money transfer service Xoom, and the shopping rewards platform Honey. PayPal is available in more than 200 markets and supports payments in over 100 currencies.

Regulation and criticism

PayPal operates as a licensed money transmitter in the United States and as a bank in Luxembourg for European operations. It has faced criticism over account freezes, dispute resolution policies, and a controversial 2022 policy update concerning fines for misinformation, which the company said had been published in error and reversed.
