Azo dye

Azo dyes are organic compounds bearing the functional group R–N=N–R′, where R and R′ are usually aryl groups. They are the largest and most versatile class of synthetic dyes, accounting for more than half of commercial dye production, and are used in textiles, food colouring, inks and plastics.

Synthesis

Azo dyes are prepared by diazotisation of a primary aromatic amine followed by azo coupling with an electron-rich aromatic compound. Their colour arises from the conjugated system around the azo bond and can be tuned from yellow through red to blue and black.

Safety

Some azo dyes can cleave to release aromatic amines that are carcinogenic; dyes capable of releasing listed amines such as benzidine are restricted in the European Union for articles in contact with skin. Others, such as tartrazine, remain approved food colorants in many jurisdictions.
