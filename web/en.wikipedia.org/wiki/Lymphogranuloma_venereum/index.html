Lymphogranuloma venereum

Lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV) is a sexually transmitted infection caused by the invasive serovars L1, L2 and L3 of the bacterium Chlamydia trachomatis. Unlike the more common chlamydial infections caused by serovars D–K, LGV invades lymphatic tissue and can cause systemic disease.

Stages

The primary stage is a small, often painless genital sore that may go unnoticed. The secondary stage involves painful swelling of the inguinal lymph nodes (buboes), sometimes with the "groove sign" where nodes swell above and below the inguinal ligament; fever and malaise may occur. In recent decades, outbreaks among men who have sex with men in Europe and North America have most often presented as proctitis (rectal inflammation with pain, discharge and bleeding). Untreated tertiary disease can cause chronic inflammation, fistulas, strictures and genital elephantiasis (esthiomene).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by nucleic acid amplification testing of swabs, with LGV-specific genotyping where available. The treatment of choice is doxycycline for 21 days; azithromycin is an alternative. Sexual partners should be evaluated and treated.
