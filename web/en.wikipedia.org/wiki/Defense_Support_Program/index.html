Defense Support Program

The Defense Support Program (DSP) is a program of the United States Space Force that operates a constellation of reconnaissance satellites forming the principal component of the Satellite Early Warning System used by the United States. DSP satellites use infrared sensors to detect the heat from missile and booster plumes against the background of the Earth, providing early warning of ballistic missile launches, as well as detecting nuclear detonations. The satellites have been the spaceborne segment of the early warning system since the 1970s.

History and operation

The first DSP satellite was launched in November 1970, succeeding the experimental MIDAS program. The satellites are placed in geosynchronous orbit, from where their spinning telescopes scan the Earth's surface. Early generations carried around 2,000 infrared detectors; later satellites carried some 6,000. The constellation is operated by mission crews at Buckley Space Force Base in Colorado, with overseas relay stations including sites in Australia and Europe.

During the 1991 Gulf War, DSP satellites detected launches of Iraqi Scud missiles and provided warning of attacks to forces in Israel and Saudi Arabia — the first time the system was used in a regional conflict. DSP data has also been used to characterise other infrared events, including rocket launches by other nations and, reportedly, large explosions and wildfires.

Successor

More than 20 DSP satellites were built by Northrop Grumman (originally TRW). The program is being succeeded by the Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS) and the Next Generation Overhead Persistent Infrared (Next Gen OPIR) satellites, which offer more sensitive and persistent coverage. The last DSP satellite, DSP-23, was launched in 2007; it failed in 2008 for reasons that remain unexplained publicly, though other satellites of the constellation continued operating well beyond their design lives.
