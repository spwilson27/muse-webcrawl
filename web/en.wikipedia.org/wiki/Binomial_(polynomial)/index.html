Binomial (polynomial)

In algebra, a binomial is a polynomial that is the sum of two terms, each of which is a monomial. It is the simplest kind of polynomial after the monomials. A binomial in a single indeterminate (variable) can be written in the form

a xm + b xn,

where a and b are numbers, and m and n are distinct nonnegative integers. More generally, a binomial may involve several variables, as in 3x2y − 5z.

Examples

Familiar examples include x + 1, 4x3 − 7, and x2 − y2. The last of these is the well-known difference of two squares, which factorises as (x − y)(x + y). Binomials are also the basis of the binomial theorem, which gives the expansion of powers (x + y)n in terms of binomial coefficients.

Operations

A binomial raised to a power can be expanded using the binomial theorem or Pascal's triangle. Products of binomials can be computed by the distributive law; the special case of the product of two linear binomials is taught via the FOIL method. The sum of two squares binomial, x2 + a2, does not factor over the real numbers, though it factorises over the complex numbers.

Applications

Binomials appear throughout mathematics and science: in probability as the binomial distribution, in combinatorics via binomial coefficients, and in physics in expressions such as the binomial approximation (1 + x)n ≈ 1 + nx for small x.
