Gyromitra esculenta

Gyromitra esculenta
Common names: False morel, brain mushroom
Family: Discinaceae
Toxin: Gyromitrin
Edibility: Poisonous raw; eaten prepared in some regions

Gyromitra esculenta is an ascomycete fungus known as a false morel, recognisable by its irregular, brain-like, reddish-brown cap. It grows in coniferous forests of Europe and North America in spring.

Toxicity

The fungus contains gyromitrin, which is metabolised to monomethylhydrazine, a toxin that can cause vomiting, liver damage, and potentially death. Raw consumption is dangerous, and carcinogenic risk is debated.

Use as food

Despite its toxicity, G. esculenta is a traditional delicacy in parts of Scandinavia and Finland, where strict preparation — parboiling multiple times with discarded water and thorough drying — reduces toxins, and its sale is regulated with warning labels. Many authorities advise against eating it.
