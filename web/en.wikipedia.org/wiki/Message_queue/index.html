Message queue

In computer science, message queues and mailboxes are software-engineering components used for inter-process communication (IPC) or for inter-thread communication within the same process. They use a queue for messaging — the passing of control or of content — allowing processes to communicate asynchronously by writing messages to, and reading messages from, a queue.

Overview

Message queues implement an asynchronous communication pattern between two or more processes or threads. A producer creates messages and places them in the queue; a consumer retrieves them, usually in first-in-first-out order. The queue may be managed by the operating system kernel — as in System V and POSIX message queues — or by dedicated middleware known as message-oriented middleware or a message broker, such as IBM MQ, RabbitMQ (implementing AMQP), or Apache Kafka-style streaming platforms.

Benefits and trade-offs

Queues decouple senders from receivers in time and in rate, smoothing bursts of work and allowing components to fail independently. In exchange, systems must handle message delivery semantics (at-most-once, at-least-once, exactly-once), ordering guarantees, poison messages, and back-pressure when consumers fall behind.
