The National Rifle Association of America (NRA) is a gun rights advocacy group and nonprofit organisation based in the United States. Founded in 1871 by Union Army veterans William Conant Church and George Wood Wingate to promote rifle marksmanship, it has become the most prominent organisation lobbying against gun control legislation, while also running firearms safety, training, and competitive shooting programmes.

History

Church and Wingate, concerned by the poor marksmanship of Union soldiers during the Civil War, chartered the NRA in New York in 1871; former president Ulysses S. Grant was among its early presidents. For its first century the association concentrated on sport shooting and supported some firearms regulation, including the National Firearms Act of 1934 and aspects of the Gun Control Act of 1968. A turning point came at the 1977 annual meeting — the "Revolt at Cincinnati" — when hard-line activists seized control and redirected the NRA toward uncompromising opposition to gun regulation, centred on the Second Amendment.

Political activity

The NRA's lobbying arm, the Institute for Legislative Action (founded 1975), and its political action committee grade candidates and mobilise members. The association has been one of the most influential interest groups in American politics, shaping federal and state firearms policy for decades, though its influence and finances weakened in the late 2010s and 2020s amid internal disputes, declining membership, and litigation.

Legal troubles

In 2020 the New York Attorney General sued to dissolve the NRA, alleging financial misconduct by its leadership; a court rejected dissolution but a 2024 jury verdict found that the organisation had mismanaged assets and that longtime chief executive Wayne LaPierre had misused funds. LaPierre resigned in January 2024. The NRA filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2021 in an attempt to reincorporate in Texas, but the case was dismissed as not filed in good faith.
