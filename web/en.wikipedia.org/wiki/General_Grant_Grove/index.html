General Grant Grove

LocationKings Canyon National Park, Fresno County, California, United States
Area154 acres (62 ha)
Established1890 (as General Grant National Park)
Named forUlysses S. Grant

General Grant Grove is a grove of giant sequoia trees (Sequoiadendron giganteum) in Kings Canyon National Park in the Sierra Nevada of California. Covering about 154 acres, it contains the General Grant Tree, the second-largest known tree on Earth by volume and the designated national Christmas tree of the United States. The grove was originally protected as General Grant National Park, established in 1890, before being incorporated into Kings Canyon National Park in 1940.

Notable trees

  • General Grant Tree — 267 feet (81 m) tall with a trunk volume of about 46,600 cubic feet; proclaimed the "Nation's Christmas Tree" in 1926 and designated a national shrine in 1956, the only example of a living national shrine, memorializing Americans who died in war.
  • Centennial Tree — cut for display at the Philadelphia Centennial Exposition of 1876; its stump remains in the grove.
  • Fallen Monarch — a huge hollow fallen sequoia once used as a hotel, saloon, and stable; visitors can now walk through it.
  • Robert E. Lee Tree — another of the grove's giant specimens.

History

The grove was named in 1867 by Joseph Hardin Thomas after Ulysses S. Grant, then commanding general of the U.S. Army and later president. Concern over logging of giant sequoias led to the creation of the small General Grant National Park on 1 October 1890 — the third U.S. national park — which was absorbed when Kings Canyon National Park was established in 1940.

Visiting

The Grant Tree Trail, a paved one-third-mile loop, passes the grove's principal trees. The Kings Canyon Visitor Center is located nearby at Grant Grove Village, which also has a lodge, campground, and store. An annual "Trek to the Tree" and Christmas service have been held at the General Grant Tree since the 1920s.
