Antipassive voice

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Diagram of antipassive argument structure

The antipassive voice is a valency-reducing grammatical voice found chiefly in ergative languages. It promotes the ergative argument (the subject of a transitive verb) to absolutive subject while deleting or demoting the original object to an oblique or omitting it entirely — the mirror image of the passive voice in nominative–accusative languages.

Function and distribution

Antipassivisation typically signals that the object is indefinite, generic, partially affected or unimportant, or that the action itself is habitual or incomplete. It also serves syntactic purposes: in syntactically ergative languages such as Dyirbal, only absolutive arguments can be coordinated or relativised, so the antipassive is regularly required to make a transitive subject available for such constructions. Antipassives are well documented in Australian Aboriginal languages, Mayan languages, Inuit languages and languages of the Caucasus, among others.
