Demoparty

A demoparty is an event that gathers demosceners and other computer enthusiasts to take part in competitions called compos, where participants present demos (small, self-contained computer programs that produce audiovisual presentations), graphics, music, and other creative digital works. Demoparties emerged from the copyparty culture of the 1980s, when software crackers and demo groups met to swap software; over time the creative competitions became the focus.

A demoparty hall, with entries shown on the big screen.

Competitions

Typical compos include demo competitions (often split by platform, such as PC, Amiga, and oldschool 8-bit machines), intro competitions for size-limited productions (64k, 4k, and smaller), graphics, music (tracked and streamed), and wild competitions for unusual platforms or media. Winners are usually chosen by audience vote. Size restrictions are a hallmark of the scene, pushing programmers to generate graphics and sound procedurally within a few kilobytes.

Notable parties

Major long-running demoparties include Assembly in Finland, Revision in Germany (successor to Breakpoint), The Party in Denmark (historical), and Evoke in Germany. Some parties, such as Assembly, grew into large LAN events while retaining their demoscene roots.

Culture

Demoparties are social events as much as competitions: sceners travel internationally, meet group members in person, and screen entries late into the night. In 2020, the demoscene was added to the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage in Finland, with Germany following in 2021 — the first digital culture to receive such recognition.
