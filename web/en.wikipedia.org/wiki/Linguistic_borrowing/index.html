Linguistic borrowing

Borrowing (linguistics)
FieldHistorical linguistics; contact linguistics
Main productLoanwords
Related processesCalque; loan shift; code-switching

In linguistics, borrowing is the process by which a speech community adopts words, sounds, or grammatical features from another language or dialect. The borrowed item is most commonly a loanword — a word taken into one language from another with little or no modification, such as English kindergarten from German, sushi from Japanese, or algebra from Arabic. Borrowing is one of the principal ways languages change through contact.

Types of borrowing

Scholars distinguish several products of contact. A loanword imports both form and meaning. A calque or loan translation imports only the structure, translating the parts with native material — English "skyscraper" calqued into French as gratte-ciel, for example. In a loan shift, a native word takes on a new meaning under foreign influence. Borrowing can also affect pronunciation, morphology and syntax, especially under prolonged bilingualism, though vocabulary is borrowed most readily. Sociolinguists such as Sarah Thomason describe a borrowing scale from casual contact (non-basic vocabulary only) through intense contact, where structural features also transfer.

Motives and pathways

Words are borrowed to name new objects, technologies and concepts (need borrowing) or because the donor language carries prestige (as with French vocabulary in Middle English after the Norman Conquest, or English words in contemporary languages worldwide). Borrowed words are typically adapted to the sound system and grammar of the receiving language, sometimes so thoroughly that speakers no longer perceive them as foreign — many core English words such as sky, egg and they are Old Norse borrowings. Successive waves of borrowing are a rich source of evidence for cultural history, trade and conquest.

Attitudes

Language purists sometimes resist borrowing, and institutions such as the Académie française coin native replacements for foreign terms, while in most communities borrowing proceeds regardless. Re-borrowing also occurs: a word may return to its language of origin in altered form, as Japanese anime (from English "animation") was re-borrowed into English with a narrower meaning.
