Alice B. Toklas

Alice Babette Toklas (1877–1967) was an American-born writer and a central figure of the Paris avant-garde, best known as the lifelong partner of Gertrude Stein. Born in San Francisco and raised partly in Seattle, she moved to Paris in 1907, where she met Stein on the day of her arrival; the two remained together until Stein's death in 1946.

Life in Paris

At the Stein apartment at 27 Rue de Fleurus, Toklas helped sustain one of the most famous salons of modern art and literature. She typed manuscripts, managed the household, cooked celebrated meals, and formed friendships with artists and writers including Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse, Ernest Hemingway and F. Scott Fitzgerald.

Writing

Stein's experimental memoir The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas (1933) was written in Toklas's voice and became a bestseller. Toklas later published her own works, including The Alice B. Toklas Cook Book (1954), a memoir of French cooking famous in part for a hashish fudge recipe contributed by Brion Gysin, and What Is Remembered (1963).

Later years

After Stein's death, Toklas devoted herself to preserving Stein's literary legacy and publishing her unpublished writings. Financial difficulties and declining health marked her final years; she converted to Catholicism in 1957 and died in Paris in 1967, aged 89. She is buried beside Stein in Père Lachaise Cemetery.
