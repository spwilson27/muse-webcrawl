Stolen and missing Moon rocks

Of the 270 Apollo program Moon rock samples given by the United States to nations of the world and to US states, a significant number have been reported stolen or missing. Because genuine lunar material is essentially priceless — and nearly impossible to sell legally — the samples have been targets of theft, and many goodwill gifts were simply lost, stored away or misplaced by recipient governments.

Notable cases include the 2002 theft from Johnson Space Center by NASA intern Thad Roberts and accomplices, who stole a safe containing lunar samples from every Apollo mission and attempted to sell them to a Belgian collector; the FBI recovered the samples in a sting operation. The "Goodwill Moon rock" presented to Honduras was recovered in 1998 in "Operation Lunar Eclipse". Samples gifted to several countries, including the Netherlands' purported Moon rock held by the Rijksmuseum — shown in 2009 to be petrified wood — have proved controversial. Apollo 17 goodwill rocks given to some nations, such as Malta's, were stolen, while others surfaced in estates and storage decades later.

NASA's Office of Inspector General has investigated missing samples, and recovered samples are held as US government property, since private ownership of Apollo lunar material is generally prohibited.
