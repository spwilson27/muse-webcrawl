College Station, Texas

College Station is a city in Brazos County, Texas, situated in East-Central Texas in the heart of the Brazos Valley, towards the eastern edge of the region known as the Texas Triangle. It is 83 miles (130 km) northwest of Houston and 87 miles (140 km) east-northeast of Austin. As of the 2020 census, College Station had a population of 120,511. Together with neighbouring Bryan, it forms the Bryan–College Station metropolitan area.

History

The city's origins date to 1860, when the Houston and Texas Central Railway built through the area. In 1871 the site was chosen as the location for the proposed Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas, which opened in 1876 and later became Texas A&M University. The railroad designated its local stop "College Station" after the school, and a post office followed in 1877. The city was incorporated in 1938.

Geography and climate

College Station lies in the Brazos River valley and has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters. The city is built largely around the university and spreads southeast of Bryan.

Education and landmarks

Texas A&M University, one of the largest universities in the United States by enrolment, dominates the city's economy and culture. The city is also home to the George Bush Presidential Library and Museum, located on the university's West Campus. Kyle Field, the university's football stadium, is among the largest stadiums in the world.

Demographics

The population is notably young due to the large student body, and the city consistently ranks among the fastest-growing mid-sized cities in Texas.
