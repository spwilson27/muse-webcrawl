Himeji Castle (姫路城, Himeji-jō) is a hilltop Japanese castle complex in the city of Himeji, Hyōgo Prefecture. It is regarded as the finest surviving example of prototypical Japanese castle architecture, comprising a network of 83 buildings with advanced defensive systems from the feudal period. Because of its brilliant white plastered exterior, it is nicknamed White Heron Castle (Shirasagi-jō), said to evoke a heron taking flight.

History

A fort was first built on the site by Akamatsu Norimura in 1333; it was remodelled into a castle by his son Sadanori. The complex took on much of its present form between 1601 and 1609 under Ikeda Terumasa, son-in-law of Tokugawa Ieyasu, who received the castle after the Battle of Sekigahara. Remarkably, Himeji was never attacked in battle, survived the Meiji-era demolitions that destroyed most Japanese castles, and escaped the firebombing of Himeji in 1945 — a bomb dropped on the top floor failed to explode. A major restoration of the main keep ("Heisei Restoration") was completed in 2015.

Design

The complex centres on a five-storey main keep linked to three smaller keeps in a highly defensible arrangement atop massive stone ramparts. Intruders faced a labyrinth of winding, narrowing paths, dead ends, steep slopes, gates that forced sharp turns, and loopholes for arrows and firearms — the "mazelike" layout survives almost intact. Inside, the keep's wooden frame, storage for weapons, and hidden rooms illustrate early-Edo military engineering.

Heritage status

Himeji Castle is a National Treasure of Japan and was among the first Japanese sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993. It is the most visited castle in Japan and a frequent filming location, appearing in works from Kurosawa's Ran and Kagemusha to the James Bond film You Only Live Twice.
