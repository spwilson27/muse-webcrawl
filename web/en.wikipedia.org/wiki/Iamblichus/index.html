Iamblichus

Iamblichus
Born: c. 245 AD, Chalcis, Syria
Died: c. 325 AD
School: Neoplatonism
Teacher: Porphyry

Iamblichus of Chalcis (Greek: Ἰάμβλιχος; c. 245 – c. 325 AD) was a Syrian Neoplatonist philosopher who determined the direction later Neoplatonism took and profoundly influenced Western esotericism. He studied under Porphyry, pupil of Plotinus, but diverged by systematising theurgy — ritual practices by which the soul unites with the divine.

Life and school

Born at Chalcis in Coele-Syria to a wealthy family, possibly descended from priest-kings of Emesa, Iamblichus founded his own school in Syria, probably at Apamea, attracting students such as Aedesius. The emperor Julian later revered him.

Works and thought

His surviving works include On the Mysteries (De Mysteriis), a defence of theurgy against Porphyry's rationalising doubts; On the Pythagorean Life, part of a ten-volume compendium on Pythagoreanism; and the Protrepticus, preserving otherwise lost fragments of earlier philosophers. Iamblichus elaborated the Neoplatonic hierarchy into triads, distinguished gods, angels, daemons and heroes, and held that embodied souls cannot ascend by thought alone — material rites appointed by the gods are necessary.

Influence

His synthesis dominated later pagan Neoplatonism (Proclus, the Athenian and Alexandrian schools), passed into Byzantine and Renaissance thought — Ficino translated him — and into modern occultism. See Neoplatonism and Theurgy.
