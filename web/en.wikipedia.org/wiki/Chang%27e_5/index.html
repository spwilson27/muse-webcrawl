Chang'e 5 was a Chinese uncrewed lunar sample return mission. It landed in Oceanus Procellarum near Mons Rümker on 1 December 2020, collected about 1.7 kg of lunar material by drilling and scooping, and returned the samples to Earth on 16 December 2020.

Mission profile

The mission used a four-part spacecraft (orbiter, lander, ascender and returner) launched on a Long March 5 rocket. The ascender performed the first robotic rendezvous and docking in lunar orbit, transferring the samples to the returner.

Significance

Chang'e 5 was the first lunar sample return since the Soviet Luna 24 mission in 1976, making China the third country to return lunar samples. Analysis showed the basalts collected were about two billion years old, the youngest lunar volcanic rocks yet sampled.
