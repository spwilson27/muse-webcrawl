Japanese occupation of Singapore

Period15 February 1942 – 12 September 1945
Japanese nameShōnan-tō (昭南島)
EventFall of Singapore, 1942
[Image: Japanese troops in Singapore, 1942]

The Japanese occupation of Singapore began on 15 February 1942, when Japanese forces captured the British colony of Singapore after a week-long battle. Renamed Shōnan-tō ("Island of Light of the South"), the island remained under Japanese military administration until the end of World War II, when its formal surrender was accepted on 12 September 1945.

The fall of Singapore

The fall of Singapore was the largest surrender of British-led forces in history. Landing on the island's northwestern shore on the night of 8–9 February 1942, Japanese troops advanced down the Malayan peninsula's west bank and seized the causeway. After days of bombardment, Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered some 80,000 British, Australian, and Indian troops to General Tomoyuki Yamashita.

Life under occupation

The occupation brought severe hardship. In the Sook Ching massacres of early 1942, Japanese troops executed thousands of Chinese Singaporeans thought to be hostile. Food rationing, inflation of the "banana currency" occupation notes, and forced labour caused widespread suffering, and the city was heavily damaged by Allied bombing in the later years of the war.

End of the occupation

Following Japan's surrender in August 1945, British forces returned to Singapore. The formal Japanese surrender ceremony for Southeast Asia was held at the Municipal Building on 12 September 1945, with Admiral Lord Louis Mountbatten accepting the surrender from General Seishirō Itagaki.

Legacy

Memorials including the Civilian War Memorial commemorate the victims of the occupation. The period profoundly shaped Singapore's political development after the war.
