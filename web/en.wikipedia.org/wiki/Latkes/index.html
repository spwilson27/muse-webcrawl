Latke

TypePancake
Place of originCentral and Eastern Europe
Main ingredientsPotatoes, onion, egg, flour or matzo meal
ServingHot, with applesauce or sour cream

A latke (Yiddish: לאַטקע) is a fried potato pancake traditionally eaten by Ashkenazi Jews during the festival of Hanukkah. Foods fried in oil are customary at Hanukkah in commemoration of the miracle of the oil that burned for eight days in the rededicated Temple in Jerusalem.

Preparation

Latkes are made from grated potatoes mixed with grated onion, egg, and a binder such as flour or matzo meal, seasoned with salt and pepper. Spoonfuls of the batter are shallow-fried in oil until crisp and golden on both sides. They are commonly served with applesauce or sour cream; debates over the preferred accompaniment are a light-hearted fixture of the holiday.

Variations

While the potato latke is best known in North America, related fried pancakes are made from sweet potato, zucchini, carrot, cheese, and other ingredients. The word "latke" derives from Yiddish and ultimately from a Slavic term for a small pancake; earlier versions in Jewish communities were made with cheese or buckwheat rather than potatoes, which became common only in the nineteenth century.
