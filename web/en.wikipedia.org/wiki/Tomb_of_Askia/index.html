The Tomb of Askia, in Gao, Mali, is a pyramidal mud-brick structure believed to be the burial place of Askia Mohamed I, the first emperor of the Songhai Empire. Built around 1495 after Askia's pilgrimage to Mecca, the 17-metre-high pyramidal tomb is the most prominent example of the Sudano-Sahelian mud-building tradition in the region.

Description

The complex includes the pyramidal tomb itself — its skeleton of protruding wooden beams (toron) also serves as scaffolding for periodic re-plastering — two flat-roofed mosque buildings, a mosque cemetery, and an open assembly ground. The structure was built with mud carried from the Niger River and, according to tradition, with materials Askia brought back from Mecca.

World Heritage status

UNESCO inscribed the Tomb of Askia as a World Heritage Site in 2004, recognising it as a fine example of the monumental mud-building tradition of the West African Sahel. In 2012, following armed conflict in northern Mali and the occupation of Gao by armed groups, the site was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger, and it suffered reported damage during the conflict before French and Malian forces retook the city in 2013.
