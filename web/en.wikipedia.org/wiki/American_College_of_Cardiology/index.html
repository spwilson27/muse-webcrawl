American College of Cardiology

The American College of Cardiology (ACC) is a nonprofit medical association established in 1949 dedicated to transforming cardiovascular care and improving heart health. It represents more than 54,000 members, including cardiologists, surgeons, nurses, pharmacists and other cardiovascular care team members, primarily in the United States.

History

The college was founded in 1949, with early leaders including Franz Groedel and Bruno Kisch. It grew out of postwar advances in cardiology as a distinct specialty distinct from internal medicine. Its annual Scientific Session became one of the largest cardiovascular meetings in the world.

Activities

The ACC publishes the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC) family of journals, develops clinical practice guidelines jointly with the American Heart Association, and maintains registries such as the National Cardiovascular Data Registry (NCDR). It accredits hospitals for chest pain and other cardiovascular services and advocates on health policy.

Education and membership

Fellows of the college use the designation FACC. Membership categories include physicians, cardiovascular team members and trainees. The ACC provides continuing medical education, board review and quality-improvement tools.
