Lumen Field is a multi-purpose stadium in Seattle, Washington, United States, located in the city's SoDo neighbourhood just south of downtown. It is the home field for the Seattle Seahawks of the National Football League (NFL) and Seattle Sounders FC of Major League Soccer (MLS). Originally called Seahawks Stadium, it was renamed Qwest Field in 2004, CenturyLink Field in 2011, and Lumen Field in November 2020 following CenturyLink's rebranding as Lumen Technologies.

History

The stadium was built between 2000 and 2002 on the site of the Kingdome, the Seahawks' former home, which was demolished by implosion in March 2000. Voters approved public funding for the project in a 1997 statewide referendum championed by Seahawks owner Paul Allen, whose company First & Goal operates the publicly owned stadium. The facility opened on 28 July 2002 with a Sounders match.

Design and atmosphere

Designed by Ellerbe Becket, the stadium is a U-shaped open-air bowl with a partial roof covering about 70 per cent of the seats, sheltering fans from Seattle's rain while leaving the field open. The open north end frames views of the downtown Seattle skyline and features the 13-storey "Hawks' Nest" seating tower. Lumen Field is famous for its crowd noise: Seahawks fans, known collectively as the "12th Man" (later "12s"), twice set the Guinness World Record for loudest crowd roar at an outdoor stadium, reaching 137.6 decibels in December 2013. The noise has caused numerous false-start penalties by visiting teams, and fans famously generated a minor seismic event — the "Beast Quake" — celebrating Marshawn Lynch's touchdown run in the January 2011 playoffs.

The stadium is also a venue for concerts, Supercross, and international football, and is scheduled to host matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup.
