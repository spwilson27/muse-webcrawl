Powdered milk

Powdered milk, also called milk powder or dried milk, is a manufactured dairy product made by evaporating milk to dryness. It is produced from whole milk, skimmed milk, or other liquid dairy products, most commonly by spray drying, though roller drying is also used. Marco Polo reportedly described Mongolian soldiers carrying sun-dried skimmed milk, and the modern process dates from the 19th century.

Production and properties

In spray drying, concentrated milk is atomised into a stream of hot air, leaving a fine powder with a moisture content of roughly 2.5–5%, which allows it to be stored for many months without refrigeration — far longer than liquid milk. Non-fat dry milk keeps the longest because milk fat can oxidise and become rancid.

Uses

Powdered milk is used in infant formula, confectionery (such as chocolate and caramel), baked goods, processed foods, and as a food-aid commodity. It is widely used where fresh milk is impractical to transport or store, by campers and hikers, and in regions without reliable refrigeration. Reconstituted powdered milk is generally considered inferior in flavour to fresh milk, though it is nutritionally comparable when fortified with vitamins A and D.
