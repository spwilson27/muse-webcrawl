Hand and brain chess

Hand and brain is a chess variant in which two players on each team take different roles: the "brain" names a piece to be moved, and the "hand" must make a legal move with that piece, choosing which move to play. The partners may not communicate further about the position.

The variant is popular in exhibitions, streaming, and club play because it combines players of different strengths: a strong player can act as the brain for a weaker hand, while misunderstandings between partners often create entertaining complications.

Rules

On each turn, the brain calls out a piece type, such as "knight" or "pawn". The hand then selects any legal move by a piece of that type. If the named piece has no legal move, the turn is handled according to local rules, often by allowing the brain to name another piece. Normal chess rules otherwise apply, including checkmate and draws.

Strategy

Effective brains must consider not only the best move but also which moves the hand is likely to find, and sometimes name a piece to restrict the hand's options to safe moves. Hands must infer the brain's intentions from the position.
