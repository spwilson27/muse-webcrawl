The Koryaks are an Indigenous people of the Kamchatka Peninsula and surrounding areas in the Russian Far East. Their traditional territory formed the Koryak Okrug, now part of Kamchatka Krai.

Traditional life

Historically the Koryaks divided into two economic groups: the nomadic reindeer-herding Koryaks of the interior (Chavchuvens) and the settled coastal Koryaks (Nymylans), who fished and hunted sea mammals. Traditional dwellings included the yaranga, a large conical tent.

Language and culture

The Koryak language belongs to the Chukotko-Kamchatkan family and is closely related to Chukchi. Shamanism and animist beliefs centred on reverence for nature were central to traditional culture; under Soviet rule collectivisation transformed reindeer herding and settlement patterns.

Present day

Today most Koryaks live in and around Palana, the former okrug capital. The Koryak language is endangered, though cultural festivals and dance ensembles keep traditions alive.
