Black Heritage Trail

The Black Heritage Trail is a 1.6-mile (2.5 km) walking trail in Boston, Massachusetts, that explores the history of the city's 19th-century African American community on Beacon Hill. The trail links more than 15 sites and is administered as part of the Boston African American National Historic Site by the National Park Service.

Sites

The trail begins at the Robert Gould Shaw and Massachusetts 54th Regiment Memorial opposite the Massachusetts State House, commemorating the first African American regiment recruited in the North during the Civil War. Other stops include the African Meeting House (1806), the oldest surviving Black church building in the United States; the Phillips School; the homes of abolitionists such as Lewis Hayden, whose house was a station on the Underground Railroad; and the Museum of African American History.

History

In the 19th century the north slope of Beacon Hill was home to a thriving free Black community of artisans, labourers and activists central to the abolitionist movement. The trail was developed so that this history, long overshadowed by the neighbourhood's later image, would be marked in the streetscape; ranger-led tours are offered seasonally.
