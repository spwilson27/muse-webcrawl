WeChat (Chinese: 微信; pinyin: Wēixìn) is an instant messaging, social media and mobile payment application developed by the Chinese technology company Tencent. First released in 2011, it has become one of the world's largest standalone mobile applications, with well over a billion monthly active users. It is often described as a "super app" or an "app for everything" because of the breadth of functions integrated into it. Inside mainland China the service operates as Weixin, which is subject to Chinese content rules; WeChat serves users elsewhere.

History

WeChat was created by a team led by Allen Zhang (Zhang Xiaolong) at Tencent's Guangzhou research and project centre, and launched in January 2011, initially as a simple messaging app with voice notes and photo sharing. The social feed feature Moments followed in 2012, official accounts for businesses and media in the same year, and the payments service WeChat Pay in 2013. Its "red packet" (hongbao) feature, launched for Chinese New Year 2014, popularised mobile payment in China by gamifying money transfers. Mini Programs — lightweight sub-applications that run inside WeChat without separate installation — launched in January 2017 and were widely copied by rival platforms.

Features

Beyond text, voice and video messaging and group chats, WeChat integrates Moments (a friends-only social feed), Channels (short video), official accounts, Mini Programs for shopping, transport, government services and games, and WeChat Pay, which enables transfers, QR-code payments, utility bills, and transit fares. For many users in China, WeChat functions as an identity layer for both online and offline life.

Privacy, censorship and bans

WeChat is subject to content monitoring under Chinese law; Citizen Lab and other researchers have documented keyword filtering and surveillance of images and documents, including files shared by users registered outside China being used to refine censorship lists. India banned WeChat in 2020 along with other Chinese apps following border clashes. In the United States, a 2020 executive order seeking to ban transactions with WeChat was blocked by courts and revoked in 2021; several U.S. states have restricted the app on government devices, and debates over data security continue.
