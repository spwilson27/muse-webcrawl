Tiffany lamp

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Tiffany lamp is a type of lamp with a stained glass shade made by Tiffany Studios in New York, designed principally between 1893 and the 1930s under the artistic direction of Louis Comfort Tiffany. The shades were assembled from hundreds of pieces of colored glass, wrapped in copper foil and soldered together — a technique that allowed far more intricate designs than traditional lead came. Motifs drew heavily on nature: dragonflies, wisteria, peonies, poppies and geometric patterns in the Art Nouveau and Arts and Crafts styles.

Design and the "Tiffany Girls"

Although long credited solely to Louis Comfort Tiffany, scholarship and the 2005 discovery of letters by designer Clara Driscoll revealed that she designed many of the studio's most famous lamps, including the Dragonfly and Wisteria models, and headed the Women's Glass Cutting Department. The women who selected and cut the glass became known as the "Tiffany Girls". Production used opalescent "Favrile" glass developed by Tiffany, often incorporating leftover glass from the studio's window commissions.

Legacy

Original Tiffany lamps are highly collectible; a "Pink Lotus" lamp sold at auction in 2018 for over $3 million, among the highest prices ever paid for a Tiffany lamp. The New-York Historical Society holds a major collection, much of it from the Neustadt Collection of Tiffany Glass. Countless reproductions, also typically made by the copper-foil method, keep the style in production today.
