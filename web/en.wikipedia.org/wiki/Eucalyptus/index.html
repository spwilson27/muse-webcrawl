Eucalyptus is a genus of more than 700 species of flowering trees and shrubs (gum trees) in the myrtle family, Myrtaceae, native mostly to Australia, with a few in New Guinea and Indonesia.

Description

Eucalypts range from shrubs to Eucalyptus regnans, among the tallest flowering plants on Earth (nearly 100 m). Features include oil-rich aromatic leaves, peeling or fibrous bark (stringybark, ironbark, box, gum), woody capsules (gumnuts), and heterophylly — juvenile round leaves differing from adult lance-shaped leaves.

Ecology and uses

Dominating Australian forests, eucalypts are adapted to fire: lignotubers and epicormic buds allow resprouting, and oils promote flammability. Koalas feed almost exclusively on their leaves. Timber, pulp, eucalyptus oil, honey and plantation forestry worldwide (with invasiveness concerns in some regions) make them economically major.
