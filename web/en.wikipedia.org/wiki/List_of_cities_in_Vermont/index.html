The U.S. state of Vermont has ten incorporated cities. In Vermont, a city is an incorporated municipality consisting of a single town — though in most cases the surrounding town government was merged into the city at incorporation — and governed independently of the county structure, which in Vermont is largely ceremonial anyway. Cities in Vermont are chartered by the state legislature and are distinct from towns and villages, the other forms of municipal government in the state.

The ten cities

The cities of Vermont, roughly by population, are: Burlington (the state's largest municipality and seat of Chittenden County), South Burlington, Rutland, Barre, Montpelier (the state capital and the least populous state capital in the United States), Winooski, St. Albans, Newport, Vergennes and Essex Junction (incorporated as a city in 2022, after separating from the town of Essex). Vergennes, chartered in 1788, is often described as the oldest and smallest city in Vermont, and one of the smallest cities in the United States.

Context

Most Vermont communities are towns — there are 247 towns and cities in total — run by selectboards and the traditional New England town meeting, so the handful of cities govern a modest share of the state's population of about 648,000.
