White's

White's
Formation: 1693
Type: Gentlemen's club
Location: 37–38 St James's Street, London
Membership: Men only

White's is a gentlemen's club in St James's, London, regarded as the oldest and most famous of London's clubs. It was founded in 1693 as White's Chocolate House by Italian immigrant Francesco Bianco (anglicised as Francis White). Situated at 37–38 St James's Street since 1778, the club has historically been associated with the Conservative Party and with the British aristocracy and political establishment.

History

Chocolate houses were fashionable meeting places in late seventeenth-century London. White's quickly became known as a gambling den for the fashionable and wealthy; its famous betting book records wagers on everything from political outcomes to matters of life and death. Under proprietor Robert Arthur in the mid-eighteenth century the establishment formalised into a private members' club. The club's bow window became an emblem of Regency dandyism, associated with Beau Brummell and his circle.

Membership

Membership has historically been male-only and highly exclusive; notable members have included prime ministers and royalty. The club is known for its traditional atmosphere, backgammon, and card play. In modern times it has remained one of the most socially exclusive clubs in London, with a long waiting list and election by ballot.
