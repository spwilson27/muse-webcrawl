The Sukhoi Su-15 (NATO reporting name: Flagon) is a twin-engined supersonic interceptor aircraft developed by the Soviet Union in the 1960s. It served as one of the mainstays of the Soviet Air Defence Forces (PVO), replacing the earlier Su-11 and complementing the Su-9 family, and remained in service into the 1990s.

Design and development

The Su-15 combined a lengthened fuselage and two Tumansky R-11 (later R-13 and R-25) turbojets with a cropped delta wing and side intakes, freeing the nose for a powerful radar. It was armed exclusively with air-to-air missiles — typically R-98 (AA-3 'Anab') medium-range missiles, later supplemented by R-60 (AA-8 'Aphid') short-range missiles — and, in later variants, a GSh-23L cannon pod carried under the fuselage. Maximum speed was about Mach 2.1, with an operational ceiling around 18,000 m.

Operational history

About 1,290 Su-15s were built between 1966 and 1980. The type was never exported and was reserved for Soviet home air defence. It is best known internationally for its involvement in the shootdowns of Korean Air Lines Flight 902 in 1978 (forced down near Murmansk) and Flight 007 in 1983, when an Su-15 shot down a Boeing 747 south of Sakhalin Island, killing all 269 people aboard.

Variants

Major variants included the Su-15 (Flagon-A), the two-seat Su-15UT trainer, the improved Su-15T and Su-15TM (Flagon-E/F) with the Taifun radar and ogival nose radome, and the Su-15UM two-seat trainer.
