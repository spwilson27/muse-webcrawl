Three-day eventing

[Hero image placeholder: horse and rider jumping a cross-country fence at Badminton Horse Trials.]

Three-day eventing (also known simply as eventing) is an equestrian event where a single horse and rider combination compete against others across the three disciplines of dressage, cross-country and show jumping. This event has its roots in a comprehensive cavalry test that required mastery of several types of riding.

Phases

Traditionally run over three days, the competition begins with dressage, testing harmony and obedience; continues with cross-country, in which horse and rider gallop over a long course of solid natural fences, water complexes and drops against the clock; and concludes with show jumping, where fences can be knocked down, testing the horse's remaining agility after the endurance phase. Scores are given in penalty points, and the combination with the lowest total wins.

Levels and major competitions

The sport is governed internationally by the FEI, with star levels from one-star (introductory) to five-star (championship). The five-star classics include Badminton and Burghley in England, Kentucky in the United States, Luhmühlen in Germany, Pau in France and Adelaide in Australia. Eventing has been an Olympic sport since 1912.
