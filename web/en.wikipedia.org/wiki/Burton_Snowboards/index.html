Burton Snowboards is a privately held American snowboard manufacturer founded in 1977 by Jake Burton Carpenter in a barn in Londonderry, Vermont. The company is credited with helping to popularize snowboarding and turn it into a mainstream international sport, and for many years it has been the largest snowboard company in the world.

History

Carpenter, inspired by Sherman Poppen's Snurfer, began building his own boards with bindings and sought to convince ski resorts, which initially banned snowboards, to allow them on lifts. Burton's lobbying helped open resorts to snowboarders during the 1980s. The company sponsored the U.S. Open Snowboarding Championships, one of the sport's premier events, for over three decades. Burton products are manufactured in Vermont at its Craig's prototype facility and by partner factories abroad, including in Austria and China.

Team and influence

Burton has sponsored many of the sport's most prominent riders, including Shaun White, Danny Davis, Kelly Clark, Chloe Kim, and Mark McMorris. Jake Burton Carpenter died in November 2019; the company is run by his family, with his wife Donna Carpenter having served as CEO and later chair of the board. Burton is a certified B Corporation and has been prominent in sustainability initiatives within the outdoor industry.
