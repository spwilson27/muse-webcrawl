Tissaphernes (Old Persian: Ciθrafarnah; died 395 BC) was a Persian soldier and statesman, satrap of Lydia and Ionia under the Achaemenid Empire, and a central figure in Persia's dealings with the Greek world during the Peloponnesian War.

Career

Tissaphernes suppressed the revolt of Pissuthnes and succeeded him in Lydia around 413 BC. Ordered to collect tribute from the Greek cities of Ionia, he negotiated alliances with Sparta against Athens, though his shifting support and pay cuts for Spartan crews — encouraged by Alcibiades — frustrated his allies.

Rivalry with Cyrus the Younger

After warning Artaxerxes II of his brother Cyrus's ambitions, Tissaphernes fought at the Battle of Cunaxa (401 BC), where Cyrus was killed. He afterwards treacherously seized the generals of the Greek Ten Thousand at a parley, an act that made him infamous in Greek sources such as Xenophon's Anabasis.

Downfall

Defeated by the Spartan king Agesilaus II in Lydia, Tissaphernes fell from favor; the queen mother Parysatis, blaming him for Cyrus's death, engineered his disgrace. He was executed at Colossae in 395 BC.
