Philadelphia Flyers

Founded: 1967
Arena: Wells Fargo Center
Colours: Orange, black, white
Stanley Cups: 1973–74, 1974–75

The Flyers have played in Philadelphia since 1967.

The Philadelphia Flyers are a professional ice hockey team based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Metropolitan Division in the Eastern Conference. The Flyers were founded in 1967 as one of the "Second Six" expansion teams, and they became the first expansion-era club to win the Stanley Cup, winning back-to-back championships in 1974 and 1975.

The Broad Street Bullies

The championship teams of the 1970s, built by coach Fred Shero and captained by Bobby Clarke, became famous as the "Broad Street Bullies" for a physical, intimidating style of play that drew both popular devotion in Philadelphia and controversy elsewhere. Anchored by goaltender Bernie Parent, the Flyers defeated the Boston Bruins in 1974 and the Buffalo Sabres in 1975 to claim their two Cups.

Later years

Philadelphia returned to the Stanley Cup Final several times without winning — in 1976, 1980, 1985, 1987, 1997, and 2010, when they fell to the Chicago Blackhawks. Notable Flyers over the decades include Clarke, Parent, Mark Howe, Eric Lindros, John LeClair, and Claude Giroux. The team plays its home games at the Wells Fargo Center, and its rivalry with the Pittsburgh Penguins is among the most intense in the NHL.
