The San'yō region (山陽地方, San'yō Chihō) is an area in the southern part of Honshu, the main island of Japan, forming the southern half of the Chūgoku region. Its name means "sunny side of the mountains" — the southern slopes of the Chūgoku Mountains, facing the Seto Inland Sea — in contrast to the San'in region on the shaded northern side.

Geography

The region corresponds broadly to the modern prefectures of Okayama, Hiroshima and Yamaguchi, all fronting the Seto Inland Sea. Shielded by Shikoku and the Chūgoku Mountains, the coastal plain enjoys a comparatively mild, dry climate with little snow and modest rainfall. The inland-sea coast is dotted with islands and industrial port cities.

History

The name derives from the San'yōdō, one of the ancient circuits (Goki Shichidō) of feudal Japan, a highway linking the capital region with western Honshu and Kyushu. The route remains the region's main transport axis, followed today by the San'yō Main Line railway, the San'yō Shinkansen (opened in stages from 1972, reaching Hakata in 1975) and the San'yō Expressway.

Economy

San'yō is far more industrialised and urbanised than San'in. The Setouchi industrial belt hosts steelworks, petrochemical plants, shipbuilding and automobile manufacturing — including Mazda's headquarters and main plant in Hiroshima. Agriculture features rice, grapes, peaches and citrus; fisheries and oyster aquaculture are important in Hiroshima Bay. Tourism centres on Hiroshima's Peace Memorial Park and Atomic Bomb Dome, Miyajima's Itsukushima Shrine, Kurashiki's preserved Bikan quarter and the port of Shimonoseki at the western tip of Honshu.
