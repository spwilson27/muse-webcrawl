Submission wrestling, also known as submission grappling, is a combat sport and martial art discipline focused on clinch and ground fighting with the aim of obtaining a submission hold — a joint lock, choke, or other technique that forces the opponent to concede ("tap out"). Unlike Brazilian jiu-jitsu competition, submission wrestling is typically practised without the gi (uniform), drawing techniques from wrestling, judo, sambo, catch wrestling, and Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

Rules and competition

Matches may be decided by submission, points for dominant positions and takedowns, or referee decision. Rulesets vary widely: some allow heel hooks and other leg locks at advanced levels while restricting them for beginners. The premier event is the ADCC (Abu Dhabi Combat Club) Submission Fighting World Championship, founded in 1998 and held every two years, whose champions — including Roger Gracie, Marcelo Garcia, and Gordon Ryan — are regarded as among the greatest grapplers in the sport.

Relation to mixed martial arts

Submission wrestling is one of the core disciplines of mixed martial arts (MMA), supplying the ground-fighting component alongside striking arts and wrestling takedowns. Its growth closely tracks the popularity of the UFC and of no-gi Brazilian jiu-jitsu.
