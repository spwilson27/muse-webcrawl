The Grose Valley is a rugged valley in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, carved by the Grose River. It lies largely within Blue Mountains National Park and the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area, between the Great Western Highway corridor and the Bells Line of Road.

Geography

The valley is known for its dramatic sandstone cliffs, deep gorges and extensive eucalypt forest, including the Blue Gum Forest near its junction with Govetts Creek — a famous stand of tall blue gums whose preservation by bushwalkers in the 1930s was a landmark in Australian conservation history. Popular viewpoints include Govetts Leap and Evans Lookout near Blackheath, and the valley is crossed by historic walking tracks such as the Rodriguez Pass and the Grand Canyon track system.

History

The valley is named after Lieutenant (later Lieutenant-General) Francis Grose, lieutenant-governor of New South Wales in the 1790s. The area has deep significance for the Gundungurra and Darug Aboriginal peoples. In 2019–20 the valley was extensively burned in the Gospers Mountain bushfire, one of the largest forest fires in Australian recorded history, which began in the nearby Wollemi wilderness.
