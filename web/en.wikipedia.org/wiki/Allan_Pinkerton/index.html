Allan Pinkerton (25 August 1819 – 1 July 1884) was a Scottish-American detective and spy, best known for creating the Pinkerton National Detective Agency, the first detective agency in the United States.

Early life

Pinkerton was born in the Gorbals district of Glasgow, the son of a police sergeant. Trained as a cooper, he was active in the Chartist movement before emigrating to the United States in 1842, settling near Chicago. He stumbled into detective work after discovering a gang of counterfeiters in the woods while gathering barrel hoops, and was appointed a deputy sheriff of Cook County.

The agency

In 1850 he founded the North-Western Police Agency, soon renamed Pinkerton's National Detective Agency, whose trademark was a wide-open eye and the motto "We Never Sleep". The agency pioneered practices such as the rogues' gallery of criminal photographs, undercover work and the protection of railroad freight. Pinkerton agents guarded President-elect Abraham Lincoln in 1861 and foiled the alleged Baltimore Plot to assassinate him on his way to his inauguration.

Civil War and after

During the Civil War Pinkerton served under the alias "Major E. J. Allen" as head of the Union's secret service under General George B. McClellan, though his organisation's estimates of Confederate strength were notoriously inflated. After the war the agency pursued train robbers and outlaw gangs, including the Reno Gang and the James–Younger Gang, and later became notorious among workers for strikebreaking and infiltrating labour unions, a role that continued under his sons after his death in 1884.
