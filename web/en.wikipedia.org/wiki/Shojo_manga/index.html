Shojo manga

Shōjo manga (少女漫画, "girls' comics") is an editorial category of Japanese comics targeting an audience of adolescent girls and young adult women. It is, along with shōnen manga, seinen manga, and josei manga, one of the main editorial categories of manga and is typically characterized by a focus on personal and romantic relationships, emotional development, and ornate visual style.

Characteristics

Shojo manga historically featured flowing panel layouts, decorative screen tones, large expressive eyes, and symbolic imagery such as flowers and sparkles. Story subjects range from romance and school life to historical drama, fantasy, horror, and science fiction. Major magazines include Nakayoshi, Ribon, Margaret, Hana to Yume, and Sho-Comi.

History

The category originated with early 20th-century girls' magazines and prewar illustrators, and was transformed after World War II. In the 1970s the Year 24 Group of women artists—among them Moto Hagio, Keiko Takemiya, and Riyoko Ikeda—revolutionized the medium with psychologically complex works such as The Rose of Versailles, They Were Eleven, and The Heart of Thomas. Landmark later series include Sailor Moon, Fruits Basket, Nana, Cardcaptor Sakura, and Boys Over Flowers.

Note: this article covers the unaccented romanization "Shojo manga"; the category is also written shōjo manga.
