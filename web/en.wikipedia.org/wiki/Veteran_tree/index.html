Veteran tree

A veteran tree is a tree that, because of its great age, size or condition, is of exceptional value for wildlife, landscape or cultural heritage. The term is used particularly in the United Kingdom, which has an unusually high proportion of Europe's oldest trees, many of them ancient oaks and yews in parkland, churchyards and former royal forests.

Characteristics

Veteran features develop with age: hollow trunks, dead branches in the crown, peeling bark, sap runs and fungal fruiting bodies. Far from being worthless decay, these features support rich communities of specialised fungi, lichens, invertebrates — such as stag beetles and rare saproxylic flies — and roosting bats and birds.

Conservation

"Ancient" trees are the oldest of their species; "veteran" describes the habitat features, which can also occur in younger trees damaged by storms or pollarding. Conservation practice retains dead wood, avoids over-tidy management, and may carry out gradual "retrenchment" pruning to mimic natural crown reduction. Registers such as the Ancient Tree Inventory record and help protect notable specimens including the Major Oak and the Fortingall Yew.
