Otoshidama (Japanese: お年玉) is a Japanese New Year tradition in which adults give money to children during the New Year holiday period. The money is presented in small decorated envelopes called pochibukuro, often printed with seasonal or cartoon designs. Otoshidama is one of the most anticipated events of the New Year for Japanese children and is broadly comparable to the money gifts given at Chinese New Year in red envelopes.

Origins

The custom derives from offerings made to Toshigami, the deity of the New Year: round rice cakes (kagami mochi) were offered to the god and then distributed to family members as toshidama, a sharing of the deity's blessing and spirit for the year. Over time the gift shifted from rice cakes and other presents to cash, and by the twentieth century money in envelopes had become standard.

Customs

Children receive otoshidama from parents, grandparents, aunts, uncles and sometimes family friends, usually until they finish schooling or reach adulthood. Amounts rise with the child's age—small sums for young children, and commonly ¥5,000–¥10,000 or more for teenagers. Etiquette dictates that bills should be crisp and folded neatly inside the envelope, and that children receive the gift with both hands and thanks. Many parents encourage children to save a portion of their otoshidama, making the custom an early lesson in money management.
