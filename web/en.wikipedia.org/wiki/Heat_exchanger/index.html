Heat exchanger

Heat exchanger
PurposeTransfer heat between two or more fluids
Common typesShell and tube, plate, finned tube, regenerative, adiabatic wheel
Flow arrangementsParallel flow, counter-flow, cross-flow
ApplicationsPower plants, chemical processing, HVAC, refrigeration, engines

A heat exchanger is a system used to transfer heat between a source and a working fluid, or between two or more fluids, without mixing them. Heat exchangers are used in both cooling and heating processes, and are ubiquitous in power generation, petroleum refining, chemical plants, air conditioning, refrigeration, sewage treatment, and internal combustion engines (as radiators and intercoolers).

Flow arrangement

Exchangers are classified by flow geometry. In counter-flow designs the fluids move in opposite directions, giving the highest effectiveness — the cold fluid can leave hotter than the exiting hot fluid. In parallel-flow designs both fluids enter at the same end and move together, while cross-flow exchangers pass one fluid perpendicular to the other, as in finned-tube coils.

Main types

The shell and tube exchanger — a bundle of tubes inside a cylindrical shell — is the workhorse of heavy industry, tolerating high pressures and temperatures. Plate heat exchangers stack corrugated metal plates to form channels, offering high heat-transfer coefficients in a compact footprint for HVAC and food processing. Other types include double-pipe, spiral, finned-tube (air-cooled), phase-change, and regenerative exchangers that store heat in a matrix, such as rotary air preheaters.

Design and fouling

Performance is analysed with the log mean temperature difference (LMTD) or effectiveness–NTU method, accounting for the overall heat-transfer coefficient and pressure drop. Over time, deposits of scale, corrosion products, or biological growth ("fouling") insulate surfaces and reduce performance, so exchangers are designed with fouling margins and periodic cleaning in mind. Nature provides models too: countercurrent exchange operates in fish gills, bird legs, and the rete mirabile of marine mammals.
