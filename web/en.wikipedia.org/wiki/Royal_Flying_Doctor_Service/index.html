The Royal Flying Doctor Service of Australia (RFDS), commonly known as the Flying Doctor, is one of the largest and most comprehensive aeromedical organisations in the world. It provides emergency and primary health care services to people in rural and remote areas of Australia.

History

Presbyterian minister John Flynn, inspired by stories of outback medical isolation and new radio and aviation technology, founded the Aerial Medical Service with Qantas in Cloncurry, Queensland in 1928. Alfred Traeger's pedal radio made outback communication practical. The service received the "Royal" prefix in 1955.

Operations

The RFDS operates a fleet of aircraft (including King Air and Pilatus PC-12/PC-24 jets) from bases across Australia, providing emergency retrieval, clinics, telehealth, dental and mental-health services to hundreds of thousands of patients yearly. It is depicted on the Australian $20 note (Flynn) and in the TV series The Flying Doctors.
