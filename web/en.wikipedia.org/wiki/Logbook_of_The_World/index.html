Logbook of The World

Logbook of The World (LoTW) is a web-based service maintained by the American Radio Relay League (ARRL) that allows amateur radio operators to confirm contacts ("QSOs") electronically, without exchanging paper QSL cards. Launched in 2003, it has become the principal electronic confirmation system in amateur radio.

How it works

Operators sign their log data with a digital certificate issued by the ARRL after verifying their identity and licence, and upload the signed records to the system. When both parties to a contact have uploaded matching records (callsign, band, mode, and time within a tolerance), the contact is marked as confirmed. Confirmations in LoTW count toward ARRL awards such as DXCC and the Worked All States award, and the system also supports awards administered by other organisations.

Security

Because award credit depends on the integrity of the records, LoTW uses public-key cryptography and certificate verification to prevent forged submissions — an approach that makes it more stringent than informal online logbooks while giving it quasi-official status for awards purposes. Millions of registered users and well over a billion uploaded QSO records are held in the system.
