BBC Radio 2 is a British national radio station owned and operated by the BBC, broadcasting on FM between 88.1 and 90.2 MHz. Launched on 30 September 1967 as the successor to the Light Programme, it is the most listened-to radio station in the United Kingdom, with a weekly audience of around 13–14 million, built on a broad adult-contemporary music mix, specialist music programmes and daytime talk aimed mainly at listeners aged 35 and over.

History and presenters

Radio 2 began as a middle-of-the-road service sharing much with the Light Programme. Long-running fixtures include the weekday breakfast show — presented for decades by Terry Wogan ("Wake Up to Wogan", 1993–2009), then Chris Evans (2010–2018) and Zoe Ball (2019–2024), with Scott Mills taking over in 2025 — and the Jeremy Vine current-affairs show at midday. Ken Bruce's mid-morning show and its "PopMaster" quiz were institutions until his 2023 move to commercial radio. Other notable voices include Bob Harris (country), Jo Whiley, Sara Cox, Trevor Nelson (evenings), and the late Steve Wright and Johnnie Walker.

The station also stages Radio 2 in the Park (formerly "Live in Hyde Park") and is the UK's principal outlet for the BBC's folk, jazz, blues and country coverage.
