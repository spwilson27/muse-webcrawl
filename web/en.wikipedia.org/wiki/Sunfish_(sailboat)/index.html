Sunfish (sailboat)

The Sunfish is a personality-sized, beach-launched sailing dinghy, notable for its low cost, simplicity, and ease of handling. Created in 1952 by Alcort, Inc., it is one of the most popular sailboats ever built, with more than 300,000 hulls produced, and was inducted into the American Sailboat Hall of Fame in 1995. The name and the fish emblem come from the ocean sunfish, which basks flat at the surface.

Design

The Sunfish is 13 feet 9 inches (4.19 m) long, built of fiberglass with a nearly flat, wide hull, a daggerboard, and a kick-up rudder. It carries a single lateen sail — a triangular sail on a long spar — of about 75 square feet, which allows the mast to be short and the rig to be struck and stored easily. The boat is sailed by one person, though it can carry two, and its one-design class rules keep boats nearly identical for racing.

History

The design grew out of Alcort's wooden Sailfish (1945), one of the original sailboard-type boats, designed by Alex Bryan and Cortlandt Heyniger — the company's name combining their first names. Designer Alvin "Al" Bryan and Cortlandt adapted the concept in fiberglass as the Sunfish in 1952. The class association was founded in 1963, and the first Sunfish World Championship was held in 1970; the class is recognized by World Sailing and the boat was a fixture of recreational fleets, sailing camps, and resorts worldwide.

Production

Builders have included Alcort, AMF Alcort, Pearson Yacht Company, and LaserPerformance, with production later passing to other manufacturers. The Sunfish's durability and vast second-hand market have made it a standard beginner's boat for more than seventy years.
