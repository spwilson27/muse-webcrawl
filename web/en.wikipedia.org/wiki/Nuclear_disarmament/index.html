Nuclear disarmament

Nuclear disarmament is the act of reducing or eliminating nuclear weapons, and the goal of a nuclear-weapons-free world. It can also refer to the process leading to complete global nuclear disarmament. Denuclearisation has been used for the partial elimination of arsenals or the creation of nuclear-weapon-free zones.

History

Calls for disarmament followed immediately upon the atomic bombings of 1945; the first UN General Assembly resolution in 1946 sought the elimination of atomic weapons. Mass movements, including Britain's Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND, founded 1958) and protests against nuclear testing, helped spur the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963) and the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT, 1968), whose Article VI commits parties to pursue negotiations toward disarmament. Bilateral US–Soviet/Russian agreements — SALT, the INF Treaty (1987), START I (1991) and New START (2010) — cut arsenals from a peak of roughly 70,000 warheads in 1986 to around 12,000–13,000 by the 2020s. South Africa dismantled its weapons, and Belarus, Kazakhstan and Ukraine gave up ex-Soviet weapons.

Recent developments

Frustrated by slow progress, non-nuclear states negotiated the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons, adopted in 2017 and in force from 2021, which bans nuclear weapons outright; no nuclear-armed state has joined. The disarmament movement and its diplomatic backers received Nobel Peace Prizes in 1982 (Alva Myrdal and Alfonso García Robles), 1995 (Joseph Rotblat and Pugwash) and 2017 (ICAN). Progress has since stalled or reversed: the INF Treaty collapsed in 2019, the ABM Treaty ended in 2002, and Russia suspended participation in New START in 2023 amid the war in Ukraine, while all nuclear-armed states modernise their forces.

Arguments

Supporters cite the catastrophic humanitarian consequences of any nuclear use and the risk of accident or escalation; critics argue nuclear deterrence preserved peace between great powers and that verified abolition is unenforceable.
