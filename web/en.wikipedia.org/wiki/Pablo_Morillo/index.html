Pablo Morillo y Morillo, Count of Cartagena and Marquess of La Puerta (1775–1837) was a Spanish general who fought in the Peninsular War and commanded the royalist expedition sent to suppress the independence movements in Spanish South America during the Spanish American wars of independence.

Early career

Born in Fuentesecas in the province of Zamora, Morillo began his military life as a sailor in the Spanish Navy, fighting at the Battle of Cape St Vincent and the Battle of Trafalgar, where he was captured. He later transferred to the army and distinguished himself in the Peninsular War against Napoleon's forces, rising rapidly through the ranks.

Expedition to South America

In 1815 Ferdinand VII appointed Morillo commander of a large expeditionary force of some 10,000 troops, the largest Spain ever sent to the Americas. Landing in Venezuela, he retook Cartagena de Indias after a brutal siege in which much of the city's population perished, and went on to reconquer New Granada, entering Bogotá in 1816. His campaign of severe repression, including the execution of patriot leaders, became notorious.

Later life

Morillo signed an armistice with Simón Bolívar in 1820, meeting him at Santa Ana de Trujillo, and returned to Spain the following year. He later served in the First Carlist War on the side of Isabella II and died in France in 1837.
