Justice K. S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India

CourtSupreme Court of India
Decided24 August 2017
Citation(2017) 10 SCC 1
BenchNine judges
PetitionerK. S. Puttaswamy, former judge of the Karnataka High Court

Justice K. S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2017) is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of India, in which a nine-judge bench unanimously held that the right to privacy is protected as an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution of India, and as part of the fundamental freedoms guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution.

Background

The case arose from challenges to the Aadhaar biometric identity programme. The petitioner, Justice K. S. Puttaswamy, a retired judge of the Karnataka High Court, argued that Aadhaar violated the right to privacy. Because earlier Supreme Court decisions, including M. P. Sharma v. Satish Chandra (1954) and Kharak Singh v. State of Uttar Pradesh (1962), had cast doubt on whether privacy was a fundamental right, the matter was referred to a nine-judge bench — one of the largest in the Court's history.

Judgment

On 24 August 2017, the bench of nine judges — Chief Justice J. S. Khehar and Justices J. Chelameswar, S. A. Bobde, R. K. Agrawal, Rohinton F. Nariman, A. M. Sapre, D. Y. Chandrachud, S. K. Kaul and Abdul Nazeer — unanimously affirmed that privacy is a fundamental right. Six separate opinions were delivered. The Court expressly overruled M. P. Sharma and Kharak Singh to the extent they held there was no fundamental right to privacy.

Justice Chandrachud's plurality opinion described privacy as including bodily integrity, personal autonomy, informational privacy, and the freedom to make intimate personal decisions. Justice Kaul's concurrence pointed to the challenges of the digital age and called for a data protection law.

Impact

The judgment laid the constitutional foundation for later rulings, including the partial striking-down of Section 377 (decriminalising homosexuality in Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India), the decriminalisation of adultery, and the 2018 Aadhaar judgment, in which Aadhaar itself was upheld with restrictions applying the proportionality test developed in the case. It also spurred the drafting of data protection legislation in India.
