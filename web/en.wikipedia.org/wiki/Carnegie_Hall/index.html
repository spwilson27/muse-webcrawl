Carnegie Hall

Carnegie Hall is a concert venue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, located at 881 Seventh Avenue. Designed by architect William Burnet Tuthill and built by philanthropist Andrew Carnegie, it opened in 1891 and is one of the most prestigious venues in the world for both classical music and popular music.

History

The hall opened on 5 May 1891 with a concert conducted by Walter Damrosch and Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky. It was the home of the New York Philharmonic until 1962. Threatened with demolition in the 1950s, it was purchased by the City of New York in 1960 after a campaign led by violinist Isaac Stern, and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1962.

Venues

The complex contains three performance spaces: the Stern Auditorium / Perelman Stage (2,804 seats), renowned for its acoustics; the smaller Zankel Hall (599 seats); and the intimate Weill Recital Hall (268 seats). A famous joke asks how to get to Carnegie Hall: "Practice, practice, practice."

Performances

Beyond classical music, the hall has hosted landmark jazz, folk, and popular concerts, including Benny Goodman's 1938 jazz concert and Judy Garland's celebrated 1961 performance.
