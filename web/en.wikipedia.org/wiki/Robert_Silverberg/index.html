Robert Silverberg


Born: January 15, 1935, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist, short-story writer
Genre: Science fiction, fantasy
Awards: Multiple Hugo and Nebula Awards

Robert Silverberg (born January 15, 1935) is a prolific American science fiction author and editor. He is a multiple winner of the Hugo and Nebula Awards and was named a Grand Master by the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America in 2005.

Career

Silverberg began publishing as a teenager and in the 1950s wrote enormous quantities of pulp fiction under various names. In the late 1960s, encouraged by editor Frederik Pohl, he turned to more ambitious work: Thorns (1967), The Man in the Maze (1969), Downward to the Earth (1970), A Time of Changes (1971) and Dying Inside (1972) are considered classics of literate, psychologically complex science fiction. His story "Nightwings" (1968) and the novel of the same name won major awards.

After a brief retirement in the late 1970s he returned with Lord Valentine's Castle (1980), opening the Majipoor cycle of science-fantasy epics set on a giant, multi-species world. Silverberg also edited landmark anthologies and wrote extensive nonfiction on history and archaeology.
