Chin Na

Chinese: 擒拿 (pinyin: qínná)

Meaning: "Seize and control"

Focus: Joint locks, grappling, pressure points

Chin Na (or Qinna; Chinese: 擒拿) is a set of techniques from Chinese martial arts used to capture or lock an opponent's joints or muscles and tendons, so that the opponent becomes immobilised or subdued. Chin means "to seize or trap" and na means "to lock or break". Chin na techniques appear in many Chinese styles rather than forming a single independent system.

Techniques

Chin na is commonly divided into categories such as dividing the muscle (or tendon), misplacing the bone, sealing the breath or vein, and cavity pressing (striking pressure points along the meridians described in traditional Chinese medicine). Finger, wrist, elbow and shoulder locks are especially characteristic. Training includes both the applications themselves and the counter-techniques (counter chin na) used to escape them.

Historical context

Seizing and locking techniques are documented in Chinese martial traditions for many centuries; the Shaolin tradition in particular is associated with systematising chin na. The eagle claw style (Ying Jow Pai) is noted for its emphasis on gripping and locking. Chin na concepts are considered an influence on later Japanese grappling arts such as jujutsu and, through it, arts such as aikido, which emphasise joint manipulation.
