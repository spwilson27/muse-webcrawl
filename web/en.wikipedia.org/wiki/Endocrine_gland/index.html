Endocrine gland

Endocrine glands are ductless glands of the endocrine system that secrete their products, hormones, directly into the blood. The major glands of the endocrine system include the pineal gland, pituitary gland, pancreas, ovaries, testes, thyroid gland, parathyroid gland, hypothalamus and adrenal glands. Unlike exocrine glands, endocrine glands have no ducts and release their secretions into the surrounding interstitial fluid, from which they diffuse into capillaries.

Major glands

  • Pituitary gland – often called the "master gland"; secretes growth hormone, prolactin, and several tropic hormones that regulate other glands.
  • Thyroid gland – secretes thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), which regulate metabolic rate, and calcitonin.
  • Parathyroid glands – secrete parathyroid hormone, regulating blood calcium levels.
  • Adrenal glands – the cortex secretes corticosteroids such as cortisol and aldosterone; the medulla secretes adrenaline and noradrenaline.
  • Pancreas – its islets of Langerhans secrete insulin and glucagon, regulating blood glucose; the pancreas is both an endocrine and an exocrine gland.
  • Gonads – the ovaries secrete oestrogen and progesterone; the testes secrete testosterone.
  • Pineal gland – secretes melatonin, involved in regulating circadian rhythms.
  • Hypothalamus – links the nervous and endocrine systems, secreting releasing and inhibiting hormones that control the pituitary.

Function

Hormones secreted by endocrine glands travel through the bloodstream to target cells that bear specific receptors. Through these chemical signals the endocrine system regulates metabolism, growth and development, tissue function, sexual function, reproduction, sleep and mood, among other processes. Its activity is commonly controlled by feedback loops, in which the level of a hormone or of the substance it regulates feeds back to stimulate or inhibit further secretion.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of endocrine glands can cause diseases from overproduction (hyperfunction) or underproduction (hypofunction) of hormones, such as diabetes mellitus, hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, Addison's disease and Cushing's syndrome. The medical specialty concerned with these disorders is endocrinology.
