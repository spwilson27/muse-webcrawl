Thinis (Egyptian: Tjeni) was an ancient Egyptian city, the legendary capital of the Thinite Confederacy and the ancestral home of the pharaohs of the First and Second Dynasties. Despite its importance in Egyptian tradition, the city has never been conclusively identified or excavated; it is thought to lie near modern Girga, on the east bank of the Nile in Upper Egypt, close to Abydos, where the earliest kings were buried.

History

According to the later Egyptian historian Manetho, the first two dynasties of a united Egypt were "Thinite" — originating from Thinis. Tradition held that Menes (often identified with Narmer or Hor-Aha), the unifier of Upper and Lower Egypt, came from Thinis, and the royal tombs at Umm el-Qa'ab in Abydos, from Narmer through the Second Dynasty, anchored the dynasty's origins in the region. After the capital moved north to Memphis, Thinis remained a significant provincial town and the centre of the cult of the war god Anhur (Onuris) and his lioness consort Mehit. By the New Kingdom it was capital of the eighth nome of Upper Egypt.

The search for Thinis

Because no securely identified remains have been found, Thinis is one of the most sought-after lost cities of Egypt. Proposed locations cluster around el-Birba and the area north of Abydos; deeply buried or flooded remains, and the shifting Nile, have hampered discovery. Mentions of Thinis in texts — including descriptions of its wealth in the Late Period and its role in the myth of Osiris, whose cult centred on nearby Abydos — attest its long-lived importance in Egyptian memory.
