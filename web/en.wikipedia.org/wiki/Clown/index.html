A clown is a person who performs comedy and arts in a state of open-mindedness using physical comedy, typically while wearing distinct makeup or costuming and reversing folkway norms. The art of clowning has existed for many centuries. The most recognizable clowns are those found in the modern circus.

History

Clown-like figures appear in many ancient cultures: the dwarf jesters of ancient Egypt, the comic actors of ancient Greece and Rome, and the pagliaccio and other stock characters of the Italian commedia dell'arte. In the English-speaking world, Joseph Grimaldi (1778–1837) transformed the role in pantomime, and the sad-faced "Joey" clown derives from his name. The modern circus clown developed in the 19th century alongside the travelling circus.

Types

The three classic types are the whiteface clown, elegant and authoritative; the auguste, the clumsy fool whose antics disrupt the whiteface's order; and the character clown, who adopts a recognizable occupation or personality such as a tramp or policeman. Famous tramp clowns include Charlie Chaplin's Little Tramp and Emmett Kelly's "Weary Willie".

Performance

Clowns use slapstick, juggling, balloon modelling, mime, and audience interaction. Clowning is also used therapeutically: hospital "clown doctors" visit sick children, and humanitarian clowning is performed in refugee camps and disaster zones.
