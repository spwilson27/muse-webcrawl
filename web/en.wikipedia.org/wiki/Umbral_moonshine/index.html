Umbral moonshine

Umbral moonshine is a mysterious connection between the theory of Niemeier lattices, mock modular forms, and finite groups, discovered around 2010 by Miranda Cheng, John Duncan, and Jeffrey Harvey. It generalizes the celebrated monstrous moonshine, which links the Monster group to modular functions.

Background

Monstrous moonshine showed that the Fourier coefficients of the j-function could be decomposed into dimensions of representations of the Monster group, the largest sporadic simple group. Umbral moonshine extends this phenomenon to all 23 Niemeier lattices—the even unimodular lattices of rank 24 containing root systems. For each such lattice, the quotient of the Leech lattice's symmetry group structure gives rise to a finite group called an umbral group.

The correspondence

For each Niemeier lattice, umbral moonshine associates a set of mock modular forms whose Fourier coefficients decompose into dimensions of representations of the corresponding umbral group. The mock modular forms involved are related to theta functions and Appell–Lerch sums, and appear naturally in the elliptic genera of K3 surfaces. The case associated with the A124 lattice recovers the Mathieu moonshine observed earlier by Eguchi, Ooguri, and Tachikawa, connecting the Mathieu group M24 to K3 elliptic genera.

Developments

The umbral moonshine conjectures were largely proven in the following decade, with constructions using vertex operator algebras proposed to explain the correspondences. The subject sits at the intersection of number theory, algebra, and string theory, suggesting deep hidden symmetries of K3 surfaces and related conformal field theories.
