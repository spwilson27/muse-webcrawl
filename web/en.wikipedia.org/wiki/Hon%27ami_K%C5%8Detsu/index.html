Hon'ami Kōetsu

Hon'ami Kōetsu (1558 – 27 February 1637) was a Japanese craftsman, potter, lacquerer, metalworker and calligrapher of the early Edo period, regarded as one of the founders of the Rinpa school of Japanese art.

Life and work

Born into a Kyoto family of sword connoisseurs and polishers, Kōetsu became a leading figure in the cultural life of the city. He excelled in calligraphy, produced celebrated tea-ceremony ceramics in the raku tradition, and designed lacquerware inlaid with lead, tin and mother-of-pearl. With the painter Tawaraya Sōtatsu he collaborated on decorated poem sheets and handscrolls combining painting and calligraphy.

Takagamine

In 1615 Kōetsu was granted land at Takagamine, northwest of Kyoto, where he established an artists' community of craftsmen and townsmen devoted to art, tea and publishing, including deluxe editions of classical literature (the Saga-bon).
