Dow Jones Transportation Average

The Dow Jones Transportation Average (DJTA, also called the "Dow Jones Transports") is a U.S. stock market index maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices. It is the oldest stock index in use in the United States, first calculated by Charles Dow in 1884, and tracks 20 large transportation companies.


The DJTA tracks airlines, railroads, trucking and delivery companies.

Composition

The 20 components span airlines, railroads, trucking, marine shipping and air freight/delivery services, including companies such as Union Pacific, FedEx, UPS and major U.S. airlines. Like the Dow Jones Industrial Average, it is price-weighted.

Dow Theory

The index plays a central role in Dow Theory, which holds that the Transportation Average must "confirm" movements in the Industrial Average for a market trend to be valid — on the reasoning that industrials make goods and transports move them, so both should prosper together in a healthy economy.
