SettingHangzhou, West Lake; Jinshan Temple, Zhenjiang
Main charactersBai Suzhen, Xu Xian, Xiaoqing, Fahai
Earliest literary formFeng Menglong, Stories to Caution the World (1624)

The Legend of the White Snake is one of China's most famous folk tales, counted among the Four Great Folktales of China alongside The Butterfly Lovers, The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl and Lady Meng Jiang. Set by the West Lake in Hangzhou, it tells of the love between a young man and a woman who is secretly a white snake spirit. In 2011 the legend, as told in Hangzhou, was inscribed on China's national list of intangible cultural heritage.

Story

Bai Suzhen, a white snake who has cultivated herself for a thousand years and gained human form and magical powers, comes to the mortal world with her sworn sister Xiaoqing, a green snake. At the Broken Bridge over West Lake she meets the gentle young herbalist Xu Xian, and they fall in love and marry, opening a medicine shop together. The abbot Fahai of Jinshan Temple, who sees Bai Suzhen's true nature, is determined to separate them. He warns Xu Xian, who tricks his wife into drinking realgar wine at the Dragon Boat Festival; the wine forces her back into serpent form, and Xu Xian dies of fright. Bai Suzhen journeys to Mount Emei to steal the immortal herb that revives him.

After further struggles — in which Bai Suzhen and Xiaoqing flood Jinshan Temple in a magical battle with Fahai — Bai Suzhen is finally defeated and imprisoned beneath the Leifeng Pagoda on the shore of West Lake. In later, softened versions of the tale she is eventually freed through the devotion of her son, Xu Shilin, who becomes a top scholar (zhuangyuan), and the family is reunited; in some endings the couple attain immortality.

Development of the tale

Early precursors appear in Tang and Song dynasty story collections, in which the snake woman is a dangerous seductress. The classic literary version is Feng Menglong's story "The White Maiden Locked for Eternity in the Leifeng Pagoda" in Stories to Caution the World (1624). Over the Qing dynasty, in tanci ballads and regional opera, Bai Suzhen was gradually transformed from a妖怪 (yaoguai, demon) to be feared into a sympathetic heroine of true love resisting feudal and religious oppression — the form in which the tale is beloved today.

Adaptations

The legend is a staple of Chinese opera — Peking opera, Kunqu, Yue opera and Cantonese opera all have signature versions. It has been filmed many times, notably the Huangmei opera film of 1962, the Shaw Brothers film Madame White Snake (1962), Tsui Hark's Green Snake (1993, from Lilian Lee's novel), the hit Taiwanese television series New Legend of Madame White Snake (1992), and the animated feature White Snake (2019) and its sequel. The story also inspired the Japanese animated film The Tale of the White Serpent (Hakujaden, 1958).
