From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zhang Zuolin (張作霖)

Born: 19 March 1875, Haicheng, Liaoning

Died: 4 June 1928, Shenyang (assassinated)

Known as: The "Old Marshal" (da shuai); ruler of Manchuria

Head of state: 1927–1928 (President of the Republic of China, Beiyang government)

Son: Zhang Xueliang

Zhang Zuolin (1875–1928) was a Chinese warlord who dominated Manchuria from 1916 until his death and, in 1927–28, headed the Beiyang government in Beijing as the last internationally recognized head of state of the Republic of China before the Nationalist victory. A former bandit (honghuzi) who rose through the Fengtian Army, he built Manchuria's Three Eastern Provinces into a semi-independent state with its own army, arsenals, railways, and currency.

Rise to power

After the 1911 Revolution Zhang consolidated control of Fengtian province and by 1919 ruled all of Manchuria. His Fengtian clique fought the two Zhili–Fengtian Wars (1922 and 1924) for control of north China; victorious in the second, he installed governments in Beijing, and in 1927, as commander of the "National Pacification Army," he became head of state. Though heavily dependent on Japanese support through the Kwantung Army, he resisted Japanese demands for concessions in Manchuria.

Assassination

Defeated by Chiang Kai-shek's Northern Expedition, Zhang abandoned Beijing in June 1928 and travelled by train toward Shenyang (Mukden). On 4 June 1928, officers of the Japanese Kwantung Army who considered him too independent blew up his train at Huanggutun station. The Huanggutun Incident shocked China and Japan; Zhang's son, Zhang Xueliang, succeeded him and soon declared allegiance to the Nationalist government, completing China's nominal reunification.
