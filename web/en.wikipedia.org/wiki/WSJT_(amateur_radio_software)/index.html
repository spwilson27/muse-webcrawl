WSJT

Developer: Joe Taylor (K1JT) and the WSJT Development Group

Initial release: 2001

Operating system: Windows, Linux, macOS

Type: Amateur radio weak-signal communication software

License: GNU General Public License

WSJT (Weak Signal Communications, by K1JT) is a suite of computer programs used for weak-signal radio communication between amateur radio operators. The software was developed by Joe Taylor, K1JT, a Nobel Prize–winning astrophysicist and amateur radio operator at Princeton University, and was first released in 2001. Its successor package, WSJT-X, is freely available as open-source software.

Modes

The WSJT programs implement a family of digital protocols designed to exchange messages at signal-to-noise ratios far below those usable by voice or Morse code. Early modes such as FSK441 (for meteor scatter), JT65 and JT9 were widely used for VHF/UHF moonbounce (EME) and low-power HF communication. WSJT-X introduced the highly popular FT8 mode in 2017, developed by Taylor and Steve Franke, which uses 15-second transmissions and can decode signals as weak as −21 dB relative to the noise in a 2,500 Hz bandwidth. FT8 quickly became the most-used digital mode on the amateur HF bands. Related modes include FT4, WSPR (for propagation probing), Q65 and MSK144.

Technical approach

The modes combine strong forward error correction (convolutional, LDPC and other codes), structured message formats and precise computer-controlled timing synchronised to UTC, allowing reliable decoding of signals buried in noise. WSJT-X also supports automatic sequence operation, in which standard contact exchanges proceed with minimal operator intervention.

Impact

FT8 and its relatives transformed amateur radio activity, enabling contacts during poor propagation conditions and with modest stations, while also prompting debate within the hobby about the social character of highly automated operating.
