Galloanserae

Galloanserae is a superorder of birds containing the two extant orders Galliformes (gamefowl, including chickens, quails, turkeys and pheasants) and Anseriformes (waterfowl, including ducks, geese and swans). Together these groups comprise some of the most familiar and economically important birds in the world. Galloanserae is widely regarded as one of the most ancient lineages of living birds, with a fossil record extending back into the Late Cretaceous.

Evolution

Molecular and morphological evidence consistently places Galloanserae among the earliest diverging branches of the crown group Neornithes. Fossils such as Vegavis iaai from Antarctica, dated to about 66–68 million years ago, suggest that anseriform-like birds were already present before the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event. The gamefowl and waterfowl lineages are thought to have diverged during the Cretaceous period.

Characteristics

Members of Galloanserae are generally ground-feeding or water-associated birds. Galliformes are typically stocky, with strong legs adapted for scratching the ground, while Anseriformes possess webbed feet and flattened bills suited to aquatic feeding. Both groups show a characteristic arrangement of the palate bones that distinguishes them from other neognath birds.

Relationship to humans

The chicken, domestic duck and domestic goose — all galloanserans — rank among the most important domesticated animals, providing meat, eggs and feathers. Many wild species are also hunted as game.
