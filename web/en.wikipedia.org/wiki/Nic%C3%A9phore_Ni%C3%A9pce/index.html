Nicéphore Niépce

Born7 March 1765, Chalon-sur-Saône, France
Died5 July 1833, Saint-Loup-de-Varennes, France
Known forInventing photography (heliography)

Joseph Nicéphore Niépce (7 March 1765 – 5 July 1833) was a French inventor, usually credited as the inventor of photography and a pioneer in that field. He developed heliography, a technique used to create the oldest surviving photograph of the real world, View from the Window at Le Gras, made in 1826 or 1827.

Early life

Niépce was born into a wealthy family in Chalon-sur-Saône. He served as a staff officer in the French army under Napoleon and later worked as an administrator in Nice before returning to his family estate to conduct scientific research with his elder brother Claude.

Heliography

Working with light-sensitive materials, Niépce coated pewter plates with bitumen of Judea dissolved in lavender oil. After exposures lasting hours or days in a camera obscura, the unhardened bitumen was washed away to leave a permanent image. His 1826/1827 view from his estate at Le Gras is the earliest surviving camera photograph.

Partnership and legacy

In 1829 Niépce entered a partnership with Louis Daguerre, who after Niépce's death developed the daguerreotype process. Niépce also worked with his brother on the Pyréolophore, an early internal combustion engine.
