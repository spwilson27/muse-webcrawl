Dark Souls is a 2011 action role-playing game developed by FromSoftware and directed by Hidetaka Miyazaki. It is the spiritual successor to Demon's Souls (2009) and the first game of the Dark Souls trilogy. Set in the decaying kingdom of Lordran, the player controls a cursed undead protagonist seeking to link the fire and end the Age of Dark — or not.

Gameplay

The game is renowned for its demanding but fair combat, stamina-based fighting, interconnected world design and sparse, environmental storytelling delivered through item descriptions and cryptic NPC dialogue. Players collect "souls" as both currency and experience, losing them on death with one chance to recover them. Bonfires serve as checkpoints. Innovative online features include messages left for other players, co-operative summoning, and invasions by hostile players. Defeated bosses and areas such as Blighttown and Anor Londo became gaming touchstones.

Reception and legacy

Dark Souls was critically acclaimed, selling millions of copies; a remaster appeared in 2018. Its influence spawned the "Soulslike" genre and the success of later FromSoftware titles such as Bloodborne, Sekiro and Elden Ring. The phrase "praise the sun", from the character Solaire of Astora, became an enduring meme. See also Soulslike.
