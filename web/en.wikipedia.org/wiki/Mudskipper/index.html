Mudskipper

FamilyGobiidae (subfamily Oxudercinae)
HabitatIntertidal mudflats, mangroves
DistributionIndo-Pacific and West African coasts

Mudskippers are amphibious fish of the goby subfamily Oxudercinae, including genera such as Periophthalmus and Boleophthalmus. They are famous for spending much of their time out of water on intertidal mudflats, where they move about, feed, and interact with one another.

Adaptations

Mudskippers breathe through their skin and the lining of the mouth and throat, and they carry water in enlarged gill chambers to keep their gills moist. Their pectoral fins are muscular and crutch-like, allowing them to "walk" or skip across mud. Eyes set high on the head provide good vision in air.

Behaviour

On the mudflats they graze on algae or hunt small invertebrates, depending on species. Males dig burrows used for shelter and breeding, and display with raised dorsal fins and jumping to attract mates and defend territories. At high tide they retreat to burrows or climb onto mangrove roots.

Distribution

Most species live along tropical and subtropical coasts of the Indo-Pacific; the Atlantic mudskipper occurs on the West African coast.
