Short-range ballistic missile

A short-range ballistic missile (SRBM) is a ballistic missile with a range of about 1,000 km (620 mi) or less. In past and potential regional conflicts, these missiles have been and would be used because of the short distances between some countries and their relative low cost and ease of configuration.

Characteristics

SRBMs are typically tactical or theater weapons intended to strike targets such as airfields, troop concentrations, and infrastructure within a battlefield region. They are usually road-mobile, using transporter erector launcher (TEL) vehicles, and may carry conventional, chemical, or nuclear warheads. In modern warfare they fill the role below medium-range ballistic missiles (MRBMs). The INF Treaty (1987–2019) banned ground-launched missiles with ranges of 500–5,500 km between the United States and Russia.

Examples

Notable SRBMs include the Soviet/Russian Scud family and its many derivatives (such as North Korea's Hwasong series), the Russian Iskander (SS-26), the American MGM-140 ATACMS, China's DF-11 and DF-15, India's Prithvi and Pralay, Pakistan's Abdali and Ghaznavi, and Iran's Fateh-110 family.
