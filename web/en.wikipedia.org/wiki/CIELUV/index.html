CIELUV is a color space adopted by the International Commission on Illumination (CIE) in 1976, along with CIELAB. It is a transformation of the CIE XYZ color space designed so that equal Euclidean distances correspond approximately to equal perceived differences in color — that is, it aims at perceptual uniformity.

The CIELUV chromaticity plane for a fixed lightness.

Definition

A color in CIELUV is specified by lightness L* and chromatic coordinates u* and v*. L* is a cube-root function of the relative luminance, matching the perceived lightness scale. The u* and v* coordinates are computed from the CIE 1976 uniform chromaticity scale (UCS) diagram coordinates u′ and v′, scaled by L* relative to a specified reference white.

Because CIELUV preserves the linear chromaticity diagram of the u′v′ space, additive mixtures of colors fall on straight lines in it, a property that makes CIELUV particularly useful for applications involving colored lights, such as display and lighting calculations. CIELAB, by contrast, is generally preferred for surface and object colors.

Color difference

The color difference between two colors in CIELUV, denoted ΔE*uv, is simply the Euclidean distance between their (L*, u*, v*) coordinates. Cylindrical versions of the space define chroma C*uv and hue angle huv, analogous to the LCh coordinates of CIELAB.
