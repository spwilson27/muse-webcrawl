Special education in the United States

Special education in the United States refers to publicly funded instruction designed to meet the needs of students with disabilities. Its legal foundation is the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), first enacted in 1975 as the Education for All Handicapped Children Act, which guarantees eligible children a free appropriate public education (FAPE).

Key principles

Under IDEA, schools must identify and evaluate children suspected of having disabilities ("Child Find"), provide services in the least restrictive environment (LRE), and develop for each eligible student an Individualized Education Program (IEP) setting goals, accommodations, and services. Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act and the Americans with Disabilities Act provide additional protections, with 504 plans covering students who need accommodations but not specialized instruction.

Scope

Roughly 7.5 million children — about 15 percent of public school students — receive services under IDEA, across categories including specific learning disabilities, speech or language impairments, autism, intellectual disability, and emotional disturbance. Ongoing debates concern funding levels, inclusion practices, discipline disparities, and the identification of gifted students with disabilities ("twice exceptional").
