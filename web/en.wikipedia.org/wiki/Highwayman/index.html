Highwayman

A highwayman was a robber who stole from travellers, typically on horseback on the roads. Highwaymen flourished in England from the Elizabethan era until the early 19th century, and were often romanticised in popular literature despite the brutality of their crimes.

History

Highway robbery became a serious problem in 17th- and 18th-century England as road travel increased. Famous highwaymen include Dick Turpin, Claude Duval, and James MacLaine. The cry "Stand and deliver!" is associated with their demands for money or valuables. Highway robbery was a capital offence, and many highwaymen ended their lives at the gallows, notably at Tyburn.

Decline

The practice declined with the spread of turnpike roads with toll gates, mounted patrols, improved policing, and the growth of banking, which reduced the amount of cash carried by travellers. Similar figures existed elsewhere, including bushrangers in Australia and road agents in the American West.
