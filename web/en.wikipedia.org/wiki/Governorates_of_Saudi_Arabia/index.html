Governorates of Saudi Arabia

The thirteen regions of Saudi Arabia are subdivided into governorates.

The governorates of Saudi Arabia (Arabic: محافظات, muḥāfaẓāt) are the second-level administrative divisions of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. The country's thirteen regions (manaṭiq, often called provinces or emirates) are each subdivided into a number of governorates, which are in turn subdivided into administrative "centres" (marākiz, singular markaz) and, below them, villages and localities.

Structure

The system was formalised by the Law of Regions issued by King Fahd in 1992 (Royal Order A/92, 1412 AH), which also created regional councils. In total there are roughly 136 governorates, although the number changes as new governorates are created, upgraded or merged by royal decree.

Governorates are officially classified by category according to the services available in them:

  • Category A governorates — the larger and more populous.
  • Category B governorates — smaller governorates.

Each governorate is administered by a governor (muḥāfiẓ) appointed by royal order on the recommendation of the Minister of Interior and the regional emir. Governors of Category A governorates hold the eleventh grade of the civil service scale, while Category B governors hold the eighth grade. The regional capital city of each region is typically administered not as a governorate but as a municipality (amāna) headed by a mayor — for example Riyadh, Jeddah, Mecca and Medina.

Examples by region

  • Riyadh Region — around twenty governorates, including Diriyah, Al Kharj, Al Zulfi, Shaqra, Dawadmi, Al Majma'ah and Wadi ad-Dawasir.
  • Makkah Region — includes Jeddah (a governorate in this region), Taif, Al Qunfudhah, Al Lith and Rabigh.
  • Eastern Province — includes Al Ahsa (one of the largest governorates by area), Jubail, Qatif, Khafji and Hafar al-Batin.
  • Asir Region — includes Abha's surrounding governorates such as Khamis Mushait, Bisha and Mahayel.
  • Al-Qassim Region — includes Unaizah, Ar Rass and Al Badayea.

Development

Successive royal decrees have raised the status of numerous centres to governorates and upgraded Category B governorates to Category A, particularly during the reigns of King Abdullah and King Salman, as part of efforts to spread administrative services and development across the kingdom's vast territory. Population figures for the governorates are published by the General Authority for Statistics following each national census (most recently the 2022 census).
