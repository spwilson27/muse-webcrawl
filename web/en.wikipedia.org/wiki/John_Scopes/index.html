John Thomas Scopes (August 3, 1900 – October 21, 1970) was an American teacher and geologist who became famous as the defendant in the 1925 Scopes Trial in Dayton, Tennessee, in which he was accused of teaching human evolution in a public school in violation of the state's Butler Act.

The trial

Scopes was a mathematics teacher and football coach at Rhea County High School who also substituted in biology classes. When the American Civil Liberties Union sought a test case against Tennessee's 1925 Butler Act, which prohibited teaching evolution in state-funded schools, Dayton civic leaders recruited Scopes to stand trial as a way to publicize their town. Scopes agreed, though he later said he was unsure whether he had actually taught the evolution content required for the charge.

The trial, held in July 1925, became a national sensation, pitting prosecutor William Jennings Bryan, the three-time presidential candidate and fundamentalist spokesman, against defence attorney Clarence Darrow. Scopes was convicted and fined $100, but the verdict was overturned on a technicality by the Tennessee Supreme Court, which nonetheless upheld the Butler Act (repealed only in 1967).

Later life

After the trial, Scopes left teaching, earned a graduate degree in geology from the University of Chicago, and worked as a petroleum geologist for Gulf Oil and later United Gas Corporation in Texas, Louisiana, and Venezuela. He published his memoir, Center of the Storm, in 1967. The trial was dramatized in the play and film Inherit the Wind.
