Nemo dat quod non habet

Nemo dat quod non habet, literally "no one gives what they do not have", is a legal rule in property law: a person who does not own property, or who has no authority to transfer ownership, cannot pass good title to another. A buyer therefore generally acquires no better title than the seller had.

Application

The rule is fundamental to English and other common-law systems of personal property law and is codified in sale of goods legislation, such as section 21(1) of the UK Sale of Goods Act 1979 and §2-403 of the US Uniform Commercial Code. If goods are stolen and sold, the original owner can normally recover them even from an innocent purchaser.

Exceptions

Several exceptions protect good-faith commerce: sale in market overt (historically), estoppel, sale by a mercantile agent or factor, sale under a voidable title before avoidance, sale by a seller in possession after sale, and statutory protections such as those for buyers of motor vehicles subject to hire-purchase agreements.
