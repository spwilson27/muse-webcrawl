Trimurti

The Trimurti (Sanskrit: त्रिमूर्ति, "three forms") is a concept in Hinduism in which the cosmic functions of creation, preservation, and destruction are personified as a triad of deities: Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver, and Shiva the destroyer. The three are regarded as aspects of a single supreme reality.

Origins and development

The triadic grouping emerged in the Puranic period (roughly the 4th to 10th centuries CE), synthesising the earlier Vedic prominence of Brahma with the rising devotional sects of Vishnu and Shiva. The concept is expressed in texts such as the Kurma Purana and is symbolised by the syllable Om, whose three sounds (a-u-m) are sometimes mapped onto the three gods.

Iconography

The Trimurti is famously depicted in the rock-cut sculpture at the Elephanta Caves near Mumbai, where a three-faced bust about six metres high shows Shiva in his aspects, and in the three-headed form of the deity Dattatreya. Temples dedicated jointly to the triad are relatively rare compared with those of the individual gods.

Interpretations

Different traditions order and interpret the triad differently; Shaivite and Vaishnavite theology each tend to subordinate the other two functions to their own supreme deity, who performs all three (or five) cosmic acts himself.
