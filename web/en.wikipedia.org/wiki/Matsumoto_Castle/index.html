Matsumoto Castle

Matsumoto Castle (松本城, Matsumoto-jō) is a Japanese castle in Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture. Nicknamed Karasu-jō (烏城), the "Crow Castle," for its black exterior, it is one of Japan's premier historic castles and, along with Himeji and Kumamoto, one of the most celebrated. Its main keep (tenshu), completed in the late 16th century, is among the oldest surviving five-storey castle keeps in Japan and is designated a National Treasure.

History

The castle's origins lie in Fukashi Castle, a fort built in the Sengoku period. After the region passed to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's vassal Ishikawa Kazumasa in 1590, he and his son Yasunaga constructed the great keep, its three turrets and defences between roughly 1592 and 1614. In the Edo period the castle was the seat of the Matsumoto Domain under a succession of daimyō families. When the Meiji government ordered the demolition of feudal castles in the 1870s, a local campaign — including purchase of the keep at auction by the resident Ichikawa Ryōzō — saved the building. The keep leaned noticeably by the early 20th century (often attributed to construction faults rather than an earthquake), prompting a major restoration from 1903 to 1913, and again in the 1950s.

Architecture

The castle is a flatland castle built on the plain rather than on a hill, relying on moats, stone walls and interlocking courtyards for defence. The complex comprises the five-storey, six-floor main keep linked to the smaller Inui keep, the Watari turret, and the black-lacquered Moon-Viewing turret (Tsukimi-yagura) added in the 1630s, in peacetime, for poetry gatherings. The black-and-white contrast of the plastered walls and the backdrop of the Northern Japan Alps make it one of Japan's most photographed castles.
