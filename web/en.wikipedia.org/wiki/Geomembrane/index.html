Geomembrane

Installing a geomembrane liner

A geomembrane is a low-permeability synthetic membrane liner or barrier used with geotechnical engineering materials to control fluid or gas migration in human-made structures, projects and systems. Geomembranes are among the most widely used geosynthetics, employed wherever containment is essential.

Materials

Most geomembranes are made from continuous polymeric sheets. The most common materials are high-density polyethylene (HDPE), linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE), polyvinyl chloride (PVC), flexible polypropylene and EPDM rubber. HDPE dominates landfill applications because of its chemical resistance and durability, though it is stiff and requires careful welding.

Applications

  • Liners and caps for municipal and hazardous-waste landfills
  • Mining: heap leach pads and tailings ponds
  • Water management: canal lining, reservoirs, dams and wastewater lagoons
  • Aquaculture ponds and floating covers
  • Tunnel waterproofing and secondary containment for fuel storage

Installation and testing

Panels are seamed on site, typically by thermal fusion welding, and seams are tested non-destructively (air-channel or vacuum-box tests) and destructively by peel and shear sampling. Electrical leak-location surveys can detect holes in installed liners, since even small punctures dominate leakage rates.
