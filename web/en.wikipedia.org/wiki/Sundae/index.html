Sundae

TypeIce cream dessert
Place of originUnited States
Main ingredientsIce cream, sauce or syrup, toppings

A sundae is an ice cream dessert of American origin, consisting of one or more scoops of ice cream topped with a sauce or syrup and, in many varieties, other toppings such as whipped cream, sprinkles, chopped nuts, crushed cookies, or a maraschino cherry. It is traditionally served in a tulip-shaped glass dish or a waffle bowl.

History

The sundae emerged in the late nineteenth century, closely connected with the ice cream soda. Several American cities claim its invention. The most famous account comes from Ithaca, New York, where on Sunday, 3 April 1892, pharmacy owner Chester Platt and the Reverend John M. Scott are said to have created a dish of vanilla ice cream with cherry syrup and candied cherries, named "Sunday" after the day. Two Rivers, Wisconsin, claims an 1881 origin involving soda jerk George Hallauer and customer George Berners, while Plainfield, Illinois, claims the first sundae was served without soda water to comply with Sunday "blue laws" forbidding soda sales — a common explanation for both the dessert and its name, later softened from "Sunday" to "sundae" so as not to offend religious sensibilities.

Varieties

  • Hot fudge sundae — vanilla ice cream with warm chocolate fudge, whipped cream, and a cherry; a soda-fountain standard, popularised by Clarence Clifton Brown's Hollywood shop from 1906.
  • Banana split — a split banana with scoops of vanilla, chocolate, and strawberry ice cream and sauces, created in Latrobe, Pennsylvania, in 1904.
  • Tin roof sundae — with marshmallow sauce and Spanish peanuts, from Potter, Nebraska.
  • Knickerbocker glory — a tall layered British relative served in a large conical glass.
  • Brownie sundae — ice cream served over a warm chocolate brownie.

Sundaes became the signature product of American soda fountains and drugstores in the early twentieth century and remain a fixture of ice cream parlours. In South Korea, bingsu — shaved ice with toppings including ice cream — is sometimes marketed under the name "sundae" in translation, though it is a distinct dessert (and unrelated to the Korean sausage of the same romanised name, sundae 순대).
