Esaias van de Velde (17 May 1587 (baptised) – 18 November 1630 (buried)) was a Dutch landscape painter and etcher of the Dutch Golden Age, regarded as one of the founders of the Dutch school of landscape painting.

Life

Van de Velde was born in Amsterdam, where his Flemish father Hans van de Velde had fled as a Protestant from Antwerp in 1585. He studied in Amsterdam, possibly under Gillis van Coninxloo, and perhaps also under David Vinckboons. He became a member of the Haarlem Guild of Saint Luke in 1612, along with Hercules Segers. From 1625 he worked at the court of Prince Maurits and Frederick Henry in The Hague, first as a painter and also as an informant reporting on political and military matters.

Work

Initially influenced by Adam Elsheimer, Esaias van de Velde painted a wide range of subjects: winter landscapes, summer landscapes, battle scenes, merry companies, and depictions of robberies and ambushes on country roads. His early landscapes, often with a high horizon and colourful detail, gave way after about 1620 to the low horizons, spacious skies, and restrained tonality that made him a pioneer of the naturalistic Dutch landscape. He was also a highly productive draughtsman and etcher, producing more than a hundred etchings, many of them after his own designs.

His pupils included Jan van Goyen, Pieter de Neyn, Jan Asselijn and possibly Pieter van Laer. His brothers Jan van de Velde II (engraver) and Willem van de Velde the Elder (marine painter) also came from his family; his cousin Jan van de Velde III was a still-life painter.

Legacy

Together with Hendrick Avercamp and Hendrick Goltzius's follower generation, Esaias van de Velde helped transform Dutch landscape from the fantastic Mannerist mode into direct observation of the Dutch countryside. His influence on Jan van Goyen in particular shaped the tonal landscape painting of the 1630s and 1640s.
