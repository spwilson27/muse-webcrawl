Walter Hans Schottky (23 July 1886 – 4 March 1976) was a German physicist who played a major early role in developing the theory of electron and ion emission phenomena.

Life

Schottky was born in Zürich, the son of the mathematician Friedrich Schottky. He studied physics at the University of Berlin, completing his doctorate in 1912 under Max Planck and Heinrich Rubens, with a dissertation connected to Einstein's theory of relativity. After postdoctoral work at Jena, he joined Siemens & Halske in Berlin in 1914, where he spent most of his career, apart from a professorship at Rostock (1923–1927). He invented the screen-grid vacuum tube in 1915 and, together with Erwin Gerlach, the ribbon microphone and ribbon loudspeaker in 1924.

Work

Schottky's name is attached to numerous effects in electronics and solid-state physics. The Schottky barrier is the potential barrier formed at a metal–semiconductor junction, exploited in the Schottky diode. He explained shot noise, the random fluctuation in current caused by the discreteness of electric charge, and described the Schottky effect — the lowering of a metal's work function by an applied electric field — which underlies the Schottky emission of electrons. His theory of the depletion layer and "empty" regions in semiconductors laid foundations for transistor physics. The Schottky defect, a type of point defect in crystal lattices, also bears his name.

Honours

In 1964 he received the Hughes Medal of the Royal Society for his work on thermionic emission and semiconductor devices. The Walter Schottky Institute for semiconductor research at the Technical University of Munich and the Walter Schottky Prize for solid-state physics are named after him.
