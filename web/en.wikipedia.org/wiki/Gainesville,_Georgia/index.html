Gainesville is a city in and the county seat of Hall County, Georgia, United States. As of the 2020 census, the city had a population of 42,296. Because of its large number of poultry processing plants, it has been called the "Poultry Capital of the World". It is the principal city of the Gainesville, Georgia metropolitan statistical area, and lies near Lake Lanier, a major reservoir and recreation area northeast of Atlanta.

History

Gainesville was established in 1821 as the seat of newly created Hall County and was named in honor of Edmund Pendleton Gaines, a general and hero of the War of 1812. Originally known as "Mule Camp Springs", the settlement grew as a trading center. The city was devastated by a tornado on April 6, 1936, one of the deadliest in U.S. history, which killed over 200 people and destroyed much of downtown; the city was rebuilt with a new civic center.

Economy

Poultry processing dominates the local economy: Gainesville promotes itself as the "Poultry Capital of the World", and a statue of a rooster tops a monument downtown. Jesse Jewell, a Gainesville entrepreneur, is credited with pioneering the vertically integrated poultry industry in the 1940s. The city is also a regional medical center, anchored by the Northeast Georgia Medical Center.

Geography

Gainesville is located in northeastern Georgia at the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains. The Chattahoochee River and Lake Lanier lie to the west and south of the city.
