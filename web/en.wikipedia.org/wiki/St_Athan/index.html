St Athan

St Athan (Welsh: Sain Tathan) is a village and community in the Vale of Glamorgan, Wales, near the coast of the Bristol Channel. It is best known for the adjacent military airfield, MOD St Athan, long associated with Royal Air Force aircraft maintenance.

History

The village takes its name from Saint Tathan, an Irish monk who is said to have founded a church and school here in the sixth century. The parish church of St Tathan dates in part from the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. Nearby stand the ruins of East Orchard Castle, a medieval fortified manor house. The village remained small and agricultural until the arrival of the airfield in the twentieth century transformed the local economy.

MOD St Athan

RAF St Athan opened in 1938 and grew into one of the RAF's largest stations, specialising in aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul, including decades of work on fast jets. Under Ministry of Defence reorganisation in the 2000s the site was developed as a defence training academy before plans changed; elements of the Army, including No. 1 Military Police Brigade units, later moved in, and the station became MOD St Athan. The airfield is also used by civilian aviation businesses in the adjacent enterprise zone.

The village today

St Athan has a population of a few thousand, with local shops, pubs and a primary school. It lies near the Wales Coast Path and the heritage coastline of the Vale of Glamorgan, west of Barry and east of Llantwit Major.
