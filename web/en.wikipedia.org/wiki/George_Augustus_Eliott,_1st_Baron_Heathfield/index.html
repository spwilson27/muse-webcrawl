George Augustus Eliott, 1st Baron Heathfield

George Augustus Eliott, 1st Baron Heathfield

Born: 25 December 1717, Stobs, Scotland

Died: 6 July 1790, Aachen

Allegiance: Kingdom of Great Britain

Service: British Army

Known for: Defence of Gibraltar, 1779–1783

George Augustus Eliott, 1st Baron Heathfield (25 December 1717 – 6 July 1790) was a British Army officer best known for his command of the garrison of Gibraltar during the Great Siege of Gibraltar (1779–1783), one of the longest sieges in British military history.

Early career

Eliott was educated at the University of Leiden and trained in engineering and artillery in France before joining the British Army. He served as aide-de-camp at the Battle of Dettingen and the Battle of Fontenoy, raised the 15th Light Dragoons (the first light cavalry regiment in the British Army), and served in the Seven Years' War, including the expedition against Havana in 1762.

Governor of Gibraltar

Appointed Governor of Gibraltar in 1777, Eliott directed the defence of the Rock through the Great Siege by French and Spanish forces, which included the massive Grand Assault of September 1782, defeated with the help of red-hot shot that set the attacking floating batteries ablaze. His frugality, discipline and personal endurance became celebrated in Britain.

Honours

Eliott was made a Knight of the Bath and in 1787 was raised to the peerage as Baron Heathfield, of Gibraltar. He died at Aachen in 1790 while travelling to take the waters.
