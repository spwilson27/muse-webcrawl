English Cocker Spaniel

English Cocker Spaniel
Origin: England
Group: Sporting / Gundog
Height: 38–43 cm (15–17 in)
Weight: 12–14.5 kg (26–32 lb)
Coat: Silky, flat or slightly wavy, well feathered
Life span: 12–14 years

The English Cocker Spaniel is a breed of gun dog known for its merry temperament, compact build and profuse silky coat. It is one of several spaniel varieties developed in Britain for flushing and retrieving game, and takes its name from its historical use hunting woodcock. The breed is divided into two strains: the "working" or field-bred cocker, bred primarily for hunting ability, and the "show" cocker, bred to the conformation standard.

History

Spaniels were distinguished by size for centuries: larger "springing" spaniels flushed game for nets and falcons, while smaller "cocking" spaniels worked dense cover for woodcock. When the Kennel Club was founded in 1873, spaniels were classed largely by weight, and the Cocker Spaniel was formally recognised as a separate breed in 1892. The foundation dog of the modern breed is widely considered to be Ch. Obo, whelped in 1879. In the United States, the English Cocker was separated from the smaller American Cocker Spaniel, with the English breed recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1946.

Description

The English Cocker is a sturdy, well-balanced dog, slightly longer than tall, with long, low-set ears and a gently expressive face. Colours include solid black, liver, red and golden, as well as particolours, roans and ticked patterns. The tail is customarily carried level with the back and in constant wagging motion when working — a trait behind the breed's reputation for cheerfulness.

Temperament and health

Cockers are affectionate, intelligent and eager to please, but require regular exercise and grooming. Hereditary concerns include progressive retinal atrophy, familial nephropathy, hip dysplasia and ear infections associated with the heavy, pendulous ears. A distinctive behavioural condition, sometimes called "rage syndrome", has been reported most often in solid-coloured show-bred males, though it is rare.
