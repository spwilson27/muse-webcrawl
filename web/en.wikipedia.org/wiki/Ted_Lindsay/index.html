Robert Blake Theodore Lindsay (29 July 1925 – 4 March 2019) was a Canadian professional ice hockey player who played left wing for the Detroit Red Wings and Chicago Black Hawks of the National Hockey League (NHL). Nicknamed "Terrible Ted" for his fierce competitiveness, he scored over 800 points in his career and helped Detroit win the Stanley Cup four times (1950, 1952, 1954, 1955).

Playing career

Lindsay formed the famed "Production Line" with Sid Abel and Gordie Howe, one of the most dominant lines in NHL history. He won the Art Ross Trophy as the league's leading scorer in 1949–50 and was named to eight NHL First All-Star Teams.

Players' association

In 1957 Lindsay led efforts to form the NHL Players' Association, challenging the owners' control over pensions and salaries. In retaliation, the Red Wings stripped him of the captaincy and traded him to Chicago. The association was initially crushed, but the effort laid the groundwork for the union's successful formation a decade later.

Legacy

Lindsay was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1966. In 2010 the NHL Players' Association renamed its most outstanding player award — voted by the players — the Ted Lindsay Award in his honour. His number 7 was retired by the Red Wings in 1991.
