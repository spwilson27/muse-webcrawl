Grimsby Town F.C.

Grimsby Town Football Club is a professional football club based in Cleethorpes, North East Lincolnshire, England, which competes in EFL League Two, the fourth tier of the English football league system. The club, nicknamed "the Mariners", was founded in 1878 as Grimsby Pelham and plays its home games at Blundell Park, its home since 1899.

History

Grimsby Town was a founding member of the Football League Second Division in 1892. The club's most successful era came in the 1930s: it reached the First Division in 1929, finished fifth in 1934–35 — still its highest league placing — and reached FA Cup semi-finals in 1936 and 1939. Bill Shankly, later the legendary Liverpool manager, managed Grimsby from 1951 to 1954.

The club declined in the post-war decades but experienced revivals under managers such as Lawrie McMenemy, who led the Mariners to the Fourth Division title in 1971–72, and Alan Buckley, whose teams won promotion in 1990 and 1991 and later won both the Football League Trophy and the Second Division play-offs in 1998. Grimsby dropped out of the Football League in 2010 and again in 2021, returning each time via the National League play-offs, most recently in 2022, just one season after relegation.

Club identity

Grimsby play in black and white striped shirts, black shorts, and white socks. The Mariners nickname and the club badge reflect Grimsby's heritage as one of the world's great fishing ports. Its traditional rivals are Hull City, Scunthorpe United, and Lincoln City.

Honours

The club's major honours include the Football League Trophy (1998) and divisional championships in the Third Division North and Fourth Division. In the 2022–23 FA Cup, Grimsby reached the quarter-finals, becoming the first team in the competition's history to beat five teams from higher divisions in a single season, including a famous 2–1 win at Premier League Southampton.
