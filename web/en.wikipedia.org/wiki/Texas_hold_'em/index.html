Texas hold 'em

Texas hold 'em (also hold'em or holdem) is one of the most popular variants of the card game of poker. Two cards, known as hole cards, are dealt face down to each player, and five community cards are dealt face up in three stages: the flop (three cards), the turn (one card) and the river (one card). Players make the best five-card hand from the seven available cards, betting in four rounds.

History

The game originated in Robstown, Texas, in the early 1900s and was brought to Las Vegas in 1963 by Corky McCorquodale. It spread through the Golden Nugget and other casinos, and in 1970 became the format of the Main Event of the World Series of Poker. Televised poker and online play in the early 2000s — the "poker boom" — made no-limit hold 'em the dominant variant worldwide.

Gameplay

Two forced bets, the small blind and big blind, start the action. Players may fold, call or raise in each round. Variants include no-limit, pot-limit and fixed-limit structures. Strategy centres on position, hand selection, pot odds and reading opponents; the game has been extensively studied with game theory and solvers.
