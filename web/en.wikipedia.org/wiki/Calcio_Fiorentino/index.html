Calcio Fiorentino

OriginFlorence, Italy
First codified1580 (Giovanni de' Bardi)
Players27 per side
FieldSand pitch, Piazza Santa Croce

Calcio Fiorentino (also calcio storico, 'historic football') is an early form of football and rugby that originated in 16th-century Florence, Italy, and is still played there in an annual tournament. Teams of 27 players compete on a sand pitch to throw a ball over the opposing team's end fence, combining running, tackling, and wrestling.

Matches last 50 minutes without substitutions for injury, and the winning team traditionally received a Chianina calf (the name of the game itself derives from 'calcio', kick).

History

The game descends from Roman harpastum and was played by aristocrats in Renaissance Florence; the famous match of 1530 was played in Piazza Santa Croce while the city was besieged. Count Giovanni de' Bardi published its first written rules in 1580. After a long decline, the tradition was revived in 1930.

Modern tournament

Four historic quarters of Florence — Santa Croce (Azzurri), Santa Maria Novella (Rossi), Santo Spirito (Bianchi), and San Giovanni (Verdi) — contest two semi-finals and a final around the feast of St. John the Baptist on 24 June, preceded by a Renaissance costume parade.

Rules and character

Players may punch, tackle, and grapple; only certain fouls such as sucker punches and kicks to the head are banned. The spectacle is as much civic ritual as sport, symbolising Florentine pride and neighbourhood identity.
