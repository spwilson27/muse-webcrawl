Pietro Antonio Domenico Trapassi (3 January 1698 – 12 April 1782), better known by the pseudonym Pietro Metastasio, was an Italian poet and librettist, considered the most important writer of opera seria libretti in the 18th century.

Early life

Born in Rome to a grocer's family, Trapassi showed a gift for improvised verse as a child and was taken under the protection of the critic Gian Vincenzo Gravina, who gave him the Hellenized name Metastasio and supervised his education. After Gravina's death, Metastasio worked in Naples, where his first great libretto, Didone abbandonata (1724), brought him fame across Europe.

Imperial court poet in Vienna

In 1729 Metastasio was appointed court poet in Vienna, succeeding Apostolo Zeno, and remained there for the rest of his life. He wrote some thirty opera seria libretti, including La clemenza di Tito, Demofoonte, L'Olimpiade, and Attilio Regolo, as well as oratorios, cantatas, and occasional verse for the Habsburg court.

Influence

Metastasio's libretti were set to music hundreds of times by composers including Handel, Gluck, Haydn, and Mozart, who used La clemenza di Tito in 1791. His reform of the opera libretto — clear three-act structures, a balance of recitative and aria, and elevated but singable language — defined the genre for the rest of the century and was admired by Voltaire and other Enlightenment writers.
