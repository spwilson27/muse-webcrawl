Dexter is an American crime drama television series that aired on Showtime from 2006 to 2013. It stars Michael C. Hall as Dexter Morgan, a Miami Metro Police Department blood-spatter analyst who leads a secret life as a vigilante serial killer, hunting murderers who have escaped the justice system.

Premise

Orphaned after witnessing his mother's murder, Dexter was adopted by police officer Harry Morgan, who recognized his homicidal urges and taught him a "Code": kill only those who deserve it, and never get caught. The first season was loosely based on Jeff Lindsay's novel Darkly Dreaming Dexter (2004). The series features Debra Morgan, Dexter's foul-mouthed sister and fellow officer, and the ghost-like memory of Harry as Dexter's conscience.

Reception and legacy

The series won numerous awards, including Golden Globes for Hall and for John Lithgow's guest role as the Trinity Killer in the acclaimed fourth season. The original finale was widely criticized; a sequel miniseries, Dexter: New Blood, aired in 2021–2022, followed by further spin-offs.
