Hever Castle is located in the village of Hever, Kent, near Edenbridge in south-east England. It began as a country house, built in the 13th century. From 1462 to 1539, it was the seat of the Boleyn (originally Bullen) family.

History

The oldest part of the castle, the gatehouse and outer walls, dates to about 1270. In 1462 Geoffrey Boleyn converted the fortress into a Tudor manor house. His granddaughter Anne Boleyn, the second queen consort of King Henry VIII of England, spent her early youth at Hever after her father Thomas Boleyn inherited it in 1505. After the Boleyns' fall the property passed to Henry VIII, who gave it to Anne of Cleves following their annulment.

Astor restoration

In 1903 the castle was bought and extensively restored by the American millionaire William Waldorf Astor, who built the Tudor Village, created the lake, and laid out the celebrated gardens, including an Italian garden to display his classical statuary. The castle is now a tourist attraction, with mazes, gardens, and exhibitions of Boleyn relics and Tudor portraits.
