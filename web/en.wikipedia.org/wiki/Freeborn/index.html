Freeborn

For other uses, see Freeborn (disambiguation).

Freeborn is a term describing a person born free, that is, not born into slavery or serfdom. It is used especially of societies in which slavery existed, where the legal and social status of a freeborn person (ingenuus in Roman law) differed from that of a freedperson (libertinus), who had been enslaved and later manumitted.

Roman law

In ancient Rome, the freeborn (ingenui) constituted the class of free citizens born to free parents. Freed slaves could become citizens, but they and, in some respects, their children carried disabilities: freedmen could not hold certain magistracies and owed duties of respect and service (obsequium and operae) to their former masters. The distinction between freeborn and freed gradually faded in the later Empire.

Later usage

The word survived in English as an element of surnames and place-names and in political rhetoric, as in the Leveller leader John Lilburne, known as "Freeborn John" for his insistence on the rights of the freeborn Englishman. Freeborn is also the name of a city in Freeborn County, Minnesota, United States, and of Freeborn County itself, named for William Freeborn, a Minnesota pioneer and territorial legislator.
