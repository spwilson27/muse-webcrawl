The Dom Luís I Bridge (Portuguese: Ponte D. Luís I) is a double-deck metal arch bridge that spans the Douro River between the cities of Porto and Vila Nova de Gaia in Portugal. When completed in 1886, its 172-metre arch was the longest of its type in the world.

Construction

The bridge was designed by the Belgian engineer Théophile Seyrig, a former partner of Gustave Eiffel, and built by the Société de Willebroeck of Brussels. Its two decks serve different levels of the steep riverbanks: the upper deck now carries the Porto Metro and pedestrians, the lower deck road traffic and pedestrians. It replaced an earlier suspension bridge and took its name from King Luís I of Portugal.

Heritage

The bridge is the emblem of Porto and stands within the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Historic Centre of Porto, inscribed in 1996. Tradition among local students and daredevils includes jumping from the lower deck into the Douro during summer festivals.
