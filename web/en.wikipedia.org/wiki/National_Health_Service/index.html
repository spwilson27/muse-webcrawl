The National Health Service (NHS) is the umbrella term for the publicly funded healthcare systems of the United Kingdom: NHS England, NHS Scotland, NHS Wales, and Health and Social Care in Northern Ireland. Founded on 5 July 1948 under the post-war Labour government of Clement Attlee, with Aneurin Bevan as Minister of Health, it was the first universal health system in the world to provide care free at the point of use, funded from general taxation.

Founding principles

The NHS was built on three core principles: that it meet the needs of everyone, that it be free at the point of delivery, and that it be based on clinical need, not ability to pay. Before 1948, healthcare in Britain was a patchwork of voluntary hospitals, municipal services, and fee-charging care, leaving many unable to afford treatment. The Beveridge Report of 1942 had identified "Disease" as one of the "five giants" to be slain by the welfare state.

Structure

Since devolution in 1999, health policy is a devolved matter, so the four national services operate with separate structures and some differing policies (for example, prescription charges were abolished in Wales, Northern Ireland, and Scotland but remain in England for most adults). Primary care is delivered mainly by general practitioners (GPs), who act as gatekeepers to hospital and specialist services. Hospital care is provided by NHS trusts and foundation trusts.

Funding and performance

The NHS is funded overwhelmingly from general taxation and National Insurance contributions, with relatively small amounts from patient charges (dentistry, prescriptions in England, eye care). It employs around 1.5 million people, making it one of the largest employers in the world. The service consistently polls as one of Britain's most valued institutions, though it faces persistent pressures from an ageing population, workforce shortages, waiting lists, and periodic funding debates. International comparisons generally rate the UK system highly for access and equity, and lower for some health outcomes relative to comparable countries.

History

Major milestones include the introduction of prescription charges in 1952, the 1962 Hospital Plan, the 1990 internal-market reforms, the creation of the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in 1999, the Health and Social Care Act 2012 in England, and the service's central role in responding to the COVID-19 pandemic from 2020.
