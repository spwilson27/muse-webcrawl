Nessus (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Nessus (Greek: Νέσσος, Nessos) was a centaur, the son of Ixion and Nephele, famous for his role in the death of the hero Heracles. After being driven from his home by the Lapiths at the wedding of Pirithous, Nessus made his living ferrying travellers across the river Euenos (Evenus) in Aetolia.

The death of Heracles

Heracles, travelling with his wife Deianira, paid Nessus to carry her across the river. Midstream, Nessus attempted to assault her. Heracles, from the far bank, shot him with an arrow dipped in the venom of the Lernaean Hydra. Dying, Nessus deceived Deianira: he told her that a potion made from his blood (in some versions, blood mixed with semen, or the blood itself) would act as a love charm ensuring Heracles' fidelity.

Years later, fearing that Heracles had taken a new lover, Iole, Deianira smeared the blood on a tunic and sent it to him. The Hydra's poison in the blood caused Heracles unbearable agony — the "shirt of Nessus" — and, unable to tear off the burning garment, he built his own funeral pyre on Mount Oeta and died, whereupon he was deified. The story is told in Sophocles' Women of Trachis and in Ovid's Metamorphoses and Heroides.

Legacy

The phrases "shirt of Nessus" and "Nessus' shirt" became proverbial for a fatal gift or a torment that cannot be escaped. Nessus also appears in Dante's Inferno, where he serves as a ferryman guarding the river of boiling blood in the seventh circle of Hell.
