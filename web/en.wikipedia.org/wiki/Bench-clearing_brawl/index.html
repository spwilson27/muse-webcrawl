Bench-clearing brawl

SportsBaseball; ice hockey (bench-clearing brawl); basketball; others
Typical triggersHit batsman, hard foul, charging the mound, provocative play
PenaltiesEjections, fines, suspensions

A bench-clearing brawl is a form of fighting that occurs in several team sports, most commonly baseball and ice hockey, in which players leave the benches, dugouts or bullpens en masse to join a fight on the field or ice. What begins as a confrontation between two players escalates as teammates rush in, nominally to defend one another; in baseball the eventual scrum often involves more posturing and wrestling than punching, but injuries and ejections are common and leagues impose fines and suspensions on participants.

Baseball

The classic baseball trigger is a batter being hit by a pitch — especially if suspected to be deliberate retaliation — who then "charges the mound" to fight the pitcher. Both dugouts and bullpens empty, and players stream onto the field. Famous incidents include the 1984 Padres–Braves brawls in Atlanta, in which fans joined in, and Nolan Ryan's 1993 fight with Robin Ventura, in which the 46-year-old Ryan famously put the charging Ventura in a headlock. Major League Baseball rules prohibit leaving the bench to join a fight, and mass ejections frequently follow.

Ice hockey

In hockey, where fighting between individual players is traditionally tolerated, a bench-clearing brawl involves an entire team leaving the bench, producing multiple simultaneous fights; these were a recurring feature of the 1970s and 1980s NHL (the Broad Street Bullies era) and the minor leagues. The NHL's famous "Good Friday Massacre" of 1984 and the 1987 "Punch-up in Piestany" between Canadian and Soviet junior teams — which caused both teams' disqualification from the World Junior Championship — are among the best known. Rule changes imposing severe penalties on the first players to leave the bench have made them rare in modern professional hockey.

Other sports

Basketball's notorious "Malice at the Palace" (2004), a Pacers–Pistons fight that spilled into the stands and involved fans, led to some of the longest suspensions in NBA history and stricter arena rules. Bench-clearing incidents also occur in soccer, lacrosse and Australian rules football, where they are often called "all-in brawls" or melees.
