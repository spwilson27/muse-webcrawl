Incentre


The incentre is the intersection of the angle bisectors

In geometry, the incentre of a triangle is a triangle center defined as the point where the internal angle bisectors of the triangle cross. It is also the centre of the triangle's incircle, the largest circle that fits inside the triangle and is tangent to all three sides.

Properties

The incentre lies at an equal distance from all three sides of the triangle; that distance is the inradius. In trilinear coordinates the incentre is 1 : 1 : 1, and in barycentric coordinates it is a : b : c, where a, b, and c are the side lengths. Equivalently, the incentre is the weighted average of the vertices with the side lengths as weights. Unlike the circumcentre, the incentre always lies inside the triangle, whether the triangle is acute, right, or obtuse.

Related points

The incentre is related to the excentres, the centres of the three excircles tangent to one side and the extensions of the other two. Together, the incentre and excentres form an orthocentric system. The contact triangle, formed by the points where the incircle touches the sides, has the incentre as its circumcentre; its vertices' pedal point is the Gergonne point.
