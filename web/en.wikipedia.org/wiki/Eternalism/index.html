Eternalism

Eternalism
Field: Philosophy of time
Key idea: Past, present and future all equally exist
Related concepts: Block universe, B-theory of time
Opposed to: Presentism, Growing block theory

Eternalism is a philosophical approach to the ontological nature of time which holds that all moments in time—past, present and future—exist equally and are equally real. It is commonly associated with the "block universe" picture suggested by the theory of relativity, in which spacetime is a single four-dimensional structure and the flow of time is a feature of perspective rather than of reality itself.

Arguments

The best-known physical argument for eternalism derives from special relativity: because simultaneity is relative to an observer's frame of reference, events that are future for one observer are past for another, which suggests that all events must have the same ontological status. The philosopher Hilary Putnam famously drew this conclusion in 1967. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument presses the point that what is real cannot depend on one's state of motion.

Rival views

Eternalism contrasts with presentism, according to which only the present exists, and with the growing block theory, in which past and present exist but the future does not yet. Eternalism is closely tied to the B-theory of time, which treats the ordering of events by "earlier than" and "later than" as fundamental and regards tensed talk of past, present and future as perspectival.
