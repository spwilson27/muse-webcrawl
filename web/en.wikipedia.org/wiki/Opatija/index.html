Opatija (Italian: Abbazia) is a town in western Croatia, just southwest of Rijeka on the Adriatic coast, at the head of the Kvarner Gulf beneath Mount Učka. It is the oldest and best known tourist resort in Croatia, often called the cradle of Croatian tourism.

History

The town grew around the Benedictine Abbey of St James (the name Opatija means "abbey"), first mentioned in the fifteenth century. In 1844 the Rijeka merchant Iginio Scarpa built the Villa Angiolina, and in 1884 the Austrian Southern Railway company's Hotel Kvarner opened — the first luxury hotel on the Croatian Adriatic. Under Austria-Hungary, Opatija became a fashionable Kurort (health resort) frequented by emperors, kings and aristocrats; an imperial decree of 1889 formally declared it a climatic spa. After the First World War it passed to Italy, then to Yugoslavia after the Second.

Sights

  • The Lungomare, a 12 km seafront promenade to Lovran, begun in 1885
  • Villa Angiolina (1844) and its park of exotic plants, now a museum of Croatian tourism
  • The Maiden with the Seagull statue (1956), the town's symbol
  • Grand hotels of the Belle Époque: Kvarner, Imperial, Milenij
  • The Croatian Walk of Fame along the main street

Climate

Sheltered by Mount Učka from cold bora winds, Opatija enjoys a mild Mediterranean climate with more than 2,000 hours of sunshine a year and lush laurel and camellia vegetation — the basis of its nineteenth-century reputation as a winter health resort.
