James A. Robinson

Born: February 27, 1960, United Kingdom

Occupation: Economist, political scientist

Institution: University of Chicago

Notable work: Why Nations Fail (2012)

James A. Robinson (born February 27, 1960) is a British-American economist and political scientist, and the Reverend Dr. Richard L. Pearson Professor of Global Conflict Studies at the University of Chicago. His research concerns the political economy of development, institutions, and comparative politics.

Career

Robinson earned his BA from the London School of Economics, an MA from the University of Warwick, and a PhD from Yale University. He has taught at the University of Melbourne, the University of Southern California, UC Berkeley, Harvard University, MIT, and the University of Chicago.

Work

With Daron Acemoglu, Robinson co-authored Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty (2012), which argues that prosperity depends on inclusive political and economic institutions. The pair also co-authored The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty (2019) and Power and Progress: Our Thousand-Year Struggle over Technology and Prosperity (2023). Robinson has conducted extensive field research in Latin America and Africa, including in Sierra Leone and Colombia.

Nobel Prize

In 2024, Robinson was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences jointly with Daron Acemoglu and Simon Johnson, "for studies of how institutions are formed and affect prosperity".
