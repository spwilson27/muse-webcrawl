Female infertility

Female infertility refers to infertility in women — the inability to conceive after a year or more of regular unprotected intercourse. Female factors account for roughly a third of infertility cases among couples, with male factors and combined or unexplained causes making up the remainder.

Causes

Common causes include ovulation disorders such as polycystic ovary syndrome, blocked or damaged fallopian tubes (often from pelvic inflammatory disease or endometriosis), uterine abnormalities, and diminished ovarian reserve associated with advancing age. Hormonal conditions affecting the thyroid or pituitary gland can also impair fertility.

Diagnosis

Evaluation typically includes a history and examination, tests of ovarian reserve, confirmation of ovulation, and imaging of the uterus and fallopian tubes, such as hysterosalpingography.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and may involve ovulation-inducing medication, surgery to correct anatomical problems, intrauterine insemination, or assisted reproductive technologies such as in vitro fertilisation (IVF).
