Georgia Dome

Location 1 Georgia Dome Drive NW, Atlanta, Georgia, U.S.
Opened September 6, 1992
Closed March 5, 2017
Demolished November 20, 2017 (implosion)
Capacity 71,228 (football)
Tenants Atlanta Falcons (NFL, 1992–2016); Georgia State Panthers (2010–2016); Atlanta Hawks (NBA, 1997–1999)

The Georgia Dome was a domed stadium in the southeastern United States, located in Atlanta, Georgia. It was home to the Atlanta Falcons of the National Football League and the Georgia State Panthers football team, and hosted Super Bowls XXVIII and XXXIV, events of the 1996 Summer Olympics, NCAA Final Fours, the SEC Championship Game, and the Chick-fil-A Peach Bowl. When completed in 1992 at a cost of $214 million, it was the largest cable-supported domed stadium in the world.

History

The Georgia Dome was built by the State of Georgia and operated by the Georgia World Congress Center Authority. Its roof consisted of a Teflon-coated fiberglass fabric stretched over a network of steel cables, spanning the stadium without internal supports. The stadium opened for the 1992 NFL season, replacing Atlanta–Fulton County Stadium as the Falcons' home.

During the 1996 Summer Olympics, the Dome hosted basketball and team handball finals and artistic gymnastics, with a temporary wall dividing the stadium into two venues. The Georgia Dome hosted Super Bowl XXVIII in 1994 (Dallas Cowboys over Buffalo Bills) and Super Bowl XXXIV in 2000 (St. Louis Rams over Tennessee Titans, decided by a tackle one yard short of the goal line on the final play).

On March 14, 2008, a tornado struck downtown Atlanta during the SEC basketball tournament, tearing two holes in the Dome's roof fabric and forcing games to be suspended and moved.

Replacement and demolition

The Georgia Dome closed after the 2016 NFL season and the Falcons moved to the new Mercedes-Benz Stadium next door, which opened in August 2017. The Georgia Dome was imploded on November 20, 2017, using about 4,800 pounds of explosives; its site became the Home Depot Backyard, a green space and tailgating area. It was the only stadium to have hosted the Olympics, a Super Bowl, and the Final Four.
