William McGregor (13 April 1846 – 20 December 1911) was a Scottish association football administrator in Victorian England who is regarded as the founder of the Football League, the world's first national football league competition. He also served as a director and chairman of Aston Villa.

Early life

McGregor was born in Braco, Perthshire, Scotland. A draper by trade, he moved to Birmingham in 1870 and became involved with local football, first with Calthorpe and then, from 1877, with Aston Villa, the club with which he would be associated for the rest of his life.

Founding the Football League

By the mid-1880s, the legalisation of professionalism (1885) had left clubs dependent on irregular friendly fixtures and cup ties, with frequent cancellations. In March 1888 McGregor wrote to five leading clubs proposing a regular league competition guaranteeing fixtures. Representatives of twelve clubs met in London and Manchester, and the Football League began in September 1888 with Aston Villa, Blackburn Rovers, Bolton Wanderers, Burnley, Derby County, Everton, Notts County, Preston North End, Stoke, West Bromwich Albion, Wolverhampton Wanderers, and Accrington as founder members. Preston North End won the first championship undefeated.

Later life

McGregor served as president of the Football League and chairman of the Football Association. He died in Birmingham on 20 December 1911. A statue of McGregor stands outside Villa Park, unveiled in 2009, honouring him as the "father of the Football League".
