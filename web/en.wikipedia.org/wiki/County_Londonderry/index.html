County Londonderry, also known as County Derry, is one of the six counties of Northern Ireland and one of the traditional thirty-two counties of Ireland. It lies in the province of Ulster, on the north-west coast, bordered by Lough Neagh to the south-east, the Atlantic Ocean and Lough Foyle to the north, and Counties Tyrone, Antrim and Donegal. The county's largest settlement is the city of Derry (officially Londonderry), famed for its intact seventeenth-century walls; other towns include Coleraine, Limavady, Magherafelt and Portstewart.

The county was established during the Plantation of Ulster. In 1613 the City of London's livery companies, organised through The Honourable The Irish Society, undertook to rebuild and colonise the city and county, and the name "Londonderry" was adopted. The use of "Derry" versus "Londonderry" remains politically sensitive, reflecting nationalist and unionist preferences respectively.

Geography

The landscape ranges from the basalt plateaus and glens in the east to the Sperrin Mountains in the south, which include Sawel Mountain, the county's highest point at 678 metres. The River Bann flows north through the county to the Atlantic at Portstewart, and the River Foyle forms part of the border with County Donegal. Mussenden Temple and Downhill Demesne, perched on the north coast cliffs, are among the county's best-known landmarks.
