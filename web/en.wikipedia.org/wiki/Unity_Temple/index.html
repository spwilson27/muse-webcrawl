Unity Temple

Unity Temple is a Unitarian Universalist church in Oak Park, Illinois, designed by the architect Frank Lloyd Wright and built between 1905 and 1908. It is considered one of Wright's most important buildings and a landmark of modern architecture — among the first monumental public buildings constructed of exposed reinforced concrete. In 2019 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright."

Design

When the congregation's previous church burned in 1905, Wright — himself a member — designed a replacement on a modest budget of about $45,000. He chose reinforced concrete for economy and fire resistance, casting the building in a single material with geometric ornament. The plan consists of two parts joined by a low entrance foyer: the Temple proper, a near-cubic sanctuary for worship, and Unity House, a rectangular block for social and educational functions.

The sanctuary is a celebrated interior: a square, top-lit space ringed by balconies on three levels, with seating facing inward rather than toward a distant chancel. Amber and green art-glass skylights wash the room in warm light, and Wright's decorative scheme of stained wood banding and earth-toned plaster creates a human-scaled, contemplative room that seats about 400.

Restoration

After decades of water damage, the building underwent a comprehensive restoration completed in 2017, costing about $25 million, which repaired the concrete shell, restored the interior finishes and art glass, and added modern systems. The temple remains an active Unitarian Universalist congregation and is open for tours.
