Church history

Church history or ecclesiastical history is the study of the history of Christianity and the Christian Church, from its origins in first-century Judaea to the present day. It examines institutions, doctrine, worship, missions and the interaction of churches with societies and states.

Church history
Field: History / theology
Key early source: Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History
Period: c. 30 AD – present

Early church

Following the ministry of Jesus and the apostles, Christian communities spread through the Roman Empire despite periodic persecution. Councils such as Nicaea (325) defined doctrine, while monasticism, bishops and canon law shaped institutions. See Early Christianity.

Medieval and Reformation

The medieval church in the West, centred on the papacy, and Orthodox churches in the East diverged in the Great Schism of 1054. The sixteenth-century Reformation led by figures including Martin Luther and John Calvin, and the Catholic Counter-Reformation, divided Western Christianity.

Modern era

Missionary movements, colonialism, revivalism, Pentecostalism, ecumenism and secularisation transformed global Christianity, which grew rapidly in Africa, Asia and Latin America in the twentieth century.

Historiography

Eusebius of Caesarea is often called the father of church history. Modern historians use critical methods, archaeology and social history alongside confessional traditions.
