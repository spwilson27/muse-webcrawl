A generation is all of the people born and living at about the same time, regarded collectively. The term also denotes the stage of descent in a family — the set of offspring of a common ancestor at one level — and, by extension, a single stage in the development of a technology or product.

Four generations of a family in a single photograph.

Family generations

In genealogy, a generation is a level of descent: children form one generation, their parents the preceding one, and so on. Length of a generation in human families averages roughly 25 to 30 years, varying with the age at which people have children. Generation time is also a standard concept in biology and evolution, meaning the average time between the birth of parents and the birth of their offspring.

Social generations

Sociologists, following Karl Mannheim's 1928 essay "The Problem of Generations", treat a generation as a cohort shaped by shared historical experiences in youth. Popular usage, especially in the Western world, labels successive cohorts: the Lost Generation, the Greatest Generation, the Silent Generation, baby boomers, Generation X, millennials (Generation Y), Generation Z, and Generation Alpha. The boundaries are informal and disputed, and demographers caution that within-cohort differences often exceed differences between cohorts.

Other uses

The word is used for successive iterations of technology (fifth-generation computers, generations of mobile networks), for cohorts of stars in astronomy, and for particles in physics, where quarks and leptons fall into three generations.
