Eastern Norway (Norwegian: Østlandet in Bokmål, Austlandet in Nynorsk) is the geographical region of the south-east of Norway. It consists of the counties of Oslo, Viken, Innlandet and Vestfold og Telemark, and is by far the most populous region of the country, containing Oslo, the national capital.

The region includes the lowlands around the Oslofjord, the great valleys of Gudbrandsdalen and Østerdalen, and the lake districts around Mjøsa, Norway's largest lake. To the west it is bounded by the Langfjella mountain ranges separating it from Western Norway.

Geography

Eastern Norway is the flattest and most agriculturally productive part of Norway, with extensive forests in the interior counties of Innlandet. The Glomma, Norway's longest river, runs south through Østerdalen to the Oslofjord. The coastal areas along the fjord enjoy comparatively mild summers, while the interior valleys have a continental climate with cold winters.

Economy and demography

Roughly half of Norway's population lives in Eastern Norway. Oslo dominates the region as its political, economic and cultural centre, surrounded by a belt of commuter towns in Akershus. Other significant towns include Drammen, Fredrikstad, Sarpsborg, Hamar, Lillehammer, Tønsberg, Sandefjord and Skien. The standard variety of spoken Norwegian, based on the dialects of the Oslo area, is most widely used in this region.
