Monte Sant'Angelo is a town and comune of Apulia, southern Italy, in the province of Foggia, on the southern flank of the Gargano promontory. It is famous for the Sanctuary of Saint Michael the Archangel, one of the oldest shrines dedicated to St Michael in Western Europe and a major pilgrimage site since the early Middle Ages.

The Sanctuary of Saint Michael

According to tradition, the Archangel Michael appeared to the bishop of Siponto in a cave on the mountain in 490, 492, and 493 AD, consecrating the grotto himself. The sanctuary built over the cave became a goal of pilgrims travelling to the Holy Land and a station on the Via Francigena; Lombard dukes, Norman rulers, and crusaders all visited it. The cave church contains a statue of the Archangel attributed to Andrea Sansovino, and the sanctuary's bronze doors were cast in Constantinople in 1076.

The sanctuary is one of the seven churches in the UNESCO World Heritage serial site "Longobards in Italy: Places of the Power (568–774 A.D.)", inscribed in 2011, in recognition of its Lombard-era significance.

The town

The old town, with its whitewashed houses and the Junno quarter, climbs around the sanctuary. Sights include the Norman-Swabian-Angevin castle, the church of Santa Maria Maggiore with Byzantine frescoes, and the so-called Tomb of Rothari. The town is also a centre for excursions into the Gargano National Park and the nearby Umbra Forest.
