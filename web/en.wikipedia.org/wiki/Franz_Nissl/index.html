Franz Nissl

Franz Alexander Nissl (9 September 1860 – 11 August 1919) was a German psychiatrist and neuropathologist. He is remembered for the Nissl stain, a method of staining neurons that revealed the granular material in nerve cell bodies now known as Nissl bodies, and for his pioneering histopathological studies of the cerebral cortex in mental illness.

Life and career

Nissl was born in Frankenthal in the Palatinate. He studied medicine at the Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich, where as a student he won a prize for an essay on the pathology of cortical cells using staining techniques he had developed with aniline dyes. He worked with Emil Kraepelin at Heidelberg, where he directed the histopathology laboratory and collaborated closely with Alois Alzheimer. In 1918 he moved to Munich to head the histopathology department of Kraepelin's new German Research Institute for Psychiatry.

Work

The Nissl stain, using cresyl violet and other basic aniline dyes, selectively colours the rough endoplasmic reticulum of neurons, allowing the arrangement and condition of nerve cells to be studied under the microscope. Nissl used the method to map cortical lamination and to describe changes in neurons in conditions such as general paresis of the insane and rabies. His systematic approach helped establish neuropathology as a foundation for biological psychiatry.
