Jan Smuts

Jan Christian Smuts (24 May 1870 – 11 September 1950) was a South African statesman, military leader and philosopher. He twice served as Prime Minister of the Union of South Africa (1919–1924 and 1939–1948), played a central role in the creation of both the League of Nations and the United Nations, and was a member of the British War Cabinet in both world wars. A former Boer commando general who became an architect of the British Commonwealth, Smuts remains a controversial figure for his paternalistic racial policies, which preceded the formal apartheid system he opposed in name but did not dismantle.

Early life and the Boer War

Smuts was born on a farm near Riebeek West in the Cape Colony, the second son of Afrikaner farmers. A brilliant student, he read law at Christ's College, Cambridge, graduating with a double first. Returning to South Africa, he became State Attorney of the South African Republic (Transvaal) under Paul Kruger. During the Second Boer War (1899–1902) he led a daring commando raid deep into the Cape Colony, recounted in his memoir Commando.

Union and the First World War

After the war Smuts worked with Louis Botha to achieve reconciliation and the Union of South Africa (1910), serving as minister of defence and finance. In the First World War he commanded forces that conquered German South-West Africa and campaigned in German East Africa. In 1917 Lloyd George brought him into the Imperial War Cabinet in London; Smuts's report on air defence led directly to the creation of the Royal Air Force as an independent service. At the Paris Peace Conference he promoted the League of Nations, drafting much of its practical blueprint, and signed the Treaty of Versailles for South Africa.

Prime Minister

Succeeding Botha in 1919, Smuts's first ministry was marked by industrial unrest, which he suppressed by force, and he lost the 1924 election to J. B. M. Hertzog's National Party. He returned to power in coalition and then fusion with Hertzog in the 1930s. When war broke out in 1939, Smuts narrowly won the parliamentary vote to join the Allies and became Prime Minister and commander-in-chief; South African forces fought in North Africa and Italy, and Smuts was made a British field marshal in 1941.

United Nations and final years

At San Francisco in 1945 Smuts drafted the preamble to the United Nations Charter, with its affirmation of faith in fundamental human rights — even as his own government maintained racial segregation at home, a contradiction increasingly attacked internationally. His United Party lost the 1948 election to the National Party, which went on to institute apartheid. Smuts died at his farm, Doornkloof, near Pretoria in 1950.

Philosophy

In Holism and Evolution (1926) Smuts coined the term "holism", arguing that nature evolves toward ever more integrated wholes — an idea that influenced both systems theory and, later, environmental thought.
