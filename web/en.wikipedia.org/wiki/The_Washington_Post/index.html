The Washington Post is an American daily newspaper published in Washington, D.C., founded in 1877. It is famed worldwide for its role in uncovering the Watergate scandal, which led to the resignation of President Richard Nixon in 1974, and for its political reporting from the capital. Since 2013 it has been owned by Jeff Bezos through Nash Holdings.

History

Founded by Stilson Hutchins, the Post passed through the McLean and Meyer-Graham families; under publisher Katharine Graham and editor Ben Bradlee, reporters Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein pursued the Watergate break-in story in 1972–74. The paper has won dozens of Pulitzer Prizes.

Bezos era

Bought by Amazon founder Jeff Bezos in 2013, the Post invested in digital subscriptions and technology, expanded nationally and internationally, and adopted the slogan 'Democracy Dies in Darkness' in 2017. In the mid-2020s it went through leadership changes and staff reductions amid losses.

Sections

Politics, national and world news, business, sports, lifestyle and opinions; the paper is also known for the Style section and its comics and food coverage.
