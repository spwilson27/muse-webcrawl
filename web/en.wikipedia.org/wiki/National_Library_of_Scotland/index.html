National Library of Scotland

The National Library of Scotland (NLS; Scottish Gaelic: Leabharlann Nàiseanta na h-Alba) is the legal deposit library of Scotland and one of the largest libraries in the United Kingdom. It is based in Edinburgh, with its main public building on George IV Bridge. As a legal deposit library, it is entitled to receive a copy of every publication issued in the United Kingdom and Ireland.

History

The library's origins lie in the library of the Faculty of Advocates, founded in 1680, which received legal deposit privileges under the Copyright Act 1710. The Advocates Library became renowned as one of Europe's finest collections, and in 1925 the faculty presented it to the nation, creating the National Library of Scotland by Act of Parliament. The George IV Bridge building, designed to house the library, was begun in the 1930s and finally opened in 1956.

Collections

The library holds tens of millions of items, including books, manuscripts, maps, music, and films. Treasures include the last letter written by Mary, Queen of Scots, the Order for the massacre of Glencoe, manuscripts of Robert Burns, and the world's largest collection of Scottish Gaelic material. Its map collection is among the largest anywhere, and the Moving Image Archive preserves Scottish film and video. Notable holdings include a Gutenberg Bible and letters of Charles Darwin.

Digital access

The NLS has digitised millions of pages, including out-of-copyright books, maps, directories, and newspapers, making them freely available through its websites. Its reading rooms in Edinburgh are open to the public, with Kelvin Hall in Glasgow hosting the Moving Image Archive and a public access centre.
