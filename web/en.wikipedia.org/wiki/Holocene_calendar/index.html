Holocene calendar

The Holocene calendar, also known as the Holocene Era (HE) or Human Era calendar, is a year-numbering system that adds exactly 10,000 years to the Anno Domini (AD) year. It was proposed in 1993 by the geologist and science writer Cesare Emiliani. Thus the year 2026 AD is 12026 HE. The system's epoch, 10,000 BCE, approximates the beginning of the Holocene geological epoch and the Neolithic Revolution.

Motivation

Emiliani proposed the calendar to simplify dating across human prehistory: the AD/BC system has no year zero, splits history at a religiously specific event, and places most of human development (the invention of agriculture, the first cities and writing) in "negative" BC years. By adding 10,000, all of recorded history and most of the Neolithic falls within positive, five-digit years, and conversion is trivial — simply prepend "1" to the AD year, or subtract the BC year from 10,001.

Examples

  • Construction of Göbekli Tepe (c. 9500 BCE) — c. 500 HE
  • Founding of Rome (753 BCE) — 9248 HE
  • Battle of Hastings (1066 AD) — 11066 HE
  • First Moon landing (1969 AD) — 11969 HE

Reception

The calendar has been taken up by some popular-science communicators and in works of "big history" as a neutral frame for human civilisation. It has not been adopted officially anywhere, and critics note that the 10,000 BCE date is only an approximation of the Holocene's start (formally about 11,700 years before 2000) and that a new era would still require global agreement. Similar era schemes include the Julian day count used by astronomers and the "Before Present" scale used in radiocarbon dating.
