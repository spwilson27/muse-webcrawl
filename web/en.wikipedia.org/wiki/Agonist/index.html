Agonist

An agonist is a chemical that activates a receptor to produce a biological response. In contrast, an antagonist blocks the action of the agonist, and an inverse agonist causes an action opposite to that of the agonist. Agonists may be full, partial, or inverse depending on the response they elicit, and the ability of an agonist to activate a receptor is described by its efficacy, while its binding strength is described by its affinity (potency). Many drugs act as agonists at specific receptors.

Opposite classAntagonist (blocks); inverse agonist (reverses)
Key measuresEfficacy (maximal response); potency (concentration needed)
ExamplesMorphine (μ-opioid), salbutamol (β₂), dopamine agonists

Types of agonists

  • Full agonists bind and activate a receptor to its maximal response.
  • Partial agonists activate the receptor but produce a submaximal response even at full occupancy; they can antagonise full agonists by competing for binding.
  • Inverse agonists reduce the constitutive (baseline) activity of a receptor, producing an effect opposite to an agonist.
  • Superagonists, selective agonists, and co-agonists (which require a second molecule to enable activation, such as glycine at NMDA receptors) are further refinements.
  • Irreversible agonists bind permanently to the receptor, often by covalent bonding.

Endogenous and exogenous agonists

Endogenous agonists are produced by the body: neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine, and hormones such as insulin, are the natural agonists of their receptors. Exogenous agonists are external compounds, including most drugs that mimic these natural ligands.

Examples in medicine

Morphine is a full agonist at the μ-opioid receptor used for severe pain; buprenorphine is a partial agonist used in opioid dependence. Salbutamol (albuterol) is a β₂-adrenergic agonist used as a bronchodilator in asthma. Dopamine agonists such as pramipexole are used in Parkinson's disease. GLP-1 receptor agonists, such as semaglutide, are used for type 2 diabetes and obesity.

Other meanings

In anatomy, an agonist is a muscle that contracts to cause a specific movement (the prime mover), opposed by an antagonist muscle. In literature, an agonist is related to the protagonist; the term derives from the Greek agōnistēs, a contestant in the games.
