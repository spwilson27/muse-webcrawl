Catalan (català) is a Romance language spoken in eastern Spain (Catalonia, the Valencian Community and the Balearic Islands), Andorra — where it is the only official language of a country — the Roussillon region of southern France, and the city of Alghero in Sardinia. It developed from Vulgar Latin in the Pyrenean region during the early Middle Ages and produced a notable medieval literature, including the works of Ramon Llull.

History

Catalan spread southward with the expansion of the Crown of Aragon. After periods of decline under Castilian centralisation, and repression during the Franco era, the language was revitalised following Spain's return to democracy; it is now the medium of education and public life in Catalonia. Valencian, spoken in the Valencian Community, is regarded by linguists as a variety of the same language.

Features

Catalan shares features with both Iberian and Gallo-Romance languages. It has eight vowel phonemes in stressed position in eastern dialects, palatal consonants written ll and ny, and a characteristic use of the periphrastic past tense formed with the verb anar ("to go").
