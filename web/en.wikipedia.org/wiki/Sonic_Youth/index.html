Sonic Youth

Sonic Youth
Origin: New York City, US
Genres: Alternative rock, noise rock, experimental rock, no wave
Years active: 1981–2011
Members: Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon, Lee Ranaldo, Steve Shelley, Mark Ibold

Sonic Youth was an American rock band formed in New York City in 1981. Founded by Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon and Lee Ranaldo, the band became a pivotal influence on alternative and indie rock through its use of unconventional guitar tunings, extended techniques and feedback-drenched noise.

History

Emerging from New York's no wave scene, Sonic Youth released early records such as Confusion Is Sex (1983) and Bad Moon Rising (1985). Drummer Steve Shelley joined in 1985. The double album Daydream Nation (1988) was a critical landmark, later selected for the US National Recording Registry. Signing to Geffen Records, the band released Goo (1990) and Dirty (1992), and helped champion younger acts including Nirvana. Later acclaimed albums include Washing Machine (1995), Murray Street (2002) and Rather Ripped (2006). The band effectively ended in 2011 following Moore and Gordon's separation.

Style and legacy

Sonic Youth's guitarists prepared instruments with drumsticks and screwdrivers, employed dozens of alternate tunings, and built songs around drones and dissonance. Their advocacy of independent music and their bridge between the underground and the mainstream made them one of the most influential American bands of their era.
