Battle of Samarra (363)

The Battle of Samarra was fought in June 363, during the Persian campaign of the Roman emperor Julian. After failing to take Ctesiphon and burning his supply fleet, Julian retreated north along the Tigris. Near Samarra (Maranga region), his army defeated a Sasanian Persian force, but Julian was mortally wounded in a related engagement on 26 June 363 and died that night, leaving the army to extricate itself under his successor Jovian.

Background

Julian invaded Persia in spring 363 with a large army, advancing down the Euphrates while a second force operated in Armenia. He defeated Persian forces before Ctesiphon at the Battle of Ctesiphon but could not besiege the city without siege equipment and secure supplies. A council of war decided to burn the fleet and march inland, then retreat up the Tigris valley, harried by Persian cavalry employing scorched-earth tactics.

The battle and Julian's death

Near Maranga/Samarra the Romans turned and defeated a Persian attack in a pitched engagement. Days later, on 26 June, during a sudden Persian assault on the rearguard, Julian rushed into the fight without his breastplate and was struck by a spear. He died in his tent at midnight. Ammianus Marcellinus, an eyewitness officer and our principal source, gives a detailed account of the campaign and the emperor's death.

Aftermath

The army acclaimed the guards officer Jovian as emperor. Trapped east of the Tigris with supplies failing, Jovian concluded a humiliating peace with Shapur II, ceding the five provinces east of the Tigris, Nisibis and Singara, and agreeing to a thirty-year truce. Julian's death ended the last pagan restoration of Roman religion.
