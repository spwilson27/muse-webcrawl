Kirkcaldy

Council area: Fife
Country: Scotland
Nickname: The Lang Toun

Kirkcaldy (Scottish Gaelic: Cair Chaladain) is a town and former royal burgh in Fife, Scotland, on the east coast on the north shore of the Firth of Forth. It is known as the "Lang Toun" (long town) for its extensive main street and seafront, and has a population of roughly 50,000.

History

The town received its royal burgh charter in 1644, though it had earlier been held by Dunfermline Abbey. Kirkcaldy grew with the textile trade and became famous for linoleum manufacture — the industry that made floorcloth and linoleum a major export from the late 19th century. It was also a significant port and a centre for linen, salt, and coal.

Notable people

Kirkcaldy is the birthplace of the economist Adam Smith, author of The Wealth of Nations, who wrote much of his earlier work while living in the town. Other notable natives include the architect Robert Adam and the former prime minister Gordon Brown, who represented the town in Parliament. Kirkcaldy Galleries houses the town's museum and art gallery, and Raith Rovers Football Club plays at Stark's Park.
