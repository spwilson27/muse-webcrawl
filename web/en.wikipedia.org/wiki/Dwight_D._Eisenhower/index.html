Dwight David "Ike" Eisenhower (October 14, 1890 – March 28, 1969) was an American military officer and statesman who served as the 34th president of the United States from 1953 to 1961. During World War II, he was Supreme Commander of the Allied Expeditionary Force in Europe and achieved the five-star rank of General of the Army. He planned and supervised Operation Torch in North Africa in 1942–43 and the Normandy landings (Operation Overlord) in 1944.

Early life and military career

Born in Denison, Texas, and raised in Abilene, Kansas, Eisenhower graduated from West Point in 1915 in the "class the stars fell on". Between the wars he served under Douglas MacArthur in the Philippines and ranked first in his class at the Command and General Staff School. His skill in organisation and coalition management, rather than battlefield command, propelled his wartime rise.

Presidency

Elected as a Republican with the slogan "I Like Ike", Eisenhower ended the Korean War in 1953, authorised the Interstate Highway System (1956), enforced school desegregation at Little Rock in 1957, created NASA in 1958, and admitted Alaska and Hawaii as states in 1959. His foreign policy combined containment of the Soviet Union with fiscal restraint ("New Look"), while covert CIA operations toppled governments in Iran (1953) and Guatemala (1954). In his farewell address (1961) he famously warned against the growing power of the "military-industrial complex".

Legacy

Once ranked as a mediocre president, Eisenhower is now generally placed in the top tier by historians, credited with prosperity, stability, and a restrained "hidden-hand" leadership style.
