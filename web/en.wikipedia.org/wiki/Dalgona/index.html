Dalgona

Dalgona (Korean: 달고나), also known as ppopgi (뽑기), is a Korean candy made with melted sugar and baking soda. It is a type of honeycomb toffee: a pinch of baking soda whisked into hot melted sugar releases carbon dioxide, puffing the mixture into a light, rigid foam. Street vendors pour the molten candy into flat discs and stamp them with shapes such as stars, circles, and umbrellas.

The game

Traditionally, dalgona is sold as a challenge: a customer who can pick or bite out the stamped shape without cracking the candy wins another one free. The candy became a fixture of Korean street food from the 1960s–1980s and was commonly sold outside schools.

In popular culture

Dalgona achieved worldwide fame in 2021 through the Netflix series Squid Game, in which contestants must carve a shape from the fragile candy with a needle under threat of elimination. The episode sparked a global viral trend, a surge in sales of dalgona kits, and street-vendor booms in Seoul. The name "dalgona" also became attached during the COVID-19 pandemic to dalgona coffee, a whipped coffee drink whose foam resembles the candy's colour — though the two are otherwise unrelated.
