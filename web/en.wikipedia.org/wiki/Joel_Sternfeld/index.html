Joel Sternfeld (born June 30, 1944) is an American fine art photographer. He is one of the pioneers of color photography as a serious artistic medium in the 1970s and 1980s, and is best known for American Prospects (1987), a sweeping portrait of the United States made on road trips across the country with a large-format camera.

Work

Sternfeld studied at Dartmouth College and began photographing in color at a time when black-and-white still dominated art photography. Supported by Guggenheim Fellowships, he traveled the United States between 1977 and 1988, producing images that juxtapose the banal and the unsettling: a fireman buying pumpkins while a house burns behind him, training exercises, suburban developments and small-town main streets. American Prospects is regarded as one of the landmark photobooks of the twentieth century.

Later projects include On This Site: Landscape in Memoriam (1996), which pairs quiet landscapes with texts describing crimes or tragedies that occurred there; Stranger Passing (2001), portraits of Americans encountered by chance; Sweet Earth: Experimental Utopias in America (2005); and Walking the High Line (2001), which documented New York's derelict elevated railway before its transformation into a park and helped galvanize efforts to preserve it. He has taught for many years at Sarah Lawrence College.
