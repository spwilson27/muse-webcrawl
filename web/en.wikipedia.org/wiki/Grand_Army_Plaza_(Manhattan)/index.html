Grand Army Plaza (Manhattan)

Grand Army Plaza at Fifth Avenue and 59th Street

LocationFifth Avenue at 59th Street, Manhattan, New York City
Named forGrand Army of the Potomac

Grand Army Plaza is a public square at the southeast corner of Central Park in Manhattan, New York City, at the intersection of Fifth Avenue and Central Park South (59th Street). It is named for the Grand Army of the Potomac, the Union army of the Civil War.

Layout and monuments

The plaza is divided by Central Park South into northern and southern halves. The northern half contains the gilded bronze equestrian statue of General William Tecumseh Sherman by Augustus Saint-Gaudens, unveiled in 1903 and notable as one of the finest equestrian monuments in the United States. The southern half contains the Pulitzer Fountain, designed by Karl Bitter and crowned by the figure of Pomona, goddess of abundance.

Surroundings

The plaza is overlooked by landmark buildings including The Plaza Hotel, which opened in 1907 on the site of an earlier hotel of the same name, and the General Motors Building. Apple's flagship Fifth Avenue store, with its distinctive glass cube entrance, opened across the plaza in 2006.
