Lambeth Conference

The Lambeth Conference is a decennial assembly of bishops of the Anglican Communion, convened by the Archbishop of Canterbury. The first conference took place in 1867 at Lambeth Palace in London. As one of the Communion's "Instruments of Communion", it expresses the collective mind of Anglicanism on matters of doctrine, mission, and social concern, though its resolutions are not legally binding on member churches.

History

The conference was first proposed in 1851 and was convened in 1867 by Archbishop Charles Longley, partly in response to the Colenso controversy. Seventy-six bishops attended the first gathering. Subsequent conferences have been held roughly every ten years, moving in 1978 and later to the University of Kent at Canterbury, with sessions also at Canterbury Cathedral and Lambeth Palace.

Notable conferences

The 1888 conference shaped Anglican identity around the Chicago-Lambeth Quadrilateral. The 1930 conference cautiously accepted artificial contraception within marriage, and the 1958 conference addressed the family and nuclear weapons. Recent conferences have debated human sexuality and the role of women bishops, including the 1998 Resolution I.10 on sexuality and the 2022 conference's "calls" on human dignity and other themes.

Organization

All active diocesan and suffragan bishops of the Anglican Communion's provinces are invited. The fifteenth conference was held in 2022 in Canterbury, having been postponed from 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
