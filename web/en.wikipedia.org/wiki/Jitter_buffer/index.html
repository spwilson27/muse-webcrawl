Jitter buffer

Jitter buffer
TypePacket buffer
Used inVoIP, video streaming, online gaming

A jitter buffer or de-jitter buffer is a shared data area where voice packets can be collected, stored and sent to the voice processor in evenly spaced intervals. It temporarily stores arriving packets in order to minimise delay variations, known as jitter, in packet-switched networks.

Variations in packet arrival time can cause gaps or distortion in reconstructed audio. A jitter buffer holds early packets briefly so that late packets can still be played out in the correct sequence, at the cost of a small amount of added latency.

Types

Static jitter buffers are fixed at a set size by the manufacturer or network configuration. Dynamic jitter buffers adjust their depth automatically based on measured network conditions, growing when jitter increases and shrinking to reduce delay when the network is stable.

Implementation

If the buffer is too small, packets arriving after their scheduled playout time are discarded and must be concealed. If it is too large, conversational delay becomes noticeable. Typical VoIP jitter buffers hold between 20 and 200 milliseconds of audio.
