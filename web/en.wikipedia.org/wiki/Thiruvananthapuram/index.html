Thiruvananthapuram, also known by its former name Trivandrum, is the capital of the Indian state of Kerala. Located on the west coast of India near the extreme south of the mainland, it is a major centre of government, education, information technology and space research. The city takes its name from the Malayalam words thiru-anantha-puram, "The City of Lord Ananta", referring to the deity of the Padmanabhaswamy Temple, whose reclining Vishnu on the serpent Ananta is the city's most famous landmark.

History

The region has traded with the outside world since at least 1000 BCE, exporting spices, sandalwood and ivory. The city rose to prominence in the 18th century under the Travancore kingdom, particularly under Marthanda Varma, who made it his capital around 1745 and dedicated the kingdom to the temple deity. Travancore acceded to India after independence, and in 1949 Thiruvananthapuram became the capital of the new state of Travancore-Cochin, then of Kerala on its formation in 1956.

Economy and institutions

Thiruvananthapuram hosts Technopark, one of India's largest IT parks, the headquarters of the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (the lead centre of ISRO), and the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology. It is also a centre of Ayurveda, film production in Malayalam, and higher education, with institutions such as the University of Kerala.

Culture

The city is noted for the Padmanabhaswamy Temple — which drew worldwide attention in 2011 when inventories of its vaults revealed treasures of extraordinary value — the Napier Museum, the Kanakakkunnu Palace, and the Kovalam beach resort area nearby. The Attukal Pongala festival, centred on the Attukal temple, is among the largest gatherings of women in the world.
