T-Bone Walker

Aaron Thibeaux "T-Bone" Walker (May 28, 1910 – March 16, 1975) was an American blues guitarist, singer and songwriter. He was a pioneer and innovator of the jump blues and electric blues sound, and is widely considered one of the most influential blues musicians of all time. In 2018 Rolling Stone magazine ranked him number 37 on its list of "The 250 Greatest Guitarists of All Time".

Life and career

Walker was born in Linden, Texas, and grew up in Dallas, where he was mentored by the blind bluesman Blind Lemon Jefferson. He began performing as a teenager, touring with medicine shows and later with bandleader Cab Calloway's orchestra after winning an amateur contest.

His recording of "Stormy Monday" (formally "Call It Stormy Monday (But Tuesday Is Just as Bad)") in 1947 became his signature song and one of the most popular blues standards ever recorded. Walker developed a distinctive, fluid single-note soloing style on the electric guitar, playing the instrument flat against his body and behind his head — showmanship later imitated by Chuck Berry and Jimi Hendrix.

Influence and legacy

Walker influenced virtually every major electric blues and rock guitarist who followed, including B.B. King, who said that hearing Walker made him want to play electric guitar. He was inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 1980 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987.
