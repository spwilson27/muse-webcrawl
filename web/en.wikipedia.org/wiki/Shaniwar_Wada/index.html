Shaniwar Wada is a historic fortified palace in Pune, Maharashtra, India. It was the seat of the Peshwas of the Maratha Empire from its construction in 1732 until the British captured Pune in 1818. Most of the palace complex was destroyed by a fire in 1828, but its massive stone walls, gatehouses and foundations survive as a major tourist attraction and a symbol of Pune.

History

The foundation was laid by Peshwa Baji Rao I in 1730, and the residence was completed in 1732 at a cost of about 16,110 rupees — modest for a palace, in keeping with Baji Rao's wishes. The name derives from Shaniwar (Saturday), the day construction began, and wada (residential complex). Under Baji Rao's successors the complex grew to include courts, audience halls, fountains — including the famous thousand-jet Hazari Karanje — temples and the residences of the Peshwa's household.

The murder of Narayanrao

Shaniwar Wada is remembered in Maratha tradition for the assassination of the young Peshwa Narayanrao in 1773, allegedly on the orders connected to his uncle Raghunathrao and aunt Anandibai. Legend holds that Narayanrao ran through the palace crying "Kaka, mala vachva!" ("Uncle, save me!") — words that feature in local ghost lore claiming his voice can still be heard on full-moon nights.

Destruction and present state

After the Third Anglo-Maratha War, the last Peshwa, Baji Rao II, surrendered in 1818 and the British raised the Union Jack over the palace. A great fire in 1828 burned for days and gutted the interior; only the stone ramparts, five gateways (including the imposing Delhi Darwaza) and foundations remain. The site is maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India, with gardens laid out where the palace stood, and a light-and-sound show recounting Maratha history is held in the evenings.
