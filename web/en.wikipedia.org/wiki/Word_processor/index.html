Word processor

A word processor is a device or computer program that provides for input, editing, formatting, and output of text, often with additional features. Before general-purpose computers became common, "word processor" also referred to dedicated machines — electronic typewriters with screens and storage — sold by companies such as Wang, IBM, and Xerox.

History

The first word processors were mechanical and electrical text-editing machines. The term gained prominence in the 1970s with dedicated systems such as the IBM MT/ST (Magnetic Tape/Selectric Typewriter, 1964) and Wang's word processing systems. Software word processors appeared on early personal computers: Electric Pencil (1976), WordStar (1978), and WordPerfect (1980) were early landmarks, followed by Microsoft Word (1983), which became dominant in the 1990s.

Features

A word processor typically offers:

  • WYSIWYG ("what you see is what you get") editing and page layout
  • Fonts, styles, and paragraph formatting
  • Spell checking, grammar checking, and a thesaurus
  • Find and replace, word count, and revision tracking ("track changes")
  • Tables, images, footnotes, indexes, tables of contents, and mail merge
  • Import and export of formats such as PDF, HTML, and ODT/DOCX

Examples

Widely used word processors include Microsoft Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice Writer, Apple Pages, and AbiWord. Online word processors such as Google Docs added real-time collaboration as a defining feature. Specialised tools for technical writing, such as LaTeX editors, prioritise typesetting control over WYSIWYG display.
