Adam and Eve

Adam and Eve, according to the creation myth of the Abrahamic religions, were the first man and woman. They are central to the belief that humanity is in essence a single family, with everyone descended from a single pair of original ancestors. Their story is told in the Book of Genesis, chapters 1–5, and they also appear in the Quran and in later Jewish, Christian and Islamic tradition.

The Genesis narrative

In Genesis 1, God creates humankind in his image, male and female. In the second account (Genesis 2–3), God forms Adam from the dust of the ground and places him in the Garden of Eden to tend it, forbidding him to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Eve is created from Adam's rib as his companion. A serpent tempts Eve to eat the forbidden fruit, which she shares with Adam. Their eyes are opened; ashamed of their nakedness, they are expelled from Eden, condemned to labor, pain in childbirth, and mortality — the event Christian theology calls the Fall of man.

Adam and Eve's children include Cain, Abel and Seth; Genesis gives Adam's lifespan as 930 years.

In Judaism, Christianity and Islam

In Jewish tradition Adam and Eve are the ancestors of humanity, though the Fall has no doctrine of inherited original sin as developed in Christianity. In Christian theology, especially following Augustine, Adam's sin is transmitted to all humanity (original sin), redeemed through Christ, the "second Adam"; Eve is paralleled with Mary. In Islam, Adam (Adam) is the first prophet and Hawwa his wife; they disobey God, repent, and are forgiven, and the episode is understood without inherited sin.

Historicity and interpretation

Mainstream scholarship reads the Eden narrative as myth, with parallels in ancient Near Eastern literature such as the Epic of Gilgamesh. Genetic science indicates modern humans descend from populations rather than a single couple, though "Y-chromosomal Adam" and "Mitochondrial Eve" — the most recent common patrilineal and matrilineal ancestors — borrow their names from the story.
