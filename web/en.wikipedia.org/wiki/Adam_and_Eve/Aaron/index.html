Aaron (Hebrew: אַהֲרֹן‎, Aharon) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the elder brother of Moses and Miriam and a son of Amram and Jochebed of the tribe of Levi. He served as the spokesman for Moses before Pharaoh and became the first High Priest (Kohen Gadol) of the Israelites, founding the hereditary priesthood whose male descendants are the Kohanim.

[Image: Aaron in high priestly garments]

Role in the Exodus

According to the Book of Exodus, when Moses protested his slowness of speech, God appointed Aaron as his "mouth." Aaron carried a rod that budded and performed signs, and he stretched out his hand in several of the plagues of Egypt. During Moses' absence on Mount Sinai, Aaron yielded to the people's demand and fashioned the golden calf, an act for which he was rebuked. At the battle with Amalek at Rephidim, Aaron and Hur held up Moses' hands so Israel prevailed.

Priesthood and death

Aaron and his sons were consecrated as priests with elaborate vestments described in Exodus, including the breastplate with twelve stones and the golden plate inscribed "Holy to the LORD." His authority was confirmed when his rod alone budded among the rods of the tribal chiefs. Aaron died on Mount Hor at the age of 123 and was succeeded as high priest by his son Eleazar, his garments being transferred in a ceremony described in Numbers 20. In Islam he is the prophet Harun, again the brother and helper of Moses (Musa).
