Cipriano de Rore (1515 or 1516 – September 1565) was a Franco-Flemish composer of the Renaissance, active in Italy. He was one of the most influential composers of madrigals in the mid-16th century, and his experimental, chromatic writing profoundly influenced later composers, including Claudio Monteverdi.

Life

Rore was born in Ronse in Flanders and probably received his early musical training there before travelling to Italy. He may have studied with Adrian Willaert in Venice. From 1546 he served as maestro di cappella at the court of Ferrara under Duke Ercole II d'Este, remaining there until 1558. After a period back in Flanders, he returned to Italy, briefly succeeding Willaert at St Mark's Basilica in Venice in 1563, and ended his career at the Farnese court in Parma.

Music and influence

Rore published five books of madrigals for five voices, along with motets, masses and chansons. His madrigals are noted for their serious tone, close attention to the text and bold use of chromaticism — most famously in pieces such as Calami sonum ferentes. His experiments helped shape the expressive language that led to the seconda pratica and early Baroque music.
