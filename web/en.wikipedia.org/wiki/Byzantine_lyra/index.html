Byzantine lyra

Placeholder image: a Byzantine lyra (lira).

The Byzantine lyra or lira (Greek: λύρα) was a medieval bowed string instrument of the Byzantine Empire. It was a pear-shaped, three-stringed instrument held upright on the knee and played with a bow, with the strings stopped by the player's fingernails from the side rather than pressed down by the fingertips. It is regarded as an ancestor of most European bowed instruments, including the violin family.

History and spread

The bowed lyra appears in Byzantine sources from around the 9th–10th centuries, possibly adapted from bowed instruments of Central Asia transmitted through the Islamic world. From Byzantium the instrument spread westward into Italy — where it became the lira da braccio lineage — and into the Slavic world, where related instruments survive as the Bulgarian gadulka, the Serbian and Croatian lijerica, and the Cretan lyra, which remains the central folk instrument of Crete and the Dodecanese. The Pontic lyra (kemençe) is played by Greek communities from the Black Sea region.

Characteristics

The body is carved from a single block of wood with a vaulted back; typical instruments have three strings, the middle often acting as a drone, and sympathetic resonance gives the sound a bright, nasal quality suited to dance music and song accompaniment.
