Vocational university

Vocational universities emphasise applied, profession-oriented higher education

A vocational university is an institution of higher education that provides tertiary education leading to academic degrees while emphasising vocational, professional and applied training rather than purely theoretical research. Such institutions are known by many national names, including universities of applied sciences (German Fachhochschule, Finnish ammattikorkeakoulu, Dutch hogeschool), polytechnics, and institutes of technology.

Characteristics

Vocational universities typically offer bachelor's and master's degrees in fields such as engineering, business, healthcare, social services, design and information technology. Curricula include compulsory work placements, teaching staff are often required to have substantial professional experience outside academia, and research is applied and development-oriented, usually conducted with regional employers. In many systems they historically could not award doctorates, though several countries have begun granting limited doctoral rights in applied fields.

By country

Germany's Fachhochschulen, created from 1968 onwards, educate roughly a third of German higher-education students and were renamed "universities of applied sciences" in English-language usage. Finland built its AMK sector in the 1990s, and the Netherlands' HBO sector is a long-standing binary counterpart to research universities. In China, a tier of undergraduate vocational universities (zhiye benke) has been developed since 2019 to supply advanced technical skills. In the United Kingdom the former polytechnics became universities in 1992, ending the formal binary divide, though their teaching mission remains comparatively vocational.
