Azerbaijan or Azarbaijan (Persian: آذربایجان, romanized: Āzarbāyjān; Azerbaijani: Azərbaycan) is a historical region in northwestern Iran that borders Iraq, Turkey, the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic, Armenia, and the Republic of Azerbaijan. It corresponds to the Iranian provinces of East Azerbaijan, West Azerbaijan, and Ardabil, together with parts of Zanjan Province. The region's historic capital and largest city is Tabriz.

Name and history

The name derives, by the standard scholarly account, from Atropates, the Persian satrap who founded the kingdom of Atropatene in the region after the death of Alexander the Great; Greek Atropatene developed into Middle Persian Ādurbādagān and then Āzarbāyjān. Historically the name applied only to the territory south of the Aras River — the present Iranian region — while the lands to the north, known as Arran and Shirvan, adopted the name "Azerbaijan" only in 1918, when the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic was proclaimed; this naming remains a source of occasional controversy in Iranian historiography.

Geography

Iranian Azerbaijan is a mountainous region of the northwestern Iranian plateau, dominated by volcanic massifs including Sahand and Sabalan, and by the basin of Lake Urmia, one of the world's largest salt lakes, which has shrunk dramatically since the 1990s. The climate is continental, with cold winters and warm, dry summers.

People and culture

The population is predominantly ethnic Azerbaijanis (Iranian Azerbaijanis), who speak the Azerbaijani language, a Turkic language, and are mostly Twelver Shia Muslims. There are also Kurdish populations in the west, and smaller Armenian, Assyrian and Talysh communities. The region has played a major political role in modern Iranian history: Tabriz was a stronghold of the Constitutional Revolution of 1906–1911, and in 1945–46 the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government briefly ruled the region before being reabsorbed by Tehran.

Economy

Agriculture (wheat, fruit, tobacco), carpet weaving, petrochemicals, machinery and food processing around Tabriz, and cross-border trade with Turkey and the Republic of Azerbaijan form the regional economy. Tabriz has long been a major centre on trade routes between Anatolia, the Caucasus and the Iranian plateau.
