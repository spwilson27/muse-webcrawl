Blaenau Ffestiniog is a town in Gwynedd, Wales, in the historic county of Merionethshire. Once a major centre of the Welsh slate industry — "the town that roofed the world" — it is surrounded by the mountains of Snowdonia (Eryri), yet was famously excluded from the Snowdonia National Park at its creation because of its industrial landscape of slate quarries and spoil heaps. In 2021 the Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales, including Blaenau Ffestiniog, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Slate industry

Slate quarrying around Blaenau began in the late 18th century and expanded rapidly in the 19th, with great quarries at Llechwedd, Maenofferen, Oakeley, and Votty & Bowydd employing thousands. Distinctive blue-grey Ffestiniog slate roofed buildings across industrial Britain and was exported worldwide. The narrow-gauge Ffestiniog Railway, opened in 1836, carried slate downhill to the harbour at Porthmadog, initially by gravity and horse power and later by steam.

Decline and reinvention

The slate trade declined through the 20th century as cheaper roofing materials spread; major quarries closed after the Second World War, and the last big underground workings ended in the late 20th century. Tourism has since grown: the Llechwedd slate caverns offer underground tours, Zip World operates zip lines and an underground trampoline park in the quarries, and the area is a centre for mountain biking at Antur Stiniog. The restored Ffestiniog Railway, reopened to Blaenau in 1982, now runs jointly with the Welsh Highland Railway from Porthmadog.

Geography and transport

The town sits at the head of the Vale of Ffestiniog, ringed by the Moelwyn mountains. It has a reputation as one of the wettest places in Wales. Blaenau Ffestiniog railway station is the terminus of the Conwy Valley line from Llandudno Junction and is shared with the Ffestiniog Railway. The A470 trunk road passes through the town.

Culture

Blaenau is a strongly Welsh-speaking community and a heartland of Welsh-language music; the rock group Anweledig and singer Gwennno have roots in the town. It is twinned with the slate-quarrying town of its heritage and hosts the annual Stiniog music festival events.
