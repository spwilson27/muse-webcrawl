Conspiracy of silence

[Hero image placeholder: three figures with hands over their mouths]

A conspiracy of silence, or culture of silence, is a term for a group of people who tacitly agree to outwardly ignore, disregard, or overlook an issue. Such a conspiracy may be directed at suppressing knowledge of a person's or group's conduct, or at maintaining social norms that discourage discussion of a taboo subject.

Usage

The phrase is used across many settings. In organizational sociology it describes the self-censorship of whistle-blowers and the collective non-acknowledgement of internal crises. In family systems literature, it refers to the unspoken rules that prevent members from discussing addiction, abuse or mental illness. In criminology it overlaps with the Mafia code of omertà. In medicine, "conspiracy of silence" historically described the practice of withholding a terminal diagnosis from patients at the request of relatives.

Historically, the concept is often invoked in discussions of how societies deal with uncomfortable truths — from the prolonged silence around the Holocaust in post-war Europe, to institutional cover-ups in organizations and political systems. It also describes the phenomenon by which official secrets remain hidden because many people know them but no one speaks publicly.

Related concepts

The "spiral of silence" theory of Elisabeth Noelle-Neumann describes how individuals suppress their opinions for fear of social isolation. The "wall of silence" is a related term in policing, where officers refuse to report or testify about misconduct by colleagues. Fear of retaliation is a common mechanism sustaining such silence.
