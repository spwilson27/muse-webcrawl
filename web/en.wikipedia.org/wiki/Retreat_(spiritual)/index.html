Retreat (spiritual)

TypeReligious / contemplative practice
DurationHours to months
TraditionsChristianity, Buddhism, Islam, Hinduism, secular mindfulness

A monastery retreat setting

A spiritual retreat is a period spent away from ordinary life for prayer, meditation, reflection, or religious instruction. Retreats are a feature of many traditions and may be undertaken alone or in a group, in silence or with guided teaching.

In Christianity

Retreats have roots in the desert monastic tradition and in the example of Jesus withdrawing to pray. The Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola (16th century) shaped the directed retreat, in which a retreatant follows a structured programme of meditation over days or weeks. Monasteries and dedicated retreat houses host thousands of retreatants each year.

In other traditions

Buddhist practice includes intensive meditation retreats, such as the three-month rains retreat (vassa) and modern vipassanā courses. Sufi Islam has the khalwa, a period of seclusion, and Hindu ashrams host periods of study and practice. Secular mindfulness and wellness retreats adapt similar structures without religious content.
