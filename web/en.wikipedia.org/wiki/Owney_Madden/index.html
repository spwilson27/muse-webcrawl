Owney Madden

Owen Vincent Madden
Born: 18 December 1891, Leeds, England
Died: 24 April 1965, Hot Springs, Arkansas
Occupation: Gangster, nightclub owner
Known for: Cotton Club; Prohibition bootlegging

Owen Vincent "Owney" Madden (December 18, 1891 – April 24, 1965) was an English-born gangster who became a leading underworld figure in Manhattan during Prohibition. Nicknamed "The Killer", Madden led the Gophers gang in Hell's Kitchen, survived numerous shootings, and served time in Sing Sing for manslaughter before becoming a major bootlegger and nightclub proprietor.

Career

Released from prison in 1923, Madden built a bootlegging empire around his "Madden's No. 1" beer and, with partners, opened the famous Cotton Club in Harlem, which showcased Black performers such as Duke Ellington and Cab Calloway to white audiences. He also owned the Connie's Inn and other clubs, invested in boxing promotions and Broadway shows, and was a behind-the-scenes power in Democratic clubhouse politics.

Later years

After a parole violation sent him back to prison, Madden left New York in 1935 for Hot Springs, Arkansas, then a wide-open gambling resort. There he became the town's underworld overlord, running casinos and the Southern Club, marrying the postmaster's daughter, and living as a respectable local figure until his death in 1965.
