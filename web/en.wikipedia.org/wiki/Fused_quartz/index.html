Fused quartz

Fused quartz, also known as fused silica, is a glass consisting of nearly pure silica (silicon dioxide, SiO2) in amorphous (non-crystalline) form. Unlike most other glasses, fused quartz contains no other ingredients typically added to lower the melting temperature.

Fused quartz
Material: Vitreous silica
Formula: SiO2
Density: ~2.20 g/cm3
Softening point: ~1,665 °C

Properties

Fused quartz has a very low coefficient of thermal expansion (about 5.5 × 10−7 K−1), making it highly resistant to thermal shock. It transmits light well into the ultraviolet range and has excellent chemical resistance and electrical insulating properties.

Production

It is manufactured by melting high-purity silica sand or quartz crystals at temperatures around 2,000 °C, either in electrically heated furnaces or by flame fusion of powdered rock crystal.

Applications

Uses include semiconductor fabrication equipment, laboratory crucibles and tubes, UV optics, telescope mirror blanks, and high-temperature viewports. Synthetic fused silica, made from silicon tetrachloride, is used in optical fibers and precision optics.
