Fast-track construction

MethodConstruction project delivery
Key featureDesign and construction phases overlap

Fast-track construction is a project delivery strategy in which construction of a building or facility begins before the design is fully complete. By overlapping the design and construction phases — starting foundations, for example, while interior designs are still being finalized — the total project schedule can be significantly shortened.

Method

In a traditional design–bid–build sequence, the owner completes the full design, tenders it, and only then begins construction. In fast-track delivery, the design is broken into packages (site work, foundations, structure, envelope, fit-out) that are tendered and built in sequence as each package is finished. The method is commonly paired with construction management or design–build contracts.

Advantages and risks

The principal advantage is earlier completion and earlier occupation or revenue for the owner, together with reduced exposure to inflation. The risks are equally notable: because work proceeds before the design is settled, changes are more expensive, coordination errors are more likely, and final cost cannot be fixed with confidence at the outset. Fast-tracking therefore demands experienced project management, strong communication, and an owner willing to accept cost uncertainty in exchange for speed. It is widely used for commercial developments, factories, and projects with hard deadlines.
