Viscount Bearsted

Viscount Bearsted, of Maidstone in the County of Kent, is a title in the Peerage of the United Kingdom. It was created in 1925 for the businessman Marcus Samuel, 1st Baron Bearsted, founder of the Shell Transport and Trading Company.

History

Marcus Samuel had previously been created a baronet in 1903 and raised to the peerage as Baron Bearsted in 1921. The Samuel family, of Jewish origin, rose through the London shell and general trading business M. Samuel & Co. into oil, playing a central role in the early petroleum industry and in the formation of Royal Dutch Shell.

Viscounts Bearsted

Marcus Samuel, 1st Viscount Bearsted (1853–1927), was succeeded by his son Walter Horace Samuel, 2nd Viscount Bearsted (1882–1948), a businessman and noted collector of Japanese art whose collection is associated with the family seat Upton House in Warwickshire, later given to the National Trust. The title has continued in the family, closely linked to the Samuel family's business and philanthropic interests.
