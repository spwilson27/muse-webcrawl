Hypocycloid

In geometry, a hypocycloid is a special plane curve generated by the trace of a fixed point on a small circle that rolls within a larger circle. It is comparable to the cycloid — which is generated by a point on a circle rolling along a straight line — and is a particular kind of hypotrochoid.

Properties

If the smaller circle has radius r and the larger circle has radius R = kr, then the parametric equations of the curve are:

x(θ) = (R − r) cos θ + r cos((R − r)/r θ),  y(θ) = (R − r) sin θ − r sin((R − r)/r θ)

If k is an integer, the curve is closed and has k cusps. Special cases include: k = 2, where the curve degenerates to a diameter of the large circle (the basis of the Tusi couple); k = 3, the deltoid; and k = 4, the astroid. If k is rational, the curve is closed with a number of cusps determined by the numerator; if irrational, it never closes and fills the annulus between the circles.

Applications and examples

Hypocycloid geometry appears in the design of gear teeth profiles (hypocycloidal gears), which reduce sliding friction, and in the Spirograph toy, which draws hypotrochoids and epitrochoids. The astroid and deltoid also arise as envelopes and caustics in optics and as evolutes of other curves.
