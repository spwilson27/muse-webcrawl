Vandwelling

Vandwelling is a lifestyle of living full- or part-time in a vehicle, typically a van, that has been modified with basic amenities such as a bed, kitchenette, storage, and auxiliary power, while remaining more mobile and less expensive than a conventional recreational vehicle or a fixed dwelling. Vandwellers — often grouped under the wider "#vanlife" movement popularised on social media in the 2010s — range from people choosing the lifestyle for travel, minimalism or remote work, to people living in vehicles out of economic necessity.

Practice

Conversions typically add insulation, a bed platform, a leisure battery or solar system, a water tank and a portable toilet. Vandwellers park overnight on public land, in campsites, at truck stops or — discreetly, in "stealth" mode — on city streets, and use gyms, laundries and co-working spaces for services. The practice overlaps with boondocking and overlanding, and differs from RV living chiefly in scale and cost.

Legal and social issues

Many municipalities regulate or prohibit sleeping in vehicles, and vandwelling sits at the intersection of debates about housing affordability, homelessness, public space and tourism. In the United States, dispersed camping on Bureau of Land Management and National Forest land (typically limited to 14 days in one place) provides a legal framework used by many vandwellers; in Europe, rules vary widely by country and commune.
