Dua Kumayl

Du'a Kumayl (Arabic: دعاء كميل) is a famous supplication (du'a) in Islam, attributed to Ali ibn Abi Talib, the first Imam of Shia Islam and fourth caliph. It is named after Kumayl ibn Ziyad, a companion of Ali, to whom Ali is said to have taught the prayer.

Origin

According to tradition, the supplication originates with al-Khidr, who taught it, and Ali conveyed it to Kumayl ibn Ziyad with the instruction to recite it on Thursday nights (the eve of Friday) and on the night of the 15th of Sha'ban. The text is recorded in collections of devotions, most widely in the Shia compilation Mafatih al-Jinan (Keys to Paradise) by Abbas Qomi, and its chain of transmission is discussed by scholars such as Ibn Tawus, who included it in his Iqbal al-a'mal.

Content and themes

The prayer is a long, intensely personal plea for God's mercy and forgiveness. Its themes include confession of sin and human weakness; reflection on the contrast between God's power and the supplicant's helplessness; petitions for strength to worship, for sustenance, and for protection from enemies; grief over wasted life; and hope in divine compassion despite one's deeds. A recurring refrain structure asks forgiveness "for every sin" of particular kinds. It includes the well-known passage in which the supplicant imagines being able to endure God's punishment but not separation from God.

Practice

Dua Kumayl is recited communally in mosques and religious centres on Thursday nights in many Shia communities, and privately by Sunni and Sufi devotees as well. Translations exist in many languages; English renderings include those published by Islamic institutes and translators of Mafatih al-Jinan.
