Legal Latin

A medieval manuscript of Roman law maxims

Legal Latin is the body of Latin terms, phrases, and maxims used in law, particularly in the common law tradition inherited from England and in the civil law systems derived from Roman law. Although statutes and judgments are now written in vernacular languages, Latin expressions survive as terms of art whose precise meanings have been fixed by centuries of judicial use.

History

The tradition descends from the law of ancient Rome, codified in Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis in the 6th century. In medieval England, court proceedings were conducted in a mixture of Latin and Law French; records were kept in Latin until the Proceedings in Courts of Justice Act 1730 required English. Even so, foundational doctrines continued to be cited by their Latin names, and maxims gathered in works such as Broom's Legal Maxims became standard references for advocates.

Common terms

Well-known legal Latin expressions include habeas corpus ("you shall have the body"), stare decisis ("to stand by things decided"), mens rea ("guilty mind"), actus reus ("guilty act"), prima facie ("at first sight"), bona fide ("in good faith"), pro bono ("for the public good"), amicus curiae ("friend of the court"), sub judice ("under judicial consideration"), and res judicata ("a matter already judged"). Procedural vocabulary such as certiorari, mandamus, and quo warranto names writs and remedies whose functions are inseparable from their Latin titles.

Modern usage and reform

Plain-language movements in the late 20th century criticised legal Latin as a barrier to public understanding. In England and Wales, the Woolf reforms of civil procedure in 1999 replaced some expressions: plaintiff became claimant, and Latin terms such as in camera gave way to plain English equivalents in official guidance. Nevertheless, courts continue to use Latin where a phrase carries a settled technical meaning that a translation would blur, and legal Latin remains a standard part of law school education worldwide.
