Overview

Decimal degrees (DD) express latitude and longitude as decimal fractions of a degree, rather than in degrees, arcminutes and arcseconds. South latitudes and west longitudes are negative. The Royal Observatory at Greenwich, for example, lies at about 51.4778° N, 0.0015° W.

Decimal degrees are the standard format in GPS receivers, geographic information systems and databases because they sort, store and compute like ordinary numbers.

Precision

The number of decimal places corresponds directly to ground precision: one decimal place is about 11.1 km, two about 1.1 km, three about 111 m, four about 11 m, five about 1.1 m and six about 11 cm. Consumer GPS coordinates are typically given to five or six decimal places.
