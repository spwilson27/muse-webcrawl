Sweep (martial arts)

A sweep is a martial arts technique used to unbalance an opponent and bring them to the ground by attacking their legs — most often by striking, hooking or reaping a supporting leg while controlling the upper body. The term is used in two related ways: for standing foot sweeps and throws, and in grappling for a reversal from a bottom position.

Standing sweeps

In judo, techniques such as deashi harai (advancing foot sweep), okuri ashi harai (sliding foot sweep) and osoto gari (major outer reap) knock or pull the opponent's feet from under them in coordination with a push or pull of the arms. Karate and kung fu employ similar low kicks and sweeping leg motions, while capoeira's rasteira sweeps the leg during the opponent's own movement.

Grappling sweeps

In Brazilian jiu-jitsu and other ground-fighting arts, a "sweep" means reversing positions from the guard so that the practitioner on the bottom ends up on top, scoring points in competition. Classic examples include the scissor sweep, flower sweep and hip-bump sweep. Timing and off-balancing (kuzushi) are essential in both senses: a sweep succeeds not by force but by attacking the leg at the moment the opponent's weight is committed to it.
