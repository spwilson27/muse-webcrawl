The Nazca culture (also Nasca) was an archaeological culture that flourished from roughly 100 BC to AD 800 in the river valleys of the Rio Grande de Nasca drainage on the arid south coast of Peru. The Nazca are famous for creating the Nazca Lines, enormous geoglyphs etched into the desert pavement, and for their polychrome pottery and sophisticated underground aqueducts called puquios.

Cahuachi

The ceremonial centre of Cahuachi was the largest Nazca site, occupied from about 1 to 500 AD. Consisting of adobe pyramids and plazas built on natural hills, it appears to have been a pilgrimage destination rather than a true city, drawing people from across the region for festivals linked to agriculture and water.

Water and agriculture

The Nazca homeland is one of the driest places on Earth, and rivers run only seasonally. The Nazca responded with the puquios — filtration galleries tapping underground aquifers — many of which still function today. With this water they grew cotton, beans, squash, maize, and other crops, and they traded for goods from the highlands, including obsidian and Spondylus shell.

Art

Nazca pottery is renowned for its thin walls and brilliant slip-painted decoration in as many as thirteen colors, depicting animals, plants, trophy heads, and mythical beings such as the Anthropomorphic Mythical Being. Their textiles, descended from the Paracas tradition, include finely woven and embroidered cloth.

Nazca Lines

On the desert pampa the Nazca created hundreds of geoglyphs — straight lines, geometric figures, and giant animals including a hummingbird, monkey, and spider — by removing the dark surface stones to expose pale soil beneath. The lines are generally interpreted as having ritual significance, possibly connected with water and fertility, and were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994.

Decline

After about AD 500 the culture declined amid environmental stress; studies suggest that clearing of huarango trees worsened flooding from El Niño events and destabilized agriculture. By around 750–800 the region came under the influence of the expanding Wari Empire.
