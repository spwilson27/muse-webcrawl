Agrarian society

An agrarian society, or agricultural society, is any society whose economy and social structure are centred on agriculture and the cultivation of land. In such societies the primary means of subsistence and wealth is farming, most of the population works the land, and social organisation, land tenure, and political power tend to revolve around control of agricultural resources.

Characteristics

Agrarian societies emerged after the Neolithic Revolution, when humans first domesticated plants and animals. Compared with earlier hunter-gatherer and horticultural societies, agrarian societies support much larger populations and permanent settlements, but most people remain subsistence farmers. Land is the principal form of wealth, often held by a small aristocratic elite, producing steep social stratification between landowners and peasants. Surplus production supports towns, specialised crafts, standing armies, priesthoods, and bureaucracies.

Historical development

Classic agrarian civilisations include ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, imperial China, and feudal Europe. Technologies such as the plough, irrigation, and animal traction greatly raised productivity compared with horticulture. From the eighteenth century, the British Agricultural Revolution and then the Industrial Revolution began transforming agrarian societies into industrial ones, shifting most employment into manufacturing and services. Many regions of the world still retain strongly agrarian economies.
