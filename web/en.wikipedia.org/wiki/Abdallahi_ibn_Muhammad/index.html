Abdallahi ibn Muhammad (Arabic: عبد الله ابن محمد; 1846 – 24 November 1899), known as the Khalifa, was a Sudanese Ansar ruler who succeeded Muhammad Ahmad, the self-proclaimed Mahdi, as leader of the Mahdist State in 1885. He ruled Sudan for thirteen years, resisting Egyptian, Ethiopian, Italian and finally Anglo-Egyptian invasion, until his defeat and death at the Battle of Umm Diwaykarat in 1899.

Rise

Born at Um Dafuq in south-western Darfur into the Ta'aisha Baggara Arabs, Abdallahi joined Muhammad Ahmad in 1880 and became his most trusted lieutenant, designated the first of the Mahdi's khalifas on the model of Abu Bakr. He commanded in the campaigns that destroyed the Turco-Egyptian administration, culminating in the fall of Khartoum in January 1885.

Rule

On the Mahdi's death in June 1885 Abdallahi overcame rival claimants, including the Mahdi's relatives, the Ashraf, whose attempted coup he suppressed in 1891. He made Omdurman his capital, built a centralised administration and standing forces, fought a costly war with Ethiopia (victorious at Gallabat in 1889), and faced famine (the terrible "Sanat Sitta" of 1888–89), revolts, and Italian and French probes at his borders. His attempted invasion of Egypt was crushed at Toski in 1889.

Fall and death

In 1896 an Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener began the reconquest of Sudan. After defeats at Atbara and the catastrophic Battle of Omdurman (2 September 1898), where some 11,000 Ansar fell, Abdallahi fled south to Kordofan. Pursued by Wingate's column, he made a last stand at Umm Diwaykarat on 24 November 1899 and was killed fighting with his remaining emirs.
