Royal icing

Royal icing is a hard white icing, made from softly beaten egg whites, icing sugar (powdered sugar), and sometimes lemon or lime juice. It is used to decorate Christmas cakes, wedding cakes, gingerbread houses, cookies, and many other cakes and biscuits. It is used either as a smooth covering or in sharp peaks.

History

Royal icing is thought to have acquired its name from its use on the wedding cake of Queen Victoria in 1840, which was decorated with white icing and was widely reported at the time. Earlier icing recipes used egg white and sugar but were not associated with royalty in the same manner.

Preparation and use

Confectioner's sugar is added gradually to lightly beaten egg whites (or meringue powder and water) until the desired consistency is reached. Glycerine is often added to prevent the icing from setting too hard. When placed on cakes, marzipan is usually used under royal icing in order to prevent discoloration of the icing.

Because raw egg white can carry a small risk of salmonella, meringue powder or pasteurised egg whites are often substituted in commercial work.
