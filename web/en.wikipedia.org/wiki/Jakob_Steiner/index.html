Jakob Steiner

Jakob Steiner (18 March 1796 – 1 April 1863) was a Swiss mathematician who worked principally in geometry. He is considered one of the founders of modern synthetic projective geometry, and many concepts bear his name, including the Steiner problem, Steiner tree, Steiner surface, and the Steiner chain of circles.

Life

Steiner was born in the village of Utzenstorf, Canton of Bern, to a small farmer's family; he was illiterate until about age 14 and learned to write at 18. He attended Johann Heinrich Pestalozzi's school at Yverdon, whose intuitive teaching shaped his approach to geometry, and later studied at Heidelberg and Berlin, supporting himself as a tutor. In 1832 he received an honorary doctorate from the University of Königsberg, and in 1834 a chair of geometry was created for him at Berlin, which he held until his death.

Work

His masterpiece, Systematische Entwickelung der Abhängigkeit geometrischer Gestalten von einander (1832), developed projective geometry from a few fundamental principles, introducing the "Steiner conic" defined by a projective correspondence between pencils of lines. He posed the Steiner problem — finding the shortest network connecting given points, now central in network design as the Steiner tree problem — and proved properties of the Steiner surface, a quartic surface discovered in Rome in 1844. His synthetic style championed pure geometry over analytic methods.
