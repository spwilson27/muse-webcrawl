The subcamps of Auschwitz were a network of 44 satellite camps of the Auschwitz concentration camp complex, established by Nazi Germany in occupied Poland between 1942 and 1944. Prisoners in the subcamps performed forced labour for German industry — in coal mines, steelworks, chemical plants and armaments factories — principally in Upper Silesia. Tens of thousands of prisoners passed through the subcamp system; the largest, such as Monowitz (Buna), held over ten thousand prisoners.

Liberation and memory

Most subcamps were evacuated in January 1945, their prisoners sent on death marches toward camps in Germany. The main camp and remaining subcamps were liberated by the Red Army on 27 January 1945. Memorials and the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum preserve the histories of the subcamps, many of which are marked by plaques and preserved structures.
