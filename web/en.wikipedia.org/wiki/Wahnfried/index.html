Wahnfried

Haus Wahnfried
Location: Bayreuth, Germany
Built: 1872–1874
Owner: Richard Wagner Foundation
Status: Museum (since 1976)

Wahnfried is the name of the villa in Bayreuth, Bavaria, built for the composer Richard Wagner and his family. Financed by King Ludwig II of Bavaria, it was constructed between 1872 and 1874 to a design influenced by Italian Renaissance villas.

Name and history

Wagner explained the name by inscribing it on the facade: "Hier wo mein Wähnen Frieden fand — Wahnfried sei dieses Haus von mir benannt" ("Here, where my delusions found peace, let this house be called Wahnfried"). Wagner lived there from 1874 until his death in Venice in 1883; he composed Parsifal at Wahnfried and is buried in its garden, as is his wife Cosima.

Museum

The house remained the Wagner family home and was damaged by bombing in 1945. Restored, it opened in 1976 as the Richard Wagner Museum, displaying manuscripts, stage designs, and furnishings, and it anchors Bayreuth's Wagner heritage alongside the Festspielhaus.
