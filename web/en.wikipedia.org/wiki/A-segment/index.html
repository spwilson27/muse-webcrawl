A-segment

A-segment cars are the smallest category of passenger cars marketed in Europe.

The A-segment is the smallest category in the European vehicle segmentation system for passenger cars, corresponding broadly to city cars in British English and the minicompact class used by the United States Environmental Protection Agency. A-segment cars are designed primarily for dense urban environments, where compact dimensions, low purchase prices and economical running costs matter more than performance or long-distance comfort.

Definition

The European Commission's segmentation places A-segment vehicles below the B-segment (superminis). Typical A-segment hatchbacks measure between about 2.7 and 3.7 metres in length and seat four occupants, sometimes five in a pinch. They are almost always front-engined, front-wheel-drive hatchbacks with three or five doors; engines are usually small three-cylinder petrol units, although a growing number of models are battery-electric.

History

The archetype of the modern city car is often traced to small post-war European cars such as the Fiat 500 of 1957, the Mini of 1959 and their successors, designed for cheap mobility in crowded cities. Through the 1990s and 2000s, dedicated city-car programmes such as the Renault Twingo, Ford Ka, Fiat Panda and the jointly developed Toyota Aygo / Peugeot 108 / Citroën C1 triplets defined the segment. From the late 2010s, tightening emissions rules and the cost of electrification pushed many manufacturers to withdraw from the segment or convert their smallest models to electric power, as with the Fiat New 500 and the Dacia Spring.

Market

A-segment cars historically accounted for roughly 7–8 percent of the European new-car market, with their strongest sales in Italy and France. Margins are thin, so manufacturers have frequently shared development costs through joint ventures. In Japan, the related kei car category is regulated separately by engine size and dimensions. In North America, the closest equivalent — the EPA's minicompact class — has included cars such as the Chevrolet Spark and Mitsubishi Mirage.
