Michael Clayton

Directed by: Tony Gilroy

Written by: Tony Gilroy

Starring: George Clooney, Tom Wilkinson, Tilda Swinton, Sydney Pollack

Release date: 5 October 2007

Budget: $25 million

Box office: $93 million

Michael Clayton is a 2007 American legal thriller film written and directed by Tony Gilroy in his directorial debut. It stars George Clooney as the title character, a "fixer" at a prestigious New York City law firm who is drawn into a crisis when the firm's star litigator, Arthur Edens (Tom Wilkinson), suffers a breakdown while defending a chemical company, U-North, in a multibillion-dollar class-action lawsuit over a toxic weed killer. Tilda Swinton plays Karen Crowder, U-North's chief counsel, who resorts to murder to protect the company.

Plot

Michael Clayton, a former prosecutor deep in debt from a failed restaurant venture and his brother's addiction, cleans up clients' messes for the firm Kenner, Bach & Ledeen. When Edens sabotages the U-North case after discovering a memo proving the company knew its product caused illness, Clayton is ordered to contain him. Edens is murdered and made to look like a suicide, and a hit is placed on Clayton himself; he survives a car bomb and ultimately confronts Crowder, tricking her into incriminating herself while police listen in.

Production and reception

Gilroy, best known as the screenwriter of the Bourne films, spent years developing the script. Sydney Pollack, in one of his final acting roles, plays managing partner Marty Bach and also served as a producer. The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival and received widespread critical acclaim for its screenplay, direction, and performances.

Awards

Michael Clayton was nominated for seven Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Clooney), and Best Supporting Actor (Wilkinson); Tilda Swinton won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. It is frequently cited among the finest legal thrillers of its era and as a defining depiction of corporate corruption and moral compromise.
