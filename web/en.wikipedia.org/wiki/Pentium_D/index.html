Pentium D

The Pentium D is a series of dual-core 64-bit desktop microprocessors introduced by Intel in 2005. Based on the NetBurst microarchitecture of the Pentium 4, each Pentium D chip essentially combined two Pentium 4 cores in a single package — in the first models (codenamed Smithfield) two dies in one package, and in later models (Presler) two dies produced on a 65 nm process.

Pentium D processors ran at clock speeds up to about 3.8 GHz in the Extreme Edition. The chips were noted for high power consumption and heat output, traits inherited from the NetBurst design, and they were succeeded in 2006 by the much more energy-efficient Core 2 Duo line based on the Core microarchitecture.

The Pentium D was Intel's first mainstream dual-core desktop brand and an important step in the industry's shift toward multi-core processors.
