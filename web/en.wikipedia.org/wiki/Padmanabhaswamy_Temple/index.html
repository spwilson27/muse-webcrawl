Padmanabhaswamy Temple

The Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple is a Hindu temple in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, India, dedicated to Vishnu in his form as Padmanabha — the god reclining on the serpent Ananta, from whose navel springs the lotus bearing Brahma. One of the 108 Divya Desams (holy abodes of Vishnu), the temple is the dynastic shrine of the royal family of Travancore, whose head traditionally rules as Padmanabha Dasa, "servant of Padmanabha". It drew worldwide attention in 2011 when the opening of its underground vaults revealed one of the largest hoards of temple treasure ever recorded.

History

The temple's origins are ancient and layered with legend; it is praised in early Tamil devotional literature. The present complex owes much to Maharaja Marthanda Varma of Travancore, who in 1750 dedicated his kingdom to the deity in the ceremony of Thrippadidanam, and to his rebuilding of the shrine, including its great corridor of carved pillars. The presiding image, about 5.5 metres long, is formed of over 12,000 shaligram stones and is viewed through three doors.

The vaults

Beneath the temple lie six vaults, designated A to F. Acting on a Supreme Court-directed inventory, officials opened five of them in 2011 and catalogued gold coins, jewellery, crowns, idols and ornaments accumulated over centuries of royal offerings, with reported valuations exceeding US$20 billion — instantly making the temple one of the richest places of worship in the world. Vault B remained unopened amid religious objections and legal disputes. Litigation between the royal family and the Kerala government culminated in a 2020 Supreme Court judgment upholding the family's administrative role in the temple.

Worship and customs

Entry to the inner temple is restricted to Hindus, and a strict dress code applies — men wear mundu (dhoti) and women sarees or similar traditional dress. The temple is famous for the Laksha Deepam festival, the ceremonial lighting of one hundred thousand oil lamps held once every twelve years.
