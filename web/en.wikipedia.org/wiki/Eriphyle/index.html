In Greek mythology, Eriphyle was the wife of the seer Amphiaraus and sister of Adrastus, king of Argos. She is notorious for betraying her husband for the sake of a marvellous necklace, becoming a byword for treacherous greed.

The myth

Amphiaraus, foreseeing his own death if he joined the expedition of the Seven against Thebes, had sworn that Eriphyle would arbitrate disputes between him and Adrastus. Polynices bribed her with the necklace of Harmonia — a divine heirloom that brought ruin to its owners — and she compelled her husband to march to war, where the earth swallowed him alive.

Revenge

Before departing, Amphiaraus charged his sons to avenge him. Years later Alcmaeon, bribed in turn with Harmonia's robe, killed his mother Eriphyle, and was pursued by the Erinyes for matricide until purified and settled in new land.

In literature

Eriphyle appears among the treacherous in Homer's Odyssey ("hateful Eriphyle, who took precious gold for her own husband's life") and in Dante's Inferno.
