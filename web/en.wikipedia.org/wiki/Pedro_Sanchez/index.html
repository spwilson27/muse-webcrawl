Pedro Sánchez Pérez-Castejón (born 29 February 1972) is a Spanish politician who has served as prime minister of Spain since 2018. He has been secretary-general of the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) since 2017, having previously held the post from 2014 to 2016.

Rise to power

An economist by training, Sánchez served as a Madrid city councillor and entered the Congress of Deputies in 2009. He won the PSOE leadership in 2014, resigned in October 2016 amid an internal party crisis, and then regained the leadership in a membership primary in May 2017. On 1 June 2018 he became prime minister after winning a motion of no confidence against Mariano Rajoy — the first such motion to succeed in modern Spanish history — in the wake of the Gürtel corruption scandal.

Premiership

Sánchez has headed minority and coalition governments, including Spain's first coalition government since the Second Republic, formed with Unidas Podemos after the November 2019 election. His governments raised the minimum wage, passed labour and euthanasia legislation, managed the COVID-19 pandemic and the EU recovery funds, and — controversially — granted an amnesty to Catalan separatists in exchange for parliamentary support after the 2023 election, in which the PSOE placed second but Sánchez assembled a majority to remain in office.
