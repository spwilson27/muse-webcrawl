Aircraft flight dynamics

Flight dynamics is the science of air vehicle orientation and control in three dimensions. The three critical flight dynamics parameters are the angles of rotation in three dimensions about the vehicle's center of gravity, known as pitch, roll and yaw. These are collectively known as aircraft attitude, comparable to the attitude of a ship or the orientation of any rigid body in space.

The three axes of rotation of an aircraft: pitch, roll and yaw.

Axes and rotations

Aircraft orientation is described using a right-handed coordinate system fixed in the aircraft, with its origin at the center of gravity:

  • Pitch — rotation about the lateral (wingtip-to-wingtip) axis, raising or lowering the nose; controlled primarily by the elevator.
  • Roll — rotation about the longitudinal (nose-to-tail) axis, banking the wings; controlled primarily by the ailerons.
  • Yaw — rotation about the vertical axis, swinging the nose left or right; controlled primarily by the rudder.

Orientation relative to the Earth is expressed by the Euler angles — bank angle, pitch attitude, and heading — while the airflow relative to the aircraft is described by the angle of attack and sideslip angle.

Stability and control

Flight dynamics analysis distinguishes static stability (the initial tendency of a disturbed aircraft to return toward equilibrium) from dynamic stability (the behaviour of the disturbance over time). The classical dynamic modes of a conventional aircraft are the short-period and phugoid oscillations in the longitudinal plane, and the Dutch roll, spiral, and roll-subsidence modes laterally. A well-designed aircraft has stability characteristics matched to its role: airliners are strongly stable for passenger comfort and safety, while modern fighters may be deliberately made aerodynamically unstable ("relaxed static stability") for greater manoeuvrability, relying on fly-by-wire flight control systems to remain controllable.

Forces and performance

In steady flight the four forces — lift, weight, thrust, and drag — are in balance. Flight dynamics extends this to manoeuvring flight, relating control deflections and thrust changes to accelerations and turn performance, and underpins the design of autopilots, flight control laws, and flight simulators.
