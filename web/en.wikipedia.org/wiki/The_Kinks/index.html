The Kinks were an English rock band formed in London in 1963 by brothers Ray and Dave Davies. Regarded as one of the most influential bands of the 1960s, they were central to the British Invasion of the United States and are noted for Ray Davies's sharply observed songwriting about English life.

Career

The band broke through in 1964 with "You Really Got Me", whose distorted guitar riff was foundational for hard rock and heavy metal. Classic singles followed, including "All Day and All of the Night", "Tired of Waiting for You", "Sunny Afternoon", "Waterloo Sunset", and "Lola" (1970). Concept albums such as The Kinks Are the Village Green Preservation Society (1968), initially overlooked, later came to be considered masterpieces. After arena-rock success in the late 1970s and early 1980s ("Come Dancing"), the band split in 1996 amid the Davies brothers' long-running tensions.

Legacy

The Kinks were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990, and their music strongly influenced punk, Britpop, and indie rock.
