Circassian genocide

The Circassian genocide was the Russian Empire's systematic mass killing, ethnic cleansing and forced expulsion of the Circassian population during and after the Russo-Circassian War (1763–1864). Estimates of the number of Circassians killed or deported range from several hundred thousand to around 1.5 million, and the overwhelming majority of the Circassian population was removed from its homeland in the North Caucasus.

Background

Russian expansion into the Caucasus brought imperial forces into prolonged conflict with the Circassian tribal confederation. The war lasted roughly a century and ended in May 1864 with the last Circassian resistance defeated in the mountains near present-day Sochi.

Expulsion and deaths

Under commanders such as Dmitry Milyutin and Yevgeny Yevdokimov, Russian forces destroyed Circassian villages and crops and drove the population to Black Sea embarkation points, from which they were shipped to the Ottoman Empire. Many died from disease, starvation and exposure in camps on the coast and during the crossings. Survivors were resettled across Ottoman territories in the Balkans, Anatolia and the Levant, where further hardship followed.

Recognition and remembrance

Georgia's parliament recognised the events as genocide in 2011. Circassian communities commemorate 21 May as a day of mourning. The Russian government has disputed the use of the term genocide, while activist groups continue to campaign for recognition and for the right of return of the diaspora.
