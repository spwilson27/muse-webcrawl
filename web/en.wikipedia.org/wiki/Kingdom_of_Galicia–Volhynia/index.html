Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia

Years: 1199 – 1349

Capitals: Halych, later Lviv

First ruler: Roman the Great

Only crowned king: Daniel of Galicia (1253)

The Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, also known as the Kingdom of Ruthenia or Kingdom of Rus', was a medieval state in Eastern Europe formed in 1199 when Roman the Great united the principalities of Galicia and Volhynia. It succeeded Kievan Rus' as the principal Rus' polity in the southwest and was one of the most powerful states of the region in the thirteenth century.

History

Roman's son Daniel (Danylo) rebuilt the kingdom after the Mongol invasion of 1240–41 destroyed Kiev, founding the city of Lviv (named for his son Lev) and accepting a royal crown from a papal legate in 1253 in the hope of Western support against the Golden Horde. Daniel's successors, including Lev I and Yuri I, maintained the royal title. The state prospered from trade routes between the Baltic and Black Seas and the salt works of Galicia.

Decline and partition

The extinction of the Romanovichi male line in 1323 brought a period of boyar rule and succession struggles; the last ruler, Bolesław-Yuri II, was poisoned in 1340. In the ensuing Galicia–Volhynia Wars, the kingdom was partitioned: Galicia was annexed by Casimir III of Poland by 1349, while Volhynia passed to Lithuania, ending the kingdom's existence.
