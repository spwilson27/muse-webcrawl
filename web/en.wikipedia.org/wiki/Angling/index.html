Angling

Angling is a fishing technique that uses a fish hook ("angle") attached to a fishing line to catch fish. The line is usually attached to a fishing rod, which may be fitted with a reel that allows retrieval of the line and hooked fish. Angling is a principal method of recreational fishing, practiced by tens of millions of people worldwide, and also a competitive sport, though commercial fishing rarely uses it because of its low yield.

Methods

Anglers use a wide range of techniques depending on the target species and environment. Fly fishing uses an artificial fly cast with a weighted line; spinning and baitcasting present lures or bait with a spinning or revolving-spool reel; trolling trails lines behind a moving boat; bottom fishing, float fishing and jigging work bait or lures at different depths; and specialized forms include ice fishing through holes in frozen lakes, surfcasting from beaches, and Japanese tenkara fly fishing with a fixed line.

Equipment

Standard tackle includes hooks, lines (monofilament, fluorocarbon or braided), sinkers, floats (bobbers), swivels, artificial lures and baits. Terminal tackle choice, rod power and line strength are matched to the species sought. Many anglers practice catch and release, using barbless hooks and careful handling to return fish alive.

History

Fishing with hooks is ancient: gorge hooks of bone and thorn date back tens of thousands of years. Izaak Walton's The Compleat Angler (1653) is the foundational classic of angling literature in English. The sport modernized in the 19th and 20th centuries with multiplying reels, split-cane and then fiberglass and carbon-fiber rods, and organized competitions.

Regulation and conservation

Recreational angling is usually regulated by fishing licenses, seasons, size and bag limits designed to protect stocks. License fees and tackle excise taxes (such as the U.S. Dingell-Johnson Act of 1950) fund habitat conservation, making anglers significant contributors to aquatic conservation. Angling generates substantial economic activity in tackle, boats, travel and guiding.
