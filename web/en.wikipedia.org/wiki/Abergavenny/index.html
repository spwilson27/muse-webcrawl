Abergavenny (Welsh: Y Fenni, archaically Abergafenni, meaning "mouth of the River Gavenny") is a market town and community in Monmouthshire, Wales. It stands at the confluence of the River Gavenny and the River Usk, near the eastern edge of the Brecon Beacons National Park, and is nicknamed the "Gateway to Wales".

History

The site was occupied by the Roman fort of Gobannium, whose name derived from the Brittonic word for "blacksmith". Abergavenny grew around a Norman castle founded around 1087 by Hamelin de Ballon; the castle was the scene of a notorious massacre in 1175, when William de Braose invited Welsh princes to a Christmas feast and had them murdered. The town was repeatedly fought over during the Glyndŵr rebellion and the medieval Marcher conflicts. It received its charter in the 1650s. In the 19th century the town was known for flannel manufacture and coal tramroads from the South Wales Valleys, and Abergavenny railway station opened in 1854 on the line to Newport and Hereford.

Geography

The town is surrounded by hills within the national park, most prominently the Sugar Loaf (596 m) to the northwest, the Blorenge to the south and the Skirrid (Ysgyryd Fawr) to the northeast, a hill with a distinctive landslip-scarred silhouette and associations in Welsh legend and Catholic recusant history.

Culture and events

Abergavenny is known nationally for the Abergavenny Food Festival, held each September since 1999 and regarded as one of Britain's leading food events. The town's market hall hosts regular markets and was used as a filming location for television productions. The 2016 National Eisteddfod of Wales was held in the town. The singer-songwriter Marty Wilde and others have associations with the area, and the town gave its name to a 1960s pop group (Abergavenny) and to a song by Wilde. Abergavenny is twinned with towns in Germany, France and South Africa.
