The Motorcycle Diaries (book)

Author: Ernesto "Che" Guevara
Original title: Notas de viaje
Published: 1993 (posthumously)
Genre: Memoir, travel writing

The Motorcycle Diaries (Spanish: Notas de viaje, "Travel Notes") is a memoir by the Argentine Marxist revolutionary Ernesto "Che" Guevara, recounting the 1952 motorcycle journey he took through South America with his friend Alberto Granado while still a medical student.

The journey

Setting out from Buenos Aires in January 1952 on a battered 1939 Norton 500cc motorcycle nicknamed La Poderosa ("The Mighty One"), the two men travelled through Argentina, Chile, Peru, Colombia, and Venezuela. After the motorcycle broke down they continued by boat, truck, and on foot. Encounters with exploited copper miners in Chuquicamata, leprosy patients at San Pablo in Peru, and the ruins of Inca civilization profoundly shaped the young Guevara's political awakening.

Publication and adaptation

Guevara's diaries were edited and published posthumously in 1993, becoming an international bestseller in translation. A film adaptation, The Motorcycle Diaries (2004), directed by Walter Salles and starring Gael García Bernal, won wide acclaim and an Academy Award for Best Original Song.
