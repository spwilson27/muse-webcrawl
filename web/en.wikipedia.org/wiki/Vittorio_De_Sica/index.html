Vittorio De Sica

Vittorio Domenico Stanislao Gaetano Sorano De Sica (7 July 1901 – 13 November 1974) was an Italian film director and actor, a leading figure in the neorealist movement. Four of the films he directed won Academy Awards: Shoeshine (1946) and Bicycle Thieves (1948) won the Academy Honorary Award, while Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow (1963) and The Garden of the Finzi-Continis (1970) won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. His collaboration with screenwriter Cesare Zavattini produced the defining works of Italian neorealism.

Early career

De Sica was born in Sora, Lazio, and raised in Naples. He began as a stage actor and became a popular matinee idol in Italian "white telephone" comedies of the 1930s, notably in films by Mario Camerini such as Gli uomini, che mascalzoni... (1932). He turned to directing in 1940 and found his direction with films about children, including The Children Are Watching Us (1944), his first collaboration with Zavattini.

Neorealism

Shoeshine (1946), about two boys destroyed by a juvenile prison, and Bicycle Thieves (1948), in which an unemployed worker and his son search Rome for a stolen bicycle, were shot on location with non-professional leads and became international sensations, defining neorealism's aesthetic of social observation. Miracle in Milan (1951) won the Palme d'Or at Cannes, and Umberto D. (1952), about a pensioner's dignity, was De Sica's personal favourite among his films, though a commercial failure at the time.

Later career

From the 1960s De Sica worked in more commercial registers, often with Sophia Loren and Marcello Mastroianni: Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow (1963), Marriage Italian Style (1964), Sunflower (1970) and The Garden of the Finzi-Continis (1970), about an Italian Jewish family under fascism. He continued acting prolifically, receiving an Academy Award nomination for A Farewell to Arms (1957). He died in Paris in 1974 following lung surgery.
