Brownie (camera)

The original Brownie, introduced in 1900, sold for one dollar.

The Brownie was a series of inexpensive, simple cameras introduced by Eastman Kodak beginning in February 1900. Conceived by Frank A. Brownell, the first Brownie was a cardboard box camera with a simple meniscus lens that took 2¼-inch square pictures on 117 roll film. It was marketed especially to children, using the popular "brownie" characters from Palmer Cox's books, and sold for just one dollar under the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest."

Impact

The Brownie is credited with popularizing low-cost photography and introducing the concept of the snapshot to the mass market. Kodak famously gave away some 50,000 Brownies to children in 1900 to seed the market for film and developing. Over the following decades many models followed, including the popular Brownie Hawkeye of the 1950s, and Brownie cameras were produced in the United States until the 1960s and in the United Kingdom into the 1980s.

Design

Most Brownie models were box cameras or simple folding cameras with minimal controls — typically a fixed-focus lens, one or two shutter speeds and little or no aperture adjustment. Their simplicity and ruggedness made them fixtures of family life, travel and wartime photography.
