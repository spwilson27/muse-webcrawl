Ascites

Specialty: Gastroenterology, hepatology

Symptoms: Abdominal swelling, weight gain, shortness of breath

Causes: Cirrhosis, heart failure, cancer, tuberculosis

Ascites is the abnormal build-up of fluid in the abdomen. Technically, it is more than 25 ml of fluid in the peritoneal cavity, although volumes greater than one litre may occur. Symptoms may include increased abdominal size, increased weight, abdominal discomfort and shortness of breath.

Causes

The most common cause of ascites is portal hypertension resulting from cirrhosis of the liver, accounting for about 85% of cases in developed countries. Other causes include heart failure, constrictive pericarditis, malignancy (peritoneal carcinomatosis), nephrotic syndrome, pancreatitis and infections such as tuberculous peritonitis. The serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) helps distinguish portal hypertensive from non-portal-hypertensive causes.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on physical examination (flank dullness, shifting dullness, fluid thrill), abdominal ultrasound, and diagnostic paracentesis — needle aspiration of ascitic fluid for albumin, cell counts, culture and cytology. New-onset ascites or features of infection prompt urgent sampling to exclude spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause and includes dietary sodium restriction and diuretics, classically a combination of spironolactone and furosemide. Large-volume paracentesis with albumin cover relieves symptomatic tense ascites. Refractory cases may be managed with a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) or considered for liver transplantation.

Compare aspartate transaminase, a liver enzyme test used in evaluating liver disease.
