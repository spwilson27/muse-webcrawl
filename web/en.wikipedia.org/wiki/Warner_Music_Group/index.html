Warner Music Group Corp. (WMG) is an American multinational entertainment and record label conglomerate headquartered in New York City. It is one of the "big three" recording companies, alongside Universal Music Group and Sony Music Entertainment, and operates in more than 70 countries through a network of labels and licensees.

History

The company's origins lie in Warner Bros. Records, founded in 1958 as the recorded-music division of the film studio Warner Bros. Through the 1960s and 1970s the Warner group expanded by acquiring or founding labels including Reprise Records (1963), Atlantic Records (1967) and Elektra Records, and under executives such as Mo Ostin and Ahmet Ertegun became a dominant force in rock and pop. The music operations passed through the Warner Communications and Time Warner conglomerates before Time Warner sold Warner Music Group in 2004 to an investor group led by Edgar Bronfman Jr.

In 2011 Access Industries, controlled by Len Blavatnik, acquired WMG for about $3.3 billion. The company bought the Parlophone Label Group from Universal in 2013, after Universal's acquisition of EMI, and expanded into streaming-era ventures, artist services and catalogue acquisitions. WMG returned to public markets in June 2020 with an initial public offering on the Nasdaq, raising about $1.93 billion.

Operations

WMG's recorded music division includes Atlantic Records, Warner Records, Elektra, Parlophone and Rhino (catalogue), while its publishing arm, Warner Chappell Music, administers one of the world's largest song catalogues. Its roster has historically included artists such as Led Zeppelin, Fleetwood Mac, Madonna, Prince, Ed Sheeran, Bruno Mars, Dua Lipa and Coldplay.
