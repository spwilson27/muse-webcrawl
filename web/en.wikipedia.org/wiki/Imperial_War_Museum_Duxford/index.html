Imperial War Museum Duxford

Historic aircraft at IWM Duxford, Cambridgeshire

Imperial War Museum Duxford (commonly referred to as Duxford Airfield) is a branch of the Imperial War Museum near Duxford in Cambridgeshire, England. Britain's largest aviation museum, Duxford houses the museum's large exhibits, including nearly 200 aircraft, military vehicles, artillery and minor naval vessels in several exhibition buildings spread over 200 acres (0.81 km²). The site also provides storage space for the museum's other collections of material such as film, photographs, documents, books and artefacts.

The airfield

RAF Duxford was built during the First World War and became one of the RAF's key fighter stations. During the Battle of Britain in 1940 it was a Sector Station for No. 12 Group, home to squadrons of Hurricanes and Spitfires. From 1943 it was handed over to the United States Army Air Forces as Station 357, base of the 78th Fighter Group flying P-47 Thunderbolts and later P-51 Mustangs. The airfield remained an RAF jet fighter station until 1961.

The museum

The Imperial War Museum acquired the site in 1976. Major exhibition spaces include AirSpace, opened in 2007, which tells the story of British and Commonwealth aviation; the American Air Museum (1997), designed by Norman Foster, which holds the largest collection of US military aircraft outside the United States, crowned by a B-52 Stratofortress; Flying Aircraft and the Battle of Britain exhibition in the original hangars; and the Land Warfare Hall. Duxford hosts popular air shows, including the Battle of Britain Air Show and Flying Legends, and remains an active airfield where historic aircraft fly.
