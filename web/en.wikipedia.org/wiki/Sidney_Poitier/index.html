Sidney Poitier (20 February 1927 – 6 January 2022) was a Bahamian-American actor, film director, and diplomat. In 1964 he became the first Black actor to win the Academy Award for Best Actor, for Lilies of the Field (1963). His roles in the 1950s and 1960s — dignified characters who confronted racial prejudice — made him Hollywood's first major Black film star and a prominent figure of the civil rights era.

Life and career

Poitier was born in Miami to Bahamian parents and raised on Cat Island in the Bahamas. He moved to New York as a teenager, joined the American Negro Theatre, and made his film breakthrough in No Way Out (1950). He earned wide acclaim for Blackboard Jungle (1955) and The Defiant Ones (1958), the latter earning him an Oscar nomination.

In 1967 he starred in three major hits — To Sir, with Love, In the Heat of the Night, and Guess Who's Coming to Dinner — becoming the year's top box-office star. In the 1970s he turned to directing, with comedies such as Uptown Saturday Night (1974) and Stir Crazy (1980).

Poitier served as the Bahamas' ambassador to Japan (1997–2007). He received an Honorary Academy Award in 2002 for his contributions to cinema, and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2009. He died in Los Angeles in January 2022, aged 94.
