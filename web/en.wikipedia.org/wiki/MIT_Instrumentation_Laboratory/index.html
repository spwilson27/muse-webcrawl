The MIT Instrumentation Laboratory was a research laboratory of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology founded and directed by Charles Stark "Doc" Draper. It pioneered inertial navigation and guidance systems, applying gyroscopes and accelerometers to the guidance of aircraft, submarines, ballistic missiles and spacecraft.

Work

Draper's laboratory developed the gyroscopic gunsights used in the Second World War, the inertial guidance systems for the Thor and Polaris missiles, and, most famously, the Apollo Guidance Computer and the inertial measurement unit that guided the Apollo command and lunar modules to the Moon. The lab's systems were also adapted for the Space Shuttle and for submarine navigation.

Separation from MIT

During the Vietnam War era, student protests over MIT's military research led to pressure to divest the laboratory. In 1973 it became an independent non-profit organisation, the Charles Stark Draper Laboratory, which continues to work on guidance, navigation and control systems from its headquarters in Kendall Square, Cambridge.
