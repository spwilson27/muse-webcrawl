Wakizashi

Wakizashi
Type: Short sword
Place of origin: Japan
Blade length: approx. 30–60 cm (1–2 shaku)

The wakizashi (Japanese: 脇差, "side inserted [sword]") is a traditional Japanese short sword worn by the samurai class in feudal Japan. With a blade between 30 and 60 centimetres long, it was worn together with the katana as the paired set known as daishō, the symbol of the samurai.

Use

Unlike the katana, which was left at the entrance when entering a house, the wakizashi remained with its owner at all times — even while sleeping, it was kept within reach. It served as a backup weapon, for close-quarters fighting indoors where a long sword was unwieldy, and for ceremonial purposes. The wakizashi was also the blade traditionally used in seppuku (ritual suicide).

History and regulation

The wakizashi came into common use during the Muromachi period (1336–1573) alongside the katana. Under the Tokugawa shogunate's dress regulations, only samurai were permitted to wear the full daishō; merchants and artisans were allowed a shorter wakizashi-like blade. Blades longer than two shaku were classified as ō-wakizashi and approached katana length.

Construction

Like the katana, the wakizashi was traditionally forged from tamahagane steel with a differentially hardened edge (hamon), fitted with a lacquered scabbard (saya), a small guard (tsuba), and a wrapped hilt (tsuka) in matching mountings (koshirae) when paired as daishō.
