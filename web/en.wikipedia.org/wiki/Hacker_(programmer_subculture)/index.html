Hacker (programmer subculture)

The hacker subculture of programmers is a community of creative, expert computer programmers who value playful ingenuity, exploration and the free exchange of information. Emerging from academic labs such as MIT's Tech Model Railroad Club and AI Lab in the 1960s, it produced a shared vocabulary documented in the Jargon File and the ethic later summarised in Steven Levy's Hackers (1984).

Values

Hacker culture emphasises meritocracy of ideas, decentralisation, and the belief that information should be free — principles central to the free and open-source software movements. In this sense, "hacker" is distinct from the security-breaker sense of the word, which the subculture often calls a "cracker".
