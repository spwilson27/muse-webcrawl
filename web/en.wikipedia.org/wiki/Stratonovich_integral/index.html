The Stratonovich integral is a stochastic integral, the most common alternative to the Itô integral, developed by the Soviet physicist Ruslan Stratonovich in the 1960s. In contrast to Itô's definition, which evaluates the integrand at the left endpoint of each subinterval, the Stratonovich integral uses a midpoint (trapezoidal) evaluation of the integrand over each subinterval of a partition.

Definition and properties

Because of the midpoint rule, the Stratonovich integral obeys the ordinary chain rule of classical calculus: for a smooth function f, f(Wt) − f(W0) = ∫ f′(Ws) ∘ dWs, with no second-order correction term. This makes it natural for problems arising from physics and engineering, where stochastic differential equations model systems driven by smooth noise taken to a white-noise limit (the Wong–Zakai theorem shows such limits yield Stratonovich equations).

Relation to the Itô integral

The two integrals differ by a correction involving the quadratic covariation of the integrand and integrator: ∫ X ∘ dW = ∫ X dW + ½⟨X, W⟩. A consequence is that Stratonovich integrals are not martingales, and their expectations are generally not zero — in finance, where the martingale property is essential, the Itô integral is preferred.
