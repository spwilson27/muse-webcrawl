Tributaries of the River Thames

The River Thames in southern England is fed by a large number of tributaries along its 346 km (215 mi) course from the Cotswolds to the North Sea. This article lists the principal tributaries, from source to mouth.

Major tributaries

  • River Churn — joins near Cricklade; sometimes claimed as the true upper course.
  • River Leach — joins near Lechlade.
  • River Cole and River Ray — Wiltshire tributaries joining near Cricklade and above Oxford.
  • River Windrush — joins near Newbridge; flows through Burford and Witney.
  • River Evenlode — joins near Wolvercote, above Oxford.
  • River Cherwell — joins at Oxford; flows through Banbury.
  • River Thame — joins near Dorchester, Oxfordshire.
  • River Kennet — joins at Reading; the largest tributary by length in the upper basin.
  • River Loddon — joins near Wargrave.
  • River Wey — joins at Weybridge; navigable, with its own navigation.
  • River Mole — joins opposite Hampton Court, famous for its "swallow holes."
  • River Brent — joins at Brentford.
  • River Lea — the largest tributary of the tidal Thames, joining at Leamouth near Bow Creek.
  • River Roding — joins at Barking Creek.
  • River Darent and River Ingrebourne — join in the estuary reaches.
  • River Medway — though often treated separately, its estuary merges with the Thames estuary at Sheerness.

London's lost rivers

Several tributaries within London — the Fleet, Walbrook, Tyburn, Westbourne, Effra and Neckinger among them — are now largely culverted and run as sewers beneath the city, though their courses still shape street patterns and place names.
