Qualcomm Stadium was a multi-purpose stadium in San Diego, California, located in the Mission Valley area. Opened in 1967 as San Diego Stadium, it was later named Jack Murphy Stadium in honor of the local sportswriter who championed its construction, before Qualcomm acquired the naming rights in 1997.

History

The stadium was the longtime home of the San Diego Chargers of the National Football League (1967–2016) and the San Diego Padres of Major League Baseball (1969–2003), as well as the San Diego State Aztecs football team. It hosted three Super Bowls (XXII, XXXII, and XXXVII), the 1978 and 1992 Major League Baseball All-Star Games, and World Series games in 1984 and 1998.

The stadium was also the home of the Holiday Bowl college football game. In 2003 it served as an evacuation center during the Cedar Fire.

Closure and demolition

After the Chargers relocated to Los Angeles in 2017 and the Aztecs moved to a temporary venue, the stadium hosted its final events in 2019–2020. It was demolished in 2020–2021, and the site was redeveloped as part of San Diego State University's SDSU Mission Valley campus, anchored by the new Snapdragon Stadium, which opened in 2022.
