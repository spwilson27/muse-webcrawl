Ruffini ending

Ruffini endings, also known as bulbous corpuscles, are slowly adapting mechanoreceptors named after the Italian anatomist Angelo Ruffini (1864–1929). They are found in the dermis of the skin, in ligaments and in joint capsules.

Structure and function

A Ruffini ending is an encapsulated, elongated structure of collagen fibres interwoven with the branches of a single nerve fibre. The receptor responds to sustained pressure and to stretching of the skin, and is especially sensitive to forces applied across a joint; in joint capsules Ruffini endings signal static joint position and changes in position and velocity.

Because they adapt slowly, Ruffini endings continue to fire for as long as a stimulus is maintained, in contrast to the rapidly adapting Meissner corpuscles.
