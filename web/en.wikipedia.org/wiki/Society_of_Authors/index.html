Society of Authors

The Society of Authors (SoA) is a British trade union for writers, illustrators and literary translators, representing more than 11,000 members. Founded in 1884 by the novelist Walter Besant, it campaigns for authors' rights, advises members on contracts and copyright, and administers grants and prizes.

History and work

The Society was established to protect authors' interests in an era of weak copyright protection; early members included Alfred, Lord Tennyson, its first president. It played a role in the passage of the Copyright Act 1911 and in founding the Authors' Licensing and Collecting Society and Public Lending Right. Today it provides contract vetting, legal advice, professional training and community networks, and speaks publicly on issues such as fair pay, library lending, copyright in the digital environment and the use of writers' work in artificial-intelligence training data.

Prizes and funds

The SoA administers a portfolio of awards and charitable funds, including the Authors' Awards, the Eric Gregory Awards for young poets, the Betty Trask Award for debut fiction, translation prizes and hardship grants through the Authors' Contingency Fund and the charitable trusts it manages.
