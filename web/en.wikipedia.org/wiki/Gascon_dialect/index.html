Gascon (Occitan: gascon) is a variety of the Occitan language spoken in Gascony, the region of southwestern France roughly between the Garonne river, the Pyrenees and the Atlantic, and in the Val d'Aran in Spain, where its local variety, Aranese, is co-official with Catalan and Spanish.

Characteristics

Gascon is the most distinctive of the Occitan dialects, strongly influenced by the pre-Roman Aquitanian language, a relative of Basque. Its hallmark sound changes include the shift of initial Latin f- to h- (as in Spanish), so that Latin filium gives Gascon hilh ("son"); the loss of intervocalic -n-; and the treatment of -ll- as -th. Some linguists classify Gascon as a separate Romance language rather than a dialect of Occitan.

Status

Like other regional varieties of France, Gascon has declined sharply since the 19th century under pressure from standard French, transmitted through schooling and administration. UNESCO classifies it as definitely endangered, though revival efforts include bilingual schooling (calandretas), broadcasting and literature. Béarnese, the variety of Béarn, is its most widely recognised subdialect.
