Cotangent space

In differential geometry, the cotangent space is a vector space associated with a point on a smooth (or differentiable) manifold; it can be defined as the dual space of the tangent space at that point. Elements of the cotangent space are called covectors or tangent covectors, and in coordinates they are the quantities that transform in the opposite ("contragredient") way to tangent vectors.

Definition

Let M be a smooth manifold and x a point of M. Let TxM be the tangent space at x. Then the cotangent space at x is defined as the dual space of TxM:

T*xM = (TxM)*

That is, the cotangent space consists of all linear functionals on the tangent space. If the tangent space is defined via equivalence classes of curves, the cotangent space can equivalently be built from the space of smooth functions vanishing at x modulo functions vanishing to second order.

Differentials and one-forms

For a smooth function f on M, its differential dfx is an element of the cotangent space: it maps a tangent vector to the directional derivative of f along that vector. In local coordinates (x1, …, xn), the differentials dx1, …, dxn form the basis of the cotangent space dual to the coordinate basis of the tangent space. A smooth assignment of a covector to every point is a differential one-form.

Cotangent bundle

The disjoint union of the cotangent spaces over all points of M forms the cotangent bundle T*M, itself a manifold of twice the dimension of M and a central object in Hamiltonian mechanics, where it serves as the phase space of a classical system, with positions on M and momenta as the covector components.
