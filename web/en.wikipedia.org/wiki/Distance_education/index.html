Distance education

Distance education, also known as distance learning, is the education of students who are not physically present at a school or university. Courses are delivered by post (correspondence courses), radio and television broadcasts, or — most commonly today — over the internet, allowing teachers and learners separated by time or place to interact.

History

Correspondence education began in the nineteenth century: Isaac Pitman taught shorthand by post in England from the 1840s, and the University of London's External Programme (1858) was the first to offer degrees by distance. The twentieth century added educational radio and television, and institutions such as the Open University (founded 1969 in the United Kingdom) combined broadcasts with printed materials and local tutorials. The internet transformed the field from the 1990s onward, and massive open online courses (MOOCs) drew global attention after 2011–2012.

Technologies and delivery

Modern distance education uses learning management systems, video lectures, discussion forums, videoconferencing, and automated assessment. Delivery may be synchronous (live sessions) or asynchronous (self-paced materials), and frequently blended with face-to-face teaching.

Advantages and challenges

Distance education offers flexibility and access for working adults, rural learners, and people with disabilities, and can be scaled to very large enrolments. Challenges include the need for self-discipline, higher dropout rates in some programmes, concerns about academic integrity, and unequal access to technology (the "digital divide"). The COVID-19 pandemic of 2020 forced a vast, rapid shift to remote teaching worldwide and accelerated research into its effectiveness.
