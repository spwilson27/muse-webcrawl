Suyab

The Ak-Beshim site, identified with Suyab

Suyab (Chinese: 碎葉, Suiye) was an ancient Silk Road city in the Chu River valley of modern Kyrgyzstan, near the present-day town of Tokmok. It flourished between the 5th and 12th centuries as a trading entrepôt of Sogdian merchants and, under the Tang dynasty, became one of the Four Garrisons of Anxi in the Western Regions.

History

Sogdian merchants from Samarkand and Bukhara founded or developed Suyab as a hub where goods from China, Persia, and the steppe changed hands. After the Western Turks and then Tang China contested the region, Emperor Gaozong made Suyab the seat of a protectorate administration in 679. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang passed through in about 629 and described its markets and its meeting with the Turkic khagan. The Tibetan Empire and the Qarluqs later controlled the city, and under the Karakhanids it declined as nearby Balasagun rose in importance.

Archaeology

Suyab is identified with the Ak-Beshim site west of Tokmok. Excavations, including Soviet and joint Kyrgyz–Japanese missions, have uncovered city walls, a Nestorian Christian church, Buddhist temples, and a Zoroastrian ossuary — evidence of the religious diversity of a Silk Road crossroads. Tang coins, Sogdian documents, and Chinese inscriptions have also been found. The site forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor" (2014).
