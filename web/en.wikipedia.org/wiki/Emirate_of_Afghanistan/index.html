The Emirate of Afghanistan (Pashto: د افغانستان امارت) was an emirate in Central Asia and South Asia encompassing present-day Afghanistan, established in 1823 when the Barakzai dynasty displaced the Durrani Empire, and lasting until 1926, when Amanullah Khan proclaimed himself King and the state became the Kingdom of Afghanistan.

History

After the collapse of Durrani authority, Dost Mohammad Khan seized Kabul in 1826 and took the title of Emir. The emirate became the central prize of the "Great Game" between the British and Russian empires, fighting the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–1842), which ended in the catastrophic British retreat from Kabul, and the Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878–1880). Under the "Iron Emir" Abdur Rahman Khan (r. 1880–1901), the state was centralised, its modern borders were fixed by the Durand Line agreement (1893) and boundary commissions with Russia, and British subsidies supported a standing army while Britain controlled Afghanistan's foreign affairs.

Habibullah Khan kept Afghanistan neutral in the First World War. His son Amanullah seized full independence in the Third Anglo-Afghan War of 1919, recognised by the Treaty of Rawalpindi, and launched an ambitious programme of modernisation before proclaiming the kingdom in 1926.
