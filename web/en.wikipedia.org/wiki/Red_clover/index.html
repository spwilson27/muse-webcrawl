Red clover

Red clover (Trifolium pratense), also called purple clover or cowgrass, is a herbaceous flowering plant in the legume family Fabaceae, native to Europe, Western Asia, and northwest Africa, and widely naturalized elsewhere. It is a short-lived perennial with trifoliate leaves, each leaflet often marked with a pale crescent, and dense heads of dark pink to red flowers.

Cultivation and uses

Red clover is widely grown as a forage crop for livestock and as a green manure: its roots host nitrogen-fixing bacteria, enriching the soil. It is sown in pasture mixes and is a valued nectar source for bees and other pollinators; bumblebees are its chief pollinators. It is the national flower of Denmark and the state flower of Vermont.

Medicinal use

Red clover flowers contain isoflavones such as formononetin and biochanin A, which are phytoestrogens. Extracts are marketed as dietary supplements, particularly for menopausal symptoms, though clinical evidence for effectiveness is mixed. Traditionally the plant was used in folk medicine for coughs and skin complaints.
