Cut and fill
FieldEarthworks / civil engineering
PurposeLevel ground for roads, railways, canals, building plots

In earthworks engineering, cut and fill is the process of constructing a road, railway, canal, or level building site by cutting material out of high ground ("cut") and using it to fill in low ground ("fill" or embankment), so that a smooth, gently graded route or platform is achieved. Ideally the volume of cut roughly equals the volume of fill, minimising the need to import or dispose of material.

Practice

The technique has been used since the earliest canals and railways, when cuttings were excavated through hills and the spoil used to build embankments across valleys. Modern projects calculate cut and fill volumes from surveyed cross-sections or digital terrain models, accounting for the bulking of excavated soil and the compaction of fill. Cut sections of a road or railway are called cuttings; fill sections are embankments. In mining and quarrying, "cut and fill" also names an underground stoping method in which ore is removed in horizontal slices and the void backfilled.
