Spanish language in the Philippines

Spanish was the language of government, education and literature in the Philippines for more than three centuries of Spanish colonial rule (1565–1898), and it remained an official language of the country — alongside English and later Filipino — until 1987. Today it is spoken natively by only a small minority of Filipinos, but its legacy pervades Philippine culture and languages.

History

Spanish arrived with the colonisation begun under Miguel López de Legazpi in 1565. For most of the colonial period the language was largely confined to the Spanish and mestizo elite and to administration and the Church; the mass of the population continued to speak indigenous languages, which missionaries used for evangelisation. In the nineteenth century an educated Filipino class, the ilustrados, wrote in Spanish, and the Propaganda Movement produced works such as José Rizal's novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El filibusterismo (1891) in Spanish. The Malolos Constitution of 1899 adopted Spanish as the first official language of the First Philippine Republic.

Under American rule (1898–1946), English displaced Spanish in schools and government. Spanish retained official status in the 1935 and 1973 constitutions but lost it in the 1987 constitution, where it is promoted only on a voluntary and optional basis. Estimates of native speakers today are in the low tens of thousands, though Spanish is studied by growing numbers of students, partly for employment in business-process outsourcing serving Spanish-speaking markets; the Instituto Cervantes in Manila is among its largest teaching centres worldwide.

Influence

Philippine languages carry thousands of Spanish loanwords — for everyday objects, days, numbers, religion and government — and Spanish naming conventions persist in Filipino personal names and surnames. The Spanish-based creole Chavacano, spoken principally in Zamboanga City and parts of Cavite and Ternate, is the most vibrant survival of Spanish in the country, with roughly a million speakers. A distinct literary tradition of Philippine literature in Spanish, including writers such as Fernando María Guerrero and Jesús Balmori, flourished in the early twentieth century.
