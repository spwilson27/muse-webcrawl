Tessarakonteres

The tessarakonteres (Greek: τεσσαρακοντήρης, "forty-rowed"), known in Latin as the quadragintaremis, was a gigantic Hellenistic galley built by order of Ptolemy IV Philopator of Egypt around 215 BC. It is one of the largest human-powered vessels ever constructed and is described in detail by Callixenus of Rhodes, whose account survives in the Deipnosophistae of Athenaeus, and also by Plutarch.

Reconstruction of the great "forty" of Ptolemy IV

Description

According to Callixenus, the ship was 280 cubits (about 128 metres) long and 38 cubits wide, rising some 48 cubits to the top of the sternpost. It carried more than 4,000 oarsmen, 400 other crew and could accommodate nearly 3,000 marines on its decks. Its name, "forty", is generally interpreted not as forty banks of oars — physically implausible — but as a total of forty rowers per vertical section or bank arrangement, possibly with several men on each huge oar (the longest oars being nearly 17 metres). Many historians think the vessel may have been a catamaran, with two hulls joined by a broad deck.

Purpose

The tessarakonteres was too large to be a practical warship. Plutarch remarked of Ptolemy's giant ships that they were "only for show" and "like buildings on land, kept for exhibition rather than use". It served as a floating palace and symbol of Ptolemaic wealth and naval prestige rather than as a fighting vessel, and nothing is recorded of any voyage or battle in which it took part.
