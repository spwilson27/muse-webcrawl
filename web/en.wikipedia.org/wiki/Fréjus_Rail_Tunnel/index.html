The Fréjus Rail Tunnel (French: Tunnel ferroviaire du Fréjus; Italian: Traforo ferroviario del Fréjus), also called the Mont Cenis Tunnel, is a rail tunnel of 13.7 km (8.5 mi) length in the European Alps, carrying the Turin–Modane railway through Mount Fréjus between France and Italy. It connects Modane in France with Bardonecchia in Italy, passing beneath the Col du Fréjus.

History

Construction began in 1857 under the direction of the engineer Germain Sommeiller, who pioneered the use of pneumatic rock-drilling machines powered by compressed air. The work was originally expected to take 25 years with hand drilling; the new machinery, together with the use of dynamite later in the project, reduced this dramatically. The two headings met on 25 December 1870, and the tunnel opened to traffic in 1871. It was the first great Alpine tunnel and one of the longest tunnels in the world at the time, as well as the first to use automated drilling machinery.

The tunnel originally bored was slightly shorter; extensions of the portals in the early 1880s, made to protect the line from rockfalls and avalanches, brought its length to the present 13.7 km. It was electrified with three-phase alternating current in 1915 and converted to 3 kV DC in 1961.

Operations

The tunnel remains a key link on the route between Paris and Turin, carrying international passenger services and freight trains between France and Italy. Since the opening of the nearby Fréjus Road Tunnel in 1980, the two bores together handle the bulk of transalpine traffic at this crossing. A much longer base tunnel, the Mont d'Ambin Base Tunnel of the Turin–Lyon high-speed railway, is under construction to supplement and eventually relieve the historic tunnel.
