Resurrection of the dead

The resurrection of the dead is a standard eschatological belief in the Abrahamic religions. As a religious concept, it is used in two distinct respects: a belief in the resurrection of individual souls that is current and ongoing, and a belief in a singular bodily resurrection of the dead at the end of the world. Some believe the soul is the actual vehicle by which people are resurrected.

Judaism

Belief in resurrection developed within Second Temple Judaism and is expressed in passages such as Daniel 12:2, "Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt." The Pharisees affirmed the resurrection, while the Sadducees denied it. Resurrection of the dead (teḥiyyat ha-metim) became one of the fundamental principles of rabbinic Judaism and appears in the daily liturgy and in Maimonides' Thirteen Principles of Faith.

Christianity

In Christianity, the resurrection of Jesus is the foundational event, described by Paul as the "firstfruits" of the general resurrection to come (1 Corinthians 15). Mainstream Christian doctrine teaches a bodily resurrection of all the dead at the return of Christ, followed by the Last Judgment. The Nicene Creed professes "the resurrection of the dead and the life of the world to come." Christian views differ on the timing and nature of the resurrection body, which Paul describes as a "spiritual body" that is raised imperishable.

Islam

In Islam, belief in the resurrection (al-baʿth) after death on the Day of Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyāmah) is a central article of faith. The Quran repeatedly describes the dead being raised from their graves for judgment, after which the righteous enter paradise (Jannah) and the wicked hell (Jahannam).
