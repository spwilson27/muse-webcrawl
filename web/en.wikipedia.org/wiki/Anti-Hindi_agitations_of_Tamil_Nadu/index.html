Anti-Hindi agitations of Tamil Nadu

Anti-Hindi agitations
LocationMadras State / Tamil Nadu, India

OutcomeTwo-language policy in Tamil Nadu; indefinite continuation of English as associate official language

The anti-Hindi agitations of Tamil Nadu were a series of protests against the imposition of Hindi as a compulsory subject in schools and later as the sole official language of India, in the state of Tamil Nadu (formerly Madras State). The agitations, led by Dravidian movement figures such as Periyar E. V. Ramasamy and later by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK), fundamentally shaped the politics of the state and the language policy of India.

First agitation, 1937–1940

In 1937 the Congress government of the Madras Presidency under C. Rajagopalachari made Hindi a compulsory subject in secondary schools. The Self-Respect Movement of Periyar organised protests, processions and picketing; more than a thousand protesters were imprisoned, and two, Thalamuthu and Natarajan, died in custody and became martyrs of the movement. The policy was withdrawn in 1940 after the Congress ministry resigned.

Agitations after independence

The adoption of Hindi in the Devanagari script as the official language of the Indian Union in 1950, with English to continue for fifteen years, renewed fears in the non-Hindi-speaking south. Protests recurred through the 1950s, including agitation against the renaming policies and Hindi signage. The DMK, founded by C. N. Annadurai, made opposition to Hindi imposition a central plank.

1965 agitation

The largest agitation erupted in January–February 1965 as the fifteen-year transition period expired and Hindi was to become the sole official language. Students led mass protests across Madras State; there were strikes, arson attacks on Hindi signs and railway property, police firings that killed dozens, and several protesters immolated themselves. The violence prompted Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri to promise that English would continue as an associate official language for as long as non-Hindi speakers wished — an assurance later embodied in the Official Languages (Amendment) Act 1967.

Consequences

The agitations destroyed the Congress party's dominance in Tamil Nadu: the DMK won the 1967 state election and Congress has never returned to power in the state. Tamil Nadu adopted a two-language policy of Tamil and English, rejecting the three-language formula followed elsewhere in India. Language remains a sensitive political issue in the state, with periodic protests against perceived Hindi imposition continuing into the twenty-first century.
