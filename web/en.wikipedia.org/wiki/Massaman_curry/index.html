Massaman curry

Massaman curry (Thai: แกงมัสมั่น, romanised: kaeng matsaman) is a rich, relatively mild curry of Thai cuisine that is distinguished by its use of dried spices more typical of Persian, Indian, and Malay cooking than of other Thai curries. It is usually made with coconut milk, massaman curry paste, peanuts, potatoes, onions, fish sauce, tamarind paste, palm sugar, and meat — most commonly beef, lamb, chicken, or duck. It is normally served with rice.

History

The dish's name probably derives from the Malay or Persian word for Muslim (Musulman), reflecting its introduction to Thailand by Muslim traders. Massaman curry is commonly dated to the 17th century, during the reign of King Narai of Ayutthaya, when Persian merchants and diplomats were active at the Siamese court. A poem by King Rama II of the early 19th century praises massaman curry, and the dish is widely associated with the southern Thai Muslim community.

Characteristics

Unlike most Thai curries, which use fresh herbs and chillies, massaman curry paste is made from dried spices — cumin, coriander, cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, and nutmeg — blended with shallots, garlic, galangal, and dried chillies. The resulting curry is sweet, sour, and lightly spiced rather than fiercely hot, and its gravy is thick and creamy from coconut milk and ground peanuts.

In 2011, massaman curry was ranked first in CNNGo's list of the "World's 50 most delicious foods".
