Flap (surgery)

Flap surgery is a technique in plastic and reconstructive surgery where any type of tissue is lifted from a donor site and moved to a recipient site with an intact blood supply. A flap differs from a skin graft in that a graft relies on the recipient bed for revascularization, whereas a flap carries its own blood supply with it.

Classification

Flaps may be classified by their composition (cutaneous, fasciocutaneous, muscle, musculocutaneous, or osseocutaneous), by the nature of their blood supply (random pattern or axial pattern), and by their method of movement: local flaps (advancement, rotation, transposition), regional or pedicled flaps, and free flaps transferred with microvascular anastomosis.

Indications

Flaps are used to reconstruct defects after trauma, burns, or tumour excision, to cover exposed bone, tendon, or hardware, and in breast reconstruction after mastectomy, where techniques such as the DIEP flap and TRAM flap are widely used.

Complications

Complications include partial or total flap necrosis from vascular compromise, infection, haematoma, and donor-site morbidity. Careful design, adequate perfusion, and postoperative monitoring are essential to flap survival.
