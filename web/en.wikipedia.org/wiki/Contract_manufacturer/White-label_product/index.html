White-label product

A white-label product is a product or service produced by one company (the producer) that other companies (the marketers) rebrand to make it appear as if they had made it. The name derives from the image of a white label on the packaging that can be filled in with the marketer's trade dress. White-label products are often produced via mass production.

Uses

White-label production is common in consumer electronics, packaged food, cosmetics and software. Grocery and retail chains sell store-brand goods manufactured by third parties to the retailer's specification; these are sometimes called private label products. In digital services, white-label software allows a company to offer a platform — for example a payment gateway, streaming service or e-commerce storefront — under its own branding while the underlying technology is supplied by another firm.

White labelling lets marketers enter a market quickly without investing in product development or manufacturing capacity, and lets producers use spare capacity and reach customers they could not serve directly. Drawbacks include limited control over quality and features, and the risk that competitors sell essentially identical products.
