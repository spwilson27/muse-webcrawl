Contract manufacturer

A contract manufacturer (CM) is a firm that manufactures components or finished products for another company under that company's label or brand. The hiring firm supplies designs and specifications, and the contract manufacturer provides the production capacity, labor, and often procurement of materials. The practice is a form of outsourcing.

Roles and related terms

Contract manufacturers differ from original design manufacturers (ODMs), which design as well as manufacture products that are then branded by the customer. In electronics, contract manufacturers are often called electronics manufacturing services (EMS) providers; prominent examples include Foxconn, Flex, Jabil, and Pegatron. The model is also widespread in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, food processing, and apparel.

Advantages and risks

Companies use contract manufacturers to reduce capital expenditure, take advantage of specialized expertise and economies of scale, and bring products to market faster. Risks include loss of direct control over quality, exposure of intellectual property, supply-chain disruption, and labor or environmental controversies in the manufacturer's facilities becoming associated with the brand.
