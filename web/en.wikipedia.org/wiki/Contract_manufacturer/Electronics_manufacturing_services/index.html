Electronics manufacturing services

Electronics manufacturing services (EMS) is a term used for companies that design, manufacture, test, distribute, and provide return/repair services for electronic components and assemblies for original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). The concept is also referred to as electronics contract manufacturing (ECM).

History

The EMS industry took off after the late 1970s when Solectron was established. At the time, most electronics manufacturing for large-scale product runs was handled by in-house assembly. Smaller companies could take advantage of the EMS model to manufacture and ship products without building large factories of their own. Through the 1990s and 2000s, many OEMs sold their assembly plants to EMS providers and the industry consolidated; large EMS firms such as Foxconn, Flex, Jabil, Celestica and Sanmina grew by acquiring plants and smaller competitors.

Services

EMS providers typically offer printed circuit board (PCB) fabrication and assembly, cable and harness assembly, system build and integration, testing, logistics and distribution, and after-market repair services. Services are commonly grouped by the complexity of the product and the volume of production. Many providers also offer design services, acting as an original design manufacturer (ODM) that designs products sold under the customer's brand.

Market

The largest EMS companies assemble products for the computing, consumer electronics, telecommunications, automotive, industrial and medical sectors. Production is concentrated in Asia, particularly China, Taiwan, Vietnam, Malaysia and Thailand, although significant capacity also exists in North America and Europe.
