Foxconn

Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd., doing business as Foxconn Technology Group in Taiwan and Foxconn internationally, is a Taiwanese multinational electronics contract manufacturer with its headquarters in Tucheng, New Taipei City, Taiwan. As of 2024, it is the world's largest contract manufacturer of electronics and the fourth-largest information technology company by revenue. The company is the largest private employer in Taiwan and one of the largest employers worldwide.

History

Terry Gou founded Hon Hai in 1974 as a manufacturer of plastic parts for television sets. The company began producing connectors for personal computers in the 1980s and established its first factory in Shenzhen, China, in 1988. The Shenzhen Longhua campus grew into a vast complex employing hundreds of thousands of workers. Foxconn became famous as the principal assembler of Apple products including the iPhone, and also manufactures for companies such as Amazon, Dell, Google, HP, Microsoft, Nintendo, Sony and Xiaomi.

Operations

Foxconn operates factories across Asia, Europe and the Americas, with its largest facilities in China, including the Zhengzhou "iPhone City" complex. Since the late 2010s the company has diversified into electric vehicles, semiconductors, digital health and robotics, and announced a high-profile (later greatly scaled-back) factory project in Wisconsin, United States.

Controversies

Foxconn has faced sustained criticism over working conditions, including a series of worker suicides at its Shenzhen plants in 2010 that drew international attention to long hours, low pay and military-style management. The company responded with wage increases, safety nets and counselling, and later audits by the Fair Labor Association found progress alongside continuing violations of overtime limits.
