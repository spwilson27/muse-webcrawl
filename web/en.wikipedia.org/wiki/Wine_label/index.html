Wine label

For the label as collectible, see Wine label collecting.

Wine labels are important sources of information for consumers, identifying the producer, origin, grape variety or varieties, vintage, alcohol content and legal classification of the wine in the bottle. Label designs vary widely, from the restrained black-and-white typographic labels of Bordeaux estates to the artistic and humorous labels common among New World producers, some of which are commissioned from well-known artists, as with Château Mouton Rothschild's annual artist series.

Information content

European Union regulations require labels to state the producer or bottler, the appellation or geographical indication, volume, alcohol by volume, and lot information. Traditional European labels emphasize place (appellation) over grape variety, whereas New World labels usually name the variety — a convention pioneered by California producers such as Robert Mondavi under the influence of Frank Schoonmaker. Terms such as reserve, estate bottled and old vines are legally defined in some jurisdictions and pure marketing in others.

Front and back labels

Many bottles carry a back label with additional details: tasting notes, vineyard history, food-pairing suggestions, importer information and government health warnings, which are mandatory in the United States. Capsules, neck tags and medallions form part of the presentation.

Language and terminology

Classic labels use the vocabulary of the producing region: Château and mis en bouteille au château in Bordeaux, Riserva in Italy, Gran Reserva in Spain, and German labels' dense Prädikat hierarchy determined by ripeness at harvest. Understanding these conventions is a standard part of wine education.
