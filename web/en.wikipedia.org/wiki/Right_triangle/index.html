Right triangle

A right triangle or right-angled triangle is a triangle in which one angle is a right angle (that is, a 90-degree angle). The side opposite the right angle is called the hypotenuse, and the other two sides are called the legs (or catheti).

The Pythagorean theorem

The fundamental relation between the sides is the Pythagorean theorem: if the legs have lengths a and b and the hypotenuse has length c, then a² + b² = c². Integer solutions of this equation, such as (3, 4, 5) and (5, 12, 13), are called Pythagorean triples.

Properties

The two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary, summing to 90°. The hypotenuse is the diameter of the triangle's circumcircle, so the midpoint of the hypotenuse is equidistant from all three vertices. A right triangle whose legs are equal is an isosceles right triangle with angles 45°–45°–90°; halving an equilateral triangle gives the 30°–60°–90° triangle.

Trigonometry

Right triangles are the foundation of trigonometry: for an acute angle in the triangle, the sine is the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse, the cosine the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, and the tangent the ratio of opposite to adjacent. The area of a right triangle is half the product of its legs.
