Imran Khan

Imran Ahmed Khan Niazi (born 5 October 1952) is a Pakistani politician and former cricketer who served as the 22nd Prime Minister of Pakistan from 2018 to 2022. He captained the Pakistan national cricket team to victory in the 1992 Cricket World Cup and is regarded as one of the greatest all-rounders in the history of the game.

Cricket career

Educated at Aitchison College, Lahore, and Keble College, Oxford, Khan made his Test debut in 1971 and played for Worcestershire in English county cricket. Over a two-decade international career he took 362 Test wickets and scored more than 3,800 Test runs, pioneering reverse swing bowling. His crowning achievement came in 1992, when he led Pakistan to its first World Cup title in Australia.

Philanthropy and politics

After retiring, Khan founded the Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital in Lahore in memory of his mother. In 1996 he launched the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (Movement for Justice) party. After years as a marginal figure, PTI swept Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in 2013 and won the 2018 general election, making Khan prime minister. His government pursued anti-corruption drives, welfare programs such as Ehsaas, and an independent foreign policy. In April 2022 he was removed by a parliamentary no-confidence vote, the first Pakistani prime minister so ousted. He was subsequently arrested and convicted in multiple cases, which he and his party maintain are politically motivated, while remaining a central figure in Pakistani politics.
