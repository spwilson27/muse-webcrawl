Sapporo (札幌) is the capital and largest city of Hokkaido, Japan's northernmost main island. It is the fifth-largest city in Japan by population and is famous internationally for its annual Snow Festival, its ramen, and its beer. The name derives from the Ainu language, from sat poro pet, meaning "dry, great river".

History

Sapporo was founded in 1868, at the beginning of the Meiji period, when the Japanese government began the systematic development of Hokkaido, previously known as Ezo and inhabited by the Ainu people. The city was laid out on a grid plan with the help of American advisers, notably William S. Clark, founder of the Sapporo Agricultural College (now Hokkaido University), whose parting words "Boys, be ambitious!" remain a famous motto.

Events

Sapporo hosted the 1972 Winter Olympics, the first Winter Games held in Asia. The Sapporo Snow Festival, held each February since 1950, draws millions of visitors to see enormous snow and ice sculptures in Odori Park. The city also hosted matches in the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the 2019 Rugby World Cup.

Culture

Sapporo is the birthplace of miso ramen and of Sapporo Beer, one of Japan's oldest beer brands, founded in 1876. The city has a cool climate with heavy snowfall, making it one of the world's snowiest major cities.
