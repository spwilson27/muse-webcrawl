Free will

Free will
BranchMetaphysics / Ethics
Key topicsDeterminism, compatibilism, moral responsibility
[Image: A fork in a forest path symbolising choice between futures]

Free will is the capacity of agents to choose between different possible courses of action, closely tied to moral responsibility, praise and blame. Philosophy asks whether free will is compatible with determinism — the thesis that every event is necessitated by prior causes — and what kind of control genuine choice requires.

Positions

Compatibilists, following David Hume and modern writers such as Daniel Dennett, hold that freedom means acting on one's own desires without coercion, whether or not determinism is true. Libertarians about free will (not the political label) argue it requires genuine alternative possibilities or agent-causation. Hard determinists, such as Derk Pereboom, deny free will but debate how practices of responsibility should change. Thought experiments like Harry Frankfurt's cases challenge the idea that responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise.
