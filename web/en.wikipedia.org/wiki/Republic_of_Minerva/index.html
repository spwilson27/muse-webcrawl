Republic of Minerva

The Republic of Minerva was a micronation proclaimed in 1972 on the Minerva Reefs, two submerged atolls in the South Pacific south of Fiji and Tonga. It was one of the few modern attempts to found a libertarian state by physically creating territory — in this case by dumping sand onto reefs to raise artificial islands — rather than merely declaring one.

The project

The scheme was organised by Michael Oliver, a Las Vegas real-estate millionaire and founder of the Ocean Life Research Foundation, who had previously been involved in other new-country projects. In 1971 barges from Australia delivered sand to the South Minerva Reef, raising a small island above sea level on which a stone tower and flag were raised. On 19 January 1972 the Republic of Minerva issued a declaration of independence, elected Morris C. "Bud" Davis as president, issued coins and stamps, and notified neighbouring states of its claim, arguing the reefs lay in international waters and belonged to no one.

Tongan annexation

Neighbouring states did not agree. A conference of Pacific states discussed the claim, and King Taufa'ahau Tupou IV of Tonga issued a proclamation of sovereignty over the reefs. In June 1972 a Tongan expedition, accompanied by a naval vessel, landed on the reefs, removed the Minervan flag and formally annexed the territory. The island built by the Minervans has since eroded back beneath the sea. Fiji also asserts a claim to the reefs, and the question of sovereignty between Tonga and Fiji remains formally disputed.

Legacy

Minerva became a standard case study in the literature of micronations and seasteading: unlike paper declarations, it briefly held physical ground, and it failed not for lack of construction but because an established state was willing to use force to extinguish the claim. Oliver went on to support other ventures, including the Phoenix Foundation's involvement in the Abaco independence movement.
