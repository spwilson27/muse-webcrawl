The South West Pacific Area (SWPA) was the name given to the Allied supreme command in the South West Pacific Theatre of the Second World War. Established in April 1942, it was one of two major Allied commands in the Pacific war — the other being Pacific Ocean Areas under Admiral Chester Nimitz. Its Supreme Commander was General Douglas MacArthur.

Organisation

SWPA encompassed Australia, New Guinea, the Dutch East Indies (Indonesia), the Philippines, and the Solomon Islands' western approaches. Its subordinate forces included Allied Land Forces under Australian General Thomas Blamey, Allied Air Forces under Lieutenant General George Kenney, and Allied Naval Forces — for most of the war built around the US Seventh Fleet under Vice Admiral Thomas Kinkaid. The bulk of ground troops in the early years were Australian, with American strength growing through 1943–44.

Strategy

From bases in Australia, SWPA forces fought the Kokoda Track and Buna–Gona campaigns in Papua (1942–43), then advanced along New Guinea's northern coast in a series of amphibious "leapfrogging" operations — Lae, Finschhafen, Hollandia, Biak, Morotai — bypassing and isolating large Japanese garrisons such as Rabaul. In October 1944 the command returned to the Philippines at Leyte, liberated Manila in early 1945, and in mid-1945 supported the Australian-led Borneo campaign. The command was dissolved following the Japanese surrender in September 1945.
