Igor Sikorsky

Born25 May 1889, Kiev, Russian Empire
Died26 October 1972, Easton, Connecticut, United States
OccupationAviation pioneer, engineer
Known forFirst four-engine aircraft; first successful single-rotor helicopter

Igor Ivanovich Sikorsky (25 May 1889 – 26 October 1972) was a Russian-American aviation pioneer in both helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft. His first success came with the S-2, the second aircraft of his design. In 1913 he designed and flew the Ilya Muromets, the world's first four-engine aircraft. After emigrating to the United States in 1919, he founded the Sikorsky Aircraft Corporation and developed the first practical single-rotor helicopter, the VS-300, in 1939.

Early life

Sikorsky was born in Kiev, then part of the Russian Empire, the youngest of five children of a professor of psychology. Inspired by Leonardo da Vinci and Jules Verne, he studied engineering at the Kiev Polytechnic Institute and began experimenting with helicopter designs as a young man.

Career in Russia

Working with the Russo-Baltic Wagon Works, Sikorsky built a series of fixed-wing aircraft that won Russian military competitions. The Ilya Muromets became a strategic bomber in the First World War.

Career in America

After the Russian Revolution, Sikorsky emigrated to the United States, teaching and lecturing before founding his company in 1923 with the support of figures including composer Sergei Rachmaninoff. His S-42 flying boat enabled Pan Am's transoceanic routes. In 1939 the VS-300 made its first flight; its configuration — a single main rotor and a tail rotor — became the standard for most helicopters. The R-4 became the world's first mass-produced helicopter in 1942.
