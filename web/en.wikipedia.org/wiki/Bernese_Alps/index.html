Bernese Alps

The Bernese Alps (German: Berner Alpen) are a mountain range of the western Alps in Switzerland, lying mainly in the cantons of Bern, Valais, and Vaud. They contain many of the highest and most famous peaks in the Alps, including the Finsteraarhorn (4,274 m), the Aletschhorn, the Jungfrau, the Mönch, and the Eiger, whose north face is one of the great challenges of mountaineering.

Geography

The range is bounded by the Rhône valley to the south, the Aare valley and Lake Thun to the north, and the Reuss valley to the east. It holds the two longest glaciers in the Alps, the Aletsch Glacier and the Fiescher Glacier. The Jungfrau-Aletsch region was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Tourism and transport

Resorts such as Interlaken, Grindelwald, Wengen, and Mürren serve as gateways to the range. The Jungfrau Railway, opened in 1912, climbs through tunnels in the Eiger to the Jungfraujoch station at 3,454 m, the highest railway station in Europe.

Mountaineering history

The Bernese Alps were central to the golden age of alpinism in the nineteenth century, and the Eiger's north face was first climbed in 1938 by an Austro-German party including Heinrich Harrer.
