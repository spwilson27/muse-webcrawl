Sir Iain Geoffrey Chalmers (born 3 June 1943) is a British physician and health researcher who is one of the founders of the Cochrane Collaboration, the international network that prepares systematic reviews of the effects of health care interventions. He is a leading figure in the evidence-based medicine movement.

Career

Chalmers practised medicine in the UK and in Gaza before turning to health services research. As director of the National Perinatal Epidemiology Unit in Oxford, he led the preparation of the two-volume Effective Care in Pregnancy and Childbirth (1989) and an electronic register of controlled trials in perinatal care — work that demonstrated how many widely used treatments had never been properly evaluated and provided a model for systematic reviewing.

In 1992 he became founding director of the UK Cochrane Centre, and the Cochrane Collaboration was launched in 1993, named after Archie Cochrane. He later co-founded the James Lind Alliance (2004), which brings patients and clinicians together to agree priorities for research, and has campaigned against bias and waste in research, including unreported trials (the AllTrials campaign) and failure to build on existing evidence.

Honours

Chalmers was knighted in 2000 for services to health care. He co-authored the popular book Testing Treatments, which explains why fair tests of treatments matter.
