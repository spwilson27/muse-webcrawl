German idealism is the philosophical movement that developed in Germany between the 1780s and 1840s out of Immanuel Kant's critical philosophy. Kant's successors judged his thing-in-itself an inconsistent leftover: Fichte founded everything on the self-positing I; Schelling balanced subject with nature; and G. W. F. Hegel's absolute idealism presented reality as Spirit coming to know itself through history's dialectic — a system that dominated German thought until its fracture into Right and Left Hegelian camps (the latter producing Marx).

Legacy

The movement reshaped theology, historiography, aesthetics and political thought; its eclipse by positivism and analytic philosophy hid debts that 20th-century scholarship (and the 'Pittsburgh school' of Sellars, Brandom, McDowell) has recovered, reconnecting Hegel to contemporary debates.
