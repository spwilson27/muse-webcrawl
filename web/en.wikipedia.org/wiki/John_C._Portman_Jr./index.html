John C. Portman Jr.

John Calvin Portman Jr. (4 December 1924 – 29 December 2017) was an American neofuturistic architect and real estate developer widely known for popularising hotels and office buildings with large atria. His designs reshaped downtown Atlanta, his home city.

Career

Portman founded his practice in Atlanta in 1953. The Hyatt Regency Atlanta (1967) revived the atrium hotel, with a 22-storey interior court. He repeated the formula at San Francisco's Embarcadero Center, Los Angeles's Westin Bonaventure and Detroit's Renaissance Center — fortress-like complexes critics said turned inward from the street.

Developer-architect

Unusually, Portman also developed and financed his projects through Portman Companies, giving him creative control. His work in Asia includes Shanghai Centre and Marina Square, Singapore.

Legacy

Portman's atria influenced hotel design worldwide; critics debate his impact on urban street life while recognising his commercial success.
