Correlation spectroscopy

Correlation spectroscopy refers to a family of spectroscopic methods in which correlations between signals are measured to extract information about molecular structure, dynamics or interactions. The best-known example is in nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, where correlation spectroscopy (COSY) is the classic two-dimensional (2D) NMR experiment; the term also applies to fluorescence correlation spectroscopy (FCS) and two-dimensional correlation analysis of infrared and other spectra.

COSY in NMR

The homonuclear correlation spectroscopy (COSY) experiment, proposed by Jean Jeener in 1971 and developed by Richard Ernst and co-workers, correlates the chemical shifts of nuclei (usually protons) that are coupled to one another through bonds. The spectrum is a two-dimensional plot with the same chemical-shift axis on both dimensions: signals on the diagonal reproduce the ordinary one-dimensional spectrum, while off-diagonal "cross peaks" appear between the shifts of coupled spins, revealing through-bond connectivity within a molecule.

Variants include COSY-90 and COSY-45, double-quantum filtered COSY (DQF-COSY), and long-range COSY. Related 2D experiments include TOCSY, which shows correlations across an entire spin system; NOESY, which reports through-space proximity; and heteronuclear experiments such as HSQC and HMBC, which correlate different nuclear species.

Fluorescence correlation spectroscopy

Fluorescence correlation spectroscopy (FCS), introduced in the 1970s by Magde, Elson and Webb, analyses temporal fluctuations in fluorescence from a tiny observation volume. Autocorrelation of the intensity trace yields diffusion coefficients, concentrations and reaction kinetics of labelled molecules, and cross-correlation between two colour channels (FCCS) detects molecular binding.

Two-dimensional correlation analysis

In generalised 2D correlation spectroscopy, developed by Isao Noda, spectra recorded under a systematic perturbation (temperature, pressure, concentration) are correlated to reveal the order of intensity changes and to resolve overlapping bands in infrared, Raman and other spectroscopies.
