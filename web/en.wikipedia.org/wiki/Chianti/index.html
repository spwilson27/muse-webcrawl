Chianti

Chianti is an Italian red wine produced in the Chianti region in central Tuscany, principally in the provinces of Florence, Siena, Arezzo, and Pistoia. It was historically associated with a squat bottle enclosed in a straw basket, called a fiasco. However, the fiasco is now only used by a few makers of the wine; most Chianti is now bottled in traditional wine bottles.

Chianti was granted Denominazione di Origine Controllata (DOC) status in 1967 and Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG) status in 1984. The earliest definition of Chianti wine was given in 1716, when Cosimo III de' Medici issued an edict delimiting the production zone — one of the earliest examples of a legally protected wine region in the world.

Grape varieties

Chianti is required to contain a minimum of 70% Sangiovese, the principal grape of the region. The remaining portion may include other red grapes such as Canaiolo and Colorino, as well as international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. Historically, white grapes such as Malvasia and Trebbiano were also permitted, and the 19th-century formula of Baron Bettino Ricasoli — often credited with defining the modern recipe — called for 70% Sangiovese, 15% Canaiolo, and 15% Malvasia bianca.

Zones

The Chianti zone is divided into several sub-zones. The most famous is Chianti Classico, the historic heartland between Florence and Siena, whose wines carry a distinctive black rooster (gallo nero) emblem. Other sub-zones include Chianti Rufina, Chianti Colli Senesi, Chianti Colli Fiorentini, Chianti Montalbano, Chianti Montespertoli, and Chianti Colline Pisane.

Classification

Chianti wines are classified by aging and quality. Basic Chianti may be released after only a few months, while Chianti Superiore follows stricter production rules. Riserva wines must be aged for a minimum period (24 months for Chianti Classico Riserva) before release. In 2013, Chianti Classico introduced a new top tier, Gran Selezione, made from estate-grown grapes and aged at least 30 months.

Characteristics

Chianti is typically a medium-bodied, dry red wine with moderate acidity and tannins. Common aromas and flavors include red cherries, dried herbs, balsamic vinegar, tobacco, and earthy notes. Its acidity makes it a classic pairing with food, particularly tomato-based dishes, grilled meats, and Tuscan specialties such as bistecca alla fiorentina.
