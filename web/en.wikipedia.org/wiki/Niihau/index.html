Niihau

Location Pacific Ocean (Hawaiian Islands)

Area 69.5 sq mi (180 km²)

County Kauai County, Hawaii

Nickname "The Forbidden Isle"

Niʻihau (Hawaiian: [niʔiˈhɐw]) is the seventh-largest island in Hawaii and the westernmost main island of the archipelago. It is privately owned by the Robinson family and is often called "The Forbidden Isle" because outside visitors are generally prohibited. The island lies about 17.5 miles (28 km) southwest of Kauai across the Kaulakahi Channel.

Ownership and life on the island

In 1864, Elizabeth Sinclair purchased Niihau from King Kamehameha V for $10,000 in gold; ownership passed to her descendants, the Robinson family. The owners have preserved the island as a stronghold of Native Hawaiian culture: its roughly 130 residents, most of them Native Hawaiians, speak Hawaiian (ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi) as a first language — the only community in Hawaii where this remains the case. The main village is Puʻuwai. Residents live without paved roads, plumbing, or running water in the modern sense; electricity comes mainly from solar power, and supplies arrive by barge from Kauai. Ranching was long the economic mainstay, and the island is famed for its intricate shell lei (pupu) jewellery.

History

During World War II, the island was the site of the "Niihau incident" (December 1941), in which a Japanese naval pilot who crash-landed after the attack on Pearl Harbor was captured by residents with the help of one resident of Japanese ancestry — an episode later cited in debates over the internment of Japanese Americans. Limited supervised tours and hunting safaris have occasionally been permitted to support the island's economy while access remains tightly controlled.
