Rubber-tyred metro

A rubber-tyred metro or rubber-tired metro is a form of rapid transit system that uses a mix of road and rail technologies. The vehicles have wheels with rubber tyres that run on concrete roll ways inside guide bars for traction, rather than on steel rails.

Technology

The technology was developed by the French company Michelin and first applied in the Paris Métro in the 1950s. Rubber tyres give superior adhesion, allowing steeper gradients and faster acceleration and braking than steel wheels, at the cost of higher rolling resistance, greater energy consumption and more complex infrastructure. Guidance is provided by horizontal guide wheels running against side guide bars.

Systems

Rubber-tyred systems operate in Paris, Montreal, Mexico City, Santiago, Sapporo and elsewhere. Fully automated rubber-tyred lines, such as those using the VAL system, are common in airport people movers and smaller metro networks.
