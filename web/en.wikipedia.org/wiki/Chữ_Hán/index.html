Chữ Hán (𡨸漢, literally "Han characters") are the Chinese characters as used historically in Vietnam to write Literary Chinese, the language of government, scholarship and formal literature for most of Vietnamese history. In modern Vietnamese usage, chữ Hán may refer to Chinese characters in general.

History

Chinese characters entered Vietnam during a millennium of Chinese rule (111 BC – AD 939) and remained the medium of the imperial bureaucracy and the Confucian civil-service examinations after independence. Official documents, dynastic histories, law codes, stelae and much court literature were written in Literary Chinese, read with Vietnamese pronunciations of the characters (âm Hán-Việt, Sino-Vietnamese readings).

Decline

Under French colonial rule and early-20th-century educational reforms, chữ Hán lost its official status; the last imperial examinations were held in 1919, and the Latin-based chữ quốc ngữ became universal. Today chữ Hán survives in calligraphy, temple inscriptions, genealogies and Tet couplets, and knowledge of the characters remains useful for understanding the large Sino-Vietnamese stratum of the Vietnamese vocabulary.
