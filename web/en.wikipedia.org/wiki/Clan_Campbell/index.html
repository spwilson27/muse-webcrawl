Clan Campbell

The Clan Campbell tartan, one of the most recognisable of Scottish clan tartans.

Clan Campbell (Scottish Gaelic: Clann Chaimbeul) is a Highland Scottish clan, historically one of the largest and most powerful of the Highland clans. The Clan Campbell lands are in Argyll and within their lands lies Ben Cruachan. The chief of the clan became Earl of Argyll and later Duke of Argyll.

History

Origins

The family name derives from the Gaelic cam beul, meaning "crooked mouth" or "wry-mouthed". The clan is said to descend from Diarmuid of the Fenian cycle of Irish mythology. The first Campbell to appear in written records was Gillespie Campbell in about 1263, and the clan's early heartland was around Loch Awe, where the MacArthurs were also prominent.

Wars of Scottish Independence

Under Sir Neil Campbell, the clan supported Robert the Bruce during the Wars of Scottish Independence, fighting at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314. The Campbells were rewarded with lands forfeited by clans that had opposed Bruce, and their power grew steadily through marriage, royal favour and strategic alliances.

Rise to power

Colin Campbell became the first Earl of Argyll in 1457. Over the following centuries the earls of Argyll were among the most powerful magnates in Scotland, acting as hereditary Justiciars and Lieutenants of Argyll. The clan expanded at the expense of neighbours such as the MacDonalds and the MacGregors, acquiring a reputation in Highland tradition for ruthlessness.

Archibald Campbell, 8th Earl of Argyll, was a leading Covenanter during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms and was executed in 1661 after the Restoration. His son, the 9th Earl, was executed in 1685 after an unsuccessful rising against James VII.

Massacre of Glencoe

In February 1692, soldiers under Captain Robert Campbell of Glenlyon — acting on government orders — killed members of Clan MacDonald of Glencoe who had been slow to swear allegiance to William III. Although the order came from the Crown and the soldiers were drawn from the Earl of Argyll's regiment, the massacre deepened the Campbells' feud with the MacDonalds in popular memory.

Jacobite risings

The Campbells generally supported the Hanoverian government during the Jacobite risings of 1715 and 1745, with the Duke of Argyll commanding government forces at the Battle of Sheriffmuir in 1715. The clan's loyalty to the Crown secured its dominance in the western Highlands.

Chief

The chief of Clan Campbell is the Duke of Argyll, known in Gaelic as MacCailein Mòr ("the Great MacColin"), whose seat is Inveraray Castle in Argyll.
