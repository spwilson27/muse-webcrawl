John Lane Bell (born 25 March 1945) is a British-born Canadian mathematician and philosopher. He is Professor Emeritus of Philosophy at the University of Western Ontario in London, Ontario, and a Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada. His research spans mathematical logic, set theory, topos theory and the philosophy of mathematics, with a particular focus on models of set theory, intuitionism, and the continuum and infinitesimals.

Education and career

Bell was born in London to Scottish parents — his father was the mathematician John Crossley Bell's contemporary in the Scottish intellectual milieu — and was educated in Scotland. He took his undergraduate degree and doctorate at the University of Oxford, studying mathematical logic at Balliol College and earning his DPhil under the influence of the Oxford logic group around Michael Dummett and Dana Scott; his 1969 thesis treated models of set theory. After positions at the London School of Economics and elsewhere, he moved to Canada, joining the University of Western Ontario, where he has spent most of his career in the Department of Philosophy while remaining active in mathematics.

Work

With Alan Slomson, Bell wrote Models and Ultraproducts: An Introduction (1969), a standard early text on model theory. His monograph Boolean-Valued Models and Independence Proofs in Set Theory (1977; third edition 2005) became the classic exposition of the Boolean-valued approach to forcing and the independence of the continuum hypothesis.

Bell is a leading expositor of smooth infinitesimal analysis — a rigorous, intuitionistic-logic-based theory in which infinitesimals are genuine and the continuum is "smooth" and indecomposable — set out in A Primer of Infinitesimal Analysis (1998). His The Continuous and the Infinitesimal in Mathematics and Philosophy (2005) traces the two rival conceptions of the continuum from antiquity to modern topos theory, while Toposes and Local Set Theories (1988) introduced categorical logic to a broad audience. Later works include The Axiom of Choice (2009) and Set Theory: Boolean-Valued Models and Independence Proofs, and he contributed reflections on Gödel, intuitionism and the nature of mathematical truth to philosophy journals throughout his career.

Personal

Bell has written on his family history, including the career of his grandfather, in Scottish historical publications. He continues as an active researcher and lecturer in retirement.
