Tsumago-juku

Tsumago-juku
Japanese:妻籠宿
Station number:42nd of the 69 Stations of the Nakasendō
Location:Nagiso, Nagano Prefecture, Japan
Route:Nakasendō (Kiso Valley)

Tsumago-juku (妻籠宿) was the forty-second of the sixty-nine post towns (shukuba) of the Nakasendō, the highway connecting Edo (Tokyo) with Kyoto during Japan's Edo period. It is located in the modern town of Nagiso in the Kiso Valley of Nagano Prefecture. Together with neighbouring Magome-juku, it is one of the best-preserved post towns in Japan and a celebrated example of community-led heritage conservation.

History

Like other Nakasendō stations, Tsumago provided inns (honjin for high-ranking travellers, hatago for commoners), stables, and porters for travellers including daimyō processions travelling to Edo under the sankin-kōtai system. After the Meiji Restoration the highway declined and the town fell into obscurity, which paradoxically preserved its Edo-period buildings. In 1968 local residents began a pioneering preservation movement under the principles "do not sell, do not rent, do not destroy" its historic assets; in 1976 Tsumago was designated a nationally protected architectural preservation district, the first of its kind for a post town.

Today

The main street is closed to vehicles during the day, utility poles and modern signage are hidden, and the restored honjin and waki-honjin (now a museum, the Nagiso-machi Museum) are open to visitors. Many former inns operate as minshuku guesthouses. A popular walking route of about 8 km along the old highway links Tsumago with Magome-juku over the Magome Pass, passing waterfalls and restored stretches of Edo-period road.
