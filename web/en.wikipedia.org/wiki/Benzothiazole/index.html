Benzothiazole

Chemical formulaC7H5NS
Molar mass135.19 g·mol−1
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point227–228 °C
CAS number95-16-9

Benzothiazole is an aromatic heterocyclic compound with the chemical formula C7H5NS. It consists of a benzene ring fused to a thiazole ring, and is a colourless, slightly viscous liquid with a faint odour reminiscent of quinoline. The benzothiazole skeleton occurs naturally in some contexts — it is a degradation product associated with firefly luciferin chemistry — and is a versatile scaffold in industrial and medicinal chemistry.

Structure and reactivity

Benzothiazole is a bicyclic 10-π aromatic system. The carbon atom between the sulfur and nitrogen (C-2) is electrophilic and is the principal site of functionalisation; 2-substituted benzothiazoles are the most important derivatives.

Applications

Derivatives of benzothiazole are widely used as accelerators in the sulfur vulcanisation of rubber — 2-mercaptobenzothiazole and its derivatives such as MBTS and sulfenamides are industry standards. The scaffold also appears in pharmaceuticals (for example the antipsychotic-related research compounds and the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis drug riluzole, a 2-amino-6-(trifluoromethoxy)benzothiazole), in dyes such as thioflavin T, and in agricultural chemicals.

Environmental occurrence

Benzothiazole and its derivatives leach from rubber products, including tyre wear particles and artificial-turf crumb rubber, and are detected in water and urban runoff, where they are studied as emerging contaminants.
