Logic Pro is a digital audio workstation (DAW) and MIDI sequencer application for macOS developed by Apple. It provides complete facilities for recording, editing, mixing and mastering audio and MIDI, together with a large library of software instruments, effects plug-ins and loops. Logic Pro is widely used in professional music production, film scoring and songwriting, and sits above Apple's entry-level GarageBand, with which it shares much of its design.

History

Logic originated with Notator, a score-writing and sequencing program for the Atari ST created by the German company C-Lab, which became Emagic. In the early 1990s Notator evolved into Notator Logic and then Logic, available for Mac and Windows. Apple acquired Emagic in July 2002 and discontinued the Windows version, making Logic a Mac-only product. Successive versions added audio recording sophistication, the EXS24 sampler (later Sampler), Space Designer convolution reverb, and the Sculpture physical-modelling synth. Logic Pro X (2013) brought a redesigned interface, Drummer virtual session players, Flex Pitch and the companion iPad remote; version 10.5 (2020) added Live Loops, and version 11 (2024) introduced AI-driven Session Players and Stem Splitter. In 2023 Apple launched a subscription version, Logic Pro for iPad.

Features

Logic Pro supports multitrack audio recording at up to 24-bit/192 kHz, extensive MIDI editing (piano roll, score and step editors), time-stretching and pitch correction (Flex Time and Flex Pitch), spatial audio mixing in Dolby Atmos, and a bundled content library of tens of gigabytes. It hosts third-party plug-ins in Apple's Audio Units format and includes instruments such as Alchemy, Retro Synth, the B3 organ and drum-synthesis tools.

Users

Logic has been the principal DAW of many prominent producers and artists; notable public users have included producers and songwriters across pop, electronic and film music, and its loops and sounds have appeared in numerous hit records.
