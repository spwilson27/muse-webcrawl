Afar people

Afar
Regions: Ethiopia (Afar Region), Djibouti, Eritrea
Language: Afar (Qafar), an Afroasiatic Cushitic language
Religion: Predominantly Islam

The Afar (Afar: Qafár), also known historically as the Danakil, are a Horn African ethnic group living primarily in the Afar Region of Ethiopia, as well as in Eritrea and Djibouti, where they form a significant minority. They speak the Afar language, part of the Cushitic branch of the Afroasiatic family.

Livelihood

Traditionally nomadic pastoralists, the Afar herd camels, goats, sheep, and cattle in one of the world's hottest deserts, and have long traded salt mined from the Danakil Depression.

History

Afar sultanates, including Aussa, controlled routes between the Ethiopian highlands and Red Sea ports such as Tadjoura. Afar society is organized in clans with councils of elders, and Islam was adopted over centuries through coastal trade.

Culture

Known for distinctive hairstyles, curved daggers (jile), oral poetry, and customary law (madaa). Population estimates are around 2–3 million across the three countries.
