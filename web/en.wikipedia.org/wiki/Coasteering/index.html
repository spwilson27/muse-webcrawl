Coasteering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Coasteering combines scrambling, swimming, and cliff jumping along a rocky coastline.

Coasteering is an outdoor adventure activity that involves traversing a rocky coastline on foot and by swimming, at or just above sea level, without the aid of boats or surfboards. It typically combines rock scrambling, cliff jumping, swimming through sea caves and gullies, and riding swell into natural features. Participants wear wetsuits, buoyancy aids, helmets, and sturdy footwear, and are usually led by qualified guides.

History

Although traversing coastlines was long a necessity for fishermen and smugglers, coasteering as a named commercial activity developed primarily in Wales in the 1980s and 1990s, around the Pembrokeshire coast near St Davids. Adventure centres there formalized the activity, and it spread to Cornwall, Scotland, Ireland, Portugal (especially Madeira and the Lisbon coast), Spain, and beyond.

Safety and environment

The activity is tide-dependent and carries risks including hypothermia, entrapment by rising tides, and injury from jumps; operators use route plans matched to conditions and check tidal and swell forecasts. Environmental concerns include disturbance of seals, seabirds, and intertidal habitats, so responsible operators avoid sensitive sites during breeding seasons. In the United Kingdom the National Coasteering Charter promotes good practice among providers.
