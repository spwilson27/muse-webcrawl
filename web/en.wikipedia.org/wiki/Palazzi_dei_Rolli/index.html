Palazzi dei Rolli

The Palazzi dei Rolli are a group of Renaissance and Baroque palaces in Genoa, Italy, included on official lists — the rolli — of aristocratic residences eligible, by public lottery, to host visiting dignitaries on behalf of the Republic of Genoa. In 2006, 42 of the palaces, together with the streets of the Strade Nuove (Via Garibaldi, Via Cairoli and Via Balbi), were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as "Genoa: Le Strade Nuove and the system of the Palazzi dei Rolli".

The rolli system

A Senate decree of 1576 established the lists, dividing the palazzi into categories (bussoli) according to their size and magnificence; the category determined the rank of guest each palace was required to lodge — popes, emperors and kings in the finest, down to lesser dignitaries in smaller houses. The system was a distinctive republican solution: state hospitality provided through the obligation of private families.

The palaces

Outstanding examples include the Palazzo Rosso, Palazzo Bianco and Palazzo Tursi on Via Garibaldi (the Strada Nuova, laid out from 1558 and celebrated by Rubens, who published engravings of Genoa's palaces in 1622), the Palazzo Balbi Senarega and Palazzo dell'Università on Via Balbi, and the Palazzo Spinola di Pellicceria, now housing a National Gallery. Many remain in private hands; others hold museums and university faculties. Genoa celebrates periodic "Rolli Days" when dozens of the palaces open to the public.
