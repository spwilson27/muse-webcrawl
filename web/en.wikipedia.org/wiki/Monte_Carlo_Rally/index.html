The Monte Carlo Rally (French: Rallye Automobile Monte-Carlo) is a rallying event organised each year by the Automobile Club de Monaco. First held in 1911 at the instigation of Prince Albert I, it is one of the oldest and most prestigious rallies in the world and traditionally opens the World Rally Championship season in January.

History

The original format was a "concentration run": competitors started from cities across Europe — including Paris, Berlin, Vienna, and even John o' Groats — and converged on Monte Carlo, where they were judged on regularity, the condition of the car, and elegance, partly to promote Monaco as a winter holiday destination. After the Second World War the event developed into a competitive special-stage rally in the French Maritime Alps and nearby departments.

Characteristics

The rally is run on asphalt mountain roads above Monaco, notably over the Col de Turini, but in January conditions vary from dry tarmac to snow and sheet ice — sometimes within a single stage. Tyre choice is therefore famously difficult, with drivers fitting studded winter tyres on one axle and slicks on another. Stages have traditionally included night running, and the Col de Turini stage, once known as the "Night of the Long Knives", is among the sport's most famous tests.

Notable winners

The event became famous in the 1960s through the victories of the Mini Cooper S (1964, 1965, 1967) — the 1966 win was annulled on a controversial lighting disqualification. In the modern era the rally has been dominated by French drivers: Sébastien Loeb won it seven times, and Sébastien Ogier surpassed that total, winning the rally a record number of times, including his tenth victory in 2025.
