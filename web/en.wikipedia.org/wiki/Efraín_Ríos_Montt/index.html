José Efraín Ríos Montt (June 16, 1926 – April 1, 2018) was a Guatemalan military officer and politician who was the de facto President of Guatemala from March 1982 to August 1983. His brief regime oversaw the most violent phase of Guatemala's 36-year civil war, including scorched-earth counterinsurgency campaigns in which the army massacred thousands of Maya civilians. In 2013 he became the first former head of state to be convicted of genocide by a court in his own country, although the verdict was annulled on procedural grounds days later.

Rise

Ríos Montt trained at Guatemala's military academy and the U.S. School of the Americas, rising to brigadier general and chief of staff of the army. As the Christian Democratic candidate in the 1974 presidential election he was widely believed to have won, but the military regime declared its own candidate the victor. After converting to evangelical Christianity, he was brought to power by a coup of junior officers on March 23, 1982, initially ruling through a junta and then as sole president.

Rule

Professing a moralising evangelical anti-communism — "a Christian should carry his machine gun and his Bible" — Ríos Montt combined anti-corruption rhetoric and Sunday televised sermons with the "beans and rifles" (frijoles y fusiles) counterinsurgency programme: food and amnesty for those who submitted, and destruction for villages deemed guerrilla supporters. Under his command the army carried out hundreds of massacres of Maya Ixil and other indigenous communities; the UN-sponsored Historical Clarification Commission later concluded that state forces committed acts of genocide in several regions during 1981–1983. Defence Minister Óscar Humberto Mejía Víctores overthrew him on August 8, 1983.

Later career and trial

Barred from the presidency by a constitutional ban on coup leaders, Ríos Montt founded the Guatemalan Republican Front, served as president of Congress (1995, 2000–2004), and ran unsuccessfully for president in 2003. After losing congressional immunity he was tried in 2013 for genocide and crimes against humanity for the killings of 1,771 Ixil Maya. On May 10, 2013 he was convicted and sentenced to 80 years in prison; ten days later the Constitutional Court annulled the verdict partway through the trial record and ordered proceedings partially restarted. Retrials stalled amid findings that he was mentally unfit; he died under house arrest in Guatemala City on April 1, 2018.
