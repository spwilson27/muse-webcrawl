Christian fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christian fiction is a genre of fiction that explores Christian themes, values and worldviews, typically for a Christian readership. Modern Christian fiction is published largely through Christian publishing houses and sold through Christian bookstores and general retailers, and includes subgenres such as biblical fiction, Amish romance, historical fiction and apocalyptic thrillers.

Early works

The genre has deep roots: John Bunyan's allegory The Pilgrim's Progress (1678) was for centuries one of the most widely read books in English. Lew Wallace's Ben-Hur: A Tale of the Christ (1880) was a landmark biblical novel, and Charles Sheldon's In His Steps (1896), with its question "What would Jesus do?", sold tens of millions of copies.

Modern market

The contemporary industry grew in the late 20th century with authors such as Janette Oke, whose prairie romances began in 1979, and Frank Peretti, whose spiritual-warfare thriller This Present Darkness (1986) broadened the market. The Left Behind series by Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins (1995–2007), about the Rapture and Tribulation, sold over 80 million copies and brought Christian fiction to general bestseller lists. Notable literary authors working with Christian themes include C. S. Lewis, J. R. R. Tolkien, Flannery O'Connor and Marilynne Robinson.

Characteristics

Publishing guidelines for the genre have traditionally limited profanity, explicit sexuality and graphic violence, and stories often feature conversion, redemption or providence. The genre is overseen in North America by bodies such as the Evangelical Christian Publishers Association, and awards include the Christy Award.
