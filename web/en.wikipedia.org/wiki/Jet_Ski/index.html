Jet Ski

Jet Ski is the brand name of a personal watercraft manufactured by the Japanese company Kawasaki. The name is frequently used generically for all types of personal watercraft, though it remains a registered trademark of Kawasaki. Introduced in 1973, the Jet Ski was the first commercially successful personal watercraft and created an entirely new class of recreational vessel.

History

The Jet Ski was developed from prototypes built by American inventor Clayton Jacobson II, who had worked on an aluminium stand-up water scooter in the 1960s before licensing his concept to Kawasaki. Kawasaki launched the WSAA and WSAB models in 1973 — flat-bottomed, stand-up machines powered by a small two-stroke engine driving a jet pump, with handlebars like those of a motorcycle and a safety lanyard that cut the engine if the rider fell. Although initial sales were modest, improved models such as the JS440 (1977) and the 650SX built a following, and organised racing began in the late 1970s.

Types and development

Early Jet Skis were stand-up craft ridden solo. In the late 1980s and 1990s the market shifted decisively to larger sit-down models carrying two or three people, produced under rival brands such as Yamaha's WaveRunner and Bombardier's Sea-Doo. Modern personal watercraft are far larger and more powerful than the originals, with four-stroke engines, electronic throttle and braking systems, and outputs exceeding 300 horsepower in the flagship models. Kawasaki continues to sell both sit-down runabouts and a modern stand-up model, the SX-R 1500. Personal watercraft are regulated in many jurisdictions following concerns about noise, emissions and collisions, and are commonly used by lifeguards and law enforcement as rescue craft.
