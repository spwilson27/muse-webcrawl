Razorbill

The razorbill, razor-billed auk, or lesser auk (Alca torda) is a North Atlantic colonial seabird and the only extant member of the genus Alca of the family Alcidae, the auks. It is the closest living relative of the extinct great auk (Pinguinus impennis).

Description

The razorbill has a white belly and a black head, neck, back, and feet during the breeding season. A thin white line extends from the eye to the end of the bill, which is black, deep, and blunt, with white lines across it. Adults measure roughly 37–39 cm in length.

Distribution and habitat

Razorbills breed in subarctic and boreal waters of the Atlantic Ocean, from eastern North America to western Russia. They nest on cliff ledges and rocky islands, laying a single egg. In winter they disperse over open ocean.

Behaviour

Razorbills dive and swim underwater using their wings to catch fish, especially sandeels, sprats, and capelin. They are monogamous and return to the same breeding sites each year.
