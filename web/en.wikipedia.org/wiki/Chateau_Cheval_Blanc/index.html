Château Cheval Blanc (French for "White Horse Castle") is a winery in Saint-Émilion in the Bordeaux wine region of France. Its wine has long been one of only a handful of estates to hold the top Saint-Émilion rank of Premier Grand Cru Classé "A", and it is widely regarded as one of the world's greatest red wines. Unusually for the Right Bank of Bordeaux, Cheval Blanc is built on Cabernet Franc rather than Merlot, giving its wine a distinctive aromatic, floral character and great longevity.

History

The estate's vineyards were assembled in the 1830s and 1850s from land carved out of the neighboring Château Figeac. Under the Ducasse and later Fourcaud-Laussac families, Cheval Blanc won medals at the great Paris and London exhibitions of the 19th century, its 1862 London medal still reproduced on the label. The legendary 1947 vintage, produced in a hot year with primitive cellar equipment, is frequently cited by critics as one of the finest wines ever made. In 1998 the estate was sold to Bernard Arnault and the late Albert Frère, and a striking new winery designed by Christian de Portzamparc opened in 2011.

Vineyard and winemaking

The vineyard sits on the gravel plateau near the border with Pomerol — including a parcel of blue clay similar to that of Pétrus — divided into dozens of plots that are vinified separately in the new chai's concrete vats. The blend varies by vintage but is usually around half Cabernet Franc and half Merlot, with a small amount of Cabernet Sauvignon in some years. A second wine, Le Petit Cheval, has been produced since 1988, and a dry white (Le Petit Cheval Blanc) since the 2010s.

Classification dispute

Following protracted controversy over the Saint-Émilion classification process, Cheval Blanc (along with Château Ausone and Château Angélus) announced in 2021 that it would not apply for the 2022 classification, ending its tenure as a Premier Grand Cru Classé "A" — a decision that shook the Bordeaux establishment, though it did little to diminish the wine's market standing.
