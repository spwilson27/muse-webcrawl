The Wagon Box Fight was an engagement that occurred on August 2, 1867, near Fort Phil Kearny in present-day Wyoming, during Red Cloud's War. A small detachment of United States Army soldiers and civilian woodcutters, protected by a corral of wagon boxes, held off repeated attacks by a much larger force of Lakota Sioux, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors.

Background

Fort Phil Kearny had been established in 1866 along the Bozeman Trail to protect emigrants travelling to Montana goldfields, in defiance of Lakota and Cheyenne claims to the Powder River country. Wood for the fort was cut in the foothills of the Bighorn Mountains several miles away, and woodcutting parties were frequent targets. Eight months earlier, in December 1866, Captain William Fetterman's command of 81 men had been wiped out in the Fetterman Fight, the Army's worst defeat on the northern plains to that date.

The fight

On August 2, 1867, Captain James Powell led a guard detachment of about 31 soldiers accompanying roughly 55 civilian woodcutters. When hundreds of warriors attacked, most of the woodcutters fled to the fort, but Powell and 32 men took shelter inside a defensive corral formed by 14 wagon boxes set in an oval, where they had stored ammunition and supplies.

The defenders were armed with the Army's new Springfield Model 1866 breech-loading rifles, which could be reloaded rapidly while prone and did not betray the firer's position by requiring him to stand. Wave after wave of charges were repelled throughout the morning. The attackers, expecting the slow rate of fire of muzzle-loaders (as at the Fetterman Fight), suffered heavy losses at close range. After several hours, a relief column from the fort arrived with a mountain howitzer and the attackers withdrew.

Aftermath and significance

American casualties were remarkably light: three killed and two wounded. Native losses are disputed, ranging from contemporary army claims of dozens killed to Lakota accounts of far fewer. The fight, together with the similar Hayfield Fight near Fort C. F. Smith the previous day, demonstrated the military impact of breech-loading rifles, but it did not alter the strategic outcome of the war: under the Treaty of Fort Laramie in 1868, the United States abandoned the Bozeman Trail forts, and Red Cloud's coalition achieved its immediate objectives.

The site is now preserved as part of the Fort Phil Kearny State Historic Site in Wyoming.
