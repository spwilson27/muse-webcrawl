Angle of view

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In photography, angle of view (AOV) describes the angular extent of a given scene that is imaged by a camera. It is used interchangeably with the more general term field of view. It is important to distinguish the angle of view from the angle of coverage, which describes the angle range that a lens can image on a given image sensor or film location.

The angle of view of a camera system depends on the focal length of the lens and the size of the film or sensor. A lens with a focal length approximately equal to the diagonal of the film format or sensor is known as a normal lens; its angle of view is roughly 53° and the perspective it produces is similar to that of the human eye. Lenses with shorter focal lengths, called wide-angle lenses, have larger angles of view, while longer focal length (telephoto) lenses have narrower angles of view.

Calculation

For a lens projecting a rectilinear image, the angle of view (α) can be calculated from the chosen dimension (d), and effective focal length (f) as α = 2 arctan(d / 2f). The dimension d may be the vertical, horizontal or diagonal size of the sensor or film. For a 35 mm full-frame camera (24 mm × 36 mm), the diagonal is about 43.3 mm, so a 50 mm normal lens gives a diagonal angle of view of about 46.8°.

Types of lenses

  • Ultra-wide-angle — focal lengths below about 24 mm on full-frame, with diagonal angles of view above 84°.
  • Wide-angle — 24–35 mm, roughly 84°–63°.
  • Normal — around 50 mm, about 46°.
  • Short telephoto — 85–135 mm, roughly 28°–18°.
  • Telephoto and super-telephoto — 200 mm and beyond, angles of view of 12° or less.

Angle of view and crop factor

When a lens is used on a camera with a smaller sensor than the format for which it was designed, the image is cropped and the effective angle of view narrows. This relationship is described by the crop factor, which allows photographers to compute an equivalent focal length on a full-frame camera producing the same field of view.
