The San Diego Chargers were a professional American football team based in San Diego, California, from 1961 to 2016. Founded as the Los Angeles Chargers of the American Football League in 1960, the team moved to San Diego after one season and remained there for 56 years before returning to Los Angeles in 2017 as the Los Angeles Chargers.

History

Under coach Sid Gillman the Chargers were an AFL powerhouse, winning the 1963 AFL championship. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Don Coryell's "Air Coryell" offence, led by quarterback Dan Fouts, set passing records. The 1994 team, led by Junior Seau, reached Super Bowl XXIX, losing to San Francisco. Quarterbacks Drew Brees and Philip Rivers led later playoff teams.

Relocation

After voters rejected a new stadium proposal in 2016, owner Dean Spanos exercised an option to move the team to Los Angeles in January 2017, ending the Chargers' San Diego era.
