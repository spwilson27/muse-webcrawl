Gibson Guitar Corporation

Founded: 1902, Kalamazoo, Michigan

Founder: Orville Gibson

Headquarters: Nashville, Tennessee, US

Products: Guitars, basses, amplifiers

Parent: Gibson Brands, Inc.

Gibson, Inc. (formerly Gibson Guitar Corporation) is an American manufacturer of guitars, other musical instruments, and professional audio equipment based in Nashville, Tennessee. The company was formerly known as Gibson Guitar Corporation and was founded by Orville Gibson in Kalamazoo, Michigan, in 1902. Gibson is one of the most famous and influential guitar makers in the world, known for instruments such as the Les Paul, SG, ES-335, Flying V, and Explorer.

History

Orville Gibson began building mandolins with carved, arched tops in the 1890s and founded the Gibson Mandolin-Guitar Mfg. Co. in 1902. Under Lloyd Loar in the 1920s the company produced the celebrated F-5 mandolin and L-5 guitar, establishing the archtop guitar design. Gibson popularized the archtop jazz guitar, introduced the first commercially successful electric guitar (the ES-150 of 1936), and in 1952 launched the Les Paul solid-body electric, one of the most iconic guitar designs ever made.

After periods of ownership by Chicago Musical Instruments and the Norlin Corporation — during which production moved to Nashville in the 1970s — the company was bought in 1986 by Henry Juszkiewicz and David Berryman, who restored its reputation and profitability. Gibson acquired numerous brands, including Epiphone, Kramer, Steinberger, Dobro, and the audio company KRK. Under Juszkiewicz the company expanded into consumer electronics, acquiring Onkyo, TEAC, and the Philips audio business.

Bankruptcy and relaunch

Burdened by debt from its electronics acquisitions, Gibson Brands filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on May 1, 2018. It emerged in November 2018 under new leadership, with James "JC" Curleigh as CEO and KKR as majority owner, refocusing on its core guitar business.

Instruments

Gibson's flagship instruments include the Les Paul, SG, ES series semi-hollow guitars, Firebird, Flying V, and Explorer, as well as J-45 and Hummingbird acoustic guitars. Its Epiphone subsidiary produces lower-priced versions and its own designs. Gibson's custom shop produces historically faithful reissues of 1950s instruments that are prized by collectors.
