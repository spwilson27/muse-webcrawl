Kösem Sultan

Bornc. 1589, possibly Tinos, Venetian Republic
Died2 September 1651, Constantinople, Ottoman Empire
ConsortSultan Ahmed I
IssueIncluding Murad IV and Ibrahim

Kösem Sultan (c. 1589 – 2 September 1651), also known as Mahpeyker Sultan, was the chief consort (Haseki Sultan) of the Ottoman sultan Ahmed I and one of the most powerful women in Ottoman history. As Valide Sultan under her sons Murad IV and Ibrahim, and regent for her grandson Mehmed IV, she dominated imperial politics for decades during the period known as the Sultanate of Women.

Life

Believed to have been of Greek origin and originally named Anastasia, she entered the imperial harem and became Ahmed I's favourite. After his death in 1617 she retained influence, and when her son Murad IV ascended the throne in 1623 she effectively governed as regent during his minority. Her power continued under the mentally unstable Ibrahim, whom she helped depose in 1648.

Death and legacy

Rivalry with Turhan Sultan, mother of Mehmed IV, culminated in Kösem's murder by strangulation in 1651. She was renowned for her charitable foundations, including mosques, schools, and soup kitchens, and for freeing her slaves after years of service. Historical assessments of her rule remain divided between admiration for her political skill and blame for harem factionalism.
