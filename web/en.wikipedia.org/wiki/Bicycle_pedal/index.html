Bicycle pedal

TypesPlatform, toe-clip, clipless
Thread9/16 inch, reverse-threaded on the left

The bicycle pedal is the part of a bicycle the rider pushes with the foot to turn the cranks and drive the chain. Pedals attach to the crank arms, with the left pedal reverse-threaded so pedalling forces tighten rather than loosen it.

Types

Platform pedals provide a simple flat surface. Toe clips and straps, dominant in mid-20th-century racing, hold the shoe in place. Clipless pedals, introduced in the 1980s and adapted from ski bindings, lock a cleat on the shoe to the pedal and release with a twist of the heel.

History

Pedals appeared on early velocipedes and were standard on the penny-farthing and safety bicycle. Rubber block pedals, rat-trap racing pedals and modern wide mountain-bike platforms reflect successive design priorities.
