Isaac Cline

Isaac Monroe Cline (October 13, 1861 – August 3, 1955) was the chief meteorologist at the Galveston, Texas, office of the U.S. Weather Bureau from 1889 to 1901. He is remembered for his role in the 1900 Galveston hurricane, the deadliest natural disaster in United States history.

Galveston hurricane

When the hurricane struck Galveston on 8 September 1900, Cline's office had downplayed the danger, and Cline himself had written that Galveston's geography made a destructive storm surge essentially impossible. The storm killed an estimated 6,000 to 8,000 people, including Cline's wife; Cline survived by clinging to wreckage with his children. After the disaster he is credited with raising the alarm by riding along the beachfront urging residents to seek high ground, a claim historians have debated.

Later career

Cline transferred to the New Orleans weather office, where he became nationally recognised for accurate forecasts of Mississippi River floods, notably in 1912 and 1927. He published a memoir, Storms, Floods and Sunshine, and is a central figure in Erik Larson's 1999 book Isaac's Storm.
