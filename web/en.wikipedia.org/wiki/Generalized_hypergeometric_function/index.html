Generalized hypergeometric function

In mathematics, a generalized hypergeometric series is a power series in which the ratio of successive coefficients is a rational function of the index. The sum of such a series, when it converges, is a generalized hypergeometric function, denoted pFq, with p numerator parameters and q denominator parameters.

Definition

Formally, pFq(a1,…,ap; b1,…,bq; z) = Σ (a1)n⋯(ap)n / (b1)n⋯(bq)n · zn/n!, where (a)n is the Pochhammer symbol (rising factorial). The series converges for all z when p ≤ q, and for |z| < 1 when p = q + 1.

Special cases

Many common functions are hypergeometric: the ordinary hypergeometric function is 2F1; the confluent hypergeometric function is 1F1; exponentials, Bessel functions, and most classical orthogonal polynomials also have hypergeometric representations. When p = 0 the series gives the exponential function up to scaling.

Identities and applications

Thousands of identities relate hypergeometric functions at special values, including the Chu–Vandermonde identity and Saalschütz's theorem. They arise in probability, statistical distributions, quantum mechanics, and the evaluation of Feynman integrals.
