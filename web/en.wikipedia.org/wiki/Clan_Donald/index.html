Clan Donald, also known as Clan MacDonald (Scottish Gaelic: Clann Dhòmhnaill [ˈkʰl̪ˠaũn̪ˠ ˈɣõː.ɪʎ]), is a Highland Scottish clan and one of the largest Scottish clans. The Lord Lyon King of Arms, the Scottish heraldic authority, does not recognise a chief for the clan as a whole, but several branch chiefs are recognised; the Macdonald of Macdonald is the primus inter pares as High Chief. The clan descends from Dòmhnall Mac Raghnuill, whose father Raghnall mac Somhairle was a son of Somerled, the 12th-century Norse-Gaelic Lord of Argyll.

Lords of the Isles

From the 13th to the late 15th century the chiefs of Clan Donald ruled as Lords of the Isles, a semi-independent maritime lordship embracing the Hebrides and western seaboard, conducting diplomacy with England, Norway and Scotland from their council seat at Finlaggan on Islay. John of Islay was the first to style himself Dominus Insularum. The lordship reached its height under John II, whose Treaty of Westminster-Ardtornish (1462) with Edward IV of England — plotting the partition of Scotland — led eventually to forfeiture: the Lordship of the Isles was annexed by James IV in 1493, and the last rising in its name, under Donald Dubh, ended in 1545.

Later history

Clan Donald branches thereafter played central roles in Highland history: Clanranald and Glengarry fought in the civil wars under Montrose, and the clan was deeply involved in the Jacobite risings — MacDonnell of Glengarry and Clanranald men were "out" in 1715 and 1745, and Flora MacDonald, of the Sleat branch, famously helped Charles Edward Stuart escape after Culloden disguised as her maid. The chiefs' seats include Armadale Castle on Skye, now home to the Clan Donald Centre and Museum of the Isles.

Castles and branches

Clan castles include Dunscaith and Knock on Skye, Dunvegan (of the allied MacLeods), Aros on Mull, and Dunluce in County Antrim, seat of the MacDonnells, Earls of Antrim. The name MacDonald is among the most common Scottish surnames worldwide, spread by the Scottish diaspora.
