Nadaraya–Watson estimator

The Nadaraya–Watson estimator is a nonparametric method for estimating a regression function — the conditional expectation of a random variable given the value of another — without assuming a fixed functional form such as a line or polynomial. It was proposed independently by Elizbar Nadaraya (1964) and Geoffrey Watson (1964) and is the classic example of kernel regression.

Definition

Given data pairs (xi, yi), the estimator of the regression function m(x) = E[Y | X = x] is a locally weighted average:

m̂(x) = Σi Kh(x − xi) yi / Σi Kh(x − xi),

where K is a kernel function (commonly the Gaussian kernel) and h is the bandwidth, which controls the smoothness of the estimate. Points near x receive large weights; distant points receive little or none. Small bandwidths follow the data closely but are noisy (high variance), while large bandwidths are smooth but oversimplified (high bias) — the classic bias–variance trade-off.

Properties and variants

The estimator can be derived using kernel density estimates of the joint and marginal densities of X and Y. It suffers from bias at the boundaries of the data range and in regions where the density of X changes rapidly; local linear (and local polynomial) regression was developed partly to correct these boundary and design biases while retaining the nonparametric flexibility. Bandwidth selection is usually performed by cross-validation.
