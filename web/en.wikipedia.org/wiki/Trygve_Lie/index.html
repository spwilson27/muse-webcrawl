Trygve Halvdan Lie (16 July 1896 – 30 December 1968) was a Norwegian politician, labour leader, government official and author. He served as the first Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1946 to 1952.

Early life and Norwegian career

Born in Kristiania (Oslo), Lie joined the Labour Party, became its national secretary, and served as Minister of Justice, Minister of Trade and Industry, and Minister of Supply. During World War II he was Foreign Minister of the Norwegian government-in-exile in London, and in 1945 led Norway's delegation to the San Francisco Conference that founded the United Nations.

Secretary-General

Chosen as a compromise candidate in 1946, Lie supported the creation of Israel, helped establish UN headquarters in New York, and backed UN intervention in Korea in 1950 — which led the Soviet Union to veto his reappointment. The General Assembly nevertheless extended his term; he resigned in 1952 amid the McCarthy-era investigations of UN staff and was succeeded by Dag Hammarskjöld. His tenure set precedents for an active, political Secretary-Generalship and for the UN's specialized agencies system.

Later life

Lie returned to Norwegian politics as county governor and minister, and died in 1968.
