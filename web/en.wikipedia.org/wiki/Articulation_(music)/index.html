Articulation (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Common articulation marks in musical notation

Articulation is a musical parameter that determines how a single note or other discrete event is sounded. Articulations primarily designate a kind of attack and release of individual notes, particularly for wind and string instruments, and when specified by changes in timbre, that is, changes in the envelope of the note's sound.

Types of articulation

There are many types of articulation, each with a different effect on how the note is played:

  • Staccato – the note is played shortened and detached from the next.
  • Staccatissimo – an even shorter, more forceful staccato, often shown by a wedge.
  • Legato – notes are played smoothly and connected, often indicated by a slur.
  • Tenuto – the note is held for its full value, marked with a line above or below the notehead.
  • Marcato – the note is played with a strong accent, marked with a wedge or caret.
  • Accent – the note is emphasized with a stronger attack.
  • Portato (mezzo-staccato) – a combination of legato and staccato, often shown by dots under a slur.

Notation

Specific symbols such as dots, wedges, slurs, and horizontal lines are placed above or below the notehead. Their exact interpretation varies by period and instrument; Baroque and Classical articulation was often left to the performer's judgement, while modern notation is more prescriptive.
