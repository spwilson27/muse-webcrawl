Quantitative trait locus

A quantitative trait locus (QTL) is a locus (section of DNA) that correlates with variation of a quantitative trait in the phenotype of a population of organisms. QTLs are mapped by identifying which molecular markers (such as SNPs or AFLPs) correlate with an observed trait. This is often an early step in identifying the actual genes underlying trait variation.

Definition

A quantitative trait is one whose phenotype shows continuous variation — such as height, weight, yield, or disease susceptibility — typically influenced by many genes (polygenic effects) together with the environment. A QTL is a genomic region containing one or more of the genes contributing to that variation.

Mapping

QTL mapping commonly uses experimental crosses (in plants and animals) or family-based and population-based studies (in humans). Statistical methods such as interval mapping regress trait values on marker genotypes; a significant association indicates a QTL near the marker, with effect size estimated as the proportion of phenotypic variance explained. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) are a related approach for detecting loci associated with traits in populations.

Applications

QTL analysis is used in agriculture for marker-assisted selection in breeding, in animal husbandry, and in medicine to locate genomic regions contributing to complex diseases, though moving from a QTL interval to the causal gene and variant remains challenging.
