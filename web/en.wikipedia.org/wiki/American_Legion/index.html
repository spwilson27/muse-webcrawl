American Legion

The American Legion, commonly known as the Legion, is a nonprofit organisation of United States war veterans headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana. Founded in 1919 by members of the American Expeditionary Forces and chartered by Congress that year, it is one of the largest veterans service organisations in the United States, with roughly 1.6 million members organised into local posts. Its motto is "Veterans Strengthening America".

History

The Legion was founded at a caucus in Paris in March 1919 by officers including Theodore Roosevelt Jr., concerned with troop morale and the welfare of returning servicemen. It quickly became a political force, lobbying successfully for the creation of the Veterans Bureau (forerunner of the Department of Veterans Affairs) and for veterans' bonuses. Legion members drafted the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944 — the G.I. Bill — which transformed post-war American education and home ownership.

Programs

The Legion sponsors American Legion Baseball (founded 1925), Boys State and Girls State civics programs, the National High School Oratorical Contest, Junior ROTC support, scholarships, and flag-education and veterans' assistance programs. Its poppy program supports disabled veterans around Memorial Day.

Positions

Legion policy, set by resolutions at national conventions, traditionally emphasises a strong national defence, veterans' benefits, "Americanism" (including flag protection amendments), and support for youth programs. Membership is open to anyone who has served in the U.S. Armed Forces during a period of war or designated conflict, and since 2019 to all who have served since December 1941.
