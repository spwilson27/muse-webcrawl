Flaxseed, also known as linseed, is the seed of the flax plant (Linum usitatissimum). It is consumed as a food and used as a source of linseed oil. Flaxseeds are small, brown or golden-yellow, and are one of the richest plant sources of the omega-3 fatty acid alpha-linolenic acid (ALA).

History

Flax was cultivated in the Fertile Crescent from around 5000 BCE. Seeds were eaten in ancient Egypt and Greece, and Charlemagne required his subjects to consume flaxseed for health in the eighth century CE.

Nutrition

Flaxseed is rich in dietary fibre, protein and ALA. It is the leading dietary source of lignans, particularly secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG). Ground flaxseed is generally preferred nutritionally, since whole seeds may pass through the gut undigested.

Uses

Flaxseed is added to baked goods, cereals and smoothies, used as an egg substitute in vegan baking, and pressed for linseed oil, which is used in paints, varnishes and wood finishes as well as in nutrition supplements.
