Payment for order flow

Payment for order flow (PFOF) is the compensation a broker receives for routing its customers' orders to a particular market maker or exchange for execution. It became a widespread practice in United States equity and options markets and underpins the business model of commission-free retail brokerages.

How it works

When a retail investor places an order through a broker, the broker may send it to a wholesale market maker, which pays the broker a small fee per share and executes the trade, often at a price slightly better than the public quote (price improvement).

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue PFOF creates a conflict of interest, since brokers may route orders to whoever pays most rather than whoever executes best, and that the true costs are hidden in execution quality. The practice is banned in some jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom and Canada, and has been repeatedly reviewed by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, which requires disclosure under rules such as Regulation NMS Rule 606.
