Pulitzer Prize for Poetry

Pulitzer Prize for Poetry
First awarded: 1919 (special prizes 1918)
Administered by: Columbia University
For: Distinguished volume of original verse by an American author

The Pulitzer Prize for Poetry is one of the seven American Pulitzer Prizes awarded annually for Letters, Drama, and Music. It has been presented since 1922 for a distinguished volume of original verse by an American author, preceded by special prizes in 1918 and 1919 funded by the Poetry Society.

History

The first regular winner was Edwin Arlington Robinson (1922), who won three times, as did Robert Frost (four times in total including poetry awards). Multiple winners include Stephen Dunn, Mary Oliver, and others; repeat honorees also include Robert Lowell, Sylvia Plath (posthumously, for The Collected Poems, 1982), and Gwendolyn Brooks, in 1950 the first African American to win a Pulitzer in any category.

Recent decades

Notable later recipients include Tracy K. Smith for Life on Mars (2012), Natasha Trethewey for Native Guard (2007), Jericho Brown for The Tradition (2020), and Diane Seuss for frank: sonnets (2022). The prize carries a cash award of $15,000 (in recent years).
