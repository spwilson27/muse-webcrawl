The Potsdam Agreement (German: Potsdamer Abkommen) was the agreement among the three main Allies of World War II — the United States, the United Kingdom and the Soviet Union — reached at the end of the Potsdam Conference on 2 August 1945. It dealt primarily with the administration of Germany (then formally styled "Germany as a whole") following the unconditional surrender of Nazi Germany on 8 May 1945. It was not a peace treaty, although it created accomplished facts; the final German settlement awaited the Treaty on the Final Settlement with Respect to Germany in 1990.

Participants and background

The conference, codenamed Terminal, met from 17 July to 2 August 1945 at Cecilienhof Palace, Potsdam. The delegations were led by President Harry S. Truman (who had succeeded Franklin D. Roosevelt), Joseph Stalin, and Winston Churchill — replaced partway through by Clement Attlee after Churchill lost the British general election. France, though it received an occupation zone, was not invited.

Provisions

Among its principal decisions, the agreement:

  • Established the Allied Control Council as the supreme authority in Germany, with Germany administered in four occupation zones (American, British, Soviet and French).
  • Proclaimed the aims of demilitarisation, denazification, democratisation, decentralisation and decartelisation of Germany (the "four Ds"; reparations and dismantling were added to the programme).
  • Set reparations: the USSR would take reparations from its own zone and a share of industrial equipment from the western zones.
  • Placed the German territories east of the Oder–Neisse line under Polish (and, in northern East Prussia, Soviet) administration pending a final peace settlement — in practice annexed by Poland and the USSR, with Königsberg becoming Kaliningrad.
  • Provided for the "orderly and humane" transfer to Germany of the German populations of Poland, Czechoslovakia and Hungary — sanctioning expulsions that in practice involved 12–14 million people and great suffering.
  • Assigned prosecutions of major war criminals (leading to the Nuremberg trials) and abolished Nazi laws and organisations.

Aftermath

Implementation quickly broke down amid the emerging Cold War: the western zones were merged economically (Bizonia, then Trizonia) and became the Federal Republic of Germany in 1949, while the Soviet zone became the German Democratic Republic. The Oder–Neisse border was recognised by West Germany in 1970 and definitively confirmed in 1990. The Potsdam Declaration of 26 July 1945 — a separate document issued during the conference by the US, UK and China demanding Japan's surrender — should not be confused with the Potsdam Agreement.
