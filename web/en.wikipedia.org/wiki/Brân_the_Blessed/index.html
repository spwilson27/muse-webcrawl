Brân the Blessed

Welsh: Bendigeidfran
Parents: Llŷr and Penarddun
Siblings: Branwen, Manawydan
Title: King of Britain

Brân the Blessed (Welsh: Bendigeidfran, "Blessed Crow/Raven") is a giant and king of Britain in Welsh mythology, central to the Second Branch of the Mabinogi, Branwen ferch Llŷr.

Story

Brân gave his sister Branwen in marriage to Matholwch, king of Ireland, but after his half-brother Efnisien insulted the Irish, Branwen was mistreated, prompting Brân to cross the Irish Sea — wading himself while his fleet sailed — to rescue her. War devastated both kingdoms. Brân, wounded in the foot by a poisoned spear, ordered his surviving seven companions to cut off his head and carry it to London.

The Blessed Head

Brân's head remained alive, entertaining his men for years at Harlech and on Gwales, before being buried at the White Hill in London (associated with Tower Hill) facing France as a talisman protecting Britain from invasion — a protection King Arthur is said to have foolishly removed. Brân is also linked to a magical cauldron of rebirth given to Matholwch. See Branwen.
