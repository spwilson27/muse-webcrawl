Minyan

In Judaism, a minyan (Hebrew: מניין, lit. "count" or "number"; plural minyanim) is the quorum of ten Jewish adults required for certain religious obligations. In traditional (Orthodox) practice, the ten must be men who have reached the age of bar mitzvah (13 years). Most Conservative congregations, and some Reform and Reconstructionist communities, count women as well. A minyan is traditionally required for public prayer services.

Source and derivation

The requirement of ten is derived by the rabbis of the Talmud through exegesis. In tractate Megillah and Sanhedrin, the word "congregation" (edah) in verses such as Numbers 14:27 ("How long shall I bear with this evil congregation?") is linked to the ten spies sent by Moses, establishing that a "congregation" consists of ten. Another derivation draws on Genesis 18, Abraham's bargaining over Sodom, and on the phrase "I will be sanctified in the midst of the children of Israel" (Leviticus 22:32), read to require a communal setting for sanctification.

Prayers requiring a minyan

The following elements of the service may only be recited in the presence of a minyan:

  • Barekhu, the call to prayer
  • Kaddish, in all its forms
  • Kedushah, the sanctification during the repetition of the Amidah
  • The public reading of the Torah and the haftarah with their blessings
  • The priestly blessing (Birkat Kohanim)
  • The seven blessings of a wedding (Sheva Brachot) and certain mourning rites

Modern practice

Outside the synagogue, Jews traditionally organise minyanim at houses of mourning (shiva minyanim). In the 20th and 21st centuries many non-Orthodox movements adopted egalitarian minyanim counting women; the Conservative movement's Committee on Jewish Law and Standards approved counting women in 1973. Independent egalitarian minyanim and partnership minyanim have further diversified practice.
