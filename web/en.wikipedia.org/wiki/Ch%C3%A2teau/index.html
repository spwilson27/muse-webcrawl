Château

A château (plural châteaux) is a manor house or residence of the lord of a manor, or a fine country house of nobility or gentry, with or without fortifications, originally — and still most frequently — found in French-speaking regions. The word derives from the Latin castellum.

Meaning and usage

Originally denoting a fortified castle, the term came to be applied to the grand, unfortified country houses built by the French aristocracy from the Renaissance onward, such as the châteaux of the Loire Valley — Chambord, Chenonceau, and Blois. In the Bordeaux wine region, château is used for a wine-producing estate, a usage popularised worldwide; the word also appears in the names of grand hotels and apartment houses far beyond France.

Notable examples

The Palace of Versailles began as a château, while landmarks such as the Château de Chantilly, Vaux-le-Vicomte, and Fontainebleau illustrate the type's evolution from medieval fortress to classical residence set in formal gardens.
