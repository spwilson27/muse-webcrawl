Graubünden (German: Graubünden; Romansh: Grischun; Italian: Grigioni) is the largest and easternmost canton of Switzerland. Mountainous and sparsely populated, it is the only officially trilingual canton, where German, Romansh, and Italian are all official languages.

Geography

The canton encompasses the upper valleys of the Rhine and the Inn, numerous Alpine passes including the Julier, Bernina, and San Bernardino, and resorts such as Davos, St. Moritz, and Arosa. The Swiss National Park lies in the Engadin valley.

History

The region corresponds to the ancient Roman province of Raetia. In the late Middle Ages its valley communities formed three leagues — the God's House League, the League of the Ten Jurisdictions, and the Grey League (from which the canton takes its name) — which joined the Swiss Confederacy as an associate in 1497/98 and became a full canton in 1803.
