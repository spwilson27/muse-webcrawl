French Navy

The aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle, flagship of the French Navy.

The French Navy (French: Marine nationale), informally "La Royale", is the maritime arm of the French Armed Forces. Founded in 1624, it is one of the oldest naval services in continuous existence. With roughly 37,000 personnel and some 180 vessels, it is among the world's major navies and, together with the British Royal Navy, one of only two European navies operating catapult-equipped aircraft carriers and a sea-based nuclear deterrent.

Fleet

The fleet is centred on the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle, three Mistral-class amphibious assault ships, Horizon- and Aquitaine-class (FREMM) frigates and destroyers, and a submarine force comprising four Triomphant-class ballistic missile submarines — which carry France's submarine-launched nuclear deterrent — and Rubis/Barracuda-class attack submarines. Naval aviation flies the Rafale M fighter from the carrier.

Roles

France has the world's largest exclusive economic zone, owing to its overseas territories, and the Navy patrols it from home bases at Toulon, Brest, and Cherbourg and from bases overseas. It conducts deterrence, power projection, sea control, and maritime security operations, and has fought in conflicts from the American War of Independence to modern operations in the Mediterranean, the Indian Ocean, and the Red Sea.
