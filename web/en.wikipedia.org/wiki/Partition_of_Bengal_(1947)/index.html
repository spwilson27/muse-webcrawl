Partition of Bengal (1947)

DateAugust 1947
ResultWest Bengal (India); East Bengal (Pakistan)
BoundaryRadcliffe Line

The Partition of Bengal in 1947, part of the Partition of India, divided the British Indian province of Bengal between the Dominion of India and the Dominion of Pakistan. The Hindu-majority western part became West Bengal, a state of India, while the Muslim-majority eastern part became East Bengal — later renamed East Pakistan — and eventually the independent nation of Bangladesh after the Liberation War of 1971.

Background and vote

Under the Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947, the Bengal Legislative Assembly voted in June 1947 on whether a united Bengal should join India or Pakistan, or be partitioned. The assembly's two halves, voting separately by majority-Hindu and majority-Muslim districts, both opted for partition. A referendum in Sylhet district chose accession to Pakistan. The boundary was drawn by the commission chaired by Cyril Radcliffe and published on 17 August 1947, two days after independence.

Consequences

Partition triggered one of the largest migrations in history: millions of Hindus moved west and millions of Muslims moved east over the following years, in waves punctuated by communal violence in 1947, 1950 and 1964. Unlike Punjab, Bengal's migration was prolonged rather than a single exchange. Disputes over enclaves along the border were largely resolved by the India–Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement of 2015. The earlier partition of 1905 had been annulled in 1911.
