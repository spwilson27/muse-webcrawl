Piano four hands

Piano four hands (French: piano à quatre mains; German: zu vier Händen) is a type of piano duet in which two pianists play at one piano, each using both hands. The player on the right, playing the upper part, is traditionally called primo, and the player on the left, playing the lower part, secondo.

Two pianists sharing one keyboard.

History and repertoire

The genre flourished in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when the piano became the centre of domestic music-making. Before recordings, four-hand arrangements were the principal way many people learned symphonies, operas and chamber works at home, and publishers issued arrangements of nearly everything. Original works include sonatas and pieces by Mozart, Schubert — whose Fantasia in F minor, D. 940, is among the most celebrated duets — Beethoven, Schumann, Brahms (including the Hungarian Dances), Dvořák (Slavonic Dances), Fauré (Dolly Suite), Debussy and Ravel (Ma mère l'Oye).

Arrangements and teaching

Four-hand playing remains a staple of piano teaching, letting a beginner play musically rich textures alongside a teacher. Orchestral rehearsals and the study of scores still use four-hand reductions. A distinct, smaller literature exists for two pianos, and for one piano six hands.
