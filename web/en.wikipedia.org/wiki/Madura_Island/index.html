Madura Island (Indonesian: Pulau Madura) is an Indonesian island off the northeastern coast of Java. It is part of the province of East Java and is separated from Java by the narrow Madura Strait. The island is roughly 160 km long and 35 km wide, and together with its surrounding smaller islands forms a group of four regencies: Bangkalan, Sampang, Pamekasan and Sumenep.

Geography and economy

Madura is relatively dry and hilly, with limestone soils less fertile than volcanic Java. Traditional industries include salt production in extensive coastal evaporation ponds, fishing, tobacco and cattle raising; the island is famous throughout Indonesia for its bull racing, karapan sapi. Offshore oil and gas fields in the surrounding waters contribute to the regional economy.

People and culture

The Madurese people, speakers of the Madurese language, are one of Indonesia's larger ethnic groups, with a large diaspora in East Java itself. Since 2009 the island has been connected to Surabaya by the Suramadu Bridge, the longest bridge in Indonesia, which has spurred development on the western end of the island.
