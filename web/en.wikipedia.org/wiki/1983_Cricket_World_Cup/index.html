The 1983 Cricket World Cup (officially the Prudential Cup '83) was the third edition of the Cricket World Cup, held in England from 9 to 25 June 1983. It was won by India, who defeated the two-time defending champions, the West Indies, by 43 runs in the final at Lord's — one of the greatest upsets in the history of the sport.

Kapil Dev's India, rated 66–1 outsiders before the tournament, won the World Cup at Lord's.

Tournament summary

Eight teams took part: the seven Test-playing nations and Zimbabwe, who had won the ICC Trophy. The teams were divided into two groups of four, each team playing the others twice, with the top two in each group advancing to the semi-finals. Matches were played over 60 overs per side with a red ball and in traditional white clothing.

India's campaign was transformed in their group match against Zimbabwe at Tunbridge Wells, where captain Kapil Dev, coming in with India at 17 for 5, scored an unbeaten 175 — then a World Cup record — in a match that was not broadcast because of a BBC strike. India beat England in the semi-final at Old Trafford, while the West Indies defeated Pakistan at The Oval.

Final

On 25 June 1983, India were bowled out for just 183, Kris Srikkanth top-scoring with 38. The West Indies, chasing a third consecutive title, were reduced to 76 for 6 by India's medium-pacers; Mohinder Amarnath took 3 for 12 and Madan Lal 3 for 31. The turning point came when Kapil Dev ran some 20 yards to catch Viv Richards off Madan Lal. The West Indies were dismissed for 140, and Amarnath was named man of the match. India's victory transformed cricket in the country, popularising the one-day game and shifting the sport's centre of gravity towards South Asia.
