L94A1 chain gun

The L94A1 is a British 7.62×51mm NATO chain gun used as a coaxial weapon on armoured fighting vehicles of the British Army. It is a version of the Hughes EX-34 chain gun, produced in the United Kingdom by Heckler & Koch's Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield (later by Manroy Engineering).

Design

Unlike conventional gas- or recoil-operated machine guns, the L94A1's action is powered by an electric motor driving a roller chain that cycles the bolt. This makes the weapon's operation independent of the ammunition's gas pressure, so a dud round does not stop the gun: the faulty cartridge is simply ejected as the chain continues to cycle. The weapon fires at a cyclic rate of roughly 520–550 rounds per minute and is fed by a disintegrating-link belt.

Service

The L94A1 is mounted as the coaxial secondary armament in the turrets of the Challenger 2 main battle tank and was fitted to the Warrior infantry fighting vehicle (in its turret before replacement programmes), as well as the FV107 Scimitar in earlier service. Its long barrel (937 mm) gives a higher muzzle velocity than the general-purpose L37A2 and L8 variants it complemented.

Characteristics

The gun weighs about 17.9 kg and has an effective range of around 1,400 metres. A safety lever locks the bolt assembly, and stoppages are rare due to the externally powered action.
