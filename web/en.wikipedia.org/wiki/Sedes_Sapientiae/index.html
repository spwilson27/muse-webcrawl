Sedes Sapientiae

Sedes Sapientiae, Latin for "Seat of Wisdom" or "Throne of Wisdom", is one of many devotional titles for Mary, mother of Jesus, in Roman Catholic tradition. In this iconographic type, the Virgin is shown seated frontally on a throne, holding the Christ Child on her lap; Mary functions both as mother and as the throne for Christ, who embodies divine Wisdom.

Iconography

The image derives in part from Byzantine representations of the Theotokos and from the biblical description of the throne of Solomon, read as a type of the Virgin. The Sedes Sapientiae became especially characteristic of Romanesque sculpture between about 1100 and 1250, when numerous wooden statues — often polychromed and sometimes serving as reliquaries — were produced in regions such as the Auvergne in France and Catalonia in Spain. Both figures typically face the viewer in a hieratic, symmetrical pose, in contrast to the more tender and naturalistic Madonnas of the Gothic period that followed.

Theological meaning

The title appears in the Litany of Loreto, approved in 1587. In the invocation, Mary's lap is understood as the seat upon which Wisdom — identified with Christ, the Wisdom of God — rests, presenting her as the vessel and throne of divine wisdom rather than its source.

Use as a name

"Seat of Wisdom" is the motto or name of several institutions, including the Catholic University of Leuven, whose emblem depicts the Sedes Sapientiae, and numerous schools, seminaries, and parishes dedicated to Our Lady Seat of Wisdom.
