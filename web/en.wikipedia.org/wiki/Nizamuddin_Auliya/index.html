Muhammad Nizamuddin Auliya (1238 – 3 April 1325), also known as Hazrat Nizamuddin, was an Indian Sunni Muslim scholar and Sufi saint of the Chishti Order — one of the most famous Sufis in the Indian subcontinent, whose influence on the spread and character of Islam in South Asia was profound. His original name was Muhammad bin Ahmad bin Bukhari; the title "Auliya" means "saint(s)".

Life

Born in Badayun (now Uttar Pradesh), Nizamuddin was orphaned at five and moved with his mother to Delhi, where he studied hadith and fiqh. At twenty he became a disciple of the Chishti master Baba Farid in Ajodhan (Pakpattan, in present-day Pakistan), who designated him his successor. Settling in Ghiyaspur, a village on the edge of Delhi (now the Nizamuddin neighbourhood), he built a khanqah that drew thousands daily, feeding all comers from its ever-open langar kitchen.

Teaching and court relations

Nizamuddin emphasised love of God manifested as service to humanity, renunciation of worldly power, and tolerance; his discourses were recorded by the poet Amir Hasan Sijzi in the Fawaid al-Fuad, a classic of Persian Sufi literature. He famously kept his distance from sultans: when Ala-ud-Din's court and later Ghiyasuddin Tughluq pressured him, tradition records his defiant reply about the Delhi court — "Hunuz Dilli dur ast" ("Delhi is still far away") — before the sultan's death en route home. His disciples included the poet Amir Khusrow, who is buried near him, and Nasiruddin Chiragh Dehlavi.

Legacy

His tomb (dargah) in Delhi remains one of South Asia's most visited shrines, drawing Muslims, Hindus and others alike, with Thursday evening qawwali sessions continuing the musical tradition associated with Khusrow. The surrounding basti, the Hazrat Nizamuddin railway station and the Auliya's Urs festival commemorate him.
