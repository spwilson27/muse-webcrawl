Lego (styled LEGO) is a line of plastic construction toys manufactured by the Lego Group, a privately held company based in Billund, Denmark. The system consists primarily of interlocking plastic bricks of various colours, accompanied by an array of gears, figurines called minifigures, and other parts, which can be assembled and connected in many ways to construct objects such as vehicles, buildings and working robots. Anything constructed can be taken apart again and the pieces reused. The name derives from the Danish phrase leg godt, "play well."

History

The company was founded in 1932 by carpenter Ole Kirk Christiansen as a maker of wooden toys, adopting the name Lego in 1934. After the Second World War it moved into plastic, producing "Automatic Binding Bricks" from 1949, adapted from the self-locking bricks of British inventor Hilary Page. In 1958 Ole's son Godtfred Kirk Christiansen patented the modern brick design — hollow tubes underneath that grip the studs above — establishing the "stud-and-tube" coupling that has remained compatible ever since: bricks made today fit bricks from 1958. Duplo (larger bricks for toddlers) followed in 1969, and the minifigure in 1978.

Themed sets expanded from generic "Town," "Castle" and "Space" lines (1978–79) to licensed properties, beginning with Lego Star Wars and Lego Harry Potter in 1999. In the early 2000s, after over-expansion, the company came close to bankruptcy (2003–04) before refocusing on the core brick under CEO Jørgen Vig Knudstorp. It subsequently became the world's largest toy company by revenue, extending into the LEGOLAND parks, video games, and films including The Lego Movie (2014).

The brick

Lego bricks are injection-moulded in ABS plastic to a tolerance of about two micrometres, so that parts hold together with reliable "clutch power" yet separate easily. A standard 2×4 brick measures 31.8 × 15.8 mm, with studs 4.8 mm in diameter on an 8 mm grid and a height of 9.6 mm. Six 2×4 bricks of the same colour can be combined in more than 915 million ways. The range includes tens of thousands of distinct elements; Technic parts add beams, pins and gears for mechanical models, and platforms such as Mindstorms and SPIKE have added programmable robotics.

Themes and culture

Major current and historic themes include City, Ninjago, Friends, Technic, Icons, Ideas (fan-designed sets), Architecture, and licences from Star Wars, Marvel, DC, Harry Potter and others. Bionicle (2001–2010) is credited with helping save the company financially. An extensive adult fan community (AFOLs), fan conventions, and user groups surround the hobby; Lego creations are used in prototyping, art, education (Lego Education) and stop-motion "brickfilms."
