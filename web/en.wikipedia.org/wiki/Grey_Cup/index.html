Grey Cup

The Grey Cup (French: Coupe Grey) is both the championship game of the Canadian Football League (CFL) and the name of the trophy awarded to the winning team. Played each November, it is one of Canada's largest annual sporting and television events, typically accompanied by a week of festivals, concerts and the Grey Cup parade, and a halftime show.

History

The trophy was commissioned in 1909 by Albert Grey, 4th Earl Grey, then Governor General of Canada, originally for the amateur rugby football championship of Canada. The first game was won by the University of Toronto Varsity Blues. Over time professional teams came to dominate, and in 1958 the newly formed CFL took exclusive control of the competition. Memorable championship games include the 1950 "Mud Bowl", the 1962 "Fog Bowl" — postponed partway through because of fog on the field — and the 1989 game, often called the greatest Grey Cup ever played, in which Saskatchewan defeated Hamilton 43–40. The Toronto Argonauts have won the most Grey Cups. Three American-based teams competed for the cup during the CFL's brief US expansion in the 1990s, and the Baltimore Stallions won it in 1995, the only non-Canadian champion.
