Pandora, also known as Pandora Radio, is an American subscription-based music streaming service centred on personalized internet radio "stations." It grew out of the Music Genome Project, an effort to classify songs by hundreds of musical attributes — melody, harmony, rhythm, instrumentation, vocal character, and more — so that a listener who names a song or artist receives a stream of music with similar characteristics.

History

The company was founded in 2000 by Will Glaser, Jon Kraft, and Tim Westergren as Savage Beast Technologies; its consumer service, Pandora Radio, launched in 2005. Listeners refine stations with "thumbs up" and "thumbs down" feedback. Pandora operated under webcasting royalty regimes set by the U.S. Copyright Royalty Board, whose 2007 rate decision nearly bankrupted the service and spurred the Internet Radio Equality movement. The company went public in 2011 (NYSE: P). In 2017 it launched Pandora Premium, an on-demand tier built partly on assets of the defunct Rdio service. In 2019, Sirius XM completed its acquisition of Pandora in an all-stock deal valued at about $3.5 billion.

Service and availability

Pandora offers advertising-supported free radio, a mid-tier Plus subscription, and Premium on-demand streaming. Because of licensing restrictions, the service has long been available only in the United States (earlier versions served Australia and New Zealand until 2017). At its peak, Pandora was the largest internet radio service in the U.S., and it remains among the most-used audio apps even as on-demand rivals such as Spotify and Apple Music have grown.
