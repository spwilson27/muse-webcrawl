Principia Ethica

Principia Ethica is a 1903 book by the British philosopher G. E. Moore, one of the most influential works of moral philosophy of the twentieth century. In it, Moore argues that the concept of goodness is simple, indefinable, and non-natural.

Summary

Moore's central claim is that attempts to define "good" in terms of any natural property — such as pleasure, desire, or evolution — commit what he calls the naturalistic fallacy. His "open question argument" holds that for any proposed naturalistic definition of good, it remains an open, significant question whether things with that property are actually good, showing the definition must fail.

Moore regards good as a simple quality, like the colour yellow, which can be perceived directly but not analysed. In ethical theory he defends a form of ideal consequentialism: right action is that which produces the most good, and the greatest goods, in his view, are the pleasures of human intercourse and the appreciation of beauty — personal affection and aesthetic enjoyment.

Influence

The book marked a turning point for analytic philosophy and ethics, shifting attention toward the analysis of moral language. It deeply influenced the Bloomsbury Group, including John Maynard Keynes, Lytton Strachey, and Virginia Woolf, and helped launch metaethics as a distinct field of inquiry.
