Epping

Epping is a market town and civil parish in the Epping Forest District of Essex, England. It lies at the northern end of Epping Forest, about 17 miles (27 km) north-east of central London, and is the terminus of the London Underground's Central line, which connects the town to the capital.

History

A settlement named Epping appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Epinga. The town grew along the old road from London to Norwich and Cambridge as a coaching stop and market centre; its market charter dates to 1253. The arrival of the railway in 1865, later taken into the Underground network, turned Epping into a commuter town while its surroundings remained protected by the Epping Forest Act 1878 and metropolitan green belt policies.

The town today

Epping High Street retains a number of historic inns and listed buildings, and a street market is held on Mondays. The parish church of St John the Baptist, with its prominent tower, was rebuilt in the nineteenth century. To the south lie the ancient woodland of Epping Forest, managed by the City of London Corporation, and the Queen Elizabeth's Hunting Lodge museum at nearby Chingford.

Transport and governance

Besides the Central line terminus at Epping station, the town is close to junction 26 of the M25 and junction 7 of the M11 motorways. Epping is administered by Epping Town Council, the Epping Forest District Council and Essex County Council, and forms part of the Epping Forest parliamentary constituency, represented for many years by Eleanor Laing.
