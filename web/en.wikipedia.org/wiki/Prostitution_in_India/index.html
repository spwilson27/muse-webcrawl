Prostitution in India

Prostitution in India occupies a complex legal position: the exchange of sex for money by itself is not expressly illegal, but a string of activities around it—including running a brothel, soliciting in public, pimping, and trafficking—are criminal offences under the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956. India has one of the largest sex-work populations in the world, with major red-light districts including Sonagachi in Kolkata, Kamathipura in Mumbai, and G. B. Road in Delhi.

History

Prostitution has a long documented history in the subcontinent, from courtesan traditions and temple devadasi practices to regulation under British colonial rule through cantonment acts and Contagious Diseases Acts. After independence, India signed the 1949 UN convention against trafficking and enacted the Suppression of Immoral Traffic in Women and Girls Act in 1956, later amended and renamed.

Legal and social issues

Debates in India concern legalization versus abolition, the rights and organization of sex workers (collectives such as the Durbar Mahila Samanwaya Committee in Kolkata), HIV/AIDS prevention programs that work with sex-worker communities, and human trafficking for sexual exploitation. In 2022 the Supreme Court of India recognized sex work as a profession and directed that sex workers be treated with dignity and protected from police abuse.
