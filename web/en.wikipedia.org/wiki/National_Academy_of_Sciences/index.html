The National Academy of Sciences (NAS) is a United States nonprofit, non-governmental organisation and national academy composed of scientists elected by their peers for distinguished achievements in research. Founded by an Act of Congress signed by President Abraham Lincoln on March 3, 1863, during the Civil War, the Academy is charged with providing independent, objective advice to the nation on matters related to science and technology.

Role and structure

The NAS is one of three academies comprising the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, alongside the National Academy of Engineering (founded 1964) and the National Academy of Medicine (founded 1970 as the Institute of Medicine). Through the National Research Council and consensus study committees, the academies produce influential reports advising the federal government; scientists serve pro bono.

Members are elected for life; election to the Academy is considered one of the highest honours in American science. Among its members and foreign associates have been roughly 190 Nobel laureates.

Publications and building

The Academy publishes the multidisciplinary journal Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), founded in 1914, one of the most cited scientific journals in the world. Its headquarters building on Constitution Avenue in Washington, D.C. (opened 1924) features a Great Hall dominated by a statue of Albert Einstein outside — the Albert Einstein Memorial, a popular visitor attraction.
