The 2012 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXX Olympiad and commonly known as London 2012, were held in London, United Kingdom, from 27 July to 12 August 2012. London became the first city to host the modern Olympic Games three times, having previously hosted in 1908 and 1948.

Bid and preparation

London was elected host city on 6 July 2005 at the 117th IOC Session in Singapore, defeating Paris in the final round of voting. A new Olympic Park was built in Stratford, East London, alongside extensive transport improvements.

The Games

The opening ceremony, directed by Danny Boyle and titled "Isles of Wonder", was widely praised. Some 10,768 athletes from 204 National Olympic Committees competed in 302 events. Women's boxing was included for the first time, meaning every sport had female competitors. Notable performers included swimmer Michael Phelps, who became the most decorated Olympian of all time, and sprinter Usain Bolt, who retained his 100 m, 200 m and 4×100 m relay titles. British highlights included Mo Farah's distance double and a record haul on "Super Saturday".

Legacy

The Games were widely regarded as a success, and the Olympic Park was redeveloped as the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park. The following Games were held in Rio de Janeiro in 2016.
