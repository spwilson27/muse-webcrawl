River Dee, Aberdeenshire

The River Dee (Scottish Gaelic: Uisge Dhè) is a river in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, flowing about 140 km (87 miles) from its source in the Cairngorms — among the highest river sources in Britain, on the Braeriach plateau — eastwards to the North Sea at Aberdeen.

Royal Deeside and salmon fishing

Its valley, Deeside, is famed as "Royal Deeside" because Balmoral Castle, the Scottish residence of the royal family, stands on its banks near Ballater; the river also passes Braemar, Aboyne, and Banchory. The Dee is one of Scotland's classic Atlantic salmon rivers, internationally renowned for spring salmon fly-fishing, and its catchment is protected as a Special Area of Conservation for salmon, otters, and freshwater pearl mussels.

At its mouth the river flows through Aberdeen harbour. The Linn of Dee, a narrow chasm near Braemar, is a celebrated beauty spot opened as a viewpoint by Queen Victoria.
