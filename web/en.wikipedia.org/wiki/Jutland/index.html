Jutland

Peninsula
Danish: Jylland
Countries: Denmark; Germany (southern tip area)
Largest city: Aarhus
Area: about 29,775 km² (Danish part)

Jutland (Danish: Jylland) is a peninsula of Northern Europe that forms the continental portion of Denmark together with the northernmost part of Germany. It is bounded by the North Sea to the west, the Skagerrak to the north, and the Kattegat and Baltic Sea to the east, and extends south to the river Eider and the Kiel Canal area.

Geography

The Danish part of Jutland covers about 29,775 square kilometres and holds roughly 2.6 million inhabitants. Its landscape of low hills, heathland and farmland was shaped by Ice Age glaciation; the west coast is characterised by dunes and lagoons, while the east has fjords such as Limfjorden, which separates the island of Vendsyssel-Thy from the rest of the peninsula. The largest cities are Aarhus, Aalborg, Esbjerg, Randers, Horsens and Vejle.

History

Jutland takes its name from the Jutes, a Germanic people who, with the Angles and Saxons, settled in Britain in the early Middle Ages. Historically divided into North Jutland and South Jutland (Southern Jutland was contested with Germany and divided by plebiscite in 1920), the peninsula has been the agricultural heartland of Denmark and the centre of its dairy and pork industries.
