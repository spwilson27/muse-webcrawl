Vicarage Road

Vicarage Road is a football stadium in Watford, Hertfordshire, England, and the home ground of Watford Football Club since it opened in 1922. Its capacity is around 22,000.

History

Watford moved to Vicarage Road from Cassio Road in 1922; the ground was built on a former chalk pit owned by Benskin's brewery, from whom the club long leased the site. The stadium grew piecemeal, with the Main Stand dating in part from the 1920s. During Watford's rise under manager Graham Taylor and chairman Elton John in the late 1970s and 1980s, the ground hosted First Division football and was modernised following the Taylor Report.

Stands and other uses

The four stands are the Vicarage Road Stand, the Rookery Stand (home of the club's most vocal support), the Graham Taylor Stand and the Sir Elton John Stand, opened in 2014 on the site of the old Main Stand. The stadium has also hosted rugby union — Saracens played home matches there from 1997 to 2013 — and Wealdstone F.C. ground-shared in the 1990s. Plans for a possible new stadium have been discussed, but Vicarage Road remains Watford's home.
