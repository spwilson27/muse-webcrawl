J. E. B. Stuart

James Ewell Brown Stuart
Born: 6 February 1833, Patrick County, Virginia
Died: 12 May 1864, Richmond, Virginia
Allegiance: Confederate States
Branch: Confederate States Army — Cavalry

James Ewell Brown "Jeb" Stuart (February 6, 1833 – May 12, 1864) was a Confederate cavalry general during the American Civil War. Known for his mastery of reconnaissance and the use of cavalry in offensive operations, he was a trusted subordinate of Robert E. Lee and famous for his flamboyant style and daring rides around the Union army.

Early life and career

Stuart graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1854 and served on the frontier, including in Bleeding Kansas, before resigning his commission in 1861 to join the Confederacy.

Civil War

He rose to command the cavalry of the Army of Northern Virginia, twice riding around George B. McClellan's Army of the Potomac during the 1862 campaigns. His absence on a raid during the Gettysburg campaign of 1863, which left Lee without cavalry intelligence, has been a subject of lasting controversy. Stuart was mortally wounded at the Battle of Yellow Tavern on 11 May 1864, fighting Philip Sheridan's cavalry, and died in Richmond the next day.

Legacy

Stuart's reputation as a cavalier — plumed hat, red-lined cape, and banjo-playing entourage — made him one of the most recognizable Confederate commanders.
