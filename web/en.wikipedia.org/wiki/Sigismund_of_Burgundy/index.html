Sigismund (Latin: Sigismundus; died 1 May 524) was King of the Burgundians from 516 until his death. The son and successor of Gundobad, he is remembered as a lawgiver's heir who converted from Arianism to Catholic Christianity, refounded the abbey of Saint-Maurice d'Agaune in 515, and—having murdered his own son—was defeated by the Franks, captured, and executed. He is venerated as a saint and martyr, one of the few barbarian kings of the age to enter the calendar of saints.

Reign

Sigismund had converted to Catholicism before his accession, under the influence of Bishop Avitus of Vienne—unusual in an Arian royal house and a step that aligned Burgundy with the Gallo-Roman episcopate and, cautiously, with the Franks, who had been Catholic since Clovis. In 515 he lavishly refounded the monastery of Agaunum (Saint-Maurice-en-Valais), burial place of the Theban Legion, endowing it with the perpetual chant (laus perennis) maintained by relays of monks—a practice that became famous throughout Gaul. He continued his father's legal work associated with the Lex Burgundionum and issued the Prima Constitutio.

Murder of Sigeric and the Frankish war

By his first wife, an Ostrogothic princess, Sigismund had a son, Sigeric. In 522, after quarrels in which his second wife accused Sigeric of plotting, Sigismund had the young man strangled. According to Gregory of Tours, the king, stricken with remorse, withdrew to Agaune to fast and pray. The murder alienated the Ostrogothic king Theodoric and weakened Sigismund's position. In 523 the sons of Clovis—Chlodomer, Childebert and Clothar—invaded Burgundy, defeated Sigismund, and he fled toward Agaune but was captured (tradition says betrayed by his own men) and carried off to Orléans.

Death and cult

Sigismund's brother Godomar rallied the Burgundians and drove the Franks out, massacring their garrison at Vézeronce's aftermath; in reprisal Chlodomer had Sigismund, his wife and their sons thrown into a well and killed on 1 May 524 at Coulmiers near Orléans. The bodies were later translated to Agaune, where his cult flourished; his feast is 1 May (celebrated especially in the Valais and at the abbey he refounded). Burgundy itself was finally absorbed into the Frankish kingdom in 534.
