Emoji

Emoji (Japanese: 絵文字, "picture character") are pictograms, logograms, and smileys used in electronic messages and web pages. The first emoji sets were created in Japan in the late 1990s for mobile phones; emoji became globally popular after their inclusion in Apple's iPhone and their standardization in Unicode from 2010 onward.

History

Shigetaka Kurita designed a set of 176 emoji for NTT DoCoMo's i-mode service in 1999, drawing on manga, weather symbols, and pictograms. Competing Japanese carriers developed their own incompatible sets. The Unicode Consortium began encoding emoji in Unicode 6.0 (2010), enabling cross-platform use; Google, Apple, and other vendors created color fonts and keyboards that spread emoji worldwide. The Oxford Dictionaries named the "Face with Tears of Joy" emoji (😂) its Word of the Year in 2015.

Encoding and variation

Thousands of emoji are now encoded. Sequences can combine code points with zero-width joiners to form family, profession, and flag emoji, and skin-tone modifiers (added in 2015) adjust the appearance of human emoji. Because each platform draws its own glyphs, the same code point may look noticeably different across devices — a documented source of miscommunication. New emoji are proposed to and approved by the Unicode Consortium annually.

Cultural impact

Emoji function as a global visual language supplementing text, and have inspired the Emoji Movie (2017), the Emojipedia reference site, and World Emoji Day (July 17). July 17 is the date displayed on the calendar emoji 📅.
