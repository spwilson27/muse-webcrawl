Gibson L-5

ManufacturerGibson
Introduced1922
TypeCarved-top archtop guitar
DesignerLloyd Loar

The Gibson L-5 is a hollow-body archtop guitar introduced by Gibson in 1922 as part of Lloyd Loar's Master Model series. The first guitar with f-holes, it became the archetypal jazz guitar and the standard rhythm instrument of the big-band era.

Design

The original L-5 had a 16-inch carved spruce top with maple back and sides, f-shaped sound holes, and a trapeze tailpiece; the body grew to 17 inches in the 1930s. Later electric versions — the L-5 CES (Cutaway Electric Spanish, from 1951) — added two humbucking pickups and a Florentine cutaway, becoming a premier jazz instrument.

Players

Early endorsers included Eddie Lang, the first great jazz guitarist. Maybelle Carter's 1928 L-5 — heard on the Carter Family's foundational country recordings — is displayed at the Country Music Hall of Fame. Later users include Wes Montgomery (whose heart-inlaid L-5 is iconic), George Benson, Lee Ritenour, Pat Martino and Tuck Andress, as well as rock musicians such as John Lennon (a related J-160E aside) and Eric Clapton.

Legacy

The L-5 remains Gibson's flagship archtop, produced in limited numbers by the Gibson Custom Shop, and its design influenced virtually every carved-top jazz guitar that followed.
