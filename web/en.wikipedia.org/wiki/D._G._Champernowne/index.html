David Gawen Champernowne (9 July 1912 – 19 August 2000) was an English economist and mathematician, a fellow of King's College, Cambridge and a friend and contemporary of Alan Turing, whom he met at Cambridge.

Career

Champernowne studied mathematics and economics at the University of Cambridge. During the Second World War he worked on statistical and operational problems, including time at Bletchley Park's wider wartime statistical effort and in government service. After the war he held posts at Oxford, where he was a fellow of Nuffield College and worked at the Institute of Statistics, before returning to Cambridge as Professor of Economic Statistics. With Alan Turing he also designed the chess program Turochamp in 1948, one of the earliest computer chess programs — written before any computer could run it, so that the two had to simulate its moves by hand.

Work

As a student in 1933 Champernowne published the construction of the Champernowne constant, 0.1234567891011121314..., formed by concatenating the decimal representations of the positive integers, and proved it is a normal number in base 10 — the first explicit example of an artificially constructed normal number. In economics he is known for his work on the distribution of income, including a stochastic model of income distribution, and for contributions to economic statistics and the theory of index numbers.
