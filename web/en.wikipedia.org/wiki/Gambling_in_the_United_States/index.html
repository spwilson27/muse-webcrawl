Gambling in the United States

A casino gaming floor in Las Vegas, the best-known centre of legal gambling in the United States.

Gambling in the United States is legally restricted in many areas, but the availability of gambling and the law surrounding it have varied over time and by state. Unlike in many countries, gambling regulation in the United States is largely a matter for state law, with federal law playing a supporting role in areas such as interstate commerce, tribal gaming, and online gambling.

Forms of legal gambling

Casinos

Commercial casinos are legal in a number of states, most famously in Las Vegas and Reno, Nevada, and Atlantic City, New Jersey. Nevada legalised casino gambling in 1931; New Jersey followed in 1976, restricting casinos to Atlantic City. From the late 1980s, many other states authorised riverboat and land-based casinos, often presented as tools of economic development.

Native American gaming

The Indian Gaming Regulatory Act of 1988 (IGRA) established the framework governing gambling on Native American tribal lands, following the Supreme Court's decision in California v. Cabazon Band of Mission Indians (1987). Tribes may operate bingo and similar games and, under compacts negotiated with states, casino-style games. Tribal gaming grew into an industry generating tens of billions of dollars annually, with major resorts such as Foxwoods and Mohegan Sun in Connecticut.

Lotteries

State lotteries are the most widespread form of legal gambling. New Hampshire established the first modern state lottery in 1964; most states now operate lotteries, including participation in multi-state draw games such as Powerball and Mega Millions with very large jackpots.

Pari-mutuel wagering

Betting on horse racing and greyhound racing (where it survives) is legal in many states under pari-mutuel systems, in which bettors wager against each other and the operator takes a fixed percentage.

Sports betting

The Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act of 1992 effectively banned sports betting outside Nevada and a few grandfathered schemes. In Murphy v. NCAA (2018), the Supreme Court struck the Act down as unconstitutional, and many states have since legalised retail and online sportsbooks.

Online gambling

Online casino games and poker are legal in a growing minority of states. Federal law, including the Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act of 2006, restricts payment processing for unlawful internet gambling without itself defining which gambling is unlawful, leaving the question largely to state law.

History

Lotteries helped finance colonial ventures and public works, but waves of scandal and moral opposition in the 19th century led most states to ban them. Gambling flourished on the frontier and in illegal urban establishments, often tied to organised crime. The 20th century saw gradual legalisation: pari-mutuel racing during the Great Depression, Nevada's casinos, bingo, state lotteries, and eventually tribal and riverboat casinos.

Regulation and social issues

Gaming is overseen by state gaming commissions and, for tribal operations, the National Indian Gaming Commission. Debates over gambling involve its tax revenue and employment benefits against problem gambling, addiction, and regressive effects on low-income players. The American Gaming Association is the principal industry trade body; problem gambling is treated as a behavioural addiction by health authorities, and most states fund helplines and self-exclusion programmes.
