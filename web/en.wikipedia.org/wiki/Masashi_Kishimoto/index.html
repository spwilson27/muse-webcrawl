Masashi Kishimoto (岸本 斉史, Kishimoto Masashi, born 8 November 1974) is a Japanese manga artist. His manga series Naruto, which was in serialisation from 1999 to 2014, has sold over 250 million copies worldwide in 46 countries as of May 2019. The series has been adapted into two anime television series and multiple films, video games, and related media. Besides the Naruto manga, Kishimoto also personally supervised the two canonical anime films, The Last: Naruto the Movie and Boruto: Naruto the Movie, and has written several one-shot stories.

Early life

Kishimoto was born in Nagi, Okayama Prefecture, Japan, the younger of identical twins; his brother Seishi Kishimoto is also a manga artist. Influenced by Akira Toriyama's Dragon Ball and Katsuhiro Otomo's Akira, he spent much of his youth drawing characters from anime and manga. While in art college he submitted the one-shot Karakuri, which won Shueisha's Hop Step Award in 1995.

Works

The Naruto pilot appeared in Akamaru Jump in 1997, and the series began regular serialisation in Weekly Shōnen Jump in 1999, running for 700 chapters until November 2014. In 2019 Kishimoto launched Samurai 8: The Tale of Hachimaru, illustrated by Akira Ōkubo, which concluded in 2020. From 2020 he took over writing duties on the Boruto sequel manga.

Style and influences

Kishimoto has cited Toriyama, Otomo, and the films of Quentin Tarantino and Michael Bay among his influences, and he drew on Japanese mythology and Confucian themes for the world of Naruto.
