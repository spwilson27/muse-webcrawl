Fundamentalist–modernist controversy

The fundamentalist–modernist controversy was a major schism within American Protestantism in the 1920s and 1930s, pitting theological conservatives ("fundamentalists") against liberals ("modernists") who sought to reconcile Christianity with modern science and biblical criticism, principally within the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America and the Northern Baptist Convention.

Background

The name derives from The Fundamentals (1910–1915), a series of essays defending doctrines such as biblical inerrancy, the virgin birth and substitutionary atonement. Modernists, influenced by higher criticism and evolution, treated these as negotiable. Flashpoints included Harry Emerson Fosdick's 1922 sermon "Shall the Fundamentalists Win?", J. Gresham Machen's Christianity and Liberalism (1923), and the 1925 Scopes Trial over the teaching of evolution in Tennessee.

Outcome

Conservatives largely lost control of the mainline denominations and seminaries, withdrawing to form independent churches, schools and mission agencies — Machen founded Westminster Theological Seminary and later the Orthodox Presbyterian Church. The controversy reshaped American religion, seeding the later evangelical movement and a lasting division between mainline and conservative Protestantism.
