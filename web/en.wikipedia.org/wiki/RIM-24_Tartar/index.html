RIM-24 Tartar

The RIM-24 Tartar was a medium-range naval surface-to-air missile developed for the United States Navy during the Cold War. It belonged to the early “3T” family of missiles, alongside Talos and Terrier, and provided air defence for cruisers, destroyers and frigates.

Design

Tartar used semi-active radar homing and was launched from twin-arm or single-arm Mk 11 and Mk 13 launchers. It was smaller than the long-range Talos, making it suitable for ships that could not accommodate the largest missile systems. Improvements in guidance and propulsion kept variants in service for decades.

Service

The missile armed several classes of United States and allied warships. It was gradually replaced by the Standard missile family, whose later members include the RIM-161 Standard Missile 3 for ballistic missile defence.
