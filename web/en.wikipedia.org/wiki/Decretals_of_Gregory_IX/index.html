Decretals of Gregory IX

[Image: Manuscript page of the Decretals of Gregory IX]
An illuminated manuscript of the Liber Extra

The Decretals of Gregory IX, also called the Liber Extra (in full, Decretales Gregorii IX), is a collection of canon law promulgated by Pope Gregory IX on 5 September 1234 with the bull Rex pacificus. Compiled by the Dominican friar Raymond of Peñafort, the pope's confessor and a leading canonist, it gathered nearly 2,000 papal decretals and conciliar canons into five books and became the standard authorized collection of the law of the Latin Church.

Composition and structure

Raymond worked from earlier compilations known as the Quinque compilationes antiquae, abbreviating, harmonizing and interpolating the texts to remove contradictions, and adding decretals of Gregory IX himself. The collection is organized in five books: judges and jurisdiction (iudex), judicial procedure (iudicium), clergy and religious life (clerus), marriage (conubium) and crime and penalties (crimen) — a scheme remembered by the mnemonic iudex, iudicium, clerus, conubium, crimen. Each book is divided into titles and chapters, cited in the form X 1.2.3 (book, title, chapter).

Authority and influence

Gregory ordered that only this collection be used in courts and universities, giving it exclusive authority and displacing the older compilations. Together with Gratian's Decretum (c. 1140) and the later Liber Sextus of Boniface VIII (1298) and the Clementines (1317), it formed the Corpus Juris Canonici, the body of canon law that governed the Catholic Church until the Code of Canon Law of 1917. The Decretals shaped medieval law on marriage, procedure, oaths, heresy proceedings and ecclesiastical office, and were the subject of extensive glosses, notably the apparatus of Bernard of Parma.
