Xi'an (Chinese: 西安; pinyin: Xī'ān) is the capital of Shaanxi province in northwestern China and one of the oldest cities in the country, with a documented history of more than 3,100 years. Known in earlier eras as Chang'an, it served as the capital of thirteen dynasties, including the Western Han and Tang, and marks the eastern terminus of the Silk Road. It is a sub-provincial city administering 11 districts and two counties.

History

The area around Xi'an has been inhabited since Neolithic times; the Banpo village site on the city's outskirts dates to around 5000 BCE. Under the name Chang'an ("Perpetual Peace"), the city was the capital of the Western Han dynasty (206 BCE–9 CE) and again of the Tang dynasty (618–907), when it was among the largest and most cosmopolitan cities in the world, with a population approaching one million. The Ming dynasty rebuilt the city on a smaller footprint and gave it the name Xi'an, meaning "Western Peace." Its massive Ming-era city wall survives nearly intact today.

Xi'an was the site of the Xi'an Incident of December 1936, in which Chiang Kai-shek was detained by his own generals, an event that reshaped the course of the Chinese Civil War and the Second Sino-Japanese War.

Geography

Xi'an lies on the Guanzhong Plain in the south-central part of Shaanxi, bounded by the Wei River to the north and the Qinling Mountains to the south. It has a temperate monsoon climate with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters.

Culture and landmarks

The city is world-famous for the Terracotta Army, the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, discovered by farmers in 1974 northeast of the city. Other landmarks include the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda, the Bell Tower and Drum Tower, the Muslim Quarter, and the Shaanxi History Museum. Xi'an is a major centre of education and high-technology industry in western China.
