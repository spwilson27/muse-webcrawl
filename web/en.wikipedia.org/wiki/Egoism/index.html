Egoism

Egoism is a philosophy concerned with the role of the self, or ego, as the motivation and goal of one's own actions. Different theories of egoism encompass psychological, normative, and rational variants.

Types

Psychological egoism claims people always act in perceived self-interest (descriptive). Ethical egoism holds one ought to act in one's own interest. Rational egoism, associated with Ayn Rand, holds acting in rational self-interest is both rational and moral. Max Stirner's The Ego and Its Own (1844) presents a radical individualist egoism; Henry Sidgwick contrasted egoism with utilitarianism in The Methods of Ethics.

Criticism

Critics argue psychological egoism is unfalsifiable, and ethical egoism cannot resolve conflicts of interest or justify duties to others. Defenders distinguish egoism from selfishness or narcissism.
