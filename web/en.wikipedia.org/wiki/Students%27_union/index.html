Students' union

A students' union, also known by many other names (student association, student government, student senate, guild of students), is a student organization present in many colleges, universities, and high schools. It is often run by students for students, and is dedicated to student governance, representing student interests to the institution and faculty, and providing services and social, cultural, and recreational activities.

Functions

Students' unions typically combine representative and service functions: they campaign on issues such as tuition fees and housing, sit on university committees, fund and supervise student societies and sports clubs, run advice and welfare services, and often operate commercial venues — bars, shops, cafés, and entertainment spaces — whose profits are reinvested in student activities. In the United Kingdom, unions are usually governed by elected sabbatical officers and are affiliated nationally to the National Union of Students (NUS), founded in 1922.

Terminology

The term "student union" in North America often denotes the building housing student facilities, while the organisation itself is more often called student government or a student association. In Australia, student unions were affected by the introduction of voluntary student unionism in 2006, which sharply reduced their funding; compulsory student-services fees were later partially restored.
