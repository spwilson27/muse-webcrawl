Blackburn is a town in Lancashire, England, north of the West Pennine Moors, forming, with Darwen, the unitary authority of Blackburn with Darwen. Once one of the great mill towns of the Industrial Revolution, it was a world centre of cotton weaving.

History

Blackburn's textile trade grew in the 18th century; the flying shuttle's inventor John Kay was born nearby, and James Hargreaves invented the spinning jenny in neighbouring Oswaldtwistle. At the industry's peak the town ran tens of thousands of looms and styled itself a capital of "the weaving capital of the world", importing raw cotton through Liverpool and exporting cloth worldwide. The decline of Lancashire cotton in the 20th century forced economic reinvention. Mahatma Gandhi famously visited nearby Darwen in 1931 to meet mill workers affected by the Indian boycott.

Landmarks and sport

Blackburn Cathedral, on an ancient church site, gained cathedral status in 1926. The town is home to Blackburn Rovers F.C., founded in 1875, a founding member of the Football League and Premier League champions in 1994–95, who play at Ewood Park. The town has a substantial British Asian population, reflected in its mosques, food and festivals.
