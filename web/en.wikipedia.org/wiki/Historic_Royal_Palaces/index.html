Historic Royal Palaces is an independent charity that manages the unoccupied royal palaces of the United Kingdom on behalf of the Sovereign. It looks after the Tower of London, Hampton Court Palace, the Banqueting House in Whitehall, Kensington Palace, Kew Palace and Hillsborough Castle in Northern Ireland.

History

The organisation began in 1989 as an executive agency of the Department of the Environment, consolidating the care of royal palaces that were no longer occupied by the Royal Family. In 1998 it became an independent charity, contracted by the Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport to manage the palaces for an initial period and subsequently on a continuing basis. Unusually for a major heritage body, it receives no regular funding from the government or the Crown and must raise its income from visitors, members, donors and commercial activities.

Role and work

The charity conserves the buildings and their collections, presents them to the public through exhibitions, re-enactment and interpretation, and conducts research into court history. It is responsible for the Royal Ceremonial Dress Collection and cares for the Crown Jewels display at the Tower in partnership with the Royal Collection Trust. Notable projects have included the re-presentation of Kensington Palace, the commemoration of the centenary of the First World War with the installation Blood Swept Lands and Seas of Red at the Tower in 2014—888,246 ceramic poppies filling the moat—and the restoration of the Great Pagoda at Kew.

Sites

  • Tower of London — Norman fortress and former palace, prison and mint, home of the Crown Jewels.
  • Hampton Court Palace — Tudor and Baroque palace of Henry VIII and William III.
  • Banqueting House, Whitehall — Inigo Jones's surviving hall of the Palace of Whitehall, with Rubens ceiling paintings.
  • Kensington Palace — birthplace of Queen Victoria and a royal residence since the 17th century.
  • Kew Palace — small palace within Kew Gardens, associated with George III.
  • Hillsborough Castle — the official residence of the monarch and the Secretary of State in Northern Ireland.
