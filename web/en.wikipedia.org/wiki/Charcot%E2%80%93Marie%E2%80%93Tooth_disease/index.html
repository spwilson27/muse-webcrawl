Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease

Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease
SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsProgressive distal muscle weakness and wasting, foot deformity, reduced reflexes
CauseInherited mutations affecting peripheral nerves
Named afterJean-Martin Charcot, Pierre Marie, Howard Henry Tooth

Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease (CMT) is a diverse group of inherited disorders of the peripheral nervous system characterised by progressive loss of muscle tissue and touch sensation across various parts of the body. It is among the most common inherited neurological disorders, affecting roughly 1 in 2,500 people.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually begin in adolescence or early adulthood and include weakness and wasting of the foot and lower leg muscles, leading to foot drop and characteristic high arches (pes cavus), hammertoes, and later involvement of the hands. Sensory loss and diminished reflexes are common.

Genetics and types

CMT is divided into demyelinating types (such as CMT1, commonly caused by duplication of the PMP22 gene), axonal types (CMT2), and intermediate forms. Diagnosis combines clinical examination, nerve conduction studies and genetic testing. There is no cure; management includes physiotherapy, orthotics and orthopaedic surgery.
