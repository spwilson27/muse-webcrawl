Fairbanks is a home rule city in Alaska, United States, and the borough seat of the Fairbanks North Star Borough. It is the second-most populous metropolitan area in Alaska, after Anchorage, and the largest city in the Alaskan Interior. Fairbanks lies on the Chena River near its confluence with the Tanana River, in the Tanana Valley.

History

The site was founded in 1901 when trader E. T. Barnette established a trading post on the Chena River; his plans changed after prospector Felix Pedro discovered gold nearby in 1902, sparking a gold rush. The city was named for Charles W. Fairbanks, a U.S. senator from Indiana and later vice president under Theodore Roosevelt. Agriculture, the Alaska Railroad (completed 1923), and military installations — Ladd Field (now Fort Wainwright) and Eielson Air Force Base — drove growth in the twentieth century. The construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline in the 1970s made Fairbanks a staging hub.

Geography and climate

Fairbanks has a subarctic climate with extreme seasonal variation: long, bitterly cold winters with temperatures dropping below −40 °F, and warm summers reaching 90 °F. Its far-northern latitude yields nearly 22 hours of daylight at the summer solstice and only about four hours in midwinter, and the aurora borealis is frequently visible on clear winter nights.

Economy and education

The University of Alaska Fairbanks, founded in 1917, is the flagship campus of the University of Alaska system and a major employer, along with the military and the mining, tourism, and oil-support industries.
