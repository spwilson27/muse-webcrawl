Raymond Queneau

BornFebruary 21, 1903, Le Havre, France
DiedOctober 25, 1976, Paris, France
OccupationNovelist, poet
Notable worksZazie dans le Métro; Exercises in Style; Cent mille milliards de poèmes

Raymond Queneau (1903–1976) was a French novelist, poet, critic, and editor, co-founder of the Oulipo (Ouvroir de littérature potentielle) group with mathematician François Le Lionnais in 1960.

Life and work

Born in Le Havre, Queneau briefly joined the Surrealists before breaking with André Breton. He worked for decades at Éditions Gallimard, directed the Encyclopédie de la Pléiade, and wrote fiction mixing spoken French, wordplay, and mathematical structures.

Major works

Exercices de style (1947) retells one trivial anecdote in 99 different styles. Zazie dans le Métro (1959), filmed by Louis Malle, became his most popular novel. Cent mille milliards de poèmes (1961) is a book of ten sonnets with interchangeable lines, a founding gesture of constrained writing.

Oulipo

The Oulipo group explored literature under formal constraints — lipograms, palindromes, combinatorial structures — influencing writers such as Georges Perec and Italo Calvino.
