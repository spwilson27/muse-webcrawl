2600 Hz

2600 Hz is a frequency in hertz — cycles per second — that was historically significant in telephone network signaling. In the mid-20th-century Bell System in the United States, an idle long-distance trunk line carried a continuous 2600 Hz supervisory tone. When the tone was present, the line was considered on-hook; removing it signaled that the line was in use.

Phreaking

Phone phreaks discovered that if a caller on a long-distance call played a 2600 Hz tone down the line, the far-end equipment would register the trunk as free while the caller's connection stayed open. The caller could then dial new routing codes using a "blue box" that generated the multi-frequency (MF) tones used by the network itself, effectively placing free long-distance calls. The trick was popularized by John Draper, nicknamed "Captain Crunch" after he found that a toy whistle from a cereal box produced almost exactly 2600 Hz.

Demise

The vulnerability disappeared as the telephone network moved to out-of-band signaling — first with separate signaling channels, and ultimately with Signaling System No. 7 (SS7) — in which control signals no longer travel in the voice path. The tone survives in hacker culture as the namesake of 2600: The Hacker Quarterly.
