Utility helicopter

A utility helicopter is a multi-purpose helicopter capable of performing a wide range of roles, including troop and cargo transport, command and control, search and rescue, medical evacuation, aerial firefighting, and general logistical support. The term is used both for military aircraft and for civil helicopters adapted to utility work.

Characteristics

Utility helicopters are typically medium-sized aircraft with a spacious cabin accessed by large sliding doors, provision for carrying external loads on a cargo hook, and the ability to be rapidly reconfigured between passenger, cargo, and ambulance layouts. Many can be armed for self-defence or light attack, though dedicated attack helicopters are purpose-built for that mission.

Notable examples

The Bell UH-1 Iroquois, widely known as the "Huey", became an icon of the Vietnam War and established the utility helicopter as a central element of air-mobile warfare. Its successors and counterparts include the Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk, the Mil Mi-8 and Mi-17 family — among the most produced helicopters in history — the Eurocopter (Airbus) UH-72 Lakota, and the Bell 412. Civil utility types include the Bell 205/212 series and the Airbus H125, frequently used for firefighting, power-line work, and mountain rescue.

Roles

In military service, utility helicopters perform air assault support, casualty evacuation, special operations insertion, and resupply. In civilian service they are used for construction lifting, logging, offshore transport, law enforcement, and disaster relief.
