Peptide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Peptide
CompositionShort chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds
LengthTypically 2–50 amino acid residues
Related termsOligopeptide, polypeptide, protein

Peptides are short chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. A polypeptide is a longer, continuous, unbranched peptide chain. Polypeptides that have a molecular mass of more than about 10,000 daltons are commonly called proteins, though the boundary between a large peptide and a small protein is conventional rather than absolute.

Chains of fewer than about 10–20 amino acids are called oligopeptides and include dipeptides, tripeptides and tetrapeptides. Peptides are distinguished from proteins on the basis of size and typically contain 50 or fewer amino acids. The smallest peptide drugs, hormones and signalling molecules can consist of just two or three residues, such as glutathione, a tripeptide antioxidant.

Synthesis

In cells, peptides are produced by ribosomes, which translate messenger RNA into amino acid sequences, or by nonribosomal peptide synthetases, large enzyme complexes found in bacteria and fungi that produce many antibiotics. In the laboratory, peptides are made by solid-phase peptide synthesis, pioneered by Robert Bruce Merrifield, in which amino acids are added one at a time to a growing chain anchored to a resin bead.

Classes and functions

Peptides serve as hormones (insulin, a 51-residue peptide sometimes classed as a small protein), neurotransmitters and neuromodulators (endorphins, substance P), antibiotics (gramicidin, bacitracin), toxins, and signalling molecules. Milk peptides, ribosomal antimicrobial peptides and peptide vaccines are active areas of research. Therapeutic peptides are a major class of pharmaceuticals, including GLP-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide.

Structure

Peptide sequences are conventionally written from the N-terminus (free amino group) to the C-terminus (free carboxyl group). Because the peptide bond has partial double-bond character, the atoms along the backbone are constrained to near-planar arrangements, and the conformation of a peptide is largely described by the phi and psi torsion angles of each residue.
