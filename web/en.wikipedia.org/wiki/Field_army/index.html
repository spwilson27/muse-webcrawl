Field army

A field army (or numbered army, or simply army) is a military formation in many armed forces, composed of two or more army corps and commanded by a general officer. Field armies are among the largest operational formations, typically numbering 80,000 to 300,000 soldiers depending on the army and period, and are usually identified by a number or a name (for example, the U.S. First Army or the British Eighth Army).

Organisation

A field army sits between the army group (theatre level) and the corps in the chain of command. Its headquarters controls corps and divisions plus army-level support troops — artillery, engineers, signals, logistics and aviation — enabling it to conduct sustained operations over a wide area. Armies became standard formations during the Napoleonic Wars and were a principal operational unit in both World Wars.

Modern usage

In peacetime, many modern field armies serve as administrative or regional headquarters; in some countries the term denotes the land component command for a region. The United States Army maintains numbered armies largely as army service component commands assigned to geographic combatant commands.
