Compressed natural gas (CNG) is a fuel made by compressing natural gas (predominantly methane) to less than 1% of the volume it occupies at standard atmospheric pressure. It is stored and distributed in hard containers at a pressure of 200–248 bar.

Uses

CNG is used as a motor fuel in modified gasoline vehicles and in purpose-built natural gas vehicles, ranging from cars and auto rickshaws to buses and trucks. Countries with large CNG fleets include Iran, Pakistan, India, Argentina, and Brazil.

Advantages and disadvantages

CNG vehicles emit less carbon dioxide and fewer particulates per unit energy than gasoline or diesel equivalents, but require bulky high-pressure tanks and refueling infrastructure, and methane leakage can offset climate benefits.

Distinction from LNG

CNG should not be confused with liquefied natural gas (LNG), which is cryogenically liquefied rather than compressed as a gas.
