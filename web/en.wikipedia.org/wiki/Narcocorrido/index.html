Narcocorrido

A narcocorrido (Spanish: "drug ballad") is a subgenre of the Mexican corrido, the narrative folk ballad tradition, whose lyrics glorify or chronicle the drug trade and the lives of drug traffickers. Performed most often in the norteño and banda styles, narcocorridos emerged in the borderlands of northern Mexico and the southwestern United States in the 1970s and became a commercially significant and controversial part of regional Mexican music.

Origins

The corrido tradition dates to the Mexican Revolution and earlier, celebrating heroes, outlaws and smugglers — including tequila smugglers during Prohibition in the United States. In the 1970s groups such as Los Tigres del Norte began recording ballads about modern traffickers; their 1974 hit "Contrabando y traición" ("Contraband and Betrayal"), about a drug-running couple, Camelia and Emilio Varela, is widely credited with launching the modern narcocorrido. The genre expanded with performers such as Chalino Sánchez, whose raw corridos, many commissioned by traffickers themselves, made him a cult figure before his murder in 1992.

Themes and the corrido commission

Narcocorridos typically tell the story of a trafficker's rise, wealth, daring confrontations with authorities, loyalty, betrayal and violent death. Many are corridos encargados — commissioned works paid for by traffickers who wish to memorialise themselves, a practice that has made some musicians wealthy and placed others in danger: several performers, including Valentín Elizalde and members of various bands, have been murdered, in some cases reportedly over the content of their songs or their associations.

Controversy and bans

Critics argue that narcocorridos glamorise organised crime during Mexico's drug war, and several Mexican states and municipalities, including Sinaloa, Chihuahua, Baja California and Michoacán, have at various times banned their public performance or radio broadcast, with fines for venues. Defenders respond that the songs reflect social reality rather than create it, and that bans drive the music underground without addressing the drug trade. The genre nonetheless thrives on streaming platforms and in the United States, where later movements such as corridos tumbados have carried its themes into a new generation of regional Mexican music.
