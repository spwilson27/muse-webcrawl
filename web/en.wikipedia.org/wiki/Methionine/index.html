Methionine (symbol Met or M) is an essential amino acid in humans. As the precursor of other amino acids such as cysteine and taurine, and of compounds such as S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and glutathione, methionine plays a critical role in the metabolism and health of many species, including humans. It is encoded by the codon AUG, which is also the start codon in most messenger RNAs.

Methionine is one of only two amino acids encoded by a single codon (the other is tryptophan). Along with cysteine, it is one of the two sulfur-containing proteinogenic amino acids. Its side chain is a thioether, which makes methionine nonpolar and hydrophobic.

Biochemistry

Because methionine is an essential amino acid, it cannot be synthesised by humans and must be obtained from the diet. Significant dietary sources include eggs, meat, fish, sesame seeds, Brazil nuts, and certain cereals.

In metabolism, L-methionine is converted to S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the principal methyl donor in biological methylation reactions, including the methylation of DNA, RNA, and proteins. Methionine metabolism proceeds through the methionine cycle, in which it can be regenerated from homocysteine. Elevated homocysteine levels are associated with cardiovascular disease.

Via the transsulfuration pathway, methionine provides the sulfur for cysteine synthesis. Methionine also serves as a precursor of the polyamines spermidine and spermine.

Industrial production and uses

Most commercial methionine is produced by chemical synthesis as the racemic mixture DL-methionine, largely for use as a feed additive in poultry and livestock production, where it is often the first limiting amino acid. It is also used in pharmaceuticals and dietary supplements.
