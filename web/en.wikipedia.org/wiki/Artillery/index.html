Artillery

Artillery is a class of heavy military ranged weapons that launch munitions far beyond the range and power of infantry firearms. Originally the word referred to any group of soldiers primarily armed with some form of manufactured weapon or armour; since the introduction of gunpowder and the cannon, artillery has largely come to mean guns, howitzers, mortars and rocket and missile systems, together with the soldiers, organisation and techniques that employ them. Artillery is one of the combat arms of land warfare and has been called the "king of battle" for its destructive predominance on modern battlefields.

History

Mechanical artillery — catapults, ballistae and trebuchets — dates to antiquity. Gunpowder artillery appeared in China by the thirteenth century and spread to Europe, where bombards and cannon transformed siege warfare in the fifteenth century. Standardisation of calibres, mobile field guns and horse artillery matured in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries (notably under Gribeauval and in the Napoleonic Wars). Rifled barrels, breech loading, recoil mechanisms and smokeless powder in the late nineteenth century — exemplified by the French Canon de 75 — set the pattern for modern guns. In the First World War artillery caused the majority of battlefield casualties and developed indirect fire, predicted fire and massive preparatory bombardments; the Second World War added self-propelled guns, rockets such as the Katyusha, and proximity fuzes.

Modern artillery

Modern systems include towed and self-propelled howitzers (typically 105 mm, 122 mm, 152 mm or 155 mm), mortars, multiple rocket launchers such as HIMARS and MLRS, and anti-tank and air-defence guns. Fire is directed by forward observers, radar, drones and digital fire-control systems using GPS; precision-guided munitions such as Excalibur allow single rounds to strike within metres of a target. Counter-battery radar locates enemy guns by tracking their shells. Artillery's role was reaffirmed at industrial scale in the Russo-Ukrainian War from 2022, where guns and rockets accounted for a large share of attrition.

Ammunition and fire

Artillery fires high-explosive, smoke, illumination, cluster and precision munitions, with fuzes that can be set for impact, time or proximity burst. Fire missions include destruction, suppression, interdiction, harassment, counter-battery fire and final protective fire in defence.
