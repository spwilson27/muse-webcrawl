Zinfandel

OriginCroatia (as Crljenak Kaštelanski)

Zinfandel (also known as Primitivo) is a variety of black-skinned wine grape. The variety is grown in over 10 percent of California vineyards. DNA analysis has revealed that it is genetically equivalent to the Croatian grapes Crljenak Kaštelanski and Tribidrag, as well as to the Primitivo variety traditionally grown in Apulia (Puglia), Italy, where it was introduced in the 18th century. The grape found its way to the United States in the mid-19th century, where it became known by variations of a name applied to a different grape, likely "Zierfandler" from Austria.

History

Zinfandel was a popular table grape in the eastern United States before being transported to California during the Gold Rush era. It became one of the most widely planted varieties in California, and many vines survived Prohibition through home winemaking exemptions. In the 1970s, Sutter Home Winery created White Zinfandel, a sweet blush wine made by fermenting the red grapes with minimal skin contact; it became enormously popular and rescued many old Zinfandel vineyards from being uprooted. In the 1990s, DNA profiling by Carole Meredith's team at UC Davis established the grape's Croatian origin.

Wine styles

Red Zinfandel wines are known for high alcohol content, robust fruit flavours of blackberry, black cherry and plum, and notes of black pepper and spice. The grapes ripen unevenly, so a single bunch may contain both raisined and underripe berries, contributing to the variety's intensity. White Zinfandel is an off-dry rosé, much lighter in body and alcohol, and outsells the red version several times over in the United States.

Viticulture

Zinfandel thrives in warm climates but is sensitive to early-season heat and bunch rot due to its thin skins and tight clusters. "Old Vine" Zinfandel, from vines often more than 50 years old in regions such as Dry Creek Valley, Lodi, Paso Robles and the Russian River Valley, is particularly prized.
