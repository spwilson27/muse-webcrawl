Asakusa

Country: Japan

City: Tokyo

Ward: Taitō

Asakusa (浅草) is a district in Taitō, Tokyo, Japan, famous as the location of Sensō-ji, a Buddhist temple dedicated to the bodhisattva Kannon. Asakusa is one of the most popular tourist destinations in Tokyo; its main street, Nakamise-dōri, is lined with traditional shops selling souvenirs and snacks.

History

During the Edo period (1603–1868) Asakusa developed as the city's premier entertainment district, home to kabuki theatres and pleasure quarters just outside the official Yoshiwara district. In the Meiji era it became a centre of popular entertainment, with Japan's first cinema and skyscraper, the twelve-storey Ryōunkaku (1890), which stood until the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923. Much of the district was destroyed by firebombing in March 1945, and after the war its entertainment role passed to Shinjuku and other western districts, though it retains the atmosphere of old Edo's shitamachi (low city).

Landmarks

  • Sensō-ji — Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645, entered through the Kaminarimon ("Thunder Gate") with its giant red lantern.
  • Nakamise-dōri — shopping street approaching the temple, dating to the early 18th century.
  • Asakusa Shrine — Shinto shrine adjacent to the temple, site of the Sanja Matsuri festival each May.
  • Sumida River — cruises depart from Asakusa pier; the Tokyo Skytree is visible across the river.
  • Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center — viewing terrace opposite Kaminarimon.

Transport

Asakusa Station is served by the Tokyo Metro Ginza Line, the Toei Asakusa Line, the Tōbu Skytree Line and the Tsukuba Express.
