Educational testing

Educational testing is the systematic assessment of learners' knowledge, skills and abilities, used to make decisions about students, curricula and institutions. It ranges from informal classroom quizzes to large-scale standardised examinations taken by millions of candidates.

Types of assessment

  • Formative assessment: conducted during instruction to give feedback and guide learning.
  • Summative assessment: conducted at the end of a course or stage to certify achievement.
  • Norm-referenced tests: compare a candidate with a reference group (e.g. percentiles).
  • Criterion-referenced tests: measure performance against fixed standards.
  • High-stakes tests: examinations with significant consequences, such as the SAT and ACT in the United States, GCSEs and A-levels in the United Kingdom, the gaokao in China, and university entrance exams worldwide.

Psychometrics

The science of test construction, psychometrics, addresses reliability (consistency of measurement), validity (whether a test measures what it claims to), and fairness. Item response theory models the relationship between a candidate's ability and their probability of answering items correctly, enabling computer-adaptive tests such as the GRE and GMAT.

History and criticism

Written examinations were systematised in imperial China's civil-service examinations over a thousand years ago. Modern standardised testing grew from early twentieth-century intelligence testing (Binet, Terman) and expanded greatly after the Second World War. Critics argue that high-stakes testing narrows curricula ("teaching to the test"), disadvantages some groups, and can generate stress, while supporters cite objectivity, comparability and accountability.
