Erector Set

The Erector Set is a brand of metal construction toys consisting of metal beams with regularly spaced holes, connected by nuts and bolts, along with pulleys, gears, wheels, and electric motors. It was invented by Alfred Carlton Gilbert and introduced by the A. C. Gilbert Company in 1913, inspired by the steel girders Gilbert saw being assembled for electrification of the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad.

History

The first sets, sold under the name "The Mysto Erector Structural Steel Builder", appeared in 1913 and quickly became one of the most popular toys in the United States. Larger sets included small electric motors, enabling children to build working cranes, bridges, and vehicles. The Gilbert Company also marketed chemistry sets and atomic energy laboratory kits. After Gilbert's death the company declined and was sold in 1967; the Erector brand later passed to Meccano, and sets have continued to be sold under the Erector by Meccano name.

Cultural impact

The Erector Set is credited with inspiring generations of American engineers, and its marketing famously emphasised teaching boys practical engineering skills. It is often compared with the British Meccano system, which predated it.
