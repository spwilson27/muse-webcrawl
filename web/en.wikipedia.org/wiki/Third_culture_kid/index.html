Third culture kid


Third culture kids at an international school

A third culture kid (TCK) is a person raised in a culture other than their parents' or the culture of their country of nationality, and who lives in a different environment during a significant part of their child development years. The term was coined by sociologists John and Ruth Useem in the 1950s and was popularized by the 1999 book Third Culture Kids: Growing Up Among Worlds by David C. Pollock and Ruth E. Van Reken.

Definition and categories

TCKs typically include the children of diplomats, military personnel, missionaries, international business employees, and aid workers. Pollock and Van Reken emphasized that the TCK builds relationships with several cultures without fully owning any, and that the resulting sense of belonging is in relationship to others of similar experience rather than to a place.

Related categories include:

  • Military brats — children of career military families.
  • Missionary kids (MKs) — children of missionaries abroad.
  • Cross-cultural kids (CCKs) — a broader category including children of immigrants, refugees, and bi-cultural families.
  • Adult third culture kids (ATCKs) — TCKs who have reached adulthood.

Characteristics

Research describes common strengths — cross-cultural competence, adaptability, multilingualism, observational skills — alongside challenges such as rootlessness, delayed adolescence, difficulties with identity and commitment, and grief for people and places left behind. TCKs are disproportionately represented in international careers and are more likely than average to earn advanced degrees and to live abroad as adults.

Notable examples

Barack Obama, who spent part of his childhood in Indonesia, is frequently cited as an adult third culture kid; others commonly mentioned include actress Julie Christie and chef Anthony Bourdain.
