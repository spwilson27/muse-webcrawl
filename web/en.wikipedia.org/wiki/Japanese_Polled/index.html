The Japanese Polled (Japanese: 無角和牛, Mukaku Wagyū) is a critically endangered breed of small Japanese beef cattle. It is one of the six native Japanese cattle breeds, and one of the four breeds known as wagyū, the others being the Japanese Black, the Japanese Brown, and the Japanese Shorthorn.

History

Cattle were brought to Japan from China via the Korean Peninsula in about the second century AD, to provide draught power for cultivation. For more than a thousand years their use was largely confined to farm work. In the early twentieth century, native cattle in Yamaguchi Prefecture were crossbred with British polled breeds, particularly the Aberdeen Angus, in an effort to improve them. The resulting hornless population was recognised as a distinct breed, the Japanese Polled, in 1944.

Characteristics

The breed is distinguished by its complete absence of horns, a trait inherited from its Angus ancestry. Coat colour is usually black, though brown animals occur. The cattle are small and hardy, but the breed's intramuscular fat (marbling) is generally lower than that of the Japanese Black.

Population

Numbers declined sharply in the later twentieth century as farmers favoured the more heavily marbled Japanese Black. By the early twenty-first century the breed numbered only a few hundred head, and it is listed as critically endangered. Conservation efforts concentrate on herds in Yamaguchi Prefecture.
