Center of gravity of an aircraft

The CG must stay within limits specified in the flight manual.

The center of gravity (CG) of an aircraft is the point at which the aircraft would balance if suspended there — the average location of its mass. Its position, determined by the loading of fuel, passengers, cargo, and the airframe itself, is critical to the aircraft's stability and control.

Importance

An aircraft rotates in pitch, roll, and yaw about its center of gravity. For longitudinal stability, the CG must lie within a certified range relative to the wing's mean aerodynamic chord. A CG too far forward makes the aircraft nose-heavy, increasing stall speed and elevator forces; a CG too far aft reduces pitch stability and can make the aircraft dangerously difficult or impossible to control.

Calculation

Pilots and load planners perform weight-and-balance calculations before flight: each item's weight multiplied by its distance (arm) from a reference datum gives its moment, and the total moment divided by total weight yields the CG position. Load sheets or electronic systems verify that both total weight and CG remain within the limits stated in the aircraft flight manual for take-off, flight, and landing.
