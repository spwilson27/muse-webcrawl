The Defense Commissary Agency (DeCA), headquartered at Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia, is an agency of the United States Department of Defense that operates the military commissaries — grocery stores on military installations — serving active-duty service members, reservists, retirees, and their families around the world.

History

The commissary benefit dates to the nineteenth century, when the U.S. Army began selling goods at cost to soldiers. Each military service operated its own commissary system until 1991, when the separate service systems were consolidated into DeCA as part of a Defense Management Review initiative, creating a single agency running about 235 stores in some 13 countries.

Benefit

Commissaries sell groceries at cost plus a surcharge (5 percent) that funds construction and modernization of stores. The agency estimates that shoppers save roughly 25 percent compared with commercial grocery prices, making the commissary one of the most valued non-pay benefits of military service, particularly for junior enlisted families and retirees.
