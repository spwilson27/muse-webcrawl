Michael III

Reign: 842–867
Dynasty: Amorian
Born: 9/10 January 840, Constantinople
Died: 23/24 September 867, Constantinople

Michael III (Greek: Μιχαήλ; 840 – 23/24 September 867), sometimes given the epithet "the Drunkard" (ὁ Μέθυσος), was Byzantine emperor from 842 to 867. He was the last member of the Amorian dynasty, and his reign saw the final end of Byzantine iconoclasm, the mission of Saints Cyril and Methodios to the Slavs, and a military revival against the Abbasid Caliphate.

Regency and early reign

Michael succeeded his father Theophilos as an infant, under the regency of his mother Theodora and her adviser Theoktistos. In 843 the regency restored the veneration of icons, an event still celebrated as the Triumph of Orthodoxy. As Michael matured he sidelined his mother, and power was exercised with his uncle Bardas, who promoted learning and founded a school at the Magnaura palace under Leo the Mathematician.

Achievements

Under Michael's generals, Byzantine armies won major victories over the Arabs, culminating in the destruction of the emirate of Melitene's forces at the Battle of Lalakaon (863). In 863 the brothers Cyril and Methodios were sent to evangelise Great Moravia, devising the Glagolitic alphabet — the foundation of Slavic literacy. The patriarch Photios I also advanced scholarship and clashed with Rome, beginning the so-called Photian schism. Michael's court promoted the favourite Basil, a peasant-born groom of Armenian descent, raising him to co-emperor in 866.

Assassination

When Michael appeared to be turning against him, Basil had the emperor murdered in his bedchamber on the night of 23–24 September 867 and seized the throne, founding the Macedonian dynasty. Later Macedonian-era historians, justifying the usurpation, painted Michael as a drunken incompetent; modern scholars regard his reign as considerably more successful than that hostile tradition suggests.
