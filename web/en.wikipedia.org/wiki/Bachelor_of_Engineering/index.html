Common abbreviationsBE, BEng, BSEng, BASc

A Bachelor of Engineering (BE or BEng) is an undergraduate academic degree awarded to a student who has completed a programme of study in engineering. Depending on the country and institution, the programme lasts between three and five years and typically includes coursework in mathematics, natural sciences, and a chosen engineering discipline, often culminating in a major design project or thesis.

Structure

Bachelor of Engineering programmes generally begin with a foundation in calculus, physics, chemistry, computing, and engineering fundamentals such as statics, dynamics, thermodynamics, and circuits. Students then specialize in a branch such as mechanical, electrical, civil, chemical, or software engineering. Most accredited programmes require laboratory work, design projects, and instruction in engineering ethics, economics, and communication.

By country

In the United Kingdom, the BEng is typically a three-year degree (four in Scotland), while the integrated MEng extends to four or five years and satisfies the educational requirement for Chartered Engineer status. In Australia, the BE is a four-year honours degree accredited by Engineers Australia. In India, the Bachelor of Technology (BTech) and BE are the standard four-year engineering qualifications, admitting students through entrance examinations. In the United States, the equivalent qualification is usually the Bachelor of Science in Engineering (BSE), accredited by ABET.

Accreditation and licensure

Professional accreditation bodies such as ABET (United States), the Engineering Council (United Kingdom), and Engineers Australia evaluate programmes against agreed standards. International recognition is facilitated by the Washington Accord, under which signatory countries recognize the substantial equivalence of each other's accredited engineering degrees. Graduation from an accredited programme is usually the first step toward licensure as a professional engineer.
