François-Étienne-Christophe Kellermann (28 May 1735 – 23 September 1820), Duke of Valmy, was a French military commander who served in the armies of the monarchy, the Republic, and the Empire. He is best remembered for commanding the French army at the Battle of Valmy in September 1792, the celebrated "cannonade" that halted the Prussian invasion and saved the young French Republic. In 1804 he was one of the first men named a Marshal of the Empire by Napoleon.

Early career

Kellermann was born in Strasbourg into a family originally from Saxony. He entered the French army as a cadet in 1752 and served with distinction through the Seven Years' War and in Poland. By the outbreak of the French Revolution he had risen to the rank of maréchal de camp (major general), the product of forty years of service under the old regime.

Valmy

In 1792 Kellermann took command of the Army of the Centre. On 20 September 1792 his army joined that of General Dumouriez and confronted the invading Prussian army of the Duke of Brunswick at Valmy. In a long artillery duel fought in rain and mud, the French lines held; Kellermann famously raised his hat on the point of his sword, prompting his troops to shout "Vive la Nation!" The Prussians withdrew, and Goethe, who was present, declared that a new epoch in world history had begun. The victory allowed the National Convention to proclaim the Republic the next day.

Later career

Kellermann was briefly imprisoned during the Terror on suspicion of royalist sympathies but was acquitted. He later commanded the Army of the Alps in a largely administrative capacity. Napoleon employed the elderly general mainly in organisational and reserve roles, made him a senator, and in 1804 named him one of the four honorary Marshals of the Empire; in 1808 he received the title Duke of Valmy. He supported the Bourbon Restoration in 1814 and died in Paris in 1820. His name is inscribed on the Arc de Triomphe, and his heart is buried at Valmy alongside his soldiers.
