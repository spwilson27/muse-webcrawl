Cerrejón Formation

The Cerrejón Formation is a geologic formation in Colombia, in the Cesar-Ranchería Basin of La Guajira Department, dating to the Middle to Late Paleocene, roughly 60 to 58 million years ago. It is famous for its exceptionally preserved fossils of the earliest known Neotropical rainforest ecosystem, recovered after the end-Cretaceous mass extinction, and it hosts the enormous Cerrejón coal mine, one of the largest open-pit coal mines in the world.

Palaeontology

The formation yielded Titanoboa cerrejonensis, the largest snake known to have existed, estimated at about 13 metres long, along with giant turtles such as Carbonemys and Cerrejonemys, crocodylians, and diverse fossil leaves indicating a warm, wet rainforest with mean annual temperatures around 30 °C or higher. The fossils, described in a series of papers from 2009 onward, provided the first detailed picture of a tropical ecosystem recovering after the asteroid impact that ended the Cretaceous.
