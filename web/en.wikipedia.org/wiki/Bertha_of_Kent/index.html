Bertha of Kent

Bertha (c. 565 – in or after 601) was a Frankish princess who became Queen of Kent as wife of King Æthelberht. A Christian in a pagan kingdom, her religious influence is credited with paving the way for the Gregorian mission and the Christianisation of Anglo-Saxon England.

Bertha of Kent
Born: c. 565, Frankish Neustria
Died: c. 601, Canterbury
Spouse: Æthelberht of Kent
House: Merovingian
Venerated: Saint, feast day 1 May (some calendars)

Life

Daughter of Charibert I of Paris, Bertha married Æthelberht on condition she could practise Christianity, bringing Bishop Liudhard as chaplain and worshipping at the church of St Martin in Canterbury. In 597 Augustine's mission from Pope Gregory I arrived; Æthelberht converted in 597 or 601, traditionally regarded as the beginning of the English Church. St Martin's Church, Canterbury, associated with Bertha, is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
