Wallonia (French: Wallonie; Walloon: Waloneye), officially the Walloon Region, is one of the three regions of Belgium — alongside Flanders and the Brussels-Capital Region — covering the southern, predominantly French-speaking part of the country. Its capital is Namur, and it is composed of five provinces: Hainaut, Liège, Luxembourg, Namur and Walloon Brabant.

History

The region formed part of the Prince-Bishopric of Liège and the Spanish and Austrian Netherlands before French annexation and, after 1815, the United Kingdom of the Netherlands; it joined the Belgian Revolution of 1830. In the 19th century Wallonia was an early industrial powerhouse based on coal and steel along the Sambre–Meuse valley, the sillon industriel. Post-war decline of heavy industry pushed the region toward regional autonomy, achieved through Belgium's state reforms from 1970 to 1993, which created federal regions and language communities.

Culture and language

Most Walloons speak French; the region's indigenous Romance languages, Walloon and Picard, are now minority tongues recognised as regional languages. The east contains the German-speaking Community, centred on Eupen. Wallonia is known for its folklore carnivals (Binche, a UNESCO-recognised event), comic-strip art, cuisine including Liège waffles, and the Walloon rooster (coq hardi) as its emblem.
