Three Choirs Festival

GenreClassical choral music
DatesLate July – early August (one week)
LocationsGloucester, Hereford and Worcester cathedrals (rotating)
Foundedc. 1715

Gloucester Cathedral, one of the three rotating hosts of the festival.

The Three Choirs Festival is an annual festival of classical and choral music held in the west of England, rotating each year among the cathedrals of Gloucester, Hereford and Worcester — the "three choirs" of its title. With origins conventionally traced to about 1715, it is one of the oldest surviving music festivals in the world. For roughly a week in late July or early August, the host cathedral presents large-scale choral concerts, chamber music, recitals, talks and community events.

History

The festival grew out of annual "music meetings" of the lay clerks and choristers of the three cathedral choirs in the early eighteenth century, initially gatherings of the choirs themselves in which collections were taken for the widows and orphans of clergy. By the later eighteenth century the meetings had become substantial public festivals with massed choirs and orchestras performing oratorios, above all Handel's Messiah. The festival survived the decline in popularity of the great provincial Handel festivals during the nineteenth century, and it has been held almost without interruption since, pausing during the two World Wars and in 2020, when it was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Elgar and the twentieth century

The festival is particularly associated with Edward Elgar, who was born at Broadheath near Worcester and whose music became central to its programmes; many of his works were performed at the festival during his lifetime, and he conducted there frequently. In 1910, at Gloucester, Ralph Vaughan Williams's Fantasia on a Theme by Thomas Tallis received its first performance at the festival, and Herbert Howells, who heard it as a young man, later composed music inspired by the three cathedrals and their choirs. The festival has continued to commission and premiere new works, notably from British composers, alongside the staple English choral repertoire.

The performers

The core of each festival is the Three Choirs Festival Chorus, assembled from the choral societies of the three cities, joined by the host cathedral choir and a professional orchestra (for many years the Philharmonia Orchestra has been in residence). Guest soloists and conductors include leading figures in British choral music. The artistic director of the festival is the organist of the host cathedral for that year.
