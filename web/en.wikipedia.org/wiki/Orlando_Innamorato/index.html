Orlando Innamorato

Orlando falls in love with the pagan princess Angelica.

Orlando Innamorato (Orlando in Love) is an epic poem by the Italian Renaissance author Matteo Maria Boiardo, first published in 1483 (first two books) and 1495 (completed posthumously). Written in ottava rima, it retells the adventures of Charlemagne's paladin Orlando (Roland) and the other knights of the Carolingian cycle.

Plot and innovations

Boiardo's great innovation was to make the stern warrior Orlando fall hopelessly in love with the beautiful pagan princess Angelica, whose arrival at Charlemagne's court sets the knights — Christians and Saracens alike — pursuing her across a landscape of enchanted fountains, magical rings and duelling champions. The poem interlaces the Carolingian matter of France with Arthurian romance themes of love and enchantment, and with comic and fantastic episodes.

Influence

The poem was left unfinished because of the French invasion of Italy in 1494 and Boiardo's death the same year. Ludovico Ariosto took up its story and characters in Orlando Furioso (1516–1532), which surpassed its predecessor in fame; Francesco Berni later produced a rifacimento (rewriting) of Boiardo's poem in Tuscan that long displaced the original text. The Innamorato remains a landmark of Italian Renaissance literature for its wit, narrative interlace and humanising of the chivalric hero.
