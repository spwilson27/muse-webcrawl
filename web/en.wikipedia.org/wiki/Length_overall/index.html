Length overall

Length overall (LOA, o/a, o.a., or oa) is the maximum length of a vessel's hull measured parallel to the waterline. This length is important for docking and berthing, and is the figure most commonly used to describe the size of a boat. It is distinct from the length of the hull measured between perpendiculars (LBP) and from the waterline length (LWL).

Definition

Length overall is measured from the extreme forward point of the bow to the extreme aft point of the stern, including any fixed projections that are part of the hull structure, such as bowsprits on some traditional definitions, though modern ISO practice for small craft (ISO 8666) excludes removable fittings but includes integral structures. LOA determines the size of marina berth required and many harbour and canal charges.

Related measurements

  • Length at the waterline (LWL) — the length of the loaded waterline; determines hull speed for displacement vessels.
  • Length between perpendiculars (LBP) — measured between the forward and after perpendiculars; used in ship design calculations.
  • Registered length — the length recorded on the vessel's registration documents, which may exclude certain deck structures.

Use in sailing

For racing yachts, LOA is often a class rule parameter, and rating rules historically used mixtures of LOA and waterline length — for instance the Universal and International rules penalised overhangs that increase sailing length when heeled without counting fully in the measured length.
