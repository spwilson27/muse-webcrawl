Tango music

Stylistic originsHabanera, milonga, candombe, European salon music
Cultural originsLate 19th century, Río de la Plata (Buenos Aires, Montevideo)
Typical instrumentsBandoneón, violin, piano, double bass, guitar

Tango music is a musical genre that originated in the working-class port areas of the Río de la Plata — principally Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Montevideo, Uruguay — in the late 19th century, in fusion with the dances and music of European immigrants, African-descended communities and creole inhabitants. It is traditionally performed by a orquesta típica built around the bandoneón (a German concertina) with violins, piano and double bass, and is inseparable from the tango dance.

History

Early tango drew on the habanera rhythm, the faster milonga, and Afro-Rioplatense candombe. Around 1917 the sung tango (tango-canción) emerged with Carlos Gardel's recording of "Mi noche triste"; Gardel became the genre's first international star before his death in a 1935 plane crash in Medellín. The 1940s were tango's Golden Age of dance orchestras — those of Juan D'Arienzo, Carlos Di Sarli, Aníbal Troilo, Osvaldo Pugliese and others filled the salons of Buenos Aires. From the 1950s Astor Piazzolla's nuevo tango fused tango with classical and jazz elements for listening rather than dancing. Later currents include the electronic tango of Gotan Project and Bajofondo. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed tango on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Characteristics

Tango is most often in 4/4 (or the earlier 2/4), with strongly marked beats, syncopated phrasing, dramatic dynamic contrasts and a characteristic "dragging" (arrastre) attack. Lyrics — often in the Lunfardo slang of Buenos Aires — treat love, loss, nostalgia for a vanishing neighbourhood, and the passage of time.
