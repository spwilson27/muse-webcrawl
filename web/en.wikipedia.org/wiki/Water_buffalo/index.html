Water buffalo

Scientific nameBubalus bubalis
FamilyBovidae
OriginSouth and Southeast Asia
UsesMilk, meat, draught

The water buffalo (Bubalus bubalis), also called the domestic water buffalo or Asian water buffalo, is a large bovid domesticated in Asia more than 5,000 years ago. It is one of the most important livestock animals in the world, providing milk, meat and draught power to hundreds of millions of people, particularly in South and Southeast Asia.

Types and distribution

Two main types are recognised: the river buffalo, found from India westward to Egypt and Europe and selected for milk production, and the swamp buffalo, found from Assam through Southeast Asia to China and used mainly for draught. Water buffalo have spread to every continent; Italy's river buffalo produce the milk for mozzarella di bufala, and feral herds roam northern Australia. The wild water buffalo (Bubalus arnee), ancestor of the domestic form, is endangered.

Characteristics and use

Water buffalo are well adapted to hot, wet environments, wallowing in water or mud to cool themselves because they sweat poorly. They thrive on coarse forage and flooded pastures unsuitable for cattle, and are the traditional draught animal of rice paddies. Buffalo milk is richer in fat and protein than cow's milk. In several Asian cultures the animals figure prominently in festivals and religious rituals.
