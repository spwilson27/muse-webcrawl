Virginia dynasty

The Virginia dynasty is a term sometimes used to describe the fact that four of the first five presidents of the United States were from Virginia. The term also can refer to a political alliance or network of Virginia politicians who dominated the young republic. Thomas Jefferson (1801–1809), James Madison (1809–1817), and James Monroe (1817–1825) served three consecutive two-term presidencies, following George Washington (1789–1797), also a Virginian. Only John Adams of Massachusetts interrupted the sequence.

Background

Virginia was the most populous of the original thirteen states and produced an outsized share of the revolutionary generation's political leadership. Its planter aristocracy — educated at the College of William & Mary and steeped in Enlightenment political thought — supplied not only presidents but secretaries of state, attorneys general, and Supreme Court justices, including Chief Justice John Marshall.

The dynastic succession

Jefferson, as Washington's secretary of state and then as president, built the Democratic-Republican Party organisation that carried his protégés Madison and Monroe — each of whom had served as his secretary of state — into the presidency in turn. This pattern of the secretary of state succeeding to the presidency reinforced the perception of a managed Virginia succession. Monroe's two terms, culminating in the Monroe Doctrine of 1823, marked the dynasty's peak and the near-collapse of the rival Federalist Party.

Decline

The contested election of 1824 ended the dynasty: although John Quincy Adams of Massachusetts won the presidency in the House of Representatives, the Virginia candidate William H. Crawford had suffered a stroke, and the fracturing of the Democratic-Republican Party into competing factions ended Virginia's half-century of dominance. Nonetheless, Virginians continued to hold the presidency — William Henry Harrison (born in Virginia), John Tyler, Zachary Taylor, and later Woodrow Wilson — bringing the total number of Virginia-born presidents to eight, more than any other state.
