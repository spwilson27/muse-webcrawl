Dies Irae ("Day of Wrath") is a Latin sequence poem attributed to the 13th-century Franciscan Thomas of Celano. It describes the Last Judgment, when the trumpet will summon souls before the throne of God, the saved will be delivered and the unsaved cast into eternal flames. For centuries it formed part of the Roman Catholic Requiem Mass for the dead, and its chant melody is among the most quoted themes in Western classical music.

Text and structure

The poem consists of rhymed tercets in trochaic metre. It moves from the terrors of Judgment Day — the world dissolving in ashes, the book of deeds opened — to a personal plea for mercy: "Recordare, Jesu pie" and the closing "Lacrimosa" ("that tearful day") and "Pie Jesu Domine, dona eis requiem" ("Merciful Lord Jesus, grant them rest"). Scriptural imagery draws especially on Zephaniah 1:15–16 and the Gospels.

Musical legacy

After the liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council, the sequence ceased to be obligatory at Requiems, but its text had already been set by composers from Mozart, Berlioz and Verdi to Britten (in the War Requiem). The original chant melody — four descending phrases — became a musical symbol of death and doom, quoted by Berlioz, Liszt, Saint-Saëns (Danse macabre), Rachmaninoff, Mahler and many film composers.
