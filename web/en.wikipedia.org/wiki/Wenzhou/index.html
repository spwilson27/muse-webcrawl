Wenzhou

Wenzhou (Chinese: 温州) is a prefecture-level city in Zhejiang province, southeastern China, situated on the Ou River near the East China Sea. It has a municipal population of over 9 million and is known as a centre of private enterprise, manufacturing and trade.

History

Wenzhou has been an important port since the Tang dynasty and was one of the first Chinese cities opened to foreign trade under the 1842 Treaty of Nanking. Its relative isolation, hemmed in by mountains, fostered a distinctive Wu Chinese dialect and a strong diaspora community. In the reform era after 1978, Wenzhou became famous for the "Wenzhou model" of development — growth driven by small private and family businesses, in contrast to state-led models elsewhere in China.

Economy and culture

Today Wenzhou is a major producer of shoes, clothing, eyewear, electrical appliances and lighters, and its merchants and diaspora — including a prominent community in Europe — are renowned for entrepreneurship. Landmarks include the Jiangxin Islet on the Ou River, the Yandang Mountains to the northeast, and the Nanxi River scenic area. The city is also notable for its large Christian population, earning it the nickname "China's Jerusalem".
