Independence National Historical Park is a federally protected historic district in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, preserving several sites associated with the American Revolution and the founding of the United States. Sometimes called "America's most historic square mile," the park encompasses Independence Hall, the Liberty Bell, and numerous other eighteenth-century buildings in the city's Old City and Society Hill neighbourhoods.

Sites

Independence Hall, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is where the Declaration of Independence (1776) and the United States Constitution (1787) were debated and adopted. Across the street, the Liberty Bell Center houses the Liberty Bell, the cracked bell that became an enduring symbol of American independence. Other sites include Congress Hall, Old City Hall, Carpenters' Hall (meeting place of the First Continental Congress), the First and Second Banks of the United States, Franklin Court, and the restored house of the bookseller and the President's House memorial.

History

The park was authorized by Congress on June 28, 1948, and formally established in 1956. Its creation involved clearing later buildings to recreate a colonial setting around Independence Square, including the development of Independence Mall to the north.
