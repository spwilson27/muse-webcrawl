United States Army Corps of Engineers

The United States Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) is a U.S. Army engineer formation and federal agency, consisting of some 37,000 civilian and military personnel, making it one of the world's largest public engineering, design, and construction management agencies. Though generally associated with dams, canals, and flood protection for the United States, USACE is also involved in military construction and in environmental regulation of waters and wetlands.

Missions

USACE's civil works programme builds and operates locks and dams, hydroelectric power stations, flood-control levees, and navigation channels on rivers such as the Mississippi, Ohio, and Columbia, and dredges hundreds of ports and harbours. It issues permits for work in navigable waters and wetlands under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act and Section 10 of the Rivers and Harbors Act. Its military programme provides engineering and construction for the Army and Air Force worldwide, and disaster-response support in hurricanes and floods.

History

The Corps traces its origins to 1775, when the Continental Congress appointed a chief engineer for the Continental Army; the corps was established permanently in 1802, the same year it founded the Military Academy at West Point—long the nation's principal engineering school. Nineteenth-century topographical engineers explored the West; twentieth-century Corps projects included the Panama Canal Zone works (in cooperation), the Pentagon, Manhattan Project facilities built with the Corps' Manhattan District, and post-disaster reconstruction from Hurricane Katrina's aftermath.
