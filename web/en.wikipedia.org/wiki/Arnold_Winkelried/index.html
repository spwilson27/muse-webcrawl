Arnold Winkelried

Arnold Winkelried (or Arnold von Winkelried) is a legendary hero of Swiss history. According to sixteenth-century Swiss historiography, at the Battle of Sempach (9 July 1386) Winkelried opened a breach in the Austrian phalanx by throwing himself upon the pikes of the Habsburg soldiers, gathering a sheaf of them into his own body and crying out that he would make a road for his comrades — thereby enabling the Swiss Confederates to break through and win the battle.

The legend

The earliest near-contemporary accounts of Sempach do not name Winkelried; the story of the hero who gathered the pikes appears in chronicles from the late fifteenth century (in unnamed form) and is attached to the Winkelried family of Stans by the sixteenth-century historian Aegidius Tschudi. In the familiar version, seeing that the Swiss could not break the Austrian wall of pikes, Winkelried called out "I will open a passage for you; protect, dear confederates, my wife and children" and embraced as many pikes as he could reach, dragging them down with his body. The Swiss then charged over him into the gap.

Historicity

Historians debate whether a historical Arnold Winkelried existed. A Winkelried family is documented at Stans, and an "Erni Winkelried" appears in records of the period, but the heroic deed itself is generally regarded as legend modelled on similar tales, and some scholars have connected it to a genuine tactic or to the death of a standard-bearer. Regardless of its historicity, Winkelried became one of Switzerland's principal national heroes; the phrase Winkelried came to denote patriotic self-sacrifice, and monuments stand at Stans and Sempach.
