Tanzania, officially the United Republic of Tanzania, is a country in East Africa within the African Great Lakes region. It is bordered by Kenya and Uganda to the north, Rwanda, Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west, Zambia, Malawi and Mozambique to the south, and the Indian Ocean to the east. Tanzania is home to Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest mountain, and to the Serengeti, famous for the annual migration of about 1.5 million wildebeest.

History

The mainland, Tanganyika, was a German colony, then a British mandate after the First World War; the islands of Zanzibar were a British protectorate and an ancient trading sultanate. Tanganyika gained independence in 1961 under Julius Nyerere, Zanzibar in 1963; after a revolution in Zanzibar, the two united in 1964 to form Tanzania. Nyerere's socialist ujamaa policies shaped a strong national identity and made Swahili a unifying language, but the economy struggled until market reforms from the 1990s onward. The capital moved from Dar es Salaam to the central city of Dodoma, though many government functions long remained in Dar es Salaam.

Nature and economy

Tanzania's protected areas — Serengeti and Ngorongoro (where a vast volcanic caldera shelters dense wildlife), plus Selous/Nyerere National Park and Lake Tanganyika, the world's second-deepest freshwater lake — draw large numbers of tourists. The economy rests on agriculture, mining (gold, and tanzanite, a gemstone found only in Tanzania), tourism and growing natural-gas production. Victoria Falls lies to the south-west of the country on the Zambezi, outside Tanzania itself, in the same wider southern-African region.
