Microtransaction

A microtransaction is a business model where users can purchase virtual goods with micropayments. Microtransactions are often used in free-to-play games to provide a revenue source for developers, and range from cosmetic items such as skins to gameplay-affecting content, extra lives, in-game currency or loot boxes.

History

Microtransactions grew with social and mobile gaming in the late 2000s, in games such as FarmVille and later Candy Crush Saga and Clash of Clans. They spread to full-price console and PC titles, provoking consumer backlash in prominent cases — for example, the controversy over Star Wars Battlefront II (2017), whose progression-linked loot boxes led Electronic Arts to suspend the system before launch. A small fraction of players, colloquially called "whales", account for a large share of spending.

Controversy and regulation

Critics compare some microtransaction mechanics — especially randomised loot boxes — to gambling, and argue they exploit children and addictive design. Belgium and the Netherlands moved to restrict loot boxes, while other jurisdictions have required disclosure of odds or probability rates. Major platform holders and publishers now commonly label games containing in-game purchases.
