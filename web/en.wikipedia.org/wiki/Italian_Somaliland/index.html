Italian Somaliland (Italian: Somalia Italiana) was a colony of the Kingdom of Italy in present-day northeastern, central and southern Somalia. It was formed in 1889 from protectorates and concessions along the Benadir coast and expanded inland until, in 1936, it was merged with Eritrea and conquered Ethiopia into Italian East Africa. British forces occupied it in 1941; Italy returned in 1950 to administer it as a UN Trust Territory for ten years, preparing it for independence.

Establishment and expansion

Italian penetration began with treaties with the sultans of Hobyo and the Majeerteen in 1888–89 and the leasing of Benadir ports from the Sultan of Zanzibar. In 1905 Italy bought the Benadir outright and organised the colony of Somalia Italiana; resistance inland, including that associated with Mohammed Abdullah Hassan's Dervish movement, continued for decades. In 1925 Britain ceded Jubaland (Oltre Giuba) under the terms of the Treaty of London. Governor Cesare Maria De Vecchi's campaigns of the 1920s disarmed the northern sultanates and extended direct administration.

Development and war

Colonial investment centred on Mogadishu — which acquired a cathedral, the port and the Villaggio Duca degli Abruzzi agricultural schemes growing bananas and sugar — and on roads linking the coast to Ethiopia. In 1935–36 Somalia served as the southern springboard for the invasion of Ethiopia. During the Second World War Italian forces briefly occupied British Somaliland (August 1940) before the British East African campaign conquered Italian Somaliland in early 1941. Under the 1949 UN settlement Italy returned as administering authority of the Trust Territory of Somaliland (1950–1960), and on 1 July 1960 the territory united with the former British Somaliland to form the Somali Republic.
