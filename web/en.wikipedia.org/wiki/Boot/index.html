Boot

Boot
Type: Footwear
Covers: Foot and ankle; often part of the leg
Materials: Leather, rubber, synthetic fabrics
Varieties: Work, hiking, riding, fashion, combat boots

A boot is a type of footwear that typically covers the foot and the ankle, and in many designs extends up the leg, sometimes as far as the knee or hip. Boots are generally heavier and more protective than shoes and are worn for work, outdoor activity, riding, military use, and fashion.

History

Boots are among the oldest forms of footwear; early examples combined separate leggings with sturdy shoes, and the oldest known depiction of boots dates to roughly 15,000 years ago in Spanish cave paintings. Boots have long been associated with horse riding — the heel helps keep the foot in the stirrup — and with the military, from Roman caligae to the hobnailed marching boots of modern armies. The Wellington boot, popularised by the Duke of Wellington, and later rubber rain boots became widespread in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Types

Common types include work boots with steel toe caps, hiking and mountaineering boots, cowboy boots, Chelsea boots, combat boots, ski boots, waders, and fashion boots in styles such as ankle, knee-high, and thigh-high boots. Boots are made from leather, rubber, or synthetic materials, often with waterproof membranes, insulation, reinforced toe caps, and lugged soles for traction.
