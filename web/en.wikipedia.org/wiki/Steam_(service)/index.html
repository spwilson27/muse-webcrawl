Steam (service)

Steam

Steam is a video game digital distribution service and storefront developed by Valve Corporation. Launched on September 12, 2003, initially as a system to automatically update Valve's games such as Counter-Strike and to distribute Half-Life 2, it grew into the dominant storefront for PC gaming, hosting well over 100,000 games, running seasonal sales events, and recording peak concurrent users in the tens of millions. The service is accessed through the Steam store and client software, and since 2022 through Valve's Steam Deck handheld console.

History

Valve built Steam to solve patching and anti-cheat problems for its online games. Opening the store to third-party publishers in 2005 (beginning with Rag Doll Kung Fu) turned it into a general marketplace; features such as Steamworks APIs, cloud saves, the Workshop for user-created modifications, user reviews, and trading cards followed. Steam Greenlight (2012) and later Steam Direct let independent developers publish with minimal curation, greatly expanding the catalogue. Valve typically takes a 30 percent revenue share, a rate criticised by some developers and by rivals such as the Epic Games Store, which launched in 2018 charging 12 percent.

Features and hardware

Steam provides digital rights management, matchmaking servers, anti-cheat (VAC), friend lists and chat, in-game overlay, Remote Play streaming and the Proton compatibility layer that lets Windows games run on Linux. Valve's hardware includes the Steam Deck (2022), a successful handheld PC, and earlier efforts such as Steam Machines and the Steam Controller. Steam's annual sales — the Summer and Autumn/Winter sales — are major events in gaming retail.

Impact

Steam is credited with legitimising digital distribution for major games and with sustaining a large independent-games ecosystem. It has faced criticism over content moderation, regional censorship, gambling-like mechanics in game item markets, and its review-bombing problems, addressed through review histograms and other tools.
