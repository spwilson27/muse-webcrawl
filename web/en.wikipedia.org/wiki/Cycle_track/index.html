Cycle track

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cycle track
TypeSegregated cycling infrastructure
Also calledProtected bike lane, separated bikeway, greenway (US)
Separation methodsKerb, bollards, parked cars, verge, grade difference

A cycle track is a bikeway that is physically separated from motor vehicle traffic and distinct from the sidewalk. Unlike a conventional bike lane, which is marked only by paint on the carriageway, a cycle track uses a physical barrier — a kerb, verge, bollards, planters, or a row of parked cars — between cyclists and moving traffic.

Design

Cycle tracks may be one-way (paired on both sides of a street) or two-way. Best practice, developed extensively in the Netherlands and Denmark, includes protected intersections where turning motorists cross the track at a shallow angle with good sight lines, and careful handling of bus stops and loading areas. Surface quality, drainage, and snow clearance are recurring maintenance issues in colder climates.

Benefits and criticism

Studies associate protected tracks with higher cycling rates, especially among women, children, and older riders, and with lower injury risk per trip. Criticisms include cost, loss of parking or traffic lanes, and conflicts at junctions if designs are poor. Advocates argue separation is the key precondition for mass cycling.
