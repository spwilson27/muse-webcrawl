Range (aeronautics)

The range of an aircraft is the maximum distance it can fly between takeoff and landing on a given fuel load, as limited by fuel capacity and consumption, environmental conditions, and reserves required by regulation. The related quantity ferry range is the maximum range with no payload, achieved with maximum fuel and minimal equipment.

Theory

Range is governed by the Breguet range equation, which shows that distance increases with the lift-to-drag ratio, with cruise speed (for jets), and with the logarithm of the ratio of initial to final weight. For propeller aircraft, range is maximised at the speed for maximum lift-to-drag ratio; for jets, at a higher speed approximating maximum L/D adjusted for thrust-specific fuel consumption.

In practice

Published ranges are nominal still-air figures; actual achievable range depends on payload, winds, altitude, temperature, and diversion and reserve requirements. Combat aircraft quote a combat radius — the distance out and back with time over target — which is substantially less than ferry range.
