HSwMS Gustav V

HSwMS Gustav V was a Sverige-class coastal defence ship of the Swedish Navy, the third and last of her class, named after King Gustaf V. Launched in 1918 and commissioned in 1922, she served as a flagship of the Coastal Fleet, guarded Swedish neutrality in the Second World War, and remained in service until 1957; she survived longest of her sisters, being scrapped in 1970.

FateScrapped 1970

Service

Built by Kockums at Malmö, Gustav V underwent significant modernisation in the late 1930s, including oil-fired boilers and improved anti-aircraft weapons. In 1940 she is associated with the Åland affair and neutrality patrols in the Baltic; after the war she made goodwill visits and served in training roles. From 1961 she was moored at Berga as an accommodation and training hulk until sold for scrap in 1970.
