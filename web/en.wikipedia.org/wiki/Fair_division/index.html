Fair division

Fair cake-cutting is the archetypal fair division problem

Fair division is the problem in economics and game theory of dividing a set of resources among several people who have different preferences, so that each person receives a share they regard as fair. It is often illustrated by the problem of fairly cutting a cake whose parts are valued differently by different recipients.

Fairness criteria

Several criteria for fairness are studied:

  • Proportionality: each of n participants receives at least 1/n of the resource by their own valuation.
  • Envy-freeness: no participant prefers another's share to their own.
  • Equitability: all participants feel they received the same fraction of their own valuation.
  • Efficiency (Pareto optimality): no alternative division makes someone better off without making someone worse off.

Procedures

The simplest procedure, "I cut, you choose" (divide and choose), guarantees an envy-free division between two people. For more parties there are moving-knife procedures and discrete algorithms of increasing complexity; the existence of envy-free cake-cutting protocols for any number of players with a bounded number of queries was only established in 2016 by Aziz and Mackenzie. Related problems concern indivisible goods, where criteria such as envy-freeness up to one item (EF1) are used, and practical applications include divorce settlements, inheritance, rent division, treaty negotiation and the allocation of spectrum and airport landing slots.
