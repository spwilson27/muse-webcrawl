General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon

The General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon is an American single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft developed by General Dynamics for the United States Air Force (USAF) and now produced by Lockheed Martin. Designed as an air superiority day fighter, it evolved into a successful all-weather multirole aircraft, and with over 4,600 built it is among the most numerous Western fighters in service. Its unofficial name, "Viper", is widely used by its crews.

Development

The F-16 originated in the Lightweight Fighter program influenced by the "Fighter Mafia" and energy–maneuverability theory. The YF-16 won the 1975 fly-off against the YF-17 (which became the Navy's F/A-18). The design pioneered a frameless bubble canopy, side-mounted control stick, reclined seat, relaxed static stability and fly-by-wire controls, allowing sustained 9-g manoeuvres.

Service

The F-16 entered USAF service in 1978 and has been exported to more than 25 nations, with production lines in Europe and Turkey. It has fought in conflicts from the Bekaa Valley in 1982 and the Gulf War to operations over the Balkans, Afghanistan and the Middle East. Upgraded variants, notably the Block 70/72 F-16V with an AESA radar, remain in production, and the type is expected to serve into the 2040s.
